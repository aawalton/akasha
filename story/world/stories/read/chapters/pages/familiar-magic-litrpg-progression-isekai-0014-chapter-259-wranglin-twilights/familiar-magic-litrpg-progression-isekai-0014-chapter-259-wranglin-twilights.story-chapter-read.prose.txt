The first week on Potentia had gone better than Ethan had feared. They’d begun by setting up the operation, and it had been an enormous relief when Apollo succeeded in channeling Ethan’s Animus. Now as they made slow progress through a wide gulley, they were followed by over a thousand demons.

Ethan stood in a small observation platform–lounge, really–that Fable had created above the second train car. Selina sat drinking a margarita–they’d brought enough food for several years, just in case–and Apollo hovered above them. Between the red sky above, and rusty ground beneath them, Ethan found himself desperately missing the color blue.

“Does your demon have any sense that it’s working?” Apollo asked.

“Tomo still isn’t sure,” Ethan admitted. “Power differences between ranks are already enormous, but it seems that the difference in accumulated corruption is orders of magnitude greater.” He gestured at the disturbingly tranquil mob following a short way behind them, each demon connected by a thin thread of golden power.

“But there’s a thousand of these weird little guys!” Selina insisted. Amusingly, being on her home world had brought out another side of the woman, who admitted that she found many of the local demons “cute”.

Tomo appeared at Nator size, sitting next to Selina. “There are many, but their power is limited. Dawns and Dusks are like ants compared to the titans that are true contenders for The Throne. I cannot imagine how many of these we might require to become worthy of that which we seek.”

“We’re still nearly a continent away,” Selina said. “Surely there has to be enough demons between here and there that we can get what we need.”

“Perhaps,” Tomo acknowledged. “And perhaps Apollo can even bind that many. But have you considered the logistics? Have you considered the challenges that would come with organizing millions of demons? Getting them over mountains? Keeping them alive?”

“This road trip lacks positivity,” Ethan said, smiling at Tomo. The demon stared back, then finally shook his head in quiet amusement.

“Perhaps there are ways we can make this work,” he said. “The simplest would be to command the stronger demons to devour the weaker. No corruption is lost in such a process, and it would consolidate our armies.”

Ethan squirmed at that, and Selina looked heartbroken. Apollo just left it to Ethan, who sighed. “Potentia is a harsh world, and what you’re suggesting is happening all around us right now. We may need to cross that line for the sake of the mission, but we’re not there yet. Tomo, is there another way?”

“Only the obvious,” he growled. “Bind more powerful creatures.”

Ethan nodded. “That’s what I thought,” he said. He stood up, stretching and looking around. “My spirit hasn’t been able to attract anything beyond Dusk. That may be a good thing, given that our army hasn’t been attacked by anything strong enough to wipe out all the hard work we’ve done. At the same time, Tomo is likely right: this is unsustainable. But it also isn’t anything we hadn’t discussed already, it just means it’s time to move on to the next phase of the plan.”

“It’s exceptionally dangerous, Ethan,” Selina said. “And the reason it isn’t Plan A is because you’ll have to go alone; I’ll only be a liability out there.”

“I’m not alone,” Ethan said with a grin. “I have three insanely powerful friends who are always with me.” At his words, his black armor shifted around on his body, releasing faint metallic sounds. “Sorry, Prime, that was rude. Four! I have four friends, some more clothing-like than others.”

“Just be careful,” Apollo warned. “If you die, I die, and that would be a real tragedy.”

“Touching,” Ethan said to the man literally looking down at him. “Alright, then it’s decided, I’ll go for a hunt. As soon as I find something with Twilight power, I’ll lure it back in this direction. Then Apollo does his thing.”

“Stay in touch,” Selina said as Ethan leapt from the Fable Train.

Between Revan’s senses, and Tomo’s familiarity with Potentia, it didn’t take long to pick up the trail of a Twilight demon. The creature itself turned out to be hard to see, even when they were close enough to spot it among the remains of what must have been a very large meal. Tomo had no name for the thing they faced, red against a rusty hill, though he assured Ethan he did have memories of consuming some in his past lives.

Ethan, never the most adventurous eater himself, could only shudder at the thought. The demon was roughly Deevee’s size, and looked something like a scorpion with thick, metallic legs. Metal demons were as rare on Potentia as the Affinity was on Potentia, but Ethan wasn’t over concerned. Prime had begun as a metal monster, and learned the hard way how devastating flare could be to such creatures.

Still, as he looked over the massive demon, Ethan had to acknowledge there was more than one threat he’d have to watch out for. It had a large, segmented tail with a deadly stinger, big enough to pass entirely through Ethan’s body. It also had two different sets of claws, and those metallic legs had sharp spikes all across them.

“A dangerous opponent,” Tomo said. They crouched behind a nearby pile of rock and bones–common on Potentia.

“I’d be less concerned if I didn’t need to lead it five miles back to Apollo,” Ethan said. Then he shook his head. “Just acknowledging that Apollo can be five miles away is odd. He’s still so connected to me that I feel as if he’s watching from right over my shoulder.”

“I would notice the company,” Tomo said, making Ethan chuckle.

“True. Alright, I have to get this bastard’s attention, do enough damage to piss it off, then lure him back with me. Preferably before dark.”

“Potentia is not kind when the sun sets,” Tomo said.

“Not exactly a warm welcome when the sun is up, either,” Ethan muttered. “Alright, let’s get to work.”

He moved out from behind cover, cloaked in illusions. He had to move across a fair amount of open terrain to reach the demon, and his eyes watched every shadow for approaching threats as he did so. As he got closer, the sounds of the demon devouring the last of its meal grew louder, and the smell struck Ethan even through the poisonous taste of Potentia air.

He managed to line up a perfect strike, going entirely unnoticed, and even conjured and empowered his odachi. The blade he’d been gifted by King Rothavaro was more powerful, but ill suited to striking from stealth.

Just as he was about to swing, the ground shifted beneath his feet, and Ethan rolled backward down the slight incline. The beast–Ethan decided to just think of it as a scorpion–crouched low on its eight legs, looking for threats in every direction from its multifaceted eyes.

Ethan cursed softly as he slid to a stop, digging his powerful fingers into the hard ground to stop himself from shaking. There’d been a number of earthquakes since they arrived, and Tomo couldn’t say whether or not they were normal in this area. He wasn’t entirely sure where they were, and didn’t really pay attention to such things when he’d been lost in this world’s dark cycle.

“To hell with this”, Ethan grumbled, when earth still shook after more than a minute. Twilight strength sent him soaring through the air, blade raised, and he slashed down with the ruinous power of [Knife in the Dark]. The creature screeched from two mouths at once as its back leg fell free, and the painful evolution of the power left a crippling effect, slowing it.

Ethan landed, then darted between the scorpion’s legs, slashing upward at its body while it still didn’t know what was attacking. That proved short-lived as one the leg on either side bent inward, the sharp, clawed tips stabbing toward him. Ethan dodged one, but took a hit to the shoulder from the other side.

The force of the blow knocked him out from under the creature, rolling and crashing against sharp stones. Ethan felt grateful that Master Ulric had been able to replace his Dusk Rank regeneration rune, as his left arm could barely move as he regained his feet. He found himself staring down three sets of eyes as the Twilight monster roared a challenge, and the ground finally settled beneath him.

Ethan couldn’t help but be impressed as he saw the severed leg regrowing before his eyes, but his own arm was ready again for combat after under a minute, so he couldn’t complain. Plus, he clearly had the thing’s attention.

Daggers shot out in a fan toward the beast, which looked down at the tiny projectiles as if in disdain. While each one erupted into five different copies, they were still too small to do any real damage, and the scorpion marched forward, ignoring them. Until they exploded.

Ethan grinned as he closed the distance on the stunned creature, tossing more daggers as he went. These ones weren’t even charged, but the demon had learned a painful lesson, and tried to shuffle its enormous body out of the way. That only allowed Ethan the time he needed to close the distance without the enormous stinger catching him, and he slashed away at its underbelly a few more times before rushing out behind. He’d learned his lesson as well, and had no intention of being stabbed by another pointed leg.

Ethan was a few dozen paces away by the time the larger creature spun around, hissing, and he grinned, expecting that the chase was on. To his surprise, the creature ran.

“Hey, what the hell?” he demanded, racing after it. He hadn’t expected this–demons weren’t exactly known for their prudence, and this thing should consider itself the predator in this scenario. Yet it ran, and on that many legs it could move quickly.

Ethan had to switch to rifts to catch up, but approaching from behind only put him in easy reach of the large stinger. It struck again and again, each time Ethan got close. He was confused by its accuracy until he realized another set of eyes watched him from either side of the stinger itself, and Ethan had to shift his focus to eliminating the offensive limb as quickly as he could.

The beast didn’t make it easy, especially as it began moving rapidly up the side of a low mountain. It moved across the boulder-covered terrain erratically, and even when Ethan tossed dimensionally charged daggers ahead of it, he’d often find the elusive beast had shifted by the time he reappeared through a rift.

He was so distracted by the frustrating pursuit that he didn’t notice the new smell until they’d nearly cleared the mountain's summit. He paused on that red peak, looking down the other side in surprise, and breathing in the salty air.

Water stretched out as far as he could see. But it didn’t belong here. On Earth, he’d be hundreds of miles from the coast right now, and yet…just how bad were these earthquakes? How much had the landscape been changed?

He turned a glare on the cowardly demon as it skittered down the mountain, which was nearly sheer on this side. Ethan looked at the waves crashing against the stone below, and squinted at the darkness of it. It was far deeper than he might have expected, as if the world had split right here.

Still determined to catch the elusive and frustrating scorpion, he shot forward, trusting his climbing ability to keep him from slipping. Yet the demon got closer and closer to those waves, and Ethan found that he didn’t trust them. He had to find some way to lure the creature back up, possibly even another demon–there were always more around.

He’d begun eyeing the mountainside for suitable caves when the grey water erupted. Ethan froze, eyes wide and mouth agape as something like the mouth of a shark burst upward. It was easily three times as large as the scorpion, and dozens of rows of teeth crunched down on the Twilight beast with a snap that echoed like an explosion.

Ethan watched, horrified as the Champion rank demon disappeared back beneath the waves. But not before tentacles reached back up, questing about for more easy food.

Ethan was already chaining rifts into the sky, putting as much distance between himself and that great white kraken shark as he could.

“Stay away from the sea, right Ulric?” he muttered to himself. “Sound advice.”
