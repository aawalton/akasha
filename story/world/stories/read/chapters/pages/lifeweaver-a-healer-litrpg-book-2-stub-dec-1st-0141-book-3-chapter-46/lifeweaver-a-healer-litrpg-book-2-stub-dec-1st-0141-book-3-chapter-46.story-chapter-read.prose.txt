The culprit in the rustling bush turned out to be just what they all expected. A young female lion shot out from behind it with a quick dash and a leap that allowed it to close the distance in the blink of an eye.

Lurker Beast of Lightning. Level 24.

Even if Luke didn't get a good look at it in that initial rush, it was clear the monster wasn't your standard lion, even if the resemblance was there. First of all, it was much larger than one. Second, its claws were more pronounced, visible even mid-leap. Also, horns. In its initial assault, it held its head down, as if it was aiming to bury the pair of long, twisting, and pointed horns right into Elijah's chest. As if that wasn't enough, lightning crackled between both the horns and the claws, buzzing.

Lemira's wolf appeared out of thin air and threw itself at the lion's side, pushing them both down onto the ground. Both animals growled, but the wolf got its jaws around the lion's neck. Lightning flashed, electrocuting the wolf, but it didn't let go, not even when it started smoking.

"Um, Lemira?" Luke asked, waiting for her to do something.

"One moment, please," she said, already holding up her hand toward the lion. Her other hand went up as well, but this one she held toward the wolf, and a greenish glow streamed from her to her pet as she healed it back to something resembling good health.

It didn't take much longer for the lion's core to explode out of its chest in a shower of gore. Lightning crackled around the core's surface even as she pulled it into her inventory.

The wolf was in a bad way, even with Lemira's healing. Luke stepped up to it and reached in with a thread of mana. The healing didn't work. Luke saw the wounds like with a human, but neither Needle of Life nor brute-forcing the wounds closed with healing mana worked. There was nothing he could do. Weird.

"Only I can heal my pets," Lemira said, waving for the wolf to approach her. It got to its feet and lumbered over, keeping one paw off the ground. One of its eyes was gone, and long gashes along its belly bled profusely.

"You did well," Lemira told her pet as the wolf disappeared back into her inventory.

"Will it make it?" Luke asked.

She nodded. "Once back in my Stable, they feed off my mana while healing themselves. The wolf will not be able to help further today, but she will be well after some time."

"Didn't complete the quest," Curtis noted.

"That was clearly a female lion," Ray said. "If we're hunting the king, we have to go for the mane."

"There'll be more females, too," Hannah said.

Elijah, who'd drawn his weapon when the lion appeared, pointed it in the direction where they'd seen the pride. "Then we will have to subdue them for our comrade."

"I do not have the strength to hold all. Another female, perhaps, and the male. With three, I will consider the hunt a success."

Curtis's mantle of darkness formed around his shoulders, going all the way down to his thighs, while he held out a hand, willing his large two-handed blade to appear. The Reaver was getting serious now. "Let's go then. We kill all of them except the boss and one of its females."

In the end, they didn't even have to move. The first monster's growls must've reached the others, because the sound soon repeated over and over again in the bushes and small thicket of trees that separated the group from where they'd seen the pride.

"Oh, hell," Ray said, disappearing into stealth.

The shadows in Hannah's hair writhed and moved down along her body and arms, extending her reach. "Here they come," she said, voice trembling a little. Not from fear, Luke realized, but from excitement. He hadn't taken her for a battle-crazed person, but her eyes glittered with energy.

Two lionesses emerged first. These did not charge full tilt like the first one. Instead, they moved gracefully near the ground, circling the group on either side. A third joined them, then a fourth. Before the party made their move, six of them skulked around them, trying to get at their backs. No two were alike, and it was clear their different elements had a hand in the differences between them.

Lurker Beast of Warmth. Level 23.

Lurker Beast of Cold. Level 23.

Lurker Beast of Disease. Level 23.

Lurker Beast of Shadow. Level 23.

Lurker Beast of Chaos. Level 23.

Lurker Beast of Soil. Level 23.

Flames danced around the warmth one's fur, while flakes of snow stuck to the cold one as it stared at the group with eyes the color of a winter storm. Those two were the most normal of the bunch, if anything about the situation could be said to be normal. Pus oozed out of open sores in the diseased one's flesh, and its eyes had a white film over them, as if the creature was blind. It looked half-dead, but Luke sensed that was not the case.

The Lurker Beast of Shadow only had eyes for Hannah, and the two leveled measured glares at each other, as if they encroached on each other's territory. Finally, the chaos monster had a haze about it that made it difficult to keep your eyes on it, not unlike Luke's robe. This would not be a simple fight.

Curtis was about to attack the closest monster when the chaos lioness turned with lightning-quick reflexes, swiping at the air above her.

Ray let out a yelp as he tumbled through the air, his arm bleeding profusely from several parallel gashes. Rather than fall toward the group, he landed outside the ring of monsters, far from Luke's reach.

"Go!" Luke shouted, charging toward his friend. All hell broke loose in that instant. Rather than go through the chaos monster, Luke pushed off with threads of mana, leaping well over its head to land in a crouch right next to Ray. His arm was in a bad state, but not from the claws themselves. Something else was going on. The flesh twisted in on itself, pulling on the wound as if having a mind of its own, a mind that wanted to hurt Ray as much as possible.

"Fuuuuuuck," Ray said, his lips white and eyes wide as he watched his own body attempting to destroy him.

"Hold still and let me heal you," Luke said, pushing Ray back to the ground.

Ray used his free hand to point. "Lion!"

Luke didn't need to turn around to see that the chaos monster wasn't through with its prey. It approached behind his back. Preparing for the inevitable assault, Luke used Weaver's Renewal on the limb, restoring it to the state it'd been in before taking damage. With a wound like that, there was nothing he could do to conserve mana. You didn't heal chaos with needle and thread.

Just as he'd anticipated, the lioness of chaos leapt at his back when Luke healed Ray. Hannah let out a cry of warning, but Luke didn't need it.

The Healer's Moment froze time just before the monster would have slammed into him, and Luke showed it how Lemira wasn't the only one able to pull monster cores right out of a monster's chest. When time resumed, the chaos monster fell dead in the dirt.

Standing, he saw Elijah battling against the one Luke hadn't paid much attention to before. The Lurker Beast of Soil was half lioness, half mud-golem. Clumps of wet dirt stuck to its body in a way that made it difficult to see where earth ended and the fur began. When Elijah's sword of light struck it, the blade made deep grooves in the monster's body, but they reformed as soon as he withdrew.

Hannah and the shadow monster looked like they had merged, turning into one writhing mass in the middle of the field of battle. Lemira kept behind Elijah, since she didn't have an available pet to fight for her. She looked more annoyed than anything else, annoyed at not being able to help.

Curtis was pulling more than his weight, swinging in wild arcs to keep the remaining monsters from crowding in and pulling him to the ground. With tight, expert motions, he directed the elemental Lurker Beasts’ attacks to the side with his blade or turned a shoulder so they struck his cloak, where the darkness seemed to feed on the monsters, eating away their flesh.

Just as Luke finished up with Ray, Curtis activated his sphere of darkness. It enveloped him and the lionesses he'd been fighting. The disease one leapt back, avoiding the touch of darkness, and so stayed out of its range. Rather than charging into it, the monster displayed a measure of intelligence as it shifted its focus to Luke and Ray.

"It looks disgusting," Ray said. "Let me deal with it."

Luke glanced over his shoulder at Ray. "You sure? You've been sick before, and I wasn't a fan."

"I'll be fine," Ray said, giving Luke a wounded look. "I'm trying to make up for past sins here."

Luke gestured to the monster as a spellweave formed over its head. "Go ahead."

Not wanting Ray to run headfirst into what Luke interpreted would be some sort of gross ichor spray - recognizing spell patterns was coming more and more to him - he tore the spell apart with Destabilize Spellweave, relieved to find it was just within his reach since the monster charged forward while casting.

Ray drove his new weapon into the monster's disgusting neck, pulled back, and dodged to the side, avoiding a different sort of spray. Rather than blood, more pus pumped out of the wound, and the smell was enough to make Luke choke back the breakfast, trying to push up and out of his stomach.

The monster was far from dead, but Luke didn’t mind letting Ray continue the fight on his own. "Enjoy," he said, letting out a small burp of sheer disgust and nausea as he retreated.

Joining Elijah, Luke ganked the earth elemental-like lioness, extending a thread into it to drain mana and refill his own pool after healing Ray. The monster noticed right away, and Luke found himself flung to the right after being struck by a packed piece of dirt. The monster had formed the spellweave so fast that he hadn't had a chance to tear it apart. Coughing, he stood, gasping for breath since the strike had knocked the wind out of him, and found Elijah standing in front of him.

"Behind me, healer," Elijah said, directing another attack of light at their opponent.

Luke shook his head. "I'm way past that." Before his new friend could protest, Luke walked up to stand to Elijah's left. "Let's just kill it together."

"Do you want to keep this one, Lemira?" Elijah asked.

"No," she answered, pointing to Curtis's darkness domain. "I would appreciate another elemental pet."

"Very well," Elijah said, raising his sword to point straight up at the sky. A spellweave formed in an instant as the earth lioness's tail whipped back and forth. The monster moved from side to side, searching for an opening in which to pounce. Before the opening arrived, Luke witnessed light streaming down from above in the shape of a round pillar, slamming into the monster. Weaver's Perception told him that Elijah's mana dropped low from that one single attack, but it did its job.

When the pillar of light receded, the monster was nothing more than a heap of dirt and clumps of fur.

"Dead," Elijah said, before rushing off to help Ray, who danced around the diseased lioness. He was being careful, dodging its blows. Luke could see why. Any single attack from that thing would be sure to give you a disgusting mix of horrific illnesses and infections. That would be an altogether bad time.

Together, Elijah and Ray faced the diseased monster. Hannah still tangled with the shadow one, and Luke wasn't sure he could help there, since he couldn't tell which parts were Hannah's and which the lioness's. As luck would have it, he saw that Curtis's domain was about to fall.

Last time Luke saw that particular skill ending, it had lost its spherical shape before coming apart, almost as if something sliced it from the inside. This time was different. The domain shrank inward, becoming smaller little by little until Curtis was revealed standing in its center. The domain moved inward, but rather than disappearing, it seemed to be filling Curtis, turning him dark as a starless night.

The Reaver's sword had changed its shape a little. Rather than the wide, heavy-looking blade, it had now transformed into a more slender shape. It was still just as long, but the grace with which Curtis wielded it seemed to have doubled. Before Luke could even blink, Curtis struck, slicing forward with an attack that took the fire monster right in its side, shearing through its body like jelly. It died in an instant, leaving the cold monster standing. If you could call it standing.

Curtis collapsed on the ground, clawing at himself, pulling darkness away from his face, almost like it stopped him from breathing. It sloughed off onto the ground, disappearing into nothingness a second later.

Luke rushed to him. The cold lioness was long past fighting back. It lay there on the ground, panting. Even so, it glared at him with a low growl coming from within its throat. That growl turned into a yelp, and a moment later, a monster core exploded from its chest. Luke didn't need to look to know where it would go. Lemira had gotten her second lioness pet.

The diseased monster was dead, and Curtis hadn't received any injuries that needed tending. He was just exhausted. That left Hannah's fight. Shadow struggled against shadow, twisting and turning in on itself, both trying to get the upper hand. Little by little, eyes, so many eyes, and maws full of jagged teeth opened in the left-hand side of the heap. Those mouths began to consume the other pieces, and soon a huge mouth opened in the middle of what Luke understood to be Hannah. It resembled The Deep Dweller's maw a little too much for comfort. That was the beginning of the end.

The shadow lioness shrieked and made its best attempt at disentangling itself, but it was far too late for that. Within a minute, Hannah had consumed her opponent, leaving it dead without a speck of its shadow or flesh remaining.

Afterwards, her body reformed, and she stood there, panting and grunting, grabbing pieces of her own shadows to pull them back, as if fighting for control.

"Hannah?" Luke asked, taking a concerned step forward.

"Hng!" she grunted, heaving, so much of the shadow disappearing into her mouth and down her throat. Unable to get it all down, she doubled over, vomiting some back out. From the look of things, she activated some sort of skill, and the shadows stopped wriggling. Once limp, Hannah forced them into her hair and around her arms, where they settled, looking like strange, living tattoos.

Once she'd caught her breath, she straightened and looked at the group. "'Sup?"
