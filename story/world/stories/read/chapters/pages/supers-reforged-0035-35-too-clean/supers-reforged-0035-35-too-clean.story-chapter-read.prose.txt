There is nothing better than to have a nice latte together with your favorite people in the world after a long day of work. Nothing normal about it either, now that I think about it, given the fact that I had just about finished slaughtering monster creatures using a superpower because I was a superhero!

Sometimes I still struggle to believe it. Then reality reasserts itself, as it always does. “Ugh,” I groaned. “I feel so sticky.”

I had changed clothes in the car, of course, while Vera and Nadia had gone ahead and grabbed a table at the corner of the bistro, where it was nice and quiet. Curiously enough, it was the same bistro where we ran the police raid and I saved a woman from being crushed to death. Even though that had been not too long ago, there were no traces of the fight and the near tragedy.

Meanwhile, I was pretty sure I stank. I needed a shower before I could feel clean again.

“Well,” Nadia said. “At least you pulled that guy Antonio out of a rather sticky situation.”

Vera cocked an eyebrow at her. “Really?”

“Or maybe not,” my girlfriend corrected herself. She tapped on her phone, scrolling down. “3.7 stars on Google Maps. One literally says ‘food crap’ and nothing else.”

“At least the guy was funny,” I supplied.

“Yeah,” she argued. “Weird but funny.”

Vera didn’t seem 100% convinced, but the server came over to bring us some food.

“Ah,” I said. “We didn’t order these.”

“It’s on the house,” the woman said, and I realized that she was not just a server but rather, the owner. “Do you think I’d forget about you? You prevented a disaster, young man, so you are eating for free here for as long as I’m in charge. Which,” she added sternly. “Is for as long as this place is mine. I’ll bring more sandwiches. You look like you are starving.”

Then she stormed off before I could stop her. Nadia chuckled, while Vera looked like she was doing her best not to smile.

The sandwiches were very good, by the way, and I realized that I was indeed a bit famished after the job. Which wasn’t finished yet, but our part was over and now it was Vera’s time to shine as the manager. There were a couple of loose ends to tie up, chief of which was dealing with the source of the infestation. Antonio asked for a crew to patch up the sewer access, but she offered to send Kimberly instead, through official CRC channels.

This way we could be sure the job was handled the right way, and at the same time the mad scientist could improve his reputation and his standing with the CRC.

“What can I say,” Nadia raised her latte. “To Vera, best manager ever.”

“Right,” the woman in question said, righting a lock of her raven-black hair that had fallen on her face. “The free food here is nice, but remember that this is our official hero job debrief, not a social moment.”

“That excuse only worked when we thought we’d have to pay for the drinks,” I said. “We can drop the pretense now that we won’t have to charge the CRC’s card.”

Her eyes glinted. “Oh, but we will. It’s not right that Claire gives us free food when we’d have had the food for free anyway. The way I see it, this is the CRC’s way to support small businesses.”

Nadia said, “Bold. Or is it in the official regulations?”

“Discretionary budget allocation,” Vera said smugly.

We sat with that a moment, then our conversation returned to the topic of Antonio’s Quick Kitchen and we debated over whether it really was a crappy place or not. This time it was Vera who was pulling up more and more bad reviews, while I tried my best to defend the man. He was likable, if a bit strange, and it wasn’t like the place was pretending to be what it wasn’t. I could see that I was pulling Nadia to my side as she argued that the reviews were all written by stingy tourists who thought they could get Michelin tier food for pennies, and that we shouldn’t care anyway since Claire’s bistro was clearly superior.

“Clearly,” I agreed easily. “Even though we have never been to Antonio’s.”

“And you’d want to go eat at a ‘quick kitchen’?” Nadia asked.

“Acting like we have a lot of money, noted. And you don’t like my cooking, also noted.”

“It’s… I was joking…” she whined a little.

I pulled her in for a kiss. “I know sweetie,” I whispered, my fingers snaking around her neck and letting her feel it. She made a little noise of appreciation into my mouth and I pulled away before it became too much in front of Vera.

Who was once again saved by Claire. “Here,” the woman said, handing us even more stuff. We tried to complain, but she wasn’t having it. Especially after Vera told her about her plot to use the CRC’s card. “Less and less tourists coming from abroad these last few years. I have to replenish my coffers some other way, don’t I? Pitch it to the CRC, dearie. I can cook superhero grade gruel at lunchtime, I’ll have you know.”

After that, and some quiet eating, the mood shifted slightly. Vera pulled out some documents she had printed. Yes, you heard that right. Printed.

“The time has come, then,” I commented. “The heist is nigh.”

“Yes. Another team took the other job, and it went well. But this means that the window is closing for this one,” Vera said, not acknowledging my joke or tone. I noticed that Nadia too had not, going full operative-mode. “We have a deadline now. Do you want the job or not?”

“Yes,” I said, now serious like the other two.

“Good. I worked out the best day to act, and it’s two days from now. As I said already, it should be an easy one, but I won’t pretend we aren’t doing some grey work here. It needs you two rested and capable. You have the winter market shift tonight, so if you accept the job I want you to take tomorrow as rest day. And no garden or greenhouse work either.”

Nadia looked at me and I nodded. “We take it,” she said.

“Good. I’ll prepare what we need and brief you as soon as I can. I’d ask to come over so we could plot it together, but I’ve got a lot of paperwork to make this operation happen, so I won’t be able to be there.”

“Makes sense,” I said.

“And now, there’s something I need to tell you.”

The expression on her face. I don’t think I’d ever seen more than tiny gestures where her composure broke. But this time it was really easy to decipher. Guilt.

“Sure,” I said. Then softly, “Is everything alright?”

“I went looking into your grandfather. After you mentioned his name, and the thing with the banks, and…” the more she spoke, the faster the words came out. “I didn’t use the work computer. I have my own, with software I had built by people I trust, and that I checked myself before and after. There are no bugs in my apartment, or in my office, and you have to understand that I only did it–”

“Vera,” I said. Perhaps a bit forcefully but it did the job. “Breathe.”

She did, and I think she calmed down a little. Her eyes went to my head and to the Mind Privacy patches there. Yep. I was paranoid. It didn’t mean she had to prove to us she had been paranoid too. Although perhaps Nadia would beg to differ.

“Thank you, Adrian,” she said. “Suffice to say, I suppose your stance about certain things rubbed off on me, and I took the utmost care. Although I’d understand if you were to be mad at me for prying.”

“We trust your methods,” Nadia said.

“And it’s fine,” I added. “What did you find?”

“That’s the worrying part. I found nothing. The mystery caller knew your name and he was looking into your house, Adrian. What else did he know? I looked. From what you said, I thought I’d find probate, a record, a mention of the estate, at least something about the debts. But no. Nothing. Too clean, so I dug deeper. I found some sealed files, and many empty queries. I had the local AI models I use for hacking go at it all night, but it came back empty handed.”

Nadia blinked. “Someone doesn’t want people to go looking.”

As for myself, I didn’t know how to react. He was my grandfather, and I had thought I’d put all the grief and anger and sorrow behind. And yet here they were, all back, raw and painful and so loud.

“We need to get to the bottom of this,” I said, the anger simmering into cold… something.

“I agree,” Vera said.

“We need to be strategic,” Nadia said. “Let’s start from the facts. Vera, did you look into the attorney who gave Adrian the house?”

“Yes,” Vera said. “It’s the only record that I could access. The hill house exists, it’s legally Adrian’s, and it’s outside the seized estate.”

“That checks out,” Nadia said, thinking out loud. “And the attorney himself?”

“He knows nothing. I went to see him in person.”

“You sure about this?”

“Completely. I have my methods.”

Nadia hummed. “Is there a possibility that they know you went looking?”

“I can’t rule it out,” Vera said. “I was careful, but…” the rest was unsaid.

“Stop digging,” I told her. “For your own sake, if anything. Alright?”

“I will, I promise.”

“Good,” I said, sighing. “What a mess. I guess the naive explanation could be that Gus simply paid a lot of money to make everything disappear, to hand me the house off the books but legally, and that’s why all records are sealed or clean.”

“It is plausible.” Vera agreed.

“Yes, it could very well be true,” Nadia said. “It would also be rather cliche to drop the matter only for it to come back to bite us in the ass in the future, wouldn’t it?”

“A classic,” I said. “What a mess indeed. Being at the center of a possible conspiracy with espionage and all that isn’t as fun as they make it sound in the superhero comics.”

“That’s because they are all CRC propaganda,” Vera said.

I chuckled.

“We’ll get to the bottom of it,” Nadia whispered. “I promise, my prince.”

I smiled at her, and noticed that Vera was nodding along, as if it was the most obvious thing in the world that she’d be a co-conspirator. Which she already was, I supposed.
