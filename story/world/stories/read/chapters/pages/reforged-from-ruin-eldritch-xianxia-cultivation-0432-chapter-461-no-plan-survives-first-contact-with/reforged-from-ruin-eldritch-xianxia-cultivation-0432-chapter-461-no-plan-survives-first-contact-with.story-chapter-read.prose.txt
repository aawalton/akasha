Onwards! To War!

–The Songs of Suffering, first and loudest verse

_________________________________________________________________________

Millions of bodies.

Beasts do not fight as armies do. They do not enact battle lines and clear-cut stratagems and communication as humanoids might. They are wild things, surging, moving in patterns almost impossible to predict. Blind panic, ravenous hunger, and reactive rage are what dominate the motivations and instincts of such an ocean, not loyalty or obedience.

And yet that is not to say that a Beast Tide is without logic. All things in the world are of logic, even those that are defined by their opposition to it. The hunger consumes, but it also directs. The panic can be enforced, guided, by acts of violence and fear from specific nodes in the swarm. A stampede, used properly, is as effective as any cavalry charge, and often many times more desperate. A rabid desperation to survive is just as useful to claws as it is to swords.

A Beast Tide, like all things, has a logic all its own.

That logic inevitably points towards ruin.

Millions of bodies become millions of corpses. Cities and sects fall, nations are razed to the ground, environments are trampled and devoured in a mad, sometimes entirely senseless dash towards no real objective at all.

As the beasts are herded, as they hunt, as they cry out and sprint in a never-ending madness that blankets the world in limbs and gnashing keratin and gouging bone, they die just as surely as those in their way.

But not the strong.

For the strong, the survivors, there is meat aplenty. Qi to spare. Concepts embedded in murdered flesh, mixed into the slurry of mud left behind or picked out from the pack.

Behind the defensive dome, the Grand Elder and the strongest Warriors of the Sect hide behind their walls and defenses. When the tide has passed, if they endure or escape, they will have a harvest unlike any other.

Beyond the magics that shield them, Divine Beasts, atop the peaks of their respective evolutionary lines, claw to get it. If they slaughter their enemies or survive, they will have a feast beyond any equal.

Caught in between them, beast and man alike die, and die, and die again.

Raika is laughing.

The Man Who Is Not There, the Feng of Fengs, the most bastard of the bastards she has met, is fast. Fast enough that she can’t track him, can’t keep up, in spite of using the biology most saturated with Qi and refined to higher forms. Sometimes he moves normally, other times, with his Truth, and yet always he leaves behind nothing but ruin and vacuum in his wake.

The beasts nip at the pieces of her they find, neglected and undefended. Cultivators carve through her and harvest pieces from towering pillars of gristle, from spasming fields of muscle, from oceans of liquid bone and metal. Over and over, she is unwritten, stolen out of reality by the will of Grand Elder Zhen Zixiang and painted over.

All this just the parts of her inside the dome.

Beyond it, everything is more.

There are other waves of the Beast Tide in other places, devouring the Republic of Morae in massive, savage bites. Some parts of her try to fight, interrupt or redirect certain waves, but most of them get pruned not long after, their thoughts interfering with her main objectives. They run wild, empowered by her flesh wherever it is found, flooding some of the islands in the meat-verse inside herself whenever they find entrances to hidden realms.

There are cultivators facing them and each other. Many battles are fought against the beasts, but many are from sects warring openly and without warning, taking the opportunities the chaos presents. Off in the distance, following in behind the Beast Tide, she experiences several ego-deaths as a collection of disparate Warrior Realm cultivators approach, pincering the tide and increasing its frenzy.

And of course, there are the Fengs.

Wherever she sees them, she attacks.

It has very mixed results.

She is spreading herself too thin.

Not that it matters. She’ll push through or she won’t, and she has to push through. She has to.

Otherwise she’ll just have failed again. Just like Raika did.

Names. Convenient until they’re not.

She’s spread herself using her Dao of Space to almost every corner of the Jianghu, and even up into the stone of the some of the impossibly tall mountains upon which the sects reside. She’s spread as a mist through the sky, as roots through the earth, as crimson tides through flowing rivers and lakes, and every cell of that spread can spread more of her.

All it takes is for her to keep up production.

Some of her spouses are having trouble keeping up.

The previous patterns she figured out allowed for more Qi overall and over time, but not with enough immediacy for her right now. She’s casting each of their flavors of cultivation against each other inside herself, their enhanced Qi pools and added “physical meridians” allowing her to absorb power far above their realms from each of them, and she uses all of it. Gold sunlight clashes against umber earth, against fire, against logic and words and shadows and plants and water and sea and more, the nuances ignored in favor of brute conflict.

In a more delicate organism, the result would be Qi deviation bad enough to splatter their insides outside. In Raika, the churning turmoil becomes more fuel.

It hurts so much.

But she does it anyway, in spite of how it might affect things in the long term. Because it gives her more.

Flesh condensed into impossible new materials suffers catastrophic decompression as it enters reality, exploding with chemical properties Raika can’t explain but which do harm. Qi deviation is weaponized, pumped into the bodies of enemies too weak to resist her Dao of Flesh. Agony suffuses the land itself as all across it, Raika sings of Pain.

She isn’t really winning any one battle.

She is doing a lot of good damage, though.

Squadrons of Feng operatives are knocked off course and delayed, half-functioning abominations throwing themselves madly in their way as tendrils of flesh come from out of space itself to assault them. Bombs of Qi rain like hailstones down over whole chunks of territory. Plants, animals, and natural sources of Qi find themselves converted and transformed by her comprehensions and her Truths.

The Feng of Fengs keeps erasing her, over and over and over. The Grand Elder keeps painting over her. She can’t hit them, can’t get to them, isn’t even qualified to properly interrupt the grand battle above between a true Divine Beast and a Grand Elder of the Republic of Morae and the Watchful Fields Sect.

But they just can’t kill her fast enough.

Even the impossible and invisible weapon of the assassin, which tastes more and more of her and learns to devour faster and faster, can’t match the way her ontology shifts. Every time it comes close, she causes herself a new Pain, infests and transforms a new body, grabs hold of a new set of minds and perverts them into the gestalt by force.

And she laughs the whole time.

Overjoyed. Passionate. Bloodthirsty.

Manic. Panicked. Desperate.

In so much fucking PAIN.

Bodies burst and are unmade and remade into avatars of flesh, spreading new roots out of cyclopean pools of eyes or spiraling tendrils of bioweapons. The skies above eat of the weakest beasts, drinking them out of the air and using their cultivation and biomass to spread thicker clouds of crimson, acidic mist.

She feels unleashed.

She feels like she’s dying.

She’s lost contact with the prison-pyramid she threw into the screaming sun. That means that her half-baked, poorly thought out plan didn’t work, or that it did, but something unforeseen happened. Either way, it didn’t work as she intended it.

That’s fine. Everything’s fine. And if it isn’t, she can just survive anyways.

It’s Just Pain

Almost there. Almost perfect. Just like her. Just a step away from being something new and worthy and worthwhile and True.

She finds, to her surprise, that she’s talking. Responding back to people talking to her.

Ko is yelling something, his blade flashing with the ringing of bells as he tears apart stray bits of gristle floating on the breeze. The few that he misses, blocking his attacks through accidental sound muffling rather than any real intention, land on some of the domesticated herds beneath him, or the mortals beyond them. The cultivators (of all kinds, she realizes, seeming to come from multiple different sects, and oh, is that one of her spouses?) below all stop whatever they’re doing to turn against those the gristle touch, which makes sense, since they seem to either collapse in agony or suddenly start sprouting a lot more meat.

The gristle whispers in the breeze, speaking with its movement.

“Left right up up left new breeze coming cluster spiraling in the middle infected on the left herd outcast sheep growing teeth keep them away keep running to your right right right again, left, down below, I’m landing on the dirt, dig it up, left, above–”

Aria is still in her office, but she’s wearing dozens of the weapons and tools made of Raika’s flesh, and they seem to be actually holding back some of the flesh-weaving of the chamber. Several half-formed bodies are babbling, bubbling out of the walls and falling still on the way out. Aria is integrating into the room itself through her artifacts, but it isn’t overcoming her, though she can’t do much more than hold it at bay here and in the rooms beyond.

“Hold fast now flex keep still protect set boundary open gate direct flow shift angle shift room shift perspective shift space flex relax then flex then bend and–”

The spouses, alongside her acolytes, are also people that are real, and they are also there, and they are also moving and desperate and under duress. They are barely recognizable, and it’s– there’s so many. There’s so many people she knows, so many she didn’t try to know, that are alien and familiar. Geng He and Yu Guanyu and oh fuck, does she know the names of any of the people she’s modified into her system? All those people promised to the Raika-That-Was and all those people who pledged themselves to her and she doesn’t even know their–

The thought gets terminated rather abruptly. That brain wasn’t being very useful. There are others to take its place.

She feels like she’s on fire.

She feels like she’s freezing cold.

Neither sensation has anything to do with the attacks being thrown against her, and everything to do with the overall pain of being.

A part of her wonders if this is how food feels, boiling atop the flames and seared upon the skillet.

She wonders how she’d taste.

A small cluster has to be unmade when they break enough to get overeager with the thought.

It just hurts so much.

But she’s almost there.

It’s Just Pain.

A new Truth, another tool to explore with and understand and weaponize and use to kill and break and win and hurt.

Because it’s all hurt. All the time. Always.

She’s burning herself up. Literally, in some cases, the Qi deviations in her body ripping through parts of her, the damage from her opponents flooding into her system, the chemistry of her innate biology going beyond conventional limits. It’s like a fever, spread across a whole continent, like a body reacting to a thousand simultaneous infections and cancers and–

Referencing a cancer wasn’t very helpful.

Focus.

Focus.

It HURTS.

And she’s still spreading. Dealing with all of this, she’s still sending out tendrils, still digging into the earth, still floating up into the sky and diluting through waters.

It’s beautiful. Genuinely. It’s a whole world, and in spite of herself, she just can’t help but see it.

If even the slightest thing were different, it wouldn’t be. Her blood leaking into the soil poisons it even as her Qi enriches it, and whatever comes next will be different than what came before. The world that brought about all of the food and animals and delights of existence falls apart in the presence of something that, in the context of a whole planet, is still so small.

It’s beautiful.

The beauty is sour and acrid because everything hurts.

She’s still laughing.

It is not a happy sound.

Into the pyrrhic stalemate, her laugh trumpets and breaks against the magic and heights of the Jiang’hu.

Whether by timing or coincidence, the madness of the sound heralds what comes next.

The sky breaks.

Five Peaks, above the atmosphere, above the horizon, above all, shine with light so bright it sears through the colors of sky-blue. Dao and the colors of Truth between the seven grand ideas it represents shine down onto the world below, somehow illuminating over mountains, over hills, across a continent all at once.

From that aurora, they descend.

Some of them leap forth, falling with gravity. Most of them fly, breaking air resistance with techniques that move them over the horizon faster than Raika can track. A few even teleport, signatures glowing with that same light from above appearing all across the now-shadowless mountains of the Republic of Morae.

The Five Peaks have, apparently, finally decided it was time to intervene.

A Beast Tide has its own logic, but there are some things about conflict that are nearly always true.

The weak suffer and die, and the strong eat of the slaughter.

The Peaks descend onto the bleeding Jiang’Hu, blazing cultivation to rival the heavens painting the world in fresh violence.

She’s still laughing.

It still hurts.
