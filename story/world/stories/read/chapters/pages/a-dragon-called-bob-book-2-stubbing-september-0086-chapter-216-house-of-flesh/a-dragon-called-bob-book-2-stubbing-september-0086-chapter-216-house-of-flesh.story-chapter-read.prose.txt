Number ninety-three Crawley Avenue… there wasn’t a nice way to say it; the place was a shit heap.  The grounds were overgrown, filled with weeds and knee-high grass.  The paint was chipped and faded, and a section of the roof had sagged and fallen inwards, no doubt letting the weather inside.

But this was the place, and it glowed in my vision.  The home of the delightfully named Dargo the Defiler.  It was a grand house, two storeys tall, and broad enough that it must have at least a dozen rooms inside.  The door was heavy oak, scratched and dirty.  An ornate knocker hung in the centre, a metallic human face, eyes closed but mouth open, had a loop of steel hanging from either side of its chin.

It registered as far too warm for it to be natural.  The outer bricks were closer to the colour of my own hands in the odd thermal vision I’d acquired.  Even the door was well above room temperature.  Weird.

I was in a seedier part of the noble district, along the west side of town.  The perpetual smog of the city clung to my skin and left me feeling clammy.

Straightening my tunic, I slammed the knocker down once, the boom echoing out onto the quiet street.  I jumped back as the eyes on the knocker opened and glared at me.  Nothing else moved on the thing, and after a moment, the lids dropped down, and the knocker returned to appearing inanimate.

“Well, that was—hello!” The door opened in the middle of me muttering to myself.  The borderline supermodel that greeted me had not been what I expected.

“My lord.  The Defiler will see you now.”

She turned, her almost gauze-like tunic clinging to her body.  I followed her into Dargo’s home.  The walls were all panelled in stone, buffed smooth and agreeably shiny.  The artwork left something to be desired.  Crude images painted in red on hides that had been cut square.  Animals, people, strange sigils I couldn’t understand.

“Do you like his home?” she asked, glancing back over her shoulder at me.

“It’s… different?”

“Many find it uncomfortable, but you aren’t concerned?”

“Aside from the paintings, it's nice eno—”

The woman had swung open a door that opened onto the dilapidated part of the house. I could see sunlight shining down into a courtyard made of ruined rooms.  The inner walls still stood in places, but they were covered in greenery.  A tree had grown up in the centre, seemingly from within an intersection where the former walls joined.

It spread out above me as I stepped in, red leaves swaying in the breeze.  There was no smog.  The city was always cloaked in mist, the canyons between the houses lost in perpetual shadows.  But this garden was in bright sunlight.

“Dargo, I’m guessing?”  I said.  The man turned and smiled at me.  “So plastic surgery abuse is a thing here as well?”

“My methods are a little more sophisticated than those scablifters.  Nothing is fake, everything is real.”

His voice was gentle, almost effeminate, despite a jawline that could be used to measure right angles.

“The mighty Baron Bob chooses to pay me a visit.  Viktarlpth is wondering what brings you here?  That bloody insect has sunk his mandibles into you, it seems.”

“Philpott gave me your name.  I’m looking to retain your services.”  Not using the words buy or spend salved my menagerie a little, but Greed was breathing heavily and looking around for something to attack.

“I would require a similar service in return.”

“Look, mate, I’m not doing bloody fetch quests for you.”

“But you don’t know what I want you to fetch.”

“I will pay you to do something for me.”  I crossed my arms and treated him to a level six glare.  Not too harsh, but strong enough to let him know I was in no mood to piss about.

He laughed, a tinkling sound and nodded his head.  His robes shifted, seeming to move against the breeze as he turned back to his tree.  “I like him.”

A face, perfect in every way, formed on the bark of the tree.  As the features coalesced, they went from a ruddy brown to a perfectly fleshy pink.

“Nyal has spoken to me.  I think we can help this one.”  This voice was ancient and evil.

“A tree-dude.  You’re Viktarlpth, I take it.”

“Call me Vik.  He’s one of us, in a way.”

Dargo turned back to me and gave me a thoughtful look.  “A true shapeshifter.  Would you like to see my real form?”

“I’m good, thanks.  I need a head.”

“Jalba can see to that.”

“Who the hell is Jalba?”

“My servant.  She met you at the door.”

I glanced back, but the beautiful woman was gone, and the door I had entered through was now a bush of some sort, covered in long spines and thin green leaves.

“It needs to be a very special head.  Needs to fool some people who will check very closely that it’s legit.”

“He’s not thought this through at all,” Dargo murmured.

“What, little dragon, will happen when you present a false head and yet reports of the alleged victim’s continued existence reach the ears of the people you fooled?” Vik enquired.

“Uh—shit.”

“Doesn’t matter.  We can still help you.  We’ll get you the head, the real one.  But you’ll have to return the favour.”  Dargo sat down, the bushes and walls flowing together behind him into a chair that sprouted hair,

“I need to grow,” Vik said.  The face bulged outwards, and a body formed below it.  A blob of pink and red stepped out of the tree.  It had the usual four limbs and a torso, but it looked like it was carved from molten wax, lumpen and misformed.  One arm was longer than the other, and the head was offset a couple of inches too far to the left.

“Can’t he just… feed you?”

“This building was a stepping stone,” Vik replied.

“You’re the whole building?”

“Indeed.  But city regulations keep me pent up.  I can’t expand.  The Dunnikindivers will file a complaint with the Ombudsman.  Karen will get involved.  Messy.  But you have lands, and I suspect you’ll have even more in the not-too-distant future.  I want…more.” Vik waved a dripping hand at the tree and the blue sky.

“I’m not sure I’d be getting a good deal, Vik.  One head for releasing a flesh sculpting demon on the wider world.”  I wasn’t completely self-absorbed, and I didn’t like the sound of this deal.  “I’ve met the thing the dwarves are fighting beneath Mount… you know, I don’t know what the mountain is called?  Saarkonig?  I think that was his name?”

“Saalkönig,” Dargo said softly, with remarkably Teutonic pronunciation.  “The Deep One.  He’s a bit of a naughty boy.”

“I would never have guessed.”

“You have a son now, do you not?” Vik asked.

“Well, I shat out an egg, but Morris is basically a grown ass adult.  Mentally speaking.  In most ways.  Kind of.”

“He’s already caused you some bother?” the demon of sagging flesh asked pleasantly. His face had warped and become ugly and deformed.

“Now that he’s banned from my lair and has his own place, I’m sure it will be fine,” I ground out through clenched teeth.  I’d need to secure all the booze, tools and other minor valuables.  It turned out that moving stolen goods, as well as making them stolen, was something of a passion for the young dragon.  I smiled proudly.  He’d do well, build a good hoard in the end, I was sure of it.  As long as he remembered not to steal from me.

“Demons are not so different from mortals.”  Vik laughed at my raised eyebrow of scepticism.  “Our young ones are often a delight.  A source of joy to us as the years pass and we witness their deeds, reliving a little of our own youth vicariously through them.  But sometimes they are rebellious, and profoundly disappointing.”

“So the Deep One is one of your wayward babies?”

“Not mine.  My wife's.”

“You want me to kill your wife’s son?  What the hell?  You got cucked, buddy.  Happens to the best of us.”

“No one said kill, Bob.  It’s unlikely that you could, anyway,” Dargo chipped in.

“I’ve fought it before.  It burns, and I can eat it.  Not so tough.”

“You’d be there a while,” Vik said drily.  He turned away and walked back towards the tree, his hand rose and melted into it slightly.  “The Deep One has a seed that he stole from me.”

“Pretty sure he got that from the guy who screwed your wife.”

“He stole it from me.  Get it back, and you will have your head.  With it, I can make a seed of my own so you can complete the bargain.  Are we agreed?”

His back bulged unnaturally, like something within was trying to explode out.  Bones creaked, and the flesh glowed faintly to either side of his spine.    He looked back, and his eyes flashed red in his melted face.  “Are we agreed?”

Did I want to set this thing free from whatever was binding it to this creepy-ass house?  He seemed nice enough, in a nightmarish sort of way, and for once, someone had just been clear with me about what they really wanted.   He melted back into the tree, and a handful of leaves fell from the branches above.

“It tires him to be away from his roots,” Dargo said softly, turning his gigachad-memeworthy face to the trunk.

“Weird for a fleshsculptor demon to be a tree.”

“Flesh takes many forms; any living thing, or even undead thing, can be changed with our powers.  You fail to exploit your shapeshifting powers; you use them like an infant.  Look at your face!”

“I like my face, pal.  It’ll take me a while to get your mate's seed-thing.”  I turned to leave.

“You will know it when you see it.  But you will have to cut through to the heart of the Deep One.  In the meantime, we will prepare to bring you Lord Bamblebunt’s head.”  I glanced back with a scowl, and he smiled.  “Nyal was very supportive of your cause.”

“I’ll see myself out.” The door had reappeared behind me while we talked.

“Farewell, Baron.”

I didn’t see his lady-in-waiting on my way out, but the front door swung open as I approached and closed behind me.  It was nice to be back in the smoky streets.  I wandered away. I’d need to detour around the Gloom if I wanted to keep my thoughts free to drift, so I headed north towards the centre of the city.

Karmically, this was all a bit of a mess.  Killing the three nobles, who I was reliably informed were all total shits, wasn’t the problem.  Killing the Deep One, or at least giving it a good kicking and stealing its heart, looked at first glance to be a moral good.

I’d need to find somewhere suitably remote to drop down Viktarlpth.  Somewhere far to the north, maybe?  Where there was nothing but roving Olric bands.  They’d love something like Viktarlpth to constantly fight.  They’d keep it in check, and it could keep them in check.  I hadn’t had any more trouble from the Orlics, not since I recruited Geeku’s band, but I couldn’t write off the possibility that another bunch might come sweeping down on Mount Bob and cause me problems.

I’d skirted around the very heart of the city, which was dominated by the Imperial Palace and grounds, as well as dodged the dangerous inhabitants of the Gloom.  They couldn’t hurt me, but I didn’t want to have to hurt them when they inevitably tried to rob me.

I made my way to the mages guild, with its illusory facade.  Time to get myself a camera crew and put the resort floor to good use.
