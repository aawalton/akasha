The Chubby Saber was a little hole-in-the-wall tavern nestled among the tall, steep-roofed buildings of Varin’s Town. Colin had discovered it early in their first term, and when he’d brought Eola there, it had quickly become her second-favorite place to eat—just behind the cafeteria at Varin’s Academy, and only because unlimited free food was better than the stew Cook Ursula made at the Chubby Saber.

After all, quantity had a quality all its own.

But she couldn’t deny that the never-ending stew did taste better than anything the school could make. Whatever the tiny purple gnome did to it, it was working.

“Only one bottle tonight, please,” Colin said as he waved from their table. Eola’s face heated up as he stared at her. “We’ve got plenty to occupy us without getting drunk, right?”

Eola nodded quickly, and Garth the barkeep waved in acknowledgment. A moment later, Ursula was there with a tray of bowls. “What do you think this time? It’s something new; I added it just to trick—“

“Mace? Blade mace?” Eola guessed, sniffing the air.

Ursula’s face fell. “I thought that’d get you for sure. How do you know every time?”

“I like the game, that’s all.” One spoonful went up to her lips, and she sighed in pleasure. “This is just as good as usual, Ursula.”

“Thank you,” the purple gnome said.

She set the other bowls on the table as Garth brought four glasses and a bottle over. “This is something from down south, well past Hightower, Mr. Tremory. For you and your polite lady friend.”

“Thanks.” Colin worked the cork out and poured four small glasses. Eola noticed that hers was a little smaller than the others’, and she sniffed, but didn’t complain. Her bowl of stew was larger than theirs, after all. “It’s good to be back in Varin’s Town.”

“”Y’aer’s curse, it is,” Patrice echoed. “To normal days, without my parents—and without Catrine.”

Catrine. Eola had almost managed to forget about the noble girl in the calm, quiet weeks between terms. Of course, the scion of House Andrese had gone home over the break. Eola just hadn’t thought about the implications—namely, that she’d travel with both Colin and Patrice—but it only made sense. All three were traveling to Highstone, after all. “That sounds awful,” Eola said.

“It wasn’t that bad,” Colin said. He fidgeted with his hands as his eyes met Eola’s, then looked away.

Patrice snorted.

“Okay, it was pretty bad.” Colin sighed. “The biggest problem wasn’t Catrine, though. It was Patrice’s parents.”

“How?” Roth asked. He hadn’t touched his wine yet, and his songbird sat quietly on his shoulder. It was one of the first times Eola had ever seen his familiar; she was glad Atta was nothing more than a warm, purring stone in her pocket.

“Well…” Patrice trailed off.

“They expect a lot of their youngest daughter,” Colin said. He stared at his wine—which was also untouched.  “So, after she introduced me, Patrice ended up spending a lot of time at the Andrese manor. A lot of feasts, a lot of balls, a lot of recept—“

“A lot of being bored out of my mind,” Patrice finished. “Every Y’aer-cursed luncheon in Alemia. It was awful.”

“Ah.” Eola didn’t have much to say to that. She’d spent her time trying to put together torn pages, then put those same pages in order. Compared to noble balls and feasts, that seemed downright boring. She sipped at her wine—a deep, almost blood-colored red. It was shockingly fruity and light, with a slightly sour aftertaste. “Colin, you talked to Instructor Tarik about your classes, right? What are you taking?”

Colin looked grateful for the subject change. “Well, I’ve got Advanced Mana Studies with Madame Reyanna. That’s fine. I’m surprised I didn’t end up in remedials for that, though.

My soul’s a lot smaller than a siege mage’s should be. Then Advanced Ideograms with Tarik and Ritual-Based Combat with Clearance. Plus our apprenticeship with Tarik again, and—“

“Patrol Duty,” Eola finished. “What is that, anyway? Something about the library and the mountains?”

“You two are ridiculous,” Patrice interrupted.

Roth snorted.

“What’s that supposed to mean?” Colin asked.

“It means that you two haven’t seen each other in two and a half weeks. You, in theory, are courting. It’s time to act like it.” Patrice waited as Eola stared at her like a deer at a Bright Ball during a hunt. After a few seconds, the redhead sighed. “Look, you’re not nobility. That means—“

“You can kiss and touch to your hearts’ content,” Roth interrupted, an unreadable expression on his face.

“So, Patrol Duty?” Eola asked, head down in her arms so no one would see how bright red it was.

“Right. Patrol Duty,” Colin started, sounding grateful. Patrice rolled her eyes in exasperation, but he continued onward. “I asked Instructor Tarik about it, and he said it’s one or the other. You and a partner pick either the mountains or the library, then spend time helping to keep it clear of monsters. Three Sixth-Days and Seventh-Days before the mid-term exams, four after. Knight-Librarian Fringe is the organizer. The first week’s a general briefing on what to expect, then selections, and then three weeks of training specific to your chosen patrol.”

Eola grimaced. “Only pairs?”

“Yes. They’re trying to cover a lot of ground, so in theory, your pairing should cover each other’s weaknesses.” Colin slipped his hand over Eola’s. The tall girl smiled and blushed at the same time as he kept talking. “I’ll probably be partnering with you. You can keep me safe while I draw up my rituals, and I provide enough magic for the two of us.”

“Oh.” Eola had been about to ask if Colin was available.

“Anyway, those five classes are close to standard-issue for siege mages. I tried to get into a few other courses—Combat Medicine, specifically—but Instructor Tarik made some good arguments. He seems to think that my attunement would benefit from even more exposure to Ordered Magic’s rules—hence the Advanced Ideograms. Other than that…”

Colin rambled on, and Eola stared into her empty bowl of stew. She had no idea when she’d finished it, but surely someone else would have a little left for her to finish up. Her eyes drifted toward Roth—who still hadn’t eaten a bite or drunk a sip of his wine. She started to ask why, but he shook his head.

Just before she looked away, Eola caught the look on his face. It wasn’t a scowl at all; it looked more like the angry boy was scheming.

As the wine bottle emptied and the four of them filled up, Patrice stood and poked Colin in the arm. “Can we tell them now?”

He winced. “Well, we can’t exactly keep it a secret now, can we?”

“Tell me what?” Eola asked.

“There’s heresy afoot,” Patrice whispered loudly, “and it’s here, at Varin’s Academy.”

“Oh.” Eola went back to her second glass of wine, then stopped. Patrice was staring at her, an annoyed expression across her face. “I mean, Oh, Y’aer save us, that’s awful.”

Patrice held her expression for a moment, then shook her head. “It’s not a joking matter, Eola. Heresy is a massive accusation.”

“How can it be a massive accusation? The Cult of Y’aer’s only sanctioned. It doesn’t have any real power, does it?”

“That would normally be true, but when it comes to Ordered Magic specifically, it’s got a lot of sway. There’s a higher than zero chance that the Cult of Y’aer is responsible for what Instructor Tarik believes is happening to magic. There’s a whole branch of them who believe Ordered Magic is Y’aer’s blessing as much as it is Varin’s.”

“And then there are the Governing Houses,” Colin said sullenly.

“Yeah, those too.”

“How do you know about this?” Roth asked. He hadn’t said much, and Eola, Patrice, and Colin all turned to stare at him. “Something like this has to be a big deal. They wouldn’t let just anyone know about it.”

“They would if someone’s mother happens to be a high-ranking member of the Cult,” Patrice said.

“Yours?” Eola asked.

Colin shook his head. “No. Catrine’s. She gave up her position as Lady Andrese—passed the title on to her husband—and joined the Cult of Y’aer. The other Governing Houses are pretty sure it’s a power grab, but you can’t stop someone from joining the Cult if the Cult will take them.”

“The letter came midway through one of those endless luncheons that turn into dinners that turn into balls. It was only a few nights before we left, and Lady Andrese disappeared as soon as she got it. The next day, all the ridiculous noble events were canceled, so I got to spend time with Colin in the city,” Patrice said. “Chaperoned, of course—noble girls can’t be with commoner boys unless their maid follows them around. I didn’t think anything of it until the carriage ride back here. My parents stayed in Highstone, so it was just the three of us for four days.”

The tall redhead shivered. “Four. Days. With Catrine. I know I spent a few years with her when I was younger, but that was plenty. The only good thing was that she told us exactly what was in that letter.”

“Not that she’s a reliable source,” Colin said.

“Right. She’s not reliable. But if she does know something…” Patrice trailed off.

Eola sipped the last of her wine. It was already giving her that heady, silly feeling, and it’d be unwise to have any more. The last time she’d drank heavily had been Y’aer’s Day, with her friends. Now, together again, and with two days before the start of their second term, it was tempting to order another bottle. But Colin had said only one…

Come to think of it, she’d heard about that group of cultists from Tagg, and there’d been the one reading in the library’s first floor. It wasn’t uncommon to have Y’aer’s cultists visit, though. They, along with traveling mages and nobles’ servants, made up a surprising number of the academy’s visitors, and most of them wanted access to the library.

Colin’s coin purse opened, and he laid the meal’s payment at the center of the table. “Come on. I want to get back to our rooms before the first-years start filtering in this evening and tomorrow.”

“Right, they’re coming!” Patrice said a touch too loudly. “It’s going to be chaos in the dormitory building. I have to see this.”

Patrice and Colin headed for the door, and Eola followed them, but as she got close, she looked over her shoulder at Roth. He still hadn’t eaten anything, and his wine glass was still untouched.

And he still didn’t look angry.

Eola waited outside of the Chubby Saber. She didn’t have anything better to do—thought Atta probably disagreed from the inside of her robe’s pocket—and Roth was acting strangely. She could only think of a few reasons for him to be so irritated, and most of them involved Colin’s list of classes.

It hadn’t been Roth’s fault that he wasn’t on the combat track. He’d done the same amount of work as Eola, Colin, and Patrice. In fact, if he’d wanted to make the argument that he’d done more than them, she’d have accepted it. His magic had saved them twice. First, against the Srilla, when they’d snuck into its lair rather than fighting the shape-changing monster. And then again, when he’d Soul Bled to buy her time to learn how to use her Cutting mark.

But instead of being rewarded for it, he’d been passed over, and Eola couldn’t help but feel guilty about that. At the same time, though…she wouldn’t sacrifice her own chance to learn more about fighting. She needed it; her parents were trapped in a dungeon, and someday, she’d be strong enough to save them. So, no, she wouldn’t switch places with him.

When he finally emerged from the Chubby Saber’s door, Eola was waiting. “Roth, what’s on your mind?”

“I got my class list,” he said simply.

“And?”

“I have three classes, plus the apprenticeship. An Independent Study with Headmaster Tomlish. Illusory Spellcraft with Mistress Gabrelli. And Ensorcellment with Lannith. But there’s also an open slot.”

“That never happens, does it?” Eola asked.

It never did. Varin’s Academy’s professors always picked specific classes for their apprentices. There was some flexibility, but Ordered Magic wasn’t a flexible practice, and the process of reaching Journeymage or Truemage before graduation was a well-beaten path, even for most of the more obscure attunements. Catrine—a scent-attuned mage—had probably had her entire three years planned out before she’d even finished applying. Even Eola, with her odd attunement, had a list of classes.

Granted, the combat track was tighter than the general track, but even so…

An open slot.

“It doesn’t. I didn’t bother thinking about my classes over the term break. Now, I have to make a choice. It’s a lot to think about.” Roth ran his fingers through his dark, messy hair. “I’ve been working through my options. Languages and Ideograms don’t make sense for me, but Combat Medicine…that’s Colin’s job. And outside of Magical Dueling, they don’t teach combat courses to general track students. I could sign up for the advanced version, but…”

“But there’s not much point,” Eola said. It felt blunt, but it also felt correct.

“No, there’s not. Headmaster Tomlish clearly has plans for me, and they don’t involve fighting.” Roth sat on a flight of stairs. He still didn’t look angry. If anything, Eola thought he might be a little relieved about that, but at the same time…it wasn’t the only emotion he couldn’t hide.

“Did you finish your stew?” Eola asked suddenly.

Roth snorted. “No. And I didn’t finish the wine, either. Too late for you, right?”

“Yes, I suppose it is,” Eola said. She’d already been making plans to go back and see if the bowl was still on the table. With a supreme effort of sheer willpower, she pushed them out of her head, along with the delicious, surprising hint of blade mace. “What was your village like?”

“What’s that got to do with anything?” Roth’s eyes narrowed, and Eola relaxed. That was the Roth she was used to.

“Maybe nothing. I don’t know.”

“It was cold. Always cold. Even by the fire, inside the cabins, the cold still got in between your furs and skin. There was never enough food, except right after Dad finished a hunt. The other kids were rough, except for…never mind. I had to learn to take care of myself when it came to them.” Roth’s fist tightened in his lap. “Fighting’s what we do up there. It’s the only way to survive.”

Eola nodded. “I don’t know much about that. Do you like fighting? Only, you don’t seem sad about missing the combat track.”

“I’m not, and I don’t.”

“Then maybe it’s time to pick something you do like.”

Roth stared at the thin, short grass across the street. It clumped around a wooden sign, sticking up out of the dirt like stubble. Eola stood awkwardly nearby, waiting for him to say something. And, after a long time, he did.

“I’m going back to the dorms.”

Eola didn’t sigh out loud, but inside, she couldn’t help but be a little frustrated. Roth was, in so many ways, the most confusing friend she had. She followed him, and they walked silently back through Varin’s Town, across the campus grounds, and all the way to their dorm.

Three minutes after they got there, the harp music started up again.

And ten minutes after that, something thumped in the hall, and Patrice stuck her head into Eola’s room. “You’ve got to see this!”

Happy for any distraction from her second-term class guide, Eola followed her to the suite’s door and peered out, then snorted. “Y’aer help them…”

The first-year students had arrived, and their luggage—and in one boy’s case, an entire set of floating furniture—had formed a traffic jam just down the hall.
