Chapter V.LVII (5.57) The Princess and the Witch

Sitting alongside Anata and Mae, both of whom were ecstatic about his previous fight, Kizu watched both Basil and Ione demolish their opponents with ease. If the academy intended to give them a challenge and stir up drama, they certainly didn’t do a decent job with those match ups. If it was up to Kizu, he would have set Basil against Faible and Ione against Sene. But perhaps both of those challenges would have been… uncouth. Plus, if Faible realized what Basil was, it could lead to all sorts of real life problems that extended far beyond the reaches of school drama. It would create far more setbacks than the encouraging pushes the matches were supposedly designed for.

The only real surprise from the matches was when Ione’s opponent got on one knee and proposed to her near the end of their bout when she successfully vanquished his team of summoned racoons. From what Kizu caught, it sounded like Horus was muttering about how she’d proved herself and now had earned the right of his hand. Kizu’s mouth went dry as he processed the words and his heart hammered faster.

Ione’s bird summon promptly ripped her opponent’s neck out.

Kizu felt a swell of pride as their rooftop terrain melded back into the mundane wooden stage. The joyful sensation almost overwhelmed his sudden profound distaste for the racoon summoner. Almost.

In a rare display of overt (sober) public affection, Ione gave him a peck on the lips as she sat down next to him after the fight. He placed an arm around her as they watched the next fight.

A plume of black smoke blasted onto the stage and it then coalesced into a form. Stina stood on the stage, arms outstretched.

“Come, my foe! Face me in combat! Honorable or dishonorable, I care not for which. Let us end this feud once and for all, wretch!”

Aoi stepped onto the arena stage next, she strolled up, completely unflustered by Stina’s grandiose display.

“My master is going to demolish the princess!” Mae loudly whispered to Anata.

This was the first fight Kizu had witnessed all tournament where Kizu truly wanted both parties to come out victorious. While on one hand, he’d be happy either way. He simultaneously would be disappointed no matter the results as well. It was an interesting sensation and, for some reason, only served to add to his anxiety.

Arclight announced their names and went over the rules. Then the stage took its form and the match began in earnest.

The terrain shifted and bubbled. In moments, the two girls now stood on mounds of dirt surrounded by black, bubbling bog water with bald cypress trees spotting the swamp. Much the same as it had in the cemetery terrain, the entire stadium darkened in color, as if caught in an overcast dusk. Kizu watched in amazement as rain pattered down from above. This was by and far the most exotic and advanced of the stadiums he’d seen in the tournament. He wondered if Aoi’s status as a princess played a role in her getting assigned this terrain. An exceptional spectacle for the exceptional student.

If that was the case, they likewise picked the perfect opponent for her. Stina cackled like an absolute villain and then started her deep chat.

Aoi moved with speed and the clear intention to end this battle quickly and efficiently before whatever curse Stina planned to cast could come into fruition. She threw two skeletal hands in the witch’s direction. A meter away, they faded into black smoke only to reform in their physical forms after passing through her. The hands splashed into the black muck where they thrashed about until they disappeared.

As Stina continued her chant, more black mist swelled around her, caressing her body and lifting her. Her wide-brimmed hat’s edges rippled as the mist swirled by. From what Kizu could decipher from his spellsense, it appeared to be a combination of hexes and elemental magic that created such a powerful mist. However, he didn’t believe it to actually be the true purpose behind her chant. It was simply a protection designed to keep Aoi away and keep the princess busy while the witch charged up the true spell.

Despite the ineffectiveness of her last attack, Aoi remained undeterred. She slammed her hands against the ground and her fingers turned into a blur of enhanced speed as she sketched out a summoning circle. A moment later, she yanked out a conjured wicked blade. Kizu had seen Aoi use the dagger a few times, though he thought he recalled it being explained as a soul severing weapon.

He sat back in his seat and pondered that. The academy tournament uniforms were designed to repair their bodies upon death. Would they also take soul damage into account? Surely it had been attempted at least a few times in previous bouts over the years, albeit it was likely rare with necromancy outlawed.

Kizu glanced up at the stands where the faculty watched the tournament. In their box, Kizu picked out the headmaster’s tall hat from amongst the crowd. He sat, smiling and chatting with Professor Krimpit while they watched the fight play out below. It reassured Kizu. If the headmaster wasn’t worried, he shouldn’t be either.

In the moments he’d looked away, Aoi had closed the distance between the two of them. It only took Kizu a few seconds to understand how she’d done so. There were still ripples on the bubbling mud from where Aoi had stepped. The princess might not specialize in elemental magic, but he’d seen her use it first hand in the past. She was a capable mage across the board. She simply excelled in necromancy.

A talent on full display as she stood toe-to-toe with the witch.

A black mist continued to block and shield Stina from Aoi’s attacks, but her knife sliced through it time after time. It was never enough for her to get close. But what she did manage to do was scatter a dozen small bones into Stina’s area through the gash before the fog wall repaired itself.

Those small bones coalesced in the dirt to form into a small ball of bones. It rolled up to Stina’s foot and then disconnected into a line that snaked up the witch’s long leg.

Stina never even opened her eyes, let alone displayed any tell that she knew about the bones creeping higher across her body. Her tone remained unwavering as she chanted.

Only as the bones touched the back of her neck, did the witch’s eyes snap open.

The uncanny words spoken by the witch throughout her chant halted on a sudden full stop. At first, Kizu thought that Aoi had successfully interrupting Stina’s magic.

Then the princess collapsed backwards, tumbling into the black bog water and crying out in pain as it touched her.

Both the water itself and something from within fought to keep Aoi trapped within the bog. Large black blotches latched onto Aoi’s body. Large bulbous leeches squirmed and writhed as they pierced through her close to sink their teeth into her flesh to drain her body.

Kizu suspected that the leeches were actually a part of the terrain, whereas the bog water’s constant pull was Stina’s doing. Though, for all he could tell through his spellsense, the reverse might also very well be true.

The bones latched around Stina’s neck, forming a collar. Stina never removed her eyes from Aoi, even as the bones tightened around her neck. Rather than raise a hand to remove the bones, the witch pointed a single thin finger in the princess's direction.

Aoi thrashed in the bog water. She tried to pull herself out, but the black liquid stuck to her like tar, hissing on contact with skin. The leeches likewise drained the blood from her body.

More than just the damage from the tar-like water, Kizu noticed Aoi’s face had discolored. And it wasn’t simply due to blood loss from the giant leeches. Her shoulders were hunched over as well, as if her body was sectioning off.

“She’s turning her into a caterpillar,” Mae whispered in awe. “She said it’s a hex passed to her by her master. She promised to teach me one day!”

Turning her into a caterpillar. Kizu frowned. He recalled the crone testing hundreds of hexes on him, including a few transformation ones. Never that one though. He thought back to the chant Stina had been reciting. It had been rather familiar. The way in which Stina cast the spell made his memory itch. He closed his eyes and felt like he was on the precipice of a realization. Something to do with casting hexes.

“Backwards,” Anata said.

Kizu’s eyes shot open but he didn’t see. Stina’s chant replayed in his mind, his mental enhancement ability helping him recite it perfectly. There had been old messaging there. An ancient language unable to be translated by his enchanted earring. The language of witches’ curses. No… that was only a piece of its modern application. It was more than that.

The language of Titans.

A hole pierced in his memory. Knowledge once locked away now burst through the opening. Not just verbal lessons from the crone in front of the cottage’s hearth. Hands on training that put his other memories to shame.

So. Much. Pain.

“Kizu?” someone said to him. “Kizu!”

He forced himself to section off everything off in his mind. He created walls.

When his vision finally unblurred, Ione had both her hands on his shoulders and was violently shaking him. She slowed her joggling of his body when he offered her an awkward smile. If anything though, her grip on his shoulders tightened.

Below, the match had concluded. Both girls were on the wooden floor of the stadium, gasping for air.

“Who won?” Kizu asked.

“Kizu? What just happened?” Ione asked, ignoring his question.

Only then did he realize that everyone sitting with him was now staring at him, rather than the exciting combat they’d been glued to moments earlier.

“I….” He cleared his throat. His mouth was dry. Like he’d left it open and forgotten to breathe for a while. “I remembered something about hexes while watching the fight. Triggered a memory. Sorry about that, it distracted me.”

“Remembered something?” Ione cocked an eyebrow, her demeanor shifting from concerned to curious.

Kizu’s eyes flicked over to the two girls. The last thing he wanted was to discuss Titans and witches in front of Mae and Anata. Not because they weren’t trustworthy with the secrets but because… well, they were children and not at all trustworthy with the secrets. If it was just Anata, then maybe. But Mae was now in the service of a witch.

Thankfully, Ione knew him well enough to immediately pick up on his look.

“Oh! You mean about your childhood?” Her face twisted in an acted out wince. “How bad was it?”

“Not great.” Glad for the excuse to move on from the topic, Kizu returned to his earlier query. He gestured down at the two girls in the arena. Aoi was helping Stina to her feet. That might imply the princess had emerged victorious. But the large smile on the witch’s face told another story. It was a toss-up and Kizu had no idea.

“So… who won?”
