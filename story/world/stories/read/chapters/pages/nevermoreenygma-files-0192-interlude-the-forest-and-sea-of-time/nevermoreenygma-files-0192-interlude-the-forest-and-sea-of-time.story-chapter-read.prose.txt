Interlude

The Forest and Sea of Time

It began without a beginning.

She looked at her hands—they were smaller.

She felt small.

Even her rain boots seemed like they belonged to a little girl.

Mai didn’t remember when she had started walking on the damp grass, or why. Only that she was there, among impossibly tall trees, their pointed pine-like tops lost in a completely white sky. The air smelled of rain, wood, iron, and something else.

The forest was alive, but it did not breathe. The leaves remained motionless, suspended, as if time had surrendered to a silent command. On the ground, on the trunks, even floating in the air, thousands of arrows surrounded her. Some rusted, others intact, and many covered in a faint glow that pulsed to the rhythm of a heart that was not hers.

At first, she thought they were illusions. Then she saw the names engraved on the shafts. Names she could not read.

And other arrows were broken, but heavy upon her soul, because somehow she knew what they meant.

The arrows vibrated with the steps of her boots. A faint melody of wood, steel, and wind rose from the ground. As if each projectile, each sharp tip, claimed to have been hers, like a gravestone or incense.

All the times you shot, she thought. All the lives you interrupted are here too.

She knew she hadn't used a bow… but she felt the meaning was the same. She loved the bow and arrow, but it was also a weapon. And that was why the arrows were there.

She tried to keep walking without looking back, but the light of the sky intensified. Some arrows lifted, floating slowly in the air, pointing at her as if accusing her.

She pressed forward. There was no other path.

Each step felt heavier, the grass wetter, more puddles reflecting the white sky. The forest behind grew darker, the trees thinner, until she reached a clearing where there was more water than grass. In the center, a single black arrow stood upon a stone.

It had no name. No reflection. No shadow.

Mai leaned down to touch it, but then a voice emerged from the trees.

A whisper, at first barely a brush against her ear:

“Shoot the arrow and save the child…”

Then more insistent, like an echo coming not from the air but from her own chest:

“Save the child. Save the child.”

“Save the child. Save the child.”

“Save the child. Save the child.”

“Save the child. Save the child.”

“Save the child. Save the child.”

“Planeswalker”

“Save the child. Save the child.”

“Save the child. Save the child.”

She began to hear the sound of the leaves, which she hadn’t noticed before.

She turned in every direction, searching for the source, but couldn’t find it. They were words carried by the wind, which had just picked up, and just as quickly, they had blown away.

She turned, searching for the source of the sound once more, and saw something that stopped her:A small figure sleeping on the branch of a nearby tree.

A girl in a white dress, long violet hair, wrapped in a soft light that seemed to breathe her in. Her face was calm, almost happy, like one who sleeps in a world without danger.

Mai approached slowly, careful not to break the silence.

“Hey… do you know where we are?” she whispered.

The girl didn’t answer. She murmured something unintelligible in her sleep and, with a smile, let a thread of glistening drool fall onto Mai’s forehead.

The contact made her shiver, the liquid was warm, almost real.

Mai frowned, annoyed.

“Ew! Hey! Slob! Close your mouth!” she protested, wiping it with the back of her hand.

But when she looked up, the girl had vanished.

No sound, no breeze. Only the trembling branch, as if she had never been there.

She was alone with the arrows again.

The silence that followed was heavier than the air.

The arrows around her began to tilt slowly, all pointing at her.

And then, a hand touched her shoulder.

Mai spun around, startled.

Before her stood two adults. A woman and a man.

They smiled, but they were not entirely real.

Their bodies seemed made of light and ash, blurred by the wind, like projections about to fade away.

She saw them so clearly that she almost felt the urge to embrace them, but her instinct told her that if she did, they would vanish.

“Dad…? Mom…?” Her voice broke between disbelief and tenderness.

The woman nodded slowly, her eyes full of a compassion that hurt. The man stepped toward her, with the serenity of one who had waited too long to speak.

Her father raised his hand, and in it appeared a bow—the same symbol engraved on the Quironellé family crest: the centaur with a bandaged arm.

The bow glowed faintly, almost dim.

“How many more will you aim at, before you aim at what your heart desires?” Quincy asked, his voice not reproachful, but almost pleading.

Mai lowered her gaze. The bow in her hands felt heavier than ever. She tried to answer, but no words came.

How had that bow come into her possession? Where was it?

As if the questions were a switch, the scene changed.

The trees around her began to dissolve. The floating arrows disintegrated into particles of light that ascended to the sky.

The forest transformed into mist.

And her father’s voice, now distant, repeated once more, like an echo no longer belonging to the world of the living:

“Not everything that flies must hurt. Aim well, my daughter.”

She wanted to answer, to say that she was trying, that she was tired, that she no longer knew if there were wars worth fighting.

Even if it was not enough for everything in the world… she wanted to be loved.

Again.

Again.

Again?

Again?

Why who?

Where?

How?

Everything was crumbling. The ground opened beneath her feet, revealing a white abyss.And in that final moment, she remembered the black arrow.

As she fell, she saw it rise slowly into the air, spinning on itself like a compass finally finding north.

Then she understood—without knowing why—that arrow was not meant to kill.

It was a promise. At some point, it had to reach someone.

When?

Where?

The forest disappeared. Only the echo of a voice remained, mingling with her own heartbeat:“Save the child.”

Mai heard it behind her. Her eyes filled with tears. Miho was there, wearing her glasses and smiling.

“Shoot the arrow and save the child,” she said.

Then darkness claimed her completely. Mai looked around and saw that the darkness was being replaced by waves and she was walking over those waves.

She was in a stormy sea, and the dark sky had been replaced by a sea of stars reflecting on the water. She was disoriented but didn’t know what to do. She looked at her hands. The black arrow and the bow were now in hers.

She didn’t understand. What was she supposed to do? She looked at her feet and realized that despite the stormy waves rising meters high, she was walking on water.

Then she saw it.

In the distance, there seemed to be a small island. A girl was on the shore with a book. She tried to run toward it, but the waves obstructed her path. She wanted to advance, but the motion of the water kept her in place.

Then she saw it. A glow behind her. She turned and saw it.

Far away, on the opposite side of the island with the girl, there was a black sphere with a bluish glow around it.

That black sphere, warped the space around it. It shouldn’t have been there. It felt out of place, yet at the same time it was… almost as if it were meant to be there. Waiting.

For what?

For whom?

But there was something else. She had just detected it. She couldn’t see it, but she knew it was there.

Who was it? Who was it?

Where? And when?

She didn’t know, but in her chest arose a desire to run toward it as well.

What should I do?

Who was behind that sphere or inside it?

“Shoot the arrow and save the child…”

Who was the other girl with the book?

Then she looked to her left and noticed something else. Two stars were spinning around each other in a kind of dance.

Almost instinctively, she looked to the left.

At first, she didn’t see anything, but it seemed she saw an animal running across the water’s surface, almost searching for something.

“Marshmallows…”

Mai turned back. The girl with the book was still there. But there was someone else. Between Mai and the island, there was a small boat.

Inside… that drooling violet-haired girl, sleeping peacefully.

Mai headed toward her to wake her since she was nearby.

But then what she didn’t expect happened.

Her feet sank. The water entered her lungs.

She struggled and tried to swim upward. The boat was close, but she kept sinking.

What should I do? What?

At that moment, as she sank beneath the surface, it seemed to her that she saw an enormous pyramid, and then…

“Not here…”

She felt three hands on her back.

Who?

Who were they?

When?

Where?

She tried to look over her shoulder.

And then... she woke up.
