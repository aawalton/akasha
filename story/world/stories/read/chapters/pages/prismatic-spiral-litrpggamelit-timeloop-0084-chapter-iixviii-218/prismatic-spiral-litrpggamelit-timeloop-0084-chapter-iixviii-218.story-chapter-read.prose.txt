Chapter II.XVIII (2.18)

A sword now formed in her hand, Mitsuko stood in an awkward fighting stance with her feet still sealed onto the skis.

The eyes of the monster moved and Mitsuko felt the snow shift under her feet. She was now without a guide. Both Sterling and Jacqueline were gone. Dead or close enough to it to not matter.

Rather than wait for the snow in the air to clear naturally, Mitsuko reached out with her aura and shoved it all down with Deteriorate. The scene cleared immediately, revealing a massive silver worm the size of a castle wall. Unlike most worms, this creature had half a dozen eyes that circled the rim of its mouth, all of which glowed an ominous blue. But it wasn’t looking at Mitsuko at the moment, it had a different target in its sights.

It surged up, its mouth agape and aimed directly at the small, purpled-haired gnome alone in the snow with a broken ski. She struggled, but fell over as the broken ski caught on the snow. A thousand teeth lined the inside of the worm’s mouth, fresh blood, fur, and ice grinded between the teeth.

“Never!” Mitsuko shouted. She reached out and cast Mend on Holly’s skis. A fraction of a second before the teeth met Holly’s scalp, the broken ski snapped together, made anew, and she zipped backwards as the worm crashed into the snow. Mitsuko continued to cast the spell, forcing Holly to retreat all the way back to the top of the mountain.

The worm twisted, its silver body glinting in the sunlight. Then all six eyes focused on Mitsuko.

She raised her blade. But without command of her footwork, she knew this entire battle would be futile. She would need to wait for it to come to her to strike, and getting close enough without those jagged teeth snagging her would be next to impossible. Her mind raced through her spell arsenal. None of her three temporal spells offered her a decent answer. Mend would let her retreat just as it had Holly. They could return to the village for the last hours of the loop. But what would be the point in that?

“Well, if I’m going to die, better to do it in a way that doesn’t risk my heart getting punctured by one of those teeth.” Mitsuko shifted her weight.

Her skis went from pointing parallel to the slope, to perpendicular.

The massive worm chased after her at an alarming speed, but she cast Deteriorate on her skis, forcing them forward even faster than gravity’s natural pull. The wind stung her eyes and the cold pierced her clothing like a thousand needles burrowing into her skin. Still, she remained steadfast in her conviction. She refused to allow her knees to fold, casting Mend to keep her body from collapsing.

She passed a boulder in a blur. Only sheer chance and luck prevented her from splattering on it.

The wind made breathing difficult. It was so hard to suck in breath. Her eyes watered to the point of blindness. But the noise of the worm chasing after her quieted.

The slope ended abruptly. Her legs buckled backwards, bones snapping, and her skull rammed into the snow. Pain. So much swirling pain. But Mitsuko Mended her body back together a second later, popping her bones back into place and silencing her pounding head. The spell reverted herself to a state of being from before the mad rush down the mountain.

The ice worm wove through the snow above her like a dolphin leaping between waves. An entire avalanche accompanied its approach. She would be buried and immobilized before being mashed between its teeth.

She kicked off her skis and scrambled forward. Somewhere during her trip down the mountain, she’d lost her sword. She considered creating a new one, but discarded the idea. Even with her footing free, what was she supposed to do when faced up against a monster the length of a city block?

She wasn’t fond of the idea of killing herself, but she still prepared herself to go out by her own hand to manually end this loop. Then she spotted a rift in the snow. A crevasse. Without hesitation she dashed forward. Her legs punctured through the crusty top of the snow but she plowed forward, uncaring as snow filled her boots and the ice scratched her calves.

A roar sounded behind her. She felt the rush of snow.

Nearly at the crevasse, she leaped forward, utterly uncaring of what might lie beneath. Arms up like a swan dive into the sea, Mitsuko passed the threshold.

A thousand kilos of snow blasted over her, catching her legs. But it was too late. She fell into the darkness below.

Her arms crumpled as she hit the bottom of the crevasse, breaking and softening her fall enough to allow survival.

Mitsuko moaned and rolled over. She lifted her bent forearms. So much pain. A cast of Mend and they jerked back into place. The pain ceased. Mitsuko took a deep breath and sat up, propping her back against the cavern wall. She flexed her newly healed fingers into a fist. Despite everything, her spell made her feel fresh and prepared for whatever challenge came next.

You have arrived, a deep voice echoed around her. Tedious. I hoped one of the others would waylay you. I should know better than to allow myself to feel disappointment at the ineptitude of mortals, and yet, I still find myself disgusted. Your existence is one that I should not need to dirty my hands to remove.

The cavern trembled. Mitsuko flicked her wrist to create a sword but the darkness remained an impenetrable obstacle here.

Then there was the sound of scampering all around her. The scratching of nails against ice. The light blue glow of the critters’ eyes was just enough of a warning and a signifier for Mitsuko. She took a fighting stance and lunged.

Her first movement skewered a critter in the face. The body slipped off her blade and slammed into one of the others as she moved to cut down another.

One dead. Ten dead. Twenty. They continued to flow out of the cavern’s nooks. A seemingly endless stream of foes. And Mitsuko was far from invincible. While the critters didn’t even reach her knee, they had teeth the size of her fingers and she felt them dig into her flesh. Still she chopped them off, Mended herself, and moved onto the next member of the swarm.

While she continued to repair her body with Mend, Mitsuko noticed very early on that Mend didn’t seem to work on the animals. It had been the same with the golem she fought on Verdant Island. Something about the guardian kept her from interfering with their pawns directly using her magic.

The battle continued. Their claws and teeth continued to rip chunks from her body and clothing. It was an endless cycle of punishment and healing. A fruitless battle against an unknowable number of foes. The bodies stacked up beside her. As each animal died, the light blue glow went out in their eyes. She’d gone through a score of ice swords. Snapped and discarded blades were buried in the bodies.

You persist, the voice said. Tenacious. I will plan accordingly for your next arrival. Begone.

One moment, Mitsuko was slamming the heel of her boot into one of the critters, kicking it away, her back pressed against the cold wall. And then the dozens of hostile glowing eyes winked out in an instant.
