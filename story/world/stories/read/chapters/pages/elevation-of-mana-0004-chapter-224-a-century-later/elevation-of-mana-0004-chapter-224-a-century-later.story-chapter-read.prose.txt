It had been the work of nearly a full century, but it was finally, finally starting to take shape. From my palace, for it could be called nothing else now, I looked out upon Atal. This was not the city I'd come into, with little to my name and no other home to return to all those years ago. It wasn't the dirt roads, or the houses of mud brick and trees, though some houses did still have trees built in and around them, even as part of their structure.

No, this city was something more. The roads were paved with cobbles, straight and layered surfaces that sent water down into sewers and far from the city itself. Windows of glass shone in the daylight from towers my predecessors wouldn't have understood, not huge yet, only a few stories, but growing slowly. The people were not in furs or leather, but cloth, real cloth. Even the beads used as currency were now mostly regulated, with each being longer, marked, and made clear as to what it was, and what it was worth, made by my own government.

“You coming to the table, or gonna stay there and look out at the city all day boss?” Chien asked from behind me, his voice laughing.

“Maybe I shall stay here, it is a beautiful city,” I replied.

“Best in the world, but we have work to do.”“Work, from my own commerce councilor? Horrible,” I joked.

“Your Majesty, the improvements are going well, the walls are better than they've ever been, and with the improved wards we've not had a single monster in the town... well since they went up, except that incident where that man brought those in and they got loose...” the council member here today was named Lory, and he spoke.“Fair, fair,” I agreed.

“Husband, your new project, we do have concerns for it though,” Isha said. “Nobody but you truly understands it, and while that's not completely new, it is rather major.”

“You have questions, please ask them,” I instructed Lory.

“This research facility, I understand the placement, but just because it is near your hometown... It's very inconvenient,” he finally said.

“I'm aware, but be that as it may that is where we shall be doing it.”“Why?” Lory asked.

“I'm afraid that information is need to know, and you do not. The parties that need to know, know.” Of course that was myself, my wife, and Chien, but he didn't need to know.

“I see I see, and you've already begun construction?”“Why indeed I have, some time ago actually, though I've been rather quiet about it. A few of the lower underground levels, nothing much.” It was important to me that this place remain safe, stable, under my direct control, and so, about fifty years ago when I'd finally gotten teleportation down, and my that had been difficult, I began building a lab there, in the old crater, hidden, protected by me personally. None other than my family would be allowed in.

“We'll need dormitories, and security...”“Already begun the latter, but yet, please do arrange, we may have to do rotational shifts, that is fine.”“The newest automatons are functioning then?”“Yes, though I feel they still need improvements.”The automatons in question were a number of different type of golem. My wife had made the living crystal I needed for my work, and I had struggled with her to form it into something magnificent, a self replicating, computer framework, modular, as few would need the more advanced versions, there were even some methods to alter it, though those would take my personal touch to do.

Isha's face flashed for a moment as we spoke of them, as we brought up the 'cores' as I was beginning to call them. It was a sore subject for her.

“Anything else?” I asked.

“Well... no, but this still seems...”“Excellent. We can take our time on this, work with Chien to arrange a schedule that won't harm us.”It wouldn't be too much of a problem, time, sure it would take time, but time we had. We had the resources of a continent to throw at it.

“I still have business,” Chien said. “Five percent of the land still isn't ours, we could take it, or boycott them.”“No, we've gained this so far through peace, we can gain the rest through the same,” I answered. “Restrict advanced works, but not basic ones.”That had been the strategy to conquer. Our weapons could defend against even the worst beasts that we'd seen in the last hundred years, but others? No, with so many elders and ancients dead many villages felt afraid, weak. We'd eventually gotten them to sign on with us as a whole, support in the form of weapons and defenses in return for a small tax (Mostly just a symbolic tribute) and pay for those weapons (Which was slowly collected). There were a few more things here and there, laws, small amounts of required work, but it wasn't arduous.“Now, please excuse me. Isha and I have some other things to discuss.”Once they'd left I turned to her.“Are you okay?” I asked, knowing she wasn't.
