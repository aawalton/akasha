It was finally time for Oswald to make his debut. The boy had gone through a lot of fights in his life, not few with spectators too. Yet, part of him did feel a bit nervous.

After all, one of his biggest dreams was to participate in the Tournament of Champions. The underground arena was a far cry from that kind of tournament, but it was still a big event nonetheless.

Oswald was now in the fighter's waiting room. He looked around, spottign how a single drop of wax clung to the copper rim of the lantern, The iron chain above it groaned with the faintest sway. Dust drifted in the air, thin, idle. The kind of air that crawled into your clothes and stayed.

The boy sat beneath it, pressed to the cold wall, elbows braced on his knees. Wonder how many didn't make it?

Beside him, the faint creak of leather straps betrayed a man's nervous habit, slow and repetitive. Further down the row, metal rasped against stone, a whetstone dragged across a blade. The sound folded into the room like breath, one of many low murmurs under lantern light.

Oswald let his fingers curl inward. Calluses scraped faintly against his palm. Focus. Breathe.

Then a voice cut through it all, dulled only slightly by the thick stone above. “Ladies and gentlemen! Tonight’s first fight starts now!”

A cheer erupted like a cannon blast, crashing through the walls. The sound rolled through the chamber like thunder, followed by a second wave of noise: stomping feet, chanting, screaming.

Even though the waiting room was under the arena, Oswald could hear and feel everything. He wondered what kind of magic was used that would allow a voice to be projected so loudly. Wonder what kinda magic pulls that off? Ain’t no way he’s shoutin’ that loud without help.

His eyes flicked up toward the stone ceiling. Some kinda sound spell, maybe? Or runes? Dunno. But whatever it is, it’s workin’ like a charm.

The waiting room stretched in a half-moon around the back corridor, carved from dark stone with vaulted ceilings supported by thick beams of blackened iron. Most of the benches were occupied by rough men in mismatched armor, draped in mail, fur, or leathers that had seen too many winters. Their faces were scarred, eyes sunken and hard, their weapons never out of reach.

A sharp crackle from the ceiling snapped Oswald’s focus. “Let the Rumble commence! And stepping into the pit first… give it up for your very own butcher of the backstreets: Knives Mallor!”

The crowd’s roar detonated a heartbeat later, and the ceiling shuddered with the impact of thousands of stomping feet. “Mallor! Mallor! Mallor!”

Dust trembled off the lantern’s chain. Halfway down the bench, a man stirred.

He rose without urgency, boot soles dragging just a little too long across the stone. A hood shadowed his face, tugged low over a frame too narrow for the title he carried. His posture slouched, shoulders bunched inward like he’d rather disappear into the wall. A curved dagger slapped loosely at his side with each step, its grip wrapped in cloth to hide the cracks.

He exhaled hard through his nose, once, like it was the only breath that mattered. Then he walked toward the door. Walks like he’s hopin’ the floor swallows him before the crowd gets a look.

As the man passed, Oswald caught a glimpse of his face, pale, and stretched too tight. Sweat gathered on his brow despite the chill down here, clinging like dew.

At the door, he stopped just enough for his foot to falter before pressing forward again. Then he vanished through the opening, swallowed by the tunnel.

To Oswald’s left, someone let out a dry chuckle. “He’s dead."

“Bet he pisses hisself ‘fore the first swing,” another said.

Oswald turned toward the source. Two men sat shoulder to shoulder on the next bench. One was built like a sack of bricks, bald with a jaw like a sledgehammer, and a rust-colored beard matted close to his neck. The other was thinner, with eyes constantly darting like a weasel waiting for something to go wrong. Both had worn axes strapped to their backs.

They were grinning like boys who’d told a filthy joke. Oswald tilted his head. “What’s so funny?”

The bigger man grinned wider, teeth yellow and cracked. “Just talkin’ about Knives Mallor. Poor bastard’s gonna die ugly, that’s all.”

“Dead meat, that one. The arena always opens with a slaughter, get the crowd goin’. That’s tradition,” the thinner man said.

The larger one dragged a scarred knuckle across his nose, then flicked whatever came off onto the ground. “First fighter’s either a fuckin’ animal… or a sacrifice. Guess which one they picked tonight?”

They snickered, like the whole thing was some joke they’d heard a hundred times and never stopped finding funny. Oswald leaned back a touch, gaze fixed on the now-closed door. Tradition, huh? Feed the lamb to wake the wolves.

Soon, another roar crashed down from above, sharper this time. A heavy impact followed. The crowd howled, high and feral.

The two across from him whooped like children watching a bonfire. One slapped the other’s shoulder with a meaty hand.

“Right on time,” the big one said, grinning.

Guess they weren’t wrong. That Mallor guy barely lasted a few minutes.

Above, the crowd hadn’t let up. Their roar had climbed a notch now, insatiable. Another body offered. Another rush to feed the frenzy.

Then the announcer spoke again. “Next into the pit, we’ve got a rookie for you! Straight from the gutters, fighting with one eye and a death wish to match. Give it up for... Black Eye!”

Right. That’s me.

Oswald rose slowly. His boots scraped cleanly against the stone as he moved, and his fingers brushed the brass tag at his belt, a pointless check, but it steadied him.

To his left, the two men across the bench broke into fits again. The thin one spoke first. “Black Eye! Fittin’ name! You’ll leave with the other one busted too!”

The bigger one slapped his knee. “Rookie’s got guts, walkin’ in with a fuckin’ eyepatch.”

As Oswald passed them, he tossed a glance over his shoulder and let his mouth curl into a smirk.“Appreciate the support, but with the way you two sit on your asses all day, I figured you were the furniture.”

The laughter stopped cold. The weasel-faced one’s grin vanished. “What’d you say?”

Oswald paused at the door, just long enough to look back. His eye met theirs. “I said I’m not dying out there. Sorry to disappoint.”

A quiet beat followed. The heavy one’s jaw tightened. The thin one looked like he might spit again. But neither spoke.

Oswald turned back and shoved the door open. It groaned on its hinges, and the warm torchlight at his back gave way to a deeper, angrier glow. The corridor ahead pulsed with reddish light, cast by glowing veins of crystal embedded in the walls. This is it.

He stepped forward. The door shut behind him with an echoing thud.

The hallway stretched on, short but tight. The crystals pulsed once more, brighter this time, like they’d noticed him. Each step echoed louder than it should have, bouncing off the walls and bleeding into the growl of the crowd above.

At the end stood a circular platform of carved stone, set into the ground and framed by a ring of glowing runes. Pale blue etched lines flickered underfoot, as if waiting. Oswald stepped onto it.

The stone beneath him shivered sharply. Light blazed across the runes. The platform lit up from the inside out, a sudden wash of blue and silver that flared beneath his boots.

He staggered, caught off-guard as the lift lurched upward. His weight shifted with the motion, and he threw one hand out instinctively. Damn, didn’t think it’d jump like that.

The motion steadied quickly, rising with unnatural speed. Oswald righted himself, planting his feet wider apart as the glow settled into a steady pulse. The platform rose into an open stone chamber above. As it stopped with a soft, final click, Oswald found himself facing another hallway, this one wider,  built for spectacle. The floor sloped gently upward toward a set of towering iron gates.

He stepped off the platform and moved toward the light ahead.The hallway opened into a rounded chamber. Ahead, were gates made with blackened iron, etched with faint runic lines, pulsing with dull light like veins beneath skin. Dust clung to the edges where metal met stone, and the hinges groaned with restrained tension.

A low clank rang out, followed by the hiss of arcane pressure releasing. With a heavy shudder, the gate pulled apart.

Oswald raised a hand slightly, shielding his eye as the light from the arena struck him full-on. Not sunlight, but artificial brilliance, an unforgiving white cast by enchantments embedded in the dome high above. He squinted against it, letting his fingers drop slowly as his vision adjusted.

White sand stretched out before him. Long, jagged grooves crisscrossed the pit, carved by blades dragged through desperation.

Oswald stepped out beneath that thundering chorus. The air felt hotter here, like the pit was breathing heat into him.

From high above, the announcer’s voice flared to life again, amplified by the same enchantments that rattled the stone. “Don’t blink, or you might miss him! It’s the rookie: Black Eye!”

A smattering of applause followed, almost polite. It scattered across the stands like tossed coins, buried quickly beneath jeers and crude laughter. Someone whistled.

Something soft and wet thumped into the sand near Oswald’s boot: a half-eaten fruit, bruised and leaking pulp. Cute.

A mechanical hiss echoed across the arena as the opposite gate shuddered open.  “And facing him tonight, a crowd favorite returning for blood, this silent killer wears poison like perfume! Make some noise for... Venomjaw!”

The arena erupted. The crowd’s roar swallowed the air whole. Fists slammed into wooden rails, voices bled together into a chorus of raw frenzy. “Venomjaw! Venomjaw!”

They shouted it like a spell, like calling him would summon the violence faster. The man who stepped through the gate wore a black hood pulled low. A black mask covered the lower half of his face, tightly strapped and marked with a single green line across its mouth. Two sickles hung at his hips, curved like fangs, each blade kissed by something sickly and unnatural.

Vials clinked faintly as he walked. Straps held them snug against his chest, narrow tubes of cloudy liquid, tucked between throwing knives and bandage wraps wound tight across his ribs.

Venomjaw raised one of his sickles and drew a gloved finger along the flat of the blade, like he wanted it to be seen. Then he pointed it straight at Oswald, held it there, and with the other hand dragged his thumb across his own throat. A mock execution.

The crowd screamed approval. Oswald didn’t move. Let him waste the energy. I ain’t here to dance.

He drew a long breath through his nose. The air stung a little, part sweat, part dust, part whatever had soaked into the sand. But his heartbeat stayed even.

The sword at his hip weighed just enough to anchor him, the familiar chill of the hilt brushing his knuckles as he let one hand fall near it. Above, the announcer’s voice climbed once more.

“Let’s count it down!”

“Three—!”

Oswald dropped his stance and drew his sword.

“Two—!”

Across the pit, Venomjaw’s right foot slid forward.

“One!”

The horn blared, and Oswald launched forward before the echo had even finished bouncing off the stone. Sand burst beneath his boots in a cloud of grit and heat. He crossed the distance in a blink, feet driving through the arena floor.

There was no wasted motion, just unrelenting speed. His blade flashed free in a smooth arc before his opponent could react.

Venomjaw jerked one arm up in a panicked defense, his sickle rising to meet the strike. But he had already lost. Oswald’s sword carved through the air, cleaving across the exposed joint of his elbow before the man could fully react. The sickle clattered away untouched as steel met flesh. Skin parted, bone cracked, and in the blink of an eye, the arm came off entirely.

Blood sprayed out in a sharp arc, droplets spinning before they splattered to the ground. The severed limb landed with a wet, lifeless smack, half-buried in the churned grit. A scream ripped out of Venomjaw’s throat.

Oswald didn’t stop to watch him fall. He pivoted on his heel as he rolled into the next movement. His grip shifted on the hilt, already preparing for another strike, body held low and steady like a predator poised to finish what he'd started. Fast enough for you?

Because he had been training with Selene all this time, lesser opponents like Venomjaw weren't much of a fight for Oswald. He was used to fighting someone much faster and stronger, and with the experience to match.

Above, the mood faltered. The crowd that had moments ago erupted with bloodlust now held its collective breath. The cheers died in their throats, replaced by a stunned hush. Their favorite fighter had been disarmed, literally, before he'd even landed a blow.

A few scattered voices tried to cheer, but they were quickly drowned out by the silence pressing down from all sides. The energy had shifted completely.

The pit had swallowed their confidence whole. Oswald stood at the center of it. And for the first time, they watched him. Really watched.
