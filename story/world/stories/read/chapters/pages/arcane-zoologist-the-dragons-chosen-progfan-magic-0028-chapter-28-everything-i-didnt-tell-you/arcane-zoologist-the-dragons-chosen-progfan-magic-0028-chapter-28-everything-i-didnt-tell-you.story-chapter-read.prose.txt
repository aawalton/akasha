The next morning, despite not wanting to wake up early, Callie woke up early and was unable to fall asleep. Not only because of the nervous mutterings of her parents in the kitchen, but also due to her thoughts.

She would have to explain everything to her parents. The magic, the fact that she could speak to arcane beasts, perhaps even her meeting with the dragon. How would Mom, the best hunter in the village, react to the fact that a dragon was living somewhere very close by? Would she even believe Callie? After all, dragons were supposed to be extinct.

But Callie was tired of lying to her parents. She wanted to tell them the truth, especially to Mom, who had understood the importance of the egg.

Frankly, she felt they were lucky that glauxis wasn't the kind of creature to hold a grudge. Callie couldn't imagine the rage and desperation the glauxis must have felt when his precious egg was stolen. Imagining that someone from her family could have been kidnapped by some strangers filled her with dread and anger.

More than her parents, however, Callie was afraid of the villagers. They had always viewed her as an oddball. Weirdo who constantly caused trouble. How would they view her after this incident? Would they blame her for it? Had anyone noticed her magic? While Callie herself had noticed the new petal only after getting home, somebody might have seen the glow coming from underneath her tunic.

It took Callie a moment to muster her courage and open the door to her room. She peeked outside, and the mutterings of her parents instantly stopped. She was met with their thoughtful gazes.

"Good morning, Callie," Mom greeted with a kind smile.

"Morning," Dad grumbled, a deep crease in his forehead.

"Good morning…" Callie said quietly and scuttled closer to Mom. It felt like it would be safer if she stayed close to her.

Mom chuckled at this while Dad seemed to grow even grumpier.

"You've got a lot of explaining to do, Calluna," Dad said.

"There, there, Rowan. Let her eat breakfast at the very least," Mom said.

Dad grumbled something Callie couldn't make out and went over to the stove. He prepared some tea and an omelet with tomatoes for breakfast. Callie was surprised that despite his grumbling, he would make them something so filling. Usually, omelets were saved for special occasions, since they tried to save some to sell or trade to other villagers.

Callen joined them soon after Dad had completed breakfast, likely woken up by the delicious smell of cooked eggs. He appeared just as tired as Callie felt, and his lips were pursed into a thin line as his eyes went from Callie to their parents.

He demonstratively sat down next to Callie, resting his arm over her shoulder.

"Brother?" Callie looked at him quizzically.

"It'll be alright," he told her with a smile, then stared at Dad with a deviant expression.

Dad just rolled his eyes. "You're indulging her far too much, Callen."

"Breakfast first," Mom reminded everyone sternly.

So they all ate in tense silence. Callie's emotions were mingled as she enjoyed the omelet with fresh tomatoes. These were the first tomatoes of this year, and they were absolutely delicious! However, even their sweet and tangy flavor wasn't enough to wash away the obvious tension in the room.

Callie was by no means observant when it came to social matters, but even she felt the heavy atmosphere. Even Mom's smile seemed kind of forced while Callen continued observing their parents with one arm firmly on Callie's shoulder throughout breakfast.

He was eating very slowly, very unlike himself. Initially, Callie ate slowly, too, but eventually she just got tired of this oppressive heaviness in the air. She hurriedly finished the remainder of her omelet and stared at her parents with a decisive expression. There was no escaping this anyway.

Seeing that Callie was done, Callen finished his breakfast in one bite and said, "Before you begin, I will say that I've known everything that has been going on with Callie since the beginning."

Dad narrowed his eyes at him.

Mom merely shrugged. "You two have been close ever since Callie was born. I'd frankly be more surprised if you didn't know. In either case, I would like for Callie to explain everything first. I suspect it has something to do with that night you spent in the forest."

Callie nodded. Her heart raced like crazy. She was afraid of her parents' reply. They had been so mad about her following Snowglow. But they both saw the glauxis and how it had bowed respectfully for her. Surely, it should make it clear that arcane beasts can't be dismissed as some murderous, unreasonable monsters.

"I… I didn't tell you everything that happened that night… The truth is…" Callie looked nervously at her parents.

Mom was fully focused on her, her expression calm while Dad… well, he still looked grumpy, but he was probably trying not to look so severe, judging by how he would occasionally try to relax his face. Rather unsuccessfully. Callen gave her a reassuring squeeze, his hand never leaving her shoulder.

"The truth is that on that night… I met a dragon," she finally managed to say. Her voice was quiet, but since everyone had been so focused on her, it still felt like her silent confession had shaken the whole house.

Mom blinked. Dad's eyes went wide.

"Callie… Did you just say a dragon?" Mom asked.

"No, no, no!" Dad shook his head. "Dragons have been extinct for a long time. Everyone knows that."

"Callie, why don't you show them your drawing?" Callen suggested.

"You drew it? Please, show us," Mom urged.

"Maeve, you can't—"

"Rowan, let her show us," Mom insisted, making Dad cross his arms over his chest.

Callie nodded and went to her room to retrieve her notes. It took a bit of time to retrieve the drawing of the dragon, which she had folded and hidden between the planks of her bedframe. Once it was in her hands, she nervously unfolded the paper and presented it to Mom.

Mom took it carefully, putting it in front of herself and Dad.

"Th-This can't be real. You've made it up, haven't you?" Dad asked, although his eyes never left the drawing.

"A dragon…" Mom whispered, studying every bit of the drawing with her eyes, her face growing increasingly pale. "It could have killed you instantly."

"I… I did have that thought for a moment…" Callied said.

"Maeve, y-you're not actually believing it. It's just a head. A very good drawing for sure, but this doesn't prove anything…"

"I couldn't draw everything else, because I didn't see it all that well. It was dark…" Callie said and then explained to them how the encounter went.

Dad's expression seemed to shift with her every word, especially when she told them about the magic flower and the heat she felt within herself after drinking. But neither he nor Mom interrupted her story.

"Callen, did you see the dragon?" Mom asked once Callie was done.

"No, but I've seen Callie use magic. When we came face to face with the glauxis she grew roots out of the ground. I-It was amazing," Callen said.

"Could you show us?" Mom asked.

"I-I can try, but I'm not very good at controlling it. At all," Callie muttered.

"Give it a try," Dad urged, looking genuinely interested. "Should we head outside, so you could uhh… grow something?"

"I've managed to burn stuff twice, so I could try that."

Dad narrowed his eyes. "Is that what happened to Aurelia? You burned her with magic?"

"No! I didn't!" Callie protested.

"So what happened with her and Gerda? Since you're telling us everything, you might as well tell us what happened then," Dad demanded.

Callie was apprehensive.

"Callie!" Dad grumbled.

"It was Wiggles…" she reluctantly admitted. "But you can't hurt him! He was only protecting me!"

"Wiggles?" Mom and Dad asked at the same time.

"Wiggles… He's my mireling friend…"

"A mireling…" Mom muttered, her voice sounding weary.

Dad stared at Callie for a while, then let out a deep sigh, rubbing the bridge of his nose. "Okay, let's return to that later. Show us your magic, please."

Callie nodded, praying that her parents wouldn't hurt Wiggles. Perhaps she should warn her friend to be more careful. In the meantime, she walked up to the hearth and took a piece of kindling in her hands.

The fire always seemed to come out when I was frustrated or angry. I should try that to make it burn.

Recalling the time when Aurelia was pushing her around and calling her names, Callie tried to focus on the frustration and anger she felt at the time. She tried to imagine what would have happened had she allowed the anger seething within her to break free.

The energy within her stirred, but it responded far more quickly than before, heating up inside her body. The sudden intensity startled Callie, so she hurriedly redirected the heat to her hand and tried to guide the piece of kindling. It burst into flames.

Not the small fire that appeared during Mister Blackthorn's class. No, the whole piece was instantly engulfed, causing Callie to yelp and drop it on the floor.

"Dear heavens!" Dad exclaimed, grabbing the bucket in the corner of the kitchen and dumping it on the flaming piece of kindling.

"I…I… It wasn't this strong before…" Callie said shakily. "My magic must have grown stronger after the glauxis bowed to me."

Mom urged Callie to sit down again, looking rather shaken herself. They all stared at the charred remains of the kindling for a moment. It had been on fire for just a few seconds, yet there was basically nothing left.

"Could you explain what you meant by your magic growing stronger?" Mom said patiently. Dad kept staring at the kindling, as did Callen, who wore a worried frown on his face.

"When I first got the mark from the dragon, it was only a small dot. But after I befriended Wiggles, this first petal appeared, and since then, I was able to understand arcane beasts. To hear their voices," Callie said, pointing at the first petal on her forearm. "After I befriended Snowglow—"

"Snowglow?" Dad questioned, narrowing his eyes.

"It's the glimmerhare that I saved," she explained, and her parents relaxed a little. "After I befriended Snowglow, the second petal appeared. After that, I was able to grow the dandilynx flower for Mom."

"You grew the flower?" Mom asked in surprise.

"Yeah, Callie never went to the Deep Forest. It's a lie I made up so you wouldn't grow suspicious of her," Callen said.

Dad frowned, but with a motion of his hand, urged Callie to continue.

"The third petal appeared just yesterday evening, after the glauxis bowed to me. It's why my magic is likely stronger now."

Mom and Dad stared at Callie's mark, gently touching it and looking at it from all angles.

"Rowan, this is undeniable. Magic like that doesn't just appear out of nowhere. You know how hard it is to produce even a little bit of magic. How much effort it takes. It's not something a nine-year-old child would be able to learn on their own. We have to assume that the dragon is real," Mom said, looking Dad deep in the eyes.

He shook his head. "Maeve… But think about it… A dragon! The ramifications of that could shake the whole country, no, the whole world to its core. They are supposed to be extinct! Wh-What do you think is going to happen if the word gets out?"

"You can't hurt the dragon!" Callie said firmly, jumping to her feet.

"For heaven's sake, child! Nobody would even dare to think of such a thing," Dad shot back.

"No? I was certain you'd try to hunt it…"

"Callie, I am not so foolish as to believe that I could ever defeat a dragon. I wouldn't even dare… I mean, all the legends, the myths say that dragons were like gods. They could shape mountains, change seasons, bring good harvests, and heal plagues. To think that one is living in our very forest…" Mom muttered, running her hand through her hair.

"What would the dragonbloods do?" Dad asked, horror clear in his eyes.

"NO! They can't know! Granny Ethel said they would take me away if they knew of my magic. If they knew of the dragon, they would kill it, would they not?" Callie demanded.

"I don't know what they would do, but nobody can find out about the dragon or Callie's magic!" Mom said firmly.

Callen glanced at Callie nervously. "But… Granny Ethel knows, doesn't she? And yesterday you told me that Tristan saw you use magic. Not to mention, I noticed Callie's mark glowed through the tunic yesterday. It is possible that I wasn't the only one to notice."

A knock resounded on the door, making all four flinch.

"Maeve, Rowan! You and your children have been summoned to meet with Chief Aemon."
