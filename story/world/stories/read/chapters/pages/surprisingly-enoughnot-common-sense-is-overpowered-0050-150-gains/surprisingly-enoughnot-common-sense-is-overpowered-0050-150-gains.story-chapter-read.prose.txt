Ning did not linger near the Treasure Abundant Pavilion for long. After Sun Jing promised to explain the situation to Shopkeeper Feng, there was little reason for him to remain.

Both Sun Jing and Xiao Fan decided to stay behind for a while longer, since Shopkeeper Feng was still occupied with customers, but Ning had already had enough excitement for one day. More importantly, he had just obtained a rare jade talisman slip, and remaining in a crowded marketplace while carrying something potentially valuable seemed like an unnecessary risk.

Especially when one of the people standing beside him was a known trouble magnet.

With his main purpose already accomplished, Ning quietly slipped away and headed back toward the sect. He used Turtle Breathing to suppress his presence while occasionally activating Shadow Steps to quicken his pace, and with no news of his discovery spreading yet, his ordinary outer sect robes drew little attention.

Fortunately, no one followed him.

Only after returning to his small hut and carefully checking the surroundings did Ning finally relax. He closed the door behind him, sat on the edge of his bed, and rubbed his hands together.

"Time to check the loot."

He laid everything out neatly on the table, starting with the pair of boots.

The High-Grade Wind-Stepping Boots were the first high-grade First-Tier equipment Ning had ever possessed. Their inscriptions were relatively simple, designed almost entirely around increasing the wearer's movement speed rather than improving control or reducing qi consumption.

In other words, they did one thing.

Speed.

Ning was perfectly fine with that.

His attention soon shifted toward the second item lying beside them.

The sock.

"...This thing."

Getting a pair of boots and a single sock on the same trip was strange enough by itself, but the sock was considerably more interesting than its appearance suggested. Ning picked it up and examined the soft gray fabric, running it between his fingers before extending his spiritual sense into the material. The weave was much finer than ordinary cloth, and faint strands of something unfamiliar had been incorporated throughout it.

Ning studied it for a moment before remembering a description he'd once read.

"This resembles Netherspider silk."

He couldn't be certain. Ning had only encountered the material in books and had never handled the real thing before, but several of its characteristics seemed to match. Netherspider silk was a high-quality Second-Tier material known for its durability, corrosion resistance, and excellent compatibility with spiritual energy. It was also considered something of a luxury material, particularly for clothing, since fabric woven from it was supposedly extremely comfortable to wear.

If this really was Netherspider silk, using it to make a sock was absurdly extravagant.

Ning tugged at the fabric experimentally. It stretched slightly beneath his fingers before immediately returning to its original shape without leaving any visible damage, which at least matched what he'd read about the material's durability.

"It certainly seems possible, but I can't tell what grade the finished item is or what inscriptions were used."

Unlike the Wind-Stepping Boots, the sock didn't noticeably circulate qi or reveal any obvious function when he examined it with his spiritual sense. There might have been a formation hidden within the weave, but if there was, it was either too subtle for Ning to recognize or required some particular method to activate.

There was also the perfectly reasonable possibility that someone had simply spent an unreasonable amount of money making an extremely comfortable sock.

Ning decided not to guess any further. One of the members of the Mutual Aid Association worked as an apprentice spiritual craftsman, so he could take it over later and have someone who actually knew what they were looking at examine it. If the material really was Netherspider silk and could be extracted without damaging it, selling or reusing the silk would probably be the most practical choice.

If not, Xiao Fan's suggestion remained available.

Ning looked at the sock for another moment.

It would make a remarkably durable container for rocks.

With that important matter settled, Ning finally turned toward the item he was most interested in.

The jade talisman slip.

Its surface was smooth and faintly warm beneath his fingers, with thin, elegant markings etched throughout the pale jade. Unlike the relatively straightforward inscriptions on the boots, these overlapped and intertwined in ways Ning couldn't begin to understand.

He didn't immediately pour qi into it.

Instead, Ning lit a stick of spiritual incense, sat cross-legged on the bed, and spent several minutes regulating his breathing until his thoughts had completely settled. Activating a jade talisman slip placed a considerable burden on the mind as information was transmitted directly into the cultivator's consciousness, and in severe cases, an unprepared cultivator could even lose consciousness.

That was precisely why Ning hadn't been stupid enough to activate an unknown one in the middle of a marketplace.

Once he was satisfied with his condition, Ning held the jade slip between his fingers and carefully sent a thin thread of spiritual energy into it.

The response was immediate.

A torrent of information rushed into his consciousness as lines of text, flowing diagrams, cultivation routes, and unfamiliar symbols appeared one after another. Even prepared, Ning felt his mind strain beneath the sudden influx, but he steadied his breathing and allowed the information to settle rather than fighting against it.

Only when the transmission finally ended did Ning slowly open his eyes.

"An ocular technique..."

His exhaustion was momentarily forgotten as he began reviewing what he'd received.

Ocular techniques were exceedingly rare among martial arts. At the very least, Ning had never encountered one on the first floor of the Scripture Pavilion, and judging from the information now contained within his mind, this wasn't some ordinary mortal or Spirit-Grade technique either.

[Pure Eyes: Refine the eyes to a pure state, allowing the practitioner to perceive truth beyond appearances. The cultivated gaze grows clear and undefiled, able to pierce falsehoods, discern hidden qi, and see through illusory veils.]

More importantly, Pure Eyes possessed multiple stages of cultivation, something generally associated with much more advanced techniques.

There were five stages in total.

Unfortunately, the jade talisman slip contained only the first two.

Ning stared at it for several seconds.

"...Of course."

It wasn't impossible to imprint an entire inheritance into a jade slip, which meant whoever had created this one had either possessed only the first two stages or deliberately recorded only part of the technique. Either way, Ning was now left with an incomplete inheritance and absolutely no clue where the remaining three stages might be.

Finding them could be like searching for a needle in a haystack.

Fortunately, Ning knew someone who specialized in finding needles in haystacks without even realizing he was doing it.

He only needed to pass the technique to Xiao Fan.

With that guy's luck, Ning wouldn't even be surprised if Xiao Fan fell off a cliff next month, landed inside some forgotten cave, and found the third stage carved into the wall.

That was simply proper utilization of protagonist resources.

Putting aside the missing portions for now, Ning continued studying the requirements for cultivating the first stage and soon encountered another problem.

Pure Eyes couldn't be cultivated through qi circulation alone. The eyes needed to be regularly nourished with specially prepared spiritual liquids made from various natural essences, including dawn dew, refined spiritual spring water, and extracts from certain spiritual plants. These substances would gradually nourish and temper the eyes while the technique itself guided the transformation, and without them, progress would eventually stagnate regardless of how diligently he practiced.

Ning remained silent for a moment as he considered what that meant.

Then he sighed.

"My poor wallet is going to suffer again."

He had only just obtained a decent amount of spirit stones, and apparently the cultivation world had already found another way to take them away from him.

Even so, Ning couldn't suppress the grin slowly spreading across his face.

Setting aside the technique's actual usefulness, there was something undeniably exciting about cultivating his eyes until they possessed abilities ordinary people could only dream of. His memories from his previous life had left him with a particular appreciation for that sort of thing.

Perhaps it was a little childish, but Ning didn't care.

Special eyes were cool.

Some things simply didn't need a better reason.

...

What was cultivation?

Some predecessors claimed that cultivating immortality was about achieving longevity. Others said it was about pursuing freedom, transcending mortality, or standing tall between heaven and earth.

Ning understood cultivation in far more tangible terms. Lofty ideals were all well and good, but they didn't tell him what was actually happening inside his body whenever he sat down and circulated the Pure Qi Sutra.

To him, cultivation was the process by which a cultivator used their spiritual root to absorb the spiritual energy of heaven and earth, then refined it through a cultivation technique into qi that their body could use. As cultivation progressed, the cultivator accumulated more qi, their meridians gradually adapted to carrying greater amounts of it, and their body became capable of sustaining an increasingly powerful circulation.

In simpler terms, cultivation involved continuously increasing how much spiritual qi one's body could contain, circulate, and withstand.

But how was that limit actually increased?

Over the past four years, Ning had not cultivated blindly. Whenever he circulated the Pure Qi Sutra, swallowed a cultivation pill, or practiced under different conditions, he paid close attention to the changes taking place within his body.

Know Thyself made that considerably easier.

He could observe the condition of his meridians, notice even subtle changes in the strain placed upon them, follow the movement and accumulation of spiritual energy within his body, and compare the results afterward through the cultivation progress displayed on his panel.

The knowledge Ning carried from his previous life certainly helped him approach problems differently, but he had long since understood that it could only take him so far. This world operated according to principles he was still learning, and trying to force everything into the framework of his previous world's knowledge would eventually lead him astray.

So he observed, compared, and experimented.

After all, knowledge truly was power.

It was through those repeated observations that Ning eventually noticed something interesting.

Whenever he cultivated, there seemed to be two different kinds of spiritual pressure involved in the process.

The first was external spiritual pressure, determined primarily by the density of spiritual energy in his surroundings. The denser the spiritual energy, the greater the pressure surrounding his body, which was one of the reasons Spirit Gathering Formations and naturally energy-rich cultivation grounds were so valuable.

The second was internal spiritual pressure, produced as spiritual energy and medicinal power accumulated within his body faster than they could immediately be refined.

The basic principle wasn't anything revolutionary. Elder Shen had already explained that there was a limit to how much spiritual energy a cultivator's body could handle at once. Take in more than the meridians could withstand, and the result wouldn't be faster cultivation but an injury.

What interested Ning was what happened before reaching that limit.

Through repeated experiments, he noticed that the rate at which he consumed the medicinal power of cultivation pills changed depending on the internal spiritual pressure within his body. When that pressure remained relatively low, the pill was consumed normally, but as more energy accumulated within his meridians, its medicinal power was drawn into his circulation and refined noticeably faster.

Naturally, he couldn't simply keep increasing the pressure.

Push too far and the strain on his meridians became obvious through Know Thyself. They would begin to swell, his circulation would become increasingly difficult to maintain, and eventually he would be forced to slow down before causing actual damage.

There was a point between those two extremes where the pressure remained high enough to accelerate the process without exceeding what his meridians could safely endure.

More interestingly, external spiritual pressure seemed to affect that balance as well.

That was where Ning's experiment began.

He activated the small Spirit Gathering Formation in his room and placed ten low-grade spirit stones into the array plate. Faint blue light spread through the inscriptions before forming a hemispherical barrier around him, and the spiritual energy within the enclosed space steadily grew denser.

Ning waited until the formation stabilized before sitting cross-legged at its center.

The increased external spiritual pressure ensured that there would be a steady supply of spiritual energy around him. Now he needed to establish the corresponding pressure within his body.

Ning took out a Purple Cloud Pill, a commonly used cultivation pill among mid-stage Qi Condensation cultivators. Its medicinal nature was relatively mild, releasing its power steadily rather than in a violent surge, which made it particularly suitable for what he wanted to test.

He swallowed it and began circulating the Pure Qi Sutra.

As the pill dissolved, medicinal power spread throughout his body while spiritual energy from the surrounding formation entered through his spiritual root. Ning guided both into the familiar circulation route, carefully observing himself through Know Thyself as the amount of energy within his meridians increased.

His internal spiritual pressure gradually rose.

Ning didn't hurry. Whenever the strain on his meridians remained comfortably within what he knew they could withstand, he allowed more energy into his circulation. Whenever the strain began approaching the dangerous point, he eased back and concentrated on refining what was already there.

Cycle after cycle passed.

Raw spiritual energy was drawn into his body, circulated through his meridians, refined through the Pure Qi Sutra, and gradually transformed into his own qi before returning to his dantian. At the same time, the medicinal power of the Purple Cloud Pill continued feeding into the process.

Slowly, Ning found the balance he had been searching for.

Internal spiritual pressure accelerated the rate at which the Purple Cloud Pill's medicinal power was drawn into his circulation and consumed, while the external spiritual pressure maintained by the Spirit Gathering Formation supported the increased rate of absorption and refinement. As long as he kept both within the right range, he could maintain a much faster process without pushing his meridians beyond what they could safely endure.

That was the important part.

Too little internal pressure and he wasn't taking full advantage of what his meridians could currently handle. Too much and he would only injure himself. Likewise, simply increasing the external spiritual pressure without regard for his internal condition accomplished little beyond giving his body more energy than it could efficiently process.

The two needed to work together.

Ning continued cultivating until the last traces of the Purple Cloud Pill's medicinal power had been exhausted, then completed several more circulations before slowly opening his eyes.

"Success."

He immediately checked the progress displayed on his panel and compared it with the results of his previous experiments.

The difference was clear.

After several attempts and comparisons using the same kind of pill, Ning estimated that he had reduced the time required to fully consume and refine a Purple Cloud Pill by roughly thirty percent.

That didn't mean his overall cultivation speed had suddenly increased by thirty percent. Pills weren't the only limitation on cultivation, and maintaining the proper balance required both a suitable environment and careful control. His body still needed time to adapt as his cultivation increased, and his meridians weren't going to magically become stronger simply because Ning had found a more efficient way to use the resources available to him.

Still, thirty percent was thirty percent.

"Finally, I'm starting to catch up with those clan disciples."

Ning wasn't arrogant enough to believe he was the only person in the cultivation world who had discovered ways to improve cultivation efficiency. Clans that had existed for hundreds of years naturally possessed their own accumulated knowledge, whether through specialized formations, carefully selected pills, unique cultivation environments, or foundational techniques refined across generations. Some probably had methods every bit as effective as his, if not considerably better.

The difference was that Ning didn't have hundreds of years of clan inheritance behind him.

He had Know Thyself.

"Thankfully, the panel really is useful."

Ning smiled as he looked over his cultivation progress.

Know Thyself was regarded as a rather mediocre ability, but that judgment made perfect sense from the perspective of most cultivators. After all, unless a supernatural ability made things go boom, or helped something else go boom, people rarely considered it particularly impressive.

Abilities like Know Thyself, whose true value lay in research, analysis, and long-term development, naturally occupied an awkward position in such a world.

Alas! True strength wasn’t just about what one could do now, but how much further one could still grow.

And for him, know thyself truly illuminated the way ahead.
