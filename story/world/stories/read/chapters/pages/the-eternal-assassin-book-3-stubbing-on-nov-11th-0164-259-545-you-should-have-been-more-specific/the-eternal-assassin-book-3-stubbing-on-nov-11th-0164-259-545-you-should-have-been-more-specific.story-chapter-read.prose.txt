Having warned his team about the other Servants and coming up with a plan to avoid them, Asher Recalled back to the scene of his battle with Smiles, shuddering as the dense Death Aura washed over him in an instant.

“Damn, it’s far worse jumping straight into it compared to easing in. Sort of like a pool of water, only your body doesn’t get used to the temperature.”

“I’ve never been in a pool of water,” Smiles admitted, trying to turn to look at him. “I want to feel that!”

“Alright, but I need you to be quiet, or you’re going back into the room again,” Asher instructed, shoving the memory of diving into a swimming pool into the undead’s mind.

“No, not the room! There’s no Death Aura, I can feel myself starving in there! I mean, it doesn’t hurt or anything, but I want to be finished off from being crushed by a big hammer, not from being left alone in a room!”

“I know, you’ve told me about the hammer,” Asher said, shaking his head as he yet again weighed the pros and cons of keeping Smiles around. On the one hand, he was more than a little annoying. Not to mention creepy. But on the other, he might still have valuable information. Asher had a feeling Smiles would spit out things he didn’t even know were weaknesses in the event they encountered another Servant, and after witnessing how dangerous Smiles was, he was more than happy to take whatever advantage he could get over the other undead monsters.

The only problem was his Personal Rift literally killed the undead monster at what seemed like an alarming rate due to the lack of death aura within the room, so Asher was forced to carry him around by hand.

Satisfied that Asher hadn’t forgotten about the hammer, Smiles lost himself in the new memories of swimming Asher had given him. Thankful for the temporary quiet, Asher turned and began heading deeper into the Deadlands once more.

On top of the more concentrated Death Aura, things got trickier as he was forced to try to dispatch the roving zombie hordes without actually killing any of them. Distortion was a fantastic tool for reaching critical spots on their bodies, like their ankles or knees, but stabbing a running opponent in the legs was a lot harder than stabbing them in the head or chest. Still, he didn’t really have any other options, so Asher fell into a routine.

Spot a group of zombies running at him from across the Deadlands. Toss Smiles off to the side and ignore his laughter while pulling out two daggers. Use Distortion to sever as many legs at the knee as he could before the others got to him. Dance around and use Astral Dip to dodge point-blank attacks while continuing to avoid ‘killing’ blows on the zombies. Retrieve Smiles and continue running into the Deadlands.

After the first group, Asher discovered he actually had to chop off the zombies’ arms as well, or they’d keep chasing after him. They were able to drag themselves and even ‘run’ using their arms strangely well, and it was more than a little freaky watching a bunch of torsos chasing after him while running on their hands.

Preventing the zombies from moving while also trying to deal with the constantly growing number of mutated zombies became more and more difficult the farther he pressed into the Deadlands. The number of mutated zombies he ran into went from only one or two in every other group, to a good three or four in every group he encountered. The sheer size of the groups continued to grow as well, and it wasn’t long before Asher found himself fighting through dozens of zombies at a time.

At least he had his own personal cheerleader.

“Oh, nice dodge on that bite there. That probably would have hurt a lot!” Smiles called out. The severed head grinned at Asher as he ducked and weaved through the crowd of zombies, making sure not to let himself get overwhelmed by their sheer number as he worked at slicing off legs and arms with every slash of his daggers. “Isn’t it great that I know what being hurt feels like now? Pain is such a novel sensation!”

“Smiles! Not helping!” Asher shouted, flickering through the astral to dodge a glowing undead hand that he really didn’t want to figure out what it would do if it touched him. Slicing the undead’s arms off at the shoulders, Asher dropped low, just in time to dodge the slash of a sword from an undead swordsman who seemed to have retained his knowledge of how to wield the blade. Cutting off the man’s legs at the knees with twin flicks of his daggers, he slipped back into the astral, narrowly avoiding getting crushed to death by a giant undead that was so inflated it looked like a big ball of rotting flesh.

“Sorry, I can’t do much more than talk like this!” Smiles said, doing his best to track him with his eyes. Asher had figured out that Smiles seemed to have turned his battles into a game, trying to figure out where Asher was at any given moment whenever he slipped into the astral. “Hey, what about burning alive. Do you know what burning alive feels like?”

“No, can’t say I’ve ever burned to death!” Asher shouted back, using his daggers to deflect a jagged bone one of the zombies hurled at him. “How about poisoned. You want poisoned?”

“Eh, I was never all that interested in poison,” Smiles admitted, his face scrunching up in concentration. “You got drowning? That could be fun.”

“I’ve got laughing while drinking soda and shooting it out my nose,” Asher countered, wincing as a zombie stretched out their neck like a snake, latching their teeth into the meat of his shoulders while he was distracted. Escaping through the astral before it could tear a chunk out of him, Asher returned to the material realm behind it, slicing through its arms and knees before moving onto the next. “Take it or leave it!”

“Oh, I’ll take it, that sounds fun! What’s a soda?”

Finishing off the last of the zombies, Asher jogged away from the mass of writhing undead torsos, each one struggling to chase after him without the use of their arms and legs. The one capable of stretching its neck out was actually inch-worming after him at a steady walking pace, but it wouldn’t be able to keep up with him.

Snatching Smiles off the ground from where he’d been unceremoniously tossed, Asher started running again, wiping the sweat from his brow as he did so. It had been about an hour since he’d last seen his team, and in that time, he’d made it a bit past halfway into the Deadlands. His pace was definitely getting slower, and the growing and ever-constant Death Aura was really starting to wear on him. Even now, he could feel his body fighting a losing battle as the Death Aura finally seemed to grow strong enough to eclipse the combined might of his Complete Resilience and Eternal Regeneration.

Of course, the constant duress his body was under did leave him with one pleasant side benefit. And the combination of killing so many zombies and keeping Smiles appeased did as well.
