When Edge opened his eyes, warm sunlight was shining through the window.

He smiled at Melenia sleeping beside him, enjoying a lingering look at her beautiful body before sneaking out of bed. Rain check. He reached into his vault and dressed himself with a thought before walking into the common area, where Trapper and Sakura were strategizing over a steaming pot of coffee.

Jumo and Sasha were cooking breakfast. She handed him a piece of cake that Lanna had baked the night before. It was sweet, spicy, and absolutely delicious. He devoured it with enthusiasm.

After polishing off the last bite, Edge played with Rue until Mel emerged from the bedroom. She helped him set the table while the others carried heaping plates of fried eggs, potatoes, and grilled meat from the kitchen. While they took their seats and filled their plates, Trapper activated the suite’s privacy device and addressed the crew.

“We only have two days until our meeting with the Coalition. We will use them to prepare as best we can. No matter how the negotiations go, we will continue pursuing our other objectives as soon as we return. We’ve been through a lot, so I’ll start by summarizing the situation before discussing the best way to proceed.

“The Coalition is one of Ord’s major factions. The reason we hadn’t heard of them prior to the disaster is because they are an alliance formed from dozens of smaller groups that banded together to give the Wardens a run for their money. Both factions are vying for control of the Prison World entertainment industry, one of the most profitable ventures in the entire galaxy.

“We don’t know what the Coalition is planning, but the anomaly seems to have sped up their timetable. The Red Shield is blocking all communication and transportation from the outside, which means a considerable fraction of the Wardens’ strength isn’t accessible right now. Regardless of the details, war seems inevitable at this point, and we need to do everything in our power to prevent our people from being caught in the crossfire.

“Even if that wasn’t the case, I’m reluctant to hitch our wagon to the Coalition after Corpse raised the kaiju from the dead and ordered it to attack Puppet Town, along with a horde of unliving beasts. I have no idea how or why she chose to engage in that course of action. While we wound up benefiting in many ways, the squid could have just as easily wiped the settlement off the map and slaughtered every person living behind the walls. I’ll never forgive Corpse for killing our friends and have no desire to ally with a group that employs such brutal tactics.”

By this point, everyone was nodding along with Trapper’s words. None of this was new information, but with the deadline looming before them, it was helpful to refresh themselves on the situation, which had been driven out of their heads while dealing with the chaotic events of the past few days.

“While I wish we could simply turn them down and never look back,” she continued, “the Coalition is too powerful to spurn out of hand. Angering them would be almost as perilous as drawing the Wardens’ attention, which means we can’t ignore their invitation or reject their offer outright. We are looking for a way out, but whether we find one or not, we need to buy time for Edge to evolve to stage three in another ten days, when the Epic skill he found on the monsters’ homeworld finishes digesting.”

Sakura spoke next. “According to the note we found on the kaiju’s body, the Coalition doesn’t know that both Edge and I are scions. I’m not sure how that helps us, although revealing that information should strengthen our negotiating position if it seems like agreeing to some manner of alliance is inevitable.

“I don’t think the factions care about Puppet Town—not unless they learn about the core manufactory at any rate—but the scions of the Lords of Ord and the Unique cores we bear are powerful weapons that will be critically important in the struggle to come. Once they get their hands on us, I doubt we will be able to operate independently from that point on.”

“While you are making your final preparations,” Trapper said, “keep an ear to the ground for anything that might help us delay committing to an alliance or negotiate favorable terms if we’re left with no other choice. We will meet for dinner and discuss any new information we come across today.”

With that, Trapper concluded the meeting and switched off the privacy device.

Before everyone went their separate ways, she distributed their reward from the monster extermination, minus a portion set aside for restocking consumables and repairs. She transferred 70,000 Credits to each hunter’s Guide, giving everyone a chance to upgrade their gear before leaving the free city.

Edge was planning to head into the Lakelands to steal his daily skills, then see if Beelo was available for a glaive-training session. But first, it was time to pop into his core and work on his skill bonds.

Every core wielder could spend a subjective hour in their central chamber each day, where only a few minutes would pass in the outside world. He used that time to develop his relationships with the avatars in his collection, which made them more powerful, responsive, and flexible thanks to his ultimate ability, Skill Bond.

He also used his inner world to train Rue while traveling through biomes that were too dangerous for the fox to join him, so his Companion would be ready to hunt with crew when he evolved to stage two. With any luck, I can work with him more often going forward. Maybe find another hunt inside the city walls.

When Edge arrived, Chibime and Sea were wrapping up of their contests. The Epic skills had divided his avatars into two teams that were going head-to-head in the skill village attached to his central chamber. They were even more intelligent and expressive than the rest of his team, and he couldn’t wait to see what emerged from the Epic egg resting beside his reactor.

Before he spent some quality time with the gang, he walked over the black door that led to the storm-swept coastline where Skill-Eater and the dark passenger had been bound by the chains of oblivion. He wasn’t sure what was out there now that the lord was freed from her bondage, but he wasn’t planning to find out today.

Materializing required a lot of energy, and she would be slumbering most of the time until he evolved, but telling Skill-Eater about the Ancient in World’s End was too important to wait. He rapped on the door and reported what he’d learned, listening as a sleepy voice replied.

“This is grim news indeed. Unfortunatly, I cannot offer much by way of advice. The dark passenger was greatly diminished when Boundless-Well perished and its mind was almost as fragmented as my own. All I can say for certain is the Ancients are working toward building a bridge between the dimensions wide enough to bring the Primes to Ord. Even the least among them would be a deadly foe—more than your team can handle right now—so avoiding them at all costs is the wisest course of action.”

“Thanks for the warning, friend. Get some rest and we’ll have a nice chat when I evolve to stage three.”

By now, the mock battle between his avatars had concluded. The rules grew more complex every time he visited, and Edge had no idea which side had won this round. The details didn’t matter. What was important was these exercises generated experience and increased his skills’ affinity toward one another, making their mergers more favorable and letting them behave in novel ways—like how Warlord’s Mantle could bolster Manifest Chain’s combat prowess, or Rejuvenation and Shadow Play could activate on their own initiative if they sensed his life was in danger.

He went around to every member of the team, saying hello and playing games. He spent some extra time working on his bond with his newest skill, the glowing silver moon that represented Tidal Stream. The moon’s face was as menacing as ever, but the impression he received through their link was open and friendly. It seemed that they would get along just fine from the start, unlike some of his more temperamental powers.

Edge spent his final minutes playing dodge-the-spray with Repel Water, hide and go seek with the stealthy Shadow Play, and dueling the triple swords comprising Elemental Blade. When his time was up, he thanked everyone for their service before stepping back into his skin, then moved on to his next objective.

He left the Lost Traveler, walked down the spiraling stairs leading to the mixing bowl, strolled down Sword street, and stepped through the front gate. He shifted into shadow form to keep anyone from following and stayed within sight of the free city while looking for a beast that had some decent skills.

He eventually came across a stage-two lizard that was sunning itself on a boulder. He snuck up and froze it with his iceblade while Foebinder held it tight and Magebane prevented the reptile from casting any spells. After Extracting three skills and adding their experience to Rejuvenation, he skinned the carcass, removed the meat, and stored it in his vault.

Next, he stopped by the arena annex to see if Beelo was available for a sparring session. Unfortunately, the gladiator wasn’t in the building. He had left a message for Edge that he was undergoing some intensive training in a bordering biome and hoped to meet up when he returned.

Instead, Edge climbed the stairs to the silver ring to check in with Helena, who had asked him to stop by and let her know how Opulence and Extinction had held up in the field. He moved through the Magisters’ checkpoint—heart pounding as he was inspected by the core sniffer—a cute piglike beast that could sense the strength of someone’s core. The city watch used them to enforce the stage restrictions the Magisters had placed on the silver, gold, and platinum terraces.

As usual, the sniffer spent a concerning amount of time considering Edge’s core, grumbling to itself before signaling that he was cleared to pass. He left the checkpoint and made his way to the Notched Blade. While he walked, he considered how much nicer it was up here than down in the mixing bowl, which was a paradise compared to the conditions in Lowtown on the bottom level of the hollow butte.

It made him wonder what the upper terraces were like, which were restricted to stage-three and four warriors respectively. I’ll get to see the gold ring in less than two weeks, he reminded himself. If I manage to survive that long.

He window-shopped as he meandered his way down the spacious boulevard, absently noting a few stores that he might stop by later. He popped into the establishment that sold spellshots, but there wasn’t anything for sale that was worth the Creds.

Edge arrived at Helena’s workshop soon after. The guard waved him in, since the owner had extended an open invitation. He stepped into the reception area where the master smith interviewed prospective customers while deciding if she was interested in accepting their commission.

Helena had two apprentices, only one of whom he’d encountered before. One was a young man named Pip. The other was a middle-aged woman who went by Coal for reasons he hadn’t found an opportunity to ask about yet. Pip was working today. Since this was their first meeting, Edge introduced himself before inquiring if the craftswoman was free.

Pip disappeared into the back of the shop and returned a few minutes later with a surprised look on his face. “She’s in the courtyard with the Silverwood tree and said you can meet here there. Helena must like you. This is the first time she has let someone intrude when she’s tinkering with a project.”

The apprentice left without waiting for a reply, which was fine, since Edge had been here several times and already knew the way. He walked down a long hallway, turned the corner, and emerged into a spacious courtyard with a sprawling tree basking in the sunlight shining through the skyscreen above.

The woman was deep into her work. Rather than disrupt her flow, he took in her features before checking out the project underway. Helena had pale blue eyes, a dark complexion, and streaks of silver highlighting her raven locks. She was in the middle of adding teeth to what appeared to be a chainsaw the size of a surfboard.

“It’s a little flashy for my tastes,” she said after some time had passed. “But working out the craftings was a fun challenge, and I’m willing to endure a bit of extravagance in exchange.”

“I like it,” he replied. “A rotating saw-blade is intimidating as hell and probably has all kinds of uses besides the obvious.”

A master like Helena was perfectly capable of having a conversation while her hands were busy. Edge shared the details of every enemy he had battled with Opulence since they’d last spoken, except for the stage-four lobster he’d fought on the monster’s homeworld.

He supposed it was theoretically possible that she was the Ancient, but the idea was absurd at face value. The smith had armed him with the best weapon he’d ever owned and made something awesome for Melenia too. Whatever the monster is up to, I doubt it is in its best interests to arm the heavy hitters living in World’s End.

She asked a ton of questions, but none of them involved why Edge had come face to face with so many powerful monsters, including some that were adapted to ocean life. She was only interested in how deeply he’d cut and anything else related to the performance of the insanely expensive glaive, which had been built from the ground up to make the most out of the damage bonus provided by Dragon’s Greed.

Part of the way through, Coal walked in with a pitcher of tea and a plate of biscuits. Edge enjoyed the tasty treats while staring at resplendent canopy of the Silverwood tree, which he had harvested during the journey from Puppet Town and used to craft his polearm’s shaft.

When they were done eating, Edge bid the weaponsmith farewell and returned to the Lost Traveler while pondering his meeting with King. No matter how the situation played out, their conversation would play a pivotal role in shaping his people’s future.
