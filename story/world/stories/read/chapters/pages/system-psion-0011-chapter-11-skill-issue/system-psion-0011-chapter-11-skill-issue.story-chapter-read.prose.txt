When his tenth attempt at shaping mental energy into something resembling a blade predictably failed, Samuel couldn’t resist cursing himself for leaving his knives in the forest of snakes. He knew he had bigger problems to worry about at the time, but his mind kept reminding him of how much easier this entire endeavour would be if he simply had his knives.

Layering mental energy on an already existing surface was much easier than making it stand on its own.

He had been trying to copy the way he used it with the knife, but that had yielded few results.

Well, that wasn’t exactly true. His attempts at forming a sharp edge were paying off, as he could feel the energy shifting into the edge of a blade. The problem came when he had to actually turn the blade into a stable construct that could actually slice skeletons up for any length of time.

This was a problem he hadn’t needed to deal with when he had the knives, and his little experiments in the house hadn’t gone anywhere. In fact, he had not managed to actually create any real construct so far with his telekinesis, instead using the skill to move things around or simply send out crude blasts.

It took a few more tries before he realised he was not going to succeed without a significant amount of time, at least. Time that he did not have.

He shook his head in frustration.

This entire situation had left him stumped, and he was not particularly happy about it. No matter what he tried, from moving the mental energy in different shapes to trying to fill up a construct with it, he still failed to create a blade.

While he was thinking about what he could possibly be missing, his eyes fell to the walking stick that lay on the ground beside him. The magical walking stick was a surprisingly good conduit for mental energy.

Samuel smacked his forehead.

As much as he would have loved to actually create a stable structure from only mental energy, it was clear it would not be a feasible option at his level. Not with dwindling reserves and limited time. In that case, perhaps it would be best to use his stick as the base and form a bladed edge from telekinesis over it.

His first attempt immediately proved he had the right idea, which made him want to hit himself for not realising it earlier. The attempt still failed, of course, but he could see a pathway to success where there had previously been none.

Even with the stick as a base, getting his mental energy to hold the shape of a blade still wasn’t easy. Despite being the energy of his mind, it did not seem to like doing what his mind wanted it to do. A few attempts later, and he came to an interesting, if obvious, realisation.

When he shaped the mental energy over his bound weapon, it automatically took a cylindrical shape that was similar to the stick. Trying to make it a blade seemed to be telling it to go against its natural state.

Indeed, when he simply let it take the shape of a stick, it held steady until he dismissed it by himself.

He took a quick look at his mental energy and was surprised to see he still had over a quarter left. He knew the stick increased his efficiency, but was still surprised by just how much, especially since it was designed to work with mana, not psi.

Or perhaps whatever mechanism powered the stick treated mana and psi as interchangeable. That would be something to look into one day.

If he could manage to leave the dungeon alive.

That thought immediately sobered him up, and he went back to practising his telekinesis with increased dedication. This time, he did not start trying to form a blade immediately. His attempts showed him that even with the stick, it would likely still take a lot of time, though it would be less than trying from scratch.

Instead, he decided to give his psi what it wanted and let it take the shape of the stick from the beginning. From there, he would tweak it until he managed to give it a sharp edge.

Samuel found himself grateful that the psi construct took a negligible amount of energy to maintain once it was created, because trying to gradually mould one side of the mental energy stick into a blade was a slow process. Even after reducing the size of his construct to only cover a small portion of the stick, it still took time for him to make sure it formed a sharp and stable bladed edge.

He managed to make his first attempt using this method hold steady for a few seconds before it reverted back to the shape of a stick once more, something that actually surprised him. He had been expecting it to disappear once it stopped being stable, but perhaps he could use it to his advantage.

He continued working on it, improving his speed and precision as he continued his attempts. The fact that he did not have to recreate the construct ensured that his psi expenditure remained low.

Gradually, he started improving the surface area of his stick. It was a slow process, and he spent hours making incremental progress with his control, as he had to get used to the new length every time he increased the construct size.

Eventually, he managed to get to half of the stick when the sky started to show signs of daylight. He was also able to increase the amount of time the blade stayed stable for up to ten seconds. It was less than he would have liked, but he already had ideas for how to incorporate it into his fighting style.

Thankfully, the system seemed to recognise his efforts as well, because he received a notification at some point during his practice

Telekinesis has reached level 5

Samuel had to admit, there was something about seeing those level up notifications that got his dopamine up and made him want to progress even more. Not that he needed extra motivation with his life on the line, but he supposed every bit helped.

Truly, whoever designed the system must have been brilliant if their goal was to get people to level up more. He immediately killed that line of thought, knowing it would send him on a whole tangent that would occupy far more of his attention than he could spare for the rest of the day.

The day was bright enough that he knew it was past six in the morning when he stood up, satisfied with his progress so far. He stifled a yawn, fighting the urge to simply lie his head on the floor and get some much needed rest. The combination of fighting and intense use of telekinesis for the past few hours had definitely drained a lot of his energy.

Yet despite a part of him suspecting that the skeletons would not move from their positions to come and find him, he was far too paranoid to actually put that theory to the test. Even during his practice in the night, he had never let his awareness of his surroundings lapse for an extended period of time.

It might have seemed a little excessive, but he liked to live by the saying, ‘it's not paranoia if your life is actually in danger.’

So even though his body and mind would have loved the sleep, he still continued forward instead, idly wondering whether anything would change now that the sun was up.

It took him a few minutes of walking before he found anything new, partly because he was simply moving slowly. He was not willing to sleep with deadly monsters around, but that did not mean he would torture his body any more than he had to. Plus, he wanted to give his psi time to recover to a more comfortable level. It was already nearly half full as it was.

Samuel met a group of skeletons walking around in patrols, these one’s more spread out than the last group. There were also twice as many, which made him very thankful that he had taken the time to actually learn a way to access a blade. This would have been a nearly impossible fight if he only had to rely on the brute force of his telekinesis blasts.

As it was, it would still be difficult even with his new improvements.

In an ideal world, Samuel would have been walking towards the skeletons with a full reserve of mental energy, his body and mind fully refreshed from a full night of sleep. He would have mastered the ability to create a full blade from nothing but his telekinesis, and without any need for his stick. In that world, he would cut down the entire group of skeletons in seconds and clear the entire domain by the end of the day.

Samuel couldn’t help but scoff at the thought. In an ideal world, he would not be forced to fight for his life in a strange dungeon, with no knowledge of whether his sister and grandmother even managed to survive. In a truly ideal world, he would not be in this situation at all.

So he shook away such pointless thoughts, and with sleep in his eyes and barely half of his mental energy reserves, Samuel charged at the skeletons.
