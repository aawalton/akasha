The grown-ups were sad, and Chubbs didn’t like it one bit.

She sat cross-legged on a fallen log, tiny fingers tracing the edge of the funny face-thing that Cade called a “mask.” It hummed beneath her touch, like it was trying to tell her secrets. The grown-ups kept talking with big, angry words, their voices sharp in the night air.

“Beyond fucked,” Cade had said, his face all scrunchy like when he tasted something yucky.

Chubbs knew what that meant.

It meant they couldn’t do the thing they wanted to do. It meant Gav might go to sleep and not wake up, his skin all burned and hurty from the mean lady with the fire hands. It meant everyone was scared, even if they pretended they weren’t.

She glanced at the mask again. They needed more of these silly things, but they only had one. Rayka had explained it earlier while wrapping Gav’s owies: “One mask, eight people, plus you. The math doesn’t work, honey.”

Math was stupid anyway. Chubbs didn’t like it.

“I’m going to think,” Cade announced, stomping away like he did when he needed to be alone. The others barely looked up, too busy with their worries.

Chubbs slid off the log, the mask clutched to her chest. No one was watching her—they were all talking about boring grown-up things like “impossible odds” and “complete catastrophe.”

Boring!

“Bunny,” she whispered, spotting the white dragonling curled near their packs. “Psst! Bunny!”

The dragonling’s head lifted, intelligent eyes finding her in the dim light. He tilted his head curiously.

“We’re gonna help,” she informed him solemnly. “Come on.”

Bunny uncurled, stretching like a cat before padding silently to her side. Together, they slipped away from the circle of grown-ups, moving to a small clearing just beyond the firelight.

Chubbs plopped down on a patch of soft moss, setting the mask before her with the care of a surgeon laying out instruments. Bunny curled around her, his scales warm against her back, his eyes watching her hands with fascination.

“They need more masks,” she told him, “so everyone can go to the swampy place without getting all monstery.” She tapped her chin thoughtfully. “But there’s only one. That’s... not enough. Trust me. I can count on my fingers,” she informed the dragonling with a sagely nod.

The veil thins...

Ancient patterns reawaken...

Call forth what was sundered...

The voice slithered through her mind like smoke, urgent and impossible.

“That doesn’t even make any sense,” she giggled, rolling her eyes. “Are you playing riddles? I’m not very good at riddles.”

Ignoring the weird raspy voice in her head, she turned the mask over in her hands, studying its contours. It was so... simple. Too simple. Like someone had just thrown it together without really trying. The metal was all wrong—too rigid here, too flimsy there. The crystal filters were aligned all funny, like someone had never heard of proper flow patterns. It was like trying to drink soup with a fork!

“It’s a very bad mask,” she informed Bunny seriously. “No wonder everyone’s worried.”

Her tiny fingers traced the mask’s surface, finding imperfections, seeing pathways of possibility. The metal seemed to warm beneath her touch, responding to something in her blood.

“See all these funny parts?” she whispered to Bunny. “They’re supposed to work together, but they’re all grumpy with each other. Like when Elena and Jer fight over the last cookie.”

Golden light sparked at her fingertips—just a flicker, there and gone. Bunny’s ears perked up, his eyes widening.

Chubbs glanced over at the furry man who always made silly faces at her when he thought no one else was watching. The lycanthrope’s head was settled on Evie’s lap, and the sight of those two together sent a strange pang through Chubb’s chest.

“Poor Gavie,” she breathed, a frown creasing her tiny forehead. Bunny nudged her arm, and she sighed, explaining, “his life is all stuck and slow. Like when syrup gets too cold and doesn’t want to come out of the flask.”

She nodded decisively. “These masks need to help everyone. Especially Gav. We need to help his life remember how to move right.”

Forge anew what time has eroded... your hands remember the weaving of worlds...

A strange certainty filled her—she knew exactly what the masks needed. It was so obvious! Anyone could see it if they just looked properly. The grown-ups were just too big to notice the little things.

Her fingers moved with unexpected precision, bending metal that should not bend, coaxing patterns where none had existed before. Tiny golden sparks danced from her fingertips, sinking into the mask’s surface.

“This part needs to be stronger,” she murmured, “and this part needs to be more—flowy.”

A technical term, she was sure of it.

The mask began to glow softly, pulsing with each beat of Chubbs’ heart. The power building within her felt like sunshine and starlight all mixed together, bubbling up from somewhere deep and forgotten.

A burst of golden energy suddenly flashed from her hands, washing over Bunny in a wave of light. The dragonling yelped, jumping backward as golden patterns momentarily etched themselves into his white scales.

“Oopsie,” Chubbs giggled. “Sorry, Bunny! I didn’t mean to make you all glowy.”

Bunny shook himself, scales rippling. His head suddenly snapped toward the dark forest, ears flattening. A chilly wind whispered through the trees—not a natural breeze, but something that carried whispers of power.

The dragonling’s entire body tensed. He looked at Chubbs, then back to the forest, indecision clear in his intelligent eyes.

“What is it, Bunny?” she asked, momentarily distracted from her work.

The wind gusted again, stronger this time, carrying with it a scent of ancient power that made Bunny’s scales bristle. With one final glance at Chubbs, the dragonling bolted, a streak of white vanishing between dark trunks.

“Bunny!” she called, half-rising. For a moment, she considered following him, but the mask in her hands pulsed insistently. It wasn’t finished. Her work wasn’t done.

“He’s probably chasing something shiny,” she told herself, settling back down. “Or maybe he smelled a mouse. He’ll be back.”

She turned her full attention back to the mask, which now seemed to vibrate with anticipation. The scattered bits of power she’d released had awakened something in it—something hungry to be more than it was.

THE TIME IS NOW... CREATION AWAKENS...

“I TOLD you,” Chubbs huffed impatiently, “I’m ALREADY awake! It’s not even bedtime yet!”

“You… You okay over there?” Jer called out from several paces away where he was resting.

“Yeah! Just yelling at the voices in my head!” Chubbs answered cheerily.

“O…Okay,” he drew out the word in that way grown-ups did when they didn’t believe her, but didn’t want to ask any more questions. She was fine with that.

Chubbs squinted really, really, hard on the mask.

The power inside her surged, straining against the confines of her small body. It felt like trying to fit an ocean in a teacup. She focused on Gav, on his flickering life-force, on how each mask needed to help not just with the swamp air, but with healing what was broken.

“You all need to be special,” she informed the mask solemnly. “You need to help Gav and everyone else stay strong. You need to remember how to make life flow properly.”

The metal flowed like water beneath her touch, hardening into perfect curves. The filters realigned themselves into complex geometric patterns. But it wasn’t enough—they needed more masks. One for each person.

“We need more of you,” she whispered to the mask. “Lots more.”

The power crested within her, a tidal wave building behind a crumbling dam. It was too much—too vast, too ancient, too hungry to be contained in her small form. For a heartbeat, panic fluttered in her chest.

Then it broke free.

Light exploded from her hands, blindingly white and gold, rippling outward in a perfect circle. The clearing frosted over in an instant, delicate patterns etching themselves into every leaf and blade of grass. The single mask in her hands multiplied—one becoming two, two becoming four, four becoming eight—each one gleaming with new purpose, etched with intricate patterns that seemed to move when you weren’t looking directly at them.

Each mask contained the healing patterns she’d designed for Gav—though the original held the strongest concentration, specially crafted for the wounded lycanthrope.

The wave of power rolled outward, washing through the trees. For a moment, Chubbs wasn’t a little girl anymore—she was vast, ancient, a being of infinite possibility seeing through eyes that had witnessed the birth of stars.

Then, just as quickly, she was small again, sitting in a circle of frost with nine gleaming masks arranged before her.

“I did it!” she whispered, then louder: “I DID IT!”

She picked up the original mask—now transformed into something beautiful and powerful. This one was for Gav. She had made it special, woven patterns of life and renewal into its very substance.

A rustling from the treeline made her look up. Bunny burst into the clearing, white scales gleaming in the moonlight. Right behind him came Cade, his face a storm of not-nice emotions.

“Cadie!” Chubbs exclaimed, holding up the mask triumphantly. “I made it better!”

He froze, taking in the scene—the circle of frost, the nine gleaming masks, the lingering aura of power that made the air shimmer. For a moment, his expression was unreadable, balanced on a knife-edge between wonder and terror.

“What did you do?” he asked, his voice barely above a whisper.

“I fixed the mask,” she explained, as if it were the most obvious thing in the world. “And I made more, so everyone can have one. They’ll all help with healing too, but this one’s special for Gav. See? I put a ‘G’ on it and everything!”

More rustling announced the arrival of the others—Orro materializing like a shadow at the edge of the clearing, followed by Elena and Jer crashing through the underbrush.

“What the hell was that light—” Elena started, then stopped dead. “Holy shit.”

“Shit’s a bad word.” Chubbs reminded her sternly.

“Yeah, Elena,” Jer managed, though his voice sounded strangled. “Think of the children. The reality-warping, mask-making children.”

Rayka pushed through, followed by Nora with her big sword drawn. They all stared at the circle of masks, expressions ranging from awe to fear.

“Are those what I think they are?” Rayka asked cautiously.

“Masks!” Chubbs confirmed cheerfully. “Better ones! The old one was stupid and didn’t work right.”

“Better... how?” Nora asked, her grip on her sword not relaxing.

“Just better,” Chubbs said with the absolute confidence of a child. “And this one is special for Gav.” She held up the original, now transformed into something that seemed to pulse with inner light. “Though I made each one able to help life remember how to move properly.”

A heavy silence fell over the clearing. The grown-ups exchanged those looks they always did when they were worried but didn’t want to say it out loud.

“Chubbs,” Cade said finally, crouching down to her level. “How did you know how to do this?”

“I just knew,” she said, shrugging. “The mask told me what it wanted to be.”

His eyes narrowed slightly. “Did someone else tell you? A voice, maybe?”

“Nope!” she lied, smiling her most innocent smile. “Can I give this to Gav now? He needs it.”

Cade studied her face for a long moment, then glanced at the masks again. “These aren’t... dangerous, are they?”

“No, silly!” she laughed. “They’re helpful! That’s why I made them.”

Cade looked to Orro, who had been examining one of the masks with careful fingers. The assassin gave a slight nod.

“The craftsmanship is... beyond anything I’ve seen,” Orro admitted reluctantly. “It seems legitimate.”

“C’mon, Cadie! I wanna help Gav,” Chubbs insisted, already on her feet. She clutched the special mask to her chest and started toward camp, determination in every step.

The grown-ups followed, their whispered conversations washing over her like ocean waves.

“—can’t let her just—”

“—did you see what she—”

“—not natural—”

“—could be dangerous—”

Chubbs ignored them. Grown-ups always worried about everything.

Back at the main camp, Evie sat beside Gav’s still form, her fingers gently stroking his sweat-dampened hair. His breathing was shallow, his skin glistening despite the cool night air.

“I made a special mask for Gav!” Chubbs announced, holding it up.

Evie looked up, her eyes widening as she took in the transformed mask. “Oh my,” she breathed. “That’s... quite something.”

“It’ll help his life move,” Chubbs explained, approaching Gav’s prone form. “His life is all stuck and scared, but this will help it remember how to flow.”

“Wait!” Nora called sharply, stepping between Chubbs and Gav. “We have no idea what that will do to him.”

“He’s already half-dead,” Elena added, reaching for the mask. “We can’t just let her experiment on him.”

“For all we know, it could accelerate the damage,” Orro observed coolly.

“I didn’t break it!” Chubbs protested, tears welling in her eyes. “I made it better! Why won’t you let me help?”

The grown-ups looked at each other helplessly. Chubbs clutched the mask tighter, lower lip trembling. All she wanted was to help, and they were being mean and stupid.

Just as the tears threatened to spill over, a gentle hand came to rest on her shoulder. Evie had risen from Gav’s side, her expression serene but determined. She didn’t speak—didn’t need to. Her calm presence seemed to fill the clearing, quieting the arguments with her silent certainty.

She simply held out her hand for the mask, her eyes never leaving Chubbs’.

“You believe me?” Chubbs whispered.

Evie nodded, a small smile playing at the corners of her lips.

“But—” Nora began.

Evie held up one slender hand, silencing the warrior mid-protest. There was something in her posture, some quiet authority that made even Nora hesitate.

“I trust her,” Cade said suddenly, his eyes resting on Chubbs. “And I trust Evie’s judgment. Let them try.”

Something warm bloomed in Chubbs’ chest at those words.

Cade trusted her. He believed in her. It felt like sunshine after rain.

The rest of the group fell silent, forming a tense circle around Gav’s still form. Chubbs passed the mask to Evie, who knelt beside the injured lycanthrope. Together, they carefully positioned the transformed mask over Gav’s burned face.

For a heartbeat, nothing happened.

Everyone held their breath. Chubbs could feel their doubts, their fears, pressing in like a physical weight. What if it didn’t work? What if she’d made things worse?

Then—

Golden light seeped from the mask’s edges, flowing like liquid sunshine across Gav’s skin. Where it touched, the angry red burns lightened, tissue knitting together before their eyes. His chest rose in a deep, shuddering breath.

The light sank beneath his skin, tracing patterns that matched his veins, illuminating him from within like a paper lantern. It wasn’t healing him completely—the worst burns remained angry and raw—but something was changing.

“His life is moving again,” Chubbs explained happily. “See? It was stuck, but now it remembers.”

Rayka pressed her fingers to Gav’s wrist, her eyes widening. “His pulse is stronger. More regular.”

A collective sigh of relief rippled through the group. Gav’s eyelids fluttered, then slowly opened. He blinked up at the circle of faces hovering above him, confusion giving way to recognition.

“Well,” he rasped, his voice like sandpaper on stone, “that’s one way to wake a guy up.” He winced, trying to sit up. “Feels like I went ten rounds with a volcano and lost spectacularly.”

“Gav!” Rayka exclaimed, relief making her voice crack. “How do you feel?”

“Like death warmed over,” he groaned, “but at least I’m in the ‘warmed over’ category now.” His eyes found Chubbs, standing anxiously at Evie’s side. “I hear I have you to thank for the upgrade from ‘mostly dead’ to ‘partially alive,’ squirt.”

Chubbs beamed, proud beyond measure. “I fixed you! Mostly.”

“That you did.” He managed to sit up with Cade’s help, grimacing as burned skin pulled tight. “Still feel like I’ve been tenderized and slow-roasted, but I’ll live.”

“So,” Cade said, steadying his friend, “what’s the verdict? You up for helping us hunt down those bastards who did this to you?”

Something dangerous flashed in Gav’s eyes—a reminder of the beast that lived beneath his skin. The golden light from the mask seemed to respond, pulsing brighter for just a moment.

He smiled, and there was nothing human in it.

“Oh, I’m more than up for it,” Gav growled, the sound rumbling from deep in his chest. “Bernard and his crew are about to discover exactly why you don’t piss off a telepathic lycanthrope with a grudge. I’m done being scared of those cowards.”

“Well,” Jer said with a low whistle, “remind me to let the divine toddler play doctor next time I stub my toe.”

“It was nothing,” Chubbs said modestly, though she couldn’t stop grinning. “The mask did most of the work.”

“No,” Cade said, ruffling her hair in a way that made her entire body light up with joy. “You did this. Thank you, Chubbs.”

Her chest felt like it might burst with happiness. Cade never did that! He was looking at her like she was special—like she was part of the team.

“You’re welcome,” she replied solemnly, then hesitated. “Cadie... how did I know how to do that?”

Something flickered across his face—there and gone so fast she almost missed it. Worry? Fear? He glanced at Bunny, whose scales now looked normal again, then back to her.

“Some questions,” he said with a smile that didn’t quite reach his eyes, “are better left for another day. For now, let’s just be glad you did.”

Grown-ups were so weird sometimes.

Chubbs settled herself beside Gav, watching the golden light pulse beneath his skin. It was pretty, like fireflies trapped under glass. The voice inside her had gone quiet again, but she could feel it waiting, patient as mountains.

She yawned, suddenly tired. Making things was hard work. Maybe she’d take a little nap while the grown-ups talked about boring things...

A hand caught her as she tipped sideways, half-asleep already. Rayka smiled down at her, gathering her into gentle arms.

“Come on, miracle worker,” she murmured. “Bedtime for super-awesome children who just saved the day.”

“I’m not sleepy,” Chubbs protested through another massive yawn. “I told the voices the same thing.”

“Of course you did,” Rayka agreed, settling her into her bedroll. “Just resting your eyes, right?”

“Mmm,” Chubbs hummed, already drifting. The last thing she saw before sleep claimed her was Bunny curling protectively at her side, his intelligent eyes reflecting the firelight.

She dreamed of stars being born, of strange trees taking shape beneath her hands like clay, of the joy of creation.

She dreamed of who she used to be.
