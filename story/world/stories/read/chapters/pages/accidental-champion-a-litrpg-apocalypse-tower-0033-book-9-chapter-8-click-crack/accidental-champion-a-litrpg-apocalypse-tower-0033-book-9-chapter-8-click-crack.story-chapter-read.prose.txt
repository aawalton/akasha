The rune-reinforced steel door to the Memory Room was wide open.

Xavier stepped back, drew Bones, and contacted all the agents and base staff he’d freed from their contracts. Through his drones, he told them to run. At the same time, he altered his own time dilation field. It had been synced with that of his drones, their fields running at maximum. But his time dilation field had always been stronger—a lot stronger.

He pushed it to its limits.

He burned Reality Energy and pulled Soul Energy from his reserve, infusing his energy channels with it. A high price, but he needed the strength.

It would take too long for the others to reach him.

The room was more heavily guarded than he’d been told. A lot more heavily guarded.

And those reinforcements at the other side…

Palini, Arrack, Dybal, and Lydad were behind him, along with the other agents he’d brought. As Xavier’s time dilation field hit full strength, he found it wasn’t enough—it was only about equal to that of the enemy time mage in the Memory Room.

Not enough. Not even close.

He summoned more drones. To clear the contracts of everyone in that room. Turn them to his side. Or lock them down with Time Prison.

“Arrack, I need—” Xavier’s words died in his throat as the big A Grade moved.

A green light covered Arrack’s body, Speed Energy infusing him with its power. He moved. Faster than Xavier. At the ends of his thick wooden staff, sharp blades extended. The man angled his staff forward.

Xavier’s instincts kicked in.

The man who’d called him Breaker, the man who’d seemed most grateful out of all the agents there, was about to stab Xavier in the back.

Xavier moved, burning more Reality Energy. Pulling out more Soul Energy. Manifesting protective spell patterns and spell patterns that would help him draw upon his own Speed Energy. Simultaneously, he snapped his time dilation field down, so it only covered himself. That should have frozen the other man in time, but it didn’t—the enemy time mage’s field was working on Arrack.

The dragonkin turned, folded his wings about him protectively, raised Bones in the form of a sword to parry the enemy strike.

Arrack’s blade pierced Xavier’s tough, leathery wings as though they were made from cotton. Xavier’s sword never rose high enough to touch the double-ended spear. It pierced through his armour, straight into his side.

When it thrust through his flesh and cut through one of his ribs, energy pulsed from the blade and locked down his energy channels.

The energy continued to pulse. Whatever it was it was toxic. It flowed through every inch of his body. Seized his muscles, paralysed his limbs, locked him in place. He couldn’t even shift his gaze or blink.

The world dimmed.

The other agents wore panicked looks. Whatever this was, they hadn’t known about it.

Arrack was smiling as the world turned black.

~

Xavier woke.

He didn’t remember falling asleep.

His eyes flashed open.

He was at the centre of a circular room with steel walls. Craning his neck, he saw the ceiling a few hundred feet up. Unlike the walls, it was one large slab of stone. He tried to move. Metal clinked. His wrists were locked together behind his back, chains connecting him to the stone floor.

Xavier pulled at them.

They didn’t break.

What the—?

A sharp pain stung his side and he remembered.

The Memory Room. Arrack’s blade. Falling unconscious—

His mind was… heavy. The pain in his side wasn’t all he suffered from. His head ached so much it was like someone had shoved needles into his brain. He swept his gaze around the room. He was alone.

There were no doors.

He tried to cast Time Alteration. But he couldn’t.

His cores. His energy channels—

Everything was locked down.

He felt weak.

On the ground three feet ahead of him the stone shimmered and a figure rose straight through it until a large man was standing in front of Xavier, hands folded neatly behind his back, looking down at him with curiosity.

“Arrack.”

“Ah. Your mind remains intact. With all that was done to it, there were… concerns. The more experienced memory mages among my staff worried you wouldn’t wake.”

“You’re Management.”

Arrack smiled. He paced around Xavier, footfalls echoing oddly in the circular room.

Xavier couldn’t turn to follow the man, prickles forming on his back as Arrack stepped behind him. The All-Seeing Eye was inactive. His Farscope lens was gone. His spiritual sense down.

“Did you like the trap that I set for you?”

Xavier thought of Palini, the planet Batrah, the portal—

“This base was the trap,” Arrack said with a self-satisfied smile. “I’ve been researching you since before you killed Sovereign Rewke Fouran in the arena. I’ve had agents watching. I’ve had a thousand briefings on your strengths and weaknesses. You can’t help yourself. You like to save people. You see a trap and you walk right in. That’s how you came to Batrah in the first place. I knew if I left that portal open, you’d slip straight through it.”

“Clever. I suppose this is where you monologue about how you made it all happen?”

“You didn’t even see it coming, did you? Did you really think you could just walk in here, turn an entire base to your cause, then walk back out? I admit, you got farther than I expected. There was a whisper you could break contracts, but the scale…” Arrack stopped pacing. “You remain alive for one reason only—the secrets inside your head.”

The pain he was feeling—those needles in his brain. The memory mages the man mentioned…

Xavier smiled. “You couldn’t pry them out, could you?”

“What?”

“You tried. I can feel it. But you failed.” Xavier cocked his head. “You’re not used to failing. Mustn’t feel nice.”

There was a creaking sound. Arrack turned from Xavier. The man’s fists were clenched so hard the bones in his fingers must be rubbing against one another. He relaxed them and turned back to Xavier with that same smile, though this time there was a slight crack in it.

“Oh, I’m not the one who failed. You’re broken, Xavier Collins. You’re sitting on the floor of my dungeon, your whole life shattered to pieces, and you don’t even know it yet. And me? I’m the only one who can help you put those pieces back together. The only one who can help you become whole again. It doesn’t have to hurt. But it will if you don’t cooperate. If you don’t tell me everything. The things you’ve done. How you’ve done them.”

Arrack’s arm came around. Something appeared in his hand—a chair. He placed it in front of Xavier then sat. “First, tell me how you broke those contracts.”

Xavier blinked up at the man. “That must have been an inconvenience for you. Tell me, what happened to all the agents I freed?”

A small smile slipped onto Arrack’s lips. “You want to know about your friend. Palani, was it? No, Palini.” He leant forward, elbows resting on knees. “You care about people, don’t you?” The man clicked his fingers.

Palini appeared next to him, shooting straight up through the floor, a muzzle around his mouth. He was chained, hands behind his back. His eyes widened at the sight of Xavier defenceless on the stone floor. He tried to say something, jaw working, but there wasn’t so much as a strangled hum—the muzzle silenced all noise.

“You’ll be pleased to know I restored his memories.” Arrack clicked his fingers again.

The muzzle disappeared. “Don’t tell this ugly old bastard anything.”

Arrack’s calm demeanour cracked. He clicked his fingers a third time. There was a terrible crunching, cracking sound as Palini’s foot rotated three-hundred-and-sixty-degrees.

Xavier’s breath remained even, his face impassive, even as his fists clenched behind him. “If this is supposed to impress me, then you’re more of a fool than you look.”

Arrack chuckled. “Bravado. How quaint.”

Click. Crack.

Palini’s other foot spun around. His knees slammed into the stone.

“You know by now, the position you’re in.”

Click. Crack.

One of Palini’s wrists snapped. At this, a groan finally escaped his lips.

“Your energy channels, your cores, are blocked.”

Click. Crack.

The other wrist. Another groan.

“Your drones are dead. Your soul bound weapon confiscated.”

Click. Crack.

Palini’s right elbow. A whimper.

“And you can’t break out of those chains.” Arrack sneered. “Breaker indeed.”

The chains rattled. One of the links snapped, releasing Xavier’s left wrist. Claws extended from his fingers as he slashed out at Arrack’s neck but couldn’t reach. His leathery, black dragon wings spread wide at his back. He tried to stand but only got up halfway, his left wrist still trapped.

Arrack’s eyes widened.

Xavier laughed. Falling to his knees he looked up at Arrack with a rabid smile. “You think you’ve broken me? I’ve been broken before, broken worse, and came back stronger. If you were wise as you were old, you’d kill me where I kneel, because you’ve been dead since the moment you decided to come after me. And not a death where your spirit gets to flee to the Otherworld. A true death.”

He pulled at the other chain. It rattled but held.

A hint of fear wafted from the man, felt with the oldest instincts of Xavier’s dragonkin body.

Arrack clicked his fingers one last time. Palini’s neck jarred to the side. He slumped to the floor as Arrack stood, bending down to whisper something, any hint of fear evaporated. “You best be careful how you talk to me, boy. The sectors you rule are all but unprotected now you’re my prisoner, and something tells me Palini isn’t the only person you care about.”

He straightened, glanced at Palini’s prone form, then faced away from them both. “Think about that while you await my return.”

Arrack slipped through the floor, leaving his chair behind.

Xavier closed the gap between himself and Palini. He reached out to touch the man, but he couldn’t—the chains stopped him a hairbreadth short.

Palini’s eyes were wide open, staring. Something wheezed in his throat—air, pushed outward.

The man’s heart still beat.
