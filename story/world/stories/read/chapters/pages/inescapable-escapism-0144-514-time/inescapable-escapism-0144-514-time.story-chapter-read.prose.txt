“Careful!” someone shouted.

Seth and I leapt back just in time as a body hurtled past us. A shriek sounded, followed by a loud laugh.

“Sorry!” another person called as they chased the first person across the foyer. “Whoever gets to the garage first gets dibs on the cars!”

“I’m not having the Mini again!” the first person shouted over their shoulder as they sprinted out the doors.

Seth and I stared after them in silence, watching as they raced across the tarmac and grass outside. They seemed pretty evenly matched. The boy in front had been faster initially, but they were losing speed quickly, and the girl was catching up.

“Want to see who gets there first?” Seth asked, cocking his head towards the door.

A smile pulled at my lips, and I took a step towards the door before I could stop myself.

“Yes!”

Seth grinned at me before following me towards the door, and I felt a swell of happiness wash over me. I was so glad he was in the main building and that he’d suggested it because if he hadn’t, I would have probably just continued on to class without ever finding out who’d won.

Not that it really mattered. I didn’t know the people, nor did I know why it was so bad to drive a Mini. I’d never done it before, and I wasn’t sure I’d ever even ridden in one, so it made no difference to me. The only reason I wanted to know who won was curiosity.

“My money’s on the girl,” Seth said after a few seconds as we watched her begin to close the distance between her and the boy.

“I don’t know,” I muttered. “They’re almost there. I’m not sure she has enough time to catch up.”

“Mmmm… maybe. The guy seems to be struggling. If she just… yes!”

We watched as the girl drew closer to him before overtaking him. The boy lunged forward, seeming to be pushing himself to run as fast as he could, but he just wasn’t fast enough. The girl’s triumphant shout echoed across the field, and I couldn’t help but burst out laughing.

“Oh no,” I said, still chuckling. “I guess he’s having the Mini again.”

“I guess so,” Seth replied with a snort. “I won. You owe me a chit.”

Confusion washed over me, and I glanced at him.

“A what?”

His eyes widened, and he laughed quickly, looking down.

“Sorry,” he said, turning away from the door. “It’s a dumb thing I used to say with my friends back home. Well… friend.”

He glanced up at me sheepishly as we started to walk across the foyer again, his cheeks pink, and I couldn’t help but smile reassuringly. Or at least, I hoped it was reassuring. I wasn’t sure, but I wanted it to be. I knew how he felt; I only had a couple of friends in real life, really.

“Oh yeah?”

“Yeah…” he said, sounding a little uncomfortable still. “Chits are money in this kind of old video game we used to play together. Neither of us really had any real money, but we’d both been playing the game pretty much since it came out, so we were pretty loaded in there, and whenever we bet on anything in real life, the loser would have to transfer the winner some chits.”

His explanation had come out in a rush, and his cheeks had turned even more flushed. I could tell that he was embarrassed, and I knew I hadn’t really done anything to cause it, but I still felt guilty.

“Oh, that’s cool,” I responded, my tone slightly too enthusiastic.

“Yeah, I mean… it’s kind of a lame game now. Like, it would probably take a while to get into it, but I like it,” he said with a shrug.

“That’s fair.”

An uncomfortable silence settled over us as we started down a corridor, and I searched desperately for something to say to break it. Guilt was still bubbling in the pit of my stomach, and that made it even harder to think of anything because I wasn’t sure whether he’d want me to change the subject to something else that made him feel less embarrassed.

Or maybe it would have been better to ask him more about the game. I did want to know about it. Kind of. I was intrigued, but at the same time, indecision kept pulling at me, making it impossible to settle on any topic.

“So… do you game much?” Seth asked after what felt like an eternity.

“Not really,” I replied quickly, grateful that he’d broken the silence.

“Oh.”

He sounded almost disappointed, and I hurried to explain.

“I played sometimes with a couple friends, but I didn’t really do it often,” I said. “I mean, I wanted to, but I didn’t have any consoles or anything. My mom doesn’t really approve of anything like that…”

That time, I was the one who laughed awkwardly.

“How come?” Seth asked.

It was a relief to hear that he didn’t sound embarrassed or judgemental. He was just curious, but I didn’t really want to tell him what Mom said.

“She just didn’t really like them. She said playing video games and stuff like that was a waste of time.”

That was close enough to what she’d said, but I’d left out some of the details, like how she’d claimed people who sat around gaming all day were lazy people who would accomplish nothing. Of course, she didn’t count the mobile games that she’d sunk hours into. They didn’t count, obviously.

“Oh,” Seth said. “My dad used to say the same things when he caught me gaming too. He really hated it.”

“Used to?” I repeated. “Did he change his mind?”

Seth snorted, his lips pursing slightly at the thought of his father.

“No. That man will never change. I just got better at hiding from him.”

I laughed softly.

“That’s good.”

Seth smiled at me before looking around.

“Which way is it?” he asked, gesturing toward the junction we were approaching.

The corridor split off in two directions, and both sides were lined with classrooms. They looked almost identical too, which had made it even more difficult to wrap my head around at first. There was nothing from the outside of each room that indicated which class was taught in there, but luckily, it was pretty obvious the door was open.

“Left,” I told him, gesturing in that direction as someone hurtled around the corner, almost taking Seth and me out.

“Sorry!” they cried as they raced past us.

“Don’t worry!” I called back to them.

My response felt instinctual at that point. I’d already been in the main building of the Academy for a few weeks, and it was fairly normal to see people rushing around. The rooms were spread out, and there was normally enough time between each class to mean that we could get to the next one in time, but trainees got distracted talking to the tutors all the time.

That was still weird to me. The tutors actually appeared to enjoy speaking to the trainees, and they didn’t try to hurry them away or dismiss them. They actually seemed to care, and I’d unnerved me. I was sure it would probably start to feel normal in time, but that hadn’t happened yet.

“When do you get used to that?”

I glanced at him in confusion, trying to figure out what he could be talking about.

“To what?”

“To that,” Seth repeated, gesturing towards another trainee as they jogged past us. “Like, you didn’t even flinch when that guy almost took us out.”

“Oh, yeah… that happens,” I laughed.

Seth shook his head, looking around at the bustling corridor.

“It’s so busy in here,” he remarked. “I mean, there are almost as many people here as in my school back home, but the teachers shouted at us if we ran there, so it wasn’t quite so...”

He didn’t finish that sentence, but he didn’t need to. I knew what he meant.

Yeah. It was the same in my school. Like, people still ran sometimes, but the teachers hated it,” I said.

“Right? But they don’t even seem to care here,” Seth muttered. “I mean, look!”

He nodded towards the end of the corridor where Fred, the law tutor, was jogging towards his classroom. I followed his gaze, smiling as my eyes found him.

“Oh yeah. I’m pretty sure he always runs to class, though,” I said.

Or maybe he was just always late. I wasn’t sure, but I could vaguely remember seeing him running through the hallways and dodging students on multiple occasions, so maybe that was just something he liked to do.

Fred must have felt our eyes on him because he met my gaze and lifted his hand.

“Morning, Grace!” he called.

“Morning,” I replied, my voice nowhere near as booming as his.

It felt wrong to speak any louder, even though he had done it. He was a tutor, though. He was allowed to run in the corridors and shout if he wanted to, but I was just a kid. I was still a trainee, and that meant the rules applied more to me than they did to him.

“This the next kid from the induction wing?” he asked as he weaved through the crowd towards us.

Fred’s question made a handful of people turn and look at Seth and me, and the desire to cower away from the attention rose within me, but I forced myself to ignore it. I wanted to be better than that, more confident too. I didn’t have to enjoy the eyes burrowing into Seth and me, but I didn’t have to hide from them.

“Yeah,” I forced myself to say. “This is Seth.”

“Nice to meet you,” Seth added awkwardly.

“Ah, nice to meet you too, Seth,” Fred said as he reached out to shake his hand. “Heard a lot about you.”

“Oh?” Seth replied, his eyes flitting towards me. “Nothing too bad, I hope?”

His expression was worried, and I knew he was trying to work out what could have been said about him, but I had no clue either.

“No, no, don’t worry,” Fred told him. “All great things. What’s your speciality?”

“Field op.”

“Fantastic,” Fred replied. “Well, I’m Fred. I’m the law and legal skills tutor, and I believe you have class with me… I want to say this afternoon?”

“Yes. At two,” Seth confirmed.

“Great! Well, I look forward to speaking to you then,” Fred said. “And congratulations on passing the induction period.”

“Oh, thank you.”

“Have a great morning, you two,” Fred told us with a smile before starting to turn away.

“You too,” I replied.

Seth waited until the tutor had disappeared into his classroom before letting out a heavy sigh.

“Well, that was sufficiently terrifying,” he said with a laugh.

“Yeah, that’s fair,” I agreed with an amused grin. “He’s… unexpected.”

Seth eyed the classroom as we passed it.

“That’s for sure. So… you never answered my question.”

I glanced at him in confusion.

“Which one?”

“When do you get used to… well, everything here?”

I couldn’t help but snort at that.

“I mean… I’ll let you know when it happens, I guess?”

A laugh slipped from Seth’s lips, and I felt a flare of happiness burst in my chest. He’d found it funny. No. It was more than that, I realised. Seth had found me funny, and that made me a lot more proud than it should have.

I wasn’t a funny person. Not really. I don’t think anyone had ever described me as that, and I didn’t really make people laugh. Not in real life, anyway. But it had happened, and I liked it. Maybe if I tried really hard, I could become a funny person.

“That’s fair,” Seth said. “Honestly, I don’t know that I’ll ever actually find this… normal. It’s so different from what I’m used to, you know? The induction wing definitely helped, but… still.”

“I know what you mean,” I agreed. “It’s still a lot.”

“Right? But imagine how much more jarring it would have been to go straight from normal life to this.”

I laughed, but my mind was instantly distracted by that thought. That was basically what I’d done. Although I’d spent a lot of time in the induction wing, and one version of me had been there the entire time, I’d also been jumping back and forth between reality and that world. Each time I returned to it, it took me some time to adjust to it again.

That was probably making it harder for me, I realised. Perhaps if I’d spent the whole time in the Academy, it would have felt more normal, more mundane. I wouldn’t have anything to compare it with, and that might have made the adjustment easier. It would have been less drastic, maybe?

I wasn’t sure, but there wasn’t exactly anything I could do about that. I couldn’t just spend all of my time in that one world and ignore my reality completely. Obviously, that was really tempting, but I knew it was a stupid idea. I had to go back sometimes.

Mom would notice if I wasn’t there. Maybe not all the time, but surely, at some point, she would realise that I wasn’t quite acting like myself. I assumed she would, anyway. I didn’t really know what I was like when my body was acting on autopilot, but even if she didn’t work out something was wrong, I was too anxious not to at least check in sometimes.

The longer I went without returning to my reality, the more it scared me. I was worried about what would be awaiting me when I finally gathered the courage to go back, and the knowledge that I was locked in the car with Mom all day only made that worse. Anything could happen there, and if I didn’t react properly, if I didn’t say the right thing, she would explode.

Fear nibbled at me, and I felt dizziness pull at the back of my mind. The corridor around me began to blur around me, slipping out of focus, and I glanced at Seth’s fuzzy face before letting myself be swept away.

My stomach tightened, and nausea boiled within me. I pressed my lips together, squeezing my eyelids shut as I inhaled deeply through my nose. The queasiness would pass. I knew it would. It always did; it just took time.

The rushing sound began to subside, gradually being replaced with the pounding beat of a song as I continued to breathe as slowly as I could. It only took a few more seconds before I was sure I could open my eyes without the car spinning around me, but I was wrong.

I blinked, trying to force the world into focus as a yawn escaped my lips. It had come out of nowhere, and confusion washed over me. Had I been sleeping? I didn’t normally sleep when I was in another world. Not during the day, anyway. I was pretty sure I fell asleep when I went elsewhere at night, but I was almost certain I stayed awake when I did it in the day.

My mind seemed to move slowly as I struggled to figure it out. It felt like I’d just woken up. My eyelids were heavy, and my neck ached as if it had been bent at a weird angle for too long, but something else could have caused the pain. Perhaps Mom had braked too suddenly again, and the seat belt had caught it. That had happened before.

Things looked different, though. It wasn’t different enough to make me suspect I was in the wrong world, but something had definitely changed since the last time I’d paid attention to it. Trees still lined the side of the road, sending a small trill of fear through me, but they were more sparse and the colour less vibrant. We were definitely back in England.

It was still bright, though, I realised as I blinked up at the sky through the windscreen. There were hardly any clouds looming overhead, and the sun was still high above us, so I couldn’t have been asleep for long, if I even did fall asleep.

I glanced down, intending to check the time on my phone, but it wasn’t there. My heart leapt in fear, and I felt some of the fog in my brain retreat quickly. I’d been holding it before. I knew I had. When I’d gone to the other world or fallen asleep, it was in my hand, but I wasn’t holding it anymore.

My eyes flicked towards my mom, checking to see whether she’d noticed that I was awake, but her gaze was still fixed on the road. Had she taken it? That wouldn’t have surprised me. She did like to go through my phone, but although I’d deleted my messages from Duncan not too long ago, he’d sent a few more potentially suggestive ones since then, and surely she would have woken me up if she’d seen them.

Continuing to watch her out of the corner of my eye, I craned my head slightly to check the foot well. Relief slammed into me as I spotted my phone, and I leant forward to pick it up.

“Good morning,” my mom said, her tone snarky as she confirmed my suspicion. “Did you sleep well?”

I must have fallen asleep, then. Or maybe I just looked like I was sleeping?

“Yes, thank you,” I replied.

That didn’t feel like the best thing to say to her, but I wasn’t sure what would have been better. My heart thudded in my throat as I pressed the lock button on my phone, and the screen lit up.

Mom scoffed, and my head snapped up to look at her.

“Honestly, you’re sleeping your life away, and I don’t know how you’re so tired,” she remarked. “You’re too young for that. You should be full of energy. I was when I was your age! But then again, I didn’t spend every single day cooped up inside…”

She trailed off, shooting me a pointed look, and I had to fight the urge to reply to her. It wouldn’t have helped to point out that I spent much more time outside than she did, but it was still tempting.

“Mmm,” was all I said.

That felt like a fairly safe response. There was nothing she could say to that, and she must have realised the same thing because she just sighed loudly before going back to ignoring me.

I waited until I could no longer feel her eyes on me before looking at my phone again. I’d seen a few messages pop up when the screen lit up before, but I hadn’t had a chance to read them. They were proof that my mom hadn’t grabbed my phone from me when I’d been asleep or in the other world, though.

There was a text from Phoebe just over two hours ago. Several texts, in fact, but one was too long for the whole thing to be shown on the lock screen. That meant Mom would have had to unlock my phone if she wanted to read it, and then it would have been marked as read, but it wasn’t, so I was safe.

My heart sank as I glanced at my mom again, my finger hovering over the messages from Duncan before I swiped across on them to clear our messages again. A disappointed sigh almost slipped from my lips, but I caught it just in time.

I hesitated, torn before wanting to read the messages from Phoebe whilst also still being half asleep. If I read them, I’d need to reply immediately. Otherwise, I’d forget to and feel bad, and I wasn’t sure I had the energy to do that.

My head fell back against the headrest, my body making my decision for me. My eyes began to flutter shut again as the world faded around me, and I felt my body begin to float away.

Time, I realised with a jolt, my head snapping up again. I was going to check the time. That was why I’d looked for my phone in the first place. I wanted to know how much time had passed since I’d checked before.

My eyebrows drew together as I stared down at the screen. That couldn’t be right. I was almost certain it was around twelve before, but my phone told me it was half-three. More than three hours had passed, but it only felt like it had been an hour, maybe two at most.

I must have fallen asleep, then. That was the only way to explain it. Time had moved slower in the other world, though. I should have been used to that. I’d realised a while back that it wasn’t always equal, but it was still a strange realisation each time.

Still, I was glad it worked like that. It made the journey so much easier, and I couldn’t help but think perhaps if I stayed at the Academy for longer when I went there again, I could miss most of the journey home. The next time I went back to my reality, we might almost be home.
