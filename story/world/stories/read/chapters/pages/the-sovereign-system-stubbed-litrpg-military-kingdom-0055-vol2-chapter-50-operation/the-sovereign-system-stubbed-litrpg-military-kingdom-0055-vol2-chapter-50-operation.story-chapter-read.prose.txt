Luke’s heart sank at the sight of the serpent’s taut body.

If he didn’t act now, the snake would return to the command tent and confront Tristan. And if the Holy Knight had received a divine blessing like the others they’d faced, things would end badly for his friend.

Before he even realized it, Luke lunged at the inky-black serpent—the supposed soul form of the knight. His body moved on instinct, surprising even himself.

Up until the snake had spoken, Luke hadn’t even considered that he might have a corporeal form while using Eagle Eye. He wasn’t sure if attacking was possible, but the raw loathing that bubbled to the surface left no room for hesitation.

The serpent’s large head turned toward him, a horrified expression etched into its ghostly features. It seemed to recognize Luke as the greater threat.

“N-NO!”

The ghastly wail that escaped its mouth was haunting, but it only brought Luke satisfaction.

His form began to change. A large golden maw manifested around him, its outline radiating power. It snapped shut over the serpent, whose body was frozen in terror.

Tightly packed scales filled Luke’s vision, glowing faintly despite the darkness. They shimmered for an instant—then the maw clamped down with a thunderous crack.

A piercing screech tore through the void. The serpent let out one final wail before dissolving.

Its body unraveled into wisps of black, the inky substance collapsing in slow motion toward the ground. Luke’s stomach twisted at the sight, a ripple of unease washing over him. But deep down, something told him it was no longer a threat.

What just happened?

His thoughts spun wildly. He hadn’t known he could attack—hadn’t even understood how he had. It had all happened so quickly that even Scholarly Pursuit offered no clear explanation.

Now’s not the time, he told himself, shaking his head to clear it.

With the serpent gone, his attention shifted back to the command tent—just in time to see Tristan stepping out, unharmed. Luke exhaled, relief softening the tension in his chest. If Tristan was walking out, that meant the Holy Knight was either dead… or no longer a threat.

But then he frowned.

Tristan wore a strange expression—one Luke couldn’t place even from afar. A few moments later, the man turned and strode off toward the next tent, resuming the operation.

From his high vantage point, Luke scanned the rest of the camp. The attack was underway, but it was hard to tell if their presence had gone undetected thus far.

He stayed still and observant, ready to intervene if needed. Even with the enemy commander dead, the situation was far from safe. Most of their men were still unarmed.

Twenty long minutes crawled by. Rain continued to fall, streaking the night with silver. Luke watched as his soldiers entered each tent, converging from all four cardinal directions toward the center of the camp. By now, the metallic tang of blood had begun to permeate the air—thick and bitter, even through the downpour.

“Young Luke.”

The voice snapped him back.

Luke returned to his body with a jolt, his vision dimming for a second as the world reasserted itself. A wave of nausea followed. Whatever state he’d been in, it had clearly taken a toll.

Luke’s eyes shifted to Tristan, who stood nearby, his robes soaked with fresh blood. The older man looked weary but uninjured—a detail that allowed a breath of relief to escape Luke’s lips.

“You did well, old friend,” he said, placing a hand on Tristan’s shoulder.

But instead of accepting the praise, Tristan’s expression turned grave.

“When I entered the command tent, the Holy Knight screamed and died right in front of me. His face was twisted in horror...” He trailed off, brow furrowed, as if still trying to make sense of it.“I think that’s the first time I’ve ever killed someone... without even touching them.”

He looked a little lost.

Luke gave a slow nod. If what Tristan said was true, then they had both been in their soul forms earlier. Destroying the knight’s soul would explain why his physical body perished.

“To be fair,” Luke said with a small grin, “if I woke up and saw you like this, I’d probably drop dead too.”

The attempt at humor didn’t land.

Tristan’s brow furrowed further. His eyes studied Luke carefully, suspicion flickering in their depths. After a moment, his hand came up and gripped Luke’s shoulder firmly.

“You don’t seem surprised,” he said—not a question, but a quiet accusation.

Luke exhaled and pulled his hand away.

“I’ll tell you later.”

Now wasn’t the time. They were still deep in enemy territory, and idle conversation could cost lives.Besides, he didn’t have answers—not yet. Whatever had happened earlier was beyond anything he could explain. Even Scholarly Pursuit hadn’t offered clarity. And there was still that system notification he needed to check.

“WE’RE UNDER ATTACK!”

The booming shout shattered the moment.

Both men snapped toward the source of the voice, eyes widening in alarm—then quickly looked back at each other.

“Sound the retreat.”

The soldiers nearby didn’t need a second order. Two of them reached for ox horns and brought them to their lips with practiced efficiency.

A moment later, a sharp, commanding wail tore through the rain-soaked air—deep, resonant, and long. It pierced the downpour and echoed across the camp. Even standing a few feet away, Luke felt it rumble in his chest.

He’d worried that the rain might drown out the signal. He no longer had that concern.

“Tristan, I need fire,” Luke said suddenly, already reaching into his knapsack.

Tristan blinked. “You don’t ask for much, do you?” he muttered, followed by a string of curses. “How the hell am I supposed to make a fire in this weather? Every damn stick around here’s soaked.”

Just then, Luke pulled out a bundle of dry tinder. Back when the rain had just begun, he’d tucked it away for later use.

Tristan shot him a brief, surprised look before grumbling again. “Lift your robe. I need to keep this dry.”

Luke crouched beside him, lifting his robe to create a makeshift canopy. It wasn’t much, but it would have to do. They only had a few pieces of tinder—and just a few chances to get it right. Meanwhile, the enemy was now fully alerted.

All around them, their soldiers were retreating—scattering into the darkness in well-drilled formations. Most had salvaged weapons from fallen enemies. That was a good sign.

Still, time was slipping through their fingers, and the true storm hadn’t even begun yet.

A few tense minutes passed, and Luke’s patience began to fray. But judging by the steady stream of curses coming from Tristan’s mouth, he thought better of voicing any complaints.

“About damn time,” the older man growled, blowing furiously at the ember he’d finally coaxed to life. With practiced hands, he added the dry tinder. Flames sparked up, casting flickering warmth and light beneath the canopy of Luke’s robe.

Without hesitation, Luke reached into his knapsack and retrieved one of the bronze thunder bombs—its cold, heavy surface oddly comforting in his palm.This one had been specially crafted back in Clayton City, intended for use against Holy Knights.

Feels like a waste... he lamented silently, though only for a moment.

Given the damp conditions, using a clay thunder bomb would be too risky. If the gunpowder inside had absorbed moisture, it might misfire—failing to deliver the chaos he needed.

He placed the oil-soaked rope fuse onto the open flame. It caught fire instantly, hissing and spitting as it began to burn down. Across from him, Tristan’s eyes widened, his expression shifting between awe and unease—not at Luke, but at the weapon in his hands.

“Is that...?” Tristan muttered, reaching toward it instinctively.

“Yes,” Luke nodded, eyes fixed on the smoldering fuse. “Once this rope burns through, the powder inside will ignite. It’ll explode with enough force to kill or maim everyone within twenty feet.”

Tristan’s face drained of color.“W-wait—why the hell are you still holding it, then!?”

Luke tilted his head.“Because you’re the one throwing it, of course.”His tone was perfectly casual, as if explaining a matter of basic logistics.“Do you seriously think I can toss this thing two hundred feet?”

Another droplet of rain sizzled on the fuse. It was already halfway gone.

“You idiot!” Tristan shouted, lunging forward. He snatched the bomb out of Luke’s hands and, without pause, hurled it high into the air with a grunt. His breathing came in ragged gasps as he watched it vanish above the treetops.

Luke watched its arc with admiration. Judging by the angle and strength of the throw, it should land squarely in the middle of the enemy encampment.

He didn’t need it to kill—just to panic.

Five seconds later, a deafening boom cracked through the forest like a lightning strike. Then came the screaming—raw, bloodcurdling, and far too human.

“Let’s go. We need to reach the ambush line before they get here,” Luke said briskly, already turning away from the fading firelight.

But after a few steps, he noticed something was wrong.

He turned back—and saw Tristan standing frozen, eyes locked in the direction of the explosion.

“Tristan? Let’s go, we don’t have time.”

The older man flinched at the sound of his name. He turned slowly, revealing pale features and a haunted expression.

“You’re a crazy bastard, Luke…”

Luke might have laughed—but the way Tristan said it carried no humor. Just quiet horror.

He’s in shock, Luke realized.

And he couldn’t blame him.

Hearing about the thunder bombs was one thing. Witnessing their destructive power was another entirely.

Luke still remembered Clayton City—the aftermath. The dismembered limbs. The cries of the wounded. The stink of burned flesh lingering in the air.Those images had burned themselves into his memory.And now, they would do the same to Tristan.

Even Master Boyd had changed after witnessing the aftermath of the thunder bombs. His stoic demeanor had faltered, if only briefly.

If Master Gale were still alive, Luke thought, he might’ve reacted the same.

But he pushed the thought aside. Now wasn’t the time to dwell on the moral weight of introducing gunpowder to this world.

He raised a hand and gestured forward, focusing on the present.

After retreating behind the pre-set ambush lines, it didn’t take long for the panicked cries of enemy soldiers to reach them. Despite the rain and poor visibility, the enemy charged through the trees, desperate to flee the explosion and escape the forest.

They never stood a chance.

The ambush caught them mid-flight. With the stolen weapons recovered from the enemy camp, the Marxx soldiers cut them down swiftly and without mercy. Screams rang out, mingling with the chaos—too sudden, too violent for the fleeing soldiers to even realize they were being hunted.

By the time they did, it was already too late.

When the clash finally settled, Luke activated Eagle Eye, his vision soaring high above the battlefield. He scanned the surrounding area with sharp precision.

No movement.

The enemy camp was still.

Success.

He released a quiet breath and restored his consciousness to his body. Beside him, Tristan stood silent—his gaze vacant, his shoulders heavy.

“Vice General,” Luke said, purposefully using his formal title.

The shift in tone snapped Tristan back to reality.

“Take a group of men into the camp. Gather any stragglers, check the supply wagons, and compile a full inventory of what we’ve gained tonight.”

Tristan nodded, cupping his fist in salute. “Yes, General.”

Luke placed a firm hand on his friend’s shoulder—a gesture he found himself doing more often lately. A flicker of sorrow tugged at him as he noted the subtle shift in Tristan’s eyes.

But this, Luke knew, was temporary.

“Once you're done, get some rest,” he added softly. “We’ll talk about our next steps tomorrow.”

He didn’t wait for a response.

Turning away, Luke trudged toward camp. The rain had finally lessened to a drizzle, but he was already soaked through. Every step felt heavier than the last. The exhaustion and hunger of the past few days pressed in around him, more real now that the adrenaline had worn off.

And yet, something else weighed on him—heavier than fatigue.

Tonight confirmed what he feared: a force from Lhair was marching from the south, headed toward Ralis City.And with Selis now present in the east, they could soon be flanked on two fronts.

They needed to return. The King had to be warned.

But that thought brought a new problem.

There’s a traitor in the court.If I alert the King... will the traitor learn of it too?

The question lingered like a thorn as he stepped inside his tent.

A wave of exhaustion nearly toppled him. It took all his willpower to strip off his muddy boots and robe before collapsing onto the stiff makeshift bedding.

Even then, instinct drove his hand to open the system window, just as he always did at day’s end.

But what he saw made him freeze.

[The Bishop has noticed your presence][The Sovereign’s Blessing has negated the skill: Soul Tracker]

His eyes widened, a chill running down his spine.

Was it when I killed the serpent?Did he sense me then...?

But the next notification made his blood run cold.

[Your subordinate Victoria has been gravely injured]

Luke’s breath caught. The fatigue vanished in an instant, replaced by a flood of dread.
