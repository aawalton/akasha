Owen's blade sliced through the orc warrior's throat, warm blood spraying across his shrouded face. He didn’t falter. His grip tightened on the hilt, the blade trembling only slightly as he dispatched three more orcs in rapid succession, each kill swifter than the last.

Sensing the deadly whistle of an incoming arrow, Owen threw himself to the side, but this time he wasn’t quick enough. The arrow struck true, slamming into his shoulder with a sickening force, sending him sprawling through the air.

Before he hit the ground, Rehan was there, catching him in mid-fall and lowering him to safety. Without missing a beat, Rehan darted behind Shelldon, the massive war tortoise, who raised his armoured shell as a shield against further attacks.

All the orcs had been killed. No Sand Hounds were left. Only the distant archer fired, but even that stopped after a few minutes. He had most likely retreated.

Sheltered beneath its formidable bulk, Owen seized the moment. He activated his power, claiming the corpses scattered across the battlefield—every one of them. With their wounded secured, Owen and his group retreated, disappearing from the chaos to tend to their injuries.

Cedric and Rohan were rushed to Rizael’s infirmary, along with other numerous Warriors and Hunters. Owen himself rested his back against the wall, and slid down until his rear was firmly seated on the sand.

“My Lord,” Draed said in concern, “Are you okay?”

“I’m okay,” Owen replied, exhausted. He glanced at his wound. It wasn’t severe, and hadn't struck an artery. “Just tired. Go and see if Rizael needs help.”

Draed bowed and left. All Owen could hear were his people in pain, their cries filtering out of the infirmary. The Builders wanted to come over and help, but they had their own mission. If the walls weren’t finished in time, the waves would wash them away.

Closing his eyes, Owen focused on his breathing. The sight of Cedric willingly take an arrow for him without hesitation burned itself into his vision. Owen snapped his eyes open. There was no way in hell he was going out without a fight. He had to focus, get stronger.

After a few minutes, Draed and Cedric stood in front of him. It was time to evolve his first two Hunters.

Draed’s eyes had blurred over, deep in thought as he gazed at the System screen. However, he stripped his eyes away a moment later, and said, “What do you think, my Lord?”

Owen shook his head. “It’s up to you. What do you like the sound of more? No doubt that orc archer was something akin to the Eagle Eye Marksman.”

Draed said after a moment, “I’m thinking of going another direction. I don’t think that type of archery suits me.”

“No,” Owen said in agreement, “I think the same.” It was true. From his memory, the orc had his feet planted firmly in the sand. Each time he fired the bow, his body would recoil backwards; like firing a high-powered rifle. Whereas Draed was always on the move, firing even while jumping sometimes. He was an archer that focused on agility, not strength.

“May I choose, my Lord?” Draed asked, unsure as he glanced at the System, then back to Owen.

Owen smiled. “Of course you can.”

Draed nodded and selected what he wanted.

Draed has selected the Tier 2 Class: Soul Hunter.

Just like with Lome, the evolution was instant. But unlike the undead warrior, it was barely noticeable. If Owen didn’t pay close attention, he would never see the faint ghostly hue exuding from Draed’s eyes.

Draed tested his new abilities. Spirit Mark conjured a ghostly arrow, nearly invisible to the naked eye, that homed in on targets with eerie precision, painfully reminding him of the orc's skill. Phantom Shot appeared similar but packed a stronger punch. The most striking transformation occurred when he activated Wraith Form. His lower legs turned misty and ethereal, allowing him to move faster and silently across the sand, no mark being made.

He glanced at the shaft, then with a little hesitation, snapped it, then pulled out the rest. Only a small amount of blood seeped from the wound, but that was it.

I really love this Skill, Owen thought, unable to muster a smile.

Anything that helped him stay calm under pressure was an incredible boon. If he had possessed it during the ambush, he might have reacted faster. This skill would also enhance his battlefield experience, allowing him to manage fear more effectively—something typically overcome only through countless battles.
