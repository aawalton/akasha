Ash was watching her sleep by the side of the bed. She had woken up early, and there was nothing else to look at. It was a poor excuse, but it was still the one she had given herself. Lysanthia lay on her side, facing the wall. Her dark hair had fanned across the pillow. Her breathing was slow and even. Ash had been watching her like this for some time. Twenty minutes, perhaps? She did not remember. There was a reason to watch this. When awake, Lysanthia always looked fierce. None of that fierceness was there now. In sleep, even Lysanthia just looked like a woman.

Ash had thought this before. In Hamel, when she had spent a night pressing wet cloth to her burns. Lysanthia looked little like the Demon Queen then. She looked even less like her now.

It was hard to imagine that they were the same person. The woman sleeping on this bed was the same person who had been in the throne room that day. The one who had cut Ronan with a single swing of her sword.

She heard stirring. Ash's senses had always been sharp. She turned and saw Sol lifting her head from her tiny pillow. Her four wings slowly started to move. Her big golden eyes opened and closed and then opened again. Sol let out a tiny chirp. Ash knew her next chirp would probably be a screech. One that would certainly wake Lysanthia up, but with some indignity. She quickly crossed the room and picked the tiny phoenix up.

"Well now, you woke up early. I think it's about time anyway," Ash murmured. Sol met her gaze and turned towards the bed. Ash grinned. "Alright, alright."

She carried Sol to the bed and held her out. The phoenix leaned forward, stretched her tiny neck, and pecked at Lysanthia's temple.

Lysanthia let out a groan. Her crimson eyes opened. It was almost remarkable just how quickly the softness left her face. Lysanthia looked at Ash first, then Sol, held out towards herself. "You have exactly one more chance to reconsider that approach," she said to the bird.

Ash had suspected how this would play out. And sure enough, the tiny phoenix craned her neck forward and pecked Lysanthia again. She even let out a tiny chirp at the end of it. Ash liked to think that was Sol's way of telling Lysanthia to 'wake up already'.

Ash pulled Solara back as Lysanthia sat up. She watched as Lysanthia swung her legs over the side of the bed. Then she tried to stand, and her legs went out from under her.

Ash was already moving. One arm went around Lysanthia's waist and the other braced against her shoulder. She felt light to Ash, despite her being taller. The woman who had once tried to burn the world. The woman who had swatted Ozriel from the sky. The woman who had killed Calith and Calithra. To Ash, she was light.

"What is this?" Lysanthia demanded. She tried to take a single step and her knees buckled again. Ash quickly adjusted and kept her from falling. "My legs are betraying me."

Ash slowly helped Lysanthia sit back down on the bed. A Hero did not say 'I told you so', but a part of Ash wanted to say it anyway. "I warned you, Lys. This is what I was telling you about. Your body isn't used to the strain you put it through, and so, this happened."

Lysanthia looked up at her. "All we did was run. And even then we only ran for a few minutes. That was enough to do this? My body has endured things that would break this Academy in half."

She was right. Ash had heard plenty of stories. Lysanthia had survived attacks the likes of which had killed plenty of Demon Queens before her. "You did. But every time you did, you used some kind of magic, didn't you? Maybe a [Spell] or even basic mana reinforcement. But yesterday, we couldn't even use Essence, remember? It was all muscle. Well…this is what happens to muscle."

Lysanthia glared at her. It was a sharp glare. Surprisingly sharp for someone who could not even rise under their own power. Ash idly wondered just how she managed something like that. Maybe it came with being a Queen. Then Lysanthia raised her left hand, and Ash saw white fire trailing down her fingertips. She was going to heal herself, as Ash knew she would.

Ash caught her wrist. "Don't."

Lysanthia frowned. "Why?"

"You are sore, aren't you? That's because you put your muscles through enough strain that some of them tore. If you give it time, those muscles are going to knit themselves back even stronger than they had been before." She had explained this to recruits before, almost a thousand years ago. How strange that she was explaining the same to the Demon Queen herself. "So, if you wait, you'll see results. But if you use the Cradle, you'll put everything back to the way it was. Your forty minutes will have been pointless."

Lysanthia stared at her. The white fire died. "You must be joking."

"Afraid not, Lys. Don’t worry, it’ll never be as bad as the first time."

"Then what," Lysanthia said, very slowly, "am I supposed to do?"

That was a question many of those recruits had asked her too, a long time ago. Ash had the same answer now as she'd had then. "Nothing. You don't really have to do anything." Ash smiled. "First, you eat. Then I'll show you some things that will help. You should be able to walk by tomorrow. Well…mostly walk."

Even sitting, Ash could see the faintest tremor running down the woman's legs. She must have been really sore then. Sol, who had been displaced a few seconds ago, hopped across the mattress and sat on Lysanthia's thigh. "Traitor," Lysanthia said to her. She did not remove the bird from her leg. Ash didn't know what made the phoenix a traitor, and she was afraid to ask.

Ash crossed the room to the table. She had woken up very early. Early enough to get two bowls of porridge from the mess hall. The cooks had not liked her request, but Ash had always been persuasive. She brought them over. Lysanthia looked at the bowls. Then at Ash.

"It seems," she said, "that you have prepared for this."

"Yep."

"Why?"

How was she supposed to answer that? So many answers came, until they crowded her throat. Ash said none of them, in the end. "It's what a retainer is supposed to do," Ash said.

Something flickered across Lysanthia's face. Her face colored. Ash knew hers had too. Lysanthia took the bowl without further comment.

They ate. Sol ate too, once Lysanthia had bathed a strip of meat in the Cradle's white fire. Lysanthia did not use the bundles of meat they’d brought just yesterday. Apparently, they were too old now. Sol chirped, and Lysanthia fussed. It was a small mercy, the noise. Without it, the room would have been quiet enough for Ash to hear the grinding.

Ash could always hear that grinding. It didn't matter what time of day it was. It hardly mattered how far away they were.

Ash watched Lysanthia eat. She was in pain, judging from the sound, and yet none of it showed on her face, no matter how close Ash looked. This was a pain Lysanthia would carry for the rest of her life. Ash could not fathom it. How someone could be in that much pain and never even mention it. All she could do was press closer, at night, and hope that did something.

"Stop staring at me," Lysanthia said, without looking up from her bowl.

Ash looked away. "I wasn't staring."

"You were. Your gaze has been fixed on me for the better part of two minutes. I can feel it." She took another spoonful. "A Queen always knows when she is being watched."

"Then a Queen should be used to it by now."

Lysanthia glanced at her. "Perhaps I am. That does not mean it goes unnoticed."

Ash said nothing. She finished her porridge and set the bowl aside and waited for Lysanthia and Sol to finish theirs.

When the bowls were empty, Ash knelt at the edge of the bed. "Lie back on the bed. I need to work on your legs."

Ash expected Lysanthia to protest. Something about her 'not needing help', or perhaps something about her 'not being in any kind of pain at all'. Maybe a third thing Ash didn't expect. Lysanthia's mouth certainly opened to say something like that. It closed without saying anything at all. And then Lysanthia lay back and extended her long legs. Ash blinked. She had not expected it to go that easily.

Solara hopped down from the bed in a tangle of feathers. She shook herself and slowly waddled over towards the fire. She sat in front of it. The bird was surprisingly intelligent.

"This will help," Ash said. She kept her voice even. Or tried to, at any rate. "The first time I ran like you did yesterday, I couldn't move for a week. My instructor at the time showed me how to work through it."

"An entire week?" Lysanthia let out a deep sigh. She might as well have said an entire century.

"You won't need a week if we do this right, and I plan to do it right," Ash said with a smile.

Lysanthia looked at her and very slowly nodded. Ash took that for the permission it was and readied herself. Then she reached out towards Lysanthia's left calf. The skin was warm. And soft, so very soft. But past the skin there was a knot so hard it might as well have been made out of metal. Ash began to work that out.

She slowly pushed her thumbs against that knot until her fingers sank into the skin, just slightly. Then she pushed them outward. She did it again, then again. She had done this hundreds of times before. There was just one difference now: she was doing it to Lysanthia. The difference should not have mattered, but it did. The fourth time Ash did it, Lysanthia's breath hitched, just for a moment. It was impossible not to hear in a room that was otherwise so quiet. Maybe it was too quiet.

Ash slowly began to use more and more force. Lysanthia's skin really was soft. What else had she expected? Ash focused every tiny ounce of willpower she had on working away at the knot and on nothing else. She tried to do that, anyway.

"This is rather undignified," Lysanthia said.

“Sorry. It really is good for you though," Ash said.

Ash worked on the calf for some time before some of the hardness finally left it. Then she moved her hand up, past Lysanthia's knee. There were more knots here, and Ash had to slowly work on all of them one by one. That’s what Ash concentrated on as hard as she could. She dared not let her mind wander now.

Her thumbs were pressed against Lysanthia's thigh. The skin here was softer than it had been down below, and the skin there had already been almost impossibly soft. Now it felt like Ash's finger might slip past the skin if she pressed hard enough.

Lysanthia made a sound that was somewhere between a breath and a hiss. She covered it immediately. "That…that is a particularly aggressive technique."

Ash paused. She had let her concentration slip. "It's supposed to be. If it hurts too much, just let me know. I'll go slower." Ash forced her hands steady, and by some miracle they stayed steady. Even her voice had been slightly less calm, and all Ash could do was pray to all the Gods that Lysanthia hadn't noticed. Ash’s eyes had moved up Lysanthia's thigh to where the tunic had ridden up. The skin there was even paler, and past the pale skin there was-

Ash stared straight up at the ceiling. Her head turned so sharply that she was sure Lysanthia must have noticed. A few more of these, and she would need someone to work out the knots in her neck.

She's hurt. You're helping her. Stop. What are you doing?

This wasn't the first time Ash had these thoughts. It wasn't the fifth or the tenth either. She had been having them for weeks now. Since the first time she had caught herself watching the way Lysanthia's hair moved when she turned her head. They never did a damn thing.

Ash moved to Lysanthia's other leg. And as she did, she looked at Lysanthia's hands, gripping the edge of the mattress. They were pale. She was in pain. Ash had to do this quickly, for Lys's sake.

It should have been simple. Someone was in pain and they needed help. That alone should have been enough for her to concentrate. But her thumbs were on Lysanthia's thigh again, one just as smooth and soft as the other had been. No, maybe this one was just a little softer than the other had been. Ash slowly worked out all of the knots one at a time. It took several minutes, during which neither of them said anything. Ash noticed that Lysanthia's tunic had risen up. She could see the curve of Lysanthia's hip now, and the way it curved again into the dip of her waist, and-

Ash jerked back as if she'd just touched fire barehanded. If she had been alone, she might have slapped herself. A Hero was supposed to be better than this. She was supposed to be selfless and clean.

Instead, right now, she was selfish and anything but clean. Her whole body felt warm. "Lys, we're going to do some stretches next, alright? I'm going to move your legs for you. Don’t fight it, okay? It's important," Ash said. Knowing Lysanthia, it might not surprise her at all to see the woman fight something that would help her.

"I do not fight things that are beneath me," Lysanthia said. Her face was just faintly red. That made Ash feel better, just a little.

The first of the stretches was the most simple. All she had to do was take Lysanthia's leg and bend the knee towards her chest. She had done this ten thousand times on herself, at least. She put one hand behind Lysanthia's thigh to brace herself as she leaned forward. She looked up from Lysanthia's leg and saw just how close they were now.

They were so close that Ash could see the ring of deeper red at the ends of Lysanthia's irises. Ash had seen them before, only once. When Lysanthia had cupped Ash's chin after Ash had made a request of her. The two of them were now close enough that Ash could see the slight parting of Lysanthia's lips as the stretch deepened.

Her lips were full. The upper one was slightly fuller than the lower one. She had noticed this last time too. Stop. She's in pain. She's trusting you to help her and you need to stop. What are you, a pig?

"Breathe," Ash said. "Take it nice and slow."

Lysanthia breathed. Ash counted to ten, and chose to stare at the bedsheets instead this time. She lowered the leg and moved to the other one.

Lysanthia's hand was on Ash's shoulder now. Ash could feel every one of Lysanthia's fingers, as if each one burned through her tunic like five small fires. All Ash could do was try not to think about it as she kept up the stretch. She noticed the one thing she most should not have.

It was impossible not to notice because of the stretch. Lysanthia's chest was so full and soft beneath the fabric. Ash had stared at Lysanthia's legs because she needed to look at them to work out all of those knots. What excuse was there to look where she was looking right now? None. She wished there were-

Her face was burning. It felt warmer than ever. You have no reason to be looking at that. She decided to look somewhere else. Somewhere farther down.

Moving her gaze down had been a good idea before she did it. It wasn't a good idea right now. Ash could see the line of Lysanthia's hip. Ash again did something a Hero was not supposed to do. She stared. Ash stared for one very long moment, and then she hastily turned her neck to look at the far wall. Was there anywhere safe to look around Lysanthia?

"How much longer?" Lysanthia asked. Her voice sounded more strained now. Someone else might not have noticed at all. Ash noticed.

"I'm almost done, Lys. Just a little longer." She lowered the leg she was holding. The next stretch was much safer for her eyes. She had to twist the ankles just slightly, moving the foot back in the direction of the calf.

Ash took a deep breath and reached for Lysanthia's feet. Lysanthia's feet were small, for someone of Lysanthia's height. They were as pale as the rest of her, and as soft as the rest of her too. Her thumb was tracing one of Lysanthia's toes. Ash stared at it and forced her thumb still.

There was a loud chirp from somewhere next to them. Ash looked over to where she had seen the phoenix last, but the phoenix wasn't there anymore. She turned and looked at Lysanthia again. Sol had hopped directly on Lysanthia's stomach and let out another chirp, even louder than the one before it. Loud enough that her tiny body trembled.

Lysanthia flinched and pulled her leg back. The leg attached to the foot Ash was holding right now. The motion jerked Ash's entire body forward and toward Lysanthia. She barely caught herself in time by slamming her palms on either side of Lysanthia's hips. Ash stared at Lysanthia wide-eyed. She had thought they were too close before, but that was nothing compared to right now. They were so close that Ash could see her own reflection in the crimson of Lysanthia's eyes. Sol was between them chirping at the ceiling as if none of this was her fault.

Her chest squeezed so hard it was impossible to breathe. Ash jerked herself back and sat on the stone floor. She pressed her palms flat against it, and just tried to get her lungs working again. It was hard.

"She has- she has remarkable timing," Ash said very carefully. She looked back up and at Lysanthia.

Lysanthia met her gaze, and looked away. "She is a creature of instinct, after all." Lysanthia was stroking Sol with one hand.

Ash did not do anything for a long time. When her breathing finally returned to normal, she worked up the courage to speak again. "Lys, lie down in front of the fire," Ash said. "The warmth will help a bit."

There was silence for two seconds. Then, finally, "Very well," Lysanthia said. Her voice sounded almost normal. Almost. Ash watched her lower herself onto the floor. She almost offered to help, but then her breathing picked up again. She stayed where she was as Lysanthia looked at the hearth and began to slowly scoot her way towards it.

Sol hopped down after her, landed with a soft tap, and waddled to her pillow. "And now what?" Lysanthia asked. "Am I simply to lie here?"

Ash smiled. "More or less. Just rest here and you'll feel better tomorrow."

Lysanthia's gaze was sharp, even now. "You know too much about this. Why?"

"Exercises like this were part of my own training. When I was a candidate, before the Hero class. I've had my share of painful...experiences." Ash sat against the wall. "Like I said, I once couldn’t move for a week."

"I cannot afford to lie here for a week. There are things I wish to-"

"You won't need a week," Ash said quickly. "Because I know how to care for this."

Lysanthia looked at the fire. Sol chirped softly, hopped off the pillow, and tucked herself against her side. The room was quiet except for the crackling. And the grinding. Always the grinding.
