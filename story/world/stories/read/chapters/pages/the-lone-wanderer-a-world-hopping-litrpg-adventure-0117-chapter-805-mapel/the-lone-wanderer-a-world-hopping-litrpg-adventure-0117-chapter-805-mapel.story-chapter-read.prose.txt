It was a good thing that Nephthys had moved into her late mother’s much-larger cavern in anticipation of her upcoming promotions, because she needed the extra space to accommodate her new gifts.

Each of the quasi-divine beast’s glands was over a dozen times larger than the wasp’s entire body, and Percy had brought her eight of them in total. The good news was that eating them seemed to do something to her, giving her carapace a more vibrant colour and glossier sheen, while also causing the organs inside her thorax to shift slightly.

Percy wasn’t sure what exactly was happening to his familiar, but he guessed that her mutation was already on the way to getting upgraded, just like they had hoped. Unfortunately, the changes were happening at a snail’s pace, so Nephthys might have to devour multiple giant crystals before her Status registered the new entry – a process that could easily last for weeks if not months.

That suited Percy just fine, because he and his familiar were more than capable of working on other things at the same time.

On the outside, his body was mechanically passing the Moirais’ Decree to one Starry Knight after the other, in a bid to prepare the sacrifices for Micky’s third core as soon as possible. The part of him that resided within his familiar’s body was nearly done creating seals inside her beast core, while the rest of Percy’s minds were studying mindset creation under her guidance.

Besides their ongoing activities, Percy decided to take a short break not long after handing Nephthys the glands, because he had to send out another soul clone. His special connection had become available again after returning from Ruby Heart, and he would rather not waste it.

‘Are we still avoiding Mithril?’ Micky asked. ‘I understand that you’re a bit shaken after what happened with Baphomet, but we’ll need to get that Focus sooner or later if we want to beat him.’

Percy didn’t deny it. Not only was claiming the coveted artifact one of the easiest ways to raise his strength, but also his best lead at extending his lifespan, since Mithril was the most developed faction that he knew the location of besides the Void Hand.

He didn’t think that he would have enough time to acquire the Focus or master a calamity affinity before his battle with the Great Houses, which was one of his main reasons for prioritizing shorter and more self-contained adventures lately, while ignoring some of the karmic compasses that he’d been holding onto for years.

But his friend was right. A big part of him was just afraid of stirring another mess.

If Percy was being honest with himself, he’d always used his bloodline quite recklessly. He’d shared his secrets with people that he probably shouldn’t have, interacted with dangerous deities like Metatron or Rhaziel on a regular basis, and risked everything to sneak into places that a mortal from an undeveloped world like him wasn’t supposed to step foot in.

It was a miracle that he’d made it this far without getting captured by the Void Hand or corrupted by the Cardinal Devil. Even his clash with Baphomet had ended more favourably than Percy deserved – all things considered.

The problem was that the types of resources that he now required could only be found on the most dangerous planets. He only had a single mental- and active-type Decree slot left, so he had to be mindful of what he filled them with. He and Micky already had blessings for half of their affinities, so there were very few Rings of Sacrilege out there that might be useful for them. And they’d already exhausted the low-hanging fruit of core-cleansing and lifespan-extension.

Even a random greater spring was no longer guaranteed to have something useful for them, so they had to keep aiming for stronger and stronger factions. They simply couldn’t afford to continue acting as carelessly as they had, or their luck would eventually run out. At some point, they’d have to reevaluate the way they approached their adventures and take certain precautions.

‘It’s something to think about later. For now, let’s stick with a simpler and safer destination,’ Percy decided.

Pulling his newest karmic compass out of his spatial seal, he guided his wisp toward the lesser spring with the demonic infestation. There shouldn’t be anything overly dangerous on that planet.

Regular infestations couldn’t follow him back to Remior, and Percy felt that his sentries were good enough to potentially fool even a weaker god if they weren’t paying him much attention. As long as he didn’t do anything crazy or directly messed with a karmic deity, he would be fine.

Upon reaching the planet – which was called Mapel according to the information that had come with the compass – he possessed an Orange beast that looked a bit like a squirrel but was closer in size to a dog.

Examining his host’s condition, Percy guessed that the creature had broken a couple of limbs after falling from a tall tree. After foolishly getting itself paralysed, it had been both lucky and unlucky enough to be left alone by predators until it starved.

Fixing its broken bones wouldn’t be easy with just healing potions, but beasts healed better and faster than sapients so long as they were given enough food. Percy had more than enough nectar in his seal to keep the squirrel’s belly perpetually full, and could support its injured limbs with his willpower until they recovered.

Borrowing spiritual blood from Micky once more, he produced twenty fist-sized sentries and had them scout his immediate surroundings out.

From what little Percy had seen so far, Mapel was the most “normal”-looking planet he had visited in a while. There were no volcanoes, giant lakes, dozens of moons, global oceans, nor other overly exotic environments. Just a regular forest that wouldn’t have been out of place on Remior. In his experience, the most peculiar planets tended to stand out from the very beginning.

The trees were admittedly a little taller and wider than he was used to, but he still found it oddly refreshing to explore a place that resembled his home so closely for a change – especially now that his enemies had basically trapped him in the Fungal Spire and made it harder for him to roam Remior freely.

It was only after his constructs reached the nearest village that he was reminded that this was still an alien planet.

The natives lived in simple wooden huts that, again, could have just as easily been found on Remior, but the people themselves looked quite different from humans. They resembled apes covered in long fur with hues from all over the colour spectrum, though that wasn’t their most unique feature.

Each person had dozens of long and thin tails growing across their entire body. Percy counted four on each forearm, one flailing behind each elbow, and five on each upper arm. There were many more sticking out of their shoulders, legs, hips, knees, backs, chests, and even a couple growing out of the back of their heads like ponytails without the “pony”.

The apes didn’t wear any clothes, but they had their tails wrapped and interlocked around their bodies in various patterns, covering their privates and giving each person a unique style. Percy saw someone with yellow fur that had their tails looping loosely around their limbs and torso like hoops, as well as somebody else with deep blue fur that had their tails coiled very tightly around their body, to the point that they traced the outline of their muscles. There was a pink-haired child that had the two tails on their head coiled upwards in the shape of a pointy hat, and another that had them wrapped around their neck like a scarf.

Outside of what was visible on the surface – or even the usual things that Percy could see beneath the surface, like the natives’ silvery souls, colourful mana cores, and ink-coloured willpower – there was something else that gave him pause.

Around a quarter of the town’s residents had some sort of strange sludge swirling inside their bodies. It was pitch black like their willpower, but Percy could tell that he was perceiving it through a different facet of his ocular mutation. A facet that he’d never had a chance to use before this trip.

Even glancing at the mysterious substance evoked an unprecedented sense of revulsion in him. Unlike the other things he could perceive with his magical senses, this was something that he wanted nothing to do with. It was something vile, which he instinctively wished to eradicate at all costs, as well as something that he knew would also harm him, given the chance. It felt like his natural enemy, simply by virtue of him being alive.

And he knew exactly what it was. It was what he had come here for. Now… he just had to figure out how to resist and eliminate it, preferably without harming anyone or alerting the local deities to his presence.
