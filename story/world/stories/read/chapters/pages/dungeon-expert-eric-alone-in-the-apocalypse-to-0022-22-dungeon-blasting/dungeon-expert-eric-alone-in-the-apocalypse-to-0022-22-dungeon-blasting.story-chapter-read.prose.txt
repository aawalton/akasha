Eric woke up early and hopped out of bed. Grabbing his boots and coat out of his inventory, he snagged a sleeping Cy off the bedside table and hurried down the stairs and out the door, grabbing the extra food he’d ordered in the dinner slot the night before out of the commercial refrigerator. He settled Cy on his shoulder and rushed out the door, shoving the cold sandwich in his mouth as he hurried down the white stone path.

“What’s all the rush for?” Cy slurred, only half awake.

“I’m testing a hypothesis,” Eric told him.

Cy yawned big, showing off his bunny buck teeth. “Can’t we do that later?”

“Nope.”

“Mmm.” He closed his eyes and slumped against Eric’s shoulder, intent on going back to sleep.

Eric jogged down the path. Wisps of morning mist still clung to the trees and lingered in the undergrowth, but he could see clearly ahead and around him; it wasn’t the cloaking, clinging fog that hid everything, just a delicate, beautiful drapery of water vapor. He ignored the lava dungeon, since he was still very poorly equipped for it, and headed the other direction at a fast jog. The gnome dungeon was still closed off, and unlike the first dungeon he’d cleared, didn’t have any sort of countdown to recharge or reset. He didn’t know what that meant, but it was a question for later. He was testing a hypothesis right now, and to do that, he had to go fast.

Beyond the gnome dungeon, more dungeons loomed on his map. The indistinct blobs were based on what he saw from above, he guessed, and didn’t hold too much information even under the close examination he’d subjected them to last night. He’d set his eyes on a pair of blobs a short distance from the safe zone that might have entrances near one another. They were shaped right for it, and their borders were right next to one another, so it seemed reasonable, but he just didn’t know. Racing down the path, he glanced at the rising sun, then turned his head forward and kept running. Only so much time until noon. Only so much time to clear the dungeon.

He was just stuffing the last of the sandwich (ham, cheese, tomato, lettuce, the bread toasted so it was mostly just extra crispy and not soggy) in his mouth when he arrived at the dungeons. Dungeons plural, thank goodness; two dungeons opened before him, their mouths pointed at one another and their bodies curving off into the distance. One emanated a bone-deep chill despite the pleasant warmth of early fall, and the other beamed with heat that likewise did not match the atmosphere. The cold one—the left of the two—featured a sturdily-shut gate with a high wall and a door rimed with frost. The hot one, on the right, had a big, open gate with an arched crossbeam and a horseshoe nailed to the center. It reminded him of the entrance to a dude ranch, and looked a little like something out of an old western. When he looked around the gate rather than through it, the forest continued without any real indication of a dungeon, but when he looked through the gate, he found himself gazing into a dry, red wasteland, where stone outcroppings climbed into the sky and dust blew over the low, scrubby plants. It was like he was being teleported to another location, rather than simply entering a dungeon.

What happens if I walk behind the gate instead of through it? Eric left the path and entered the forest, arcing around behind the dungeon… or so he intended. He barely took a step before the fog began encroaching around him. At two steps, the path began to fade away behind him, and at three, he even lost sight of the gate. Something crunched in the not-so-distance, and a low growl emanated through the fog.

Nope, nope, nope. He backed up one step at a time, moving slowly and deliberately. With every step, the fog faded, until he stood on the path again, with no sign of the fog once more.

Eric wiped his brow, his heart racing. That was as clear a no as the Game had ever given him. It did not want him going back there.

He crossed his arms, looking from one to the other. Freezing cold or blazing hot? He was typically a hot guy, not a cold guy, but he was wearing a longsleeved jacket right now. It wasn’t the warmest jacket in the world, and he suspected it was well below what he needed for the cold dungeon, but it would be sweaty hell in the hot dungeon.

Let’s frame it another way. He had lots of clothes available to him back at the safe zone. If he needed to, he could run back, layer up, then run out to the cold dungeon. His clothes right now were pretty much as good as he could get for the hot dungeon, whereas he could greatly improve his gear before challenging the cold dungeon. On top of that, the hot side felt hot, but it didn’t feel hot hot. He’d been to Vegas in the summer, when walking outside felt like walking into an oven and taking a thirty-minute walk outside was legitimately a threat to his health. It felt significantly cooler than that kind of impossible heat.

The cold dungeon, on the other hand, was frozen. There was frost. Just being below freezing was a threat to his health; not if he was just going for a stroll, no, but what if the worst came to pass? What if he got injured and passed out, or was put to sleep by an enemy spell? In below-freezing, that alone was a threat to his life. Or what if he fell into, or had to take cover in, a body of water? Again, it immediately escalated to a threat to his life in below-freezing. And not only that, but it looked to be significantly worse than just “below freezing.” He could be in serious danger just walking around inadequately dressed in there.

If anything like that happened in the hot dungeon, he’d be uncomfortable, but perfectly fine. Maybe some minor heat exhaustion or dehydration, which yes, less than ideal, but he’d dealt with it before, and he could push through it again. In the freezing dungeon, he’d have to stave off numb hands and freezing toes the way he had on childhood ski trips and make sure he could feel all his extremities to ensure no frostbite was going on… meanwhile, in the hot dungeon, he’d have to sweat. He’d have to deal with mild discomfort and make sure he rested often enough and drank enough water to not get heat exhaustion.

It really was a no-brainer for him. He was starting with the hot dungeon.

He walked left and right, getting as much information on the interior of the dungeon as he could. Heat, dust, rock… it was a desert, or at least a dry rocky expanse not unlike Colorado’s flatter regions. A scorpion skittered by, and he smiled. The boots of bug damage are back in style! He shuffled out his bat, though he mentally planned to mostly use spells in this dungeon, if he could. His mana pool was big enough now it felt possible, for the first time ever. He paused for a moment, then added the snake-themed blade to his belt. Its description claimed it was a focus for magic. He didn’t know what that meant, and he’d kind of forgotten it until this very moment when he was thinking about weapons and using his magic in the dungeon, but it sounded like something he could use while spellcasting.

Drawing the blade, he turned it over in his hand. He hadn’t practiced with it, and he would’ve preferred to have tried it out before using it in anger, but sometimes life didn’t work out that way. Guess I’ll just have to try it on the fly. If it didn’t work out, he could always send it back to the inventory during a quiet moment. With a mental shrug, he sheathed it and stepped into the hot dungeon.

Heat instantly enveloped him. He stepped from a cool, damp dawn to a dry, hot midday. The sun beamed down from a cloudless sky, a brutal, heartless death beam.

“Hey, everyone,” he started, keeping his eyes open as he walked. Some scorpions skittered around, but nothing attacked him yet. “For those of you wondering, I picked this dungeon over the other one because…”

He went over his thought process again, but aloud this time, as he wandered further into the dungeon. Ahead, the rocky outcroppings made a kind of maze, and he headed that way out of hope that it was the right way to go. It wasn’t like the ravine from earlier; each rock stood on its own. Rock pillars pushed up out of the earth, some of them balancing smaller rocks atop them, while others lingered near a larger rock plateau, with rocks trapped between the plateau and the pillar. There was even a rock arch, which, naturally, framed the entrance to the stone maze.

As he walked, one or two of the scorpions skittered toward him, and he crushed them under his boots, putting that +5 damage to great use. He crushed five or six before, with a horrific screech, a scorpion the size of a small cat lunged from under a dog-sized stone. Eric backstepped as fast as his basketball drill history could muster, and thrust his palm at the creature. “Firebolt!”

A bolt of heat and flame smashed into the scorpion’s carapace… and bounced off. The scorpion kept charging, hissing in fury.

Okay, that didn’t work. How about… “Explosion!”

He aimed the blast at the scorpion’s eyes, hoping to blast its softer eyes and mouthparts with the spell. With a sharp crack that resounded off the stones all around him, the scorpion reared up, hissing in anger and pain. It flailed around, its eyes ruined and ichor leaking out of its broken carapace.

Explosion cost more than Firebolt, so he reverted back to using the cheaper spell as he aimed again. This time, the firebolt found the scorpion’s weakened facial carapace and seared into it, burning away its face and cooking its brains. It thrashed a few more times before it flopped to the ground, dead.

Eric drew his bat and approached carefully, giving the corpse a nudge. It didn’t respond, but it hadn’t dropped anything, either. “Hey, dungeon. Aren’t monsters supposed to drop things?”

Monster drops (deprecated): monster drops cluttered dungeons and detracted from the flow of combat, and thus were removed in the previous Game. Initialization accidentally loaded in an older model of the Game. This has been rectified, and as a result, monsters will no longer drop loot. However, you still receive the same quantity of loot, you simply receive it in a different format. Loot can be found in chests and, failing that, in a specially spawned catchall chest at the end of the dungeon.

“Huh.” He didn’t expect an answer, let alone one that professional. Am I under a different admin? Am I not talking to Joe right now?

Don’t get too excited, kid. The higher ups hand out preformatted responses all the time, and you better believe I set them on auto-proc. I can’t always take time out of my busy schedule to personally respond.

For a busy guy, he sure did seem to like chatting with Eric, but Eric wisely chose not to bring that up. Instead, he nodded and kept on going, approaching the stone arch.

“Let’s see what we have over here. Looks like a maze of some kind?” He craned his neck, looking up. It was unfortunate, but he didn’t have any sort of climb, fly, or traversal spell yet. Naturally, everyone’s favorite cheat was to climb onto the maze walls and just hop from wall to wall rather than walk the maze, but as happy as he was with his two offensive, one utility, and one healing spell, he had to admit that it was an obvious gap to lack traversal spells.

Future goals. He was restricted to whatever he could find. If the dungeon was kindhe’d find a traversal spell in one of its chests or rewards. If it wasn’t, he’d have to make do with what he had.

He glanced at the snoozing rabbit on his shoulder. That’s not quite true. Cy had learned magic the old-fashioned way and clearly had some experience teaching it. So far, Eric had focused on learning the basic building blocks of being a mage—aura manipulation and mana recovery—but Cy surely knew spells as well. No—I know he knows spells. Cy could even cast Swarm. Whether or not Cy could teach those spells was still up in the air, and whether or not Eric was competent enough to figure out a spell the traditional way was also unknown, but the point was that he had options. As long as he had Cy, he could always pester the rabbit into teaching him more magic.

If it’s one of those things that requires I put 10,000 hours into learning each spell, I’m probably not going to bother asking him for something like Climb, but if I can pick up spells the way I could pick up a maneuver in basketball or a new math equation back in the day, it’s worth looking into.

The entrance to the maze loomed before him. Eric took a deep breath to gird himself, then stepped inside.
