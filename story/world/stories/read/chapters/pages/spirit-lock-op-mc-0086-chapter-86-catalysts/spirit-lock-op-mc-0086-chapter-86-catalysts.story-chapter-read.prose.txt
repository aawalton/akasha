The bottom of the cavern had a garden of cora crystals jutting out of the ground, many four feet tall. In the center, there was a glorious spirit pond. The water was a catalyst—and a powerful one. It warped the air like a mirage—uninviting.

There was an island in the center of the pond with two podiums, one tall and one short. The tall had a second catalyst that was bending the atmosphere around it so aggressively that it seemed to be tearing rifts in space.

The second podium had an obsidian rock that left ominous trails around it.

Fringe chuckled insanely when he watched the catalyst bend the air. “No wonder he didn’t use a catalyst. That’s the craziest shit I’ve ever seen.”

“Is that even normal?” Kayline asked. “It’s spiraling like a… helix. No… what the hell is that?”

Normal catalysts moved clockwise, but this one was similar to a double helix if it wasn’t bound together. One pattern moved clockwise; the other moved counterclockwise at the same time. It was like two corkscrews that were offset so they never touched.

“The pond has a life thread catalyst,” Hayden explained calmly. “It has that clockwise, counterclockwise pattern you’re seeing, one deeper cut than the other. It allows both patterns to run concurrently, albeit dangerously. It has profound use cases. As for what that is”—he glanced at the podiums—”don’t even speculate. The RDA will worm your brain for information, and you don’t want knowledge that’ll harm you.”

“Sure, sure,” Lina said. “But do you know what it is? I’d just be satisfied knowing that.”

The team looked at him.

“No,” Hayden lied with his unbreakable pokerface. He was so consistent that no one questioned him. Lina just deflated, and Kayline folded her arms.

“Then are you still gonna take it?” Tibbi asked. “That thing looks like it’ll tear your body in half if you even touch it.”

Hayden nodded. “It’s strange that the founders presented such an unreasonable trial. But it has to be done.”

“Yeah, but… Wait!” Kalia panicked when Hayden approached the pond. “If you touch that, your channels are going to explode.”

“She’s right,” Photel said. “That’s moving counter… wait. You haven’t opened a gate…” She almost said counterclockwise, but realized that Hayden’s core wouldn’t be harmed by anything—considering he hadn’t done anything to it.

Hayden turned and smiled. “Correct.”

“Bullshit!” Fringe said. “You knew this was down here. Otherwise, you would’ve opened your core!”

“False.” Hayden pulled out a small box with his catalyst. “I brought one in case I didn’t find a better one. Now if you all wouldn’t mind…” He knelt and cupped water from the pond into his hands, drinking it as he panicked and waited for him to explode.

No such thing happened. Hayden simply walked to the center of the island and sat, closing his eyes to burn his core. The catalyst was remarkably pure, brilliant as the Seed of Purity, but with the added benefit of being able to create counterrotational cora. That would allow him to attack people’s channels with raw aura, as well as manipulate space and blood.

Perhaps he could even manipulate time.

Life thread catalysts were extremely rare, and their spirits were even scarcer.

Catalysts that could bend space and time to create spatial rifts were the rarest of all. He only knew one demigod with a spatial spirit powerful enough to create alternate dimensions—and they were exceptionally powerful. If Hayden could obtain his power as well as his spirits, perhaps he could face lesser gods.

That was what he was offered—and he had the tools for ultimate purity.

His dream was about to come true—and causality was too late to do anything about it.

It took ten minutes to perfectly burn his core with the catalyst, and when he finished, a massive blast of cora spread across the cavern, spreading upward toward the sky.

Kayline watched Hayden in a state of existential dread. Somewhere along the way, she had convinced herself that Hayden had opened his gate, or that even if he did, he’d just be a little more powerful—

She was wrong.

Hayden wasn’t using his suppression, so his body glowed as the air around him warped into clockwise and counterclockwise spirals. It bent the air, giving him a godly figure as he stood, staring up the shaft with a satisfied expression. He summoned Gluttony and said, “Drink. I’ll guide you.”

Gluttony drank, and Hayden lifted the little beast, holding him in his arms. Gluttony glowed as Hayden dimmed, and soon, the tiny antlers on his head began to grow, obtaining a third prong.

“Is he evolving again?” Lina cried.

“I think so…” Kayline said.

“Shhh!” Kalia shushed them. “Let him concentrate.”

The group watched in a state of spellbound stupefaction as Hayden guided his spirit beast into a proper core burning, something that Kayline didn’t know was possible. Or, rather, knew it was possible but was advised against, as it could cause serious problems, but there Hayden was, defying expectations once again. It was all so unbelievable. Then, she blinked, and she woke up, staring at Hayden as he walked to the catalysts.

“Before I do this…” He turned to Lina. “Can you help me burn my wind gate?”

Lina’s eyes lit up, and she smiled with teary eyes. “Yes!”

“What?” Tibbi asked.

“Just watch,” Kayline said, grinning as Hayden jumped over the pond and landed before them. He sat down, and Lina sat behind him, putting his hands on his back.

“On you,” he said.

A moment later, a beautiful wind pattern exited Lina’s hands as it entered Hayden’s body. Hayden took it and accepted it into another section of his core, burning the gate as his teammates broke down with speculation. “Be quiet!” Lina snapped. “He’s burning his core!”

The area fell silent for the next three minutes as Hayden skillfully burned his wind core. Once it was over, Fringe spoke.

“Did you just… are you… a catalyst?” he asked.

“Something like that,” Lina said, flashing him a cheesy smile.

Hayden smiled and turned to Kayline. “Spirit?”

“I got you!” Kayline rummaged through her pack.

“So that’s why they’re here…” Yaka muttered when Kayline handed the Wind Dance spirit to Hayden.

“Yeah…” Photel said. “Guess he didn’t come here defenseless afterall.”

“Not gonna lie,” Lent said. “I don’t even wanna see him use a real spirit now. It gives me the sweats.”

Kayline wasn’t afraid. She was excited as she watched him open the jar that held the dancing flower. He opened it, touched the pedal, and a golden orb entered his fingers. Even without a seeing spirit, she felt it moving to his core. The wind pressure told the story.

“And just like that, he’s an S-rank,” Photel said with a grim expression.

“Oh, relax siege,” Fringe said. “It’s just a C-rank spirit.”

“Yeah, but if it’s leads wieldin’ it,” Lent said.

Fringe frowned. “Point taken.”

“Thank you,” Hayden said, glancing between the sisters. “You did good.”

The sisters lit up in a bright display of happiness. Hayden’s praise meant more to them than anyone else in the world. He smiled at them and then turned to Yaka.

“Bring the others to the island.” He jumped over the pond and approached the warping catalyst, studying it as he waited for Yaka to fly them over. It was a gorgeous obsidian stone cut into a gem. It was on a pedestal. The sign had strange glyphs.

Hayden ran his fingers along the carved grooves, then stood beside the spirit relic. It was a gray rock, but it radiated a warping black energy. Hayden read the glyphs with a wonder-struck expression. “Empyrean Rift…”

“Wait…” Kayline said. She had arrived just in time to hear him. “Can you actually read that?”

Hayden smiled mysteriously, using that as his answer. “Listen. I’ve been in many tombs, and after examining this, I’m certain that beasts will attack after I touch this catalyst. Don’t attack back.”

“Excuse me… what?” Tibbi said.

“This pond is a fuse,” Hayden said. “It will destroy the core of any creature with a clockwise core—which I expect the beasts to be. They’ll die, slowly splashing and sucking up the water. Once the water is gone, or there’s enough bodies to survive the water, they’ll attack.”

“What kinda bullshit is that?” Fringe said.

“A fuse,” Hayden repeated. “If I don’t complete the catalyst and use the spirit by the end—I’m dead. That’s the trial.”

“Why are you so certain?” Kalia asked.

“Because I’ve made many such trials before,” Hayden said, clearly leaving out key details. He turned to Kayline and Lina. “Prioritize each other’s safety. Unless it directly hurts me, I want you to do anything and everything to protect each other. Do you understand?”

Kayline stared at her boots. “Yes…”

Lina bit her lip and nodded.

“Now prepare yourselves,” he added as he reached for the catalyst. “Let’s begin.”

Hayden picked up the catalyst—and it erupted with a sinister aura.

Tiny yet familiar spatial rifts then shot up one after the other, encircling them, and millions of insects poured out of them. In only a few seconds, the bugs carpeted the bright, glowing crystals, snuffing out their glow. The entire cavern became a living organism, a breathing mass of shifting bugs that made Kayline sick.
