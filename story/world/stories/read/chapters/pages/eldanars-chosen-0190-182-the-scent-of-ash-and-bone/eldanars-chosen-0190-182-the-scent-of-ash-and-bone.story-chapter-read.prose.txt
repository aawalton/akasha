The towering timber walls of Ashenfall had always represented a beacon of absolute safety on the untamed frontier. As the weary party approached the heavy, iron-reinforced gates, bathed in the dying orange light of the setting sun, that illusion of impenetrable security began to crack.

The entrance to the city was heavily bottlenecked. Two dozen city guards, clad in gleaming but battered chainmail, stood in a tight defensive formation across the main thoroughfare. Their spears were lowered, and their expressions were tight with exhaustion and deeply ingrained suspicion.

Josh stepped forward, raising his empty, gauntleted hands to show he meant no harm. The heavy steel plates of his armour clinked loudly in the quiet evening air.

"Halt right there," the lead guard barked, his voice rough. He levelled the tip of his spear at Josh’s chest. "State your business and your numbers. The city is currently operating under emergency protocols."

"We are registered adventurers," Josh replied smoothly, keeping his tone perfectly level and respectful. He jerked a thumb over his shoulder, gesturing to the massive, creaking line of wagons and exhausted people stretching out behind him. "And we are escorting a refugee caravan from the Kobold portal town. There are roughly a hundred civilians back there, along with several heavily laden merchant carts."

The guard lowered his spear a fraction of an inch, a deep, weary grimace pulling at his features. He looked past Josh, taking in the hollow-eyed children, the limping draft beasts, and the sheer volume of desperate humanity waiting on the muddy road.

"I hope there is room for them all," the guard muttered, rubbing a hand across his soot-stained face. "We have had folks pouring in from every single farming village in a fifty-mile radius over the past few days. The town is packed to the absolute rafters. Taverns, stables, alleyways. Everywhere is full. The whole world has basically fallen apart."

"Will you let them in?" Carcan asked softly, stepping up beside Josh. Her silver eyes were filled with quiet pleading.

The guard looked at the elven healer and let out a long, heavy sigh. He stepped back and waved his hand towards the open gates. "We won't turn anyone away. Not with what is going on out there in the dark. Get them inside, but tell them to expect to sleep on the cobblestones tonight. We are out of beds."

Josh turned back to Geth, who was sitting atop his massive, iron-laden wagon.

"This is where we part ways, Geth," Josh called up to the blacksmith. "The guards will direct you to a district. Keep your head down and look after your family."

Geth wrapped his thick, leather-clad hands around the reins. He looked down at the party, his expression thick with unspoken gratitude. "You kept us alive when we had no right to be. If any of you ever need a blade sharpened or a breastplate beaten out, you come find me. I owe you blood."

"Just buy us an ale when you get your forge running again," Bhel rumbled, offering the massive man a sharp, respectful nod.

With quiet farewells exchanged, the party broke away from the slow-moving caravan. They slipped past the guard checkpoint and walked under the deep, oppressive shadow of the gatehouse, stepping onto the familiar paved streets of Ashenfall.

The shock was immediate.

Ashenfall had always been a bustling, chaotic hub of commerce and adventure. Now, it looked like a disaster camp. The wide cobblestone streets were lined shoulder-to-shoulder with desperate, terrified people. Entire families were huddled under makeshift canvas tents strung between lampposts. The sick and the elderly sat on wooden crates, wrapped in dirty blankets, staring blankly at the stone walls.

The market square, usually a deafening riot of hawkers shouting their sales pitches and merchants haggling over monster parts, was entirely silent. The stalls were either boarded up completely or surrounded by long, desperate lines of people waiting for heavily rationed bread and clean water. The usual vibrant, aggressive energy of the city was missing, replaced by a thick, suffocating blanket of collective misery.

"Gods above," Brett whispered, his pale hands clutching the edges of his thick fur cloak. He looked around with clinical horror, completely ignoring a splash of muddy water that soiled the hem of his fine robes. "I have read historical accounts of wartime displacement, but seeing it in person is entirely different. The infrastructure simply cannot support this many people."

"Keep your coin purses hidden and your weapons visible," Perberos advised quietly, his silver eyes constantly scanning the shadows between the tents. The elven ranger moved with a fluid, predatory grace, naturally avoiding the grasping hands of desperate beggars. "Hunger makes honest people do terrible things."

They navigated the crowded, miserable streets with grim determination, pushing their way towards the affluent central district. Their destination was the Adventurers' Guild. If there were answers to be found regarding the continent-wide collapse, they would be found there.

They climbed the wide stone steps and pushed through the heavy oak doors of the guild hall.

The interior was a chaotic, deafening madhouse. The massive hall was packed shoulder-to-shoulder with filthy, bloodstained adventurers. The air was incredibly hot, thick with the smell of unwashed bodies, stale ale, and the sharp, coppery tang of old blood. Men and women were shouting over each other, demanding bounties, begging for healing potions, or simply arguing out of pure, terrified frustration.

Josh led his party through the throng, using his heavy, armoured bulk to physically part the sea of panicking fighters. They made their way to the long row of reception desks at the back of the hall.

Behind one of the desks, frantically stamping paperwork and arguing with a heavily scarred dwarf, was Mich. The blonde elven guild rep looked completely frazzled. Her usually neat hair was a messy nest, and ink stained her fingers.

As the angry dwarf stormed away, Mich looked up. Her bright eyes locked onto Josh and Brett.

Her jaw dropped slightly. For a brief second, the professional, administrative mask completely slipped. She let out a sudden, loud gasp of pure relief and practically threw herself over the top of the wooden desk, beaming brightly at them.

"You are alive!" Mich cried out, completely ignoring the massive queue of angry adventurers waiting behind the party. She leaned over the wood, her smile radiant. "Oh, thank the Light! I was absolutely certain you were dead!"

"It is lovely to see you too, Mich," Brett replied dryly, offering the clerk a polite, incredibly tired bow. "Though I am slightly offended by your lack of faith in our survival skills."

"It has nothing to do with your skills!" Mich said, her voice dropping to a frantic, guilty whisper. She looked around nervously. "I have been sick to my stomach for days. I was the one who recommended the Kobold portal town to you. When the reports came in that the dungeon had completely broken and a horde had overrun the settlement, I thought I had sent you to your deaths. I felt terribly guilty."

"You couldn't have known," Josh assured her gently, resting a heavy gauntlet on the desk. "Nobody could have predicted a stable portal tearing open like that. We held the line, and we escorted some of the survivors back here."

"What is actually going on, Mich?" Brett asked, leaning closer, his sharp features pinched with genuine concern. "We saw the streets outside. The guard at the gates said the whole world has gone mad."

Mich’s bright smile finally faded, replaced by a look of deep, profound exhaustion. She pulled a massive stack of official reports towards her, gesturing to the parchment.

"It is a catastrophe," Mich explained, her voice remarkably tight. "It wasn't just the Kobolds. Almost every portal surged simultaneously, spitting out hordes of beasts; some are now under control, but most are still bleeding through. Refugee columns have been flooding into Ashenfall. Small farming villages have been completely overrun by displaced native beasts. The outer defensive perimeters have entirely collapsed. The world has basically fallen apart in the span of a week."

She paused, taking a shaky breath, before forcing a bright, painfully fake smile back onto her face. It was the smile of an administrator trying desperately to maintain order in the apocalypse.

"But look on the bright side!" Mich chirped, trying to put a positive spin on the nightmare. "You boys are going to have your work cut out for you! The bounties on rogue beasts have tripled, and the city is paying premium gold for competent guards. You will be rich! But that is for later. You need to rest. I can process your bounties tomorrow when the queue is shorter."

"Thank you, Mich," Josh said softly, appreciating her attempt at optimism. "We will be back."

They stepped away from the desk, moving towards a relatively quiet corner of the massive, chaotic hall. The sheer volume of noise was overwhelming, a stark contrast to the eerie silence of the deep woods they had just travelled through.

As they stood by a structural pillar, taking stock of the room, Bhel suddenly grunted and pointed a thick, calloused finger towards a small table nearby.

Sitting alone at the table, nursing a tankard of ale with trembling hands, was a young human rogue they vaguely recognised from their very first training sessions in Ashenfall. His leather armour was torn to shreds, his face was covered in deep scratches, and his eyes were wide and unfocused.

Josh walked over and gently placed a hand on the young man's shoulder. "Toby? Is that you?"

The young rogue jumped violently, nearly spilling his ale. He looked up, his eyes darting around frantically before finally focusing on Josh.

"Josh," Toby whispered, his voice cracking. "You made it back."

"We did," Josh nodded, pulling up a stool and sitting down. Brett and the others gathered around the small table. "What happened to you, Toby? You look like you fought a bear."

"Goblins," Toby shuddered, taking a desperate gulp of his ale. The tankard rattled against his teeth. "We were doing a routine clear of that low-level goblin dungeon you found. It was supposed to be easy. We were just farming copper cores. We were about to go back in when the whole thing just went to hell."

Toby stared down at the sticky wooden table, his knuckles bone-white as he gripped his cup.

"They didn't just spawn," Toby continued, his voice dropping to a terrified whisper. "Hundreds of them. Maybe thousands. A literal green tide of blades and teeth. It was an impossible number for a low-level zone. They completely overwhelmed those outside the portal in seconds. I... I didn't even try to fight. I dropped my daggers and I ran. I just ran as fast as my class skills allowed. I didn't look back. I heard my party screaming, but I didn't stop until I saw the city walls."

Carcan closed her eyes, a look of profound sorrow crossing her beautiful features. She silently mouthed a quick prayer for Toby's fallen comrades.

"You survived," Bhel said gruffly, attempting to offer comfort in his own blunt way. "There is no shame in running from a broken dungeon, lad. A dead rogue tells no tales."

Toby didn't look comforted. He just took another trembling sip of his ale and stared blankly at the wall.

Leaving the traumatised rogue to his drink, the party continued their slow circuit of the guild hall. They listened to fragments of frantic conversations, piecing together the terrifying scale of the disaster. Every group had a similar story. Unprecedented monster surges, impossible numbers, and frantic, bloody retreats.

Sitting at a large, circular table in the centre of the room was a veteran adventuring party. Josh recognised them immediately. They were a famous, highly respected group of fighters in their third tier who regularly took on the most dangerous bounties in the region. Their leader, a massive, heavily scarred warrior named Vane, wore enchanted steel armour that usually gleamed like silver.

Now, Vane’s armour was completely ruined. It was dented, covered in dark, foul-smelling stains, and completely missing its left pauldron. The rest of his party looked equally destroyed. A powerful mage was resting her head on the table, weeping silently, while their scout was staring into a tankard of spirits with hollow, dead eyes.

They looked completely shellshocked.

Josh approached the table cautiously, his hand resting respectfully on his sword hilt. "Vane? It's Josh. Are you alright?"

Vane slowly turned his massive head. He looked at Josh for a long, uncomprehending moment before recognition finally sparked in his dark eyes. He let out a harsh, bitter laugh that sounded like breaking glass.

"Alright?" Vane mocked, his voice thick with a mixture of alcohol and profound grief. "We are alive, Josh. That is the best I can offer. We just got back from the Undead Crypts."

Brett stepped forward, his brow furrowing. "The Crypts? The undead dungeon that Bhel’s original party found? I thought it was nearly under control?"

"It was," Vane spat, slamming his heavy fist against the table. The wood groaned under the impact. "Until the surge hit. It was like the earth simply split open, and they marched out. It was an ocean of rotting flesh and bone. There were thousands of them. Skeletons, ghouls, heavily armoured wights. They just kept coming."

Vane grabbed a bottle of strong spirits from the centre of the table and took a long, ragged pull, ignoring the cup entirely.

"We fought," Vane continued, his voice trembling slightly. "Gods, we fought for hours. But there was nothing we could do. We were pushed back, and back, and back. Our mana was gone. They were going to drag us down into the dark."

Carcan stepped closer to the table, her hands clasped tightly together. "How did you escape?"

Vane looked at the elven healer, and a single, silent tear carved a clean path through the grime on his scarred cheek.

"Kal Gledatsk," Vane whispered, his voice breaking completely on the name.

Carcan gasped softly, her hand flying to her mouth. Josh and Brett exchanged a quick, horrified look. Kal Gledatsk was a highly respected human cleric. He was the primary healer trainer for the Adventurers' Guild in Ashenfall. He was the man who had taught Carcan the advanced triage techniques she had used to save lives just yesterday.

"When the horde broke out, he intercepted them. He told us to run. He stood in the middle of the narrowest corridor and unleashed a wall of pure, blinding radiant magic. It was the most beautiful, terrifying thing I have ever seen. He held the line by himself, burning his own lifeforce to fuel the spells, allowing us, the novices, and the veterans to reach the surface."

Vane took another desperate drink from the bottle, wiping his mouth with the back of his dirty gauntlet.

"We watched from the upper ridge," Vane said, his voice entirely hollow. "The undead tide finally broke through his light. He fought them with his mace until the very end, but there were just too many. He was cut down. They dragged him into the horde."

Carcan turned away, her shoulders shaking with silent, devastating sobs. Perberos stepped up behind his twin sister, placing a gentle, incredibly rare hand of comfort on her shoulder.

Josh felt a cold, hard knot of pure dread settle deep in his stomach. Kal had been a fixture of the guild. He was a mentor, a protector, and a genuinely good man. To hear that he had been slaughtered like an animal was a heavy, sickening blow.

"I am so sorry, Vane," Josh said quietly, finding the words entirely inadequate.

"Save your apologies," Vane muttered, looking up at Josh with eyes that promised violence and death. "The northern forest is completely covered with them now. The trees are rotting. The ground is turning black. They are gathering, Josh. The horde isn't just wandering aimlessly. They are massing."

Vane leaned heavily across the table, his breath reeking of cheap spirits and fear.

"Kal bought us time," Vane warned, his voice dropping to a chilling, prophetic whisper. "But they are coming. With the numbers they have out there in the dark, it wouldn't surprise me if an undead legion attacks these very walls before the week is out."
