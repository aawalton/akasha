“We’re close,” the scout with the tracking instrument murmurs. I hum in acknowledgement, barely able to spare even that amount of effort. Bastet is bearing the brunt of the strain, but I’m only just holding on as it is.

The larnatis dropped his own domain a while ago, and is following us with exhaustion written in his body language that’s only eased a little in the time since he stopped helping. There’s shame there too, at being unable to keep up with Bastet. I can’t spare much sympathy for him – his failure means the pressure on Bastet and me is greater than ever.

At least I released so many of the Tier three Bonds earlier – every one of those that I have to hold forcibly means just a little less Willpower that I can dedicate to supporting Bastet’s domain from the inside, and it’s challenging even with Willpower Share aiding.

Riding on Bastet’s back is also helpful due to the proximity that aids my efforts considerably, and not having to focus on avoiding the treacherous carpet of bones underfoot.

Still, it’s been well worth the struggle – not a single trap has activated the way it was designed. With the layer of slime scraped away from the tunnel around us, we have been able to see every hole, every pit long before we get to it. Ambushes have still happened, of course – and in increasing frequency the closer we get to the nexus of the Tier four signature – but without the slime deadening our senses, none have taken us by surprise. Even better, without the parasite-infested slime covering all of us, we didn’t have to worry so much about every scratch from the ensuing fights.

But I’m all-too aware that everything so far has just been getting to the Tier four. I feel a knot in my stomach as I consider what the controller might have in store for us as final defences.

I know when we reach the inner sanctum even before the scout’s quiet announcement. The domain pressing against Bastet suddenly doubles in strength and I feel her come to a halt under me, trembling with the effort just to hold on.

“My lord?” I hear distantly, but pay no heed. My whole focus is on my companion.

Release it, I tell her. But control the release. I’m worried that if she just allows the domain to collapse, it’ll end up hurting her.

Bastet is too exhausted to respond verbally, but I feel her agreement a moment before she starts withdrawing her domain. It isn’t as controlled as I would like – it deflates in fits and starts that show how long she’s been fighting, and how few reserves she still has to dedicate to the effort. I do what I can to help, but I’m not much better – I feel the strain somewhere deep inside that I know I won’t be able to touch even with Flesh-Shaping.

Finally, her domain has reduced to sitting just under the top layer of her skin. There, it is far easier to hold, protected from the Tier four’s influence by her flesh and the mana suffusing it.

Since she’s stable, I gently pull away my support. Opening my eyes, I resist the urge to groan – even Aingeal’s dim light is enough to send shards of glass through my eyes and into my throbbing brain. I ache – everywhere.

But this is no time to rest. Stroking Bastet’s feathers, I slide off her back, my feet splashing into the slime that returned with the deflation of the protective domain. My companion is looking as bad as I feel – her head and wings hanging limply. The larnatis male moves closer to her, nuzzling at her neck and sending me a fierce look.

You pushed her too far, he accuses me, his wings mantling protectively. I wonder cynically if he’s trying to gain points with her after proving his weakness earlier

Before I can answer, Bastet growls and then snaps at the male, making him shift backwards, his body language changing from protective to wounded.

You are not my mate, she tells him firmly. And he is my pack. Back off.

The male doesn’t argue, taking another step back, but I can tell from the resentful glint in his eyes as he glances at me that he wants to say ‘not yet’. I resist my desire to stick out my tongue or something equally childish – if he was trying to win appreciation from Bastet, it backfired.

With every second that passes, my headache subsides little by little. The strain I feel to my Willpower remains – that will take longer to heal. I think that even pulling this Tier four into a Battle of Wills is off the table for now.

“Markus?” Valence asks. I blink and see that everyone is looking at me. “Must we turn back?”

I frown in surprise at the question. Turn back? Why…? Then I realise – he must have taken the deflation of the domain as a sign that things were getting too much for us. Which they were – but not the extent where I’ll call for a retreat. Not when we’re this close.

“No,” I tell everyone firmly, and see the release of tension in some shoulders – and the building of it in others. “But we cannot keep the tunnel clear of slime any longer.”

Layton nods.

“We will be careful,” he agrees. “I believe it’s not far now.”

“I’ll check,” I tell him, then close my eyes and gather my earth-magic again. Pressing it through the barrier of slime is even harder this time than the previous times – whether because of my fatigue or the proximity to the Tier four, I can’t be sure.

“It’s here,” I confirm, opening my eyes and glaring at a junction ahead. “To the left, there’s a bottleneck, and then a large chamber. I can feel it inside.”

“Not an ideal entry point,” Layton muses, “but at least we have confirmation the Tier four is present.”

I nod – it was a concern that the Tier four might try to flee – or might bait us into an area where it wasn’t at all. But as far as I can tell, what sits waiting for us in the chamber is the real deal.

“And it’s not alone – there is one being present with it in the room, and multiple waiting in the walls around it,” I warn.

“How do you wish us to proceed, sir?” Maxen asks briskly. “The first to enter will be in significant danger due to the bottleneck.”

I consider for a moment, then a grim smile plays on my face.

“Like this,” I start, then proceed to describe my plan.

Fortunately, the Tier four seems willing to wait for us to come to it – it hasn’t changed position for the whole time we’ve been travelling down here.

The Water Mage steps next to me and directs a jet of water at the slime below my feet, chasing away the slime in a small area, allowing me to make contact with the earth. Hurrying as quickly as I can, I pour mana into the earth, directing it towards where the tunnel narrows to allow a single, short person passage.

Though the Tier four’s domain protects its slime, that doesn’t extend into the earth – in less than a minute, the tunnel has widened to accept four people abreast, and now reveals the entrance to the side chamber within which our target sits.

As soon as the tunnel has widened enough, the archers and other mages advance forwards and work to chase out the hidden creatures beyond where I made my changes – those in pockets of earth that I was moving have already been crushed. They’re a good team – the Air Mage is able to track where they are by the movement of air as they breathe in and out; the other mages and archers follow his direction to strike at the beasts themselves.

Meanwhile Lathani briefly dips into and out of the chamber, using the shadows cast by Aingeal to full effect.

When I receive the image of the inside of the chamber, a quiet sound of surprise slips past my lips.

“What is it?” Layton asks sharply. Valence turns his head so his ear is facing me even as his eyes keep careful watch on the progress of the vanguard.

“There’s a human in there,” I tell them, frowning.

Valence blinks.

“A human is the Tier four?” he asks incredulously.

“No, no,” I hurry to clarify. “The Tier four is there too.” And looks like a massive version of the parasites I pulled out of my people. “But there’s a human standing in front of it.”

Valence and Layton exchange worried looks. I agree. There are three scenarios that seem most likely – the human is a captured member of our settlement, a captured member of another settlement, or the assumption that there are no humans on the Lost Continent is false.

All of which have serious implications. Honestly, I’d almost prefer it to be one of our settlement – at least that only means I’ve utterly failed one of my people rather than indicating that the parasites are far wider-spread than we thought, or that there are factions other than the colonists that we’ll have to deal with.

“My lord, the beasts around the chamber are dealt with,” one of the scouts reports. “Do you wish us to advance into the chamber?”

I swallow. This is it.

“No. I’m coming.”

With more confidence than I’m feeling, I stride forwards, Valence and Layton at my sides, Bastet, Lathani, Ninja, and my other companions following, prepared for battle.

The Tier four has to know we’re coming so I don’t try to conceal our presence in any way as we step through the tunnel and then into the chamber.

The inside is much as I saw in Lathani’s memory – a chamber so thickly-coated in red slime that it looks like the inside of some bodily organ. The way it’s pulsing as parasites spread out from the massive opaque red lump sitting in the middle of the floor makes it feel like some birthing chamber.

And standing before the squashed sphere is a vaguely-familiar figure. Lathani’s memory showed a blurry face – my beast companions are far less tuned into facial features than I am and their memory reflects that – but now I can see it in reality, I realise I recognise it.

His expression is utterly blank, and he’s not blinking. Were he not standing and breathing, I might think he was dead. I step forward uneasily.

“Thiakar,” Valence breathes next to me, regret clear in voice.

You know him? I ask through our Alliance Bond.

One of my hunters. He was taken by another beast during one of the early hunts – we thought he’d died.

You didn’t find the body?

His party looked, but they were attacked shortly after. They deemed it more important to preserve the living.

Understandable, I sigh. Guilt claws at my belly, but I’m quietly grateful that this is the scenario that occurred. Actually, if we can resolve this without catching Thiakar in the crossfire, we might even be able to save him.

“Heir Fell,” a voice says in a flat tone without a trace of emotion.

It takes us a moment to realise exactly who spoke.

“Thiakar?” Valence asks uncertainly. The figure standing before us doesn’t react to his name.

“Do not kill me. I can be of use to you.”

The words are said with the same complete lack of feeling as everything prior.

“Of course we’re not going to kill you, Thiakar,” Valence replies with a small incredulous laugh, stepping forwards.

I hold up a hand. Valence, seeing the movement in his peripheral vision glances at me.

“Well, we’re not…are we, Lord Markus?” he continues, ending without the confidence that he’d started with.

“That’s not Thiakar,” I tell him levelly, not taking my eyes off the figure – or the parasite controller behind him.

“What do you mean?” Valence asks with bewilderment.

“Markus, you don’t think…?” Layton asks in tones of dawning – and horrified – understanding.

I nod grimly.

“The Tier four is speaking through Thiakar.”
