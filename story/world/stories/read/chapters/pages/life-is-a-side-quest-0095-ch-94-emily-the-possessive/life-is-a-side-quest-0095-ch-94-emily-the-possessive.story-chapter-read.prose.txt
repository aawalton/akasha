Emily sat in her room staring into space in a daze. Flick was laying in her lap trying to nap but was having difficulty as Emily was subconsciously tapping her foot. She was startled out of her stupor as Janelle opened the door and greeted her.

“Hey, Emily. I’m home! Howdy, Flick!” she said, happy to see the dragon. “I got something for you!” She pulled out a large bone made for a full-size dog and unwrapped it. Flick jumped down and spun in a circle in excitement. Janelle held it out for him, and he rushed up, smelling it before slowly putting his teeth around it and pulling it from her fingers.

He licked it and paused to consider whether the bone suited his refined palate or not. He took a little nibble and decided it would do as a treat, then dragged the bone to the couch and tried to get it up onto it. After struggling for a second, he managed. He pulled it over, laid down on Emily’s lap, and began chewing on the corner.

“Hey! How long have you been moping on the couch?” Janelle asked.

“Not long…”

Squeak! Chip.

“Flick is tattling on you. I’m pretty sure that meant you’ve longed for your long-lost love all day, writing poems of your heartache at being separated from him. Pining for the day he returns to sweep you away into a life of love and adventure.”

Flick snorted as it attacked the bone.

“Shut up, you don’t understand him any more than I do,” Emily grinned at her.

“No, but I think he’s expressive enough to tell me you’ve been moping.”

“I’m just thinking, okay?”

“Oh? What lofty thoughts have you pondered today?” Janelle asked as she put her bags down on the table.

“Um… nothing important.”

Janelle pulled a chair out and sat in front of her sister, staring her in the face, letting her know she wasn’t letting this conversation go.

“I can’t concentrate,” Emily relented.

“Why?”

Emily sighed and looked down at Flick.

“It’s Alexander.”

“Obviously.”

“Since… I kissed him, I feel like I can’t… feel settled with him gone.”

“Ha! The mighty Emily, the strong-willed woman, is completely smitten!” Janelle smiled.

“It’s a bit more than that… I feel like I have an unhealthy attachment to him, logically speaking. I almost feel dependent!”

“Sounds like a girl in love to me,” Janelle said. “I know you thought you were immune to such fancies, but that’s pretty normal, I’ll have you know.”

“Yes, watching you grow up, I am aware how addicted to a boy a flighty girl can get,” Emily poked.

“Well, then take it from me—it’s not a bad thing.”

“I know, but… Alexander is different,” Emily sighed.

“Obviously! He’s a weirdo, in a good way I’ll grant you, but he might be a real catch. You know, with him obviously being one of the ability users. He is terrible at hiding that, from what you’ve shared. He’s a manager of an orc horde, for goodness’ sake.”

“When he is around, I feel secure. When he is gone, I feel vulnerable. When I leave the apartment, I feel even worse—like I’m a ghost bound to this apartment somehow.”

“Have you thought about talking to a therapist?” Janelle asked, raising an eyebrow.

Emily looked at Janelle with a put-upon face. “No. I’m not that bad.”

“Yet… just make sure you go for a walk. That normally puts you in a better mood.”

Emily was quiet for a minute. “Fine… Flick, do you want to go for a walk?”

Flick grunted and got off Emily’s lap to let her up.

“I’ll make dinner tonight. You just go get some air,” Janelle said, giving her sister a quick hug.

Emily went to the walking path behind the apartments. There was an undeveloped piece of land with a creek running through it. As she walked, Flick ran up trees and darted from shadow to shadow. Seeing a squirrel, he stalked behind it and tagged it. The squirrel turned and started chattering. Flick ran around chasing it, tagging it again just to annoy it.

Emily would stop occasionally and look at the plants. She would touch them and subconsciously categorize them. She decided to come back and dig one up to put in a planter for her balcony.

Halfway through the walk, she was startled when she saw an overly large figure looking at a vine growing on a tree. She relaxed a bit as she recognized Bru-haha. She was still uncomfortable around the orc, but decided not to be rude and walked up to greet him.

“Hello, temporary neighbor.”

Bru-haha turned to regard her.

“Ah, Emily. Good evening,” he said with a slight bow.

“How are things at Congo?”

“Everything is running surprisingly smoothly, I’m happy to tell you,” Bru-haha smiled.

“Are you going for a walk?”

“Hmm, you could say that. I was just looking at this odd vine.”

“Oh, are you into botany?”

“It is a recently acquired hobby. I am learning.”

Emily walked up and took a look at the vine. “I don’t recognize it… It looks invasive.”

“Really?”

“Yes, if it continues growing, I think it will eventually be harmful to this tree.”

“Interesting. So you wouldn’t be upset if I pruned it?”

“Um… I don’t think so.”

Bru-haha shrugged off his backpack and pulled out a pair of clippers. As he went to the base of the vine, he made one quick cut that likely would have taken her some time to get through.

Her heart skipped a beat as she felt the vine’s energy sever from the ground. A slight dizzy spell hit her and she put her hand on her forehead to steady herself.

Bru-haha ripped the vine off the tree before wrapping it into a loop. Opening his backpack, Emily saw a variety of plants she didn’t recognize.

“What are those!?” she asked.

“I’m not entirely sure. All samples I found after looking in various areas.”

Emily reached down and touched a few of them. “They all seem… medicinal. Where did you find this?” she said, pulling out a large piece of bark.

“Don’t tell anyone… but I went for a walk outside the gates of the botanical garden. I figured nobody would be offended if some non-native plants that had left the boundaries of the garden were pruned, and I found an odd dead tree.”

“It… feels strong.”

“Oh? That’s an odd description for dead tree bark.”

Emily shivered and put it back in the backpack.

“Any word from Alexander?” she asked, changing the subject.

“Unfortunately not. The expedition is taking longer than expected, but I’m sure he’s fine.”

“Expedition?” she asked.

“I’m not really supposed to say. My apologies.”

Emily’s heart began to race. Irritation built into a rage that boiled over. She felt a pulse of energy as she reached out and grabbed the orc’s arm.

“TELL ME.”

Bru-haha gasped. “A girl from the local high school went missing during a play. They found a portal to another plane and went looking for her and to assess the danger the portal posed.”

Emily slumped, feeling a slight panic at what she had just felt.

Bru-haha caught his breath and looked at Emily. His monocle glowed without her noticing.

“I’m sorry, Bru-haha. I don’t know what’s wrong with me. That was rude.”

“Not to worry, Miss. Most she-orc would have just stabbed me with a knife if they wanted to know something,” he chuckled.

“Say, I have a few more plants at Alexander’s apartment. Would you like to come look at them and tell me your thoughts?”

Emily attempted to regain her composure before processing what Bru-haha had asked.

“Sure, I would be happy to.”
