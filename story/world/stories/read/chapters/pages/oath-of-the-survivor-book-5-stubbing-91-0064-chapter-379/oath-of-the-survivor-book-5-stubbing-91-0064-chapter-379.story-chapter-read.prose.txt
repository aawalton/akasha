After all of the deliberations he’d completed, Kyle’s mind was ready for something a little less intensive.  So, he turned his attention to the skill upgrade available to his World Tree Druid class.  The Aspect of the Tree skill he unlocked already had clearly-defined additions, and the decision between Root and Bark was one he’d made quite a while ago.  Even though Bark would give a solid boost to his resilience, he was much more interested in the absorption abilities provided by Aspect of the Tree: Root.  Bark would have to wait until he reached high-tier C Grade and the next class evolution for the World Tree Druid.  Kyle was excited to see how his upgraded Aspect of the Tree would interact with Devourer’s Domain, but turned his focus toward this class’s first D Grade skill selection.

As usual the three indistinct gray orbs appeared before him, and he focused his attention on the first.

World Tree’s Blessing

The impression offered by the skill was both the shortest yet most complete of any skill selection he’d seen so far.  It was a passive skill that provided a meaningful, permanent boost to any attribute of his choice.  He didn’t have an exact number, but he got the sense it was a flat increase of between ten and fifteen levels’ worth of free investment from the class.  It felt a little underwhelming, until Kyle considered the impact it could have on something like his Strength attribute.  This would be an instant ten percent increase in his base attribute, which would only be further amped up by his Devourer’s Anatomy and Genesis Engine.

The obvious next questions that this skill choice raised was what would happen with its future upgrades?  Could he keep adding to his attributes with each grade?  And if so, how meaningful could those boosts get?  The potential that it possessed was intriguing, and despite not being the most unique skill he’d been offered it bore serious consideration.  And it only made him more excited for the next two offerings.

Nature’s Bounty

The impression he got this time was much more abstract.  The clearest part was a simple increase in yield for anything grown using the World Tree Seed, but as he focused on it, the true aspect of the skill became more evident.  The how behind the increased yield seemed to be connected to an increase in mana conductivity for anything developed with the World Tree Seed.  He got the sense that these improvements would have an impact on the total number of chaddlings he could keep in storage.  The only downside to the skill was that it didn’t appear to have an impact on the World Tree Seed itself; only on creations grown directly within the seed’s space.

Kyle knew that this was the skill C.H.A.D.D. would want him to choose.  It would add a tremendous amount of utility to their projects, not to mention the potential improvements in his combat abilities.  And with the upcoming work he’d need to do on Kertan Six, Nature’s Bounty would prove its value quickly.  It was a cut above World Tree’s Blessing in Kyle’s mind, but he tried to keep a clear mind as he turned his attention to the last option available to him.

Wither

The impression here was a simple one, but it made Kyle’s breath catch.  Wither was a mana blast with an area of effect, that would directly attack an opponent’s vital energy and reduce the effectiveness of their physical attributes for a while; namely Vitality, Endurance, Strength, and Dexterity.  If that were all, it would be a decent offensive supplement, but not terribly noteworthy for Kyle.  However, it had two components that got Kyle excited.

The first was that it was a Willpower attack, like Devourer’s Domain.  The effectiveness of Wither would depend on the level of Kyle’s Willpower compared to his opponent’s, and Kyle had a lot of Willpower.  Secondly, the skill could be activated like Repel; meaning that he could project it from any part of his body.  Or project it from anywhere he had direct control of the mana.  With Devourer’s Domain and Aura Force, he was confident in being able to use Wither within his domains, albeit with somewhat reduced effectiveness.

Kyle wasn’t a physical powerhouse by any means.  Genesis Engine and Reflexive Evasion helped him keep up with most of his opponents, but as they’d just experienced with Mangalica, that wasn’t a foregone conclusion.  Rather than needing to rise to their level, Wither would lower that threshold by weakening his opponents.  It would be less effective against creatures like the most recent hodag he faced, though still an effective means to help even the odds.  And while limited against other magic-focused combatants, even small reductions in their physical power would make his more traditional attacks much more dangerous.

The decision between Nature’s Bounty and Wither wasn’t going to be an easy one for Kyle, as both had their merits.  Like Phaegos, Kyle really just wanted to say “yes” to both.  The question came down to whether or not he valued the broader utility over a more specialized combat upgrade.  There’s been a lot of general utility lately, Kyle thought, considering some of the other recent decisions.  Aura Force, Devourer’s Domain, and Aspect of the Tree: Branch were just a few examples of that.

He didn’t regret any of those decisions, but he also had to reckon with the fact that his life hadn’t been a quiet one.  Even now, he knew there were battles on the horizon, and more would naturally follow.  Kyle had enemies.  Enemies who wanted him dead.  And that meant that he needed to be powerful enough to actually do something about it when they came after him.  There was a chance he could find some similar effects to Nature’s Bounty as he got to upgrade Cultivate, Infuse, and Graft.  Replacing a rare offensive option was a different matter.  With a slow exhale, he chose Wither.

Finally, he turned his attention to the upgrade available for the Unbroken Survivor Class.  His original intent had been to select Adaptive Regeneration, but his recent fights made it clear to him that Storm Shelter was the next skill that needed it.  Kyle’s healing was still keeping pace, largely due to the compounding effects of Tenacious Vigor stockpiling rejuvenating power inside his cells, and Healing Impetus priming his body to keep recovering.  The effects were beginning to slow down as his body got increasingly robust, and something he’d still need to address.  However, the ability to block big hits from getting through couldn’t be put off any longer.

Looking at the three upgrade options, he got started.

BLAST BUNKER

Kyle saw himself standing in a field, which was promptly consumed by a massive explosion of fire and lightning from who-knows-where.  The ground was sundered, the sky burned, and Kyle instinctively knew that the attack was likely from a B Grade.  An area the size of a city was wiped out in a single blow, save for the space directly surrounding Kyle.  He was surrounded by a shining golden barrier, inlaid with faint wisps of silver that seemed to flow across the shield’s surface.  It was a little larger than the current Storm Shelter, and Kyle got the sense that it was an order of magnitude more durable than even a Heart of Creation-empowered version of his current skill.  The concepts of Immutability and Timelessness were both present within the skill’s structure, alongside concepts he didn’t quite understand.

As upgrades went, it was straightforward.  A skill that worked the same way as before, just better at doing everything it did.

The bar set high, Kyle moved to the second option.

MOBILE SHELTER

This time, rather than the larger skill, Kyle saw himself wrapped in layers of phantasmal golden armor as he moved across a battlefield to retrieve faceless wounded allies.  Attacks rained down on him, all simply ignored by the weightless armor as he made it to his allies and withdrew, sheltering them with his armored form as he completed triage.

The barrier itself didn’t seem to be much stronger than the current Storm Shelter, though all of the defense was focused directly around his body, slightly improving its defenses by virtue of being more highly concentrated.  It also seemed to cover every vital point, including his eyes, nose, and mouth.  He got the sense that he could still breathe in the armor, and that it would still block out deadly toxins from the air around him.  With the added benefit of not impacting his mobility in the slightest, it was a solid step up from his current ability.

Keeping his mind open, Kyle turned to the last option.

MODULAR FORTRESS

Fortress.  That was a word Kyle liked.  The vision this time was Kyle on a battlefield, moving massive hexagonal plates of force to block attacks and create triage stations all around him.  Each plate was nearly three meters across, and they layered and shifted to create defenses across a wide area.  At first, he used them simply as fortifications.  Then he lifted the barriers to block a rain of attacks from above.  Finally, he layered them in front of each other to stop a single, powerful blast that threatened to rip through all of Kyle’s forces.

The defensive power of each individual plate was just slightly more robust than Storm Shelter was now, and the ability to stack them up one atop another meant that the defensive potential was unmatched if he had time to layer them prior to a big attack.  The battlefield control it offered was also unparalleled, potentially allowing him to isolate enemies or shield allies.

With all the options considered, it was time to make a decision.  First, he dismissed Mobile Shelter.  There was a time when he’d have jumped at the ability to move around with Storm Shelter surrounding him, though now it wasn’t something he necessarily needed. It was a great skill, but Kyle was looking to improve his defenses enough to handle attacks from outside his weight class.  Mobile Shelter wouldn’t be a meaningful enough improvement in that regard, especially when compared to Blast Bunker and Modular Fortress.

That left him with two choices that did solve his problem, albeit in different ways.  Blast Bunker was just a more powerful version of his current skill.  It activated instantly, had a modest cooldown period between uses, and would continue to scale in power along with Kyle’s Willpower.  If Storm Shelter was anomalous among D Grade skills due to its power, Kyle hoped that Blast Bunker would be the same for C Grades.

Modular Fortress, by contrast, was a skill that focused on creating flexibility and planning.  He could control an entire battlefield using it, and block even the most potent attacks if he could position enough of the barriers in the right places.  With the right planning and positioning, he could even use it in conjunction with his domains to make him even more difficult to handle.

Strangely, that was also a drawback to Kyle.  The biggest limitation to Modular Fortress was his own ability to manage the battlefield and put all the pieces in the right place.  Between Devourer’s Domain, Aura Force, and managing prosthetics and other items from the World Tree Seed, he already had quite a bit on his plate.  His Perception and Intelligence gave him a strong enough foundation that he could add this layer, but it certainly wasn’t foolproof.  Against a tactically-minded opponent, his barriers could be caught out of position, leaving Kyle exposed.

That wouldn’t be an issue with Blast Bunker.  He’d already seen how Instinctive Intelligence helped him activate Storm Shelter with incredible speed.  There was no reason to think that Blast Bunker would be any different.  He wouldn’t need to rely on fancy positioning to keep the skill relevant; it would simply be an ace he could keep in his back pocket to neutralize his opponents’ biggest moves.

Kyle only felt more confident as he thought it through, and he made the final decision of his mid-tier C Grade evolution, selecting Blast Bunker.  He took a final admiring look at his Center, taking in the ever-expanding view.  High-tier C Grade will be here before we know it, he mused, finally exiting meditation.

“Did anything exciting happen while I was meditating?” Kyle asked, standing and stretching.  His connection to the World Tree Seed felt more aligned than ever before, and he could almost feel the wood moving with each breath as he spoke.

[NOTHING URGENT, DR. MAYHEW.  A MESSAGE CAME FROM THE LEATHERWORKERS, APPARENTLY THEY FOUND SOMETHING ODD WITHIN THE HODAG THAT THEY WANTED YOU TO LOOK AT.  OTHERWISE, IT WILL LIKELY BE SEVERAL DAYS YET BEFORE WE HEAR BACK FROM DUROC’S TEAM.  I TAKE IT YOUR EVOLUTION WAS A SUCCESSFUL ONE?]

“It was,” Kyle confirmed.  “Once we go check things out with the leatherworkers, we can do a little more testing.”
