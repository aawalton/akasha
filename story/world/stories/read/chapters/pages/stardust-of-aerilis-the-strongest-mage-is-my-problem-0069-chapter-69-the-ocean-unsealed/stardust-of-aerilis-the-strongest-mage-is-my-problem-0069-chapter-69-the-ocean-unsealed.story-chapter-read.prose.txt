The Vulpine airship descended. Its turbines humming a low, mournful chord as it touched down.

The island was little more than a jagged spine of rock. There were no white marble ruins here, no tropical resorts—just a wide, wind-swept clearing of grey dirt and a few stunted, salt-crusted trees clinging to the cliffs.

Pelka was the first off the ramp, her boots hitting the grit with a crunch. She didn't look at the scenery. Her sheep ears were twitching with a frantic, rhythmic intensity as she began hauling the heavy, obsidian-cased artifacts from the cargo hold. Vane assisted her, his massive strength making the deployment of the suppressive towers look effortless.

Aiven stepped out next, the cold wind tugging at his shirt. Beside him, Virelle drifted in a heavy, unusual silence. She wasn't commenting on the lack of aesthetics or the peasant-tier vegetation. Her eyes were fixed on the horizon.

"What’s the matter, Virelle?" Aiven said, stepping into her line of sight. "You’ve been restless since you told Pelka off at the ship."

Virelle stopped her drifting. She looked at him.

“It is not that I lack faith in you, Master," she whispered. "But there is a weight in my core that I cannot shake.”

Aiven looked at his matte obsidian arm, the cyan etchings glowing faintly in the dim light. "I know. There are still so many things that are uncertain. I don't know where this power comes from. I don't know why it was sealed in the first place. And Lucien..." Aiven’s brow furrowed. "The fact that he simply let us walk away with the Loom-Breaker... it feels like he’s inviting us to do exactly what we’re doing. It’s a questionable act for a man who claims to want to get rid of threats."

Virelle’s fingers twitched, her prismatic orb humming a low, anxious note. She looked at the obsidian artifacts Pelka was calibrating, then back at Aiven.

"Master," she said, her voice dropping into a desperate, fragile register. "Do you truly wish to do this? Even now, I can take you. We can leave this rock, leave the Fox, and flee to a place where neither the government nor Lucien can find us. I can protect you from whatever threats are out there, just as I am."

Aiven looked at her properly. He saw the sincerity in her eyes—the willingness to turn her back on the world just to keep him in a safe, quiet cage. He thought of his life as a clerk, of the sidelines he had occupied for years, watching the world move from behind a desk.

"I know you can, Virelle," Aiven said, his voice gaining a rare, steady strength. "I know you would do anything required to protect me. But we’ve been through this before. I’ve had enough of being the one who needs to be hidden away."

He stepped closer, his obsidian fingers brushing against the fabric of her sleeve. "All my life, I’ve been on the sidelines. I was fine with it then because I didn't have anything worth stepping forward for. But now? There’s a promise I need to honor. And more importantly... there’s someone important at my side who deserves a dependable partner, not a burden."

Aiven looked down at the Loom-Breaker, which was resting in a cushioned case nearby. "And strangely enough, the world gave me the instrument to back it up. This ocean of mana... it’s part of me. Knowing there’s such a thing inside me, being passive is no longer something I want.”

Virelle’s breath hitched. She stared at him, seeing the clerk from Lowhaven finally dissolve into the man he was becoming. The fear in her eyes didn't vanish, but it was overlaid with a look of profound, shimmering respect.

"I understand," she said softly, her head bowing slightly. "I... I apologize, Master. I allowed my own selfishness to cloud your resolve."

Aiven let out a small, thoughtful chuckle. "There’s no need for an apology, Virelle. Weren't you the one who told me not to apologize for every little thing? Now you're the one doing it."

Virelle froze for a second, then looked up. A familiar, smug smirk began to play on her lips, though it was tempered by a newfound warmth.

"Hmph," she huffed, crossing her arms and drifting back into her regal posture. "It is a Scion's privilege to be inconsistent when her heart is involved, Master. Do not expect me to be bound by the same rules."

She spun in a slow, graceful circle, her hair catching the first true ray of sunlight breaking through the mist. "Very well. If you are determined to become the ocean, then I shall be the storm that guides you."

"I-I have the first dampening field active!" Pelka shouted from the center of the clearing, her voice cracking with nerves. "Sir Aiven... the Loom-Breaker is ready. Please... step into the circle."

Aiven took a deep breath, looked at Virelle one last time, and stepped toward the center of the obsidian towers.

Aiven took a step forward, his boots crunching on the grey grit of the clearing. Beside him, Virelle drifted with a heavy, watchful silence.

Pelka stood near the primary control pylon, her hands hovering over a glowing interface. She looked up as they approached, her bangs swaying in the gale. "Sir Aiven... please, center yourself between the four obsidian pillars." She hesitated, her sheep ears twitching as her gaze drifted to the mage floating at his shoulder. "Miss Virelle... it’s okay for you to stay outside the circle. You don't have to follow him in."

Virelle’s eyes snapped to Pelka, flashing with a sudden, lethal glare that made the analyst stagger back a step.

"You are in no position to dictate my movements, snack-creature," Virelle hissed, her voice cutting through the wind like a blade. "I know exactly what you are doing. These artifacts of yours...This is a containment barrier."

Pelka’s face went pale, and she adjusted her glasses with trembling fingers. "I-it is a containment barrier, yes. But these are just precautions for—"

"Precautions?" Virelle interrupted, her silver hair flaring with a violent lavender light. "Whether you call them precautions or safety protocols does not change the fact that you, your CEO, and the entire collective of primitive feral beasts at Vulpine thought of my Master as someone dangerous. You view him as a bomb that might go off.”

She drifted closer to Aiven, her translucent sleeves brushing his arm, her expression shifting into one of absolute, terrifying devotion. "Unlike them, I do not fear the ocean he carries. I will stay by my Master’s side no matter what manner of tide he unleashes. If he is to be a storm, then I shall be the center of it."

Pelka opened her mouth to retort, but found no words. She looked at Vane, who remained a stoic, silent sentinel at the perimeter, then back at the two figures standing in the center of the jagged rock.

Aiven looked at Virelle and offered a small, grateful nod. He didn't tell her to stay back; he knew better by now.

"Do it, Pelka," Aiven commanded, his voice steady.

Pelka swallowed hard and slammed her palm onto the central data-crystal.

HUMMMMMMMMM—

A low-frequency vibration groaned from the earth, so deep it felt as though the island itself were waking up. From the tips of the four obsidian towers, beams of pale blue energy shot upward, meeting at a single point high in the grey sky.

Suddenly, a semi-transparent, shimmering dome erupted from the pillars, expanding with a pressurized whoosh of air. It grew rapidly, curving over the clearing until it formed a massive, shimmering hemisphere. It was colossal, the size of a mini-stadium, turning the grey clearing into a private, isolated arena.

Inside the dome, the sound of the ocean vanished, replaced by an eerie, artificial stillness.

Aiven reached into the cushioned case and pulled out the Loom-Breaker. The crimson crystal felt hot in his hand, pulsing in rhythm with the "star" buried deep within his soul.

Aiven stood in the center of the dome, his boots crunching on the grey dirt. He took a final, steadying breath and reached into his pack, pulling out the Loom-Breaker.

Pelka’s instructions from the flight echoed in his mind. “It is not a complex machine, Sir Aiven. It is a conceptual key. You simply hold it, point it toward the lock in your mind, and think of the word: Open.”

It sounded deceptively simple for a task that carried the weight of his entire future.

Virelle hovered inches away, her eyes wide and unblinking, watching him with an intensity that felt like a physical touch. Aiven raised the jagged crystal key. It felt heavy—not just with physical mass, but with potential. He closed his eyes and looked inward, seeking the cold, unyielding iron wall that had suppressed his mana.

Open, he thought, pouring every ounce of his will into the artifact.

The Loom-Breaker reacted instantly. The crimson crystal began to emit a low-frequency hum that vibrated the very air inside the dome. It slipped from Aiven’s fingers, floating upward as it began to expand, growing from the size of a pendant to its original, forearm-length form.

VREEEEEE—

A blinding white light erupted from Aiven’s chest, matching the pulse of the crimson key. The cyan etchings on his Armvil Mark 4 screamed with light, the Aetheric Echo vibrating so violently it felt like his humerus was being ground to dust.

"Sir Aiven! The readings are—!" Pelka’s voice was cut off by a sudden, crystalline CRACK.

The Loom-Breaker didn't just vibrate; it buckled. Jagged fractures raced across the legendary artifact before it exploded into a thousand shimmering red shards that dissolved into the air before they even hit the ground.

"It... it shattered?" Pelka’s voice was a whisper of absolute disbelief from the edge of the dome. "A legendary artifact of that class only respawns if it is forced to unseal something... something tremendous..."

The light didn't stop. Aiven’s feet left the ground. He began to float, suspended in a pillar of incandescent mana that threatened to erase his very silhouette.

"Master!"

Virelle didn't care about the radiation. She surged forward, wrapping her arms around Aiven’s waist, her weight anchoring him as they drifted ten feet into the air. "Master! Do you hear me? Speak to me!"

Aiven’s head lulled back. His eyes were open, but they were glazed with a brilliant, terrifying white. "Dizzy..." he rasped, his voice sounding distant, as if he were speaking from the bottom of a well. "Virelle... my consciousness... it’s slipping. Everything is... too loud."

Virelle’s heart hammered against her ribs. This was the thickets all over again. After the fight with Valerion, when his arm was taken, his mana had begun to leak exactly like this. Back then, she had been useless—a witness to his near-destruction until that mysterious hooded girl had appeared to patch the seal.

I will not be useless again, she vowed, her mana flaring in a protective lavender shroud around them.

She knew she couldn't re-seal it. She didn't have the spell, and the Loom-Breaker was gone. Her only choice was to help him tame the flood.

"Master! Listen to my voice!" she cried out, her hair whipping wildly in the mana-storm. "You must control it! Imagine a vast ocean—an endless sea! Now... construct a pipe in your mind. A massive, unbreakable conduit with a valve at the center! Direct the water into the pipe! Do not let it wash over you—guide it!"

Her instructions were frantic. Virelle had mastered magic through instinct and raw talent; she didn't know the dry, technical terms of a professor. She only knew the feeling of the weave.

Aiven gritted his teeth, sweat pouring down his face despite the cold wind of the dome. He visualized the sea. He saw the Ocean of his mana—a terrifying, infinite expanse of white starlight. He built the pipe. He felt the flow stabilize for a heartbeat, and the blinding light around them receded into a duller glow.

But the pressure was absolute.

"The pipe..." Aiven groaned, his body tensed in a mask of agony. "Virelle... it's about to burst! It’s too much!"

"Then make the pipe larger!" Virelle shrieked, her own mana straining to keep them airborne. "Make it as wide as the sky if you must! Just don't let go!"

The white light intensified again, surging with a renewed fury. Aiven’s vision began to blur, the grey dirt of the island and Pelka’s panicked silhouette fading into a sea of white. He was fading.

CRASH.

Something struck the Vulpine containment dome. A jagged hole of energy tore open in mid-air, and a hooded figure blurred through the breach with a speed that defied the eye.

The figure didn't hesitate. She dashed across the clearing, leaped into the air, and snatched Aiven out of the mana-pillar, her momentum carrying them both crashing back down to the dirt.

Virelle landed a second later, her hands glowing with a lethal fire as she prepared to erase the intruder. But she stopped, her breath hitching. She recognized the robe—the heavy, dark fabric from the thickets.

The girl was on top of Aiven, her hands pressed firmly against his chest. She leaned down, her lips brushing against his ear, and whispered a single, rhythmic sequence of words that sounded like a lullaby played on a harp.

The mana-storm died instantly.

The white light retreated into Aiven’s chest, settling into a calm, steady rhythm.

Aiven blinked, his vision slowly returning. The world was grey and dull again, but the weight in his chest was gone. He looked up.

A girl was hovering over him, her hood having fallen back during the impact.

Honey-blonde hair spilled over her shoulders, catching the pale morning light. Her eyes were a deep, calming blue. She was looking down at him with an expression of profound, tearful relief—a look of "I’m so glad you’re okay" that he had seen many times.

It was the same look she gave him when they were in their teens, when he had tried to fight off a small-wyvern to impress her.

Aiven’s heart stopped for a completely different reason.

"Lyra?" he whispered.
