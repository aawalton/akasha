Sunup found the group around Adon much rested and generally much better for their rest.

Carolien still looked tired and weak, but slightly better than the previous day.

Rosslyn appeared fully restored physically, though she moved delicately, as if every step reminded her of all that she and her family had lost the previous day.

Samson, Adon thought. The name brought a small pang of sadness, but it wasn’t nearly as deep as it might have been. The slight funk that hung over him was substantially mitigated by the simple fact that he had been there in his brother’s final moments. He had witnessed what felt and looked like Samson’s ascension to some higher plane. He’d seen his soul.

If I ever thought the universe was indifferent… that seems like evidence to the contrary. Some power wanted Samson and me to connect one last time, wanted me to see that. The timing for Rosslyn and me was too convenient. We completed the dungeon at just the right time. Whatever power made that happen loved Samson, just like I do. It feels like his sacrifice restored him or something. Or maybe it was how he lived his life. It’s hard to be sure what I was supposed to take from this experience, except for the fact that Samson emerged bigger and brighter from what happened. His soul definitely wasn’t shrunken or deformed…

A knock at the door broke his train of thought.

Rosslyn rushed over there before Carolien could grab her cane and try to limp to the doorway.

Adon sensed that the Dowager Queen wanted to say something about how rulers did not answer the door themselves, but she recognized the folly of that remark and kept silent.

When the new Queen opened the door, Baltazar was standing there.

“You missed the little ones?” Rosslyn asked.

Then she caught the expression on her oldest brother’s face.

“Frederick and William,” he said. “I have been calling them, and they—they just refuse to wake up. Please come. I have no idea what to do.”

Rosslyn, Carolien, Goldie, and Adon rushed together from the cottage they had spent the night in. Goldie had been in a quiet, tense mood since she began moving that morning, but this seemed to break it. The possibility of another loss loomed large over her.

When they entered, they immediately knew that Baltazar had not called them over for nothing.

The brothers were in almost identical conditions. Both feverish, covered in sweat, heartbeats weak and unsteady when Rosslyn and Carolien took their pulses.

“William! Frederick! Wake up!” Rosslyn commanded.

That had no effect.

She surrounded her hands with green healing mana and then pushed it into William’s and Frederick’s chests.

Their bodies absorbed it, but to no apparent effect.

“Useless,” Rosslyn muttered. She turned away from the brothers. “Adon, could you try shouting into their brains telepathically?”

Absolutely, Rosslyn, the butterfly sent.

He sent a loud, wordless telepathic noise through the brains of both William and Frederick, carefully containing it to just their heads.

Both brothers jerked and spasmed in their beds, but only Frederick showed real signs of brain activity that pointed toward waking up.

Adon sent another telepathic message to Frederick.

We need you to wake up, Fred! You and your brother are in danger of dying. You have to get up and perform self-maintenance! Our healing magic isn’t doing a damned thing.

As he sent the message, he realized he didn’t really know what the exact function of waking them up would be. He trusted that Rosslyn had a plan in mind, but that was all.

As the message ran through Frederick’s head, the young lord’s fingers clenched and unclenched on the sheets. He stilled.

Freddy!!! Goldie shouted telepathically. Wake up!

The noise was so much louder than what Adon was used to that even he jumped at the sound. Rosslyn did as well, while Carolien just looked mildly startled.

Most importantly, Frederick finally stirred.

“Bacon and biscuits, ma’am,” he slurred.

Adon used Telepathy to enter his perspective for a moment. The world was blurry, and the butterfly could feel haze over all his thoughts, obscuring things—like wandering around one’s home in complete darkness and not knowing where the furniture was.

Try to be careful with him, he sent quietly to the others. He’s really not functional.

“He needs to be, though,” Rosslyn replied brusquely. “We cannot be patient.”

She reached out and grabbed Frederick by his shirtfront, and she pulled him up to make direct eye contact.

“Frederick, you have to get up now!” she demanded. “You need to exercise control over your mana and do your best to purge the bad blood from your system. Any bad energy or corruption you can detect, we need your help!”

“I just want to sleep,” he said, slurring the last word. He yanked back against her so hard that he managed to pull his shirt loose from her grip and fall back onto the bed.

“Damn it, Frederick!” Rosslyn exclaimed. “Do not do this to me. To yourself! We cannot treat you without your help!”

“It will be an honor to have died in support of your claim, Your Majesty,” said Frederick. He sounded drunk. As he finished speaking, he lay still again.

“Goddess damn it, you already said that,” Rosslyn muttered. “You do not get to speak the same dying words twice, Frederick.”

He murmured something that sounded like, “Sorry,” and then Adon sensed the young lord had passed out again.

He conveyed that fact to Rosslyn, and her eyes narrowed with determination.

She shifted from sitting on Frederick’s bed to William’s, and she surprised everyone.

She reached down and slapped the young lord across the face, hard. Once, twice, three times.

“Wake up! Wake up, damn you! I know if you wake up, you can get your brother up, William! Do you not wish to save your brother?”

She slapped him across the face again, so hard that Adon could tell she had almost split his lip. The young lord still didn’t stir.

As the Queen reared back to slap him again, Carolien stayed her hand.

“That is enough, Rosslyn,” the widow said quietly. “None of this is working.”

“What am I supposed to do, Carolien?” Rosslyn snapped, turning angrily to face her. “We lost my father, we lost Samson, I am not losing any more of my friends! We are not even in a combat zone anymore!”

Adon saw tears welling up in her eyes.

Carolien pulled Rosslyn into a firm embrace, and after a moment’s resistance, the younger woman rested her head on her stepmother’s neck. She allowed herself to properly cry for the first time since they had escaped.

Adon watched, and he wanted to go to her and comfort her, but he decided that Carolien was doing everything that Rosslyn needed. If he transformed into human form just to go and join the hug, he would only be a distraction.

“What are we going to do?” Rosslyn asked after a few minutes of this.

Always solutions-oriented, the Queen refused to rest without exploring any possibility that might end the intolerable situation. Over the next few minutes, she and Carolien went over their options.

“Do you know what your father’s healing process was like?” Carolien asked.

Rosslyn nodded. “The healers would cut out contaminated flesh and be there to ensure that it grew back quickly with healing magic. That was why I was so determined to wake the brothers up. At least one of them. I thought they would be able to sense where the poison was concentrated within their own bodies. If they could, then I could try and play the part of the healer. I could cut out the poisoned flesh while you, Adon, or Goldie used healing magic.”

“Right. Without one of them waking up, though, we would be relying on blind luck, just hoping we were not cutting out things that were healthy. If we made a mistake, we would only make things worse.”

“I think treating them ourselves without one of them giving us some kind of direction is not an option,” Rosslyn agreed.

“Healing magic was not helping on its own, right?” Carolien asked.

Rosslyn shook her head.

“We could try to travel more quickly to the border with Dessia so that their own healers could handle this,” Carolien began, but she stopped as she saw Rosslyn shaking her head.

“You saw father’s condition, and you have seen theirs,” Rosslyn said. “Can you say that William and Frederick are in better shape than father was before he died?”

“No.”

“Then such a long journey is out of the question. We are still hundreds of miles away. The griffins flew free after they reunited Adon and me with the rest of you, but even if we still had them, a griffin could not bear the brothers’ weight for any significant distance. Even if they could, the time they would take to make the trip would still be too long.”

“You are considering the inconceivable,” Carolien said softly, matter-of-factly, not asking a question.

“We need to give them over to the Empire and hope they will treat the brothers.”

“The Dessians explicitly declined that as an option before.”

“Because they wanted to stand by us, to escort us to the border.”

“If we make contact with the Empire, it is impossible that we will not give them some clue as to our whereabouts. No matter how indirect the method used. Just knowing our direction of travel would be a valuable clue for them. Whatever might happen to William and Frederick now, a fate worse than death awaits our family if we are caught.”

Not to mention the amount of time it would take away from actually trying to move toward the border, Adon chimed in.

Rosslyn gave him a sharp look and continued talking.

“They signed up to fight alongside us, perhaps to die, but can we truly say they would want to die this way? Sick in bed, of an ailment that might be curable by another party that would probably willingly help them? The Empire would love having that over the Duchy.”

“We could try to bleed the bad matter out of them,” Carolien suggested weakly. “Better than randomly cutting hunks of the brothers out and healing them.”

That’s not really good for this sort of medical condition, Adon sent.

This time Rosslyn smiled slightly.

“Very well,” Carolien said in a tone of quiet despair. “If you make this move to give away our location, please at least arrange a head start for yourself, me, and the children. If the Empire is not on our trail already, they will be shortly.”

“I know it,” Rosslyn said softly.

I could take them, Adon sent. If we know where the Empire is, I would happily transform and escort them.

I will go, too, Goldie sent, speaking for the first time in a while.

Rosslyn and Carolien shared a look.

“Out of the question,” Rosslyn said, in the same moment that Carolien said, “No way!”

“At least we agree on something,” the Dowager Queen said after a moment.

Rosslyn gave her a small smile.

“The Empire would love to exterminate the remaining mystic beasts that their enemies were not foolish enough to destroy themselves,” she said. “You cannot walk willingly into their hands.”

Maybe it would be dangerous for Goldie, but I can fly away and essentially turn into the wind, Adon sent. I don’t think it’s possible to catch me if I don’t want to be caught.

“Normally, I would believe you, Adon, but this is the same Empire that discovered an anti-magic mineral and saved the stone for just the right time to destroy our city walls and use it as a weapon of war. I would not put it past them to have a magic-nullifying cage that robs you of your powers. Even though you often seem larger than life to me, I think a magic-draining fly swatter would be enough to kill you. Your power is all magic. No, if we send them to the Empire, we have to rely on our peasants to help.”

“At least that is better than throwing away the mystic beasts,” Carolien muttered.

“I will consult with the Sergeant—” Rosslyn began.

As the Queen was speaking, however, the bed behind her began to shake.

William appeared to be having a seizure.
