Chapter 36: Restless, Part 1

To say Jessica Vanderborn was tired would be a gross understatement. Today had been the longest day of her life, and it wasn’t even close. Watching her best friend’s incredible match against Raeleq had been draining, as had her own match a couple hours after. Kim hadn’t been a strong opponent to anyone, but fighting was still stressful.

The party had been almost nice, though thinking about it made her want to cry. Watching all of her friends get honest attention would’ve been nice, if Jessica hadn’t had to fend off people trying to kiss up to her grandfather through her. She still felt proud of each and every one of them, but it was hard not to be envious. All of them were so strong, so capable.

Jessica was…well, she was smart, and she knew it. No one could memorize facts like her, and it had never been difficult to follow along with magical theory, engineering, anything she put her mind to. But it didn’t matter if she was smart, when all she saw were weaknesses.

Chief among them: cowardice. When facing down Charles Vanderborn, Jessica froze every step of the way. From the office to the fight against his army of thralls, she felt all but useless.

Until now.

“You are doing quite well, Ms. Vanderborn,” SNAC said in his soft, soothing tone. “Are there any further questions on the operation of the Wanderer?”

“No, I think I understand it all,” she said, looking at the panel of unmarked levers behind the helm itself. They’d spent the last thirty minutes going over everything from the acceleration to how to change altitude -- or whatever passed for altitude in this weird, dark place -- and how to activate their stealth mode, when needed.

Thirty minutes of silly rhymes while she touched different levers, little memory games, and she’d never forget it. Jessica may have been weak and a coward, but she would never be useless. She refused to be the weak link.

“Then might I make a suggestion, Mistress?” SNAC couldn’t sound anything other than chipper and calm, but Jessica thought she detected hesitation.

“Go on.”

The golem tilted his head to the side. “You should perhaps get some rest. It is my duty to take care of and protect your family, and you show signs of exhaustion and distress.”

Jessica couldn’t help but burst out laughing. “Do you think?” The laughs wracked her body like dry sobs, until they were no longer dry and breathing became difficult.

The next thing she knew, she was on the deck, curled up in a ball, hyperventilating while the golem hovered over her silently. Distress? Distress? It was so funny it hurt. Everything hurt and wouldn’t leave her alone. Her body wanted to collapse, but her stupid brain wouldn’t shut up, the past day wouldn’t shut up.

“Mistress?” SNAC asked.

She ignored him and stared off into the distance, where countless stars and other worlds floated amidst the black. At first, the Aetheric Expanse had scared her. It was chilly, dark, and so empty at first.

But it was also a contradiction. A riot of colors and shapes made of far off light. Endless emptiness, filled with endless people. World after world, just like theirs.

Just like theirs. Maybe even with the same chains and victims.

“SNAC?” she said in a shaky voice.

“Yes Mistress?”

“How many worlds are there out there?” She pulled herself into a sitting position, still staring into the expanse.

“Unknown, Mistress. There are estimated to be billions, if not trillions of worlds. For as much time as the Hermanic Empire has spent mapping out worlds, they do so within the framework of the lifetime of young immortals. We know personally of tens of thousands of worlds in range of the empire, but have control over three thousand, five hundred and seventy-two, as well as partial control over another four thousand and ninety-nine.”

It was useful information, she knew. It helped quiet the cacophony in her head, the voices telling her she wasn’t good enough. Learning more would keep them at bay. She just had to hold out a little longer.

“What about opposing worlds? How many worlds are there that actively oppose the empire?”

“Six hundred and twenty-six enemy worlds that have proven difficult to conquer. However, a majority of them do nothing more than defend themselves, independent from the others. With your help, perhaps we can bring these numbers down and crush their resistance.”

A chill went down Jessica’s spine. As much as she’d grown to like and appreciate SNAC already, she couldn’t forget that he was loyal to her grandfather and the empire above all else. The moment he came back, SNAC would turn on them.

“That’s not many worlds resisting,” she muttered. Their task grew ever bigger and more nebulous. Maybe Ambrose was right, and it was hopeless. That didn’t mean she’d want to stop, but maybe she was pushing herself too hard.

If she was, it was to make up for her cowardice. If that was even possible.

Jessica pulled herself up with the help of the plush captain’s chair. Her limbs shook with the stress. “SNAC, what’s the time on Amaria?”

“Five in the morning, Mistress.” The golem’s glowing eyes never left her.

That meant she’d been up for…Gods, nearly twenty-two hours. And in that time, she’d fought, drank, fought, ran for her life, and drank a little more. No wonder she was about to collapse.

“Will you fly the ship?” she asked. “And wake me if anything happens?”

The golem gave a solitary nod. “I will, Mistress. You can count on me. Please, get some sleep. When you wake up, I will prepare a large breakfast for you and your party.”

Jessica chuckled, and took one last look out from the ship. The Aetheric Expanse was beautiful, alien, and their home for the foreseeable future. Eventually, they would go to a new world and explore, but…rest sounded good.

She headed below deck and stopped in the ring of six cabins. Four of them were obviously occupied, with closed doors. At dinner, they’d agreed to each take one of them, and it looked like they left her a choice between either the last normal cabin…or her grandfather’s.

Through his open door, a larger cabin waited, perfectly organized and packed to the brim with books and storage. The colors were his usual blue, red, and black combination, seen on the bedsheets and an abstract tapestry on the wall. It was as cold and practical as Charles himself was, and it even smelled like him. That, more than anything, made it an easy decision.

Jessica took the other empty cabin, and was about to close the door when she heard, “Jess?”

Raeva Ransa went from lightly dozing, to suddenly being wide awake. It happened sometimes, when she drank before bed. She’d fall asleep, but it would be shallow, and often resulted in ten second long dreams, repeating until dawn. On a good night, she’d relive highlights and fantasies, never quite getting there but not minding as she cycled through them.

Tonight, however, she dreamt of loss after loss.

The first was her mother, at five years old. She didn’t remember much of that day, but she remembered the sharp light at sunset hurting her eyes, the songs of their funeral party, and Raeleq sobbing relentlessly while she tried to be strong.

It happened sometimes, like an old wound that hadn’t healed so much as scabbed over so much the area was numb. Every so often, it got ripped open, and the hurt was fresh again.

If that had been it, it would’ve been sad, but not miserable. So much of her dreams went back to the failures of the day. Out of the two, the match with Molly haunted her more. She’d tried her best and come up short against their class’s fiercest fighter, and it was so much worse than losing to the world’s most powerful wizard.

All affinities except shadow had at least two students in their year, which created an automatic rivalry. There was usually only one slot per affinity for the graduating class, and she wanted to, needed to be part of it. It wasn’t just her future and pride on the line, it was the future of the Bedamin people too.

They needed strong leaders, and as much as she loved her brother, he would never be king. Not only was she a whole twenty minutes older, he was too soft-hearted to make the hard decisions required of them. One day, he would make a good, wise leader, but on a smaller scale. Someone who could do good and help people and animals alike. It was on Raeva to win big, represent their people in high society, and fight to keep everything they could.

In her dreams, Molly was an untouchable, unbeatable monster who slapped down everything she tried, and eventually buried Raeva in a rock tomb. It hadn’t been like that, not really, but it was how she remembered it.

When she woke up gasping after the fifth repetition of the stupid tournament that no longer mattered, she decided she was up for good. Raeva sat up and turned the lamp up. She looked around the cabin she chose, trying to decide whether she liked it or not.

All the rooms had been decorated in the style of one of the provinces Amaria had conquered over the last hundred years. She let Raeleq have the Bedamin room, figuring it would comfort him, while she took the cabin done in the style of the oceanic paradise of Dalfina to the southwest. Silken sea green sheets, and walls of the bluest sky with little paintings and sculptures of ocean creatures and shells tastefully filling in the gaps.

It was soothing, in its own way, but a reminder of what Amaria did to its conquered territories. Dalfina was no longer their own people, with culture and history unique to them. They were the beaches and fishing fleets of the country, the vacation spots for the rich. Their father had even taken them there once, during a diplomatic trip. It was beautiful, but they had been defeated long before the surrender was signed a couple of years later.

Vanderborn took those proud, peaceful people, and turned it into a gimmicky room. And the same was being done to their entire world.

The anger came fresh, and was a balm on her scared, troubled mind. Anger was easier, and empowering. But without anything to do with it, it just turned inward and burned her. Raeva knew better than to play with fire for too long. It was a tool, to be used responsibly, not to cling to until she was burnt. She slipped out of her room, still in her sleeping tunic, in time to see Jessica about to go to bed.

“Jess?” Raeva said, just above a whisper.

The mind mage turned around, looking about as bad as Raeva felt. “What?”

“Are you just now going to bed?”

“Yes?” Her weary voice went high at the end. “Do you need something?”

Did she? Raeva wanted company, but maybe not if her friend was that ragged. “No, just checking on you. Get some sleep, okay?”

Jessica waved half-heartedly and disappeared into her new room, leaving Raeva alone, and unsure of what to do.

There wasn’t really anywhere to go. Going above the deck weirded her out, seeing the stars and worlds with their odd, flashing images. A lot of the ship was dedicated towards supplies, and that was useful, but not helpful for her. Her restlessness grew, and she considered going down to the combat lab and see if she could work out some frustration.

Nothing sounded good, but she couldn’t just go back to bed. Not when her head swam and the nightmares waited for her. So, it was time to make it someone else's problem. With Jessica out of the running, that left Luthor, Raeleq, and Ambrose.

Raeva immediately ruled out Luthor. They got on well enough, but he was a bit too serious for her. He’d likely look for solutions, and be too reasonable for her current mood. Her first instinct had been her little brother. She needed to check up on him anyway, but…now wasn’t the time. She wanted to be a bit selfish.

Which left one last option, and maybe the best one. She went up Ambrose’s cabin and knocked. It took a few seconds, but she heard an annoyed groan, a sigh, and then the shuffle of him getting out of bed. He opened the door in just dark underwear, his pale, wiry body covered in vivid bruises and some leftover burns and chars, including singed chest hair.

“Am I dreaming, or has the day finally come?” he said with a playful leer.

Raeva gave him a dirty look. “Are you going to make me regret coming to you?” she asked.

“Probably. But is that going to stop you?”

“...no,” she sighed, and pushed past him. Ambrose closed the door after her.
