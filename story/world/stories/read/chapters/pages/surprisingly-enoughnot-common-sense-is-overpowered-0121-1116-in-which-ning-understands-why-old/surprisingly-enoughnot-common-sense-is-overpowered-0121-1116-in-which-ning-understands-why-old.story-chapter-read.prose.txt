The inner room of the Alchemy Hall was surprisingly simple, furnished with little more than several bookshelves, a tea table, and a pill furnace set aside for refinement.

Fu Xuan was already waiting for them inside.

Ning had seen her from a distance before, but this was his first time observing her properly. Despite being a Purple Mansion cultivator and the head of the Alchemy Hall's lineage, she looked like an ordinary old woman, with gray hair and wrinkles around her eyes.

It made Ning wonder why she had never taken a Beauty-Preserving Pill.

Such pills could preserve a cultivator's youthful appearance for decades, making them particularly popular among wealthy cultivators. Ning himself planned to learn the recipe eventually; although it was a difficult high-grade First-Tier pill that required expensive ingredients, the selling price was equally impressive.

There would never be a shortage of cultivators willing to spend spirit stones to avoid looking old. After all, this was a world where people cared so much about face that "You dare not give me face?" could apparently serve as a perfectly reasonable prelude to attempted murder.

Beauty-Preserving Pills were practically guaranteed business.

Ling Xin walked straight into the room. "Master."

Ning followed a step behind and cupped his hands. "Disciple Ji Ning greets Grandmaster."

Fu Xuan looked up from the book in front of her and gave a small nod. "Hm. Let's not take too long. Refine a pill for me."

Ning paused, then glanced toward Ling Xin, who smiled as though suddenly throwing her disciple into an examination was nothing unusual.

"This is how Master assesses younger alchemists," she explained. "Whenever she meets one for the first time, she has them refine a pill so she can see their ability for herself."

You couldn't have mentioned that on the way here?

Ning sighed internally, but he had refined enough pills by now that an unexpected demonstration wasn't particularly frightening. "What may I refine, Grandmaster?"

"Anything you're familiar with."

That made the decision easy.

Ning selected the ingredients for a Red Bud Pill, choosing something he could reliably refine rather than attempting a more difficult pill simply to impress her. Fenghuang was still asleep back at the farm, so he wouldn't have access to the raven's white flame either, but that was fine; his own flame was more than sufficient for a normal refinement.

After checking the ingredients, Ning cleaned and processed them according to their individual properties, removing unusable portions while preserving the areas where the medicinal essence was concentrated.

Years of spiritual farming and hundreds of alchemical preparations had made the work familiar enough that his hands rarely stopped moving, and Fu Xuan remained silent throughout, observing not only whether he prepared everything correctly, but also how he handled each material.

Once the ingredients were ready, Ning activated the furnace and circulated the Crimson Flame Control Technique. A stable flame formed beneath his guidance, after which he began introducing the ingredients according to their required temperatures, extracting their medicinal essence while separating the impurities that rose during refinement.

His eyes gradually turned luminous blue, faint four-colored rings appearing around his pupils.

When one medicinal essence finished releasing its useful properties, Ning lowered the flame before it could be over-refined; when another began reacting with the first, he carefully adjusted the temperature and qi according to the changes he observed.

Fu Xuan's gaze sharpened. "Interesting. His sense of timing is very good."

Ling Xin smiled. "That's one of the things he's best at."

"His control as well." Fu Xuan watched Ning make another subtle adjustment before adding, "Quite the opposite of you when you were his age."

Ling Xin's smile disappeared. "Master, I'm standing right here, you know."

"I'm only speaking the truth. Teaching you restraint was a nightmare." Fu Xuan sighed. "You always relied on instinct. Whenever I asked why you changed the flame, your answer was simply that it felt right."

Ling Xin crossed her arms. "And I was right."

"That wasn't the problem. I was trying to teach you why."

Ning naturally heard none of their conversation. His attention was entirely on the furnace, his mind following every change in the medicinal essence as the refinement progressed. Focus was one of the most important aspects of alchemy, and once Ning entered that state, there was little room left in his mind for anything else.

Simply put, he was locked in.

As the extracted essences gradually gathered into medicinal liquid, Fu Xuan continued watching, and the longer the refinement continued, the clearer Ning's strengths became. His preparation was clean, his flame remained stable, and he rarely made unnecessary movements, as though every action had been practiced and corrected until it reached its current form.

After watching for a while longer, Fu Xuan's expression grew thoughtful.

"He's different from what I expected."

Ling Xin glanced at her master. "How so?"

"From the way you spoke about him, I expected someone closer to you, an alchemist who relies heavily on instinct."

Ling Xin considered that for a moment before nodding. "No, Ning is not like me. I'd say my disciple's approach to alchemy is almost the complete opposite of mine." She paused, then added, "Though I've told him many times that a little more flair wouldn't hurt."

Fu Xuan ignored that last remark and continued watching Ning, gradually forming a clearer picture of this new grand-disciple of hers.

After teaching alchemy for so many years, Fu Xuan knew talent came in many forms. Ling Xin possessed an extraordinary instinct for the craft, while others excelled through medicinal theory, flame control, spiritual sense, or simple practice.

Ning was different again.

"He's methodical," Fu Xuan said. "He observes what's happening, compares it against what he knows, and responds. More importantly, he's good at making his different abilities support one another."

Her gaze briefly settled on his luminous eyes. His experience as a spiritual farmer gave him familiarity with medicinal plants, his qi control supported his flame control, and his eye techniques gave him perception that another alchemist might have needed years to develop.

None of those advantages alone explained his ability. What interested Fu Xuan was how naturally he brought them together.

Ling Xin's smile widened. "That's what I've been telling you."

"You mostly told me that your disciple was amazing, that he takes care of you, and that having a disciple is much more enjoyable than you expected," Fu Xuan replied blandly.

"It's not wrong, though," Ling Xin mumbled.

Several steps away, Ning remained completely unaware of their conversation, his attention fixed on the furnace. Fu Xuan watched him make another small adjustment to the flame before saying, "He still has quite a few problems."

Ling Xin immediately brightened. "Then you'll help him with those, right, Master? You can consider it another chance to give all that careful guidance you always wanted to give me."

Fu Xuan gave her a flat look. "And whose fault was it that I never got the chance?"

Ling Xin suddenly found Ning's refinement very interesting.

Fu Xuan shook her head and returned her attention to her grand-disciple.

For an alchemist of her experience, a single refinement could reveal quite a lot. How someone responded to unexpected changes, when they chose to intervene, what they trusted, and which mistakes they instinctively tried to avoid often said as much about the alchemist as the pill they eventually produced.

By then, Ning had entered condensation. He gradually reduced the moisture within the gathered medicinal liquid while maintaining an even heat, then guided the thickening mass into pill form. Once its internal structure had stabilized, he lowered the flame, sealed the remaining medicinal qi with Locking the Spirit, and finally used the Collecting the Elixir technique.

The furnace hummed as a red pill bearing a faint bud-shaped pattern rose from within and landed neatly in the waiting jade dish.

Ning released a slow breath and deactivated his eye techniques. The refinement had gone smoothly, and although Fenghuang's white flame could have improved the result, this was close to what he normally achieved on his own.

He carried the pill over. "Grandmaster."

Fu Xuan inspected it briefly before sweeping it with her spiritual sense. "Seventy-eight percent purity."

Ning nodded. That was about what he had expected. With Fenghuang's assistance, his recent Red Bud Pills had reached around eighty-three percent, but without the raven's superior flame, seventy-eight was fairly normal.

Fu Xuan set the pill down. "Your fundamentals are solid. There aren't many obvious mistakes."

Ning waited. He had a feeling the important part was coming next.

"Your biggest problem is that your refinement is too orderly."

Ning blinked. "Too orderly?"

"You treat each stage separately. You finish extraction, stabilize the medicinal essence, and only then begin Gather Liquid. You do the same when moving into condensation and Spirit Locking." Fu Xuan tapped the rim of the furnace. "Those stages are useful when you're learning, but once your control improves, you don't need to separate them so strictly."

Ning thought back through the refinement. "Grandmaster means I could have started Gather Liquid before the extraction was completely finished?"

"With this batch, yes. The Red Bud essence had already reached the state you needed. You could have introduced the secondary medicine near the end of extraction and allowed the two processes to overlap."

He could remember the exact moment she was referring to. The Red Bud essence had already reached the state he wanted, but he had still waited for the extraction to finish before introducing the secondary medicine.

"Grandmaster, if I begin Gather Liquid during the final part of extraction, wouldn't that also change the point at which I introduce the secondary medicine?" Ning asked. "The Red Bud essence would still be undergoing its final transformation, so the reaction between the two should be different from what I'm used to."

Fu Xuan gave a small nod. "It would. That's one of the reasons you shouldn't rush to overlap every stage simply because I've told you it can be done."

She gestured toward the furnace. "You're no longer judging only whether the Red Bud is ready. You also have to judge whether the change it's still undergoing will help or interfere with the medicine you're introducing. With some combinations, that remaining transformation helps bring the two together. With others, introducing the next ingredient too early will only make the refinement harder to control."

Ning thought about several pills he already knew, mentally comparing their ingredients.

"So the useful transition won't necessarily occur at the same point for every formula."

"No. And even with the same formula, it may shift slightly between batches." Fu Xuan paused before adding, "Don't look for another fixed point to memorize. Learn what you want the two medicinal properties to do when they meet, then judge when the conditions are right for that reaction."

Ning nodded respectfully. "I understand, Grandmaster."

That was much more useful than simply shortening the pause between two stages. Ning wasn't being taught to refine faster; he had to start considering what was still happening in one transformation when he began the next.

Ling Xin nodded along. "Once you get used to it, it's easy. When this part starts going..." She made a small circular motion with one hand before bringing two fingers together. "You catch it here, bring this part over, and then they go together."

Ning stared at her fingers blankly.

Ling Xin looked at him expectantly.

"Master, what exactly was 'this part'?"

"The medicinal essence."

"Which one to be exact?" Ning asked plainly.

Ling Xin paused. "The one you're refining."

Somehow, that made the explanation worse.

Fu Xuan sighed. "This is why I spent so many years trying to teach you how to explain what you're doing instead of simply saying that it feels right."

"But it does feel right."

"I know." Fu Xuan turned back to Ning. "Your second problem is somewhat similar. You're very quick to intervene whenever you notice something deviating from what you expect."

Ning thought back over the refinement. That was certainly true. Pure Eyes allowed him to catch even small changes in the medicinal essence, and his first response was usually to correct them before they could develop into something worse.

"Is there a particular adjustment Grandmaster thinks was unnecessary?"

"During Gather Liquid." Fu Xuan gestured toward the furnace. "When the Red Bud essence began pushing against the secondary medicine, you immediately used your qi to keep the two together."

Ning remembered the moment. The resistance had been minor, but he had noticed it almost immediately and corrected it before it could grow.

Fu Xuan continued, "There was nothing wrong with what you did. The pill still formed successfully. But you chose the most direct solution because you knew you could control it."

That made Ning pause.

She was right.

His qi control was one of the parts of alchemy he trusted most. Whenever something began drifting out of place, directly correcting it was usually the fastest and most reliable solution.

"What would Grandmaster have done instead?"

"I would have lowered the flame slightly."

Ning considered the answer before looking toward the furnace. "To reduce the activity of the Red Bud essence?"

"Yes. Its resistance would have weakened on its own, and the secondary medicine could have settled into it without you holding the two together."

Ning replayed the refinement with that change in mind. It would have required less direct control, but there was something he wasn't certain about.

"Wouldn't that also change how quickly the other medicinal properties were settling, Grandmaster? The temperature was already close to where I wanted it."

"It would," Fu Xuan said. "Which is why you would only lower it briefly. Just enough to change the balance between those two essences before allowing the temperature to recover."

Ning nodded slowly.

That was much subtler than simply using qi to force the two back into balance.

"So why is that preferable?" he asked. "If both methods produce the same result, wouldn't directly controlling the essences give me more certainty?"

Fu Xuan smiled faintly. "That's precisely the habit I'm pointing out."

Ning immediately became more attentive.

"You prefer the solution that gives you the greatest control. That's useful when something truly goes wrong, but every time you directly manipulate medicinal essence, you disturb it to some degree. Once or twice, the difference is negligible. Do it throughout an entire refinement, and those small disturbances begin to add up."

Ning looked at the Red Bud Pill again.

That was something he hadn't considered seriously before. He had always judged an adjustment mainly by whether it solved the problem without causing another one. If the correction was precise enough, he considered it successful.

Fu Xuan was asking him to consider whether the correction had been necessary in the first place.

"If I can achieve the same result by making a smaller change to the conditions, that would preserve more of the medicinal essence's natural state."

"Usually." Fu Xuan nodded. "If directly controlling it is safer, then control it. If changing the flame would disturb three other ingredients just to avoid touching one, then use your qi. There is no virtue in making a refinement harder for yourself."

Ning nodded respectfully.

That made much more sense. Fu Xuan wasn't telling him to intervene less simply for the sake of intervening less. She wanted him to stop defaulting to direct control whenever his perception showed him something he could correct.

It was a flaw that had probably developed precisely because his methods worked so well.

Pure Eyes let him notice problems early, his qi control let him correct them quickly, and repeated success had taught him to trust that combination. Somewhere along the way, I can fix this had started becoming I should fix this.

Those were not quite the same thing.

"I understand, Grandmaster. I need to judge the cause of the change before deciding whether it actually requires direct correction."

"Exactly. Your control is already good. Now you need to learn when to use it."

Ling Xin nodded sagely beside them. "Sometimes you just have to let the medicine do its thing."

"Yes, Master." Ning smiled and readily agreed.

Ling Xin's nose immediately rose a little higher, clearly pleased that her disciple had finally appreciated one of her explanations without complaint.

Fu Xuan picked up the finished pill again. "There's one last thing. You're paying too much attention to purity."

Ning considered that. "Grandmaster means I'm prioritizing it over the overall quality of the pill?"

"Yes. You know better than to judge a pill by purity alone, but from the way you refined, you've clearly started treating it as your main measure of improvement."

Ning couldn't deny that.

Purity was convenient. If one batch reached seventy-eight percent and the next reached eighty, he knew immediately that something had improved. With Know Thyself making him accustomed to measuring his own progress just as precisely, it was probably inevitable that he had started approaching alchemy in much the same way.

Fu Xuan held out the Red Bud Pill. "Look at this one again."

Ning accepted it and examined it more carefully with Pure Eyes. The pill was stable, its medicinal qi properly contained, with no obvious conflict between its properties. It took him several seconds to notice what Fu Xuan wanted him to see.

"The secondary medicinal essence hasn't completely settled."

"Correct. It's minor enough that it won't affect the pill greatly, but the medicinal properties could have been brought together better."

Ning turned the pill between his fingers. "If I corrected that without removing any additional impurities, the purity might barely change."

"Or not change at all."

That was interesting.

Ning already knew that two pills of equal purity weren't necessarily equal in quality, but he had never given much thought to what that meant for his own training. If he kept chasing a higher percentage, he could improve the number while overlooking aspects of the pill that were harder to measure.

"Then Grandmaster thinks I should judge each refinement by more than how much I increased the purity."

"Exactly. Purity is useful, so keep measuring it. Just don't mistake an easy measurement for the whole result."

Ning nodded respectfully. "I'll keep that in mind."

"There's also something you could have done better with this particular pill." Fu Xuan indicated the faint imbalance he had noticed. "Your Spirit Locking was a little too quick."

Ning thought back to the final moments of the refinement. "I began sealing it as soon as the condensation stabilized."

"That was the safest choice, and the seal itself was good. But the medicinal qi was still circulating inside the pill. You could have used that final circulation to settle the remaining imbalance before completely closing it."

Ning examined the pill again.

He had always treated Spirit Locking primarily as preservation. Once the pill reached the state he wanted, his priority became preventing medicinal qi from escaping and preserving everything he had achieved during the earlier stages.

"How far can that be taken, Grandmaster?" he asked. "If I continue guiding the medicinal qi after condensation, there should be a point where the loss from keeping the pill open outweighs whatever improvement I can still make."

Fu Xuan nodded approvingly. "There is. That's the judgment you need to develop. If the remaining imbalance can settle quickly, guide it. If you're still struggling with it after the pill has formed, seal the pill and accept the result. Keeping it open while chasing a small improvement can easily cost you more."

"Yes."

Fu Xuan continued, "Think of it as the end of the same refinement rather than a separate step. The medicinal properties are still settling as you begin the seal, so guide what you reasonably can and close the pill as that process finishes."

Ning nodded.

That connected surprisingly well with Fu Xuan's first criticism. He had been thinking of extraction, Gather Liquid, condensation, and Spirit Locking as distinct procedures. Even at the very end, he was still drawing a firm line between refining the pill and sealing the pill when the two could overlap.

"I understand, Grandmaster."

"Good." Fu Xuan leaned back slightly. "That's enough for now. Don't try to correct everything at once during your next refinement. Work on one thing at a time until it becomes natural."

Ning looked down at the Red Bud Pill once more.

He had entered the room expecting Fu Xuan to judge his ability for the upcoming competition. Instead, she had taken a refinement he considered reliable and pointed out several habits he hadn't even realized he was developing.

Ning rose and bowed respectfully. "Thank you for the guidance, Grandmaster. I'm beginning to understand why Master has always spoken so highly of your teaching."

Ling Xin immediately straightened, looking oddly pleased with herself. "See? I told you Master was amazing."

Fu Xuan gave her a flat look. "He was complimenting me."

"I know." Ling Xin nodded without the slightest embarrassment. "It proves I have excellent judgment in masters."

Ning glanced between the two of them and wisely kept his thoughts to himself.

Apparently, humility wasn't a particularly abundant virtue in this lineage.

"Come with me," Fu Xuan said as she rose. "There are other things I want to discuss."

Ning followed her and Ling Xin through the rear door into a spacious courtyard connecting several buildings. Stone paths wound alongside narrow streams and carefully maintained gardens, with medicinal plants growing almost everywhere he looked.

His attention immediately wandered.

Beneath one of the pavilions grew a Seven-Leaf Moon Orchid, the silver veins across its leaves suggesting it was already several decades old. Farther ahead stood a Redheart Peach Tree, along with several medicinal plants Ning couldn't recognize at all.

His steps slowed slightly.

Fu Xuan noticed where he was looking. "Ling Xin mentioned that you've already earned your First-Grade Spiritual Farmer certificate."

"Yes, Grandmaster."

"He's quite good too," Ling Xin added immediately. "He takes care of most of the fields on our peak."

"Mostly the ordinary ones," Ning corrected. "Senior Wang still handles anything particularly valuable or difficult."

Wang Zhen was the Second-Grade Spiritual Farmer contracted to maintain the medicinal fields on Spiritual Flower Peak. He had far more experience than Ning, particularly with valuable plants that required specialized care, and Ning had learned quite a lot from him.

Fu Xuan nodded. "Wang Zhen is a capable spiritual farmer. Learning from him will do you good."

"I've already benefited greatly from his advice, Grandmaster."

Fu Xuan glanced toward the medicinal plants surrounding them. "Has your experience with farming helped your alchemy?"

"Quite a bit." Ning thought for a moment. "Mostly with understanding the ingredients themselves. Growing a plant makes it easier to recognize how its medicinal properties change with its environment and when it's harvested. It also helps when preparing ingredients, since I have a better idea of where and how the medicinal essence is concentrated."

"Good. Keep learning both." Fu Xuan continued along the path. "Most alchemists only handle medicinal plants after they've been harvested and prepared. Knowing how they were grown gives you another perspective."

Ning nodded. After today's assessment, he could see even more ways the two professions complemented each other.

Ling Xin, meanwhile, looked pleased enough that Ning suspected she was about to start listing his other accomplishments.

Fortunately, Fu Xuan kept walking.

They soon entered another building overlooking the garden. Unlike the refining chamber, this was clearly part of Fu Xuan's residence. Shelves filled with books and jade slips lined the walls, while several medicinal plants occupied the spots nearest the windows.

Fu Xuan settled beside an open window and began preparing tea while Ning and Ling Xin took their seats across from her.

When the tea was ready and Fu Xuan poured the first cup, Ling Xin's eyes immediately lit up.

"Ah, you're serving this one!" She leaned forward with obvious delight. "It's been ages since I've had your tea, Master."

Fu Xuan glanced at her. "It wouldn't have been ages if you remembered to visit your master occasionally."

"I visit."

"You come when you need medicinal ingredients, want me to examine one of your experiments, or have caused a problem you would prefer someone else to solve."

Ling Xin thought about it. "Those still count as visits."

Fu Xuan gave her a long look before turning to Ning. "Don't learn this from your master."

Ning accepted the cup with both hands. "Of course, Grandmaster."

"Hey." Ling Xin pointed toward his cup. "More importantly, make sure you drink all of that. Master can be considered a Third-Grade Tea Master, and getting her to serve her good tea isn't easy."

A Third-Grade Tea Master was capable of preparing spiritual teas that could benefit even Purple Mansion cultivators, so the title was no small distinction.

Ning wasn't particularly surprised that spiritual tea had developed into an entire cultivation craft of its own. Spiritual cooking already existed, after all, and aside from water, tea and alcohol were probably the two most popular drinks in the cultivation world.

Considering how often cultivators seemed to spend their time either calmly drinking tea or dramatically emptying wine jars before doing something reckless, it would have been stranger if neither had developed its own profession.

Fu Xuan calmly poured Ling Xin's cup. "Visit more often and perhaps you'll discover otherwise."

Ling Xin immediately smiled. "I'll keep that in mind."

Judging by Fu Xuan's expression, she had very little faith that she would.

Ning decided this was none of his business and reached for his tea.

The first sip sent a gentle warmth down his throat and into his chest. His muscles gradually loosened, the tension in his shoulders faded, and even his breathing became slower and more comfortable. It felt remarkably similar to sinking into a perfectly heated bath after an exhausting day.

Then Ning noticed that relaxation wasn't its only effect.

With Know Thyself, he could sense a small amount of remarkably mild spiritual energy spreading alongside the warmth. Instead of gathering in his dantian, most of it seeped quietly into his flesh and meridians. The effect was slight, but it was genuinely nourishing his body.

More interestingly, the energy seemed almost completely neutral. Ning followed a small strand as it circulated deeper and encountered the lingering influence of the Worm Overlord. Rather than conflicting with it, the tea's energy gradually took on a trace of coldness before being absorbed.

Most importantly, it tasted so damn good that Ning couldn't help letting out a long, satisfied sigh.

This was the taste of heaven in a cup.

Fu Xuan noticed his reaction and took a leisurely sip from her own cup. "The leaves come from a Four-Season Mist Tea Tree. They're allowed to mature through an entire cycle before being harvested, and the roasting process is deliberately slow. Too much heat damages the spiritual essence, while too little prevents the different properties from settling together. A properly prepared batch takes nearly a month."

Ning glanced down at the pale tea with considerably greater appreciation. "No wonder the energy is so gentle."

"You noticed that as well?"

"Yes, Grandmaster. It seems almost neutral. Some of it nourished my body directly, but when a portion encountered the cold influence within me, it gradually took on the same nature."

Fu Xuan nodded. "That's one of the reasons this tea is valuable. Many spiritual medicines have strong properties of their own, and the cultivator has to accommodate them. This tea is different. Its nature is mild enough to adapt to the person drinking it."

She lifted her cup slightly. "Much like water. Pour it into a round vessel and it becomes round; pour it into a square vessel and it becomes square."

Ning considered that as he took another sip. Something about the comparison reminded him of an old saying.

"Water yields to the vessel, flows around the stone, and accepts whatever enters it without losing its own nature," he mused. "I suppose there's something to be learned from that."

Ling Xin stared at him for a moment before laughing. "I thought you hated all that cryptic metaphor talk. You're always complaining that most of it is, as you like to put it, a 'nothing burger.'"

"Only when it's written into cultivation techniques and spells." Ning's expression darkened slightly. "Those should contain instructions."

One day, he would find whoever had started the tradition of hiding perfectly useful cultivation knowledge behind mountains, rivers, moons, and cryptic poetry.

Dead or alive, that person owed him an explanation.

Fu Xuan regarded him over the rim of her cup. "For someone who complains so much about prose, you seem reasonably comfortable with words."

"I've simply gotten used to them, Grandmaster. I didn't have much of a choice."

And wasn't that the truth? After reading enough cultivation manuals, deciphering unnecessarily profound metaphors had practically become a survival skill.

"One would hope so," Fu Xuan said. "Especially after writing all those books."

Ning's cup stopped halfway to his mouth.

Ling Xin slowly turned toward him. "What books?"

Ning quietly finished his sip. "Nothing important. Just something I wrote in my spare time."

"You wrote books?" Ling Xin leaned closer. "What kind?"

"A small hobby."

"What's the name?"

Ning became deeply interested in the tea leaves floating at the bottom of his cup.

Fu Xuan raised an eyebrow. "You never told her?"

Ning looked at his grandmaster with a silent plea for mercy, only to find none whatsoever.

"Considering some of the rumors I've heard, I assumed she already knew." Fu Xuan took another sip. "Though I must admit, some of the things you've written are rather... unconventional."

Ning felt the judgment contained in that brief pause more keenly than if she had simply criticized him outright.

What had he done to deserve this?

He had merely been a hardworking young cultivator trying to survive in an unforgiving cultivation world. Was earning an honest living such a terrible crime?

Ning slowly adopted the solemn expression of a man forced to reopen old wounds. "Master, before you judge me, you have to understand what my life was like back then. I was a poor outer disciple with a mid-grade spiritual root, no family backing, no master, and barely enough spirit stones to cultivate. I had ten acres of farmland that demanded money long before they were generous enough to give any back."

His voice grew heavier as the old grievances returned. "Pills cost spirit stones. Techniques cost spirit stones. Seeds cost spirit stones. Formations cost spirit stones. Even learning how to make more spirit stones somehow cost spirit stones. Other disciples had families sending them resources, elders guiding them, or inheritances waiting for them."

Ning lowered his gaze toward his tea.

"I had a hoe."

Ling Xin stared at him, momentarily speechless.

"Every morning, I woke before sunrise to tend the fields, cultivated whenever I had time, took missions whenever I needed money, and prayed that nothing expensive decided to die." Ning sighed, looking every bit like a man who had endured hardships far beyond his years.

All that was missing now was some mournful instrumental music in the background. Then the atmosphere would be perfect.

"Those were difficult days, Master. Just one young cultivator, alone against the cruel machinery of cultivation."

Ling Xin's expression softened. "Ning..."

Ning looked up, almost touched that his master had finally come to understand the depths of his suffering.

"What did you write?"

His expression went blank.

Darn it! The sympathy approach didn't work.

"It's nothing important, Master. Just some things I wrote to earn spirit stones. It's all in the past now."

That only made Ling Xin more curious. Ning was usually straightforward with her, so seeing him work this hard to avoid a question practically guaranteed that she would remember it.

"All right, that's enough." Fu Xuan intervened before Ling Xin could continue. She looked toward Ning. "I don't particularly care what you write in your spare time, but be more careful now that you've attracted attention. Rumors that would once have gone unnoticed will spread much more easily."

"Yes, Grandmaster." Ning finally breathed a sigh of relief.

Beside him, Ling Xin was still watching him with barely contained curiosity.

With Ning's literary career temporarily spared further investigation, Fu Xuan turned to more serious matters.

"I examined the pill you've been developing, Ling Xin."

Ling Xin's attention shifted instantly. "So, what do you think of it, Master?"

"It's good for a prototype, although there is still considerable room for refinement." Fu Xuan refilled her cup. "More importantly, I believe the original idea came from your disciple."

Ning looked between them.

Ling Xin nodded. "Yes. It's based on the external and internal pressure method Ning gave me."

Ning paused.

He hadn't even known where he would begin turning the Spiritual Pressure Cultivation Method into a pill, yet Ling Xin had apparently gone from hearing the underlying principle to producing an actual prototype.

Sometimes it was easy to forget that beneath all the teasing, impulsive challenges, questionable explanations, and spontaneous experiments, his master really was an absurdly talented alchemist.

Fu Xuan nodded. "The principle is promising. If refined properly, the pill could reproduce the internal response your method requires while external spiritual pressure provides the other half. For our lineage, the benefits could be considerable."

"That's what I thought." Ling Xin became noticeably more animated. "The Qi Condensation version isn't too difficult anymore. The problem is Foundation Building. Their circulation and physical tolerance are completely different, so simply increasing the strength won't work. I need a different balance."

"Then find it."

Ling Xin grinned. "I will."

Ning quietly drank his tea.

Geniuses are terrifying.

Fu Xuan's attention shifted toward him.

"I believe your ability to develop the original method had quite a lot to do with your supernatural talent, Know Thyself."

Ning nodded without hesitation. There wasn't much point hiding it from Fu Xuan.

"Yes, Grandmaster. It helped considerably. Being able to observe the changes in my own body made experimenting with the method much safer."

"I thought so."

Fu Xuan took another sip of tea, considering him for a moment. "Ling Xin has told me quite a bit about your cultivation as well. From what I understand, alchemy and spiritual farming are hardly the only things you've been practicing."

Ning glanced at his master.

Ling Xin smiled innocently at him.

Apparently, Fu Xuan really had received a fairly comprehensive report on her new grand-disciple.

"Tell me what you're currently practicing," Fu Xuan continued. "Include the techniques you're still learning. I want to understand how you've been dividing your time."

That immediately caught Ning's interest.

He had originally expected today's meeting to revolve around alchemy and the upcoming competition. If Fu Xuan was willing to look over the rest of his cultivation as well, he certainly wasn't going to turn down the opportunity.

"Of course, Grandmaster."

Ning started with his cultivation technique before moving through his body-refinement arts, movement techniques, spear and archery skills, spells, and the various other methods he had accumulated over his time in the sect.

There wasn't much he needed to leave out. Almost everything he had learned since arriving in this world had come from the Pure Qi Sect in one way or another.

Thinking about it, that was rather convenient. For all the strange things Ning had gotten involved with, he didn't have some forbidden inheritance or mysterious old monster hiding in a ring that he needed to explain away.

By the time he finished going through everything, Fu Xuan had fallen silent.

She regarded him for several seconds before finally saying, "You're practicing too many things."

Ning wasn't particularly surprised.

He had heard variations of the same criticism before, and looking at the sheer number of techniques he had just listed, it wasn't difficult to understand why.

"I'm aware that I've spread myself quite broadly, Grandmaster."

"Broadly is putting it mildly." Fu Xuan set down her cup. "There's nothing wrong with learning a technique because it fills a genuine need. The problem begins when you keep adding them. Eventually, you'll have dozens of techniques you can use and very few you've taken far enough to truly rely on."

Ning lowered his gaze toward his tea for a moment.

That was a fair criticism.

His breadth hadn't happened entirely by accident. Most of the techniques he'd picked up had solved some problem he genuinely had at the time, and several had later become useful in ways he hadn't originally expected. Still, when he looked at the whole collection at once, he could understand why Fu Xuan was concerned.

Fu Xuan continued, "Do you know the story of Sect Leader Ge Xuan's Clear Sword Heart?"

"A little," Ning said. "I know it's related to his supernatural ability. It allows him to maintain an exceptionally clear state of mind, makes him highly resistant to illusions and other disturbances of the mind, and helps him remain certain of his own path even when facing doubt or confusion. I don't know much about how he actually developed it, though."

"Then you know the result, not how he reached it."

Fu Xuan rested one hand against her cup. "His supernatural ability gave him exceptional potential for developing a Sword Heart, but potential alone didn't create it. When he was still in the early stages of Qi Condensation, he reportedly swung his sword thirty thousand times every day."

Ning blinked.

Thirty thousand?

He automatically began doing the math.

Even if each swing took only two seconds, that was more than sixteen hours. Every single day.

Ning stared into his tea.

When exactly had the man cultivated? Eaten? Slept? Did the Sect Leader simply consider those optional activities?

Perhaps the Clear Sword Heart hadn't been developed so much as beaten into existence thirty thousand swings at a time.

Fu Xuan continued, apparently seeing nothing particularly unreasonable about any of this. "He kept that routine for years. The same fundamental movement, repeated until he no longer needed to consciously consider his grip, his stance, his breathing, the movement of his qi, or the path of the blade. Eventually, all of it became instinct, and that foundation allowed his Sword Heart to truly take form."

She looked directly at Ning.

"You understand why I'm telling you this."

Ning did.

Fu Xuan obviously wasn't suggesting that he imitate the Sect Leader's frankly concerning relationship with sleep. She was showing him what could happen when someone devoted that much time and effort to a single path.

The problem was that Ning had never really cultivated that way.

His approach had always been almost the opposite. When he encountered a problem, he learned something that could solve it.

If he lacked mobility, he practiced movement techniques. If he needed ranged attacks, he learned archery. Farming gave him resources, alchemy gave him pills and another source of income, while techniques like Pure Eyes, Phantom Veil, and Turtle Breathing each covered weaknesses or opened possibilities he hadn't possessed before.

At the time, every choice had made sense.

Put together, however, the result was the rather ridiculous list he had just recited to Fu Xuan.

"I understand, Grandmaster," Ning said. "You're saying that even if every technique I learn is useful on its own, there's still a cost to dividing my time between so many of them."

"Exactly." Fu Xuan nodded. "Sect Leader Ge Xuan had his supernatural ability, but he still spent years building the foundation that allowed him to make full use of it. Talent may determine what you're capable of reaching. It doesn't remove the work required to reach it."

Ning nodded slowly.

He couldn't disagree with that. In fact, he had seen enough talented cultivators by now to know exactly what Fu Xuan meant.

The problem was that his circumstances were somewhat different.

Ge Xuan had his Sword Heart, Ling Xin had her absurd instinct for alchemy, and Xiao Fan seemed to acquire new bloodline abilities whenever fate felt particularly generous. Their strengths naturally pushed them toward certain paths.

Know Thyself wasn't like that. It showed Ning himself with extraordinary clarity, but it didn't tell him what he ought to become. Perhaps that was why he'd ended up building his cultivation differently, collecting useful advantages and finding ways to make them support one another.

Fu Xuan watched his expression for a moment. "You don't entirely agree."

Ning considered his answer before shaking his head. "I agree with Grandmaster's reasoning. I just don't think abandoning most of what I've learned would necessarily be the best path for me."

Fu Xuan showed no displeasure. "Why?"

"Because I've been trying to make the breadth itself useful."

That finally drew a little more interest from her.

Ning set his cup down.

"That's also part of what I've been trying to accomplish with the Infinite Circle."

Fu Xuan was quiet for a moment before nodding. "I see."

She had already heard about the technique from Ling Xin, but Ning could tell that something had finally clicked into place.

"So that's why you've been collecting so many techniques," she said. "You never intended to master all of them."

"Not all of them, no."

Most techniques progressed through roughly the same stages: Starting, Minor Accomplishment, Great Accomplishment, and finally Mastery. Reaching Starting meant Ning had properly learned a technique and could use it, but there was still an enormous gap between that and what someone at Mastery could achieve.

Infinite Circle couldn't erase that gap. No amount of stored qi would suddenly give Ning years of experience, better control, or a deeper understanding of the technique.

Power, however, was another matter.

"If it's something I use regularly, then of course I'll keep practicing it," Ning explained. "My spear arts, movement techniques, things like that aren't going to stop at starting just because I have the Infinite Circle."

Fu Xuan nodded for him to continue.

"But suppose I find a spell that's extremely useful against one particular kind of enemy. I might only need it once every few years. Spending years mastering it just in case that situation appears doesn't make much sense."

"So you learn enough to use it properly and move on."

"Exactly. Then, if I ever actually need it, I can use the Infinite Circle to compensate for some of the difference in power."

That was the idea. Ning didn't need to master every technique he learned. He only needed to become proficient enough to use each one reliably, then use the accumulated qi within the Infinite Circle to make up for what they lacked in raw power.

If it worked, he could eventually build up a collection of techniques broad enough to have an answer for almost any problem.

Fu Xuan considered that for a moment. "That makes much more sense than trying to master everything you've learned."

Ning smiled. "That was the idea."

"Don't look so pleased. I said more sense."

Ning's smile faded slightly.

Ling Xin snickered into her tea.

Fu Xuan ignored them both. "There is a limit to how far that can take you. More qi can strengthen a technique, but it won't improve your understanding of it. It won't give you the control or judgment of someone who has practiced it for decades."

"Of course." Ning nodded.

Fu Xuan tapped a finger against the table. "I understand your reasoning now."

Then she paused.

"The problem is that your Infinite Circle can't actually do most of that yet."

Ning sighed. "Yes."

At present, the technique was still crude. It could accumulate prepared qi, but once Ning drew heavily on that reserve, controlling exactly how much came out was difficult. Integrating that power cleanly into other techniques was harder still.

He knew what he wanted.

Getting there without blowing himself up was proving considerably more difficult.

"I've tried several approaches," Ning said, "but I haven't found one I'm comfortable testing with a full reserve."

"Good."

Ning looked at her.

Fu Xuan's expression remained perfectly calm. "Knowing when not to experiment is also a useful skill."

Ning couldn't argue with that.

Fu Xuan glanced toward Ling Xin. "Your master told me about the Infinite Circle some time ago. She wanted me to look over what you've done and see whether there was a safe way to continue."

Ning turned toward his master.

Ling Xin smiled proudly. "See? I was helping."

"I never doubted you, Master."

"Good."

Fu Xuan gave her youngest disciple a look. "Don't become too pleased with yourself. You came to me because you couldn't figure out how he was supposed to advance it safely."

Ling Xin's smile immediately disappeared. "I understood what he was doing."

"You told me, and I quote, 'He makes the qi go around, keeps putting more in, and somehow it doesn't explode.'"

Ning slowly turned toward his master.

Ling Xin crossed her arms. "Was I wrong?"

"No, Master." Well, not technically anyway.

Fu Xuan sighed.

Ning wisely reached for his tea.

"Your master understands cultivation extremely well," Fu Xuan continued, "but she relies heavily on instinct. That becomes a problem when she has to explain why something works."

Ling Xin frowned. "I'm a good teacher."

Ning wisely concentrated on his tea.

"Disciple."

Ning immediately looked up. "Master has always given me whatever help I needed."

Ling Xin brightened. "See?"

Ning smiled. "Of course, Master."

Fu Xuan looked between them before a faint smile appeared on her face. "Ling Xin truly did find herself a good disciple."

"Exactly." Ling Xin nodded enthusiastically, accepting the compliment as though it was a given.

Fu Xuan ignored her and returned her attention to him.

"I do have several ideas regarding your Infinite Circle."

Ning's expression immediately became serious.

"But explaining them while we're sitting here would accomplish very little." Fu Xuan finished the last of her tea and set down her cup. "Your technique was developed through experimentation. I would rather see what it currently does before telling you how to change it."

She stood.

"Show me."

Ning rose as well and followed her gaze toward the courtyard.

Fu Xuan added, "And don't hold back. I want to see the strongest application you can currently manage."
