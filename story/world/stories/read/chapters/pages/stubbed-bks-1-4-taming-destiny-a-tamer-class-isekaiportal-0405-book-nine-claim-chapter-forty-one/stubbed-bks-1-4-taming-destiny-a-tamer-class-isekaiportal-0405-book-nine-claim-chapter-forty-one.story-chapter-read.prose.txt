Three days later, I feel considerably better. My mind feels like it’s back to its usual condition rather than slowed by fatigue, and my Energy channels are significantly less inflamed than they were. And I’m not the only one – as I look around I see the unlined faces of those who are rested, upright postures rather than bent from exhaustion. Standing near me, Bastet and Ninja are also standing far more proudly, having both fully settled into their Evolutions.

That’s not to say the last few days have been relaxing – in fact, they’ve been anything but. It feels like the jobs have been never ending – processing lists of the dead, reviewing resources used in both battle distributing materials harvested from the roc, determining those who behaved with outstanding bravery during the battles…and sniffing out those with less virtuous intentions. However, all that work has meant that we have a far better picture of what is happening across the ships than we used to.

My investigators have managed to question every single member of the fleet – and we now know that the sabotage that almost killed everyone on my ship might have been the most egregious, but it wasn’t alone.

With everything finally reckoned, it’s time to deal with the rot that has crept in. I want this to be fully public, so I’ve ordered the captains to bring our ships as close together as we dare, and asked Elarion to create a platform of ice to serve as a stage.

Now, I stand at the raised centre of the ice floe, surrounded by my Bonded, those who have been found guilty of sabotage, and their guards. On the ships around us, everyone who isn’t needed to sail the ships is lining the railings and rigging – whether because they want to see justice done, or because this is better entertainment than anything else on offer.

Turning my attention onto those around, I survey those I have to sentence today. In addition to the two who tried to kill me and everyone else on my ship, my companions found three others who had committed acts of sabotage, and eleven more who have agreed to work for our enemies but haven’t actually done anything yet.

The sixteen people stand before me now in two different groups. The five who have committed sabotage are all bound with chains at their wrists and ankles – a security measure, and a statement for the crowd watching.

I turn first to the largest group, my gaze playing over each of them slowly, memorising their faces and body types so I will know them at a glance in the future. The people react in various ways to my probing gaze – some shiver, some try to make themselves smaller. Only one meets my eyes defiantly – I will need to watch her carefully.

“You have all admitted to intentions to betray us to our enemies,” I start steadily, without flourish or ceremony – they don’t deserve it. Around me and on the ships, everything goes silent. Even the sea birds seem to cry more quietly, the waves hushing their lapping. “Some of you,” and my eyes linger on those my companions pointed out to me, making them shiver, “have agreed to spy. Others,” my gaze shifts targets,” have agreed to cause acts of sabotage when asked or a good moment appears. For your intended disloyalty, I am sure that few would fault me for ordering your execution here and now.”

I give the words a moment to settle, watching the blood drain out of several faces and hearing the anticipatory quality of the hush on the ships around. One of the accused even casts herself to her knees on the icy floor mumbling pleas. Another looks like he's a moment away from following her. When I raise my hand, though, even the pleading dies away in an instant.

“However, such is not my way. Though you have intended disloyalty, you have not actually acted on it. For now, I will allow you to continue in your roles knowing that you have been identified – and that you are being watched,” I warn. I expect they’ve already taken my meaning, but just in case some of them are particularly dense…. “If I ever receive a report that you have posed a threat to the expedition – whether through words, actions, or the failure to act when you should have, we will be having a very different conversation. Do you understand?”

There’s a murmur of subdued agreement.

“I asked, do you understand?” I snap. Perhaps the stage is an appropriate prop – this feels about as performative as anything I ever did in a school play. But the consequences are far more real. Though I truly don’t want to punish people for something they haven’t even done yet – for all that some might argue that agreeing to be disloyal is almost as bad as acting on that intention, I don’t agree – I can’t afford to let them go without being assured that they have taken the warning to heart. And not only them, but those watching too, in case some of them ever consider betrayal in the future.

Either way, my brief show of anger has had the results I want.

“Yes, Lord Titanbend,” several of them yelp in response while two more of them drop to their knees and prostrate themselves, shaking in fear.

It doesn’t give me any pleasure, but I do feel a sense of grim satisfaction. If scaring thoughts of disloyalty out of these people protects those close to me, I will consider it a small price to pay.

“Then, dismissed. I do not want to hear of any of you again unless it’s with words of praise for exceptional service.”

The eleven before me don’t take long to scramble away, seeking the rope ladders up to their own ships. Though, they find little anonymity even when they are back – I notice how even as everyone’s attention returns to me, the eleven are given something of a wide berth and several looks of disgust are sent their way.

I make a mental note to make sure that the eleven aren’t isolated completely – people showing their displeasure with potential traitors is one thing, but shunning them completely won’t lead to anything good.

Next, I call forward the three who are previously unidentified saboteurs.

“Ker Ullusim, you are accused of intentionally ruining or ‘losing’ multiple weapons, leading to a shortage on Lord Softrain’s ship during the last battle. This most likely resulted in unnecessary deaths. Kera Terril, you are accused of damaging protective runes on Lord Fell’s ship, weakening his shields. Ker Mus, you are accused of spoiling needed food supplies in your role as cook’s assistant. Do any of you deny the accusations?” They all look like they’re about to protest, fear in their eyes. I add a qualification. “Note that if you do claim innocence, you shall be required to take a truth serum to confirm it.”

At my cue, Sera Kerst, River’s teacher and our most experienced resident Alchemist, steps forward with a glass potion vial filled with a vivid pink potion glittering between her fingers.

Mus’ face drains of blood and Terril swallows and shuts her mouth firmly.

I raise an eyebrow at them.

“I know you are all capable of speaking. If you do not choose to refute the accusations, I will take your silence as admission of guilt.” Ullusim stares at the potion fearfully while the other two transfer their gazes to the decks below. I wait for a long moment, allowing the silence to draw out uncomfortably.

When they have had ample time to speak and are clearly not intending to, I nod.

“Ker Ullusim, Ker Mus, Kera Terril, I pronounce you guilty of sabotage and the cause of more loss of life than necessary.”

Murmurs go through the crowd around us, the tone dissatisfied and focused on the three saboteurs. I even hear a few swear words hissed at them.

Mus and Terril flinch as they sense the threat from those around, perhaps those who blame them for lost friends. Those on Josiah’s and Valence’s ships are particularly angry, understandably.

“Again, execution would certainly not be considered too harsh for your actions. I would also be entirely within my rights to sentence you to the collar.” In a situation where there are no judicators available to convict and sentence criminals, a lord’s justice is the next choice. However, I am personally very reticent to sentence people to something I’m morally opposed to. That said, there must be consequences for their actions.

My words make the three of them flinch and shake again. None can meet my gaze, though Mus is looking around wildly as if there might be another way out of this. There isn’t.

“I am a strong believer that the punishment should fit the crime. Ker Ullusim and Ker Terril, your interference caused unnecessary deaths, particularly among the fighters. Therefore, I sentence you to replace them for the duration of this competition.” Terril’s face pales even further; Ullusim looks like he’s been punched in the gut. “You shall train with the fighters and be on the front lines of any fight that eventuates.

“Similarly, Ker Mus, your actions have reduced the amount of food we have available. Therefore, you will work with the fishers and farmers to replace what you have spoiled. If we are forced onto short rations, yours will be shorter still.” Mus swallows but looks slightly relieved – perhaps that he hasn’t been assigned to the fighters like the other two.

“Your superiors will report on your cooperation,” I continue, giving them all warning looks. “Disobedience or attempts to dodge your new duties will be met with immediate and severe punishment. At the end of the competition, I shall review your cases. If you have worked hard to make up for your actions, then you shall have your freedom. If you have not, you will be given a new sentence appropriate to your attitude. Report to your ship’s lord to have your supervisor assigned.”

Valence and Josiah, like the other vessel leaders, are also on the floe with me. I meet their eyes and they nod at me in acknowledgement, though I notice hesitation in Josiah’s gaze.

Can we trust them? he asks through our new Alliance Bond. He’s practised enough with it that he’s able to direct it to me personally. They have worked against our interests once before.

Do you plan to leave them unsupervised? I ask slightly pointedly. I don’t need a verbal rejection – his immediate disgusted reaction answers it all. Then we give them a chance. If they prove themselves to be a threat again now they know the consequences, then I will deal with them. Permanently.

If we’re all still alive, Josiah mutters a little darkly – I’m not sure I was meant to hear that. I respond all the same.

That’s why we keep an eye on them, I point out again. It should never get that far.

Josiah is a little reluctant, but accepting. I wave at the three saboteurs and they hurriedly step back, Mus almost stumbling over his chain in his haste.

Finally, I turn to the two who have committed the greatest crimes in my eyes – and everyone else’s, though not for the same reason.

“Kera Wallis, step forwards.” The herbalist does so, her lips pressed in a thin line, her hands shaking slightly and making the short chain between her manacles jingle. “You are accused of attempted murder, of both a Great House heir and a whole ship of both combatants and noncombatants who are allied to your own liege lord. Do you deny these accusations?” I nod at Sera Kerst who is still there with her pink serum. “Note that if you do claim innocence, you shall be required to take a truth serum to confirm it.”

It’s mostly a formality to clearly show that I’m attempting to have a fair trial – she has already admitted her guilt to me. Now, in response to my question, she glances over to Elarion whose face seems to have been carven from stone, his eyes fixed somewhere over my left shoulder. Finding no support there, she redirects her gaze at the surface beneath our feet, giving a faint shake of her head.

“Then I pronounce you guilty,” I say evenly. “However, as you did not act alone, I will deal with your coconspirator before deciding your sentence. Ser Falkonir, step forward.”

With a glare on his face and anger in his movements that doesn’t quite cover up the fear eating at him below the surface, the Warrior does so. He’s one of the reasons I decided to move forward with the chains – as a Classer, he could do a good deal of damage if he suddenly decided that he’d prefer to go down fighting than accept my sentence. But, with enchanted chains that Master Mirena carved herself to sap his stats and block his Skills, he wouldn’t make it very far before being taken down.

“You, too, are accused of attempted murder, of both a Great House heir and a whole ship of both combatants and noncombatants who are allied to your own liege lord. Do you deny these actions?”

Unlike Wallis, Falkonir doesn’t glance at Elarion, nor does he turn his eyes to the ice below. Instead, he sneers at me.

“I do not. And I would do it again if I thought it would work. The sooner the Titanbends are wiped from the face of Moriax, the better!”

I wonder if he expects to be executed – technically, by the laws of Moriax, a commoner speaking like this to a Great House heir would be worthy of significant punishment by itself. I’m very tempted – I’ve been warring with myself over this for days now.

But my companions reminded me, both explicitly and simply by their existence, that where there’s life, there’s hope. Several of them tried to kill me – even Bastet did the first time we met – but now they’ve become my closest allies. Death is the end of the road and I will resort to it if I must…but it’s not my first choice. Especially since I have far better options available to me to ensure their compliance.

“I will not execute you,” I start, and a ripple of surprise goes out through the crowd around me. I note some muffled shouts of protest too – apparently some were hoping for that sentence. “However, you have incurred a debt which will take much to repay. And like the previous three, you will repay it in service. A month for every soul on board this ship who you would have condemned to death. A little over twenty years in total. Even once the competition has ended, you will continue to serve this community – and you will do so loyally and willingly or I will rethink my decision not to have you executed.”

I stare at the two until I’m certain that my words have sunk in. Given the maelstrom of emotions that I see play out over their faces, this has come completely out of left-field for them.

“However,” I continue and the murmur of voices dies away. “This is not enough. The threat you posed to us was of a magnitude I will not risk being repeated.” My quiet but implacable words still manage to ring out through the utter silence that grips the deck. Even the waves seem to be lapping more quietly against the ship.

It seems justified when I consider that I’m going to step over a boundary that I put in place for myself only a couple of months ago when I was deciding what to do with a group of bandits. But here is the difference: unless I kill them or set them adrift on the ocean by themselves – which is just another form of execution – these two continue to be active threats to me and mine. Much like the enemy samuran Tier twos, these are hostile combatants and I can’t afford not to nullify their threat, no matter how I might feel about it personally.

The two conspirators tense as the pause stretches on, their changing expressions being replaced solely by apprehension.

“You attacked me and mine with intention to kill,” I continue finally, quietly. “Neither of you are noble, therefore I am exercising my right to Bind you to me.”

“No!” Falkonir cries, taking a determined step forward. Before he can do more than that, two of Maxen’s burliest fighters grip onto the chain between his wrists to pin him in place, He wrestles with them but, his stats drained to almost a baseline human’s, he can’t break free. “I will never submit to a Titanbend!”

I fix him with a stern gaze and then step down from my platform, girding myself for the unpleasantness to come.

“Prove it to me,” I challenge, then trigger my Class Skill.

The resistance that meets me is clearly as much as Ser Falkonir can muster, but, Classer or not, Willpower clearly isn’t something he’s dedicated many stat points to. No matter how much he tries to push me away; no matter what insults and threats he calls down on my head, he fails to make me falter even for a moment.

Personally, I do my best to pay no attention to his words that fade from threats into pleading, to his emotions that roil with anger that conceals the deathly fear below. I can’t let these people have a second chance to betray us, I remind myself as I reach arm’s length of Falkonir.

When the Bond snaps into place, I feel vaguely nauseous, yet I cannot regret it.

“Cease struggling,” I order Falkonir, holding the Bond tightly. He stills, his eyes the only place now that shows his defiance.

Wallis recoils as I approach, already pleading before I trigger the Skill. Unsurprisingly, as a non-Classer, she’s even easier to overcome than Falkonir and it isn’t long before I feel a second sickening Bond connect us.

“You will obey your supervisors in both letter and spirit, you will never act against me or any of those on this fleet, you will confess to your supervisor any intentions to harm others or yourself, you will serve those on my ship as if they were your dearest comrades. Do you understand?” I grind out, enforcing each of my orders with the Bond.

“Yes, my lord,” Wallis whispers, eyes fixed on the icy surface beneath her feet even as tears drip from her chin.

“Yes, master,” spits Falkonir, his eyes flashing bright with defiance. But I know better – I can feel the desolation that the defiance barely masks. Alone, he will crumple. Still, I’d better reinforce the orders from time to time – this is the first time I’ve needed to wield the Bond so blatantly and I don’t know how long they’ll last if the subjects are actively fighting them.

I turn to eye their guards.

“Show them to their new quarters, please,” I direct. “You may remove the chains then.

“Yes, my lord,” the guard to the right acknowledges, bowing, then nudges Falkonir none-too-gently over to the correct rope ladder. Wallis doesn’t need any such direction, following behind, her shoulders drooping.

I step back up onto the platform. The show isn’t yet over.

“Thus you can see that betrayal and sabotage will not be tolerated,” I say, using my air-magic to make sure my words will be heard by all present. “However, to show you that loyalty and excellence will be rewarded as well, let us recognise those who particularly distinguished themselves. Ker Iliam, step forward, please.”

There’s a commotion on Valence’s vessel and a man soon shimmies down the rope ladder. He approaches me with nervousness written clearly on his face. Reaching the platform, he bows deeply.

“My lord?”

I smile at him.

“Ker Iliam, reports indicate that when two of your comrades fell into the sea during the battle with the wind roc, you risked your life to swim out to them with a rope. They owe their lives to you and I wish to give you my thanks.”

Iliam’s face colours and he looks a little awkward.

“Just…just doin’ what anyone else woulda done,” he tells me gruffly.

My smile deepens.

“Clearly not everyone else did do that,” I point out. “And as well as my thanks, I’d like to offer you this.” Opening my Inventory, I pull out a bag. From the bag I withdraw a gleaming stone. Iliam’s breath catches when he sees the stone, recognising it at once. Murmurs pass between those watching – they know what it is too. “I believe you are a fisherman by trade – a warrior only when you must be. Is that right?”

“I…it is, my lord,” Iliam replies hoarsely.

I lean forward and offer him the deep blue stone on my open palm.

“Then take this Fisherman Class stone.”

For a moment he seems frozen, then he reaches out with a shaking hand and takes it. Eyeing it for a long moment, his hand closes tightly around it. When he looks up, I see the gleam of tears in his eyes.

“My lord, how can I ever thank you?”

“Use your new Class to benefit the fleet – that will be thanks enough,” I tell him firmly, then stand upright again and call the next name – Iliam needs space more than more attention.

The list of names is long, but I don’t mind too much. I’d far rather be handing out Class stones than forcing Bonds, and every new Classer helps bolster our forces just a little. Even the crafter Classers will be hardier or stronger than the average human – or they will be once they’ve levelled up a bit.

Finally, I dismiss the crowd and they go willingly, many congratulating those who have earned a Class. The trial seems almost forgotten – though not by everyone.

When I step off the stage, Elarion blocks my way, his expressionless face seeming to have been carven from stone.

“You were more merciful than I expected,” he says finally, his eyes fixed to the horizon. I’m not sure how to interpret his tone – it seems to be equal parts chiding for my naivety and gratitude for the sake of those who acted out of loyalty to him, however misguided their actions.

“I believe in restorative justice, not retributive,” I tell Elarion quietly, sensing that this is a moment to be completely honest with him. “If all a punishment does is cause pain, then it is not worth the words. Yet, I have also experienced betrayal, and will not be twice-burned. They have a chance to make up for what they tried to do, but only that. If they show that they cannot change, they will not be allowed to hurt any of me or mine again.” I turn to face him full-on. “If they still mean anything to you, you will warn them of that – and tell them of what might await them if they prove their loyalty.”

Nodding shortly at him, I brush past and leave him contemplating the horizon.
