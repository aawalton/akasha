As they traveled, Tressa limped along, using magic to help, but she couldn’t keep it up all day. Her feet were wrapped up in bandages and felt tight in her boots, and every so often they would stop to let Lisha reapply ointment to her feet to help the healing process.

“So how far are we from the city now?”

Lisha pulled out the map as they came to a fork in the road. “Hmmm. I’ve never been to the Kobold capital, so I think it is this way. Honestly, they should put up signs with directions. The map doesn’t really show this fork. Did we make a wrong turn somewhere?”

Briadon looked over her shoulder at the map and furrowed his brow. “I can’t really see the map too well with the sun fading. Tressa, some light, please?”

“Oh, sure. Lumis,” she said, and a small ball of light lit up over the map.

“Thanks,” Briadon said as he looked closer at the map. “I think we took a wrong fork back here,” he said, tapping the map closer to the south than where they were.

Tressa looked at them and then summoned her staff. “Give me a second. Levant.”

She slowly rose above the tree line. Trees stretched into the distance, but to the northwest, a broad expanse of water gleamed in the fading sunlight. She rose a little higher, trying to see more.

“What do you see?” Briadon called up to her.

“There’s a huge lake! A river too, though I can only see parts of it through the trees. It makes a sharp turn just before it reaches the lake.”

Lisha leaned closer to the map. “Which way is the lake?”

Tressa glanced toward the setting sun. “Northwest!”

Lisha traced the river with her finger, then stopped at a bend. “That would put us… Oh. Come back down!”

Tressa lowered herself until her feet touched the ground gently. “So you know where we are?”

“I think so. If you can see that bend to the northwest, we must be at this fork.” She tapped a junction well away from the spot Briadon had pointed out.

“So we did make a wrong turn?”

“It looks like it,” she said, looking closer at the map.

“So what do we do?” Tressa asked.

“Well, we have a few options. We can continue down the path to our left and double back by going on this path,” she said, guiding her finger up the map. She tapped the map as she pointed out the river to the northwest.

Briadon looked at it. “That means we will need to travel up and cross the Corrian River.”

“That’s what I’m worried about.”

Tressa looked at the map as Briadon stood straight and scratched at his horn. She could see the path that Lisha had pointed out and the massive river that connected to it.

“Um if I may ask, why is crossing that river so make you upset?” she said, messing up her words but still getting the point across.

“It’s not so much that it makes us upset as it is a pain to cross,” Briadon said. “And pricey.”

“I don’t understand.”

“Lisha, do you want to explain?”

“I mean, sure.” She tapped the map and pointed at the crossroads they were currently at. “So this is where we are.”

Tressa looked at it. “I understand that, but what does it have to do with the river?”

“The river?”

“Is that not what I said?”

Lisha shook her head, deciding not to get into the proper pronunciation, and continued.

“Nothing. But see how we are here, like I was saying?”

Tressa nodded after Lisha waited for a response.

“Okay, so we have chosen to go up here to the bridge. It’s owned by the Dwarves of the north. After fleeing the mountains, they built the bridge that connects two massive parts of land. See this lake here?”

She tapped the map where a massive lake opened up near the base of the river.

“This is Corrian Lake. It connects to the river, and most parts of the river are in places where bridges would be almost impossible to build. So, about two hundred years ago, the Dwarves decided to build a massive bridge across the lake and over the only part of the river that is passable. The problem is that they tax people heavily at the entrance to the bridge, and they have a single tavern and inn on the island in the middle of the lake.”

She tapped where the river broke off toward the lake with a very sharp turn. In the middle of the drawing on the map was a large landmass, and then, on the other side, was the river once more. Tressa couldn’t wrap her head around how the land had come to look like that. She tilted her head, thinking maybe a giant had somehow created the mass.

“So for all of us to pass, it would cost about five silver.”

Tressa’s eyes widened in shock. “Just to cross a bridge!?”

“Yeah. It’s to constantly maintain the bridge. The weather around the lake is unpredictable. One moment it can be sunny; the next, it’s windy and foggy. It doesn’t take much time for the climate to change, so damage happens every so often. If we wanted to take the boat across, it would cost less, but then we’d run the risk of lake creatures trying to sink it. Or we turn around and spend the next two days trying to backtrack. Or there’s this fork here.”

She tapped at another bend a bit closer to them. “We could pass through here, but I don’t really want to deal with the monsters in that area.”

“Why not? Would it not be easier to take this path?”

“That’s the thing. Most don’t take that road because of what is in that part of the forest,” Briadon added. “It’s home to a dungeon. And not a friendly one at that.”

Tressa chuckled nervously.

“It’s called the Misty Woods for a reason. So we tend to just bypass it. The last group that went in hasn’t returned.”

Tressa shuddered. “No thanks. But what about this path?” she said, pointing to the fork that led right.

Lisha guided her finger across the map. “No good. That will most likely take us back up to Boteg. Not really looking to go that far. But if we do head this way to our left, it will be faster than backtracking. We are roughly about half a day away from the bridge entrance and then half a day to cross the bridge. Then maybe three days at most to drop back down to the southeast and hit the smaller kobold town, and finally we would reach the larger city of Sarif.”

“What of this town here?” Tressa asked as she tapped the map.

Lisha scratched at a scale. “We could, but then we would have to cut through this area here. And I don’t like farming towns. So Koliad is out.”

“Why?”

“Well, when an animal sees Lisha, they tend to haul ass, as one would say, because they are scared of the way she looks.”

“You’re not much better, meathead. After all, remember last year at the festival to the west?”

“Please don’t bring that up.”

“What about it?” Tressa asked, intrigued.

“Meathead here decided to chase after some farm animals, and, well, he destroyed a chicken coop and let some horses free. Then another bull—or human farm animal, a cow—decided that it didn’t like another male in its pasture and charged him, wounding Briadon pretty badly. Though he did fight back and broke off its horn, and the owner was angry.”

“Can we end it there?” he pleaded.

Tressa looked at him, imagining the scene, and began to laugh.

“As I said, it was an accident. And I didn’t know there were chickens!”

“Anyways, farming towns are out. So our best bet is the Corrian Bridge, and then we head to Sarif.”

Tressa’s eyes lit up as it finally dawned on her. “I get to officially meet a dwarf.”

Lisha chuckled. “Have you not met one?”

“Well, we have had a few interactions with them, but if we are going to stay on an island that is owned by the dwarves, I get to see some of their culture.”

“Hahaha, don’t be too impressed. They aren’t much different from humans. Though the women tend to be so much louder than the men.”

“Really?”

“Yeah, but one other thing you need to know: they have a ton of pride, so do your best not to insult them.”

Tressa looked at Briadon, insulted. “I’m much more dignified than you think. I am, after all, the daughter of a council member, so I do know a thing or two about respect.”

“That’s what I’m a little worried about. Just don’t let your pride get in the way, okay?”

“I’m not that prideful.”

Lisha let out a deep sigh. “Let’s just get moving before we lose any more daylight.”

“R-right.” Tressa chuckled. She reached into her pack that was on the ground and dug through it until she found a small bag and pulled out some jerky.

“Seriously, a snack break? Didn’t you just hear Lisha? We need to get moving.”

“I know, but I’m hungry,” she said, stuffing the piece in her mouth with the end sticking out. She gathered the few pieces she had pulled out, restuffed them into her bag, and hefted it over her shoulder.

***

Tressa moaned in pain as her feet felt swollen and throbbed. The pain made her slow down, much to Briadon’s annoyance.

“You should have gotten more footwear before we left.”

Tressa glared at him and groaned in pain. “Shut up,” she said, flipping him off.

He narrowed his eyes at her.

“Regret teaching her that?”

“Maybe a little.”

Tressa groaned again as she walked. She was hunched over as the pain in her feet radiated into her back and shoulders. She hated herself for not getting new boots sooner. Her feet throbbed, and she let out another groan. Looking up at the sky, she stared at the fading sun and found a few stars twinkling as the sun began to set. She knew the season would be changing in just a couple of months to summer and then the rainy season. She would have to head home soon before her trials.

“What are you thinking about?” Lisha asked as she slowed her pace to match Tressa’s hobble.

“The stars.”

Lisha looked up to see the few stars twinkling in the sky and nodded. “It will be dark soon.”

“Well, that isn’t really what I was thinking about. But you’re not wrong.”

“Then what were you thinking of?”

“Well, the seasons are beginning to change. As you can see, the summer season is about to end, and it will head into the rainy season before it becomes fall.”

Lisha’s lips curled. “So elves can read the stars as well? Most I’ve met outside of the islands cannot.”

“Well, I don’t know about most elves, but a merchant who comes to Falidorn told me, and I was interested, so he sold me a book of it. The star that is the brightest is the North Star,” she said, pointing to the right where the star was shining the brightest. “It’s part of the Doran constellation. Then next to it is the Sterean constellation, followed by the Canis Lupus constellation.”

“Wolf constellation?”

“Yes,” Tressa said. “Then to the Felis Lupa.”

“Which one is that? My people do not name them in this way,” Lisha said.

“It’s a wolf-lion hybrid. It’s the Leodergn. It’s supposed to be, anyways. They used to be common in the south and overseas. At least that is what I was told. We High Elves have not really traveled the world in years.”

“Leodergn? I have never seen one,” Briadon added as they walked around a bend and out of the trees to where it became a bit of flat land and a massive bridge could be seen in the distance.

“Is that the bridge?” Tressa asked, seeing a gate and a tower on each side, with an iron gate closed to a line of people.

“Yep, that’s it,” Briadon said.

Tressa felt her jaw drop as they grew closer, the pain in her feet almost all but forgotten. The bridge was massive, slightly arched with pillars holding it up. She had never seen any type of architecture like it before.

“Stunning, isn’t it?” Lisha said.

“Yes, it is amazing,” she said, wanting to get closer and look at it. She wanted to study it, and she reached into her bag, already pulling out her book to describe what she was seeing. She loved seeing new things, and this was by far the most unnatural thing she had ever seen.

“And there she goes,” Briadon huffed.

Lisha laughed agreeing with him.

“This place must be world famous…” Tressa said with awe.

“You’re right about that. It absolutely is,” said Briadon. “People with too much money and time on their hands come here just to see it.”

Tressa was about to say she couldn’t believe she’d never heard of it, but then she knew the answer why. A structure like this was even beyond the High Elves, so undoubtedly the Mad King burned any records they had of it and would have made it forbidden to talk about. It would be just one more thing lost to her people over the years.

“Tressa?”

“Huh?”

Lisha gave a toothy smile. “I’m surprised you haven’t pulled out your sketchbook yet.”

Tressa grinned sheepishly, casting off her thoughts of the Mad King and pulling up her hood to hide her ears. “I will later. But I draw monsters and plants better.”

They grew even closer and soon stopped in line. Several merchants stood in front of them and produced papers as they were called forward to cross the bridge. Several looked back and noticed Lisha and nudged a friend.

“What the hell is a Kobold doing all the way up here?”

“I don’t think that is a kobold. Never seen one that tall,” said the friend. “Shit, a minotaur!?”

“They come this far north?” another asked.

“You know we can hear you,” Briadon said as he set his traveling pack down.

“Hehe,” one chuckled nervously.

“I do so apologize. We are just a little surprised to see a minotaur, human, and kobold this far north.”

“Statik,” Lisha said. “I’m not a kobold. I’m a statiki.”

The merchant’s eyes widened even more. “Now this is truly a sight to behold. I never thought in all my years I’d see a statiki. Your kind tend not to leave your island. Why are you here?” he asked, intrigued.

“Wanted to see the world and ended up joining the Hunters Guild,” she said with a slight shrug.

“Hunters?” he asked.

“Yes,” Briadon added.

“Now tell me, would you be willing to escort us?” asked the merchant.

“Escort?” Lisha asked.

“Yes, we are headed across the bridge, and lately the weather has gotten worse around the island, making the few monsters that live there act up even more. We would like to get an escort across to the far side and then west for the harvest festival in Dengrom.”

Lisha sighed. “We can take you across the bridge but beyond that we are heading down to the south.”

“The south?”

“Aye, we are in the middle of a contract and on a time limit,” Briadon explained. “Our next stop is Sarif.”

The two merchants exchanged a look.

“Is there something wrong?” Lisha asked.

“Well, there are some things going on down in the kobold cities,” he said nervously, looking at Lisha.

Lisha ground her teeth, and her tongue flicked out as the spines and feathers around her neck and gills moved around. She knew exactly what he meant.

“Thank you,” she said. “We will see if we can lend some aid to them, as we will have some time.”

“Search for Grena. She will be your best bet to let you know what’s going on.”

“I know of Grena. She is a well-known healer.”

“Aye, that she is. She will help you out,” he said.

Lisha held out her hand, and the merchant shook it. “Will two silvers work as payment for some aid?”

“That works just fine. Nice to meet you. I’m Lisha. This is Briadon, and Tressa.”

“Nice to meet you. We are Mitchel and Luke.”

“Next!” called the dwarf at the front, and they each approached.

“Names and cargo?” the dwarf said. He sat at a desk with a window and a quill in hand and looked extremely bored. He had a massive bushy beard, bushy eyebrows, and deep gray eyes.

“Mitchel and Luke. We are merchants carrying seeds and leather. We are headed across to make it to the west. We have four oxen,” said Mitchel as he held out a paper showing their affiliation with the Roken Trade Guild.

“Well then, that will be two silver for the cargo and seventy five copper for each person.”

Mitchel reached into his purse at his side and pulled out the total amount.

“Thank you,” he said, and stamped a piece of paper and passed it through the window. Mitchel took it, storing it in his coat pocket, knowing that was the pass across the bridge.

“Next,” he called out, and Lisha walked up to the window. “Names and cargo?”

“Lisha, Briadon, and Tressa,” each one said as they pulled out the guild papers showing who they were and their race.

“Cargo?” he asked as he looked at the papers. “Oh, wait. You are hunters?”

“That’s correct,” Briadon said.

“Hmmm. All right, here you go,” he said, stamping the paper. “We appreciate your kind coming through. Tell you what, we’ll reduce the fare down to one—"

The other dwarf nudged him and pointed at the paperwork, giving him a look. They both then looked at Tressa. She turned away and lowered further into the shadows of her hood, but she already knew what they realized from her paperwork.

The dwarf cleared his throat. “That’ll be three silver.”

Briadon let out a low growl, clearly gearing himself up to say something, but Lisha hit him and gave him a look.

“Three silver, then,” Lisha said, paying the fare.

The dwarf picked up the coins and passed the papers to the group.

As Tressa grabbed hers, he focused his gaze on her. “Be on your best behavior, or you will not be welcome back.”

Tressa bowed. “Of course, sir. Thank you.”

They took the paper and walked back over to the merchants. Tressa opened her mouth to apologize to her companions, knowing that her presence had cost them extra, but Lisha nudged her back and pulled her hood back enough so it stopped covering her eyes. She pointed a claw up and said, “Come on, you’ll want to get a better look at the bridge.”

Tressa smiled, then picked up her pace as they caught up with the merchants and continued to travel under the towering stone arches.
