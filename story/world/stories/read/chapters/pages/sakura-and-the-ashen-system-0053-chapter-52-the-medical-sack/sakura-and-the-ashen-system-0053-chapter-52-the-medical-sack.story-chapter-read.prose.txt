Gilbert was being led down the corridor so quickly, as though he had not just tried to lose consciousness. The guards held him under the arms, but they were not supporting him. They dragged him, letting his feet catch on the uneven stone. It was uncomfortable, painful, and fortunately rough enough that no one looked at him closely.

He took three steps, then sharply doubled over. One of the guards cursed and yanked him up.

"Again?"

Gilbert did not answer. He coughed, dug his fingers into his own robe, and let his body shake the way it already wanted to. It burned under his collarbone, and a heavy, wrong tightness sat in his chest. But right now, that pain was once again very useful.

While one guard swore and the second glanced toward the end of the corridor, Gilbert slipped his hand under his robe with one short, sharp movement. Bent over and coughing hard, he did not look suspicious at all. Under his fingers, Gilbert found the spot he needed. It was an old scar under his lower rib. Gilbert pressed on it. It hurt. Gilbert clenched his teeth so his face would not change too much. Something shifted under his skin. He hooked a small, smooth ampoule with two fingers and pulled it out in one sharp motion.

A little blood came out. The shirt under his robe only grew slightly damp with a warm spot, but it was not dangerous bleeding.

The important thing was that the ampoule remained sealed.

The ink could not mix with blood. That was the entire point. Blood could be easily explained after interrogations, beatings, and fits. But the ink had to remain clean and invisible until the moment Sakura shone the special lantern on it, the one Gilbert had obtained for her.

The mage quickly and deftly hid the ampoule in his sleeve.

"Get him up," the second guard said. "The healer will write again that we brought him half dead..."

"He’s half dead already," the first one muttered.

"And we’ll be the ones blamed, not the interrogator. Damn it."

They dragged him forward again. Gilbert lowered his head as if he did not care where he was being taken, but his thoughts worked quickly. Gilbert remembered that today was red mist, tomorrow would be green, and the day after tomorrow would be blue. Gilbert was most interested in the green day. If everything went the way he and Sakura had calculated, she would need to prepare for that one. If not, only blue remained in reserve, their Plan B. There were no other plans. After the blue mist, the magical core would fail. It was only a matter of a short time. That meant Gilbert’s disguise would break. And then their plan would be over.

But right now, Gilbert did not want to think about failure. He wanted to think about how to send the message to Sakura. He could not simply scratch it onto an outer wall, where he was not being taken anyway, or pass it through a servant, because they had no one bought off here. Any direct sign would be dead before it reached the outer yard.

But dirty and torn cloth left the Castle. It happened only a few times a week. Dirty medical items, ruined uniforms, work robes, protective canvases, everything that had to be washed, boiled, repaired, and returned. Transport through the mists was too expensive to keep sending wagons for a couple of shirts. They were collected and sent out on schedule. And today, that schedule became the only normal channel of communication between Gilbert and Sakura.

If he managed to leave a sign on the cloth, Sakura would look for it in the wagon between the Castle and the city. Because logistics were the weakest point of such deliveries. There was too much protection in the prison or in the workshop itself. But on the road, the target was the most vulnerable. It was also not guarded too heavily, because it was not money or prisoners, only a pile of rags.

The infirmary had an unpleasant medical smell. The healer stood by the table, writing something in a thin book. He wore a dark work robe over his ordinary clothes, thick, with old solution stains near the sleeves and a wide inner pocket at the chest. A thin chain hung from the pocket. Judging by the round shape bulging under the fabric, it might have been a watch or a medallion.

The healer raised his eyes and looked as if they had brought him not a prisoner, but a sack of rotten fish.

"Did you decide to finish him off on the way?"

"He falls on his own," the guard said.

"We were ordered to bring him," the second added.

The healer clearly cursed under his breath and closed the book.

"Onto the cot," he ordered.

Gilbert was seated on the edge of the narrow examination couch, and the guards left. He and the healer remained alone behind the screen, though the guards stayed somewhere nearby. Excellent. The healer came closer, lifted his chin with two fingers, and frowned.

"Pupils even. Breathing is awful. Nose bled again?"

"A little," Gilbert said hoarsely.

The healer bent lower to examine Gilbert’s face, then pressed his fingers to his neck, counting his pulse. The robe was very close. Gilbert waited. He needed the perfect moment to do what he intended to do.

The healer reached toward the table for a sharp-smelling jar, and Gilbert moved. He sharply drew in air like a man whose insides had been seized and twisted. His body pitched forward. Gilbert grabbed the healer with both hands, as if trying not to collapse face-first. His bloody fingers slid across the healer’s cheek, leaving a dark streak from cheekbone to chin. With his other hand, he yanked the edge of the robe near the pocket. The fabric split along the seam.

"Damn you," the healer snapped, recoiled, grabbed Gilbert hard by the shoulder, and shoved him back onto the couch. "Sit."

A guard’s voice immediately came from behind the screen.

"What is it?"

"Same as always," the healer barked. "You brought me wreckage."

"Need help?"

"I need silence and people who know how not to get in the way."

The guard muttered something displeased. The healer wiped his stained face with his palm, looked at the blood, then at the torn robe. The disgust on his face was stronger than the anger.

"Wonderful. Just wonderful."

He pulled the outer robe off his shoulders too sharply and threw it onto the edge of the table beside the couch. Then he took a clean rag and went to the washbasin, which was hidden behind the screen.

"Sit," he threw over his shoulder at Gilbert.

That was the window. His only chance. Gilbert did not raise his head. He sat hunched over, as if trying to wait out the pain, but in reality, he was listening to the sounds of the infirmary. The healer’s steps, the splash of water, the guards whispering to each other.

The robe lay close enough. Gilbert shifted his fingers inside his sleeve, and the ampoule settled between his thumb and forefinger. He cracked the wax edge with his nail. The soft click drowned in the splash of water.

The ink was cold. He felt it on the tip of his finger, but did not see it, because without the special lantern it was impossible to notice.

Gilbert slowly raised his head. The healer was still behind the screen, washing off the blood. The guards were talking near the entrance, close enough to hear noise, but not close enough to see his hands. The mage had only a few seconds. Maybe less. In an infirmary, one could not count on more.

Gilbert braced his palm on the edge of the couch and rose. Getting to the table was risky, but necessary. Invisible ink did not forgive smeared haste. Sakura had to read the sign on the first try.

The robe lay in a crumpled dark heap on the edge of the table. Gilbert hooked it with his fingers and turned the inside toward himself. Then, quickly and deftly, he wrote:

17 GR

18 BL

Under the first line, he drew two short strokes, which meant this was their main plan. If the plan went exactly as intended, Sakura would understand everything. The seventeenth was Green. The eighteenth was Blue. She did not need anything more. Focus on green.

Behind the screen, the splash of water went quiet. Almost at the same time, a sole creaked near the entrance. Gilbert was finishing the message. He did not even turn around, feeling that he would no longer make it in time. His fingers left the invisible writing and slid higher, toward the torn pocket of the robe.

"Hey," the guard’s voice sounded very close. "What are you doing?"

If the guard realized now that Gilbert had been leaving a message, everything would fall apart. The robe would not go where it needed to. Sakura would not read the message. Everything they had done would be wasted.

Gilbert yanked the chain so the round object from the inner pocket slipped out and flashed in his fingers. The guard saw it.

"You bastard."

The blow came from the side, into his jaw. Gilbert’s head snapped aside, and the taste of blood immediately filled his mouth. He let his body lose balance and slam his hip into the table. The second guard burst behind the screen too, grabbed him by the shoulder, and twisted his arm behind his back.

The ampoule cracked in his sleeve, and a shard bit painfully into his skin, but that no longer mattered. The important thing had been left on the cloth.

"Trying to steal?" the guard hissed.

Gilbert drew a hoarse breath and unclenched his fingers. The chain fell to the floor with a quiet ring. It was a silver pocket watch.

The healer came out from behind the neighboring screen with a wet face and an absolutely murderous expression.

"What the..."

"He reached into your pocket," the guard said. "Tried to steal it."

The healer looked at the watch, then at Gilbert, then at the guards, and said nothing.

"He reached into your pocket," the guard repeated, as if speaking to someone stupid.

"I can see that," the healer snorted.

"We stopped him."

"Congratulations. One sick mage and two grown men. A victory worthy of song."

The guard reddened with anger, but did not argue. The healer picked up his watch, wiped it with the clean side of the rag, and tucked it into the inner pocket of his vest this time.

Gilbert stood bent over, held by the shoulders, and breathed heavily. The pain in his jaw, ribs, and arm had mixed into one tight knot. It was unpleasant, but the plan had been saved.

The guards had received an explanation for why the mage had reached for the robe, and the healer had received only another reason to be angry. Although he looked like the kind of man who would easily find one wherever he was.

No one had been given a reason to look at the inside of the robe under a special light and suspect a secret message in the pile of junk.

"Sit him back down," the healer said. "And hold him this time, since you’ve ended up here anyway."

He looked again at the stained robe lying on the edge of the table and grimaced.

"And that goes in the medical sack. Mark the tear separately. Let them wash it and sew it up if the fabric can still be saved."

One of the attendants by the door quickly stepped forward. He took the robe with two fingers, squeamishly, trying not to touch the bloody edge. He did not look at the inside. Why would he? There was nothing visible there.

The attendant went to the wall, where a sturdy sack with the dark mark of the infirmary stood, lifted the lid, and threw the robe inside.

The fabric fell dully onto the rest of the dirty laundry. The letter had just dropped into the mailbox. Now the main thing was for the mailman not to miss.

Gilbert said silently, Your turn, Sakura.
