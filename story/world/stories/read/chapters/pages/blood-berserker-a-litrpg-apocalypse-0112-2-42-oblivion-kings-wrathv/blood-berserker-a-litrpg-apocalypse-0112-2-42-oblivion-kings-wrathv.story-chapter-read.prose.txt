Oblivion moved in a way that was both unnatural and enchanting.

Each swing of the blade left a flash of all-consuming darkness in its wake. A phenomenon tightly tied to the blade's inability to reflect light, and as far as Nathan was concerned, that quality of the blade was another thing that made things difficult for the Xharnton to keep up with him.

So far, the demon had managed to keep Oblivion from cleaving through its neck, but both fighters knew that the tide of the battle had started to tilt towards Nathan's direction.

Despite its somewhat solid defence, the demon was rapidly losing ground due to Nathan's relentless attacks. With the way that it was going, the berserker had scored multiple shallow hits on the body of the fish demon compared to the demon, who barely managed to score more than a handful of hits.

Instead, the demon struggled to break through Nathan's attacks, but unfortunately, it was far too preoccupied to find the space to even break away, and Nathan had no interest in giving it the space to even try to break away.

So, for the moment, the Xharnton and Nathan were locked in a stalemate, one that was brutal to the demon's body. But the demon wasn’t the only one to suffer in the fight. Even Nathan was starting to flag. His muscles being stretched in different directions to afford him the ability to move at such breakneck speeds was starting to exact its toll on him, and the berserker already knew that he couldn’t afford to take a break.

That was what the Xharnton was waiting for. Just a sign of him being sluggish, and it would begin its own wave of attacks.

Nathan had known that this was going to be a battle of attrition from the start, and yet he’d chosen to go first—a choice he still believed in. And yet, given how the battle was going, he was the one who had to figure out how to handle the Xharnton.

Oblivion struggled to cut deep into the monster's scaled skin, and Nathan was starting to get angry with his lack of progress since the last hour and a half since this battle began. It felt more like he was striking his blade against a rock than a living thing, yet he persevered, eager to get this fight over with.

But the berserker held on to the faith that he could break past the Xharnton’s defence before it could get its chance to truly test him, and if he couldn’t… no, there could be no doubt here. I can do it, I just need one good hit to break its defence.

As though he was in a trance, Nathan ignored the sounds, the way the wind felt as he moved beyond what should’ve been possible for a normal human, the way his heart burned with the hunger for victory, and, more importantly, the way Oblivion thrummed for the demon's soul.

Never in his life had he assumed that he’d be wielding a real sword, let alone one that consumed souls, but here he was, with the black blade causally whistling as he swung it at the demon's kneecap just for the Xharnton to batter aside blade like a twig. The attack had failed, but Nathan was already following up with a shoulder barge.

As it was, Nathan was fighting two battles, one against the Xharnton and another against his body, and he couldn’t afford to lose the latter before winning the former.

His blonde hair had matted onto his skin from all the sweat pouring through his pores, but he paid no heed to that. The berserker was in the zone, and the only thing that could break him out of the trancelike state he was in was either victory or death.

And I’d rather die than lose. A soft chuckle escaped Nathan’s lips as his shoulder connected with the Xharnton's chest.

His shoulder barge seemed to have only pushed the demon three feet backwards, but that was still a success. Nathan couldn’t revel in his small victory. The real battle still loomed ahead, and the shoulder barge had done nothing but push the demon a few feet backwards as well as enrage it. And that was something that made Nathan raise an eyebrow involuntarily as he readied Oblivion for a sword strike, his mind whirring as he tried to understand why the demon was annoyed.

Maybe it was because it didn’t like losing in a straight-up battle of strength.

To be fair, Nathan could understand why the demon felt offended and enraged that he had pushed it back, even though it was just a little bit.

It was like the Neran bunny pushing him back with a head charge. Of course, Nathan doubted that the bunny would be able to pull that off even if he stood still. In the end, he was much closer to the Xharnton than the bunny was to him, but that didn’t matter to the enraged demon—if its flaring gills were anything to go by.

Great, enraged, I can work with. So far, the demon had been calm. Evading and blocking Nathan's attacks with little to no emotion, as it had been content to let Nathan attack it and tire himself out in the process. Still, the shoulder barge seemed enough to tip the Xharnton's emotional balance.

And now the berserker was going to have to fight an enraged version of the demon, which was somewhat good, in the sense that creatures who attacked rabidly were often easier to deal with than those that were actually calm and collected. On the flip side, monsters that had gone rabid were often unpredictable, and there was no telling what the demon was going to do.

“You… Die!” The demon pointed a clawed finger at him, then charged. Its heavy footsteps echoed in the maze as it rushed at Nathan, who’d had enough common sense to put distance between him and the monster the moment he’d noticed its enraged expression.

Come to think of it, that is the first time it has spoken since we began our fight. Nathan mused.

The words had sounded strange coming from the Xharnton, but there was no doubt that he’d heard correctly, and judging by how the demon was stomping its way towards him, Nathan could tell that the monster meant every word. All two of them.

Nathan considered his options even as he backtracked. The former arrangement with the demon had worked well for him because he expected to be able to kill it before the tide shifted in its favour, but it hadn’t worked out like that, and now Nathan was on the backfoot. Literally.

Surely, the demon in its enraged state would offer him many openings for him; it was just a question of whether he was going to be alive or with all his limbs intact to be able to take advantage of it.

Well, time to see if a sword can cut a mountain. Nathan reluctantly dug his heels in; there was no telling if he was going to backpedal into a dead end, especially since this maze was so confusing, so it was in his best interest that he stand and fight.

The Xharnton had gone through its shift of being battered relentlessly, and now it was Nathan's turn to endure the same. After all, this was only happening because he’d been unable to take down the huge demon when it’d let him attack it to his heart’s content.

That’s enough beating myself up; this demon is about to bash my face in, so it’s best I keep myself fresh for it. Nathan smiled morbidly at the thought, Oblivion at his side, ready to move in any needed direction with just a thought.

It was a testament to his ability to learn fast that the black blade already felt like an extension of his body. Then again, he had to reserve some praise for the corrupted priests as well as the harpies for his improvement with his blade.

Alright, it’s showtime!

Nathan barely managed to duck out of the way of a wild haymaker courtesy of the Xharnton. The demon’s huge, scaled fist had been mere millimeters from crushing his nose before the berserker's instincts kicked in, and he dodged the attack.

How the hell is something built like a tank moving that fast?

Nathan was already on the counter before his thoughts fully formed. His eagerness to take advantage of the first opening he spotted was evident in the awkward way Oblivion arced upwards towards the demon's armpit.

The black blade greedily bit into the demon's underarm, eliciting an irritating shriek from the huge demon. Black blood ran down the blade, but Nathan ignored that as he savagely tore free his blade and quickly hopped back just in time to avoid the demon’s retribution in the form of a kick at the spot he’d stood at a heartbeat ago.

Sheesh, that would have done a lot of damage to my nuts.

With the attempted attack on his lineage narrowly avoided, Nathan whistled in realization at what he’d just escaped. But this wasn’t the time to dawdle, so he quickly pulled himself into the fight.

The Xharnton had chosen to fight dirty, and well... nothing. To Nathan, no move was off-limits, so he had to begrudgingly accept that he’d met a foe as morally loose as he was in life-and-death situations.

Come to think of it, that move was similar to the one I used to kill that demon lord. Of course, the only key difference between the two attacks was that Nathan had used his sword while the Xharnton had used its leg.

“Well, that was uncalled for.” Nathan charged at the Xharnton with Oblivion in tow.

The black blade’s endless darkness was marred only by the black blood that ran down its length. It wasn’t far-fetched to say that Nathan was now highly motivated to drench his blade in the Xharnton’s blood.

Both fighters clashed mid-way, with both sides suffering serious injuries from the coming together. The demon had managed to orient itself in a way that it delivered a devastating punch to Nathan's midsection, with the hit shortly followed by the sickening sound of broken bones as the Xharnton tossed the berserker into the maze bush like a ragdoll.

Unfortunately for the demon, it hadn’t gone unscathed, as Nathan's blade nicked its exposed eyes. And with tissues as delicate as those of the eyes, a nick was all that Natthan needed. The berserker had successfully blinded the demon in a single strike.

A horrible shriek ripped free of the demon’s throat as it registered the loss of its eyesight, just as Nathan let out a blood-curdling scream as the pain from his broken ribs wracked his body.

Both sides had suffered fatal injuries from the single clash, but from what Nathan could see, the Xharnton had suffered the most.

The ripple effect of the demons' lost eyesight was instantly noticeable. The Xharnton had begun swinging widely with its sense of orientation lost.

As pained as Nathan was, he knew that he could not afford to miss this opportunity and risk the chance that the Xharnton could heal itself.

Ignoring the pain that wracked his body, Nathan slowly pulled himself out of the bush with labored breaths. With every shred of willpower and strength he could muster, he half-ran, half-stumbled his way to the flailing Xharnton, and without a shred of hesitation, he swung his sword, cleanly decapitating it.

The head of the demon fell to the floor with a thud, then Xharnton’s body shortly followed it, and in another heartbeat, so did Nathan’s.

The berserker lay on the floor, gasping for air as the pain tripled from his forced movements. But slowly but surely, his ragged breathing began to even out as a wave of euphoria enveloped him.
