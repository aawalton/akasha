Camp Pt. 2

I didn’t think we needed the upgrade, but apparently, working with Magical Girls comes with an actual power buff that the System recognizes as a new trait: Fairy Blessing. They even give it a whirl and try it on Ayre anyway, and it seems to be sticking stably enough for now.

He and Korrigan got incredibly excited when they read it, but I didn’t get to see its effects for myself. Much to the staff’s confusion, despite multiple attempts, the blessing refused to apply to me. Instead, every time, the Essence System gave me an error message.

ERROR:

An external entity is attempting to connect to your being in a manner that conflicts with an existing process.

The more powerful effect has been automatically retained.

Olivia claimed no knowledge of such a thing having ever happened before. Certainly, while one can’t become a Magical Girl twice at the same time, their operations span countless dimensions, and they’d never encountered any other effect that prevented it. Even if I had no magic potential at all, which certainly wasn’t the case, the contract could still be made. It just wouldn’t amount to much of anything.

And then there’s the fact that the System automatically decided that its version is more powerful and took its own initiative to shove the fae pact into the trash can. I think that raised a whole bunch of philosophical questions they aren’t ready to consider and Yorin will want to hear about when we get back.

In the meantime, though, I decide to interpret it as meaning that my friends are now basically lesser Heroes, and the next morning, Isabelle takes us out to one of the dungeons they have set up for letting trainees practice against captured Magicos. She says she won’t be doing any of the fighting and is purely there to observe because of our unique situation. Not only does Ayre need supervision for any sudden spikes of instability, but they apparently have a respawn system for the camp, and I’m not plugged into it.

There’s also the issue that I theoretically shouldn’t be able to harm the Magico boss without being a Magical Girl, so seeing how it reacts to my “superior” Essence Hero blessing first-hand is important intel-gathering.

“So, girls,” Isabelle starts as we approach a rather simplistic brick building that looks something like an old ruin, then catches herself, turns to Ayre, and apologizes. “Oh, sorry, force of habit.”

Of course, something like that doesn’t bother Ayre at all, and the archer immediately waves it off. “Please, don’t worry about it. I’m used to it. If that’s easier and causes fewer problems, don’t stop on my account.”

Honestly, it would have been a larger problem if he’d been sharing a room with a stranger, but that’s exactly why we made sure Ayre and I share a room, while Korrigan moved in with a girl closer to her own age. Apparently, they’d hit it off quite well and discussed magic until lights out. Meanwhile, I got to listen to Ayre complaining about how weird the furnishings are, since they’re of a decidedly western style foreign to Furinshao, but at least it meant we didn’t have to worry about any uncomfortable disclosures.

Now, Isabelle gives an appreciative nod at Ayre’s cooperative attitude. “Thanks, it’ll definitely be easier if you’re really sure you don’t mind. I was going to ask if you have any previous experience with fighting slimes.”

She gets a confused look on her face as, instead of answering, Ayre and I immediately start switching our gear. I start unloading the Paralysis rounds I normally keep loaded when we’re in civilized areas, including popping the one out of the chamber, and swapping it for a red-striped magazine of explosive fireball rounds. Meanwhile, Ayre has removed his two quivers and passed both to me. They slide into my pack and disappear within despite being longer than the bag, and, with a shift of my shoulders, I tilt the pack so that another two quivers stick out in their place. These have flattened heads that will relay more kinetic energy into an amorphous target than a sharp arrow that will pierce right through.

“Uh, I guess I have my answer,” she concludes after a moment, then glances to Korrigan. “Do you need to change anything?”

The little girl shakes her head. “My spells are anatomically agnostic.”

Isabelle and I both grin at the kid pulling out the thesaurus on that one, and she ruffles the mage’s hair before continuing. “The first Magico trainees usually challenge, unless they’re just better suited to another, is Slime Princess. She’s a bit unusual if you’re accustomed to enemies with a skeleton, but her minions are pretty weak. Even if we do run into problems, you shouldn’t be in too much danger.”

“Alright, then,” I say as we approach the entrance. “We’ll treat this as a Bronze-rank dungeon until we have reason to do otherwise. Everyone ready?”

Korrigan and Ayre give me nods, and Isabelle steps back, ready to let me take the lead, while she waits in case we need emergency evacuation.

The inside of the ruins definitely has the whole slime theme down. There’s blue slime crawling up pillars like fungal creeping vines, and violet pools of slime that bubble caustically. The general path is mostly clear, but there are some places off to the sides it looks like we could get to if we feel comfortable with jumping or don’t mind a light, quick acid bath.

“If you have any questions,” Isabelle is saying, “please do your best to figure it out for yourselves. The staff isn’t supposed to be giving direct assistance to trainees, so try to pretend I’m not here.”

I’m not really listening, though, as a slime the size of a small dog had been passing by the entrance hall and has taken notice of us, slowly pulling itself our way.

With little thought about it, and less warning, I quick-draw and fire at the thing from the hip.

The magical explosive round pierces through it with energy sufficient to cause a cavitation and then detonates with enough force to rattle the walls, causing all three people with me to jump like they’d been struck by lightning.

“Hey, Korrigan, good news,” I announce cheerfully, “they really don’t like fire!”

“What the fuck was that?!” Isabelle immediately demands. “Are you using explosive rounds?!”

I toss her a grin before blowing on my barrel. “Magic explosive rounds! They’re one of my favorites!”

Ayre’s scolding is different, even as it’s delivered with legs rigidly together and arms clamped straight against the side of his body with his fists curled outward. “Remmi! You’re supposed to warn us before you start shooting!”

I just shrug in reply. “It was right there, coming up on us like the world’s lamest gumdrop. I had to act. We might have only had three hours to live before it reached us!”

Isabelle is just left burrowing the palm of her hand into her face. “Maybe I picked the wrong dungeon …”

“Eh, it’s a trial run,” I remind her. “It’s fine. Everyone, move forward. Keep your eyes peeled in all directions. If it’s drippy, droopy, or shivering, assume it can attack.”

And that’s how we proceed. It turns out the place has some rats of unusual size, too, and Isabelle explains that a Magico causes mutations in lesser animals as a passive effect. They’re not true minions like the slimes, but they’ll fight for the boss all the same.

It doesn’t really matter much, though, as it’s all very much below our pay grade, and the dungeon is a very small one. A few rooms, some boxes of crystals that Identify tells me are healing items, and one hair clip against poison that I pass to Ayre, and we find ourselves standing around a fairly large hole in the floor that’s surrounded by slime.

“Well, that’s a one-way trip,” I observe. “It feels kind of early for a mid-boss, but I guess it is just the tutorial.”

Ayre gives me side-eye. “This is another realm entirely from our own, Remmi. You can’t possibly think your dungeon logic still applies here.”

“It applies everywhere,” I argue. “It’s the most efficient way to design things, so any dungeon system is going to converge on it. That’s how I knew it would work in Toleste in the first place.”

“That still looks like a long way down,” Korrigan says, poking her head over the hole. “We can get down safely enough, but getting back up could be a problem.”

“On it,” I reply, reaching over my shoulder to pull a long length of rope from my pack, as well as a piton and a hammer. The piton goes into the stonework far enough away to be clear of the slime with a couple of solid swings, and I fasten the rope to it with a quick slip knot and hitch before tossing the other end of the rope down into the hole.

“Wow, you all really are prepared,” Isabelle marvels.

“Always,” I agree. “Our whole job is running dungeons and purifying them, so we get a lot of practice.” I turn to face the hole once more. “Well,” is all I say, then I jump. “Allons-y!”

My boots land with a splat in several inches of slime, but fortunately not the caustic stuff. I take a few steps forward so I’m out of the way of the others and take in the new room.

Yes, room, singular. There are no exits except for the hole we came through, and though the room is large, probably about a third that of the entire floor above, the middle is consumed by a much deeper pool of slime, to judge by its darker color.

There are several more splats behind me as the others follow me down.

“That didn’t translate, Remmi,” Ayre immediately complains.

“That’s because it’s not my language,” I reply, “and that being the case was the point. But it basically means, 'Let’s go!'”

Korrigan is looking around the new space, though, instead of obsessing over what I said. “Where are we?”

“Boss room,” I conclude immediately. “It really is a tutorial dungeon.”

Ayre readies his bow immediately at those words. “But then, where’s the Magico?”

“In the big slime pool in the middle of the room.”

That gets me a glare like my confidence is cheating somehow, but it stops when the “waters” begin to ripple, and the top of a girl’s head pokes up out of it.

Except she’s every bit as translucent as the slimes we fought on the floor above.

She looks around the room for a bit before spotting us and gasping, then she rises up higher. Turns out, she’d actually be pretty cute if it weren’t for the whole slime thing. And, y’know, being buck naked. Though she’s got Barbie doll anatomy below the waist, so it’s probably fine enough.

“Oh, company!” Her voice is bubbly - not in the literal sense we might have expected; her enunciation is actually quite clear. Rather, it’s got that airy quality one associates with very positive people that have very few thoughts in their heads. “Hello, there!”

For all of our dungeoneering experience, we’re not all that accustomed to being welcomed in by the boss, and we find ourselves giving somewhat stiff replies.

“Hi,” I try as I hold my thumb and forefinger up in front of my eye like a monocle. “Are you the Magico in charge of this section of dungeon?”

NAME: Slime Princess

RACE: Magico

AGE: N/A

LEVEL: N/A

CLASS: Slime (Evolved)

STATUS: Curious

HEALTH: Unharmed

ENERGY: High

VITALITY: High

CONDITIONS:

None

She mimics my gesture playfully. “I am! Did you want to be friends?”

“That depends,” I reply. “Is it true that you eat people’s minds and turn them into mindless monsters?”

That makes her frown, and she really does have an adorable pout. “The only people that ask that want to fight, and I’m not good at that! Either I lose, and when I come back, I get lectured by my big sister, or they just disappear and come back to beat me up later!”

“Sounds rough,” I agree as I unfasten the belt securing my gun, “but it also sounds like a yes. Sorry, I’m going to have to turn you down. Pity, my party could actually use an indestructible tank.”

“Remmi?” Ayre asks.

“Lemme have first shot,” I instruct the archer. “Everyone keeps telling me I can’t hurt Magicos and guns are useless. It’s starting to get under my skin.”

“Don’t bring the roof down on our heads just because you’ve got something to prove,” my friend warns. “I’m not sure these walls are inviolate like ours.”

That makes me grin. “I’ll try not to make a mess.”

It makes Isabelle, on the other hand, frown. “She can’t possibly bring the whole building down with a pistol. Get real. Heck, she probably couldn’t even do it with a bazooka! The place is enchanted to contain monsters from beyond reality. It’s not going to collapse from a few pock marks.”

“Oh, we’re gonna fight now?” Slime Princess asks. Despite her claim of not being any good at it, she’s swaying back and forth in excitement. “Okay, here I come!”

The moment she starts forward, magical energy explodes from around me. Empower’s aura ignites as I draw, an action that itself triggers a class skill that further enhances my damage. Seafoam green energy swirls around the gun from Spiral Shot.

The barrel glows an angry red, and the moment I pull the trigger, the bullet that emerges multiplies into a cloud of lead from Spread Shot and Multi Shot. Each technically weakens the individual resulting projectiles relative to the original, but there are so many, and bullets are already so powerful, that it’s a radical net gain.

The bullets, glowing golden from Holy Arrow, spread out because of Spread Shot, only to flash and turn from the effect of Homing Shot, causing them to all pivot and swarm back in on Slime Princess like a plague of angry, fiery locusts.

The resulting explosions rip through her aqueous form until it’s obscured from sight, but I’m not content to wait for the smoke to clear, and before the bonus from my quick-draw can expire, I’m already pulling the trigger again.

“Now, PURIFICATION CANNON!!!”

The pink, spiraling beam erupts from the barrel of my gun as the rest of my magazine of explosive rounds vanishes to fuel it, and it crashes through the remains of the ditzy Magico to slam into the far wall with enough kinetic energy to feel like a massive earthquake.

Behind me, my friends scream as the room trembles and great chunks of the ceiling fall to slam into the slime around us.

Isabelle makes a motion with her wand, then she speaks into it like it’s a radio. “Olivia, emergency evac, now!”

There’s a sensation that fills me like what happens ahead of a teleport spell, and then the entire collapsing room fades to white. When shapes and colors return, we’re standing in what must be the nurse’s office. Olivia is there, and there are several cabinets of medicine and equipment, as well as a number of curtained-off beds.

Olivia takes only a moment to confirm none of us are critically injured, though she still looks saddened. “They couldn’t do it, then?”

But Isabelle storms forward and slams her hand onto the head nurse’s desk. “Not without leveling the entire place! One attack, and all of the wards could have been crayon doodles for all it mattered!”

The white-haired woman gives a thoughtful hum and moves over to a computer to begin checking something.

Ayre, meanwhile, stomps over to me and winds up a swing of his bow with both arms to smash it into my upper back. “What’s wrong with you?! Firing off a Purification Cannon in an enclosed, underground space?! You could have killed us all!”

I nearly collide with the floor from the blow, but I catch myself, and I reach over my shoulder to rub at the impact site. “A little rubble wouldn’t have done worse than bruise our heads; we would have been fine!”

“You took your something to prove too far!”

The exchange makes Olivia’s eyes widen as she pauses whatever she's doing. "Remmi was the one who did it? With a gun?"

"Yeah!" I'm starting to suspect Isabelle isn't furious so much as she's excited. "You should have seen it, Olivia! She must have cast half a dozen augmentation spells, all without a single incantation or pose, and they all went off without so much as a hiccup! I almost feel bad for how much she splattered Slime Princess over every wall!"

She doesn't sound like she feels bad. She sounds like someone just gave her an espresso at a pop concert.

I take a step forward. "Is, uh, that not how it's supposed to work? I was kind of under the impression that was the point of augmentation magic."

"It isn't the augmentation magic that is the problem," Olivia patiently explains. "Magical Girls have been trying to find a way to use projectiles for as long as we have existed. It is incredibly hard to argue against the theoretical effectiveness of a siege cannon augmented by magic. The problem is that a separate projectile fights against the magic, weakening both. That is why you need a weapon with an absurdly high base power or incredibly powerful magic, and in either case, you are going to be many more times as effective doing literally anything else."

"Even I've tried to make them work," Isabelle puts in, "and I definitely meet the incredibly powerful magic requirement. Firearms are cool, but they just don't do the job. And it's not just powder pew-pews, either. It's all physical projectiles. Magical Girls that use bows actually fire bolts of magic, not real arrows."

She crosses her arms. "Sure, you can apply that to guns and shoot energy bullets, but then you might as well up your caliber with a magic staff and cut out the middle man. Or at least paint the gun pink with sparkles so it's cute."

"Hey, I will never spit on a pink pistol if that's what somebody likes," I reply immediately. "I've seen plenty of cutesy guns, and every one of them will shoot you just as dead as edgelord matte black."

"I've seen Gun Gale Online too," Isabelle pushes back, probably unaware I get the reference, "but it's not about preference. Cuteness is a literal power multiplier for Magical Girls."

She gets a more thoughtful expression. "Where did you say you all were from? Toleste?" The blonde tilts her head toward Olivia. "Maybe we should visit them with a magical research team, see why their magic works and ours doesn't."

I scratch at my chin. "I mean, I could put you in touch with some people, but I don't know how much it depends on the Essence System to function. I've never used magic from anywhere else. If it does work, though, it should work with anything. Toleste doesn't actually have guns; the spells I used were designed with archers in mind, but I've never had any compatibility problems. One projectile weapon interfaces with them the same as any other."

The witch gives a deep sigh and lowers her head with another cross of her arms. "Well, at the very least, Remmi, I owe you an apology. I was wrong to dismiss your firepower, and you were right to be proud of it."

Korrigan throws her arms up in the air like a celebration. "And she blew the Magico all to pieces!"

It's Olivia who speaks up next, however. "That may be the case, but that unfortunately doesn't mean that she did any damage to it."

Ayre cocks a hip while folding his own arms in disagreement. "In what world does 'blown to bits' mean unharmed?"

"This one." The nurse turns the monitor toward us, where sensor data is listed. "This is a training camp, which means we need records of exactly how a team performs. These are the records from your battle against Slime Princess. The results are clear: You knocked her out, which is certainly impressive for a single attack, but you failed to cause her any actual harm."

Isabelle turns back toward us already inhaling, probably to tell us about the importance of the fairy blessing that they couldn't put on me, but I hold up a hand to pause her because of a blue window that just appeared in front of my face.

"Hold on, looks like the marketing department may already be on it."

"... Marketing department?"

I give the message a quick read-through before flipping it around so that everyone else can see it.

DEAR HERO

We here at Heavenly SystemsTM know even better than you do how full of wondrous things the multiverse can be. We can completely understand the desire to see all of it with your own eyes!

However, a primary obstacle in an infinite multiverse is preparing our precious heroes for everything they might encounter when they venture beyond our standard operating range. For instance, you might encounter giant, humanoid squids, environments that don't obey the expected laws of physics, or even monsters with an incredibly specific vulnerability that makes no sense whatsoever and we couldn't possibly be held legally liable for not anticipating when applying certain automatic filters that are in place for your own safety!

But there's no need for you to worry, because you may be traveling outside of your normal service area, but we're still here to help you be the Hero you need to be!

In order to finalize the necessary settings, we need to observe a dataset where conditions operate as natively intended in order to compare them against your recorded baseline. You can facilitate this process by accompanying others with the necessary settings already applied, and our systems will automatically record all critical data!

Remember, a little hardship now can lead to great rewards down the road!

We at Heavenly SystemsTM look forward to seeing the incredible otherworldly trails you'll blaze together with us!

Isabelle is staring at the window floating in the air before us all with an incredibly flat gaze. "... Uh, and you say this is normal?"

"The System spoils her rotten," Ayre confirms. "But what is it wanting?"

"I think it just wants to see someone that already has the Fairy Blessing trait beat a Magico," I guess. "It's not going to admit that it's not the better blessing, so it's hoping there's just a toggle it's missing somewhere."

"So we're going back in?" Korrigan asks.

"Soon as Director Isabelle says it's fit to do so," I confirm, "or we go after a different one while it's recovering. Either way, all we need is for you two to have a shot at doing the fighting this time!"
