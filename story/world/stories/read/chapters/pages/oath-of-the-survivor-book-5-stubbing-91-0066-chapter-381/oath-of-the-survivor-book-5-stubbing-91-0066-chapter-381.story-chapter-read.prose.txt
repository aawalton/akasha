In his haste to get home, Kyle forgot one small detail.  During his departure, he’d given off an unstable aura; one that left the D Grade guards uncertain about his ability to survive the wilds of Guancious.  Now, he was flying with Aura Force and Devourer’s Domain active, greedily drinking in atmospheric energy to fuel the domains further.  Beyond that, he’d taken a clear step into mid-tier C Grade, making his aura stand out even more.  So, when a reptilian humanoid cloaked in a literal storm cloud met him in the skies near the city, Kyle was taken by surprise.

“State your business,” the reptilian hissed, lightning crackling in the air around them.  Their aura was impressive, comfortably in mid-tier C Grade.  Not that much stronger than Mangalica, though, Kyle thought.

“I’m with the Courier’s Guild.  I just finished my job, and am heading back to the Hub to drop off some unorthodox material with the Collective and collect my payment.  I take it you’re the city overseer?”

The reptilian frowned, but made no move to get out of Kyle’s path.  “I am.  And I had no records of a Courier at your level departing the city.  Am I to assume you used some sort of stealth skill?”

“Something like that.  I wasn’t in a big hurry then, and I am now.  Not to mention having some lucky encounters along the road.”  As an afterthought, Kyle sent a note with his credentials to the overseer, whose frown only deepened.  They pulled up several screens, looking over them with impressive speed.

Finally, they sighed.  “Looking at your record, I’m not surprised you arrived right in the middle of major unrest.  Tell me, how did things go with the local orc clans?”

“The Tournament of Ascension is done, and the upstart up has been handled.  There are also quite a few less hodags nesting along the roads, though I expect there are still some holdouts.  With the threat of Mangalica handled, the War Chieftains should be taking the initiative to clear out the rest.”

“At least that’s some good news,” the overseer said, finally dispersing the cloud of power around them.  They gestured for Kyle to proceed.  “Now, please get to the teleporter and out of my city.  No offense, but you seem the type to stir up far more trouble than you should, and I’d prefer not to deal with it.”

Kyle was going to argue, but thought better of it.  Instead he offered a curt nod to the overseer and made his way to the city’s border. The city itself was quiet as he arrived, and it wasn’t until he’d gone a couple blocks toward the teleporter that he realized he was the reason why.  Oops.

Once he arrived at the teleporter, he arrived back at the Hub, where a delegation from the Collective was waiting.

Duroc owes me more than fifty percent, Kyle grumbled internally, doing his best to keep a patient expression on his face after the ninth hour of questioning.  He’d messaged Suierrillax about the unorthodox material he was bringing back, and upon returning to the Hub he was met by the Collective Inquisition.  Well, that’s what Kyle decided to call them at least.  The formal name was the Collective Department of the Investigation of Unorthodox Means and Behaviors, which didn’t exactly roll off the tongue.

They’d swooped in, ushering Kyle to what could only be described as an interrogation chamber as both his and Duroc’s nav bracelet video feeds were sent for review, and a gaggle of goblins began to meticulously sort through every item and scrap of paper in the bag.  At first, things seemed to be moving swimmingly.  Then, they came across something that required a supervisor.  Which then required another supervisor.  Which is how Kyle wound up sitting in an uncomfortable chair being asked questions by a high-tier C Grade goblin.

The wizened goblin paced about the room, brushing wisps of long, white hair out of spectacled eyes.  He wore a Collective uniform, though it was rumpled and ill-fitting in a few spots.  The goblin never shared his name, but the impression Kyle got from the man was that he was tired.  Then and again, a lifetime of investigating the unorthodox wasn’t without consequences.

Adjusting his spectacles after reviewing a screen, he turned back to Kyle.  “You mentioned that the orc – Mangalica, was it?  You mentioned that he drained something from the C Grade creature.  Pray tell, precisely how did he manage the feat?”

“As I said, when he was pushed into a corner he punched a hand into the creature’s chest and drained most of its energy, killing it.  The result put Mangalica into some sort of berserk state, with physical power approaching the top of C Grade.”

To emphasize his point, he encouraged C.H.A.D.D. to share its recording of the event for the fourth time, which the goblin reviewed with great interest, despite having seen it before.  Once the slowed-down recording was complete, the goblin stroked his chin thoughtfully, then finally sat down across the table from Kyle, folding his hands in the middle.

“I’m going to be direct with you, Mr. Mayhew.  Your story holds up, but what I’m struggling to understand is how you and your colleague managed to survive this encounter.  I’m sure you both have your means, but finding a solution in the middle of a fight that intense doesn’t seem plausible to me.”

“We encountered creatures created using similar means during a job for the Verdant Republic a handful of years ago.  The method there was more refined, and through trial, error, and study, we were able to find a weakness to exploit.  The hurried nature of Mangalica’s absorption made those weaknesses easier to spot, that’s all.”

Kyle spotted a brief flash of understanding in the goblin’s eyes before they returned to their neutral, tired look.  He nodded slowly.  “You were more involved in Er’Mithren than your file led me to believe, weren’t you?”

“I have no idea what my file says, but the least eventful part of my visit there was completing the Verdant Republic’s job.”   The scars on Kyle’s stomach ached.  His right hand unconsciously drifted toward them, though he managed to stop it.

The goblin nodded to him, a hint of sympathy in his eyes.  “It makes sense, then, that you recognized the general methods in use, and had a countermeasure to deploy.  I’m also going to assume that you understand some of the broader concerns surrounding your findings on Guancious.  The techniques you encountered were ultimately derived from the same source, though their author is different.  You encountered a renegade A Grade; this is the work of his would-be disciple.  An early B Grade, and former member of the Collective Department of the Investigation of Unorthodox Means and Behaviors.  He is a goblin, or was, once.  He is called Yogh.”

“So the reason this caused such a stir isn’t just because of the contents of the documents, but rather because of who was behind their creation?”“It was a contributing factor,” the goblin confirmed.  “You mentioned you have devised a means to deal with these amalgamations, is it something that could be more widely utilized?”“Unfortunately, it’s fairly unique to some skills I have.  I don’t think that it’s something that most people could take advantage of.”

“That makes sense, given the notes I see about your ‘death field.’  It was still worth asking, in any event.  Now, I think that we’re done here.  Your contributions in this matter have been noted, and compensation will be calculated and sent to both you and your orcish friend, per the agreement in your recordings.”  The goblin rose from his chair, then paused for a moment.  “May I ask one favor, before we part ways?”

“What is it?” Kyle asked.

“Should we find more evidence of Yogh’s presence or creations, could we call on you to consult?  You’d be compensated, of course, but scarce few have survived these encounters as well as you have, and your advice and support could be crucial to our success.”

Kyle thought long and hard about that question.  Anybody seeking to be Zierlan’s disciple would be dangerous.  And even if Kyle was able to help the Collective in this endeavor, he would only be walking down a path that would eventually lead to the source of so much of his pain.  On the other hand, he and Zierlan were on a collision course eventually.  Kyle understood enough about A Grades to know that they were patient, and didn’t take slights lightly.  Administrator Gamma’s Sanction wouldn’t last forever, and when it expired, Kyle would have a target on his back.

If his work alongside the Collective could lead to Zierlan’s capture and defeat before that happened, he’d be free and clear.  Even helping to take out somebody following the same path could set the A Grade back, and potentially help Kyle develop countermeasures if he crossed path with the genuine article.  That, ultimately, was what pushed him over the edge.

“I’ll need details of any operation before committing, but you can reach out to me as things develop,” Kyle said.

“That’s all that I can ask for,” the goblin said.  “Take care of yourself, Mr. Mayhew.  And thank you for turning in this material.  I’ll ensure your rewards are split appropriately and credited to your respective accounts.”

With a nod of acknowledgement, Kyle left the room and made his way back out to the bustling activity of the Hub.  Kyle waited until he was well into the thrum of people before he spoke softly.

“You’re not going to chide me for being too foolhardy?”
