The muscular guardswoman had a look in her eyes that Edge had come to know well.

Melenia was done warming up. The axe-wielding warrior was ready to bring the pain. She wouldn’t target his vital points or do anything that might kill him before he could heal the damage. She would, however, derive great pleasure from beating the stuffing out of him if he couldn’t defend against the furious barrage that would inevitably follow.

Truth be told, he was just as excited. Mel had defeated him handily during their boxing matches, but he had grown by leaps and bounds since the last time they stepped into the ring. Her strength had skyrocketed too, and finding out who would come out on top had his blood pumping in more than one way.

Edge was satisfied with Tidal Stream’s performance and would familiarize himself with its particulars during the battles to come. For this duel, he wanted to see who would prevail in a pure contest of attributes and technique.

“No skills?” she asked, echoing his train of thought.

He nodded while deactivating his domain. He left Mantle running, since it would let them go all out without killing each other in the process and didn’t offer either of them an unfair advantage. He turned to face his lover, bowed, and brough Opulence around in a sweeping flourish he had learned from Beelo the gladiator.

Mel returned his bow with a predatory grin that told him he was going to enjoy what happened as soon as they had access to a comfortable bed, a hot shower, and at least half an hour of privacy, then gestured for him to make the first move.

He was happy to oblige, circling around as the warrior turned to track him. Edge had been planning to probe her defense, trade some exploratory strikes, and attempt to create an opening to exploit. But in that moment, he changed his mind, taking a page from Mel’s book and going with the direct approach instead.

He came to a skidding stop while spinning around, transferring his forward momentum into a wicked horizontal slash that would have bisected an uncored warrior with no appreciable resistance.

Melenia was made from sterner stuff and had no intention of letting him land the blow regardless. She didn’t block, and she didn’t dodge. Instead, she mirrored his swing, sending their weapons streaking toward one another like two bladed freight trains coming down the same track.

Earth weapons from before the dawn of magic weren’t designed to be used like this, as the thin edges ran the risk of being chipped or shattered. But the high-grade materials their mastercrafted weapons were forged from were more difficult to destroy by multiple orders of magnitude.

In the moment before the killing tools collided, time slowed to a crawl, letting Edge appreciate their imminent impact.

It almost felt like the weapons wanted to be tested against one another. In his mind, he heard the axe issue a primal roar like the war cry of a primeval god. The glaive’s twin dragons flashed in the sunlight—so detailed they seemed to be rearing back to strike. Then the frozen instant dissipated, reality resumed its full course, and the blades came together in a monumental crash.

Opulence was a heavy weapon, but Extinction was fucking massive.

They met each other with a clang that reverberated across the biome. The impact picked Edge up and flung him back, cackling as his boots left twin trails in the dirt. Mel went staggering to one side but recovered first, following in his wake to finish the fight.

They exchanged a fierce series of strikes, using every trick at their disposal to break through the other’s guard and land a decisive hit. He had learned a great deal in a short span of time, but Melenia had been fighting with an axe for years. Even still, now that he’d hit the peak of stage two, his attributes were well into the realm of the superhuman.

Edge gave as well as he got, thrusting, slashing, ducking, and leaping as their laughter filled the air. He took a boot to his chest that bruised his ribs, returning a favor with a stop thrust from the glaive’s shaft that drove the air from her lungs.

For a full five minutes, they went all out. After a blinding barrage of blows that pushed him to the limit and beyond, he found Extinction’s blade pressed against his throat. Mel stared into his eyes with a smoldering gaze that was equal parts bloodlust and ardor. “Checkmate.” She pulled him in for a passionate kiss that left him speechless.

“Okay,” Jumo said. “I’m not into their preferred form of foreplay, but I must admit. That was fucking hot.” Sasha smacked him in the back of his head as Sakura snorted. Even Trapper let out an uncharacteristic giggle.

Jumo, Edge, and Mel all had Second Wind in their skillsets. It only took them a few seconds to recover from their vigorous sparring session. He sprayed them down with Repel Water, used it to dry them off, and the crew continued on their way while discussing everything they’d observed, brainstorming on how to make the most out of Edge’s new domain.

He tried using Stream and Maelstrom at the same time, but the drain from the combo penalty was so intense that it would have bottomed out his reservoir in less than a minute. That explains why I’ve never seen anyone combine domains before. I’ll have to save it for special circumstances or when Overdrive is active, although as I advance into stage three, my Generation should grow high enough to make the combo more feasible.

The crew finished the final leg of their journey in short order, letting out a spontaneous cheer when the free city came into view. Even after living within its walls for weeks, World’s End was an awe-inspiring sight.

The most famous city in human history rose from an island in the middle of a vast freshwater ocean. The frontier settlement had been built atop, around, and inside a massive butte that had been hollowed out over the course of decades. The skeletal remains of the largest kaiju ever defeated sat perched atop the rocky rise, an immense shelled creature that seemed equal parts dragon and turtle.

The thick outer wall was riddled with passageways and chambers, and more buildings had been secured to the exterior of the butte. Extensive docks and floating blocks ringed the rise like a skirt, and though he had never seen them in person, Edge knew portions of the city had been built along the bottom of the lake.

Hundreds of vessels and aquatic creatures crossed the open water, and dozens of flying beasts bearing riders made their way through the sky above. Even after the disaster, there were thousands of high-stage hunters willing to brave the dangers of the Lakelands, and he was proud to be numbered amongst their ranks.

“That mission didn’t exactly go according to plan.” Jumo grinned as the crew stopped to take in the sight. “But we more than accomplished what we set out to do and were richly rewarded for our efforts.”

Sasha reflexively raised her hand to her throat. “I’d rather not go through that again, although we obtained some strength that we desperately needed.”

“I’m ready for a hot bath, a bottle of wine, and a king-sized mattress with a comfort modification,” Melenia said as they stopped at the end of the short line of hunters waiting to enter the settlement.

“I’ll get us a room as soon as we return the wagon and report the completion of our mission,” Trapper replied. “Then we can figure out what to do about what we learned while we were away.”

Edge realized she was being vague because they were surrounded by people with superhuman Perception, not to mention a vast array of sensory boosting skills. The other hunters were casually inspecting the crew’s floating wagon. Even though they had thrown a tarp over the top, he was certain the contents were being analyzed by at least a dozen people.

They reached the front of the line soon after and were greeted the Magister on duty, exchanging nods with an elite squad from the city watch who was guarding the gate. They knew each other on sight by this point, so after inspecting the wagon for anything that might endanger the city, the woman waved the crew through. They crossed through a short tunnel in the wall of the hollow butte and emerged into the massive enclosure containing the free city of World’s End.

The settlement was divided into several key areas. The largest was known as the mixing bowl, which comprised the entirety of the ground level. The bowl featured extensive commercial, industrial, and residential districts, with the Grand Arena of World’s End sitting at the heart of it all.

Six terraced rings extended from the butte’s walls. The first three were accessible to the general public, but the silver, gold, and platinum rings could only be entered by stage-two, three, and four warriors respectively. Beneath the mixing bowl was Lowtown, where the city’s poorest and weakest residents dwelled.

Although it was an enclosed space, warm sunlight bathed Edge’s face, shining through a magitech skyscreen that spanned the entirety of the ceiling. The device’s magic offered an unobstructed view of the heavens without any of the buildings or the kaiju’s remains in the way. It was a strange arrangement that must have cost a fortune to install, turning the settlement into a fortress without feeling cramped or confined.

Hanging from the ceiling, barely visible thanks to the screen that obscured its surface, hung the Magisters’ fortress, where the ruler of the city, the First Among Equals, resided, along with her administrative staff.

The instant they were inside the city limits, Rue materialized into Edge’s backpack, which he had turned into a padded carrier for the golden fox. After his Companion’s successful solo mission, they had agreed that he could come out whenever he wanted while within the walls. Rue had promised not to take foolish risks like antagonizing the free city’s sausage vendors and was on his best behavior, despite the hunger flowing through their bond.

As the crew made their way to the Hunters’ Association plaza, they passed by powerful beasts pulling mundane wagons. It Edge wonder how Violet and Blue were doing, along with Riller, Lilly, Ann, Rita, Sam, and everyone else in Puppet Town. Edge was certain they were experiencing trials of their own, but after everything they had endured together, he had faith that his people could thrive if they managed to stay out of the crosshairs of the planet’s major factions. Or any Ancients or unfriendly scions for that matter.

There was always a chance that another kaiju, beast stampede, or monster horde would attack the settlement, but the odds were low. The Ivory Plains had far more magicytes flowing through them than before the anomaly. However, the concentration was still thin compared to extreme-threat biomes, which should dissuade the largest and most dangerous predators from hunting in the grasslands for a while longer

By then, Puppet Town should have dozens of stage-three warriors numbered amongst its ranks, along with scores of stage-ones and twos. Maybe even thousands if Dialla can realize her ambitions and upgrade the manufactory. A few years past that point, and Puppet Town will be powerful enough to quality as a full-fledged faction and a major frontier settlement—a far cry from that starter town I stepped into at the dawn of my adventures, where uncored tourists learned the ropes of surviving on Ord before seeking fortune and fame on the feed.

His musings were cut off when Sakura coughed to get everyone’s attention. Adrenaline spiked his veins when she flashed the hunters’ hand sign for “powerful enemy detected,” followed by, “don’t let them know we’ve caught on.”
