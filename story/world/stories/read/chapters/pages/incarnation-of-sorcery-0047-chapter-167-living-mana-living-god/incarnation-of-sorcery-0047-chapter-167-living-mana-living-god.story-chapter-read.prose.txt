Ryland forgot all about the hardest battle he had fought in decades. Thankfully, so did Mavar.

For they were both staring at what was going on at the moon.

Its surface was cracking. The sections of the satellite body had split apart with world-rending fractures. Some parts of it sank into the artificial planetary body, while other, smaller pieces just broke off and began floating away. All the cracks were glowing now, as though there were detonations going off within that moon’s core.

The worst part was how the compressed magical energy was emerging from the moon’s depths.

“No!” Mavar cried out. He looked like he was seconds away from tearing off his own hair. “This can’t be. This is impossible! That brat you sent in couldn’t have… couldn’t have…”

His eyes widened with horrific realization. With what looked almost like guilt.

Ryland ignored most of all that and simply rifted back closer to the moon. Which really wasn’t the moon any longer.

An enormous figure was pushing out of it, like a hatchling emerging from within an egg. Its form was hard to make out, but it almost seemed like two distinct figures conjoined together, their pearlescent radiance turning as bright as sunlight. Honestly, at this point, Ryland felt like he was being bathed in far too much brilliant luminescence recently.

The figure continued rising. Power rippled off it in waves so potent, he was sure that the effects were likely to be felt across the entirety of the cosmos at this rate.

Ryland needed to stop it. A part of him desperately hoped that Acrypha and Viren were alright within its bowels. They had done something. Viren had succeeded in his endeavour to free Acrypha to some extent, which was what had led to this. An unintended consequence.

One Ryland needed to put a stop to immediately.

“[Mad God’s Cage],” Ryland incanted, his voice echoing even through a cosmos devoid of air, just like when he and Mavar had spoken.

Divine energy had a very different… flavour to it, compared to mana that Ryland channelled regularly. The Overrealm was filled with a vastly different energy source, one that left Ryland’s body heating up and freezing at the same time, his manasoul splintering and reforming like glass being shattered, molten, and reformed instantaneously.

It was a heady sensation. Even Ryland’s control over it was strained.

Whatever the case, the spell went up. All the radiant golden strands Ryland had summoned with [Overrealm Rift] dug scores and grooves straight through space itself as they shot like lightning to Ryland’s location. There, they gathered at one point high above them all before unleashing a thousand more of those gleaming, divine strands.

One blink later, Ryland and the moon-monstrosity of Living Mana were surrounded by a cocoon of the auric threads.

Another flash, and they turned fully dark. Blackness blotted out the rest of the universe. The only sources of light left were Ryland’s own Incarnation form and the shining body of Living Mana rising from the broken moon.

There. Now they were isolated from the rest of the cosmos. Just in time too.

Glowing Living Mana was turning into pustules the size of mountains on that enormous figure’s back. They exploded in moments, unleashing solar flares of Living Mana. Other sections of the titanic monstrosity released jets of the same substance, which struck the barriers of [Mad God’s Cage] like a hammer striking a bell. Tendrils that could cross oceans lashed out with fury.

All of it failed to break through the divine spell Ryland had cast. They didn’t even leave a mark.

Now, the next thing was pulling Viren and Acrypha free. Whatever barrier Mavar had cast over the moon had well and truly shattered away at the monstrosity’s rise. As such, he wouldn’t need to struggle as much to quest into the creature’s interior.

A few seconds later, Ryland breathed out a sigh of relief. He was now assured that his two proteges were alive inside that thing. All he had to do was get them out.

Unfortunately, the titanic being chose that moment to be a direct nuisance for Ryland.

Jets of the same energy now shot towards him. It was literally Living Mana, just compressed to a degree that felt almost unfathomable, with a proclivity for intelligent life in an axis Ryland hadn’t thought possible.

For the magic that attacked him was alive.

Ryland’s [Awakened Avatar] and [Animus Aegis] were more than potent enough to stop the initial effect of the beam. And the combination of spells would have been enough to keep it at bay, had it not been for how the magic actively changed its effect, evolving in real time in an attempt to achieve its goal.

He was almost academically fascinated by how the spell shifted. First, it had been a pure blast of obliterating energy. Ryland had no trouble stopping that.

But when that had failed, it changed strategy. Now, it was eating away at Ryland’s barrier on purpose, revealing the Glyphs that made it up and attacking those Glyphs directly. It was emulating Ryland himself in his Incarnation form when he changed Conceptual Glyphs around.

A moment later, the beam broke through. The barrier had been overcome, part of it almost converted to Living Mana.

But Ryland had escaped away with [Translocate] as soon as he had deciphered the creature’s intention. Hmm. He started deliberating. This was going to take a different strategy.

The titanic conjoined beings roared. One side of it was bulkier and more monstrous, the other almost lithe and slim. Right along their centre, a jagged slash separated the two different forms.

Ryland frowned. Somehow, the two halves reminded him of Acrypha’s guardians. It made sense since she was in there. But he didn’t want to think about the implications. For the time being, he focused on his main goal. His magic blazed out. His eyesight sharpened. If the creature wanted to play with Conceptual Glyphs, then—

Impossible. Ryland should have been able to read the Conceptual Glyphs that it was made of. Yet, when he peered at it even with the help of [Omniscience], he found nothing that he could decipher.

It wasn’t that there weren’t any Glyphs constructing that thing. There were. They were just… smudged, like ink running loose on wet paper. The Glyphs and the Glyph Prints making them up were constantly changing, melting, reforming and twisting into new shapes. Refusing to let him understand what was going on.

This enormously powerful being made of Living Mana itself was something else entirely.

“Mavar,” Ryland growled. As far as he was aware, the Demiurge was outside the barrier of [Mad God’s Cage], which served that idiot right. “Were you trying to build your own Great God?”

It was clear—Ryland would need to unleash everything to free Viren and Acrypha.

“[Grasp of the Gigantes],” he incanted.

Before his spell circle had finished coming out and bringing forth the enormous arm, the monstrous titan of Living Mana was twisting around. For something so colossal, it moved terribly fast. Had there been a stable atmosphere between Ryland and the creature, just its motion alone would probably have unleashed a storm of historic proportions.

As it was, there were still explosions of light and heat that Ryland had to fend off with his barriers. Thankfully, they were easy since they weren’t accompanied by the Living Mana itself.

Ryland’s enormous arm burst out of a rift and reached across the length of the cosmos to grab onto the creature. Despite its size, the gap was still quite large, and even when it reached the titanic being, it looked like a doll holding onto its owner.

Pitifully small, really. That was probably why the monster ignored it and attacked Ryland again. With a roar that sent out pure radiation, violent bursts of corrosive Living Mana surged through the lightless void towards Ryland. Was it actively targeting Ryland by recognizing him as a threat, or was it mindlessly blasting out its magic?

A moot point. Ryland needed to deal with it either way.

He chose the easy way out. “[Void Barrier].”

Reality cracked all over Ryland himself. The blazing barrier of his Incarnation was now replaced with pieces of broken realms with nothing in between. Honestly, it had taken him some time to realize the inspiration that Mavar had drawn from when constructing the temporal infinite barrier around his artificial moon.

Ryland could have done something similar, but he wasn’t familiar with Living Mana’s decay rates. As such, he’d just eradicate it directly.

His new barrier did just that.

There was a slight thrill in whether it would work as perfectly as Ryland was imagining it, and he revelled in pride when it did. The flood of Living Mana that struck him with furious force sank into the cracks of unreality wreathing his body. In moments, the creature’s magic had been snuffed out entirely.

It was time for Ryland’s counterattack. “[Thunder Petrify].”

The same rift through which that gargantuan arm was flowing out now unleashed a torrent of grey lightning. It conducted along the huge metallic arm wrapped with wires and pipes, instantly shocking the colossal monster.

Ryland’s opponent cried out. Enormous chunks of it started turning to stone. Living Mana really was alive in truth. [Thunder Petrify] only worked on living flesh, turning it into stone like the glare of basilisks. Not that Ryland needed further proof. The way the magic could adapt and modify actively even when assaulting him was already enough evidence.

It was time to push his advantage. That he hadn’t done so already was a mistake. After the battle against Mavar, Ryland was almost looking forward to a more clear-cut victory.

Sadly, the creature had other ideas.

More Living Mana burst into being all over the place. It manifested from nothing, emerging from every point of existence inside the barrier of [Mad God’s Cage] in no time at all. Worse, it wasn’t benign Living Mana either. Ravenous tendrils crawled along Ryland’s golden, divine barrier, colossal arms and appendages swung about within seconds, foul organs and parts taking form to secrete their noxious energy everywhere.

Ryland was soon assaulted from multiple directions. Tails whipped him, maws blasted him with violent breaths, limbs punched and kicked hard enough to crater planets. He survived it all thanks to his barriers, yet slowly but surely, the Living Mana was seeping past them.

Even [Halo of Undying Might] was only a temporary stopgap. He could already see the Living Mana eating away at it, slowly manipulating the very Conceptual Glyphs making up his advanced barrier spell.

It would take the Living Mana some time to get through. The sheer density of Conceptual Glyphs in his barrier would make it last long enough.

Which meant it was yet again Ryland’s turn to counter. For the final time.

“[Eye of The Archfiend],” Ryland incanted.

A demonic eye bigger than Ryland was tall opened up through a rift, revealing the baleful gaze of a trapped Calamity. One whose destructive power couldn’t entirely be eradicated. But Ryland had found uses for it, like killing off pesky members of the Flourinus Order.

Now, the gaze turned superheated, the energy that blazed out from the rift screamed as it alighted upon this enclosed world. Pure destruction became all that existed within Ryland’s [Mad God’s Cage]. If the Living Mana made a sound as it was obliterated, as every iota of its mixture of matter and energy was wiped out from existence, it was impossible to hear.

Everything besides Ryland himself was getting deleted from existence. Ryland was rerouting some of the divine energy keeping up his intact overarching barrier into the trapped demonic Calamity, which just made the beast even more enraged.

Which further led to levels of eradication Ryland himself hadn’t seen in a while. His own barrier was crumbling under the overwhelming malice of its glare, the world now a blaze of black and red miasmic magic. But while Ryland’s [Halo of Undying Might] was falling against his own magic, the titanic construct of Living Mana was falling faster.

It was a race, honestly. So many points of tension were playing on Ryland’s mind, he was a little surprised he could maintain his concentration.

On the one hand, he was shattering his own barrier. On the other hand, he was attacking the Living Mana titan and everything it had created. On the third hand, he was aiming to free Acrypha and Viren. And all of that didn’t even account for the fact that he was sacrificing [Mad God’s Cage] just to empower [Eye of The Archfiend] even further.

All these goals created inflection points where things could fail, so if he didn’t act at just the right time, he’d be the architect of his own demise. It worried him. It enlivened him too.

And of course, because Ryland was making good progress and setting things up for his success, Mavar chose that moment to interrupt.

The barrier of [Mad Gods’ Cage] shattered at the far end. It was the same location where Ryland himself had first started battling against the gargantuan creature, and it was here that the Demiurge chose to break through.

He didn’t even care there was a terrific amount of demonic mana wiping everything from existence. Instead, he just focused on casting his spells.

“[Incision],” he incanted. Then he pointed his hand straight at Ryland. “[Realm Reinforcement].”

At that distance, Ryland failed to read the spell circles from Mavar in time. Even with a quick cast of [Omniscience], he wasn’t able to comprehend the spells’ intentions before their effects came into play. Which was why he failed to stop the rift behind him sealing up entirely as the very matter making up Rhel flowed in through the breach on his [Mad God’s Cage] barrier.

The red and black demonic malevolence faded in just a few heartbeats. Ah, so that was it. The very first thing Mavar had done was insert his controlling Soul Glyph into every iota of space within Ryland’s barrier.

Then he had used that very control on space itself to close the demonic rift.

Ryland could have summoned it up again, of course. It would have taken him little to no effort. But he focused on his real goal.

[Eye of The Archfiend] had lasted long enough to cut open the Living Mana titan. It had been scorched and was broken all over, bleeding out dying Living Mana in all directions. Ryland cast [Translocate] to appear right before the wound on the gargantuan creature’s chest, a cavity through which he could enter and retrieve Acrypha and Viren.

If only he hadn’t collided with Mavar just as he had appeared next to his target.

Somehow, he had closed the distance with teleportation-levels of speed. Maybe it was teleportation. Ryland wouldn’t be surprised if he possessed magic that allowed him to appear next to anyone or anything with his Soul Glyph within it.

The collision between them was less of a direct hit and more just their barriers impacting against each other with a violent crackle before they were both flung back.

“You’ve done enough,” Mavar said, almost shouting. “See what your actions have led to? You’ve broken it all free far too early. The damage you’ve done is going to be irreparable.”

“I don’t care about your little project, Mavar,” Ryland said. He didn’t bother reining in the viciousness in his voice. “I have two children to save and protect. Get out of my way, or I will simply eradicate you as I should have done from the beginning.”

“What? Like you eradicated me earlier?” Mavar’s grin was just as vicious. His expression sobered quickly, however. “Looks like you truly have changed, Ryland. I found it hard to believe, but… I can’t close my eyes to it any longer.” He sighed. It felt like an affectation. “How about this? Allow me to free your brats in return for not destroying it completely.”

“You no longer enjoy my trust, Mavar.”

“What choice do you have?” He jerked one finger back at the collapsing titan. “Your last act almost destroyed it. Everything inside is falling apart. Do you really think you have time to—wait!”

Mavar was right. Ryland had no more time to waste. Which was why another [Translocate] took him right into the heart of the titanic being.

It wasn’t dead yet. For all the damage that [Eye of The Archfiend] had dealt, for all that Mavar claimed Ryland had ruined everything, Living Mana still throbbed and glowed with virulent life within the seeming carcass of the great beast.

With the help of his Glyph of Detection, it took Ryland no time at all to locate the children. Yet, there was something strange going on. There was a cocoon of translucent Living Mana encasing both Viren and Acrypha, essentially creating a wide chamber within which they were ensconced at the moment.

Ryland breathed a sigh of relief that they appeared unhurt, though Acrypha looked much the worse for wear.

He was about to start breaking through to get the children out, but he was forced to stop. Acrypha had come right up against the barrier of Living Mana, hammering on the membrane keeping her from the rest of the world.

“Don’t do it!” she shouted, her voice muffled behind the wall of Living Mana. “Don’t attack, sir!”
