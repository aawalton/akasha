Severence, Lunar Judge, The Unwatched Ascendant and (until very recently) The Negator, was running in a blind panic when a wave of taunting, arrogant, controlling power rolled over him.

[Dragon Fear] has counteracted [Devourer’s Dread]

Sev came to an abrupt halt halfway toward the valley entrance, blinking. It had been a long time since he was caught so thoroughly off guard.

His soul ached at the absence, one of his paths torn out of him and consumed right in front of him. Even thinking about it threatened to return him to that blind panic, but he retained enough presence of mind to adjust. Normally he’d have used Nullify or Dispel to remove negative status effects, but those were gone. Thankfully, The Negator wasn’t his only control path.

[Uneventful] has repealed [Dragon Fear]

[Uneventful] has repealed [Devourer’s Dread]

With both fear effects gone, he was able to calm down and make a plan. Which, really, didn’t require a whole lot of thinking. The Devourer was what the shard-bound entity called itself, but it was clear that something of that power level couldn’t simply roam around in B-Rank bodies for long. All Sev needed to do was wait and watch until the entity returned to dormancy, then grab the hosts and bring them in.

But as simple as the plan sounded, he didn’t turn around.

He couldn’t stop noticing the empty place where his second path should have been, couldn’t stop straining to activate abilities that no longer existed. He’d often imagined what it would feel like to fight someone with his abilities, someone who could cancel, erase, and undo what he did. He’d never imagined anything like this.

Sev felt diminished, intensely violated. Something had torn open who he was and taken a bite out of the middle… and he was supposed to go back, face it again.

His organization wanted this being. Needed it, if they were going to understand the truth of the ancient powers they so ardently pursued. But for the first time since he’d become the Lunar Ascendant, he felt mortal again.

He didn’t want to go back. He didn’t want to look into those lifeless eyes, the endless gulf of unchecked hunger.

Let the Void Huntress deal with her own rogue adherent.

But the moment he imagined that scenario, his smothered pride perked back up and demanded he seize this opportunity. When else would the High Lunar Order have an opportunity to study a Voidtouched of this strength?

Whatever The Devourer was, it was clearly a very strong Void beast, and that it was bound and sealed within such a weak human vessel meant it was highly mutable.

No one in their order would ever have such a perfect chance again. Shouldn’t he ensure his sacrifice was worth something? What was the point if he lost his path and the Abyssal cult took the power for themselves?

Sev shook himself and gave himself another check over with Uneventful to verify that he didn’t have any more lingering fear effects. This was incredibly unlike him. He didn’t hesitate, didn’t quibble over trivialities when there was a job to be done.

How much of his personality and knowledge had been eaten along with his path? Would he ever be able to reclaim it?

That thought, finally, was what gave him the spark he needed. Anger buried the fear, smothered the concern, and discarded every other tangent.

He had a job to do. Negator or no Negator, he was still Severence, Lunar Judge, Unwatched Ascendant, and those B-Rankers would pay for what they’d dared do to him.

“‘Not until I’ve taken all of you there is to take,’ was it?” he murmured as he turned around, face returning to its usual calm smile. “I’ll show you how much you have to lose.”

—

Noah snapped awake as Dragon Fear rolled over him, followed by the memory of fighting Aurelia and then losing control of the Devourer, and he jumped to his feet. “Aurelia!”

She was nowhere to be seen. The cave was empty except for him. There wasn’t even any mana in the vicinity, ambient or otherwise.

He swayed and nearly collapsed, barely catching himself on the wall before dizzily sliding down to a seated position. His head was trying to turn itself inside out, his skull was trying to boil his brain, and his body felt strangely distant and lifeless.

“No, no… I can’t have. Please, Aurelia, be alive.” He strained his memory, trying to recall what the Devourer had done while Noah was unconscious, but there was nothing. Only blankness between passing out and waking up.

That’s when he saw the ring lying abandoned in the middle of the cavern next to an unfamiliar sword. He crawled over to Aurelia’s ring and cradled it against his chest, tears of relief springing to his eyes. Of course, she’d had her escape option. She was safe. She had to be.

“Thank you, thank you, thank you,” he murmured, mentally running through every deity who might have helped ensure her survival, then added another, “Please protect her,” for good measure. He wasn’t normally a praying kind of guy but he’d accumulated enough divine blessings to think it worth a shot.

Why was he so weak? He was breathing hard, his arms were trembling, his legs wouldn’t support him… what had he done?

Health: 12%

Mana: 0%

“Yikes. So I almost died.” He stared at the numbers a minute, then frowned. “Tony? Bun Bun?”

Tony is here, his symbiote’s voice reported faintly. Tony is keeping Noah’s body functional.

That sounded ominous. But, hey, he’d survived, so… “Thanks, Tony. Good job. Keep it up.”

He looked around the cavern and saw no sign of his familiar.

The rabbit is in your storage. Tony said sadly.

Noah reached into his vault to pull out Bun Bun and found only a perfectly ordinary rabbit.

"What happened?" Aurelia had tried to fight him, he'd lost control of The Devourer, and...

Nothing else came to mind. He stretched his memory back further, to the rest of the day. He'd used the gravity field to escape the Ascendants attacking Zax, they'd run here to hide, Bun Bun had admonished him for cowardice, then after they entered the cavern, Aurelia had seemingly forgotten who he was. She'd attacked him, he'd tried not to hurt her, but apparently she was stronger than he remembered. There shouldn't have been a reason otherwise for him to be anywhere close to letting the Devourer out.

[Bun Bun]'s status is currently sealed.

[Regression] is a locked status and cannot be contested.

All his familiar's stats were greyed out, everything from his power and agility numbers to his racial perks and species skills, but the fact that they still existed at all was enough for Noah to let out a sigh of relief. He didn’t know how to undo whatever Regression was, but at least it was an imposed status rather than a true destruction of Bun Bun’s hard-won progress.

Unable to do anything else for his familiar at the moment, he left him where he was and turned his attention to the weapon lying on the floor. A jagged shard of silver-edged obsidian that radiated power in a way he’d never encountered before. Just standing near it was dizzying, and looking down into it made him feel incomplete.

He picked it up carefully and examined it, trying to discern what exactly it was. And where it’d come from. Was it Aurelia’s? But, no, its power felt far, far denser than anything either of them could have created.

[Unforged Blade of The Negator (Legendary)]

What can be bound can be severed and what is made can be unmade. Only that which is eternal continues.

That had to be one of the most ambiguous descriptions for an item he’d seen in a while. It made him smile faintly, like one of those poems from people trying to be deep but ended up just repeating themselves. He tried stuffing it into his vault for later, but encountered the message he was growing too familiar with.

Target is too powerful to contain.

Noah was still staring at the sword when the calm voice cut across his thoughts.

“That belongs to me.” He looked up and met the blandly forgettable eyes of that support guy, Sev, who’d been hanging around with the Ascendants. “Give it back.”

Noah raised an eyebrow as he stood and held up the sword incredulously. “To you? I don’t think so.” He may not know anything about the weapon, but it was still a legendary item and a very powerful one at that.

“You are in no position to refuse.”

He wasn’t wrong, Noah was barely standing and his mana hadn’t regenerated even a single drop yet. Still, he held the sword leveled stiffly at the intruder, willing his arms not to tremble. “I’m not in the mood right now,” he growled.

“Drop the sword. You’re coming with me.” Sev had moved toward him without him noticing. He took hold of the Unforged Blade with one hand. “I’ll be gentle.”

“I don’t trust you.”

But when Noah blinked, the sword had disappeared from his hands as though it had never existed at all.

He stood alone in the center of the cavern, frowning down at his hands. Hadn’t he found a sword? Why did it feel like he was in the middle of a conversation?

He looked around slowly, summoning his spear as he turned, but saw no one. Only the scratches and gouges from the previous fight.

Something was wrong, more than just Noah’s loss of control. His memory wasn’t lining up, gaps and jumps and inconsistencies. A minute ago there’d been a complete mana void, now it was swirling around him like tidal eddies washing into a hole in the sand.

Lingering echoes of hunger tore at his mind. The Devourer’s urge to consume had only grown stronger, though it felt oddly less intent at the moment, almost sleepy.

He felt drained and on the verge of passing out again, but he fought it off with sheer stubbornness and forced himself to keep moving. Without Aurelia, Zax, or even Bun Bun to look out for him, he didn’t like the idea of being vulnerable and unaware. Especially in Death Valley of all places. Tony could only do so much without mana to work with and Zax’s wards were down, leaving him with only the painfully thick mana of the middle depths to work with.

He paced the cave, pushing through the ache and burn of having pushed himself far beyond his limits. There was blood dripping from between his scales and his skull just kept trying to melt its way out of his body with remorseless intensity. He was sure it would have hurt less if his blood had been turned to magma.

He felt in his pocket for the A-Grade core he’d been holding onto, and found nothing but dust. The Devourer must have eaten that too.

Noah finally sat down to meditate, but found himself entirely unable to claim the sense of calm. Something felt unfinished, wrong. Maybe just the part of him that was convinced if he fell asleep here he’d never wake up.

“You coming?” Sev asked, waiting by the exit. “I lured them away for a little, but they’ll be back any minute. We’re going to be trapped if you keep lounging around.” The man looked familiar, but Noah couldn’t place him. He was only level 388, so right around the same as Noah himself, but unaccountably felt like a threat.

Noah slowly stood, keeping his spear ready. “Who are you? What’s going on?”

The man huffed impatiently. “I’m from the Guild, we have to evacuate you before it’s too late.”

“I’m not here on behalf of any guild…”

Was he? He didn’t think he was. Everything about the past day was hopelessly muddled. He retained flashes of overwhelming hunger or all-consuming fear or desperation such as he’d never felt before in his life, none of which told him enough about the circumstances.

Even the reconstructed set of events he’d almost convinced himself to believe when he woke up a few minutes ago were blurred and faded now.

“You were ambushed by three Ascendants,” Sev said gently. The man was standing right beside him now, a look of concern in his normally blank eyes. “Do you remember that?”

Zax roaring for them to flee. Throwing himself into the gravity field to escape. That part still made sense, at least. “I do, yes.”

“And after they killed the dragon, they came here.”

Noah’s heart just about stopped. “What? Zax is dead?” No. They couldn’t have. He’s too strong to go down just like that.

“There were three of them. All Ascendants. The Crimson Cultists and Void Cultists joined forces and sent their strongest against you together.” Sev looked up sharply, tilted his head, and lowered his voice to an urgent whisper. “They’re on their way back. We have to get out, report to the Guild that the cults have joined forces.”

“You go. I have to stay.”

Sev hissed in irritation. “You will die here. Is that what you want? There’s nothing for you. The dragon’s dead, the fire girl is gone, what do you have left?”

“I have to keep going.” Numbness tore at his heart as he tried to deny the man’s words. Zax had been there for so long, always such a towering force, untouchable. He hadn’t been alone since he arrived on Erandir, not properly. Now he went from his closest, strongest, core of allies to… nothing. Just him and Tony, and the promise of death. “I have to keep going,” he repeated, in case he started to forget. “I have to keep going.”

Sev slapped him, his calm finally snapping. “Wake up! We have to go now. I don’t care if you want to throw yourself back into some suicide mission after, but right now you’re in no condition to be out here on your own. Can you at least acknowledge that much?”

Noah nodded mutely. He still made no move to follow. Something about the whole situation bothered him. “I need to meditate first,” he finally said. He doubted anywhere else would be any safer, and given the choice between being tracked down with no mana or cornered with at least some tiny way of fighting back, no amount of a head start would be enough to be sufficient. “Keep watch for me, then we can go.”

“You want to wait to meditate when you have three Ascendant cultists hunting you right now? You really are insane.”

Noah shrugged and sat down. “You won’t be the last to tell me that.”
