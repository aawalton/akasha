Ning had been on the losing end against a red-robed woman whose movements seemed to predict his every attack. Out of curiosity, he had asked, "How do you know all my moves?"

Her expression became proud and complicated.

"I have researched you since your first match…"

Then she began explaining her entire preparation process, her childhood inferiority complex, her rivalry with her elder sister, and why defeating Ning would prove her worth to her clan.

Ning listened for about three breaths.

Then he won.

By the fourth and fifth rounds, he finally understood the truth.

When someone began explaining their tragic past and motivation in the midst of an epic battle, everyone seemed to think this was normal.

Ning alone had apparently not received the notice.

So he decided he would be the one to teach the world that talking on the battlefield was not a free action.

Or perhaps… he looked at his own hand. A terrifying thought rose in his mind.

"…Do I have a supernatural power?"

And so, Ning continued his journey.

From sect to sect, city to city, tournament to tournament, his surroundings changed. His companions changed. His cultivation rose steadily.

But one thing never left him.

Young masters.

From the beginning of his journey until now, Ning had laid waste to countless young masters. At first, he had tried to understand them. Then he had tried diplomacy. Then he had tried avoidance. Then he had tried openly stating his background so that no one would mistake him for a helpless independent cultivator.

None of it worked.

Keeping a low profile made him a country bumpkin. Showing strength made him arrogant. Looking at them was courting death, and so was ignoring them. Eating quietly meant he was at the wrong table, and breathing near a beauty was apparently an insult to her dignity.

At some point, Ning became genuinely curious about how young masters survived long enough to become adults.

One day, while Ning was eating in a restaurant, trouble arrived again.

A servant-like cultivator walked up to his table and spoke haughtily.

"Oi, you. Young Master Xian wants this table. Move along. Young Master Xian is the son of an elder of the Blood Wing Sect. You should count your lucky stars that an esteemed person like Young Master Xian even fancies your table."

Ning looked down at his food, then up at the man.

He could flip the table and finish the man right then and there.

But as always, Ning chose diplomacy.

For some reason, a tiny part of him still believed that one day, one of these situations might resolve peacefully.

That tiny part of him was very stupid, but Ning admired its persistence.

It was his dream.

So he stood up and prepared to leave.

He had only taken a few steps when the same servant stopped him again.

This time, behind the servant stood an extravagantly dressed young man with a beauty on each arm. His robes were lavish, his smile sleazy.

"You," Young Master Xian said lazily. "I find you quite offensive for some reason. Break one of your arms, and you may leave."

The beauties on his arms giggled.

To him, this was clearly entertainment.

Ning blinked.

"What?"

He was not even being given a proper reason.

Young Master Xian chuckled darkly.

"You filthy mongrel. I told you to break your arm. Looking at you makes my eyes sore. Or would you prefer my bodyguard, Miao Gong, to help you?"

A burly cultivator stepped forward.

"You should know that Young Master Xian is the son of an elder of the Blood Wing Sect. You are getting off lightly. Break your arm and scram."

Ning took a deep breath. At that moment, he already knew exactly how the rest of this would unfold.

He would beat the young man. Then the young man would call his father. He would beat the father. Then the young master would call his grandfather. Ning had even had someone call their ancestor to chase after him once.

Ning simply sighed and broke his arm.

For a body cultivator like him, this injury was no great matter. It was not even particularly painful by the standards of how he trained his body, which was truly masochistic.

The restaurant went silent.

Young Master Xian stared.

For a moment, he looked genuinely unsure what to do. This was not in the script.

Then he burst out laughing. "So you do understand your place. Hahaha! Filthy mongrel, take this as your reward for being a good dog."

He threw a few spirit stones onto the floor.

Miao Gong and the beauties immediately praised him.

"Young Master Xian is so generous."

"As expected of Young Master Xian."

"Such mercy."

Ning looked at the spirit stones scattered near his feet. Then he smiled.

"So, you are my next target."

In the past few years, a rumor had been circulating. Multiple demonic sects had been destroyed lately, and it was said that this was because all of these demonic sects had enraged the heavens with their wicked practices, drawing divine retribution.

But the truth was slightly different. It was not heavenly retribution.

It was a single man.

"Hello, I would like to join the Blood Wing Sect." Ning smiled and registered himself as a disciple.

There was another curious thing Ning had gradually noticed during his travels.

The first sect he joined was destroyed by a demonic invasion.

The second sect, where he became a guest elder, was destroyed by a massive beast tide.

The third time, he joined a cultivation family as a dependent, and somehow, the family head gambled away the entire estate. To a chicken farmer. Ning was still not sure how that happened.

The fourth time, the same thing happened.

After careful observation, repeated experimentation, and several unfortunate collapses of local powers, Ning finally discovered another one of his talents.

Wherever he joined, that place eventually fell apart.

Thus, instead of joining righteous sects and causing innocent people trouble, Ning began joining demonic sects instead.

After becoming a peripheral disciple, standing beneath the Blood Wing Sect's blood-red mountain gate, Ning looked up at the dark clouds gathering above it.

His arm had already healed, and his smile was calm.

"Blood Wing Sect," he murmured, "nothing personal."

Then he paused.

"No, actually, it is a little personal."

He remembered Young Master Xian's laughter, the broken-arm demand, the spirit stones thrown at his feet, and the beauties praising his "mercy."

Ning's smile became brighter.

"Blame your young master."

For he was Ning.

He was death, the destroyer of sects.
