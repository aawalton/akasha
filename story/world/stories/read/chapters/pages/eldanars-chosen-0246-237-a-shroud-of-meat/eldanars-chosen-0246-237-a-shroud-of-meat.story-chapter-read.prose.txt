The elven longbow thrummed violently as the thick, magically reinforced string snapped forward.

Perberos did not lower the weapon. He remained perfectly frozen on the thick oak branch, his left arm locked as steady as carved marble. His luminous silver eyes tracked the dark, spinning streak of the Shadow-Strike arrow as it tore across the vast, corrupted expanse of the valley. Time seemed to drag exponentially, stretching a mere two seconds of physical flight into an agonising, suffocating eternity for the elven scout.

The arrow had struck the boss monster directly in the centre of its right eye socket.

The visual impact was flawless. The heavy steel bodkin had met the dormant, invisible magical barrier protecting the creature’s skull. The sickly green runes etched into the ancient metal flared violently, desperately attempting to snap shut and harden against the sudden kinetic force. Perberos’s Shadow-Strike enchantment was designed specifically for that exact moment. The spinning dark mana violently chewed through the rising barrier, shattering the magical ward like brittle glass. The heavy steel shaft punched cleanly through the shattered shield and buried itself deep into the glowing green soul-fire burning within the General's skull.

He’d watched the massive, towering monstrosity stagger violently as Garth and Silas’s projectiles struck a fraction of a second later, cratering the breastplate and tearing into the shoulder joint. He’d watched the boss monster drop heavily onto one iron-plated knee, its dark broadsword slipping from its grasp to crash against the frozen mud.

After another moment, a profound, suffocating wave of relief washed over the elven scout. His tense shoulders finally dropped an inch. The impossible mathematics of the assassination had aligned perfectly. They had actually done it.

Then, the piercing scream tore across the valley.

It was not a sound meant for mortal ears. It possessed no vocal cords, no lungs to push air. It was a massive, concussive shockwave of violently unravelling necrotic magic.

The psychic wail hit the eastern ridge with the force of a physical gale. Perberos was forced to drop instantly to one knee, clutching the rough bark of the oak branch tightly to stop himself from being blown entirely out of the high canopy. The dead pine needles and frozen leaves surrounding him were violently stripped from the trees, swirling into the air like a localised, dark hurricane.

The terrifying sound rattled deep inside his skull. It was a piercing, freezing agony that made his enhanced vision blur and his ears ring with a high-pitched whine. It felt as though a jagged shard of ice was being driven directly into the centre of his brain.

He forced his silver eyes open, squinting through the tearing wind and the flying debris to look back down at the valley floor. He needed confirmation. He needed to see the General dissolve into a mountain of golden dust or crumble into loose bones, to ensure the threat to Ashenfall was permanently ended.

But the reaction of the horde to the death of their commander was entirely alien, a gruesome spectacle that defied all standard logic.

The dark intelligence commanding the army had been shattered, or so Perberos hoped. He rationalised the psychic scream as a violent death rattle. Perhaps it was a bound wraith, or a high-tier spectral bodyguard anchored to the boss monster's runic armour, screaming in sudden agony as its magical tether was violently severed by the Shadow-Strike arrow.

Whatever the source of the wail, the effect on the lesser monsters was immediate and horrific.

Hundreds of the lesser zombies, rotting ghouls, and Crypt Weavers stationed in the immediate rear lines reacted with a sudden, panicked frenzy. They did not turn their heads to face the source of the arrows. They completely ignored the distant eastern ridge. Instead, they turned inward, swarming directly towards their fallen commander.

Perberos watched in absolute disgust as the undead literally threw themselves on top of the kneeling Bone General.

They did not form a traditional shield wall. They formed a living, rotting mound of expendable meat. It looked as though the mindless monsters were being drawn to the violently unravelling dark magic of their master’s core, acting like mindless insects swarming the fading warmth of a dying fire.

Zombies scrambled over the backs of skeletal knights, throwing their bodies onto the behemoth. Crypt Weavers scuttled upward, wrapping their thick, hairy legs around the pile to anchor the flesh in place. Rotting villagers crushed themselves against the General's runic armour in a chaotic, disorganised frenzy.

Within seconds, the towering Bone General was completely buried beneath a massive, writhing hill of hundreds of corrupted bodies.

Perberos let out a slow, steadying breath. It was a horrifying sight, but it made a twisted sort of sense. Without the General's controlling intelligence, the horde was devolving into basic, primal chaos. The magical anchor holding the army together was broken, and the resulting vacuum was pulling the weakest monsters into a mindless collapse.

He lowered his longbow, resting the curved lower limb against the bark of the branch. The muscles in his back and shoulders burned with a fierce accumulation of lactic acid, but the pain was entirely eclipsed by the profound sense of victory.

The deafening, psychic scream continued to tear through the valley, echoing off the hills, though the pitch was slowly beginning to ebb. It was transitioning from a sharp roar of agony into a low, sustained, miserable wail that vibrated in Perberos's teeth. It sounded exactly like a dying spirit mourning the loss of its master.

A soft, distinct rustle of dead leaves directly below him made the elf pivot smoothly, looking down into the shadows of the lower branches.

"Easy, you twitchy bastard," a familiar, raspy voice grunted from the gloom.

Ronald dropped silently onto the thick branch beside Perberos, moving with the liquid grace of a falling leaf. The feline beastkin’s tawny fur was ruffled from the wind, and his jade-green eyes were wide, glowing with a massive, euphoric rush of adrenaline. He was breathing heavily, having sprinted up the steep incline of the ridge the moment the arrows had flown.

A moment later, Garth and Silas hauled themselves up the thick trunk, panting heavily as they climbed into the safety of the upper canopy.

"By the Light, did you see it?" Silas gasped, completely unable to contain his raw excitement.

The young ranger’s face was flushed a deep red from the freezing cold and the sheer thrill of survival. He practically bounced on the thick wooden branch, entirely forgetting his rigorous stealth training. "We hit it! We perfectly synchronised the volley! I saw my arrow take it right in the shoulder!"

"It went down on one knee," Garth confirmed, his gruff voice carrying a profound, undeniable sense of awe. The scarred tracker leaned heavily against the trunk of the oak, wiping freezing sweat from his brow with a trembling hand. "I saw the armour crack inward. I saw the green light inside the breastplate flicker and die down. It was a beautiful shot, lad."

Perberos looked at the three hunters, his stoic mask slipping completely to reveal the profound, desperate relief shining in his silver eyes.

"The attack on the walls?" Perberos asked, his voice tight and clipped, needing absolute confirmation that his sister was safe. "Did it stop?"

"It broke entirely," Ronald confirmed, a sharp, predatory grin splitting his face, exposing his long fangs. "The second that scream echoed across the plains, the pressure on the city vanished. I could see the main iron gates from the brush. The horde just stopped moving. The mindless ones are simply standing there, staring at the grass like lost sheep. They are completely leaderless."

Silas let out a loud, triumphant laugh, clapping Garth hard on the shoulder.

"We did it!" the young ranger cheered, throwing his arms wide. "We saved the city! The siege is broken! We are bloody heroes!"

Perberos closed his eyes, leaning his head back against the rough bark of the oak tree. He let out a long, shuddering sigh.

The suffocating terror that had gripped his heart since he watched Josh leap off the wall as a literal comet of lightning finally released its hold. Carcan was safe. His twin sister was alive. His friends had survived the apocalyptic meat grinder at the gates. The impossible, suicidal gamble had actually paid off.

"I will admit," Ronald chuckled, crossing his arms and settling comfortably against the tree trunk, "that was arguably the finest piece of coordinated marksmanship I have ever witnessed in all my years with the Guild. Your Shadow-Strike pierced that barrier exactly as planned, Perberos. It was flawless work under impossible pressure."

"Thank you," Perberos replied softly, opening his eyes and offering a genuine, warm smile to his companions.

They allowed themselves a few minutes to simply exist in the euphoric, adrenaline-soaked aftermath of the strike. The tension that had kept them coiled like springs all morning slowly began to bleed away. They passed a leather waterskin between them, drinking deeply to soothe their dry, burning throats, savouring the taste of pure victory.

The low, psychic wail that had been echoing across the plains was slowly fading away, replaced by the natural, howling sounds of the winter wind sweeping across the ruined landscape.

However, the cold, pragmatic reality of their physical location eventually forced its way back into their minds.

"Right then," Garth grunted, capping the waterskin and securing his heavy crossbow tightly to his back. "The job is done. The city is safe. But we are currently sitting in a tree, behind enemy lines. The horde might be aimless and passive right now, but there are still thousands of the rotting bastards standing between us and a warm bed at the Bullhead Inn."

"We can’t traverse the open plains," Ronald noted, his tactical mind re-engaging instantly. He looked down into the valley, his green eyes narrowing as he assessed the sprawling sea of monsters. "Even without a commander directing them, walking directly through a dense crowd of ghouls is sheer suicide. They will react to our scent, our body heat, and the sound of our footsteps. We would be swarmed in minutes."

"We stick to the forest," Silas suggested, pointing towards the thick, corrupted treeline hugging the eastern ridge. "We circle wide around the valley, follow the hills, and approach Ashenfall from the southern gates. It will add a few hours to the journey, and the terrain is rough, but it keeps us entirely out of sight of the main horde."

"Agreed," Perberos nodded, looking back down at the massive, writhing mound of meat where the Bone General had fallen.

He wanted to leave. He desperately wanted to get back to the city to ensure Carcan was truly unharmed and to properly thank Josh for protecting her.

But something about the valley floor caught his attention. The chaotic flow of the undead had shifted into a rigid, structured pattern that did not align with a leaderless, passive horde.

"Ronald," Perberos said, pointing a slender, leather-clad finger towards the northern edge of the plains. "Look at the elite guard."

The beastkin stepped to the edge of the branch, following the elf's gaze.

Marching rapidly out of the freezing fog, returning from their brutal assault on the city walls, were the Wights. The dozens of heavily armoured, blackened steel knights had not scattered or grown aimless like the lesser infantry. They had not stopped to stare blankly at the grass. They moved with absolute, terrifying military purpose, their heavy boots floating just a fraction of an inch above the churned mud.

The four assassins watched in tense silence as the phalanx of elite knights arrived at the command position deep in the rear lines.

The Wights did not attack the writhing mound of lesser zombies. They did not attempt to dig their fallen commander out of the grotesque pile. Instead, they formed a perfect, impenetrable ring of heavy tower shields and raised greatswords around the horrific hill of flesh, facing outward to defend the position from all angles.

"They are forming a defensive perimeter," Garth whispered, his scarred face paling slightly as he gripped his crossbow. "Are they protecting the remains?"

"A final, lingering command," Ronald reasoned, his green eyes narrowing as he analysed the tactical formation. "Or perhaps a deeply ingrained military protocol. Guard the fallen king. It does not matter. The core is shattered. Without the General constantly feeding them ambient mana, those elite knights will eventually run dry and crumble into dust. We just need to stay out of their path."

Perberos nodded slowly, accepting the logic. The Wights were high-tier monsters, possessing enough residual dark magic to operate independently for a short time. Guarding the corpse of their commander was a perfectly logical final directive for an elite guard.

"Then we leave them to their vigil," Perberos stated quietly, turning his back on the valley to look towards the deeper forest. "We move south. Stay high in the canopy until we cross the first ridge."

"Sounds like a brilliant plan," Silas smiled, securing his quiver.

Before any of them could take a step toward the adjacent branches, the low, miserable psychic wail that had been echoing across the plains completely stopped.

The sudden silence was absolutely deafening. The howling winter wind seemed to die away entirely, leaving the corrupted valley in a state of suspended, terrifying stillness.

Down in the valley, the ring of Wight knights stopped scanning the open plains.

In perfect, terrifying, mechanical unison, every single blackened steel helmet turned slowly to the east.

The bright, piercing blue pinpricks of soul-fire burning within their dark visors locked directly onto the eastern ridge. They completely ignored the rest of the forest. They ignored the city walls. They locked directly onto the ancient oak tree where the four assassins were hiding.

"What are they doing?" Silas whispered, his voice trembling violently as the previous bravado evaporated in an instant.

"They know where the arrows came from," Garth replied, his scarred hands shaking as he unhooked his heavy crossbow.

The Wights did not hesitate. The elite phalanx broke their circular defensive ring. They raised their heavy greatswords and turned their backs on the horrific mound of meat. Moving with a rapid, completely silent, and highly coordinated military march, the blackened steel knights began to move toward the treeline.

Behind them, the massive mound of zombies began to shift. The pile of rotting meat trembled violently, and a deep, resonant, and entirely telepathic voice echoed directly into the minds of the four hunters in the tree.

Did you truly believe, the voice hissed, carrying the cold, ancient malice of the grave, that I was that easy to kill, weak mortals?

The voice did not just rattle in their skulls; it resonated across the entire valley. The terrifying realisation crashed over Perberos with the physical force of a falling boulder. The General was not dead. It had used the psychic scream to summon a living shield, burying itself beneath its own soldiers to safely regenerate its shattered core away from the sniper fire.

The thousands of aimless, passive zombies and ghouls standing on the open plains suddenly snapped their heads towards the eastern ridge. The milky, lifeless eyes of ten thousand monsters locked onto the forest. The mindless stupor vanished instantly, replaced by a violent, shrieking frenzy.

The horde surged forward, a massive, unstoppable ocean of rotting meat flooding toward the treeline.

"Move!" Ronald roared, abandoning all pretence of stealth and pulling his curved blades from his hips. "Run!"

The celebration was truly over. The assassins were now the hunted, and the entire army of the dead was coming for them.
