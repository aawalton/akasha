I stare at him. “You need a favor from me?”

I’d think he was joking if he didn’t look so deadly serious. What in the world could this be about? What’s something I can do that he can’t?

Is it something to do with gods? With being a champion? My stomach sours at the idea.

“I do,” he says, still just as grave as before. “Please. I’ll never ask for anything from you again.”

“Uh, sure man,” I say, too caught off guard to ask what the favor is, first. And I mean, come on, who could say no to a friend with a plea like that? “What do you need?”

Cyros turns back to the job postings and nods toward the wall. “The lindwyrm one. For collecting scales.”

I scan the jobs until I find the one he’s looking for. My eyes widen. “But that’s in B Tier!”

“I know,” he says, still staring at the job, as if it might vanish if he takes his eyes off of it. “That’s why I need your help.”

Cyros is still only rated for D Tier jobs, mostly because he hasn’t made the job board a priority. He takes one every once in a while, just when he needs the money, I think, but he doesn’t prioritize rising the ranks like I have been.

Technically, you’re not allowed to accept a job for someone else. That’s why there’s a Tier system in the first place; so people don’t get in over their heads and screw up bad enough to bring heat on the Guild. But there’s no rules about not letting someone in a lower Tier help you with a job.

I grab my token and put it on the job to mark it as claimed, just so no one else would grab it out from under our nose. Then I take a moment to read through the whole brief.

[Wanted: Lindwyrm hide]

[Scope: Adult male lindwyrm pelt is needed with scales intact. Minimum length of 10 meters is required.]

[Reward: Adderstone]

Honestly, going after an adult lindwyrm doesn’t exactly thrill me. I saw one from a distance, once, and that was as close as I’ve ever been planning to get; they’re big, territorial, and mean. Somewhere between a snake and a dragon, lindwyrms have two front legs, but no hind legs or wings, and they can grow up to fifteen meters long. Some of them even spit acid or fire, Echo told me, though I’ve thankfully avoided experiencing that firsthand. This feels more like an A Tier job than a B Tier one.

And more than the danger level, I’m worried about not being able to complete the job. If I take this and it ends up being too much, I’ll have to relinquish my claim on the job. Not only is that a bad look, it can actually get you knocked back down rank—especially if it’s the very first job you take in a new rank. You might as well be shouting to the rest of the Guild “I’m in over my head!”

“Why are you even interested in this one, anyway?” I ask him. “For the reward? An adderstone?”

He nods curtly, but doesn’t otherwise respond.

Echo, what’s an adderstone? I ask.

[Adderstone: A rare magical object occasionally found in nature—especially near Ruins—capable of containing powerful and random healing properties.]

Weird. Random healing properties? Why not just pay for a healer at that rate?

But it’s clear Cyros isn’t about to go into detail about what he wants the stone for—no matter how much I’m dying of curiosity.

“This one is going to be dangerous,” I tell him. “I mean, an adult lindwyrm? I’m not sure you and I would be enough to take one down.” While remaining in one piece, at least.

He gives me a sharp look. “You want to rival the gods and you’re worried about a wild animal?”

Heat raises up my cheeks and anger bubbles up my chest. “That’s not—!” I stammer. “I mean—”

Cyros lets out a breath, shaking his head. When he looks back up at me, it’s with a sad smile. “Sorry. That’s not what I meant. It’s just, I really need this.”

I wrinkle my nose at him, trying to tap down on my irritation. As much as I hate to admit it, he’s right; if I’m hesitating over fighting some giant worm, then Widengra’s nowhere in sight.

“Fine,” I grumble, snatching the paper from the wall. Not like I could say no to the guy, anyway. “But I don’t even know how we’re supposed to find one of these wyrms. Aren’t they super rare?”

“That’s why it’s B Tier,” he says, following me over as I move the job posting into the Claimed column and sticking my marker on it. A spell flashes beneath the wall, indicating that my claim has been logged; no one can try to swap the job out now.

“Besides,” Cyros adds, “what about that guy you mentioned the other day? The one you said was a Monster Hunter?”

“What?” I cry. “Álvaro? No way. That guy was a jerk! He stole my kill!”

Cyros smirks. “Sounds like he owes you, then.”

I mean, he does, but that doesn’t mean I’m any more excited by the prospect of asking him for help. And I’m not even sure if his Class would work that way. I know it has to do with hunting creatures, but can he even choose what he wants to hunt?

On the other hand, I do kind of want to warn him about Widengra’s champions. They might not be after him, but since he’s another Traveler, he could still land in the gods’ crosshairs without even knowing it’s a risk. The guy might be rude, but I don’t want him dead.

“Fine,” I grumble. “We can go track him down and see if finding a lindwyrm is something he can even do. But I’m not going to be the one asking him for help—that’s going to have to be you.”

“Fine by me,” Cyros says. He stops, grabbing my shoulder as I start to stomp away. “And hey. I just want to say thanks. Really. This means a lot to me.”

The sincerity in his tone and expression unnerves me. Like I just promised him the world or something. It makes me feel uneasy; you can’t let someone down who doesn’t expect anything from you.

“Sure,” I say, shrugging off his hand. “No problem.”

I hurry ahead, leading the way out of the Guild just so I don’t have to look at him any longer.

Tracking down Álvaro ends up being more annoying than I’d thought. I guess I should have expected that; a tracker probably doesn’t like to be tracked themselves. Still, with Echo on my side, it shouldn’t be that hard—unless he knows we’re looking for him and he’s actively avoiding me.

Which is entirely possible, given he stole my scorpid.

I visit the next Night Market, but he’s not there. I head back out into the desert, searching for more animals he might be hunting or trying to pick up any signs of a trail, but I wouldn’t even know what to look for. Finally, after several days of fruitless wandering, I catch a lead in the merchant district: a boy with the same description as Álvaro had been shopping around dangerous animal parts for the last month or so.

Cyros joins me as I post up in the merchant distract to keep an eye out for Álvaro. Cyros hasn’t met him, so he probably wouldn’t recognize him just from my description, but more eyes can’t hurt.

Besides, when he’s on look-out duty, I have time to play around with the refiner.

I haven’t made much progress with it, to be honest. It seems unbreakable, despite the hairline crack that exists along one side. It also really doesn’t feel like some sort of god-threatening weapon; so far as I can tell, it can’t actually do anything. Except give me the faintest, staticky sense of something when I touch the crack. And something tells me that’s not going to kill a god.

On one such look-out session, Cyros and I are staked out in an empty shop in the merchant district. Cyros is on watch, and I’m supposed to be sleeping, but I’m not particularly tired, so I’m rolling the marble around between my fingers instead.

Open Sesame, I think. Well, it was worth a shot. I’ve tried just about everything else, short of eating it or making some kind of blood sacrifice.

Actually, a blood sacrifice might not be the worst idea.

I mean, not literally. (Okay, literally, too, but that’s a last resort.) But so far I’ve just been trying to break it open. Maybe it would be more receptive if I gave it something it wanted. And what might a mysterious magical relic want?

Magic.

I’m all out of other ideas. And though I don’t exactly have an abundance of mana, even I can squeeze out a few drops now and then.

So I do. Why the hell not?

Squeezing the refiner in my fist, I close my eyes and focus on summoning a couple drops of mana. Just like activating a spell circle, I mentally press my magic into the refiner.

And this time, something happens.

It’s subtle. The marble doesn’t open up like some sort of fancy flower. But I do feel something. A different something from before. This feels like a stirring. A rousing of whatever is inside. Like an ancient beast fell asleep long ago, and now it’s taken its first deep breath as it prepares to wake.

I grin, pushing a few more points of mana into the refiner. Nothing changes this time, but to be fair I barely gave it any mana at all. If only I had a bigger mana pool at my disposal! I’ve got a couple of mana potions I could take, but those don’t generate more mana: it takes mana to make mana potions, so really it’s just like saving mana for later.

But if I’m on the right track here, then I’ll just have to keep adding a little bit, day by day, and see if the refiner does anything else. I hope it’s a threshold thing. I hope eventually I’ll hit the point where I can get it to do something.

At least this is a start.

“Sal,” Cyros hisses, and I jump. I quickly tuck the refiner away, glancing up from my bed roll to see Cyros at the shop’s window. He isn’t looking my way, though.

“What?” I ask, sitting up.

“Someone who fits your description,” he says. “Come quick.”

I quietly move to his side, looking out. Cyros nods toward the end of the street just in time for me to catch someone disappear around the corner. “I don’t know. I didn’t get a good look.”

“I think it was him,” Cyros says, standing. “Hurry, let’s go see.”

“Now? Wait—hold up—”

Cyros is already out the door while I haven’t even had a chance to snatch up my boots. I hurriedly stuff them on my feet, glance at the bed roll, and decide to come back for it later. No time to get it stored properly in my Apothecary.

I have to jog to catch up to Cryos. “Thanks for waiting for me.”

“Sorry,” he says, his pace quick, eyes locked at the end of the street. “Don’t want to let this opportunity slip past us.”

He really wants to complete this job. I consider pressing him about it again, but now’s not the time to chit chat, even if I thought he’d actually answer me. I activate Soft Step as we round the corner and hurry down the street.

I soon catch sight of Álvaro. The boy is wearing loose, sand-colored clothing—or maybe it just looks like that because he’s covered in sand. He must have just come back from a hunt, judging by the stuffed satchel on his back and the tangled mess his black hair is in.

“Yeah,” I say to Cyros under my breath. “That’s him.”

Cyros nods and picks up his pace. I fall in line behind him.

We catch up as Álvaro stops at a stall, talking to the merchant within. They’re haggling over a price or something when Cyros steps up behind the human.

“Hello, Álvaro,” Cyros says. “We’ve been looking for you.”

Álvaro jumps, spinning around. His hand goes to his whip and his eyes narrow, looking the dryad up and down. “Who the hell are you?”

“Friend of a friend,” Cyros says. “I have something I’d like to talk to you ab—hey!”

Álvaro is already sprinting down the street.

I snort. “Maybe don’t act like you’re going to threaten him.”

“I didn’t!” Cyros says, bewildered.

“You’ve been spending too much time with Guild members,” I tell him. “That was definitely shady.”

Both Cyros and I take off after him.

“I was just going to ask for a favor,” Cyros grumbles as we run.

“Try opening with that next time,” I say. People have stopped in bewilderment as Álvaro races down the street, which provides fewer moving obstacles for us to dodge around.

Álvaro turns down a side street, and a few seconds later, we turn after him.

The alley is empty. Cyros skids to a halt, glancing around. I glare at the empty alley; I’ve seen him use this trick before.

“Hey!” I shout. “Álvaro! It’s me, you jerk. We just want to talk!”

There’s a pause.

Then a disembodied voice calls back to us. “That’s not code for ‘kick your butt because you stole my scorpid,’ is it?”

I glare in his general direction. “No. Actually, we need a favor. Help me with this, and I’ll call it even.”

There’s another pause, and then Álvaro abruptly appears, melting into existence like a chameleon shifting colors. He’s seated casually on a crate halfway down the alley.

“Well that’s an offer I can’t refuse,” he says with a grin. “Did you miss me that bad?”

I’m already having regrets.
