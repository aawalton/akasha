“This the prettyboy who’s been thinking he’s hot shit?” a guard asked. When Ezra saw her clearly, he had to suppress a series of sulfurous curses. She was wearing, instead of the leather armor and simple metal helmets that the usual guards wore, a metal breastplate and helmet with a plume of feathers from the top. That meant this was a detective, one of the few in the city, and Ezra couldn’t hope to bully his way through or past the group now. Immediately, he began cursing himself for getting a big head, for forgetting that, even if these fuckers didn’t think of him as a slum rat, they didn’t need a reason to hate one of the soldiers. More interestingly, she had bright green eyes, a deeply uncommon sight around these parts, just like his own bright blue ones.

“Yeah, it’s him,” one answered. The three men and two women in front of him had gathered together in front of the gate that led into the inner city, where Ezra needed to go, but now they started to spread out, the detective in the middle and with the other woman at her side. “Kept talking like he thought the Count’s rules didn’t apply to him.”

“You think I believe that any of the money you’re taking from the people coming in from the slums to the inner city is going to the Count?” Ezra scoffed, his blood already up and ready for a fight.

“The way that we enforce Villy’s laws are up to the discretion of the guards of the city,” the detective said, already drawing the nightstick from her waist. “And, for all we know, you’re a deserter or a thief who’s gotten hold of a real soldier’s uniform, and now you’re using it to try to evade the legal guardians of the city’s attention.”

“That’s easy to get an answer for,” Ezra replied, holding himself tall as he looked at the five people who surrounded him. “My name is Ezra, and I’m one of the recruits for Lieutenant Georg of the garrison. I can take you straight there and they’ll show you that I was right in keeping you from fleecing one of the people brave enough in the city to sign up to protect you lazy fucks from the goblinoids.”

“You’ve got a mouth and a whole lot of gall to think his name will save you,” the detective laughed, her green eyes seeming to darken as she spat the words. “Sounds like you’re one of the slumrats, that’s for sure. I guess, if you surrender your weapon and everything else you’re holding, we can take you to the stockade for the night and send a messenger to the army’s leadership tomorrow morning. Give you time to cool that hot head.”

“That’s not gonna happen,” Ezra shook his head. “I’m due to get back tonight, just like every soldier given leave. We’re back on duty in the morning, and I’m not going to miss it because you got your panties in a twist.”

“It’s not a request, rat,” the detective snarled. “Now hand over the weapon and we’ll pat you down, give you time to cool down.”

With an angry laugh of his own, Ezra shook his head again. “You think I don’t know what a group of pissed off guards does to someone who surrenders to them? I’ve seen the blood and teeth that get left behind after a beating like that. You’ll have to take my weapon from me before I surrender, and I wish you good fucking luck on that.”

“As you want, then,” the detective lifted her nightstick as she replied. “Get him.”

Two men crashed in towards Ezra from both sides, and he lowered his center of gravity as he cupped his left arm over the back of his head while he twisted. In his right hand, the cane swept out and cracked into the guard on his right’s knee. The man grunted in pain as his leg buckled, and his companion to Ezra’s back smashed his nightstick exactly where Ezra’d been waiting for it.

His arm screamed in pain, but it wasn’t anything Ezra couldn’t take. In fact, he blessed the army for feeding him so well, because with so much more muscle along his shoulder and forearm, the wood mostly bounced off of his muscle before it could crack against bone. Unfortunately, Ezra didn’t have much time to face the single guard facing him, because the other three were closing in on him.

With a roar, Ezra continued twisting, his cane whipping through the air to crack into the man who’d stuck him. The nightstick fell from his hand as Ezra’s cane splintered against the man’s elbow. He screamed as his arm went limp, and Ezra twisted to throw his ruined cane at the detective. She startled and stepped back, fear in her eyes as Ezra swept up the nightstick he’d knocked out of his attacker’s hand. His breath misted in front of him, the cool air of the approaching night making it cold enough once again that the mud they were fighting in was starting to stiffen underfoot.

“Take him down! This is a dangerous criminal! He’s struck the policing force of Villy and deserves to go to prison!”

The detective’s commanding voice had disappeared, and in its place was a quivering, childish demand. In response, the two people who’d been hit gathered themselves, though the man whose knee had been bent inward couldn’t do much but sit on the ground and the other had a busted arm and no weapon. There were still two untouched guards, though, and they came forward with a dogged persistence that Ezra knew he wouldn’t be able to avoid. After all, he couldn’t rely on luckily avoiding hard hits.

However, his inner world was as full as it’d ever been, and the storm inside demanded to be loosed.

Ezra roared as he pushed aura over his whole body in what Jormun had called his “beginner’s shroud”. Wind whipped around him and the ache in his left arm faded somewhat as the aura fortified his body. The two approaching attacks seemed to slow in his vision, and he ducked between them as he closed in on the detective. Her eyes swelled in her head as he grew closer, and her mouth, already open to shout orders, dropped wider open as she screamed.

“I’m sorry,” Ezra shouted as he stepped through the encirclement, though something caught his foot as he did so. He stumbled, but with the assistance of his shroud, he managed to keep his feet. Behind him, four pairs of eyes glared hatefully, before him screamed a terrified face. Without more than another thought, Ezra determined which side had the greatest danger for him.

Drawing heavily on his inner world, Ezra took nearly half of his internal aura reserves. He knew that his inner world had continued to grow since he officially ascended to Adept, so this attack was stronger than even the one that he’d used to kill the orc. Instead of trying to make his weapon fly faster, further, and more accurately, he sent a wave of aura surging up his arm and out of the tip of his nightstick. Gale-force winds whipped out of his weapon, ripping dirt, mud, and slushy snow from the ground and flying at the four guards.

The three on their feet were forced back a step as dirt flew in their eyes and wing blew them away while the poor man on the ground was thrown into a roll. Mud and filth covered that one from head to toe as he tumbled away, and Ezra felt himself sag as his inner world lost the reserves to keep his shroud going. Knowing what he had to do, Ezra abandoned any thoughts of continuing to fight, though he didn’t want to beat any of the guards to unconsciousness anyways. Instead, he turned towards the detective, who’d stumbled back at his display of aura.

“You—you can—you’re!”

He didn’t care to attempt to get into some sort of a conversation with her. Instead, he decided to go to the only people who might be able to get him out of this mess. Without saying a word, Ezra ran towards the detective, who jumped to the side as her hand holding her nightstick shook. Without trying to fight her, he just darted past, running with all the speed that had been drilled into him over the past months.

Arguments and fearful words erupted behind him, but Ezra didn’t pay them any mind, simply continuing his running onward. So far as he knew, salvation was waiting for him at his destination, and, with nobody close enough behind him to call any other guards’ notice to him, he just looked like a soldier on his way to a destination. After hiding the nightstick up a sleeve, Ezra had nothing other than his speed and frame to call other people’s attention to him, and nobody was going to get in his way, guard or otherwise.

In a few short minutes, Ezra ran into the compound that made the headquarters for the army, where a dozen of his fellow soldiers saw his sweaty approach.

“Calm down, kid!” someone called out. “Dinner just ended, but I’m sure you can still get something for yourself!”

Ezra ignored the voice, instead looking for anyone from the elite squad. He was about to curse, seeing none of them, when a familiar voice spoke up to him from behind.

“What’s wrong, Ezra?” Amy asked, concern evident in her voice.

“I need to meet with Georg, immediately,” he answered.

At his tone, Amy stiffened. “Do you or anyone else need medical attention?”

“No, I don’t,” he shook his head. “Someone else? Maybe…?”

“Shit,” she breathed. “Follow me.”

She jogged lightly towards Captain Riyal’s office, not in as much of a hurry that Ezra had been in, but still drawing eyes from people all around seeing that it wasn’t just one of the rookies who was hurrying around. There wasn’t nearly enough time for anyone to ask them questions before Amy was at the door and knocking in a deliberate pattern. No sooner than had the sound faded did Georg’s voice call out, “Come in.”

Amy pushed through the door, pulling Ezra behind her, and then she shut it behind them. “Ezra said he needed to speak with you immediately, Georg. Knowing him, this wasn’t something to wait around for.”

“Thank you,” Georg said, nodding to her. Then, he looked at the Captain and asked, “Is it alright with you if the genius interrupts our game?”

“Of course,” Riyal said, gesturing magnanimously. Only then did Ezra see the checkered board in front of the two men. It held pieces he’d never seen, of various shapes and sizes, but Georg turned and faced Ezra as he tried to gather himself.

“Now, Ezra, tell me what happened.” Georg’s voice was firm, demanding. His neck was still bound, but he’d grown more and more confident in his bearing over the past weeks, and he looked like the powerful soldier he’d always been in Ezra’s eyes as he spoke.

“Yes, sir. I was visiting my family in the slums, and helping them relocate today. We were getting some supplies from the inner city as we went, and I accompanied them as we passed through the checkpoint. When the guards tried to give us trouble, I shut them down.”

“Shut them down how?” Georg asked, his tone strange.

“Similar to how I’ve seen you do it,” Ezra answered. “Call them out for their cowardice and keep moving.”

Georg’s jaw clenched while Riyal’s face seemed to hold itself back from… laughing? “Continue.”

“Yes, sir. On my way back in tonight, a full patrol of five were waiting for me, with a detective. They demanded that I give them all my belongings and go with them to the stockade. I refused.”

“Shit,” Georg said, his eyes widening. All trace of humor had drained from Riyal as well. Ezra’s mouth went dry, but he continued speaking.

“I fought back, taking a few small hits. I wasn’t wounded,” he said hurriedly. “Then, after drawing on my aura, I drove them away. Once I was free of their ambush, I ran straight here, sirs. Now, I’m here, and asking for your help with anything that I might need.”

“What did the detective look like?” Riyal asked, sitting forward, his eyes intense.

“Maybe… a hundred and sixty-five centimeters tall?” Ezra hazarded. “Looked like she had dark hair under her helmet. It was pulled out of her face though. Green eyes, though. You don’t forget those easily.”

“Dammit,” Riyal breathed.

“Fuck. Fuck!” Georg said.

“What?” Ezra asked.

“I’ll explain on the way,” Georg said, standing and looking at Riyal. “But you’ve kicked a hornets’ nest, and we’re not getting out of this without being stung.”

“I can’t really get between you on this,” Riyal said, shrugging helplessly. “You’ve left behind any rights of succession, even if you’re still a Standage, while the Wainwrights are influential and she’s the apple of her father’s eye.”

“I know,” Georg sighed. Then, squaring his shoulders, he looked at Ezra. Then, he said, “Now, we need to go see if we can spare you from the worst of these consequences.”
