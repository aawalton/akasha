It was a little before noon by the time the three of them reached Maftem.

The road had long since turned from a narrow, unbeaten path into a steady stream of wagons, horses, carriages, travelers, civilians, and adventurers alike.

The journey to Maftem had been quiet and pleasant. It had taken a little under two months to arrive, as expected. Their rations had also lasted well enough, just as Sage had calculated before they departed Ronswick. During that time, they rarely encountered any monster attacks. They could mostly sit back and relax, keeping themselves stress-free instead of further aggravating their injuries.

“Guys, I think we’re here.” Gwen sat at the coach like always, being the least injured one in the party. She leaned forward, spotting the city in the distance.

Veronica pushed aside the curtain and peered out.

Ahead of them, Duke Pelotema’s city rose beyond a stretch of open land, with several lush patches of forest trees resting outside its walls.

The city’s walls were built from pale stone, reinforced by an outer layer of darker, mana-conductive metal bands. The metal served to spread the flow of mana from the city’s mage towers, forming a strong barrier. It was similar to the one in Annesheim, though slightly weaker. Likewise, magitech cannons were mounted atop the city walls, spaced evenly along the perimeter.

Magitech cannons harnessed the energy within pure mana crystals, converting it into powerful blasts of concentrated mana. Though they couldn’t stop something like a dragon, they would usually leave a wound if all twelve cannons fired at once.

“This is Maftem?” Nasha said, peering through the curtain beside Veronica.

“Yeah. The big city. Second only to Annesheim,” Veronica said.

She had been here a few times in her previous life, though never for long. Back then, it was usually for business. Political moves to build relations. Never leisure. She had carried authority whenever she stepped into the city’s sphere, but now she would experience it from a commoner’s point of view.

Their wagon followed the paved road closer to the city. Several adventurers passed them, heading out to complete their commissions. Veronica couldn’t help but notice that the three of them received a few looks of interest. Nasha simply smiled, while Gwen waved excitedly at a few of the stronger, sturdier men who walked by.

Veronica couldn’t care less.

The line into the city stretched far, but the wait was bearable.

In front of them were several other wagons, most loaded with crates and escorted by guards or mercenaries. Some carriages bore the marks of house crests, most likely vassal nobles who had sworn fealty to Duke Pelotema instead of receiving their titles directly from the king. Citizens and adventurers were in line as well, waiting to get back in. One man was even holding a wolf’s corpse over his neck while talking with his party.

“Hey,” Gwen said, nudging Veronica with her elbow. “Those two in front are dark elves, right? I’ve never seen others before. Do you know them?”

Veronica peered over at her with a raised brow. “You know, not all dark elves know each other.”

Gwen shrugged. “Yeah, I know, but dark elves are pretty rare. There are elves in Ronswick, but I’ve never seen a dark elf before. Besides you. But you said you’re only part dark elf, right?”

Veronica looked ahead again. “Dark elves usually live and stay beneath the Elven Forest’s undergrowth. For someone like me, it’s a rare exception that I came to Annesheim.”

“What about them, then?”

She shrugged. “Can’t say. Could be for business. Could be for leisure.”

Eventually, it was their turn at the front of the queue.

A guard in pale-gray armor stepped up beside the wagon, a sword strapped to his hip as he glanced over each of them.

“Purpose of entry?” he asked.

“Travel and lodging,” Veronica replied. “We want to settle in Maftem for a while and explore the auction.”

The guard shifted, one eyebrow raising ever so slightly. “Names and affiliation?”

“Veronica Everwells.”

“Gwen Finch.”

“Nasha Lowell.”

Veronica raised her hand, showing the guard Viscount Leopold’s signet.

The guard hummed upon seeing it. After asking a few more routine questions and checking their identification cards, he waved over another person. Not a guard, but perhaps a clerk.

“Give these three a map of the city,” the guard said. Then he turned back toward them. “Do you need a guide to the noble district?”

Veronica shook her head. “No need. We’ll be fine by ourselves.”

“Very well,” the guard said. “Have a nice stay in Maftem.”

The clerk came over and handed them three maps of the city.

They passed through the gates, and the city opened around them. As they moved beneath the metal mana bands of the city walls, the first thing that hit them was the noise. Merchants calling from stalls. Hooves and carts clattering over stone. Distant laughter and conversation from large groups passing through the streets ahead.

Gwen’s eyes widened. “Woah, this place is so much bigger than Ronswick.”

Even Nasha leaned forward despite herself.

Broad roads stretched ahead, lined with stone buildings, colorful awnings, and hanging signs and banners representing different houses, guilds, and businesses. As they rolled through the city in their wagon, they spotted auction notices posted on message boards and walls. Some were simple announcements, while others were advertisements for various merchant houses.

“Let’s find a place to stay first,” Veronica said.

Following their map, they ventured through the streets and visited several different inns.

Unfortunately, nearly all of them were already at full capacity. Because of the auction, finding an open inn was difficult. Their only choice for residence forced them toward the noble district, using the viscount’s signet for access. The only problem was the exorbitant cost.

“Of course, we’d be delighted to have you,” said Niko, the clerk at the Maristar Gold Inn. “Did you want separate rooms?”

The three of them looked at each other.

“I was planning on getting us separate rooms, but considering our expenses…” Veronica said.

“Do you have any rooms available with three beds?” Nasha asked.

“Apologies,” Niko replied. “All of the rooms only have one bed.”

“I can sleep on the floor. It’s fine,” Gwen said, looking between them.

Veronica frowned. “I can’t let you do that. It wouldn’t feel right.” She looked back at Niko. “Get us three rooms.”

Nasha stepped forward before Niko could reply. “Can we get two rooms instead? One for Gwen, and Veronica and I will share one. I assume the beds are large enough?”

Niko nodded. “Yes. We have rooms for singles and rooms for couples, so the larger beds can accommodate two people.”

Nasha turned back to Veronica. “That works, right?”

Veronica looked at Gwen, who simply held up a thumbs-up.

“We’ll do what Nasha suggested,” Veronica said. “We’ll take two rooms.”

Niko beamed. “Perfect. I’ll register your names and have a porter show you to your rooms.”

“Guys! These rooms are amazing! The view is beautiful! I didn’t even know they had such a pretty garden outside!” Gwen said, running over to their room.

“It is certainly above anything I’ve stayed in at Ronswick, and I’ve stayed in many of the wealthier places,” Nasha said.

She was currently sitting in a chair, stretching her legs and massaging her hamstrings.

“It’s alright,” Veronica chimed in, busy putting away extra clothes. “It’s clean, I suppose. There is a lack of artwork on the walls. I’m surprised that for such an expensive inn, they don’t even have scent runes around the room or an automatic wardrobe fitter.”

Both Nasha and Gwen stared at her.

She turned. “What?”

“You really did live like a queen, didn’t you?” Gwen asked.

Veronica shrugged. “Even a noble would have that much.”

“This is just an inn, you know. One of the more affordable ones, too,” Gwen continued.

“I know, I know. I’m not complaining. It’s nice. I just expected a bit more, considering that for two weeks of stay, we had to pay around six thousand vix. That’s a little over four hundred vix a day,” Veronica said.

She set the last piece of clothing on a hanger before turning around. “Speaking of which, we’ll be staying here for a while. If the auction has anything good, we’re going to need to make more money.”

“How much do we have right now?” Nasha asked.

“After spending money on food for travel and two weeks here, we have a little under sixteen thousand vix left,” Veronica replied. “But we also need to receive some healing. I’ll need to buy recovery potions for my mana cores, while Nasha needs a full body check-up for any internal injuries. So expect us to have a little under six or eight thousand vix left.”

Gwen’s shoulders dropped. “Sorry I couldn’t be more useful. I just don’t have good enough healing spells yet.”

“Don’t worry about it,” Veronica said. “You’re only a second-tier mage. I don’t think anyone expects you to have recovery spells just yet. For now, focus on studying and trying to improve your spells.”

Gwen smiled, just slightly. “Thanks, Veronica.”

“So,” Nasha cut in. “What’s our next move? Why are we in Maftem in the first place?”

Veronica walked over and sat on the edge of the bed. It was soft.

“Maftem is a large city with many opportunities. We can find nearly everything here. That includes resources. There should also be a few people here that Sage deems important. I’ll have to try making contact with them and, hopefully, recruit them.”

“As new party members?” Gwen asked.

“No. More like people with influence. That includes Duke Pelotema. I’ll have to try and get in his good graces. He’ll be an important person during the invasion, one of the people who leads an army. The earlier I can warn him about the demons, the better.”

Veronica lifted a finger. “First, we should try to get familiar with the city. I’ve been here a few times before, but I assume you two haven’t. It would be useful if you two could explore and maybe find anything important.”

“Like what?” Gwen asked.

“For instance, the most reputable mage tower of the four here in Maftem. Or perhaps the most trustworthy or cheapest merchant house. The best craftsmen or smithies. People or places of importance, like a specific spot nobles prefer to idle around.”

“Oh, I can definitely do that,” Gwen said. “It’s been a long time since I’ve gotten to go around a city by myself.”

Right… Veronica thought.

She had been captive under Lorence for several years. Besides her job as a waitress, she hadn’t been allowed to go anywhere else by herself without his permission.

“If we’re in need of funds, I’ll go around and find a theater or entertainment plaza,” Nasha said. She lifted one leg over the other, resting steepled fingers on her knee. “I’m actually quite well known, even outside of Ronswick. I should be able to snag an opening, or even a position.”

Veronica nodded. “That’s perfect. As for me, I’ll probably head over to the Adventurer’s Guild to check the level of commissions they have here. Not just for the vix, but because we need to work on our teamwork a bit.”

“Our teamwork?” Gwen repeated. “I think we work together quite well.”

Veronica shook her head. “The teamwork itself isn’t the problem. It’s specifically how well we work together under pressure. That fight with Hevestra made me realize my limits. I’m not used to fighting at such a low tier yet. And because of that, I nearly got both of you killed by overestimating myself against a demonic centurion.”

She sighed. “I’ll find us some lower-rank commissions, and we’ll use those to train. Not just for me to get used to fighting at a lower tier.” Veronica turned to look at Nasha. “But also for Nasha to get used to fighting with spiritual energy. Using a power that strong isn’t easy. We’ll need you to adjust to it.”

Nasha nodded.

“As for you,” Veronica said, turning to Gwen, “we need to get you more spell books so you can study different spells. But I’ll also teach you ways to heal us more effectively, and how to fight offensively when needed.”

Gwen nodded. “Right.”

“That’s the plan for today. Any questions?” Veronica asked.

“None,” Gwen replied.

“Sounds good to me,” Nasha said.

Veronica nodded, and the three of them began to move. She handed each of them a split of their remaining vix.

Gwen went back to her room.

Just as Veronica was about to exit and head toward the Adventurer’s Guild, she felt a hand settle on her shoulder. She turned.

“Nasha?”

Nasha wasn’t frowning. Not exactly. But she certainly had a look of disapproval.

“Hey. I know we’ve had a pretty easy time getting here from the Valley of Silence, but don’t push yourself,” she said. “We just got here, and all you’re doing is planning for work and more work.”

Veronica lowered her eyes. “It’s fine. We had plenty of time to rest during the trip. I’m just setting things up so we can be efficient with our time.”

“Managing your stress is also important,” Nasha said. “Tomorrow, take a break. I’m sure Gwen would love a day to herself, too. She’s been working nonstop the entire trip here trying to help us recover. You and I are used to being in danger and fighting for our lives, but Gwen’s still just a normal girl.”

Veronica opened her mouth, but couldn’t find the words.

Nasha was right. They had been traveling for four months since leaving Ronswick, and during that time, they had come extremely close to death. But it was hard. Even now, Veronica wanted to keep planning. There were several figures she needed to meet in Maftem. Not to mention, she had already been set back by the damage to her cores.

“Don’t overwork yourself,” Nasha said, cutting through her thoughts. “You followed me that night because you wanted to feel normal for once, right? Just listen to me. One day won’t kill you.”

Veronica stared at her for a moment longer before releasing a deep exhale.

“Alright, alright. We’ll take a break tomorrow.”

Nasha smiled and lifted her hand. She walked beside Veronica, taking the first step out the door. Then she paused and glanced back.

“It’s a date, then.”
