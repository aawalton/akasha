Fighting mana without mana was a strange concept. Will was tied to mana, connected to it in unknowable but unmistakable ways. That had been the premise in Luke's mind since the first time he got an inkling that will could aid you in and out of combat. An incorrect assumption, as it turned out. The two were more like friends who cheered each other on in a race, but wouldn't reach out and pull the other forward to help, not unless forced to. Now, Luke was attempting to do just that, to force the two to interact. The Willpower attribute aided any Integrated when they attempted to assert their will upon anything touched by the system, but a higher attribute alone did not mean a stronger will. It helped, yes, allowing you to get a feel for and flex your own will. In Luke's case, the other way around was closer to the truth. With Boon of Potential, his will would often drag the Willpower attribute along, raising it up to a matching level, which then allowed for even greater control.

That was a long way of saying that Luke's will worked just fine without mana, even if asserting it was more difficult with his head feeling like a herd of elephants was marching inside it. Weakened by a lack of mana and the pain of void mana ravaging him, it took some time for Luke to get a grasp of the situation. Unsure how many times he'd passed out just by wielding his will as a weapon against the foe, he at least made some progress.

Boon of Potential grants you an attribute point. (+1 Willpower).

On his third attempt, he touched the mana. On his fifth, it even moved. Aware that things happened around his physical body, but unable to process what his normal senses were telling him, Luke pushed on. Weaver's Perception wanted to drown him in even more information. The skill shouted about every Singer's illness, what their meta-heart and mana channels looked like, how strong they were, along with so much more, but Luke didn't listen. Couldn't listen. The sliver of consciousness he kept in a chokehold needed to stay focused on the task at hand. Unless he won the internal battle, nothing in the external world would matter.

Considering mana an enemy was another strange concept, even if it was void mana. Once it transferred over from the void being to Luke, it became his own mana, as much a part of him as his limbs or even his meta-heart. Even so, it represented the void, and that all-encompassing nothingness was the antagonist, so the piece of it inside him was an opponent in need of eradication.

Boon of Potential grants you an attribute point. (+1 Willpower).

Little by little, Luke did what he at first had considered impossible: he pushed the void mana back. It had spread while he was unconscious, taking over parts of the otherwise dry mana channels. Wherever that mana touched, the void followed. Or maybe calling it the void was a misnomer. Yes, it was of the void, but it wasn't the void itself. The void being was the same, of the void while not part of it, because nothing can be part of that nothingness. That was anathema to life itself, and that being, like Smudge, was very much alive. As was Luke, and he wasn't about to stop now that he was getting a handle on the void mana. Pushing with will alone, he forced it back, refusing that its hold on the metaphysical side of him was reality. It wasn't spreading. In fact, it was diminishing, losing its foothold and retreating back to the first affected spot inside his meta-heart.

Boon of Potential grants you an attribute point. (+1 Willpower).

No matter how much he tried, there was just no way to destroy the void mana. Not with the tools at hand. Perhaps it was just a matter of not being able to imagine void mana being torn apart and losing in that way, and thus his will would not allow that to happen, or maybe there was some limitation of will Luke didn't know about yet. He was still new to this. Either way, with no ability to make the void mana disappear, he did the next best thing and pushed it into as small an area as possible, forcing it to compress in a corner of his meta-heart. The invasive void mana writhed as if trying to escape the clutches of Luke's mind, almost like a living thing, but he refused it. Void mana was not part of this system, or any system, and it did not hold power over him. In Luke's meta-heart, he was the sole arbiter of what was and what was not.

Boon of Potential grants you an attribute point. (+1 Willpower).

Pushing and pressing with his will, Luke formed all that void mana into a sphere of nothingness, then kept going, compressing it more and more until it was almost too small to see. It was still there. Perhaps it always would be, but at least for the moment, he had cowed the intruder. The pain stopped. Blessed release.

Gasping, Luke came to. The bright room spun around him as he took in what appeared to be a cell. It had the same sort of glowing walls. The small openings for, he guessed, gas to be released were much easier to spot within the smaller confines of the room. A narrow gap in the wall, right in front of him, indicated the outline of a door. Other than that, the place was empty. Except for the restraints holding him in place, of course. Luke's arms were covered up to the elbows in metal contraptions, and his feet looked like they'd been placed into now-dry cement blocks.

Being stuck in that spot did little to ease the nausea and vertigo of being without mana. It was like having a migraine while also being massively hungover, hungry, and having gone without sleep for a couple of days. Not a good time. Luke had been close a few times, but never reached zero. He'd been afraid that the spot he was in now would lead to death, but that was now proven to be false. It was no wonder, though, that most people, himself included, passed out once mana ran low enough.

Head throbbing and his mind frayed from having pushed so hard with his will, Luke probed the room with Weaver's Perception, finding nothing of interest. Expanding the passive skill made the spinning worse, but he needed to confirm something. Yep, there he was. Ray was in the next cell over, unconscious but alive. For a moment, when Luke woke up alone, he'd been afraid they'd executed his friend, but this meant that the whole thing had been a fake-out. A trap. An obvious one, but Luke had figured he'd fight his way through it. There was that arrogance he was supposed to be careful of. That hadn't gone over all that well, and now he was stuck in another system, having his mana zapped away by some machine, all of it gone except the small sphere of void mana still holding strong. For some reason, that bit of mana didn't count in his interface. Far from admitting defeat, Luke's first idea was to harness that void mana and use it, since the machines seemed to be set to drain human mana. Or was it just regular mana humans used? Thinking was hard. His thoughts were sluggish, and parsing them into ideas was like climbing a mountain, but even in that state, he knew that touching that mana would be the end of him. If it got free, it would take over again, and he knew for a certainty that if that happened, he wouldn't be able to put it back into its box.

An orange glow flashed on the door, and it slid open, admitting the male Singer Lifeweaver that he and Luom found in the pink dungeon. He pulled on someone's arm, dragging Dot in with him.

Luke blinked. "Dot?"

"Luke! Oh, Luke!" she cried, pulling on her arm in an attempt to break free of the Singer's grip. It did her little good, so she kept talking instead. "They're being horrible to me! It was fine until right before they caught you, but now they're treating me like a prisoner!"

Seeing her squirming there confused him, if nothing else, and he looked from Dot to the Singer. "What's going on?"

"You murdered Luomina of the Fourth Choir," the Singer said, his mouth a thin line of suppressed anger. "And that was after destroying one of our glorious paths to power."

"Yep," Luke said, feeling too tired and slow-witted to argue.

"You killed Luom?" Dot asked, showing that expression of hers again.

Luke couldn't help but chuckle as he shook his head. Dot would never change. This time, it was a relief. The ever present influence of The Fallen Shepherd had made Luke suspect he would aim to take over Dot, but even in his current state, he could tell there was no hint of corruption in her. Instead, he focused on the Singer. What was his name again?

"Cage," Luke muttered, focusing on the male Lifeweaver after swirling his head to indicate the cell. "How ironic."

"You still live because this one," Cage said, raising Dot's hand, "hasn't been able to show us what you did in that dungeon. You created a pathway out of nothing. This you will teach us. Perhaps then, the Assembly will show you mercy and allow a quick death, murderer." Luke was about to answer, but Cage wasn't quite done. "Destroyer! You will also teach the other new Singer Lifeweavers and me the trick of how you traveled to our world. You did it after taking Luomina's life as well, disappearing so our scouts lost track of you."

"Well-"

"Murderer," Cage hissed.

Luke sighed. "Are you done? Can I talk?"

"Talk!" Cage ordered.

"You were spying on me?"

"Of course, we were following you and gathering information! You are a subject, whether you want to admit it or not. All you know and all you are belong to us. Now share your knowledge, or we will torture this one," he shook Dot's arm, squeezing so hard her flesh paled, "and your other murderous friend!"

Dot gave him a pleading look that was still somehow tinted with her disgust at him having killed Luom. Gathering himself, Luke gave his best grin. "I'll think about it."
