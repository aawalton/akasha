Another basket sat atop the dais. A silver watering pail shone beside it.

I eyed them as I wrung my hands and curled my toes into soft loam. My gaze flicked to the windows nearby. Polished crystal-clear, practically sparkling beneath the moonlight.

No movement from within. The manor was silent.

I crept up slowly, pausing for a breath between each step. Waiting for something, anything to happen. Another scream, another cruel laugh, the sound of footsteps just behind me…

Nothing.

The ivory was cold beneath my feet. I reached the top, spent a moment glancing around, then stooped to examine tonight’s gift.

Lush crimson silk lined the inside. A sparkling crystal bottle of ruby-red liquid and several long wedges of pale cheese lay nestled in the fabric. Closer examination filled my nose with the rich smells of kindled campfires and baking bread.

“What are you playing at?” I frowned at the little pile, then reached for another cream-colored piece of vellum.

This message was wordier, though more confusing. Curling black script read, ‘Don’t wander.’

And, beneath that, ‘The goblin is harmless.’

I straightened, brows knitting together. “What’s a goblin?”

“Greetings, human!”

I yelped, whirling to find a green-skinned little Fae standing just behind me. She resembled an extremely short, thin human with a tangled mop of wispy black hair. Her cheeks and forehead bore smatterings of darker-hued scaly patches, almost like the skin of a lizard.

She beamed up at me from about waist-height, revealing two rows of very spiky white teeth. Her expression quickly twisted into one of dismay.

“Oh, dawn!” Her voice was a croaky wheeze, as though every word scraped its way up a raw throat. The creature clapped dainty, claw-tipped fingers against her cheeks. One side of her jaw bore several deep, jagged white scars that trailed down her neck and disappeared beneath the collar of what appeared to be a shift made entirely out of leaves. “Failed already! That was quick.”

I stumbled back up the dais. “W-what-? Who-?”

“I wasn’t supposed to frighten you,” she moaned, falling into a squat and giving her head a mournful shake. “I knew I should’ve stayed tucked! ‘Always go with your gut, Slip.’ That’s what my nan used to say.” She snapped her teeth, making me jump. “I was just excited. I’ve never met a mortal.”

I tentatively examined the unusual creature, thinking over her first comment. “You… weren’t supposed to… frighten me?”

She blew a raspberry. “Well, he said to try not to. But he laughed when he said it, so I suppose the jest’s on me. Humph!”

“He?” I swallowed, surveying the garden behind her. But, no, there was still no sign of my captor.

“Lord Vel, of course.” She hopped back to her feet, which were bare and scaled, much like the patches of skin along her cheekbones. “He said I’d like you.” She crept up the stairs, claws clacking in a way that made my gut clench, and bared her sharp teeth in a little grin. “But even if I don’t, I still wanted to say hi. A goblin meeting a human? It’s unheard of!”

I edged back at her approach, stopping only when my back hit the cold door. “Goblin?”

“Dreamweaver Clan, and proud of it! I’m Sslipssinia, by the way, but most kin call me Slip.”

I blinked. “Oh. It’s n-nice to… are you going to eat me?”

She giggled and dropped into another crouch, arms draped over her knees and yellow eyes shining. “That would probably count as frightening you, wouldn’t it? Besides, I’m supposed to keep you safe from the mordelain. Can’t do that if you’re all chewed up!”

“What’s a mordelain?”

“A big monster.” She snorted at my expression, flicking a finger through the air dismissively. “Oh, don’t worry. They’re slow and dumb. If it comes by I’ll shadowstep you, quick as can be.”

“You’ll what?”

She gave a mischievous little wink and hopped to one side. The night air beside her shivered, almost like it had blinked, and she reappeared at the bottom of the steps. “See? Nothing to it.” She clambered back up, round face scrunching. “I’m supposed to do a blood summoning after, though, if you stop breathing or your tongue falls out or… the master gave me a list.”

The blood drained from my face. “What?”

The goblin winced. “It’ll probably be fine! I can step with little things easy. I just haven’t tried with something that’s alive before. I don’t know what’ll happen to your insides.”

My knees trembled. I slid against the door until my bottom hit hard ivory. “My insides?”

She prowled closer, now at eye-level and frantically waving both hands. “Oh, don’t faint! Lord Vel said the leshars have stepped humans and that mostly went fine, and of course I can be much more considerate than one of them. Opposable thumbs, and all.” Her narrow chin jutted in apparent pride.

Something between a moan and a whimper rose in the back of my throat.

“Oh, dawn, have I frightened you again? Already? No wonder he was laughing.”

“I just…” I closed my eyes, nostrils flaring with a deep breath, and said slowly, “Right. You’re… not going to hurt me.”

“Not on purpose,” she croaked.

Another breath. “Okay. Okay. And there’s a monster in the garden, but you’re going to keep me… um… safe?”

“Safe as dreams! And it’s not in the garden, it’s just in the area. It probably won’t wander this way.”

“Right. Sure.” I slid my eyes back open, taking in the diminutive creature and her expressive, earnest face. “Why can’t I just go in the house? Surely it’s safe in there.”

“Lord Vel said you make messes and break stuff.” One shoulder lifted in a shrug. “Guess you’re an outdoor pet.”

Heat bolted up my throat. “I’m not a pet!”

She snickered. “Oh? That was the nice word the clan started calling you. Heh. But I can use the, uh, ruder ones if you-”

“Please don’t,” I growled, nails digging into my palms. “I… look, I don’t know why your master,” I spat the word, and her face scrunched with obvious displeasure, “wants me here, but I’m a person, same as you!”

The goblin’s forehead wrinkled. “Uh, no. Not even close. You’re all pink and soft and… mortal.” She shuddered quite dramatically, making the leaf-dress rustle. “What’s that like, anyways? Can you feel yourself aging? One step closer to death with every moment?” Her eyes widened. “Wait, can you? Dawn, no wonder you’re so on edge!”

“I can’t feel myself getting older, and I’m not pink! I’m ‘on edge’ because I’m trapped here! And where is your… er, master, anyways?”

“Right now?” She thought for a moment. “Uh… probably real deep in the Felthamere. Probably covered in guts, too. He wants us to get ready for another door.”

“A… door? I assume you don’t mean,” I gestured to the pristine ivory behind me, “like that?”

Her sharp teeth flashed. “Hah! You’re funny. No, a door. A portal. Not like the little slippery ones he pushes the leshars through, but a real one. You know; blood everywhere, goblins chanting all night, huge flash of light? Then the air rips open. A door.”

Portal. He said that word once before, when he spoke of how he came to Fellbrook. “Do you mean to the mortal realm?” Ice flooded my veins and I sat up straight. “Why is he going there?”

Slip shrugged. “I dunno. Hopefully it won’t be like the last big one, though. He tried to rope me in and, uh, that just sounded awful.”

I shook my head, growing more baffled with each word. “What?”

She folded both legs, settling onto the dais with a skyward roll of her large, shining eyes. “‘Sslipssinia,’ he said, “We’re ripping a hole in reality so I can go explore a place covered in those silver nightmare trees that will definitely eat you, and I’d really like your company. The leshars are fine, but when it comes to hunting down magical stuff you’re the best, really, the absolute best. I don’t know what I’d do without you.’”

She looked at me expectantly. Eventually my lack of a response earned a sigh and another roll of her enormous eyes. “Okay, maybe he didn’t say it quite like that. But he was thinking it! I could tell.”

My brows knit together, “You keep saying ‘leshar.’ Are you talking about the shadow monsters? The ones that look like dogs?”

“Dogs? Big hairy things, right? Say ‘woof?’ Yeah, leshar. Loyal to a fault, so I get why he likes them, but they’re not too bright. They think ‘fetch’ means ‘go rip that thing’s leg off.’ They’ve got nothing on us.”

“Us?”

“The Dreamweavers, of course! What, you thought this little paradise built itself?” She laughed, rocking back and beaming up at the gilded spires above. “Hah! I’d like to see a leshar think up a chandelier made out of diamonds or a never-ending hot spring!” She winked, raspy voice dropping to a whisper, and added, “The trick was to pad coals beneath the tiles. Imaginary fire can’t last forever, but imaginary heat? Easy!”

I puzzled through her words, turning to press one palm against the door. “You… are you saying that you and your kin built this place? All of it? How?”

She tapped her claws against the stone, grinning mischievously. “Dreams. Magic. Lots of stuff that took a long time, and would take a long time to explain.”

I stared at the little creature in awe. “You dreamt of this place? And that made it real? How? Why? Does your clan live in there, too?”

“Nah.” She nodded towards the silver-tipped willows. “We stay out of sight. Some of the lords and ladies that come by are vicious. They’d skin us all if they got the chance, just because they were too thick to ever ask nicely for anything. Short-sighted, if you ask me. But lord Vel likes his house, and sometimes we come in to add something for him. Shoulda seen the little chest of jewels he had us kiln the other night! It was a rush job, all of us going glassy-eyed and dizzy. Some of the shapers passed out right there in the house, and of course he didn’t like that, but serves him right for pushing.”

I shook my head in disbelief as I envisioned the mound of glittering, flawless gems and jewelry I’d been offered just a few nights ago. Should I even be that surprised, though? I’d already guessed some parts of the house were magical. I just… well, I never imagined the entire house was magical.

I drew a deep breath. “Is it not… not real, then? The jewels, the house, the food inside… will it disappear after a while? Fade?”

“Nah, not the way we did it.” Her golden eyes shone. “We can’t make complicated things- nothing alive, nothing with its own power- but rocks and carpets and wine? Heh. The whole clan, dreaming hard enough? It’s there for good.”

“But why do you make things for him? Are you slaves? Trapped here, like me?” Tentative hope at having a potential ally, perhaps an entire group of them, swelled in my chest. “Do you want to be free?”

“Heh, you are funny! Dreamweaver slaves? Who’d want a thing like that?” She spent a moment cackling, and my shoulders sagged. “That’d be rich, wouldn’t it? ‘Sslipssinia, I’m hungry. Make me a block of cheese or I’ll drown your family.’ Pretty sure some noble-types tried that kind of thing centuries ago, when the realm was civilized the first time around. Next thing they knew, the food was eating them. Hah!”

I shuddered at the thought, and at the growing implications of what incredible, mind-boggling power this little Fae held. “But then… why do you build things for him?”

“For lord Vel? He asked. Nicely.”

“I don’t believe that. Not for a moment.”

Slip snickered. “Heh. Okay, he asked after killing a bunch of things that were killing us. Then he promised he’d keep murdering anything that tried to hurt the clan if we helped him out. After a few hundred years of living in literal fear, we all thought that sounded like a pretty good deal.”

I gazed skyward, drinking in the tapestry of silver and black. “But why would you need protection like that? You just made it sound as though your clan is incredibly powerful.”

“Sure, but our magic takes time, pet. You get the whole clan together, keep us safe and give us a decade to dream? Well, that’s how you get a palace.” She swept both arms up, beaming at the structure above us. Then her face fell, dark bangs tipping forwards and shadowing her features. “But if a High Fae decides he’s gonna flay a youngling every time one of us steps out of line, well… ”

Ice crept up my spine. “That can’t be… saints, how awful. I’m so sorry.”

She flashed me a mirthless look. The scars along her jaw and throat shone white in the moonlight. “Darker times, yeah? But things are safer, now. Well. They’re safer here. The other High Fae haven’t figured out how to get a hold of us, and any monsters keen to a Dreamweaver scent meet lord Vel. Then there’s screaming and blood and… heh. We’re all too scared to watch, but it’s sure fun to listen to! The aftermath’s usually a good snack, too.”

I shuddered at the thought, and at the image that accompanied it.

Her gaze trailed to the basket of food, eyes flickering with light, and she asked in a very not-casual croak, “Speaking of snacks… you, uh, gonna eat that?”

“Help yourself, Slip.”

The goblin scuttled forwards, red tongue darting out to sweep across her lips, and seized the basket with both dainty hands. She gave a little giggle, lifted her prize, and took an enormous bite out of the handle.

I stared as she happily crunched strips of woven wood. She eats baskets. Sure. Sure. Why not? Why shouldn’t the all-powerful magical Fae woman eat a basket? Why should I be surprised by anything, anymore?

Slip munched through several more bites of wood, then scooped out the little crystal bottle. She held it out to me, raising her eyebrows.

I shook my head. “I don’t know what it does.”

“What, wine? Makes your head get fuzzy, turns the world all bright. Humans get drunk, right?”

“I… yes, of course, I just meant… I’m not convinced your master isn’t trying to poison me, Slip.”

“Heh, that’d be rich!” Her voice lowered into what I was beginning to recognize as a very poor impression of Velidan and she crooned, “‘Sslipssinia, keep this helpless mortal safe from a roving monster. Dazzle her with your magical shadowstepping prowess and don’t let the mordelain eat her. Oh, and make sure she drinks the poison.’” Slip spent a moment cackling. Once her little body stopped shaking she offered me the drink once more, took in my frown, and shrugged. “Suit yourself, pet.”

“I’m not a pet,” I grumbled.

She just snickered, uncorking the bottle with an airy pop. The faint scents of jasmine and lavender filled the already-fragrant air. She shivered. “Ooh, we made this one a long time ago! Back when the shapers thought lord Vel ate flowers.”

“Back… what?”

She shot me a toothy, abashed grimace. “Well, he was always growing them, and of course you’d think a Fae that practical has an actual reason. I assumed, and no one bothered to actually ask him, and… look, it’s not important. The food was a disaster, but the drinks turned out great!”

A grin tugged at the corners of my mouth. Then I spent a long moment marveling at… well, at how bizarre this entire situation was.

I’m sitting in another realm, on the steps of a home built by goblins with magical dreams, having a conversation with a Fae who eats baskets. And I almost like her.

A low, familiar coo interrupted my musings. I looked to the silver-tipped treeline, catching my breath at the sight of a very familiar little figure.

Sable feathers unruffled by the warm breeze. Sharp talons that gleamed beneath the moonlight. Round eyes shining like molten gold.

Horace.
