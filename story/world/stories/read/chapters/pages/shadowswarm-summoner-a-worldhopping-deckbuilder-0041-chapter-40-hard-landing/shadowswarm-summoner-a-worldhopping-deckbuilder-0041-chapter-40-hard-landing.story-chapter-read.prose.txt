Chapter 40: Hard Landing

It shouldn’t have been possible. The ritual cards required to bind intelligent beings were out of their hands, and a simple rare enchantment shouldn’t have been able to do that, right? And to make it become an imp version? It didn’t make any sense to him…but there were a lot of things people didn’t understand yet about card magic. The important thing was he had accidentally enslaved someone trying to kill them, and as a Level 5 Shadow card, it was just outside his ability to use.

If he would even be willing to accept it.

The ship lurched violently, reminding Ambrose that they had bigger problems to worry about. He steadied himself and tucked the new card in his pocket. Scattered around the deck were their foe’s decks. Or at least, parts of them. When someone died, they dropped most of their cards, but some disappeared into the aether. No one knew why, but lower rarity cards were more likely to vanish than rare ones.

Which was certainly a relief to a ragtag group badly in need of upgrades.

Raeva wasted no time in picking the cards up off the ground, and Luthor did so as well, wiping the blood off of them first. Ambrose went to help, but after the first step, a wave of weariness and pain washed over him and he dropped to the deck.

Stars swam in his vision, and he distantly heard Raeva call his name, but it didn’t seem too terribly important. He was both heavy and light, like he was rising. Strength drained out of him, and darkness encircled the edges of his vision before a golden glow brought him crashing back down into his body.

When he opened his eyes, Luthor had him in his arms, a concerned look on his face. “Ambrose? Talk to me.”

“My…hero…” he groaned with a bit of a slur.

Raeva laughed while Luthor said, “That was a bad hit to your head. I used the one healing charge that just renewed, but you might be a bit off for a bit.”

Off? He blinked, and sat up slowly. Craning his head, he saw bloody spots on the wood, going back a few feet. A chill went through him as he touched the back of his head. It came away warm, wet, and sticky.

“Yeah,” said Raeva, “that big guy got you good. And you still got up. And got a new card, apparently.” Her cheer disappeared, and even in his ‘off’ state he understood the conflict behind her eyes.

The ship lurched again, and Ambrose nearly fell back over. Dizziness struck him once more, but Luthor steadied him with a hand on his shoulder.

“They’re making a run for it,” Jessica shouted as they turned. “Hold onto your asses!”

The enemy vessel had pulled away and put distance between them, and the Wanderer jolted forward. The motion was too much, and Ambrose turned to the deck and lost last night’s dinner and wine, and then his consciousness after.

*****

“Mistress, must we fire upon our own people?” SNAC asked, and Jessica detected stress behind the chipper tone.

“It’s for the safety of the mission,” she said, hating the lie they’d come up with. It wasn’t like they could afford the golem suddenly deciding he wouldn’t help them. “And when we tried to tell them who we were, they attacked first. Because of Charles Vanderborn.”

Her grandfather had some enemies, it seemed, and it was unfortunate they attacked. Through her sleep deprived haze, her pain and rage simmered bright. Jessica pushed past it and focused on keeping up with the scout, picking up speed by the second.

The problem was, the ship didn’t turn and go out into the expanse. Instead, they turned towards the world closest to them.

“Mistress? I think they are going for the colony in Stonskarr, to alert the rest of the Empire.” SNAC twitched, almost nervously.

He was right. With the others taking care of Ambrose and cleaning up the cards, it was on her to make this call. It was on her to decide for everyone. And take the blame if it went wrong.

Jessica wanted to cry, but the answer came out. “SNAC, go below and man the cannons. When I give the order, I need you to fire.”

The golem actually jerked in place this time. “I cannot do that, Mistress. I am not capable of harming a member of the Empire, not even to save a life.”

Of course not. The enemy ship got a little further away. Every second of hesitation cost them. Jessica bit back an angry retort and said, “Fine, then take the helm and aim while I -- “

“I cannot do that either.”

“Raeva!” Jessica screamed. “Get below deck and man the cannon. Get ready to fire on my command!”

She sprinted to the stairs, disappearing below.

Three seconds later, once she was sure it wouldn’t throw her friend to the floor, Jessica spun the helm and the Wanderer tilted dangerously as they turned their cannon in the right direction. One, two, three seconds passed before Jessica screamed “FIRE!”

There were a couple of other weapons on the ship, though not many. Grandfather’s ability to fly and fight from the air made most weapons redundant, and he obviously hadn’t anticipated anyone stealing it. The cannons were the only ones she knew how to use, and knew her friends would be able to operate them too.

A second later, the explosion rocked the ship, and a ball of enchanted iron flew out…and detonated just off the scout’s starboard bow. The ship’s wing flew off sideways, and they listed to the side. Still, it continued towards the world, getting closer and closer. Jessica panicked and spun the wheel, following after them.

The scout froze where it was, and then disappeared entirely. There were only seconds remaining until they followed. Did she turn back, or follow and make sure they couldn’t report in?

Luthor would know. He’d make a decision. What decision would he make? Shit shit shit.

“Mistress?” SNAC said. “We don’t have much time, and Stonskarr is bad for flight and -- “

The light from Stonskarr flared bright with all the fury of an unleashed sunrise, and then it all went black. Not the darkness of the Aetheric expanse, but something closer, more intimate and claustrophobic.

“What the hell?” Raeleq said, loud enough to pierce the rush of sudden wind blowing past them, absent from that space between worlds and now back in full force.

Jessica opened her mouth, but the ship dropped suddenly, like a giant hand yanked them out of the sky. They didn’t plummet straight down, but it was like the ship couldn’t stay up. Magelights illuminated the ship, but at their level, shapes came from the dark. At the last second, she saw a stalactite approaching.

“Hang on!” she shouted, and spun the wheel, praying her friends wouldn’t get tossed over the edge. The Wanderer pivoted, and barely missed clipping the spire of stone, but didn’t miss the next one. The left wing broke through another stalactite, but the one after that snapped the wing off.

Her stomach dropped, and she pulled a lever for the backup lift. Their descent slowed, but they continued forward at an alarming rate, coming closer and closer to the ground. After a few seconds, the darkness gave way for dozens, if not hundreds, of small, gentle glows in the form of bioluminescent fungus growing up the side of stalagmites and hills.

“Jessica, what’re you doing?” Raeva’s voice came through distantly, but it was unimportant.

She wanted to answer, but couldn’t. The darkness of Stonskarr was borderline oppressive, even with the chaotic, rainbow scattering of mushrooms the size of people, getting increasingly more common the further they went. The ground was finally visible, though it sloped downwards dangerously.

More importantly, they were finally close enough to see the scout flying ahead of them. It too had problems staying in the air. They fell out of the air and into a patch of purple with a muffled crunch.

The rush of wind intensified, the ship rattled violently, and Jessica made a dozen rapid decisions in the span of three seconds to get them down as painlessly as possible. “Hold oooooon!”

The crash wasn’t as bad as she expected, honestly. She braced herself as best she could, and somehow didn’t fly out of the seat. SNAC, however, went over the railing and crashed into Raeleq, who dropped and slid toward the bow of the ship. Luthor held on tightly to Ambrose, who looked like he wanted to puke again as they hit and crashed along the deck. Only Raeva kept her footing, and it was a near thing.

Rocks and dirt scratched their side as they slid to a long, agonizing stop, accompanied by a terrible tearing sound. The aft of the ship hung up, with the bow pointed down at the very rock-bottom of the chasm. Jessica slipped out of the sweat on shaky legs, fighting against the slope on her way down to the others.

They were as rocked and battered as she was, with Ambrose in particularly bad condition. He was paler than usual, and his eyes struggled to focus. Luthor grimaced, and looked up at her. “We’re alright, mostly. Did you see what happened to the other ship?”

“They’re behind us a ways, up that hill,” said Raeva, checking on her brother. She helped him to his feet, while SNAC got up slowly, rotating impossibly on his metallic joints. “What the hell kind of flying was that, Jessica?”

It was like being punched. “I’m doing my best, okay?” she shot back. “No one else knows what they’re doing, I’m new, and this place is weird!” The tears welled up, but didn’t flow.

Raeleq stepped between his sister and Jessica, and offered her a reassuring smile and a pat on her shoulder. She was immediately grateful for his support. “We’re all alive, and that’s what matters. We are all alive, right?”

Ambrose moaned pitifully. “I wish I wasn’t.”

Everyone ignored him.

“We need to go after them, right now,” said Luthor. He looked towards the raised aft, as if he could see past it and their foes, who knew how far away. “We can’t let them get a warning out, and there is a good chance they know this world better than we do. SNAC, do you know where we are?”

The golem finished adjusting himself and looked around, eyes glowing brighter. “Yes, Master Graves. I believe we are in an area the locals colloquially refer to as the Forlorn Pit, a ways away from the Imperial outpost in Bedrock Spire to the south. At least two days travel, if I am not mistaken.”

So they had time, at least. Something else hit her, and she gasped. “What about the ship? I couldn’t fly it for long here, and I think we took some damage along the way. What if we’re stuck?”

“What if we attracted attention already?” Raeleq asked.

Raeva pulled at her increasingly frizzy braids. “Shit. We’re in an unfamiliar world, our ship’s damaged, we have to chase some jerks and silence them. Anything else?”

Ambrose groaned.

“Right, he’s out of commission too. What do we do?”

As one, they turned to Luthor. He half-smiled. “Jeeze, no pressure, right? The first thing we do is get information, go through those cards they dropped, see if there’s anything in the ship we can use to help get our current heavy hitter back on his feet. From there, we catch our breaths, check the ship for damage, and then get moving so we can take care of our unwanted attention. After?”

His smile fell, and he couldn’t hide the uncertainty as he looked out into the dim, cool cavern. “I have no clue. I’d hoped to have another day or two to decide before we got into a fight, but obviously that ship’s sailed. We’re going to have to rely on each other now, so remember that.”

They fell silent, and Jessica let his words sink in. He was right. Everyone was counting on her, and she needed to step up and be stronger.

“Okay,” she said. “Let’s see those cards those jerks dropped.”
