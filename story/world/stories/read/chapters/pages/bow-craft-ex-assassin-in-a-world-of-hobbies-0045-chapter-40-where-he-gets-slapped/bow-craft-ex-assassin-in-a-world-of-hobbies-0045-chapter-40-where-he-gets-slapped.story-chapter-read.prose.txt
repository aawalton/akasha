Chapter 40where he gets slapped

Lei-rei was kneeling with her hands clasped in front of her lips. All she could see was dried leaves and Grace's feet. Perhaps she'd feel less shame for calling someone only when she needed help.

"I see," Grace said. "You don't know how to scold him."

"My apologies."

"What's there to apologize for?"

Lei-rei shook her head. "Nothing, I..." She thought Grace would interrupt, but she didn't. Bless her patience this time and all those other times. "It feels as though I might spring a loose floorboard with a bad step."

Grace hummed. "Well, if you feel that you must walk on eggshells for him, that is half his fault."

And the other half is mine. Lei-rei closed her eyes. Blaming herself for anything was unproductive, she knew, but in her head, she lived in a city of strangers, mirages, and moving figures. Sometimes she felt like she might melt into this city and become one of its denizens. That might've been preferable, she thought, if it meant not looking at her own face anymore.

She felt cold fingers under her chin. "Look at me," Grace said, and she looked up and opened her eyes. Grace squinted down at her like an archaeologist who had found something intriguing. The angel brushed away bits of leaves that had clung to Lei-rei's hair. "Not charging from the front is also one of your weaknesses. Now, what will you do?"

Lei-rei looked down and quietly sighed, taking her time to stand—slowly, still thinking, still staring at the ground; still believing that Craft had to stop. For once, she thought, there finally might have been someone who could understand her, but he had been going in circles and circles, lingering just always out of reach. He was so close to causing her the same kind of pain that everyone else makes her feel simply by labeling her "different," and that he hadn't done enough to notice this, and that he might not have even cared enough to do so.

Yet at the same time, she knew he was too distracted with himself, and it was dangerous to ask him to do more than he already had. If he were to learn that he wasn't doing enough, he'd overcorrect, dealing even more damage to himself, and that wasn't a scenario she wished for.

But Grace was right. This fear of losing the status quo—this fear of being angry, of pointing harsh words at someone she was supposed to care about, of not uttering a single complaint even as she continued to feel more and more alone in Craft's presence—was also just cowardice.

She checked the salt shaker in her pocket.

"You still have that," Grace remarked.

"Has it been that long?" Lei-rei said. "My apologies."

"What for?"

She looked to the side, making something between a sigh and a deep breath. "I look for you only when it's convenient to me." Lei-rei looked at her, letting her gaze linger on the concrete dust that had turned into a thin layer of cement over the angel's cheeks, as if she had been in the midst of petrification herself. Grace had always had a permanent emotionless expression, she thought to herself, but the cement had rain lines. She might have noticed them sooner if she had ever looked. "I was wrong all this time to assume that you stand stronger than me."

Hence how she knew Craft must have thought the same of her. This emotional attrition had to end.

Craft juggled a grenade and placed it on the playing table. His heart beat weakly like he'd been given a sedative, and the grenade in his hand was a convenient distraction. It was actually kind of funny how it was so shoddy; what was supposed to be the safety pin was actually a flint striker. Even a subtle jolt would set this thing alight.

He stuck his finger through the striker's pull ring. Given the overall situation, blowing himself up was the optimal choice: it would cause a violation of Pruned's contract, let Lei-rei escape, and ultimately, he could run away. Enty, he thought, your world's too high difficulty.

Pruned hadn't reacted yet, and Craft could yank the pin at any time, but there was a part of him that doubted his doubts. Was what he was doing right? How would he face her again after this? He didn't know who to side with.

"Some things, you can't reassure away," Pruned said, and Craft's finger flinched. "Before you blow yourself up, why not tell someone about it, bro?"

"It's not like we're friends."

"More than acquaintances, less than friends. I mean, we ain't close, but it's not every day you get to meet a black hole for your thoughts. How about that?"

Craft chuckled. "Not the worst idea," he said without much energy. "But I don't know what else to say. You've already said more things about me than I could say about myself."

"It ain't complicated, bro. Just tell me how you feel."

"I think—"

"What you feel, bro."

He hadn't expected that to give him pause, and as he mixed and matched the right and wrong words, he realized he was slow because he hadn't be asked this before.

Seconds dragged on, then without him realizing, minutes. Becoming aware of the time that had absently gone by, every second from there on was suddenly all he could think of. Suddenly, he was incompetent about the things regarding himself. Suddenly, all he could feel was a nameless something trying to worm its way out of his system.

His eyes drooped as he looked at Pruned. "I don't know," he said, and his shoulders sagged. He thought he'd feel more empty and less of a person to admit that, but he felt...liberated. How? How did being more uncertain make things better?

But the fact was, among all the words he could've said, there could have been nothing more true than a simple "I don't know."

Pruned bobbed. Silently, he took away the grenade from Craft's hands. "How's it feel knowing a lot more than you started with?"

Like an idiot. That was probably not something Pruned wanted to hear from him, so he didn't say it. Apparently, he was wrong to assume his self-knowledge was infinite in all directions for the sole reason that he was standing in the middle. There were edges, he realized, and right now, he was falling off the map.

"Maybe I should just run away after all." He pulled out another grenade.

"Where'd you even hide that—no, hold on, bro! Don't you think you're jumping to conclusions here? Miss Sushi hasn't left you behind! That's gotta count for something, right?"

It did. In the larger scheme of things, this wasn't even that grand of a betrayal. Perhaps she was just hesitant to reveal whatever it was. Perhaps she was just like him, and she was somehow protecting him by keeping things to herself. He didn't, after all, have the right to pry and deeper than what she'd let on.

No, the problem wasn't her. For reacting the way that he did, it was obvious he was the broken one. He was an emotional landmine that couldn't even stomach the thought of having the rug pulled from under him.

Up until now, he'd been holding back the fear that he'd either run away or explode, but thanks to Pruned, he realized that the very act of holding back would just make things worse, and he wasn't at all slow to come to a conclusion and make a decision.

Now he knew, he was never ready for this world.

"Even if that's the case"—Craft's finger tightened around the ring—"I'm the villain of this story. I don't fit in this world. I don't fit in her life. Even now, it doesn't feel like you actually care."

"That's not true—"

"You're panicking because if I die, it'll count as a contract violation on your part, right?"

Pruned paused. "Wait, really?"

That's a bluff if I've seen one. He subtly shook his head. "Well, whatever—"

"Bro!"

"—see you next time."

It was satisfying to feel the scratch of the flint as he pulled the pin. The fuze inside fizzed like soda, and white smoke spouted out in a steady stream from his hand. He held the grenade with both hands, clamping it down right before he felt a yank from Pruned's telekinesis.

Smoke filled his vision, and the less he saw, the calmer he became. The knot in his chest had disappeared, and it was a strange feeling to be this calm while knowing full well that he still owed Lei-rei too much.

If I could give her some last words, he thought to himself, what would they be? He chuckled to himself. I just wish this could've gone on a litte longer.

Only then did he notice the unnaturalness of that thought. Years in hostile alternate planes had given him an acute sense of "which one was himself"; though his thoughts had been spoken in his voice, he couldn't locate from which part of his brain the question had actually come from. By that, he knew that something was amiss.

The fuze fizzled out. It was just for a moment that the smoke parted, and he glimpsed a streak of blackfire. Like a genie sucked back into its bottle, the haze all around him wafted and flew back into the grenade.

With the smoke gone, his gaze was drawn to the blackfire. It wreathed a sword, he realized, and he already knew who it was before he met her gaze.

Her eyes burned with anger, and it filled him with relief. She didn't want to abandon me. He should apologize. He reached out towards her, but he gasped as a different woman came between them, descending with her wings of such purity unlike Amacus'.

Her feet touched the ground toes-first, with nary a sound—and she slapped him.

The force knocked him straight into the ground, and he felt its percussion travel across his skull and twist his neck to a point unsafe for a biological body. His face hit the ground with a crunch, and he couldn't tell if it was him or dried leaves.

"One coward," Grace said in a motherly tune, "two cowards, running and coming back like a pendulum, every bit well-meaning and poison to each other at the same time."

She was the one who'd manipulated my thoughts. He turned over, looking at the one who'd knocked him down. Lei-rei was still just behind the angel. The moment they met gazes, she averted her eyes and closed them, furrowing her brows as deeply as he did when it landed that she'd turned away.

The angel bent down, and it terrified him how gracefully she did so, keeping her gaze trained on him for every inch she lowered herself. She maintained a humble posture, keeping her knees together and sitting on her heels. He sensed her protectiveness of Lei-rei from the moment she came between them, but the fact that she did meant he had become an enemy. Of course I did. Of course, he didn't want to be.

"Have you ever wondered why no amount of kindness can change this toxic you?" she continued, and Lei-rei sat beside her. He really, really wished she hadn't—no matter how much he knew he needed to hear the answer.
