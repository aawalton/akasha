Turtleclaws were precisely the type of creatures that one defeated through pure endurance. They combined several elements of human creatures and several that had come out from an insane artist’s imagination. The first time I had seen them, I thought that they had been thought up by Giger himself.

Eighty percent composite shell, they had six octopus-tentacles, a solid internal skeleton, viper fangs on every limb, and a thin layer of dark fur. I had no idea who had come up with the name, but it had stuck. For all practical purposes, they were a combination of a turtle shell and claws.

Infant Turtleclaw

Level: 10

Cards: One of Chalice, Two of Chalice, One of Chains

Special: Tough Skin, Poison Touch, Poison Immunity

Weapon: NONE

The creatures were particularly annoying with their ability to seal and heal wounds, not to mention had a natural resistance to most sharp attacks. Bashing them was also difficult due to their thick composite shells.

The monsters would gather, aiming to poison their enemies with the slime they released. There was nothing smart or elegant about it, and yet it worked.

Got you! I thrust my divine blade forward, piercing through the creature’s tentacle all the way up to its mushy brain.

Seconds later, the turtleclaw was dead.

“You alright?” I glanced over my shoulder.

“Not that I was ever in danger.” Sparta replied.

She was still smiling, but I could hear the note of fear in her voice—fear relating to me. No doubt she was already questioning whether it was a good idea to tag along. The woman was the sort that enjoyed hanging out with people she liked, but consistently made a point that they were weaker than her. If the difference in skills was too large, she’d ignore them, or use them as a one time stand at best; but if the person proved to be stronger, she’d venture away, carefully observing him from a distance.

“It’s the blade,” I said in an attempt to alleviate her concerns. “The merchant made it extra special.”

That was only partially true. After the second awakening, my blades had the ability to slice through flesh and leather as if it were nothing. Fortunately for me, young turtle claws were just that. It took some additional effort to hit the precise areas between shell sections, but once that was done, each strike was in effect a kill.

Sparta nodded, though it was clear that her doubts remained. Three dozen turtleclaws weren’t supposed to die just like that, but they had.

I would have done better, Serp grumbled. One hit and all of them would be dead.

I didn’t doubt it, but for the moment I preferred to keep his abilities somewhat hidden. Sparta had already seen part of what he was capable of during our fight against Hyena. Showing any more would have felt like bragging.

The divine blades kept pulling me forward.

“There’s still a big one,” I said.

“Will you even need me for that?”

“Sure, I will.”

As much as I would have enjoyed showing off a bit, a fully grown turtleclaw was an entirely different matter. While these were roughly the size of a horse, the fully grown ones could reach the size of a mansion. Annoyingly, due to their segmented shell and movable internal skeleton, they could squeeze through tunnels considerably smaller. This cave was more than enough should the turtleclaw decide to leave. The fact that it hadn’t meant that it suspected how strong we were and was waiting where it held an advantage.

There was no longer any doubt that the creature was a side-reward. The only question was what I would get from killing it.

Continuing forward, we came across more and more bones. The turtleclaws were smart enough not to leave them in the entrance.

“Human skulls,” Sparta said. “How do they eat?”

“They dissolve the flesh into goo then suck it up like soup.”

“Lovely.” She stepped away from a pile of bones.

According to my Beastmaster card, only a handful of creatures remained, including the one we had come for.

Remaining on guard, I continued forward. The further we went, the more the smell of sour milk became pronounced. Due to the turtleclaws’ specific way of eating, there wasn’t any smell of rot or decaying flesh in their lairs. The stench of milk indicated the presence of a female. Apparently, this was a full lair, not just a single monster. It was never obvious how in-depth the rule-masters would go; a single monster or an entire group—it was always based on their whims. The only thing for certain was that the larger the group, the stronger the monster in question would be and the greater the prize.

The corners of my mouth curved up in a measured smile. In the past, I would have done anything to avoid such beasts. Now, I couldn’t afford to. A card missed was a card lost, especially since there was never a guarantee when Harlequin would revert to the less-generous system.

“It’s a while ahead,” Sparta said, showing me she had skills of her own. “Why so obsessed with side-monsters? You did the same in the previous trial.”

“Cards,” I replied. “It’s all about the cards.”

“There are other ways of getting cards.”

Maybe she was right, but I wasn’t prepared to chain my existence to a deity. At least not yet.

Soon enough, we entered a grand chamber. Judging by the shape, there must have been a pool of water here at some point. It had long retreated, leaving a bubble of space within the rock. At present, a dozen large turtle claws lay in wait, all curled up by the chamber walls. No doubt they were trying to take advantage of the darkness, using their shells as camouflage. In a different situation, this might have worked, though not with the necklace I was currently wearing.

Most of the monsters were pretty much the same as the ones we had fought before, with a few additional cards. They weren’t the focus of my interest. The alpha, on the other hand, was completely different.

Alpha Turtleclaw

Level: 20

Cards: One of Chalice, Two of Chalice, One of Chains, Fourteen of Chalice, Acrobat

Special: Tough Skin, Poison Touch, Poison Immunity, Diamond Fangs

Weapon: NONE

You really are a big boy, I thought.

Only two cards separated it from the younglings, but they made all the difference. The Fourteen of Chalice provided minor regeneration which, although not to the level of the Major or Grand Arcana, remained a constant boost in battle. As for the Acrobat… that was going to be a pain in the neck.

“Do you see them?” I whispered.

“Yes,” Sparta whispered back.

“Take the smaller ones. I’ll find the champion.”

“Why do I get the feeling you actually like this?” She took her weapons out of her inventory: two thread blades.

“You’ll get used to me.” I dashed forward.

Turtleclaws sprang to life as I dashed through the chamber. Like living traps, they extended their tentacles in an attempt to poison me. I didn’t even slow down.

“Ice wind, fire, and steel,” I sang, relying on my Bard card.

Strands of aether extended, provoking the alpha monster into joining the fight. To little surprise, it did. The massive shell broke up, allowing a tentacle to stretch in my direction. At least thirty feet long, it centered onto me, aiming for a one kill strike.

Arc, I thought, unleashing a pair of thread strikes at the creature. The aether thread of the attack shattered, unable to penetrate the tentacle’s thick skin. The creature wasn’t considered a threat for nothing. Even mage veterans would have a tough time fighting it.

My subsequent attack changed everything. The divine blades sliced through the tentacle like butter. Massive chunks fell off before the monster even got a chance to react.

Combining dual and circular attacks, I kept on running forward. Each second the length of the tentacle was reduced. Just a few more steps and I would be in front of the main body itself.

Got you! I performed a power thrust.

Both tips of my blades split the air. Before they could sink into the oozing flesh, the turtleclaw leaped into the air.

Acrobat… I mentally hissed.

Without that card, it would have been as good as dead. Instead, the fight continued.

Behind me, Sparta was engaging several of the standard turtleclaws. Her combat style was completely different from mine. For the most part, she relied on them making the first move, then using her thread weapons to wound them severely enough so they had to disengage. Likely knowing that no reward would come from victory, she focused on keeping them at bay, rather than going for the kill.

One of the minor pests sped in my direction, perceiving me as an alternative target.

Clenching my fists, I unleashed my pent-up frustration by performing another power thrust. This time nothing came in the way. My blade ripped through flesh like silk, singing to the hilt.

Fire, I thought creating a spell circle on the blade, cooking the entire inside of the monster.

Eyes on the prize, old man! Serp reminded.

I pulled out my sword and looked around. The alpha was using its remaining tentacles to bounce off the chamber walls like a ball in a table tennis tournament. I disliked that type of attack. Each time, the speed increased, making the creature exceedingly difficult to follow.

Earth! I cast a large circle on the floor. Walls of stone rose up to the ceiling, only to be shattered. My magic remained too weak to be used effectively, especially against enemies of this caliber.

Combining my Observer and Archer cards, I threw one of the divine blades at where the turtleclaw was going to be. Silver cut through flesh, sinking in like a pin dropped in water. Sadly, nothing else happened. While I had struck my target, I hadn’t killed it. The blade was probably inside, stuck to the beast’s skeleton. No doubt the regeneration and healing spells were already reducing the effects of the injury.

Four of Rings, Twelve of Rings, Eleven of Chalice! I thought.

The second blade I was holding lit up in green flames. I could only hope that once awakened, the weapons were considered part of my body even if not in my possession.

The creature changed trajectory, aiming straight for me. The Observer showed how it was aiming to crush me like a bug. At this point, there was no escape. Leaping out of the way would result in nothing. No magic was going to help me, and I strongly doubted that the Eight of Chalice would save me from getting crushed.

I blanked my mind. Focusing on the approaching creature, I threw the second divine blade straight at it, then summoned Serp.

“Do it!” I slid my left palm along the blade.

Serp pulled out part of my blood like a ribbon, growing as he did. The blade extended, striking the space between shell fragments ten feet away.

The weapon shook. I could feel it move through the thick skin of the turtleclaw. Three blades shot out of the creature, striking the cave floor, taking the role of makeshift anchors. It was a good attempt, though too late. My only hope was that the effort would slow down the momentum to the point I could withstand it.

Five of Rings! I strengthened my bones.

The monster kept moving closer, then spontaneously burst into a cloud of green smoke.

I braced myself for an impact that never came.

Both my divine swords dropped to the floor with a clang, now free from the monster’s body. They were followed by five cards.

Finally! Serp rejoiced. He was glowing a deep orange. The growth had transformed him into something that definitely wasn’t a sword anymore.

Quickly, I unsummoned him. With luck, the remnants of my earth had prevented Sparta from seeing what had happened.

“Sparta?” I shouted, rushing to grab the blades on the floor. “You alright?”

“Almost done,” she shouted back. “You?”

I could sense only one creature remaining.

This was too close. I looked at the spot where the cards had landed. Far too close.

I needed to use more of my cards in battle. The Emperor might have been useful in a situation such as this, although it would also have impacted Sparta.

Threads sliced through the walls of stone that remained, allowing the woman to step through. I couldn’t sense any other creatures. Her glances fell on me, then at the cards.

“Now I see why he likes you so much,” she said.

“I’m a real barrel of joy.” I looked at the cards. “I’ll get the Major,” I said. “You keep the rest.”

There was no fixed exchange rate between cards. Some claimed that a Major Arcana went for five Minor. Some thought it was more. Given the presence of two healing cards, I didn’t think Sparta would mind.

“So, what now?” She picked up the cards. “More hunting?”

There was nothing of interest remaining in the cave. Furthermore, being so close to aelf territory made me feel uneasy. Either of us could easily take on an opponent as strong as the second trial’s boss, but that was no guarantee we could pull it off here. More importantly, any reckless action could be treated as the start of the war and trigger the standard resolution of the trial.

“Let’s go back,” I said. “I’ve got a place we could stay.”
