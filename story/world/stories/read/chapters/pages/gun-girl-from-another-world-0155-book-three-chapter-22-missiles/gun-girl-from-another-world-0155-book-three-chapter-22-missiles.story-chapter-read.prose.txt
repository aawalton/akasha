Chapter 22

Missiles

I, for one, am quite satisfied with our thoroughness in clearing the dungeon. We did, in fact, flesh out the rest of the map, with a small mountain of useless junk to prove it, and our stomachs are warm and fuzzy with fresh custard that would have fully topped us off, plus a little stat boost besides, if we had needed it.

It would be a lie to say that we feel ready for this boss fight. Rather, we feel like it’s pre-determined.

After all, I’ve already defeated Mr. Golem twice. Sure, the dungeon setup gives him some new toys now, but how hard could it really be?

A heavy weight reverberates against the stone floor a moment after the heavy portcullis slams shut behind us. The golem is standing there waiting for us like it has something to prove, and, boy, does it. My jaw drops as I crane my head to take it in.

The heavy weight is a massive iron ball at the end of a long chain, effectively the siege equivalent of a meteor hammer. In the other hand, a massive tower shield that looks like it could tank small arms fire. And that's not where it stops, either. Two more weapons rest above, one on either shoulder. On its right is a heavy crossbow assembly designed for quick, automatic reloading of its heavy bolts to achieve near semi-automatic fire rates. On the other, a large, wide brace of cylindrical tubes. Fire lances, low-yield, primitive black powder rockets designed to be dumb-fired en masse from a single fuse.

“Ohhh thumpers,” I mutter, my French thesaurus utterly failing me in the moment. “It rolled a full set.”

“Remmi?!” Ayre demands desperately as an incendiary flash ignites behind the brace.

“Cover!” My shout in reply is followed by me diving for a pillar. I grab Kyuuga while Ayre yanks Korrigan behind downed architecture.

The next instant, the air around us detonates as the entire bracket of lances goes off, sound waves echoing off of the inviolate walls as rubble from the floor and architecture showers down around us.

“What the [otso] is that attack, Remmi?!” Ayre's tense screaming is completely understandable. I'll even ignore the return of The Word.

“Missiles,” I reply back. “Or, at least, very primitive ones. Nice kick, huh? Black powder has three million joules of energy per ignited kilogram! It's no Tsar Bomba, but …” I briefly pause, considering that prospect. “Actually, that's probably a good thing.”

“Stop sounding so proud of it!” The archer looks over the rubble to check, only to dive back down as several bolts shoot through roughly where his head had been. Accuracy isn't great, then, but it's more than good enough.

“Great, the fight only just started, and it's already got us pinned,” Ayre complains. “And it's still got some of those powder things left in reserve!”

“Oh, no, it doesn't,” I correct my friend. “It pulls that trigger, and they all go off. It's not reserving. It's regenerating.”

The elf shoots me a very dirty, petulant glare. “Remmi, I hate you so much right now.”

“Relax, relax,” I assure him with a wave of my hand. “It's only a problem if he comes over here to flush us out with that ball and–”

I'm interrupted by said iron ball flashing past my pillar with enough force to crash against the wall behind us. Four sets of eyes watch as the chain retracts it back over the terrain and out of sight again.

“... Yeah,” I reconsider my position, “maybe it's better if we keep moving.”

We split up and come out from the cover from as many directions as possible to keep the golem from targeting our clusters. It tries anyway, but with no one around us, we're able to get out of the way of anything but the still-recharging fire lances.

Once we're out, the fight becomes much more straightforward as we automatically adopt our standard strategy of surrounding a single opponent, but the golem is undeterred. Whenever one of us tries to set up for a spell or combo, it uses that ball or a quick bolt to keep us moving.

We are on the brink of gold rank, however, and it's only a matter of time before we get ourselves into position. Ayre’s arrow is swirling with seafoam green energy. Korrigan holds fire in her hands. Kyuuga is bouncing off of a pillar to set up for a devastating blow to the rear.

And I freeze. “WAAAAAIIIIIIIT!!!”

The whole room stumbles to catch itself. Even the golem seems to pause, probably trying to process the sudden change in its opponents’ tactics.

With everyone's attention on me, I shout, with all the urgency of an emergency, “We forgot to test the life insurance!”

Ayre stares at me like maybe my words didn't translate. “You want one of us to go down to the boss?”

“Yeah,” I confirm, happy that there hadn't been a misunderstanding after all. “You ready to die for the cause, Ayre?”

His confusion shifts to total panic. “Wait, why me?!”

“Well,” I explain as I tilt my head, “I promised Kyuuga he'd get to be the first wild monster to clear a dungeon, and he can't do that if he gets kicked out right at the end. And the ESRB would can our rating if anything happened to Korrigan. And it can't be me, because I'm the hea–”

A sudden metallic mass collides with the side of my face, and I don't really feel the impact so much as I spontaneously find myself sprawled against a far wall, staring disorientedly up at a traumatic injury alert.

All I can think to do is unsteadily raise a hand into the air vaguely in Mr. Golem's direction. “Tha … tha wush a cheap shoot, Mr. … Mr. … Automaton …”

He apparently doesn't care, because his cooldown on the brace just finished, and he immediately lights it up with my immobile form in his sights.

I never feel the blast. Instead, the next thing I'm aware of, the alert is gone, and I'm opening my eyes to see Yorin and Angel in front of me, at weird angles, and the sun-like light source that illuminates the main chamber is behind them.

Oh. I'm on my back.

At their worried faces, I put on a bright smile. “Good news, the retrieval system works! Also, three million joules has a lot of kick …”

“It is good that your recovery system works as you intended,” Yorin replies, “but was it wise to try it on yourself? Not that your friends are not valuable, but the fate of the empire does not rest on their shoulders.”

“Oh, well,” I fumble, suddenly having trouble meeting their eyes, “short straw, and all that. And if an engineer can't stand by her work, does she even deserve the name?”

“How very noble of you,” the sacred priestess intones, and I'm pretty sure even a blind, deaf, illiterate dingus could tell she isn't buying it. She drops it, though, and steps back. “Given the injuries with which you were delivered, I suspect that the dungeon's newfound sanctity of life is not the only thing that you were able to ascertain.”

I sit up, swinging my legs off of the pavilion cot I'm on, and I nod as I rub my cheek. “Yeah, Mr. Golem holds a grudge.”

That gets an arched eyebrow from Yorin and a very confused look from Angel.

“But he doesn't really do any thinking,” the latter points out.

“Well, he sure seemed to remember losing to me twice,” I counter, “and made it a point to take it personally.”

“I believe you may be engaging in a process known as anthropomorphication,” Yorin suggests. “Are your people familiar with it?”

I nod. “It translated just fine, yes.”

She nods back. “Then you understand that you may be interpreting completely independent behaviors as erroneously humanoid?”

“Sure,” I agree, “but you didn't see that thing focus on me with maximum firepower.”

“Remmi, I say to you again that you are a Hero,” Yorin stresses to me with surprising patience. “In any group of lesser warriors, it is the basest of rationality to prioritize your removal due to your great power and adaptability. Indeed, if the dungeon has kept records of your past performance and is feeding that knowledge to the dungeon lord, or if the dungeon lord itself preserves such information, it needs not possess an ego to recognize the sheer power of your weaponry.”

I frown at being pinned down with such impeccable reasoning. “Yes, okay, I recognize all of that, but it’s a lot harder not to ascribe malice when it’s slugging you in the face!”

“Emotional projection,” the priestess answers immediately.

My frown progresses in its severity as I grip the edge of the cot. “I know! I get it! Just let me have my pout, will you? I’d much rather tell myself I went down to a grudge than to an algorithm!”

We’re interrupted by a giggling laugh from Angel. “You’re both so serious about something so silly,” she explains when we both turn to look at her in confusion. “Miss Remmi is too worried about being fun, but Miss Yorin is too worried about being right! But do either of those really matter? Isn’t the important thing that we’ve made sure everything’s working so we can finally help everyone?”

Yorin’s face is surprised for a moment, probably at being called out, but it quickly retreats into a warm smile. “That is well said, Angel. Indeed, it is good that the functions of the dungeon have been confirmed.”

She turns back to me. “Nevertheless, I would have a record of your alterations, Remmi. It is important to know exactly what has been done in case anything akilter arises.”

I nod to that and pull my pack off of my shoulders and around in front of me. “Yeah, sure, I had a feeling you’d ask, so I made another copy last night. Just, you know, the usual– hm?”

When I pause, Yorin raises an eyebrow. “Is there something amiss, Remmi?”

“Not amiss, just unexpected,” I answer. “There’s a portion of my infinite storage space specifically being held in reserve that I’m not allowed to access.”

“Can you determine why?”

I concentrate on the notice a moment before deciphering the answer. “Oh! It must be because I’ve got loot from the dungeon run stored in there! You don’t get to keep anything you get in there unless you successfully finish the run. If you go down, you lose everything you’ve acquired inside. But our party hasn’t actually lost yet, so the loot hasn’t been retrieved. However, since I’ve been rung out, it’s still tied to the dungeon, and I’m not allowed to access it.”

“Oh, that’s good,” Angel cheers. “If you were storing everything you found right up to the end, it would be bad if all of that was lost!”

“Eh,” I disagree with a wiggle of my hand, “it would matter more to a level-appropriate party. For us, it was mostly just junk and loose change. But it’s good to know that the dungeon core thought of something like that. It’s possible someone with special storage could try to get knocked out on purpose to make off with loot without finishing the run.”

“Another thing that is helpful to know,” Yorin agrees patiently. “It will reduce the policing necessary for the guild to concern itself with.” But she holds out her hand nonetheless. “The records, Remmi?”

“Oh, right …” I dig out the promised copy and place it in her outstretched palm. “That’s my plans, stipulations, propositions, and programming logic. Everything I gave the core. As I was saying, not that you specifically need to hear it, but it should go without saying that this is information that probably should be considered highly confidential. If it’s at all reproducible and gets into greedy hands, the unscrupulous could try to transform dungeons across the land into treasure printers.”

The sacred priestess gives a very solemn nod, and she seems even more serious than usual. “You have my word, Remmi, that it will receive all due protection.”
