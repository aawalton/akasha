The Last Great Beast King

Matted, golden locks draped down across the regal beast’s face as he sat upon his throne.

Ever since he could remember, he had been a king. The leader of the pride and king of the jungle, even before the Integration, the humanoids had shown him respect and marveled at his beauty.

And when the System came, none of that changed all too much. Beasts took over the dormant world, and he claimed his rightful spot at the top, challenged by only a handful.

During the early days, before he gained intelligence, he had thought that those changes would last. That a new chapter had turned. One that saw the beasts reclaim their world.

Then it all got turned upside down. The humanoids returned, bringing other strange species along with them. But kings do not bow, and he was no different.

He clung to his territory, and slaughtered any who would dare to challenge his reign. And that had worked all too well. But all good things came to an end eventually.

From his throne, he had watched as one great beast lord after another fell. As the humanoids endlessly expanded their territories. As their hunters and warriors used beasts to power their own ascension, getting stronger and stronger.

Their growth had been exponential, even before the dungeon, and he was not too arrogant to see the threat that they posed. Like a virus spreading across their world, if something wasn’t done soon, the outcome would be fatal.

And yet, that foolish ant had refused to work with him. Pure hubris had been its undoing. A belief that it could subjugate all, both beast and humanoid alike, and claim their world for itself.

And now it was dead.

He wished he could mock it. But no solace would come. He knew what he was up against, and laughing at fools gave him no comfort.

And for those reasons, he had declined the dungeon. After all, the beasts had already lost their edge, and their advantage. And he would have been outnumbered had he entered.

The golden king growled. It infuriated him so dearly. They had gained more levels on average, and were the stronger warriors. They should have been able to win. To conquer their world. But they hadn’t, and now any chance of undoing the mistakes of their past was quickly slipping past their grasp.

Even now, as a C-grade monarch amongst beasts, he could not be sure they stood a chance.

Could he beat the greatest humanoids should they fight one against one? Of course. He had all the confidence in himself. But so had the ant, and he was not so foolish as to follow in their footsteps.

The reality was, no matter how strong he had become, the humanoids had more top warriors. And his pride was short in number, and the other beasts were too paranoid to join his cause in numbers significant enough to make a difference.

Why did beasts have to be like that? Could they not see what would happen should the humanoids claim their world? They would return to their former status, seen as little more than mindless creatures to be slaughtered for food and levels.

The System was the great equalizer. An opportunity to turn everything on its head, and claim something better than subjugation.

And yet, most beasts seemed entirely oblivious to this obvious fact. Ready to be herded back into the same shameful existence they had endured before the Integration.

But not the king. He loathed such a thought. He loathed the condescending concept of being called a king whilst being kept in a cage for the humanoids to gawk at.

He wasn’t a pet, or a slave for that matter. He was a regal warrior, and soon he would show those arrogant humanoids the errors of their ways and teach them a lesson for thinking that they belonged at the top.

“Our time is fast approaching,” he muttered to himself as he watched the map markers.

It seemed that the same System that had given them a chance to turn society upside down, hadn’t yet entirely given up on them. For it had granted him a quest.

A quest to claim their world. And thanks to that quest, he could see the objective plainly on his map.

“The world core…”

The beast king’s lips curled up to expose his fangs. Thanks to his quest, he didn’t need to conquer all of the obelisks. He just needed that world core. However, he had to move with caution.

He had paid dearly for missives on his enemies, but thanks to that, he already knew that several humanoid champions were moving to claim the core for themselves. And he just had to pick the right moment to act.

It was a simple strategy. Divide and conquer. Have the humanoids fight each other, and then, once weakened, swoop in at the final minute to claim the prize.

If he played his cards right, then he could claim it all for himself and cement his and beastkind’s rule over this world.

All he had to do now was be patient and move his pieces into position. He would not end like that greedy ant, or the other foolish beast kings who could not see reason, and were now all dead.

“Soon, soon you shall all see the cunning that a beast is capable of,” he growled.

The king had one great advantage over the other fallen kings. He had always had strength, far more than the humanoids could ever dream of. And he had known how powerless he was despite it. It had given him insight, and it had taught him a powerful lesson.

Strength alone would win no thrones.
