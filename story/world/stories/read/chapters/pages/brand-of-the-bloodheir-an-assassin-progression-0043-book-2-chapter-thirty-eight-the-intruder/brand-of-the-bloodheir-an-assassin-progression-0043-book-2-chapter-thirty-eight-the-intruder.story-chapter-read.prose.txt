In the safety of his home in the Barren, limitless choices were now the most insidious threats to Risens’s sanity. The Under had not proven to be a drain on his stamina as the tests in the Roost had been, though something about the nature of the challenges begged pause. He’d only begun the trials, but in each, he’d been forced to return to the exit instead of being presented with a path farther into the unknown. Mother Raven and even the Talons had warned that the dangers that lurked within were grave, adding further emphasis to not rushing onward with reckless abandon.

The powers granted to him upon completing the trials within the Roost were unheralded. Already, in only a few doors,Risens possessed greater skills and more Brands than any in recorded history. And, he had only scratched the surface. In the vaulted heights, he could see another four floors worth of what appeared to be doorways before the darkness overhead swallowed any hopes of higher views. He could spend an eternity here, pressing forward in timelessness, furthering his skills until he would be untouchable. However, something about the idea felt wrong. Windwake begged his attention, and it was only there that he would find an answer to many of his growing list of questions.

The tasks at hand were considerable. There were still perhaps two days before his return to the king would be called into question. Risens assumed he could return now, though his haste would demand increased scrutiny that he had no desire to face. His shadowed task of collecting the trove of information from the Gilded Cage had shifted his focus. It was time to see how much the male healer knew, to ferret out how much his loose lips had put Risens’s life in danger.

And maybe it was time to silence him for good.

With a wave of the Quillkey on his finger, the door to the Roost slid silently open. He braced himself for the infinite cold and the painful rush and pressure of the passage. Each time, the overbearing effects seemed dulled compared with the last trip. The Roost greeted him in silence—as it always had—with the condemnatory eyes of the stone ravens bearing down on him.

Again, there was a particular sense of disappointment that rolled through his mind. The Quillkey had granted access to the Barren from any location, and the only risk was that there was no dedicated return beyond that same doorway. His previous portal to the Gilded Cage would be of no use to him, forcing him to traverse the hallowed, ancient halls for the exit to the Raven’s Court. The lure of the powers that lurked beyond the sealed doorway was strong, though his focus on the task at hand steered his decision.

“I’m sorry, friends,” he whispered to the ravens. “Now is not the time. I will return soon, I promise.”

Only silence answered him as he slipped into the wavering, blackness of the void that would take him to the Ravens Court.

The familiar abandon and disrepair greeted him. Even without sight of the city proper, commotion reached his ears. Distant chimes, hollow and ominous, cut through the quiet of the night as the fire bells sounded their alarm. He needed no clues to understand the cause, for it was he who had torched Lady Myrenas’ estate, and with the halted passing of time within the Roost, Barren, and Under, it had only just begun to do its intended damage.

The unfurled wings of the Shrine of the Appraiser folded silently, returning to their original disfigured pose as he stalked across the dirty tiles. Making use of the narrow protruding ledges built into the walls, he ascended the southern wall without difficulty. A few quick leaps found him perched carefully on one of the many avian effigies on the rooftop.

Broad District hemmed in the Raven’s Court, but its streets were currently deserted. The twisted maze of buildings only made it more difficult to navigate, due to the ravages of time and neglect. Structures beyond blocked his view of the rest of the city, but his sense of direction was keen. The sweeping coast of the Sea Solace dominated much of the area south of his position. The shoreline to the southwest led to Quayside and the Estates beyond. The horizon glowed with a distinct orange hue, though not from the rising sun. The angular forms of the buildings were silhouetted, black, rigid shapes against the pulsing amber halo of light from the raging inferno.

Risens had no remorse for his destruction of the grand estates that bordered the seaside or the complaints or setbacks for those who would lose property. They could easily afford to rebuild and replace what was lost. The dead that he’d cremated in the basement of the lady’s estate had neither the means nor were they offered such courtesies.

Risens pivoted on his perch, letting his gaze sweep back over the Raven’s Court as he plotted his descent. Motion near the crumbling arch of the entranceway froze him in his place. A shape moved among the shadows of the ruined pathway.

The figure that crept through the archway was clear—small and cloaked in black—moving quietly, with an obvious attempt at stealth. Instinctively, his hands fell to the hilts of the blades strapped to his hips. He mentally steeled himself for the screaming urge for death as his grip closed around the feathered handles of the Raven Talons. The resonating silence was startling.

“What, nothing to say now?” Risens grumbled.

Still, he was met with quiet.

He found a strange solace in this, as any pause to violence from the blades had become a telltale sign—one he’d come to trust. The blades, like the Conspiracy of Ravens, answered to his call, but had minds of their own. If they chose silence, history said that the task before him was not worthy of their ire. In the mountain ravine, when faced with the abomination of ice, stone, and death, they had refused his touch. When he had tried to draw them against Fendri’s vile throat, they had been likewise restrained. He knew not the reason for their hesitation, yet he would trust them.

Tracing the shifting form across the Raven’s Court, it became readily apparent that although the effort for stealth was admirable, the figure’s movements were clumsy and untrained. Preconceived notions and assumptions about one’s stature or competency could be deadly, but in this situation, there was no confusion. The thin, frail form was not an elaborate act. This was no assassin in disguise.

This was an amateur.

An amateur invading the secrecy of the forbidden court.

In all the years that he’d haunted the off-limits shrine, Risens had never seen hints of any others intruding on the sacred space. Never had he noted the slightest hint of footprints in the dust of the path, or smudged soil on the dirty stone tiles. Every twig remained unbroken, every pebble untouched.

He doubted that the wretched, the poor, the haggard, and mad denizens of the Broad frequented the place.

This was something altogether different.

Something new.

The unexpected newcomer padded through the crumbling arch, scanning the features of the court.

After a thorough watch and confirmation that nothing lurked in the darkness of the tumbledown ruins, the cloaked figure moved cautiously through the interior.

Risens himself was a trespasser here, yet the Branding, the call of the voice, and the messages of Mother Raven had imparted in him a sense of propriety, a twisted perception of ownership over the space. A stab of anger lanced through him as he watched the figure trespass where none were allowed to tread.

The intruder shuffled slowly toward the sacred shrine in the center of the small clearing. Its head tilted from side to side,with a calculating gaze, investigating every measure of the statue’s twisted features. A part of Risens almost expected the visitor to cry out, to fall back in pain as it reached out and ran its fingers gently across the folded wings of the half-man, half-raven Shrine of the Appraiser. Unfazed by the contact, it rounded the stone, pausing again when a full circle had been made.

For the first time since the visitor had entered the Raven’s Court, moonlight reflected off the eyes beneath the shadow of the heavy cowl. Though the pale blue light only illuminated a portion of the face, Risens could make out features that were young, dirty, and feminine.

The interloper stared up at the shrine, frozen in place for several minutes while Risens considered his options. In the heat of battle, choices were clear, though now he was torn on the path forward. Even from so high, he was confident in the ability of the Dull Wind to move him to nearly any position in the small stone court. He could surprise her as Mother Raven had done to him so many times. Furthermore, he could end her life, though the silence of his daggers told him that was not the move to make.

In the end, he chose to merely be a passive observer of the bizarre scene.

After a long span of silence, she abandoned her investigation. Whether she simply studied the idol or pleaded for a Brandwas unclear. He smiled, wondering how many times Mother Raven had watched him as a youth from this very spot. How similar would he have looked to her? A dirty, broken orphan turned killer, begging for a Brand to give his life some sense of meaning beyond death.

The woman approached the stagnant pools in the long-silent fountains. The withered plants in each bed curled into gnarled stalks like dead fingers, frozen in rigor and death. Stopping at the closest ancient fountain, she fell to her knees and inspected the withered brush of the garden before reaching out and clearing a small section of dirt and detritus. She then reached into her pocket with one hand while digging a small hole in the soil with the other. With a gentle, reverent motion, she pressed an object into the dirt before tenderly patting it down.

Risens strained to hear anything beyond the hushed whisper of a voice as the individual who worked the land in the corner of the Ravens Court spoke softly to herself as she completed their act. Though her voice was quiet, it was airy and youthful, and it struck him with an instant sense of recognition.

It was an individual he’d only just met, a voice he’d only heard on a few occasions, yet there was no mistaking the tone and timbre. He could have overlooked their previous interactions as a coincidence, though that presumption had now been thoroughly dashed. Something far more consequential was at play.

Aleth, the servant girl from Lady Myrenas’s estate, the youth hiding in the grounds of Excelsior, was here in the Raven’s Court.

For a few moments, panic riddled his mind, and he struggled for understanding. How had she found him? Had she followed him? Had she seen his shadowed movements through the city? If so, how could he have missed it?

Impossible. Risens had utilized the portals here and through the Quillkey to enter the Barren and the Roost.

With a few rapid breaths, he settled his rampantly running mind. None had followed him, let alone her. He’d watched as she skipped down the avenue to the temple doors, unperturbed by the attack in the alley, and he had disappeared into the shadows. He knew she couldn’t have followed. It was beyond the bounds of possibility, yet here she was. Risens’s mind rebelled, refusing to accept the fact that this was merely another random coincidence.

This was something he couldn’t put his finger on... Fate?

That the youth had stumbled unknowingly or unwittingly into the Ravens Court was a stretch even for his recently expanded viewpoint.

There was a purpose, a correlation that he would find out.

Soon.
