Chapter II.III (2.3)

Food turned out to be a lot more interesting to Deteriorate that cloth. It could spoil in a day, which fit Mitsuko’s criteria perfectly. She was quickly coming to realize this spell might be quite a bit harder to level than Mend had been. While she could reliably break and fix things herself with Mend, Deteriorate was instead tied to a nebulous sense of fate. Who knew what an object’s future might hold?

The good news was that it had a longer range of time to reach into than Mend had when she’d first acquired it at level 1. But even still, how could she reliably use it to her advantage?

They sat in The Selcouth Sable’s small mess hall. While some passengers had retreated to their own chambers after the day’s events, some were huddled around in the dining area. They kept their voices down, but Mitsuko suspected they were here because they craved some sort of companionship after their near death experiences. She largely ignored them as she continued her experiments.

Across from her sat Holly, peeling the skin off of an apple and offering it to Juniper. Despite being below deck, they had yet to be questioned about the bird’s presence. People certainly saw and stared at Juniper, but nobody commented. Likely because the sailors already had enough tasks on hand to keep them busy and none of the passengers felt like stirring up shit.

As she watched Holly finish shaving the apple’s skin, an idea occurred to Mitsuko.

“Hand me your knife.” Mitsuko held out her hand and Holly set the hilt into her open palm.

“What are you thinking?” Holly sat up, tucking her knees under her to get her small body a better vantage point.

“Hold on, just watch. If it works I think you’ll understand immediately.”

Mitsuko held up the metal knife, dangling between her thumb and index finger with the point down. Her other hand pressed up against the wooden table.

She dropped the knife, then cast Deteriorate on the table before the blade struck it. She watched with fascination as a gouge formed on the wooden surface before the knife struck the table. When the knife did strike, it hit the marred section of the table and created an even deeper gouge.

Mitsuko felt a grin take hold of her face. This was exactly what every bladedancer dreamed of. A way to wound foes before she even cut them down. Sadly, it wouldn’t work on living opponents just yet. But it could still damage armor.

Of course, there was also the drawback that at the moment she was required to make physical contact with the target, but she assumed that would change in a level or two. She had unlocked Mend’s Aura very early in her progress with that spell. Since Deteriorate held so many similarities with Mend, she suspected that she would be receiving similar boons as she leveled the spell.

“Interesting.” Holly poked the gouge in the table. “You damaged it twice. I can see how that might be useful. But what if you only intend to drop the knife but don’t actually do it?”

Mitsuko blinked. That was an excellent question.

She raised the knife up and prepared to drop it. Then cast Deteriorate. Nothing. She lowered the knife with a frown.

“You didn’t drop it,” Holly surmised. “It’s not going to work unless you actually intend to drop it. So if you sit there waiting for the damage to happen and never commit to the action, it will never come.”

“An excellent analysis,” Juniper praised. “You have a smart friend with you, Poppet. It’s as she says. Paradoxically, you must truly be intending to commit to the action to not commit to the action. Easier said than done. I recommend that you try it on another rather than yourself. It’s easy to get in your own head about such things.”

“Here.” Mitsuko passed the knife back to Holly. “On the count of three, I want you to drop it on the table. One. Two….”

And then a new scratch marred the table below where Holly dangled it. Instead of dropping the blade, Holly sat back and met Mitsuko look with excitement dancing in her eyes.

“Mitsuko. You know what this means, right?”

Mitsuko nodded. “That I can cut down enemies twice, but slicing them only once without followup myself will prove more challenging unless I change my mindset.”

Holly threw her head back and laughed joyfully. “Mitsuko! You’re a mage! Like, a truly powerful mage!”

A smile cracked Mitsuko’s face. “All I did was dent a table. I’m not exactly tossing about lightning blasts.” But despite Mitsuko’s words downplaying her spell, she felt lighter from Holly’s declaration.

Juniper tittered, an odd sound from a seagull. “Poppet. You’re fishing for compliments.”

To change the subject, Mitsuko set her hand on the table and Mended the damage. The wood popped back into place, as if never scarred by her knife.

“Interesting.” Still grinning, Holly poked the table. “So you’re reversing time with that spell? Can you make the table fall into a bunch of wooden planks?”

“I might be able to. Actually, let me check something.” Mitsuko cast Retrospection. In a moment, she saw the table’s existence flash in her mind. Stains, nicks, beatings, cleaning. A weirdly wide variety of events when condensed in her mind. But the table was too old for her Retrospection to give insight on its creation.

“I might be able to revert it back to a time when someone spilled their beer over it,” Mitsuko offered. “But I don’t know how far back ago it was created. Further than I can see. Still might be able to but I’d be performing the act blind.”

“Well done,” Juniper complimented sweetly. “An excellent display of skill and forethought. Using Escara’s spell to scout ahead and before performing Pilroth’s spell.”

“Can you call him Sterling?” Mitsuko asked at the same time Holly said, “what?”

“I’m talking to the bird,” Mitsuko clarified for Holly’s sake. “The Sage of Mending. I know him as Sterling. It would be easier to understand who you’re talking about if we both refer to him by the same name.”

“Sterling,” the sage said the word as if tasting it in her mouth. “It makes him and I sound like we are on more friendly terms than we are, which I don’t like. But if it helps reduce confusion I can amend my language to accommodate your request.”

“Thanks.” Mitsuko leaned back in her seat. “I guess we should talk about our next move. We need to get over to Porcelain, Amber, and Azure Island.”

“Well, you did rescue the crew, passengers, and ship,” Holly pointed out. “I imagine you can leverage that for a free ride to most places in the archipelago.”

Mitsuko drummed her fingers on the table. “Most. But not all. I’ve already freed the sages on Verdant and Ashen. Unfortunately, Amber Island moves around and is hard to predict. And Azure Island is a myth.”

“That leaves us Porcelain,” Holly pointed out.

“Yeah.” Mitsuko considered. “Let’s first drop off the passengers on Mauve. Maybe do a bit more information gathering while we’re there.”

“Sounds good to me! I’ll see about shmoozing the captain for you. See if he’ll bring The Selcouth Sable over to Porcelain.” She paused. “Unless you’ve gained a bit more skill at negotiations while trapped in the timeloops? You want to try your hand at diplomacy and bartering?”

“No.”

Holly sighed and reached up to pat Mitsuko’s shoulder. “Don’t ever change my friend.”
