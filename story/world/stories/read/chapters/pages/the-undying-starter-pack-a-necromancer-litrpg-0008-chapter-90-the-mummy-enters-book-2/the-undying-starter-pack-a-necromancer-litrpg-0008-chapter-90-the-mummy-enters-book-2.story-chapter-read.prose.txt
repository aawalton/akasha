An undead wolf emerged before Ethan and the others. Its front body bulky with muscle, its fur as dark as midnight. Half of its face was stripped bare to the bone, the exposed skull glaring with two crimson eyes that burned like embers.

“The wolf… it’s grown so much bigger, hasn’t it?” Lina said, stepping closer to the undead beast, which now stood nearly as tall as she was.

This creature had evolved into an Uncommon-grade Death Fang Wolf. Yet, despite its size, it still wasn’t large enough to serve as a proper mount for Ethan, at best, the top of its head only reached a little above his stomach. But for Lina, it was just the right fit. Paired with her fighting style, striking from range with her bow, this wolf could become her perfect partner in battle.

“Call the wolf Fang. I think that name suits him,” Ethan said.

Lina glanced back at him and nodded. “Yes… I like it,” she replied softly. “So, I can ride this wolf?”

“If you can learn to handle him in the middle of battle, it will only make you stronger,” Ethan answered.

Lina smiled, stepping right up to the undead wolf and gently stroking its jaw. “Hey, Fang. From now on, let’s become good friends, alright?”

The undead beast gave a low growl in response.

Lina then leapt onto the back of the undead wolf, shifting her balance as she tried to get used to the seat.

She gave Fang a light pat on the shoulder. “Come on, Fang. We’ve got a lot of hunting to do.”

The wolf responded with a low growl before turning around sharply. Lina glanced at Hollow Leon and waved her hand. “Leon, follow us,” she called.

Without hesitation, Hollow Leon started moving, then broke into a run, quickly keeping pace as the undead wolf dashed farther into the distance.

Ethan had already given the command that both Fang and Hollow Leon were to listen to Lina’s orders. More than that, since he intended to leave this hunting ground to Vexxa and Lina, he issued his second layer of commands, first through Vexxa, then through Lina, ensuring they both had authority over the undead.

Ethan would need to secure a proper undead mount for himself sooner or later. After all, there were certain areas where their battle car wouldn’t be effective, and having a steed he could ride would make things far easier.

Perhaps a larger wolf… something agile, fast, but sturdy enough to carry his massive frame. Or maybe something entirely different, whatever he might come across next.

As his undead thralls scattered across the field to begin their hunt, with Lina accompanying them, Ethan decided to make use of the waiting time for something else. He wanted training, real combat practice. And this time, he chose Vexxa as his sparring partner.

He stepped forward, taking position a short distance from her.

“How should I fight, Master?” Vexxa asked calmly.

“I like the way you fight with the dagger,” Ethan replied. “So I’ll face you with my battle axe against your dagger technique.”

Vexxa nodded and summoned her dagger.

Ethan gripped his battle axe, switching it into Dark Element mode, the weapon’s edge seething with shadowy energy. He braced himself, waiting for Vexxa to make the first move.

He had already spent the past few days training against Goldy and Franky, testing himself against their raw strength. Through these drills, he had begun learning how to adapt his style depending on the type of opponent he faced.

Fighting a large, tough enemy like Franky required power and endurance. Blocking heavy strikes, withstanding their crushing weight, and finding openings in the slower rhythm of their movements. Against such foes, brute strength and solid defense were key.

Then there were enemies of equal size and stature. Those who wielded swords or axes with similar reach to his own. Against them, combat became a test of technique, timing, and patience. Every stance, every clash of weapons demanded careful judgment, because one mistake in distance or tempo could decide the outcome.

But fighting a smaller, faster opponent like Vexxa was entirely different. Here, speed, precision, and reaction mattered more than sheer power. He needed to watch for sudden bursts of movement, quick strikes at his blind spots, and feints that could punish him the moment he overcommitted his swing.

Until evening came. By then, Ethan had trained enough for the day, and he had learned quite a lot. Vexxa’s movements were naturally quicker and more agile than his own, and no matter how hard he tried, he still couldn’t fully keep up with her speed alone.

It was time for him to leave.

“I’ll be back, no later than tomorrow,” he said, turning as he headed toward his car.

There was nothing to worry about. Lina was here, and his undead thralls were far from weak.

On top of that, Ethan had the advantage of seeing through their eyes from afar. More importantly, he would still receive experience from their hunts, even while he was away doing something else.

“Fight! Fight! Fight!”

The chants rose and fell, overlapping in chaos, like a thousand beasts howling in the dark.

At the center stood a massive iron-walled ring. The structure was caged tight with thick bars, as if designed to keep monsters inside. Overhead, harsh floodlights poured down, casting long shadows and amplifying every drop of tension.

Two hosts stood elevated on a platform, microphones in hand, their booming voices cutting through the madness.

“Ladies and gentlemen! We are back tonight with something truly extraordinary!” one of them bellowed. “Our undefeated champion is about to be challenged by a newcomer!”

The second host jumped in, laughing almost mockingly. “Oh, don’t they always try? Every single one of them steps into that ring thinking they can take down the champion… and every single one has fallen!”

The screens above the ring flashed with numbers as betting rates updated in real time.

“The stakes are higher than ever! Wagers for the newcomer’s side have skyrocketed, over three million dollars placed already, with seventy percent of the odds favoring him!”

“And the question is… just who is this mysterious newcomer that’s driving the bets so high?!”

“And let’s not forget. Our champion has been on an unbroken winning streak of twenty-nine victories! If he secures this match, he will push his record even further, solidifying his name as one of the greatest fighters this arena has ever seen!”

The crowd erupted again, their chants thunderous, shaking the very steel of the cage.

“Fight! Fight! Fight!”

The thunder of the crowd still shook the arena, but slowly, the chants dulled, a hush rippling through as the spotlight shifted toward the entrance tunnel.

There, a lone figure stood.

He wore blood-red shorts, his long black hair spilling out from beneath a black cap. His stance was solid like a gladiator about to walk into war. Calmly, he adjusted the wraps on his hands, tightening the cloth with precision.

From the side, another man approached, a sharp figure in a crisp black suit, his hair dark but streaked with silver, betraying his age somewhere in his fifties.

“If you win tonight, your debt will be cleared, and you’ll walk free. If not…” the man paused, his gaze narrowing. “You know you’ll have to start it all over again.”

The fighter gave no reply. He simply stepped forward, moving down the tunnel as the iron gates ahead screeched open.

The crowd on either side erupted into madness. He didn’t flinch. Passing them, he broke into a jog, then a run, his stride carrying him into the heart of the storm.

The steel door of the cage swung wide, and he entered the ring. Leaping lightly on his feet, he began to bounce, his body loosening, rolling his shoulders as he warmed up. All around him, the noise rose again.

The host’s voice rose above the frenzy, dragging every eye back to the far side of the arena.

“And now, tonight’s challenger! A so-called newcomer who dares to set foot in this arena! He’s got no record, no reputation, nothing to his name. Yet somehow the crowd thinks he can take on our champion! Give it up for… The Mummy!”

The crowd exploded with laughter and jeers.

“The Mummy?!”

“Send him back to the tomb!”

“Rip him apart, Champ!”

From the opposite tunnel, a towering figure emerged. His body was bulky, his height imposing, every inch of him wrapped in thick cloth bandages that made him look like some ancient warrior dragged back from the grave.

Over his legs, he wore black shorts marked by a bold red strike, the fabric stark against the pale wrappings.

Step by step, he approached, climbing into the ring, the floor quaking beneath his weight.

“The Mummy?” the champion muttered under his breath.

“Damn it!” he spat. “What the hell are you doing here?!”
