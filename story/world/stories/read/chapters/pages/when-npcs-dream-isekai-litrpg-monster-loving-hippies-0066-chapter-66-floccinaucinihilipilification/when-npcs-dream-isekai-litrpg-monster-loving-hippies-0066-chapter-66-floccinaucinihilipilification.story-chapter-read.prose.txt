Another roar. Lori charged Hash, faster and stronger than before. The ringing cling-clash of their blades echoed in my ears; If they weren't using high-quality magical steel, the knives would have shattered instantly.

But Hash’s speed was preternatural—he was a flickering ghost. His mid-air movements were impossible, his tail an invisible whip. Lori kept missing. And as scary as she was, the fact remained: she couldn't hit him.

Small trickles of blood began to appear on Lori’s arms and face. They were shallow cuts, and they didn't seem to slow her down, but the truth was clear: Hash was faster than the demon.

Lori shifted her knife to her left hand and traced a magic circle with her right She rained fire stones upon Hash, but he evaded them with a effortless twist. Another circle—electricity buzzed and hissed, lighting up the dark air, but he dodged that, too.

Of course Lori could use fire and lightning magic; she’d probably seen it what? Once? Her super talent was so unfair!

Hash surged forward, and Lori struggled. More cuts. More blood.

For the first time, it looked like Lori was losing. I looked at Geshem and saw real, deep fear on his face. He probably felt useless, just like me. He couldn’t help her—even if he could ignore his own opponent, Hash and Lori were dancing too fast across the field. Any poison bubble he threw would hit Lori as easily as the agent.

Another clash of knives, but this time, something went wrong. With a loud CRACK, Lori’s blade snapped in half.

Damn it! I should have given her my shadow knife. It was a better blade, and it was being wasted in my shaking hands.

Hash kept coming, slashing and striking. Lori’s wounds were getting deeper. She was starting to move slower. Even I could see it. She was getting tired, demon or not.

Suddenly, Hash stopped. He took to the air, looking solemn.

“I thank you deeply, demon Lori. I will never forget you,” he said. “But this is goodbye.”

He performed a complex swivel in the air, stretching his wings, and then he shot forward like a bullet. He was moving at incredible speed, his tail-blade aimed straight for the kill. This was the finishing blow.

Lori then did something crazy. She jerked her arms aside, leaving her torso completely unprotected.

Hash’s blade struck true, sinking deep into Lori’s stomach. But in that same heartbeat, Lori’s hand flashed out. She caught his tail, hauled it to her mouth, and bit down with everything she had, ripping his tail clean in half.

With her left hand, she ripped the severed tail and the blade out of her own gut, while her right hand, still holding the part of the tail attached to the Agent, slammed him into the ground with incredible force. There was a wet, crunching sound—the sound of a body breaking.

Hash looked up at her, eyes wide. Then he smiled. Not a creepy smile, but for the first time, a pleasant, warm one.

Lori traced a final electricity circle. A bolt of jagged lightning struck the Agent, cooking him to a blackened crisp.
