Callie couldn't believe her eyes. She had to blink several times before she could truly believe that a mireling had wobbled up to her all on its own accord. This never happened!

Everyone in the village knew what a mireling was, but seeing one was actually a rarity. These little arcane beasts were incredibly elusive and shy. They never went anywhere near humans and somehow managed to disappear really quickly after being spotted.

Yet here one was, getting closer and closer to Callie. She put her hands over her mouth to stop herself from squealing with joy and instead watched the creature.

"Hello, little mireling," she said in a barely audible whisper.

The mireling wobbled slightly in response. It was so close that Callie could reach out and touch it. Should she touch it? It could dissolve flowers in its slime in a matter of seconds. Would it even like to be touched? Would her touching it hurt the mireling?

She held back, instead placing her hand on the ground, wondering if the mireling would approach her on its own. It was best to let it do what it wanted, instead of grabbing it without any invitation.

At first, the mireling seemed cautious, wobbling in place. But after a minute or two, it slid closer to Callie's hand. To her surprise, a tiny tendril of slime grew out of its body and reached out to touch her. She barely withheld a flinch as the cool slime touched her skin. It didn't burn her and felt slightly moist, but also firm. Like jelly.

"That's amazing…" Callie watched the slimy tendril move about, poking at her arm, as if examining it.

She turned over her hand, letting the mireling examine her palm. It grew another slimy tendril and poked at her palm. The sensation was slightly ticklish, making Callie chuckle.

But then the mireling grabbed the sides of her hand with its tendril and pulled it upwards. The strength behind the pull was minuscule; it obviously wouldn't be able to lift Callie's hand on its own. But she raised it, wondering what the creature would do.

It pulled her hand closer to its main body until she was basically patting it. Mireling seemed to like her touch, leaning into it.

"Do you like being patted?" Callie wondered, continuing the soothing motion.

The mireling wiggled approvingly. Somehow she could understand, or rather feel, that it was pleased. How? She had no clue.

As she continued patting the creature, Callie noticed the glow coming from the mark on her forearm. Rolling up her sleeve, she saw that it was rather intense at the moment, almost as much as in the morning after her encounter with the dragon.

The mireling noticed the glow and extended the tendril to poke at the mark. It lit up as its touch, and then dimmed again when the tendril retreated.

"You're making it glow? But why? And how?" Callie wondered. Could it be that the mireling felt safe to approach her because she had the dragon's mark, and now it was reacting in turn?

She wanted to understand exactly what was happening, but voices could be heard coming from the village. The mireling hurriedly withdrew its tendril and wobbled away to hide among the flowers. It was surprisingly fast. The moment it was gone, the glow on Callie's arm disappeared almost instantly.

"A mireling can make my mark glow… Would that happen with other arcane beasts, too?" She wondered.

With the mireling gone, Callie quickly realized that the sun was beginning to set. If she didn't get home soon, then she could end up confined to home again. That couldn't happen. She needed to understand what was going on with the mireling.

Covering the forearm again, Callie rushed home, relieved to find that Dad still hadn't returned. Instead, she was greeted by a rather peeved-looking Callen.

"Callie, where were you for so long? I was about to start searching for you," he said, arms crossed over his chest.

"Brother, you won't believe what just happened!" Callie jumped up and down, unable to contain her joy. She hurriedly retold him what happened with the mireling, not leaving him room to speak or object.

"Wait, wait, calm down," Callen raised his hand. "Did you say that you… touched a mireling? And it didn't burn you?"

"No, why would it burn me?" Callie cocked her head in confusion.

At that moment, the door swung open, and Dad returned home. "Hello, kids! What are you two doing standing around here?"

"Callie was just telling me about her lessons with Mister Blackthorn," Callen said.

"Is that so? How did they go?" Dad asked as he went over to the bucket of water they used for washing hands and cleaned off the soot that had settled over his fingers after a day of work.

"It… was fine… I think," Callie said, unsure of what else to say.

"What did you learn today?" Dad asked.

"About what things to bring with you when travelling…" Callie said uncertainly. "I didn't get it right…"

"That's alright. It was only the first lesson after all," Dad said, wiping his hands dry. "Shall I prepare dinner now?"

"Yes, please. I'm hungry," Callen nodded. "By the way, Dad. I have an unrelated question."

"What is it?" Dad arched an eyebrow.

"Mireling slime burns, doesn't it?"

Dad nodded. "Yes. Their slime is made of a highly potent acid. If you ever come across a mireling—don't touch it! It can even eat through gloves with ease. Granted, catching one is a challenge in its own right. Despite being made up entirely of slime, they are very fast."

Callie couldn't help but glance down at her hands. They looked completely fine and not at all burned from acid, just a little moist. How was that possible? She didn't think that Dad was lying. There was no reason to. But how come the mireling slime didn't hurt her? Was it once again because of the dragon's mark, or was it something else entirely?

"Did something happen?" Dad stared at Callie suspiciously.

"I… I just saw a mireling today at the flower meadow. That's all," Callie said. It wasn't even a lie. She just wasn't telling the whole truth either.

"What were you doing at the flower meadow in the first place?" Dad crossed his arms over his chest. Uh-oh! He was getting mad…

"I just didn't want to run into Aurelia on the way home, so I stayed in the flower meadow for a bit to avoid her…" she admitted in a whisper, hoping that Dad wouldn't get mad.

Dad instantly deflated, and Callen's expression grew rather sad. They were looking at her in that pitiful way again.

"I'm going to my room!" Callie announced and ran off before anyone could object.

She hated it when her family looked at her that way. It wasn't like she wanted to become the outcast of the village. It wasn't like she asked the likes of Aurelia and Tristan to bully her. All she wanted was to learn more about arcane beasts and stop the constant killings done by hunters. It just wasn't right, and it made her heart ache when she saw other arcane beasts slain and gutted for meat, pelts, and other materials.

To distract herself she looked at the pages had drawn and wondered if there was a way to improve them. They still looked quite simplistic when compared to Granny Ethel's encyclopedia. Perhaps, she could draw the dragon mark. But Callie quickly dismissed that thought. What if somebody accidentally found these pages?

In fact, Callie decided that she wouldn't keep the page with the dragon together with the others. She took it and, after some internal debate, folded the page into a small rectangle. After that, she pushed it in between the boards of her bedframe, beneath her mattress.

Even if somebody lifted it, they would have to look really hard to spot the hidden piece of paper. Perhaps burning it would have been a better choice. But paper was so precious, and she didn't want to forget the dragon. Who knew if she would even meet it again? It would certainly be better for it to remain hidden.

"Until I can convince everyone that arcane beasts shouldn't be killed at least," Callie muttered firmly to herself. She swore that she would do anything in her power to convince people of that, no matter how much of an outcast that would make her.

Two days passed without much happening. Mom hadn't returned from the hunt, while Callie worked hard with Granny Ethel to prepare herbs and concoctions. Oddly enough, Tristan didn't bother her at all, acting uncharacteristically meek.

Maybe he actually felt bad for hitting her in the nose. It wasn't a serious injury. Nobody at home even noticed that something had been wrong. But it clearly made Tristan think twice before acting. Maybe Granny Ethel told him something to keep him in line.

Callie tried going to the flower meadow again in hopes of finding her mireling friend, but sadly, it didn't show up. Perhaps she had come at the wrong time. She couldn't spend too long in the flower meadow, only about half an hour, before she had to return home and do her share of the chores.

But today was the lesson day with Mister Blackthorn. It was both exciting and horrifying. Aurelia and other village girls were giving her nasty glares the moment she showed up for the lesson. Those eyes promised trouble. Were they mad because they hadn't managed to catch her last time?

Thankfully, with Mister Blackthorn around, they obviously wouldn't dare to start an open confrontation. Callie would have to hide away in the meadow just like last time. Perhaps they would give up in time. At least Callie hoped so…

"Today we're going to learn how to make fire," Mister Blackthorn announced.

"Again?! We've already learned that," Aurelia complained once again.

"Then this lesson will be very easy for you," the man shot back sternly.

Aurelia looked away with a huff, but as soon as the man relinquished his gaze, the girl wasn't shy and glared at Callie. As if she had been the one to decide this. Although she was grateful to the retired hunter for taking his time with her and revisiting old lessons. Maybe he wasn't so bad after all.

"There are multiple ways of lighting a fire, but each one requires something crucial—fuel. Be it dry sticks, old grass, or kindling," Mister Blackthorn began his lecture, showing the girls various types of fuel that needed to be used.

The gist of it was rather simple. You needed something dry that burned easily to start a fire. Once you have a tiny flame, you add firewood to keep the fire going for a longer time. He also explained how to make a secure and safe fire site outdoors.

This was especially important in the forest like theirs, which was filled with coniferous trees rich in resin. This resin burned incredibly well, so a forest fire could quickly turn into a catastrophe. Mister Blackthorn repeated this point at least a dozen times. Callie understood why he was doing it, but still found it a  little annoying.

Once he was done with his safety explanation, he showed how to properly start a fire with flint and steel. Callie had seen Callen do this several times, although at home it was far more common for Dad to start a fire with his magic. It was quick and easy.

Flint and steel seemed hard. Callie and the girls were also given a turn to try. It took ages before Callie managed to get a spark and set the dry grass on fire. Unfortunately, that was only the beginning of her troubles for the day.

"There might be an occasion when you might not have flint and steel on hand to start a fire. That is why it is crucial to learn how to start a fire with a bow drill and how to make one in the first place," Mister Blackthorn said and then demonstrated the method.

He showed how to make the bow drill using sticks and twine, how to prepare a fireboard, which apparently required soft and dry wood, and how, by using friction, one could start a fire. It looked really easy when he did it. Two minutes of intensive rubbing with the bow drill, and a bit of blowing to get the little sparks to ignite, and then he got a fire.

Callie was fully convinced that she could do it. But as soon as she got everything in her hand, it became painfully clear that this wasn't easy. She rubbed and drilled and moved the bow drill as quickly as her hands could, but she never saw any smoke or coal or anything resembling a fire. It remained the same plank of wood.

The other girls were doing much better. Aurelia was the first to start a fire, which took her a total of fifteen minutes. Other girls took over twenty minutes but still managed to do it. Mister Blackthorn allowed them to go home as soon as they did. But no matter how hard Callie worked, she couldn't do it. More than forty minutes passed, and she had yet to see anything resembling a spark.

"Calluna, you can't stop to rest. You must keep drilling with the bow if you wish to start a fire," the man said sternly, clearly annoyed by her lack of progress.

"I-I'm trying…" Callie squeaked out. "My hands hurt…"

The man came over to take a look at her hands. There were two small blisters forming on them already. Surely that was a sign that Callie should stop for the day.

Mister Blackthorn's eyes hardened. "You can keep going. In the wild nobody will care whether your hands hurt. You will have to be able to start a fire or die from hunger and cold."

Callie wanted to protest. It wasn't fair! This was her first time attempting to make a fire. She… She needed more time. But the cold gaze of the retired hunter left no room for argument. All Callie could do was keep trying to get a fire going.

But it was painfully obvious that she didn't possess the hand strength necessary for the task. Not to mention that she was getting more and more tired, and her hands hurt more each time she tried.

Mister Blackthorn wasn't even looking at her anymore, instead having turned his attention to sharpening a sword with a whetstone. Would he really just keep Callie here until midnight? Would she have to wait until Dad came looking for her and then see his disappointment that she couldn't complete the task given to her?

Stupid piece of tree!

Callie glared at the plank with all her might and whispered angrily. "Burn already!"

All of a sudden, she felt a hot surge of energy within her. She blinked in confusion as she saw the mark on her forearm light up. A moment later, a little flame appeared on the plank.
