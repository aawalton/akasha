Alright, it wasn’t exactly a fair fight when we’d already created a weak spot on the dragon’s neck. In my defence, it was also a Gold-tier monster.

Together, though, our attributes probably stacked up to something similar to it. Perhaps even higher, considering it wasn’t the strongest Gold out there.

The point was, I wouldn’t let myself get cocky. This was a dragon, not a skeleton that we could kill in our sleep.

“Brawl, you’re bait. We’re going to need to bring its head low. That arrow should be in a deadly spot, right, Anchor? We just need to push it in a little more.”

“I don’t miss anymore, Sarge.”

“Perfect. I’ll give it a bash with my shield, finish off the hole, then put my spear in it for good measure. I’ll only be able to hit it hard enough if I can launch myself perfectly horizontal. Up at an angle is never as powerful.” I paused, still backing up as the dragon approached us. “Anchor, hit that spot as many times as you can.”

He nocked another arrow. “They won’t penetrate, as we’ve seen.”

“No, but they’ll give me other options in case I miss the first one. Now—”

The dragon wasn’t kind enough to let us finish planning. It opened its mouth and blasted a column of air and crystal dust at us. Remembering what had happened last time, I didn’t even try to block it.

“Split!” I shouted, then rolled to the side. “Sage, you stay with Brawl and keep him alive! I’m with Anchor!”

The wave of wind and crystal dust rolled between our two pairs, scoring the ground but not harming anyone. Since Brawl rushed toward it, the dragon focused on him first, and it redirected its wave of dust.

Though Brawl wasn’t the most nimble, he had a shield. He ducked to the side as quickly as he could, dodging the wave of debris, and protected himself from the rest with his shield. The dragon’s breath scored the edge, ripping away the wood and regular steel frame. It spared him from a single hit, and that was it.

Hopefully, though, this was the last time we’d use basic shields. Soon, we’d have our own much more durable shields.

The dragon cut off its stream of wind-breath, then slammed a foot down toward Brawl. He jumped back, though a claw raked across his chest, ignoring his armour entirely. Sage healed him in a matter of seconds, and he rolled back to his feet, flicking his flail. At the same time, Anchor fired more enhanced shots directly at the beast’s neck.

None of the arrows penetrated the weak spot, and they all landed within an inch of each other.

I stayed in my stance, right beside Anchor, ready to rush in and attack at a moment’s notice.

It attacked with its claws. Like a cat swiping at a snake, it preferred to use quick swipes of its front legs. Its head stayed high up in the air, and it didn’t concern itself with its weak patch, no matter how many arrows lodged in it. They didn’t sink in all the way.

If we did nothing, the dragon’s tactics would work, and it wouldn’t have expended itself at all.

Chasing Brawl around the edge of the plaza, it swiped at him. He dodged a few of the strikes, but he wasn’t fast enough to stop all of them. Sage used Morph to create shields, which slowed the dragon’s attacks enough, but not nearly enough to stop all of them. Reaching up, the dragon poised itself to land a deadly blow. Brawl was still stumbling, and he was still bleeding from a gash on his chest, which Sage worked to repair.

Before the claw could land, I intercepted, slamming myself into the side of the dragon’s arm with the help of my Art. I struck with enough force to deflect the swipe, but I put myself in the way of another swipe from the dragon’s second arm.

I wasn’t as durable as Brawl.

As I crouched down, preparing to dodge, Brawl activated his Art, venting the stored up energy throughout the fight. The pulse bought me enough time to escape without risk. I rolled back, then positioned myself beside Brawl. “It doesn’t fight with its mouth. It’s not bending down.”

“It is a predator,” Sage said. “It eats with its mouth.”

“Hey, there are plenty of predators that fight with their mouths…”

“Well, not this one.”

“Actually, there’s an idea,” I exclaimed. “Brawl, play dead. Sage, back up with me.”

His eyes lit up with understanding, and Sage nodded. Falling limp, Brawl splayed his arms out to the side. I wasn’t sure how this fit into his code of honour, but he didn’t protest.

Maybe he realized the need to get out of here quickly just the same as I did, and it wasn’t worth it.

“What the hell are you doing?” Anchor yelled.

“Be quiet!” I yelled back.

Anchor’s eyes widened, then he dipped his head in understanding.

The dragon's senses were probably strong enough to detect a heartbeat, but that didn’t concern me much. As long as Brawl seemed mostly dead, this should work. It would still eat partially alive prey

The dragon approached. I circled to the side, getting into position. The last thing I wanted was to let the dragon sink its teeth into Brawl and actually eat him.

I held my shield up, then plotted a trajectory that would take me perfectly horizontally. Then, I settled into a runner’s start.

The dragon sniffed, then bent its head down. Its jaw opened, and the hairs inside its mouth dangled, threateningly close to Brawl. Their tips sparked with orange energy, like live wires. I figured they were actually for integrating new material into the dragon’s body, so the Keepers would see it as part of itself. Anything inorganic on Brawl would become part of the dragon.

It wasn’t going to take him.

The moment I had a horizontal trajectory, I sprinted forward and pulled myself toward my target with my Art. My feet lifted up off the ground after a few steps, and I fell sideways. It happened in a blink, and all I could do was hold my shield up.

I slammed the arrows into the side of the beast’s neck as hard as I could. My arm buckled, and I collided with the beast’s neck, deflecting it to the side and launching it away from Brawl. Its jaw snapped shut, closing on empty air.

Some of the arrows snapped, but most penetrated in. They didn’t go deep, but black blood still welled up from the holes. For good measure, I used my spear with its resonance Skill, driving it in as hard as I could, following the route of one of Anchor’s arrow holes.

The dragon stumbled. It gave a breathy roar, and its legs trembled, before finally, it fell to the side. It created a shockwave of dust, then fell still.

While Sage ran back to finish tending to Brawl, I gave my spear a whirl, then turned to Anchor and said, “Any idea where its core would be?”

“We’ll have to go digging for that,” he said.

“We don’t exactly have the strength to cut its flesh,” I replied.

“You’ll have to use your siphon in the exact right place, Sarge. Melt away some of the armour. Uh…Sage, where is that core?”

“It will be closer to the beast’s navel. Above its hips,” Sage replied. “Once a regular Path-walker reaches Gold tier, he’ll have a physical core, and the same goes for monsters like this. It’s always in the same place.”

“And that core is the thing we need?”

“Indeed. It likely embedded the right crystal in its own body when it reached the right stage, then used that as a foundation to form its core off of. It will have chunks of crystal and chunks of shaped maegen.”

I shivered with disgust, but we didn’t have time to get squeamish. Holding out the siphon, I said, “Anchor, get some blood from its neck. We’ll trigger the siphon with that. Sage, once you’re done with Brawl, make sure you gather up enough of the hairs from its mouth for plumes.”

I navigated down the length of the dragon’s body until I reached its hind hips, then backtracked a few steps until I reached its navel. I pressed the siphon up against its body. When Anchor smeared a handful of the dragon’s blood on the siphon’s base, it activated, drawing energy through the runes and creating a sphere of misty orange Presence at the top of the siphon.

As soon as the Presence formed, I handed it to Anchor. “You should take it. Your arrows made the kill.”

“You will need the Presence more than I.”

“I’ve got plenty of Presences waiting for me at Hyrdford.”

“That’s no guarantee. Take it, Sarge. You are going to need it.”

“If you insist.” I crushed the Presence in my grip. The orange Maegen swirled around my fingers, but it didn’t soak into my flesh. After a few seconds of tingling, my body rejected it, and it swirled outward as a puff of orange sparks.

Better yet, siphoning the beast’s maegen had disintegrated a chunk of its armour right in front of the core.

We dug around for a few minutes, cutting through the beast’s weaker inner flesh, until finally, we found a sphere of glowing amber crystal. A perfect sphere, the size of a bowling ball, marbled with a few red and white streaks. Even when I wiped off the dragon’s black blood, it was the most slippery thing I’d ever held.

I brought it back to Sage and Brawl. Between cutting the hairs loose from its mouth, they’d also scooped up another few jars of Labyrinth Keepers.

“This has to be the core, right?” I asked, holding it out to them.

“That’s it,” Sage said with a nod.

“Excellent. Let’s grab some extra crystal, then get out of here.”

When we reached the surface, the ground was vibrating.

It wasn’t an earthquake, and I probably wouldn’t have noticed if not for my Perception. But thousands of tiny footsteps were approaching the castle, making the ground rumble.

Before running to the stables, I raced up to the wall’s ramparts. Sunlight began to leak over the eastern horizon, illuminating the hills and fields around the castle in pale pink light.

I narrowed my eyes. The horizon itself was fuzzy, and it rippled. Humanoid creatures charged along the ground, rolling over the horizon and swarming toward the castle like a plague of zombies.

Orcs were approaching.

Lord Ceonwin was right. I wanted nothing to do with that, not right now. Even if most of them were Irons, they would overwhelm us soon.

I jumped down to the castle grounds, ignoring the stairs, and ran back to the stable with my squad. “We have to leave. Now.”
