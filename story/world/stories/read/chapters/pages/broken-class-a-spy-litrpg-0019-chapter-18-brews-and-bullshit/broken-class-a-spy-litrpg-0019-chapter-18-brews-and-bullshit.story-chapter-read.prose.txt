The tavern was loud, hot, and thick with mana-drenched smoke. It spilled from enchanted pipes and spelled hookahs that turned flame-colored mist into shapes - tigers, wolves, a girl dancing in midair with eight arms and far too few clothes. Somewhere in the back, a bard was butchering a noble ballad with all the grace of a drunk war golem, and no one cared.

This was The Gilded Fang, the capital’s finest hole in the wall and the unofficial headquarters of every bored noble brat and off-duty Guardian within city walls.

The Great Hunt had begun, and that meant the real sport was on: gambling.

Not the kind done in royal arenas or sanctioned duels. This was darker, faster, dumber. Bets flew over which faction would rack up more kills, how fast they’d do it, who’d die first, and who’d chicken out.

Wagers clinked across tables in enchanted coins, heirloom rings, house crests, and favors no one would admit to out loud.

At the center of it all stood a mana-crystal display, etched with glowing runes and enchanted to show the live leaderboard. Everyone stared at it like it was the sun itself. A single name burned at the top:

Arthur – 84 kills

“Eighty-four?” gasped one of the twins from House Lurellia, her third drink forgotten mid-sip. “Already?”

“That’s gotta be a glitch,” muttered a Guardian captain, frowning into his mug. “Solo kills? This many, this early?”

“Glitch my ass,” snorted a lean, green-robed archer lounging against the bar. “It updated again - eighty-five now.”

More gasps. A few chuckles. One guy fell out of his chair and took a hookah stand with him.

“It’s not possible,” said a noble from House Valcraith, eyes narrowed. “Even Rowler only managed three by now and he has a team of five.”

“Maybe it’s a joke?” someone offered. “A prank from the System? Has that ever happened before?”

“No,” said a pale Guardian with a scar running down his lip. “And if the System’s joking now, we’re all screwed.”

At the back of the tavern, cloaked figures whispered. A tall woman with gold-threaded gloves tapped her nails against the table, mana curling up her fingers. She didn’t speak, but she didn’t need to. Half the tavern was already asking the same question:

Who the hell is Arthur?

No crest. No team. No background. Just a name, rising on the leaderboard like it had something to prove.

Some thought he was a prodigy from a mythical A-grade world. One drunken soul swore he’d seen Arthur training under a dragon last year, which prompted his friends to immediately order a truth potion and make him swear it again.

A Guardian raised his mug in a new toast: “To Arthur the Monster Slayer, may his enemies die confused!”

The whole tavern roared in approval.

Then, as if on cue, the leaderboard updated again.

Arthur – 86 kills

Chairs scraped. Drinks spilled. Bets were made and unmade in seconds.

Someone started a tally board in the corner labeled “Arthur’s Body Count” scrawled in blood-red chalk. Someone else offered odds on whether Arthur was even human. A few joked he was a chimera in disguise. One older mage half-believed it.

“I daresay,” he muttered, “no one racks up those numbers unless they’re swinging a family heirloom forged by celestial fire and bathed in dragon blood. Likely the boy’s never fought a real fight without a safety net woven from gold.”

“Maybe he’s cheating.”

“Cheating the System?” The table laughed so hard a drink shot out someone’s nose.

Another noble muttered, “Well, if he’s this fast now, he’ll be the first to hit four digits.”

More cheers. More gold thrown down.

In the corner, the barkeep raised an eyebrow, muttered something about doubling ale prices, and quietly ordered his assistant to add an Arthur Special to the menu. No one knew what it was yet, but they all agreed: it would probably taste like flaming victory and existential dread.

Thus spread Arthur’s legend - louder, bolder, and more ridiculous with every retelling.

Some claimed he fought barehanded to keep it “sporting.”

Others swore he was half-beast himself, raised by chimeras, fluent in monster growls.

One particularly drunk noble insisted Arthur tamed a wyvern just to ride it between kills, dismounting mid-air to crush his enemies with a boot to the skull.

“He is not slaying them,” one noble muttered. “They’re just dying out of respect.”

Another swore he flexed so hard, three monsters died of emotional damage.

There was even a hushed rumor that he didn’t use weapons at all. Just stared monsters into submission with eyes so cold they gave frostbite.

And by the time dessert arrived, someone had bet twenty gold that Arthur was a runaway prince with a divine quest and a cursed ring.

Nobody had facts. Nobody cared.

But everyone agreed on one thing:

Arthur was real.Arthur was solo.And Arthur was winning.
