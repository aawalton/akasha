Barry sat in the chieftain's hall with both hands on the prayer crystal.

The next prayer surfaced: Make my mate love me more oh god, She's sleeping with other men.

"Eesshhh, pathetic." he muttered, and shoved it to the bottom of the pile.

He sorted the way he always did. Near-death cases first, clean water second, the sick-but-not-dying third.

For now, he ran the people through Bela. The whole city, or town, or whatever this place was, had hauled its almost-dying relatives into the hall.

The lower house was warmer than it had been in years. Several of the sick were already on their feet, talking and laughing instead of lying there coughing. Out in the streets, the constant cursing and muttering had gone quiet. For the first time since he'd started, Barry saw something that looked like balance: a kid in the corner, eating, actually smiling.

Bela moved between the warm crystals that scanned and slowly healed. She gave short orders, pulled the feverish ones away from the rest, and stared down anyone who looked ready to argue. Nobody did. Not after watching what the crystals could do, one miracle after another.

Hardly anyone noticed the frost giantess come through the main doors. Snow still clung to her hair and furs. She was taller than most giants, yet she moved as if she had no presence at all. Without anyone seeing, she went straight to an empty chair beside one of the crystals and sat down to check herself over.

The crystal chimed. A flat, robotic voice said, "Host is healthy and full of energy. No analysis checkup needed." Then it drifted away.

The data reached Barry a moment later, and he knew exactly who the giantess was.

She plucked the crystal out of the air, pulled it close, and ran her magic through it.

"Aren't these supposed to convert faith?" she murmured.

She dug deeper and confirmed it. But this one didn't just take faith the way it should. It took her name, her origin, her mood, everything about the person holding it. And it was layered with advanced spells, stacked on top of one another.

That should be impossible, she thought.

Barry kept his eyes on her. Across the hall, Bela had noticed too, and her hand was drifting toward her weapon.

The giantess didn't spare Bela a glance. Her attention stayed locked on Barry while Bela closed in, so he did the only thing he could and sent a message to Bela's earpiece.

"Let her be."

His whisper reached her, and she stopped short.

The giantess crossed the hall and stood in front of him.

"You fed them and warmed them. A strange way to conquer. You should have made them kneel and put them to use." She conjured a drink out of thin air. "What happens when they're fed and healed and come for you? Do you think they'll be grateful? They won't. They'll take more, and more, until their greed destroys them."

Barry didn't look up. "Ah, a sermon on sin. How holy of you. What's next, slavery? Soul-binding? Devilish contracts? You're really going for the full god package. Mischief, thievery, every synonym for stealing."

The giantess smiled gently. She had no intention of denying it. "Clever. How did you work it out? The drink?" She finished it off.

Barry tapped the crystal in his hands, then pointed at the one she was holding. "You let yourself get scanned. That was your first mistake."

She studied the crystal for a moment. "I'll sit here and watch one full round of your work. In return, I'll tell you something true about Asgard. Something that changes how long you're stuck in this mud pit."

Barry paused for half a second, hands still moving. "Talk first. I'll decide afterward whether you're any use to me here."

The crystal began to glow in Loki's hands as she spoke. "Odin's body is turning to living gold. Faster than any of us expected." She watched his face for a reaction.

"He told Thor to take the throne now and leave his favorite new tool behind in Jotunheim." Still nothing.

"And my dear old mother is stalling him for the moment. With this gap, you and I could take over."

Barry's jaw tightened, once. He turned back to the crystal without looking at her. "Good to know. Not useful. Thanks, goodbye."

He watched her stare at him for a full second, feverish with confusion. From her side of things, she had just handed him a secret big enough to knock stronger people flat. Barry didn't care. His hands didn't slow down.

"I see you're not interested. May I at least stay and watch?"

"Bela. See our guest out."

Bela wanted to. Her foot started forward, then stopped. She wasn't stupid, and she knew exactly who was standing in front of her. But after a few seconds of hesitation, she made herself walk toward the giantess anyway.

Loki looked at her. "Don't you dare."

Bela froze. "Okay. I won't."

Barry glanced over, disappointed. "What do you think she can do here? Kill you? Burn down a place made of ice? She's a goddess. Nothing more, nothing less."

Bela swallowed. "This is beyond me, Barry. I'm just a mortal. Can I go back to leading my people?"

He stared at her. "Fine. But I don't want to hear the 'I'm just a mortal' line again. Go."

Thunder rolled overhead.

Loki flinched. Barry smiled without looking up. "It's just thunder. You're a god. Why jump?"

"It's not the thunder. It's what follows it."

Outside, someone had arrived. Thunder cracked across the streets, and a huge god with a hammer in his fist came striding through them.

Thor. He'd forced his way through, and Mjolnir still sparked in his hand. He looked like a man who had just told his father no and wasn't sure he wouldn't regret it.

He sensed Barry almost immediately. He swung the hammer, let it haul him into the air, and landed right on the balcony.

One glance took in the room. Barry hunched over the crystal, sweat on his neck despite the cold. A strange giantess sitting a little too comfortably nearby, and for some reason looking away from him.

"Barry! I have come to save you, my friend!" Thor stepped inside.

"Thought you'd be busy ruling Asgard," Barry said.

"I refused."

Barry just stared. Part of him had wanted Thor on that throne. Retirement would have been so much cleaner. Instead, the god was standing in his hall.

"Do not look at me like that," Thor said. "He called you an instrument and left you alone here." He set Mjolnir's head against the stone.

The moment Thor came close, the crystal reacted. It flared, overheated, and started pulling in faith again, harder and faster than before.

"What in the hell," Barry muttered.

He walked out onto the balcony. Just as he'd expected, the people below were already bowing and praying toward the new arrival. He turned back to Thor.

"Your presence is wrecking my work."

"What do you mean?"

Another wave of prayers slammed into the crystal. It went white-hot, and Barry's vision went white with it, his focus shattering.

When the light faded, most of the careful code he'd built into the crystal, the triage filter and the healing limiter, was gone, overwritten by raw prayer.

On the floor below, two of the people who'd been walking and laughing earlier were already sinking back down, coughing. A third had gone still.

Barry's hands shook once before he forced them steady.

"Okay. Sit."

Thor looked at him, unsure whether the order was meant for him.

"Yes, you. Sit."

Thor sat.

Barry spent the next hour walking him through the prayer list, the priority order, the exact way the crystal had to be handled so it wouldn't eat itself. Thor stayed perfectly still and nodded in all the right places. He didn't care. Barry knew it. Loki knew it. Even Bela, watching from the doorway, knew it.

Barry looked at the three of them and understood the new reality. The one god who might have kept Asgard busy was sitting in his hall, pretending to listen.

He walked back to the balcony and stared at the sky.

"You couldn't do one thing right, old man," he said. "You couldn't even keep your son focused on your damn throne."
