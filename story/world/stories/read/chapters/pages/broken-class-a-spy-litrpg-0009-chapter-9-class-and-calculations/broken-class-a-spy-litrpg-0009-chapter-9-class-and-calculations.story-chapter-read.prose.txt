Arthur hadn’t slept.

He couldn’t, not with the System still burning questions into his skull and a dead Mana Nexus Beast cooling beside him like a divine corpse.

He sat in the moss-lit cavern, eyes open, mind spinning, as Drogan the Skullbreaker continued his unsolicited commentary from inside Arthur’s head.

“So,” the voice boomed, “you’ve got a Legendary Class, Infinite Wisdom, a mana pool I’d kill for - and you’re still sitting on your bony ass.”

Arthur didn’t respond right away. He was thinking. Not just about the ridiculous situation he’d fallen into, but about Drogan. The so-called mentor was undoubtedly knowledgeable. But he was also a condescending, overconfident meathead. Arthur had met enough egotists in his former life to know: you didn’t argue with them. You manipulated them. Feed the ego. Appear weak. Let them talk.

That’s how you glean information.

“I’m… still figuring things out,” Arthur said finally. “There’s a monster right outside the cavern that looks like it eats people like me for breakfast. Not exactly eager to fight that right now.”

“Coward,” Drogan spat. “You think strength comes from waiting in caves? Just go out there and punch it to death. What’s so hard about that?”

Arthur blinked. “The punching part, mostly.”

Drogan groaned like a disappointed drill sergeant. “Fine. I forgot I’m dealing with a newborn. Let’s start from the beginning. Think: ‘Status.’”

Arthur did. And a panel blinked into his vision:

---

Name: Arthur

Race: Human

Rank: F

Level: 1

Class: Mana Lord (Legendary)

Title: Infinite Wisdom

Health: 500

Mana: 180

Stats:

Strength: 3

Agility: 4

Dexterity: 4

Perception: 5

Endurance: 2

Intelligence: 18

Constitution: 50

Wisdom: MAX

Arthur scanned the numbers. “That’s… a lot of low stats.”

“Well, except for that Constitution. That 50 is the only reason you’re not in a coma. The System had to brute-force your body to keep you from melting under corruption. You’re a walking bunker with no guns.”

Arthur frowned. “So how do I improve these? Do I get points or something as I level up?”

Drogan scoffed. “This isn’t some toddler-tier game where the System showers you with stats every time you ding. It doesn’t hand out power for free, it tracks what you’ve earned.”

His voice pulsed with amusement in Arthur’s mind.

“Now, if you do something ridiculous - like, say, kill a legendary mana beast before you’ve even unlocked the System? Then yeah, it'll reward you. That’s why you got Infinite Wisdom. Not because you leveled. But because you did something damn near impossible.”

A beat.

“For everything else? You want Strength? Lift rocks. Punch trees. Kill things. Want Endurance? Run. Fight. Bleed. Want Agility? Move faster. Dodge harder. There are no shortcuts, only sweat.”

Arthur sighed. “Figures.”

Drogan's tone sharpened. “Your Intelligence lets you hold more mana. That 18 means you can hold 180 units. Most F-ranks start with half that. And your Wisdom is off the charts. Which is why you even have a chance with the class you got.”

Arthur hesitated, then asked, “What does the Mana Lord class actually do?”

“Legendary Class. Focused entirely on mana control. And not just spells, but raw manipulation. You’ll be able to do things that make archmages piss themselves… eventually.”

“Eventually?”

“At Rank F, you’re restricted to internal control only,” Drogan explained. “Body-based manipulation. You want to light a torch with your mind or make someone’s head explode with a thought? Climb the Ranks. With effort.”

Arthur nodded, absorbing it all. “And the Ranks go?”

“F, E, D, C, B, A. Then there’s Mythical S, if you believe in bedtime stories. You’ll need EXP to level up, and enough levels to Rank up. Which, by the way, requires killing things.”

Arthur studied his screen again. “And I’m F-rank, Level 1.”

“The bottom of the bottom,” Drogan said cheerfully. “But you’ve got potential.”

Arthur’s voice hardened. “Potential doesn’t keep me alive.”

“No, but killing monsters does. Which brings us back to my very helpful suggestion: get off your ass and go kill that chimera outside.”

Arthur raised an eyebrow. “Before I do something suicidal… what about the ring?”

“What about it?”

Arthur glanced at the silver band on his finger. “How does it work?”

Drogan huffed. “Ah yes, the spatial artifact. Soul-bound, Legendary-grade. Stores both living and non-living matter in stasis - no decay, no aging, no mana bleed. Perfect for hauling around food, tools, or in your case, high-value eggs.”

Arthur crouched beside the glowing eggs. “How do I use it?”

“Just think it. Visualize the object, will it into the ring, and poof - it vanishes. Same for pulling it out. Simple stuff, even for someone with your… cognitive disadvantages.”

Arthur extended a hand toward one egg and concentrated. Store.

The runes on the ring flared.

In a blink, the egg disappeared.

“Huh.”

“You actually did it,” Drogan said. “Color me surprised.”

Arthur repeated the process for the second egg. It too vanished into the ring’s stasis.

“Let’s see if it comes back…”

He focused on retrieving one and it reappeared without fuss. He sent it back in.

“Useful,” he muttered. “Very useful.”

“Legendary artifact,” Drogan said smugly. “Not that you earned it.”

Arthur stood slowly and turned toward the cavern mouth. The wind whispered across the rocks. His stomach twisted.

He was afraid. No point denying it.

But he was also out of options.

“…Fine,” Arthur muttered. “Let’s go hunting.”

Drogan cackled. “Now that’s the spirit!”

And so Arthur walked forward - into danger, into uncertainty, and whether he liked it or not, into the game that had already begun.
