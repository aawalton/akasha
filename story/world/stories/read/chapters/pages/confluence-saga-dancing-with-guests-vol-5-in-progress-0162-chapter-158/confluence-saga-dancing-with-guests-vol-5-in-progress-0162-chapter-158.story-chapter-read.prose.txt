The Silver Bladesinger.

Once upon a time the name meant something. Combat Pathers would find excuses to travel to other Ponds at the mere mention of a sighting of this warrior. Once entire battlefields found themselves without battle soon after his arrival.

Little is now remembered beyond the name and evidence of his existence. Records do not explain why battles ended, why warriors avoided him, only that these events occurred. Those Awakened who might have crossed his path, who might be able to answer directly refuse to. Those who are old enough to have heard the tales firsthand will not elaborate.

We know he existed, we know he was extremely dangerous. We know he fought against Awakened Humanity, but not why. Lord Percivus has become a myth forgotten by living legends.

"Did you know about her cousin?"

Fay sighed. Of course Sandra would ask that now that they were back in their guest quarters. She'd known the detail would be obvious the second anyone took a moment to think about it but she'd hoped it would be in the morning. She'd wanted to have a chance to explain to Briana why she hadn't said more than that Anya and Seb had made friends in private.

"You'd mentioned they made friends, I'm guessing that was them?" Briana added.

"Yes," she said. "I met them on the first visit, got to know them at least slightly. I knew she and Gwen were fairly certain they were cousins but didn't feel it was my place to say anything."

"I suppose that makes sense," Briana said, her tone nearly as cold as when she dealt with problematic customers.

"You could have told us," Sandra argued. "She finds out she has cousins, she meets people who look like her, so people who might have known her father and you don't bother telling anyone? They're weird, different."

"They are different, yes. And you found out right after arriving. Which means you got a chance to say hello to your sister before you started asking her all the questions I know you would have if you'd had six months to think about it."

"I wouldn't have- Fine. I would have asked questions. I will ask questions, tomorrow, or the next day."

Fay smiled. Sandra hadn't been nearly so bad to explain things to. The look on her girlfriend's face made her suspect their conversation would take a bit longer.

"Goodnight Sandra," she said.

"It's not that late."

"No, but we are heading to our room."

She glanced at Briana, trying to smile playfully and getting only a tight-lipped grin in return. It could have been worse she supposed.

"Goodnight," Sandra said before heading to her room and shutting the door.

"I wouldn't have told her," Briana said, turning towards their room.

"I know."

"You didn't tell me. You didn't even hint at it."

"I couldn't find a way to, not without saying too much."

"Too much?" Briana said, shutting the door behind them. "All you had to say was 'Briana, I met Anya's cousin, well one of her cousins, the other doesn't like her.'"

"You didn't meet Bern, how do you..?"

"Bern. Gwen's brother?"

"Her twin brother," Fay clarified.

"His voice carries, his tone carries. And he didn't even try to meet us."

"It does," she agreed. "I don't know the details there. Only that he has a problem with who Anya's mother is."

"So they know about Holding Lys?"

"No," Fay sighed, realizing she'd said more than she should have. "More that they don't know. That it makes Anya…"

She paused, the easy word, to call Anya other, to call her mixed, to call her anything would raise questions. But this was Bri, she couldn't lie to her.

"It makes her what? From the wrong family? The wrong Pond?" Bri asked.

"Yes."

"So he's pretentious too. I'm guessing Gwen doesn't mind."

"She doesn't. Everything I've heard is she gets along well with Anya."

"She was all but standing in their shadow, following right behind them."

"We're staying here because Gwen and Cara are using their spare room. Probably would have made Sandra sleep on the floor otherwise."

"How do you..? Band message?"

She realized she could have shared the information with Bri the same way, that it would have taken only a few extra moments, moments that might have made this conversation easier. She hadn't, she never thought to use the band that way, to switch to it simply to explain something when others were there.

"Very brief," she said. "Seb connected just long enough to let me know, when Sandra didn't want to stay alone."

She sat on the bed, Bri settling beside her almost immediately. Leaning against her she sighed.

"I wanted to tell you, I didn't want to keep her cousin a secret."

"Why then?"

"Seer knowledge and rules are involved. The whole situation involves those restrictions."

"I'm past the Wall, we all are except for Sandra."

"This isn't like that, I wish it was. Hopefully we'll be able to explain before we leave. At least a bit of it."

She felt Bri tense then push her away so they could lock eyes.

"The secrets Lord Percivus mentioned? The precedent?"

"Yes."

"Who is he?"

"Someone old enough that I doubt many people know his name."

"How old?"

"Best guess from the bits of information that I know of? That let me recognize the name? Easily over a thousand years."

"I didn't know anyone lived that long, not and still look anything other than ancient."

"Not many do," Fay said. "Him being here is stranger than Anya meeting her cousins."

"Why is he here? Do you know?"

"He's mentoring Seb, and now Anya as well. He's why they've been exhausted every time Alex and I meet with them."

"For Seb? I would have thought if he's connected to the cousins he'd be here for Anya."

"Seb's Path has rare benefits. Extremely rare benefits. Lord Percivus is, from the few accounts we have of his past, an expert in those benefits."

"I'm not happy," Bri said.

"I know, I…"

"You had to. I understand that."

Fay nodded, closing her eyes. She hadn't had a choice, if she'd told Bri about the Fae, if she'd shared that knowledge and anyone found out, the consequences would be significant. She wasn't exactly sure what they would be, but at the very least she doubted she'd see her family for a long time.

"I'm sorry," she whispered.

She felt Bri's lips press against her forehead, felt her arms wrap around her and pull her close. She leaned in, not opening her eyes, not wanting to see any pain in Briana's.

"You could have told me there were details you couldn't share."

"If I had you would have wondered, you would have tried to figure it out."

"I wouldn't have been surprised. I wouldn't have started wondering why you'd kept that detail from me."

"It isn't that simple."

She heard Bri laugh, no pain or anger in the sound.

"You're right, it isn't. This situation isn't fair to either of us."

"As long as Lord Percivus doesn't say no, I'll explain everything I can as soon as I can."

"And you'll find a way to apologize to your girlfriend."

"And I'll-"

Fay paused, her eyes snapping open to see Bri's looking into them from close enough their noses were almost touching. She blushed before smiling.

"I think I know a way."

"I know you do," Bri said.

Fay felt herself relax into the moment. Bri understood. She might not be happy with the revelation, she might be bothered by the fact that the situation existed, but she understood. They were together, they were alone. That was all that mattered for tonight.
