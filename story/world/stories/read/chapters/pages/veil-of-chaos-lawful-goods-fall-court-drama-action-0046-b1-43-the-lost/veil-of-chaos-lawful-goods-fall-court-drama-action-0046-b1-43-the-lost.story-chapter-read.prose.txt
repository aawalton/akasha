Marquess Aldric adjusted his collar for the third time in as many minutes, the silk suddenly feeling too tight around his throat despite being perfectly tailored.

The private chamber assigned to him during palace functions was comfortable enough—mahogany paneling, plush carpets, a well-stocked wine cabinet—but tonight it felt like a cage. The sudden change in venue and urgency was not to his liking, especially given how visually noticeable his business partner was and the reputational implications.

The boy will be here any moment, he told himself, feeling his plump belly tighten at the thought. It’s just a simple trade. Nothing illegal about trading family heirlooms. Nothing at all. It isn’t as if my grandfather or my grandfather’s grandfather knows its purpose.

His fingers found the amulet beneath his tunic, tracing its familiar shape. The protection against mental influence had been his grandmother’s, passed down through three generations of Aldrics who’d learned to navigate the treacherous waters of noble politics.

His great-grandfather was a hero who actually sealed a great deviless. It wasn’t just succubi it worked against either; it was of purple, epic quality, working on even the denizens of the Darkvein. It had indeed saved him from more than one Bard’s charms and more than one siren’s song. The king dealt in his pure, idealistic law, but someone had to deal with the darkness.

Someone practical. Someone willing to do what the crown politely pretended not to see.

Aldric liked to imagine that made him important. Necessary.

Such as walking that line of having your daughter spit in your face and raise a damn Nightcrawler as a prince. As both a man of pure law and goodness, the king relied on men like him, who had the political pull, cover, and resources to hire the kind of people to make such…problems go away.

A quiet guardian of the shadows, the crown refused to sully its hands with.

That was Aldric’s place in this kingdom, he’d decided.

He reached over to roll around his glass of wine, trying to purge the jitters running down his spine. He told himself these things each night, yet recently, the king’s blunders had made him have to step out of even his comfort zone. Aldric needed insurance. Greater than he’d already acquired, and that was saying a lot.

Though I doubt a sun elf child reincarnate needs such mental tricks. Still, two sun elves in the palace when typically, it’s lucky to hear about one visiting a nearby kingdom in your entire life? At least the female one was drawing all the attention… Gods, why couldn’t it have been her to get in contact with me? I would have rather dealt with a woman.

He felt the little spark again—embarrassing, adolescent excitement fluttering in his belly like a caged bird trying to escape. It was ridiculous. A marquess of his standing shouldn’t feel giddy over gossip, yet…

Gossip of her divine beauty hardly did her justice, if even half the rumors were true. Hair like living flame. Eyes like the sacred blue fire spoken of in ancient murals. Skin kissed by celestial light. An impossibility—and yet she was here.

I really want to see her. His mouth was suddenly moist, heartbeat rising, arms tingling. If I could get her to—

His thoughts tripped over themselves, veering into fantasies both political and…less strictly political.

If I could get her to look at me.

If I could get her to smile at me.

If I could get her to acknowledge that I—Marquess Aldric—existed at all.

A sun elf’s attention could rewrite a man’s reputation in a single night.

A sun elf’s favor could rewrite a lineage for a generation.

And if she truly radiated the divine warmth they claimed, unlike the cold indifference in how the Archmagus portrays their Solar Incarnas, then…

Imagine what it would feel like…being seen by someone like that.

His fingers tightened around the amulet, knuckles whitening. The cold metal reminded him of reality—his reality:

A plump, aging noble playing dangerous games in dark corners. A man with debts piled higher than his titles. A man whose supposed “heroic lineage” had been living off its own myth for three generations.

No. Pathetic. Zorya would be mocking me right now… Get control! I am turning my father’s squandered wealth into a new empire! Focus! Breathe… Pretend I’ve already composed a dozen flattering introductions and plots… As Zorya tells me, ‘Fake it until you make it.’

If the boy didn’t arrive soon, Aldric might actually start rehearsing salutations out loud. Wouldn’t that be a sorrowful picture—a marquess mumbling sun-elf greetings like a starstruck merchant’s son.

He swallowed, dragged a hand down his face, and forced the fantasy aside.

Business first. Then…maybe a glimpse of her.

If the Holy Emperor is kind, she may even…

A soft knock—three precise taps—and his thoughts dissolved. Aldric’s personal attendant, Utren, slipped inside. His family was deeply in debt, which made his many daughters and even his wife practically his slaves…

They were taken care of, well, unless the man didn’t serve faithfully.

“My lord,” the man whispered, his usual composure slightly cracked. “The…the package has arrived. As you requested.”

“And the guards?”

“Saw nothing unusual. A servant delivering wine to your quarters, nothing more.”

Aldric exhaled slowly, tugging his collar again. “Send him in. Then make yourself scarce. No interruptions… None, Utren.”

Utren bowed and withdrew. Moments later, the door opened again, and Aldric’s mouth went dry.

The boy was small, perhaps nine or ten in appearance as he looked up at him, though Aldric knew better than to trust such things with sun elves or reincarnates, much less both in one.

He wore a heavy traveling cloak despite the warm evening, the hood drawn low over golden features that seemed to hold their own inner light. One eye glowed emerald-green with an intensity that made Aldric want to look away. The other was milky-white, clouded, and strange.

This—this is the kind of reincarnate we should have gotten instead of that damn Nightcrawler.

“Marquess Aldric.” The boy’s voice was cultured, precise, each syllable placed with the accuracy of a master musician. He didn’t bow, didn’t even incline his head. “I trust your discretion in this matter is absolute. We wouldn’t want the royal family knowing I visited you and not them.”

Aldric cleared his throat, trying to project confidence he didn’t feel. “Oh. Just me? I wasn’t aware of that detail, but yes—of course, of course. The Aldric family has kept secrets for centuries for the kingdom’s better ignorance. Your…visit will be no exception.”

“Good.” The boy moved further into the room, and Aldric, with what little Paladin training he had, noticed how his movements carried an almost swordsman-like grace, despite his claims of being a Wizard in the letter. “Then let us conclude our business quickly. I have little time.”

“Right. Yes. The item?” Aldric gestured to the velvet-lined box on his desk. “May I see it first? Not that I doubt your word, but—”

“But you’re a human merchant at heart, and merchants verify their goods.” The boy reached into his cloak, producing a leather collar inlaid with silver runes that seemed to writhe in the lamplight. “A control collar, as promised. Effective on any creature level 40 or below. Binding is instantaneous. Resistance is…inadvisable for the subject.”

Aldric’s hands trembled slightly as he accepted it, examining the craftsmanship—sun elf work, no question. The magic thrummed against his palms like the powerful amulet around his throat.

“Remarkable,” he breathed. “And…you’re sure this will even work against, say…a devil?”

“Now, that is an odd question,” the boy said flatly. His clouded eye seemed to focus on Aldric with uncomfortable intensity. “A devil is not a dungeon monster born out of the Void Lords, but classified as a creature born in Hell. Though the item itself only has the restriction of a ‘surface creature,’ meaning one born on the surface of Prime and not a reincarnate.”

“So it will work?” His fear flipped instantly. “You’re sure? You must be sure!”

The sun elf’s gaze slid to the collar, mysteriously opening a status menu that flipped for him to see. “As it states… It should work on devils and Darkvein creatures. Now, I am curious what you intend to use it for, when before I was indifferent, Marquess.”

Shit! No, no—I can’t let anyone know! No one.

“My Lord, I’m sorry, but that’s my business.” Aldric tried to hide the heat rising to his face. He was trying not to shake as he set the collar down carefully, moving to his desk. “We are merely conducting a trade. As agreed, our reasons remain our own. The key.”

He produced the small iron object, unremarkable except for the faint Imperium maker’s mark on the shaft. It had sat in his family’s vault for generations, passed down with stories that had long since faded into myth, and now even myth was gone with the fire his idiot father set in the library.

“This was given to my ancestor in 101 R.S., as far as I’m aware,” Aldric explained, trying to mask his nervousness with historical recitation. “During the founding of the Imperium Trade Empire, I assume. The records were lost, so if you wanted to do a follow-up deal on those, you’re out of luck.” He shrugged. “Whatever it was, my ancestors never found what it unlocks. It’s worthless to me.”

The boy took it with the same intensity he showed in his powerful eyes. “Worthless. Yes, it would be for you,” he repeated, his voice carrying a razor edge of amusement. “Our business is…”

He trailed off, and both of them went stiff as the door opened.

Aldric’s heart stopped.

Princess Miriam swept in with a warm smile, her blue gown rustling softly against the carpet. She moved with practiced grace, every inch the royal she’d been raised to be.

“Lord Aldric, forgive the intrusion,” she hummed sweetly. “I was hoping to discuss the upcoming harvest festival arrangements, but I can see you’re…occupied.”

Except…Miriam didn’t hum.

The real Miriam had a lovely speaking voice, but the young woman couldn’t carry a tune to save her title. Yet, this girl—this thing wearing her face—hummed softly as she entered, each note placed with mathematical precision as his amulet activated.

The sun elf boy had gone absolutely still, his whole body rigid with sudden tension. His clouded eye had cleared to match the other, both now glowing with inner light as he stared at the princess.

“How did you get past my eye?” His voice was ice.

“Madame,” Aldric breathed, feeling the needles on the back of his neck as those sharp eyes, far too sharp for a fifteen-year-old girl, slid to him. “How…did you get a contract with—”

The girl put a finger to her slightly smudged lipstick, a deliberate touch, he was sure, knowing her. The false princess’ lilac irises slid back to the sun elf.

“Shhh. And did you keep an eye out there, sweetheart? I noticed.” Madame Zorya tilted her head with mock innocence. “I suppose they were simply charmed by my presence. It happens. I’m just…that captivating.”

Aldric’s amulet grew warm against his chest. Bard magic. She’d charmed the guards, his men, and somehow, even the sun elf’s wards.

“I see. A Bard changeling?” the boy sneered, contempt dripping from every word. “How pedestrian. I see you have a reputation… I know every law of this nation, which places you on the list I shouldn’t be that concerned about. And…judging by the damage I’m seeing, you were in a rather nasty fight. Blessed silver?”

“You can see all that with your eyes?” Aldric gulped at her honeyed words and cold eyes, fixated on the boy as if he were the real snake. She laughed, the sound like silver bells mixed with breaking glass. “How invasive to a young girl’s privacy. Though I must say, for a servant of Mortifax, you’re rather quick to judge others’ choices in profession.”

The temperature in the room dropped ten degrees.

Aldric’s teeth began to chatter as his wide eyes slid to the cloaked boy beside him.

“Mortifax?” His voice cracked embarrassingly high. “The Pale Shepherd? The—”

“The Bone-Father, The White Reaper, yes, yes.” Zorya waved a hand dismissively, still wearing Miriam’s face as she examined her painted nails. “Such dramatic titles. Though I suppose when you’re a High Deity of Neutral Evil, you can call yourself whatever you like. Tell me, boy, does your black book whisper sweet promises? Or does it simply demand? I’ve read so many things regarding that cursed item. It’s hard to determine what is myth or real.”

The sun elf’s hand moved to his cloak, where Aldric now noticed a leather-bound tome, its cover glistening with an oily sheen that made his stomach turn.

“…Careful. You know too much to be a simple changeling, woman,” the boy whispered dangerously. “You’re a reincarnate of who? Tempestas, The Tide Turner?”

Zorya’s smile widened. “Do I look Chaotic Neutral to you? Well, perhaps I do, depending on which mask I wear,” she smiled, an edge not meant for the face she wore unsheathing as she faced the sun elf.

“Though I do wonder, darling—is that even important if you truly believe the Cindralux Effulgentia is meeting with the queen as we speak, shouldn’t you be fleeing for your miserable life rather than standing here trading barbs with a lowly changeling? Scamper along, kid.”

Aldric felt his knees go weak, not following any of this wordplay.

“The what? Cindra—what are you talking about, Zorya? Answer me. You’re bound under contract!”

“Such ignorance.” The boy didn’t even glance at him. His attention was fixed entirely on Zorya. “Do not rejoice, however you gained such knowledge, creature, for your victory is hollow. Your schemes and plans, whatever your intentions, are for waste.”

A tension entered the room—sharp, metallic—before the small figure began to chuckle. He pulled back his hood, revealing a smirk and a parietal, skull-like second face glimmering beneath the illusions.

“Death really isn’t so bad.”

He pulled the hood back into place, his cadence still aimed at Zorya. But Aldric felt the words slide beneath his skin like hooked needles.

“Your greed. Your foolishness has brought you to this end. I know what secrets hide in the light.”

His glow intensified.

“By dawn, judgment will fall on this city as the Holy Emperor’s hammer meets His anvil. All who do not repent—all who cling to their sins and do not give in to His will…”

He smiled.

And it was a terrible thing on such a young face.

“They will be purged—my evil, yours…all of it.”

His voice dropped into something darker. “You would do well to escape, as I am, swallowing your pride… Though it is too late. Everything within fifty miles will meet His law at light’s first cry.”

“Strong words,” Zorya purred, moving closer. Aldric noticed she was adjusting Miriam’s dress slightly, making it more provocative while hiding small rips at the bust. “But where’s your proof, my sweet paranoid elf?”

“I saw her.” The boy’s voice carried absolute conviction.

“As did I. Just now, in fact,” the changeling challenged. “Convince me.”

“Such…arrogance.” He laughed, incredulous and hearty, for one who had been so cold. “I cannot express how much fun I am having imagining the look on your face when she reveals herself for what she truly is.”

He held up a hand, whispering, “I am feeling generous. Behold—a glimpse through my familiar’s eye before she killed it instantly:

“Ruby-red hair that burns like starfire in sunlight. Time, Gravity, and Unity Magic. Instant Cast.” His hands were shaking now, though whether from fear or some form of fervor, Aldric couldn’t tell. “Only princesses of the solar line possess those abilities. Only one is permitted to leave the Crimson Palace because not even the Solar Queen herself can control her passage. Do you understand now?”

“Oh! Quite the interesting card, and how considerate of you to warn a rival! Yes, that is her—and she is now meeting with the queen,” Zorya confirmed casually. “Quite the political sensation, actually. Does that mean the whole court is going to be summoned for—”

“A warning.” The boy’s voice lowered with glee. He reached into his cloak, producing a crystal that pulsed with sickening, potent light. “I will not be here to hear it, though I will hear it nonetheless over a hundred miles away… I will see its effects and feel her power, for that is what it means when a demigoddess descends upon your city.”

Aldric’s mind raced. The palace was built on a leyline. If the boy had a leyline crystal, he could teleport out but not in. The security wards would redirect any incoming teleportation to the focus point, but outgoing…

Should I go with him? If it’s true—if a being outside the System has truly come to the capital to judge us… W-What do I do?

The sun elf raised the crystal, his voice taking on the cadence of prayer. “Do your Paladins not sing these psalms? You seem to have forgotten the purpose behind them… The promise of deliverance when evil has taken hold of a nation under His protection:

“Holy Emperor, when the broken fall alive, let the light take me, too. When the waters turn to fire, Heaven, please let me through… Let the light take me in.”

He coiled his fingers around the stone, the words he’d heard a thousand times taking on new meaning as he found himself mouthing those solemn songs:

“God, take the earth, forever blind, and now the sun will fade…and all we are is all we made.”

The sun elf drew a knife as Zorya watched, her smile never faltering. “When one forsakes their High Deity, there are consequences. You are fools. All of you. That closing statement rings true, no matter who one worships—all we are is all we made. We build our own coffin in the end.”

The crystal shattered as he brought the blunt end down on it with a sound like reality breaking. White light exploded outward, tearing a rift in the air itself. The edges crackled with power that made Aldric’s teeth ache.

Zorya tilted her head, still maddeningly calm. “You know, darling, I know a little something about balance…and I make sure there is a careful balance not to invite exactly what you fear, child.”

The boy laughed—a harsh, bitter sound.

“You think you have control over the morality of a city? You think your little games, your information networks, your manipulation can stand against a demigoddess princess of the Holy Emperor—his Left Hand? One of the few beings on this planet who has surpassed the system levels entirely?”

He stepped toward the rift. “I repeat myself as blasphemous as it is, as not even a follower of your emperor: God, take the earth, forever blind, and now the sun will fade…and all we are is all we made.

“May the Cindralux have mercy on the light in your hearts… All will become His. All will arise…as ashes or light. Let the dawn take me too.”

“Wait—” Aldric started, sweat leaking out of every pore.

But the boy was already gone, vanishing into white light. The rift collapsed with a sound like thunder, leaving only the faint smell of ozone and the echo of mock prayer.

The silence that followed was suffocating.

“What… What in the Holy Emperor’s name just happened?”

Aldric realized he was trembling, his hand clutching his collar so hard he’d probably bruised his own throat. Zorya’s smile fell—actually fell. For the first time since entering, she looked genuinely troubled.

“This, my dear Lord Aldric,” she said quietly, Miriam’s face shifting subtly into something harder, colder, “is why you should come to me rather than trying to deal with the underworld yourself. Do you have any idea what kind of monster you just invited into your life that I saved you from?”

She moved to the desk, picking up the control collar with distaste.

“Ignorantly inviting a servant of Mortifax into the palace? The Pale Shepherd doesn’t grant power lightly, darling, or select reincarnates on a whim. That boy is playing games that make mine look like children’s tea parties. It is lucky for us that his knowledge and ego so blinded him that he decided there was no other explanation for a female sun elf appearing.”

She held up the collar, and Aldric saw that her hand was actually shaking slightly. “And you… Let me guess, you intend to use this on the devil woman sealed below your family estate. Don’t you?”

Aldric’s throat went dry.

“How do you—yes,” he managed after the false fifteen-year-old’s lilac eyes narrowed. “What better, uh…holy cause than turning a servant of the Lord of Chains to the Holy Emperor’s side?”

Zorya stared at him for a long moment, then let out a sharp, humorless laugh. “Not only have you tried to tempt Mortifax, but now you aim at a servant of the Lawful Evil High Deity who should remain sealed? There is a reason I plot and poke at the good side of the spectrum. Uma kak u kúritsy… Bestólach. Nedóumok.”

Aldric had never seen her so agitated, devolving into speaking nonsense. Yet he straightened as she finally released a laughing, strained sigh.

“In any case, make whatever excuses for your appetites you like.” She gestured toward the side chamber where he kept his…collection. The beast women, indentured servants kept pliant with careful doses of alchemical compounds. “But tell me you’ve at least verified this devil woman is actually level 40 or below. Because if she’s stronger…that disaster could affect me.”

“She is.” Aldric found his confidence returning slightly at Zorya’s affirmation that the sun elf boy was an idiot with an ego. The sun elf girl wasn’t this demigoddess. And this, of all things, at least, he was certain of. “She is Level 40—exactly. I’ve had seers verify it. Family records confirm it. She was bound at that exact level, and the seal prevents any growth.”

Zorya set the collar down carefully, as if it might bite, while releasing a soft set of Bardic notes. “Playing with hellfire, darling. You’re playing with hellfire.”

“I’m well protected, and this is all for the kingdom.” Aldric touched his amulet. “You work for me, Zorya. Do you forget who is keeping you from being fully prosecuted under the law with my special privileges? Also, your little songs won’t work on me.”

“Oh, that.” She waved dismissively, rolling her eyes. “It was a calming charm for myself… To think I wouldn’t have noticed. You had at least one clever ancestor. And as if I need magic when we’re partners… I need a living partner, though.”

Her expression shifted back to something more familiar—calculating, predatory. “I need you alive, Lord Aldric. So do me a favor and wait at least a year before trying your little experiment with the devil woman. You’ll need that long to break the seal anyway, won’t you?”

“You…really do know too much,” he admitted, shifting uncomfortably under her cool gaze. “The binding is…complex.”

“Fantastic.” Zorya moved toward his wine cabinet with familiar ease. “In any case, I’m actually here on business. I need a specific vintage from your personal collection for the next act in my play. The Aurelian red from 770 R.S. You do still have some, yes?”

Aldric blinked at the sudden shift in topic. “I…uh, yes. Though that’s extremely rare and from the—Zorya, it’s valuable—and one of my favorites. What could you possibly—”

“Oh, I’ll buy you another one. I need it now. You can go without it for a week.”

She selected a bottle with the confidence of someone who knew exactly where it would be. “And a word of advice, my dear—try not to cross from the gray areas into the black. It’s rather easy to see when everyone around you is pure white.”

She paused at the door, glancing back with Miriam’s face but eyes that held a creature’s calculation.

“Oh, and about that prophecy of doom? I’d suggest not panicking just yet. The court is still intact, the king still breathes, and I very much doubt the Holy Emperor’s Left Hand has come to smite us all…while being charmed by a count.”

She smiled. “Though I will be monitoring the situation. Twilight approaches, after all. Our paranoid warlock friend was overzealous in his faith…in his own importance to elicit a demigoddess coming after him. Ego…the death of men.”

The door closed behind her with a soft click.

Aldric stood alone in his chamber, staring at the control collar on his desk and the space where a reincarnate warlock had torn a hole in reality. His hand found his collar again, tugging at the silk that suddenly felt impossibly tight.

I made a deal…with a Natural Evil reincarnate? No, but…I got the collar. If I just get her under my control… Everything changes. Everything.

Outside, through the palace windows, he could see the fading light in the sky. The many moons hung heavy and perpetually full, scattered around Prime.

Twilight was here, and dawn would be here soon enough.

Aldric poured himself a drink with shaking hands, trying not to think about prayers for the broken, about waters turning to fire, about the possibility that somewhere in this very palace, a demigoddess princess walked among them.

Judgment at dawn.

Hah… Right. That’s not going to happen. After all… I’m doing all this in the Holy Emperor’s name. He’s smiling on me… My God is smiling on me.

He drained the glass in one burning swallow and prayed—for the first time in years, truly prayed—that the sun elf boy was wrong.
