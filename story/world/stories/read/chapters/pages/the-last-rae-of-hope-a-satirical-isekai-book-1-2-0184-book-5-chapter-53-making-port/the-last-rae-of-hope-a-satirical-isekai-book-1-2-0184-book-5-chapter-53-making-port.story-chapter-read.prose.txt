Vernie directed me towards the bluff, but refused to accompany me there.

“His meticulous Majesty made sure I promised not to eavesdrop on you two,” she explained as she turned to head back. “Went so far as to demand I uncross my fingers behind my back, so I’ll let you two have your private moment.”

“Oh... Well, thank you,” I replied, feeling my chest tighten a little.

The path leading to the bluff ran haphazardly along the island’s eastern edge, where tidal flats filled in a narrow band before climbing to form the high cliffs that were its original namesake. The wind was stronger here, and it did everything it could to upend my below-the-shoulder bob.

If I’m having this much trouble, I can’t imagine what Olethros’s hair looks like.

During one fruitless attempt to tame my locks, I noticed a large black bubble forming in the stone-ringed stretch of tidal muck. It swelled impossibly large, carrying the stench of salty, seaside decay.

“What the?!”

I tore Will from my sheath and held out Faith in front of me, but both felt a bit heavy in my hands.

Hey guys, come on! We’re under attack—

The bubble popped, goop spraying out as the form of a familiar leech demon appeared. He surfaced from the center of the bubble slowly, his arms crossed across his armored chest. Around his curled, segmented tail wriggled several tiny leeches, glossy and shimmering in the surrounding muck pile.

“Oh,” I said with a sigh of relief. “It’s only you, Morcelos.”

His bulbous head tilted, and his parasitic mouth quivered around pointy, flat teeth. “We’re not on a first-name basis, Captain.”

I frowned as I sheathed my sword, unable to recall his rank under General Ragnerus. “How should I address you, then?”

“You shouldn’t.”

“Er…” I paused awkwardly before trying again. “You know, in the other world I was in, leeches were used to help wounds heal. Something to do with helping restore blood flow after surgery where the wounded tissue didn’t have good perfusion—”

“So a leech only has worth if it has an ability to serve you?”

I flinched. “No—that’s not—I was just trying to make conversation.”

“Why bother? We have no relationship, and we both know you don’t belong here.”

Taking a big breath and folding my arms kept me from snapping back. “I’ll leave the island as soon as I can.”

“I don’t mean this island,” he replied. “I mean in Speranza. After you defeat him—if you defeat him—don’t expect all the demons to congratulate you on your success. Many are waiting for the right moment, when our demon lords turn toward other matters.”

“Many? Many of them, or many of us?”

Morcelos didn’t answer, but it wasn’t really a question that required one.

“I’ll leave once my job is done,” I clarified through gritted teeth.

He looked into the tidal pool. One of the nearby leeches started heading toward the edge of the muck, but he carefully curled his tail to cut off its adventure.

“Leech mothers care for their young until they’re ready to go out on their own and find their own place. That’s the value I found in befriending them in Chairo. These ones do the same.” He hissed then, though I didn’t take it as being directed at me. “If you have no place here, you should find the right place somewhere else. There is no point in simply settling. If you find that the next place is not good enough? Keep looking for a friendly port. There are more than two places out there.”

I blinked, then relaxed my arms. “Thanks for your advice—such as it is.”

Morcelos let out a strange sound, and it took me a moment to realize it was a chuckle, low and wet, but genuine. “And thank you for your animus. My duty to warn you has paid off.”

Even though I was grateful for the validation, I rolled my eyes. “Sure.”

He started to melt back into the muck. “Destruction on your enemies, Captain—in whatever places they inhabit.”

I watched him disappear into the shallows, the leeches bobbing down after him. Once he was gone, I let myself shiver out all creepiness I had accumulated.

Then I went on to find the bluff.

I could see the top of it, but I couldn’t get myself to climb it.

Olethros was up there with his back to me, looking out over the hazy water. His head was turned sideways into the wind, and his long, lavender hair blew in perfect, undulating waves. His horns sparkled more than was necessary, scattering glints of light in multiple directions. His robes flapped crisply in slow, repeating arcs.

In my dream, I worried about a line between us.

Now there’s a whole damn world there instead.

He’s truly a demon king now, acknowledged by his siblings, recognized by humans and hybrids.

He’s finally being seen by others the way he should be.

Why can’t I just be happy about that? Why do I have to have complicated feelings instead?

Without moving his head, his left eye turned to me.

Then he blinked out of view.

“Wha—”

There was a buzz just beyond my right ear, and his voice soon followed. “You were taking so long to join me, I thought I’d come fetch you.” His smile appeared on the edge of my vision. “Shall we?”

“Okay,” I replied, taking a step forward.

He put his arms around me from behind. “Walking is so pedestrian.”

“You know, as a pun, I give that a 1/10 and an eye-roll of epic—”

The world flattened smoothly, and suddenly we were both at the top of the bluff, where the view opened across the tumultuous ocean.

“What was that you were saying?” he asked, his cheek against mine.

I swallowed a forthcoming compliment, which would have pointed out the fact that it was the smoothest translocation I’d ever experienced.

“We’re not supposed to be using amity and animus unnecessarily,” I muttered instead.

“I made no such promises,” he replied with a scoff, still staying close. “Neither did you, as I recall. You were asleep during the discussion and thus not subject to its restrictions.”

I sighed deeply, looking down at the ground between my feet. “In all seriousness... there’s something important I need to talk to you about. You know, my long-term goals, and what I plan to do after we fight his Unholiness.”

“And I promise to listen,” he agreed, letting me go and stepping forward. “However, there’s something else that requires our imminent attention.” He pointed out the water. “Look.”

All I saw at first was the sea. Then a dark flicker about half a mile out. Black vines sprouted from a circular void above the water’s surface. A large wooden ship dropped from nowhere, its sails full of wind. It hit the water fast and hard, churning through the waves. It quickly slowed, however, and began to sink more than seemed safe for its crew. Another set of vines grabbed it again, pulling it into another forming portal.

Hope surged through me, and I forgot everything else.

“They’re having trouble navigating,” Olethros noted. “I suspect a deeply unstable core of mixing animus and amity. Shall we tow them in?”

I glanced up at Paradise looming in the distance, noting that its barrier was still flickering with iridescent strikes.

Sparkles is still at it, I suppose... But how long will she last?

“Let’s do it.”

He held out his hand first this time, and I took it. Our attunement was near instantaneous. I could feel his determination meld with mine. Underneath it, however, was something else. A sense of forthcoming loss, dark and shapeless.

Before I could better trace the outline, however, a wall went up—literally.

“I ask that we make this part of our later discussion, please.”

I nodded and turned away, finding a better complementary hold on his conviction instead. The aura that came from both of us was iridescent once more, with darks and lights and colors all between. Rather than pushing the aura upward, we pushed it out to the ship instead, locking on to its hull.

Magic tractor beam!

Even using our skills, it was hard work. The ship was cracked in several places, with a barrier of animus that was all that was holding its ribs together. We pulled against gravity, wind, and current alike, aiming the ship toward the beach below the bluff in slow and stubborn increments. We tipped it every once in a while carefully, trying to drain whatever water we could without ripping the ship to shreds.

Sweat ran from my brow, saltier than ever.

We eventually grounded it in the shallows, unable to haul it onto the sands themselves. The prow was broken nearly in half, wood splintering from the split on both sides.

Well, no wonder… That giant crab claw on the end wasn’t doing it any favors—

“Captain Morin’s ship!” I shouted, my hope confirmed. “That crazy-ass pirate!”

Olethros grinned. “Let’s go see how many of our crew he managed to drag along with him, shall we?”
