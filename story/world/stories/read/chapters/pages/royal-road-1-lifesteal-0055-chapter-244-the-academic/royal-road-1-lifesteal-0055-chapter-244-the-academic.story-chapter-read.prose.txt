“What exactly happened?” The soul therapist cupped his chin as he examined the most recent scan of Freddy’s soul. “This might very well be one of the greatest leaps in soul healing I’ve ever witnessed in my career. Did you get revenge on a mortal enemy? Accomplish a life-long dream? What could have possibly done this?”

“I dunno.” Freddy coughed awkwardly, red in the face.

“I see. So it was sex.”

Freddy sputtered.

“Given that you’re acting like a little kid about it, I’m not surprised it was such a significant event. Nevertheless, well done. I…” He looked over the scan. “If you were an ordinary patient, this is usually the point where I’d authorize the soul elixir.”

Freddy perked up. “Really?” He tempered his excitement as he asked, “But you won’t?”

He shrugged. “There is honestly no reason for you to rush. Your emotional circuit is back online, your personal agency is functional, you’re not experiencing severe ennui, losses in memory, or any other severe side effects.”

“What about my abilities?”

“You can use them just fine,” he said. “Granted, you shouldn’t overdo it still, but other than a bit of discomfort, an ability or two won’t revert any progress.”

“So… I’m done?”

“In all ways that matter, yes.” The doctor sat down. He pulled out a piece of paper and jotted something down, then handed it to Freddy. “Ask for this elixir at the ATA. At this point, if you really insist, you can take it. But the more you wait, even if it's just a month or two, the closer you’ll get to perfect healing.”

Freddy stared at the paper, unable to believe it. “I’m… done?”

“Yes, Freddy Stern. You have become one of the rare few who actually stuck with therapy and successfully rebuilt your soul. Thank you.”

“You’re thanking me?”

“Of course I am. Do you have any idea how much I’ve studied soul theory, and how rarely I get to actually treat someone who doesn’t jump to elixirs the moment things get difficult?” He scoffed. “So yes, I am thanking you. Now, if you have nothing else to say, please leave my office. We’re done.”

Freddy slowly got up. “Thank you as well, Doctor:”

The man shooed him away.

With a big, bright smile on his face, Freddy walked out. The moment he left, he called his ATA contact and commissioned the elixir. “Sorry, how much did you say it costs? I’m not sure I heard you properly.”

“Twenty billion dollars, sir.”

“… So I did hear you properly…”

“Apologies, Sir. This is the most effective soul elixir currently available, and the demand for it is high. Would you like to buy a cheaper alternative?”

He was tempted for a long moment. Freddy might have a crap ton of money, but his supply wasn’t infinite. With how he’d been spending out his ass lately, he was starting to run low, relatively speaking.

He was down to his last thirty billion. With this purchase, he’d get uncomfortably close to broke. But out of everything he’d wasted his hard-earned money on, this was, relatively speaking, one of the smarter purchases he would make. “No, that’s quite all right. I would like to buy one.” And I might have to auction off my Milliure set to stay afloat for now…

Regardless, he wasn’t too worried about his funds. Even if he wasted his last coin, he would be going up to the third star soon. Then, he doubted he would ever worry about money again.

Just a bit longer…

His days proceeded in relative, yet tense, quiet. He was always alert, more so than usual. He memorized all the places he could hide in the event of a big attack on Starhold. If he was inside the university, he would most likely be fine. Most likely. Inside his apartment, he had built a small hiding spot—a hidden, closet-sized hole in the wall where he would be hidden behind thousands of layers of enchantments.

If he was anywhere else… Things might get a bit scary.

With the use of his abilities back, he delved more often. He finally auctioned off the sledgehammer, for a mouth-watering sum of 43 billion dollars, and the Milliure set for only 23 billion, which stung given he’d paid 30 billion for it, only to then bid on a nearly 70-billion dollar pair of unique earrings.

They were hewn of an absolutely stunning orange gem, which shimmered like the surface of the sun itself.

Sunjade Earrings: +7% fire resistance.

While hardly exciting, they made for a highly desirable item. Specific resistances were rare, and usually, very effective. Fire in particular was a pain point for almost anyone. Nobody wanted to become a burn victim.

Their growth depended on defeating powerful fire-affinity foes, but also, they could be grown through enduring fire damage. Which was to say, yes, Freddy would be setting himself on fire and healing until he exhausted that option.

Time passed.

With every passing day, Freddy found it easier and easier to study new material. As the gaps in his foundation were filled, building upon that foundation became shockingly easy. Needless to say, the cocktail of substances he was abusing to study faster were helping, and the help was compounding a lot harder than expected.

One by one, he started passing exams. Some were easy enough, even without chemical assistance, like anything taught by Wisdom, and some were tough even with the pools’ worth of spa brew he was downing.

Foundations of Runesetting proved to be particularly tricky, mainly because the test was excruciatingly difficult. Just memorizing wasn’t good enough. The test tasked the students with setting a rune shell for a described ability. This was one of those things Freddy found particularly hard because he hadn’t gone through the same kind of upbringing as the average elite.

Most people experimented with countless abilities as they grew up, constructing and deconstructing shells over and over until they developed a better intuition on how they were meant to be formed.

Freddy had, for the most part, stuck with whatever he developed the first time around.

At this point, even just looking at the inside of his soul was a painful experience. His abilities were a tragic mess. Even the Crimson Dragon Scales, which he had thought would be fantastic given the source, were awful.

Needless to say, this made learning runesetting a bit tricky. It was one of those things best learned in practice, not through pen-on-paper exercises.

Then, the exam rolled around. “Oh, yeah… I’m failing this for sure.”

The question seemed simple on the surface—design an ability that could turn blood acidic. Easy enough, no? Just plop in Red Venom and boom, done.

Except, there were a couple of problems with this. For starters, Red Venom couldn’t actually make blood acidic. No, blood was alkaline by default, and could only be turned acidic through the concept of acid at Stage 1.

This was a Stage 0 shell.

Meaning, there was only one affinity that could do the job—”Poison, huh…?”

Freddy could hear countless other students groaning as they all came to the same conclusion as himself. This was a tough exam, even by the standards of others around him.

Using the poison affinity to modify blood into an acid was very complicated. It involved a complex multi-step process that required some serious knowledge of chemistry, and because it was such an impractical application of the affinity, there was no chance that anyone present would have ever encountered an example on how to do it.

The professor, who was easily among the most punchable teachers Freddy had ever seen in his life, had a smug expression on his face as he watched the students struggle.

This prick just wants to see the world burn, doesn’t he…?

Freddy scoffed, turning back to the page before him. While he had no idea where to even start, he wanted to give it a shot. At the very least, he could—”Wait…”

An idea came to mind. An utterly mad, ridiculous idea. He got started on constructing the shell, and as he did, he nearly laughed—because his insanity sense was helping him fill out the details. The idea was just that crazy.

With a bright smile on his face, Freddy got up and handed the paper to the professor.

“Giving up already?” the professor mused, clearly entertained. Then, he looked down at the paper and scoffed. “What is this even…?” His joviality vanished as he scowled, cupping his chin. “Hold on…” He burst into laughter. “That’s completely insane. Nice try, though, but it wouldn’t work because—no wait,” his joviality vanished for a second time, and he frowned deeply as he examined the ability in question.

As for what Freddy had done? It was quite simple, really—he had designed a lightning-affinity ability that could convert blood into a quasi-battery.

That battery would immediately discharge, and the blood in question would explode—and not even hard enough to be practical. It was an utterly insane, stupid ability that would, if only for a fraction of a second, satisfy the requirements of the exam question on a technicality.

“Well, well, well…” The professor mused, nodding at the paper. “Frankly, I don’t think I could have designed an ability like this. While it just barely satisfies the requirement, I’ll give you points for creativity. You pass.”

Other exams came around and as well, and thankfully, none of them were as bonkers as this one. The vast majority could be passed through brute memorization, and the exceptions, Freddy managed somehow.

There were three classes that had turned out quite different from what he expected.

First, Heavy Swordsmanship.

For the most part, this class was kind of useless. For starters, an executioner’s sword was too different from regular heavy swords for it to be practical to use the same techniques. On top of that, the combination of Freddy’s awakened soul construct and Abyssal Depths tempering made all the standard stances utterly useless. He still passed the class, with flying colors, at that, but he didn’t believe he’d learned much of use.

Next up was Tempering Studies.

This was a class all about moderation. In essence, how to not get hurt by tempering techniques. It was, by far, the most useless class Freddy had signed up for. Well, that was harsh. It did have some uses, but really, it was nothing a few books at the library couldn’t have replaced.

And finally, there was Primordial Domain Studies.

Since it was taught by Wisdom, passing it was easy enough. But the class taught some extremely interesting things Freddy had known nothing about.

For starters, spirits apparently vanished from reality altogether before turning into eidola, and during that disappearance, they apparently took on a primordial domain trial of all the affinities they were relevant to. For Wisdom, this had been the dream affinity, and also water, for some reason.

Then, there were the different kinds of rewards one could get. It gave Freddy a much better idea of what to aim for in the water domain, whenever he took it.

And then, they reached the topic of prime remnants, where Freddy finally learned something he’d been wondering about for a while.

Wisdom didn’t mention unique prime remnants, and probably, it didn’t actually know what they did. But as soon as it explained how regular prime remnants worked, Freddy knew exactly what made his one unique—the fact that it could consume two items rather than one.

It seemed like a small detail, but it was an absolute game changer.

The thing about awakened soul constructs was that they grew every ascension, evolving just as talents did. In a sense, they were almost a talent in their own right. And much like a second talent, their growth did not in any way take away from the pool of latent aura that was consumed on ascension. Effectively, just having an awakened soul construct made one’s latent aura go that much further.

Not quite as far as a second talent, but it was close.

Now, Freddy had no way to prove whether his theory was true or not. Well, not unless he asked.

“Indeed,” the golden lips chorused at him. “The fact that I am a unique means that your latent aura will apply twice—once for the ring, once for the pendant.”

“Holy shit,” was all he had to say to that.

No wonder the empress wanted a unique prime remnant so desperately. They grew twice as hard, apparently.

He learned some other interesting facts about awakened soul constructs.

For starters, there were three types: body-augmenting, equipment-augmenting, and familiar-augmenting.

Freddy had the second type.

The wield assist function was exclusive to the equipment-augmenting type, while body-augmenting types offered a boost to ability-casting speed, and the familiar-augmenting types offered a greater connection between the archhuman and whatever familiar they applied it to.

Freddy could technically apply the Cycle Scythe to his body, but that was very different from how the body-augmenting types manifested. He could basically just treat his body parts like weapons.

No, the way body-augmenting types usually manifested was a bit different. They took the form of a tattoo while dormant, just as Freddy’s Cycle Scythe, but when active, they transformed a specific body part—or added a new one.

On that note, Freddy was now about eighty-five percent sure that Emily Umbra’s hair was an awakened soul construct. He didn’t know for sure because talents could manifest in a wide variety of ways, and it wouldn’t be completely outside the norm for her hair to, indeed, be a part of her talent. But it was unlikely.

Her talent already did way too many different things, and that was very rare. Rare enough that an awakened soul construct simply made for a more logical explanation.

As expected, Freddy had no issue passing Primordial Domain Studies. Between being taught by Wisdom itself, the fact he was extremely interested in the topic, and the fact it was, admittedly, pretty easy, it was hardly a challenge.

Time continued passing. Before long, two months had passed. The ring on Freddy’s finger burned as he could barely resist taking the elixir, but he endured. Not much longer, he promised himself.

He passed more exams, and soon enough, his schedule was starting to grow thin.

The alchemy class had started, and he was doing incredibly well. His ability to focus for long periods of time, and his incredibly precise control over his body and keen perception made him a natural. Overall, Alchemy 101 really was a simple class, only teaching the most fundamental of basics. Blood Alchemy was a lot harder.

Three months passed. He had passed every single exam, with the exception of three—Advanced Blood Runeology, Advanced Water Runeology, and Blood Alchemy.

The tests for these three were coming over the course of the following two weeks. He was incredibly nervous. Once he ascended to the third star, he would most likely not be continuing to study at the university, not for a long while, at least. With how the Tower of York worked, he would not be able to hide the fact he’d gone up to the third star, and once he did, he wanted to focus on his growth, rather than studying.

He had already memorized the entire runepedia.

He had worked tirelessly to catch up with extra material.

He had passed easier versions of the classes.

There was every reason for him to be confident in himself.

And he was, for the most part. No longer did he feel like an utter idiot among the people in the university. In fact, with the exception of those with the dream affinity, Freddy could confidently say that he was among the more accomplished students. He had already gotten a second-rank academic badge for successfully passing 10 classes. Once he passed the last three. he’d make it past 25, which would put him at third-rank.

Excluding those dream-affinity cheaters, not many people had a third-rank academic badge. 25 was a lot. For most, that would have taken at least half a decade of hard work.

For him, it didn’t even take a single year.

Sure, he had his own ways of cheating, but he had also simply worked extremely hard.

And that was something to be proud of.

Those two weeks passed in a flash.

On a beautiful, magical day, Freddy got up from bed, having slept for 12 hours straight, given he had no reason to get up any earlier. He stretched, got into his living room, and sat down on the couch.

He smiled at the one final headache his soul was causing him. It was dim, barely even noticeable compared to the nightmare he’d lived through back when he first started healing.

“Here goes nothing.” He took the elixir out of the ring, and downed it.

It put him through an unbelievable amount of pain. For a whole day straight, he felt like his very soul was on fire as the remnant scarring was burned away and replaced with fresh soul tissue, finally piecing him back together, just a little different. And a whole lot better.

Once the effects finally ebbed, he got to his feet, and clenched his fists, double-starring Hydraulic Flex for extra effect.

Using an ability without any pain for the first time in so long felt incredible.

He left the apartment with a wide smile on his face.

In two weeks, he would be visiting the Tower of York. Until then, he had a couple things to take care of.

First and foremost—”Time to put some dragon nut juice in my veins.”
