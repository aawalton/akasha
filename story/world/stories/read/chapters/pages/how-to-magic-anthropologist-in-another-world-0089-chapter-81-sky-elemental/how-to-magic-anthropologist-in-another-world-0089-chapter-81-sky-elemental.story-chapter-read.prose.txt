The next morning, everyone was awake at dawn. Well, I assumed it was around dawn since there was no sun in the Sunless Reach. Still, I trusted my circadian rhythm because I didn't wake up tired or anything. We started preparing for what would almost certainly be our hardest fight since forming the team. Unlike undead, fighting an elemental would carry its own challenges.

We discussed our plan over breakfast, checked our weapons one last time, and changed our armor.

"We leave the chain mail behind," Raik said as he pulled a set of leather armor from his Bag of Holding. "Anything metal stays at camp, except for Kan's chains and Alice's spear."

Everyone kept their spider silk underarmor on, while Raik and I wore leather armor over it. His was a fancy blue set, while mine was the first training set I got during my first month here. The leather was mostly an extra layer of insulation. We didn't expect much in the way of physical attacks after all.

"It feels wrong going into battle without proper armor," Vena admitted, putting her cleric robes over the skin-tight silk armor.

"This is how I used to dress before," Kan shrugged.

Once we were ready, we started walking toward the tower. The structure overlooking the camp felt menacing, a ten-story building perched on the peak of a mountain. The bright, eternal night sky contrasted with the mismatched windows.

The hike up the final stretch of the mountain was mostly uneventful, interrupted only once by Ja'a.

"I can feel it," she said quietly.

"How strong?" Raik asked.

She frowned slightly before answering.

"Around thirty-five SB."

Calr let out a slow whistle.

"So it's strong."

"You survived a blast from that thing?" Kan asked me.

"Yeah," I said, remembering the painful experience. "But it burned me badly enough that I needed Vena's healing."

"You should be even more resistant now," Raik gave me a look. "Your Soul Strength nearly doubled."

I nodded, ready to be the tank for once instead of Shingo. I was the best suited for it, and it was my idea to clear the tower after all.

We arrived at the foot of the tower a few minutes later. The massive front doorway was still buried beneath collapsed stone, leaving one of the side windows as the only practical way inside.

"Right here," I said, showing them the window I had used last time.

Raik stepped forward, placed a hand against the ancient stone, and pushed against it. He tried again, then grabbed the ledge and pulled down before nodding.

"The outer walls still look relatively sound."

"You sure?" Ja'a asked. "Maybe Shingo should try instead."

He tapped the stone with his fist.

"I was checking if the stone would get loose or shaky," he shrugged. "The seams feel solid enough."

"Whatever caused the entrance collapse happened a long time ago," he added. "I don't expect the tower to come down during our fight."

"Seconded," said Calr. "You'd expect old buildings like this to start tilting, but it is as straight as it was when it was constructed more than three thousand years ago."

I climbed onto the window ledge first and dropped inside, the rest of the team following me. Shingo had a bit of trouble fitting through the narrow opening. Had he still been wearing his plate armor, he would have needed another way in.

The first room was dark, so I attached a lightstone to my belt.

The inside of the tower looked exactly as it had during my first visit. Each outer room had a single window framing a specific constellation. Together, they formed a ring surrounding a corridor and a central circular chamber. The walls were covered in carved writing. Long, flowing lines of ancient Telepath script circled the rooms between elaborate stone carvings of stars and constellations.

"Can you read it?" I asked Calr, who was brushing his hand against the script of his ancestors.

"No." He shook his head.

"I think you could do it," I said. "With Analyse, I mean."

"Your sister is working on it," I added. "With Ko'i Outspring."

"I know," he grimaced. "I could help her..."

"But?" I prompted.

"That would put too much attention on me." He shook his head. "Learning the language of the Telepaths is one step closer to being outed as a Telepath."

"Aren't you worried the same thing will happen with Sara?" asked Vena.

"No, not really," he said. "Unlike me, she doesn't have Analyse feeding her answers. She can figure it out the hard way, piece by piece, and spend a reasonable amount of time doing so."

"Sounds like you don't want to steal your sister's credit," said Kan with a smile.

"Credit, shmedit," scoffed Ja'a. "The Outspring will claim all the benefits in the end."

"Not if she marries into the family," I teased, and Calr's face immediately grew pale.

We continued deeper into the tower until we reached the entrance to the central chamber near the staircase leading upward.

Unlike the surrounding rooms, this chamber had no carvings on the walls. Instead, a massive model occupied the stone floor. Ten rings surrounded a crystalline sphere. A heliocentric depiction of the planetary system.

"This is where I learned there are ten planets," I said, pointing toward it.

As everyone looked at the model, I walked around it until I reached one of the outer orbits.

"This one." I pointed toward the ninth sphere. "That's where we are."

Calr stepped closer, studying the craftsmanship.

"The proportions aren't random..." His eyes fogged over. "I think I can match the planets we found with this model."

"You can?" I asked, surprised.

His fingers hovered near one of the rings without touching it.

"The builder understood the star patterns in exacting detail."

Before we could continue the discussion, Ja'a's expression hardened.

"The elemental is stirring."

Everyone immediately became alert.

"We should save the sightseeing for after we kill the monster," Raik said.

"Agreed," I nodded.

We climbed the spiral staircase in silence.

Each floor brought us closer to the roof. The air gradually changed, smelling faintly of ozone. By the eighth floor, I could already feel a faint static prickling against my skin.

Activating my Stormshark's Whisper Soulbook, I was able to see that the air was heavily charged, as were the ground and spots along the walls. Normally, I would have been happy about that since it meant I could use my lightning with improved efficiency, but I wasn't the strongest lightning user around.

I stopped before the final staircase leading upward and looked back at the group.

"This is it," I said. "Past these stairs is the roof, and the monster waiting for us."

"Ja'a, you remain here," said Raik.

"I think Shingo should stay with her too," I said.

"Why is that?" asked Calr.

Raik also looked my way, expecting an explanation.

"His heart is too large," I said, thinking about André the Giant's medical condition. "It needs to be to pump blood through his giant frame. I'm afraid prolonged exposure to lightning might cause it to stop."

"Since we aren't expecting physical attacks," I added, "and Shingo lacks any ranged combat options, I thought it might be better if he sat this one out."

"Plus," added Ja'a, "I would prefer not to be left alone in this creepy tower."

"Okay," said Raik. "Shingo will stay, but you'll have to train his lightning resistance at a later date."

Both Shingo and I nodded.

"Okay then," Raik continued, returning to giving orders. "You take point, Alice. Kan, you're with her."

He turned toward Vena.

"And you keep them healed."

As we climbed the final staircase and stepped onto the roof, the world seemed to open around us.

The rooftop itself was far larger than I remembered, a broad circular platform of ancient stone exposed directly to the eternal night of the Sunless Reach. Stars stretched endlessly overhead, interrupted every few minutes by the familiar trails of shooting stars crossing the heavens. At the center of the rooftop stood a giant brass telescope, its tarnished metal frame somehow still intact despite centuries, perhaps millennia, of neglect. The instrument pointed eternally toward the sky, as though its long-dead owner had been searching for answers among the stars.

Next to it floated the Sky Elemental.

The creature looked less like a monster and more like a piece of the heavens torn free and given life. Its core was a perfect sphere of darkness roughly three meters across. It resembled a fragment of the night sky that had somehow been folded inward and compressed into a single impossible point. Countless stars glittered within the void, shifting and twinkling exactly like the heavens above. Looking directly at it made my eyes ache. My brain kept insisting I was staring through a hole punched into reality itself, a window opening onto an infinite distance that should not have fit inside something so small.

Surrounding that stellar core was a thick mantle of clouds. The clouds rotated slowly around the sphere like a miniature atmosphere, swirling through overlapping currents that constantly merged and separated. They never completely obscured the darkness beneath, leaving occasional gaps through which the stars hidden inside remained visible. Lightning flashed constantly beneath the cloud layer. Brilliant white arcs danced from one side of the sphere to the other, illuminating the void within for fractions of a second before vanishing again.

Beyond the clouds existed a final layer. Wind itself had become visible. Cyclones circled the elemental in overlapping spirals, carrying pebbles, dust, fragments of broken stone, shattered glass, and debris ripped from the tower over countless years. The debris orbited the creature like a swarm of miniature moons trapped within its gravity. Every object moved along a different path, weaving through the storm without ever colliding, as though the winds possessed an intelligence of their own.

The entire thing looked like a thunderstorm, a hurricane, and a star-filled night sky compressed into a single floating singularity.

The elemental slowly rotated toward us. Lightning crackled through the cloud layer while the surrounding winds intensified, causing loose stones across the rooftop to rattle and vibrate. Even from twenty meters away, I could feel static crawling across my skin, raising the tiny hairs on my arms.

"Positions," Raik ordered.

Everyone moved immediately. Kan and I advanced to the front while Vena remained behind us. Raik positioned himself farther back, ready to support us with fire once the battle began. Calr pointed his crossbow while Katar held his wooden practice sword. Shingo and Ja'a stayed below the staircase exactly as planned, prepared to intervene if anything went wrong.

I took a deep breath, and lightning crawled across my body.

Unlike the way I normally used my lightning magic, I wasn't creating a single discharge. Instead, I continuously maintained an electrical current flowing through myself. Negative charge accumulated around my head and shoulders while the current traveled downward through my body before dissipating harmlessly into the stone beneath my feet. My hair immediately rose into the air, and tiny sparks danced across my fingers. The static field surrounding me grew stronger with each passing second.

Maintaining the technique steadily drained my mana reserves, but under the skies of the Sunless Reach, that hardly mattered. Another shooting star crossed overhead, replacing what little energy I had spent before the loss could even register.

Beside me, Kan stepped forward and thrust out her hands. Her chain shot forward like a living serpent. The metal links raced across the rooftop before spreading outward into a wide net between us and the elemental. Rather than suspending the chain entirely in the air, she anchored sections directly into the stone floor. The metallic links touched the ground in dozens of places, creating a sprawling conductive barrier that stretched across nearly the entire rooftop.

I reached into my Bag of Holding and started extracting spears, one after another. I drove the spare weapons into cracks throughout the stone floor. The spears acted as supports, lifting sections of the chain higher off the ground while simultaneously providing additional paths for excess electricity to travel safely into the tower itself.

The elemental answered by trying to kill us.

Lightning erupted from the cloud layer.

A brilliant white bolt crossed the rooftop faster than thought and slammed directly into Kan's chain barrier. The entire world flashed white. The chain absorbed most of the energy instantly, dispersing it through the network of grounded links. Electricity raced through the metal like liquid light, branching in dozens of directions as it sought paths into the earth.

Not all of it.

Part of the charge continued along the chains toward us.

Pain shot through my body as my muscles clenched involuntarily. The current wasn't nearly strong enough to seriously injure me thanks to my affinity and preparation, but it still hurt. Kan wasn't as fortunate. She yelped and released the chain immediately.

The moment she let go, the animated metal collapsed into a tangled heap.

"Damn it!"

I was already moving.

More spears appeared in my hands. One after another, I drove them into the stone, raising sections of the fallen chain off the ground and restoring the improvised fence between us and the elemental. Kan recovered quickly and reasserted control over the chain. The links rose once more, this time forming a much denser barrier than before.

The elemental attacked again.

Another bolt descended from the swirling clouds. The lightning struck the chain network, and the charge spread through the grounded metal.

This time, as the chain went limp and the elemental's magical authority overcame Kan's, the spears held most of the net together.

The lightning vanished harmlessly into the tower's foundations, and not a single spark reached us.

For several seconds, nobody moved. The elemental floated in place while the winds continued to howl around it. Lightning flickered beneath the cloud layer. The chain barrier hummed faintly with residual energy.

A grin spread across my face.

"Success."

Kan's answering smile was equally savage.

"It's our turn."

I summoned my bident spear.

Lightning exploded across its prongs, brilliant arcs dancing between the blades as power gathered inside the weapon's shaft. The air filled with the sharp scent of ozone, and the familiar hum of electricity vibrated through my hands.

Then I charged forward.

After only a few steps, I planted my foot and hurled the spear directly at the elemental.

The weapon crossed the rooftop in a blur of silver and blue. I probably would have missed my shot if the creature hadn't been enormous. The spear punched through the thinning cloud layer and struck the star-filled core.

Thwack.

The sound was strangely disappointing. It wasn't the impact of metal against stone or flesh, but something more like two marbles colliding.

The elemental shuddered.

"I wish my spear was back in my hand."

Star mana vanished, and the weapon instantly teleported back into my grip.

A second impact echoed across the rooftop.

Thwack.

Calr's crossbow bolt struck the core a heartbeat later. Unlike my attack, his projectile bounced away almost immediately, but it still seemed to hurt the creature.

Beside him, Raik launched a compact fireball. The sphere of blue flame slammed into the rotating cloud layer and spread across its surface. Fire hissed against moisture and turbulent wind. Steam erupted outward in thick white clouds before being ripped apart by the elemental's cyclones.

Even Vena joined the assault. The cleric extended a hand and began cycling through her Seven Lights Soulbook.

Yellow, red, orange, then blue.

The final beam struck the elemental's cloud layer and immediately produced the most visible result. Wherever the blue light touched, clouds condensed into water. Droplets formed, merged together, and began falling from sections of the monster's body like rain.

For the first time since the battle began, the monster seemed to be hurt.

The monster didn’t like that, and it answered with Lightning exploding from its core.

The entire world turned white as a blinding flash burned itself into my vision. For a fraction of a second, I couldn't see anything. When my eyesight finally returned, blinking spots still danced before my eyes.

The chain barrier had held.

Mostly.

Several of the spears supporting it had tilted or been driven deeper into the stone by the force of the discharge. One more attack like that and sections of the fence would probably collapse.

"Raik!" Calr shouted. "Switch to cold!"

The fire mage grimaced.

"That won't be easy."

"Why not?" Kan called back.

"The elemental's Soul Strength is stronger than mine." Raik pointed toward the floating monster. "I can't directly influence the air around it. Its aura will resist the effect."

"Then don't cool the elemental." I pointed at the rooftop. "Freeze everything else."

"Interesting." Understanding appeared on Raik's face almost immediately.

"Even if you can't affect the monster directly," I continued, "the environment will still steal heat from it."

"I like that plan." A grin spread across the fire mage's face.

Raik took a deep breath, and the temperature plummeted. The already cold air became freezing. Frost spread across the rooftop, creeping over the ancient stone in glittering white patterns. Moisture condensed out of the atmosphere and immediately crystallized into thin sheets of ice.

I shivered violently. Even through my armor, the cold bit into my skin.

"I regret..." I said through chattering teeth, "helping you through that breakthrough."

"Don’t be like that?” grinned the fireboy. I'll restore the heat once the monster is dead."

The effect became visible almost immediately. The elemental's cloud layer began thinning. Not rapidly and not dramatically, but steadily. Water condensed faster than the creature could replenish it. The storm surrounding the starry core grew weaker with every passing second as portions of its cloud mantle dissolved into rain and frost.

The elemental finally realized it was losing.

The winds intensified without warning. A hurricane-force gust slammed into us, forcing everyone to brace themselves. I dropped into a crouch and could see Kan doing the same next to me. Across the rooftop, the others lowered their center of gravity and dug in against the storm.

The chain barrier wasn't nearly as fortunate.

The entire structure collapsed. Spears toppled. Chains crashed across the rooftop. The carefully prepared defense disintegrated in seconds.

"Damn it!" Kan cursed.

Still, the elemental's attack had come too late. The cloud layer was almost gone. Even the outer winds had thinned substantially. Only the lightning remained strong, arc after arc crackling around the exposed stellar core.

And suddenly I knew exactly what it was about to do.

The thing wasn't preparing just another attack. No, this was its final attack. I could see it with my Perception Soulbook.

"Ah, shit!" I shouted.

Nobody had time to ask what I meant. I raised my hand and focused on the star mana that was still being replenished by the shooting stars overhead.

"I wish I was closer to the monster, spear in hand."

The world blinked.

A heartbeat later, I stood almost directly in front of the elemental. The star-filled sphere filled my vision, countless miniature stars glittering within the impossible darkness.

Lightning erupted as the creature reacted instantly. Every drop of its remaining mana became lightning.

Everything.

At the same time, I thrust forward with my spear, cracking part of its shell.

The blast struck my spear and surged into my body. The current passed through my hands, raced up my arms, crossed my chest, and tore through every organ in its path before traveling down my spine, through my legs, and finally into the stone beneath my feet.

Pain became my everything. My muscles locked, my body convulsed, and I could smell my flesh burning.

For a moment, I couldn't even scream. The world became nothing but white light and agony.

Then warmth crashed into me, as a golden light covered me in a soothing aura.

Right. I wasn’t alone; Vena was with me.

The pain lessened enough for me to breathe again. I stumbled backward, barely remaining upright, using my spear as a crutch. My vision was still blurry, and dark spots danced before my eyes, but I could hear the battle continuing around me.

Katar had charged in.

His wooden training sword struck repeatedly against the exposed core, each impact producing another marble-like crack. Kan was using a weighted whip. Her chain had been blown across the tower, and the weighted end slammed into the elemental again and again like a flail. Calr's crossbow fired relentlessly while Raik launched concentrated bursts of flame.

The elemental had exhausted itself trying to kill me.

Now it had nothing left.

For nearly a full minute, my team attacked without interruption. A minute was an eternity in combat, especially against a weakened opponent.

My vision gradually returned, the blinding spots faded, and the rooftop came back into focus.

Cracks covered the starry shell, dozens of them spreading across the surface like fractures in glass.

Another strike from Katar landed.

Crack.

Finally, the shell shattered. Star-filled fragments exploded outward as glittering shards rained across the rooftop like pieces of the broken night sky.

At the center of the destruction remained a single object.

A monster core roughly the size of my fist. Blue and bright, still crackling with tiny sparks, its surface was filled with miniature stars.

"You idiot!" Vena shouted as she rushed toward me, slowly lowering me into a seated position. "That was reckless."

I was still breathing heavily, my muscles spasming as Vena held me and continued healing me while praying about heroes, saints, and sacrifices.

"No, she did the right thing," said Raik, his voice solemn. "Katar and Calr wouldn't have survived that final attack if she hadn't stepped in."

Katar gave me a proud look. I guessed I had regained the little respect I lost back in the slime village.

Kan picked up the monster core and inspected it as Ja'a and Shingo joined us on the roof.

"Remind me why we needed this monster dead," Calr asked as he bent down to pick up his crossbow bolt, or at least the one that remained whole.

"I am..." I said between heavy breaths, "claiming... this tower... as my home."

"Seriously?" said Kan as she placed the star core into my hand.

I gave her a cheeky smile.

"Thank you for the housewarming gift."
