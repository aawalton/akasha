Monster Hunter is an honorable profession that is unfortunately dominated by thugs and bullies. It is up to you all to be better.

- Marcelline Halvern, the first human to reach S-rank. Quote from a recorded lecture to newly signed Monster Hunters at Worldshield HQ on January 5th, 17 FPE, a month before she gave her life in the failed Second Raid to reclaim the San Alvero Arcologies in eastern Veritania.

“Stay back,” Kal said, as he peered into the cavern, feeling the rising clamor of maddening whispers as he caught sight of the shadow-shrouded black Dusk Wraith—the third to arrive in as many minutes, drawn presumably by the sound of the detonations required to kill the Hemorath. Or perhaps the stench of its demonic blood painting the walls.

The Dusk Wraiths were challenging, but after three kills, the hunter team seemed to have this monster’s number. The psychic assault was by far the most devastating of its abilities, but the harvesting team had escaped the worst of it by remaining out of range in the entry tunnel. Kal had checked that hiding out of sight of the monster had no effect on the ability, but curiously, the power grew more potent if he was directly observing the demon, as if the mere sight of the horrifying monster was enough to fry his brain. He closed his eyes, and the whispers immediately muted dramatically.

With a sudden flurry of activity, Drex shoulder-checked the demon, giving Sarn a clear shot at burying both his daggers into its flank, and Brannic swooped in for the kill with a dramatic low slash that slit the monster’s throat right as it raised its head to hiss in pain.

Still, the harvesting team waited, huddled in the damp tunnel for more than ten minutes before Brannic gave the all-clear.

“Ok, let’s go,” Erin said, rousing the shaken harvesting team. “Kal, can you fill those vials with as much of that demonic blood as you can before it all drains away? Start with the Hemorath. The rest of you, go collect as many of those Bloodroot Mushrooms as we can carry. Come, look lively. I know it’s terrifying, but that’s the cost of the first harvester position. They’re all dead now.”

“Right,” Kal said, hefting his pack and reentering the cavern. He made his way over to the mound of smoking demon flesh that was as high as mid-chest on him, even lying dead and burnt. He paused, staring at the enormous fangs and the powerful obsidian claws, not quite sure if the trembling in his muscles was from mana exhaustion, adrenaline, or fear.

Quickly, he unslung his pack, opened it, and retrieved one of the nearly indestructible crystalline polymer vials with NexCraft’s logo printed on the side. He popped the cap and held it to the thin stream of black blood still pouring from the deep wound Brannic had inflicted near the end. It seemed that the monster’s incredible regeneration did not work after it was stabbed through the heart, nor had the enchanted blade fully cauterized the wound inside. If it even has a heart. Given the surprise of a mirror portal, he’d not done as much research on the Bloodshade demons as he would otherwise have. Some blood-trait monsters thrived without hearts, and he remembered reading that some demons had two. Something for later.

Kal worked efficiently, sealing each vial and setting it aside before filling the next. Ten minutes later, the stream of blood was noticeably slowing, and he had worked his way through more than half the vials. Good enough. He carefully stowed the vials on the nearest cart, in one of the iced collection bins, and then worked his way through the Dusk Wraith corpses until he had no more vials left.

As he was stowing the last of the vials and preparing to help with the Bloodroot Mushroom bounty next, Drex strolled back into the cavern, wiping blood and strands of white fur off his silver gauntlet with a rag that he stuffed into a pocket.

“Where the fuck were you?” Brannic demanded.

“Taking care of some business,” Drex said. “You know… not too many bushes around here.”

“We’re in a delve. You don’t get to take a shit without telling me.”

“Yeah. Sorry, boss,” Drex said. But while he looked down, making a show of meekness, Kal saw a mean glint in his eyes.

There’s going to be a showdown between those two…

“Kal? Are you done over there?” Erin called.

“Yep,” he said, hurrying over and beginning to pull up the rubbery, glowing crimson mushrooms that dotted the entire side of the cavern—fortunately, the side that wasn’t scorched and blackened by Tess’s fireball.

By the time he wrapped and stashed the last mushroom, Kal’s muscles ached, and his head felt like it was stuffed with cotton while somebody was playing drums on the inside of his skull. He straightened up, stifling a groan.

“As soon as you guys load up the corpses, we’re moving out,” Brannic said.

Kal grabbed a cart and followed Erin to one of the Dusk Wraiths, while the rest of the team swarmed over the other corpses. He bent and wrapped his arms around one of the front paws of the beast, taking secure fistfuls of velvety fur while Erin took the hindquarters.

“On three,” she muttered. “Uno, dos, tre…urgh.”

Kal heaved, trying to shift the demon toward the cart, but even with two of them, they barely budged it. It didn’t help that his muscles were operating at half strength or worse right now. “Ugh, this is heavy. We’re going to need some more strength.”

“Yeah,” Erin said, panting.

She’s strong. Kal glanced around, searching for a telltale flash of white, but he frowned when he didn’t find it. Where is she? “Hey, Erin. Give me a second, ok?” Quickly, he searched, making sure to check behind the carts and down the entrance passageway they’d hidden in during the fight, but the Lycan girl was nowhere to be found.

“Hey, has anyone seen…” he caught his hands moving toward his ears and stopped. “… the Lycan?”

“I saw her,” Drex said, his grin widening. “Stupid bitch ran away in the middle of the fight with her tail between her legs.” He pointed toward the entrance tunnel.

“We need to go get her,” Kal said. Portals were dangerous, especially for those without mana seeds—and this one was particularly so. Besides, she had been beside him most of the battle and was the only other person without a seed that had weathered the psychic attacks of the Dusk Wraiths unscathed. She definitely didn’t run. There’s no way.

“We don’t have time to go chasing after strays,” Brannic said. “If she’s not here in five minutes, we’re going. We have a portal guardian to clear and an anchor to break.”

“Hey,” Erin said. “You’re legally responsible for protecting us in here. She’s part of my team.”

“I’m legally required to protect a harvesting crew that follows my orders,” Brannic said. “I don’t recall giving anyone permission to run off and explore on their own.”

“We’re going back to find her,” Erin said. “And that’s final.”

“No, we’re not.”

“Do you want to harvest this portal by yourself?” Erin demanded.

“Do you want to fight your way out without me?” Brannic countered, gesturing toward the charred Hemorath still slumped by the wall.

The two of them stood there glaring at each other without speaking, but Kal knew which way it would go. It was obvious. As a D-rank, and the overall authority in this portal world, Brannic held all the cards. He would barely even be reprimanded for a decision like this, if anything, provided he spoke the right words about the dangers, safety of the team, and protecting the mission.

“I’ll go find her,” Kal said.

“Kal…” Erin said.

“Aah, so the guy who risks the entire team by entering a portal with mana sickness wants to be the hero?” Brannic sneered.

Kal glanced at Nirelle, who at least was embarrassed enough to look away. So, she did tell him. Just a little casual betrayal. He turned back to look at Brannic. “So, are you going to give me permission or what?”

Brannic waved a hand dismissively. “Go, if you want to kill yourself. You’re worthless to the team and finished as a harvester as soon as we get out. You go by yourself. You, and only you, have permission.”

Erin froze in the middle of checking the harvesting dagger strapped to her utility belt and frowned. She turned, glaring at Brannic, and opened her mouth.

“Don’t look at me like that,” Brannic said. “I’m not heartless. We have a mission, and I’m responsible for the entire team’s safety—not every little special flower. We’ll wait for them to rejoin us at the portal guardian once we’ve secured the exit portal. The path will be cleared, so it should be safe.”

Erin leveled a glare at him.

“It’s ok, Erin. I’ll be quick,” Kal said, laying a hand gently on her shoulder to stall her from getting into any more trouble. “It should be safe.” Ish.

Erin stared at him for a moment before she broke eye contact and unclipped her dagger. “Here, take this. Just in case.”

“Thanks,” Kal said, leaving unvoiced the obvious—if he encountered anything that required a fight, the dagger was likely to be just as useless as his father’s pistol. He just hoped his legs wouldn’t give out if he had to run.

“Be careful,” Erin said.

“I will,” Kal said and turned, heading out through the tunnel leading back toward the portal entrance. It was only when he was halfway to the next cave that he remembered seeing Drex returning from this direction, wiping blood from his gauntlet. Crimson blood. He had just spent ages collecting nearly a hundred vials of demonic blood. Every single vial had been black. And he was certain he had seen white hair stuck in that sticky red.

Fuck! If he…

Heart suddenly pounding, Kal broke into a run.

Kal sprinted down the long, deserted tunnel toward the chamber where they had found the unexplored passage that had so intrigued him, but as he reached it, he froze at the sound of a grating cough that vibrated through the rock beneath his feet and made his skull shake. His breath caught in his throat as he tried desperately to still his pounding heart.

The chamber was exactly as he remembered it. Expansive and gloomy, with two other exits leading away—one downward and into the unknown, and the other leading to where they had first entered this portal world. A diffuse reddish glow filtered in from the vast underground world beyond the jagged window-like opening in the far wall.

At the opposite end of the cave, silhouetted against that shattered window by the light from beyond, stalked a fearsome silhouette of gleaming black scales and heavy muscles. Beneath the translucent armor of shifting, jagged obsidian spikes, veins of crimson throbbed with regenerative power.

Kal recognized it instantly. Hemorath!

It took a silent step forward, leaving a billowing wake of shadows and a dark red mist that reeked of blood. The fact that such a huge monster could move so silently froze the blood in his veins. It took another step, scales rising into wickedly sharp, protruding spikes running across its flanks in sinuous rippling crimson waves before morphing to black and settling back down flush against its body as an impenetrable layer of obsidian armor. The rumbling growl grew louder.

The demon paused, sniffing the air, and then turned to a massive boulder propped up beside the descending tunnel exit and took a bite. Heavy obsidian fangs crunched through granite like butter, making grinding cracks of splintering stone that rang out through the cavern.

As gravel spilled from the monster’s maw, Kal’s eyes drifted down, drawn to a tuft of white as pure as snow. White fur, at the end of a long stain of crimson blood painted across the floor. From within a small crevice, cowering beneath the fallen boulder, a single violet eye stared out at him.

“Run…” the Lycan whispered.

The Hemorath stooped down and took another massive bite out of the boulder. A rain of pebbles and dust fell from its grinding maw as it crunched and swallowed, drawing a quiet whimper from the Lycan girl as the dubious protection of her hiding spot was steadily devoured.

Kal pulled the trigger.

How the pistol had found its way into his hands, he did not know. He did not remember drawing it. The Chekhov bucked hard against his palm as a deafening bang filled the chamber, and Kal found himself knocked off his feet as his weakened muscles betrayed him. The Hemorath froze, slowly lifting its head as the high-pitched whine of the bullet ricocheting from its heavy obsidian scales echoed within the cave.

Quickly, Kal aimed again, grateful to suddenly find some remnant of strength to steady his shaking hands and fired twice more. The first shot hit an obsidian plate, leaving a spiderweb of cracks along armored glass. The second, pulled off target by the recoil, found a gap between the hind leg and the belly. Dark blood burst from the wound, and the monster roared, wheeling to face him as the crimson veins within its body began to pulse faster.

Kal dragged his pistol back on target as he scrambled back to his feet, beginning to circle wide, and pulled the trigger again. This time, his bullet passed between the obsidian fangs as the monster roared and found its mark deep in the demon’s gullet. The jaws slammed shut with a crack as it coughed once. Then it turned to fix him with a baleful glare, opening its maw to roar once more.

Kal pulled the trigger again, but this time, the monster vanished, and his bullet left whirling vortices through a cloud of red and black mist, ricocheting off the wall behind.

Fuck, it has a second skill! Where…

An instant later, the massive demon appeared from within a dark burst of mist right in front of him. Huge jaws crunched down on his left shoulder, snapping his ribs in sharp flashes of pain. Kal screamed as he struggled to escape the impaling fangs.

Then, seen out of the corner of his eye, a bloody white streak shot across the chamber, violet eyes flashing in the darkness as the Lycan bit down with a ferocious snarl on an unarmored spot near the base of the demon’s lashing tail.

Damn, she…

The Hemorath flinched and, with all the leftover momentum from its improbable movement skill, crashed heavily into Kal’s chest.

Kal gasped as the sudden impact of several tons of demonic fury carried them sliding across the uneven, rocky ground toward the broken hole in the wall at his back—the window into the larger portal world outside. The fangs were lodged deeply in his shoulder and ribs, but he still had a good grip on his father’s pistol. As his feet lost all contact with the ground and his gut lurched with sudden weightlessness, he ground his teeth together and twisted against the agony of his broken body. Demonic fangs scraped in concert with the creaking of his strained ribs inside his chest, but he shoved the muzzle up against a hateful red eye, yelled, and pulled the trigger. A deafening crack rang out, and the jaws loosened. He fired again. Dark blood sprayed everywhere, searing his hand like boiling acid, but he bore down and pulled the trigger a third time as they tangled, tumbling in the air.

Click.

The heavy demonic obsidian scales beneath him leaped up and hit his body with the force of a speeding truck.

Several sickening cracks greeted the impact, felt from within more than heard. The surge of overwhelming pain was mercifully muted as he plunged headlong into the icy cold embrace of darkness.

Mira… I’m sorry. I wasn’t strong en—

Then, everything faded.
