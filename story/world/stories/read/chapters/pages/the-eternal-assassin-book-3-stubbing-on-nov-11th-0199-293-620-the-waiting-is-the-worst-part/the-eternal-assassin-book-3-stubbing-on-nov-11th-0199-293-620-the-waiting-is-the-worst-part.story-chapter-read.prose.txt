While the village near the cave system The Lost Lady was hiding in wasn’t quite as far as the Deadlands had been, it still took a dedicated pegasus courier the better part of three days to reach it. Asher supposed he’d simply grown a little too used to the convenience of overnight shipping back on Earth, and he tried not to let himself grow impatient as they waited.

He had plenty to keep himself busy regardless.

“Oh ho, would you look at that! That stab actually looked like it came from an adult man!” Mornamol called out, laughing at him as Asher gritted his teeth and went right back to trying his damndest to gut his dagger instructor. Mornamol had made it abundantly clear by now that trying to defeat him with his own element was a lesson in futility, but Asher was damned if he wouldn’t try. Half the time he got so aggravated from Mornamol’s teasing he had to physically prevent himself from going for his void dagger just to cut one of the man’s magical daggers that kept appearing out of nowhere in half.

The two of them sparred for another thirty minutes before Mornamol eventually called for them to take a break. While the man was far better at fighting with daggers than Asher was, if there was one thing Asher had over him it was the fact that Eternal Regeneration pretty much gave him infinite stamina.

“That’s one lucky gal you’ve got,” Mornamol grinned, the old slayer winking at him after taking a long drink from his canteen. “I won’t tell you how to live your life, but if I had that level of stamina when I was your age, I wouldn’t have tied myself down so soon.”

“I bet not,” Asher said, rolling his eyes as he waited for his instructor to recover. He wasn’t sure exactly why he’d done so, but he’d ended up telling the old man about his accidental proposal during one of their sparring sessions, leaving out anything related to his Eternity element. He certainly didn’t see Mornamol as anything even closely related to a father figure, but having spent enough days dealing with his teasing and instruction, he’d almost grown into that weird, mildly annoying uncle that was sort of fun to have around and occasionally offered good life advice.

Occasionally.

“Never too late to change your mind!” Mornamol said, tipping his canteen to him as he laughed. “You’re too young to worry about any of that stuff anyway! Life goes by faster than you know it, you have to have a little fun in your youth before you get old and crotchety like me!”

“I can honestly say I’m not planning on ever getting old and crotchety like you.”

“Nobody ever does,” Mornamol snorted, capping his canteen and tossing it to the side. “Anyway, what’s this team of yours up to next? Going after another one of those Council members?”

“That’s the plan,” Asher nodded. “To be honest, it’s still weird to me that you don’t seem at all fazed by the fact that I’m actively trying to kill them, and slowly succeeding. Most people seem to freak out about the Council when they come up.”

“Most people aren’t old as dirt,” Mornamol grinned, gesturing to the way his knees popped as he pushed himself back to his feet. Despite the fact that the retired slayer couldn’t be older than his late fifties or early sixties, Mornamol had a bad habit of pretending like he was on the cusp of death from old age. “I told you, I may not be one of those fancy greater powers we talked about earlier, but I’ve seen plenty of crap. If those liches want to come after me, then let them. If they’re as bad at fighting as you are, I've got nothing to worry about.”

“You just can’t help yourself, can you?” Asher laughed, pulling out his steel daggers once more. “Good to go?”

His answer was a dagger punching into his back out of nowhere. Rather than gasp in pain, he simply shook his head and glared at the grinning slayer.

“How do you keep doing that?!”

“Did you seriously buy another rug since the last time I saw you?” Asher asked, raising an eyebrow as he took in Rosh’s small bedroom.

“Excuse me for enjoying the finer things in life beyond finding a new target to stab,” Rosh scoffed, looking offended before smiling and motioning for Asher to come in. “Thanks for using the front door this time instead of scaring me half to death. To what do I owe the pleasure?”

“Nothing much,” Asher admitted, grabbing one of the two chairs in the room and taking a seat. “My team and I are just about to head out on another dangerous mission, and I thought I’d make a quick stop to see you. It’s been a bit since we last hung out, and I’ll admit I had a few questions for you as an expert in logistics as well.”

“You do, do you?” Rosh said, his eyes twinkling as he sat across from him. Asher was well aware that Rosh loved logistics more than anything, which was the main reason why Draken had been so quick to hire the man to take care of most of the annoying parts of running such a large gang. According to him, Rosh was a genuine miracle worker. “Ask away!”

“Straight into it? Don’t even want to catch up first?” Asher laughed.

“We can catch up after,” Rosh snapped, tapping the table between them. “Come now, let’s hear it!”

“Alright, it’s a pretty simple question. My team and I are about to start exploring a giant cave system in the hopes of finding a single person. We’ve got most of the basics figured out as far as I can think of, such as potions of Darkvision for our members who need them and chalk to mark tunnels we’ve already explored so we don’t waste time backtracking. Are we missing anything?”

“Well, there’s the obvious,” Rosh said, drumming his fingers along the table as he thought. “For starters, is this person someone who wants to be found, or are they hiding?”

“Presumably hiding. Though I suppose there’s a chance they are so arrogant they might just be relying on the cave’s complexity to be enough to keep people away.”

“If they are hiding, then that makes your search infinitely more difficult,” Rosh explained. “You need to factor in elements and skills that could possibly allow a person to fit through cracks and crevices no normal-sized person could. Teleportation skills are always an issue of course, but I’m talking about things like turning oneself into a liquid or shrinking down in size. If the person you’re searching for can do that and you can’t, you’ll never find them. There could be any number of tunnels or caverns only accessible through such methods.”

“That… is a very good point,” Asher muttered, realizing he’d completely overlooked that fact. There was no reason why the lich couldn’t have a skill along those lines, doubly so seeing as they didn’t even know what two of her elements were.

“It’s a fairly common problem to pose to students in the military academy,” Rosh continued. “When keeping one’s eyes peeled for ambushes, skills such as those have to be taken into account.”

“Thanks, Rosh, I’ll run that by the rest of the team and see what we can’t come up with. There’s got to be a potion or something that can help.”

“Always happy to help! Now, with that out of the way, I actually have a question I’ve been meaning to ask you for some time now,” Rosh admitted, giving him a strange look. “It’s been a couple of months now, and things seem more stable than ever in Whikoga… Do I really have to keep all these shards on me like you told me to?”

“Huh?” Asher said, blinking at the question. “What shards?”

Shaking his head, Rosh gave him a pointed look. “Are you serious? Ages ago now, you came up to me out of the blue while I was out shopping and dragged me into an alleyway before shoving a bunch of shards into my hands and demanding I keep them on me. You spouted some strange nonsense about a trusted source of yours being able to see the future and then all but ordered that I keep the shards on me at all times. You seemed pretty panicked and stressed at the time, so I figured there was no harm in at least humoring you. But as I said, that was months ago now, and here I am, still holding your original eighteen thousand, three hundred and forty-two shards on my person for seemingly no reason. I personally prefer to keep my shards secured in a bank so they can be donated upon my death in the unlikely event of my demise, and keeping this many on me is a tad unnerving.”

“Wow, I completely forgot about that,” Asher admitted, unable to stop himself from laughing at Rosh’s annoyed look. “Sorry! I didn’t mean for my request to make you uncomfortable! Though I appreciate you’re still holding onto those. I’ll admit I don’t actually know why I was told to give you those, just that I would be thankful if I did.”

“Yes, well, Loratta is long since dead and even the Assassin’s Guild in Whikoga has been dealt with,” Rosh pointed out. “Would you like your shards back, or can I at least put them in the bank for now?”

“Sorry, Rosh, can you hold onto them for a bit longer? I can ask my original source and see if I can’t get a more straightforward explanation out of him. Hypothetically, do you know how many shards you would need to bring yourself back from the brink of death? I know your Shard element has that healing power, I’m wondering if that might have been the reason.”

“I’ve never been on the brink of death, so I don’t know,” Rosh admitted. “It only took me a few thousand to restore my legs when Loratta had her goons smash them to pieces. Eighteen thousand seems like a decent-enough guess. But how would your source know that?”

“Trust me, you don’t want to know,” Asher smiled. “You told me before you didn’t want to delve back into all this serious stuff I deal with, right? I’m assuming that still holds true?”

“You know what, you’re right,” Rosh nodded, leaning back in his chair and looking gratefully at his small bookshelf filled with dozens of novels. “I think I’ll stick with my books and my rugs and keep my forbidden element under wraps. You do plenty of dangerous stuff for the both of us. But please do ask about the shards the next time you get the chance.”

“I’ll do what I can,” Asher promised.

The rest of his evening was spent catching up with the former instructor and getting to hear all about his mundane life that was everything Rosh had ever wanted. Despite the fact that he was on the payroll of the city’s largest gang leader, Rosh lived an astonishingly boring life. He ate the same meals for breakfast and lunch most days, spent his free time reading or perusing the markets, and played cards with some friendly faces in a nearby tavern every so often. His most exciting stories often centered around trying new dinner foods that Asher would hesitate to even call ‘daring’, and Asher couldn’t help but laugh when Rosh all but forced him to remove his shoes and try walking on the two different rugs, pointing out the difference in thread count and materials used.

With the sheer amount of craziness Asher’s life consisted of these days, from hunting down members of The Council of Death to living within the royal palace and having the High Prince on speed dial, something about the mundane, simple aspects of Rosh’s life that made him so happy were a welcome sight. Rosh was literally his first friend he’d ever made in this world after Samantha and Brian, and he liked seeing how a semi-normal person lived. It helped keep him grounded and reminded him that they weren’t just going after the Council for their own sakes, but for the sake of ordinary people like Rosh who weren’t even aware of what was at stake. By the time Asher finished catching up and headed back to the palace, only to discover that the pegasus courier had arrived and his Eternal Mark was in position, he was refreshed and ready to go.

If everything went according to plan, the Council of Death was about to be brought down into the single digits.
