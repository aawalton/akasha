They reached the forest at a run. Only when the filthy runoff and the high wall of Ogrom were somewhere behind them did Sakura finally let herself slow down a little. Her legs ached, her clothes clung to her body, and the smell on them was still so bad that Sakura had already accepted one simple truth: if some monster stepped out of the bushes right now, it would not attack. It would just politely ask them to leave.

The forest met them with damp silence, darkness between the trunks, and the rustle of branches. Somewhere far away, a night bird cried out. Moss gave softly underfoot, and after stone, slime, tunnels, and sewers, that felt almost luxurious.

“I think it’s here,” Gilbert said quietly, looking over the brush.

Sakura recognized the place right away too. The same low slope. The same pile of branches, moss, and leaves. It was the makeshift shelter they had thrown together for Pampli, only now it looked denser, as if the forest itself had decided to help hide something very dissatisfied.

A muffled growl came from inside the branches. Then something moved. A second later, Pampli crawled out of the shelter.

At first he saw Sakura, and it was like his whole body came alive at once. He leaned forward, let out a delighted snort, and even twitched his tail like he was ready to flatten half the forest with the force of his happiness. Sakura smiled without meaning to, already opening her arms to hug him.

Then Pampli caught the smell and came to a sharp stop. He drew in the air slowly, and his expression changed so dramatically that Sakura did not even fully understand how insulting it was at first. He took one step back. Then another.

“Are you serious?” she asked.

Pampli looked at her with such a mix of joy, horror, and reproach that it was as if she had not returned from a dangerous city, but from some deeply questionable swamp where she had chosen to live for the last three months.

Sakura stepped toward him again, but the beast immediately jumped back to the side. That was too much. She burst out laughing.

“All right,” she said, half breathless with laughter and exhaustion. “All right. I get it. Poor thing.”

Pampli made a sound full of profound moral injury.

Gilbert’s only response was to pull a cleansing scroll from his Inventory. He unrolled it, and cold white light slid over his shoulders, hair, and clothes. A few seconds later there was not even a memory of Ogrom’s sewers left on him. He looked like someone who had never, at any point in his life, encountered anything that humiliatingly foul.

Sakura slowly turned her head toward him. Gilbert met her gaze with a perfectly innocent face. For a second, they just looked at each other.

“Don’t even think about it,” Sakura said.

“Think about what exactly?”

“About continuing to pretend you don’t understand what I want.”

Gilbert tilted his head thoughtfully.

“Hm. Difficult question.”

“Gilbert.”

“I think the swamp look suits you.”

Sakura stared at him.

“What?”

“Very practical, if you think about it. No one would recognize you as the Obsidian Hierophant like this. They’d assume you were some kind of swamp monster.”

Sakura immediately noticed that, unlike many other scrolls, this one was not single-use. Along the edge of the paper, a short line was clearly visible:

Uses Remaining: 2/3

She snorted and reached insistently for the scroll, but Gilbert simply lifted his hand a little higher.

“Think about it again,” he said in a completely serious tone. “Maybe it would be wiser to keep the foul-smelling disguise. Judging by Pampli’s reaction, even other obsidian creatures won’t want to come near you now.”

At that exact moment, Pampli very helpfully lifted a paw and covered his nose with it, making a sound somewhere between a wounded whine and a deeply embarrassed one.

Sakura nearly choked with indignation.

“Even he’s mocking me,” she said. “Give me the scroll right now, you greedy menace.”

“‘Greedy’ is a rude word.”

“Leaving me like this is rude too.”

She made another grab for the scroll. Gilbert moved his hand aside with ease, clearly enjoying the situation far more than he should have. Sakura could feel herself actually flushing with outrage, her blood boiling. This was one of those moments when she wanted not only to snatch the scroll from him, but also to hit him over the head with it once, purely for educational purposes.

“Sakura, just think about the strategic value.”

“I’m about to hit you strategically.”

She looked at him with such sincerity that Gilbert finally gave in. He sighed and handed the scroll over.

“All right. The world isn’t ready for you to smell like that any longer.”

“Otherwise you’d have made yourself a terrible enemy, Gilbert.”

Sakura unrolled the scroll with almost reverent care and drew on its magic. Cold light slid down over her from head to toe, instantly wiping the clinging filth off her skin, hair, and clothes. She almost moaned with relief.

“Oh...” was all she managed. “All right. I’m human again.”

And in the exact same second, Pampli launched himself at her.

He knocked her off her feet so enthusiastically it was as if he had been waiting all this time for the chance to show his feelings without risking his nose. Sakura hit the moss before she could even complain, and Pampli immediately shoved his snout into her shoulder, neck, and hair, rumbling with his huge body and rubbing against her like a delighted dog.

Sakura only smiled and finally wrapped both arms around the beast.

“There,” she murmured, resting her forehead against his neck. “Now you’re allowed, right?”

Pampli snorted happily. She ran a hand along his side, then added more quietly,

“I’m sorry you had to wait so long.”

The beast flicked an ear and pressed himself closer.

That short, oddly warm moment ended as quickly as it had begun. The forest was not a place for long rest. Not now.

Sakura got back to her feet, brushing pine needles and moss off herself, then walked over to Gilbert. For several seconds she simply looked at him. The way he stood there so calmly, without the bracelets, with his magic finally his again. After everything that had happened, it suddenly felt almost unreal.

“I really am glad,” she said. “That your strength is back. We did it.”

Gilbert gave a small nod.

“Yes.”

There was no wide smile on his face, but his voice deepened.

“For a mage, that matters,” he said after a brief pause. “To feel your magic with you. Not somewhere nearby. Not in memory. Not in hope. In yourself. It’s as much a part of your body as your hands or your legs. When it’s taken away, you’re still alive. You can still walk, think, talk. But you still understand that someone made you incomplete.”

Sakura listened in silence. Those words sounded too honest for her to answer with a joke.

Then another name rose inside her again.

Lir.

Sakura looked away.

“Now we have to go after Lir,” she said. “In spite of Faerd. In spite of everything. We have to come for him.”

The moment she said it out loud, guilt tightened painfully in her chest again. Lir. Where was he right now? What were they doing to him? Was he sitting alone in some cell, scared, hurt, not understanding why any of this had happened? Were they hurting him? Pressuring him? And the ugliest part was that deep down, Sakura felt as if, while she was here and he was there, she was letting it happen. She couldn’t pull him out right now. She couldn’t tear through the distance between them by force of will alone. And that hurt physically.

Gilbert watched her closely. Then he sighed and sat down under a tree, resting his back against the rough trunk.

“Lir is probably being held in the Castle of Justice,” he said. “Beyond the Lake of Six Mists.”

Sakura frowned.

“What kind of place is that?”

“A fortress. Heavily guarded, not just by people, but by poisonous mists. They send the important prisoners there. Special captives. Anyone considered too dangerous for an ordinary prison. Ordinary city dungeons are for crowds. The Castle of Justice is for the ones they can’t just throw behind bars and forget.”

Those words made her even colder.

“So we can’t just break in, take Lir, and run,” she said, less as a question and more as a conclusion.

“No,” Gilbert answered calmly. “No matter how hard we try, that won’t work. At least not like that.”

Sakura clenched her fingers.

“Then we need to come up with something else.”

“Already thinking.”

She looked at him. Gilbert was silent for another second, then said,

“To get into the Castle of Justice, one of us will have to let the soldiers take us. That’ll be me.”

Sakura stared at him as if he had just proposed lying down in a grave voluntarily and seeing how it went.

“What?”

“It’s a workable option.”

“No, it’s a catastrophically bad option,” she shot back at once. “What do you mean, surrender? Why are you even saying that this calmly? It’s too risky.”

Gilbert didn’t argue head-on. He just tilted his head slightly, as if he had expected exactly that reaction.

“I’ve been imprisoned before,” he said. “And I know places like the Castle of Justice. I know how they’re built, how they hold prisoners, how the internal posts work, what the security patterns look like, and where even the smallest chance to pull something off from the inside might appear.”

Sakura still looked dark.

“That doesn’t make it better.”

“Actually, it does.”

He went on, a little harder now.

“If it’s staged as an accident, they’ll be happy to take me. Especially if I present myself as a rogue mage, not someone connected to the Obsidian Hierophant. Then they shouldn’t tie you to it right away.”

Sakura lifted her head sharply.

“‘Shouldn’t’ sounds wonderful. And you’re suggesting I stay outside somewhere while you go in alone and look for Lir?”

“Yes.”

Sakura’s stare grew heavier.

“This is too vague,” she said at last. “There are way too many ‘ifs.’ And I don’t want to risk you.”

Gilbert closed his eyes for a second, as if he had expected that argument too, but had no intention of answering it gently.

“We’re already risking me. And you. And Lir. The question isn’t whether there will be risk. The question is whether it gives us a chance.”

Sakura exhaled heavily and shook her head.

“No. If you’re going in there, the plan has to be thought through down to the smallest detail. Down to the ugliest, most obsessive details. Otherwise they’ll just lock you up beside Lir and that’ll be the end of it. I’ll lose both of you.”

“Of course,” Gilbert answered calmly. “I need more time to think through every detail. For now, we should head deeper into the forest and get at least a little rest. After Ogrom, they’re probably searching the entire area for us. You know that as well as I do.”

Sakura nodded. That was hard to argue with.

They kept going, leading Pampli deeper between the trees. As they walked, Gilbert circled back to the subject Sakura couldn’t stop thinking about.

“The Castle of Justice,” he said quietly, stepping over a thick root, “isn’t just a prison. It’s a fortress with an internal system that cuts off magic, a multi-stage inspection process, and identification checks at every critical level. The outer zones are guarded with brute force and mist. But everything involving mages is handled much more seriously than in ordinary city dungeons.”

Sakura listened with a dark expression.

“How much more seriously?”

Gilbert gave a humorless huff.

“The good news is, they don’t put anti-magic bracelets or shackles on the mages there. The bad news is, they check everything. Belongings, Inventory, artifacts, even certain alchemical alterations. That’s why the Castle of Justice is where they keep the people who can’t be trusted to ordinary guards.”

“You talk like you’ve been there.”

“I’ve been in places like it,” he answered dryly.

Sakura shivered.

“So how exactly are you planning to get in? Just walk up to the soldiers and say, ‘Hello, I’m a suspicious mage, please arrest me’?”

“Not exactly, though your version has its charm.”

He paused at a short incline, waited for Pampli to climb up ahead, and only then continued.

“To get sent there specifically, it won’t be enough just to get caught. I have to become valuable and dangerous enough. Not so dangerous that they kill me on the spot, but dangerous enough that they won’t throw me into the first pit they can find. That means we’ll need to spread rumors.”

“What kind of rumors?”

“About a rebellion. A magical rebellion that may be about to start. Supposedly there are mages gathering somewhere nearby. Supposedly they already have an organizer. Charismatic, smart, not entirely sane, and with very bad taste in enemies.”

Sakura slowly turned her head toward him.

“And that organizer would be you.”

“Under another name,” Gilbert clarified calmly. “Not Gilbert. Someone the System will accept as a different person.”

Sakura actually stopped in shock.

“Wait. I don’t understand anything. How can the System accept you as someone else? It’s the System. Does it even make mistakes?”

Gilbert gave her exactly the sort of look that always made Sakura want either to shake him by the shoulders or demand a real explanation immediately.

“It’s a complicated setup,” he said. “A manipulation of how the System reads the host. It’ll see something different from what it saw before. Or not all of it. I’ll explain later. After we get proper rest.”

Sakura stared at him for another few seconds, then let out an irritated breath. More mysteries. More promises. But at least Gilbert really did intend to tell her the truth later. Maybe that was a good thing, because after the sewers and the lab, her brain still felt like it was boiling, and they really did need a break. The local sun had climbed high enough that this could now be called a gray morning, and Sakura still had not slept.

It took them a while to find a place to stop. Gilbert rejected open areas right away, and he rejected damp-bottomed ravines too, unwilling to add pneumonia to their list of current problems. Pampli grumbled every time they had to make another useless circle. Eventually they found an old fallen tree that had once been ripped up by the roots. Its massive root system, fused with the earth, formed a natural niche hidden behind thick brush. Another large tree leaned over it, and the branches above tangled tightly enough that even from nearby it did not draw the eye.

“Here,” Gilbert said.

Sakura looked over the hiding place and nodded. By now her legs had gone almost completely numb with fatigue. She wanted nothing more than to drop to the ground and fall asleep, even on bare dirt.

“It looks a little like some forest monster’s den.”

“Which means no one will come check.”

She didn’t argue with that iron logic. Pampli climbed in first, sniffed the moss, gave a token grumble, and finally settled down.

Sakura sat beside him and forced herself to remember that her stomach was empty before she passed out. She pulled a ration from her Inventory. Dry, not especially inspiring, but even this felt luxurious after the nightmare of a night they had just survived. She silently offered part of it to Gilbert, and he took it without comment.

For the first few minutes, they ate in silence.

Simple as it was, the food did what neither words, nor the forest, nor temporary safety could do. The world stopped feeling like one endless seizure of survival and became the taste of food, the cold air, and the comfort of real fatigue.

Sakura swallowed another bite, then looked at Gilbert.

“My talent was identified.”

His gaze lifted immediately.

“Finally.”

“‘Bond Weaver.’”

Gilbert narrowed his eyes slightly, as if fitting the name into a picture he had already begun to see.

“That’s a good talent.”

“Just good?”

“Very good, if you learn how to use it. It isn’t about direct power or aggression, but sometimes that’s actually better. Especially in a world full of magically maimed and forcibly altered beings.”

After a second of silence, Sakura finally asked:

“What about yours? What’s your talent?”

And that was when Gilbert gave his first real smirk in several minutes. Not broad, but obviously amused.

“You’d never guess.”

Sakura frowned.

“Answer properly for once. No riddles.”

“Oh, I already answered.”

“Gilbert. I’m serious.”

“So am I. You won’t guess.”

She stared at him with growing suspicion.

“Why are you dodging the question?”

Gilbert looked away somewhere into the trees and gave a small shrug.

“Because it’s... personal.”

Sakura blinked.

“A talent?”

“Yes. People don’t always talk about that openly. Usually only family, very close people, or those you share a strong bond with know your talent.”

He said it without joking, and Sakura understood at once that pressing further would be wrong.

“All right,” she said more quietly. “But one day I’ll find out anyway.”

“One day,” Gilbert agreed.

After that, the conversation died down on its own. The fatigue had grown too strong again. Gilbert took the first watch, while Sakura removed one of Pampli’s saddle bags, tucked it under her head, leaned against his solid side, and dropped into a heavy sleep.

When Gilbert woke her, the forest had changed a little. Either it had grown lighter and less hostile, or her head simply worked more clearly after a few hours of sleep.

“Your turn,” he said quietly.

Sakura nodded, straightened up, and took the watch while Gilbert lay down by the roots and shut his eyes almost immediately. Even asleep, though, he kept that same taut readiness that sometimes unsettled her. As if he rested the same way he did everything else, expecting a blow at any second.

After that, time blurred a little. When they had both recovered at least somewhat, they got ready to move again. Pampli was already on his feet, shaking himself off and making it very clear that he had no intention of sitting in one place forever.

Gilbert adjusted the strap of his bag, and Sakura tucked the remains of the ration back into her Inventory before glancing at him.

“Why do you even need a bag when you have an Inventory?”

“For starters, so I don’t feel like an idiot when someone blocks my Inventory.”

Sakura blinked, then remembered the ugly metal bracelet he had broken off his ankle by the river.

“All right. Fair point. But someone could steal your bag.”

“They could,” Gilbert agreed. “That’s why there’s nothing in it I’d cry too much over.”

“Oh, so you can cry?” she joked.

“In theory.”

Gilbert rolled his eyes tiredly, then added more seriously, “You keep the most important things in the Inventory. Everything else gets distributed so that one bad moment doesn’t leave you with absolutely nothing.”

“Fair...”

They were just about to head deeper into the forest and return to the plan on the move when Pampli froze.

Not merely tensed up. Froze completely, with his head raised and his ears rigid.

Sakura stopped too.

“What is it?”

And only then did she hear it.

Music.

Faint. Very far away. But unmistakable. It drifted through the forest in such a strange way that it felt as though someone were playing not for people, but for the trees themselves. A thin melody, much too light and beautiful for this place after everything they had been through.

Pampli let out a low growl.
