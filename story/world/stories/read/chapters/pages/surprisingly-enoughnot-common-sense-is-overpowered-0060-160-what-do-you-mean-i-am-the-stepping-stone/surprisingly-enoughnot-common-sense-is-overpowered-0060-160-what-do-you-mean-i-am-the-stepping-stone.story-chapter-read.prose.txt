In the competition, Ning was in a strong position.

So far, he had not lost a single match. His record stood at three wins and zero losses, which naturally meant he would now be paired against opponents with similar standings.

After breaking through to the Sixth Stage, Ning was also about as strong as he could reasonably become before the competition ended. Among the remaining participants, there were only a few he was genuinely wary of.

Two in particular stood out.

The first was, obviously, Xiao Fan.

For Ning, Xiao Fan might be the most dangerous opponent, not purely because of his strength, but because of his unpredictability. He was exactly the kind of person who might suddenly power up in the middle of a fight or pull out some outrageous trump card at the last second.

After all, he had already revealed an Earth-grade technique out of nowhere, something most outer sect disciples would never have the chance to acquire. Ning wasn't willing to put anything past him.

The second was Qiu Han.

For a much simpler reason.

He was clearly the strongest participant Ning had seen so far. His combat fundamentals were solid, his martial arts were equally formidable, and Ning had yet to notice any obvious weakness he could exploit.

Unfortunately, the information he had gathered from various brokers was sparse. Qiu Han had defeated most of his opponents using basic techniques, revealing very little of his actual arsenal. He also had a habit of allowing his opponents to unleash their strongest moves before crushing them head-on, apparently just to prove that he could.

Even from the reports alone, the man sounded like a piece of work.

As long as he avoided those two, Ning felt confident that he could put up a good fight against anyone else.

Unfortunately, as the saying went, Man proposes, Heaven disposes.

His opponent for the fourth match was Qiu Han.

Ning had known he would have to face one of these monsters eventually. That didn't mean he had to be happy about it.

The formation barrier rose around the platform, pale light sealing the two contestants inside.

Across the arena, Qiu Han stood with his hands loosely at his sides.

As in his previous matches, he was empty-handed. Like Xiao Fan, he simply preferred fighting without a weapon.

Even at a glance, Ning could tell he wasn't nervous in the slightest. His posture was relaxed and his breathing steady, carrying the confidence of someone accustomed to standing above his peers.

Qiu Han was the first to speak.

"I remember you," he said calmly. "You finished your preliminary match faster than everyone else."

A faint smile touched his lips.

"Besides me, of course."

Ning blinked. "...Okay?"

To be honest, he was genuinely puzzled. Why did everyone insist on exchanging a few dramatic lines before a fight? It seemed to be some kind of unwritten rule among cultivators.

Qiu Han continued as though Ning's response hadn't been unusual.

"I want to see whether that performance was genuine," he said, "or merely good fortune."

Ning offered no reply. There wasn't much worth saying.

Qiu Han stood at the top of the rankings, while Ning was the challenger. If he wanted an answer, the arena would provide it soon enough.

The elder lowered his sleeve.

"Begin."

Ning moved the instant the signal was given. His fingers flowed through a series of hand seals as cold spiritual qi burst outward.

[Hidden Ice Mist]

Frost-white mist erupted across the arena in a single breath, swallowing the stone platform beneath a dense veil of cold. Before the fog had even finished expanding, Ning had already shifted into Turtle Breathing, suppressing his presence while drawing his bow in one smooth motion.

Thwip.

The first arrow vanished into the haze.

Ordinarily, that opening shot was the most dangerous. Most opponents needed at least a moment to adjust after suddenly losing their vision, and that moment was often enough for Ning to establish control of the fight.

Qiu Han, however, simply continued walking through the mist.

Ning's eyes narrowed as he launched several more arrows from different directions, Shadow Steps carrying him silently through the fog between each shot. He wasn't aiming for a decisive hit yet. A dodge, a flinch, even the slightest disturbance would be enough to tell him how Qiu Han was dealing with the mist.

None came.

Ning had encountered plenty of counters to Hidden Ice Mist before. Some cultivators used wind techniques to disperse it, while others relied on sharpened senses or specialized perception arts.

Qiu Han did none of those things. He simply moved through the fog at a leisurely pace, weaving around every arrow that entered his vicinity.

Then, without warning, he accelerated.

Ning immediately changed position, but Qiu Han adjusted just as quickly, following him through the mist as though he had known where Ning would move before he did.

A sharp crack echoed through the fog as another arrow reached him.

This time, Qiu Han snapped it in half.

Ning's hand instinctively brushed across the compartments of his quiver. Standard arrows, Poison Lily-coated arrows, and a handful tipped with Serpent Flower extract.

Under the tournament rules, poison had to be delivered through direct contact; anything capable of lingering in the environment had been prohibited.

Which meant none of it mattered if he couldn't land a hit.

Ning abandoned the idea of sniping and began firing in rapid succession instead. Arrows tore through the fog from one direction after another, not to strike Qiu Han directly, but to restrict his movement and force him to reveal exactly how he was avoiding them.

At the same time, Pure Eyes quietly circulated.

Although Clear Sight couldn't see through Hidden Ice Mist, it could still help Ning catch the subtle disturbances within it. After watching Qiu Han avoid several more arrows with the same effortless precision, the answer began to take shape.

Even while strolling through the mist as though taking an afternoon walk, Qiu Han still found time to speak.

"Mist to obscure sight. Not bad," he said calmly. "Unfortunately, you chose the wrong opponent."

Ning ignored the second half of that sentence. He was more interested in confirming his suspicion.

"Have you..." He released another arrow as he spoke. "...broken through to the Seventh Stage?"

Qiu Han tilted his head slightly, allowing the arrow to pass beside him. "Hm? Your perception is quite good."

He made no attempt to conceal it.

"Yes. I've reached the Seventh Stage of Qi Condensation."

The moment he admitted it, the crowd erupted.

"What?! Seventh Stage already?"

"No wonder! He's awakened spiritual sense!"

"Then concealment techniques are practically useless against him!"

The reaction was understandable. The Seventh Stage was the first true bottleneck of Qi Condensation, and crossing it came with one particularly troublesome benefit.

Spiritual sense.

That explained almost everything Ning had seen so far.

Qiu Han wasn't seeing through Hidden Ice Mist. He didn't need to. His newly awakened spiritual sense might only extend a short distance around his body, but anything entering that range would be detected without relying on sight.

As long as he moved carefully through the mist, Ning's arrows would eventually enter that area and reveal themselves before they reached him.

Ning had simply run into one of Hidden Ice Mist's natural counters.

This is troublesome.

Qiu Han continued advancing without hurry. "I saw your previous match."

Another arrow entered his reach and broke between his fingers.

"But if you can't touch me, none of those tricks will matter."

Ning didn't respond. Qiu Han wasn't wrong, and revealing the poison against Jin Shu had only made the situation worse. His opponents now knew to watch for it, while the tournament restrictions prevented him from using anything that could spread through the mist itself.

Qiu Han took another step forward and suddenly accelerated.

This time, Ning barely had time to react. One moment Qiu Han was several paces away; the next, he had already entered striking range.

Ning abandoned the bow and pulled Friendship from his back, bringing the spear across his body just as Qiu Han's palm descended.

Clang!

The spear shaft collided with flesh, yet the impact rang like steel striking iron. Ning immediately redirected the force through Four-Sided Stability, rooting his feet and aligning his body so the shock traveled into the stone beneath him instead of taking the full impact through his arms.

Even so, numbness raced from his palms to his shoulders.

Qiu Han gave him no time to recover. His palm flowed into an elbow, then straight into a knee, every movement compact and aimed at a vulnerable point. Ning blocked the first attack and redirected the second, but the third slipped through his defense and crashed into his ribs, sending him skidding backward across the frost-covered arena.

Qiu Han followed at the same unhurried pace as before, as though Ning's retreat merely meant the fight had moved a few steps farther away.

"You've tempered your body well. Otherwise, that would have ended the match."

Ning wiped the blood from the corner of his mouth and immediately began forming another set of seals.

[Small Cloud Rain Technique]

Dark clouds condensed overhead, and a heavy downpour crashed into the arena. Ning guided the cold qi lingering within Hidden Ice Mist into the falling water, freezing the droplets into countless tiny shards before they reached the ground.

He wasn't expecting them to injure Qiu Han. What he needed was interference.

A newly awakened spiritual sense could only process so much information at once. If every direction around Qiu Han was suddenly filled with thousands of tiny disturbances, Ning might be able to create enough noise to hide his own movements within them.

For a brief moment, it seemed to work.

Qiu Han looked upward and sighed. "You aren't the only one who can cast quickly."

[Quick]

His fingers blurred. Hand seals that should have required several breaths were completed almost instantly.

[Blazing Surge]

A controlled wave of fire burst outward, melting the frozen rain into steam before it could reach him. The sudden change in temperature disrupted the carefully maintained battlefield, and white vapor swelled through the arena as Hidden Ice Mist mixed with the heated air.

When Ning's vision cleared, Qiu Han was already in front of him.

Another palm slammed into the spear before Ning could fully raise it, throwing him sideways. He rolled across the stone, barely regained his footing, and stamped down just as Qiu Han followed.

[Soil Refining Technique]

The stone beneath Qiu Han's next step softened enough for his foot to sink slightly.

"Tsk. Still using petty tricks."

Against most cultivators, that tiny disruption would have been enough to ruin their footing. Qiu Han's balance shifted for only an instant before he turned with it, converting the failed step into a pivot and driving his knee directly into Ning's abdomen.

The air burst from Ning's lungs.

He staggered backward as the last traces of steam dispersed around them. By now, the problem was obvious. Against someone whose spiritual sense covered everything within a few meters, concealment had lost most of its advantage, and if Ning wanted to accomplish anything, he would have to fight inside that range.

Pure Eyes circulated once more, and Clear Sight sharpened his vision as Qiu Han attacked again.

This time, Ning was ready for the sudden acceleration. He had already experienced Qiu Han's speed several times, and although his body still struggled to keep up, his eyes were gradually adjusting to it.

Pure Eyes caught a subtle shift in Qiu Han's shoulder before he moved. Rather than aiming at the man himself, Ning thrust Friendship toward the space he expected Qiu Han to occupy.

The spearhead skimmed across Qiu Han's sleeve.

For the first time, Qiu Han stopped and glanced down at the torn fabric before looking back at Ning. A faint smile crossed his face.

"So you can keep up. Good."

There was no mockery in his voice. If anything, he sounded genuinely pleased.

"But seeing me isn't enough."

Qiu Han stepped forward again, and Ning answered with another attack.

[Scorching Purge]

Crimson flame burst from the spearhead, lancing toward Qiu Han's forearm just as qi gathered there for another strike. A metallic sheen spread across his skin before the fire arrived, and the flames scattered against his arm, leaving little more than a scorched sleeve behind.

That tiny distraction was all Ning needed.

[Golden Finger]

A compressed beam of qi shot toward Qiu Han's wrist, aimed at disrupting the flow of spiritual energy through the meridian.

Qiu Han merely turned his hand, and the attack grazed past.

Still too slow.

With Quick increasing Qiu Han's speed even further, Ning immediately retreated, Shadow Steps carrying him backward before the next attack could arrive.

"Quick."

Qiu Han blurred forward.

Every unnecessary movement seemed to disappear. He took the shortest possible route into Ning's guard, and even with Pure Eyes pushed to its limit, Ning remained half a beat behind.

A palm slipped around the spear and struck his shoulder. Before Ning could properly absorb the impact, an elbow came toward his ribs. He twisted enough to lessen the blow, only for a knee to drive into his abdomen immediately afterward.

His breathing faltered as he staggered backward, barely keeping his footing.

Qiu Han didn't rush after him. He simply walked forward, forcing Ning farther onto the defensive with each measured step.

"Your body is sturdy, your fundamentals are good, and you've clearly spent a great deal of time on your techniques."

Ning barely redirected another palm with his spear before retreating again.

"But that's also your problem."

Golden Finger flashed toward Qiu Han's chest. He shifted slightly, letting the attack pass beside him without breaking his stride.

"The mist, the spells, the body refinement, the poison... you keep adding one thing after another to make up for what you lack."

Ning's eyes narrowed, though Qiu Han continued before he could respond.

"Others might call that versatility. I call it compensation."

Spiritual qi flowed through Qiu Han's arm, bringing the metallic sheen back across his skin as he stopped a few paces away.

"Talent. Comprehension. Constitution. Those decide where your foundation begins. Everything else is built on top of them."

Qiu Han looked directly at him.

"You've built yourself remarkably well, Ji Ning. But your foundation was never equal to mine."

Ning had to admit, the man had a point.

His spiritual root was ordinary. He didn't possess some powerful bloodline inherited from an ancient clan, nor had he been born with a special constitution. Almost everything that allowed him to stand here had been accumulated piece by piece over the years.

Still, Ning didn't particularly care. If something was useful, he learned it. If he lacked something, he found another way to make up for it. There wasn't much point worrying about what he should have been born with.

More importantly, Qiu Han was currently beating the shit out of him, so this probably wasn't the best time for philosophical reflection.

Qiu Han seemed to lose what remained of his interest in continuing the exchange.

"I'm already tired of this farce. Use your strongest move."

"...Okay."

Ning let out a slow breath.

He already knew the match was unwinnable. His spiritual energy was nearly exhausted, his body ached from head to toe, and he had long since run out of meaningful options. Qiu Han was simply stronger than him right now.

Still, Ning had spent the entire match being knocked around the arena, and one stubborn thought refused to disappear.

At the very least, I'd like to hit him once.

Winning was impossible, but landing one clean hit was another matter.

Ning adjusted his grip on Friendship and quickly ran through what he had learned during the fight. Qiu Han's spiritual sense could detect anything that entered his range; Pure Eyes couldn't fully keep up with Quick, and trying to overwhelm him with spells hadn't worked either.

But that didn't mean he was out of options.

Shadow Steps activated, and his figure shot forward in a blur, spear held low as he committed everything to one final charge.

Then, loud enough for the entire arena to hear, he shouted, "Magical Spear!"

It was the first time he'd announced the name of a technique during the entire tournament.

The remaining qi in his body surged toward the spearhead, causing its tip to glow brilliantly. It looked every bit like the desperate, all-or-nothing attack of someone gambling everything on one final strike.

Qiu Han's spiritual sense immediately swept over the spear.

So that's where he gathered his qi.

He didn't retreat. Rather, the thought of retreat never even crossed his mind. He stepped forward to meet the attack head-on, fully intending to crush Ning's final hope with overwhelming superiority.

The spear thrust toward him.

At the very last moment, Qiu Han activated Quick once more. His body shifted half a step sideways with perfect precision, and the spear sliced harmlessly past him.

Qiu Han's lips turned upward. It was just as he expected.

Then—

Thud!

A fist crashed into Qiu Han's ribs.

The impact forced him back three full steps. It wasn't powerful enough to seriously injure him, but it drove the air from his lungs and left a dull ache spreading across his side.

For the first time, genuine surprise appeared on his face.

He stared blankly at Ning. "...Why are you punching me after shouting Magical Spear?"

His composure finally cracked. Everything had pointed toward a spear technique, whether it be the gathered qi or the shouted name. Every sign had been there.

Yet the actual attack had been... a punch.

Ning remained standing only through sheer stubbornness. He wiped the blood from the corner of his mouth before looking at Qiu Han through the slowly dispersing mist.

"That's what makes it magical."

For a heartbeat, the entire arena fell silent.

Qiu Han's expression stiffened before darkening considerably.

"You..." His voice had finally lost its earlier composure. "Even at the very end, you resort to tricks."

A crimson aura slowly rose around him as spiritual qi gathered once more.

"Quick-"

"I surrender."

Ning immediately raised his hand.

The declaration echoed clearly across the arena.

"...Hm?"

Qiu Han stopped mid-step. Even the presiding elder froze for a moment, as though he'd been waiting for some earth-shattering final exchange.

"...Ah." The elder coughed awkwardly. "Ji Ning concedes. Winner... Qiu Han."

Qiu Han stood there for a long moment before finally letting out a slow breath.

"...You truly are infuriating." He looked at Ning with obvious dissatisfaction. "I was about to use my strongest move to teach you a proper lesson, yet you chose to run away."

Despite the blood staining his lips, Ning managed a faint grin. "And spend the next month lying in bed? Not a chance."

He wasn't exaggerating. Ning was already running on fumes, while Qiu Han had clearly been preparing to unleash the strongest technique in his arsenal. Staying around just to find out what it did sounded like a terrible idea.

If Ning could revive like in a game after watching an ad, he might have taken the chance. Unfortunately, real life didn't come with that feature.

Qiu Han's expression twitched faintly before he turned to leave.

After two steps, however, he paused.

"People romanticize struggle," he said quietly, his voice carrying across the arena without effort. "They believe iron can become gold if it suffers long enough."

His eyes returned briefly to Ning.

"But iron remains iron, and gold remains gold. Despite your amusing performance, you still surrendered."

Qiu Han's gaze then shifted past him, settling on someone among the watching disciples.

"So remember my words. True strength is innate."

Ning followed his line of sight.

Xiao Fan stood there.

At some point during Qiu Han's words, Xiao Fan's fists had clenched tightly at his sides, and the look in his eyes had changed completely.

Seeing that, Ning narrowed his eyes.

Ah. So that's what this was about.

...

"Ouch, that hurts," Ning hissed through his teeth as he lay on the lacquered wooden couch of Moon Dew Pavilion.

"It's supposed to," Si Sihua replied calmly, not even glancing up as she pressed the cool, silver-blue paste into the bruised flesh along his ribs. "If I used the mild formula, you'd still be limping during your next match. This one works faster."

She pushed harder.

Ning sucked in a sharp breath. "…And it's more expensive, isn't it?"

Si Sihua's lips curved faintly. "Guess."

Ning closed his eyes in quiet despair. He already knew the answer.

Still, compared to mortals, cultivators were blessed. What would have taken months to heal on Earth would close in days here. Bones mended. Meridians stabilized. Bruises dissolved beneath spiritual medicine.

Provided, of course, one could afford the good stuff.

Si Sihua finished applying the moonlight gel, her movements practiced and efficient.

"I heard about your match," she said casually while sealing the ointment jar. "You did well."

Ning gave a small snort. "I practiced for years. It would be embarrassing if I didn't at least manage that much."

"That's true." She straightened. "Rest. The medicine needs time to circulate. I have to return to the counter."

"Mm."

When she left, silence settled over the room.

Ning lay back against the soft cushions, staring at the wooden beams overhead. Every muscle in his body ached after the tournament, yet despite his exhaustion, his mind remained completely awake.

It seems... I've become a stepping stone.

Being matched against Qiu Han hadn't been unusual. With three consecutive victories under his belt, facing the strongest remaining contender had been inevitable.

What bothered Ning wasn't the pairing itself, but everything surrounding it. Qiu Han's philosophy, the timing of his speech, and, most importantly, the way his gaze had drifted past Ning before delivering those final words.

Iron remains iron. Gold remains gold.

At first, it had sounded as though Qiu Han was speaking to him.

Looking back now, those words had never truly been meant for Ning.

They had been meant for Xiao Fan.

Ning let out a slow breath.

If this really were the kind of novel he suspected it was- and he had consumed more than enough of those in his previous life- then the structure was almost painfully familiar.

The protagonist's friend enters the tournament. He performs beyond everyone's expectations, displaying talent, cleverness, and determination. For a while, the audience even begins to wonder whether he might challenge the strongest competitor.

Then comes the inevitable match against the arc's mini-boss.

He fights valiantly, reveals every trick he has, and even manages to surprise the stronger opponent once or twice.

Then he loses.

Had Ning lacked the common sense to surrender when he did, he would've undoubtedly been beaten half to death. That would've only served to further demonstrate Qiu Han's overwhelming strength while giving the audience a clear measure of just how terrifying the final obstacle truly was.

It was the perfect buildup.

Especially when the real protagonist was standing right there, watching everything unfold.

Ning blinked at the ceiling.

If someone simply replaced the words the protagonist's friend with Ji Ning, the pattern fit disturbingly well.

He had exposed Qiu Han's Spiritual Sense.

He had demonstrated the enormous gap between the Sixth and Seventh Stages of Qi Condensation.

He had given Qiu Han the perfect stage to proclaim his philosophy that strength was determined at birth.

And, by sheer coincidence-or perhaps not-Xiao Fan had heard every single word.

Similarly, if Ning had stubbornly refused to concede and ended up gravely injured, it would've become the perfect source of motivation for the protagonist before the final battle.

Viewed from that perspective...

He had played the role of the stepping stone almost flawlessly.

Ordinarily, Ning would've dismissed the entire thought as his imagination running wild.

But after hearing Qiu Han's philosophy, the pattern became much harder to ignore.

If Qiu Han represented those who were born as gold, while Xiao Fan embodied iron tempered through hardship into steel, then their inevitable clash wouldn't simply determine who won the tournament.

It would be a clash of Dao Hearts.

A battle of two completely different beliefs.

Ning rolled onto his side and let out a chuckle.

The finale is going to be interesting.

That left him with only one question.

Despite the overwhelming difference in cultivation, should he bet on Xiao Fan?
