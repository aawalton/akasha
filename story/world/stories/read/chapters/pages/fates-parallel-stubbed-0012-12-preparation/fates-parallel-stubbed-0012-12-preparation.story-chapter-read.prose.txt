Lee Jia had nothing, she had always had nothing, and she was always going to have nothing. That was what she had believed for her entire life. Despite her new life, she had still been believing it, and even now it was hard for her to shake the idea.

It takes more than a few days to completely transform one’s outlook, after all. However in that moment, Lee Jia had realized she did have something. A resource she hadn’t considered—one that the other disciples for all their wealth and nobility couldn’t replicate. Something that was hers, and hers alone.

In an ironic sort of way, the one resource Lee Jia had that she could use against Yan Zhihao didn’t exist until he had demanded it from her—the secret to unlocking one’s ki. The technique Lee Jia had discovered wasn’t really that amazing. It was a trick that a mage could use to unlock their ki faster, but Yan Zhihao’s conspiracy theories had dramatically increased the value of this secret.

Now half the disciples in the academy were convinced that Lee Jia held the key to learning to harness their ki, and she had no intention of correcting them. She had nothing, but thanks to Yan’s spectacle, somehow people now wanted her nothing.

It was during her conversation with Dae that she had realized it. Dae, with his limitless curiosity and studiousness had never even asked what the secret was. He’d asked if there was one, but never for clarification—and he had gotten very excited when she’d offered to trade him the information.

Dae was just the first—a test of just how much value she could get out of trading her technique. It hadn’t been a very good test, though. Dae was overeager and would have probably offered most of what she asked for freely. For now, she had secured his continued tutoring—which he insisted he needed no payment for—and one copy of his strongest attack talisman. Apparently one was all he could offer, as he would have to make it from scratch to be usable by someone else, and it took a long time to create.

It was late by the time Jia made it back home, and she didn’t have a chance to explain what had happened to Eui until breakfast the next morning.

“A duel? Why in your ancestors names would you accept a duel? You don’t even know how to fight.”

Jia shrugged as she tried to explain her decision to Eui.

“I didn’t have much of a choice, really. Besides, he’s wagering everything against nothing. Even if my chances are tiny, it seems like a pretty easy bet.”

“I guess. What are you going to ask him for if you win?”

Jia stopped to consider—she hadn’t really thought of that.

“I honestly don’t know. I’m more focused on trying to figure out how I can actually win.”

“How can you actually win?”

“Not in a fair fight, that’s for sure!”

Both girls laughed at that before Jia went on to lay out her strategy.

“Dae is going to help me with magical techniques. Hopefully I can have a few spells prepared in time for the duel. Otherwise I’ll only be able to rely on the one I bought from him. I’m also going to use the day off Ienaga gave us today to attend an extra magic lesson.”

“That’s your brilliant plan? Pay attention in class?”

Jia puffed out her cheeks and glared at Eui.

“No! Obviously there’s more to it. I’m going to see if I can sell the technique to a few other people for favors. I’m sure Eunae will be interested, Takeda—well I don’t have anything to offer her, but she might be willing to help just to get back at Yan. I’m a little leery about asking Xin and Guan since they are Yan’s countrymen, but I need all the help I can get.”

Eui grimaced when Jia mentioned Eunae.

“So you’re banking entirely on some undefined support from the literal princess, and the racist dickhead who may or may not be pals with the guy you’re trying to beat?”

“If not for that dickhead, we would never have met—and not entirely, no. I was also hoping maybe you could give me some pointers. You’re pretty scary with a knife and you said you’d fought mages before, right?”

Eui froze, staring wide-eyed at Jia.

“Y-you want me to teach you? Jia, I don’t think you know what you’re asking.”

“I’m going to be fighting above my weight class, both figuratively and literally. Who else but you has that kind of exper—”

“Stop!”

Eui stood up and slammed her hands on the tea table as she shouted.

“Jia, stop. You keep saying fight this, fight that. I know you saw my past, or part of it at least. I’m not a fighter, Jia, I’m a killer—a murderer.”

Eui was glaring as angrily as Jia had ever seen her. She swept her hair back behind her ear in a rare display of the brand on her forehead.

“You want my advice? Don’t fight him. Find out where he lives and kill him in his sleep, or throw down your weapons and surrender, and then stab him in the back with a hidden knife when he lets his guard down. Jia, if you are going to be my friend you can’t lie to yourself about who and what I am! This brand is not something that was done to me, it’s the consequence of something I did. I have fucking killed people, Jia, and if push comes to shove I won’t even blink before doing it again.”

Jia stared at Eui in stunned silence as Eui caught her breath after her tirade. Jia didn’t know how to respond.

“I—I don’t—I mean, I didn’t—”Eui held up a hand to stop Jia and shook her head. Her tone was sullen as she spoke.

“Forget it. Just—you don’t need my help, ok? Not yet, at least. I hope you never do.”

Before Jia could respond, Eui retreated into the bedroom. Jia decided to let her have her space as she finished her meal in silence.

It felt strange to sit by herself in the lecture hall. Dae was in training with Ienaga and Eui was still locked in her room. It had only been a few days, but Lee Jia was surprised at just how reliant on Dae and Eui she had become. Hwang’s lesson was unfortunately almost exactly the same as the one she had attended earlier in the week. Still, the lesson wasn’t a complete waste of time. Thanks to Dae’s tutoring, Lee Jia understood the simple talismans and formations Hwang demonstrated far better.

The defensive mana shield talisman was the easiest to make. It formed a crude barrier of mana that would block incoming attacks. Since it was made of mana, it was most effective against spells and elemental phenomena. A fireball would have most of its effect dispersed back into mana, but a ki-empowered punch would only lose a small portion of its ki and none of the physical force behind the blow. It wasn’t a very powerful spell, and its mana cost was quite high, but it was easy to make and it would be effective against Yan’s flames.

Offensive talismans, it turned out, were more complicated—raw mana was awful at creating physical phenomena. The simplest solution was to infuse an attack talisman with elemental mana so that it could be activated with raw mana, but in order to get that elemental mana Lee Jia would first have to create a formation that converted raw mana into the element she wanted.

It was beyond the scope of her current ability, and would take more time that she had to create even a single talisman. Lee Jia would just have to rely on whatever martial arts she could learn and the talisman that Dae would provide.

After the class ended, Lee Jia found Xin Wei among the departing students. He was, as always, accompanied by his towering companion, the stone-faced Guan Yi. Lee Jia waved to get his attention as she approached.

“Xin Wei! Stonef—Guan Yi! Good afternoon.”

“Ah, good afternoon Miss Lee. What can Stoneface and I do for you today?”

Lee Jia glanced nervously at Guan who of course did not react to Xin’s teasing.

“I was wondering if I could ask for—or rather exchange favors.”

Xin raised a curious brow at Lee Jia, crossing his arms as he spoke.

“Favors, is it? I heard about your little bout with Yan Zhihao. You shouldn’t have accepted that—the Yans love making examples of people. He’s not going to stop at a few bruises, you know.”

Lee Jia gulped nervously, but she hadn’t really expected any less.

“Well it’s about that, actually. Did you hear about what Yan wants from me?”

“The secret to awakening ki, yes? Why do you think I’m here and not sweating myself to death in Ienaga’s class? Once Yan knows, he’ll blab about it to all his cronies, and then the secret is secret no more.”

“Uh, well you really shouldn’t count on that. You’d be better off just staying in training.”

Guan nodded at Lee Jia’s words.

“That’s what I told him. He wastes valuable training time for both of us by trusting Yan’s baseless rumors.”

Xin glared at Guan Yi for a moment before turning back to Lee Jia.

“Yan confirmed it, did he not? It was a brutish method, but he assaulted your meridians and your body rejected it. It’s a trick used to root out Yamato spies, back home. You are a martial artist now, no?”

Lee Jia scratched her cheek.

“That’s true, but—well, never mind. If you promise to keep it to yourself until after the duel, I’m willing to tell you myself in exchange for a favor.”

Xin narrowed his eyes at Lee Jia.

“Why would I pay now for something I can get later for nothing?”

Lee Jia winced. She hadn’t considered that. She furrowed in brow as she tried to come up with a way to answer that without giving away that the secret was worthless.

“I can’t explain without giving it away, but I can tell you that the information is worth far more to you now than it would be next week.”

“Hmm, I must confess that my curiosity is piqued. And what am I to offer in exchange, then?”

“Help unlocking my qi, as well as any information you might have about Yan’s techniques. I’d also appreciate a sparring partner. I don’t know any other cultivators, and I have no experience fighting them.”

Or anyone else, but Lee Jia kept that part to herself. Xin smirked at Lee Jia’s request.

“You’re really overselling it, Miss. To the first request, hard no. Just follow the technique the Elder gave you—I can vouch for its authenticity. To the second, I’m afraid I have little to give. I know of Yan, and I have met him a few times. I doubt you have any interest in his social proclivities, however. So it comes down to only a sparring partner.”

Lee Jia had really been hoping for more, but it was still something. And something was more than the nothing she was offering.

“Okay, a cultivator sparring partner for one week in exchange for the secret that Yan Zhihao is after, on the condition that you keep the secret to yourself until after the duel.”

Xin appeared to ponder the offer for a few moments before he shrugged and offered Lee Jia a smile.

“I think we have a deal! Now, don’t keep me waiting any longer! What is the trick that has Yan so excited?”

Lee Jia told him. As she explained, Xin Wei’s smile slowly faded until it fell into a frown. By the time she finished, he was down to a full-on scowl.

“Stoneface, I dare say we’ve been had.”

“Yes, Xin, it would appear you have been tricked. Also, I told you so.”

Xin backhanded Guan’s shoulder ineffectually and sighed.

“What was that about the secret being more useful now than a week from now? It seems rather useless in either case, seeing as we’re not mages yet.”

Lee Jia smiled sweetly as she responded.

“Now you can get back to training before the rest of your countrymen have realized that Yan led them on a wild goose chase!”

“Hmph, I suppose I can’t be too upset. The technique might be useful once I’ve learned all three disciplines. Besides, the real victim here is Yan! Actually, just thinking about the look on Yan’s face when he learns what he’s made such a fuss about is making me smile already.”

True to his word, Xin was smirking once more as he regarded Lee Jia contemplatively.

“You’re quite the devious little cat aren’t you? Yes, I think we’ll get along nicely in the future, you and I. Very well! As promised--Stoneface, you are at Miss Lee’s disposal for the next week as a sparring partner.”

Lee Jia’s internal celebrations were interrupted as her mind caught up with her ears.

“Wait, what? I was hoping to come to my own arrangement with Sto—with Guan Yi.”

“Well you should have thought of that before blabbing away right next to him. In any case, he’s my retainer. He’s here on his own merit, but he still follows me around like a lost puppy. Some time away will do him good, and I will have kept my promise.”

Guan glanced down at Xin, then back to Lee Jia.

“Do not mind Xin. We are old friends, but he is too shy to admit it. In truth, he’s the one who follows me around. It would be my pleasure to practice with you, Miss Lee. Anything to get away from the endless stream of what Xin passes off as humor.”

Xin’s smile steadily grew stiffer as Guan deadpanned, and Lee Jia had to cover her mouth to desperately suppress a giggle.

“Alright! Does tomorrow at the training field after lessons work for you?”

Guan nodded.

“I shall meet you then, Miss Lee.”

With their business concluded, Lee Jia bid the two boys farewell and moved on to meet with her next target.

“A trade?”

Lee Jia nodded. She was sitting with Seong Eunae and Takeda Rika in their home. As usual, Eunae had prepared tea and snacks. Lee Jia was still unsettled by the way Eunae seemed to look anywhere except for her eyes, but she knew that the alternative was even more unsettling, though.

“For the technique I discovered when I unlocked my ki. I probably would have just told you, but this thing with Yan has forced me to be a bit more frugal.”

Takeda made an irritated noise as she chewed on a biscuit.

“Mng. Who does that jerk think he is? ‘Put me in my place’!? That was supposed to be a friendly spar. I just wanted to see what fighting a cultivator was like. He’s the one who took it way too seriously and tried to cook me alive.”

Eunae set her teacup down and levelled a stare at Lee Jia’s shoulder.

“Leveraging the resources you have is wise, but I am afraid you haven’t got anything to offer to me, Jia.”

“That’s not true! It’s not like Yan Zhihao thinks, the technique is-”

“Only useful to mages, yes? Let’s see, you use a slightly modified combination of the breathing techniques taught to us by Master Ienaga and Magus Hwang in order to replenish the mana in your aura while relying on the natural ki absorption that comes from bodily exhaustion to simultaneously cultivate your ki. Does that sound right?”

Lee Jia’s jaw dropped.

“How did you—?”

Eunae smiled sweetly at Lee Jia.

“I paid attention, dear. After the incredible pace of your first awakening, I have no intention of underestimating you. It took me a few hours to puzzle out the changes to the breathing techniques, and I am still not entirely certain how you managed to keep running while you meditated but I think I’m getting close.”

Takeda nodded.

“You wouldn’t think so, but Eun-eun’s a crazy hard worker. She’s been pushing herself constantly ever since we got here.”

Eunae puffed out her cheeks and glared at Takeda.

“What do you mean ‘you wouldn’t think so’!?”

Lee Jia ignored the giggling pair and stared down into her lap. This was a disaster—she had forgotten how perceptive Eunae could be. Even if she had remembered, she didn’t think she would have expected her to figure out the technique just from watching. Eunae reached out and patted Lee Jia on the shoulder.

“Don’t fret, Jia. I still have every intention of helping you. Besides, you should be pleased—you’re uniquely talented in a way that I can scarcely describe. You and that unusual exile friend of yours—in her own way.”

Lee Jia almost spit out her tea, but barely managed to restrain herself.

“U-um, about Eui, she’s not um—”

Not dangerous. Lee Jia recalled the argument from earlier this morning and had to bite back her words. Eunae sighed and frowned slightly as she spoke.

“I’m sure she has her own circumstances. I have my reservations, but I will try to withhold judgement.”

Takeda looked between the other two girls with a confused expression.

“Are we talking about Lee Jia’s shy friend? I don’t really get it, but you should get her to come over some time. She reminds me of some martial artists, the way she carries herself like a coiled snake ready to strike in an instant. I’d love to spar some time.”

Eunae clapped her hands together and smiled brightly.

“There you have it! If Rika says so, that’s enough for me. Now then, Jia, let’s talk about what we can do to help you prepare for your bout with Yan.”
