I brushed aside the larger flap to the tent that Fade had led me to, exchanging a nod with one of the guards next to it as I did. I actually recognized this guy, as he was a member of the expedition. Julius something or other, I believed.

As Fade had guided me through the encampment, I had started to actually encounter my people. It was gratifying to see them, but nobody looked to be in great shape. It was a loss to all of us that the Ashen Bride had been destroyed, and my people looked like they felt it. I was clearly recognized as I had started to encounter members of the expedition more and more.

But the situation was complicated. I hadn’t forgotten that Marcel had tried to wrest the expedition from me, and had mostly succeeded. Nyx had been killed in the fighting, though, while Gustave and Marcel were…up in the air.

All that to say, some people were able to muster up a small smile at the sight of me, some couldn’t look me in the eye, and some…scowled, and turned away.

Complicated.

I soon got an answer to what happened to the other leaders of the expedition when I stepped into Bleddyn’s command tent. I wouldn’t call it packed in here, but there were certainly more than a few recognizable faces.

And they were very surprised to see me.

“Nathan!” A very disheveled Sylvia, dressed in ripped and stained black leather armor, breathed at the sight of me. She veritably teleported in front of me, grabbing me by my shoulders with hands that felt…stronger than I remembered them being. I withheld a wince and chalked it up to the difference in strength with one of my clones. In the meantime, she disbelievingly patted my collarbone, searching for any evidence of my injuries. “How are you…wait…” She paused, and I noticed a great deal of her enthusiasm drain out of her voice. “This is one of your…?”

I reached up and grabbed her hands with my own, smiling apologetically at her. “Yes, sorry to say this, but it is.”

Sylvia sighed and slumped forward to rest her forehead against the very collarbone she had just been inspecting. “I see.”

Though this was just a clone, I still wrapped my arms around the Sculpted woman and pulled her close. She didn’t resist, returning the gesture.

Over her head, though, I was easily able to see the reactions of everyone else in the command tent to my presence.

Bleddyn was here, of course. While the werewolf looked a little haggard, he was uninjured, which I chalked up to his healing factor. I had outright seen the man regrow an entire eye before, so I was unsurprised. He sat bare-chested at the head of a table upon which piles of parchment could be seen, discarded. My old friend had stood up at my entrance and looked cautiously optimistic at the sight of me.

Sadly, I was going to have to burst his bubble soon.

Throughout the tent, and on either side of the Mynydd man, I saw a number of people that I could only assume were members of his Unchained. They almost seemed to be attending Bleddyn like members of a court.

Of the expedition…the only people within the tent were Bella, Renauld…and Gustave.

Of Olag and Alveron, I saw no sign.

While Bella simultaneously looked relieved to see me while trying to hide it, it was the reactions of the last two that caught my attention. Gustave had difficulty in even meeting my eyes, as he remained in his seat before the table. Unlike Bleddyn, the young Herztalian officer did look injured.

And not in a mild way.

Gustave’s entire right arm was flat out missing. From the shoulder, it was just gone, without even the upper arm left beyond. The wound site was completely covered in bandages, though thankfully, no blood was showing through. Presumably, he had been able to get it healed enough that he wasn’t in danger anymore…but that meant he was still missing an arm. But judging by the downcast nature of his posture, and how he had trouble even looking at me?

I’m not sure which situation was depressing him more.

Speaking of depression, though…

Renauld was here, yes…but he was almost hiding in one of the corners of the tent. The Gnoll Healer was sitting quietly in a chair to himself, looking down at his hands, looking rougher than I could recall him ever looking before. Not even in the very depth of the Netherim bunker had he seemed as quiet and as still as he did at this moment. His Healer’s robes were stained black and red, with the soot and blood of battle, and yet he didn’t seem to care. At my entrance, the Gnoll had only briefly glanced up at me before looking down.

Alright.

I…was probably going to have to deal with all of this if I wanted to get things on track.

Nehushtan, and his promised way through the shield over the Barren Forest, waited for us in the north. We quite literally didn’t have time to be moping around.

I forced a smile on my face, trying to direct it to everyone in the room. “Good morning, afternoon, or possibly even night, everyone,” I said, hiding a wince as my joke fell flat. “It’s nice to see you, once again.”

“Nate…” Bleddyn said, looking incredibly lost. “How the hells are ye up? I saw what that bastard did to ye. I watched him crush your godsdamned bones to dust before we drove him off. How…?”

My smile took on an apologetic tint as I approached the able with a downcast Sylvia to my side. I gingerly lowered both of us into a pair of chairs there, dragging an extra over to us for my love. “I’m sorry to say that I’m not. Up, that is. This body…it’s only a clone, created through one of my Skills. My real body is back in that tent you all left me in.”

Realization flashed across the face of anyone who might remotely care about me, and excitement turned to disappointment. “I see,” Bleddyn said heavily, slumping back down into his own chair. “So you’re still…?”

“Deboned?” I smiled mirthlessly. “Correct. But that’s not the most important matter we have to discuss. Now, Fade told me that with his help, you and the others were able to drive Daffyd off,” I nodded to said Spirit Wolf, who came to sit down on the side opposite of where Sylvia was currently slumped against me. “But that’s all I know. I’m…assuming we’re not beneath Rhoscara anymore. That you gave the order for all of our combined forces to retreat, once it was clear the battle for the city was lost, and evacuation efforts reached a…peak, for the lack of a better word.”

“Aye, that’s the long and short of it,” Bleddyn sighed.

“Is that a problem?” One of Bleddyn’s aides butted in sharply, narrowing his eyes at me.

I gazed back at him calmly. Ah.

Hostility.

I suppose I shouldn’t be surprised. I was perfectly willing to accept a measure of responsibility for the shitshow that Rhoscara had become. It wasn’t all my fault, of course. I had been blindsided by the unexpected, literally divine capabilities of Anguis and his co-conspirators just like everyone else.

As a commander, you were only able to make decisions as best as you could, with the information you had at hand.

I couldn’t help but notice that he wasn’t the only person among the Unchained who looked a bit displeased with me.

Still, I nodded respectfully at the man. Brown of hair, brown of eye, and brown of very long beard, he was a pretty big guy, from what I could see. Not fat, but certainly bulky with an amount of muscle on his frame that wasn’t typical of Classers.

In fact, that was the kind of muscle I would expect from a man who had spent his whole life hauling very heavy objects.

Almost ursine like, I would say.

“Not at all,” I said calmly. “I had come to the same conclusion only moments before I engaged with Daffyd. I dearly wish I could have been there to aid in the evacuation, but as I’ve said…I’m currently laid up in a medical bed missing several bones.”

The man snorted, seemingly unconvinced by my speech. “Bah. Just get ‘em regrown by a church-man. Ye ain’t gonna have an excuse to laze about then. Like everyone else, ye need to pull your own weight if you’re gonna run with the Unchained.”

I blinked slowly, cutting my eyes over toward Bleddyn. That was quite a tone I was being spoken to in. Was this deliberate?

I found Bleddyn grimacing, and I could tell from the pleading look in his eyes that he wanted me to play along.

Ah.

I had to prove myself, even in some small way.

Very well.

I nodded at the still unnamed, bear-like man. “That would be true…” I drawled, reaching for the black leather glove that covered my left arm from the elbow down. “If it was an option for me.”

I pulled the glove off, and revealed to the world what was beneath.

A curious thing, my clone Skill. Umbra Gemina Exactoris was very exacting when it came to reproducing my body and all of it’s imperfections. I was currently nothing more than a construct of Mana and Aether, shaped to resemble everything about the true form of Nathaniel Hart. And so, it wasn’t out of the question for the Skill to copy even my prosthesis, if only visually.

A gleaming white metal arm was revealed to the world, threaded through with coruscating swirls of multi-colored light. It was as if veins of scintillant rainbow were threading their way through the surface of it, an artificial mirror of a true circulatory system.

Primordium

Sadly, not the real thing, on this clone. That was back in the tent.

Ah. Wait.

If…someone got it into their head to try and rob the real me, and just so happened to peel back the glove, they would find a hunk of metal valuable enough to make them a King.

I…really shouldn’t have left my body alone and unguarded, should I have? As those in the tent who hadn’t seen my prosthesis reacted with shock at the sight of it, I glanced down quickly to Fade and sent him a question.

He didn’t mind.

Without a word, the Spirit Wolf rose from his haunches and sauntered out of the tent, and back towards my immobilized form.

“A little quirk about me is that I don’t get the luxury of just regrowing limbs if I lose them,” I said calmly. “The last time this happened, I needed to devise a way around the problem, and a prosthesis was my solution. Just as I did with the Bond Breakers,” I locked eyes with the man as I very heavily reminded him of that fact. “Give me time, and I’ll work out a way around this.”

The tent fell quiet as the man and I locked eyes. Neither of us blinked as he very obviously took my measure.

I must have passed his test, because after a moment of tense silence, he nodded at me and then stood from his chair. I could practically hear the chair sigh in relief as he did. “Aye, maybe you will. Bleddyn, I’m going to go see if anyone needs some help. Ye don’t need me here.”

Bleddyn nodded back at the other man, looking a bit relieved. “Aye, I’ve got it. Thanks, Theo.”

As the man left the tent, I found the remaining members of the Unchained in the tent with us had…relaxed a bit. Whoever ‘Theo’ was, he must have the confidence of the Unchained in a way that even Bleddyn didn’t.

Speaking of…

“Well, now that that’s over with,” Bleddyn rolled his broad shoulders. “To answer your question, Nate, you’ve been out for a week now.”

I reeled back at that, rapidly blinking in shock. I almost instinctively looked down at Sylvia for confirmation. She nodded back at me sadly, which…explained a lot.

Meanwhile, Bleddyn had continued talking. “We’ve been on the move nearly the whole time,” He said heavily. “We didn’t expect it, but the damned Savoy didn’t pursue us very far down here, once they realized we were retreatin’. There were a few probin’ attacks, but nothin’ massive. It’s been one long sprint as we pushed everyone we got out as hard as we could, tryin’ to get to the safest place we’ve got. There, we were going to force you awake, because we have some Gyreite men who’ve sworn to our cause that stay back.”

Sylvia turned away from me to frown at Bleddyn. “You didn’t tell me that.”

“Or me…” Renauld finally spoke up from his corner, for the first time since I’d arrived. It was only a mutter, though, and I’m not sure most people in the tent heard it.

Bleddyn still winced. “Aye, I should’ve, and I’m sorry. But in my defense, we’re runnin’ scared from what might be the biggest damn army left on the continent.”

I put a hand on Sylvia’s shoulder to stop her. “It’s fine,” I said to the both of them. “I’ll find a way to deal with this myself, and I already have a few ideas. I…might need access to whatever stores of material you have on hand, however.”

“What’s mine is yours,” Bleddyn swore.

I nodded. “Alright. Then we need to discuss what’s next. I have a few ideas, but we can discuss them later, after we get to wherever we’re running to. Where…is that, exactly?”

For some reason, I noticed that Sylvia winced.

Raising my suspicions even more, Bleddyn had almost hunted look on his face. “Ah…well…” He hemmed and hawed.

I raised an expected eyebrow at him, as I tugged my fake glove back over my doubly fake arm. “Yes?”

“It’s Addersfield,” Bleddyn eventually admitted with a sigh. “We’re takin’ one of the Long Road paths to Addersfield. That’s…where the main base of the Unchained is, in the tunnels we found beneath the place.”

I paused in the middle of fitting my fingers back into the glove.

“What?”
