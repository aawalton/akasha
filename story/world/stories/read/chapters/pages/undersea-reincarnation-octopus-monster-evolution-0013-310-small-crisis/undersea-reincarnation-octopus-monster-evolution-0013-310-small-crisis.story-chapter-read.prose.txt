With every hour that went by, the energies suffusing the Pillar—or what the whales had called the Great Current—built up a little more. At first Henry couldn’t tell, having gotten used to the small variation that took place at any given time, but as the phenomenon went on, it was undeniable that the energies were trending up. By now, he could feel it, and it reminded him of the first time he’d been subjected to the lights of the Current, all the way back when he’d been invited into the dolphin’s space and had met Stormsong. The slow increase of its intensity was, in a way, like being under a sky full of dark, low, and grumbling clouds that continued to swell and press down, ready to unfurl a torrent of rain and thunder upon everything below it.

For Thalis, this made the day to day a little more dangerous. The predators were more active, some even frenzied, and already, Thalis’ handful of trade ships have been hauled out of the water for their safety as neither those on the ship nor the ship themselves were safe and more and more B-ranks came out of the depth, most likely attracted by the high number of prey the trees were hiding, both below and above water.

Henry wasn’t too worried for himself and Maurice. It’s been two days since he had fused his Aspects, and with that out of the way, he had turned all of his focus on the next stop before he officially moved on to B-rank. Skill fusions. He could have rushed things with the System’s assistance, but he didn’t want to. Plus, the process wasn’t that complicated. He’d already fused a couple of skills and was right now working on integrating Invisibility within Mimicry, which involved him trying–and often failing–to activate both from the same skill-nexus. Just like his Aspects, fusing skills involved meshing both together into a working ability, at which the two skill sources would ‘resonate’ so to speak, and they would be possible to merge. It was pretty much a brute-forcing approach, but it worked. Unfortunately, his current session was interrupted when one of his many scouts caught something below the waves.

Rocky was in trouble, and Henry had to act fast.

Activating Recalling Blink toward the scout in question, Henry funneled his mana into the skill and had a brief to take in the training grounds. Maurice had been working hard, listening to Arisia’s instruction while the two young ones from Velistraine’s ship sat next to the crab. As for Velistraine herself, she had been pouting, trapped into creating concept shards for Arisia to pay for… something.

It seemed like the captain had accrued a lot of debt with the instructor.

Now though, all of them looked up and abandoned what they had been working on as they sensed the large built-up of power coursing through and around him. Maurice shook his claws. “Henry! Where are you going?”

“Anything fun?” asked Velistraine as she hopped away from her seat, eyes shining.

“Rocky. I’ll be right back,” Henry said. He had no time to continue explaining, as the skill had had enough mana already.

After all, he wasn’t teleporting that far. The location was only a half-hour swim away without skill. At most.

Henry was swallowed into a current of dark purple and reappeared under water, a hundred or so yards above a vista of craggy and jagged rocks. Immediately, he dove toward one of the three creatures chasing after a panicked and terrified kraken that kept chucking rocks behind him, shuddering whenever one of the ear-piercing clicks slammed into him. Rocky was trailing blood from two missing arms, which made his attempts at hiding difficult. Plus, it wasn’t as if there was any hole deep enough around that the whales couldn’t just obliterate like they’ve just done twice.

Do I go for the kill or try to negotiate? Henry wondered as he closed in on the three whales, body inflating as he burned mana to reach his maximum size. The largest of them, a giant with scarred, blood-red skin was nearly thrice as large as his maximal size, which he should reach in a few seconds.

550 tons. I’m in literal kaiju territory at this point, Henry realized as he sent a couple of clones down after Rocky who’d just dove into a crevice, desperate to escape the three hounding sperm whales.

That was when the largest of them turned and looked up at the oncoming Henry. Henry was at his maximum size, easily crossing the 250 feet mark. He’d be longer if he’d put less weight in his arms, but he needed those to hit hard. Without slowing, he spoke to the three whales, using a hidden clone to put them in reach of his Telepathy. “[Hey there, friends. Mind letting the kraken go?]”

A couple of clicks from the largest one were his answers. Orders, Henry assumed. Because right after, the second largest came up to intercept him, while the strongest began head-butting the crevice in which Rocky had disappeared. The kraken was shaken, but his injuries weren’t too bad and already, Henry’s clones were patching him up. Turning his attention to the whale trying to rush him, Henry’s colors began changing to black and yellow, and the whale’s next words made the next course of action even clearer.

“[No. It's our hunt. And you will be next,]” the second strongest said, its telepathic voice terse and shrill. Then it clicked, and it was like a bomb had detonated a few paces away from Henry.

Which… wasn’t that big of a deal to him, by now. Henry’s brains shook a little, sure, and he even tasted a little blood in his beak. But that was mostly it.

With a sigh, Henry pulled on all of his Octominds—even those who had been on mana-refilling duty—and put their might behind one single action: To freeze the oncoming B-rank.

These were intelligent creatures. Maybe dick-ish, but he would rather not kill them, if possible. If this show of force didn’t convince them, then he wouldn’t mind some more target practice.

Henry activated the skill and braced as a prodigious amount of mana was instantly siphoned away as a nimbus of purple materialized around the giant creature. Now comes the hard part, he thought. Of course, a mid B-rank whale was not so easy to pin down. It furiously bucked its powerful body and clicked, and if Henry had been alone, it might have already broken the skill. But he wasn’t alone. And he still had plenty of resources at his disposal to make sure the predator went nowhere.

With its movements restrained without him even putting an arm on it, the whale’s effort grew more frantic and considering the alarm he sense from it, it seemed that the truth he was trying to communicate had finally reached its destination.

Between him and the cachalots, Henry was the real predator.

Henry pulled on his new Aspect, Predation of the Trickster, and even added a dash of Thaumaturgy before channeling both to the skill nexus currently maintaining his grasp on the whale. The nimbus of Telekinesis swelled instantly, becoming almost tangible, and Henry even believed the magic was starting to look like seeking arms wrapping themselves around the whales.

A flurry of frustrated clicks echoed out of the impotent creature, attracting the stronger individual’s attention while the youngest and smallest watched. Sensing their gaze on him, Henry slowly and purposefully fused two arms together, then fashioned the side of the new limb into a gleaming, dangerous looking blade he let float next to him.

“[Now that I have all of your attention,]” he slowly sent. “[I’d rather you leave. That is my friend you have hurt. I’d like not to have to kill all three of you. Last warning.]”

Unfortunately, Henry’s mercy was swiftly ignored as the largest blood-red sperm whale looked away from Rocky’s hideout and beat his flukes, propelling himself up. His sharp head cut through the water fast and quick, and its eyes nearly glowed with rage. The leader didn’t say anything as its pointed head shone for an instant before the most powerful click Henry had ever heard exploded out of it.

This one was a little more effective than the one Henry had shrugged off earlier. If the one from before had been a stick of dynamite, this might have been a bundle. Stapled right to Henry’s face.

The click slammed into Henry and burst both of his eyes and a decent number of his veins and arteries, if Henry was to interpret the immediate hot flashes and hematomas that soon covered his skin. Meanwhile, the water around him literally boiled and considering the intense, odd feeling still clinging to his skin, he believed the monster had used some essence to bolster the attack.

But it still wasn’t enough. Not to make Henry back out, and not enough for him to let go of his grip on the second whale. After all, he could still perceive all of his opponents through a handful of clones watching from a distance, though the concept-empowered click almost broke his skill.

It was a good thing Henry had boosted his Telekinesis with two Aspects instead of just one. It was also probably because the click was about offense and not about about disrupting other magics. It was just raw force, Henry mused while a pulse of Lifeweaving-empowered regeneration brought his eyes back. Grimly, Henry blinked forward as his flesh continued to heal toward the largest of the three, his bladed arm pulled back. Evershifting Strike activated, and Henry funneled his mana into it, greatly empowering the cutting, unimpeded, and accelerating effects, then he added some conceptual energy from Predation for good measure.

“[No!]” telepathically blasted the one still trapped into his Telekinesis as it thrashed more violently.

Henry’s arm whipped forward in a blur, meeting the cachalot’s charge. The latter had been fully intending on biting into him, unworried about the damage Henry could do to it, which would be its mistake. Its body had been engulfed in a red-hue as it accelerated, and Henry’s arm met the tough skin and blubber of the whale and cleaved right through.

Unlike some other beings Henry had beaten in this manner, the cachalot was much larger. And tougher. But it still bled the same and considering the change of consistency Henry’s arm could feel, deep in its wound, he was pretty sure he had bisected its brains. The notification didn’t show up. But it would if this fight continued.

Ever since Tevarius and that week Henry had spent fighting B-ranks, he didn’t feel threatened by them anymore. He still needed to empower his skills with concepts to bring them down, and not engage when he was out of essence, but they weren’t a danger anymore. He didn’t see himself losing to any other B-rank. Unless it was someone like him, that is. Someone with plenty of synergistic skills and fused Aspects.

The cachalot’s body, wreathed in its own blood, hung limply in the water. Its arms twitched, while blood poured out of the meters-deep gouge in its head. But it was healing. Slowly. It had a chance of recovering before it bled out.

Henry considered finishing it off right here and now, but he was not gonna level from it. Well, he might, but he didn’t need the core. Not really. And in case the smallest one was the child of these two, he wouldn’t mind passing on murdering one of its parents in front of it. He would if he had to, though. Maybe it had been the Current’s denizens, or maybe it had been the handful of life-and-death fights, but Henry didn’t feel so averse against putting down an intelligent creature that stubbornly came after him or his loved ones. Intelligence wasn’t a pass for any creature, human or otherwise, to be a threat to him or his friends.

Turning to the one still trapped, Henry asked.“[How about now? Will you leave the territory and never come back?]”

The still-trapped cachalot froze, then dipped softly clicked. “[Yes. Yes, I promise.]”

“[How about this one,]” he asked, reaching out to the one still twitching and bleeding, causing a sense of panic to swell from the one he had trapped. The third was still frozen in shock. That one made him feel bad, a little.

Looking back at the one still in his telekinetic grip, he continued. “[Is it going to abide? Or do I need to finish it off?]”

The trapped whale shook. “[I will drag him away. We will not come back. I swear it on the Absolute.]”

Henry sighed. “[It didn’t have to come to this, you know,]” he said. Releasing the cachalot, he whipped down and cut a slice out of its twitching flipper, which he swiftly threw in storage, gave the massive creature a pulse of healing to stave off its bleeding, then Henry swam down toward Rocky’s hideout.

“[We’re done here. Get moving,]” he ordered, not even bothering to look behind him as he swam past them.
