Main Quest: Muddied Waters

Details: The Marxx Kingdom has been infiltrated by the serpent, its minions spread throughout the realm. But what is a mere snake in front of the Dragon?

Task: Reclaim Ralis and ascend to power

Reward: System function

Failure: Defeat

Luke stared at the quest window, a mix of awe and trepidation twisting in his chest. He had expected a new quest soon—had dreaded it, in fact—especially after everything that had recently come to light.

The system rarely issued quests arbitrarily. It always waited until he had enough information, like a puppeteer pulling strings only when the pieces were aligned.

It had been that way in Clayton City too—after he deduced the arrival of Lhair’s forces. And it was the same now.

It did it on purpose…

The previous quest had commanded him to preserve his troops and retreat from Xiandong—an impossible mission given the starving, dwindling numbers of his men.

But now he understood.

Had he remained, he would never have uncovered the enemy's plot—the capture of the second prince. Without that knowledge, he would have been blind to the southern march of the Lhair army.

Lyon would have remained a pawn. A political prisoner-turned-figurehead. And through him, the throne would shift from King Julius to the traitors, all without a single Lhair soldier spilling blood in the capital.

He inhaled sharply.

The system was orchestrating everything.Every decision, every triumph, every setback—it was all part of a grand design. One that intertwined the fate of the Marxx Kingdom with his own.

Whether he liked it or not, Luke’s destiny was shackled to the realm.

Unlike the Bishop—the zealot commanding the Lhair forces—Luke held no formal authority. If given enough time, perhaps he could have clawed his way up through the ranks, earned influence through valor and battlefield victories.

But time wasn’t a luxury he possessed.

I need power, he thought grimly.

His gaze drifted to the horizon, where tendrils of smoke curled from the cookfires, slowly vanishing into the amber-streaked sky. The sun sank lower, painting the clouds in fiery hues—burnt orange, deep crimson, and hints of purple.

Beautiful… but fleeting.

Just like peace.

His mind returned to the Bishop. The man had known Luke was from Earth—there was no mistaking the way he’d said it.

Could he be from there as well?That would explain how he knew about gunpowder.

But instead of comfort, the thought unsettled him.

In another life, he might’ve felt drawn to such a person—a fellow outsider, someone who understood his world. But this Bishop wasn’t an ally. He was a fanatic.

Unstable. Dangerous.

And if he also has the knowledge to develop modern weapons... their advantage will grow unstoppable.

Luke clenched his jaw.

I can’t let that happen.

“This will be tough…” he muttered, the words barely audible over the crackle of distant flames. He held back a sigh, steeling himself.

The waters ahead were muddied. But he would wade through them, no matter how deep or dark they ran.

“Aye, but at least we don’t have to climb that damn mountain again,” came a grim laugh from beside Luke, followed by a familiar hand clapping onto his shoulder.

“I’ll take the mountain any day,” Luke replied with a half-smile. “All I had to do was hold on tight—you did all the work.”

Instead of laughing, Tristan’s grip tightened. His tone turned solemn.“Luke… you’re a brilliant man, I’ll give you that. You come up with the most ridiculous, daring ideas—things no sane person would ever consider.”

He gestured toward the wooden bridge behind them, where the last of the ten thousand men were just finishing their crossing.

“All of them are alive because of you. If you hadn’t found the loophole in the royal decree… if you hadn’t had the gall to suggest surrender, we’d all still be rotting in that damned fortress—half-starved or worse.”

He let the silence settle before adding, “I won’t even mention your other exploits… or those thunder bombs of yours.”

Luke's lips curved into a crooked smile. “Tristan, I appreciate a good ego boost as much as the next guy, but I’m guessing you have a point.”

Tristan scoffed. “Aye, I’m getting to it,” he grumbled, visibly annoyed. “You’ve got another habit—one that’s not quite so admirable.”

Luke raised a brow, genuinely intrigued. “Oh?”

“You don’t rely on anyone else.”

The words landed like a stone in still water, rippling through his mind. Luke froze, eyes widening as he met the older man’s gaze.

He opened his mouth to respond, but Tristan held up a finger.

“I know what you’re going to say,” he continued, shaking his head. “You had me climb that gods-damned mountain. You sent Victoria to guard the Crown Prince. You delegate, sure—but only when you have no choice.”

Luke’s smile faded as he absorbed the words, confusion prickling at the back of his thoughts. Wasn’t delegation a form of trust?

“But you only hand off the reins when you physically can’t do the job yourself,” Tristan said, cutting through his thoughts.

Luke frowned. His instincts bristled against the accusation. Just as he was about to argue, Tristan pulled him into a one-armed hug, firm and brotherly.

“Don’t get worked up,” he said, smiling. “I’m not tearing you down, Luke. You’ve done more than any of us could’ve asked. I’m just saying... you can lean on me. Alright?”

Luke remained quiet, the unexpected warmth of the gesture keeping him still.

“I’ve been a soldier my whole life,” Tristan went on, his voice low and steady. “I’ve seen wins. I’ve seen crushing defeats. Not every situation needs a genius solution. This isn’t a chessboard—every piece bleeds differently. No two men are the same out there.”

The words sank in deep, heavy with the weight of years and battle scars.

Is he right? Am I really carrying everything alone?

Luke's thoughts drifted—back to the start of the campaign, to his decision to head south to Xiandong… to the choice of sending the second prince to Yunxi… the surrender at Selis...

Each moment a choice. Each one a burden he had borne in silence.

Part of Luke agreed with Tristan’s words. There was truth in them. But it wasn’t the whole story.

It’s not just me… it’s the system.

That was what truly drove him—what compelled him to make decisions that often bordered on reckless.

He had already come to recognize his short-sightedness when under the pressure of those quests. Back in Xiu Fortress, he had taken bold action—sabotaging the granaries to hasten victory under the Ascent to Power quest.

And it worked. For a time.

But when the Qin army descended to lay siege, the shortage of supplies proved disastrous. He had been forced to abandon the fortress and retreat to Valand City in a scramble for resources—setting off a chain of events that ultimately led to the fall of Xiu altogether.

Luke swallowed bitterly, the memory heavy on his tongue.

“You’re right, Tristan,” he said at last, his voice quiet but sincere. “My blessing… it gives me tasks. Divine ones. And those tasks push me to make decisions—rash, sometimes even foolish ones. If I fail to complete them, I’m punished. And some of those punishments… would kill me.”

Tristan’s eyes widened, the revelation landing with force. The man said nothing at first, clearly trying to grasp the weight of what Luke had just admitted.

“I just received another task,” Luke added, forcing a bitter smile. “And this time, I’ll definitely need your help.”

The ominous tone sent a ripple of concern through the older man, but he quickly steeled himself. His lips thinned. His eyes hardened with determination.

“You can count on me,” he said firmly.

Luke gave a faint nod, his expression softening. He had planned to speak with Tristan, Richard, and the second prince about their next steps tonight—but he dismissed the thought. They were all bone-weary, running on little more than instinct and fumes.

Right now, they needed food. Sleep. Peace.

Let that come first.

The cookfires burned for hours into the evening, flooding the town with the rich scent of grilled fish. For the first time in what felt like months, the soldiers relaxed—shoulders loosened, laughter echoing under the darkening sky. Even the most hardened men found themselves smiling, their exhaustion momentarily soothed by the scent of real food and the crackle of open flames.

This won't last, Luke thought.

But he wasn’t about to ruin it.

Let them celebrate, just for tonight.

He sat down with a plate of roasted fish, the heat still radiating off its golden skin. As he bit into the tender flesh, a low groan of satisfaction escaped him. After two weeks of dried rations even a simple meal felt divine. His teeth ached slightly—unused to anything but chew-resistant food—but it was a welcome pain.

His gaze wandered across the sea of faces—scarred, worn, but momentarily at peace. The flickering firelight danced off their features.

Then, unbidden, his mind drifted to Earth.

The quiet apartment. The cold glow of the monitor. The silent dinners, eaten while gaming through the night, lost in fantastical worlds.

He chuckled—soft and breathless.

I miss my PC…

Not long after, he retired for the night.

Sleep came fast, wrapping around him like a long-lost friend. His dreams were filled with flickers of his old life—mundane, uneventful, even boring. But peaceful. No looming threat. No weight of command. No fear of dying tomorrow.

It was the best rest he’d had in weeks.

The sun filtered in through the latticed window, streaking golden light across the room.

Luke’s eyes fluttered open. The familiar scent of burnt woodsmoke lingered in the air—comforting in a strange way.

He reached across the bed, hand searching the surface of the nightstand out of habit. After a few seconds of grasping at empty space, he paused.

Then he chuckled dryly.

“I haven’t done that in a while…” he murmured, shaking his head to clear the lingering stupor of sleep.

Luke had been reaching for his phone—an old habit, formed after years of waking up the same way every morning on Earth.Funny.He only seemed to do that after a truly restful sleep now.

“Young Luke,” came a voice as the door creaked open, letting in a flood of sunlight.

“I’m awake…”

Tristan stepped inside, his expression somber.“There were some who tried to flee the town last night,” he said, his tone heavy. “Most of them are women and children. What should we do with them?”

Luke arched a brow. “You really need to ask me that?”

The older man shifted, eyes flicking across Luke’s face as though searching for confirmation.

“Let them go,” Luke said, waving a hand dismissively. “Tell them we’ll be leaving the town tomorrow morning. They just need to stay put until then.”

Tristan let out the breath he’d clearly been holding and gave a faint, relieved smile.“Yes, General.”

As the door shut behind him, Luke shook his head and muttered, “Surely he didn’t think I’d kill them... right?”

But after a pause, his tone softened.

“I suppose he had no choice but to ask.”

Tristan had been in the military far longer than Luke. He’d likely served under generals who wouldn’t hesitate to slaughter civilians to send a message. Luke could even name a certain one himself.

Hayden Hart.The descendant of the Blood General.

His thoughts turned to Victoria—the subordinate he had entrusted with protecting the Crown Prince. He hadn’t heard from her since.

Still, her name remained active in the system’s subordinate list.

She’s alive… at least for now.

It wasn’t much, but it was enough to keep hope alive.

If only I could use my soul form…

The thought came unbidden—but Luke immediately shook it off.

No. Not yet.I don’t know enough to use it safely. If the Bishop were to catch me while I was out of my body... he might be able to trap me. Or worse.

Though he had managed to resist the Bishop’s strange abilities thanks to the Sovereign’s Blessing, that didn’t make him invincible. The Bishop could have other methods—hidden weapons he wasn’t prepared for.

There’s too much I don’t know…

He walked to the latticed window and gazed out at the morning sky, his voice low and uncertain.

“I just have to pray that you’re safe, Victoria.”

The words felt strange on his tongue.

He’d never been religious on Earth. He’d scoffed at the idea of fate, gods, or divine purpose.

But here… it all felt too real to ignore.

Something—someone—had brought him to this world. Had given him the system. And with it, a path he had no choice but to walk.

Whoever they are… they clearly have plans for me.

And so far, Luke had followed along—gritting his teeth through every battle, every loss, every narrow escape.

Maybe he’d continue to be strung along until he conquered this world.

If I even survive that long.

He let out a dry, humorless chuckle.

“Here’s hoping,” he muttered, closing his eyes for a brief moment of calm.
