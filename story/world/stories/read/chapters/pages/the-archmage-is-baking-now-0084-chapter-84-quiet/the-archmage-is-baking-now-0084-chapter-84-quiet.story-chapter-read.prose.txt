Jane woke to sunlight streaming through an unfamiliar window, and for a long, disorienting moment, she had no idea where she was. Then the memories came flooding back. She was in Willowbrook, in the inn Felicity Cast had told her to find.

Jane let out a long breath and let her muscles relax. The panic faded, and she recalled that she was happy. This had been a good trip. Yesterday had been a good day. She had lived out a dream, then slept deeply and well. Everything was fine, except for the fact that she was wearing day-old travel clothes and a head of mussy hair. She set about to fixing that, washing up with a basin of cold, pumped water and changing into a clean set of in-town clothes.

She now needed to find the innkeeper. She had let herself into his establishment in the middle of the night without permission, and while Felicity had assured her it would be fine, Jane still felt she owed someone an explanation as soon as she could possibly give it.

A man emerged from what Jane assumed was the kitchen as she entered the common room, wiping his hands on a cloth. He was younger than she might have expected, only five or ten years older than herself. He didn't seem surprised to see her.

"Morning," he said. "I thought someone was up there."

"I'm sorry," Jane said quickly. "I came in late last night and didn't want to wake anyone. I hope it's all right that I just found a room."

"It's a small town, miss. I knew who it was." He gestured toward one of the tables. "Sit down. I'm Dayle. I run this place."

"Jane." She took a seat, setting her bag on the floor beside her. "How did you know?"

"Word travels fast around here. Cindy told anyone she could find about our visitor, yesterday." Dayle disappeared into the kitchen and returned a moment later with a plate. "By evening, everyone in town knew we had a visitor."

He set a plate of eggs and toast in front of her. Jane's stomach growled audibly at the sight of them, a fact the man studiously ignored.

"This is included with the room," Dayle said. "Eat up."

Jane didn't need to be told twice. She had been so absorbed in baking yesterday that she had barely eaten anything, surviving on samples and the occasional cookie. Jane ate quickly, finishing the plate of delicious eggs in a matter of minutes. When she was done, she reached into her bag and pulled out her coin purse, counting out several coins and sliding them across the table toward Dayle.

He looked at the money but made no move to touch it. "That's too much. The room's not worth half that."

"Please take it." Jane pushed the coins closer to him. "I'm in a great mood, and I really needed that sleep. It's worth it to me."

Dayle hesitated, but something in Jane's expression must have convinced him that what she said was true. He finally nodded and swept the coins into his palm.

"If you're sure."

"I am." Jane stood and gathered her things. "Thank you for the breakfast. And the bed."

"Come back anytime.” He smiled. "It's nice to have someone who actually came here on purpose."

Jane made her way out of the inn. She had a plan for the morning. Yesterday, Felicity had provided everything. Today, Jane wanted to give something back. The older woman wouldn’t want repayment, of course, but Jane wanted to give her a gift if she could, something that showed Felicity again how much she appreciated the time she had spent making Jane’s life better.

The general store was easy to find. It was one of the larger buildings in town, with a wide porch and a hand-painted sign that simply read Supplies. Jane climbed the steps and pushed open the door.

"Good morning," the woman said, looking up. "You must be the visitor. I'm Mrs. Halloran."

"Jane," she said. "I was hoping to buy some things."

"Well, you've come to the right place. We're the only option for miles." Mrs. Halloran gestured at the shelves. "What do you need?"

Jane walked through the store slowly, examining the offerings. The selection was limited compared to what she was used to in Glenfall, but there was enough variety to work with. She picked out an assortment of fruit, then flour, eggs, salt, and sugar enough to replace what they had used the day before ten times over.

"That's quite a haul," Mrs. Halloran observed as Jane brought everything to the counter. "You planning on doing some cooking?"

"Something like that." Jane surveyed her purchases and nodded.. "Do you have a basket I could carry all this in?"

Mrs. Halloran produced a woven basket from beneath the counter, large enough to hold everything with room to spare. "This do?"

"Perfect."

Jane paid for everything and carefully arranged her purchases inside. The result was a colorful assortment of ingredients, the kind of gift she hoped one baker might give another. It wasn't much, but she would have been glad to get the same gift herself. It would be enough.

She made her way back down the main road toward Felicity's house, letting the basket swing on her arm as she walked. The morning was warming up now. A few people were out and about, and they nodded at her as she passed.

Felicity’s garden came into view, just as impressive in daylight as it had been the evening before. The berry bushes were heavy with fruit, which made Jane feel a little silly about the portion of her gift that probably was sourced from the same bushes. It was a silly mistake to make, but she hoped Felicity would feel it was the thought that counted.

My, it’s quiet. Shouldn’t she have seen me by now?

There was no sign of movement anywhere, none of the small indicators of life that she would have expected from someone who worked on a baker’s schedule. Jane felt something cold settle in her stomach, an inexplicable worry with little form to examine. It was not a rational feeling, she knew. But it was there, and her hope that it was a passing, silly thing died the longer she went unnoticed in Felicity Cast’s yard.

She walked up the stone path and climbed the steps to the porch. The front door was closed, just as she had left it the night before. She knocked, louder than she usually would have. There was no immediate answer. Once the noise of the knocks faded, there was no sound in the air but the chirping of birds. To all appearances, she was alone.

She knocked again, harder this time. "Hello? Felicity? It's Jane. I brought you something."

There was still only silence. The cold feeling in her stomach spread, crawling up her spine and settling frozen in her chest. The house felt different than it had yesterday. Emptier, somehow.

She didn't want to break down the door. It felt like an overreaction to assume the worst regarding a woman whose schedule she hardly knew. Maybe she was just sleeping late. Jane knew that was plausible. She might be up and about and somewhere else by now, for all she knew.

The feeling still wouldn't go away. Jane set down her basket and placed her palm flat against the doorframe. She closed her eyes and let her magic flow outward, pushing it through the wood and into the structure of the house itself.

The house was occupied. She could feel a presence upstairs, in what she assumed was the bedroom. But force of life that she sometimes sensed in living things when she looked for them with her magic was weak. It was there, but as quiet as a whisper to her senses.

Jane's eyes snapped open.

She didn't hesitate this time. She gathered her magic and focused it on the door's latch. A pulse of force went out, snapping into the wood and metal and wrenching them apart.

The sound that resulted was far louder than she had intended. It rang out across the quiet town like an explosion, but Jane didn't care. She was already through the doorway, already running for the stairs.

The bedroom was at the top. Jane burst through the door and found exactly what she had feared.

Felicity lay in her bed, her white hair spread across the pillow. She was wearing a simple nightgown, the blankets pulled up to her chest. Her eyes were closed and her breathing was so shallow that Jane had to watch for several seconds to confirm it was happening at all.

"No," Jane breathed. "No, no, no."

She crossed to the bed and grabbed Felicity's shoulder, shaking her gently. "Wake up. Please wake up."

No response.

Jane shook harder. "Come on. Open your eyes. Talk to me."

Felicity's head lolled slightly with the motion, but her eyes stayed closed. Her breathing didn't change. She was alive, but barely, and it was clear she wasn’t going to wake up from a simple shake.

"Please." Jane's voice cracked. "Please don't do this. Not now. Not yet."

Jane heard footsteps on the stairs behind her. She turned to find Cindy standing in the bedroom doorway, her eyes wide with shock and fear. The girl must have heard the explosion of the door and come running. It wasn’t surprising, given what Jane knew of her.

"What's happening?" Cindy's voice was small, trembling. "What's wrong with the cookie lady?"

Jane forced herself to think clearly. This child knew the town. She was a resource, one that shouldn’t be seeing what she was seeing. She could use her and protect her all at once.

"Do you know where the doc is?" she asked. "Edna. The woman I met yesterday."

Cindy nodded, mute with fear.

"I need you to run as fast as you can and get her. Right now. Can you do that?"

The girl nodded again, already backing toward the stairs.

"Go," Jane said. "Run. Don't stop for anything."

Cindy ran. Jane heard her footsteps clatter as she burst out of the ruined front door and sprinted down the path, screaming at the top of her lungs for the doctor.

Jane turned back to the bed and took Felicity's hand in her own. It was colder than it should have been, especially compared to the warmth she had felt from her the night before. She reached out with her magic, trying to sense what was wrong, trying to find some clue that might tell her how to help.

The woman's body was failing. Jane didn’t know much about medicine, but she could tell that every system was struggling. Her body was working harder than it should have to just to keep functioning.

Jane had no idea how to fix something like that. She wasn't a healer. She had some small skill with minor injuries, cuts and bruises and sore feet, but this was beyond anything she was capable of. The amount she could help would be pitiful, really. A drop in a bucket.

But she had to try. She had to do something.

She closed her eyes and let her magic flow into the older woman's hand, willing it to help in whatever way it could. She didn't know if it would work. She didn't know if anything would work. Besides that, all she could do was hold on and wait for help to arrive.
