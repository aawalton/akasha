The chaotic slaughter in the valley was finally over, but the grim, methodical work of securing the territory had only just begun. The vast, muddy basin was rapidly transforming as the thousands of monstrous corpses broke down into ambient mana, leaving behind shimmering, ankle-deep drifts of golden dust and a terrifying amount of rusted, discarded weaponry.

Josh stood near the pitch-black mouth of the cave, his breath pluming in the freezing air as he orchestrated the containment effort. He directed two parties to form a tight, overlapping half-circle directly in front of the dimensional rift.

It was a simple, highly effective rotation. The portal was no longer a surging, uncontrollable floodgate, but it was far from its natural, dormant state. It was still actively broken, practically bleeding ambient mana. Every few seconds, the erratic purple light would flare violently, and one or two confused goblins or a blinking, disoriented orc would stumble out into the cold air. The moment a creature materialised, a pair of fighters would simply step forward, thrust a spear or swing a sword, and dispatch the threat before it could even raise a weapon. They were holding the line, but it was an endless chore.

With the immediate choke point fully secured, Josh waved for his own party to follow him away from the cave. They needed to assess the wider damage.

They walked slowly up the slight, muddy incline towards the eastern ridge. This was the exact spot where the Adventurers' Guild had established the novice training camp a few months ago.

The sight that greeted them was profoundly sobering.

The heavy wooden palisade wall, the very same wall Josh and Bhel had spent hours digging trenches and hauling heavy logs to construct, was an absolute ruin. Massive, ten-foot sections of the timber had been violently splintered and pushed outward by the sheer, crushing weight of the horde. The simple wooden barracks, the supply tents, and the covered training rings had been completely torn down or burnt to the ground, leaving only charred, blackened squares on the frozen earth.

"They completely destroyed it," Carcan whispered, her silver eyes scanning the desolate, ruined campsite. She tightened her grip on her amber-topped staff, her pristine white robes standing out starkly against the mud and ash. "All that hard work. All those memories of our first weeks here. Just gone."

"Timber can be rebuilt," Bhel grunted, kicking a charred piece of firewood out of his path. The dwarf had used a handful of water from his canteen to scrub the worst of the monster blood from his face, but his braided beard was still a tangled mess. "The important thing is that the guild at least evacuated as many of the novices as they could before the surge broke the walls. We can dig new trenches tomorrow."

Josh surveyed the surrounding treeline, his instincts refusing to switch off entirely. The main horde was broken, and the native beasts had been slaughtered, but the deep woods were notoriously vast and unforgiving.

"I need to be absolutely certain the perimeter is secure," Josh said, turning to Perberos. "I do not want to find out a splinter group of a thousand orcs is quietly reforming in a ravine half a mile from here. Take the scouts from the other parties. Sweep a two-mile radius around the camp."

Perberos merely offered a silent, crisp nod. The elven ranger turned and gestured sharply to the group of younger rogues resting near the wagons. They scrambled to their feet, looking incredibly nervous to be following the lethal elf back into the dark woods. Within moments, the scouting party melted seamlessly into the dense pine canopy, leaving not a single footprint behind.

With the scouts dispatched, Josh, Brett, Bhel, and Carcan returned to the rocky basin near the cave mouth to wait.

The next hour passed in a state of bizarre, almost mundane relaxation.

The adrenaline of the massive battle had completely faded, leaving behind a comfortable, heavy exhaustion. They sat on scattered boulders near the glowing purple-black portal, casually assisting the other parties with the constant trickle of monsters.

It was a stark, almost hilarious testament to their newfound power.

Months ago, a single goblin emerging from the dark would have demanded their full focus, frantic spellcasting, and desperate shield blocks. Now, it was treated like a minor, irritating pest.

A goblin shrieked, stumbling out of the purple light with a rusted dagger raised high. Josh did not even bother to stand up. He simply reached out with a heavy steel gauntlet and casually backhanded the creature. The sheer, passive strength of his tier three class sent the goblin flying ten feet through the air, where it landed with a dull thud and immediately dissolved into golden dust.

A few minutes later, a heavy-set orc roared, stepping through the rift. Bhel, who was busy using a small whetstone to sharpen the edge of his left axe, didn't look up from his work. He simply extended his right arm, letting the flat, heavy iron side of his other axe drop squarely onto the orc's skull. The beast collapsed instantly, its skull crushed under the blow.

Brett was perhaps the most nonchalant of them all. The mage was leaning back against a cool stone wall, his eyes closed, seemingly half-asleep. Whenever a cluster of three or four goblins appeared at once, Brett would lazily flick his wrist without opening his eyes. His deep red, drake-scale gloves would spark, and a tiny, perfectly formed, pea-sized bolt of highly compressed plasma would shoot across the cave, neatly incinerating the targets in a flash of blinding blue light.

"It is almost insulting," Brett murmured, brushing an imaginary speck of dust from his fur cloak. "To think we once considered these pathetic creatures a genuine threat to our continued existence. The scaling of the system is truly fascinating."

"We were weak back then," Josh noted reasonably, taking a long drink from his canteen. "And they were numerous. They still are numerous. If we had stood in the middle of that valley an hour ago without a choke point, we would have been torn to pieces, regardless of our levels."

Before Brett could launch into a theoretical debate on the exact limits of their area-of-effect damage, a soft rustle of leather announced Perberos's return.

The elf stepped out of the shadows of the cave entrance, entirely alone. He walked over to the group, his silver eyes completely calm.

"Report," Josh said, sitting up slightly.

"The perimeter is secure," Perberos stated quietly. "We found and destroyed several very small, disorganised groups of fleeing goblins caught in the ravines. There is no evidence of any larger threat reforming in the immediate area. The native beasts have completely fled the territory. The valley belongs to us again."

"Excellent work," Josh sighed, feeling the last, lingering knot of tension release in his chest. He looked past his party, staring directly into the swirling, hypnotic purple light of the dimensional rift. "Well then. If the outside is settled, it is time to fix the source. The portal is still spewing out beasts too quickly to just leave. We need to go inside, kill everything in the instance, and steady the ambient mana. It is the only way to force it back into a stable, dormant state."

He stared at the portal, feeling a strange, highly irrational urge bubbling up in his chest. He felt an intense desire to simply draw his sword, step through the purple light entirely alone, and see just how much damage he could do to the floor without anyone backing him up. It was a reckless, arrogant thought, born entirely of his recent class advancement and the sheer high of surviving the horde.

He immediately squashed the thought. It was silly. A shield-bearer without support was just a heavily armoured casualty waiting to happen. He needed to clear the interior with his party to steady the rift.

Before Josh could voice his sensible plan, Bhel stood up, resting his twin axes on his broad shoulders.

"I want to run it by myself," the dwarf announced loudly, his dark eyes gleaming with absolute, unadulterated confidence.

Josh blinked, incredibly surprised to hear his own secret, reckless thought spoken aloud. A moment later, a loud, genuine bark of laughter escaped his chest. Brett immediately joined in, chuckling softly into his hand, while Perberos merely offered a small, highly amused smirk.

"What?" Bhel demanded, looking entirely offended by their reaction. He puffed out his thick, muscular chest, gesturing widely to the portal. "It is a perfectly reasonable request! Look at us! We are level twenty-five. We have tier three skills. We just slaughtered a small army. I could walk through the cave blindfolded and entirely backwards!"

"I do not doubt your physical capability to massacre low-level goblins, Bhel," Brett replied, his tone dripping with his usual academic condescension. "It’s a question of endurance. You would swing those heavy axes for three hours, run out of breath, and then get overwhelmed by a swarm of beasts right at the end."

"Bah!" Bhel scoffed, crossing his arms. "Ronald ran this exact dungeon entirely by himself without a single issue. The man treated it like a morning stroll. We must surely be at his level of combat proficiency by now."

The mention of Ronald brought a sudden, nostalgic silence to the group.

Ronald was the lean, agile feline beastkin ranger who had been their very first combat tutor. When they were terrified novices, practically shaking in their cheap leather armour, Ronald had seemed like an untouchable god of war. He was firm, terrifying, and fiercely demanding. The memory of the beastkin casually walking into the goblin caves alone to rescue a stranded party, and returning without a single scratch, had been a defining moment of awe for all of them.

"Ronald was a highly specialised, incredibly experienced solitary hunter," Brett pointed out logically, adjusting his gloves. "His entire class build was designed around evasion, stealth, and lethal, singular strikes. You are a heavy front-line fighter built for sustained engagement and burst damage. You are loud, Bhel. The entire dungeon would hear you coming."

"Brett makes a fair point," Josh agreed, standing up and stretching his sore back. "Going in completely alone is a massive, unnecessary risk. However, sending all five of us in to clear out basic goblins is a massive waste of time and resources. The corridors in there are narrow. We would just be tripping over each other."

"Then what do you propose, oh fearless leader?" Bhel grumbled, though he seemed perfectly willing to drop the solo idea.

"We run it in pairs," Brett suggested smoothly, completely preempting Josh. The mage held up his hands, outlining the tactical geometry. "We split the combat roles evenly. I will accompany Josh. A heavy, highly resilient fighter to hold the narrow choke points, paired with overwhelming, concentrated ranged elemental damage. We can effectively glass the corridors."

Brett turned to the dwarf and the elf. "Bhel, you will pair with Perberos. The exact same tactical dynamic. You provide the immovable, heavy iron wall at the front, while Perberos provides lethal, high-precision ranged support from the shadows behind you. It ensures no one is fighting alone, and it effectively doubles our clearing speed."

It was a brilliantly simple, highly effective plan. Josh nodded in immediate agreement, perfectly comfortable trusting Brett to watch his back in the dark. Bhel and Perberos exchanged a look, and both nodded as well. Their combat styles complemented each other perfectly.

Suddenly, a loud, highly deliberate cough echoed in the quiet cave.

Brett froze. He slowly turned his head.

Carcan was standing a few feet away, her arms crossed tightly over her pristine white robes. She was tapping her booted foot rhythmically against the stone floor, her silver eyes fixed on the mage with an expression of intense, highly amused expectation.

"Right," Brett stammered, a rare look of genuine, profound guilt washing across his pale face. The scholar had completely forgotten to include the healer in his grand tactical equation. "Ah. Yes. Well."

Brett cleared his throat, pushing his non-existent spectacles up his nose in a nervous gesture. "My apologies, Carcan. Naturally, you are free to accompany either pair into the depths. Your protective wards and wound regeneration would be invaluable."

He paused, looking at her exhausted, pale face, his tone softening considerably. "Or, alternatively, you could simply remain out here and relax. It is not as if any of us will be gaining any meaningful experience points from this delve. The level disparity is far too vast. We are simply acting as magical janitors, culling the interior population to force the rift to stabilise."

Carcan let out a small, warm laugh, the sound entirely devoid of any actual offence. She uncrossed her arms and leaned comfortably against her staff.

"Do not worry, Brett," Carcan smiled gently. "I am not insulted. In fact, your tactical oversight is entirely welcome. I have absolutely no desire to go marching through damp, foul-smelling goblin tunnels today. My mana reserves are completely fine, but my feet are incredibly sore from all the walking over the past few days."

She gestured towards the other parties, who were currently huddled around a small fire, swapping out bandages and drinking water. "Besides, the junior clerics and healers out here are exhausted and terrified. I think my time would be far better spent offering them some guidance and ensuring the perimeter remains stable while you four go and do the heavy lifting."

"Are you absolutely certain?" Josh asked, stepping closer to her. He frowned slightly, not entirely comfortable leaving her behind, even if the perimeter was secure. "We can make it a three and a two."

"I am certain, Josh," Carcan assured him, her silver eyes meeting his storm-grey ones with quiet, unwavering confidence. "You and Brett will be perfectly fine. Just promise me you will not get reckless just because the monsters are weak."

"We are never reckless," Brett declared indignantly, completely ignoring the fact that Josh had literally charged a massive alpha beast head-on only a few days ago.

"We will be careful," Josh promised, offering her a warm, highly reassuring smile.

With the plan finalised, Josh turned his attention back to the wider logistics. Two of the other parties were assigned to remain outside with Carcan, holding the secure perimeter around the portal mouth. The other two were instructed to follow Josh and Bhel's pairs inside.

"This dungeon is currently functioning as an instanced pocket dimension," Brett explained smoothly, stepping closer to the purple light to inspect the magical fluctuations. "It only consists of a single floor right now. That means if we stagger our entrances by roughly thirty seconds, the portal will generate a fresh, completely isolated copy of that floor for each group."

"So we cannot interact or help each other once we step through," Josh concluded, understanding the mechanics immediately.

"Precisely," Brett nodded. "Each pair or group will be entirely alone."

"Brilliant," Bhel grinned, hefting his axes. "No one to steal my kills."

The instructions were simple. Each group would enter, clear their isolated single-floor dungeon of every monster they could find to bleed the ambient mana, cull the boss, and exit. They would then swap out with one of the exterior guard groups, creating a continuous, rotating cycle of magical janitorial work while allowing everyone to rest in turns.

Josh stepped right up to the swirling, hypnotic surface of the dimensional rift. The familiar, deep humming sound of compressed magical energy vibrated in his chest.

"We will go first," Josh announced, looking back at Brett with a fierce, competitive grin. "Ready?"

"Always," Brett replied confidently, stepping up beside his best friend.

"Count to thirty before you follow," Josh instructed Bhel and Perberos.

Without another moment of hesitation, Josh and Brett stepped forward together, crossing the threshold of the portal. The cold, biting air of the valley vanished instantly, and the absolute blackness of the Goblin Caves swallowed them whole.
