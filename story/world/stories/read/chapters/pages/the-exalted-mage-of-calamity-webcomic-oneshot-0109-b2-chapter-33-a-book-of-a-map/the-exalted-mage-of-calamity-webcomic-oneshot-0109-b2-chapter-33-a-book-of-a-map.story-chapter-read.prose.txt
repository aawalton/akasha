“Core Reformation Theory… Elemental Transfusion, Volume Three… Wonders of Teleporting…”

Veronica moved slowly along the shelf, dragging one finger across the spines as she read each title. Every few steps, she paused, pulled a book halfway out, skimmed the cover, then pushed it back into place with growing disappointment.

“All of this is junk.”

“I don’t know,” Gwen said beside her, leaning closer to read over Veronica’s shoulder. “That teleporting book sounds interesting.”

“It’s a book about the wonders of teleporting.” Veronica pulled it from the shelf and flipped it open to the glossary. Her eyes passed over the page once before her expression flattened. “This doesn’t explain how to teleport.”

“Don’t you know how to teleport?” Nasha asked from the opposite shelf, trailing her hand over a separate row of books.

“Yes, but I’m not that good at it,” Veronica said, closing the book. “It uses a significant amount of mana, which I have, but the spellwork needed for teleportation or spatial warping is complex. I usually stick to flying.”

She slid the book back into its place.

Gwen planted her hands on her hips and looked down the long aisle. Towering shelves rose on both sides, filled with books both old and new. Some were leather-bound and worn along the edges. Others looked recently copied, their titles still dark and fresh against the spines. Just this aisle alone had thousands of books.

“What are we looking for exactly again?”

“Blooming-specific spellbooks,” Veronica answered, already moving farther down the shelf. “Anything useful for the Path of Longevity. I can teach you magic theory and mana control, but direct healing spells for your path are another matter.”

Gwen reached up and pulled a thin green book from the shelf. After reading the title, she made a face and put it back. “That doesn’t sound like something we’ll find on the first floor. Most of these books kind of suck.”

“They’re public-access books,” Nasha said, sliding another book out just far enough to check its cover. “That usually means the best ones are somewhere else.”

“Can’t Sage just tell you the spells?” Gwen asked.

Veronica shook her head. “Sage doesn’t contain every piece of magical knowledge in the world. Most of her information comes from Martin’s repository.”

“Martin?” Gwen asked.

Nasha glanced over from across the aisle. “I think you mentioned that name before. Who was he again?”

“Martin was, or is, a great inventor from the Arkavail Empire,” Veronica said. She paused before a section on healing theory, then crouched slightly to read the lower shelf. “Specifically, a magitech inventor. He designed Sage, along with numerous other inventions Vitian used in the future, or eventually adopted.”

Gwen blinked. “Sage was made by someone from Arkavail?”

Veronica nodded. “Yeah. Sage was supposedly only meant to be his assistant. But when he created the time-space artifact, he incorporated her into it at the last moment. He reprogrammed her to assist me in saving the world.”

“Was Martin a mage?” Nasha asked.

“No. He was a regular commoner. No aura. No magic.” Veronica pulled another book free, frowned at the title, and tucked it back into the shelf. “That’s why Sage doesn’t have much information on magic. Only what Martin considered important enough to research or archive.”

Gwen followed beside her, occasionally tilting her head to read a spine before moving on. “That’s kind of strange. A commoner with no magic made something like Sage?”

“Geniuses are inconvenient like that,” Veronica said.

Nasha gave a small hum, but didn’t argue.

Veronica continued down the aisle, her gaze narrowing as she searched. “I’m also looking for another particular book. But I’m not sure if it’ll actually be here.”

“A spellbook?” Gwen asked, tilting her head.

“No.”

Nasha and Gwen both walked with her, looking side to side for anything that might have stood out. The aisle ended a few moments later. Veronica stopped, glanced back at the shelves they had already checked, then turned toward the next row.

“It’s a book called the Map of Avalon.”

Gwen blinked. “A book called a map?”

“Avalon?” Nasha asked, interest sharpening in her voice. “If it’s a map, then is it a treasure book?”

Veronica nodded and started into the next aisle. “A legendary dwarven treasure trove. An island hidden somewhere far beyond the western sea. Supposedly impossible to reach unless certain conditions are met. There are old legends about it, but most people treat it as a sailor’s myth or a lost noble fantasy.”

“But it’s real?” Gwen asked.

“It will be found eight years from now.”

Gwen stopped in the middle of the aisle, mouth slightly open. “Someone actually found an ancient treasure island? That sounds amazing. And you think the book is here somewhere?”

“Maybe,” Veronica said. She continued scanning the shelves, forcing Gwen to hurry after her. “In eight years, someone finds Avalon. Or at least, they find the way to reach it. Supposedly, it was from a book they found in this archive.”

Nasha walked a step behind them now, her eyes shifting between Veronica and the books. “And I’m guessing it didn’t end peacefully.”

“No,” Veronica said. “What followed was a complete disaster. Somehow, the information was leaked. Once word spread, several nations, noble houses, mage towers, and martial factions all fought over access to the island. Archmages and high-tier martial artists were sent in groups. Some were hired. Some came on behalf of their countries. Others came because they wanted the treasure for themselves.”

Gwen slowly lowered the book in her hands. “A war happened over it?”

“Not exactly.” Veronica reached the end of another shelf and crouched to examine a lower row. “It became a contained expedition. Only thirty or forty people actually reached the island. There was an agreement beforehand that, because of Avalon’s ancient and historical importance, anything that happened there would not be used as grounds for open war.”

“That sounds hard to enforce,” Nasha said.

“It was,” Veronica replied. “But it was either that or risk the entire world burning over one island. Too many factions were involved, and all of them had enough power to cause a disaster if things escalated.”

Gwen looked down at the book in her hands again, though she no longer seemed to be reading it. “Who did Vitian send?”

“An archmage,” Veronica said. “Their seventh-tier student. And an eighth-tier aura user.”

She rose back to her feet, her eyes continuing along the shelves.

“Only the archmage came back alive.”

“An aura grandmaster actually died on the island?” Nasha asked.

“Grandmaster?” Gwen asked, looking toward her.

“Mages have their tiers, with ninth-tiers being called archmages. Aura users have their own titles. Those who reach the eighth tier are called grandmasters. Ninth-tiers are called supremes. For regular people, it can get confusing, so using tier numbers like mages do is usually easier.”

Veronica nodded as she checked several more books along the lower shelf. “It was a dangerous journey. Every faction was fighting for itself, and that constant conflict damaged the formation around the island. By the end of it, Avalon sank beneath the sea.”

“What about the treasure?” Nasha asked. “Did no one find it? Or was it just lost?”

“I don’t know.”

“You don’t know?”

“No one does,” Veronica said. She stood and looked up at she shelf in front of her, filled with countless more volumes to check. “Some tried diving down to retrieve it, but the pressure beneath that part of the sea was monstrous. Even archmages couldn’t withstand it for long. The currents were abnormal too, making proper searches nearly impossible, even for specialized water mages. No one knows if the treasure was ever found. And if someone did find it, they made sure to stay quiet.”

Gwen hugged the book in her hands closer to her chest. “That sounds horrible.”

“It was also avoidable,” Veronica said. “Which is why I’d prefer to find the map before the entire world starts killing itself over it.”

Nasha crossed her arms and tilted her head. “So we came here for Gwen’s spellbooks and a treasure map to an island that will one day start a war.”

“But this all sounds extremely important,” Gwen said, one finger resting against her cheek. “This isn’t something Sage has recorded? She doesn’t know where the island is?”

“It’s… complicated,” Veronica said. She paused in front of another shelf, eyes narrowing as she read the spines. “It’s not a real island. Not in the usual sense. It exists in its own dimension.”

“Huh?” Gwen and Nasha said at the same time.

Veronica turned to face them. “You’ve both heard of the Elven Forests, right? It’s not an ordinary place you can reach just by walking in a certain direction. It exists in an isolated dimension. The only way to enter is through a transit method exclusive to the elves. Avalon is similar. The map should contain the method needed to reach it.”

She turned back to the shelves and continued walking. “The title might not be exact. It could be listed as Avalon’s Map, Cartographer of Avalon, The Lost Isle, or something similarly annoying. Legends tend to change names over time.”

Nasha pulled out a book with a faded blue cover. “What about The Fifty Greatest Islands of the Western Sea?”

Veronica took it from her, flipped through a few pages, then shut it with a flat look. “Tourist nonsense.”

“This place has tourist books?”

“It has public books,” Gwen chimed in, slipping her own book back onto the shelf. “I’m telling you. The first floor sucks. It has anything and everything, but most of it is useless. I was lucky to find some usable spell theory books last time. If there’s a book about some ancient treasure trove, it’s probably on the upper floors.”

Veronica handed the tourist book back to Nasha. “Then we’ll try to enter the upper floors.”

“Can we?” Gwen asked.

“We’ll find out.”

The three of them left the long aisle of bookshelves and made their way back toward the central hall of the first floor.

The Starfault Archives was far busier than Veronica had expected. Commoners browsed the open shelves. Students sat at long tables, copying notes from thick theory books. A few mages in robes moved through the area with stacks of books floating beside them.

Near the far end of the hall, a wide staircase led upward to the second floor. Two massive stone puppets stood at the base of the stairs, their smooth faces blank, their bodies etched with faint blue lines. A single swing from one could probably tear through a thick steel wall. Beside them was a counter made of dark, polished wood.

Behind it sat a man in deep blue robes embroidered with silver thread. He didn’t look like a simple clerk. Instead, he appeared more like someone attached to the archive’s authority itself.

His posture was straight. His hair was neatly combed. A small metal badge shaped like an open book rested against his chest.

He looked up as Veronica approached.

“Can I help you?” he asked.

“We’d like access to the second floor,” Veronica said.

The man’s eyes passed over the three of them. “Are you registered contributors of the Starfault Archive System?”

“No.”

The man folded his hands on the counter. “The Starfault Archives operate on standing and contribution. Those who donate ancient texts, rare manuscripts, original spell theories, verified historical records, or other materials of recognized value may earn archive honor. Enough honor grants access to the second floor, then higher floors depending on the scale of contribution.”

His gaze swept over them, scrutinizing their clothes with quiet precision.

“Are any of you of noble blood or particular high standing?”

Veronica raised her hand, showing the viscount’s signet. “Does this work? It represents Viscount Leopold’s House of Ronswick.”

The man looked at the signet for several seconds, then gave a thoughtful hum.

“A viscount’s standing is usually sufficient for second-floor guest access,” he said.

Gwen perked up beside her.

“However,” the man continued, “only if the viscount is present in person, or if a direct member of his family is acting as escort.”

Gwen’s hope immediately died.

Veronica looked down at the signet, then back at him. “The signet represents his authority.”

“It represents his house,” the man said. “Not his physical presence. If Viscount Leopold came here himself, he could bring you in as guests. If his heir or a direct family member arrived with formal identification, that would also be acceptable. But a signet alone is not enough.”

“Why?” Gwen asked before she could stop herself.

The man’s gaze shifted to her. “Because belongings can be borrowed, gifted, lost, or stolen. The archives will not allow random visitors to wave noble property around and tarnish the dignity of the upper floors.”

Veronica’s lips pressed thin.

“I apologize,” he added, folding his hands once more, “but this rule applies to everyone.”

Veronica studied him for a moment. He was not exactly rude. Not openly, at least. But the meaning was clear: they were not important enough.

She tucked the signet away. “Then how much honor would be required for second-floor access?”

“That depends on the contribution.”

“And if we don’t have one?”

“Then the public floor remains open to you.”

Gwen looked back toward the endless first-floor shelves with open disappointment. “Great.”

The man gave a polite nod, already preparing to dismiss them. “If you need assistance finding public materials, one of our attendants can help direct you.”

“That won’t be necessary,” Veronica said. She turned away from the counter.

Gwen followed with a sigh. “So we’re stuck with tourist books and books about how teleporting is wonderful.”

“It is a book about a legendary treasure trove,” Veronica said as they moved away. “There is a chance it might be on the first floor. Perhaps some people would consider it nothing more than a fairytale novel.”

“Can’t you give that man some knowledge that Sage has? Surely there’s something in there,” Gwen asked.

“I don’t think that will work,” Nasha chimed. “That man seems to be very cautious about authenticity. Even if you write down something important, unless it’s from a credible source, he might not even accept it.”

Gwen’s lips tilted to the side. “Damn… Then I guess it’s time to look through tens of thousands of books on the first floor.”

She glanced back toward the staircase leading to the second floor. The stone puppets there did not move, but their blank faces remained fixed forward, watchful and unmoving.

They continued walking, about to check the next aisle of shelves, when a sharp voice cut across the first floor from the side hall.

Veronica slowed.

“This is ridiculous,” a man said. “I was told the Starfault Archives had scholars capable of handling rare material refinement.”

Several people nearby turned.

Near the side of the first floor, beyond a roped-off section marked Material Appraisal, a group of robed scholars stood around a warded refinement table. The table itself was made from pale stone, covered in carved circles and thin gold lines. A transparent barrier shimmered over the top of it, flickering faintly with enchantment.

Standing in front of them was the same nobleman from the carriage earlier. The dark green coat embroidered with silver thread. The black hair styled to look messier than it truly was. The faint impatience in his eyes. Though now, there was something more than impatience: frustration.

Gwen noticed him too and immediately frowned. “It’s that rude noble from earlier.”

Nasha crossed her arms. “Looks like his hurry brought him here.”

Veronica said nothing. Her eyes moved past the noble and toward the refinement table. Sitting in the center of the array was a fist-sized lump of hardened golden resin. It shone beneath the lamps like polished amber, but darker veins ran through it, twisting around something hidden inside. Whatever the object was, the resin had completely swallowed it.

One of the scholars adjusted his glasses, his expression stiff. “Lord Vaun, we said we could examine the material and search for a method of extraction. We never guaranteed that such a method would be available to us.”
