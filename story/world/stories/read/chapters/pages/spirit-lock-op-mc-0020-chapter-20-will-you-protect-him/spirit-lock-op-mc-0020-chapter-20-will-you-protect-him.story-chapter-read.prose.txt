Chapter start.

Nabian knew Hayden was stupid when he sat next to him in the written portion of the test—but he didn’t think he was suicidal!

He guessed wrong—and other people seemed glad about it. The crowd was deafening—everyone was waiting for the fight. Everyone except two.

“I can't watch this.” The woman who laughed at Hayden with Nabian stood and walked down the bleachers.

He thought about following her until he heard someone say, “Wait… Isn't that the scholarship student?”

The second someone said that, the entire atmosphere shifted. Students calmed in a chain reaction, with non-committal statements like, “Yeah… Wait, I think you're right…” bouncing around like popcorn. Nabian's acquaintance stopped on the staircase, looking around with interest. Nabian did the same.

That's when the panic began.

A blonde and her two friends were the first to move. They flew down the stairs, pushing Nabian’s friend aside as they screamed, “That’s the scholarship student! That's Kayline’s boyfriend!”

Nabian’s heart pounded when he put two and two together. Vestra was about to wipe the floor with the most influential student’s boyfriend. The entire crowd understood the severity of the situation, so they started yelling, “Don’t fight him!”

Vestra had grinned at the challenge, but it disappeared when his girl screamed, “Don’t do it Vestra! Call the match!" The entire crowd was saying similar things. His mind dampened when he heard, “That’s the scholarship student that—”

I don’t care who it is, Vestra interrupted in his thoughts. He blocked out the noise and spat. His mom must’ve had a pretty good PR team if people didn't know he killed a student at a real B-rank university. It was an accident, of course, but he still got kicked. That's why he was mingling with trash delinquents at Junolyx—and also why he was seeking out scholarship students for a challenge.

Vestra was too powerful. He was so powerful that he needed aggressive training to restrain his power. And these kids didn't think he could handle a first-year import?

What a joke.

“Start the match,” Vestra demanded.

The referee saw someone in a suit running and screaming the word “stop” in the distance. “I think we should—”

“Start the match,” Vestra interrupted. “I swear to God, if you don't say the word begin, I'm going to have people dangle your son off a bridge. It's a match. No one's dying. But your son might. That's a fact. Now call it!”

The referee gulped. “You can start in three… two…”

“You’re delaying!” Vesta yelled. “Go!”

Vestra planted his foot, summoned his spirit, Razor Net, and threw it at Hayden. It was a unique line spirit that formed a massive net, covering an opponent like a tidal wave. Then, the rifter contracted the net, making it smaller until it strangled their foe with razor wire. It was similar to the Razor Wire spirit, but it covered a wide area—and was far more grisly.

Vestra wasn't going to hurt Hayden too bad. He was just going to wrap him and teach him the consequences of talking shit.

There were rich-kid healers everywhere. It would be fine.

Vestra pulled the net.

Hayden barely got his arms out before the net sucked onto his body in two-inch diamonds.

The crowd screamed—

But there was no blood.

Vestra was shocked to find that Hayden was just standing still, warping the air with aura—completely unaffected.

The whole crowd was gasping, puzzled, and lost. The referee was frozen, and the suits trying to stop the match halted in their tracks.

Then Hayden spoke.

“Its clear your trainers were afraid to put you in danger. This spirit is weak, and your control of it makes it vulnerable to freezing. I'm barely using any aura, and there's no resistance."

He sounded calm and reasonable, but that only made it worse. It was as if he were leading a home showing through a burning building.

“If that scholarship student was in a battle club, he would have closed the distance, froze out your spirit, and bashed your skull into the tiles. Far lesser students could do the same. The fact that you're confident indicates that your opponents throw the match. And you know why they do that, Vestra? Because they fear your parents. Not you. Your parents. Doesn't that embarrass you?"

Vestra gritted his teeth and noted his surroundings. The candidates were mesmerized by Hayden's apparent charisma. Even the officials were ground-locked, as if publicly humiliating Vestra was a historical event that couldn't be interrupted.

Vestra glowered at the officials. Stop the match! he tried to say with his eyes. Unfortunately, their gazes were transfixed on Hayden. They appeared afraid—but they weren't doing anything to stop the event.

“Ask yourself that after the match,” Hayden said ominously. “You’ll have plenty of time to think about it.”

“W-Wait!” Vestra tried to plead, but it was too late.

Hayden grabbed the net with his bare hands, planted his foot, and yanked with surreal force. The officials screamed something incoherent as Vestra stumbled forward—but it wasn't fast enough.

Hayden’s fist crashed into his jaw so hard that he felt teeth and bone fragments fly into his left cheek.

His spirit disappeared, and he flew to the ground, rolling three times before coming to a stop.

Then the pain sank in—

And he screamed.

Sia stared unblinkingly, heart raging in her chest. It was all too stimulating. The crowd was screaming, and the officials were running, and everyone was waiting to see Vestra murder Kayline’s crush—

And then Hayden just shrugged off his attack like a beast, gave a hot lecture, and then backed up his warning by breaking Vestra's jaw.

Vestra Alicibria’s jaw.

It was so shocking that no one knew how to process the scene. Then Huri said, “Dayyum, that girl knows how to pick ‘em,” and the silent crowd detonated with laughter. It was as if people didn't know whether they should cheer or be silent in mourning. But as soon as Huri reminded them that Hayden held strong backing, the bleacher section lost it.

“Looks like he'll have to say yes, after all.”

“Right? But like, why does she even like him? Is he actually a scholarship student?”

“Nah. There's no way a scholarship student would act like this. He's probably a rich natural born.”

Sia felt like she was dumped into a vat of boiling gossip, and she embraced the heat. “Oh… migod,” she said. “Juno Licks has a live stream running. I so can't right now.”

“Seriously?” Varra asked.

“Yeah. There's like twenty videos already. We're talkin’ insta-viral.”

Huri stared at the stage, watching Hayden get patted down for relics, catalysts, and artifacts. “Hey, guys? I know this is gonna make me sound like a hoe, but… Does anyone think he’s kinda hot now?”

Sia connected to a live feed that showed a close-up of Hayden. His skin was still hideous, but despite the stimulation, cheering, and gravity of the event, his face was still as cool and impenetrable as when he spoke to her.

“Girl, that boy's just as hideous as the second you laid eyes on him,” Varra said. “But yes. One thousand percent.”

Huri giggled, and Sia grinned.

“What about you, Sia?” Huri mused. “You confessin’ now? Or are you gonna hoe stalk him ‘till you're alone?”

“Oh, I'm not denying it,” Sia said. “That default on the outside, mad boosted on the inside thing he's got going on? That's a look.”

“That's a look, she says…” Varra remarked. “Girl, that man's gonna to put you down in his contacts as Future Rebound and dot it with a question mark.”

Huri giggled, and Sia shrugged. Then, Hayden pumped his fist, and the crowd lost it.

“Hey guys…” Varra said, nervous all of a sudden. “Time out on the hoe talk. It’s real time. Do you guys, like… think we should—”

“I think we should say sorry to him,” a man said behind them.

Sia turned and saw the woman she had bumped into on the stairs. She was speaking to a man, and both looked sickly and nervous as they spoke to each other.

“You think he'll do something?” the woman asked nervously.

“Nah, he doesn't give me that vibe, but…” The man folded his arms. “I don’t know. That’s one dude I don’t wanna fuck with.”

Sia grinned, “Guess we weren’t the only haters.”

Varra and Huri exchanged looks—and burst into giggles.

Mayle wasn't giggling as she watched Hayden on her office widescreen.

“So?” Loricks asked. “Your rules failed. Vestra committed a crime. The fight was fair… Are you going to protect him or not?”

Mayle turned to him sharply. “Protect him? Do you even know who that kid's mother is?”

“And this…” Loricks slapped his knees and stood up with a dramatized groan. “Is why I refuse to work here. Have fun, Mayle.”

“What? You’re leaving? Now?”

He turned back. “What’s the point of staying? You’re just gonna call me in a few hours.”

Mayle rolled her eyes and flicked her fingers, shooing him out of the building. Then, she winced as she watched the healers work on Vestra's face. It wouldn't heal right. She'd place money on it.

Mayle’s longtime assistant, Keaden Markol, cracked her office door. “Ms. Haya, it's—”

“Tell her we're investigating! " Mayle snapped. “That's final.”

“I already did. This is a call from the academics department. I think it'll interest you.”

“From the academics department? Patch it through.” Mayle accepted a link request. “What's happening? Speak clearly. Who? What's the name? On the test? Are you sure? Yes. Send it over—Immediately.”
