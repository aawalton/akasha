Chapter 128

A keeper commands a chamber because frightened people would rather hold a warm hand than a cold truth, and a hand can be followed anywhere. Seal the chamber if you must. You will not seal the want that raised the priest on top of it. — Codex of the Long Watch, Field Observation, 13th Century, Epirus

“Don’t be stupid. You cannot win,” Arthur screamed in desperation.

The iron bar came down on Breakwater despite the warning.

Arthur pushed back and the shield caught the man across the chest and lifted him off his feet. The shove sent him backwards through the gap between two tabards. He slid on the stones and lay where he landed with his eyes open and unfocused.

Arthur could have killed him. Everybody in the yard saw that he hadn’t.

“Go back home. There is nothing here for you.” Sarah was pleading.

The followers along the wall were still. The woman with the child pulled the boy closer. Some shuffled backwards; some held each other for support. For a long breath nobody moved, because the man with the shield had just shown them what restraint looked like when it was backed by something they could not hope to match.

Then the tabards closed the distance and it stopped mattering. Six drifted in on the porch. Two held back near the altar table and stayed there.

They carried blades, hand-axes, improvised spears. Arthur held the porch with Breakwater covering the approach and the maul ready in his right hand. Donna was on his left with Ravener, her weight forward on her toes. Steve stood behind them with Harrow raised, and Sarah behind Steve.

Arthur set Breakwater across the front and ran the sum he always ran. The fight with the wolves had taken everything they had; here that would not be the case and that was the problem. They were going to win, and winning meant putting down men whose only crime was believing a priest. He also knew that there was no reasoning with fanatics.

The first pair swung together and Arthur turned both blows on the shield without answering either. The man on the right over-committed and stumbled past, and Donna caught his blade on the flat of her axe and forced it down. She could have followed through. She let him go and he scrambled back towards the yard.

“Last warning. If you keep trying to kill us, you will force our hand.” Arthur heard Sarah translate in Greek behind him.

Arthur felt something wrong in the second pair that came at him before he could name it. The one on the left was faster than a man with three months of rough training should have been. He took another blow on the shield and before he could shove the man back, he had reset his stance and made another jab aimed at Arthur’s eye. As Arthur blocked again, a second tabard caught the edge of the shield with his axe and the blow rang Arthur’s arm.

“Techniques. They are using technique,” Arthur said under his breath.

Arthur held the line. He turned the blows, shoved them back and did not swing the maul. Every swing of a Tempered weapon against these men would end the same way. Ending it was not the problem. Donna moved beside him in short controlled arcs, using the flat and the haft of Ravener, turning edges aside. Steve pushed them back with concussive strikes that hit the flagstones and scattered gravel. Sarah tried to talk past the line to the yard beyond, still speaking Greek, still asking them to stop.

There were two men wearing the white tabards who had hung back near the altar. Two men who had put on the tabard but found no stomach for the work. He put them last in his order and turned to face the six. That was the mistake.

One of the men at the altar table brought his hands up and fire leapt towards them. It was nothing like Steve’s clean lance. It was raw and wide, a wash of flame from open palms. The flame crossed the yard and Arthur turned into it without deciding to. He got Breakwater across Sarah and Steve and most of the fire broke over the shield. The rest wrapped his shoulder and the smell of his own skin reached him before the pain did. He heard Sarah swear behind him in two languages.

The fire was not able to penetrate his armour, but it was able to travel down his bicep. Arthur staggered sideways and Breakwater dropped six inches. The gap in the shield line opened for a few seconds and two men came through to exploit it. The first came at Steve and Harrow responded with deadly force. Steve, still looking at Arthur, either did not or could not control the output. A lance of flame blew a hole straight through the man’s chest.

The second through the shield gap swung at Sarah. She sidestepped, caught his wrist, and the overload dropped him where he stood. Next thing he knew was an axe blade in his skull and Donna’s kick shoving him back.

Arthur gritted his teeth and reset his stance. There was no going back now.

Donna looked at the two casters charging their second volley. They had taken a few steps back and were now mixed in with the other followers. That sealed their fate. She triggered Threshold Drive. The yard saw a woman disappear from the porch and reappear behind two men who had not yet finished raising their hands. Ravener took the first one across the shoulder. The second turned to run and collapsed before he had completed the step.

After the casters fell, the fight changed. There was only one end to it. Arthur stopped fighting to contain and started fighting to finish, and finishing was quick.

Arthur came forward with Breakwater. The remaining melee tabards met something they had no answer for. They had levels. They had Ether-fed speed and strength beyond anything mere training could provide. What they did not have was months of fighting things that lived at underground nodes.

Arthur blocked a blade on his shield and brought the maul around in a short controlled arc. The man behind the blade went down. Donna came back through the melee line and took two more in rapid succession, each strike precise with no wasted motion. Steve put one down with a lance at ten metres when the man tried to break for the gate. The last one pulled back to shield the priest.

Papa-Damianos had not moved from the altar. He stood where he had stood through the entire fight. He was looking at the four of them with an expression of pure malice and hatred but no fear.

“We will not kill you, if you surrender. The Council can decide what to do with you,” Arthur said.

In return Papa-Damianos lifted a two-handed maul lying next to the altar and charged them with the last remaining tabard. As he brought the maul down, the Nadi-Matrix on his arms glowed for a fraction of a second and the force behind the strike made Arthur grunt. Before he could shove with the shield, the Matrix glowed again and the maul came back at an impossible speed.

This time it found a shield angled and the maul skidded upward. The next instant, Arthur’s maul met Papa-Damianos’s temple and the big man crashed into the last tabard. Donna was amongst them a second later and the fight was over a second after that.

Arthur sat down on the church steps and put the shield beside him. His right side burned where the fire had caught him and his hands were shaking from something that had nothing to do with the injury. Steve was on one knee near the altar with Harrow across his lap and his head hanging. Donna stood over the body of Papa-Damianos with Ravener’s head resting on the ground.

Sarah had left the porch. She went to the man with the iron bar that Arthur had put down at the start, knelt and put two fingers to his throat before her knees had settled. The warm glow gathered under her other hand.

“Concussion. He’ll wake,” she said, first in Greek and then, catching herself, added in English. “This one will live.”

When the man’s breathing was steady, she moved to a young man with a split scalp. He had been caught in the commotion. Her hand was on it before he could pull away.

“You were down there,” he said in Greek, once the bleeding had slowed. “You saw it.”

“I saw it,” Sarah said without stopping. “It was a wolf. It was only ever a wolf.”

The belief had not collapsed cleanly. Some of them sat where they sat. The praying woman had not opened her eyes. Two young men near the shelters were looking at Papa-Damianos’s body with expressions that could have been grief or fury or both, and Arthur could not tell whether they were mourning a prophet or considering revenge. A girl of fifteen or sixteen stood near the gate with her arms folded tight across her chest, staring at Sarah with something that looked like hatred.

After she had treated those who needed it, Sarah came back and put her hands on Arthur’s arm.

“You keep putting this arm in front of things.”

“It was in front of you.”

“I noticed.”

The woman who had sat through all of it with a child asleep in her lap was the first to move. She got to her feet with the boy on her hip. She looked once at the church and once at the open gate, then started towards it. An old man near the fire got to his feet and went after her.

Sarah called to them. “The road is not safe. We are going back to the city. We will take you.”

It was slow work. Arthur opened the gate wide and stood off it so the open road looked like the safe direction and not the church. Donna put herself between the crowd and the bodies, turning people by the shoulder when they came too close, keeping the dead out of the children’s line. Steve went down to a boy’s height and got him to unclench a fistful of his mother’s skirt.

Steve stood in the middle of the emptying yard, the pouch glowing faintly at his hip. It held the heart of the sanctuary, the core they had been accused of stealing. He looked at it as though he had forgotten it was there.

“Put the god away, Steve,” Arthur said. Steve tucked the pouch under his coat and the light went out of the yard.

A knot of them would not move at all. They stayed by the altar table near the priest, close to the body, unwilling to go. An old woman knelt over it, her hands clasped and lips moving.

The archbishop had wanted his lost sheep walked home. The sheep were here. The shepherd was dead. The walking-home would be the hard part for them.
