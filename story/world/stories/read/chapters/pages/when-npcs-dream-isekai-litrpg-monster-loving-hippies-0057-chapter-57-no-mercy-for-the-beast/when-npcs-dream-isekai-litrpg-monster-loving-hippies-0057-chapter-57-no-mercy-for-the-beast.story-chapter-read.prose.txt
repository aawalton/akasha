We headed north with our large army, more than 200 strong.

This area wasn’t forested, and the road was in good condition, which made things easier for our supply wagons. It didn’t take long before we reached Nish-Iral, the massive capital of the Bird Kingdom.

The architecture in the bird-people city was... different. All the buildings had tall, elegant lines, each wall curving ever so slightly, giving everything a graceful, almost fluid motion—like it had all been drawn by an architect who hated straight rulers.

The rooftops curved upward at the edges like in these traditional Japanese temples, but with weirder proportions and brighter colors.

I had visited this place before, a few months back, and I’d liked it. They had some cool alchemy and magic shops, with the kind of weird-ass items you couldn’t find anywhere else.

It was also just nice to see the huge population of bird alien NPC citizens—brightly dressed in colorful, toga-like outfits, with small, silly hats perched proudly on their heads.

When Duchess first joined us, she sometimes tried to wear these ‘normal’ bird folk outfits herself. But after too many incidents where Stormcrow members burst out laughing in her face, she gave up. Pity. Now, here in this city, we were the weird Naked-Nula aliens with the funny clothes.

Not long after we arrived, we were spotted by a player from the local player guild—The Shining Stars Guild—and were herded to their headquarters, which was stationed inside a huge church building.

As I understood it, their guild had some official role or a questline related to the church. I never really got how this religious quest business works.

But before we could get inside the church and dine with them, they told us we needed to stand in rows, and have their bug folk inspect each of us for controller spores.

I was so relieved they did just that! In contrast to those aristocratic players in Aldiolin, this group took the controller threat seriously.

They even brought along an NPC to check our soldier NPCs — to make sure no player was disguised as an NPC to avoid inspection. Smart.

Well, they did share a border with the Bamboo Agent kingdom, and probably had experience with controller attacks.

Once they confirmed we were all clean, they invited us players to join them for a meal and a religious ceremony.

Once again, it felt like a waste of time. But since they were offering to volunteer some of their members under our command, once again, we obliged.

The meal was simple and humble—in sharp contrast to the grandiose banquet we’d had at the Aldiolin mansion. Here, we were served fluffy bread, orange-dotted eggs, and some seriously strong wine.

Then the bird priest stood up to speak.

“I want to welcome you,” he began. “All Naked Nula and bug people, to our humble church. You came here as heroes, soldiers sent to help free us from the tyrannically evil Vruumstick, and their devil Bamboo Agent allies!”

Ugh.

I knew Vruumstick was a racist slur. It was what bird people called other bird people with a slightly different greenish shade to their feathers, claiming that they didn’t have a soul.

The Vruumstick referred to the bird folk of Sin-Irsh—our enemies. But obviously, we were fighting them because they were being controlled by the Bamboo Agents—not because of who they were.

Still, trying to argue with a racist priest in a fantasy world was just as dumb as doing it on Earth.

“According to our beliefs,” the priest continued, “each person is given a soul by the goddess Tashlil, at the age of two. Even Naked Nula and bug people were given smaller souls by Tashlil. But when the Vruumstick people betrayed the prophetess Yayrashi, by eating her sacred bread, twenty thousand years ago, the goddess punished them by denying them a soul entirely. Instead, they were given a black core by the evil god Tarshish—who, as we remember, betrayed the goddess by taking a pagan god lover—”

This went on and on — stupider and gossipy by the minute — until the priest drifted into full-on god porn. Honestly, the god porn was not boring. Way I saw it, a little celestial scandal makes sermons much livelier.

When he looped back to the boring parts again, I just kept glancing at Juliette, whose ears were red and whose face was about to explode. She did not do well in these situations, and it was absolutely hilarious to watch.

“And now,” the priest finally said, probably after a whole hour, “we may begin the ceremony.”

I swear I heard a quiet pop sound from Juliette’s brain, and Hyun-woo mumbled, “Fascinating culture…”

The ceremony—meant to ensure Tashlil would watch over us in battle and bless us with the righteous slaughter of the soulless Vruumstick. It was a long, uncomfortable mess.

We had to stand completely still while the priest placed a slice of orange like fruit on each of our heads. Then, holding a glass jar filled with thick, gooey white stuff, he walked down the line and smeared some on our foreheads, chanting:

“Tashlil’s sweat shall mix with your blood.”

Gross.

It was disgusting. And boring. But watching Juliette’s angry, funny face was enough to keep me going.

When it was finally over, the priest handed each of us a small religious book and a small jar with the gooey white stuff, and explained how Tashlil welcomed all alien species, and how through her teachings we could grow our souls and all that.

Honestly, he was a nice guy — totally passionate about this Tashlil gal.

After the priest left, the leader of the birdfolk guild, a short fellow named Armasheth, came to talk to me and Juliette.

“It was so kind of you,” he said, “to participate in the priest’s ceremony! Most of us don’t bother, but you made him really happy today.”

“I WILL KILL YOU ALL,” Juliette growled.

“Wha... what?” Armasheth stammered, confused.

“Ah, that’s a Naked Nula joke,” I explained. “Don’t worry about it.”

“Cool,” I said. “What kinds?”

“That’s not something I should reveal,” he said, “but they’ll gladly introduce themselves once you're on your way.”

Then Juliette stepped forward.

“Listen,” she said to the bird leader, “there’s something I need to clarify.”

“Of course. What is it?”

“Our enemy is not the Sin-Irsh birdfolk. Our enemy is the Bamboo Agents. Our primary objective is to secure and conquer towns—preferably without bloodshed. That means not killing innocent Vruumstick.

Armasheth nodded solemnly.

“I understand,” he said. “I know some of us birdfolk are... what you’d call racist. But despite what you may think, we don’t judge based on fur color. The Vruum—I mean, the people of Sin-Irsh—have a different culture. A different faith. Believe it or not, I had Vruumstick friends who—”

“I will make it much simpler for you.” Juliette cut him off. “If I see any birdfolk or anyone else kill an innocent Vruumstick without cause, I will execute them on the spot. If that’s a problem, don’t send your people.”

Armasheth looked at me.

“That’s not a Naked Nula joke,” I explain to him. “She’s very serious.”

He sighed.

“I’ll talk to them. We want to help. This is our battle too.”

“Good,” said Juliette.

“Also,” Armasheth added, “after a lot of back-and-forth, King Melebeth agreed to dispatch fifty soldiers to support your cause.”

“I was hoping for a hundred,” Juliette replied. “Still—better than nothing.”

“With all our troops together, ”Said Armasheth proudly. “We’re now close to three hundred strong! Rumor has it the Order of the Phoenix has amassed a thousand-strong force to attack Sin-Irsh’s second-largest city from the west. From what I hear, Sin-Irsh has less than a quarter of that! And we have the Order’s elite troops, and Geshem the poison mage! Victory is all but guaranteed!”

Juliette gave a small, skeptical chuckle. “It’s really not so,” she said.

And she was right. I had a gut feeling this war wasn’t going to be easy. But having Juliette as our commander? That increased our chances a lot in my book.

We all got ready — players, NPCs, and beasts — and started walking north to Tnu-Sha, the small town close to the Sin-Irsh border. We would spend the night there and attack Raz-Rash the next morning.

We walked for the better part of the day. Honestly, we had wasted an awful lot of time on that nudnik priest ceremony.

It was getting close to evening, but according to the birdfolk, we were less than an hour away.

Then I heard a squeaking argument among some of the bird players

Juliette, Duchess, and I went over.

“What’s that about?” I asked.

They stopped talking and looked at the tall birdman with brownish-green feathers.

“Something’s wrong!” he squeaked. “Its the forest. Too quiet.”

“It is,” said another. “You Naked Nula have horrible hearing, so you wouldn’t notice. But the forest is... very quiet. And that’s never a good sign.”

“Cumkettle-snortpiss…” muttered Duchess. “He’s right. I don’t like it either.”

“EVERYONE STOP!” Juliette shouted. “Form a defensive group!”

The army had been marching in column formation, but they quickly broke into defensive clusters, squads forming around captains and mages.

“Keep listening,” Juliette instruct the birdfolk.

“Something…” said the brownish-green bird, “it’s getting stronger.”

“Yes,” said another. “I hear it too.”

“Something is coming! Chu!” said Duchess. “That… that’s not a good!”

“Many things. Many sizes. Coming fast.” The green-feathered bird’s eyes were wide.

“Scorpion-Cat’s beast army attack,” I said grimly.

Juliette tensed, then shouted, “BEAST ATTACK! EVERYONE, READY!”

We all got ready — weapons drawn, mage circles forming, archers nocking arrows. The goblins smiled.

We tried to form something resembling a condensed formation — a proper defensive line with reserves and overlapping fields of fire. The NPCs did it faster and with more discipline; their captains gave orders and within moments the rows were neat and ready.

Maybe they meant it to be a surprise attack, catch us off-guard. But thanks to the birdfolk’s superb hearing, we wouldn't be caught unprepared.

Now we waited.

It was a smart move by the Bamboo Agent—using the Scorpion-Cats.

Those creatures lived in tribal groups. All the Agents had to do was take control of the tribal leader—or a few elders—and they could effectively command the entire tribe.

A single tribe might have fifty or more individuals, and each adult Scorpion-Cat could control multiple beasts: three to five large ones, or many more smaller animals.

It was an efficient, throwaway army—easy to recruit, and just as easy to sacrifice.

After a minute or so, even we Naked Nula could hear the horde coming.

It started as a faint rustling—like wind brushing through bushes—but grew louder. A rattling wave that vibrated through the trees.

And then they emerged.

Horned rats.

Dozens at first. Then hundreds.

They poured out of the treeline like a living tide—an endless, writhing mass of fur teeth and horns.

This was bad.

Archers were nearly useless—rats were too small and fast—but the mages could do a lot.

Some of our mages fired off fireballs—small, but effective. The rats caught fire easily, and burning ones ran into others, spreading the blaze.

But the rats were smart. As soon as a few got torched, the others adjusted formation—widening their spread, creating space between themselves to avoid chain ignitions.

I formed a wide O₂ suppression bubble and moved it over the densest cluster of rats—not to kill, but to confuse them. Interrupt their oxygen intake, scramble their coordination. It worked—many slowed or stumbled.

I saw another birdfolk gas mage beside me, releasing a cloud of green mist that choked and killed several rats.

I walked over to my two padawans. They still had a lot to learn, but they had enough experience now to contribute meaningfully—so long as they didn’t do anything too stupid.

“Padawans! Listen up!” I shouted.

“I told you to stop calling us that!” snapped Lakshmi.

“Focus—enemy is small and fast,” I explained. “You’re in support roles. Use wide O₂ bubbles. Disrupt enemy coordination, confuse their movement. Let the front line clean up the rest.”

They nodded and started forming their circles. The geometry was decent, but I corrected a few angles and positioning to increase range and coverage.

“Also,” I added, “you’re on a battlefield. You can’t just focus on magic. Many things around you wants to kill you. Stay aware. Watch your backs. What seems orderly now will become a chaotic nightmare soon enough.”

They exchanged nervous glances, but nodded again. It was their first real battle. It wasn’t going to be easy.

When the rats got close enough, archers joined in. Gas, electric, and fire mages were especially effective in closer range. By the time the horde reached us, we had managed to kill about two-thirds.

But one-third still made it.

They bit, scratched, and slammed into our troops with their horns. The rats’ attacks weren’t always lethal — but they were hell to deal with. Every soldier in the front line had three or four clinging to them.

The only ones not struggling were the goblins. They were loving it—slashing, stabbing, laughing maniacally. But they were only twenty in number.

The rest of us? We were occupied. And as I suspect, that was exactly the point. The rats were a diversion—to occupy the ranged units and harass the soldiers so the main force could arrive.

And arrive it did. From the trees, thundering forward:

Huge beasts.

Dozens of Horned Snakes. Huge monstrous Deer-Zebras. Around fifty Armadillo-Bears. Even a few Gorilla-Birds in crude leather armor, wielding spears.

It was a biblical sight—like the entire goddamn zoo had gone berserk.

Then came the Scorpion-Cats themselves. The entire tribe had appeared—some riding the deer-zebras, others on foot, giving commands to their beasts.

This… This was the real battle.

NPC archers were confused by the rats and the soldiers were struggling; they weren’t ready for the larger beasts. The cavalry, luckily, rode tall horses and met the charge head-on, ignoring the rats.

I looked at Juliette. She was ready to enter the fray.

I thought about telling her to stay behind the lines. She was the commander, and irreplaceable and such. But… nah. She had leveled during her last quest. She was stupidly strong. It was her decision to make.

Mages, including me—regrouped behind the infantry lines. I made sure my padawans were among them. I also called over the other Stormcrows mages, medics like Charlotte, and non-combatants like Shooya.

This was no place to wander or improvise. Everyone had to know their place

I spotted Samar, Kim, and Stalin with their beasts. Santiago with his bow. Nayeem with his spear. Everyone was in motion. Everyone was in place.

I even saw Hyun-Woo darting between NPC captains, giving out battlefield strategies. They listened. Troops shifted where needed.

It was time to stop watching.

I had to be a soldier now. It was time to fight.

Some horned rats slipped past our melee lines and came straight at me.

I had a thin sword—light, but wicked sharp. I stabbed one rat through the neck and kicked another aside, finishing it off with a second strike.

“Rats! Pay attention!” I shouted to Padawan Oleksandr. “Take care of them without breaking your circle!”

Fighting while maintaining a magic circle was something we’d trained for—but it was hard. And now, under real battle pressure, it was much harder. Still, they were managing it. I was… kind of proud.

Okay.

Time to show the battlefield what a poison mage can do.

I pulled the cork from a large glass bottle—CO₁ gas, mixed with arsenic sulfide dust I’d recently acquired. I didn’t have a specific casting cycle for the arsenic compound, but I had enough control over my air bubble to direct it.

The plan was simple: use the CO₁ to suffocate, while the arsenic burned soft tissue—eyes, lungs, throat—disabling the target in seconds, and letting the gas finish the kill.

A horned snake—unengaged—was slithering not fifty feet from me. All the nearby soldiers were tied up with their own battles. The snakes were extremely poisonous; if they got through, they’d drop soldiers fast.

My poison bubble reached it in under five seconds.

The snake halted, eyes closed as the sulfur compound scorched them. It made a gagging motion, then dropped dead not ten seconds later.

Next, I saw a soldier struggling to hold back two armadillo bears. He had wounded one of them, but the injured beast didn’t slow. These creatures were being controlled—they didn’t care about pain.

I shifted the same poison bubble over to them. It was still potent. I adjusted its size and position carefully—just enough to engulf both bears without harming the soldier.

Their eyes shut. Their legs stumbled. The soldier stabbed them while they were vulnerable.

Next.

I spun frantically, looking across the battlefield for my next opportunity.

I saw Samar—alongside Dubadilu, Dubadila, Thuban, and Thubana—locked in combat with a horned rat horde. But they were fine. The snake was tearing through the rats like a buzzsaw. The armadillo bears wielded spears with terrifying efficiency.

Samar didn’t need me.

Three soldiers were struggling with a massive zebra-deer.

“GESHEM! BEHIND YOU!”

I spun—just in time to see an armadillo bear charging me, seconds from skewering me on its horns.

I rolled to the side, used my left hand to make a quick mock magic circle to blur the beast's sight, and with my right formed a real O₂ circle to choke its oxygen intake.

The bear didn’t realize what was happening. It didn’t move. Just stood there—choking.

Then I stepped in and stabbed it in the throat.

Damn it. That was too close.

If I died here, Miriam would never see her father again. I’d also be a terrible example for my padawans.

I uncorked another one and went after that zebra-deer.

That’s the thing about poison gas—it doesn’t care how big the target is.

I made that zebra-deer drop, killing it, and then kill its scorpion-cat rider too.

Around me, other gas mages—birdfolk mostly—were doing the same. Setting up O₂ cycles, releasing toxic fog.

Gas mages were having a real impact across the field.

Another armadillo bear gotten too close to me, Santiago snipe him with an arrow. Good shot.

The battle seemed to be going our way.

Some soldiers stabbed. Others bitten. A few were dying.

A birdfolk mage was skewered through the gut by a charging bear. I shouted to draw the beast’s attention, then hit it with a suffocating cloud. I don’t know if the player will make it.

It made the fight hellish.

Gorilla-bird were a sapient species capable of speech and generally peaceful; it was horrible that controllers had turned them into soldiers we had to kill.

Maybe a hundred more had superficial injuries—mostly rat bites and horn stabs.

A single Bamboo Agent probably took a few minutes to take control of a Scorpion-Cat tribal leader, and look what it cost us. All that pain. All that loss.

When you looked at it that way… it didn’t feel like a win at all.

Hopefully, there were only so many Scorpion-Cat tribes the Bamboo Agents could find and control.
