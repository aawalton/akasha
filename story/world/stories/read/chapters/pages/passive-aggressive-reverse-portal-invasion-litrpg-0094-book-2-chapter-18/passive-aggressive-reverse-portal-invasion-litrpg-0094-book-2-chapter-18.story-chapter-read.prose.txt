“Wait. We know you. Or, rather, we’ve seen you.” I said.

He was muscular, for a Hiscariat. Back on Earth that would translate to him being supermodel-thin with just a touch of eating disorder. Here, I couldn’t quite tell if that meant he was buff or just fat. The green robes didn’t exactly show off a whole lot. His facial features, meanwhile, were a bit less emaciated than others, showing off a young man, with short hair and a hint of half-hidden amusement. “Just a moment ago. I was part of the meeting at the Palace that is still ongoing. Yes. My name is Fortune-Unfolds, and I am pleased to meet you properly.”

Victoria frowned. “And you being here instead of at the meeting, what does that mean?”

His tentacles moved in what proved to be a very expressive shrug. Turns out tentacles are versatile enough to really get the point across. “It means, clearly, that I am shirking my duties in order to obtain an opportunity to talk with you. It means that perhaps I may obtain results I never would by rehashing the same boring points for the twentieth time.”

“And what about the part where the Hiscariat don’t trust others of their kind who do not speak mind-to-mind?” She continued.

He cleared his throat. “That… is absolutely part of why I am here as well. Listen. You have just spent an hour with the Holders of the Telvan Outpost. Are you any wiser as to the situation here? Of course you aren’t. Because nobody told you.” A tentacle jabbed back at the Palace. “That is what we do best. We keep our feelers close and our options open. Especially when we deal with outsiders. That way, we can still feel good as we stick to the truth, even as we mislead and guide others to come to their own conclusions about what is happening.”

“Okay, that’s enough beating around the bush, pendejo. What are you saying?” Victoria had clearly had it.

He ignored her and looked straight at me. “Do you actually think that the attack was a mistake? They were testing you! They still are! Trying to find your strengths and weaknesses, find out how we can use your kind to our advantage.”

I grimaced. It did sound a bit far-fetched that somebody would mess up like that and attack without provocation. “If that’s the case, what’s your angle?”

He grinned. There was a quality to it. Like a hyena or something. His smile was just a little bit too wide. “Well, obviously, I am here to outstage the competition. My plan is simple. First, I intend to tell you exactly what’s going on here in the Telvan Outpost. Then, I’m going to tell you all the ways my kind has gotten themselves stuck in a self-perpetuating circle of misery. And finally, I’m going to offer you a solution that would cheat all of them, end with a win for both Hiscariat and humans as a whole. As a side note, it should also win me a lot of positive attention and power.”

I whistled. “That’s a tall order. And you’re going to do all that without pissing off anybody?”

Fortune-Unfolds laughed. “Definitely not. I am going to tie up a lot of feelers before this is over and done with. But yes, I do intend to start in secrecy. Hence the absence of mental speech.”

Steve raised an eyebrow. Then he slowly raised a finger and pointed straight at a pair of Hiscariat under another shade sail, just a dozen meters away. “Secrecy?”

“You’re in a shelter.” He said, as if it explained everything. He indicated the pair, a mother and her son, from the looks of things. “Are you hearing anything from them, mental or otherwise?”

“Oh.” I finally got it. Even if I tried to mentally listen in, I got nothing. There had to be something in the shade sails or perhaps the area itself, that kept any noise or mental speech from spreading. “But if this place is private, why talk out loud?”

“Because I’m a suspicious bastard.” He smiled. “These things aren’t foolproof. So, I take my precautions.” His tentacles grasped in front of his chest. “Now, who wants in on the secrets of the Hiscariat?”

“Okay. Let me see if I get this straight.” I rubbed my forehead and looked at the ground with annoyance, lamenting the lack of any pillows or just a damn chair. I weighed the desire to play my cards close to the chest against the need for personal comfort and folded, pulling my bed roll from my Internal Universe and plopping down on it with a sigh. “The Hiscariat, as a species are pretty far down on the ladder, as far as power and influence goes. Whichever areas you occupy, you defend well because you have impressive mental attributes and focus on. However, you trade that for a weaker physique, with all the downsides that entails.”

“Great job so far.” Fortune-Unfolds didn’t look like his ‘weaker physique’ was affecting him negatively in the least. In fact, he looked like was bubbling with enough surplus energy to keep talking until the rise of the next Calamity.

“Since the Eternal Shard died physically rather close to your strongholds, you jumped at the chance to occupy an area that would let your young and low-leveled gain levels in relative safety. Only, since this was all on a voluntary basis, there are a lot of different interests involved here, working at cross-purposes.”

“Just so. Drift-Ashore is the overall leader. She needs us to prosper, but will be called out for any losses in life, material or anything. Duty-Abounds, that stuffy spawnling, cares only about his trainees and practice. Care-Of-Ways, the bleeding-hearted pacifist, actually wants the Outpost to fail, because her family was outvoted for the leader post.”

He smirked. “Set-Alight and I might agree with our path, but we don’t see eye to eye when it comes to methods. He would never use underhanded methods like this.”

“Not sure I see the underhanded part yet, I admit.” I frowned, picking up the thread. “Anyway, you arrived in good order, got yourself installed and prepped the defensive perimeter – good job on that, by the way.”

“Thank you.”

“Except, the Ceaseless showed up and started attacking. And now, you’re deadlocked.”

“Scoot over.” Victoria said and said down next to me. She waggled a finger up at Fortune-Unfolds. “That part, you’ll have to expand on. We’ve tangled with the Ceaseless. Or rather, Steve and Liam here have. And they aren’t too tough. Especially considering the power we’ve seen from your Mages.”

He obliged. “You got it in that one word. Mages. Our natural trait is just as Liam here said. Our mental attributes grow at an increased rate compared to other species, while our physical attributes pay the cost.” He shrugged. “Logically, we’ve focused on the only classes that make sense with that in mind. Crafters and Collectors that use magic as a main focus, and Mages. That’s it.” He shrugged, a faraway look in his eyes as he kept speaking. “Fighters and scouts are, well, frowned upon. Mostly because they tend to die early deaths.”

For a moment, he didn’t speak. Then a soft shudder went through his tentacles, and he continued. “Yet, that leaves us exactly where we are right now. We have tried to expand from here. Erect outposts to the outpost. Defensive turrets and the like. Yet, the Ceaseless, while stupid, do react. And they have other types of creatures that they employ. Diggers. Ranged attackers. All three times we have tried to expand, we have bled.”

His gaze burned now, with something undefinable. His voice was hoarse with emotion. “We never managed to erect the supply lines we need to thrive. We cannot gather and harvest with our collectors constantly beset by Ceaseless. Our reserves are running out, and…” He hung his head. “We need more than just magical power to expand. We need the force to stand up against the Ceaseless and hold our ground. We need the time to erect our defenses properly, strengthen them enough that the Ceaseless diggers cannot work the soil, prepare the traps. We need-“

“A diversion.” I finished. “Somebody who’ll make them focus in one place and kick their asses, while you get expand and prepare your defenses.”

He exhaled softly. “Exactly. We’ve tried diverting them. Drawing their attention elsewhere. But the Ceaseless are as dogged as they’re dumb. They will not look elsewhere unless their attention is dragged and kept there.”

“And why exactly are we doing this clandestinely?” Steve asked. “Why wouldn’t you just propose this plan to those Holders outright?”

“Because nobody would back it.” His gaze was downcast. “You understand the different interests that are colliding in the city now. Nobody would want to throw their backing at such a plan. Some might vote against it, just because it might work. But. I lead the intelligence gathering efforts of the city. And I can claim just about anything is real.”

“Ohoho.” Victoria guffawed, wagging a finger at the Hiscariat. “You are one twisted pendejo, aren’t you?” She snorted. “Reporting that you are planning an unapproved joint venture with the humans would have all the others come down on you. But if you tell them all, say, that the crazy humies decided to test the strength of the Ceaseless, and you might as well use the chance to expand, while they’re all distracted elsewhere?”

Steve said it for us. “A true victory is to make your enemy see they were wrong to oppose you in the first place. To force them to acknowledge your greatness.” He drily added. “And since they’re Ceaseless, probably to kill them all.”
