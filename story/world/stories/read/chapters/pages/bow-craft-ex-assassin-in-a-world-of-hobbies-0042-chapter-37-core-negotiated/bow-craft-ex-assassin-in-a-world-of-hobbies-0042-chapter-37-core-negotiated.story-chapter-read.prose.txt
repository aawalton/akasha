The searing heat, the shock, the shake—he ignored all of that. What was important to him was what to do in the next couple of seconds, because Lei-rei's arrow had only struck close to the weakpoint. Not close enough.

He only knew what not to do. The core had moved at the last second, and the arrow had glanced off its armor and hit the ground instead, so he put his hands down. If there was no point, he wouldn't aim anymore, and he wouldn't rely on Lei-rei's support. He could only rely on his legs, now, but with how fast it had moved, the only certainty now was the core swallowing his existence whole—

"Bro, what gives?!"

Even that, he couldn't trust.

Did it just—no, not now. He cocked his legs and got ready to run. Was the core stunned? Would it chase him? He couldn't tell. All he knew was that the enemy was faster than him—

"Hey, bro, I'm talking to you!"

Except reality was faster, and no one could run from that.

The core's voice was like some laidback teenager speaking through a distorted microphone with a slight warble. Why? Why are you like this? To him, this guy's mere existence atomized the definition of whatever constituted a "living thing" in Amatoria. The implications...

"Bro—"

Craft held up a finger and closed his eyes. "J-just, give me a sec."

Of course someone would've asked to be reincarnated as a core. With both hands, he covered his face, breathing out a sigh so voluminous that if one were to open up his chest, one would see his lungs all crumpled up inside. Enthusia giving someone casual command over mysterious and terrible energies was strange, but not impossible; possible, but not reasonable!

Anything goes in a peaceful world, doesn't it!... He couldn't really curse Enthusia for it, though. All he could was give up, and you know what? It was easy. The words "Well, This Is A Thing Now" had been common in his line of work; it tended to be when the job entailed confronting incomprehensible forces.

Well, the core wasn't attacking anymore, and in some ways, that made things more complicated. He was more practiced in ending fights than keeping them from happening, after all, but choosing not to fight was a luxury like fine wine, and he still appreciated having the option.

He straightened his legs and relaxed his shoulders, tip-toeing for a moment and stretching up his arms. Right, here we go. “Sorry about that. I didn’t realize you could talk.”

The core slowly rotated like a yakuza who'd been disturbed from enjoying his drink. "Huh?"

Shit, did I mess up—

"This is, like, discrimination, bro."

Craft had to stop. He couldn't even tell if he'd failed. "What?"

"Bro, you're violating like five privacy guidelines just being here, and now you're saying spirit-cores aren't people? Shame." It threateningly bobbed closer. "Shame. Shame, I say."

H-huh? He stepped away and quickly raised his hands before the core got the wrong idea. "H-hold on, just wait a sec!"

"Oh, yeah?"

What the hell's going on here? He backtracked through the facts. The core could shoot lightning, and it was happily wearing black holes like a trench coat. ... Oh no. If it could casually withstand the immense pressure of its existence wanting to collapse at any time, then it could do anything...like manipulate space-time.

If it could do that, maybe—just maybe—it had thought of making a Matrioshka loop for the kicks. Perhaps "for the kicks" wasn't a reasonable rationale, but even among the coldest, most logical world leaders, there were no truly rational people, and so he had no trouble believing that the core had a Hobby of spatial manipulation. He just hoped it didn't have a Hobby of twisting its words, too.

But, with the way that it had spoken, he could only arrive at one conclusion: he and Lei-rei had trespassed into the core's house.

"We're not on the same page!"

That gave the core pause. "You sure, bro?"

Oh, thank God, he listens. "Well, yeah! I've been alive for"—he shrugged and showed a 'so-so' gesture—"two days?"

It froze, and a silence so quiet drew so long that even the wind whispered, guys, say something. "Wait, rly?" It recoiled. "O-ohh! So you're a newbie!"

Craft's chest loosened up; he didn't even know he was holding his breath. It was as if the fog of war had lifted, and what he'd thought was the smoking aftermath of a battlefield turned out to be a backyard barbecue—and through the smoke walked not a man looking for his arm, but a man with a bucket of iced bear.

The core bobbed away, and its intonation took on more whimsy. "Damn, bro, I thought you were trolling me there!" Its color settled to a dim, purplish hue, and it settled closer to the ground. "Name's Pruned. We really got off the wrong foot there, huh? Only other spirits swing by here usually."

Spirit-core. That's what he'd called himself. Craft had an inkling of what it meant, but imagination rarely matched reality. Right now, he was just happy he wasn't getting million-volted any time soon.

That said, neutral didn't mean friendly. He and Lei-rei still had to leave, and Pruned was still a core, the heart of their little arborial jail cell. "Not a problem. Name's Craft. You surprised me, but honestly, it's the least of my"—hiding Lei-rei's existence—"problems."

The core kept quiet for a while. "Problems, huh?"

There was an itch on Craft's nose. Shit, was I found out—

"Yeah, man, same, same..."

W-what's going on---

"Y'know! There was this time I met this guy in a bar---"

While Pruned went on with his story about making a friend and losing him to the wiles of a good card game, Craft couldn't help but consider how utterly wrong he had been at every single turn of his interaction with the guy so far. Maybe, just maybe, he should stop jumping to conclusions so much anymore. It clearly wasn't working.

"My girlfriend even left me for that sucker! I really thought we were friends, man!" Pruned continued.

You had a girlfriend? So you're really a guy? "Sounds rough, buddy." Did spirit-cores have a dating culture? Was it superior to human dating culture? Who knew? All he knew was it never got easier being betrayed, no matter how inevitable. "Well, at least it wasn't the end of the world, right?"

"Bro, my social life ended right there. ... Hey, what would you have done?"

"What do you mean?"

"I'm saying, if your girlfriend fell for some edgy dark knight type, whatchya gonna do about it? For reference."

Ah, shit, he hadn't listened to the whole story. Well, there was enough to go on. Hopefully.

"I'm not sure what you're hoping for, but if she's the one choosing to walk away from you, I don't really see the point of fighting for anything more."

"Not really a romantic, are you, bro?"

"Not by a longshot." He wished he'd been, though.

"Bro, you were talking about something?"

“Right. I was actually on my way to Monstera, but the bridge got destroyed. I ended up taking the scenic route through the forest and, well, I wandered in here and annoyed the hell out of you.”

"Nah, don't worry about it, bro! Like I said, people don't come here often, so it's actually pretty great to bump into you like this—well, we could've made better first impressions, but it ain't as important as the last impression, right?"

Just when I thought this was some easygoing guy. Pruned might actually be wiser than he sounded. If he was that smart, he would've put two and two together and figured out that Craft had wandered into his spatial loop with no way out. The fact that he was still asking meant he was either sometimes wise---or intentionally skirting the subject.

In that case, asking "Where's the exit?" would be too straightforward. "Right. Anyway, I was on my way to Monstera because of an emergency, so I was hoping you could help me out here."

Craft plastered on a lost traveler's smile. He wasn't lying. Take the hint.​

"No can do, bro."

Craft hid his balled fist and shifted his feet. He didn't expect Pruned to put his figurative foot down. "Any particular reason?"

"Oh, yeah. You dunno about spirits, right?"

"Well, I don't. But what's that got to do with anything?"

Pruned's light came alive again, and he floated up and around Craft; his demeanor only made Craft more nervous. "To do anything—and I mean anything—a spirit's gotta sign a contract." It paused. "And no lol, we can't sign contracts with other spirits."

Craft shifted uncomfortably. He pronounces 'lol.' "And you've got a contract with my name on it, I take it."

"Both your names, bro."

He knew all along. How good was Pruned's intel? Well, the fact was he had the advantage from the start. By all accounts, Craft and Lei-rei had already lost every possible physical confrontation.

He raised his hands in front of him in surrender. “A contract?” he asked, half to know and half to stall. "Sounds like a pain. Maybe we can talk about it."

“Sorry, bro, I signed an NDA too.”

"Seriously?"

"Yep. Well, I can't tell you about it, but..."

Small fairies with pointy hats settled on the branches of the trees around them, and wherever they sat, the chalk-white bark turned into basaltic stone, and wherever Craft looked, there seemed no way out. Just what had happened to that easygoing world Enthusia had planted in his head?

"Okay, guys, go check out the other one for me," Pruned said, and suddenly Craft was in a race against time. Two of the fairies flew in front of him, saluted, and darted off. Lei-rei would be captured, and it was up to him to get a better deal before they brought her here.

Between the carrot and the stick, he didn't have any carrots here.

“Listen, we’re actually out getting Hobby supplies for a friend of mine. She’s down with Anima deficiency.”

“Oof, that never goes easy.”

The fairies recoiled in sympathy. One of them fell off their branch.

"Say, what's her name?" Pruned continued.

Craft thought it'd be safe to give him the name of the assistant temple manager. It sounded like a respectable position that no reasonable person in this society would touch. "It's Nightshade. She's the assistant at the temple."

"Oh! Yeah, yeah, I know that gal! Creeped me out a little at first, but she's nice!"

H-hm? Nightshade, what did you do? He shook the thought. “Right?” he continued. “So if we don’t get out of here, she won’t get to do her Hobby.” He gulped. Just let us go with this one. “You get it, right?”

If Pruned were to keep them entrapped, he'd be indirectly getting in the way of Nightshade's Hobby. It was an interpretative stretch, but Craft hoped it would weigh on Pruned's conscience.

The core floated away an inch. “You’re putting me in a tough spot, bro"—thank God—"If it were up to me, I'd just let you do your thing, but shit, bro, I ontologically can’t break contracts.”

Craft narrowed his eyes. Ontologically. You can't use that word lightly. “Not even for the Three Rules?”

Pruned dimmed his shell. “Really sorry, man. That’s just how spirits work.”

Craft couldn't understand why Pruned couldn't do what was so clearly the moral choice. For the sake of sticking to the letter, he'd block someone's medicine from reaching them. It just didn't make sense.

"A slave to contracts?" he muttered. "That's how you work?"

Pruned didn't take that kindly.
