The heavy oak doors of the Guildhall remained closed, but to Carcan, it felt as though they had just sealed her twin brother into a tomb.

The frantic, deafening noise of the adventurers preparing for war around them seemed to instantly fade into a dull, underwater hum. Carcan stood perfectly still by the wooden booth, her silver eyes fixed blankly on the spot where Perberos had just vanished. For months, she had been the unshakeable pillar of the party. She had been the serene, calming light in the absolute darkest of places, pouring her soul out to keep her friends breathing.

But right now, the absolute terror finally broke her.

Her aristocratic elven features crumpled. A ragged, choking sob escaped her throat, her shoulders trembling violently beneath her pale green robes. The tears she had bravely held back for her brother's sake finally spilled over, leaving hot, shining tracks down her porcelain cheeks.

Josh did not hesitate. He stepped immediately into her space, completely ignoring the grime and dried mud coating his heavy steel plate armour. He wrapped his massive, muscular arms around the elven healer, pulling her securely against his chest.

Carcan did not push him away. The proud, dignified elven woman completely melted into his embrace. She buried her face into the thick, padded gambeson at the crook of his neck, her hands gripping the cold steel of his backplate as if he were the only solid thing left in a crumbling world. She wept openly, her slender frame shaking with the raw, terrifying force of her fear.

Josh held her tight, resting his chin gently against the top of her silver-white hair. Beneath the scent of expensive incense and dried lavender from her robes, he could smell the faint, comforting aroma of pine needles. He could feel the incredible, radiating warmth of her body pressed against his own.

For a very long time, Josh had been deliberately ignoring the quiet, persistent flutter in his chest whenever Carcan smiled at him, or whenever she scolded him for being too reckless. He had pushed those feelings deep down, burying them under the constant, overwhelming need to simply survive another day in this brutal world.

But holding her now, feeling the desperate thudding of her heart against his breastplate, that quiet flutter solidified into a heavy, unbreakable anchor of pure iron. The oath he had just sworn to Perberos was no longer a simple tactical promise between warriors. It was a profound, personal vow. He would absolutely rather die in the mud, his bones shattered and his shield broken, than let a single undead creature lay a rotting claw upon her. He would protect her with his life.

They stood there in the shadows of the booth for several long minutes, the chaotic Guildhall rushing past them entirely unnoticed.

Eventually, the violent trembling in Carcan's shoulders began to subside. She took a deep, shuddering breath, her hands slowly relaxing their desperate grip on his armour. She pulled back slightly, wiping her wet cheeks with the back of her elegant hand.

A bright, sudden flush of deep embarrassment coloured her pale face. She looked away from Josh, staring down at the scarred wooden floorboards.

"I am so sorry," Carcan whispered, her melodic voice thick and raspy from crying. "That was incredibly unseemly of me. I am a healer of the Mother Tree. I should be projecting strength."

"You are allowed to be terrified, Carcan," Josh replied softly, keeping his hands gently resting on her shoulders to keep her grounded. "We are all terrified. Sending him out there is a horrific gamble."

Carcan sniffed, offering a weak, self-deprecating smile. "You do not understand, Josh. Perberos and I are twins. From the very moment we were born, we have been a single, unified entity. I have never been separated from his side for more than a few days in my entire life. We learned to walk together, we learned to channel magic together."

She looked up, her silver eyes shining with fresh, unshed tears. "And now he is walking into a frozen expanse of death, and I cannot follow him. If he falls, if he is bleeding out in the dark, I will not be there to weave the golden thread. He will die entirely alone. That thought is completely paralysing."

"He is the best scout in this city, Carcan," Brett interrupted gently, stepping out from the shadows of the booth. The elemental mage had wisely given them a moment of privacy, but he now carried three massive, sloshing wooden tankards of dark ale. "And he has Ronald with him. They are ghosts. The undead will not even know they are there."

Brett set the heavy tankards down onto the scarred wooden table and slid into the booth, pushing his imaginary spectacles up his nose. He offered the elven healer a warm, highly uncharacteristic smile.

"Now, sit down," Brett ordered softly, patting the wooden bench beside him. "Drink this. It is terrible, it tastes like fermented dirt, but it will warm your blood. And we are going to talk about absolutely anything other than the rotting army currently marching towards us."

Carcan let out a small, wet laugh. She allowed Josh to guide her into the booth, taking the heavy wooden tankard in both hands. She took a long, tentative sip. She grimaced slightly at the bitter, utilitarian taste of the dwarven ale, but the alcohol immediately sent a warm, soothing flush through her chest.

"I remember a time when Josh was convinced he was invincible," Brett began, taking a massive gulp of his own ale and leaning back against the wooden wall. "We were much younger, growing up in our home city. Josh found a steep, incredibly dangerous gravel hill near the edge of town, and he decided to build a crude, wooden sled out of discarded shipping crates."

Josh groaned loudly, burying his face in his large, calloused hands. "Brett, please do not tell this story. I was an idiot child."

"You were a very confident idiot child," Brett corrected, pointing a long finger at his best friend. He looked back at Carcan, his pale eyes dancing with genuine amusement. "He dragged this heavy wooden contraption to the absolute highest peak of the gravel pit. He did not test the steering. He did not account for the sheer drop at the bottom. He simply sat down, yelled a battle cry, and pushed off."

Carcan’s eyes widened, the horrific image of her brother alone in the dark momentarily replaced by the absurd mental picture of a young Josh hurtling towards his doom. "What happened?"

"He achieved a terrifying velocity," Brett laughed, the memory clearly bringing him profound joy. "The sled hit a hidden rut halfway down, shattered into a dozen pieces, and launched Josh directly into a thick, highly unpleasant patch of thorny bushes. He broke his arm in two places and had to be carried home by a passing merchant. He cried for three hours."

"I did not cry for three hours," Josh argued defensively, taking a long drink of his ale to hide his growing smile. "I was in a state of severe tactical shock. There is a very distinct difference."

Carcan let out a genuine, melodic giggle, the sound cutting beautifully through the heavy gloom of their corner. "It seems your inclination to throw yourself headfirst into dangerous situations is a deeply ingrained personality trait, Josh."

"He simply lacks a self-preservation instinct," Brett agreed, raising his tankard in a mocking toast.

The alcohol and the warm, engaging conversation slowly began to act as a potent, much-needed balm for their frayed nerves. They ordered another round of drinks from a passing, exhausted tavern maid, resigning themselves to a long, sleepless night of waiting.

As the second round of ale began to sink in, the conversation naturally drifted towards the families they had left behind. It was a topic they rarely discussed, usually too focused on the immediate horrors of the dungeon to look backwards.

"Perberos was not always so cold and stoic," Carcan revealed softly, tracing a finger around the rim of her wooden tankard. The ale was bringing a soft, rosy flush back to her pale cheeks. "When we were children, he was an absolute terror. He was constantly climbing the highest branches of the Mother Tree, getting stuck, and forcing the village guards to fetch him down. He was loud, and he laughed constantly."

"What changed him?" Josh asked quietly.

"Our older brother, Marek," Carcan sighed, a deep, ancient sorrow shadowing her silver eyes. "Marek was a ranger of the high border guard. He was everything Perberos wanted to be. Strong, silent, and incredibly deadly. When Marek was killed during an orc raid on the western borders, Perberos stopped laughing. He picked up Marek's bow and simply decided to become him. He took all of his grief and forged it into a weapon."

Josh and Brett remained silent, offering a quiet, respectful moment for the fallen brother they had never met. It perfectly explained the terrifying, uncompromising chill that Perberos always carried with him.

"My family is entirely the opposite of stoic," Brett chimed in, leaning heavily on the wooden table. The mage was clearly a lightweight, his words beginning to slur very slightly at the edges. "My father, Arthur, was a minor archivist. He spent his entire life buried under mountains of dusty scrolls. But my mother, Beatrice... she was an absolute force of nature."

Brett smiled, a fond, distant look in his pale eyes. "She ran our household like a strict military encampment. We had schedules for reading, schedules for eating, and schedules for sleeping. And I have three younger sisters who simply never stopped talking. They were a chaotic, swirling vortex of noise and drama. I think that is why I ended up preferring the quiet company of dusty library books."

"I would very much like to meet your sisters, Brett," Carcan smiled gently. "They sound incredibly vibrant."

"They would talk your pointed ears completely off, Carcan," Brett chuckled, taking another sloppy drink of his ale. "They are exhausting."

Carcan turned her attention to the heavy fighter sitting across from her. "What about you, Josh? What kind of family produces a Guardian?"

Josh looked down at his scarred, dirt-caked gauntlets resting on the wooden table. He had to tread incredibly carefully here. He wanted to share his life with her, but he could not shatter the illusion of their origins. He needed to translate the world of concrete, cars, and electricity into a format she could understand.

"My dad is named Tomas," Josh said quietly, his voice rumbling with a deep, fond affection. "He is a very quiet, hardworking man. He builds things. He is not a fighter, just a man who works with his hands all day to make sure we always had food on the table. My younger sister, Elia, is the smart one. She is fierce, fiercely protective, and she always managed to talk me out of trouble when I did something incredibly stupid."

"Like jumping a sled into a gravel pit?" Carcan teased softly.

"Exactly like that," Josh grinned.

He took a slow breath, the memory of his childhood bringing a sharp, unexpected pang of homesickness to his chest. "My mother... she passed away when I was quite young. She was a keeper of the peace in our city."

He paused, carefully selecting his next words to describe a police officer to an elven healer. "She wore a uniform, and she walked the local districts to make sure the common people were safe from thieves and violent men. She was incredibly brave. She died in the line of duty, protecting strangers."

Carcan reached across the scarred wooden table, her elegant, pale fingers gently resting over Josh's heavy steel gauntlet. The touch was incredibly warm and grounding.

"She sounds like a true Guardian, Josh," Carcan whispered, her silver eyes filled with profound, empathetic understanding. "It is entirely clear where you inherited your unyielding spirit. You honour her memory every time you raise your shield for others."

Josh looked at her small hand resting on his armour, feeling that heavy, iron anchor in his chest settle even deeper. He simply nodded, unable to trust his voice for a moment.

They sat together in the dim, flickering light of the corner booth as the hours slowly ticked by. The chaotic energy of the Guildhall gradually died down as the exhausted adventurers finally succumbed to sleep, curling up on the wooden benches or the floorboards to rest before the morning light brought the siege.

The alcohol hit all three of them very differently.

Carcan simply grew more relaxed and talkative, her usual serene composure replaced by a warm, unguarded affection. Josh felt a pleasant, heavy buzz in his blood, dulling the sharp, physical ache in his cracked ribs and his bruised shoulder.

Brett, however, completely lost his battle with the dwarven ale.

The elemental mage attempted to launch into a highly complex, slurred explanation about the magical theory of thermal displacement, but halfway through his sentence, his eyes simply drifted shut. He slumped forward, his face landing squarely on his folded arms on the wooden table, and he began to snore softly, completely dead to the world.

Josh and Carcan laughed quietly at the sleeping mage, careful not to wake him.

The noise of the hall faded into a low, ambient murmur of crackling hearth fires and distant snores. They were left in a quiet, intimate bubble of shared warmth and shadows. They did not speak of the undead horde, or the creeping corruption, or the terrifying scouting mission Perberos was currently undertaking in the frozen dark.

They simply sat together, sharing quiet stories of their childhoods and the families they loved. In the face of absolute, impending annihilation, they stubbornly held onto their humanity, finding a fragile, beautiful comfort in the dark before the storm broke over Ashenfall.
