“I’ll admit it, this is a new one for me.” Kyle Hunter had learned in his years as a general practitioner that he shouldn’t tip his hand and admit any kind of uncertainty in front of a patient, but this was so far out of left field that he had no idea what was happening, and there was no hiding it.

This one was a head-scratcher.

Kyle probed the young man’s deformed arm, feeling the subtle changes in the boy’s bone structure that had led to this sorry state of his arm.

He followed the carpals up to the ulna and humerus, logging each deformity in his mind as he did so.

My best bet is some kind of parasite, Kyle mused as he probed further, finding subtle and not-so-subtle changes all the way down into the kid’s ribs, hips and legs. Pretty much his entire body, save for his left hand had been wracked with mutations.

“Have you gone to any strange places, eaten anything uncooked?” Kyle asked, opening his computer, logging the deformities and requesting the full battery of tests.

Bloodwork, stool sample, CAT scans…the works.

“No more strange than usual, and no, I always cook my food.” The young man said.

“Hmm.” Kyle hummed as he logged the order and turned back to the young man watching him patiently.

“And you’re sure this isn’t congenital? This is the first time I’ve seen anything like this. We’ll run a full battery of tests to try and figure out what’s going on, but there’s a strong chance you’ll have to be referred to a specialist.”

Kyle slipped on his stethoscope and listened to the kid’s heart, looking for any other problems that might slip past if he was too focused on the obvious.

The kid’s heart sounded strong. Almost unnaturally strong.

“like a drum.” Kyle mused aloud, slipping the earpieces down his neck and scooting his chair back to his station, grabbing his reflex hammer and tapping the kid’s knees.

Nothing obviously wrong with the nerves. Whatever it is isn’t messing with his spine.

Pupillary response was also good. Pretty fast, actually… Kyle frowned, flickering the pen light back and forth over the kid’s eye, watching as the pupil snapped open and shut at inhuman speed. He couldn’t even see the transition with his naked eye. One instant they were dilated, and the next they were contracted, and vice versa.

Kyle was halfway to the next test when his mind caught up with his own observations.

Inhuman speed.

Kyle broke out in a cold sweat.

He’d heard a few horror stories about…monsters coming from the wilderness the last few years. Monsters wearing the skin of hikers, who would brutally slaughter their friends and family.

Kyle considered himself a rational man who didn’t give credence to rumors and make-believe, but the rumors of monsters were everywhere, and they had taken up residence in the back of his mind.

As his grandfather used to say: where there’s smoke, there’s fire.

Kyle cleared his throat, trying to hide his nervousness. Gotta pull yourself together, nothing proves this kid isn’t human…other than the weird flipper-hand, messed up bones, unnaturally strong vitals, and eyes that can snap his iris open and shut faster than a machine could.

“I’m just gonna take a blood sample.” Kyle said, pulling out his draw kit.

“What for?” The kid asked, watching Kyle with predatory intensity that made his hair stand on end.

“For the blood tests?” Kyle lied. Well, it was still going to be a test of sorts, but probably not to find the source of the deformations.

Kyle took out the needle and put one of the vials on it, swabbed the crook of the kid’s arm and gently pressed the needle into the young man’s skin.

A moment later, Kyle withdrew the needle. The thin steel had blunted and rolled up against the kid’s skin.

“Is that normal?” The kid asked, watching Kyle staring at the bent needle.

“It happens.” Kyle lied, standing up and backing away on trembling legs. “Sometimes we get a bad batch of needles and they’ll roll up instead of doing their job. I’ll just go…get a needle from a different batch.”

“Any advice for how to fix my arm?” the kid asked as Kyle backed towards the door.

“Surgery and lots of physical therapy,” Kyle said, maintaining a straight face as he gathered up his clipboard and pen, trying to look at normal as possible. “You may need someone to help you getting dressed. Do you have a spouse, girl slash boyfriend or caregiver you can rely on?”

The kid held up his left hand to forestall any further questions.

“No need, I can move things with my mind.” The kid said. A moment later, the nearby pen he’d been using lifted up and spun around on its own.

Kyle’s skin broke out in a cold sweat as he got the final confirmation that this young man didn’t belong here. The thought echoed in his mind with unnatural intensity, growing in volume as rebounded through him.

Doesn’t belong, doesn’t belong, DOESN’T BELONG!

What is going on!? Kyle thought as his stomach churned painfully, forcing him to his knees, his very bones vibrating and creaking as something washed through him, bringing with it a certainty that this young man had to DIE!
