“Congratulations,” Ferris said, happy for Brix. She’d taken failing her first test to being delving hard. “Does that mean you found a solution to the attack ability problem?”

“Oh, did I ever.” She pulled out what looked like a tube with a dozen glass bottles attached to it. It reminded Ferris of tomatoes on a vine. “I liked the idea of a bunch of throwable bottles, but I was having trouble filling them one by one. My ability wants to shoot out all at once, not stopping at all. Instead of making it less useful in battle by changing it, I was able to adapt how I fill them. My father and I cooked up this little baby, watch!”

She took a pitcher of water and poured it into a funnel at the top of the tube. The water ran down, filling the glass bottles from the bottom to the top. Ferris remembered her father worked with mundane pipes or something similar, leading to the idea for her strange bonded cauldron.

“Then,” she said, setting the pitcher down, “I remove and prepare them with stoppers and flammable cloth.” She pulled one of the bottles off, and somehow the liquid inside the tube didn’t spill out. It looked like something was now blocking the hole to the tube. She sealed the bottle, which now had a piece of cloth on top that she would presumably light on fire before throwing.

Mother Lily came out of the kitchen, forcing Brix to put her demonstration away. The house mother must have heard ahead of time that Brix had passed, because dinner that night was a celebration. Ferris eyed Cyrus a few times, wondering how he felt about the feast. The day he had passed his examination was the same day Brix had failed, and the mood hadn’t been as jolly. The young man appeared to be happily enjoying the impromptu party.

Ferris and Jade spent longer at dinner than he had planned, but he couldn’t say he regretted it. The joyful atmosphere was infectious, and Jade was having a ball. At one point, the slime tried to fit inside the tube of glass potions, but their core got stuck at the funnel. Brix thought it was hilarious.

“Please,” Brix said to Cyrus, for maybe the fifth time, “you've got to tell me more. You have been delving for two weeks now. Gimme the details.” She had gone over to Cyrus, and was lightly pulling his arm back and forth dramatically.

Ferris couldn’t help but want to hear as well, although he wasn’t as persistent as Brix was. All night, Cyrus had waved away the question or answered very abstractly. This time, however, it appeared she had worn him down. After a moment of reflection, he answered.

“The monsters,” he said, “you hear about them your entire life, but nothing prepared me for the reality of them.”

Ferris had only entered a rift for the first time about a month ago, but that wasn’t the first time he’d seen monsters. Watching Cyrus, the look in the boy’s eye reminded Ferris of his own nightmares. Over the last two weeks, he’d assumed Cyrus wasn’t talking about his experience delving to spare Brix’s feelings. Maybe there was something more.

“The first day I saw a monster,” Ferris said, meeting Cyrus’ eyes, “was the day I lost my leg.” Ferris wondered if Cyrus had been dealing with personal demons over the experience. He wasn’t sure if there was anything he could do, other than show he wasn’t alone. “I was raised with a martial tradition, and nothing prepared me for the sight of them.”

Cyrus didn’t respond, lost in his thoughts. Brix had apparently sensed the shift in mood and sat back down in her chair.

“Well,” she said, a glint in her eyes, “I guess tomorrow I will see what the fuss is all about.”

After dinner, Ferris and Jade returned to meditation.

The first thing he wanted to do was test Jade’s mental body’s ability to heal, and see how it affected the slimespace. The question had been bugging him since before dinner, so he went to gather supplies for the test.

He needed food and a way to cause a small cut, but if he used his spear, it could be damaged. The corpse pile had plenty that he could scavenge for food. He considered finding a couple of sharp bones to use as a knife, but then noticed that he had an abundance of demon horns. Every type of demon he had encountered so far had a pair of horns, and Ferris was able to break a few off with his axe.

After entering the slimespace and returning to his perch, Ferris fed Jade a few morsels of chopped demon. The grey birds circled above the Consume Pool, although he was too far away to see the small chunks fall.

Watching along the wall, Ferris spotted the same grey birds gathering. That confirmed that the food eaten by the mental body interacted with the wall, at least.

Climbing down, Ferris and Jade went closer to the wall where he’d seen the birds gather. When they were close enough to try another piece of food, Ferris ran into a problem. Jade had been rolling along on the ground behind him, so he had no idea where to feed the slime from. Previously, Serena had just remembered to place the food at the same location, but Jade didn’t have anything to differentiate front from back. The slime rolled around, with no way to keep track of a specific spot on their outer layer.

Shrugging, Ferris started to feed Jade more food in different locations until he spotted the birds circling close enough to see. Oddly, the wall didn’t lose its glow like normal.

He carried Jade closer to the wall, then gave the slime a dozen more pieces.

When they’d tested chopping off a large portion of Jade’s body, the dome’s wall had been damaged in a repeating pattern of spots.

The same pattern applied to where the food’s effects appeared. Ferris finally got the birds to circle in front of him, right in the center of a pristinely repaired portion of the wall.

Watching carefully, Ferris was able to see that the wall did lose its glow, but in a much smaller area. He probably wouldn’t have noticed if he hadn’t known to look for it.

Previously, Ferris had noticed that food eaten by Jade’s mental body stayed the same size, while food from the real world was more than one thousand times larger. It must be the same for this glow.

Beginning to suspect what would happen, Ferris pulled out one of the horns. After warning Jade and carefully backing up, he stabbed the slime in the same location he had fed them.

A small hole appeared right where the wall had darkened, and a thin, pressurized stream of acid shot out. The birds appeared to circle above. The pressure shooting in died down to a calmer flow, followed by a few ants sacrificing themselves to repair the hole. It was still hard to see exactly what was happening, as a cloud of smoke burst out around where the ants worked.

So, damage to the mental body worked the same as from the real world, but without the increase in size.

Ferris tested a few more quick stabs with the horns. To the ants’ dismay, the holes in the wall kept reappearing as they frantically worked to fix them. The horn looked no worse than it had at the beginning, matching Ferris’ experience in the real world with quick stabs of the knife.

Slowly pressing the sharp horn into Jade didn’t cause a hole in the wall. He had to stab with moderate force, or else the slime’s outer layer would mold itself around instead of being damaged.

Stabbing in various nearby places caused the hole to appear at different spots on the pattern. Moving up or down stayed at the same location, but left or right jumped one spot over. It was like someone had drawn lines vertically on Jade and assigned each section a spot in the pattern.

Next, Ferris tested if the pattern stayed the same after Jade moved around. Would rolling change which part of the slime was considered up? At first, Ferris had some trouble with inconsistent results, but eventually figured out that the sections were based on Jade’s core, and not their outer body.

As the slime rolled around, the core would stay upright and stable. It appeared to have a natural gyroscopic balance.

Ferris thought back to the first time Jade’s mental body had died. The fish had likely caused a hole in the wall after all, but small enough that Ferris hadn’t noticed. This would mean that Jade’s mental body was a lot more resistant to damage, at least when it came to the number of ants required to repair the dome’s wall. Even better, if they could figure out what controlled the size difference, then maybe they could selectively shrink attacks from the real world. The possibilities were enticing.

On a roll, Ferris decided to further investigate exactly when an object would be dissolved inside Jade’s body.

After returning to the Consume Pool, Ferris watched carefully as he stabbed Jade with the demon’s horn. The tip of the horn didn’t appear above the Consume Pool until he had pushed it in past Jade’s outer layer. For a brief moment in time, Ferris could see two versions of the demon horn: one in his hand and one falling into the pool. Then, once it splashed down, ants swarmed in. Both versions dissolved away together.

Ferris knew that Jade’s acid was tied to the pool, but this was the first time he’d been able to see the precise moment everything happened on both sides. As he suspected, the liquid inside Jade’s body was fairly mundane if you just touched it. The magically strong acid only worked after a copy had appeared in the slimespace and fallen into the pool. This was why a quick stab of Ferris’ knife hadn’t harmed his blade: it didn’t have time to fall into the pool.

In comparison, when Jade had swallowed Ferris’ hand, there must have been enough time for a copy of his hand to fall into the pool before Foreman Carter noticed, grabbed Ferris, and pulled him out. Given the limits of mundane human reaction speed, that made sense to him.

This supported his theory that the yellow scarab had infected Jade by being consumed. The copy could have appeared above the Consume Pool, but not been hurt by Jade’s acid as they flew away before falling into the liquid. The copy either continued to exist after the original died, or had used some technique to fully enter the slimespace. He suspected the latter.

Interestingly, if Ferris held something partway into Jade’s body until it had been completely dissolved, and then pushed it in further, the item would need to reappear and fall from above the Consume Pool again. If he just slowly kept pushing an item into Jade before it melted away, its copy would grow larger inside the pool.

After Jade finished consuming all the test material, they returned to the soulspace to work on the next project.

It was time to figure out how to replant the Grow seed.
