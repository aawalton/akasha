In the middle of the war in its waning stages, one zombie burned with rage.

Sally’s clenched fists shook as she glared across the melee as the Hive King discarded the limp body of the vampire. The Outsiders were meant to be near demi-gods beyond reproach. How did they all die so easily? To a bug.

If that wasn’t bad enough, after years of pestering, she had finally lent Theo her Skeleton Key - and he had died with it in less than ten seconds. If the Radoch didn’t pick it up, then it’d be lost amid the muck. Plus, it had gotten rid of her wights when she had passed the dagger over.

Irritated beyond belief, she opened up the menu of her new ring as her dwarves tackled an elite roach to the dirt beside her.

She hesitated before opening her Inventory, noticing a text field near the empty weapon slot.

>Nearby: Crossbow (2), Spear, Sword (3), Sword (Owned)

Her eyes went over to look at the discarded weaponry on the floor, and to the Radoch trying to fight off her skeletal pals with a sword.

“Huh.” She ran her tongue across her sharp teeth. “Well, that’s a risky plan, but…”

At this point, she had limited options. Originally, she had been hoping that this final battle would narratively mirror her first fight way back in the diner. Something to come full circle and show how far she had come after all these years. But it all came down to doing goofy things and eating brains.

She wasn’t the strongest Outsider, nor the smartest, most inventive or efficient. Somehow things were just her responsibility, and through mysterious means she came out on top. Normally, she hated being alone, but she had grown into quite the necromancer lately.

“Joe? I need you to run a distraction for momma.” Sally looked up at the giant zombie. He gave her a low grunt in response and lumbered away.

The Hive King blew through several normal zombies before turning his attention to the approaching giant. With a dart forward and upward lash of his lance, he split open Big Joe from stomach to sternum. As guts spilled out to the ground, a chain burst out through the zombie’s innards. Sally’s Meat Hook pulled her through her summon to collide with the large Radoch.

He struck back at her almost instantly, and she bounced and tumbled away on the ground, eventually staggering back to her feet. Her right arm was gone, but her left held one of his trophies.

The Hive King glowered at her, raised his hand to the side and exploded the dying Joe. Blood and muscle showered the area.

“Gore of my comfort character?” Sally grinned, her eyes blazing bright crimson. “Unacceptable.”

[Carrion Trophy][Grants Aura: Heavily reduces all forms of healing]

She pocketed the stolen item as white threads dragged a severed zombie arm up to attach to her shoulder joint. With the summoning ring able to give a list of nearby applicable items, it had just taken a quick eye to spot the right trophy - and a quicker hand to pull it from the Radoch as he forced her away.

Now that he knew her plan, however, she hoped she had picked the right item to grab.

“Now, I am unkillable,” she declared. “My regeneration is bar none. Dare I say… even worth collecting?”

Sally had to buy time. It took a while to set up her self-resurrection skill, and the bug had been blocking it. She also had a single resurrection scroll in her Inventory, but she needed a plan before wasting that.

The Hive King held his lance pointed toward her. “You are not worth collecting. You insist on yourself far too much. You are to be erased.”

“Annoying? Moi?” Sally held her new hand up to her face in faux shock, but the arm was slightly longer than her own, so she missed the mark. “That’s the first time anyone has said that to me.”

“And the last.” The Hive King burst up into the air, hovering with his large wings. Along the lance, bright golden light glowed. He held it back, energy continuing to fill the weapon.

“If this is your kamehameha stand-in, then that’s a little unfair, as I didn’t train my ki.” She grimaced as the amount of light produced by the empowered attack blinded her. It also seemed unfair that the Radoch leader only had holy and divine attacks, as if he had done his-

He fired the beam, illuminating half the world in golden light. The ground shook as every combatant in a mile radius was burned away by the divine blast.

Sally blinked, expecting the darkness of death to greet her. All she could see was a shadow. A man standing in front of her with several comets of golden light spiraling his dark clothing. Revolver raised to point at the floating Radoch.

“Apologies for my absence,” Hunter grunted. “I was baiting out an attack like this.”

Although her skin burned, she had survived. The ground was dry and cracked, any semblance of grass or the warring factions long gone. Her eyebrows raised as she watched Hunter return the attack in the form of a dark beam of energy. The Hive King lowered in the air, sagging, but wasn’t killed.

“Just what is this asshole made of?” Hunter griped.

“Keep him busy for me. I have a plan.” Sally opened up her messages as the man gave her a nod and zipped off to meet the Radoch.

Was it irony that the original Outsiders became so powerful due to stacking Auras, which had now become their undoing?

Chuck appeared beside her. “Came as soon as I could.”

“Archie?”

His wireframe form flickered as he shook his head. “The Omen has vanished too. Make of that as you will.”

“Hold onto that resurrect for now, then. I might mess this up and need a backup plan.” She summoned a horde of zombies to put some clutter between her and the duel. “Can you access my home storage?”

“The actual physical object has been destroyed—along with most of the goblin village—but the contents are stored in a kind of array I am able to access.”

“I sent you a small shopping list. Hand them over.” Sally held out an open hand while the other raised her resurrection scroll. Golden light ran around the edges as it unfurled, and a sing-song voice echoed in her ears alone.

‘Sanctuary graces the soul of Max Russet with life anew’

With a shimmer of blue light, the magician appeared beside her. He blinked a couple of times, before registering what had happened. The flashes of Hunter fighting the Hive King etched across the concern on his face. “Ah. What can I-”

Sally pushed a flask of gray liquid into his hands. “We’re killing this nuisance now. You know what part to play.”

Max glanced at the description on the potion before smiling. “Give me my cue, conductor, and I shall set the scene.”

She turned to Chuck. “Actually, go to these coordinates and resurrect Ren. She’ll know what to do.”

Hunter slid across the dirt on his feet as sparks radiated from his silver revolver. He looked back at the Radoch leader with curiosity across his shadowed face. “Ain’t met someone so resistant to my attacks before. I’m wonderin’ what it takes to break you.”

“Unlike these other worms, you might be worth collecting.” The Hive King paced slowly, keeping his bright eyes on the man.

“Unfortunately, reality doesn’t care for brazen desire. Everything I shoot turns to dust. You’re just slower on the uptake.”

The Radoch paused, ready to leap forward, but twisted at the last moment, impaling Max as he darted into the fray. The real magician appeared behind the bug as his fake corpse hit the ground.

Every single trophy and armor piece on the Hive King vanished, shortly followed by Max himself turning invisible.

The King lashed out, but hit empty air.

“Using a Master Thievery Flask with Max’s excellent Inventory management ideas to fully loot every trophy is a novel idea,” Chuck said. “Impressive.”

Sally beamed, staring at the stripped Radoch through her horde of undead. “Theo once used one to try to steal a candy bar from me, but accidentally took my outfit too. We can’t go back to that church.”

“I’m not sure I believe any part of that story. Best of luck.” The Architect vanished.

Max reappeared beside her, dropping his invisibility. “Did you want any of these items? They could prove useful.”

“Nah.” Sally shook her head. “Look how stunlocked he is without his toys. He’s now only a +2 Threat.”

The Hive King was clearly having second thoughts. While still a competent adversary, he had lost almost fifty Auras and other buffs. He wanted to turn and kill that persistent zombie, but he couldn’t keep his eyes off the shadowy man - who was now much more dangerous.

Escape would be a better plan, but there was no point wishing for a ship.

As if in answer to his call, a Radoch ship burst into the atmosphere above him.

Sally put her hand over her eyes. “Nothing like a bit of false hope to drive the screws in, huh? I guess we found Arch and the Omen. Alright, pals, you’re up.”

The Hive King barely deflected a shot from Hunter, who was clearly more injured than he was letting on. The roach staggered away before turning to meet the zombies sprinting toward him. Just as he was about to swipe several away, his shoulder burst open as a bullet pierced him. Entangling vines grew around his legs.

“Sniped from far out of earshot. Impressive as always.” Max grinned as he readied an arcane card in his hand.

“No fighting,” Sally said, stopping him. “I’ve got something more important for you to do.”

“Enough!” the Radoch yelled out in anger. With a flick of a hand, a bubble of golden light surrounded his body. “You disgraceful parasites will suffer a fate ten times as terrible as this when I return. Every Radoch fleet in the Sea will-”

A purple beam, swirling with blues and distant stars, shot down from the Omen and struck the roach mid-teleportation. The bubble snapped away with a pop, leaving the alien leader frustrated and confused.

“Normally, I’d have a speech prepared for something like this.” Sally pushed through her crowd of zombies to confront the King. “As it is, I’m just sick of you existing.”

“You!” His eyes snapped to her. “I will end you completely.”

Sally clicked her tongue. She didn’t even need to say the line.

𐕣 (You cannot kill what has no life)𐕣

The ground cracked, tearing apart the scorched earth as a skeletal dragon burst up into the air. With a quick swoop, it snapped up the Radoch leader into his jaws. The Aspect of Death shook the pinned leader about before tossing the roach back to the ground.

He bounced, spilling blood and gore from the bite wounds. As he struggled to stand, a shadow loomed over him.

Eat Brains tore his head open, leaving an empty shell.

The Hive King was dead.

Without so much as a smile, the dragon flew off into the distance. Hunter hunched over, using his hat to waft steam coming off of his body. A flash of blue illuminated Sally’s disgusted face as Max returned.

“It worked! How strange, although often the best tricks are…” He paused as the zombie spat out the Radoch brains. “Unexpected.”

“Some silly blood brothers crap.” Sally shook her head. “If Edward is ever betrayed, Theo would go to any lengths to wreak vengeance. That’s why he’s flown off to find some more bugs to exterminate.”

“At least he didn’t take it out on me.” Max rubbed the back of his neck. “Ed looked a little pissed, though.”

She sighed. “At the end of the day, what I’m best at is utilizing those around me. You had to take the gear we couldn’t brute force past. Ren needed to restrain him by surprise so that Archie’s whatever-beam could stop him escaping. Theo had to aura farm like a dork.”

The sound of distant battle was dull over the crackle of dried earth and occasional groan of a zombie waiting instruction.

Sally wiped her mouth and smiled a sharp-toothed grin.

Sanctuary had been saved.
