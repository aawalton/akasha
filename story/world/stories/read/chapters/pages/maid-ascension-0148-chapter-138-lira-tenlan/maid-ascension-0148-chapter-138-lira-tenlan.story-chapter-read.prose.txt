Her name was Lira Tenlan, and she had grown up on the Musgrove estate, where she worked as a maid in name only while spending most of her days in the library, reading whatever she could get her hands on. She suspected that Lord Musgrove and Steward Martell tolerated her idleness because she was the illegitimate granddaughter of the lord, born to his deceased son and former heir. Nobody had ever told her this directly, but she had heard the whispers among the staff and noticed how the rare visiting nobles always seemed to look at her for a moment too long.

But none of that mattered now, since she was no longer at the Musgrove estate, and the old lord was probably dead by this point, with Steward Martell having taken over the estate while pretending the lord was still alive.

She was in a better place, working in the royal palace as the new library caretaker, with friends and peers she could respect instead of being the pariah everyone gossiped about.

Last night, it seemed she had fallen asleep at her desk once again, which explained the stiff neck and the dull ache behind her eyes that had refused to leave since sunrise. She remembered sweeping the floors late at night, then sitting down to rest her eyes for a moment. When she opened them, it was already dawn.

It was a little strange that she felt so tired after one late night. It felt almost as though she had pulled an all-nighter. She shrugged, putting it down to overwork, and went on with her morning.

By midmorning, she was dusting the shelves with the ever-chatty Tessa, who was halfway through a long story about the new maids who had joined and their interactions with Old Marga, while the ever-quiet Sera polished the floor at the far end of the room without saying a word. Lira was enjoying the nonsense more than she would have admitted. It was the kind of ordinary morning she had never had on the Musgrove estate.

The door flew open, and Celene came rushing in with her skirts gathered in one fist and her face pale.

“Lira, we need to talk. Now.”

Lira dropped her duster on the shelf and hurried after her, while Tessa’s story died in her mouth and Sera looked up from the floor. The headmaid did not slow down. She walked past the front desk toward the spiral stairs leading to the storage rooms. Lira followed behind her with a growing knot in her stomach. She had rarely seen Celene this flustered. The only other time she could remember was the night the headmaid had needed Lira’s help to hide Maren from Prince Kavard.

Once they were inside one of the storage rooms, Celene checked the corridor to make sure nobody had followed them, then closed the door and turned around.

“Did you have anything to do with it?”

“Anything to do with what?” Lira asked, confused.

“Prince Kavard’s death.”

Lira’s eyes went wide, her surprise was entirely genuine.

“Kavard is dead?”

“Are you telling me you had nothing to do with it?”

“What are you saying, Celene? How could I possibly have anything to do with the death of the first prince?”

“I don’t know,” Celene cried, pressing her hands to her temples. “You said Maren would only be safe once Kavard was dead, and it felt like a promise. I keep hearing your words echoing in my head.”

“Well, yes, Maren would only be safe if Kavard were gone. That much is true.” Lira shook her head. “But for me to be the one to do it... how would I even go about it? I am a maid who reads books, not an Archmage.”

“I know, I know, I just...” Celene dropped into a chair and rubbed her face. “I have already been questioned over Taren’s death, and now I will show up in the Detect Hate Divination spell for Kavard as well.”

Lira swallowed. “So will I, probably.”

“Oh, you definitely will,” Celene said with a nod.

“It will be fine. As long as I tell the truth, I have nothing to worry about,” Lira said easily, since it was true, though she felt an odd flicker beneath her words, something like relief that this truth would protect her.

“For you, maybe,” Celene said bitterly. “As for me, I have to look King Argon and Queen Elara in the eye while they both know that, deep down, I hated their son enough to be suspected of killing him.”

Celene left after that, and Lira spent the day dreading the interrogation.

Frankly, after Kavard had threatened her in front of his guards two days ago, she could hardly pretend to be sorry that he was gone, even if it meant she would be investigated. At first, she had been confident that she would be fine as long as she told the truth, but the truth had a way of branching out, and any question about why she hated the prince could lead to how she had helped hide Maren from the guards, got her out past the palace walls, and then dyed her hair blonde,

Would she be punished for that, even though the king had retroactively pardoned Maren?

She didn’t know, and that was what worried her.

News of the prince’s death reached the rest of the palace by midday. Marco brought the news to the library, since a few of the footmen had been called to retrieve the body.

Apparently, the word was that Prince Kavard had snuck away from his escort at night, headed to a brothel, and drowned in his bath. The circumstances were so suspicious that no one believed it was an accident, and everyone was talking about how he had probably been poisoned, especially after some of the guards gossiped that the prince had never learned the Detect Poison cantrip that every noble knew.

Once the news spread, Lira left the library and headed straight to the main hall, then to Prince Lyth’s chamber, where Sir Henry let her through. Inside, she found the young prince crying. Lira hugged him tightly, while Henry watched them both, his face conflicted.

Around two hours after lunch, every bell in the castle rang, and Lira walked with Prince Lyth as he went to the front gate. Four footmen carried a coffin into the palace. Estophon followed behind them, looking ragged, his armour dusty and his eyes tired, as if he hadn’t slept all night. Behind him came a man she had seen only once before, Dario, who had served as Captain Salvek’s second during the duel. Both his hands were shackled.

The royal family was all there except for Aric, who was still away on the expedition. Queen Elara was sobbing, King Argon looked angry, and Queen Dowager Wisteria looked unconcerned.

Lira found herself standing behind them. She placed a comforting hand on Lyth’s shoulder as the young prince held back his tears, trying to give him whatever comfort she could.

Once the footmen reached the royal family, they lowered the coffin and lifted the lid. Inside, Prince Kavard was wearing his purple suit. He looked almost as if he were sleeping.

Queen Elara sobbed even louder when she saw her son, while King Argon merely shook his head.

Estophon knelt in front of the king.

Seeing the giant captain, Lira couldn’t help but wonder if Estophon had killed Kavard, the same way he had killed Taren.

Wait, where did that come from?

She remembered talking to someone about seeing Estophon murder Taren, though for the life of her, she couldn’t remember who she was talking to or any details about the murder itself. She might be more tired than she thought.

She watched Sir Estophon lower his head until it almost touched the ground.

“My king, my queen, your son has died under my watch. I take full responsibility for this and will accept whatever punishment you deem fit.”

“Rise, Estophon. You have served me well for the past thirty years,” said Argon. “I do not blame you for this, but I need to know: was this truly an accident, or do you suspect foul play?”

“I don’t know for sure yet, my king, and I will make sure to find out.”

Estophon finally raised his head, his eyes scanning the gathered crowd and lingering first on Celene, then on Lira.

She felt a cold shiver travel down her spine as his harsh stare pierced through her. The jovial, apologetic man who had conducted Taren’s investigation was nowhere to be found. And Lira knew this investigation was going to be far more contentious.
