Should I mention Creation? That feels like a bad idea.

Up until recently, this had not seemed like a problem.

Brother Caldus let out a quiet sigh. “I somehow don’t think this is a man trying to outwit doctrine. If he was, he’d have a hundred excuses. And if his beer was corrupted, I would be sick by now.” He added this while glancing over at Donovan.

He said it like a conclusion rather than a blessing.

This is going to become an issue.

He stopped mid thought.

“That…” Alric said carefully, now wishing he had not asked at all.

“I would suggest you don’t ask Hal,” she continued, as if offering a practical warning. “Ask Vale. If you want a very… thorough account, you could ask Henry.”

There was just enough emphasis on thorough to make that sound like a terrible idea.

Seren’s lips pressed together briefly, and she looked back down at her ledger before she could smile outright. “Mm.”

Seren brought it in with the sort of expression usually reserved for condolences. She held it between two fingers, as though proximity might make it worse.

“For you,” she said, setting it down on Alric’s desk with a faint tap.

Alric glanced up, already frowning as he broke the seal. The parchment was thick, the script elaborate, and the message buried somewhere beneath a great deal of very deliberate phrasing.

He read it once. Then again.

“It says I need to be at the temple in three days,” he said at last, lowering the page slightly. “For… clarification purposes.”

Seren’s expression did not improve. “Of course it does.”

This was no longer a question.

It had become an event.
