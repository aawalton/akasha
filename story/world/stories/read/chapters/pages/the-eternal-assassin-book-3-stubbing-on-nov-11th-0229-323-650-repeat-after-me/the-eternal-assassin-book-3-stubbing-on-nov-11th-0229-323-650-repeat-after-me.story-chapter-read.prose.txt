Gradually, Asher’s awareness returned to him. Seeing as his body had been completely disintegrated via the forceful passage of time, his first thought was surprise that he wasn’t in excruciating pain.

His second was panic as he realized he was drifting in a familiar, endless space devoid of all matter.

Ah, crap… he thought, before recoiling at the fact that he’d actually had a thought in the first place. He’d only appeared in this place three times before, but each time he’d shown up, his entire focus had been devoted solely to the colossal existence of what they were presuming was the deity of Eternity. Usually, he’d bask in the thing’s galactic power for the briefest of moments, before snapping awake in his freshly healed body.

But not this time.

Asher drifted there in nothingness, trying to turn his head and look around before realizing he didn’t actually have a head to turn. He didn’t have any physical body whatsoever. Somehow, he was still aware of what was going on, but he didn’t have hands to clench or lungs to gasp at the revelation.

What is this… my soul? Wait, were my visits here always like this and I was just too freaked out to notice?

Asher continued drifting about as he pondered the thought. It wasn’t as if he had anything else to do. Strangely, despite his lack of body, his mind wasn’t separated from his emotions like when he utilized Comprehend’s second-tier effect to buy himself time to think. He still felt nervous and more than a little concerned as to what was going on, and he was pretty certain if he had a heart, it would be beating at a mile a minute right about now.

Hold on… Where’s the deity?

It took Asher some time for the thought to arise, and he swept his awareness around the empty space that stretched on forever. Usually, it was the shock of sensing a single, powerful emotion radiating off of the supposed deity that acted as the catalyst to send him flying back into his body. Only this time, it was silent.

Seeing as the deity wasn’t contained within the empty space so much that it was the empty space, it wasn't as if it was hiding from him. Asher wasn’t sure how, but he somehow got the sense that it was shying back from him for some reason. A realization that was almost as startling as finding him conscious within this void once again. The deity was the size of a universe. It was eternal, in the very sense of the word. The thought that someone with that kind of power and presence was trying to keep itself away from him of all things was almost impossible to imagine.

Asher briefly contemplated trying to reach out and communicate with it, before striking the thought from his mind almost instantly. Would a germ expect to be able to hold a conversation with a human being? Not even that was an apt comparison for the sheer level of distance that existed between him and whatever this thing was he was drifting within, but it was about as close as Asher could get.

Not willing to speak out but without so much as a pair of thumbs to twiddle, Asher drifted there in empty space, running over the events leading up to this latest death. He didn’t exactly have anything else to do, so he replayed his conversation with Donvath again and again, trying to figure out why in the world the High Prince had betrayed him.

At first, Asher was angry. Donvath had been one of the few people in this new world who he’d trusted almost without question, and the High Prince had stabbed him in the back out of seemingly nowhere. But after reliving the memory a few hundred times, the anger eventually bled away, and he was able to think about it with a cooler head. Slowly but surely, Asher pieced together what had to have happened. It was the little things that gave it away.

The fact that Donvath specifically said how he ‘needed to die’ for the good of the world at that precise moment… His declaration that there was nothing Asher could do ‘as he was’ that would save him… And, most notably, how Donvath had looked both pained and relieved at Asher’s parting comment about coming back before he’d fully disintegrated. It took Asher some time to work it out, but he got there in the end.

Donvath wanted to force a specific evolution for the third tier of Eternal Regeneration. I suppose in order to do that, for some reason I had to think I was being betrayed, and he needed some way to destroy my entire body as thoroughly as humanely possible. Damn it, having a friend who can see into the future is a real pain.

Despite feeling confident in his assumption, Asher was still furious with the High Prince. He didn’t care if this was ‘for the greater good’ or anything along those lines. He wasn’t a fan of people deciding his future for him.

Donvath is going to get a serious piece of my mind, and maybe even one or two pieces of my dagger when I get out of here, Asher decided. Of course, the only question now was…

When was that going to happen?

Without a physical body or anything to even look at in the empty space that stretched on forever, the passage of time was more than a little tricky to keep track of. Already, Asher couldn’t tell if he’d been drifting there for hours or weeks. A small part of him began to panic as he realized it very well could have been years for all he knew, and he pictured coming back to his new life only to find that all of his friends had long since passed from old age, always wondering what had happened to him. It was that spark of fear that gave him the courage he needed, and Asher did the mental equivalent of clearing his throat.

Uh… Deity…? Sir? I know you’re ignoring me for some reason, but can I ask if you know when I’m getting my body back?

At first, Asher wasn’t sure if it had actually heard him. It wasn’t as if he could see any sort of change to the empty space around him. But ever so gently, like a human laying the tip of a curious finger against an ant they had found, he felt something brush against the back of his mind.

The moment the potential deity made contact with him, his awareness split, and Asher suddenly found himself reliving a memory from months back. Like an outside observer, he watched as the him from a few months ago calmly jogged up to an overturned wagon located on the road leading up to Whikoga. He looked so young and strangely carefree, not yet having gone through all the trials and tribulations that had forged him into the man he was today. Into The Reaper that he was now known as. This memory had been barely an hour before he’d first met Rosh, and Asher watched his past self in awe as he approached the overturned wagon that he now knew contained a fast-talking leatherworker and cleared his throat.

“Hello? Is anybody in there?”

Just as quickly as the memory had surfaced, it faded away. If Asher had a body he would have recoiled in shock. That one ‘hello’ had somehow stood out to him far more than the rest of the memory, making it immediately clear to him what the deity was attempting to do. If trying to use its own concept of language was so powerful that it knocked Asher out of whatever weird fugue state this was, then using his own memories to converse with him might just function as a work around. The fact that it had both devised such a method of communication and was capable of doing something like this was more than a little worrying, but Asher was too excited to finally get the chance to talk to the supposed deity.

Hi! I’m Asher. Do you have a name? Or should I just keep thinking of you as the deity for Eternity?

Again, there was that sensation of a faint touch on the back of his mind, and Asher found himself back in another memory. Funnily enough, one that took place only a couple of minutes after the previous one he’d been shown. Not much time had passed, and now Asher was in the middle of his fight with the very same bandits who had pretended to be slayers before overturning the leatherworker’s cart and running off with the man’s horse. Asher had already killed the boxer and Rosh was cowering off to the side as he stayed out of the fight, but the sharpener, one of the first people to have nearly killed Asher in this world, was currently cursing as he discovered the poison-tipped blade he’d planted in Asher’s kidney wasn’t working quite as well as he’d hoped.

“A god-damned healing skill? Seriously? I don’t know what noble family you belong to, but I am going to enjoy killing you.”

Asher snapped out of the memory, and if he’d had a jaw, it would be dropped right about now. The deity that evidently didn’t even know what it was quickly showed him another memory, and Asher was suddenly transported into the room he’d spent a week in with Rosh while the two of them had worked together to hunt down a handful of Loratta’s elites. Asher had barely known how this world worked back then, and Rosh had been kind enough to take the time to help explain the basics.

“Yes… But you should be careful,” Rosh warned him. “Not that I doubt your ability after watching you take down Latch and Wes… but you did take quite a nasty wound in the process.”

You… You’re worried about me? Asher realized, wishing he could laugh at the startling revelation. Seriously? But you’re a God of some sort. Why would you bother to worry?

A new memory surfaced, this one a memory that filled him with warmth as he watched himself and Samantha hug one another inside her old cabin. Horntho Village had yet to burn to the ground, and Asher hadn’t even gotten roped up in all that business with the Assassin’s Guild just yet. He was waiting for Brian to get set up for their game of thieves versus seekers, and while doing so, he talked about his old life with Samantha as he reassured her how much happier he was with her and Brian in this new world.

“It’s real,” past-Asher said before leaning down to kiss Samantha on the head. “Trust me, this is all far crazier for me than it is for you. I told you, the world I come from didn’t have anything like magic or demons. No origin elements or evil liches. My biggest concerns were trying to figure out how I was going to survive pulling so many all-nighters in a row, and watching the previous generation destroy the economy while I tried to finish my apprenticeship.”

There was so much added emphasis to the scattered parts of that memory that Asher actually felt his awareness start to flicker a bit, as if he were struggling to maintain his current form. As the memory faded, he pieced the different highlighted bits together.

Trust me… I come from… elements… biggest concerns… figure out how… survive… destroy…

The deity clearly knew how to choose the right memories, because Asher was able to figure out what it was trying to convey easily enough.

You only exist because of the element of Eternity, and I’m the only person in existence with the element of Eternity. If I die… you might die too.

In response, Asher slipped into another memory, and he felt his awareness start to waver as the repeated acts of the deity conversing with him finally began to take their toll. Even so, he managed to catch one last message before the strain became too much and everything went black. He was back in the outer edges of the Deadlands, watching his entire team converse as they took down some basic zombies.

“Alright, that’s probably the nastiest thing I’ve ever seen,” past-Asher said, putting the zombies out of their misery with twin thrusts of his living wood dagger to the temple. “So even if they can run, they still follow the same zombie rules? Headshots to take them down for good?”

“For these basic ones, yeah,” Moxy said, peering down at the finished zombies. “The further you get into the Death Aura, the stronger the zombies will be. You’ll probably find a good number far more powerful than this.”

“Remember, when you feel the Death Aura taking its toll, Recall out to safety,” Samantha said for the third time, giving him one last quick hug for encouragement. “Good luck in there,” she muttered into his shoulder before giving him one last kiss.
