The dark had filled the forest, and Callie couldn't help but worry how they would get back to the wagon. She didn't know exactly how long they had walked through the forest in an attempt to find the thunderfox, but it felt like a long time. How would they even get back in the dark? Callie had no clue which way the wagon was.

"Walking won't work. I'll summon the horses," Captain Voss announced. She placed two fingers in between her lips, took a deep breath and blew.

Callie had expected a loud whistle, but no sound came. Did it not work?

But Captain Voss kept walking as if everything was as it should be, Lysander following after her. Mom and Callie shared a glance but nobody dared to question the rangers. Surely they knew what they were doing.

They kept going through the forest, guiding only by the torchlight. Rangers somehow knew exactly where to go, and they even managed to keep a brisk pace. Mom was struggling to keep up, and Callie suspected it was because she had to carry Callie on her back.

"Mom, I can walk on my own," Callie offered, although she didn't feel too confident.

"It's okay, dear. I can manage this much," Mom reassured her.

"Don't worry, help has arrived," Captain Voss announced.

Callie heard a steady sound of hooves in the distance and before long the two ranger steeds appeared before their eyes. Callie had many questions. How did they detach themselves from the wagon? Was it even alright to leave the wagon unattended? How did these horses manage to navigate in the forest in the dark?

"Okay, two on each horse," Captain Voss announced.

"I'm not riding together with a hunter," Lysander spat, glaring in Mom's direction.

"Then you take the kid," the captain said briskly.

"I can't hold the thunderfox and the kid," he complained.

"I can hold the thunderfox!" Callie offered without hesitation.

"Callie, no!" Mom instantly objected. "You saw it bite the ranger!"

"Only because she was scared. Besides a little bite isn't going to kill me. I'll just apply ointment and bandage it up later!"

"Callie!" Mom growled.

"We don't have time for this. Every second is crucial," Captain Voss said and lifted Callie up onto one of the horses.

"I didn't agree to this!" Mom growled.

"I warned you, if you come with you, you need to follow our rules. Or would you prefer to make your own way through the forest at night," Captain Voss challenged.

Mom looked like she was ready to reach for her sword. Callie knew that one wrong word could make Mom act… rashly.

"Mom, it's fine! Please! We need to worry about the thunderfox first," Callie insisted and reached out her hands. "I'm going to hold her firmly and not let go."

Lysander looked skeptical. "You must hold her firmly beneath the hind legs, so she is comfortable and support the front paws as well. Do not push against her belly and be mindful of the wounds."

"I can do it!" Callie reassured.

He didn't seem to want to give up the thunderfox in his arm, but eventually he settled her into Callie's arm. The thunderfox was actually much lighter than Callie had expected. It was about the same weight as the neighbor's cat. Has she lost a lot of weight?

Lysander positioned Callie's arms so they would be secure around the thunderfox, then jumped onto the horse behind Callie. As his arm took the reins, he was sure to position them in a way that would keep both Callie and the thunderfox stable.

"I'm ready!" Callie confirmed.

"Let's get going!" Captain Voss ordered.

Mom begrudgingly, jumped onto the horse, sitting behind Captain Voss and before long they were off. It was a bumpy ride. They only had one torch, held by Mom, so Lysander had to follow after them so as not to run into any trees.

However, for the most part, horses were maneuvering through the dark quite well on their own. In fact, it seemed like the rangers allowed the horses to lead. While Callie hadn't yet been given the opportunity to ride on Gale properly, since she was too small, she knew how one was supposed to ride a horse. How to signal it to move in the proper direction, how to make it slow down and speed up.

While the rangers kept their hands on the reins, they were not pulling them at all. Neither were they using their heels to slow or speed up the horses. It was strange how well they were able to navigate in the dark.

But Callie didn't have time to wonder about ranger's superior horses much. She was focused on keeping the thunderfox as comfortable as possible. An occasional whine would escape her when the horse jumped or made a sudden turn.

"It'll be alright. We're going to your babies. Just hold on a little longer," Callie said to the fox soothingly.

My… babies…

Callie was overjoyed to hear the fox's voice. It was strained and very tired, but there was also an unyielding will to live.

"Yes! They are safe. They are in a warm place with food and water. They are waiting for you," Callie said.

Waiting… I'm coming…

Callie kept soothing the fox the whole way to the wagon. They arrived there fairly fast with the horses. Two of the other rangers were waiting by the wagon, their steeds tied to the wagon. Callie had no clue how they knew to come to the wagon or where to find it. Had it something to do with the captain's unhearable whistle?

"Captain, you found the thunderfox!" One of the rangers, a large man who was likely twice the size of Dad, said. He was holding a large cage in his hands laid out with hay, water and food.

The other one, a tall woman with unusual blonde hair, ran up to the horse with Callie and Lysander. "Give her to me!"

Callie nodded and gently passed the thunderfox to the ranger woman. She noted that the woman smelled a bit like Granny Ethel, a distinct waft of herbs and medicine coming from her. She must be the healer of the group.

The moment the fox was in her eyes, the woman hurried into the wagon with the large bulky man following after her.

"I-Is there anything I can do to help?" Callie asked.

"Stay out of the way, kid," Lysander said coldly and lifted her off the horse.

"I'm afraid not. This night is critical for the thunderfox. Best you can do is not disturb them," Captain Voss said, then turned to Lysander. "Set up the tents. We'll remain on the side of the road for tonight. I'll be keeping the first watch."

"Understood, Captain!" Lysander said, and got to work.

"We're going to make a shelter for the night too," Mom said, looking rather annoyed with the whole situation.

"Sorry, Mom…" Callie apologized.

"What are you sorry for all of a sudden?" Mom asked.

"For causing trouble. We have to sleep in the woods now. But I am not sorry for saving the thunderfox!"

"Sorry, yet not sorry at all?" Mom gave Callie the look. The one that clearly showed she wasn't happy.

"I'm sorry you have to spend the night in the woods, but I'm not sorry for saving the fox," Callie clarified.

"Let's just hope our efforts were worth it," Mom said, ruffling Callie's hair.

She got to building a crude shelter, and Callie initially set out to help her. But she was so exhausted by the day's events. At some point Callie felt herself drowsing off and before she knew it, Mom had pulled her inside the shelter and pulled the wool cloak over them both.

Callie woke up early the next morning. The sun was barely peeking above the treetops. She had considered trying to sleep some more. It was so warm in Mom's embrace and it felt wrong to disturb her. But her thoughts quickly filled with worry.

Had the thunderfox survived? Was she alright?

Callie was listening intently to every noise, every step that rangers make, in attempt to figure it out. Eventually, she couldn't help herself anymore and gently tried to wiggle free. This of course woke Mom up.

"Can't sleep anymore…" Mom let out a loud yawn.

"Sorry…" Callie muttered.

"It's alright. I'm sure you're dying to know what happened to the fox. Let's go take a look."

Callie nodded and hurriedly crawled out of their shelter. Lysander was sitting next to a fire, watching the surroundings with his cold gaze.

"How is the thunderfox?" Callie asked him. His cold expression didn't give her much hope.

"The worst is over," Lysander noted. "She's weak, but she managed to drink and even eat a little. While her recovery will take time, she should be fine eventually."

Callie exhaled with relief. "Thank the heavens!"

The beautiful man studied Callie with a scrutinous gaze. "Why do you care so much, kid?"

"Huh?"

"Your mother is a hunter, is she not?" Lysander spat, glaring at Mom, who glared back at him without flinching.

"I became a hunter to protect my family and my village," Mom snarled.

"Yeah, yeah. That's what all of you say."

"You don't have any idea how difficult things are in Greyfen! Monsters and large predators prowl the forests, eager to—" Mom stopped midsentence, noticing Callie looking at her. She cleared out her throat. "I… umm…"

"See! You hesitate! Your child can see that arcane beasts are more than just monsters, yet you refuse to do so."

"I did come along to help, did I not?" Mom snarled.

"Do you have to fight?" Callie muttered dejectedly. She never knew what to do when adults were arguing. Especially in an argument like this where she thought that Lysander was right. But it wasn't like Callie could dismiss everything that Mom did either.

Without the game that she bought home, it was likely their family would have starved to death two winters ago.

Lysander clicked his tongue in annoyance, and turned away from Mom. "You didn't answer my question, kid. Why do you care about arcane beasts?"

"I… I don't think it's right to kill them… " Callie said, but she was all too aware of Mom's gaze. Lysander however waved his hand, urging her to continue.

"Th-They have families, just like we do. They have things they like and things they don't like. They can be kind and helpful, but also dangerous. However, I was told that people can be even more dangerous than arcane beasts. Nobody would ever dare to kill a person, even if they were bad. Why should it be different with arcane beasts?"

"Callie…" Mom's expression grew darker. "We have talked about this…"

"You wouldn't even have an arm if it weren't for arcane beasts," Callie muttered quietly.

Mom seemed to have heard that and flinched back.

"What's with the heavy atmosphere first thing in the morning?" Captain Voss asked, appearing out of the tent. "Lysander, don't bully civilians!"

"Sorry, Captain!" Lysander straightened at once, although he didn't look very sorry for what he did.

Mom looked to be deep in thought.

"Would you like to see the thunderfox?" Captain Voss offered.

"Yes!"

"Is that safe?" Mom questioned.

"Mom!" Callie whined. She had already held the thunderfox in her arms, so why was she still worried?

"They are in a cage and their tails covered so they can't discharge any lightning. It is completely safe," Captain Voss assured. "Just don't disturb them."

"I won't!" Callie promised, and ran up to the back of the wagon.

The captain opened the door and hoisted Callie inside. The ranger woman who smelled of medicine greeted Callie curtly with a headbob.

"Quietly. They are asleep," she said.

Callie nodded and carefully approached the large cage which was covered with a large black cloth. The smaller cages were the kits had been were standing empty in the corner of the wagon.

The ranger gently pulled back part of the cloth to reveal the thunderfoxes curled up and sleeping. The kits were all pressed up against their mother, sleeping peacefully.

The thunderfox mother stirred slightly at the disturbance, but she didn't look alarmed.

Thank you…

She then nuzzled one of the kits contentedly and laid back to sleep.

"You're welcome…" Callie whispered.

She could feel her heart fill with joy. Thunderfox's mother was safe. The kits were safe, and it was all thanks to the rangers. Callie felt so grateful towards these people. To think that there were actually people who cared for arcane beasts just as much as Callie did. She could hardly believe it.

The happiness in her chest seemed to swell and fill her whole being… Wait… something wasn't right. That wasn't happiness. It was magic swelling within her with an alarming rate.

Callie darted out of the wagon, fearing her magic might just explode uncontrollably again.

"MOM!" She called, hoping that somehow Mom would be able to stop it.

Mom noticed Callie's panic running up to her immediately. But it was too late. The energy within her was too much. She… She couldn't hold it back. It burst out.
