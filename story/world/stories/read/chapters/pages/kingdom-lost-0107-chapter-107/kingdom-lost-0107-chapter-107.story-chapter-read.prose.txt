Chapter 107

The cells at the back of the barracks held the morning light in narrow strips through the slats above the doors. Three cells along the rear wall. Two of them with someone in them. The third empty, kept that way on purpose.

Valrik stood in the corridor outside the first cell with his helm under one arm.

He had set it down on the bench by the wall when he came in, and then he had picked it up again, and now he held it the way he had held it standing for inspection. A soldier waiting to begin work.

Thorne stood at the back wall.

He had taken the position when they came in and had not moved from it. Arms folded across his chest, weight even on both feet, the long shadow of him resting across the opposite wall. Two soldiers stood at the corridor door, one in and one out. A brazier burned low in the corner where the corridor turned and threw dull red light along the lower stones.

Valrik nodded to the soldier at the door.

The soldier turned the key in the cell lock and pulled the door open and stepped back, and Valrik walked through.

The prisoner was the smaller of the two. The bruise that had come up along his jaw on the ride home had gone from purple to the yellow-green of the second day. He sat on the floor against the back wall with his knees drawn up and his hands flat on his shins, and he looked at Valrik as the door opened and then looked at the floor near his boots, in a way that was already an answer to a question that had not yet been asked.

Valrik stood three paces from him.

"Stand."

The prisoner looked up at Valrik and did nothing.

“Stand!”

The prisoner paused then stood. He stood the way a man stands when he doesn’t wish to but knows he must.

"Where were you posted before you rode with Korr-Va."

The Clawborn glared into Valriks eyes and bared his teeth. Valrik drew his sword and extended it just under the prisoner’s chin, the tip touching his throat.

“You can either answer questions using your tongue or I can cut it out and look for the answers myself.”

The Clawborn’s teeth disappeared behind his lips and he looked down, defeated.

"Garrison at the river fork. Two seasons,” said the prisoner.

"And before that?"

"South circuit. Four months under another collector.

"How long did you ride with Korr-Va?"

"Eight months.”

The prisoner's shoulders had begun to settle. These were questions he could answer without feeling that he was answering anything at all, and his body knew it before his mouth did.

"What is the news from the north?" Valrik said.

The prisoner glanced up at that and then back down.

"The Clawborn army," he said. "Has been dispatched far north to the valley.”

"How many?”

"I do not know the number. Most of them.”

"What were they fighting?"

The prisoner's mouth opened and then closed

"I do not know."

Valrik waited.

"I do not know!"

Valrik let it stand. He did not push it, he realized a low level resource collector wouldn’t know that much about the larger movements of the army. He filed it and moved on.”

"Tell me about Korr-Va's route."

The prisoner's posture shifted.

"What were the villages on his circuit?" Valrik said. "In what order. How long for the full loop?"

The prisoner's eyes came up back up to meet Valriks, then went past Valrik to Thorne at the back wall and then to the floor

"That is not something I can speak about."

Valrik pressed the sword tip into the prisoner’s throat and a drop of blood rolled down the prisoner’s neck.

"What were the villages on his circuit? In what order? How long for the full loop?"

The prisoner said nothing.

Valrik waited as tensions rose once again, Valrik wasn’t going to leave until he got his answers.

***

The prisoner was on the floor again.

He was on one knee, then on both, his breath coming through his teeth in short measured pulls, and his hands hung at his sides as though he had decided several beats ago that they were not going to help him. The bruise along his jaw had opened in a thin line at the edge. He did not look at Valrik. He looked at a point on the floor between his knees.

"Six," he said. "Six villages. Sometimes seven.

"In what order?"

He gave them. The names came out flat, in a string, the cadence of a man who had recited them privately many times without ever expecting to say them aloud. Valrik did not interrupt. The prisoner finished the list and then said again, "Six. Seven if the new one was holding."

"How long for the full loop?"

"A week. Sometimes eight days. Depending on the roads, the weather."

Valrik nodded once.

"What happens when a village over-produces."

The prisoner blinked at the change of direction. He breathed for a moment and then he answered.

"He backtracked. He would rest a day at the end of the circuit and then turn back and hit one of the villages early on the next loop, before the regular time. He liked to come at them when they were not ready. Sometimes he did it because they had been short and he wanted to take it out of them, and sometimes he did it because he wanted to send more upstream than he had sent the last time. He did it more in the last months than he did at the start."

"Who does Korr-Va report to?"

The prisoner shook his head. He shook it the way a man shakes his head at a question he has been asked before and could not answer the first time either.

"I do not know."

Valrik waited.

"I do not know," the prisoner said again. "We were told the quotas.”

There was a kind of frustration in it that read as truth. Valrik registered it and held the silence for a moment to see if anything else came after it. Nothing did.

He turned his head slightly toward the back wall.

Thorne had not moved.

But Thorne spoke now, low, the way a man speaks half to another man and half to the air around him.

"The timing does not work."

Valrik turned more fully.

Thorne unfolded his arms and stepped forward a half pace, no more, and his eyes were on the prisoner but his words were still going toward Valrik.

"Six villages on a one-week loop. Backtracking adds weight. He took ore and grain and timber and the lot of it goes onto the wagon as he moves. The horses he had with him could not carry several villages' worth across that distance without breaking down. And if he was riding the round trip back to the Empire after each loop, the math is impossible. He could not be at the start of one circuit and at the gate of the Empire and back to the start of the next inside the week."

He let that sit.

"Where does it go between collections."

The prisoner did not answer.

The room was quiet for a count of several breaths.

Valrik stepped back into the cell. He did not close the distance. He did not raise his voice. He stood where he had been standing and he asked the question again the same way he had asked the others.

"Where does it go between collections."

The prisoner exhaled.

"There are depots."

"How many."

"Two on this circuit. One at each end of the loop. The wagons are unloaded there. The resources sit until a transport detail comes through to haul them onward to the Empire."

"How often does the transport detail come."

"Once a month at the long depot. Twice for the short one. It depends on what they are carrying."

"Where is the nearest depot to this circuit."

The prisoner thought about it, possibly considering the ramifications of the answers he was giving.

"East," he said. "From the last village on the loop, you go on the same road another day and a half at the wagon pace. There is a stand of bare hills with a cut through them. The depot is on the south side of the cut, against the slope. It is not signed. You would not see it from the road."

"How is it guarded."

"Six," the prisoner said. "Eight on some days. More when a transport is expected."

He had volunteered the last piece. Valrik had not asked for it. The prisoner said it with the small forward lean of a man who thinks that the giving of one more piece, on his own, might tip the calculation in his favor; that cooperating now might still be the thing that decided whether he walked out of this cell at all.

Valrik did not give him anything back.

He looked at the prisoner for a long moment, then turned without speaking and walked out of the cell.

The soldier at the door closed it behind him and turned the key.

***

The second cell was eight paces down the corridor.

Valrik picked his helm up off the bench as he passed it and tucked it back under his arm and stopped outside the second door. He nodded to the soldier.

The door opened.

The larger of the two prisoners sat against the back wall in the same posture the smaller one had been in when they came in, but his eyes did not move when Valrik stepped through, and his hands did not shift on his knees. He looked once at Valrik and then past him to Thorne in the corridor doorway and then at nothing.

"Stand."

He did not stand.

Valrik stood three paces from him.

"Your name."

Nothing.

"Where were you posted."

Nothing.

"The name of the man who commanded you before Korr-Va."

The prisoner's eyes moved up to Valrik's face. He held the look for a count of two and then his eyes went back to the wall behind Valrik's shoulder. He did not speak.

Valrik watched him for a moment longer.

He turned and walked out.

The soldier closed the door behind him. The key turned in the lock.

Thorne was at the corridor door now, one hand resting on the frame, his eyes on the second cell.

"One is enough," he said.

"Yes," Valrik said.

***

They walked out of the corridor together into the gray light of the inner yard and stood for a moment in it without speaking. The morning had moved on while they were inside. The forge column was up. The wall walk had men on it at the rotation.

Valrik shifted his helm under his arm.

"I will wash," he said. "Then I will report to her."

Thorne nodded.

They split at the post in the inner yard. Valrik turned toward the soldiers' quarters with his helm under his arm and his pace even. Thorne turned toward the tower.

The door of the tower stood half open. Thorne went through it. The ground floor quiet and empty.

He stood for a moment then turned his head towards the stairs leading up to Riley's quarters. He wondered what would unfold once Riley was briefed. Regardless of the outcome there was much to prepare for and with that he turned and headed to his room.

Above him, in the upper room, Riley stood at the table with her hand on the strongbox and her eye fixed on the its lock.
