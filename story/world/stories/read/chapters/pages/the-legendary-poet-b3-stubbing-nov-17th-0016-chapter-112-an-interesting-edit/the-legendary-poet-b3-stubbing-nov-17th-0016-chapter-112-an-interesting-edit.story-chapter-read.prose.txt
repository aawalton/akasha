The loose letter strained from Kage’s wrist.

It had no weight, no physical reason to hold position, yet it tugged toward the dying stage direction like a dog at the end of a leash. The rest of the black script around his forearm continued its slow orbit. Calm. Patient. Horribly pleased with itself.

Kage held his hand still.

The fading line near the Alpha’s corpse continued to dissolve.

and the reef-king called the unfinished sea...

The last word frayed into thin black dust.

Kage lowered his hand by an inch.

The loose letter followed the motion, reluctant.

So that’s the button.

He hated buttons with no labels. In games, unlabeled buttons opened secret doors, activated boss enrages, or dropped the party into lava because some designer wanted to feel clever and had never been punched by a tank in voice chat.

The letter continued tugging.

Gold upward arrows had appeared beside most of the party portraits. Little shining indicators. Eight leveled. Kage and Vami stayed where they were.

Reasonable. Kage’s level twenty-five bar was a starving pit. Sixty-two thousand five hundred experience sat in front of him like a landlord with perfect posture. Vami was level 25, too.

Kage looked back at his hand.

The loose letter still waited.

Lily stepped closer. “Kage?”

“Testing something.”

Zherdev perked up, “How dangerous?”

“Here?” Kage looked at the unfinished storm hanging above them. “Good question.”

That earned silence.

Even Vami gave him a look, though Vami’s look had more excitement than concern. The man probably heard “unknown consequences” and saw a loot multiplier.

Kage turned away from the fading Alpha text and scanned the sky.

The Draft of Storm and Water stretched around the reef in weird layers. A sea stood frozen mid-crash. Rain hung in the air in bright beads, each drop containing a distorted reflection of the broken ship. Far above, storm-clouds smeared themselves across the vaulted darkness like charcoal rubbed by a careless thumb.

The floating stage directions had changed after the fight.

The combat lines had dissolved. A few larger lines remained embedded in the sky, farther away, written in the same archaic script only Kage seemed able to read. They moved with the environment. Some wrapped around cloud banks. Some sank into the frozen waves. Some clung to distant frames half-visible through the rain.

Kage let his eyes soften.

Synesthesia dragged the text closer through sensation before sight fully caught up. The words had texture. Old ink on damp parchment. A faint scrape like a quill nib catching a fiber. The bigger lines carried more pressure, heavier to the gaze.

There.

Something long moved inside the storm.

At first it looked like a pale crack in the cloud cover. Then it curved. A body, thin and immense, slid through the gray layers above the suspended sea. It had no proper scales. Its surface flashed in segments like glass plates briefly catching hidden light. A spine of broken calligraphy ran along its back, letters scattering every time it turned.

The shape swam through the cloud as if the storm were water.

Finn saw it too, though probably as movement instead of text. His head snapped up. “Uh. What's that sky thing?”

Jax followed his gaze. “Please define sky thing in a way I hate less.”

“Long,” Finn said. “Above us. Moving behind the clouds.”

Kage ignored them and focused on the line wrapped around the cloud-serpent.

The letters resolved.

and the Glasscoil passed between unfinished rains, its pale hunger pinning the depths beneath the reef

Glasscoil. Pale hunger. Pinning the depths.

A predator, probably. Or a roaming hazard. The line implied it kept something below the reef suppressed, though “pinning” could mean pressure, territory, gravity, fear, a dozen messy design concepts wearing one coat.

Good test target? No. Available test target. Kage lifted his marked hand. The orbiting letters tightened.

Zara’s voice came sharp. “Maybe explain before you do something.”

“I think my mark edits the lines I see.”

Jax stared at him. “Edits the dungeon?”

“Maybe.”

“That sounds… broken.”

Vami raised a hand. “For the record, I vote yes.”

Kage pointed at the Glasscoil, and the loose letter sprang forward. It stopped a foot from his fingertip, quivering. Waiting.

Kage breathed once. He chose a small edit.

Remove the entity from the active line. Leave the storm. Leave the rain. Leave the rest of the sentence if possible.

Kage spoke.

“And the Glasscoil left the margin.”

The words felt ordinary in his mouth. They weren't. Every black letter around his hand snapped out of orbit.

For half a second, his forearm became a burst hive of ink. Characters launched upward in a violent stream, so fast they looked like a black blade splitting into insects. They struck the sky-line around the Glasscoil and wrapped around the existing text.

The original sentence resisted. Kage felt it through his palm. Pressure. Friction.

The line in the storm brightened.

and the Glasscoil passed between unfinished rains, its pale hunger pinning the depths beneath the reef

His letters swarmed the first clause. Some crossed out words with neat black strokes. Others jammed themselves between existing characters. A few curled around the name Glasscoil and pulled.

The serpent in the clouds twisted.

Its pale body arched through the storm. Glass plates flashed. Its mouth opened, showing no teeth, just a long white interior filled with raining letters.

Then the edit resolved.

The sky text rewrote itself in real time.

and the Glasscoil left the margin

The rest of the sentence peeled away.

its pale hunger pinning the depths beneath the reef

Those words hung for one heartbeat, severed from their subject.

Then they collapsed into black rain.

The Glasscoil vanished. The cloud simply lost the shape. The long pale line passed out of the world as if someone had turned a page.

Kage’s hand dropped.

The orbit around his arm had thinned by nearly a third. Large gaps opened where dense script had circled moments before. The remaining letters rotated slower, almost full. Sated was the word his brain supplied.

A tiny new character formed near his wrist. It rose from nothing, floated a hair above the skin, and joined the orbit. Slow recharge. Separate resource.

The voice returned.

It came from everywhere inside the storm draft. From the broken ship’s wet ribs. From the frozen drops hanging around them. A cultured voice with a cracked-paper rasp, reverent enough to make Kage want to wash his hands.

“An interesting edit, Master.”

Every player froze.

Zherdev whispered, “Bro.”

Notifications unfolded.

For Kage, the panels arrived in black-framed calligraphy, sharper and more ornate than the normal system text.

[Author’s Ink: 37%]

A second set of panels dropped beneath the first.

[Syllable Debt accrued: 18.]

[Debt will be expressed through compensatory revisions to the active draft.]

Kage’s stomach tightened.

The rest of the raid reacted to their own smaller message.

Jax read aloud, voice climbing. “RAID NOTICE: The Author has altered the stanza. The Author? Why is it capitalized?”

Vami pointed at Kage with both hands. “Because our little murder poet just changed something in the raid.”

Zara’s eyes moved rapidly across a panel only she could see. “Mine says Draft Ecology has changed.”

Finn swallowed. “Mine too.”

Kage read the next notification. This one was visible to everyone.

[Ecological Line Removed: Glasscoil of the Upper Rain.]

[Predation pressure removed from submerged draft fauna.]

[Submerged draft fauna no longer suppressed.]

[The Stanza compensates.]

Kage stared at the words.

For one clean second, nobody spoke.

Then Jax said, “That sounds bad.”

“Correct,” Zara said.

Vami’s eyes shone. “That sounds incredible.”

Julie turned on him. “Vami.”

“I mean terrible. Terribly incredible.”

Kage flexed his hand again. The missing letters left his arm feeling strangely cold, like skin exposed after removing a glove. Another character formed near his knuckle and lifted into orbit with painful slowness.

One edit spent more than half of the resource. Slow recharge.

He looked at the frozen sea around them. The reef under their feet gave a low groan.

I edited out a predator.

The reef groaned again, deeper this time.

So the prey gets brave.

Finn’s hawk shrieked.

The sound cut through the suspended rain. Finn turned toward the bird, then toward the water beneath the broken ship. His face drained of color.

“Kage.”

Kage already saw it.

Below the crystal reef, under the layer Julie had revealed and he had legitimized, something moved.

The hidden sea had stayed frozen before. A wave paused mid-collapse. Rain paused mid-fall. Fish paused inside glass. Now the darkness under the reef shifted with thick, muscular purpose.

The water was remembering motion.

Zherdev took one careful step back. His cape snagged on a shard of ship rib. “Guys, my cape says retreat.”

Zara snapped, “Your cape also says orange and purple belong together.”

“Fashion is subjective during emergencies.”

Valdrias planted his glowing tower shield. Its light spilled across the reef in hard bands.

Kage watched the surface below swell.

Vami’s Lux began to build from small movements, white leather brightening as he shifted his stance.

The reef cracked.

A long fracture raced between Kage’s boots and the broken mast. Canvas marks flared as Lily and Jax pressed their hands down, forcing the floor to hold. Chalk dust streamed from their marks and sank into the crystal. The crack slowed, then split around the stabilized circle instead of through it.

The hidden sea bulged beside them.

A mountain of unfinished flesh pushed upward through the frozen wave.

At first Kage saw a blunt head, wide as a wagon, sketched in rough charcoal. Then wet pigment flooded the outline. Gray hide gained color in patches. Bone plates surfaced along its skull, each plate covered in scratched-out paragraphs. Its mouth hung low and circular, bound by thick black lines like someone had drawn a jaw too many times and kept every failed attempt.

More body followed.

Two forelimbs slammed onto the reef. Each limb ended in flat digging claws made of layered parchment and glass. The claws punched through crystal. The entire platform lurched.

Jax cursed and caught Lily by the shoulder before she slid. Ren moved in the same beat, one hand steadying Zara without looking away from the creature. Finn dropped to one knee, already nocking an arrow with shaking fingers. His hawk took off from the spar and climbed hard, feathers cutting through suspended rain.

The beast hauled itself farther out.

It smelled like pond mud trapped in a sealed jar.

A new line wrote itself above the creature.

and the mouth beneath the reef rose to eat the silence left by the coil

Kage’s hand twitched.

The remaining letters around his arm leaned toward the sentence, then settled, almost sulky.

The system panel slammed into view.

[Mawbound Draft-Behemoth - Lvl 28 (Mini-Boss)]

HP: 38000/38000

A second message followed.

[Compensatory Revision Applied: Released Quarry]

[Effect: The Mawbound Draft-Behemoth gains +15% Max HP and increased aggression while Glasscoil of the Upper Rain is absent.]

Kage stared at the HP bar.

“Thirty-eight thousand,” Zara said, voice flat. “With a compensatory buff.”

Jax lifted his sword. “So we put the sky snake back?”

Kage looked at his depleted hand.

The orbiting letters crawled around his forearm with far too much empty space between them.

“Sure,” he said. “Give my arm ten minutes.”

The behemoth opened its circular mouth.

Inside, rows of half-written teeth rotated like printing blocks. The air pulled inward. Frozen rain trembled toward the maw. Loose ink from the dead Alpha skittered across the reef, dragged by suction.

Valdrias raised his tower shield.

Kage drew Mumyo.

The sentient blade purred. Ready. Maybe judgmental. Hard to tell with a sword.

Kage stepped forward, eyes on the monster born from his edit.

“Valdrias,” he said.

The Guardian inhaled with terrifying sincerity.

Kage added, “Taunt.”

Valdrias slammed his glowing shield into the reef and roared, “BEHOLD, BEAST. YOUR FREEDOM ENDS BENEATH MY INEVITABLE RULE.”

The behemoth’s many scratched-out eyes snapped toward him.

Kage exhaled.

The dungeon had edited back.

And the edit had teeth.
