Harry's complaints about his body seemed awfully similar to the Are we there yet? kids. Of course, these kids didn't have a way of conjuring magical doors, nor did they miss their right hands. The air often changed between being sharp and harsh in nature, but for it didn't matter for the young thief; it all hurt the same.

Goldman and the group entered the hotel and began scanning the rooms for clues. The place was deserted. The only guest of the hotel was the wind that whistled through the rotten wood on the sides. Who would build something like this on a top of a mountain? They all thought. It was almost uncanny why the structure remained there.

The ceiling on the entrance was low, and so was the temperature that kept on falling. It wasn't as bad as the outside, of course, but that was like comparing a tree to a forest. The pirate reached the reception and grabbed a flyer. It read: 'Welcome to the Millbourne Hotel!'. Beside it, there was a mill painted in blue and a couple smiling at one of the windows of the building. He kept the flyer.

On his side, Daniel was jumpy, ready to summon an arrow at even the slightest of movements. He kept following Brun, who found the same flyer in one of the tables in the right. The entire place seemed well-maintained back in its commercial season. The leaflets and propaganda were of quality and the structure seemed professional, just... oddly generic. Like if a person who never had an original thought wrote it.

Broken pieces of wood betrayed that vision of the hotel as they lay near thrown benches.

Harry went with Vela, who held his candelabrum. In front of Goldman, Daniel, and Brun, a stairway led to the second floor. The pirate then declared he'd go up and continue the search. Manes and Daniel followed him.

"The walls are too thin." Said Harry with a scrutinizing gaze as he flicked the wall. "Furthermore, the structure is very tall and curved at a certain angle that makes it prone to collapse. I don't understand this architecture. It's almost as if it wasn't designed for these conditions."

"It's because it wasn't." Brun answered whilst reading the leaflet. "I think... Look at this image and the name of the place; 'Millbourne Hotel'. The origins of this establishment certainly weren't near a mountain. That explains the low ceiling and the width of the floors; it is needed for the mill blades to work. Now the reason that is here is out of my reach."

Accepting the explanation of the old lady, Harry went through the room in the right. It was a common area, possibly to stimulate the interactions between the guests of the hotel. It had a few tables with cards in it and a broken mug on the ground. What could've possibly been a counter was visible on the far right corner of the room, but what captured the Locksmith's attention was in front of him.

It was a fragile pane of glass, so fragile that he almost thought it would shatter just from touching it. Behind it lay a full view of the mountain above the clouds. A sea of ​​white met the blue ocean of the sky; an almost celestial contrast. It was an immensity of clouds, which further emphasized the size of the mountain and the hotel itself. Harry didn't know what the original view from that window might offer was, but he was almost certain it couldn't be better. If it weren't such an inaccessible place, it would be popular.

"You are not thinking of throwing yourself, right, Mr. Locksmith?" Vela approached him with a smile.

"Oh, don't even mention it." Harry said without taking his eyes off of the view. "I wouldn't like to ruin the landscape by putting myself in it." He chuckled.

"Weren't you afraid when you fell?" Vela asked, suddenly curious. "I do not doubt the control you had, but... Well, I don't know if I'd have the same courage."

"Of course I was afraid." Harry answered with a smile. "Without fear, one can't be called brave. And like all things, bravery can also be taught. I wasn't in real danger, at least not during those moments, but while I'm falling, my mind shuts down for a moment. It's not exactly as floating like Goldman or having an erupting volcano at the tip of my fingers like you, but they make me feel like nothing and everything matters at the same time. Does it make sense? I don't know."

"I see what you mean, Mr. Harry."

"It is just like you and your candles, right?" The young thief added.

"...Almost." Vela said, his tone slightly more serious. "With fire, I feel... out of reach. The flames burn away both truth and lies as the candles speak to me. They whisper the secrets as they dance; truths that were consumed long ago. I like being near the light, and controlling the candles makes me feel like I could be one with the fire. I almost feel like an oracle, you know?"

Can't say that I do, Vela... The Locksmith added inwardly as he simply nodded and sighed deeply.

"It's funny how this is a combination that few people would have the courage to do, but one that would be quite interesting. I don't know about you, but I would definitely pay to have this view when I woke up."

"You could have." Madeline said, leaning against the door. "It's called mountain climbing, you know? People do it all the time. And the best part is: you don't have to pay the mountain a penny."

"That's the thing, I like paying people." Harry said as he turned around. "That's why I was a thief; I need money to spend it. A renowned thief has his rights, doesn't he?"

"If you were a renowned thief, then I was a snobbish critic..." She replied with a grin. "Now come on, Goldman said to call you guys. Brun thinks she knows where the hatch is."

The three climbed a few steps until they heard the voices of Brun and Goldman. Waiting for them at the second floor was the pirate with his characteristic smile. Manes could also be seen fidgeting with the rubble as Daniel was frowning, as expected.

A narrow corridor led to an entrance that had its door missing. Inside was a spacious place; two beds, each with their own visions of the mountain and sky. Three tiny desks were near each bed alongside a counter. A destroyed cabinet lay on the floor to the right, and a bear rug covered the floor in the middle of the room.

Sitting on the animal carpet, Manes was now covered in his shadows as he continued fidgeting. Goldman, near him, took turns observing the boy and focusing on the discussion of Brun and Daniel.

"...Up there!?" Daniel now exclaimed. "Are you sure?"

"Why does everyone keep asking me that!?" Brun fiercely stood up against her father, also yelling. "No! I am not sure, Daniel. But that's what makes sense. The first floor hatch was on the end of the prison. The second floor hatch was near the flame. It would be logical to assume that the third floor hatch is on the top of a mountain. That is, in fact, way better. It means we won't have to keep searching all around this huge mountain in order to find it."

"I..." Daniel lowered his voice, staring deeply into his daughter's eyes. "I'm not just Daniel; I am your father."

"Well, I'm sorry that I don't feel like your daughter, then." The old woman replied sharply as she returned to a piece of paper.

"Ouch..." Harry inadvertently broke the silence that followed. The young thief then closed his eyes in embarrassment and fear; Daniel heard it. But as he waited for a slap or prepared his doors to flee, nothing came. Opening his eyes, he saw only the shattered state of the once barbaric viking. A deep texture of melancholy washed over Daniel, one that affected Harry profoundly.

"Aye, come on..." Goldman came near Daniel as he gave an awkward smile. "We don't even know if that is the case. Maybe the two past hatches were nothing but a coincidence. Do not let that affect you, okay?"

At first, the Locksmith thought the pirate had missed the point of Daniel's sadness, but it was the opposite; he understood it well enough. By giving something else for the viking to latch on, he gave Daniel a breather. At least for a second.

Creak! The small sound of a chair squeaking suddenly made the whole group tense. They each looked around, desperately trying to find the source of the sound as it squeaked again. It came from above. Harry looked at Vela who gripped his candelabrum and Madeline whose brush was aching for ink.

Goldman made a Shush it! gesture as he gently stepped on each wood.

"Screw this." Daniel said as he marched to the stairs to the discontent of everyone else. Looking back, he saw the indignant expressions of the group. "What? We were yelling five seconds ago. The intruder certainly heard it."

I mean, we are probably the intruders in this situation... Harry added inwardly but had to agree with Daniel. The stranger probably heard it all. In fact, the sudden appearance of silence was even more suspicious.

The viking then reached the third floor and tore all the doors down and inspected the place. On the last one, though, Harry touched his shoulder.

"Wait!" He said as Daniel growled in front of him. "...You know what? Forget it. It was, um, it was nothing."

The brute opened damaged the door as he entered the last room. In the middle of it, a man of about 5'3 feet was holding a fire poker. He had a fantastic mustache. Yes, that is the only word that could fit the majesty of his facial hair. With brows as thick as a brick, the man took a step forward and yelled as he tried to puncture Daniel in the chest. The viking, slightly confused by the man's appearance and attitude, simply took a step back and let the man fall. Once in the ground, Daniel grabbed and disarmed him.

"Let me go!" He bounced his legs and scratched Daniel's fingers like a feral cat. "I want to go on my own terms! Am I not entitled to the blood inside my heart? Is a man not entitled to determine his own demise? Let me go, you brute...!"

Outside the room, the whole group turned their heads slightly to the right as they observed the strange man.

Daniel, as if he had just found a chest full of gold, shook the mustache man and gave a sincere smile like a child on Christmas. Or the equivalent of that for vikings.

"I did it!" He exclaimed. "I found a baby troll! If we eat him before the night comes, we shall have good luck!"
