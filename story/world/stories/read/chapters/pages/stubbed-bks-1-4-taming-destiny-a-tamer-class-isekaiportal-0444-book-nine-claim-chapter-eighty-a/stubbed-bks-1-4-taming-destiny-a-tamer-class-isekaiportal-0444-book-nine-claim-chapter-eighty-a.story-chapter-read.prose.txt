Unable to face the three people in the room – one still feral – I pace the corridor outside, turning the pieces over in my mind.

This is bad. This is very, very bad.

The unknown illness hit approximately one in five people in the settlement – and that's only those who actually sought treatment. If it is, in fact, just the symptoms of a parasite settling into place, then we have a major problem.

But how were they infected in the first place? Looking back with all the information I have now, the answer seems obvious. Our original theory was right – the wells. Now, knowing how powerful the parasite’s stealth is, our difficulty in confirming that that was the source of the illness makes complete sense.

That leaves me with some difficult questions. First of all, is water the only way the parasites can infect someone? Or are the feral people’s zombie-like attempts to bite and scratch people actually just another way of transferring eggs?

I’m reminded of how one of the guards earned a bleeding scratch on his face while binding the feral patient in place. I suddenly wonder whether I just sealed a parasitic sleeper agent into his body.

But there’s a quick way of checking that.

Stepping back into the room, I clear my throat, doing my best to keep my near-panic away from my expression and the Bonds.

“I would just like to examine your nails and teeth – do you mind?”

Kera Milith makes it clear through her body language that I can do what I want; Ker Bolis is more outgoing.

“Our nails an’ teeth? What for?” he asks. “My lord,” he adds, apparently realising that it didn’t sound too polite. Despite the same collar around their necks, the two have clearly had very different experiences in their lives.

“Well, you were scratching and biting a lot of people while under the influence of the parasite,” I tell them pointedly. “I want to make sure there haven’t been any complications.” Let them take that as they will.

It satisfies Ker Bolis as he quickly mutters a polite acquiescence.

Starting with Kera Milith, I gently investigate underneath her nails, using a knife to pull out what’s underneath. I feel for anything that might be an egg. My stomach sinks as I feel some small lumps in the material.

I sink flesh-magic into them, searching for anything that is similar to the parasites I pulled out of Bolis and Milith. My breath catches as my flesh-magic reacts to them.

Then I dig deeper and realise that they’re fragments of skin. Human skin. Not eggs.

I breathe out a long, slow breath, then continue investigating – just because one nail has come away clean doesn’t mean all of them will.

A few minutes later, having extracted all the grime from beneath their nails, and even sunk flesh-magic into the tips of their fingers, my heart slows down from its rapid beat. Their nails are in bad condition and were very dirty from their feral stage, but I’ve seen no evidence at all of eggs, mini parasites, or anything more than the normal bacteria and dirt that exists in such places.

I even manage to check the still-feral man’s fingernails – they’re also clean of parasites.

I thank them absently and return to pacing in the corridor outside.

Apparently their attacks weren’t vectors of new infection. As reassuring as that may be – at least we only have to worry about the violence and not about any injuries spreading the parasites – it’s also confusing. Why on earth did they choose to attack that way, then? The people weren’t aware of what they were doing – and if they were, I would have expected them to grab the nearest tool to hand if they were bent on violence. But the parasite doesn’t even have hands or teeth, so why would it direct them to attack with completely unfamiliar weapons? And relatively ineffective ones at that.

It’s a conundrum, but one I push to the back of my mind. For now, the most urgent matter is dealing with this new crisis – hopefully without creating another one. Because the second difficult question I was considering earlier before being side-tracked by the possible secondary infection vector, is how we deal with potentially our whole water supply being compromised.

It could just be Districts Five and Two, but can we bank on that? I curse at the fact that Elarion is currently stuck outside the settlement – I can check the other wells, but I didn’t even detect as much as he did, and I can’t count on my instincts.

I think we have to assume that all the wells have been infested. But then what? I can't simply cut off the water supply to the whole settlement, especially not while we have an army of beasts at the gates that means we can’t get it anywhere else. Because who knows how long that will last? Preventing more infections from taking place means little if we end up with a revolution led by people watching their loved ones die of thirst.

Arguably, not everyone suffered the symptoms. Perhaps some are more resistant than others? We all share the same water supply, after all, and if it’s all infected…

I stop dead as a horrible thought occurs.

What if I have a parasite in me?

Horror shivers through me at the thought, but I steel myself. Better to know than not. I haven't suffered the illness, but that might not mean anything. I pulse my flesh-magic through my body, searching for the faintest hint of that blank spot I'm now increasingly familiar with.

Nothing. No evidence of either the parasite or its network. And I’m certain I would be able to see it in my own body.

I breathe out slowly and begin pacing again, relieved, but with something else to think about. Why am I exempt when others are not? We've all been drinking from the same wells.

Is it to do with my Class? Or my Constitution? There were definitely fewer Classers who reported the symptoms, and those who did fall ill tended to be common crafter Classes who probably didn’t put many points into Constitution.

Or is it something else? I pause and pull out my half-full waterskin, eyeing it thoughtfully. I usually drink water stored in my Inventory – a space that doesn’t store living things. Then again, I haven't had any bottles refuse to enter the Inventory. I would have been suspicious if that had happened. Perhaps I've simply been lucky.

I resume pacing, my emotional state steadying as a plan slowly comes together in my thoughts.

I’m just finishing speaking to the other lords through the Alliance Bond – updating them on what I’ve learned and asking for their help in various ways – when I hear footsteps at the end of the corridor. I look up to see Healer Thessalanor returning with three other healers in tow, their expressions ranging from curious to apprehensive.

"My lord," Healer Thessalanor greets me, then glances at the others. "I've brought those I believe will be most useful. Healers Marin, Yssel, and Tor – all of whom have experience with parasitic or neurological cases."

I nod to each of them.

“Healer Thessalanor has explained to you the situation?”

They glance each other, then Healer Marin steps forward.

“Yes, my lord,” she confirms with a quick bow. “If you are amenable, we would like to examine the parasitic remains and then the patient.”

“Please,” I gesture towards the room. “Go ahead.” When Thessalanor moves to follow them in, I put out my hand to stop him. He glances at me questioningly, so I give him an intent look. He hangs back obligingly.

“I suddenly realised something,” I tell him quietly, my mouth set in a grim line, then go on to outline my realisation. I see the healer’s face change just as I imagine my own did – understanding, then horror as he imagines the consequences.

“But if all of them are infected…”

“Yes,” I reply, my mouth twisting. “Exactly.”

Thessalanor is silent for a long moment. I give him the time to absorb everything. Finally, he swallows, closes his eyes briefly, then opens them and looks directly at me.

“What do we do, Lord Markus?” And I hear the hope, trust, and responsibility heavy in the title he uses for me. A lord is given respect when times are good because he takes responsibility for his people in crises like this one. I will not shy away from it now.

“First, we need to prevent more infections,” I say firmly. “I’ve just asked Layton and Valence to block off all the wells to public access.”

Thessalanor's expression tightens immediately.

"Are you sure that's the vector?"

"Almost certain,” I say with more confidence than I feel. Ultimately, it fits, but there might be another explanation we haven't considered. “We came to that conclusion before, and both Elarion and I sensed something wrong in Districts Two and Five before we even knew what we were looking for. That can’t be a coincidence."

"No," he agrees, his voice grim. “But we need water.”

“I know,” I acknowledge. “We’re going to use Class-based Inventories – those that are not capable of holding living creatures to test water before it’s handed out. We will need to find a way of cleansing the wells eventually, but this will have to do in the meantime.”

The healer eyes me thoughtfully.

“Not everyone has an Inventory,” he points out. “I don’t, for example.”

His words are true – Inventories are much more common among those with a Rare or better Class than lower rarities, though most merchants have some form of extra-spacial storage.

“I know,” I repeat. “Valence and Layton are going to set up camp around the wells in Districts One and Four respectively. They will continue commanding their own troops from those locations, while using their Inventories to ensure the water given out is uncontaminated. They’re going to look for more people with the right kind of Inventory too. As soon as they find someone, they’ll send them here to help ensure all the water used by the healers is fine.”

Thessalandor seems surprised.

“Are lords really willing to sit next to a well and hand out water?”

“I would,” I point out, “if I didn’t suspect I’ll be needed more here.” Thessalanor nods emphatically.

“Even if my colleagues manage to withdraw the parasite from Ker Himlan without difficulty, given what we now know, we’ll need all the help available.”

“That’s what I thought. In the meantime, I need you to start compiling a list of everyone who has presented with the symptoms since – and including – those first eleven cases."

Healer Thessalanor's jaw tightens.

"That list will be long," he warns.

"I know." I take a breath. "We're going to have to quarantine them. Or at least order for them to be restrained so if they go feral, they won’t be able to hurt anyone."

A moment of silence. He doesn't argue – I can see him working through the logistics of it, the same way I have been.

There aren't enough rooms. There aren't enough healers. There isn't enough of anything. And the pressure of the beasts at the wall is not helping.
