Allen's mother's restaurant was only a short walk from the inn, though Jane didn’t realize that until they were close to arriving. She usually approached ‘Food and Ale’ from another direction, going straight there with Allen from his stall at the Underbridge Market. The trip was routine for her. It was a pleasant way to mix dinner dates with a sense of family.

This trip, however, was far from routine.

Allen pushed open the door and held it for the royal family to enter. Jane followed close behind, watching the restaurant owner’s face as she looked up from the table she was wiping down.

The expression that crossed Ashley's features was exactly what Allen had predicted. Her eyes went wide. For just a moment, she looked like someone who had opened her front door to find a wilderness beast snarling at her from a foot away.

Jane had never seen Allen's mother flustered before, though she herself had been flustered by Ashley’s open and honest manner more than once. Despite her sympathy for Ashley in this situation, Jane found herself oddly gratified to know such a thing was possible.

Yet the moment of panic was over almost as soon as it had begun. Ashley set down her rag, straightened her apron, and began moving.

"Everyone up," she announced to the handful of drowsy holiday drinkers scattered around the room. "We're rearranging. You, help me with this table. You, wake up. We have guests."

The patrons were regulars and knew Ashley well, at least judging by how they scrambled to obey. Within a few short moments, they had transformed a portion of the space to a large combination of tables.

Finally, Ashley turned to face the royal family and curtsied deeply.

"Welcome, Your Majesties. Please, sit. Make yourselves comfortable."

The king seemed amused by the whole production. The queen was watching Ashley with what could have been either admiration or envy in her eyes. Even the prince had lost some of his perpetual discomfort, apparently entertained by the spectacle of a middle-aged woman conscripting her own customers to rearrange her restaurant for different guests.

It’s certainly different, Jane thought. But nobody could deny it worked.

Once everyone was settled, Ashley stood at the head of the table with her hands clasped in front of her.

"Your Majesty," she said, addressing the king directly. "What would you like for lunch? I can make almost anything."

The king waved a hand. "Anything would do, so long as it's good. I trust your judgment."

"I can handle that. With your permission, then?"

"Granted. Do what you think is best."

She nodded and trotted back to the kitchen. Jane watched her go, impressed despite herself. She had known Allen's mother was capable, but recovering from a surprise regal visit so quickly was a whole other level.

"She doesn't look very flustered after all," the king observed, leaning back in his chair. "I’m a little disappointed, honestly."

Allen shrugged, looking vaguely disappointed himself. "She recovered faster than I expected. It's a shame, really. I was looking forward to seeing her off-balance for once."

"Allen!" Jane tried her best to appear disapproving. "Be nicer! That's your mother."

"I know. That's why it would have been funny."

The king laughed. It was a nice, normal thing to do. Despite any worries he’d dragged into town alongside his family, Jane was finding she liked both him and them just fine. They weren’t normal people, exactly, but that didn’t mean they shouldn’t be treated like normal people.

"Now then," the king said, turning to face the prince. "Introductions are in order. Everyone, this is my son, Brand."

Brit gave an audible sigh of relief. "Oh, thank goodness. I thought I was going to have to ask."

Bella raised an eyebrow. "Did you really not know the prince's name?"

"I probably did once. They probably taught it to me in school. But most people just say 'the prince,' you know? I'd never met him before, and I didn't expect to, so it never seemed that important to remember."

Brand, to his credit, didn't seem offended. If anything, he looked almost interested in the matter of trying to remember his name, and Brit’s troubles with it.

"That's the way it always is.” The queen’s voice was thoughtful. "The titles are bigger than any person could be. Doing the job is so important that the job replaces a lot of how the person is seen."

She paused. Jane felt the queen's gaze settle on her.

"When I go to a charity event, I'm never Anne. I'm always ‘the queen.’ I imagine you understand that feeling, at least a bit."

Jane felt her cheeks warm. She did understand, more than she would have liked to admit. In Glenfall, she had worked hard to be ‘just Jane,’ the baker who happened to do magic occasionally. At first, it had seemed that being an archmage at all would ruin her attempt to be a baker and normal person, no matter what she did. Things hadn’t turned out that way, thankfully, but it could still be tricky sometimes.

"I do," she said quietly. "It's not always easy."

"No," the queen agreed. "It isn't."

The moment ended, but Jane felt she had found a kindred spirit in the queen. She wanted to spend a bit more time talking to her. Aunt Cecelia also knew what it was to be famous, but it didn’t seem to bother her nearly as much as it bothered Jane. The queen seemed like a woman Jane might be able to complain with, not just to.

Lunch turned out to be sausages, sliced thin and mixed with sauteed vegetables, all seasoned with salt and a spicy mustard. The whole mixture was spread across thick slices of hearty bread, creating a filling, hand-held food. Ashley made sure there was plenty of it.

"This is wonderful," the king said after his first bite. "Exactly what I wanted. It’s hard to get this in the palace. The cooks always feel like they have something to prove."

Ashley, who had been hovering nearby to wait for a verdict, relaxed visibly and moved on to next steps. Without being asked, she began bringing out tankards of ale and distributing them around the table. Everyone took one, and soon, the mood was even lighter and looser than it had been.

Even Brand was starting to open up. He had been quiet through most of the meal, observing more than participating, but he began to engage as the ale flowed and the conversation grew more animated. He asked Allen about his work and listened to the answers with genuine interest, leaning forward in his seat to hear Allen describe the mechanics of the winch he had built.

"How did you know it would work?" Brand asked. "Before you tested it, I mean. How could you be sure?"

"I wasn't,” Allen admitted. “And it only sort of did, you have to understand. But I had worked through the design enough times that I was confident it wouldn’t break, and it would do as good of a job as anything would. That’s usually the best you can do.”

Brand nodded slowly, as if filing that wisdom away for later. After a moment, he seemed to be reminded of another idea.

"One of my combat instructors told me I need to find a way that suits me for carrying weapons. I feel compelled to obey, but I don't want to be seen with a sword at my hip always. It feels like too much.”

"That's good advice," the queen interjected from across the table. "You should listen to your instructors more often."

"I know it's good advice, Mother. That's why I said I feel compelled to obey." Brand turned back to Allen and Brit. "I was wondering if you might be willing to spare some time for me. To find a way I could be armed without always looking like it. Your work with tools and metals suggests you might have some ideas."

Allen and Brit exchanged glances. Jane could see them both calculating the implications of agreeing to help the prince with what amounted to a personal project, especially if they somehow managed to fail at it.

"We'd be happy to help," Brit said finally. "Between Allen's tinkering and my smithing, we should be able to come up with something."

"Excellent." Brand's expression brightened, the closest thing to genuine enthusiasm Jane had seen from him since his arrival. "I appreciate it."

The meal wound down gradually. If Brand had been told by his parents that he was to be betrothed to Jane, he certainly showed no sign of it. She saw him glance at her once or twice, but never for long, and not so often as to be inappropriate.

Eventually, the king pushed back from the table and stood.

"This has been wonderful. What a pleasure to share this meal with all of you.” He looked to Ashley, who had never gone far, and gave a slight bow of his head. "Please accept my thanks. Your establishment will be receiving a visit from my household staff. I plan to tell them how much I enjoyed myself here, so they'll want to know your secrets."

Ashley looked as if she wasn't entirely sure how to feel about that, but she nodded anyway. "You're welcome anytime, Your Majesty. And you don't need to send your staff to get tips from me. Just tell them to give you want you want, and not what they think you want.

The king laughed that nice, normal laugh again. "I'll try to remember that."

The royal family made their exit with considerably less fanfare than their entrance. Brand hung back for a moment to exchange a few more words with Allen and Brit about their potential collaboration, while the king and queen spoke quietly with each other near the door.

"I am heading to the Underbridge Market, I gather," the king announced. "I've heard there are craftsmen there worth seeing, and I’d like to see them. Would Brit and Allen be willing to join us?"

The two men exchanged glances and shrugged in unison. Jane had to smile at Brit’s easy acceptance. Apparently, the idea of wandering through the market with the king didn't seem nearly so unreasonable a way to spend an afternoon as it had that morning.

Jane watched them go. They were trailed by what looked like a good number of guards, all of whom were trying very hard to appear as naturally occurring parts of the environment. It was almost convincing, if you didn't pay attention to the guards’ deliberate positioning or how their eyes never stopped moving.

"Interesting," the queen said, appearing at Jane's elbow.

Jane startled slightly. She hadn't noticed the queen staying behind. "Your Majesty?"

"The guards. You noticed them."

"It's hard not to, isn’t it?"

The queen's voice was matter-of-fact. "Most people don't. They see the royal ‘Us,’ so to speak. A few extra people in the crowd tend not to stand out."

Jane wasn't sure how to respond to that, so she said nothing. Bella had disappeared into the kitchen to help Ashley clean up after the meal, apparently unable to leave anyone else’s kitchen without offering some amount of help. Emily and Sadie had each gone home. For the time being, Jane was alone with the queen.

"That's how it always is.” The queen’s gaze followed the departing group through the window. "We are popular in the kingdom, and I'm thankful for that. But popularity never means that evil people don't exist, or that other countries are completely without their own threats to us. That’s why I approve of those young men helping my son arm himself."

"I understand," Jane said. "You can never be too careful."

"No. You can't." The queen turned to face her fully. "Thank you for being so hospitable, Miss Jane. For sharing your friends with us. Brand needed this kind of experience."

"He seems kind enough."

"He is. He's been safe growing up, which is exactly what we wanted for him. But he’s sheltered." The queen's expression softened slightly. "Even his magical training only helped with that a little."

Jane blinked. "The prince can do magic? I never saw him at the academy."

"He was rarely there, and never obviously as the prince. Mostly, he had private tutors working with him in the palace." The queen sighed. "Recently, though, I think they've been falling short. He's learned the mechanics well enough, but he hasn't moved past what his tutors describe as the basic level.”

Jane could imagine what that meant. She had seen students at the academy who knew every spell in the textbooks, but couldn't improvise their way out of the most minor of problems.

"I hate to ask so soon," the queen continued, "but I wonder if you might be willing to give him some pointers. Not formal instruction, necessarily. Just some guidance. A different perspective than what he's been getting."

Jane thought about this carefully. She was certainly capable of helping, but there were implications to consider. Teaching the prince, even informally, would create a connection between them. And given the rumors about why the royal family was really in Glenfall, she needed to tread carefully.

"I've heard some things," she said slowly. "About why the prince is along on this trip."

The queen nodded, unsurprised. "I imagine you have. Rumors travel fast, especially when they involve royalty."

"I don't want to create any expectations. By spending time with him."

"Let me be clear, Miss Jane." The queen spoke gently but firmly. "I can't deny every bit of those rumors. The king does have thoughts about Brand's future, and those thoughts do sometimes involve the question of who he might eventually marry. But as far as I'm concerned, this is a request from a mother about her son's education. Nothing more. I have no expectations beyond that."

Jane studied the queen's face, looking for any sign of deception or hidden agenda. She found none.

"Of course.” Suddenly, Jane found herself smiling. “I’ll be glad to help.”

Relief flickered across the queen's features, there and gone so quickly that Jane almost missed it. "Thank you. I appreciate it more than you know."
