Trine let out a laugh that slowly turned into a girlish giggle. Her stress about her family forcing her to change tacts, and her failed assassination attempt hadn’t faded entirely. Yet she was still having a good time. Her cousin Simo, having joined her, was in many ways going to be a liability, what with his erratic and often dangerous nature, but the pale blonde boy had always been like a little brother to her, and more than anyone, he had an uncanny ability to make her laugh.

That ability was on full display as they caught up in the stands watching the remaining entrants battle their way through the Frostwing Trials.

Simo had grown while Trine had been away; he was still on the skinny and short side as male Blackwinters went, but he had lost the goofiness of expression and movement that had always made Simo a target for mockery among his brothers.

Somewhere in the last two years, her cousin had developed a confidence that oozed from his every movement.

That didn’t mean the frantic madness was gone from his eyes; if anything, Trine thought Simo seemed more dangerous and predatory than ever. He simply wasn’t ashamed of it anymore. Trine didn’t judge her cousin for his wild eccentricities and vicious desire to hurt things. Having an ally that would never get squeamish at the possibility of violence was, if anything, reassuring. It wasn’t like Simo would ever try to harm Trine; if his tendencies started to get the better of him, he would simply have his entourage bring him something or someone to torture and kill.

Glancing at the hard-eyed warriors doing their best to look like natural members of the crowd in the arena stands, Trine concluded that entourage was actually the wrong word. The eight or so bodyguards forming a loose circle were honestly not doing a good job at seeming like they were watching the matches with their stern, joyless faces,but in all likelihood, it wouldn’t matter. Someone would have to be actively looking for them to spot the warriors from the far north.

“So we are here to watch that girl lose?” Simo asked with an amused half-smile.

“Her opponent is also definitely going to win this whole thing, but yes, I hope she has her teeth knocked out of her head.”

“I am so glad you haven’t gotten any less petty in your time away. I was scared blending in with these southerners would.” He gestured with his hands as he searched for the words.

“What?” Trine said with her eyebrows raised. “ Make me nice?”

“Yes! Thank you, that's exactly what I was trying to say.”

She couldn’t help it; Trine burst back into laughter.

“You are such a little asshole.” She replied before punching her cousin in the arm hard enough to leave a bruise. Even with his rank high enough that the mark would be gone in minutes, Simo acted like the blow was practically life-threatening.

His theatrics done with, the prince leaned forward in his seat as the match between Kyera and Jackson. There was no question in Trine’s mind who was going to win, as no one could stand up to the kind of power the man with the alien writing above the skull face on his chest held in his Cards was simply too much. Trine had never seen anything like it.

“Whoa.” Said Simo in genuine awe. “ You’re right, no one is beating that in a fair match.”

“It gets worse,” Trine said, taking pleasure in the beating the woman who had gotten in her way was about to take. Down in the center of the arena, Jackson had just finished using a Spell Called Cross The Abyss, which, at the cost of two vials and sacrificing a creature in play, had allowed him to get a Creature Card and add it to his Hand. That alone wouldn't have been enough to impress Trine and her cousin, after all, plenty of Decks, including their own, had Cards that let you search your Library. What made this particular Spell so intimidating was that it allowed Jackson to add a Card from his collection.

With access to an effect like that, the only limits to his adaptability were what Cards he owned and how many Vials he had left.

Unlike a normal Card effect that allowed someone to look at their Library, Cross The Abyss hadn’t caused Jackson's eyes to glow blue. Instead, a disc of swirling darkness had appeared at head height in the air to the right of the man. He reached out and plunged his arm into what could only be some sort of portal. That was when his eyes finally changed, except they didn't become shining blue but a pitch darkness that managed to devour light so effectively that it left the man's face in shadow, even though he was standing in the open beneath the afternoon sun.

“Oooh spooky.” Said Trine and Simo in almost perfect unison, before sharing a quick laugh.

After a few seconds like this, Jackson pulled his arm back out of the shadowy portal, grasping a Card that immediately floated up and became the Card image floating before the amber-skinned man in his Hand. He and Kyera traded a few words that were too far and quiet to be heard over the noise of thousands of fans, not to mention multiple matches being played in the arena at once. Whatever had been said made Jackson laugh and gesture at his chest before explaining something.

Trine might have no idea what the conversation between the two players consisted of, but it seemed far more amiable than she would prefer, given that part of why she was here was to watch Kyera get brutalized.

Fortunately, she didn’t need to be able to hear what they said when they played Cards, even from here, Trine could read the runes and numbers that appeared with a Card. With the perceptiveness that came from her Deck, she could even read Spells and Relics with ease, despite sitting further back in the stands.

‘The Cards in that Deck really are ridiculous.’

She thought as Jackson played another spell.

A set of runes that Trine couldn’t read appeared suddenly over about half of the ground on Jackson’s side of the field. Seemingly out of theme with everything else he did, the runes glowed a piercing white.

Next, the dark-haired man made use of the spell’s effect, presumably to Manifest the creature that he had pulled from his collection with the earlier use of Cross The Void.

“You know…” Simo said, turning to Trine, who looked over to meet her cousin’s gaze.

“ I’m starting to worry how we are supposed to deal with this guy in the real world.”

“You aren’t wrong.”

Trine couldn't disagree, of course she didn’t mention the monstrosity she had encountered on the bottom floor of the hotel she’d burned down. One terrifying potential opponent at a time felt like plenty for now.
