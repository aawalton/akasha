Nathan ran with the vines hanging loosely from his legs and arms as he charged at the ranger.

It felt weird to the berserker, having vines hanging from his limbs, but he ignored the feeling as best as he could, choosing to focus his senses on the ranger—the vines were just a minor inconvenience, and his main problem was the person behind the skill.

Even though Nathan had already discarded the vines as a threat, he knew that he couldn’t get too cocky where the ranger was concerned.

For all he knew, the vines he’d just powered through might have just been a distraction that the ranger would use to buy time for a heavier attack.

The ranger is definitely beneath me, but an arrow to my brain wouldn’t care about the level difference.

Either way, it wasn’t going to do him any good to underestimate the ranger, especially since he himself had come up clutch against much stronger foes multiple times, and for a large percentage of those fights, he’d only won due to his foes' massive egos.

He’d be wise to keep his head down and do what he had to do. Or else risked having his ego be the death of him.

His inner thoughts proved enough to keep him grounded as Nathan kept his ego in check, choosing not to fixate on his ability to destroy the ranger’s skill with minimal effort. Instead, he kept his focus on the wind, preferring to strengthen his connection to the wind that was as frail at the moment as he continued his charge towards the ranger.

Nathan had barely made it about five steps before his foot suddenly sank knee deep into muck, causing the berserker to briefly open his eyes in surprise to the sight of muddy ground that appeared from nowhere.

The berserker couldn’t help but curse at the sight, but he had no other option but to wade through it as he continued to close the distance between him and the ranger.

Frustration was starting to seep into his thoughts, the emotion fraying his already frail connection to the wind as Nathan thought more on the fact that his ‘sixth’ sense hadn’t warned him of the impending danger—then again, he supposed that there wasn’t much danger if he isolated the mud by itself.

Still, Nathan felt hard done by the wind, but he quickly clamped down on the emotion as soon as he noticed his fraying connection to the wind. He couldn’t lose access to his most useful skill because of a little mistake in its parts.

To be honest, Nathan wasn’t quite sure how his connection to the wind worked, but it sure behaved like it had a mind of its own, and he’d be loath to offend the wind because he was annoyed by a minor inconvenience.

Plus, there is also the fact that it isn’t an actual skill.

His ‘connection’ to the wind wasn’t an actual skill given to him by the wind, it was simply something he’d gotten after he’d gotten his dao or that was how it shouldve been but nathan vividly remembered relying on the ability, when he’d been fighting wolves in a dungeon a while back, and he’d also used hte same skill when he’d been fighting the bird back at the shore.

And even sometimes when I’d been spinning signs for Mr. Wong.

He’d used the wind so many times in the past, but he hadn’t thought much about it until now. And he supposed that he would've gone his whole life oblivious to the fact that the wind was alive in its own way if he hadn’t unlocked his dao.

But whatever the case may be, his ‘connection’ was still weak.

Its growth had sped up considerably since he unlocked his dao, but it hadn’t grown enough that he could rely solely on it just as he was currently doing; he had no choice in the matter, and that was why he had to let the frustrations of its occasional failures run down his shoulders.

With a quick exhale, he cleared his head as he focused on his surroundings using his ‘sixth’ sense.

While the mud did indeed limit free movement, it wasn’t something that overly concerned Nathan as he continued wading towards the ranger.

There was very little that could change Nathan's mind once he set his mind to it, and a minor inconvenience such as a patch of ground was far from that list.

The berserker continued moving towards the ranger who fired more arrows towards him, but Nathan did his best to evade them, even with the mud restricting him from moving as fast as he could.

So far, he had made considerable progress towards the ranger with no injuries received despite everything that the ranger had thrown at him. Nathan was starting to believe he could finish this without getting one, that was until the wind whispered in his ears, causing the berserker to open his eyes to the sight of the ranger notching and firing arrows rapidly in his direction.

He tried to move out of the way, but with him knee-deep in the mud, his options were severely limited. He could only close his eyes and tense his muscles as the arrows flew towards him.

All three arrows struck his side, with only the last one managing to do any real damage, causing a small gash in his side before falling into the muck just as the others had.

None of the three arrows had managed to do any significant damage to the berserker, and Nathan ignored the small gash at his side as he continued wading towards the ranger, his eyes still closed.

In the brief moment he’d had them open, he'd spotted the end of the muddy patch, and that was enough to spur the berserker to move forward as the end of his goal was now within his reach.

As soon as Nathan got to the end of the muddy stretch, he emerged close to the ranger, who had notched another arrow. His gut warned him that this arrow was different from the others, but he was already so close to reaching the ranger that he didn't consider it a real threat.

He tried to sidestep the attack but failed, as the arrow hit him in the spot where he had already been injured. The arrow dug into his side, but it was a mild inconvenience, so Nathan simply pushed past the pain.

The ranger was finally within reach, and the berserker no longer had to play the long game. His time to turn the tables has finally come, and Nathan has no plans to waste his chance.

His fist made contact with the ranger's face, a crunching sound following as he broke the ranger's nose, the force behind his pulled punch enough to send the ranger flying several feet away, until he bounced on the dirt, coming to a stop unconscious with blood coating the entirety of his face.

Well, that was easier than I imagined. Nathan thought as he opened his eyes, gazing on the unconscious form of the ranger.

The fight had taken place over a few minutes, and Nathan supposed that it was a testament to his strength and durability that he had suffered except for the single arrow sticking out of his side.

The beserker absentmindedly ripped the arrow free, tossing it to the side and turning his focus to the demon prince who’d cracked open an eye to stare at him with undisguised disgust.

"You didn't kill him."

"I didn't need to."

That was the truth.

It would have been a lot easier for Nathan if he’d gone in with the mindset of killing the ranger, but he hadn't, because he didn’t need to.

The ranger wasn’t a real threat to him, and he wasn’t in control of his actions.  For those reasons, Nathan believed he could afford to show compassion by not going savage mode on the ranger, mostly because that was the kind of person he was.

Nathan believed there was nothing wrong with making an effort not to kill someone, especially when it wasn't necessary to win the fight. Besides, the ranger had saved his life—or at least his arms—earlier, when he had stepped in to prevent the demon prince from ripping Nathan's limbs apart.

Besides nathan had used the opportunity to train his connection to the wind.

But more than that, Nathan had decided to change ever since he had cut ties with Sparrow and become a new person. Even when he had struggled for food and rent, spinning signs for Mr. Wong, when it would have been far easier to return to a life of crime, he had chosen otherwise.

It had seemed illogical at the time, because crime was the only thing he had ever known.

He had grown up surrounded by criminals, practically destined to become one himself. But he had decided not to, and he had stuck with that decision.

A large part of why he had changed was because of Sparrow. He had seen how his mentor had killed and tortured people at the slightest inconvenience. Deep down, he had known he did not want to become that same type of person. That was why he had decided against killing Pelo and the rest of the group when he had first met them.

To someone like Sparrow, that would have seemed like weakness—allowing his generosity to be trampled upon.

But Nathan had made the decision to be different from his mentor, and he had stuck to it. And that decision was beginning to shape the person he was becoming. It had become evident in the choice he had just made—to spare the ranger's life rather than kill him when he'd had the chance, when he'd had multiple opportunities to do so.

His life had been shaped by two men, and in one way or another, he was the one who had the final say in the kind of person he was going to become. He knew it deep down. He knew what power could do — how it could corrupt, how absolute power was intoxicating.

He had seen it in the way Sparrow had treated the people under him. But he also remembered how Mr. Wong had treated those who worked for him. Regardless of how difficult they had been, Mr. Wong had always dealt with them fairly — disciplining them when necessary, giving them clear instructions when they strayed. But the man had also given all of them opportunities, free food, and care when he probably hadn't had to. He had stayed on, making sure everything was running smoothly, even when he could easily have sat back and let the people he'd hired handle it.

Nathan couldn't help but believe there was a lesson in that — that someone at the very top could still be humble enough to get their hands dirty, whereas Sparrow rarely ever did. If he was being honest, Nathan knew which of the two men he'd rather be. That was why he had chosen this route.

But at the same time, maybe he needed a little ‘Sparrow’ in him too.

He had to be harsh as well as fair. Strict, and yet generous.

Offering opportunity to those who needed it, and even to those who didn't yet know they needed it, just as Mr. Wong had done for him.

Some would call that kind of life weakness. But Nathan didn't see it that way. In fact, he thought Mr. Wong was about as far from weak as a person could be.

Then, just as it had when he received his Dao, time seemed to stop entirely.

Nathan had finally understood who he was and why he mattered in the world. He was the one who would give opportunity to those who had none but needed one.

He would be the shield for the weak and defenseless.

And just like that, time resumed.
