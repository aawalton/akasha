For Ling Yinran, the past few days had been an absolute bore.

Her junior brother was off in the tower. Her aunt had locked herself in the observation hall days ago, and the last thing Ling Yinran had heard out of her was some rambling about being a genius at teaching disciples. Hearing that alone told her everything she needed to know: her master was almost certainly making herself insufferable in front of the other elders again.

"There are quite a lot of people gathered near the tower today, don't you think, Yinran?" Zheng Mengqi asked, practically bouncing on her toes as she peered over the edge of the peak's outer terrace.

"Yes," Ling Yinran said, not looking up from the ledger in her hands. Supply counts. Spirit herb inventories. The small, tedious work of keeping a peak running while its two residents were both otherwise occupied. It was for this reason she'd called her friend Zheng Mengqi to accompany her.

"So are we finally going to meet this fabled junior brother of yours?" Zheng Mengqi asked. "I've got a few words for him, you know. Taking up so much of my dear Yinran's time."

"Please don't."

"You telling me not to only makes me want to do it more." Zheng Mengqi grinned. "Especially after everything you've said about how ridiculous his ideas are. I want to pick his brain myself."

"You're welcome to, if he allows it." Ling Yinran finally looked up. "Honestly, I think my junior brother deserves some credit. He's the only person who keeps Aunt in check and drags her out of her worst habits. Though the explosion count hasn't gone down much."

"Elder Ling Xin, kept in check?" Zheng Mengqi's eyes lit up. "You're only making me more curious by the second."

"Mm." Privately, Ling Yinran thought her junior brother was good for her aunt, the same way her aunt was good for him. Both of them radiated a particular kind of unhinged energy, and somehow that energy fed off itself rather than causing outright disaster. Well. Mostly.

She had known her junior brother was unusual ever since he started treating his storage bag as part of his fighting style, constantly switching between different weapons mid-battle to create combinations no sane cultivator would even consider. That alone would've been enough for concern.

But after she'd learned he had devised a way to use a storage bag to make off with someone else's clothes, she'd quietly filed him under people who required close supervision, right alongside her aunt.

But he was still her junior brother, and she still worried.

She was halfway through turning a page in the ledger when the front gate of Spiritual Flower Peak shuddered violently.

"What's happening? Did the earth dragon turn over?" Zheng Mengqi jumped to her feet as the ground shook beneath her.

An earthquake?

"This doesn't feel like the aftermath of two Foundation Establishment elders fighting at full strength," Ling Yinran muttered. "If that happened inside the sect, they'd be punished."

How she knew that was best left unasked. Her master had exploited that very rule more than once to discourage people from challenging her over one invention or another.

"So, it's natural? But if that's the case, then the earth path masters in the sect should have announced it beforehand." Zheng Mengqi added. "Maybe they are all busy sleeping or something; it would be pretty funny if that was the case."

"Probably." Both of them weren't worried; the buildings in the sect were reinforced, especially the spiritual flower peak's buildings, due to the sheer number of explosions they'd had to endure because of her aunt.

It was then, suddenly, both of them felt a presence.

Ling Yinran's head snapped up.

A man stood just inside the outer boundary, robes torn and filthy, chains still dangling broken from both wrists. His eyes were wild, darting across the peak's storage buildings with a hungry look.

"Oh?" He grinned, revealing several missing teeth. "A little peak, all alone, practically begging to be picked clean. Don't mind me, girls. I'll just be taking whatever's valuable and be on my way."

Zheng Mengqi's mouth fell open. "Who let you onto the peak? Yinran, the boundary formation should have-"

She didn't get to finish.

A shape unfolded from beside the front step, silver light flashing as a jade puppet with a narrow, needle-fingered hand seized the man by the throat before he'd taken two more steps. He gagged, clawing uselessly at the grip, and White Thread simply held him there, patient and unmoving, until his struggling slowed to nothing.

Ling Yinran hadn't even reached for a talisman. She hadn't needed to.

"That answers that," she said flatly. She set the ledger down and walked toward the gate, eyes narrowing as she took in the man's chains, the state of his robes, the particular stupidity of a man who'd dare come and try to rob the lair of someone like Ling Xin. "You are not a disciple of the sect."

"W-wait, I can explain-"

"You don't need to." She flicked two fingers, and White Thread dragged him off toward the peak's holding shed. Then her gaze moved past the gate, toward the boundary array that should have screamed the moment anyone with his aura crossed it.

It hadn't done so at all.

Her expression went cold. "The early warning formation didn't trigger."

"What?" Zheng Mengqi hurried over. "That's impossible."

Ling Yinran crouched by the boundary stake and pressed two fingers to the formation plate embedded in the stone. There was no response at all. "It's not damaged. It's just off. As if someone cut the connection somewhere upstream."

The two of them exchanged a look.

"Let's go see what's happening," Ling Yinran said, already moving.

They didn't have to go far. The moment they cleared the tree line at the edge of the peak, the sound reached them first: a rolling wave of alarm bells, shouting, and something that might have been a beast's roar layered underneath it all. Smoke rose from at least three different directions across the mountain. Somewhere below, a formation flare burst red against the night sky and didn't fade.

"Is someone attacking the sect?" Zheng Mengqi asked, all her earlier bounce gone.

"Seems like it." Ling Yinran then used a token to activate the second-tier puppet at the peak. This puppet wasn't connected to the formation and worked just fine.

She always knew asking her aunt to invest in one would have its uses. It was totally not because she was drooling to examine a second-tier puppet firsthand or anything. Truly.

Above the square before the Heavenly Origin Tower, far in the distance but visible even from the peak, two enormous water-mirror screens hung suspended in the air, their surfaces flickering with unstable light.

"Screens? Are those showing people fighting?" Zheng Mengqi squinted at the floating projections. Her gaze landed on the one to the left. "Who's that? He's getting absolutely demolished."

Ling Yinran's expression changed instantly.

On the nearer screen, within a scorched corridor streaked with blood, stood a familiar figure. His robes were torn in several places, and blood ran down one side of his face as he faced an enemy far stronger than himself.

Her breath caught.

"That's Ning," she said, her voice tightening. "That's my junior brother."

She stared at the projection, her thoughts racing.

Why is he inside the Heavenly Origin Tower? Why is he fighting? And... why is all of this being shown outside?

The Heavenly Origin Tower had never publicly displayed its first five-floor trials before. Yet now, the entire sect was watching as though someone wanted every disciple and elder to witness what was happening inside.

Without another word, Ling Yinran broke into a run.

Zheng Mengqi hurried after her as they made their way toward the central square, where the crowd had already swollen into a sea of disciples. Shoulder pressed against shoulder, countless necks craned upward toward the enormous projections suspended above the plaza.

Ling Yinran pushed through the crowd without hesitation. The closer she drew, the clearer both screens became, and with them came the anxious murmurs spreading through the gathering.

"This looks like the screen from the tower trial. But where is that? I've never seen that floor."

"That looks like the lower sect prison."

"The lower prison? Impossible. Why would a trial disciple be there?"

Then someone near the back of the crowd shouted, voice cracking.

"That's Mei Long! I recognize him; my grandfather was a steward when they brought him in!"

Heads turned. The young disciple kept talking anyway to give all of the people information, as it was the norm among the disciples.

"He was a disciple of the Blood Fang Sect, decades ago. There was some kind of border dispute, and he ended up massacring three mortal villages trying to force a breakthrough. My grandfather said the death toll was in the hundreds."

"But that's not what makes him dangerous."

"Then what does?"

The young disciple's throat worked before he answered, like he didn't much like saying it out loud. "His supernatural ability. Wolf Devouring the Moon. It lets him shift into something between man and beast. My grandfather said the sect nearly lost three elders capturing him the first time."

A former Foundation Establishment cultivator?

Ling Yinran felt her heart tighten as she watched the battle unfold. Ning unleashed one talisman after another: fire, lightning, wind, each one carefully timed to slow Mei Long's advance or force him off balance. The attacks were clever, and under normal circumstances, more than enough to overwhelm an ordinary Qi Condensation cultivator. Yet against someone who had once stood in Foundation Establishment, they achieved almost nothing. Mei Long simply tore through them, each step carrying him inexorably closer.

Her fingers clenched so tightly that her nails dug into her palms.

Junior Brother. A terrifying thought surfaced despite every instinct telling her to reject it. The difference in strength was simply too great. If this continued...

No.

She bit down on her lip, her eyes refusing to leave the projection.

No, he won't die. He can't.

"Snap out of it, Yinran." Zheng Mengqi brought her out of her thinking.

"W-why is my junior brother fighting such a man? What the hell is happening?" Ling Yinran said. Her composure was cracking fast.

On the second one, two young men stood in some vast underground chamber, chains as thick as tree roots buried in the walls around them. Golden qi and dark lightning collided again and again, shaking the chamber walls with every clash.

Ling Yinran recognized one of them, Xiao Fan, though not because they were close. Because Ji Ning had mentioned him. Repeatedly. And in increasingly strange terms.

"Senior Sister," Ning had once told her, with the solemnity of a man reporting a natural disaster, "if you ever meet a disciple named Xiao Fan, please just leave immediately. That guy is a trouble magnet, so if you ever see him, run."

Ling Yinran didn't know what to say to that; she had never seen her junior brother so serious.

If it had been her aunt who was cautioned like that, she would have tried to meet this fellow out of sheer curiosity, but Ling Yinran understood there must be a deeper reason for this, and never really sought the guy out.

She hadn't fully understood it then. Watching the second screen now, she was starting to.

"Lei Dong!" Xiao Fan's voice rang through the mirror, a little distorted but still clear enough to understand. "What are you doing?"

She quickly understood that this was the name of the boy standing before Xiao Fan.

On the screen, Lei Dong stood near the central seal, his expression twisted between triumph and pain. Purple-black lightning crawled over his body, and beneath it, ancient chains of light had wrapped around his arms, sinking into his flesh.

"What am I doing?" Lei Dong laughed. "This is why I entered the tower. This is why I endured the trials. This is why all of this was arranged."

"You betrayed the sect."

Ling Yinran narrowed her eyes at the accusation. She had a feeling that everything would become clear in a few seconds.

"Betrayed?" His smile grew uglier. "This sect betrayed us first. The tower, the elders, the seat of sect master, the so-called righteous rules, all of it was stolen from my bloodline."

The tower spirit shouted, "Idiot! You don't understand what you're touching. That seal is meant to restrain Lei Shen."

"Hahahaha! You can't stop me. I know my purpose." With another talisman, Lei Dong broke the seal.

What happened next terrified even the stoic Ling Yinran.

"W-what's that?" Zheng Mengqi came closer to her.

Suddenly, a scream started as Lei Dong's body began to bloom with dark blood-red lines. "Wha-? Grand-Uncle? What are you doing?"

It was as if Lei Dong was losing control; half his face resembled the young boy from before, while the other had taken on a serene look.

"I am just doing what was planned. You did your job well, little Lei Dong. Leave it all to me. Your everything." Then, amid Lei Dong's scream and confusion, the muscles beneath his skin shifted. His eyes rolled back and opened again, filled with a deep purple aura far older and colder than his own.

When he spoke again, the voice no longer belonged to Lei Dong.

"Hehe... AHAHAHAHA! I'm back! I, Lei Shen, have returned!" Lei Shen threw his head back, laughing as ecstasy spread across his face. "Ah... spiritual qi. How long I've missed you."

The moment his name left his lips, the square outside the Heavenly Origin Tower erupted into chaos.

"Lei Shen?"

"The Purple Mansion traitor?"

"Impossible! Wasn't he sealed in the deepest prison?"

"How is he outside?"

Cold ran through Ling Yinran's fingers. Lei Shen, Lei Fu's brother, the old rebel sealed beneath the Heavenly Origin Tower, one of the Pure Qi Sect's biggest betrayals.

In the mirror, Lei Shen lifted Lei Dong's hand and looked at it with mild distaste. "A poor vessel, but useful enough. My descendant carried my blood, my mark, and the key Lei Fu prepared."

"For decades this sect kept me sealed beneath a formation that fed on the prison's own suppression field," Lei Shen said, almost conversational despite what he had just done. "So my brother and I built a plan. A second ritual, layered beneath the first, waiting for a night when the tower's authority would loosen on its own. Every time a disciple enters for trial, the formation opens itself just enough to test them. Every time it opens, my ritual drinks a little more from that gap."

The golden-eyed young man on the screen bared his teeth. "You used the trial itself."

"I used everything. The beasts loosed across your sect aren't chaos for its own sake; they're a drain, forcing every spare cultivator above to fight instead of reinforcing the tower. Every formation node my brother's people cut is one less thread holding the suppression array together. Piece by piece, this entire sect's protective formation is being unwound from the inside."

The chains ignited fully, purple light pouring down into the stone.

"When the last thread goes, I will not merely be free within this prison; the entire formation in the sect will go down. After that, you will see true horror, being besieged by others in the years to come." His smile stretched too wide for his borrowed face. "That is the ritual, little ant. A resurrection and the upcoming fall. And you are watching its final stage."

Xiao Fan stepped forward, golden qi rising around him. "Then I'll stop you before that happens."

Lei Shen's smile widened. "Well, do your best. Outside, Lei Fu should have already begun the plan. I won't kill you; I want to see your face upon witnessing the destruction of this sect you're defending."

As if answering those words, a roar split the sky outside the tower.

Ling Yinran turned sharply. Across the sect, alarms erupted, formation bells ringing from the inner mountain, smoke rising near the Beast Hall. A great shadow leapt over one of the distant courtyards, wings spread wide beneath the night sky, and several disciples screamed as a scaled beast crashed through a line of buildings, its body covered in binding brands that had clearly just been shattered. More roars followed, one from the eastern gardens, another from the outer training fields, another near the General Affairs Hall.

The crowd around Ling Yinran had gone eerily quiet, and then all at once, it wasn't. Several inner disciples drew swords and rushed toward the nearest roar. Others tried to reach their masters through transmission talismans, only to find them flickering uselessly in their hands.

Ling Yinran was one of them as she took out her own transmission jade. "Aunt."

Nothing answered but spiritual static.

"Aunt Ling Xin," she tried again.

The jade stayed dark.

That was impossible unless something was blocking communication, or her aunt was trapped somewhere and unable to answer.

"Then I can't wait for my aunt." Her voice didn't waver at the revelation; instead, her face became determined. "I'm going to the sect prison. My junior brother is down there fighting someone who outclasses him in every way, and I'm not going to stand here and just take it. No one bullies my junior brother."

"Alright! Let's kick some butt. I am with you."

Zheng Mengqi fell into step beside her without another word of protest, and the two of them ran through the burning square toward the Heavenly Origin Tower.

In the past, it was a good thing to have such an accomplished beast tamer, but now, facing a riot of beasts, it was not so much a blessing.

At the northern gardens, a three-eyed black ape smashed through a medicine field wall, broken beast-taming chains still hanging from its neck, its body covered in shattered control brands, crushing spirit herbs that outer disciples had spent months raising. Several disciples screamed and scattered.

Then a sword light descended.

A young inner disciple in blue robes landed atop a stone lantern, sword held behind him, face pale but eyes steady. "Everyone run. We are the guards in charge of the inner sect. Formation A! Don't fight it alone; bind its legs first!"

Three younger disciples reacted at once. One threw a vine-binding talisman. Another struck the ground with a frost one. A third, trembling badly enough that his sleeves shook, still managed to activate a golden net formation. The ape charged, tore through all three in a single breath, but that breath was enough.

The blue-robed disciple's sword descended, and though the strike was nothing special, it was aimed very well. The sword light pierced the beast's third eye, and it staggered and crashed into the ground hard enough to shake the medicine fields.

The youngest disciple stared at him in awe. It was his first day serving with the Inner Sect Guard. "S-Senior Brother... that was incredible."

The blue-robed disciple sheathed his sword and gave the younger disciple a reassuring pat on the shoulder. "You gave me the opening. Without those few moments, I couldn't have landed that strike."

The young disciple's eyes immediately lit up.

Before he could say another word, another deafening roar echoed from beyond the shattered wall.

The blue-robed disciple's smile disappeared.

"Save the excitement for later," he said, already turning toward the sound. "We still have much to do."

On the western side of the inner sect, the situation was stranger. A group of escaped criminals had burst from one of the underground prison exits, expecting panic, easy hostages, and a chance to take revenge on the sect before fleeing into the night. Unfortunately for them, they emerged beside the kitchen courtyard.

The kitchen courtyard wasn't as famous as some other factions, but it was still recognized as the foremost place for spiritual cooking in the sect. Plus, it did have twenty-seven outer and inner disciples preparing late-night medicinal meals for those waiting near the Heavenly Origin Tower, three cauldrons of boiling spirit-oil, seven cleavers, and a head cook who'd once served in a border garrison before deciding that cooking was less stressful. Which, in hindsight, couldn't have been more wrong.

The first criminal climbed out laughing wildly. "I am free!"

A reinforced iron ladle, the kind used to stir bone-marrow soup in a spiritual cauldron, caught him full in the face. He fell back into the hole with a scream.

The second criminal emerged right after, only to be greeted by a wave of boiling spirit-oil. He howled, rolled across the ground, and got hit by three chopping boards, two fire-control talismans, and a thrown jar of pickled thunder peppers for his trouble.

The head cook raised his cleaver. "Kitchen disciples!"

Twenty-six voices answered in panic and fury. "Yes, Senior Brother!"

"Protect the soup!"

"For the sect?"

"For the soup!"

The escaped criminals, men who'd committed innumerable crimes, found themselves being beaten back into the prison exit with wok lids, spice jars, and terrifyingly accurate vegetable knives. One old criminal, hair white, his aura still faintly at peak Qi Condensation, stared in disbelief as a young kitchen disciple, Fatty Li, threw something into his face and shouted, "Blindness Powder!"

The criminal quickly covered his eyes, but his lips had already tasted it. "Damn you! This is just flour."

"Brother Ning said, if it works, then it works."

"Not bad, little Li. You're crushing it." The head cook said, using his cleaver to injure the cultivator in one stroke.

"Hehe. I'm just using what I've learned." Fatty Li said, then looked up at the screen where Ning and Xiao Fan were doing everything they could to fight.

Brother Ning and Brother Xiao Fan would definitely survive. With such thoughts, Fatty Li moved, ready to attack other criminals for daring to target his friends.

Near the Beast Hall, the fighting was less amusing. Several enclosures had been opened from the inside, their control brands tampered with, and spirit wolves, armored lizards, horned cranes, and other creatures rampaged through the courtyards, attacking anyone who came near. But the Beast Hall disciples weren't helpless.

For heaven's sake, their whole schtick was taming unruly beasts with the power of their fists, then winning them over with love and friendship.

A tall female disciple with a whip stood atop the head of a blue-scaled bull, her hair flying wildly in the wind as she shouted commands across the courtyard. "Don't kill the young ones! Their control marks were tampered with! Subdue them first. Kill only if they can't be restrained!"

Around her, Beast Hall disciples broke off in pairs and trios, each flanked by their own bonded beasts, moving to meet the rampaging creatures head-on rather than simply defend.

A stocky red lizard, its claws digging into the stone, slammed shoulder-first into a charging spirit wolf's front legs before it could reach a fleeing steward. The wolf yelped and toppled sideways into a flowerbed, and before it could scramble upright, the lizard's tail whipped twice around its snout, cinching its jaws shut. It thrashed and muffled to no avail.

"Nicely done!" the whip-wielding disciple called, already turning toward the next threat. A horned crane circled in from the side, murder in its eyes. She raised her arm, and a hulking iron-feathered crane dropped out of the sky to meet it, wings striking wings and driving the wild crane sideways into a wall hard enough to crack the stone.

"Senior Sister," the junior disciple said, ducking as a loose feather flew past his face, "that's terrible strategy. If they know each other's tricks, doesn't that mean they're evenly matched?"

She blinked. "...Huh."

Before she could think too hard about it, the iron-feathered crane slammed the wild crane out of the air with a triumphant screech.

"...See?" she said immediately, pointing at the result. "It worked. My strategy isn't stupid."

"Senior Sister, if it's stupid and it works, then it's still stupid. It's just a miracle that it worked," the junior disciple said blandly.

"Y-You shut up!" she shot back, puffing out her cheeks. "I was simply demonstrating that my crane is stronger through overwhelming force. Beasts respect strength above all else. Once they realize they can't win, they'll naturally lose the will to resist and surrender."

She stumbled through the first half of the explanation, but by the end, she sounded so confident that even she seemed convinced.

The junior disciple considered her words for a moment before giving a slow nod.

"...You know what? That's actually not a terrible strategy."

Whether or not it had been her original intention hardly mattered anymore. Against beasts, overwhelming them into submission really was a valid approach.

Elsewhere across the courtyard, the pattern repeated itself. A disciple with a scarred jaw sent his hawk shrieking after a half-demonic bat, the two of them wheeling and diving through the smoke until the bat, disoriented and exhausted, dropped low enough for a thrown net to catch it. Another disciple simply stood her ground as her own armored serpent coiled around a wild one twice its size, squeezing until the wild beast stopped struggling.

One by one, the beasts fell, pinned, exhausted, or muzzled shut by their own kind. This was the difference between beasts that were wild and those that had bonded with a beast tamer. The strength of a beast and its beast tamer was not a simple one plus one equals two. Rather, it was multiplicative in nature.

"Bind them!" the senior sister shouted, already crouched beside the half-strangled wolf and slapping a talisman across its brow. Golden characters flared, then dimmed into a faint, steady glow. Two more disciples moved in with coils of spirit rope, looping them tightly around the beast's legs before it could recover its wits.

Across the courtyard, the same scene played out again and again: beast pressed down by beast, talisman pressed to brow, spirit rope cinched tight, until one by one the escaped creatures were dragged, growling and bound, back toward the holding pens.

The junior disciple wiped the sweat from his forehead and glanced at his senior sister, who was now admiring her handiwork with entirely too much pride for someone who had nearly been trampled twice. He decided, not for the first time, that it wasn't worth the argument.

Elsewhere, the Formation Hall had become a battlefield of its own. Normally, the formation masters spent their days arguing over spirit veins and array geometry. Today, they were being escorted across the sect by the Discipline Committee.

A blood-crazed criminal lunged from an alley, but three sword flashes later, he was no more.

"Keep moving!"

An Iron-Tusk Boar burst through a courtyard wall. The escort met it head-on. Sword lights crisscrossed the boar, and moments later the beast crashed to the ground, dead.

The formation masters never slowed, even as they watched the screen in the sky.

On the screen beside Xiao Fan, a ritual was continuing. In the ritual, one could see a miniature version of the sect's core formation. The normally golden threads making up the formation were being dyed blood red; when the whole miniature formation turned completely red, Lei Shen would win.

One formation master pointed at the projection. "Trace every blood thread. We don't have time to repair the entire formation. Untangle whatever Lei Shen and his group tampered with and reverse it section by section."

Dozens of formation disks lit up.

Calculations began immediately.

One younger formation master clenched his fists. "...Please hold on."

Xiao Fan was sent flying yet again by Lei Shen. Even though he was using Fist Intent and everything else, he still appeared to be no match.

"We only need more time."

Every moment Xiao Fan stayed alive was another section of the formation they could reclaim.

They were not joking. The reason the formation being disrupted was such a big deal was that it was the thing that protected the sect the most. As long as the formation ran smoothly, unless a Nascent Soul cultivator attacked head-on, the Pure Qi Sect would remain standing; formations were just that powerful.

On the other hand, at the alchemist peaks, the alchemists didn't share the same worry as the formation masters.

Foundation Establishment Cultivators were also not idle.

Similar battles erupted across the mountain, one after another. But despite everything, it showed one thing: the Pure Qi Sect was not going down without a fight.

Zhou Chen was looting.

Ning had planned his own growth to leave an actual trail behind it. Techniques purchased. Time spent training. Teachers involved. Matches fought. Breakthroughs earned after visible effort. Even his performance in the tower had been shocking, but not rootless; people could credit it to comprehension and talent.

No one would be suspicious of him possessing some heaven-defying inheritance or something. After all, he was not Xiao Fan, who could keep showing abnormal growth without any rhyme or reason to the name.

Ning had always planned to gradually reveal more about himself as he continued to grow stronger. That was the path he wanted and took, not truly low-key, nor being absolutely always in the limelight.

But this was too much at once. A Qi Condensation disciple killing Mei Long, in public, was a bit too fast for his plan of showing himself in the sect.

The tower spirit's expression softened. "I understand your concern. But there were reasons."

"They'd better be good." Ning just spoke blankly.

"They are. Without the broadcast, Lei Fu's faction could claim the tower malfunctioned and bury the truth. The sect needed to know Lei Shen was waking. If your achievement stayed hidden, someone else could steal or suppress the credit later. And visible heroism helps morale; right now the sect is under attack, and seeing a disciple bring down Mei Long matters to the people watching."

Ning said nothing.

"Also," the spirit added, "if you die later, it will be useful for people to already know you died impressively."

"Alright." As he had said time and time again, Ning had just accepted that shit was going to happen, and would adjust accordingly. "So why are you here?"

The tower spirit then spoke hurriedly, "Oh right, I forgot, amid everything, Xiao Fan has a plan."

The spirit flew forward and slapped a small seal against Ning's forehead before Ning had time to object, and a flood of images poured into his mind at once: it was all about how Xiao Fan was fighting against Lei Shen, his plan, and the true conspiracy.

Overall, he had meta-guessed parts of it, so he wasn't exactly surprised. He was, though, surprised by what Xiao Fan's plan actually was at the end.

Ning opened his eyes and said nothing for a moment.

"You also think it's stupid, right?" the spirit demanded, pointing at him. "The plan's obviously insane. You're not going to follow that plan, are you?"

Ning smiled faintly. "I would do it."

"You would what?" The tower spirit exclaimed aloud.

"I would do it." Ning's eyes shone a brilliant blue.

The tower spirit exclaimed. "You two are both insane."

"But I do have one concern," Ning suddenly said.

"Good. You're finally thinking sensibly. What's the concern?" the Tower Spirit asked.

"If I go through with this, you'll need to close the water mirrors outside." Ning glanced upward. "I'd rather not have the entire sect misunderstand what they're seeing."

The Tower Spirit sighed.

"That's what you're worried about? Don't be. What comes next can't be shown through the mirrors anyway." It shook its head helplessly. "If the two of you are really insane enough to follow through with this plan, then let's do it."

Ning nodded, and then slowly stood up despite his body crying with pain.

He was searching for an arrow. On the battlefield, most had been snapped in half; others were bent beyond repair.

After searching for a moment, Ning finally picked up an arrow whose shaft remained mostly intact. He checked the fletching, tested its balance with a light flick of his fingers, then gave a small nod.

"It'll do."

He wiped away the blood coating the shaft before taking out his bow.

Love.

The bow's once-pristine body was now marred by countless scratches, one limb slightly warped from the abuse it had suffered.

Ning reached for the string, only to wince.

The wrist that had blocked Mei Long's strike still throbbed relentlessly. Simply gripping the bow sent a sharp ache shooting up his forearm.

"Right..."

He tore a strip from the least-damaged part of his sleeve and wrapped it tightly around the swollen joint. The pressure dulled the pain just enough that he could still move it, though every flex of his fingers reminded him the injury was far from healed.

Satisfied, he brushed a thumb along Love's battered frame.

"One last shot."

The Tower Spirit floated beside him. "You get one chance."

"I know."

Ning slowly nocked the arrow.

His eyes gradually turned a brilliant blue as Phantom Veil silently circulated. Every trace of leaking qi vanished, his presence fading into the surroundings with each passing breath.

"I don't have much spiritual energy left," he said, steadying his breathing. "I'm pouring everything into this one arrow. Can you help?"

"I can." The Tower Spirit flew forward. "It's fortunate the Spiritual Energy Seal already faded from your palm. Otherwise, your body wouldn't be able to hold another."

A different seal settled into Ning's palm. Warmth spread through his meridians.

Ning blinked. "I didn't even notice the old seal disappearing."

"That's normal." The Tower Spirit shrugged. "Temporary seals gradually fade away. The human body simply isn't meant to bear seals for long." It glanced toward the black doorway. "Only the Original Seal is different. Without it, a person can only carry one seal at a time, and even that seal will eventually dissipate."

"The Original Seal." Ning's eyes narrowed. "The one Xiao Fan has?"

The spirit nodded.

"Exactly. That's why I was able to keep placing seal after seal on him before his fight with Lei Shen. The Original Seal stabilizes them. Without it, every seal I gave him would have faded quickly, and trying to stack them would've torn his body apart."

Ning finally understood how Xiao Fan was able to survive for so long.

Just like Ning was doping on pills, Xiao Fan was getting buffed to the very heavens by myriad seals.

Wait...

Ning's expression changed.

"What about the Heavenly Origin Orbs?" he asked suddenly, a bad feeling creeping into his chest.

The Tower Spirit's expression immediately soured. "Those?" It sighed heavily. "Without the Original Seal, the Heavenly Origin Tower can no longer condense Heavenly Origin Orbs."

Ning's eyelid twitched.

The spirit continued, "Fortunately, the other trials aren't affected too badly. Their seals can eventually be replaced with formations with little loss in function. The Origin Trial is the only exception. Condensing a Heavenly Origin Orb requires the Original Seal. There's no substitute for it." The Tower Spirit let out another weary sigh. "Looks like I'll have to rethink part of the tower's future."

"I suddenly got a lot more motivation for what I'm about to do next," Ning said. Stupid protagonists, and their rule of always taking the best thing and making it unusable for everyone else.

He'd had so many plans for the origin orb, if he could ever have acquired one.

Amid this rage, Ning began to charge the arrow.

Lightning Cry Arrow gathered first. Then came a thread of Crimson Flame Control, a line of crimson fire wrapping around the lightning without fighting it, circling instead like a burning serpent along the outer edge. Ning's control was rough, since this was the first time he'd tried to use the two techniques layered together.

Then came ice qi from Jade Skin and Icy Meridians, pale blue cold wrapping around the flame. Fire and ice should have clashed, and did; the arrow trembling violently enough that Ning nearly coughed up blood again.

The tower spirit flew forward, tiny hands forming seals faster than sight could follow, golden runes wrapping around the arrow, separating the layers further apart from each other. "Don't merge them. You can use that spiral arrow of yours to circle them around each other."

Ning's eyes sharpened, and the arrow began to turn: lightning at the center, crimson flame circling it, ice qi circling that, all three forces twisting together without blending, rotating along a single deadly path. The lightning pierced. The flame burned. The ice suppressed. The spiral held it all steady.

Finally, Ning drew from the Infinite Circle. The thin purple-gold thread in his dantian trembled, and with that, the last qi he had accumulated was gone too.

Purple-gold light sank into the arrow's core.

Ning exhaled slowly.

Then, carrying the fully charged arrow, he stepped through the black doorway.

Phantom Veil circulated silently.

His presence faded until it was scarcely distinguishable from the surrounding air, his aura so thoroughly concealed that even the flow of qi around him remained undisturbed. Keeping to the deepest shadows of the chamber, he moved without a sound, the drawn bow never wavering.

At the center of the chamber, Xiao Fan and Lei Shen remained locked in fierce combat.

As Lei Shen's fist came crashing down once more, the Tower Spirit's voice quietly echoed within Xiao Fan's mind.

He's here. Xiao Fan's eyes shifted for the briefest instant.

Hidden within the deepest shadows, he could barely make out the faint silhouette of a bow drawn to its absolute limit.

That was enough.

A faint smile tugged at the corner of his bloodstained lips.

The subtle exchange didn't escape Lei Shen.

His eyes narrowed, and for the first time since the battle began, he spared a glance toward the darkness behind Xiao Fan.

"What are you smiling at?"

Xiao Fan only grinned wider. "Nothing."

Lei Shen's instincts screamed that something was wrong, and he hesitated for a moment.

It was all Xiao Fan needed.

Instead of meeting the incoming blow with Heaven-Shattering Fist as he had throughout the battle, he suddenly changed.

Lei Shen's lightning-wreathed fist shot toward his chest, but Xiao Fan twisted away with a flexibility no ordinary body cultivator should have possessed. His shoulders folded at an impossible angle, his spine bent like a serpent, and one arm slipped beneath Lei Shen's elbow while the other hooked across his shoulder.

"What?" Lei Shen's expression changed as Xiao Fan pulled him in close and held on.

He'd learned this against the puppet in the fifth trial: that his body didn't need to attack only with fists. His beast-derived flexibility, used properly, could turn his entire frame into a binding technique. His legs locked around Lei Shen's waist, one arm trapped his shoulder, the other forced his chest open against his own spine.

Lei Shen's lightning erupted around them both. Xiao Fan screamed through clenched teeth and did not let go.

"You mad little-"

"Now!" Xiao Fan roared. "Do it, Ning!"

Ning released the arrow.

The arrow crossed the chamber in silence, thunder arriving only after it, the spiral tearing through the air with purple lightning at its heart, crimson flame circling it, pale ice sealing the outer edge, and a single purple-gold thread hidden within the turning. It passed between hanging chains, cut straight through drifting smoke, and struck the two joined figures at the center of the chamber.

[One Shot, One Kill: Lightning Cry Arrow + Crimson Flame Control + Ice + Infinite Circle + Spiral Arrow]

It hit Xiao Fan first.

Then Lei Shen.

Straight through the heart line, and then both of them collapsed, blood painting the ground red.

They were dead.
