At some point during the night, I woke and rose to feed the fire.

The normally orange firelight was tinged with blue. The whole ruin was soaked in a soft eerie light, barely there between the latticed lacework of leafy shadows. It took me a moment to locate the source. An enormous crescent moon was poking out from between the treetops.

I blinked up at it.

The disc was larger by far than earth’s moon, three times as large, easily. The sickle shape was sharp and rather beautiful. I watched it in awe, absentmindedly feeding the fire. Then I lay back down to sleep. If I was living in a world where magic was possible, I should expect wonders. But for some reason, the obviously alien, unearthliness of the giant moon really brought home that I was in another world.

As I lay, I watched it, through the gap in the roof, and just before I nodded off I became aware of a second moon - this one much smaller, and pearly white, rising beside the first. Was it smaller or further away? Was I dreaming? No, not dreaming. When I woke with the rising sun, I located the large, faded crescent, and the smaller, paler orb in the haze of the horizon. The big one was hard to miss.

“Well, them’s some fancy moons,” I muttered as I stretched. “Not in Kansas anymore, Toto, blah blah.”

“Eggs!” said Eggs, from up on the roof.

“Good morning to you to,” I said, then remembered I could switch my base points with every new day.

I switched them to strength from endurance. My tank was full of mana, and I wanted to see if the additional three points of strength would be enough for me to pry one of those crystals from the dungeon.

I flexed a bicep speculatively. It was pleasingly firm. I was certainly a lot stronger than old Rachel, who groaned and hyperventilated trying to open a jam jar. Stronger than Ian Fuck-Face, probably. Now that would be fun to test. Could I perhaps unlock a ‘swift kick to the nethers’ skill in the process?

But Ian was worlds away. Good riddance.

I looked around for things to test my strength against, and after some thought, heaved myself up onto the roof with my arms only. It was still an effort, and involved some unladylike grunting, but I could do it. There was no way I could have done that back home. I jumped up onto a rough stone, approximately hip height, and made it clean, feet landing flat and stable.

“Ha!”

I tried a higher one and stumbled, whacking my knees. More in dexterity would also help, but still, I had almost made it! Outside the ruin I entertained myself seeing how many fallen branches I could carry in one go. The answer was a great many, and I ran out of space in my arms before I ran out of strength to carry the weight of them all. I needed something heavier. My eyes landed on the gargoyles. Back inside, I leapt up onto the roof.

“Excuse me,” I said to Wistful. “Can I pick you up?”

A sound came out of his mouth that I realised after a moment, was laughter.

“Try,” he said.

I wrapped my arms around him and heaved. The gargoyle did not move so much as an inch.

“Mefff!” demanded Eggs. I repeated the experiment with the same conclusion.

“I’ll be back,” I said, “when I’m stronger,” and went back down to devour a meaty breakfast to the sound of stony chuckles.

Food finished, I marched down to the dungeon.

The [Demon Door] greeted my arrival in her usual manner.

“But soft! What smell through yonder stairwell wafts? It is a stench, and Rachel is the source.”

“Hey, I washed!” I said. She hmphed. “Open up, please.”

The door swung wide to reveal the misty portal.

I stepped through, excitement bubbling in my chest. The [Demon Pigeons] did not notice me, and I wasted no time, attacking one of the smaller crystals without delay. I hammered on it with the hilt of my stiletto, I punched down with my fist, I jumped, slamming my whole body into them. But try as I might I could not crack any off. They remained resolutely attached to the wall. The noise of my activities, of course, attracted the attention of the roosting sky-rats, so I took out my disappointment on them.

I sliced the first to reach me out of the air, a [Curse] dropped a second, a third bit my hand before I flung it bodily against the cavern wall with so much force it fell to the ground, stunned. Staring at it in surprise cost me precious moments, but then I came to my senses and leapt through the portal to safety.

[Congratulations! You have defeated x3 Feral Demon Pigeons, lvls 3-5!

Experience is awarded!]

The additional strength made a subtle but noticeable difference to my fighting style. My blade had slashed the first feathery body clean in two. Punching things was now apparently an option too! Well, smallish, light things made of bird bones. Was the flock thinning? I could not tell for sure, but I thought maybe. A pity, I had been hoping I could farm them for experience.

I cleaned and bound my hand, rubbed some more of the unicorn dust into the wounds since that seemed to help, then went in again.

And again.

Four times more I went into the dungeon, chasing a level up in the hope that another shot of strength would help me dislodge a crystal.

Slowly but surely my slaughtering of the stupid birds became more efficient as I learned their flight patterns, their habits of aggression, and their weak points (necks, feathers, eyes). I learned things about my magic too. Targeting one bird with one [Curse] killed it dead, but only if I focused on its neck. Centred on the eyes it didn’t kill, just maimed. Eyes were tougher than necks, it seemed. Feathers were weak. Spreading the [Curse] across several birds’ feathers, rather than an individual, was most effective.

Birds needed feathers to fly, and [Feral Demon Pigeons] feathers withered most beautifully and satisfactorily. And so, I fought, [Cursing], punching them out of the air with one hand and slicing and stabbing with the other.

Once or twice, I had overestimated how many birds I could take at once, was blindsided by new attackers, or swarmed more quickly than usual. Those encounters earned me a few talon slashes and beak stabs before I managed to throw myself back out the door.

I took a break to recover the bodies, and to [Forage] for eggs in the forest outside. I cooked and ate lunch, fed the gargoyles (eggs and [Feral Demon Pigeon] corpses for dessert) and then went in again. The gargoyles were very approving of the bird bodies, and I felt like our little alliance was well on its way to mutual satisfaction.

A few hours later there was no doubt. The flock was most definitely thinning, and the [Demon Door] had finally got tired of making snarky remarks every time I dived through her portal, to lie gasping on the stones.

“A fine [Soap Witch] you are,” said the Demon door. Oh, I was wrong. “Covered from head to toe in pigeon viscera.”

I grinned but said nothing. A fine pile of bloodied [Feral Demon Pigeons] lay next to me.

[Inspect] told me the meat was poisonous (to me). I did not fancy eating something I had [Cursed] anyway, but my stone friends seemed to have steel-plated stomach linings. The surviving feathers had interesting properties - [Poison, flight, fortify, sight] - so I kept a few aside, placing them in my Beginner Witch’s Pantry of Magical and Bizarre Ingredients.

Said pantry was just a flat slab in the closed room part of the ruin, the only part that I could guarantee was out of the elements. So far, there was only the remains of the unicorn dust and the feathers, and a few bunches of drying herbs, but I knew my supplies would grow as I explored.

The door was right about one thing: nothing about the [Feral Demon Pigeon] carcasses was useful for soap making. At least, not as far as I could see.

“I will be an excellent [Soap Witch]. I’m going to render down the deer meat to make fat,” I said to her. “Soon.”

“I don’t care,” said the door.

We glared at each other.

Then I went back into the dungeon and killed some more damn [pigeons].

Just as I was about to give up for the day, I got the notification:

[Congratulations! You have defeated Feral Demon Pidgeon Swarm lvls 4-6! Experience is awarded!

Curse has reached level 4!]

[Congratulations! You have reached Witch level 4! 3 Stat points available.]

My blade gripped tight in one bloodstained hand I searched the nooks and crannies of the cave ceiling above, waiting for more [pigeons], despite the notification, but none came. It seemed I had killed every last one! I beamed into the darkness, and then immediately threw all my new points into strength.

Flexing my palm, I immediately seized the nearest mana crystal and leant on it with all my weight. The crystalline edges bit into my hand, hard. Was it moving? …No.

I had left a very dramatic blood-stained handprint on the pristine edges. I wiped the worst of the gunk off my hands, stepped back, and looked at the mana crystals consideringly. Wrapping the cloth of my skirt around my hand, I tried again, this time on the smallest crystal I could see. I pushed down, with a groan, then jumped, slamming my whole body’s weight onto it.

There was a sharp crack, that ran through the cavern like a pistol shot. There was a dull thump from somewhere in the stairwell. I paused, ready to flee. Seconds passed. A minute passed and nothing emerged, so I got back too it, hurrying as fast as I could, my eyes fixed on the splintering crystal. A stark white vein formed, running jagged across the rock. Just a little more… With a grunt I heaved. It shattered, and the mana crystal ripped away from the cavern wall, leaving behind jagged, vicious splinters, which I was careful not to touch.

In my palm lay a single mana crystal, about the size of a rather skinny lipstick.

It was surprisingly heavy, hard, prismatic and very, very pretty, throwing dazzling rainbows of light across my face. I glanced between the still empty stairwell and the remaining crystal, moving a little to see if anything was lurking. If there was I couldn’t see it.

One more crystal then, one for each gargoyle. Setting the crystal down, carefully, I employed the same strategy on a second, and managed to pull the second, slightly smaller one from the cavern wall in another spray of mana splinters. They pinged against the floor, chiming like a bell, the noise loud, clear, and more than a little uncanny.

Something large arrived behind me with a dull, wet, cavern shaking thump.

I whirled, staring into the giant orb-like eyes of a toad the size of a small garden shed. Reflecting the ghostly violet light of the portal, they were fixed on me with wet malevolence.

[Demon Bufo Bufo Toad, lvl 12. Venomous. Poisonous. Charming]

Charming?

Now I looked closely, I could see its skin was actually kinda beautiful. Wet but shiny like a marsh under moonlight. I was dazzled by the stickiness, by the play of light, the ghostly violet on brown. What would it feel like? I reached out my hand, imagining the moist texture beneath my fingertips.

I lifted one foot to step forwards.

No, what was I doing? A headache burst across my temple making me wince. Wet trickled out of my nose and down over my lip. It tasted like blood. The toad’s throat pulsed. Ew. EW.

[Mental Fortitude has reached lvl 2!]

Coming to my sense, I dived for the portal, scooping up the precious crystals in one hand, my dagger in the other. I almost made it, but something thick and pink lashed out of the toad’s mouth to wrap around my ankle. The stinging tongue hauled me backwards, wrenching me clean off my feet.

It stung. It burned. I screamed and kicked out as it dragged me over the rough stone floor. My strength was nothing to the strength of the toad. I was being dragged bodily towards its dripping maw. With a yell I twisted, driving the jagged edge of one of the crystals into the giant, sticky organ with one hand, and my dagger in with the other. Blood spurted everywhere, and I twisted, sawing into the stringy strands. The tongue snapped.

Gasping, I threw myself at the portal. This time I made it.

I landed on the other side with a bruising crash and lay for a while hyperventilating on my back. Still on my back I held my crystals up triumphantly. I had them! Everything was covered in blood.

“Disgusting,” said the door.
