“I saw Chloe,” Gary said, slipping next to Asim on the way back.

“You what?”

“I saw Chloe. She’s with the enemy. That’s why she hasn’t replied to any of our messages.”

Asim sighed. “I thought so. She probably can’t even see them, let alone respond. Communications between the armies are severed.”

“Yeah.”

“What do you want to do about it?” Asim asked.

“What can we do?” Gary replied. “Chloe’s smart. She can handle herself. All we can do is pray she’s okay, and make sure nothing hurts her if we meet her on the field.”

“I’m glad you said that. Let’s hope for the best,” Asim said.

“Yeah.”

Gary shook his head. New weight rested on his shoulders. For some reason, the more he climbed, the heavier he felt…

***

The army that returned to the gym was more triumphant than broken. While people were missing, the spirits were high. The Legion marched. Dozens of legionaires formed dozens of rows, like a wall of bronze and skin, and walking behind them were the witches and spear-wielders who survived the onslaught. Finally came the gymnasium monsters, who’d suffered the greatest casualties.

Riding at the very front was Boulderus Johnus, chin held high.

As they approached the gym, however, Gary noticed that not everyone shared their joy. The atmosphere was downcast. The beef boys running around were clearly fewer than before, while climbers walked with lowered eyes and small steps.

“Hey, they aren’t happy for us!” Goldie said.

“I don’t think that’s the case, Goldie,” Gary replied.

The reason became evident through whispers and envious gazes. Of the three missions, Gary’s was the only one which succeeded. The rest were hurt. Badly.

“Wasn’t Feng Shen leading one of the missions?” Gary asked.

“The Kamowaga defense, yeah,” Asim replied.

“And he lost?”

“Apparently.”

Gary felt more shocked than he expected. Two people approached then, and his spirits rose. “Alicia! Cog! You’re both okay!”

“Luckily. We were placed at the back,” Cog replied.

“What happened?”

“I don’t know… We were pushing them back, and then it just…”

Cog shook his head. He seemed shaken. Both of them did.

“Hey, we got one out of three. It’s going to be okay,” Gary said.

“Gary, it’s… The things they did… They were pouring tentacles into people’s eyes. Not monsters. People. Real, breathing people, and they were taking their bodies and using them to break the rest of us. They came out, and the battle just…poof,” Alicia said.

“Poof?”

“Yeah. All our hopes went poof.”

“Listen, we’ll get them. Whatever happened must have been a surprise, but we have so many clever people on board. You should have seen the Legion. They slaughtered the horrors and made it look easy. Whatever weapons they have, we can do better. And, if they’re scary… That’s just the tower,” Gary said. His eyes had darkened for that last part.

Alicia shook her head. “I think I need some sleep.”

“Me too,” Cog said.

“Rest well. We’ll catch up later.”

The two of them took off, leaving Gary alone with Asim. The Egyptian watched them. “Those things they described…”

“Yeah. It’s exactly what the chimera did to your summons.”

“Do you think…”

“They’re the puppeteer’s hidden weapons. They just didn’t have enough for our battlefield. If we faced more of those, in the real battle, things might have been different.”

“Yeah,” Asim said.

“I wasn’t lying, though. We really have smart people. Whatever shit they throw at us, we can work against it. At least we now have merit points.”

“How many did you get?” Asim asked.

“Four hundred and fifty.”

“Dude, really? I got a hundred.”

“A hundred and fifty of that came from my beef boys. You could hire some yourself.”

“I got enough to summons to organize. Fuck it. Let’s see what a hundred points can get me.”

They entered the gym. Gary got a new notification.

Mission complete! For your contribution to the mission objective, you have been awarded an extra 100 merit points.

“Now you got two hundred,” Gary said.

“What?” Asim asked.

“Oh. Nevermind.”

“You got a hundred points just now?”

“Uh, no.”

“Bullshit! I only got twenty!”

“Sucks to be you, I guess.”

Gary walked next to a grumbling Asim, hearing and seeing the beef boys and pro boxers training. They seemed fiercer than normal. Even the champ was taking it seriously, sparring a poor pro boxer in the central ring.

As he approached the merit board, he saw Boulderus Johnus rush into the coach’s office and close the door behind him.

“Let’s see,” Gary said. Five hundred and fifty merit points was more than he expected to get, and it unlocked a whole world of rewards. There was still a way to go, of course. His eyes landed on the Star-Forged Steel, shining with longing. At two thousand merit points, it was far out of his budget.

“But it shares my last name…” he muttered.

He then turned to more budget-friendly goals. The Star Grass remained at fifty points a blade, so he could buy eleven of them. If each gave one to two percent progress in the Starvein Manual’s first stage, eleven would get him close to twenty percent. He sighed. That wasn’t very much.

He also noticed another astral resource, the Moon Dirt, which sat at two hundred merit points. It hadn’t been there last time he checked.

“Gary Steel,” an aged voice pulled him out of his thoughts.

“Yes, Coach?” he asked, turning around respectfully.

The coach stopped a few paces away and inspected Gary. Everyone hurried to make space. “You’re not as bad as I thought. Walk with me,” he said.

Amidst stunned gazes, Gary followed the coach to an emptier side of the gym. Only beef boys were here, practicing on old instruments. Someone lifted a bar that had almost snapped by rot.

“I heard good things about you,” the coach said. “You located a dangerous monster, saving potentially dozens of our boys. You also killed a chimera.”

“I wasn’t alone in that, Coach.”

“But you were there. Osteo and Canc spoke well of you, too…”

“Can I ask you something, Coach?”

The coach gave him a side-glance holding tiredness, but none of the previous hostility. “Ask,” he said.

“That chimera monster… What was it?”

This took the coach’s face down. “Alas,” he said. “These monsters are the puppeteer’s most twisted creations. They can turn our captured or fallen boys into tentacle beasts, instantly. We didn’t know this. We thought they could only raise zombies after the battle.”

“It’s terrible,” Gary said.

“Your mission was the only one where they didn’t send chimeras—maybe one or two. The rest were flooded by them. We had no idea. We lost in the first round. It is my failure as the coach.”

“Don’t say that, Coach! You couldn’t have known.”

“Even so, it remains my failure. But enough of that. Your contributions during the battle and the opinions of those around you have convinced me you’re worth more than your level. I offer you the position of Field Boss.”

A screen appeared.

Promotion! Beef Boy→ Field Boss

A Field Boss may command up to ten climbers and as many mercenaries as you want. You receive a mini-map indicating the positions of your boys and live status updates about their condition, and in return, you get 10% of the merit points they gather. Climbers must be willing to join your squad. Field squads cannot change during a mission.

“This is, uh, a great honor?” Gary said.

“Of course it is! Why did you hesitate?”

“I was just reading the screen, Coach!”

“Hmph.” The coach snorted. “When I was young, we had no screens, only grit and sparring. Don’t disappoint me, Gary. I see a pro in you.”

“Thank you for your trust, Coach. I won’t let you down.”

Coach Bayonet nodded. “Train hard,” he said, walking away.

Gary watched him. “Excuse me, Coach!” he said.

“What?” the coach snapped back.

“I have a question about my training! Can you help me?”

“Hmph. That’s my job, you idiot. What is it?” the coach asked, returning closer.

“Well, I’m cultivating this body manual that requires astral resources. A blade of Star Grass gave me very little progress. I could use my points to buy a bunch of them now, or buy Moon Dirt, or save up to buy Star-Forged Steel. What do you think I should do?”

Halfway through the question, Gary had started feeling dumb. This was a boxing monster. What could he know about astral resources and ancient body manuals?

He was surprised when the coach nodded. “I see your problem. Which manual are you cultivating?”

“The Starvein Immortal Body Manual, Coach.”

“I am not well-versed in that, but I know enough about the first stage. You infuse astral aura into your bloodstream, yes?”

Gary felt like he’d won the lottery. “Coach!” he exclaimed in joy.

“I take that as a yes. Astral Grass is not a good resource for you—its power escapes easily, so you lose a lot in the absorption. It’s best used to power astral formations. Active Dirt is better, and definitely Star-Forged Steel. You’d need a lot of merit points to finish the first stage.”

“Can’t you give me a discount, Coach?”

“No, but I can give you advice. You seem like a competent boy. If I were you, I’d use my merit points on life-saving treasures. The climb is a marathon, not a sprint. Also, you should use all your aura stones to recruit more beef boys.”

“Really?” Gary asked.

“Yes. Merit points are much more precious than aura stones. If you command your boys well, they can multiply your investment, and it becomes easier as a field boss. Hiring many of them early is your best bet to eventually getting that Star-Forged Steel—that should really kickstart your body art. Just one chunk should be enough to finish the first stage.”

“Really? It’s that easy?”

“You’re almost at the third stage of your energy cultivation and only at the first of your body cultivation. What did you expect? Any more questions?”

“Uh, no, Coach. Thank you so much.”

The coach nodded. “Train hard,” he said, taking off—and Gary was sure he walked faster this time.

Gary clenched his fist. If I can get that steel… Let’s do this!

Returning to the board, he found it less crowded than before. The mercenary options stood proudly at the top, but he looked at the merit stone rewards first.

Life-saving treasures…

The coach made perfect sense, actually. Advancing his Starvein manual by twenty percent now would be far less useful than a literal life-saving treasure. As he scanned the rewards, however, he only found one such thing in his price range.

“Flying Fools card, 500 merit points…” he muttered. “That’s almost all I got.”

“Money’s to be spent, silly!” Goldie said.

“You’re right. This isn’t the place to be cheap.”

Five hundred merit points disappeared from his balance, and a playing card materialized in his hand. It wasn’t from any game he recognized, but the format was very similar to Yu-Gi-Oh and other such games. Its image showed three hobbits on an eagle with a fourth hanging from its leg. The description read: “Activate to fly away. One-time use.”

It wasn’t particularly descriptive, but this exact card had been the third place prize at the Heaven Reaching tournament. The announcer had said this could let him escape from even higher-realm enemies.

It was perfect.

Gary slung the card into his space ring, then considered his remaining fifty points. There wasn’t much he could buy with it. He could always hang on, but…

A handful of dirt appeared in his palm, shining blue with astral energy. It wasn’t mentioned in the manual, and its name—Active Dirt—wasn’t related either, so Gary had missed it originally. It was fifty points, the same price as Star Grass. He’d absorb it later.

Done with merit points, Gary returned to the mercenary list. He patted down his robes, finding six sacks. That was six thousand aura stones—almost everything he’d gotten from the Muscle Sap operation. He took a deep breath, steeling himself.

System, give me six beef boys, he commanded. His aura stones disappeared. Nothing else happened at first. Then, a slight tremor shook the ground, along with a few exclamations from outside. Gary left the gym to find a pack of beef boys sprinting into the grass field from the forest. The second he appeared, they locked onto him.

“Boss!” the first one yelled.

“Brothers!” Osteo yelled, also rushing to Gary’s side.

“Wait,” he said, realizing, but it was too late. Eight beef boys crashed into him from both directions, sandwiching him between their large, sweaty bodies. “No, blegh!” Gary said, trying to escape, but their joy was so much they couldn’t hear him. They gathered in a massive group hug with Gary at the center and started jumping up and down, saying, “Bat-tle! Bat-tle! Bat-tle!”

In one of those jumps, Gary slipped out between their feet. “Holy Jesus fuck,” he said. “You guys are intense.”

“Oi, boss! What are you doing over there?” a beef boy asked.

“I’m, uh, resting.”

“But we’re celebrating!”

“Yeah, I— Oh, what the heck?”

He joined their group hug and started hopping along. When they finally got tired, he sat them down. “These are Osteo and Canc, who will be nicknamed Beef One and Two,” he said. “The rest of you are Beef Three through Eight. I’m sure you have lovely names coming from not-so-lovely diseases, but it would just confuse us during the battle.”

“Yes, Boss!” they cheered.

“We’ll catch up later. Go train for now, and I’ll call for you. Osteo, Canc, show your new brothers the ropes.”

“Yes, Boss!” Osteo said with pride, and the two beef boys led their new comrades into the gym. Gary was left alone, except for the dozens of climbers staring at him.

“Mind your business, jeez,” he muttered, looking for somewhere empty to absorb his Moon Dirt.

“I like them. Can we keep them?” Goldie asked.

“We’ll see, Goldie. We’ll see.”
