Chapter 365

Eik stared through narrowed eyes as the meaning of the words slowly seeped into his brain. “You want to.. use me to make a grade 7 potion. Did I hear that correctly?”

“You did,” Guild Master Ganyak hir Gawak said with a nervous nod.

Eik looked to the granddaughter. “And you expect this crazy idea to work, Meri?”

She wrung her hands. Seventeen more alchemists from the Alchemist Guild of Gimleh were in the room, watching Meri and the guild master deliver the bad news to the most powerful man in the Nidafjeld Alliance, and most of them looked ready to run out of there the moment the opportunity showed itself.

“To be precise, we don’t think anything,” she said. “This is a few leagues beyond the frontier of alchemy. What we’re attempting here is something nobody in living memory has ever even tried to do. It’s more than likely that it’s just not possible. The only thing that brings some hope is that there have been potions suitable for every other rank before it, so reason suggests that there might as well be one for the seventh grade.”

“And what does it all have to do with me? Besides the fact that I would be the one making the potion in the end.”

The guild master stepped in to answer that one. “Let me first clarify that we have managed to create what we believe to be useful foundation for the recipe, but that what we’ve failed to find is the active material that would actually provide the empowering effect. That is what we had hoped the spawn would become.”

“I understand that.”

“As far as the reason why it didn’t work as we had hoped, that could just as well be a matter of luck as anything else. Sometimes, the ingredients are just not compatible.”

Eik nodded. “Even at lower ranks, you can’t just use whatever to make whatever. I know. Get to the point, please.”

“All right. You’re a Worldbreaker, right?”

“Yeah.”

“Well, we had a thought… Normally, Awakened like us can never be suitable ingredients in an alchemical recipe, just like we can’t be used in magical smithing or other such artisanal production. We just can’t. But, you’re not a normal Awakened, are you?”

Eik’s brows knitted together as he began to grasp what they were telling him. “You’re saying, because I’m technically not a normal Awakened, whatever might otherwise protect Awakened from being thrown in the metaphorical cauldron doesn’t recognize me in the same way? That, in this specific case, my body might be considered a monster because I'm not strictly considered a creature entirely contained within the system?”

“More or less, yes.”

“And… how would we test this?” Eik asked, already apprehensive of the answer.

“Well, that’s the less pleasant part of this experiment,” he admitted. “We would have to harvest parts of you to test one by one. Or rather, you would have to do it yourself, since I doubt we would be able to cut into you at all.”

At the thought alone, Eik felt sick. There was just something grisly about cutting into one’s own body.

But, really, there was nothing to think about. If this was the price to pay for that extra oomph, then he would have hung himself on a hook for slaughter for a thousand years.

“All right, I’m in. Let’s get started right away.”

Meri clapped her hands with excitement. “I was hoping you would say that!” she sang and ran back to a desk overflowing with documents and dried up pieces of ingredients. “Can you come over here please?”

Eik narrowed his eyes as he followed, the rest of the alchemists, Guild Master Ganyak hir Gawak included, trailing like hungry dogs. “Don’t tell me… Do you already have a plan for my dissection?”

Rather than confirm or deny that particular question, Meri flashed him a beaming smile and unrolled a length of parchment. On it were numerous sections of neatly handwritten notes, more than a dozen diagrams of various sizes and types, and lastly an incredibly concerning outline of a human body with lines pointing to various body parts.

“This is you!” she told him and stabbed a finger at the human outline. “We’ve been talking and we think it would probably be best to start with your calves.”

“Uuh… Why?” Eik questioned, taking a couple of involuntary steps back.

She frowned. “You really want to know?”

“Actually, no.”

***

It hurt.

There was no denying that. Whether one was an X-ranker or an F-ranker, getting your flesh cut into, sliced off, and removed hurt.

Maybe the pain wasn’t nearly as intense and unbearable as it might have been if he was an F-ranker, but the fact was that pain served a very useful purpose of helping you identify harmful conditions. It was probably good that he could still feel it.

As suggested by the team’s prepared plan of action, they had started with his calves, taking out both bone, muscle, and tendons, all to be put in stasis and tested for suitability in the grade 7 potions later. It was probably one of the most stomach-turning experiences Eik had ever had.

Lying down and having your leg taken apart, completely awake, just so it could be experimented on was horrible. Sure, he could heal and reconstruct the limb near instantly, but that didn’t take away from the terrible sensations as it happened.

After extracting anything that might even be remotely useful from his calves, they had first moved down to the ankle, where every individual bone structure had to be taken out one by one.

Skin and nails of the feet went first, before the internal parts were taken out.

Then it had been knees and thigh, the kneecap scooped off with a spoon-like instrument created from blue crystal. Eik must have been the first person in the history of the universe stupid enough to actually make the instruments used for his own extended, immortal torture session.

Ganyak hir Gawak was directing the precise movements of a Living Manifestation Raised to the Occasion as it removed muscle from Eik’s glutes, the powerful structure there hopefully able to help them advance in the creation of one of the grade 7 recipes.

They had been cutting into his without pause for a little over four hours.

“You know, Mr. Magnasen,” the old man began, nose mere centimeters from Eik’s butthole. Eik wished the geezer would stop talking and just get it over with, but he was so focused on his work that he didn’t seem to realize the discomfort he was causing. “I don’t think I’ve ever really expressed my gratitude for what you’ve done for us. And for me, personally, not to forget.”

“Nah, don’t mind it, mate. You’ve said thanks more than enough,” Eik told him through clenched teeth.

“No, I don’t think it will ever really be enough. I’m tempted to ask what I can do to repay your benevolence, but I also understand completely that I can never truly do anything that is anywhere close to equal.”

You could stop talking, is what Eik wanted to say, but he held his tongue. “It’s really okay. Just… be careful with the knife, please. Butt cutting wasn’t really on my bingo card this year, and it’s making me a little nervous, if I’m honest.”

“Oh, not to worry, Mr. Magnasen. I will only cause as much pain as I must in pursuit of our goal,” the alchemist assured him.

“Yeah, great…” Eik muttered as the edge of the knife struck bone with a grinding noise. He winced.

“Sorry about that one, Mr. Magnasen. I’m still getting used to harvesting humans.” Whether the guild master was trying to remove some of the tension from the situation by putting on a nonchalant facade, or was genuinely so desensitized to this kind of thing after multiple decades, it wasn’t working.

“Use a different word, please.”

“Slice? I’m not used to slicing into humans yet.”

“Just forget it…”

“Did you know that before you helped me regain my strength and freedom, both my dexterity and eyesight had both become so terrible that I was certain I would never have the joy of doing something like this again. Well, your summon is doing the actual cutting because your flesh is too tough for me, but still. You’ve relighted a flame in me that had been extinguished.”

Jaw tight, Eik answered. “Glad I could… help.”

“Even the pain that had become a constant companion in my life, and prevented me from doing much of anything, has more or less vanished, and it is all thanks to you, Mr. Magnasen. Thank you, from my heart, for accepting me into your divine fold, and for the pain-suppressing mist you still provide.”

Eik chuckled. “All right, all right. I know that my title is the Supreme Divinity of Toxin and all that, but you make me sound like some kind of—”

When Eik suddenly fell silent, Ganyak hir Gawak paused his work and asked what was going on. Maybe something had stung. When no response came, he pushed himself out of his chair and bent down to look at Eik’s face.

The X-ranker’s jaw hung gaping, his eyes bulging as he stared hard into the floor. Eventually, he mouthed something wordlessly under his breath. “Mr. Magnasen? What happened? Should we take a little break? Our schedule does permit.”

“I am… an idiot,” Eik whispered into the floor. In an abrupt burst of motion, he flew up from the operating table he had been lying on, flipped thrice in the air, and landed perfectly and lightly on his feet, all while the crystal knife was still stuck in his ass cheek. With a groan, he buried his face in his hands and shrieked as if into a pillow. “I’m so fucking stupid!”

Taken aback, the guild master and the other alchemists in the room watched silently as the hero of Unified Mass howled with his ass on display.

“Pain-suppressing mist! I’ve had it all along, for fuck’s sake! I’ve been wincing on this table for hours and I never even had to!” Then he grew quiet for a few seconds, face still in his hands, before suddenly inhaling with force, hazy, blue substance escaping from between his fingers. A sigh of relief followed.

“All right, I’m ready to continue. Slice, dice, do whatever you want to me, baby. I’m impervious to pain!” he sang in celebration and hopped back onto the table. He was in a considerably better mood after that.

Another three hours later, they broke to get something to eat. Eik took all of the alchemists back to Forest to eat at his father’s restaurant. Both Meri and the guild master had gone a couple of times before, but the rest of them had never had food from Earth before.

“What is this?” one of them leaned over the table to ask Eik.

“Gravy,” he mumbled through a mouthful.

“I think I love gravy, then.”

Eik laughed. “I believe you’ve just had the most human experience there is. Congratulations.”

“What is it made of? And can I get some to go? I’d love to get an excuse to take my husband and kids here later.”

Eik pursed his lips. “We have a native animal called a cow. The main ingredients is the thing that comes out of their tits. And I’ll get my dad to fix you up a whole bag for free. Don’t even worry about it.”

“I’d like one as well,” another piped up, closely followed by a third.

"The benevolent father shall grant upon every man and woman the medicine of life that is gravy!” Eik declared with his arms spread wide. Cheers erupted at the proclamation.

“Thank you, Mr. Magnasen. I believe your role is over, at least for the time being. I promise to treat your organs with the utmost respect. If we f—No, when we get results, we will contact you immediately.”

Eik grinned and slapped the old man on the shoulder. “Show me what the guild master of the Alchemist Guild of Gimleh is capable of.”

“I am more than that,” Ganyak hir Gawak said with a smile. “I am the holder of the divine skill, Aggressive Countermeasure, which summons Profound Toxin to quell the hostile outbursts of powerful, alchemical ingredients. And, finally, I am the Alchemical Advisor to the Supreme Divinity of Toxin.”
