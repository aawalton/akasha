Chapter 118: Losing One's Navel

Who in the hell was that? Is he really just a general?

When Jack finally could tear his eyes away, he saw that someone else had noticed his distress — Délight. She caught his eyes with concern in hers and mouthed without sound, ‘Are you okay?’

He immediately waved it off, shaking his head and avoiding her gaze, steeling his own expression by force of will.

And then he saw that Stitcher was walking by them.

By then, everyone’s salute had been wavering. As they became aware of the famous Non there, the two groups stiffened again and threw up salutes. Jack was definitely a hair behind them, but did it anyway. He could feel Délight’s eyes. She was watching his every little move now. Great.

Stitcher was walking between them with her hands behind her back, and she slowed quickly, beginning to inspect them, with the faint air of indulgence. “At ease, cadets.” Once everyone had ‘relaxed’ — standing with arms clasped behind them — she paused dead between them all, and did a few little turns, as if she were noting things among them, and nodding approval. “It seems you’re ready after all, then, hmm? For the battle.”

“Yes, ma’am, Agent Nonpareil!” Highfive said immediately, loudly, and proudly, his eyes kept firmly forward.

Everyone else was saying it immediately after. Once again, Jack was the slacker here, being more out of military practice. He’d practically forgotten this element of being ‘them,’ things being generally so informal. It might’ve been like riding a bike, but he was still rusty.

Stitcher stopped in front of Highfive, looking waaay up. “Well, well, aren’t you an oversized specimen, then? I look forward to seeing you in action. I shouldn’t easily miss you in observation, hmm?”

“No, ma’am, Agent Nonpareil!” Highfive reported, keeping composure with a hint of both awe and humor.

Stitcher also betrayed a touch of humor. “No, indeed,” she said as she turned around and found herself in front of Délight. Her back was to Jack, now, but she was obviously inspecting the girl closely. “My dear, I’ve never seen a Charmer achieve what you have in combat operations, not in all my years. Bravo.”

“Thank you, ma’am, Agent Nonpareil!” Délight chirped loudly, and it seemed she was having trouble keeping her composure, likely because the words had struck her emotionally.

“Yes, yes, keep it up, hmm?” Stitcher was already turning away, apparently done with the lot of them.

Jack suddenly recalled that he technically had Mini-Mem access direct to Stitcher. Despite the fact that he should probably know better, he could not resist asking. <Apologies, Doctor, but may I ask who that other guy was? With the mask.>

Stitcher, walking away, paused dead and spun around, staring right at him. “No, you may not, Cadet.” Her eyes immediately slid away casually despite the firm, almost parental finality of the words. “But I suppose you should feel honored, nonetheless.”

The silence was deafening in the embarrassing aural fallout that left, as the other side in particular held their breath and struggled not to bug their eyes out at Doctor Stitcher talking so casually to him.

Jack’s mouth was open a while before words came out. “Y-yes, ma’am, Agent Nonpareil.”

Stitcher nodded vaguely, half-turned, then said, “Don’t forget you are authorized to check out that location sometime. You know the one. Hmm?”

“Yes, ma’am. Agent Nonpareil. Thank you.”

She nodded again as she turned and walked off toward the stairs.

The enemy team took the opportunity to stare at him as if a new creature had suddenly spawned in the room. Defiant was studying him especially judiciously.

Just then, a mem-text notice with team tags flashed in his head. <Your team-exclusive facilities are now unlocked! Please proceed to Door 1 to the right of the front desk for your final preparations before entering the arena. If you have not already done so, register your designated leader for the match. You have roughly 13 minutes. Good luck!>

His team wasted no time in rushing toward the door, so Jack followed suit in running after them. In the team gestalt, he said simply, “Sorry, guys.”

“Nah, bro, it’s cool,” Highfive replied, as he was the first to the door and opened it for everyone else, nodding to them firmly, and grinning finally at Jack. “The look on their faces was worth it!”

“It’s in our favor,” Inkblot said. “Ferronaut is already in their heads, now.”

“I agree,” Bo added. “But, Jack, that other shit isn’t still messing with you, is it? That Central spook walking by. The spookiest of the spooky, probably here just to sneer at us ingrates.”

“I’m good,” Jack said assuringly. He was deeply curious to hear something, anything more on that guy, but at the moment, it was a useless distraction. “To hell with him. We got our own stuff to focus on. I’m rarin’ to go, Dark Pudding!”

Immediately behind the door was a long, curling hallway, going up a gentle slope. As Highfive came through the door and closed it, Jack clapped his hands together and called, “Hell yeah, team! Let’s do this!”

Highfive grinned and made a fist. “Hell yeah! Time to kick their snooty asses!” He cleared his throat and gestured ahead. “But, uh, keep moving, Jack.”

“Right.”

Bo was already walking ahead, flashing a glare Jack’s way. “What is with you boys giving me those weird names?”

“Uh…” Jack scratched the side of his head, soon shrugging as he jogged to catch up. “I dunno. It just comes out. Should we stop?”

Bo scoffed, rolled her eyes, and turned back ahead.

Jack turned around briefly to exchange a glance with Highfive, and they both ended up grinning in silent understanding.

It wasn’t a no. Does she secretly find it cute? Best step lightly, though. If we accuse her, she’ll ban it in a heartbeat rather than admit it. We’ll keep this silent agreement going.

The room they found themselves in beyond the hallway was a pared-down breakroom, seemingly made more for function than comfort. The seating was utilitarian, and there was only a drink machine, as opposed to any snack machines. There was also a door labeled ‘Gear Room’ to the side.

Most prominently, there was an upraised circular platform in the middle that they were vaguely familiar with, which had all of their sequestered gear already on top of it. From information on their scenario setup and the way it was outlined to them, this platform would rise up into the simulation room at just the right time to perfectly lock in just as the seven-minute timer began. It would place them directly in a room in the fortress, though sadly it was one in a corner, which they thus would not use as their hidey hole chamber because they wanted it as centralized as possible. In any case, every drill had started in that room, so they’d be ready for it.

Just as Jack was thinking to start gearing up, Bo stopped dead and took a few steps off to the side, in a way that made it clear she was getting a message.

Something about the look on her face made Jack stop, too, hesitating, waiting, though her back was turned.

Highfive, who’d caught on a little late and had moved to the platform, called, “What is it?”

When Bo turned around, her face was angrier than he’d ever seen. Red. Eyes filled with murder. She took a breath, eyes flickering to each of them. When she spoke, it was with teeth clenched, like she was holding in a scream. “Délight got to Quillote. They know what Ten’s team does. Ten just found out and contacted me.”

Dead silence persisted in the wake of this. Jack walked off to the side, thinking.

What do they know, then? About Bo’s Sandy. My trickery with the decoy. Inkblot’s stealth capabilities and the anatomical strike on Abundance. How handily we won. Possibly all exaggerated in Quillote’s mind. Maybe that loss got to him more than he let on. A weakness for Délight to exploit. Damn it. What does it mean, then? More caution. They’ll begin just as every sim did, at least. Poking and prodding, completely paranoid.

The feeling of betrayal stabbed at him, which annoyed him at the same time. This was all expected shit. But what was the timing? Before or after Délight came to ‘soften him up?’ It cast everything about her confession and such into doubt. Maybe it was cultivated to make them underestimate her. Assume she’d failed for once in intel gathering, for example. Perhaps she’d asked Quillote not to spill the leak to his team, and counted on that holding up for at least the day.

Damn you, Délight. It’s just always like this with you, isn’t it?

Jack turned around and put his hands on his hips as he looked at the others. Bo was breathing hard and eyeing Jack with a mingled expression of vulnerable anticipation and something that could only be called sourness, as if she had moved from wanting to punch the wall to wanting to puke. Highfive had both hands on his head, slowly blinking up at the ceiling.

Inkblot was frowning in disappointment, shaking his head. “Worthless narc,” he muttered balefully. “Though maybe it’s all bullshit. Maybe Ten had it leaked deliberately.”

Bo shook her head slightly. “Why tell us now, then? Why at all?”

“A convenient time to terrorize us, upon Délight’s request.”

“Ten wouldn’t do it.”

“She’s your buddy that you know so well, suddenly? It’s beneficial to make it look unintentional on her part. But alright, fine. Maybe it was Corroline. She sucked up her spite for Délight to get revenge on Jack, whom she hates a little more at the moment. She conspired to leak information through Quillote, somehow, or simply convinced him to take the blame for her.”

Bo didn’t respond, just glared at Inkblot, retaining that sour look on her face.

Jack held up his hands. “Guys, forget the blame game. It’s irrelevant. Actually, even the info is irrelevant, because what they know won’t save them.” Jack approached the platform and gestured at the gear. “If we’re gonna agonize about what changes, though, agonize while getting geared up.” He suited his words by doing so, getting what he was carrying on belts and harnesses on his person, checking his backpack, and otherwise preparing both physically and mentally.

It was both practical and emotional, as the distraction was needed to break their anxieties up.

Highfive was the first to comply, watching Jack begin for merely a moment before he started nodding to himself and following suit. Bo wasn’t too far behind, quietly going through the motions with a perturbed look still on her face.

Inkblot, more so initially de-geared, taking his uniform off for the sake of more efficient invisibility, leaving only his customary short tights and belt with a knife sheath and the backup knife sheath, long ways on the back of the belt. After that, though, he loaded up with gear that he’d be moving once the platform rose. In addition, he put on a minimalist but fully enclosing visor, which he temporarily pushed up onto his forehead.

Now that they weren’t so consumed in dread and intrigue, Jack said, “They’ll likely be more cautious with however Quillote framed us. Me as the super-genius, hyper-mega-mastermind, no doubt. A real shame that the truth got out.”

Bo scoffed as she checked her handgun’s magazine, soon sliding it back in with a snap. “You’re a legend in your own mind, at least.”

Jack gave her a big, dumb, leading kind of grin, and she finally sniffed and returned a little of her own, shaking her head.

“Bo’s Sandy is leaked,” Highfive said soberly. “Sucks ass, bro. They likely trained against it after hearing it described.”

Jack resisted a wince. That was almost certainly true. But he said, “Well, it’s a shame they probably didn’t train on all the new shit they have to get through, before they can even come and get a Sandy in the face, in close quarters within the fortress, right before we hammer them full force.”

Highfive nodded reluctantly as he put on the foam sprayer reserve backpack and prepped the ‘gun’ he’d use. “We can only hope it plays out that way, my dude.”

“It won’t decimate them,” Inblot said, “but it doesn’t need to in this scenario. They can’t bait it out until they expose themselves inside the fortress.”

Everyone silently geared up for a while longer.

Bo seemed to have been deliberating in her head, and this was reinvigorating her confidence. She sighed. “Damn that scum-shit bitch Délight for ruining our cordial relations… at least Abundance had nothing to do with this.”

Inkblot looked as if he wanted to say something, but he wisely held his tongue this time around.

“Guess it’s true, what they say,” Highfive offered. “There’s only teams, and other teams, in PACC season.”

Bo grunted as she put her helmet on. “Yeah, I almost forgot, Fiver.” Typical sarcasm. “Please anoint us with more of your wise sayings.”

Bo is practically back to normal.

Highfive grinned, big and exultant, as if he’d been re-summoned into the world. With sacred cadence, he declared, “The only man completely at peace is the man without a navel.”

Bo let out the most exhausted and disappointed combination groan-and-sigh possible. “Holy goddess, man…”

“When you've got a wolf by the ears, it’s sometimes easier to hang on than it is to let go.”

Bo glowered at him dangerously. It was a good thing her gun was holstered right then. “If you drop another of those, I swear I’ll paralyze you and have Ink stab you in the face.”

Inkblot, who was kneeling and dumping some metal bits into a backpack, paused to look up at Highfive. Brows rising subtly, he said with insistent conviction, “I’ll do it.”

This made Jack, Highfive, and even Bo burst into brief laughter.

Jack peered over at Highfive as he put his helmet on. “Blondy, you have at least one very prominent family member from a very rural place, yeah? The source of such wisdom?”

Highfive widened his eyes and put a finger up to his lips.

Bo glanced between them. “You two hicks can keep each other’s secret. We’ll never know.”

“I always say,” Inkblot declared idly, “I know nothing and can’t say much about anyone or anything.”

“Exactly.”

“Exactly,” Jack and Highfive said simultaneously.

Jack glanced at his teammates and realized… they were good. They’d be fine. Whatever bullshit Délight’s team did, and whatever they knew, it remained simply a matter of what Team Highlife had to do, and they were going to do it confidently on the backend of rigorous training, drilling, and strategic planning. A thing they were already proud of. Was any simulation the enemy team ran going to represent them doing something as ludicrous as soundproofing and sealing a room, distributing cameras, laying copious networks of traps, in a seven-minute window? Hell no. At most, a sim might have them hiding in the dungeons, not bursting out from a wall.

Nothing was going to change, and whatever happened, they’d be ready to adjust. The enemy had to come to them.

All too soon, they were loaded up and ready. This time around, it actually wasn’t a big deal that stuff was still sitting in the middle of the platform. They’d been instructed that anything on it would get brought up and stay. Just another benefit to being a fortress defender — something they’d once thought of as a curse, but had managed to turn into the opposite.

They had minutes to spare, and they ticked down with painful slowness, as everyone anticipated the rise. Showtime.

“You two have the right faceplate, right?” Jack asked. “Discarded the decoy?” Highfive and Bo nodded confirmation.

It was worth asking, just because of Highfive’s absentmindedness, especially considering he wasn’t wearing it yet, opting for a chemical mask while he worked. It had specially treated lenses in addition to the heavy air filter — the latter was dubious as relevant for a Non’s health, but a ‘might as well’ sort of thing. The lenses were the big thing, as the foam wouldn’t stick to them, unlike pretty much everything else in existence. He’d had to work to get the shit off his faceplate, as simply pouring the dissolver solution on the specialty piece of tech was not a good idea.

Jack’s faceplate was already split down the middle with the color voids in each eye. Blue on one side, red on the other. It was weird only when he focused on it, as practicing for the last day or so with it had adjusted him to it. A proper color spectrum was not particularly required to get today’s job done.

“Alright, convert to all gestalt talk from here, team,” Bo said, and everyone nodded agreement. No one wanted even the slightest sliver of a chance of giving Chime a better bead on where they’d be. Bo had rightly pointed out them slipping too much during training.

Nothing more was said, verbally or in gestalt. The minutes passed in silence until there was a chime and a countdown ticking in their head, and the platform began to rise rapidly up toward an open ceiling.

It took them up through the small dungeon area of the fortress, into a bare stone room, light filtering vaguely through an open door.

This is it. We’re inside the real deal. Our sweet, beautiful fortress.

As soon as the circle of the floor locked into place, another chime went off in their head. <Your team leader is registered as [Highfive]. The enemy leader is registered as [Defiant]. You now have exactly seven minutes until the match start! Go!>

But they were already moving, knowing that every second counted. In Jack’s case, he already had his memorite channeled and around him, staunchly ready, held just closely and thickly enough to be visible around him as a glittering aura.

The first act was a collaborative effort moving metal panels into the room they’d use, so that Jack could make the first, wall-facing layer of metal for the inside, before the foam was sprayed. This was timed in such a way that once Jack began the final smoothing process to link all the panels, Bo and Inkblot could move out to deal with the separate alcove that Inkblot would use. Then, Jack moved on to help and finish the alcove, as Highfive was already beginning his foam work in the main room. Nothing more was needed in there for now.

With that done, everyone rushed to begin their other varied tasks as Highfive was consumed with the soundproofing. Jack checked the clock. They were making good time. Just another drill, just another drill…
