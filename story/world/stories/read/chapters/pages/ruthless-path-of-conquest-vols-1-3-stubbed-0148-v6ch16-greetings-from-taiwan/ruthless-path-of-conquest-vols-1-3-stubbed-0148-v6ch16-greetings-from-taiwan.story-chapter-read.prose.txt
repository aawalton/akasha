“Depends on who’s asking,” James replied, barely mustering an imitation of a rogueish grin.

He could swear, in addition to the Skills for controlling other people, the System might have deactivated his Skills for controlling his own emotions, too. His heart was pounding despite his smile, and he felt a trace of sweat on the back of his neck.

Fuck me, he thought. I kind of figured you might be here, but I wasn’t prepared to see you so suddenly like this…

The face of the man who confronted James was unforgettable, almost too pretty to be that of a man. It was the sort of visage that men envied—when they weren’t mocking it as slightly effeminate—and women fell for effortlessly. He had flawless skin and long, flowing hair that reminded one of the hero of some Asian period drama. Yet the Ruler was not some delicate twig of a man. He certainly wasn’t heavily built, but he had noticeable muscle on his frame. He gave off the presence of one who was familiar with the instrumental use of violence.

And, of course, James knew well how capable the other man was of dealing death.

This was the man who, in one of Sister Strange’s visions of the future, had decapitated James with a stroke of his sword. It was hard to look at this Taiwanese Ruler normally, like the deadly man was just another dude—rather than James’s possible executioner.

Fortunately for James, the other man seemed to be distracted from James’s nervousness by the fellow on stage, who was beginning to speak.

“I am called Zhang Chāo,” the man in front of James replied. Then Chāo muttered, under his breath, “A pompous windbag.”

Zhang Chāo’s expression was tense as he spoke, his eyes locked on the Chinese figure on the stage, the conversation that Chāo had begun with James almost forgotten in a fraction of a second.

“My fellow humans, in this time of turbulence, we must band together…” the man on stage had begun.

“Pompous windbag, huh?” James said, raising an eyebrow.

“Oh, um, not you!” said Zhang Chāo, returning to the conversation with James after his moment of distraction. He shook his head, smiled ruefully, and pointed at the man on stage with an annoyed, dismissive gesture. “It’s just that I am familiar with him. He’s from my corner of the world. Something of a dangerous rival.”

“You’re both Chinese,” James said, nodding to himself. “It makes sense you’d be butting heads with your neighbors. I’ve had my own share of disputes.”

His heart rate was beginning to calm down as he spoke to Zhang Chāo, who didn’t seem to bear any hostility to James at all. Indeed, all Zhang Chāo’s ire appeared to be directed at the other man, the one who had taken the initiative and swept onto the stage.

This is my opportunity to cultivate Zhang Chāo as a friend, James told himself. That will make the decapitation less likely.

From the context of the vision Sister Strange had shown him—he distinctly recalled the event taking place in an arena—James’s possible death was likely to occur during the upcoming Victors Tournament. A short period in which to win a reliable ally.

“Oh, it’s not just that we’re neighbors,” said Zhang Chāo, wrinkling his nose. “We are somewhat close geographically, but… a mere territorial dispute? No… are you listening to what he’s saying?”

James turned his head back toward the speaker on the stage. His brain had caught the entirety of what the speaker said, and now he reconstructed the first couple of sentences and the present continuation into a stream of speech.

“My name is Pú Tiě Shān, and I want to call everyone’s attention to how the System’s appearance has affected the Earth’s population. I know this is a time that has been given to us to plan for the future, but as I often like to say, you cannot know how to go forward without considering where you’ve come from. I invite you to look back and consider how you have struggled these past two months.”

“He certainly does like the sound of his own voice,” James said quietly, conscious of the fact that the speaker might hear him. Most of those in the room besides the man who had taken the stage were staying silent or at least relatively quiet out of respect for the speaker.

Despite the negative spin James placed on the speaker’s actions, he admired the fact that Pú Tiě Shān had taken the stage so quickly, before anyone else.

His initiative paid off for him, James thought. No matter what else is said during this Summit, people will remember this Pú Tiě Shān guy. Going first by itself has a powerful effect.

Zhang Chāo nodded and gave James a small smile.

“Keep listening,” Zhang Chāo said. “From what I’ve heard about you, you’ll disagree with him as I do. Bear in mind that at the end of this speech, someone must get up there and rebut him.”

What you’ve heard about me? James thought. Do I have an international reputation that stretches all the way to the other side of the Earth? And what do you mean, ‘someone’ must get up there and rebut him? If you know that you disagree with him, isn’t that your job?

But James simply nodded and listened to the speaker as the other man had suggested. James would raise these questions later, if he had the chance.

“The System initiated a cruel challenge for humanity,” Pú Tiě Shān continued. “In a world in which the governments were run by men with soft hands, and social control was dependent on a technological infrastructure that suddenly stopped working, it was only natural that the weakly enforced order would collapse. The best of the intelligence my people have gathered indicates that nearly every one of our countries descended into civil disorder following the System’s initiation—excepting the Kingdom of Eswatini. Congratulations, Your Highness.” He nodded to an African man standing among the other humans somewhat near the stage. “Now that the rest of us have restored a semblance of order in our respective corners of the world, it behooves us to reflect on the fragility of the previous systems of governance, which collapsed so readily in the face of a relatively small and brief challenge.

“Consider that this could happen again.”

There was a loud murmur of discontent in the room—including, James was pleased to observe, a number of people confidently asserting that they would not allow the chaos of the System’s descent to happen again—until Pú Tiě Shān silenced the hubbub by speaking over it.

“I am here today to propose a solution. Simply put: authority. Centralized, unified authority. A return to authoritative government. Many of us have explicitly sought it to address the problems of the day, but individually, no Ruler has sufficient power. If we unite our resources together, however, we can beat back chaos worldwide, destroy the monster populations that plague our planet—” There was a displeased stir from some of the nonhumans in the audience at this, though Pú Tiě Shān did not acknowledge it—“and subjugate the unruly populace, bending them toward the reconstruction of civilization. A better version of what we once had.”

Authoritative government? James thought. Or authoritarian government?

Even after losing the Sino-American War, the Chinese Communist Party had never weakened its grip on the population. If anything, they had cracked down ever harder on dissent as their external markers of authority diminished.

He frowned and shook his head. Subjugating the population sounded like a return of the bad old days of dictatorships from the Twentieth Century—and very consistent with the recent history of China in particular. It was exactly what James had been scrupulously trying not to do. He felt that he had struck a decent balance between liberty and security for the most part, and his population did not hate him as a result. They probably feared him a bit, as all Rulers were likely feared to some degree by their peoples. But he suspected that Pú Tiě Shān had a different balance in mind.

“What about freedom?” called one Ruler on the other side of the room. “This is the freest—”

But Pú Tiě Shān spoke over the other man, his voice seemingly projected at a higher volume than the critic’s. Whether that was a Skill, a magical feature of the System’s stage, or simply his natural speaking capabilities, James couldn’t tell.

Nevertheless, the would-be heckler’s next words were drowned out by Pú Tiě Shān’s surprisingly passionate rejoinder.

“Don’t lie to yourselves! You have cried out for it in your secret hearts, but you were too timid to acknowledge what it was you were crying out for. Humanity does not want, need, or deserve liberation. Liberty is mainly a burden to humans. They need our control! People want stability, prosperity, healthy children, a good harvest, a secure retirement… and to be told what to do. This is an elite gathering, where only the strongest are in attendance, along with those we will need to fight, the invasive species that have suddenly appeared in our world. Surely, liberty must take a back seat to unity! We must use this opportunity to form a global governance organization. Specifics about how leaders within that organization are appointed are of little importance to me, but it is critical that we be capable of standing together against those who are not our kind, as well as against agents of disunity and dissent.”

The gathered humans mostly grumbled a bit at this, but as Pú Tiě Shān stepped from the stage, more than a dozen Rulers gathered around him, including that African king he had specifically called out. The number of this suddenly developing faction was swollen by all their hangers-on, though James was relieved to note that most of the people around Pú Tiě Shān were Chinese. Perhaps he had brought them with him, and they were preexisting allies.

Surely, this brief, authoritarian-sounding speech could not have won over many new supporters.

“Food for thought, at least,” James said, forcing a tight smile onto his face as he turned back to Zhang Chāo.

“I know you don’t mean that,” Zhang Chāo replied, frowning, eyes still following Pú Tiě Shān and his cohorts.

“You seem to know a lot more about me than I do about you,” James said. “You knew my Job name, although it’s a little out of date. You knew what my stance would be on Pú Tiě Shān’s little anti-freedom speech… Maybe that was just easy to guess, since I’m an American. I don’t know anything besides your name, Zhang Chāo.”

The Taiwanese man flushed slightly.

“That’s right,” he said. “Where are my manners? I should at least introduce my friends.” Zhang Chāo gestured to the group accompanying him, consisting of four men and one woman. “This is Han Jianguo, my strong right hand…”

The other names passed in and out of James’s mind, not sticking as they normally would. The longer this interaction went on, the more certain he felt that the System had actually nullified almost all of his Skills in this place, rather than just the ones that would allow him to mind control other people. His control over his own emotions was basically gone, his memory was slipping, and who knew what else would be inactive?

At least Universal Language Comprehension is still working, he grumbled internally, or this situation would be very fucking awkward. But if I got up and spoke… would I be persuasive?

“So, now you know us a bit…” Zhang Chāo said. His voice trailed off expectantly, and James realized that now he was the one being socially inept.

He quickly introduced his wife and the rest of his companions, and the Taiwanese party looked impressed by James’s faction. It didn’t take long to figure out why.

“Your mother’s a Ruler, too,” Zhang Chāo remarked. “I haven’t heard of two members of the same family both holding Ruler Titles before. And no one had told me about it, either, until now. You must both be very talented.”

“What a well mannered young man,” Zora said in a sweet, gentle voice.

“And you also persuaded several nonhuman Rulers to join your Fisher Kingdom,” Zhang Chāo added. “I don’t even know how you managed to get along with them without killing each other in the first place. To be honest, that is the reason I’ve heard of you. Out of all the countries to have emerged in the world since the System appeared, yours is the only one I’m aware of that is ruled by humans but treats nonhumans as citizens.”

“Really?” James asked, raising an eyebrow.

Why would that be? Monsters are so powerful, there are obvious benefits to including them…

“Look around,” Zhang Chāo said. “There are hardly any human Rulers relative to nonhumans. Even most of those of human origins who are here have used Race Evolution to become some other Race. There are thousands of monster Rulers—” He looked at the monsters all around James and added, “I mean no offense by using that term; it’s just how we make sense of nonhumans suddenly living among us who we do not fully understand.”

“I never considered that it could be offensive,” said the Panther Queen, with a note of amusement in her tone.

“Well, in any case, in most territories, humans have been exterminated or subjugated by nonhumans,” Zhang Chāo continued, looking directly at the Panther Queen as he spoke now. “Many of our kind are suspicious of your types, and in territories where we have sufficient strength to rule ourselves, the usual reaction to our previous experiences has been to eliminate monsters as thoroughly as possible.”

That makes more sense, now that he mentions it, James thought. I didn’t put together just how badly humanity is doing. We’re being subjugated, huh? A lot of people flunked Orientation or died, I guess. And we’re certainly wildly outnumbered, especially if the number of insect monsters is anything like the number of insects pre-System.

“This makes sense to me,” the Panther Queen said, nodding from James’s side. “The Fisher Emperor is the strange one.”

“That’s what I thought,” said Zhang Chāo, smiling. “When I first heard that there was a country where nonhumans and humans were on friendlier terms, I wondered if I was doing something wrong in administering my own Kingdom. I went out and tried to negotiate with the nearest group of monsters, to see what the result would be. Then they tried to kill me, and I ended up acquiring the Xenocide Title.” He turned back to James. “There might be a couple of other Rulers who are doing the same as you, but I think it must be a fairly rare exception to the rule. At least, not enough of them for anyone else to have come to my attention. Also… Fisher Emperor?”
