Nothing happened.

I tried activating Threads of Fate again, but still nothing.

Pirhani tilted her head to the side. "I'm waiting. You said 'we'll see' so ominously. I was expecting you to at least try something."

After all that. After everything I'd suffered to advance to Emerald. I'd essentially come back from the dead to have the slimmest chance at… something.

And the skill was a dud.

"No? Very well then. Let's move this little shindig along, shall we?"

My fingers were shaking. Heart thundering in my chest.

Pirhani walked closer, making a show of it. No one made a sound or movement aside from the rumblings of the Unchained crushing benches as the lumbered about the stands beyond. The Reapers under her oath lounged among them, smug and satisfied.

"You know, Valciferous. I really wish it were you standing down here instead of this one. There's still time. You could take the oath like I did. Settle this like gods?"

Pirhani was only a few feet away know.

I closed my eyes.

"I would never bind my Will to Penumbra like you, Pirhani," Valciferous replied. "That's worse than sin."

"Sin? Hah! Sin? Valciferous you always did have a sense of humor if nothing else."

Pirhani continued speaking, egging Valciferous on, but I didn't hear it. Suddenly lost in my own thoughts.

Will? Valciferous just implied Pirhani had a Will. But I'd examined the gods before with my Second Sight. There'd been nothing.

I opened one eye.

My right eye.

The one those skull beings had contaminated with black sludge all that time ago. The one that was black, instead of white, an iris now gleaming with flecks of Emerald.

The world blossomed into new color.

Everything was darker, as usual when I used Second Sight, as if a dark cloud cast a shadow upon all. But The Wills of all the Reapers and Sins in the colosseum glowed brilliantly, piecing the shadow. So many colors. Orange, purple, green, blue, the higher colors of the more advanced team Lancelot brought to aid.

I examined Pirhani. Still no sign of a Will.

But there was something else.

A golden line stretching from her chest down into the ground.

A thread.

Threads of Fate.

I almost laughed. The skill wasn't a dud. It was passive! An upgrade to my Second Sight.

I now noticed additional threads throughout the arena. The other Reapers each had one, not golden, but the color of their Will descending into the ground like Pirhani. The Unchained Sins had threads too, but theirs were gray. And they didn't go down. Instead, they were strung along horizontally, connecting with Prihani's chest. Whatever the puppet master had done must have tied them to her, which is how she was able to control them. Similarly, the Reapers bound to Pirhani didn't have threads connecting them Penumbra, but to Pirhani instead.

I looked down at myself. My own string didn't go into the ground, but trailed up to the balcony above, straight into Valciferous' chest.

Jericho and Calliope stood beside the god. My heart sank as I saw their threads. Not bound to Penumbra like they should've been. But bound to Valciferous like mine.

They'd sworn an oath to him.

Fuck. No… How could they? After all the arguing they'd done over his oath. Why would they do this to themselves? I couldn't see the floor around them, but I had no doubt their shadows were gone like mine… Trapped in Penumbra, cut off from Vitria, from their real lives. And for what? For me?

I didn't deserve it.

Valciferous that bastard. He'd coerced them into this. Somehow. I was going to—

That's when I noticed something different about Valciferous.

His thread didn't go down like everyone else's. Like Pirhani's. It went up, vanishing into the sky like the beacons of light from the pillars.

He'd said Pirhani was bound to Penumbra. She was a god, true. Invincible, sure. But did that mean she now had to follow other rules of Penumbra like the Reapers?

And what if her thread was severed?

I took a step toward the goddess. My body didn't move. Instead, a shadow of myself stepped forward as I'd intended to. It looked back at me and winked.

I shuddered, then commanded it to continue.

Pirhani didn't seem to notice its approach. No one did.

The shadow Zephyr plucked on the string between Pirhani and the ground. It reverberated, letting out a low thrum like the string from a bass guitar, resonating in my mind, making my thoughts and vision fuzzy.

Something flashed before my eyes briefly like a digital communication trying to break through interference. A glitch focused entirely around Pirhani. She didn't seem to notice though.

He continued testing Pirhani's thread like a musician tuning his instrument. Each time he touched it, the glitch crackled into existence. I focused on the image forming around the goddess, it was like she was being replaced in those moments with another person.

A tall woman, jet black hair and olive skin. She wore excessive amounts of expensive-looking jewelry. But it was gone an instant later.

The shadow version of me covered his mouth playfully, then plucked the string several more times to the tune of 'Happy Birthday.'

I plastered a palm to my forehead.

The glitch flashed with each note, rapidly appearing and disappearing. She moved the same way Pirhani did, overlapping the goddess like a living double-exposure photograph. Even a different voice harmonized with Pirhani's, continuing the boastful monologue.

I didn't really know what to make of it. Was this the person behind the mask? I'd always just assumed there was a skull face beneath that, or just nothing at all. Who knew what the physical existence of a god really was.

Then shadow Zephyr drew his scythe and he cut the thread binding Pirhani to Penumbra. No preamble. No fanfare. Just a single quick slice.

The glitched-out Pirhani vanished, leaving her looking like the white and gold clad goddess I'd come to know.

A skull image appeared over her head.

Marked.

For death.

I'd just marked a god for death.

Hushed murmurs started in the crowd of Reapers, growing louder. People pointed. Scythes summoned.

Pirhani paused her triumphant tirade and glanced around hesitantly, sensing the changing tension in the air. She looked at me, eyes growing wide. Her mouth opened to say something, but I didn't give her the chance.

I Aero Shifted into her, my scythe slicing horizontal across her neck.

A dark line formed. Blood leaked out. A dribble at first, then a deluge.

Her mouth bubbled, bright golden eyes dimming to no more than embers. I shoved the scythe into her chest and they went out fully.

Her white faceless mask disintegrated, blowing away in shreds to reveal the skull beneath, no the face I'd seen in the glitches. The rest of her body collapsed in on itself, melting and decaying no different than a slaughtered Sin. Her skeleton remained standing for a moment, frozen in shock and agony, then it too crumbled into dust.

All that remained was a single golden orb.

It clunked to the ground, the same symbol that represented her oath etched into the surface.

I knelt above it and gently tapped the point of my scythe against the symbol.

Cracks screamed out across Pirhani's Will, golden light bursting out and swirling around me. It carried a weight with it. A wind that lifted my cloak and played with my hair. For a moment, I feared it would continue up and vanish into the sky, but as it ascended baying my head is revered direction and corkscrewed back down, feeding my blade.

The Will of a god no more than an empty shell.

I stood and lifted my scythe high in the air, a light golden aura around it—the last remnants of Pirhani, Goddess of the Jungle.

The other Reapers simply stared at me, awestruck. I didn't blame them. I could hardly believe what I'd just done.

Up on the balcony Jericho stood, slack jawed, and Calliope clasped her hands together with a huge smile cracking through. I waved at them sheepishly. Jericho closed his mouth, then opened it, then closed again.

Beside them, Valciferous had vanished.

That tugged at my heart more than I'd expected. What had I wanted to see? A cheer? An approving nod? He's not your father, Miles. He's a god. And you're nothing more than a pawn to him. He tricked you and he's using you.

It annoyed me how easily I'd forgotten that. Forgotten that the lack of shadow beneath me was his doing. That I couldn't return home to those I loved because of him.

Calliope tugged at Jericho's cloak motioning that she wanted to come down into the arena. He pulled an item from his inventory.

That's when the first screams came.

I turned to the source.

More screams followed. Pain. Fear.

A body flew from the stands and landed near me, rolling another twenty meters and leaving a trail of blood behind. The Reaper's cloak twirled and pulled him away.

It took me a moment to realize what the fuck happened.

In the stands, the Unchained Sins were no longer bound to their master.

And the Reapers who'd allied themselves with Pirhani were the closest victims. One moment, they'd been lounging, cocky and sure, the next… slaughtered like pigs.

My grip tightened on my scythe. Should I help them? What would Avery want me to do..? She'd help. She'd always help. But I… I couldn't bring myself to do it. Not after all they'd done.

I took a step forward. It was the honorable thing to do, right?

A silver armored hand rested on my chest, stopping me. Lancelot looked down at me, eyes stoney. "Let them suffer," he said. "They fight one another then we clean up the mess that remains."

"We should help," I said, though I didn't believe my own words. I wanted them to pay. They deserved this. And most wouldn't even truly die. They'd be back again next time they slept. If anything this wasn't punishment enough.

Another voice chimed in. "He's right." I turned to see Calliope, Jericho behind her, nodding his ascent. They'd already made it down. "Let them feel the pain they forced on us."

I let my shoulders loosen and retracted my scythe, my body giving in to the permission to feel the way I felt. It was freeing, allowing myself to sit and do nothing. It felt like I'd spent every second since I got here running from one task to the next, handling even a moment to think about what I wanted.

I wanted them to suffer.

So I closed my eyes and basked in the symphony of death in the stands.
