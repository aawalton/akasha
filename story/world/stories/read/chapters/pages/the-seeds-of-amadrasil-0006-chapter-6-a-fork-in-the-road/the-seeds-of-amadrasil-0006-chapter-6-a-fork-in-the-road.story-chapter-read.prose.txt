When a mana seed is harvested, the brittle crystalline structures of the aether blossom that produced it shatter. For years, those aether crystals were discarded as worthless, the pretty-looking waste product of a useful process, unable to augment or produce a skill.

However, the empty crystals retained the blossom’s most valuable property: an aether lattice naturally shaped to contain mana. Arkanis learned to stabilize that lattice, recharge it, and release the stored energy on command—a mana battery, if you will.

Yesterday’s harvesting refuse now powers the Barrier Projectors standing between our cities and the monsters spilling from the portals.

There is no such thing as worthless material. There are only uses we have not yet been clever enough to discover.

- Dr. Adrienne Calloway, unveiling of the first Arkanis Barrier Projector.

“Alright, you heard Brannic,” Erin said, springing to her feet to rouse the harvesters. “Everyone up, we’re moving out. Come on, Jenna, there’s no more food in there. Luis, wake up. Double check that you have everything.”

Amid the groans and grumbling, Kal levered himself up onto his feet and shoved his cart to get it moving, grunting at the frustrating weakness in his arms. The big rubber bubble tires rolled easily across the uneven rock-strewn cavern floor to the low whine of the battery-powered motor, a vehicle designed for navigating far more challenging terrain, even if mana interference inevitably took out the small and intentionally simple batteries or power train. He had no desire to jinx things, but he was incredibly grateful that it had held up so far. Unfortunately, even with the assistance, his mana sickness meant he felt every bump and boulder like a punching session with the heavy bag after an intense arm workout.

He was slightly out of breath by the time he pulled up near Brannic, Erin, and the hunters as they discussed the two paths before them.

“So, which is it?” Brannic asked, eyeing Sarn.

“Up… I think,” he said, gesturing toward the wider passage—the one filled with denser shadows that seemed to spill endlessly from the walls—with an unusual matte-black device clutched in one hand. “The mana compass says there’s denser mana in that direction.”

“You’re using tech?” Nirelle asked, raising an eyebrow. “In here?”

“It’s a pretty simple gadget; the latest from Horizon Gate Research. A bit like a mana-exposure badge,” Sarn said, shrugging. “Hasn’t fritzed out yet.”

“I haven’t heard of them,” Nirelle said, eyeing it suspiciously, but then she shrugged.

Not like it was easy to keep track of all the international magitech corporations that seemed to be springing up like weeds nowadays. It was already challenging enough to keep track of the developments in Veritania’s industry.

“How much is it?” Kal asked.

“I got it on a promotional discount,” Sarn said, brightening immediately at the show of interest. “Only forty-five thousand. It’s basically just a fragment of aether crystal suspended in a charged fluid. Probably why it still works.”

“Cool,” Kal said, suppressing the wince at just how casually the hunter dismissed spending an amount of money that would be life-changing for him and Mira for what was essentially a speculative device. It just highlighted the divide between them and the hunters. Still, he was always on the lookout for useful tools, and Erin might be convinced to get one for the team—maybe if they earned enough from this job. He filed the intel away for later.

“I don’t like the looks of that passage,” Tess muttered.

Kal hadn’t missed the speculative glance Nirelle had cast in his direction when Sarn had mentioned the mana-exposure badge, but he deliberately ignored it, instead focusing on the two tunnels they faced. The scout was recommending the wider, shadowed path, but Kal’s eyes paused on the narrower path leading downward. Something there still called to him, drawing his attention like a magnet, but he didn’t have the slightest idea what it might mean. Portal worlds and mana seeds occasionally influenced him in unusual ways—ways he had discovered that not everyone experienced. It was like a fragment of his dreams was being dredged up by the mere sight of the tunnel, and yet he remained lucid, struggling to interpret the meaning.

If it even means anything. He pursed his lips in thought as he groped about blindly through the sensation for anything that would help make sense of it. Maybe it’s better to stay clear.

Still, he couldn’t deny the lure of uncovering whatever secrets lay at the heart of this portal world. Not for the first time, he wondered if he should study xenoarchaeology or xenobiology at Ashmont University when Mira finally completed her degree. But that would cost more. Time and money. It was also unlikely he’d be able to apply his engineering credits to a degree like that. He shook his head. Their plan was clear. First, Mira’s degree. Then she would support them while they saved up for a common warrior mana seed for him. If they were frugal, they might be able to do it in a few years. With a warrior seed, he could take jobs and get on the lucrative hunter train, and they could begin the marathon of saving up for Mira’s healer seed. If that’s even possible. He grimaced at the insidious sabotage of the intrusive thought and shoved it away. Regardless, their plan did not allow him time to study something fun for four years.

Maybe later. After… everything. He shelved the idea, like always, stepped forward, cleared his throat softly, and suggested, “The smaller tunnel looks more profitable.”

“What do you mean?” Sarn asked.

“The lilac glow,” Kal said, waving a hand toward it. “I’m guessing there might be more Shadestone Crystals down below.” There was also the matter of the entire giant entrance cavern they were not harvesting, visible through the hole in the wall, but the shadows down there seemed to crawl on their own.

Brannic narrowed his eyes as Kal spoke.

“Good point,” Sarn said, glancing at Brannic. “What do you think? Crystals are worth a lot.”

“This is a red seed world and a mirror portal,” Brannic said. “The protocol is clear. We head for the anchor and close the portal with as few detours and risks as possible. In and out. We keep it clean. Take everything we can find along the way, but we’re not going to deviate from the goal.”

“Up then,” Sarn said, nodding. “That’s the most likely path to the guardian.”

Kal shut his mouth, sparing a last glance for the intriguing passage downward. After all, he had no other arguments with which to convince them, other than claiming some quasi-mystical dream impression that probably didn’t mean anything. He was already skating on thin ice with Brannic and Nirelle.

The protocol was written for a reason. If there had been monsters dropping green-hued human-compatible mana seeds left and right like a goblin or elf world, or if this had been a high-value resource world filled with mana-infused metal ore or artifacts, he knew the protocol would be drastically different. With a normal portal, they’d spend days scouring every corner for monsters before sending in the rest of the harvesting teams to overturn every stone and strip the world empty of anything of value, but with a mirror portal, even if it dropped compatible seeds, they would not risk sending teams back in after the anchor was shattered. It was far too dangerous.

Every single mana seed that appeared as green for a hunter—a sign of a high degree of compatibility with their current skills—represented a significant step up the rank progression for that hunter. Nobody understood precisely what caused a mana seed to show various colors for different people, but each one with high enough compatibility they recovered was an opportunity to awaken; a fresh opportunity to implant a seed and add a new hunter to the ranks. They were by far the most valued resources to be found in all the portal worlds, and Valmyra needed every single ounce of power to stave off the endless incursions. But a demon portal like this one dropped only red seeds—each worth nothing more than a week or two of mana regeneration for an E-rank hunter, and a trickle of incremental aether growth to be fed to already implanted skills. Worth using, but not worth taking unnecessary risks for.

“Sorry,” Erin muttered as they gathered up and began to move out.

“Not a problem,” he answered, tearing his gaze away from the path downward. This is precisely the reason for the first harvesting position. It was why they went in with the hunters. It was why they received hazard pay. “Just curiosity, is all. I’m sure we’ll find enough resources along the way, and there’s always the portal guardian.” Portal guardians—sometimes called bosses in the forums—had a higher chance of dropping artifacts and better mana seeds with a wider variety of skills. Occasionally, they even dropped more than one. Of course, that meant defeating those skills in the hands of a furious monster, but there was no way to avoid fighting the portal guardian if they needed to break the anchor and prevent the destruction wrought by a portal break and the subsequent inclusion.

“Yeah,” she said, nodding, but Kal could see the grim reality on her face. This portal world was not what she’d hoped for when she signed on to work it. The payoff just wasn’t worth the risks. Their goal, as Brannic had just clearly articulated, was to hunker down and fight their way out. Safety was the highest priority.

Twenty minutes later, the Lycan stiffened up and sniffed the air.

“What is it?” Kal asked, and immediately Erin glanced at her too.

“Blood,” she hissed, shivering. “Scary.”

“Yes. This is a Bloodshade demon portal,” Brannic said, surprising Kal with how keen his hearing was. “Everything smells like blood, and it should be scary to you guys. Don’t stop us again for mundane shit. Let’s move out.”

Ok, what’s his problem? Kal was familiar with the gruff commander archetype, but this seemed to be pushing it a bit far. It was clear he had an axe to grind beyond just the general unpleasantness. Still, it shouldn’t affect the job. At least the encounter with the Dusk Wraiths and the smaller demons in the tunnels afterward had demonstrated Brannic’s competence in combat.

“Big cave up ahead,” Sarn said, and sure enough, after ducking through a low, oval entrance, they found themselves in a sizeable, shadowy chamber. The walls glistened with moisture, and the back half of the cave was submerged in glowing crimson water. Drips falling from overhead plinked musically into the pool and made circular ripples that refracted the crimson glow onto the walls, creating the illusion that the entire chamber was underwater.

Or, submerged in blood. Kal wrinkled his nose at the metallic tang in the air. His stomach churned uncomfortably.

Brightly glowing button mushrooms sprouted along the walls and nestled among the stalagmites and the boulders rimming the perimeter of the pool, colored a pure crimson that evoked uncomfortable images of arterial blood often favored by some of the low-budget horrors produced by Novaran B-movie studios. Nightlights—more suited for a nightmare.

“Bloodroot Mushrooms!” Erin gasped. “Now we’re talking!”

“Score!” Drex crowed, dancing out a quick little jig. “Money, money, money!”

An excited hubbub rippled through the harvest crew as they filed into the chamber and stared. Bloodroot mushrooms were well known—and a rare find at that. Xenova used them as a base for their signature Chrysalin Wound Repair Gel and several other vitality-related salves and concoctions, making just this chamber worth more than everything they’d gathered so far. With the possible exception of the two Dusk Wraith corpses.

“Ok. We’ll pause here,” Brannic said. “Strip it bare.”

“Right. Let’s do it,” Erin said, directing pairs of harvesters to the glowing find.

Kal reached for the handles of his cart, but then froze midway as the Lycan growled, her violet eyes fixed on one of the exit tunnels. “It’s here.”

Barely had she spoken when a deep, crackling rumble like splintering shale reverberated through the cavern, disturbing the surface of the crimson pool and making the blood-like reflections shudder and warp along the walls. The sound began low, vibrating the ground beneath Kal’s boots, rising steadily into a guttural snarl punctuated by unsettling cracks like heavy bones in a trash compactor. Laced within the primal roar lingered faint, ghostly whispers, the echoes of something twisted and ancient, trailing off into the darkness and leaving the hair on the back of Kal’s neck standing at attention, an echo of the Lycan’s dramatically raised hackles.

Despite his disagreeable temperament, Brannic reacted instantly to the threat. “Take cover!” the armor-clad warrior bellowed, drawing his sword in a single fluid motion and bathing the chamber in a sudden glow of orange-red flames. “Hunters! Form up behind me. Incoming!”

The shape of a hulking demon clawing its way through the tunnel entrance to the shriek of tortured obsidian and crunching rock sent shivers running down Kal’s back.

That’s not a Dusk Wraith! It was far too massive. Something else.

As the demon demolished the too-small entrance to get at the assembling hunters, Kal ducked down behind the cart next to Erin and reached out to tug on the wolfkin’s arm while glancing around the chamber, cataloguing boulders and walls for potential cover and noting the positions of the frozen harvesters caught out in the open. Chaos erupted, rippling through the chamber like a shockwave of terror as the harvesting team scrambled or dove for rocks or carts, desperate to hide amid the echoes of several hastily stifled screams. The Lycan growled, but she retreated after a few moments, joining them by one of the cart wheels and peering around the side.

“Fuck me,” Erin whispered, her voice cracking midway. Her eyes were wide and glued to the behemoth forcing its way into the cave. “…the fuck is that?”

“Shit, that is ugly!” Kal spluttered, trying to catch himself from backpedaling so fast he’d fall on his butt. Calm down. Think! Find a position, find cover…

The demon emerged with deliberate menace from the tunnel. Rubble and debris sluiced off the heavy, glass-like armored scales that covered its back and shoulders and glinted in the crimson light like polished obsidian. It had a feline predator’s grace, prowling on four paws with claws longer than Sarn’s daggers slicing into rock, but all resemblance to its Dusk Wraith brethren ended there. Wider than it was tall, it still stood nearly eight feet at the shoulder, towering over even Brannic and Drex. The heavily muscled frame belied the fluidity of its grace, and as it moved, glowing crimson veins pulsed an impossibly slow heartbeat beneath the rippling armored scales. It cracked open heavy jaws, wider than Kal’s torso, revealing rows and rows of sharp black fangs, and let out a guttural grinding cough that sounded like boulders being pulverized.

“Hemorath!” Sarn shouted.

“Shit, that’s peak E-rank,” Erin gasped. “Maybe higher.”

Piercing crimson eyes glowed fiercely from beneath the demon’s armored brow, radiating malicious cunning and a palpable hunger. It fixed a predator’s glare on the hunter team arrayed before it, and then, it moved.

If someone had explained to Kal how fast it was before now, he would have laughed it off as a joke. The monster took three steps and crossed thirty feet in a heartbeat, leaving billowing clouds of red mist and swirling shadows in its wake as it bounded across the gap. Massive, corded muscles bunched, sending a ripple through the shoulder-scales as if a field of black glass daggers were made to stand to attention. The crimson veins pulsed, and the monster lashed out with a heavy paw. Four razor-sharp, gleaming black claws the length of Kal’s forearm whistled through the air to slash deep gashes across Drex’s chest before the big hunter could even blink. The follow-through left four mirrored ribbons of blood trailing from the broken-glass-sharp tips as the monk was swatted across the length of the cavern aimed right at their hiding spot.

Kal ducked, rolling to the side and pulling Erin out of the way, but the Lycan sprang up and blocked the heavier, flying man with both hands, decelerating him enough to prevent his face from smashing against the wall. “Kill?” she said, cocking her head at him.

Drex ignored her, snarling with incoherent rage as he wheeled about and charged back into the fray while little wisps of Nirelle’s emerald-green healing magic spilled from his wounds like fireflies in the darkness.

Brannic rebuffed a raking swipe across his sturdy Obsidian Aegis armored chest plate and unleashed a precision riposte with his flaming sword that found a gap between the uncannily similar obsidian plates protecting the monster’s vitals. Flames bit into the wound, and dark blood sprayed sizzling onto the ground.

Kal’s muscles twitched in sympathy as his eyes tracked the path of Brannic’s unique technique and played it out in his mind, exploring how it might feel to match the strike, the stance, the angle, the precision of that blade.

“Harvest team, move! Everyone out!” Erin yelled, her voice cracking as she failed to keep her outward calm. “Kal, help me.”

“I’ll take the ones on the right,” Kal said at once, and they split up, sprinting in opposite directions while the Lycan dragged their cart out into the passageway.

Kal glanced up at the sound of a loud crunch and a grinding squeal like a heavy pickaxe being dragged across concrete to find those heavy jaws clamped across Brannic’s left shoulder, scoring grooves into the surface of his resilient armor.

“Heal!” he yelled. “Drex, tag with me!”

I’ve got him right where I want him! Kal’s mind supplied savagely, counterpoint to the horror rising in his throat as he saw their D-ranker being shaken like a rat. His gear was protection against laceration, but how much crushing pressure were those massive jaws inflicting upon the warrior?

The monk raised his shining, silvery gauntlets and unleashed a solid punch that cracked against the side of the demon’s head with enough force to dislodge the jaws. While Kal couldn’t admire the man’s sloppy technique, he certainly produced enough power to attract the full attention of that mountain of muscle and fury.

That has to be Heavy Strike. I’m sure of it. It was unsurprising, really. Brute Strength paired with Heavy Strike was a popular melee fighter build. A purely offensive approach, easily assembled from relatively common skills. It even explained the gauntlets—without a weapon or a passive skill to reinforce his fists, he would pulverize his own hands on every punch.

The demon roared and swiped with its claws, and Drex went flying across the room to smash into the opposite wall this time, trailing green sparks and ribbons of healing magic as he flew. He grunted loudly as the breath was forced from his lungs, and the snapping of ribs could be heard from across the cave.

Nirelle crouched behind a large boulder, wearing a frown of intense focus as she wielded her glowing staff, certainly the sole reason nobody had died yet.

The flaming enchanted sword howled through the air as the now-released warrior struck back with the full weight of his D-ranked strength. He scored a direct hit along the monster’s flank, and one of the scale armor plates shattered, but to Kal’s dismay, the original wound he had inflicted seemed to be growing closed before his very eyes.

Fuck. This is bad. Is that blood magic?

“It’s regenerating!” Drex screamed as he leapt back into the fray.

“I have it pinned down,” Brannic hissed between gritted teeth as he blocked the wicked claws on the back of his forearm and sliced across the demon’s face, barely missing one of the crimson eyes. “Tess, Sarn, take it out!”

Kal reached the nearest pair of cowering first-time harvesters and dragged them to their feet. “Move,” he said, before shoving them toward the entrance passage with a quick, “Go, get out of here!”

The demon roared in pain from within a dark, billowing cloud of shadows and crimson mist as Sarn appeared from nowhere, wielding a sickly green dagger that flickered and flashed like an industrial sewing machine, punching through the scales to deliver the Stalker spider demon’s fluorescent green venom. That’s definitely Pierce. No way is he strong enough to get a dagger through that heavy armor without help. Then the entire cavern lit up with the brilliant orange-red radiance of a stream of firebolts blasting from Tess’s outstretched hand.

“Take that, you… ugly… mangy… thing!” the mage screamed, her face twisted into a sneer of haughty disdain, but the whites of her eyes and the trembling of her outstretched arm were clearly visible in the orange glow.

The demon twisted with uncanny agility, biting down on Drex’s leg and thrashing its head back and forth, shattering stalagmites with his flailing back and shoulders. It flung him into Sarn, sending both of them sprawling. Nirelle yelped in alarm, but the monster was not done yet.

With a sudden, twisting lunge, it caught Brannic squarely in the solar plexus with a powerful lashing paw, and the hunter went cartwheeling right into the last cart sheltering a shaking harvester, his flaming sword still clutched desperately in his right hand. The monster immediately leapt after to finish its prey, trailing bloody mist and shadows through the air. Its crimson veins pulsed quicker and brighter as it landed heavily right beside Kal.

Kal nearly froze at the sudden immensity of the behemoth looming over him. It radiated such incredible power that he felt he must flee, screaming. Instead, he clenched his teeth and snatched the cowering harvester from between the monster’s legs, dragging him out by the collar with a herculean yank of his mana-weakened muscles, and only then, did he indulge his primitive instincts to flee.

“Reposition,” Brannic yelled. “Fight it here!”

Kal approved. Fighting up against a wall would restrict the monster’s graceful movement and allow any near misses from Tess’s fire magic to do damage by proximity, but he could already see the burnt flesh and cracked plates regenerating with unbelievable speed under the ominous crimson pulsation of its veins.

“Brace!” Tess yelled, and the orange-red light surged in intensity.

Kal dove for cover, dragging his charge with him as an intense blast wave of heat radiated from the explosions that drowned out the Hemorath’s thunderous rage. When the barrage faded, Sarn appeared on the monster’s back, with his freshly anointed daggers flickering a sickly green in the light of the dimming flames.

“Drex. All out! Now!” Brannic demanded, wrestling with the monster’s snapping jaws.

The monk answered with his gleaming fists and a roar of fury.

“Tess, call the next shot,” Brannic continued, and then unleashed such an exquisitely executed display of swordsmanship in the cramped quarters that it left Kal stunned and re-evaluating the man’s skill. The blade seemed to shimmer through alternate dimensions, and his wrists flexed impossibly, driving the beast backward with an unending hail of cuts that hemmed it in and stymied its attacks, biting into flesh time and time again.

Hungry flames splashed against the wall, sizzling and hissing as the stench of dark, demonic blood and burnt meat filled the chamber. Kal shoved the last harvester out of the entryway and into the passage where everyone else cowered or crouched behind rocks and boulders, hoping for their salvation.

“That’s rough,” Erin said, the worry lines on her face exaggerated by the interplay of shadows and flames on her features. “It’s regenerating fast.”

“Mhm, fire good,” the Lycan said, crouching by the entrance as her darting eyes followed every strike.

“What?” Erin said, a frown creasing her brow.

“What she means is that Tess is doing a lot of damage. If she can keep it up, I think Brannic can finish it off,” Kal said, his despair and dread fading a little after their leader’s display of skill.

It was far harder to guess the skills of a D-rank by observation, but he was pretty certain he had it figured out now. The main clue had come when he’d realized Brannic had also not invested in any defensive skills. That meant he was spending one point of his aether capacity on a Flurry variant to give his sword those short, sharp bursts of speed that had allowed him to keep up with the Dusk Wraiths. A second point was obviously spent maintaining that fantastic sword. For his remaining two, Kal was almost certain he was sporting a D-rank version of Ogre’s Power or Brute Strength—not much else could explain those incredible jumps to reach the Dusk Wraiths on the ceiling, or how he was physically holding back several tons of demon with his bare hands.

“God, I hope so,” Erin said, glancing back at her charges. If the hunters did not prevail, there would be nowhere to run.

“Here we go,” Kal said.

The wolfkin nodded, but her eyes remained glued to the action.

Out in the center of the cavern, the fire mage extended her hand, palm outward. “Fire!” she yelled, her voice cracking like fine porcelain. The air in front of her rippled briefly, and she produced a streaming inferno that Kal could feel from right across the cavern.

“Nirelle! Heal me! I’ll hold it,” Brannic yelled as Drex and Sarn scrambled to clear the blast radius.

Then Tess launched her giant ball of flame across the cavern and slammed it into the demon’s side. A deafening boom shook the chamber as a shockwave of flame engulfed the demon and the D-rank warrior. Rubble and dislodged stones rained down from the ceiling, bouncing off the ground while Nirelle gritted her teeth, her entire body tensed in supreme focus.

As the smoke and the scarlet inferno faded, two silhouettes emerged, backlit by lingering flame. Several heavy plates had been blown off the Hemorath’s flank, leaving scorched and heavily burnt flesh, but if they didn’t act fast, the monster’s insane regeneration would repair it. Already, those crimson veins were pulsing as the wounds started to knit back together.

“I’m saturated,” Tess croaked, shaking visibly as she sagged to the ground.

Brannic grunted and thrust his sword forward, using it like a lance or a spear. Kal frowned to see such a beautifully wrought blade used so crudely, but the warrior’s immense strength buried the burning blade up to the hilt behind the Hemorath’s right shoulder.

It roared in agony, a discordant blend of subsonic bass and mind-rending screech that snuffed the fading flames and shook the cavern again. Brannic twisted the blade viciously, making the demon spasm until it collapsed onto the ground. Smoke billowed up from the wound as his enchanted sword cooked the monster from the inside, but he stood a silent vigil, poised for instant action over the fallen demon with a two-handed grip on the hilt of his blade that never faltered until the glow of light appeared and the crystal branches of the Aether Blossom began to sprout up out of the corpse.

Brannic whipped his sword out of the demon’s body, slashed it through the air to clear the blood, and sheathed it. Then he grabbed the glowing red mana seed, stomped across the room, and tossed it at Nirelle. “Good work.”

“Aah, thanks,” she said, glancing at her teammates and straightening her robes before absorbing it.

Kal swallowed. Most of his experience inside portal worlds came after the hunter teams had cleared them out, in the safe phase where most of the harvesting teams were allowed in. He understood hunter combat—he’d been training for it for years. He’d even occasionally witnessed E-ranked hunters clearing the odd remaining monster while he harvested resources, and he’d thought he’d grasped the essence of it.

This battle… was something else.

Kal’s eyes lingered on the massive, burnt corpse while his fingers brushed against the hilt of his pistol. While Brannic and Drex had both pursued unbalanced builds, they were hardly alike. With a powerful sponsor providing that heavy armor, and his clearly superb training, Brannic’s build was surprisingly well-rounded, even given his skill choices—a stark contrast to Drex’s mindless Brute Strength approach. Watching a skilled D-ranked warrior taking on something as deadly as that Hemorath with his team had left Kal’s heart pounding in his chest. If this was what it was like, the jump from E- to D-rank, what exactly had his father been capable of? S-ranked martial arts had to be… well, insane!

He turned as he felt a gaze upon him and met a pair of violet eyes dancing with a gleam of excitement. He nodded to her. Yeah, I want to be that strong, too.
