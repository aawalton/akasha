Far above the battlefield, beyond the reach of mortals and divine warriors alike, a circular chamber floated within the boundless expanse of Divine Will.

At the center of the chamber stood a vast round table crafted from pale celestial stone that shimmered like a calm lake beneath moonlight while reflecting the events of the battlefield below in countless shifting images. Three figures occupied the table. Each represented a newborn god participating in this Battle of Divine Will.

The first was a towering man clad in bronze armor polished to a radiant gleam. His muscular physique resembled the statues of ancient warrior kings, every movement exuding restrained yet overwhelming power. Long white hair flowed freely down his back, and a massive spear rested casually beside his throne-like chair.

This was Aurelion, the God of War.

His presence alone carried the suffocating pressure of someone who had claimed countless victories on the battlefield. Even while seated, he looked as though he belonged on the front lines of war rather than within a divine observatory.

Opposite him sat a far more unassuming figure. A slender young man cloaked in dark robes leaned lazily against his chair, the hood shadowing most of his face. Only the faint curve of his lips was visible, forming a smile that appeared both knowing and faintly smug. From within the darkness beneath the hood, his eyes gleamed faintly.

This was Velkhar, the God of Deceit.

Unlike Aurelion’s overwhelming presence, Velkhar’s aura was subtle and elusive, like smoke slipping through unseen cracks. One could never quite tell what he was thinking—or whether the expression on his face meant anything at all.

The third seat was occupied by a girl with impossibly long silver hair cascading down her back like flowing silk. Her crimson eyes glowed faintly with divine power as she held a small black lantern in her hands, which was emitting a soft golden light. She wore an elegant black dress decorated with lace and delicate frills, a violet ribbon tied neatly at her waist.

Despite her seemingly harmless appearance, the quiet authority surrounding her made it clear that she was anything but human.

She was Istellia, the Goddess of Imagination.

Right now, she was observing the battlefield projection hovering above the table. Within the projection was the ruined city, where the three fortresses were located, each housing thousands of divine warriors preparing for war.

“Hmm.” Velkhar was the first to speak as he rested his chin against his hand. “It seems the first stage has begun rather dramatically.”

His eyes wandered toward the image of Istellia’s faction securing the supermarket fortress.

“How unfortunate,” he continued. “Istellia, not only is your army the smallest, but it also appears to be surrounded by quite the impressive monster horde.”

At that, Aurelion scoffed, his voice rumbling like distant thunder as he said, “Monsters are nothing. What matters is whether the divine warriors under your banner possess the power to endure the incoming monster waves.”

His gaze shifted toward Velkhar’s projection, where his own divine warriors were already mobilizing in disciplined formations.

“Victory is decided by strength. My divine warrior army is undoubtedly superior in quality.”

In response, Velkhar’s smile widened slightly. “How very predictable of you, Aurelion.” He gestured lazily toward the battlefield and said, “Strength alone does not win wars. If we’re talking about unity and overall combat power, then mine is the best,” before turning his gaze toward Istellia, as though seeking affirmation. “Wouldn’t you agree?”

They were both well aware that Istellia’s faction was the weakest and had the smallest number of divine warriors. It was pretty obvious that Aurelion and Velkhar underestimated Istellia and thought that it was a battle between the two of them.

Istellia remained silent for a moment, calmly observing the battlefield with her crimson eyes. Then she spoke softly. “It is too soon to determine the outcome. The game has only just begun.”

Her gaze settled on a particular figure moving through the ruined city streets: Maximillian Anderson. A faint smile touched her lips—one that the other two gods did not notice.

They were unaware of his existence and what he was truly capable of. For now, she intended to keep it that way. She would do her utmost to conceal his presence from both of them, as well as from the other divine beings observing this Battle of Divine Will. Only when victory was all but certain would she allow them to witness even a fraction of what he could accomplish.

After all, he was her trump card in this battle.

With me being alone, it didn’t take long for the two marks on my arms to light up. In the next moment, Ember and Solana emerged in twin bursts of crimson-black emberlight and golden radiance. Ember materialized first, hovering beside me.

“Ahh… finally some fresh air,” Ember said, stretching his back. “Being cooped up in there is boring, nya.”

Golden light followed immediately after, and Solana descended gently, folding her wings neatly against her sides. She hovered just above the ground before settling on my shoulder with quiet elegance.

“Master is conducting reconnaissance alone. Shouldn’t you have called for us, coo?”

“That’s right, nya,” Ember added.

I simply smiled at their concern before replying, “There are too many enemies at the intersection ahead. Anyway, can you help me scout further?”

“That’s easy, nya!”

“Leave it to me, coo!”

Before I could say anything else, the two of them shot forward.

Ember dissolved into a streak of crimson-black embers that slipped between the broken buildings like living fire. Meanwhile, Solana ascended quietly into the gray sky, gliding above the ruined streets gracefully. Both of them were far better suited for reconnaissance than I was. If they could guide me toward potential points of interest, my scouting would be far more efficient than wandering blindly without an eagle-eye view.

I remained where I was, taking cover behind the wreckage of a collapsed storefront while keeping my senses sharp. Even a single careless step could draw the attention of the monster swarm roaming nearby, even with my cursed cloak, magic armor, and the Predator’s Veil skill.

“Huh, maybe I should learn flying magic…”

Thinking back, my Gravity Magic should be able to let me fly if I used it correctly. I hadn’t had much opportunity to experiment with it beyond practicing {Gravity Launch} for mobility, but now that my control over this magic had improved, perhaps I could develop a proper flying spell while waiting for Ember and Solana.

With that in mind, I began circulating my mana and activated Gravity Magic, carefully manipulating the force around my body. Instead of propelling myself upward like with {Gravity Launch}, I focused on reducing the gravitational pull acting on me, attempting to make my body lighter than air. Almost immediately, my boots lifted off the ground.

“Oh…”

I floated upward a few centimeters, then a full meter above the pavement. The sensation was strange, almost as if gravity itself had loosened its grip on me. However, maintaining balance proved to be another matter entirely. The moment I tried to move forward, my body drifted awkwardly to the side and nearly tipped forward.

“…This feels like what I imagine floating in space.”

Without gravity anchoring my movements, even the smallest motion sent me drifting unpredictably through the air. Controlling my direction proved far more difficult than I had expected.

After a moment of trial and error, I tried using {Graviton Pull}. A faint gravitational force tugged my body forward, allowing me to glide slowly in that direction. Then I reversed the force, pushing myself backward. It worked but barely.

Each adjustment required precise control, and the slightest miscalculation sent me wobbling like a balloon caught in a weak breeze. More importantly, the mana consumption was far from ideal. Maintaining the reduced gravity field alone was already draining, and repeatedly using {Graviton Pull} to steer myself only worsened the problem.

After hovering there for a while, I sighed and gently lowered myself back to the ground. “This definitely needs refinement.”

It was probably wise to learn standard flying magic first before attempting to combine it with Gravity Magic. That way, I would have a proper foundation to work from before merging the two. I could already imagine how useful it would be—allowing me to travel across vast distances and maneuver through the air with incredible precision.

As I was thinking about that, the marks on my arms blinked faintly. A moment later, Solana descended from above and landed lightly on my right shoulder.

“Master,” she said softly, “we have discovered something, coo.”

Ember followed immediately afterward, emerging from a swirl of emberlight with a wide grin. “Heh. Jackpot, nya.”

I crossed my arms. “So what did you two find?”

Solana spoke first. “Approximately one and a half kilometers from our current position, beyond the collapsed overpass to the north, there is a large encampment, coo.”

Ember added with a smirk, “We found people, nya.”

My eyes narrowed slightly. “Divine warriors?”

“Yep,” Ember confirmed. “Lots of them.”

They were most likely one of the other factions. It was highly unlikely that any person at this stage would not be a divine warrior.

“Their fortress appears to be an industrial complex surrounded by makeshift barricades and watch posts,” Solana continued. “There are thousands of divine warriors stationed there, coo.”

Yup, that confirms it.

Ember’s ears twitched. “But that’s not the interesting part, nya.”

“What do you mean?”

He pointed toward the distant skyline with his paw. “Not too far from their location are several camps of thralls. And there are hundreds—maybe even thousands—of them in each camp.”

Thrall camps?

Now that I thought about it, it wouldn’t be strange to encounter thralls in this special scenario. Back during the second scenario, I vividly remembered that the ones responsible for summoning those flying octopuses were none other than those thralls.

Nevertheless, this could actually become an opportunity.

Those thralls served an eldritch being, which meant their camps would almost certainly contain statues or totems imbued with faith. If I could absorb the faith stored within them, it would significantly boost my plausibility reserves. At the same time, provoking those thralls into attacking the nearby divine warrior fortress would create chaos for that faction.

In other words, this was a chance to kill several birds with one stone: gathering plausibility while sabotaging my enemies—and potentially reducing the enemy divine warriors’ numbers in the process.

“All right, lead the way, Ember, Solana!” I ordered, pointing ahead.

“Will do, nya!”

“Leave it to me, coo!”
