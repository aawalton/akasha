"So..." Harry kicked the ground as he and Madeline walked down a long hallway, "You come here often?"

Still embarrassed from the previous conversation, Harry tried to break the ice with a... not so swift line. Yeah, who could've thought that a reclusive thief would be socially inept. No. Not socially inept; just... awkward.

"Um..." Not sure what to say, the woman chuckled and replied. "Yeah... all the time."

There were two rows of cellars in each side. Goldman and Brun's father could've been in any of them, so they split up again. Harry and Madeline would search on the right side, and the other two would go by the left. Maybe they could even find Vela. If Prism and Brun went together already, it would make sense for them to go again. That was Harry's rational side speaking up. Too bad he didn't realize he'd be stuck with Madeline.

However, as much as the atmosphere wasn't exactly helping — with all the dirty cellars and barely lit candles — he felt safer with her. They knew each other's weaknesses, his being the lack of offense and her being the very few escape options. Madeline had the Brush Emblem, which allowed her to "paint" attacks and defenses. So if either tried to fight, he'd know how to react. Besides, his time alone with Prism wasn't so pleasant. Brun would be even worse.

Refraining from using his doors, Harry just kept walking as he thought about something to say.

"We don't need to chat, you know?" Madeline said. "We can just be quiet; no interaction whatsoever."

But deep down he needed this. Madeline, the closest thing he had for a friend, reminded him of his life before the Logos Tower.

"...What will you do? You know, when you reach the top." The Locksmith asked in a calmer tone.

"Oh, do you mean with the God Emblem and all?" She replied. "I don't know, really... When you are in control of so much power, I guess it never comes to just one thing, right? I could cure all diseases, or maybe be the cause of its destruction. Or I can — you know — just summon Mary Crescendo to paint me a few things. I mean, she was a slave for a few years, so it wouldn't feel unfamiliar for her..."

Her silent chuckle, which felt as sincere as snow in the winter, calmed Harry's rapid heartbeat.

"Who's, um, Mary Crescingo?"

"Crescendo!" Madeline stared at him, dumbfounded. "She's the one who painted 'World of Clouds'? Really, Harry, you need some guidance."

"Look, I didn't steal for a grand ideal, okay?" Harry replied as they resumed their walk. "An honest thief is as good as a dead one."

"Well, but I'm not asking for honesty," She added. "But identity. And I don't mean a name people call you on newspapers on Sunday mornings. I'm talking about real identity. A cause to stand against, or perhaps a lover to fight for. That's what we all live for, right?"

"...Didn't know you were so passionate about the world, Maddy." The nickname didn't have an effect on her. At least not a negative one. "It's easy for you to say; you already found your place in society."

"If I had, I wouldn't be here, would I?" Madeline retorted.

"Maybe the Tower is all we have left; humanity's last bastion against the mediocre."

"...You can't seriously believe that." She replied with a strange look, as if not buying Harry's ramblings.

"I don't." He added with a chuckle. "Don't worry, I'm not a cultist... Not yet, at least."

"Yeah, remind me to never put you two together, Locksmith." She said.

But he had a purpose... didn't he? All that thieving and robbery had to mean something. His mother wouldn't be proud, of course, but after he arrived at the top and changed his destiny, things would be fine. Matthews wouldn't be turned into a living skeleton and Devon would still be selling his books. That was Harry's motivation, and it was reason enough. Like a soldier defending his land, though, the path of control was always tainted by chaos. For how long could he follow orders without understanding its consequences? The answer was: For as long as it takes until I am in her embrace. Even if the world burns.

"...You okay?" Madeline asked. "You haven't said anything in a while." Afraid she touched on a... touchy subject, the woman felt awkward.

"N-No, I'm fine. Just tired." The young thief replied. "Me and Vela had this fight with a kid who... Anyways, I'm just wondering how things will turn out in the end. I mean, with these cultists and that Shepherd and stuff..."

"Oh, yeah, we should definitely be worried about that guy." Harry narrowed his eyes and looked at her. "What? I'm just being realistic. When I saw he looks like a golem, I mean it. Seriously, you shouldn't mess with someone with two Emblems."

"Yeah, I've had my fair exchanges with the likes." Harry thought about his brother. "But it all depends on what format of Emblem you choose as a second one. The Quadrangular, for example, can permanently change your body, but your mind remains the same."

"What I can say is that he's not, um... sane, then."

"Actually, now that I've thought about it... Why are you here, still? Don't tell me you've had a change of heart and want to help the poor wanderers of the Logos Tower?"

"What, you think I can't be sympathetic towards a couple of strays?" She joked. "One hand washes the other. It's simple, isn't it? It seems you can't climb on your own around here; there will either be a sadistic group of weirdos on one side or unknown threats waiting on the other. I'm tired of it. I just want to go home and live peacefully by the lake or something."

"Well, then I think you shouldn't have picked a career where the mortality rate is higher than the king's trousers."

Madeline gave another genuine laugh as she opened her mouth to answer, but was quickly cut off by something at the end of the hallways. Without thinking, Harry opened one of his doors and jumped through until he reached the source of the sounds. The woman tried to follow, but when she went to cross the passage, the door shut in her face, pushing her to the floor.

With his hood on, he saw nothing but empty cells and wondered how large this underground area was. It could possibly be connected with all the other cabins on the site through tunnels and secret passages, but Harry didn’t observe enough to see any.

Listening closely, he noticed one of the voices laughing. Slightly scared, he also wondered if this wasn’t another trap. A few steps away, his door closed and quickly disappeared.

With stealthy steps, he approached the cell that had a single candle on a table. Even the hallway lights had already gone out in many cells, meaning the place was rarely visited. If the prisoners were here, it would make sense that his return point in the cycle was there as well.

“Get out of there now…” A gruff, grumpy voice said from inside the cell, making Harry slip from the shadows, “We heard you back there. Damn jailers, we don’t even have privacy…”

Maybe they heard me while I was jumping from door to door. When you’re this alone, any noise becomes a roar… Harry thought internally as he approached the numerous dense wooden bars. Sheesh, they really don't want these two escaping...

A little to the left, on the back wall, faintly illuminated by the candle in the center, lay a man laughing uncontrollably. He had a full beard that matched his short blond hair. His face was friendly and reminded Harry of a priest, if not for his large and intimidating shape. Besides that, the man wore nothing but a pair of underwear.

Regretting his situation, another man was a bit to the right of the bearded man. His build was similar to his cellmate, except for the good attitude and the beard. He had black hair and also wore nothing but his underwear. His black hair, along with his personality, contrasted with the blond man who wouldn’t stop laughing beside him. He also looked like a viking.

What the hell is happening? Am I interrupting something? Harry added inwardly. Am I the third wheel on this scenario...?

“Who are you?” The viking asked, his strong accent reeking of northern influence. “You must be a rookie they put on guard duty. Look, kid, we can’t get out of here even if we tried. Besides, you’re way too skinny… It hurts to see your dry, soulless face.”

What a lovely guy. Harry thought as he gave him a deadpan look. Any sense of danger was gone after the insults when the blond man stopped laughing and looked at the Locksmith.

“Oi', don’t mind my friend!” He said, warmly, “He’s just a tiny bit grumpy because he hasn’t eaten… for a few cycles. I’m sure if you were outside, you’d have a much better shape. In here, since your body doesn’t develop, you… Well, look on the bright side, you still have hair! Many here arrive bald with no chance to grow it back.”

“You also have hair, and good quality from what I can see.” Harry said, leaning on the wooden bars.

“Oh yes…” The man replied, embarrassed, “Unfortunately, not everyone can have my physique… but you can try!”

“Try?” The viking's laugh was similar to rotten tree being chopped down. “HAHAHA! If he tries to punch a polar bear, he’ll break his hand!”

“What the hell do you mean?” Harry said, confused. “Anyone would break their hand if they tried to punch a fucking polar bear.”

“Then you’d break it twice as bad! HAHAHA!” The Viking kept laughing, when suddenly he fell through an illusory door and appeared in the sky, falling hard to the ground.

Surprised, the two stopped their antics and quickly turned to the enigmatic man. They lost their fighting positions when a woman arrived, panting, near the man.

“Damn… Harry!” She shouted between breaths, “Why didn’t you… wait for me!”

When the two saw the girl, a surprised smile appeared on their faces. "Wait, who are you guys?"

"Oh, so these are the prisoners?" She said, still panting. "Have you figured out who is Brun's father?"

He looked at the viking and slightly turned his head, “Unfortunately, yes.”
