Seven seconds left.

A spear whistles past my cheek, choosing air over flesh by a margin it will not be allowed to enjoy again.

My sword sweeps out in reply, and a skeletal head clatters to the grassy forest floor.

Six seconds.

My boots skim the ground as I step forward, slipping neatly between two sets of grasping hands that close on absolutely nothing.

I pivot on my planted foot, spin once, and two lightly armored zombies fold apart mid-motion, bisected so cleanly they don’t seem to realize they’re dead yet.

Five seconds.

I Dash.

Distance collapses.

The last undead—this one a skeleton knight—swings for my neck, clearly attempting to divorce my head from my body.

Rude.

The blade connects—

—and passes straight through the illusion.

Four seconds.

I tap its shoulder politely, having already stepped behind it with Astral Step.

It tries to turn.

It does not succeed.

My sword takes its head before the movement fully forms.

Silence settles.

I glance over the remains of nearly a dozen undead. Some familiar, like the zombies, and some new additions that started appearing after midnight, including the spear-wielding skeleton and armored zombies.

…And the skeleton knight, which is no longer even a horde unit now, having joined the ranks of wandering undead instead.

Still no bows.

Unacceptable.

I sigh internally and sheath my sword.

Then—slowly, elegantly—I plant my hands on my hips and beam up at the obscured sky.

“Told you I could do it in under fifteen seconds.”

A voice answers, deep and heavy, carrying the weight of cosmic resignation.

“Yes, yes. I suppose you win the challenge,” he says. “I owe you one favor.”

My tail swings in peak satisfaction.

Perfect.

Exactly as planned.

Now I can locate a crepe stall the second I enter Asteria.

…Not that I’m going to tell him that yet. Much better to let him sit with the knowledge that this fox can make him do anything she wants at any time.

Something he appreciates, I’m sure.

With the impromptu challenge concluded, I finally spare a glance for the notifications I’ve been deliberately ignoring.

Optimization can wait.

Tutorial points have taken another massive jump since I last checked, courtesy of the forest thinning out and making hunting significantly more efficient.

Naturally, I killed anything unlucky enough to wander into my detection range.

Approximately four hours have passed since midnight, which was right around the point when the undead abruptly transitioned from a complete non-issue into the threat equivalent of a very angry chihuahua.

Which is to say: still not dangerous.

Just… louder.

Most other people probably wouldn’t agree. But they aren’t here, so their opinions do not count.

I also don’t have a lot of faith that there will be many survivors besides me by the time this Tutorial wraps up.

Point totals are going to get ridiculous, and there is absolutely no timeline where the dragon and I don’t try to kill each other.

That leaves me as the most likely final participant.

Unlucky.

The universe must really want everyone else dead if it decided to put them in the same Tutorial block as me and a dragon.

My condolences.

No new skill selection at level thirty.

Tragic.

A shame.

They’re helpful, sure. Some of them are even kind of neat. A little esoteric. Very fancy. Gold-star material.

But for damage?

Couldn’t I just… make my own?

My thermal grenade exists. The flashbang exists. Both of them came into being through nothing but spite, curiosity, and aggressive manipulation of the universe’s laws.

So why am I outsourcing my violence?

Ten out of ten.

The ultimate close-range fighter.

Annoying.

These things are huge.

Eight out of ten.
