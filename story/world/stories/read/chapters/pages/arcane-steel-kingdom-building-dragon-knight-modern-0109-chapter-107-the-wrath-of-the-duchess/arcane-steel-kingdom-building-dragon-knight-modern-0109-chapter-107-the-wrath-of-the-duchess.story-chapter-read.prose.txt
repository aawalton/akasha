It wasn't a red alert. It wasn't a hull breach warning. It was a personal notification on my HUD, tagged with a priority level that overrode fleet command.

[Message from: The Duchess][Subject: Dinner][Body: You are late.]

My blood ran cold. The adrenaline from the launch evaporated, replaced by a primal, instinctive dread that every son knows deep in his marrow. She knew. I didn't know how she knew—maybe mothers had their own version of The Oracle powered by guilt—but she knew I was back.

I stood up so fast my chair spun. "We need to go."

Nyxia looked up from the tactical map, confused. "Go? We just launched the fleet. We need to monitor the drop zones, ensure the Hegemony air defenses don't—"

"The fleet can fly itself," I said, walking briskly toward the exit. "My mother cannot feed herself dinner if her son isn't there to eat it. And if we miss the soup course after... everything..." I gestured vaguely between us. "There won't be a war because there won't be a commander left to fight it."

Nyxia blinked. "You are afraid of the Duchess?"

"Nyxia," I said, stopping at the blast doors. "I have nuked armies. I have fought necromancers. I have built a titan out of steel. None of those things defy the laws of physics. My mother’s ability to hold a grudge defies time and space. Move."

We left the CIC, heading for the residential wing located deep in the central hull of The Aegis.

Bob and Patricia fell into step behind us. Bob looked confused, checking his sword and scanning the hallway for threats.

"Why is the Lord Commander sweating?" Bob whispered to Patricia. "Is there a breach? An assassin?"

Patricia didn't look at him. Her face was pale, her posture stiff as a board. It was completely out of character for the woman who had just dismantled a spy network while making tea.

"Bob," she said, her voice hollow. "Pray."

"For what?"

"For mercy."

We reached the transit bay. While Nyxia had been washing off the dust of the flight, The Vengeance had completed its docking sequence with The Aegis. Now, the two-kilometer-long behemoths were locked together by massive hydraulic clamps, creating a single, continuous fortress of steel.

The transit bay was the junction point—a cavernous hall where the gravity plating of one ship seamlessly merged with the other. Through the reinforced viewports, I could see the massive docking arms groaning under the strain of holding the two vessels together against the winds of the Maelstrom Sea. It was a marvel of engineering, a bridge between two floating cities.

Waiting for us was the Express Link. A sleek, bullet-shaped mag-lev pod sat in its cradle, humming with magnetic potential. It was designed to shoot us through the pressurized tube connecting the ships and deep into the residential heart of the flagship in under two minutes.

I hesitated. My hand hovered over the call button.

"My Lord?" Patricia asked.

"We walk," I said.

She stared at me. "Walk? It's three kilometers. We're late."

"Exactly," I said, turning down the corridor. "If we take the pod, we arrive in two minutes. If we walk, we arrive in twenty. That gives me eighteen more minutes to live."

I started walking. Nyxia hurried to catch up, her heels clicking on the deck plates. She didn't take my hand. She didn't lean into me. Instead, her hand reached out and gripped the cuff of my uniform collar. Her knuckles were white. She held on tight, staring straight ahead, her face a mask of stone. It wasn't romantic. It was the grip of someone on a rollercoaster who had just realized the safety bar was loose.

I looked at her. The Ice Queen of House Black, who could stare down a torture master, was trembling. Just a little.

"Ready?" I asked softly.

"No," she whispered back.

. . .

We arrived at the entrance to the pocket dimension twenty minutes later. I had used the time to compose my eulogy, edit it, and then discard it because nothing I wrote sounded pathetic enough to earn forgiveness.

The transition was always jarring—stepping from a hallway of cold, humming steel into a world of perpetual, golden afternoon sunlight.

The air here smelled of blooming jasmine and fresh grass, a stark lie compared to the ozone and oil of the ship. We walked down the path toward the gazebo that sat on a rolling green hill overlooking the simulated river.

My parents were there. My father was reading a data-slate, looking relaxed. My mother was arranging silverware with the precision of a surgeon.

My boots felt like lead. Kaelus, sensing the mood, shrank down to his smallest possible size and zipped behind my legs, using my calves as a shield. I’m not here, he projected. I’m a ghost. A figment of your imagination.

"Brother!"

The shout broke the silence. Lyra launched herself from the gazebo steps, a missile of silver hair and pink fabric. She slammed into my legs, wrapping her arms around my knees and looking up with wide, accusatory sapphire eyes.

"You left the story on a cliffhanger!" she demanded, ignoring the terrified woman clinging to my collar. "What happened to the Citrus Dragon? Did he eat the bad knight? You said 'and then' and you never came back!"

In truth, she had fallen asleep before I could finish the sentence, but logic didn't work on five-year-olds.

"He... he negotiated a peace treaty," I improvised weakly. "Lyra, let go. I have to face the boss."

"Boring!" she huffed, but she didn't let go. She looked at Nyxia, then at Patricia. Her eyes narrowed. "Why does Patricia look like she's gonna throw up?"

Before I could answer, my mother’s voice floated down from the gazebo. It was light, airy, and carried the stopping power of a railgun.

"Alarion! Finally. You know how I feel about cold soup."

She descended the steps, her gown rustling softly. She smiled, but the smile didn't reach her eyes. Her gaze swept over me, checking for injuries, then slid to Nyxia. Her eyes locked on Nyxia's hand, still clutching my collar.

The smile faltered. Then it brightened, cranking up to a level of wattage that was visibly painful.

"And you brought Nyxia!" she exclaimed, clapping her hands together. "How wonderful. I was just telling Kaelen that you two needed to spend more time together outside of... whatever it is you do with the robots."

She walked over, beaming. "That's a lovely grip you have there, dear. Very possessive. I approve. Are we announcing an engagement? Is that it? Did he finally ask?"

She looked between us, her excitement building. "Oh, Kaelen, look! They're blushing! It's finally happening!"

I felt the sweat trickle down my back. This was it. The bandage had to come off. I wanted to run. I wanted to call in an airstrike on my own position.

"Mother," I said. My voice cracked. I cleared my throat and tried again. "It's not an engagement."

My mother paused. "No?"

"No."

I took a deep breath. I looked at Nyxia, who looked ready to phase through the floor. I looked at my father, who was watching with mild amusement, sipping his tea.

"Mother," I said. "This is my wife."

In the background, there was a sharp splutter. My father sprayed his tea across the table and dropped the cup.

My mother stood there. She didn't move. She didn't blink. She just stared at me, the smile frozen on her face like a mask that didn't fit anymore.

"Your... what?"

"My wife," I repeated, the words rushing out now. "We... it was tactical. Strategic. We needed House Black's full support immediately to secure the food distribution network and destabilize the Hegemony's command structure without bloodshed, and Morpheus was willing to back us, but the council required a binding contract and—"

"Alarion."

Her voice was soft. Quiet.

"Yes?"

"You got married."

"Yes."

"Without me."

"It was... sudden."

"Without. Me."

She turned slowly toward Patricia. It was the movement of a turret tracking a target.

"Patricia," she said.

Patricia dropped to one knee instantly, head bowed, surrendering to execution. "I tried to stop him, Your Grace. I logged a formal protest. I prepared the tea."

My mother looked back at me. The smile was gone. In its place was a look of such profound, crushing disappointment that I actually wished I were back fighting the Void Terror. At least the Void Terror just wanted to kill me. It didn't want to make me feel like I’d kicked a puppy.

"My only son," she whispered. "My baby boy. Got hitched in a Gurden."

"It was a courtyard," I corrected helpfully.

"A COURTYARD!"

The volume shift was impressive. Bob jumped three feet in the air. Kaelus whimpered and tried to burrow under Lyra's dress.

My mother took a deep breath, her hands trembling. "I have been planning your wedding since you were three days old, Alarion. I have a binder. A binder, Alarion! I have color swatches! I have a list of cakes categorized by density and moisture content! And you... You eloped?"

"We didn't elope," I said, retreating a step. "We just... did the vows. Fast. To save the kingdom."

"To save the kingdom," she mocked, advancing on me. "You have an army of giant robots! You can save the kingdom whenever you want! You stole my wedding!"

She stopped in front of Nyxia. Nyxia, who had faced down assassins and political rivals, looked like a deer in headlights.

"And you," my mother said, her eyes narrowing. "You let him? You let this... this engineer convince you to skip the most important day of a girl's life for... what? Logistics?"

Nyxia opened her mouth. Closed it. "The grain shipments were... very important."

My father walked over, wiping tea off his chin. "Now, Seraphine," he started, putting a brave hand on her shoulder. "It's done. And it's a strong alliance. We should be happy—"

She spun on him. "Happy? Kaelen, our son, is married, and I wasn't there to cry! I wasn't there to fix his tie! I didn't get to glare at the bride's father until he cried! This is a disaster!"

She turned back to me, the fire in her eyes cooling into something far more dangerous: calculation.

"This won't do," she stated. "No. Absolutely not. I reject this reality."

"Mother, the World Spirit accepted the vow," I pointed out. "It's legally binding."

"I don't care what the World Spirit thinks!" she snapped. "The World Spirit didn't change your diapers! This... this secret little ritual doesn't count. We are doing it again."

I blinked. "What?"

"A renewal," she declared. "Immediately. Right now. I want to see it. If I don't see it, it didn't happen."

She pointed to the gazebo. "Get up there."

"Mother, we're in the middle of a war council—"

"GET UP THERE."

I gulped. I looked at Nyxia. She looked back at me with a mix of horror and helplessness.

"We should go," she whispered.

We walked up the steps to the gazebo like prisoners ascending the scaffold. My father hurried to stand behind us, acting as the officiant because he was the only one brave enough to move.

My mother stood at the bottom of the steps, arms crossed, tapping her foot. Patricia stood beside her, looking like she’d just survived a firing squad. Lyra was bouncing up and down, clutching Kaelus, who was peeking out through her fingers.

"Well?" my mother barked. "Go on. Say the things."

I stood facing Nyxia. The setting sun cast long shadows across the garden. It should have been romantic. Instead, it felt like a hostage negotiation.

"I..." I started. My throat was dry.

"Louder!" my mother commanded. "With feeling! Pretend you aren't a robot for five minutes!"

I took Nyxia's hands. They were ice cold. Her face was flushed bright red, a shade that clashed violently with her crimson eyes.

"Nyxia," I said, trying to block out the audience. "I... I ask you to stand with me."

"Skip to the good part!" Lyra yelled. "Do the kiss!"

"Quiet, Lyra!" I hissed. I looked back at Nyxia. I had to say the lines. The lines I’d said in the dark, when it was just us. Saying them here, in the cold light of day, felt like stripping naked.

"I need you," I said, my voice rough. "I can build the walls, but I need you to make it a home. I can fight the war, but I need you to give me a reason to come back. Will you... accept me?"

Nyxia stared at me. Her lip trembled. She looked past me at my mother, who was watching with hawk-like intensity, then back at me.

"I..." She choked. "I do."

"And?" my mother prompted. "The rest of it? The part about his emotional stuntedness?"

Nyxia took a breath. "And I promise to... to tell you when you're being an idiot. Because you are blind to everything that isn't made of metal."

"Good!" my mother shouted. "That's the spirit! Now kiss her!"

I leaned in. It was awkward. Our noses bumped. It was a quick, chaste press of lips that lasted about half a second before we pulled apart, both of us burning with embarrassment.

"Acceptable," my mother judged. "For a first draft."

She marched up the steps, grabbing Nyxia’s arm with a grip of iron.

"Now," she said, her voice dropping to a terrifyingly efficient tone. "That was the rehearsal. The real wedding—the public wedding—will happen in one month. I don't care if the continent is on fire. I don't care if the sky is falling. We are having a reception."

She looked at me. "You will provide the venue. I want something impressive. And Nyxia and I will handle the rest. You are banned from planning. If I see a single robot holding a bouquet, I will dismantle it myself."

She began dragging Nyxia away toward the castle. "Come, dear. We have much to discuss. The dress you're wearing is practical, but for the ceremony, we need impact. We need drama."

Nyxia cast a desperate look back at me, her hand reaching out. Save me, her eyes screamed.

I didn't move. I was a coward. I let her go.

My mother stopped and turned back one last time. Her gaze landed on Lyra and Kaelus.

"And you," she said to the dragon.

Kaelus froze. Me?

"You are spending too much time in Alarion's room," she declared. "A newlywed couple needs privacy. Even if they are emotionally constipated."

She pointed a finger at Lyra. "From now on, the dragon sleeps in your room."

Lyra’s eyes went wide. Then she exploded.

"YAAAAAAAAY!"

She tackled Kaelus. The dragon let out a mental squawk of despair as he was crushed in a hug. Betrayal! Abandonment! Brother, save me! She drools in her sleep!

Sorry, buddy, I projected, feeling a wave of immense relief wash over me. It’s for the greater good.

My father walked over, clapping me on the shoulder. He handed me a flask.

"Drink," he said. "You survived."

I took a long pull. It burned, but it settled my nerves.

"Is she always like that?" I asked.

"Only when she loves you," he said, watching his wife drag my new wife into a planning hell. "War is easy, son. Armies are simple. They just want to kill you."

He shook his head.

"But mothers? Mothers want you to be happy. And they will break the world to make it happen."

I looked at the retreating figures. Nyxia was nodding frantically as my mother gestured at the sky, presumably redesigning the stars to match the table settings.

I had faced down necromancers. I had nuked armies. I had built a Titan out of steel.

But as I stood there in the garden, watching the women of House Wight plot my future, I realized one thing with absolute certainty.

I was terrified.

And, strangely, I was happy.

"Come on," my father said. "Let's go look at that reactor of yours. I need to hide before she remembers I haven't picked a suit for the reception."

"Good plan," I said.

We turned and walked back toward the workshop, two soldiers retreating from the only force they couldn't defeat.
