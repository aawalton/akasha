As the evening wore on and the heavy, iron cauldrons of stew were gradually scraped clean, the atmosphere within the makeshift tavern shifted dramatically. The immediate, crushing terror of the Kobold invasion began to fully recede from the collective psyche of the surviving townsfolk and adventurers, replaced by the giddy, reckless, and deeply intoxicating euphoria of simply being alive.

For Josh, Bhel, and Brett, the transition from exhausted silence to boisterous celebration was a rapid one. The sheer volume of dark, bitter ale they were consuming certainly helped. With every sloshing tankard downed, the knot of stress that had been pulled taut in their chests over the past forty-eight hours loosened a fraction more. The phantom aches of brutalised muscles faded beneath a warm, heavy blanket of inebriation.

A revolving door of soot-stained, bandage-wrapped locals descended upon their table, demanding to buy the town 'Heroes' a drink. Not wanting to offend, and frankly far too relieved to care, the trio accepted every single one.

The night quickly devolved into a loud, messy blur of newfound camaraderie. Bhel, his stoic dwarven reserve completely dissolved by his seventh pint, found himself locked in a fierce, red-faced arm-wrestling tournament with a massive, one-eyed local blacksmith. A crowd of cheering, heavily intoxicated survivors had formed a ring around them, slamming their tankards on the tables in rhythm.

Brett, meanwhile, had entirely abandoned his usual haughtiness. His pristine robes were stained with spilled ale, and he was currently leaning against a structural pillar, loudly and passionately explaining the deeply complex, highly theoretical mechanics of fire manipulation to a group of local archers who were far too drunk to understand a single word, but were cheering enthusiastically anyway. At one point, he tried to demonstrate a minor light cantrip, completely lost control of his mana, and accidentally set a nearby decorative tapestry briefly, mildly on fire, which only earned him a roaring round of applause.

Josh just sat back, his massive frame taking up two chairs, a broad, genuine grin plastered across his face. He traded exaggerated war stories with a group of veteran guardsmen, laughing so hard at their morbid, gallows humour that his bruised ribs actively protested. It was a chaotic, beautiful release. They weren't thinking about portals, or monsters, or the crushing weight of their responsibilities. For a few glorious hours, they were just three men drinking away the darkness.

Inevitably, however, the adrenaline crash caught up with the alcohol. Sometime in the deep, pitch-black hours of the early morning, the tavern finally quieted down, the floor becoming a sea of snoring, unconscious bodies. The boys didn't even make it back to their corner table.

"Get up, you absolute degenerates."

The command was sharp, crisp, and entirely devoid of sympathy. It was immediately followed by a solid, remarkably painful kick to Josh’s ribs.

Josh groaned, a deep, rattling sound that vibrated in his chest. He tried to open his eyes, but the morning sunlight streaming through the shattered tavern windows felt like physical daggers stabbing directly into his retinas. His mouth tasted like stale copper, old mud, and regret. His head pounded with the rhythmic, punishing intensity of a dwarven forge hammer.

He cracked one eye open, wincing against the glare. Carcan was standing over him, her arms crossed tight over her chest. She had clearly managed to clean herself up; the blood and gore were gone, her white robes were magically pristine once more, and her hair was back in its severely neat braid. Beside her stood Perberos, looking entirely serene, impeccably rested, and deeply unimpressed with the scene before him.

"Five more minutes," Josh rasped, his voice sounding like two pieces of rough sandpaper grinding together. He tried to roll over, only to realise he was lying awkwardly half-underneath a heavy oak table. His cheek was resting against the sticky, ale-soaked floorboards.

"It is two hours past dawn," Carcan stated flatly, delivering another swift, measured kick, this time aiming for Bhel, who was curled up in a ball near the cold hearth, snoring loudly enough to rattle the remaining windowpanes. "The quartermaster has been open since sunrise. We have business to attend to, and I will not be kept waiting by three men who smell like a brewery explosion."

"My skull is actively attempting to secede from the rest of my body," a muffled, pathetic voice groaned from beneath a pile of empty burlap grain sacks nearby. Brett slowly emerged, his hair sticking up in wild, chaotic directions, his face a pale, sickly shade of grey. He clutched his head with both hands, squinting at the light. "I believe I am dying. Carcan, as a healer, I formally request that you end my suffering. A quick, painless death, if you please. Or failing that, can you spare a cure spell?"

"Your alcohol-induced misery is entirely self-inflicted, Brett," Carcan replied, though the tiniest hint of an amused smile tugged at the corner of her mouth. "I do not waste precious mana on hangovers. Now get up. All of you. Before I ask Perberos to start pouring buckets of cold well water over your heads."

"I have the buckets ready," the elf noted mildly, gesturing to two wooden pails resting by the doorway.

That threat was enough to finally spur them into motion. It was a slow, agonising, and highly undignified process, involving a lot of groaning, swearing, and leaning heavily against the walls for support, but eventually, the trio managed to peel themselves off the floor and stagger out into the cool morning air.

The town looked different in the stark, unforgiving light of day. The piles of golden monster dust had largely been swept into massive, glittering mounds by the morning work crews, leaving the cobblestones relatively clean, though the deep scorch marks and shattered masonry of the inner wall remained a glaring testament to the violence. The air still smelled faintly of ozone and woodsmoke, but the cloying scent of death had thankfully dissipated on the morning breeze.

They made their way slowly towards the temporary guild headquarters, which had been set up in a large, relatively intact merchant's manor on the edge of the central square.

The queue for the quartermaster was surprisingly short, likely because most of the local adventurers were either still unconscious, wounded, or actively packing their bags to leave. When they finally stepped up to the heavy mahogany desk, the man sitting behind it looked like he hadn't slept in a week. He was surrounded by towering stacks of parchment, ledgers, and overflowing bags of monster cores.

"Name and party," the quartermaster grunted, not even looking up from his ledger, a quill scratching furiously across the page.

"Josh and... well, we don't really have a formal party name yet," Josh replied, his voice still rough. He reached into his spatial pouch and unceremoniously dumped a massive, clattering pile of Kobold ears, broken runic trinkets, and the dark, dense core of the vanguard boss onto the desk.

The quartermaster’s quill stopped. He slowly raised his head, taking in Josh’s battered plate armour, Bhel’s grim expression, and the sheer volume of high-level defensive spoils resting on his desk. His eyes widened slightly as they locked onto the boss core.

"Right," the quartermaster said, his tone instantly shifting from bureaucratic annoyance to deep, genuine respect. He stood up, pushing his ledger aside. "Word came down from the militia captain. You guys were key in fighting off the breakout wave. Bought enough time for others to join, likely helped save the town."

He didn't bother counting the individual ears. He carefully picked up the boss core, inspected it for a brief moment, and then reached under his desk. He hauled up a heavy, remarkably chunky leather bag that clinked with a deep, thoroughly satisfying metallic weight, and dropped it onto the desk with a heavy thud.

"That’s your bounty payout, plus a significant hazard bonus authorised directly by the guild," the quartermaster explained, pushing the heavy bag towards Josh. "It's a small fortune, frankly. You’ve earned every copper. Furthermore, I’ve been instructed to update your profiles."

He paused, leaning forward slightly, his voice dropping to a more confidential volume. "And unofficially? I’d expect a formal rank advancement for the lot of you. You pulled off a Silver-rank feat out there."

"We appreciate that," Josh said, genuinely surprised by the generosity of the payout. He swept the heavy coin purse into his inventory, the weight of the gold doing wonders to briefly alleviate his headache. "Thank you."

"Don't thank me. Thank whatever Gods gave you the sheer, unbridled stubbornness to hold that wall," the quartermaster muttered, already pulling his ledger back towards him. "Next!"

With their pockets considerably heavier, the party left the manor and walked the short distance to the shattered ruins of the inner wall. The scene here was vastly different from the chaotic, blood-soaked nightmare of the previous few days. The portal that had threatened to consume the town was no longer a violent, blinding vortex of aggressive purple energy. Instead, it had settled into a calm, humming, deeply stable oval of mid-blue light. It looked less like a tear in reality and more like a serene, heavily enchanted doorway.

Sitting on a large chunk of shattered masonry about twenty feet from the portal was Hopeless.

The "administrator" looked exactly as he had the day before, wrapped in his worn, unassuming cloak, his staff resting casually across his knees. He hadn't moved. It was entirely obvious he had sat there, keeping a silent, solitary vigil over the dimensional gateway, for the entire night.

He looked up as the party approached, a small, tired smile touching his weathered face.

"Morning, kids," Hopeless greeted them, his voice calm, though his eyes bore the deep, profound exhaustion of a man who had seen too many battles. "You look like absolute hell. Tavern floor?"

"Under a table, actually," Brett admitted, rubbing his temples gingerly. "The floorboards were remarkably sticky."

"The mark of a good victory," Hopeless chuckled softly.

"You've been here all night," Perberos noted, his sharp elven eyes scanning the older man for signs of injury. "Is the rift stable?"

"It is, well mostly," Hopeless nodded, gesturing casually towards the blue light. "Stable, and entirely manageable. Terry has been running the thing non-stop. The mad bastard hasn't taken a single break. He's just aggressively slaughtering his way through the floors, bleeding off the ambient mana of the dungeon before it can build up enough pressure to spark another surge."

"By himself?" Bhel asked, his thick eyebrows shooting up in surprise.

"Mostly," Hopeless replied. "A few of the veteran local parties woke up a few hours ago, saw the rift was stable, and decided to join him inside. Back to business as usual for some I guess. The more mana they burn up in there, the safer this town gets. Soon this place will be back to being a standard, managed delve site. The town will recover, though I imagine there will be a lot more guards for a fair while… but that will likely be the case everywhere."

Carcan stepped forward, her expression professional and deeply serious. "I wanted to give you an update on the wounded, Hopeless."

Hopeless gave her his full attention. "Go ahead."

"The physical casualties are stabilised," Carcan reported, her voice carrying the quiet, confident authority of her profession. "Everyone who made it off the wall alive is largely back to one hundred percent physical capacity. A few lasting aches and pains, a few scars that the magic couldn't completely erase, but no one else is going to die from their physical injuries today."

She paused, a shadow crossing her pale face. "However... the mental trauma is severe. Half the militia has the thousand-yard stare. The civilians who were trapped near the inner wall are terrified of their own shadows. I did what I could, but my magic knits flesh, not minds. I've handed over primary care to the Sisters. They are much better equipped for spiritual and mental soothing. But this town... it's going to carry the psychological scars of this breakout for a generation."

Hopeless sighed, a deep, heavy sound that seemed to carry the weight of the world. He looked out over the ruined plaza, at the townspeople slowly, fearfully picking their way through the rubble.

"I know," he said softly. "I've seen it happen once or twice before, though not to the scale of what is happening in the world at the moment. You can rebuild the walls, you can heal the broken bones, but the fear... the fear lingers for a fair while. It takes a while to wash the terror out of the cobblestones. You did excellent work, Carcan. Better than anyone had a right to expect."

"Which brings us to our next move," Josh interjected, his tone shifting back to business. "We’ve collected our bounties. The town is physically secure. There isn’t much left for us to do here that the other parties can't handle as they continue to level. We're planning to return to Ashenfall."

"It's where we can do the most good," Perberos added, his eyes narrowed against the morning sun. "Especially if we can help the caravan arrive safe."

"Aye," Bhel rumbled, crossing his thick arms over his barrel chest. "I did a lot of talking last night between the arm-wrestling and the drinking. Found out quite a bit. There’s going to be at least a hundred people leaving this town. Maybe more. Mostly civilians who stayed behind to help fortify the walls, or those who were simply too terrified to run on the first day. There’s a few broken adventurer parties going with them too, the ones who lost members and have lost their taste for delving."

Bhel spat a glob of phlegm into the dirt. "They’re terrified the rift will tear open again. They're packing up everything they own and heading for the safety of Ashenfall's high walls. They're forming a massive, slow-moving caravan. We're going to escort them."

Hopeless looked at the dwarf, then looked at the rest of the party, a look of profound, quiet respect settling over his features.

"That is a very good thing to do," Hopeless said sincerely. "A convoy that size, full of terrified, exhausted civilians... it’s a prime target for rogue beast packs, or anything else looking for an easy meal on the long road. They’ll need a strong vanguard. And frankly, I can't think of a better group of hard-headed bastards for the job."

"What about you and Terry?" Brett asked, leaning slightly on his staff for support as a fresh wave of nausea washed over him. "Will you stay here to manage the rift?"

"No," Hopeless shook his head, leaning heavily on his staff as he slowly pushed himself up to a standing position. His joints popped audibly. "My job here is done. Terry will run the dungeon for another few hours to ensure the mana levels are completely neutralised, get it nice and light blue, but then we're moving on. There are reports of severe mana fluctuations and broken portals tearing open all over."

Hopeless looked out towards the horizon, his expression hardening into a mask of grim determination. "Something is stirring up the ambient magic across the entire continent. Terry and I... we're going hunting. We need to find the source of these fluctuations before any more towns get swallowed."

The gravity of his words hung heavily in the air. The Kobold invasion hadn't been a freak accident of nature; it was a symptom of a much larger, continent-spanning problem. Josh felt a cold knot form in his stomach that had nothing to do with the ale he had consumed.

"When do you leave?" Josh asked quietly.

"Tomorrow morning, most likely," Hopeless replied, adjusting his worn cloak. "And you lot won’t be rushing your departure either. A caravan of a hundred people doesn't just pack up and leave overnight. They need to repair wagons, gather stores, and organise their draught animals. The convoy won't be ready to break camp and hit the road for at least a day or two is my bet."

Hopeless offered them one last, tired salute with his staff. "Take the downtime, lads. Rest your bones, fix your gear, and spend some of that heavy gold you just earned. You're going to need your strength for the road ahead. May the Gods watch over your path to Ashenfall."

"And yours," Josh replied, returning the salute with a respectful nod.

The party stood in silence for a long moment, the ruined plaza stretching out around them. The news of continent-wide portal tears was terrifying, but the immediate, practical reality of Hopeless's final words was slowly filtering into their exhausted, hungover brains.

A day or two of guaranteed, uninterrupted downtime. No fighting. No running. No immediate life-or-death decisions. Just a town slowly rebuilding, a heavy bag of gold in their pockets, and time to kill before the convoy departed.

Slowly, deliberately, Bhel turned his head. His broad, mud-caked face was perfectly deadpan, but his dark eyes sparkled with a sudden, intense, almost fanatic devotion. He looked at Carcan first, to Josh, then at Brett, and finally at Perberos.

"So..." the dwarf rumbled, his voice thick with unyielding hope. "...spa?"
