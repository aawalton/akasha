The next few minutes were the strangest of my entire life.

Instinctive fear reared its head when Velidan lifted the blade. I stepped back as he calmly looked at me, tossed the pouch to Slip… then carved a crimson gash into his empty palm.

The Fae stepped past me, knelt, and clenched his injured fist over the rune. Blood pattered to the stones. Slip opened the pouch and hopped around, sprinkling handfuls of glittering black dust atop the blood.

Then Velidan murmured, “Haven, felinor,” and rose. He sheathed the knife as silver flickered along his palm, sealing the wound.

The two of them looked for all the world as if the entire scene was perfectly normal.

It took me a moment to make my mouth work. “What in the realms are you doing?”

“Magic!” Slip chirped, as if it should have been obvious. As she answered, pale blue light swirled up from the blood-spattered rune. Then she winked at me, hopped into the glow, and vanished.

“Slip!” I gasped, startled but fairly certain that the goblin was safe. I glanced towards Velidan, who stood watching me with obvious amusement. “Is she alright?”

The light shimmered, tracing each plane of his face. “Of course. You’re next, mortal.”

I paced along the rune’s edge. “It’s a doorway, isn’t it? A portal? It goes somewhere else?”

“It does. Step in.”

Wide, eager eyes drank in the vivid hue, searching for some hint of whatever lay… beyond? Within? I wasn’t sure, and could see nothing but light.

“It won’t last long, Brin. Go.”

“How does it work?”

The glow dimmed and his voice hardened. “I dislike stabbing myself, Brin.”

“But will- wait!”

He didn’t. Strong hands closed over my hips and I was sent staggering forwards.

Blue light swallowed my world. With it came a rush of wind, the smell of ozone, and a sudden pop in my ears. Then, with a feeling reminiscent of surfacing from underwater, the sensations faded and muted colors streamed back into my vision.

I blinked, steadying both feet on solid ground and taking in my surroundings with a gasp. I was in an enormous, circular chamber ringed by pale stone walls and dense cascades of ivy. There were no windows and only one huge door. The room’s center held an iron brazier lit by smokeless fire.

Slip beamed up at me from several paces ahead. “Fun, right? Like riding a bolt of lightning!”

Blue flickered beneath and behind me, and a moment later I felt Velidan’s presence at my back.

I’d scarcely turned when more light flared nearby. Glancing over showed that nearly identical runes were carved every few paces into the floor, and a large one flashed briefly before-

I yelped as a large, four-legged shape appeared from thin air. Rust-red fur covered a lion-like body, with four claw-tipped paws the size of my head. A scaled scorpion’s tail snaked out on one end, and two leathery wings arched up from the monster’s back.

It turned at my cry, and the sheer humanness of its face made me shriek again. A lion’s dense red mane framed a wizened, deeply wrinkled expression of surprise. Tufted white eyebrows arched over blinking brown eyes. A broad, flat nose sloped down over pink lips open in a little ‘o’. It even had a scraggly white beard, and for some reason that fact shocked me more than everything else.

The creature looked me up and down, snorted, and grumbled something unintelligible before prowling towards the door.

“I believe you offended him.” Velidan rumbled at my ear. He sounded like he was trying not to laugh.

“What?” I squeaked, searching for more words but unable to find any. “I… what?”

“Dawn, pet, it’s just a manticore.” The goblin snickered, scampering towards the door, and called over her shoulder, “You’re too jumpy, you know that?”

“What?” I gaped after her.

The manticore seemed to be conversing with an unseen figure at the doorway. One great wing flexed in a lazy gesture and its scaled tail bobbed against the floor. Then, its conversation apparently concluded, the stone swung wide and the creature painstakingly shifted its bulk through the opening.

Velidan headed towards the door, chuckling, and I trotted after. My legs trembled with a mixture of fear and nervous, giddy anticipation.

We just magically jumped to somewhere else in a Fae realm. We’re in a room full of magical portals where monsters can just appear at a moment’s notice and… and… and we’re going shopping.

It was all so bizarre that I wanted to laugh. What came out was a strangled shriek, though, as a noseless face of bubbling stone formed in the now-closed door.

“Name and purpo- oh. An honor, my lord.” Its voice was a mixture of scraping stones and a serpent’s raspy hiss. Rocks ground together as the head dipped into a tiny bow. “Presiding?”

“No. Just leisure.”

“Understood. We are grateful.” The head dipped again, then sank back into stone as the door swung wide.

I scarcely had time to marvel at the head’s existence, or at its disappearance. My attention was immediately captured by what lay beyond the door.

Ivy-strewn walls, some crumbling and some intact, towered around a vast field. The space was dotted with wooden stalls, tents made of tanned hide, and colorful silk pavilions. Hundreds of figures moved among the structures, some of them vaguely human-shaped and some, like the manticore up ahead, clearly more bestial. Voices and music babbled from every direction.

I was vaguely aware of a deep voice saying my name, several times in fact, but was too busy spinning in a slow circle with my mouth wide open to answer.

Plumes of lavender smoke hung over everything. Little wisps of light in every shade imaginable floated through the air. One hovered right above us, and a dazed glance up revealed tiny gossamer wings fluttering within the glow.

The deep voice continued, said my name several more times, fell silent for a bit, laughed, and then a hand at my back nudged me forwards.

For once, I didn’t fight him.

Creatures wandered all around us. Figures and faces came into vivid focus as I continued my slow turns. I saw a man with the head of a bull sitting cross-legged in a wooden stall; a group of scaled, dog-like creatures cluttered around a large black cauldron; an unnaturally tall woman, every feature blurring and curling like smoke, writhing snakelike before a campfire; a stooped, faceless figure with far too many fingers weaving thread that gleamed like starlight; tiny, human-shaped men and women with feathered wings and jutting fangs cackling around what appeared to be a pile of animal bones.

It went on and on, each creature’s visage- some of them frightening, some beautiful, and many simply bizarre- capturing my attention in turn. Before I knew it we were beside one of the enormous walls. I placed a steadying hand against the stone, gasping in lungfuls of warm, pleasant-smelling air.

“Your task lies indoors, Slipssinia,” Velidan was saying.

Slip cackled, looking between us with glittering eyes. Then her gaze settled on me and, in a sly tone, “Any, uh, color preference, master?”

He scoffed.

One claw-tipped little hand waved her pouch through the air. “And the rest…?”

Full lips parted in a smile, and for once there was no edge to the expression. No sharpness, no mocking curve. “Have fun.”

She squealed and rushed away into the crowd.

“No!” It took me a moment to realize I’d cried aloud. I shook my head, still thoroughly dazed by my surroundings, and watched the goblin’s diminutive form vanish into a sea of much more intimidating figures. “Won’t she be in danger? She said other Fae hunt goblins.”

“Not here.” Velidan inclined his head. “This is a space dedicated to interspecies trade. Violence is forbidden.”

I scanned the crowd. A few creatures loomed and stalked, arrogant and ominous, but many more simply seemed… calm. Friendly. It occurred to me that I’d received quite a few curious glances, but no obvious malice, on our brief walk along the market’s edge.

Wings and laughter fluttered above us. I looked up and saw two glinting figures, like very large fireflies, zip past in what appeared to be a game of chase.

I watched the pair, grinning, until a grim thought came to mind. “Slip said there were other places like this. Moonlight Markets.” I let my gaze fall, tracing the outline of a nearby stall and a gloomy-looking figure beneath it. “And that they…”

“Ended disastrously.” Velidan finished coldly. Then he pushed off the wall and began to walk along it, clearly expecting me to follow.

I did. “What happened?”

“Well, mortal, consider what I’ve told you about the Nightmare, and consider what it means for the state of this realm. Most of us spent centuries in a state of perpetual fear. Many Fae went mad.”

“Saints.”

“You’re looking at remnants of a hundred different species.” He paused, sweeping a glare across the field. “And many more went fully extinct. You can imagine the impact on the economy.”

I swallowed, wrapping both arms around my middle. “So… other places like this formed after the Nightmare died? So that surviving Fae could trade with each other? But… ashes, but-”

“But many of them were still half-mad, and the strong prey on the weak. It’s the way of things. The first markets ended in carnage.”

I didn’t try to hide my horror. “That’s terrible! Why is this one different?”

He regarded me for a long while, silver-lit eyes gleaming as brightly as the stars above. “Because some High Fae have a vested interest in this realm’s regrowth. Collaborative trade is the heartbeat of that growth, so we take turns visiting. Presiding.”

Regrowth. Salt, he almost makes all this sound… noble. “Presiding.” My nose scrunched. “That’s what the, er, face in the door asked you about. What does it mean?”

Velidan’s smile was slow, and this time there was a definite edge to it. “It means every creature here behaves themselves very well, or has their spine ripped out.”

“Right. Saints.” Perhaps it’s not as noble as he pretends. Perhaps the actual appeal is ripping something’s spine out. I mulled for a moment, then asked, “Because you’re all in charge of everything? Of the realm?”

“Because we’re powerful enough to kill anything that disagrees with us.” He paused, thought over the statement, and resumed walking with a dark laugh. “So, yes.”

I trailed along, frowning at the back of his head. “But what if you make a mistake? Who keeps you accountable?”

He just laughed again.

My frown deepened. “And what’s to stop a High Fae from abusing their power? What if one of you shows up and decides to… ashes, I don’t know, eat everyone? Take all the goods for themselves?”

“Don’t be ridiculous, Brin. Most of us take the responsibility seriously, and the lesser Fae are grateful for our presence. We’re even reserved a place of honor.” He gestured in the direction Slip had gone.

I followed the path of his hand and found myself looking upwards. The enormous stone wall, though crumbling in places, rose into an impressive circular tower in one area. Its top was a crown-like shadow looming high above the haze of smoke.

I gazed up at the structure, then found myself turning in another slow circle. But this time, rather than examining the crowd of Fae, I looked at the wall. A few other towers rose from the stone, but all were caved in and ended in masses of broken rock. Across the field there was a wide gap in the wall, with an enormous interior space beyond. Shadowed halls flickered with more wisps and the occasional glint of starlight off of cloth or stone. Another gap sat behind us, though this one was mostly sealed by an enormous slab of wood held upright by black iron chains.

It almost looked like…

“We’re in a castle!” I exclaimed, whirling towards Velidan. “Aren’t we? This is a giant courtyard!”

He chuckled. “An ancient and ruined castle, but yes. No one’s bothered to remember the name. It’s simply referred to as Haven.”

“Wow. I’m in a castle. A magical marketplace in a castle. Royce will never believe this.” I bit down on a grin, shaking my head and glancing towards the drawbridge. “There’s only a few castles in our realm, you know. He’s always wanted to go see one of them. Do you come here very often?”

“When the need arises.” Velidan paced along the wall, then stopped at a place where thick roots had split the stone. Broad, dark wood was visible through each crack. “My most recent request had to be commissioned. It wasn’t ready when I wanted, but will serve its purpose now.”

“Its purpose?” I cocked my head to the side.

Fathomless eyes flicked to mine as one hand pressed into a knobby, dark mass of wood. The structure shifted with a heavy groan, opening like a narrow door straight into the wall. “Keeping you asleep, Brin.” Every word was low and made the night air around me grow colder.

“You… you wanted to keep me asleep? Wait, before now?” One foot trailed back unbidden. Fresh unease twisted through my insides. “Why? Saints, when?”

“Several nights ago.” His smile held a dark edge, and his tone matched it. “When I found out you could kill me on a whim.”
