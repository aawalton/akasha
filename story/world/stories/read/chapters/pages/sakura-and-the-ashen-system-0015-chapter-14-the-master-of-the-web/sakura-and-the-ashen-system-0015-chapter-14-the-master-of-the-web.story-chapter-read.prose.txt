“Don’t move,” Gilbert said quietly, but very clearly. “And no aggression. At all.”

Sakura had no intention of moving even without him. Her whole body was already wound into a tight, icy spring. She stood there without taking her eyes off the cave, and Pampli had gone still beside her too.

Spider legs slowly emerged from the cave. First one pair, then the second. Then the upper half of a body appeared out of the darkness. In some ways, the creature looked human. It was tall and thin, with a pale face and narrow features that might have seemed beautiful if not for the cold otherness of its whole appearance. Its skin was grayish white, as if it had never seen the sun. Its hair was long and silver, falling in tangled strands over its shoulders and partly clinging to the dark fabric of its garment, which looked more like layers of old silk than ordinary clothing. But the face and the clothes were not the main thing.

Long spider limbs grew from its back, above the shoulder blades, as a natural extension of its body. Four pairs of dark, predatory legs supported it better than human legs ever could, and it moved as if this shape was the only true one for it. The human part seemed like nothing more than a mask, something convenient for speech. The creature’s eyes were strange, completely black, glossy and depthless, without any visible pupil at all. There was no fear in them. Quite the opposite. An ancient irritation, the kind carried by someone forced once again to pay attention to the world of the living. That was how it felt to Sakura.

Sakura instinctively focused on it more sharply, and after a brief flicker, the System gave her only two lines:

SAOREXRace: Arachnoborn

There was no level, just as she had expected. But Sakura could clearly feel the powerful, ancient force coming from this being. Saorex was not just strong. He was old, so old that he seemed to look at people as a temporary inconvenience.

The creature stepped a little farther out of the shadows and stopped on a stone in front of the cave entrance, studying them with a grim kind of interest that promised nothing good.

“S-so what have you come for?” he said at last, and his voice was rough, with a faint hiss on certain consonants, as if the words had to be pushed through a different anatomy. “And why have you dis-sturbed my rest? The fores-st is already full of unrest. And human foots-steps-s bring even more.”

He spoke slowly, as if tasting each word first.

“Who are you? Prey? Fools? Or di-s-saster?”

Gilbert stepped slightly forward without any sudden movement. He held himself cold and deliberately confident, as if trying to become harder than stone.

“Good evening, Saorex,” he said. “If you haven’t forgotten, you owe me a debt.”

The Arachnoborn’s dark eyes narrowed.

“I remember debts better than humans remember their dead,” he replied. “But I owe you, Gilbert. Not anyone else.”

He said the last words after shifting his gaze to Sakura. The look was so unpleasant and so long that Sakura wanted to take a step back. But she held herself still. She only whispered very quietly to Gilbert:

“Maybe we should just leave and find another shelter?”

“Everything is under control,” he replied just as quickly and quietly, without taking his eyes off Saorex. “Easy.”

Pampli let out a low, warning growl.

Sakura lowered her gaze without meaning to, and only then noticed the cocoons beneath the trees near the cave entrance. A lot of them. Some were small, no bigger than a fist, while others were already the size of a human head. Whitish, half transparent in places, they hung on strands between roots and stones or lay in hollowed-out spaces, neatly gathered, like someone’s future provisions. A chill ran down her spine so hard at the sight that the words died on her tongue.

Gilbert, as if noticing none of it, kept speaking:

“We only need to stay here for a little while. Not long. We won’t cause any trouble.”

Saorex shifted one of his rear legs and moved a little closer.

“‘We won’t cause any trouble,’” he drawled with lazy contempt. “One of my favorite human phra-s-ses. It always turns out to be a lie.”

And in the next second, he moved too fast. Sakura did not even understand how. One moment he was standing at the cave entrance, and then suddenly he was right in front of her, face to face, so close that she could see the thin web of nearly invisible silver veins beneath his skin and catch the damp, cold smell of spider silk. The spider limbs behind his back lifted and spread slightly, like a huge black fan.

Sakura’s breath caught, and she could not move. It felt as if she would die instantly if she shifted even an inch. From the corner of her eye, she saw Pampli already tensing to lunge at Saorex, and Sakura almost blindly thrust a hand back, stopping the beast from moving.

“No,” she whispered. “Don’t.”

They definitely did not need that.

Saorex looked at her as if he was not simply studying her face, but reading something beneath her skin. His gaze slid across her eyes, then lower, to her throat, and then returned to her eyes. Very slowly, he drew half a step back and turned his head toward Gilbert.

“What have you brought here?” he asked, almost with disgust. “This is not a good sign. My peace will be broken.”

Gilbert did not flinch.

“It won’t be,” he said firmly. “We’ll stop here only for a short while. We need some sleep, a chance to discuss a plan, and a way to put together basic gear for Sakura. Then we’ll move on.”

Sakura blinked and turned toward him. She did not fully understand what kind of gear he was talking about. Gilbert caught the look and, unable to help himself, glanced at her.

“Don’t take it personally,” he said, almost like a joke. “But right now you’re wearing some kind of rags that won’t do anything to improve your chances of survival.”

Sakura rolled her eyes. It was hard to argue with that. After the arena, the escape, the dirt, and the forest, she probably did look less like the heroine of some grand journey and more like a person life had spent half a day slamming into every available surface. And if she was being completely honest, her chances of survival really could use something besides a black cloak.

Saorex stayed silent for a long time. A very long time. His gaze shifted from Sakura to Gilbert, then to Pampli, then back to Sakura, as if weighing a heavy decision. The silence only made everything worse.

At last, he said:

“I will help you.”

Sakura did not believe at first that she had heard him correctly.

“I will let you into my caves,” the Arachnoborn continued. “But the girl must prove that she de-s-serves it. Of Gilbert, I am certain. Of her, I am not.”

Gilbert let out an irritated breath through his nose. He clearly did not like that, but arguing with a creature that had just appeared in her face on spider legs would have been the height of idiocy even by their current standards.

“Her life must not be in danger,” he said firmly.

Saorex nodded slowly.

“It won’t be.”

Sakura looked from one to the other. Apparently, “shelter” now came with terms and conditions.

“So I’m getting another trial now?” she asked.

“You could s-say that,” Saorex replied calmly. “And you must pass it alone. Without any help.”

“Of course,” Sakura muttered.

Saorex tilted his head slightly to the side, as if irony as a concept interested him, but did not particularly impress him.

“Do you agree?” he asked.

Sakura nodded uncertainly. The main thing was that her life would not be in danger. At least if a creature with cocoons full of future spiderlings behind him could be trusted.

“G-good,” said Saorex.

Then he deliberately turned toward the largest cave and pointed one of his long legs at the dark entrance veiled in strands of webbing.

“Welcome.”

Gilbert exchanged a look with Sakura. His expression clearly said: we have no choice. Sakura’s said about the same, only with a very sincere desire to be literally anywhere else.

But they moved forward anyway.

Pampli, clearly not thrilled, followed after them, growling low and unhappily under his breath. Sakura took a deep breath and stepped toward the black opening of the cave. The darkness inside met them like a host that had been waiting too long.

But that darkness did not last long. Soon, a soft light began to illuminate the inside of the cave. In the black walls, between the layers of stone, veins of some unfamiliar mineral ran through the rock, and it was that mineral that gave off a crystal-white glow. It was very strange. The light did not flicker like torchlight, but seemed to live inside the stone on its own. Because of that, the whole cave felt beautiful and eerie at the same time, as if they had entered some ancient lair.

The farther they went, the more noticeable the webbing became. And too many things were stuck in it, things that had no business being here at all. Sakura first spotted an old halberd hanging almost vertically, tightly bound to the web by its shaft. A little farther ahead, a child’s toy stuck out from the threads, faded, with one arm torn off. Farther still, she saw little statues, some kind of dishes, a crushed fishing float, scraps of netting, a broken wooden frame, and a lot of other human junk, absurd in a place like this. It looked as though the cave had been slowly and patiently gathering other people’s offerings, like a miserly collector.

Sakura slowed her pace, looking over all of it, and suddenly her eyes caught on three items lying a little out of sight, but still impossible not to look at longer. A music box, darkened with age, with a note carved into the lid. Beside it, a folded fan, once clearly beautiful, now a little worn, but still elegant. And also a spider-shaped brooch, dark, with a dull metallic sheen. Sakura stepped closer, stopped beside them, and just let her gaze run over them without daring to touch anything. For some reason, those were the things that caught her eye most, and she did not understand it herself.

Saorex had been watching her carefully the entire time. He had not said a word, but Sakura could feel his gaze almost physically. It was mildly irritating. Even more irritating was the thought that came to her on its own: for a creature that so openly disliked humans, he had a suspicious amount of human belongings in his cave. A funny paradox. Almost amusing, if everything around them had not been so unsettling.

Gilbert noticed her pause too, but said nothing. Pampli walked a little behind them, moving unexpectedly quietly for such a large beast. He clearly did not feel relaxed here.

At last, Saorex stopped and turned his head toward Sakura.

“The trial begins-s-s now,” he said evenly. “From here on, you come with me.”

Sakura tensed, though she tried not to show it. Gilbert immediately stepped forward as if about to object, but Saorex did not even let him begin.

“You s-stay here. The beast too.”

Pampli let out another low growl, louder this time, clearly not appreciating being pushed aside so casually. Sakura turned and looked first at him, then at Gilbert. She really did not like this idea, but arguing right now would have been pointless.

“It’ll be fine,” she said more to herself than to them. “Probably.”

Sakura threw Gilbert a brief glance. He looked grim, but not like he intended to interfere. Apparently, he understood that there was no choice anyway. She nodded to him, then looked at Pampli and gently touched his neck with her hand.

“Wait for me, okay?”

The beast huffed, twitched an ear in annoyance, but stayed where he was.

Saorex was already moving on, and Sakura had to hurry after him. They went deeper into the cave, to where the crystal light in the veins of the walls grew denser, but somehow darker in feeling too, as if the whiteness was slowly turning into a cold gray shade. The air was cooler here. There was more webbing, and the stone vaults rose higher, disappearing into shadow in places.

They walked in silence for a while, but then Saorex spoke on his own without turning around:

“You will bring everyone a great deal of mis-s-sfortune.”

Sakura frowned.

“Everyone?”

“Everyone,” he confirmed calmly.

“How do you even know that?”

Saorex shifted one of his spider limbs slightly, as if the question did not interest him.

“I can smell it. In the scent of your magic. It is still young, unfinished, but it already smells like trouble. And I am not the only one who can s-sense it.”

Sakura caught onto those last words immediately.

“What do you mean, not just you?”

She wanted to ask who else could sense it, what exactly that meant, and how bad everything really was, but Saorex did not answer. He only quickened his pace, and a few seconds later he led her to a place that made Sakura stop short.

A huge ravine yawned open before them. Or rather, not just a ravine, but a deep break in the body of the cave, so wide that the opposite side disappeared into half-darkness. Above that black emptiness hung webbing, and not in one layer, but in a whole complex system of interwoven strands. It stretched from edge to edge, dropped down inside in several levels, sagged, rose again, joined in knots, forming something like a giant tangled net that covered the chasm like a multilayered film. In some places the threads were thin and nearly invisible, while in others they were thick and looked strong, like ropes. In certain spots the web trembled with movement, because little spiders were skittering across it, tiny things, clearly newly hatched. There were a lot of them. And here and there among the threads, small objects glinted where they had gotten caught in the weave: a piece of metal, pendants, shards, more scraps or perhaps treasures. From this distance, it was hard to tell.

A chill slowly ran down Sakura’s spine.

“Oh no,” she muttered almost soundlessly. “Don’t tell me…”

Saorex stopped right at the edge, turned his head slightly toward her, and looked at her with that unreadable stare of his, the kind that made a person want to immediately reconsider every life decision they had ever made.

“Right here,” he said. “Your trial begins-s-s.”
