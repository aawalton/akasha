Fire seized up Gary’s body, pulling his spine taut, making him scream in silence. Everything hurt, all at once. He saw the smoke rising from his eyes.

He collapsed like a puppet with its strings cut. He tried to move, but all he achieved was incoherent spasming.

“Not yet,” Colorful said darkly. “I know how hard this is, disciple, but it’s your only chance. You must succeed. Get up.”

No. I don’t want this! Gary’s thoughts screamed. Pushing a fist against the ground, he forced himself upright, bloodshot eyes staring at his master’s stick-spear.

“Song of Lightning.”

The stick pulled back and stayed there. Tremendous amounts of aura gathered around it, suddenly sparking like storm clouds. The longer Colorful held that pose, the more the clouds accumulated. Within seconds, he was holding a storm. He let it rip.

The storm crashed against Gary, who tried hopelessly to defend. His body, forged by black lightning, ignored most of the damage. The storm was also hollow—Colorful had filled it with form, not with power. Yet, as the clouds crashed against Gary and revealed the massive lightning bolt hiding beneath, his world went white. An instant was all he could last. When he awoke, he was charred on the ground, his body regenerating but his mind not.

“Can you keep going?” Colorful asked.

No! Gary wanted to scream. “Yes,” he replied. He tried to stand, only to falter and fall back down.

“The Song of Lightning gathers aura in the form of a charge,” Colorful explained. “The longer you charge it, the more powerful the result. It’s a versatile attack at the extreme of both power and speed—once it’s launched.”

“The first three songs were just tricks. This is much more powerful. Is it because you’re using it for real?” Gary asked from the ground.

“Every three songs within the Nine Songs represent a different tier of power. Songs one through three are variations of normal strikes. They barely qualify as techniques. It’s more about teaching a cultivator the basics he needs to learn the rest of the songs.

“Songs four through six is where the Nine Songs of the Spear begin to show their might. Each song could be an Earth-grade technique of its own. The Song of Lightning, the weakest of those three songs, is already many times more powerful than your greatest strike yet, though that’s partly because you’ve never used a real technique. They’re rare in Birth of Aura.

“The gap in difficulty between the third and fourth songs is as great as their gap in power. The only reason you can learn it with your weak aura control is that you happen to specialize in lightning. And, the only way to learn it within a day is to experience it—every twist, every aura pattern, every interaction held within the charge, all will be burned into your conscious and unconscious through extreme pain. That is the path to survival you have unlocked through your grit so far. If not for your frankly suspicious regeneration, your black lightning vibration, and your resolve, it would have been impossible.”

Gary wanted nothing more than to let Colorful speak, but he knew he’d spent long enough on the ground. Rise, he commanded himself. Rise!

He got shakily to his feet. Panic speared through his heart. He needed to run away. He couldn’t do this anymore. Even death was preferable. He could escape, leave the clan, run from his duel. He didn’t want to stay one moment longer. He needed to leave. He needed to. Right fucking now.

“I’m ready,” he said.

“You can do it, Gary! I believe in you!” Goldie said.

The Song of Lightning struck him anew. Gary stayed conscious for an instant longer than last time—he sensed the clouds disperse against his skin like mere gales, saw the lightning hiding underneath, experienced for a split-second the charges and transformations hidden inside it. The patterns of its aura.

When someone is close to death, their mind opens up. Everything slows down and is imprinted to memory forever. In an instant, Gary learned a dozen new truths about lightning aura. Then he was zapped. His nerves got fried. All six of his senses were cut off, his world a place of pain. He even lost sense of time. His torment felt eternal, but worse than the pain was the fear. Endless, panicking fear, which sapped at his resolve and amplified the pain tenfold. Alone in darkness, he screamed.

When he came to, it felt like he’d been awake but unconscious for a long time. The sun hadn’t moved. Colorful was kneeling over him. “Can you keep going, Gary?” he asked, his eyes soft for the first time. There was something more in his voice—uncertainty. Respect.

That propped Gary up.

“There’s something I need to do,” he muttered. “Earth was so much larger than me. I need to avenge it. The billions of souls lost unjustly. Before that duty, how could anything else matter? All the pain my body could ever experience is a drop in the bucket before that ocean of hatred. I can keep going. I can always keep going.”

He stood up again. His mind was hazy. Distantly, he could tell he was wide-eyed and panting like a wild beast. He couldn’t keep going. Not now, nor forever. But he had to.

I can’t give up now. I can’t give up. One more time. Just one more.

Gary was struck by lightning. Again, and again. His body’s regeneration slowed down. His mind shattered into pieces. Each held different insights and a different hell of pain—a hundred different Garys suffering to contribute one tiny piece of the puzzle. The Song of Lightning stretched wider and deeper than he’d imagined. It was a vastly more complex technique than the third song, but he was making progress, real progress.

He didn’t know how long it had been or how long he had left. All he could do now was follow orders and comprehend. Over and over. Until he won.

***

Colorful no longer saw the human in his disciple. He was an animal. A senseless beast. As a practitioner of the Nine Songs himself, he was aware of the pain his disciple was experiencing. How could anyone with free will endure this long? How could he not quit? This wasn’t about stakes anymore—Colorful suspected he was torturing an unconscious, standing man.

And yet, every time Gary stood up, there was rage in those eyes. There was reason, hidden behind layers of despair. Wide, bloodshot, enraged eyes started at him from below messy dark hair. There was nothing heroic about it. It was just one man, one body, stubbornly getting up long after anyone else would have quit. Colorful didn’t even know why.

Could I have lasted this long? he wondered. When I fell that time, could I have gotten up again?

Familiar shadows crept in his heart, but for the first time in years, he refused to let them take over. He had a duty. If Gary could persist through the pain, how could Colorful lose to mere shame?

A master feeling awe towards his disciple… This can’t be healthy, Colorful thought with mirth, shooting the Song of Lightning yet again. He kept the aura perfectly balanced—one wrong move and he’d fry Gary to death.

Suddenly, his aura field, which had remained around Gary for the entire time, detected what he’d been looking for. The young man’s aura vibrated with the attack. It lasted only an instant before the resonance collapsed and so did Gary, but it had been enough. Elation filled Colorful’s heart.

It worked!!!

Gary struggled blindly against the ground, his fingers grabbing handfuls of dirt to prop him up again. “You can relax now,” Colorful said.

“Never…” Gary muttered, putting a knee below his belly.

“Idiot disciple… You have succeeded. You did it. You won. We still have work to do, but you can’t do it in this condition. You need to sleep. When you wake up, we’ll train again.”

Gary had already fainted at the words, “You won.” Colorful sighed as he raised his body. His eyes gazed into the sky as if he could see the floor’s ceiling, and through it, the endless climb beyond. The one he’d experienced and which Gary, too, would experience. The climb connecting every single person in the Cosmic Tower.

This man can’t lose, he thought. With this resolve, if he’s allowed to grow… Just how terrifying will he be?

***

Gary awoke to the sudden realization that he was fully conscious. He bolted upright and drew his spear.

“Easy,” Master Colorful said.

“What happened?” Gary asked before the memories returned. He bent over and emptied his stomach.

“You did well,” Colorful said with a neutral expression.

“My duel! When’s the duel!?” Gary asked.

“It’s still the afternoon. We have twelve hours left. Let’s make them count.”

At the sound of action, Gary gathered himself. “Yes, Master.”

“During the last strike you endured, I sensed your aura harmonize with it. That’s proof that the basics of the Song of Lightning were burned into you. In the time we have left, you will learn how to use it yourself. Assume a thrusting position. Good. Now, try conjuring the Song of Lightning.”

Gary closed his eyes. His aura felt numb, and his body was so weak it could collapse. The constant regeneration had pulled every bit of aura out of his body, leaving him feeling hollow and greatly weakened. This had never happened before—he could hopefully recover in time.

He didn’t know how to use the Song of Lightning. As he thought about it, however, the memories of his torment came back. He remembered how his body was burned over and over. How the hostile aura had moved across him. Panic rose inside him unbidden, and his aura fizzled out.

“Hang in there, Gary! I believe in you!” Goldie yelled. Her voice brought him back. He sensed it cap the memories like clear glass, letting him experience the knowledge without the fear. It fit perfectly, somehow, but he didn’t dwell on it. He refocused.

His understanding of lightning was much more complete now. He first remembered the hollow clouds that broke against his skin. He discharged aura from his spear to form incorporeal clouds all around it, half-hiding it from sight. These clouds of aura were harmless by themselves—their only use was to store the electrical charges he’d later use in his strike, the same way his legs held different electrical charges when using Thunderstep.

The precise arrangement of vibrating aura needed to create these statically charged clouds was completely unknown to Gary, and he had no way to calculate it. To his surprise, however, he could use a mixture of his prior understanding of lightning, the fact that his aura already existed in lightning form, and the patterns his body had memorized to somehow intuit the correct arrangements.

While experiencing the strike before, he’d sensed how the clouds, though hollow, contained upward and downward streams of aura. When he tried to make it happen, he realized that the opposite streams within each cloud rubbed against each other, creating one charge at the cloud’s upper part and its opposite in the lower part. Each cloud existed in balance. Alongside the streams, which he knew never stopped, little arcs of lightning traveled inside each cloud, as well as between a cloud’s top and another cloud’s bottom. His spear became the eye of a thunderstorm. The more he maintained this arrangement, the more aura it could absorb, and the tenser the charges grew.

The precise mechanics of all this were lost on Gary, but he discovered he could more or less keep it going on instinct.

And then what? he realized. Unable to stop more aura from getting sucked into the storm, it exploded. He flew sideways and crashed into a wall, vomiting blood.

“Gary!” Goldie exclaimed.

“I’m okay,” he muttered, holding onto his ribs.

“He’s okay! Why didn’t you protect him from that, you old geezer!?”

“I didn’t think you’d get it right on the first try,” Colorful said, helping Gary stand. “This is marvelous. Because you lasted until the end, the teaching worked better than I expected. You’re getting there—all that’s left is to learn the actual strike. Try again, only this time, you need to siphon one kind of charge out of the clouds and push it through your aura field in the direction of your enemy. You must also impose a vacuum in between them, and fast. Otherwise, the discharge will be local, and you will harm yourself again. Once you succeed, you will have formed the complete Song of Lightning.”

“Hmph! As if Gary could fail. Show him, Gary!” Goldie said.

Gary let his body and aura recover for a moment, then fell into a thrusting stance. He repeated the previous steps. When he felt that enough aura had gathered in the clouds, he slapped it with the “positive” vibrational pattern, sending the positively charged aura into the distance.

Success! he almost thought.

The second the positive charges left the clouds, the negative charge that remained pounced on them. A huge arc of lightning traveled from Gary’s spear shaft to its tip, and the excess energy detonated all his excess aura. He was once again flung into the wall.

“You’re close! Don’t forget about the vacuum. Again,” Colorful commanded.

“Yeah Gary, again!” Goldie said,

Gary grumbled as he stood. He retried. This time, the positive charge with which he swept the clouds was followed by his version of a vacuum—an uncharged field. It failed, of course. Gary got slammed into the same part of the wall as before, making stones drop.

“You’ll ruin my wall, disciple… Good thing I made the construction extra sturdy,” Colorful said. “Keep trying, you’ll get it.”

Gary did exactly that. The attempts bled into each other until he could recognize which section of the wall he was slammed into by the stones that dug into his ribs. It was nothing compared to the agonizing pain he’d experienced only moments ago, but somehow, it was so much more annoying, almost frustrating.

Over time, however, he figured it out. The vibration of lightning was actually comprised of two different vibrations—the positive and the negative—and the violent interaction between them. The “black” part of his lightning wasn’t as clear, but he ignored it for now. He suspected it made his positive and negative vibrations different from the regular ones, making their interaction even more brutal, but the rest of the concept was the same as regular lightning.

These were all things Gary knew already. What he’d just discovered, however, was that the vacuum—the non-electrically charged aura—was not part of the vibration of lightning. That was why he kept on failing. Through the third song, however, he already knew how to generate that vacuum. He focused his attention on the negative charges left around the spear and the positive ones flying out, letting the aura in between sort of die out, conceptually speaking. It was a dizzying feeling, like trying to turn off his peripheral vision.

The positive charge flew off without an immediate assault of the negative charge. The non-charged aura in between blocked them. In response, the negative aura dove into his spear—something he hadn’t expected. That’s when the positive charge reached the end of his aura field. He lifted the vacuum. The negative aura pounced like a pack of starving tigers. The clouds flew forward with it, as did his spear, shooting with so much force that it ripped free of his hands and crossed the air like a javelin.

When the strike reached a wall, a massive explosion was set off. Gary saw the strike from the outside for the first time—it was a huge, spear-shaped arc of lightning hidden inside hollow clouds. In this case, the arc of lightning wasn’t just spear-shaped, it was contained inside his actual spear, which made it slightly more powerful but also robbed him of his weapon.

The clouds dispersed on impact, but the lightning spear dug deep into the wall, shaking it in its entirety. Part of it collapsed. Colorful rushed there so quickly it seemed like he teleported, using earth aura to rapidly reconstruct the wall.

“Don’t throw me!” Goldie yelled with deep offense.

“Sorry,” Gary replied.

“I have great news and bad news, disciple,” Colorful said, handing Gary back his spear. “You got it! That was the Song of Lightning! It wasn’t fully charged, but with a bit of practice, I’m confident you’ll be able to charge it at least fifty percent more than you just did. It’s your very first finisher—an extremely mighty strike! With this, let alone Sui Duang, even Feng Shen would need to be wary against you! You have a real shot at victory! Congratulations!”

Gary felt all his efforts bearing fruit. The elation was indescribable. “Thank you, Master! But, what are the bad news?”

Colorful scratched his head. “The negative charge diving into your spear is not supposed to happen. It usually just sticks to the surface. This only occurred because the Golden Bolt is extremely attuned to lightning by itself.”

“How is that bad news?”

“Well… You saw how the spear flew out of your grasp, right? You probably think it happened because you were unprepared, but unfortunately, it will keep happening. That spear strike contained the full force of the Song of Lightning. Stopping it with your bare hands is like trying to stop a raging glyan by the toes.”

“This is outrageous! I’m not a javelin!” Goldie said.

“What if I just grip it harder?” Gary asked.

“With your current strength, gripping it harder would mean your shoulders getting ripped off their sockets and thrown at the enemy like bony caps.”

“That’s ridiculous, Master! A spear wielder should never let go of his weapon!”

“It is what it is, disciple. You can only use this strike once, at most twice if you can pick up your spear afterward. You’ll have to make it count.”

“I guess it means I’m more powerful than Gary…” Goldie muttered. “I can live with that…”

Gary crossed his arms. “I did not go through all that for another joke move!”

“There’s nothing funny about it, I assure you. If anything, throwing the Golden Bolt makes the Song of Lightning even more powerful. If it hits, Sui Duang will be grievously injured or killed outright—it’s that “if” that troubles me. Since you just learned to use this move, your charging time is slow. Any experienced opponent can dodge it. You’ll need to somehow corner or immobilize him first, but also, you need to practice until you can cut your current charging time in half.”

Gary was about to protest. He finally sighed in resignation. “Shouldn’t I sleep? I’m honestly exhausted,” he said, motioning where the sun was setting.

“Absolutely, but first you need to practice more. Your Song of Lightning is usable, but not in real combat. Two or three more hours should do the trick. After that, you can rest, and then it’s time to duel.”

Gary nodded with a heavy head. “Got it,” he said, assuming a thrusting stance.

“Oh, and Gary? Please don’t break the wall again. It’s important.”

Gary threw him a scalding glare and returned to practice.
