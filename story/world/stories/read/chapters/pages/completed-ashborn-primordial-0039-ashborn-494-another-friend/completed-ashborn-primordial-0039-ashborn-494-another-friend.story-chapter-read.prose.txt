“Our first task is to locate Andros,” Vir said.

“His skyship was headed west,” Tia replied. “And there’s just one thing in that direction. A new garrison he’s set up deep in the wilderness. His primary base in the demon realm.”

“Then that makes it simple. What of Saunak?”

“He should be there too,” Tia said. “The two are practically inseparable.”

“Then we can...” Vir trailed off, his mind unable to digest her words. That Saunak had a friend in this universe? Vir wondered in awe just how Andros had managed to get the demon to care about anything other than his research. Or anyone.

Maiya helpfully coughed, breaking Vir out of his thoughts.

“We can achieve both of our objectives,” Vir finished awkwardly.

“But how exactly do you intend to take Andros down?” Tia asked. “Believe me, we’ve tried. Not only is he a monstrous Talent wielder in his own right, but with all the Artifacts he has, he might as well be a god himself.”

Vir smirked. “I have it on good authority that not one of his artifacts protects him against Chakra-based magic.”

Tia’s eyes widened. “That's... You're certain of this?”

“Positive. I’ve confirmed it myself.”

Tia looked very much like she wanted to ask him how, but his pointed look silenced her.

“In that case, any demon with Warrior Chakra would suffice,” Tia said. “We have several.”

While that was a relief to Vir's ears, he didn't want to take chances. “Do you have someone who wields Crown Chakra?”

Tia frowned and shook her head. “Unfortunately not. Those are only found among the highest echelon of demonic warriors. And as many demons as aid my cause, there are just as many who refuse to interfere, content to sit and wait it out. They think this storm will blow over, that Andros will eventually return to his realm after seeing how little prana these lands have to offer.”

Vir exhaled. In any reasonable world, they might’ve been right. Developing arable land in the demon realm, building out infrastructure, and increasing the population… all of it required monumental work. Most kings would pale at the very prospect of such a task.

But Andros was anything but reasonable. He conquered simply to feed his own ego. For the glory of his empire.

But that future would not come to pass. Not with Saunak supporting him.

Soon, Saunak would recreate the prana distributor that had reshaped Vir’s own universe. This universe's demon realm would flourish, yes, but in all the worst ways. The land would thrive while its people wilted under Andros and Saunak, who would ascend to heights of power worse than ever before.

“Then it seems we need a demon capable of that feat,” Vir said. “Tell me. Where is Cirayus?”

“The Ravager?” Tia blinked. “He might support our cause, sure, but it’s no use. He surrendered himself under threat of blackmail soon after Andros arrived. It seems Saunak identified him as a priority threat and came up with a plan to deal with him. He wastes away deep in a fortified prison, captured and stripped of his powers.”

Vir smiled, guessing exactly what Saunak had used as blackmail. Or rather, whom. “Then that will be our first stop.”

“That distance isn’t small,” Tia protested. “Samar Patag is an entire nation away! We can’t simply hop over there without a skyship or an Acira, and I'm afraid I'm fresh out of both. Just how rich do you think our little resistance is?”

“No matter,” Vir said calmly, trying and failing to keep his smile from widening. “I have a way to ferry us there.”

“Oh? You do, do you?” Tia asked, crossing her arms and looking somewhere halfway between amused and skeptical. “And how do you intend to free him, hmm? I assume they fitted him with a subjugation collar that drains him of his prana. How will you get him out?”

Vir chuckled. “I wouldn’t be so sure about that. And yes, I do have a way of getting him out. Now, come. Let’s go free our friend before Andros decides to move again.”

He grabbed Tia’s wrist before she could protest and shot a look at Ashani and Maiya. “Mai, you'll have to ride with Ashani this time.”

“Of course,” Maiya said.

“What are you—” Tia began, but Vir once again didn’t give her the chance. He pulled her out of the safehouse and into the street where he and Ashani immediately leapt skyward, shooting high above the rooftops, bounding out of sight before anyone could see.

Tia screamed that entire first jump.

“Relax,” Vir said. “This is the most efficient way to travel. Unless you have a better way?”

“N-no,” Tia managed weakly.

“As I thought. Hold on then. It’s a bit of a journey, and I’m about to pick up speed.”

Tia thankfully endured the rest of the journey in silence, and an hour of bounding later, they reached the walls of Samar Patag. Vir couldn't begin to guess what went through the Matali princess' mind during that time.

Vir stopped on a high ridge overlooking the outer district—and felt his gut twist.

This was not the Samar Patag he remembered.

Yes, he had expected this world’s version to be a pale shadow of the thriving city he’d shepherded in his own universe. But this place…

Was it his memory playing tricks on him, or had Andros’ occupation truly hollowed the city out to this degree?

Fifty years of broken rule had left Samar Patag looking pale and withered, a shell of what it should have been.

For both Tia and Andros to exist at the same time, for Samar Patag to have suffered under his thumb for fifty long years… it was yet another reminder that this universe wasn’t his. Its people had been shaped by different circumstances and different histories, and were thus wholly different. No matter how familiar it and its people might seem.

“Let’s get going,” Vir said. “Maiya, Ashani? Stay behind and keep watch. We’ll return shortly.”

“And how exactly do you plan on doing that?” Tia asked. “You may be able to fly through the sky—which, if you ask me, looks an awful lot like the Bairans’ Balancer of Scales—but even that won’t get you inside the compound. It’s heavily defended, and two people aren’t enough to—”

Tia froze as Vir sank straight into the ground. Rather than an explanation, he figured it would be faster to simply show her.

Sinking into the shadows, Vir stayed there a moment, observing the exits, and there were many. There always were in this realm of eternal sunset.

It didn’t take long at all to find an empty storeroom inside the compound. He rose back into the physical world, clamping a hand over Tia’s mouth as she appeared, stopping her from shouting.

“That… that was Dance of the Shadow Demon!” she hissed when he let go. “And don’t tell me it’s not! How can you use that? That’s impossible. You can’t have a Bairan Ultimate Art and an Iksana Art! You’re not a giant or a ghael!”

Vir almost laughed at how casually she spoke of demonic bloodline arts—of their culture. His Tia would not only have been clueless, she’d have found the very idea of learning disgusting.

It showed just how different this world was, and how differently its people had been shaped.

“I’m afraid I’m not at liberty to explain,” Vir murmured. “And I ask that you keep this between us. If this information gets out, it is no exaggeration to say it could doom the entire realm.”

He nearly said your entire universe, but of course, Tia wouldn’t understand the implication.

“Fine then,” she whispered, staring at him with a mix of petulance, fear and awe. Vir was getting used to that look.

“One more stop,” he said before slipping back into the shadows.

He emerged inside a dark chamber. This room was not empty.

A demon guard turned toward him, but a well-placed strike and the slightest surge of prana into the man’s veins, and he dropped to the floor, unconscious before he uttered a single sound.

The sight made Vir’s stomach knot up. Demons serving Andros. Demons subjugating their own people. It disgusted him, though he knew Andros likely forced them into compliance through threats against their families and clans.

Cirayus, however, looked the same as ever, which somehow steadied Vir’s quavering heart.

“Strange. If I am not mistaken, that was Dance of the Shadow Demon,” Cirayus said, nearly parroting Tia’s earlier words. “Yet you are no Iksana clansmen. Who are you? Name yourself!”

“One who’s come to retrieve you. I’d say ‘save you’, but we both know you never needed any saving,” Vir said. “And also to end the one who did this to you.”

He stepped closer, placing a hand on the half-giant’s shoulder, and met his eyes. Every part of him wanted to pull the demon into a hug, to thank him for everything he’d ever done and tell him he’d do everything in his power to help the demons here—but this wasn’t his Cirayus.

And even though Vir knew the giant was a big softie beneath his rough exterior, he doubted the demon would appreciate being embraced by a stranger.

Cirayus stared into Vir’s eyes, frowning. “You look… like them. But that is impossible. Maion and Shari died decades ago. They never bore a child.”

Vir’s breath caught, but he shook his head. “I’m sorry. I don’t know them.”

So that explained why this world had never seen an Akh Nara. His parents had never had him. Greesha had never had her prophecy.

It still didn’t explain the larger timeline divergence though, but who knew what all had changed here? Without the Primordial in this universe, affecting history through his various incarnations, any number of things could be different. Even the smallest change might have monumental impact over a thousand years.

A strange thought flickered through Vir’s mind. If the Akh Nara existed in his universe… did he exist in others? Another version of him? And were there others like him traveling the universes in pursuit of Janak?

He sincerely hoped not. Too many versions jumping universes would draw the Fateweavers’ attention in an instant.

That they hadn’t descended upon him led him to believe that he was either the only one, or a rare enough occurrence to avoid their sight.

“Come,” Vir said, snapping back to the moment. “Let’s get you out of here. We need someone with both the Crown and Warrior Chakras to take down Andros.”

“Oho? Bold words… though I admit your impossible set of arts have me curious, and believe me, lad, it is not a common thing to pique my interest.”

“I’m aware,” Vir smirked. “Now, snap those collars so we may leave.”

Cirayus scoffed. “And what makes you think I can simply break a prana subjugation collar? Besides which,” he gestured to the shackles on his wrists, his ankles, and the collar around his neck. “Do you not see that I am imprisoned?”

“Come on, Vir,” Tia said. “It’s not fair to ask him to—”

“Oh, please,” Vir interrupted with a snort. “We both know the collar never did a thing. No device can restrain you, Cirayus, so stop pretending. We have work to do.”

As Vir expected, the half-giant threw his head back and let out a booming laugh so loud it shook the room. Half the prison had probably heard it.

“Yes. You are an interesting one,” Cirayus said. “Yet despite knowing all of this, you fail to understand why I am here. Why I have chosen this fate.”

“Oh, I think I have a pretty good idea. You're here under threat of blackmail. Andros has captured Aida and holds her in a location you cannot learn. Likely several locations, knowing him. He'll have her on the move specifically to protect against any such attack.”

Cirayus' jovial mood darkened, and his voice dropped to barely more than a whisper, and there was awe in his expression. Awe, and even a bit of fear. “How can you know of this? Of any of this?”

“I know many things,” Vir said, activating Aspect of the Demon God. Jet-black armor wrapped around him and the horns that sprouted from his head nearly touched the ceiling. Ash prana manifested as black flames so thick it became visible, setting his demonic ablaze.

The display, terrifying as it was, left Tia frozen and her teeth clattering, but intimidation was not Vir's aim. No demonic form would do that to a warrior of Cirayus' caliber.

No, he was sending a message. That he possessed arts no demon should be capable of. That his prana was of the level never seen in history. On par with the mightiest of Ash Beasts.

It worked better than any attack he could have flung or word he could have spoken, for even the Ravager was left speechless at the sight.

“I promise you,” Vir said evenly. “By the time this day is done, Aida will be free from Andros' clutches because everyone will. For in just a few hours time, Andros will be nothing but a memory.”

Cirayus was still a long moment. In the end, he said nothing. He simply reached up and crushed the collar like it was made of paper, the magic shackles shattering under the smallest effort. Rising to his full height, he rolled his shoulders, cracked his neck, and flexed his fingers.

Vir nodded. “Now, let us begin.”

Poor Tia stood frozen in the doorway, staring with her mouth hanging open as Cirayus knocked down the door with his bare fists and stormed out.
