“Are you okay?”

Arielle had been lying in silence for some time, staring at the thatched ceiling.

“No,” she admitted. She moved to sit up, and her messaging scroll, which had been lying on her chest, clattered to the floor.

She reached for it and set it on the table. She had already used it to contact Elric, but there was no response. She doubted Elric had taken the messaging book with him, seeing as how he had never used it to contact her while he was in the demonic realm.

Arielle had also been reading her parents’ last messages about the welcome dinner they were hosting in her honor. She didn’t know how to tell them that it wouldn’t happen.

Inara moved closer with Sir Spendulon behind her. She slid into the seat beside Arielle, and the dragon settled at her feet and began licking his hands, running them over his head.

“I don’t know how to tell them that I won’t be coming home,” Arielle told Inara. "My family...they've been waiting for a long time, and I haven't seen them in months. It's the first time I've ever been away for this long, and I haven't been doing a good job of communicating with them, mostly because I can't tell them about a lot of the things I'm up to. They're worried, and they wanted to make sure I was okay when we met, and now I have to tell them that they can't."

Arielle wasn't sure why she was telling Inara any of this, only that she felt the urge to talk about it, but didn't want to do that with Vittoria, who might think she was simply complaining.

Inara appeared to contemplate the words for some time before she spoke again.

“Why don’t you wait until Lady…I mean, Vittoria makes a decision? That way you can soften the blow by inviting them to visit you as well.”

“Do you think she’ll allow that?”

“I’m not sure," Inara admitted. "You know her better than me.”

“Not really. I only met her a few weeks before you did.”

“Truly?”

Arielle nodded. As much as she and Vittoria had grown more familiar over the last few days, she didn’t know the woman well enough to predict her decision yet. Vittoria seemed to care about Arielle's feelings and had gone against her own dictates before to demonstrate it, but at the same time, she was fiercely protective of her space and extremely untrusting of outsiders. Plus, it was Arielle's mistake that had made things more difficult.

“It’s just that it seemed like the two of you had been working together for a long time,” Inara told Arielle. "You appear like family."

Arielle shrugged. "I don't know how close we really are. She is a friend of Elric, whom I met a few months ago. And she has only taken an interest in me because she thinks that I should be a hero and help people with my powers."

A puzzled expression hung on Inara's face. "Isn’t…isn’t that what you do already?”

“I suppose,” she said. “I don’t know if I’m good at it, though.”

“You’re very good at it!" Inara's words were impassioned, with an encouraging, admiring smile. "You saved me."

“Only because it was a consequence of saving Elric. If not for that, I'm not sure I would have bothered."

Inara reacted to the statement, the smile falling off her face.

Arielle immediately regretted saying it. It was the truth, but it occurred to her that it might be too harsh for Inara to hear that.

"I'm sorry I said that," she said.

"No, don't be," Inara said.

"I sense I hurt your feelings."

"You didn't." Inara shook her head, but Arielle could tell she was lying, even without her detection spell.

"I'm sorry again," she said. "For what it's worth, I'm happy I saved you. And my saying I wouldn't have bothered was not a measure of your value. It's just my lack of foresight and my inherent self-centeredness. As I said, I'm not sure I make a good hero." She looked down at her hands. "I just... I'm not sure if I'm meant to be here or doing any of these things. I've failed a lot lately. I have also endangered you many times without meaning to. I make Vittoria frustrated all the time, and make things harder for her and Elric too. Yet they spend their time and risk their lives helping me anyway. I don’t know if any of it is worth it.”

Arielle wasn't sure where this conversation was leading her. It seemed there was more on her chest than she realized, or maybe it was just her general melancholy that was making all these doubts arise.

Arielle had never wanted to be a hero, but at the same time, she didn't want to be bad at it either. Not only did she dislike failure as a concept, but being bad at being a hero ultimately put others in danger, which she didn't like.

Not to mention, in the grand scheme of things, it felt like her problems were piling up faster than she could solve them.

She still hadn't been able to access the abyss to find Plutarch or Cornelius.

She had ultimately failed at solving Lyra's problem even though Lyra didn't seem to mind.

She had not read Yseult's diary yet.

And even though Vittoria had caught many demons and killed them, there were apparently even more that were trying to invade the human world.

“I don’t think you have to do everything perfectly all the time to be a hero," Inara spoke up suddenly. "You can make mistakes and not think far enough. You just have to want to do the right thing ultimately."

"Do I do the right thing?"

"Yes. You make people safer, make their lives better."

“Yeah, maybe.” She leaned back against the seat, thinking about everything that happened, about Riorke and Lyra and how her interference had affected their lives.

Had she actually made anyone's life better?

Riorke might be getting punished for her actions as they spoke. Lyra had still lost her cores, and if they couldn't make the spells work for her, she might still get kicked out of the academy.

Even with Inara. She seemed happier, but then again, she hadn’t left Vittoria’s home since she got here. It was likely so that her family couldn’t find her or compel her to return, but still. It was a sort of trap, one that she seemed content with, but a trap nevertheless.

Sir Spendulon stretched forward and buried his face in Arielle's lap, chuffing. She petted him, noticing the more exaggerated hunch in his back.

“Did he get taller?" she asked.

“Yes," Inara said. "By a quarter of an inch. I measured." She gestured to the opposite pillar where there were two tiny marks carved in, a quarter of an inch apart. "Lady Vittoria said I could."

"That's good." Arielle reached out and patted his head, and he waddled closer to her. "He's going to start breathing fire soon. And maybe outgrow this house."

"Lady Vittoria said his mother may come for him by then."

"Maybe. Maybe not."

"I see." She picked at her skirt. "I was wondering if I should knit him another bow tie in the meantime."

"Yes." Arielle adjusted the bow tie on Sir Spendulon's neck. It was elastic and not too tight. "He'll outgrow this one soon, and we need something to recognize him by." Not that many young red dragons were flying about, but still.

"Yes," Inara said. "That makes sense."

"Do you know dragons can develop a sense of style?" Arielle said. "They don't wear clothes, but they can grow their scales in a pattern they find fashionable."

"Oh. That's...interesting."

"Yes." Arielle had thought as much when she'd read the book on dragons from her school library. She had so many other dragon facts swimming in her head, but she said nothing as her mind drifted back into her melancholy. They wouldn't be able to hide Sir Spendulon for long. He would eventually grow too big to be contained, and if his mother did not come for him in time, it might be a problem for Vittoria.

Yet another problem Arielle had created for her.

"What else?" Inara said suddenly.

"What?"

"What else can you tell me about dragons?" Inara's eyes were wide and eager. She seemed enthusiastic, although Arielle didn't know why. Maybe she wanted to know more about Sir Spendulon.

"I can tell you a lot," Arielle said. "Like the fact that two-legged dragons exist. Dragons don’t actually need four legs to walk, and some don’t walk on their legs at all. Occasionally, some dragon tribes stitch their legs to their bodies in protest.”

"Protest of what?”“I’m not sure. The book didn’t say.”

“I see.”

"Yes." Ari continued to recite dragon trivia, everything she had learned from the book, with Inara nodding at intervals and asking pertinent questions.

Arielle couldn't tell exactly when, but somehow the melancholy faded as they fell deeper into their conversation.

By the time Vittoria returned, her doubts were all but forgotten.
