The bridge lay ahead of us, and I couldn't help but shudder... not from excitement this time. Who the fuck thought that connecting two islands that were in the sky with just bridges made out of not-so-trustworthy-looking wood would be the best option that they could create? The railings were just ropes, too!

Whoever created this, I had a serious talking point that I wanted to bring up with them. These motherfuckers couldn't even create it to be wide enough or stable-looking. It seemed like something right out of the Pirates of the Caribbean movies.

Why the hell did I know what the Pirates of the Caribbean movies were?

We would have to walk one after the other, and it seemed almost as if anyone could just walk up and cut one of the ends of these bridges. They didn't seem protected when I used analyze on them either. I questioned this. I did mention my worry to Mark, and he nodded grimly, probably coming to the same conclusion as I.

"Yep, it's not good, Shrimpie. If the players, some of them, decide to isolate the others, they might as well give them a death sentence. Well, maybe not that, but they can make it… how do I say this?”

"Obnoxious," Lia added.

"Kinda but not really. Just.. annoying, I would say. Each of the islands connects to at least three or four others, and walking around trying to destroy them while there are beings that could slay you at any moment's notice is stupid, especially if you could just kill the players you dislike by a direct attack instead.“

“But what if you can't beat them in a direct attack?” Lia noted. “What if somebody who hated us but was weaker than us, two of the top ten players, decided that they wanted us dead?”

“Yes, let's hope that doesn't happen. I don't think we can do much to prevent that at this moment.”

“There's little reason to go from island to island once we know where the legendary dungeon is anyway.” I said, pointing behind us. The castle was no longer visible in the distance.

These islands were deceptively big. Not as big as the biomes on the first floor, but still large enough to hide several nasty surprises. There were a few traps placed throughout, but no damn mobs. The level 25 checkpoint was so elusive that I was beginning to wonder whether the gods just wanted to tease me with it.

“Well, let's go, no time to lose!” Aura said cheerily, and without discussion, started walking on the not-so-trustworthy-looking bridge. She didn't seem concerned.

“Mark?” I whispered.

“Yes, Shrimpie, what is it?”

“If I die on a fucking bridge, I want you to take revenge on the gods yourself.”

“You do realize that if you die, I die with you?”

“True. But do it anyway.”

Well, Leah started walking on the bridge as well, and I wondered if I should follow or wait until they get to the other side, but Mark hastened me and Crudia on, saying that it would hold a dozen players at once easily. It creaked under my feet, and if I slipped, I could have fallen to my demise towards the sky below my feet. I didn't know how long I would have to fall before finally dying, and I didn't intend to find that out.

In the middle of the bridge, Crudia seemed to start shaking from stress, and I wasn't too far from doing the same. The only two people enjoying this whole fiasco were Lia and Aura, who seemed to be talking the whole way throughout, and they walked on the wooden planks with surprising ease.

"You don't fear dying? Falling down there?"

“We were birds, Shrimpie.” Aura said, showing off the feathers on her human-looking arms. “We would be dead long ago if we feared the heights.”

"You don't have wings anymore, may I remind you?" Mark added, defending my point.

"Oh, don't we?" It was Lia who smirked this time around. She turned on her heel and gave me a pearly white smile. “Then, watch!”

And without further ado, she jumped off the bridge. It swayed under my feet, and for a short moment I didn't even give a damn about where she fell. I just focused on not following her in the fall, but once I settled on the wooden planks once again, I looked down, but she wasn't there. She was flying right next to the bridge, her arms transformed into feathery wings, which she beat every few seconds to stay afloat.

"That's your mutation?" I asked, looking it over. It was so different compared to Xila's wings, but I like this a little bit more. It made me not focus on the swaying bridge for a moment, which I appreciated.

“Evolution, you mean? No, it's from my class.” She gave me a grin and then looked towards the other island. It was finally becoming visible. The bridge was so long that we could barely see the outline from the other side, but now it seemed surprisingly white.

Was that snow? Why snow? Why couldn't it be an ocean island? I was about to complain, but I thought better of it. Currently surviving and not dying was my number one priority, and it was kind of hard to focus on when I was standing on the precipice of whatever the fuck was below me.

“Well, see you on the other side.” She smiled and then flew ahead.

“Scout ahead a little bit!” Mark shouted behind before typing the same thing into our guild chat.

“One step forward, one step forward, one step forward.”

When the end of the bridge came. I was relieved for all of two seconds before the cold bit into my ass, and I considered walking back towards the much warmer islands from which we came.

“Well, a snowy biome now. This is an interesting one. Been a while since I’ve seen snow like this.” Mark said, smiling.

“I hate it already.”

“Don’t be a spoilsport.”

“Oh, it's kind of nice! Reminds me of home a little bit. I would just cuddle up somewhere warm and wait out these months during which it looked like this outside, but when I woke up, it was still sometimes snowy.” Crudia looked at the snow wistfully, a dumb grin on her face.

“I hate this. I hate it. Why do we need snow?” Water is just good enough, but then it needs to freeze into this white bullshit? I hate it!

“Calm down, Shrimpie. It's just snow. You'll be fine.”

“Why is it so cold anyway? Oh, wait, I know the answer to that.” I said, realizing the answer to my question almost immediately. Damn this idiotic knowledge! I couldn't even ask questions and complain. Ughhhh.

Lia messaged us that she was scouting the island from far above. Apparently, this is how they found the previous dungeon before us. We got there based on luck, but they found it immediately because she was a better scout than almost anyone. Flying on this particular floor seemed like a very potent option. I almost regretted not picking something similar, but a flying shrimp was a bit too silly even for me.

She came back soon and looked mostly at Lia and Mark as she told them what she saw.

“This island is a bit larger than the last one. There is a small village, but it's actually a village, not a town like on the previous island. A few buildings in a circle, that’s it. And there appears to be a giant cave on a small mountain back there.” She pointed to the distance. I could barely see the outline because of the falling snow.

“You think it's a dungeon?”

“Yep, I assume it is. I don't know the difficulty, but it's most definitely something of that sort. One other thing, though: we are not alone here on this island.” She crossed her arms.

Mark nodded, writing things down into his interface. “So there's other players here. Are they heading towards the cave?”

“No, no, no,” she shook her head. “There aren't any other players. I would have seen them, but nothing showed up on the map either. I'm talking about monsters. There were none on the previous island, but here it's basically one of those snowy things on each square kilometer or so.“

“Snowy things? Can you be a bit more detailed?” Mark said, looking at me as if I was the one who didn’t provide enough info.

"Hey, I give good descriptions."

"Uh-huh, sure you do. Well, a better description, please, Lia?"

"I didn't see it from up close. Couldn’t use [Analyze]. I'm not dumb enough to fly right into its mouth, but it seemed to be white, furry, and it had two horns on the front of its head."

“How big? Trying to connect it to something from the written guides.”

“Like bear, a little bit larger, I think.”

Mark nodded, scrolling through something, probably his notes. "There's a few monsters that could be what you speak of, but I think the only way to be sure is to see it from up close and analyze. None of them should be above level 35 or 40, though. Unless it’s a 6 Legged Yeti. If it is, we’re fucked. So let's hope that you can all beat it if we meet it.”

“I think we should hunt one if it’s level 35." I added, trying to not shake.

“Whatever they are, we are more likely to meet low-level enemies in the dungeon,” Mark said. “The common and uncommon dungeons will have monsters of level 25 to 30 and are meant to be the first thing you tackle on this floor.”

“But do you think they would make it that easy for us?” Lia joined the conversation. Aura nodded as if she was thinking the same thing.

"What do you mean?" I asked.

"The gods apparently enjoy seeing us die and battle for our lives. Would they really make it so easy that we could simply progress from a common to uncommon dungeon and so on?"

She was right, and I knew it. Even Mark knew it. He sighed and shook his head: "No, but all the more reason to go there now before whatever they have planned rolls out. Perhaps they increase the level or do something similar? We need to destroy it now. I'll share the information with Dusk."

Without much else to do, we headed straight towards the cave. Lia and Aura are walking at the forefront. Regrettably, we didn't meet any of the monsters that she mentioned before, and I didn't see any more dragons flying above us like on the last island. I wondered whether the monsters increased in strength depending on which island we walked on, based on the difficulty of the dungeon that lay inside. It would make sense, and Mark seemed to agree, though Leah and Aura didn't seem so inclined.

The snow was never-ending, and I genuinely hoped we would just be done with it and then go somewhere else. My biggest prayer right now was that the dungeon itself wouldn't be snow-themed. I didn't hold high hopes for that, though; I didn't even say it out loud. If the gods were listening, they would do anything to torture me. I was certain of that fact.

The cave, once we got there, was far smaller than I thought it would be. It was just a hole that seemed to go downwards at the very base of the small hill. The hill itself was about as large as the one I ran up on in the last floor. I missed snowboarding.

There wasn't a door or anything similar to the legendary dungeon, but [Analyze] did show me that this was, in fact, what we came looking for.

Maw of Ice

Dungeon - Uncommon

There was no snarky comment. Nothing that would make fun of me or tell me about what I could expect inside. Even if it did say something about how I would definitely die and the gods would enjoy it, or whatever the hell the gods that made these descriptions seemed to like to say, I was still focused and determined to go inside alone.

“Well, are we ready? Stay close to each other, don't underestimate the enemy, and prepare to retreat if it's necessary.” Mark said, going from one of us to the next, inspecting our gear. Once he was satisfied, he nodded. “Well, then let's go.”

I stepped inside, and the air changed.
