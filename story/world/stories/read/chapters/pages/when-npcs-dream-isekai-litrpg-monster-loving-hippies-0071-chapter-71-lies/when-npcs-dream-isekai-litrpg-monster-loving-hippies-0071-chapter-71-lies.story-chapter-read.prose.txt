It was late evening and dark, but the Lithurion port town of Verthill was awash in light.

The pirate ship didn’t fly a skull-and-crossbones flag, or display any other silly sign that screamed 'We are illegal pirates.' Which made sense, really.

I wasn’t actually sure if "Pirate" was a collectively illegal status here. I guessed that if the pirates never attacked a Lithurion vessel, there was no reason for the Lithurion NPCs to attack them.

Whatever the logic, there wasn’t any trouble at the port.

Shooya, Anna, and I said our goodbyes to the pirate captain and her daughter, Harilelle.

We hadn't been on the docks long before we were spotted by Order of the Phoenix guys.

“Geshem! Shooya! Over here!” Pedro waved his arm from down the pier.

“Hi Pedro,” I said as we approached. I turned to the faces I recognized. “Abel, Nigina. How are you all doing?”

“We are fine,” Pedro said, looking relieved. “Though you are the one Felix sent on a suicide mission into enemy territory.” He turned his gaze to our new companion. “We haven’t met, but it is good have you back.”

“Sure is good to be back.” Anna offered him a sweet smile.

“So, why are you here, Pedro?” I asked. “It’s nice meeting you all, but is there a specific mission reason for the welcoming committee?”

“We were nearby,” Pedro explained. “Felix wanted us to meet you, to see if you or Anna needed any assistance.” He glanced at her again. “I’m guessing you want to head back to Kenzebeth? To Linda’s group?”

“Not at all.” Anna kept her smile fixed. “I wasn’t very close to them, really. What I want is to help the war effort. I want to defeat the Agents.”

“I see,” Pedro said, then looked at me for confirmation.

“She is clean,” I said.

“Yes,” Shooya added, nodding vigorously. “I checked her an hour ago. Not a single remaining trace of control spore on her.”

“Anna’s got chemistry and biology degrees,” I explained. “She is a smart girl and wants to contribute on the nerd front.”

“The nerd front,” Pedro mused. “We have the guys working on the gas masks, like your friend Hyun-Woo. They are under enormous pressure and really short on manpower. They’re working themselves sleepless. They could really use any help they can get. Did you two talk about it?”

“No,” I said. “Like Shooya said, she was only confirmed 100% clear an hour ago. So I didn’t tell her anything important before then. But now we can, and to tell the truth, she seems trustworthy.”

I remembered her neck, covered in red dots, and the memory made me shiver.

“A gas mask…” Anna chimed in, eyes widening. “Zylil suspected you guys were up to something! That’s exciting. You really did it? What spore size can they filter? How did they manage the sealing?”

“It looks creepy, and it works,” I said. “That’s all I know. Mind you, I asked Hyun-Woo specifically not to make it creepy.”

“But maybe it will scare the enemy,” Shooya suggested. “I would definitely be scared of a creepy mask!”

“You are scared of vines, Shooya,” I remarked. “You aren’t exactly a fair example.”

“Hey!” Shooya protested. “Vines are all tangly and writhing! They look like snakes that don’t have the decency to hiss before they grab you!”

“So, I can help with the mask?” Anna asked, bringing us back on track. “I really want to. I have like ten ideas already! It will be so much fun.”

“From what I heard,” I said, “it’s really not. They work under huge pressure. Not long ago, someone was half-dead after inhaling poison due to a faulty mask experiment. They don’t really sleep.”

“I get it,” Anna said, her expression sobering. “But it’s important, too. This is our only defense against the spores.”

“Not the only one,” I corrected. “We do mass player training on positive air bubble gas magic.”

“Seriously?” Anna said. “But only a rare few are Gas Mages, and to train a new one usually takes a year at least.”

“Well, I don’t want to brag,” I said, adopting a bragging tone immediately. “But I invented a simplified positive air bubble circle and a simplified training method. With that, and the help of the charm to stabilize beginners circle, I could train muggles, I mean, non-mage players—in fricking weeks. It works on about a third of the players who have minimal natural talent.”

“To train a non-mage to actually make effective magic circles…” she murmured in wonder. “You are a really incredible mage, Geshem.”

“Why… I maybe… well, that…” I smiled, embarrassed. Gosh, it was nice to get some appreciation for my amazingness.

“So Anna,” Pedro interrupted my ego trip. “If you’re going straight to the gas mask research facility—or factory—you really don’t need us. It’s on the way to Kamatouu.” He looked at me. “And that’s where you are going, correct?”

“Yes,” I said. “I talked to Felix yesterday. According to him, Lori should already be there. Also, he said… well, never mind.”

“What?” asked Shooya.

“He heard from the guy escorting her that, well, she was really pissed.”

“Ah,” said Pedro with a knowing nod. “Angry girlfriend. I know about that well. Good luck.”

“She should be angry,” Shooya said. “You said you would come back and you didn’t!”

“That is because of you,” I pointed an accusatory finger at her. “The pirate quest was your idea!”

“And it was a good idea,” Shooya countered. “We got to be pirates after all!”

“That is not a good thing!”

Anna laughed softly. “You two are always funny together.”

“Well, we really have to go,” said Pedro.

“It’s night and all,” I said, looking at the dark sky. “Are you really okay traveling in the dark?”

“Yes, we are five, and high levels,” he said. “And we have these magic lamps for light.”

“Cool,” I said. “We will stay the night in this town. You be careful.”

“Sure,” said Pedro. “Oh, and when you get in Kamatouu, gather Juliette and your officers. John Smith wants to have a strategic meeting with you guys.”

We said our goodbyes and went to the business district of Verthill to search for a nice Inn. We had endured a long day—being pirates for the best part of it—and we had plenty of money on us.

“That one!” Shooya pointed at basically the first inn we saw.

It was a wooden structure with colorful painted shutters and a hanging sign that squeaked in the wind.

“We can really find a better one,” I said. “I have like ten gold on me.”

“It looks cozy,” Anna said, observing the warm light spilling from the windows. “It has an authentic vibe. I like it too.”

“Fine,” I said, and we went in.

The interior was warm, smelling of roasted meat and sawdust. The NPC innkeeper beamed at us. “Welcome, traveler, to the Tooth’s Vein Inn. Food for the belly or rest for the weary? How can I be of service?”

“We would like three—”

“One room!” Shooya cut me off, holding up a finger. “With one large bed.”

I turned to Shooya and squeezed my eyes shut, pinching the bridge of my nose. “I am not sleeping in the same bed with you! I don’t want to offend you or anything, but you might notice you have a hard exoskeleton, and you tend to hug me in your sleep! And Anna is a naked Nula girl; there is no need for us to sleep in the same room.”

Shooya looked suddenly somewhat distressed, antennas all tangled together.

“I don’t mind,” said Anna quickly, turning to Shooya. “You can hug me in your sleep if you like.”

Shooya gave Anna a quick squeeze. “Thank you! You are a wonderful person.”

“Okay, make it a two-bed room,” I said to the NPC.

“One!” insisted Shooya.

I sighed. “What is it this time?”

“You shouldn’t sleep alone,” Shooya said solemnly. “You will get scared.”

“Huh…”

The NPC interrupted our debate. “We have one room with two large beds, in the Honeymoon Tooth Suite. It will be twenty silver. Bread and butter breakfast included.”

What was with the tooth trope? Couldn’t they think of something else? But luckily there weren’t many teeth in our room; it actually looked really nice. It had that medieval fantasy aesthetic I like—sturdy timber beams, fur rugs, and a crackling fireplace.

“Good night,” I said, claiming a bed.

“Good night.”

I wanted to say to Anna, 'Enjoy sleeping with hugging armor suit,' but that would have been… ah, a lack of tact on my side.

Tomorrow morning, we ate the bread and butter the innkeeper had promised—which was surprisingly damn good—and went to rent a carriage.

We found a fast one with two strong horses, which took us quickly to the southernmost part of Lithurion that still had paved roads, right before the forest between the kingdoms began.

The ride was good. With a normal road and a fancy carriage, you could travel fast and in comfort. Life was comfortable here when you had a lot of money.

But once we hit the forest, the NPC driver wouldn’t go further. And even if they did, a forest carriage ride sucked ass. Walking was much better.

“Anna,” I turned to her when the trees got dense. “How are you with forests? Got any experience?”

“Not at all,” she said.

“This forest can be very dangerous,” I warned. “You need to recognize signs of territories. Deer zebras, horn snake packs, scorpion suricata tribes. Better just stick close to us, okay?”

“Sure,” said Anna.

“And Zebra Antlion pits,” added Shooya. “Those are mean creatures.”

“By the way,” I asked, realizing I hadn’t checked. “Do you have any levels on you? Some physical skill?”

“I have a black belt in Judo,” Anna replied.

“Uh… that is not exactly what I meant…” I said. Good luck Judo-throwing a two-ton horn snake. “Just stay close to us and try not to get eaten.”

“Yes,” agreed Shooya, puffing out her chest. “We are forest masters.”

I was a forest master. Shooya was a forest apprentice.

“I like it,” said Anna, looking around. “The smell, all the alien plants and sounds. It’s beautiful.”

She moved with grace, too. I saw a lot of people moving in the forest, stumbling over roots, but she was one of the naturals.

At some point, Anna pulled out her flute and began to play. She did that from time to time. She was great with the instrument, playing a jumpy, fun tune that wove itself into the chirping of the insects and the rustling of leaves. It felt like a proper forest theme.

We made a good pace, and in two hours we got to the clearing and saw the gas mask factory.

Originally, it was just a place we built in the middle ground between Aldioly and Lithurion. They were at war, and we needed a neutral place to meet with the Order of the Phoenix. But the place grew over time, and now it was a massive wooden structure used for meetings, a research facility, and gas mask manufacturing.

“Wow, it’s big,” Anna said, impressed. “I couldn’t wait to meet these guys. I have so many questions.”

“Sure,” I said. “They really do need every bit of help they can get.”

“But before I dive in,” Anna said, stopping, “can I get your phone for a quick call? I didn’t really get along with my old group, but I did have one good friend there. I just wanted to tell her that I’m okay.”

“Sure,” I said. “You know how to operate it?”

“Yes,” she said. “Use the magic compass, try to aim the tablet at the receiver location, use the vibration to lock in on it. I’ve done it before.”

I gave her the compass and the tablet.

She took a few steps aside, toward a nearby tree. Probably wanted privacy for her call.

I sat on the grass floor, waiting for Anna to finish.

And then, suddenly, I had this clench feeling in my stomach. Like a bad feeling, but I wasn’t sure why.

Was I being paranoid again?

“Shooya,” I said in low voice. “You trust Anna completely? With your compassion skill and all that?”

“Yeah, I trust her,” she said. “But I don’t think my compassion works well on her. She was controlled, and that gave me a lot of mixed readings. And also, my skill is new; I’m still getting the hang of it. But she is really sweet.”

Yeah, she was.

She said I was a really amazing mage, that was sweet of her.

She was impressed with my simplified training method because she really understood how much time it should take to train a new mage in positive air bubble magic. She said it takes a year to train a new gas mage, and this was normally true.

But on the pirate ship, she said she didn’t know the first thing about magic.

She was controlled then, but she didn’t correct herself after.

I put my hand on my forehead and closed my eyes. Yeah, I hadn’t noticed it because I was flattered. My ego had blinded me.

There were more things… her smile, too. It was exactly the same when she was under control spore, and after she was cleared. It shouldn’t be the same. There were more things that were off.

I suddenly felt very cold. The phone. The factory… FUCK.

I ran toward Anna. She stopped with the phone and gave me a confused look.

“In the end, it didn’t connect,” she said. “For some reason, it wouldn’t work.”

“Maybe because you aimed it in a straight line to Sin-Irsh,” I said quietly.

I then shattered a vial of CO1—Arsenic Sulfide—and enclosed the expanding gas in an air bubble instantly. Then, I shot it fast toward Anna.

She didn’t look scared. Her facial expression barely changed—she didn’t run, just looked at me with a small, almost apologetic smile.

My poison gas was seconds from her when she moved. It was a blur, a cycle executed with supernatural speed. Before I could even register which cycle she used, the air began to crackle. Large, jagged bluish lightning tore through the space between us.

A moment later, the world turned white.

I was hurting. Hurting in a million different places.

I lay on my back, unable to move, barely able to breathe.

Shooya hurried to my side. “No!” she shouted. She felt me all over, checking for damage, though somehow her frantic hands made the pain worse.

Then she looked at Anna with the most hateful look Shooya could manage. It wasn’t much, but it was there.

“How could you!” Shooya all but screamed. “We were friends! I… I hugged you while you slept!”

“I really like you both,” Anna said calmly. “Although, it really wasn’t comfortable. Sleeping while you hugged me, I must admit.”

“You traitor!” Shooya shouted. She stood up, pulling her shadow knife, and started running toward Anna.

But Anna didn’t cast a magic circle. She just waited. She let Shooya get to her, and with a masterful, fluid movement, she caught Shooya’s knife arm. She pivoted, using Shooya’s own momentum against her—a perfect Judo throw—and launched her into the air.

Shooya flew some thirty feet and hit the ground with a heavy THUD. It looked painful, but not dangerous.

“I didn’t lie about the Judo black belt,” Anna said, dusting off her hands. “But I do have strength and magic skills, too. Sorry!”

“You…” I managed to wheeze. “Kill us?”

I was just really curious about it.

“No,” Anna said, walking over to me. “You know, it actually took a lot of effort to electrify you with that range without causing death or internal burns.”

Did she want gratitude? It fucking hurt!

She crouched beside me and gave me a quick kiss on the forehead.

“Thank you for trying to help me,” she said softly. “It’s not like you think. Me and Zylil. We are not like the others.”

“You… are a bamboo slave,” I corrected her, coughing. “And a traitor!”

“We will see about that…” she said, standing up. “Oh, it seems my ride is here.”

I followed her gaze and saw about ten giant seagulls descending toward us.

At first, I thought they would bomb the factory, but they flew to her. And they were carrying something—ropes.

As they got near, I could see each rope was connected to what looked like a reinforced hammock.

They lowered the hammock, and Anna jumped into it, sprawling comfortably as the birds began pulling her up.

“See you around,” Anna shouted, flying up into the air. “Oh, and you might want to tell your friends at the factory to evacuate. They got about one minute.”

Shit! Fuck! Shit-Fuck!

I glanced upward and saw a second flock of giant seagulls. They were higher up, and they were definitely flying toward the factory.

The factory was a few hundred yards from us. I couldn’t shout loud enough.

I started crawling toward the phone. I used all my willpower to force my paralyzed muscles to move, dragging my body through the dirt, inch by agonizing inch.

I picked up the phone and frantically called the factory.

“Camila here,” the phone connected. I didn’t have time to think why she was there.

“Seagull bombing!” I spat at the phone. “Get everyone the fuck out NOW!”

“OUT! EVERYONE NOW!” I heard Camila scream on the other end. She didn’t hesitate, not for a moment.

Seconds later, we saw them spilling from the factory doors.

Someone spotted us and pointed, and they started running toward us.

Camila was running, carrying Hyun-Woo like a baby in her arms. That was… weird.

She dropped him a safe distance away and ran back to the factory. Was there still someone in there?

The seagulls were now directly above the massive wooden structure, dropping something.

“RUN!” I managed a small shout. It didn’t do much good.

The objects hit the ground with a sickening CRACK, and clouds of orange gas escaped them.

Orange spore gas was new. And in this war, new things were always bad.

Camila was out again, running and carrying someone else. Both wore gas masks. But…

WOOSH!

The sound of arrows tore through the air from somewhere in the nearby forest. A small volley of fire arrows, arcing straight at the factory.

Then the fire arrows hit the gas clouds.

BOOM!

The explosion shattered the ground. It was so loud it felt like the earth itself had cracked open. A massive shockwave of heat and force ripped outward as the orange gas ignited, acting like a fuel-air bomb. The factory didn’t just burn; it disintegrated in a blinding destruction.

I looked at Camila and the guy she was carrying. They were both on the ground now, thrown far from where I had last seen them by the force of the blast.

I looked at Camila. For a long time, she didn’t move. Then, suddenly, she stirred. She sat up, looking very confused.

When she came to her senses, she crawled to the other guy. She tried to stir him, to get him up, shaking his shoulders. But he didn’t move.

Camila was high level; it made sense she would survive the shockwave. But a normal guy…

She picked him up and carried him to the others, then they all walked toward me and Shooya.

It was a sad group. A scared group with one dead body. The project they had poured months of their heart and soul into was now nothing but poison ashes and burning timber.

Fucking Agent had explosive gas now.

Fucking gas mask operation was ruined.

And fucking Anna had got us really good.

I suddenly had this small thought at the back of my head, a simple realization: we probably weren’t going to win the war after all.
