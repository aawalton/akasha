The maid set the tray down upon the table, the porcelain clinking softly against the polished wood as she arranged each piece. She placed the cups one after another, her master’s first, then Viktor’s.

“Please,” she said, a warm smile touching her lips. Her aquamarine gaze lifted to meet his for the briefest instant before she straightened once more, resuming the composed demeanor she wore so effortlessly.

A feeling of déjà vu settled over him. When he had gone to Rennald’s house with Claire, they too had been served tea by this very same woman, in this very same manner, down to this very same smile, as though the scene were repeating itself for his benefit.

“Thank you, Agnes,” the Overseer said, and the maid inclined her head in response, before excusing herself from the room without another word.

“Is she always here? At the caravan station?” Viktor asked once the door had closed and her footsteps had faded from earshot.

He knew that Rennald was keeping a few servants with him on the top floor of the caravan station, a space that served as both his office and his residence throughout the working week, until the weekend arrived and he returned home, only to make his way back here come the first day of the following week. Yet, Viktor recalled when he and Claire shared a coach with the Overseer on his journey home, the man was alone, save for the coachman and a single guard. There were no servants accompanying them on that particular trip, and when they arrived at the estate, Agnes had already been waiting at the entrance.

“Not always,” Rennald said. “She makes excellent tea, so I make a point of having her here whenever I have an important guest.”

Viktor chuckled. “So I am an important guest, then?”

He still hadn’t managed to figure out why Rennald placed so much faith in him and Claire. What could possibly account for it? The man himself had claimed to have taken notice of him during Gideon’s meeting, but that hardly seemed reason enough for this sort of attention. Also, when Isadora gave Claire the task of delivering an invitation to Rennald on behalf of the Guild, she had done so because she believed Claire alone was capable of accomplishing it. Viktor’s involvement had never factored into her calculations. So, why? After all, when it came down to it, Claire was but an ordinary employee like any other, nothing more and nothing less.

Instead of giving an answer to the question, Rennald simply lifted his cup and took a sip. Then, setting it back on its saucer, he asked, “How is your sister?”

“Much the same,” Viktor said. “I owe you my thanks, though. If not for the Emerald Mages you sent, her condition might have taken a considerably darker turn.”

“Even so, we have yet to find a permanent solution,” Rennald said with a sigh. “Well, not really...”

Viktor snapped his gaze toward the man. “You found something?”

Rennald looked down into his cup, as if the answer he needed might be found swirling somewhere under its still surface.

“I’ve managed to get in touch with a high-ranking member of the Emerald Order. I told him about Claire’s situation and asked for his opinion. And the response he gave me was... troubling, to say the least. He said this was an extremely delicate matter. He believes that unless one of the Enlightened Twelve intervenes—you know of the Enlightened Twelve, don’t you?”

Viktor nodded. “The leaders of the Emerald Order, right?”

“They are,” Rennald said. “He told me that only someone with power comparable to that of the Enlightened Twelve would be able to give her a real chance of recovery.”

Ugh.

This was becoming more and more ridiculous. According to Lloyd, the Twelve were a bunch of self-important geezers and hags who fancied themselves the equivalent of gods walking among mortals. How the hell was he supposed to drag one of them here to save Claire?

“He said he could attempt it himself,” Rennald went on, “though he wouldn’t put the chance of success higher than five in ten.”

“And what happens if he fails?”

“That’s... hard to say. It may have no effect at all, and she remains as she is. It may cause her condition to deteriorate. Or, in the worst possible outcome, it could kill her.”

“...”

“Anyway, that’s the offer he gives us. Whether to accept it or not is entirely up to you.”

So this is why you called me here, huh? To make this terrible decision.

Should he decline and keep things as they were, leaving Claire in that state for who knew how much longer? Or should he take the gamble, knowing full well what might be lost if it went wrong?

He truly did not want to bear this burden, because if the attempt failed, there would be no one else to blame. If she died as a result, it would be very much the same as if he had killed her with his own hands. But of course, he was the only one who could make this call. Nobody was going to step in and do it for him, however much he might wish otherwise.

“I... I’ll think about it.”

“Yes, take the time to think it through properly. There’s no need to rush.”

Viktor reached for the cup on the table and drained it in one swallow. Whether Agnes’s tea was as good as Rennald said, he had no idea. He didn’t taste anything at all.

“Thank you for telling me. And for finding someone who might help her.” He set the cup back down. “Is there anything else?”

“There are other matters I’d like to discuss, but if now is not the time, they can wait.”

“No need for that,” Viktor said, waving a dismissive hand. “I’m fine. Go ahead.”

Rennald nodded, taking another slow sip of tea. “It seems you don’t have to look for Gideon’s murderer anymore,” he then said.

Ah, that.

“I’ve heard from Chief Secretary Calyssa,” Viktor said. “Apparently, those masked men were also responsible for his death. Though, honestly, I don’t understand why. The last time we met, you asked us a question: who benefited the most from the Guildmaster’s murder? Not you, certainly, nor anyone who wanted the dungeon sold to Clovis. So if those masked men were employed by Iskora, as everyone seems to assume, why would they have gone and killed him? What exactly were they hoping to accomplish?”

“Iskora is not a single entity,” Rennald said. “There are countless interests fighting for control within it. From what I’ve gathered through my own channels, the current Guildmaster of Iskora, Khalid, is at odds with Clovis. So it’s hardly a stretch to imagine he’d want to sabotage the deal.”

Viktor suppressed a chuckle. This was why he liked clever men. They could so easily arrive at an answer that made perfect sense without him having to manipulate anything.

“Other than those two, there are also many other factions, each pursuing their own goals,” Rennald continued. “Some of them are far from happy with the way Khalid has been conducting himself here in Daelin. As it happens, my own friends are among them.”

“The oil merchants?”

“Yes, them. The oil trade had been what kept our town’s economy afloat for so many years, long before young Cedric found that dungeon. Oil is produced in the South and then transported to the North, so that the good people there can burn it for their sacred fire. You know the Eternal Flame, right?”

“I do,” Viktor said. More personally than Rennald could ever imagine, in fact.

“That trade was, and still is, a good business. It’s great for our town, and it’s equally great for the merchants who have built their fortunes on it. But now, with Khalid and his men spilling so much blood here, the relationship between Daelin and Iskora has gone sour, and that’s bad for everyone involved.”

“If those merchants’ profits are being threatened, why don’t they push back?”

“They do try, but the factions who want to seize our dungeon are simply stronger, so there hasn’t been much they could do about it until now. Until, that is, after Iskora suffered a series of great losses. That night, not only was their outpost here burned to the ground, but the construction site back in Iskora was completely destroyed as well. And ever since, opinions within Iskora have begun to shift. More and more people of influence are openly saying it’s time to cut their losses and mend the relationship with our town before it decays any further. It is, after all, simply business to them. If the cost outweighs what can possibly be gained, then it’s time to quit. So, all it takes now is one more major setback, and then Khalid and his ilk will be finished for good.”

Viktor nodded. “The competition.”

“Yes,” Rennald said. “Tomorrow, huh? Tomorrow the fate of our town will be decided, one way or another.”

“You talk like we’ve got decent odds,” Viktor said with a chuckle. “Almost everyone I know isn’t nearly so optimistic. They seem to think that Iskora’s victory is more or less a given.”

Rennald smiled as well. “You are the one who accepted this contest in the first place. Which means, you are confident that we could win this. Am I wrong in thinking so?”

“It seems that unlike Guildmaster Isadora, you’re not mad at me for agreeing with that proposal.”

“Ah, yes. She’s furious, make no mistake about that. That very day, after the meeting had concluded, she came to me almost immediately to discuss what to do next. And even though she tried her very best to keep herself looking composed, to maintain that dignified mask she wears so well, her anger still slipped through. So I told her that you must have had a good reason to agree to such a proposal, and that we should be patient and let you explain yourself before anyone did anything rash.”

So you’re the one who has kept her from biting my head off, huh? But still, this only circled back to the very first question that had been nagging at him: why did Rennald trust him so much?

“Have you heard about my theory?” Viktor asked. “Do you find it a good enough reason to justify the risk?”

“I have. You’re gambling on the dark spirit killing that man for you. Isadora is still not convinced, though. She thinks it’s a stretch to assume the dark spirit will do exactly what you expect of him.”

“And you? What do you think?”

“Me? I find myself wondering whether you know more than you’re letting on. Perhaps you know the dark spirit personally. That’s why you’re willing to stake everything on him.”

What?

Viktor suppressed a curse. This was why he disliked clever men. They could connect the dots and arrive at a conclusion close enough to the truth to make him uncomfortable. Had he done anything careless, anything visibly suspicious, that might have warranted such a pointed guess?

“What makes you think I know the dark spirit?” he asked, plastering an expression of perfect neutrality across his face.

Rennald did not reply. He just took another sip, unhurried, like a man with all the time in the world. “Who do you think the dark spirit is?” he asked.

Was he demanding an answer from the person who knew the dark spirit’s identity, or merely asking for an opinion from someone who had access to nothing more than the same information everyone else possessed?

Viktor gave a shrug. “I suspect that he works for a faction that doesn’t want Khalid and his allies to take our dungeon. It could be Clovis, or the oil merchants who wants peace with Daelin, or another group we don’t know yet.”

“That so?”

“How about you, then? Who do you think he is?”

Rennald was silent for a while, before at last uttering the words.

“The Protector of Daelin.”

...What?

Of all the things he could have said, he chose that?

Viktor could understand if some ignorant folk in Daelin believed such a thing. But Rennald? Rennald, with his keen judgment and careful calculations? Rennald, the King of Daelin himself?

“Why would you think so?”

“Why can’t I? Everything he’s done so far has benefited this town, am I correct? At the very least, I’d say my theory holds as much weight as yours.”

“If he’s really the Protector of Daelin, then where has he been until now? What did he do during the monster attack eight years ago, when the town needed its protector the most?”

“The crisis was averted in the end, was it not?”

“That was because the monsters simply left on their own.”

“Yes, that was what we saw, what was visible on the surface. But we don’t know what actually happened behind the scenes. What if someone took care of the problem without anyone ever knowing? Destroyed the monsters’ lair. Killed whoever had set them loose on us. And that, in turn, forced them to retreat.”

Interesting story, Viktor thought, but completely wrong from start to finish. There had been no dark spirit here eight years ago; that much he knew with absolute certainty.

Yet, when those words came out of Rennald’s mouth, they seemed to carry absolute conviction. The Overseer truly thought that was the case, didn’t he? Why? What could possibly have led him to believe such a thing?

Rennald let out a breath. “Apologies. Forget a man’s rambling. Anyway, I don’t know if you two truly have a connection or not. But I believe you’re both doing what is best for Daelin. So if you—and he, if he can hear this somehow—ever need anything from me, just say the word, and I’ll see what can be done.”

This is... weird, Viktor thought.

Still, Rennald didn’t appear to be a threat. So he should just accept his offer and save the questions for later. He had an enemy to kill tomorrow, after all, and that was all that mattered at the moment.

“I’ll keep that in mind,” he said with a shrug.
