Moonlight

It was a headache working with the Visitors. The Scientist hadn't worked with many, but largely, they were the same. Visionless. Cowards one and all. They had their uses, but not many, and they tended to fuck them up. Case and point, the pair that had been collecting specimens for him. Other Visitors, apparently, which was fine by him. Where the specimens came from didn't matter so much as they kept rolling in. But then they failed and had to make it his problem.

Now he was stuck chasing around some cretin and was none too pleased when he kept finding the corpses of the Homo Fertili scattered about. He collected the Chimeras strewn around the field as he walked. The rodent was heading to the barn. He was sure of it. Good. The generator was in his basement anyway and protected by an intricate spatial puzzle that even he found difficult to navigate at times.

The fact that the barn hadn't lit up yet was all the proof he needed to know his theory was correct.

After the barn was lit up the visitor would have to get past his failures, which had their own set of challenges.

Since he started helping the Instance Holder, no one had ever escaped their grasp. It was like they were designed for each other. The divine and the mortal coming together for beautiful creation. It was so perfect that there could only be one explanation. Fate.

He was someone fated to achieve his goals, so much so that some entity had descended from on high to give him the perfect conditions to complete his work.

His track record was flawless, which is why the fact that the pest had made it so far bothered him so. He needed to collect the troublemaker so there would be no mar on his soul. Better to hit a nail with a sledgehammer than to risk stepping on it, as the saying goes.

He was close to perfecting his Chimeras, to achieving a perfectly balanced being. Balance just happened to look a little ugly, which was unfortunate, but it was a kink that could be worked out. A work in progress, if you would.

He was making his way back, the one called Maggie in tow, with another 19 Homo Fertili trailing behind.

The Scientist snapped his fingers repeatedly to get the hag's attention. "The barn. Go keep watch with the Homo Fertili." He grabbed a silver amulet around his neck, and his words became thick in the air. "Bring the Visitor to me alive." Every Homo Fertili shivered before going still. The command had been set.

The hag hesitated for a moment, and the Scientist had to quell the frustrated weariness inside of him. He turned and began walking to his house, a dismissive wave following him. He had to prepare the operating room.

She would follow his instructions, or she wouldn't. If she didn't, he'd scrap her for parts after this was all over; he had more than enough Homo Fertilli that he no longer needed Visitors as helpers, yet the elusive Instance Holder still insisted on keeping that option open as a condition for "winning."

He found it silly, but the thing was his benefactor. At least until he perfected the careful balancing act of the humors. Then they could move on to the next project.

These narcissistic delusions of grandeur filled the scientist's walk to his house, but as he approached, his tired expression turned into a frown. Even from a distance, he could tell something was wrong. His house looked off, and as he moved closer, he realized why. All the windows were shattered, and wood planks stood on their ends, making it seem like a tornado had ripped through the area.

A heavy pang of sorrow filled his heart. His home, his house, the place where he worked and rested had been ransacked. Destroyed and marred to the point that it was ugly to look at. Every step closer he took, the more he noticed that was wrong. It was easier to point out the things that weren't out of place than the inverse.

He carefully stepped inside the building, the lack of the usual spatial magic that wreathed his home painfully evident. There wasn't much time to process how it had happened when he saw a single disembodied leg in the threshold to the living room.

Putting two and two together on how the creature died didn't take a man of The Scientist's intellect. The more interesting, and rage-inducing, question was how the spatial magic was broken in the first place.

All other pieces of destruction could be accounted for through that.

He autopiloted himself to the hall closet and opened the door to step through into his master bedroom, only to be met with the actual contents of the closet. He scowled. This alone was too much of a slight to bear. The Visitor who did this was truly rancid. Would make for a horrible test subject. He'd take out the rat's toenails. Maybe staple a tail to his neck. Infinite possibilities.

For the first time in a long while, The Scientist took the stairs to the second floor. It was a precarious thing as they were shattered and barely climbable, but he managed regardless. The portraits caught his eye next, but he paid them little mind. He wasn't sure who the people in the portraits were or why the house had so many guest beds, but it was the House the Instance Holder had provided for him. So, who was he to complain, but now it was gone. The Instance Holder could probably– would probably remake it, but still. It was his house. It was his, and despite his best efforts at protecting it, someone had broken it.

His nose twitched as he smelt the strong odor of gasoline. Right. The generator.

He made his way down to the basement, keeping an eye out for if the intruder was still around, but it seemed the house was truly empty. Making his way to the power switches, the Scientist tried to shut off the power, but a force repelled him. He scowled, stomping in indignation over to the generator. The same force repelled him. Everything looked to be in place, some tools, a single gas canister, and the like, but he couldn't help but frown. Something was missing. He couldn't place his finger on it. He couldn't put it together, and that only sought to frustrate him more.

He shouted in anger. He grabbed the nearest object and chucked it at the generator, but that same damn force again.

The anger built and built, and suddenly, the scientist was by the operating table. His yells bounced and echoed through the basement as he slammed the table to the side. His tools scattered across the floor. He stumbled to the desk and tore at the pages, repeatedly slamming his fists into the table.

His fury turned to the caged creatures as he kicked the cage, causing the beasts inside to cower deeper within.

"STUPID STUPID THINGS." He took a deep breath, kneeling down to one of his oldest creations. He held his hand out, and the Chimera moved closer and plopped their grotesque chin into the Scientist's hand.

The man raised his thumb, slowly rubbing the creature's muzzle as he breathed deeply. "You poor mess of an abomination." his voice was sweet despite the vicious words and the creature, not knowing better, preened at the tone. "I look at you every day to remind myself how far I've come—the obstacles I've left behind—but also to shame myself. Shame myself that I could have created something like you once upon a time. You're disgusting and once I perfect my work, I'll kill you and I'll have been forgiven for the transgression that is your creation."

The man retracted his hand and dusted off his coat jacket.

The Homo Fertillis and the female Visitor had probably captured their little rat by this point. Maybe that one would be the catalyst for his perfect Chimera. He'd love to just kill the cretin, but that would be such a waste of material, and at the end of the day, that was the only thing holding him back.

Moonlight illuminated the man's path to the barn, its silhouette looming over the cornfield, and for the first time, the Scientist paused. The chill wind finally reached its tendrils into the man's mind as he felt something off. He stared at the barn, but couldn't figure out what it was.

He made it to the doors and swung them open. Or tried to, but something was barring them from the other side.

"Visitor Maggie," he hissed out, "I don't know what games you think you are playing, but it'd be in your best interest to cease with them and open the door."

His eyes widened as he saw a shadow recede from the corner of the barn further down its edge. There was someone here. Was it his little helper, or was it the rat? He didn't know, but he knew that the power relay for the telephone was at the back of the barn. So whichever Visitor was heading back there planned to beat the Instance, which meant he'd have to kill them.

He pulled a gun from his side, released the safety, and crept along the opposite edge of the barn. He found the door to the shed. Leveling the gun, the Scientist turned the corner and locked eyes with someone. There, next to the final switch, sitting on a raised box, empty-handed, was the rat who had presumably destroyed his house. A vicious smile spread on the rat's face. His skin was that of rich chestnut, and his eyes were wide and focused. He wore torn or shredded pieces of clothing, and … the Scientist's eyes widened as he saw the Homo Canine's leg melded with the rat's hand.

What kind of madman would do something like that?

'A desperate one,' the Scientist thought.

The Visitor that had been causing him so much trouble raised his good hand. He briefly looked to the side, using his thumb to scratch the stubble on his chin. After a moment of contemplation, the Visitor switched to his pointer finger and began rhythmically flicking his wrist towards the scientist

"Ahh. You know. I was getting worried you wouldn't show up in time."
