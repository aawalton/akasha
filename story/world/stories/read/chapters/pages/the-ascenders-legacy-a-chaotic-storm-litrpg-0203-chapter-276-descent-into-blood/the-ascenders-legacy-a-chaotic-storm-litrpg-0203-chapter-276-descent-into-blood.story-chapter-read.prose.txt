"What!" Brennon exclaimed, shaking his head. "What gave them that idea? Does Arkhan think so too?"

"Arkhan doubts it's possible," Aodhán replied cautiously.

"But you don't." Baxter deduced, his gaze narrowing. "You think it's possible."

Aodhán's lips tightened into a firm line of determination. "I think many things we deem impossible are actually possible. We just haven't found the means to make them so."

"Be that as it may, can we trust this Arkhan?" Magda asked with a troubled expression. "I mean, all we know so far is what he's told us. Isn't it possible that he's lying?"

Besides, what reason would he have to come up with such an elaborate lie and then go through the trouble of gathering everyone together?"

Aodhán shook his head, highly doubting that was true. Arkhan was weird, sure, but he doubted it was to that extent.

His certainty, however, was brought into question a moment later when Destra asked, "Do you trust him then?"

Brennon frowned at him. "I thought this Arkhan was your friend? Don't you trust your friends?"

Brennon grimaced. "Such a bleak view. I trust everyone here with my life."

"Of course you do," Destra scowled at him. "I would be surprised if you didn't."

"It's not," Aodhán nodded. "But it's better than our chances alone, except—" He shuffled awkwardly. "Except my plan works."

Everyone perked up at that, and Destra asked, "What plan is this again?"

"It's an alternative plan I came up with when I was discussing with Arkhan," Aodhán replied a bit defensively. "It's not a solid plan yet, but it could work, and if it does, it could improve our chances of survival to fifty or sixty percent."

"What?" Magda jumped to her feet, eyes wide. "Tell us the plan. We will carry it out diligently."

"Calm down, Magda," Baxter shouted, looking agitated. "We still need to hear out this plan and decide on its risks versus reward." He glared at Aodhán. "Aodhán here isn't known for his rational plans."

Aodhán bit back a snide remark and just sighed. "I'm not telling you guys the plan yet, not until it's solid. There's no need to raise your hopes until I'm certain."

Everyone deflated at once, their minds having gone through a roller coaster of emotions in the last few hours.

"What happens if your plan doesn't work?" Destra asked after a few seconds of silence.

"Then we head to the twin-peaked mountain at sunrise and prepare for the worst."

A terse silence settled over them until Magda broke it, a bit hesitantly. "Baxter told us you intend to use your miracle rain to boost our strength by morning."

Aodhán nodded. "Among other things. I'm more interested in you all accessing your origin planes, but I've had a rethink of the timing, and it's better to do it very close to the forty-eighth-hour deadline. That way, if you all gain seals and go berserk, you'll have a target."

Disbelief rippled across their faces, and they all exchanged glances.

"Wait a minute," Brennon began, scratching his ears as if to make sure he was hearing right. "These targets you speak of—are they the same creatures waiting to unleash their wrath on the surface? The blood phoenix and the likes?"

Aodhán sighed. "I have no intention of using you guys as bait or wasting your lives."

"Then help us understand what you're saying," Baxter replied. "Because I don't think you're phrasing it well."

Aodhán sighed. "I want you all to get stronger, not just for your sakes, but because it also improves our overall chances against the creatures. There are no cores in the realm, so there's no way for anyone to get stronger except through insight and inspiration."

"With a sufficiently strong inspiration," Aodhán continued, "a seal is almost guaranteed, giving you an edge you will no doubt need. That edge, however, comes with a twist, as you well know, and that's why I'm suggesting that timing."

"If any of you succumb to the twisted effect, I'd rather you channel all that chaotic rage against the creatures rather than your fellow captives, inadvertently reducing our already meager numbers."

That's how it is." She locked gazes with Aodhán, and her certainty faltered. "Is there a way to avoid it?"

Aodhán opened his mouth to say he wasn't sure, but before he could speak, Baxter cursed, his eyes widening as he realized a tiny yet crucial detail he'd missed during Aodhán's evolution.

Aodhán had gained a seal during his evolution and hadn't gone berserk.

Oh, he'd fought brutally against the winged gargoyles and had flooded the earth in a deluge to drown their earthbound counterparts, but he hadn't gone berserk.

He looked up at Aodhán, viewing him with newfound respect and a touch more admiration. "How is it possible?"

"I don't know," Aodhán replied honestly. "I stumbled upon it, and now I want to test it out with you guys. If it works, then I'm right, and you'll never have to suffer from the twisted effect ever again."

The words thundered in all their ears, and each person stared at Aodhán with wide eyes. His words sounded impossible, but so also was gaining a familiar at the evolved tier or an aspect at the advanced.

This man before them was a walking, talking impossibility. If anyone could somehow stumble on a cure for the twisted effect, it would be him.

The tension in the air intensified, though not from the group themselves. The air thickened with anticipation, as if a thousand gazes had just landed on them in an instant.

Magda shuddered. "It's like there are a thousand eyes on me."

"That's probably true," Aodhán replied, unfazed by the cultists' reaction. They were most likely even more curious about the twisted effect and how he'd avoided it. Knowing he was a transmigrant made them doubly interested, but Aodhán wasn't about to declare his thoughts for them to hear.

He turned his gaze back to the others and said, "We have about twenty-seven hours left till the forty-eight-hour deadline. That's a little more than a day. You all should get some sleep."

"What about you?" Magda asked. "Don't you need to sleep too?"

"No," Aodhán shook his head. "There are some things I need to test out first."

He left for a secluded corner of the camp after that, his comrades watching him leave with troubled expressions.

"How can we sleep when he's over there strategizing on how to keep us safe?" Magda muttered. "He's already saved me once, and he's planning to do so again. The least I can do is be useful."

She stood up from her cosmic chair and retreated to a corner of the camp, muttering something to herself. Baxter and Destra did the same, leaving Brennon to stare at Aodhán's cloud bed in longing before he too turned his back on the cloud and headed to his own corner of the camp to meditate and train.

Back in his corner, Aodhán began laying the foundation of his plan. If Serenya Serenity was convinced the creatures could be persuaded, there was probably a way forward.

Aodhán didn't know the woman personally, but anyone who could cultivate the affinity of serenity to the forty-eighth tier must have gained some wisdom along the way.

More likely than not, her suggestion required her, the telepath, and a powerful empath working together, but Aodhán's plan didn't require any of that. All it needed was Varéc and the hope that the dragon would respect kin—a creature like itself—and not try to kill it immediately.

That was assuming it had any kind of intelligence, but from the wolf's actions in Brennon's dream, Aodhán assumed it could at least think.

"How are you feeling, Varéc?" he asked his familiar, even though he could already feel the excitement bubbling from Varéc. For his familiar, this was nothing more than a slightly dangerous adventure.

Whatever the dragon's reaction to him was, Varéc couldn't die as long as Aodhán was still alive. And in the event that he was completely obliterated, he would simply reappear in Aodhán's spirit—severely weakened, but ultimately fine.

Aside from that, the supposedly dangerous mission was risk-free, and Varéc was more than excited to see a dragon who wasn't a simulation or a construct for the first time.

We are not cultists, as the cultists would have the creatures believe, but fellow captives held prisoner by the same people who imprisoned them. If anything, we should be allies."

Varéc growled satisfactorily in his mind, and Aodhán got the distinct impression that he was taking notes.

Aodhán smiled. "It's not the best plan, but you can't die anyway, so we have nothing to lose if this fails."

Varéc growled again, his excitement growing, and Aodhán's smile widened. "Now, as for finding the dragon's quadrant—what are the chances we're standing right above it?"

Varéc growled, guessing an outrageously impossible number.

Aodhán smiled. "Well, we can't know for sure until we find out."

But to find out, Aodhán realized he had to first overcome the static interference of essence affecting perception in the realm.

Clearing the frost from beneath him, Aodhán crossed his legs in a meditative pose, placed his palms flat against the cold ground, and closed his eyes.

Usually, he extended his core sense outward in a wide arc to cover a circular area. This time, however, Aodhán channeled his senses downward, probing into the earth beneath him.

At once, he felt the drag of the realm itself resisting him—static interference humming through the earth like off-kilter radio signals. It was disorienting, making it almost impossible to sense anything of worth, but Aodhán wasn't discouraged.

He had only just begun.

He pushed deeper into the earth, probing. The deeper his senses went, though, the more chaotic the interfering weaves of essence became—interlaced purposefully into the soil itself to prevent anyone from sensing what they'd hidden beneath.

But Aodhán was a man on a mission. Had he not known that something was down there, he might have given up. But now that he knew, he was determined to push through until he found them.

His focus sharpened with determination, and everything else fell away as his senses probed deeper into the earth until the interference became so loud that static was all his mind's eye picked up.

The interference clawed at his concentration, each pulse a disruption that completely drowned out any other signals.

Aodhán pressed forward regardless, pushing his range further until, suddenly, he could push no more.

Core sense had reached the limit of its range—only a foot or two beyond 150 meters.

It was impressive that Core Sense had managed to probe that far considering the interference and all the solid matter in his way.

However, now that Aodhán had reached his limit, he realized he wouldn't even be able to complete the first phase of his plan without taking drastic action. He would have to go down there himself.

The mere thought of it—the claustrophobia, the lack of air or space—made him shudder in repulsion, and he could almost hear Daruk scolding him for being too reckless. But there would be no point to his plan if he didn't do this.

Besides, with {Lightning Body—Silver}, he could probably be up and down in an instant. No problems save for the claustrophobia, of course, and the interference that would no doubt try to pull his lightning form apart.

"Brennon," he called out in a loud whisper, and the illusion awakened jerked awake, looking left and right in confusion. When his gaze landed on Varéc, his eyes widened, and he nearly let out a squeak that would have jolted the others out of their meditation had Aodhán not teleported to shush him immediately.

That was where the interference really began, and Aodhán felt a slight itchy sensation as his immaterial body passed through it. The weight of the earth pressing down on him intensified slightly after that point, pressing down on the edges of his existence—a constant drag tugging at the cohesion of his lightning body.

But Aodhán barely spared the pressure any mind. He had more than enough energy and willpower to keep {Lightning Body } active for weeks, if not months.

But Aodhán was determined not to return to the surface without an answer. Bracing himself, he phased down once more, his body blurring until he reached the point where core sense had stopped.

He was now approximately 300 meters deep within the earth.

Aodhán doubted he could afford to descend much further than this, and so it was with a mental prayer that he extended his core sense once again, pushing deep into the earth.

At first, he sensed nothing other than the roar of static. He nearly gave up.

But just as his core sense hit the limit of its range, a sensation of power brushed against his mind—a blazing core brimming with energy.

But it wasn't the fiery essence of black flames Aodhán had hoped for.

It was blood.
