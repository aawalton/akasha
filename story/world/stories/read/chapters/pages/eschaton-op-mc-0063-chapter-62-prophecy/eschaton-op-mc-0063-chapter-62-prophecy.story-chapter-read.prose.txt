The dozen or so of us follow Pythia to her home in Greece, along the coast. It’s on the outskirts of an isolated village, situated on a lonely, rocky cliff. It’s actually quite idyllic. The late summer day is warm, the ocean breeze is pleasant—or would be, if I was flesh and blood. The crashing of the waves against the cliff face below is almost hypnotic. Birds squawk and cry, with Estrella joining them high above us. She’d set the house and the landscape on fire if she drew too close.

A young man exits the humble house and staggers. The combined metaphysical weight of so many Ascended must be overwhelming. Even the world struggles to handle it. He recovers quickly enough, and Pythia ruffles his hair endearingly.

“Ah, Grandson, help me and my old bones, hm?”

He unnecessarily helps her onto the porch and into a rocking chair.

“Dearies, before we begin, you should know that while I’m never wrong, I’m never entirely right, either. I can paint broad strokes, but never the details. I may predict things that will take weeks to pass in the same breath as others that will take place tomorrow.”

“Can you at least point us in the right direction? For everything we need to find.” Morgan asks.

“That’s the plan, dearie! I can least give you a starting place, hm? But there’s one thing you all should know.”

She takes a glass of cherry iced tea from her grandson, who gives us all a stiff nod before kneeling down next to her with a hand on her shoulder.

“Ascended are hard to see. The more they’re involved in something, the harder it is for me to be sure. You dearies are all so bright, yes,” she says beaming with a grandmother’s pride.

“I can still see a little, though. Enough to help! Now, let us begin!”

She takes another sip of her cherry tea before setting it down on a side table. She closes her eyes and takes a deep breath. The world breathes with her, and an energy swells in the air. A breeze swirls around us, bringing with it the faint smell of smoke, ash, and incense. A song dances on the wind, just loud enough to be sure it’s there but too quiet to make out the words. She breathes out, and the world matches her in time.

With every breath the smells grow more intense and the singing louder. Instruments join in, and the world sings an acapella with her. The squawking birds her chorus, the pounding waves her drums, the soft wind a quiet flute. She starts to glow as Potentia pours out from her in waves only ever matched by Morgan in her most energetic moments.

Pythia twitches and writhes with painful looking spasms. Sweat beads her brow, and her grandson wipes it away with a cloth. Her hands scrabble at the chair, nails digging deep gouges into the aged wood.

The music reaches a crescendo, and Pythia’s trembling, glowing body tenses once more. Her back arches and a groan of pain escapes from her before she exhaustedly collapses back into her chair. Her eyes flutter open and she grasps at the cherry tea next to her. Her grandson helps her take a sip.

After a few minutes of Pythia gasping with shuddering breaths, she finally turns her gaze to us, her eyes blazing with light. She coughs and groans, clearly wrung dry by the ordeal. She’s barely lucid, hovering on the edge of unconsciousness.

“The Sorceress and her Brightest Knight will lead the way through the dark. The General will command the troops and battle against the horde. The Devourer, The Blur, The Wanderer, and the Open Sky will struggle against the King of Monsters. The Honest and The Passionate will combat The Liar’s falsehoods. The Blacksmith will forge the plow and the sword. The Glorious Four will fight against the Lord of Darkness and his Vassals of Horror.”

Her eyes flutter; only sheer force of will is keeping her awake.

“The Stalwart will show the meaning of honor, and The Keeper will contain his greatest threats. The Many-Faced and the Widow will stymie The Vile.

“Brightest Knight of Valor, head to a land of sun and swamp for the heart of growth, the first of three things you will need. Beware the avarice and those await in shadow.”

Her eyes flutter close, and she slumps to one side.

“Go,” she says weakly before falling asleep. Her grandson carries her inside her humble home. The rest of us retreat a distance away, and all of us politely ignore Pythia’s flaring and uncontrolled Aura. Whatever she did cost her dearly.

General Guan Yu looks perturbed. I prod him for his thoughts, and he sighs.

“I had hoped warfare between us could be stopped. That we could be better. I suppose it was a vain thought.” He draws himself up. “I shall have to attend to my duties, both mundane and Ascended. It seems I have been proclaimed to lead humanity’s forces against the horde of monsters.”

“We all have our tasks, it seems,” Singularity says. “Though I am not sure if everyone mentioned is actually here. As for who the Devourer is… well, I’m not sure I care much for the title. Who is The Vile? The Liar? And more importantly, who is the King of Monsters?”

We all look at each other warily, unsure of who exactly is who. Though not all of the given titles were so vague.

“Well, I guess we know who The Sorceress and The Brightest Knight are,” Estrella says wryly.

“That is not up for debate,” Sĭwáng rasps in agreement. “Neither is The Widow. That name would belong to me.”

“As important as determining the deeper meaning behind this prophecy, we have more pressing matters,” Guan says firmly. “Such as beginning the Arcology project. We do not know when or where the events described will happen—if they will happen at all. Pythia herself did say she had trouble seeing us, and only paints broad strokes.”

“Agreed,” Hashimoto says. “We have too much work to do with too little time. I need to return to my own laboratory and start my own work on it.”

“I’m going to look into possibly growing additional crystals,” Morgan says. “If we only find a couple of the water and sunlight crystals, we need a way to make more.”

“Even lesser copies would have a great deal of value,” Guan Yu agrees. “Even better if they can be mass produced. Though no cost should be spared when it comes to feeding the world.”

“The rest of us will learn more about who the unknowns are. We cannot afford to have them interfere with the work,” Galahad says. “I’ve heard of another Ascended in France, though they are only rumors. Whoever they are, they’ve done their best to stay hidden.”

“I can come with you, in case they’re The Liar,” Estrella offers.

“I will come with as well,” the Chinese man wearing a suit and cape says. “I’m probably The Honest mentioned.” He grins. “I never lie.”

The world agrees with him. What he says is never false.

“Feng Wei, at your service,” he says with a melodramatic bow.

Galahad nods gratefully. “I will not turn down your assistance. We cannot afford to underestimate our enemies.”

“I suggest we leave at least one of us to guard Pythia while she recovers,” Guan Yu says. “I would offer Warden’s assistance in this matter.”

“Why?” I ask.

“If she truly can tell the future, then our enemies that we so clearly have will seek to turn her against us. Or silence her. As much as I loathe to admit it, we are at war. We must act as such. Valuable strategic assets must be defended.”

“I will defend Pythia with my life,” Warden says, standing at parade rest, eyes locked straight ahead. He looks the consummate soldier. None of us disagree with either of them, but all of us look uncomfortable over it.

“We have a plan of action,” the General says, seeing no dissent. “Let us carry it out.”

I step through the return portal, made by Morgan’s will made manifest and brought into reality by her brush. She follows quickly behind, and we’re left in her gallery. She quickly busies herself with preparing for the days and weeks ahead.

“Morgan, I’m going to go see Second Squad, see if they can help. I’m not sure where this ‘land of sun and swamp’ is, but when we do find it we’ll want to be ready to go.”

“Sounds good. If I have any updates on what I need, I’ll let you know. I need to set up a line of communication with Hashimoto. I think we’re going to need robots or golems to make this practical. We’re really gonna have to pack in those hydroponic platters very tightly if we want a chance in hell of growing enough food. Definitely too tight for people to work them. I’ve heard he’s good at those.”

A whirlwind of a random assortment of objects fly off tables and sort themselves in a series of cubbies against one wall. Half built prototypes—wards included—are shoved into a corner to make more space.

“Alright, I’m off, see you later Morgan.”

“In a while, crocodile.”

After a blistering sprint towards Denver, I reach one of the many huge gates. Each one is double-layered, with a pair of doors setup like an airlock. Each door is a foot thick slab of infused steel. They take huge motors to hold open, so in the event of power loss, they slam shut.

When I arrive, I’m directed towards one of the smaller side gates, still set up airlock style. This one is smaller and much more manageable for small groups of people or single vehicles. The other one was designed for huge convoys or large numbers of vehicles at once. The trains have their own dedicated gates built around the pre-existing lines.

After finally making it to one of the many new military airbases around Denver, I come across the Pineapple Express and Second Squad.

“Good, you’re here,” I tell Captain Rains after finding him. “I’ve got a lot to fill you in on.”
