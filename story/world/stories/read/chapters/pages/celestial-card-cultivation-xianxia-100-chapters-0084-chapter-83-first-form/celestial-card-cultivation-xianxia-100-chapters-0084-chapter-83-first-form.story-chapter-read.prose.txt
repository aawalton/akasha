Jianyi spent the next two days recuperating his strength and training.

The manual had made it very clear that one was not supposed to attempt the next stage of body cultivation without at least ten days of rest between imbibing the elixirs. And that was at the first stage only; at later stages—and even more so at later realms—the gaps would grow.

So even those with access to unlimited resources couldn’t rush through multiple realms in weeks or months.

As he trained, his focus shifted more and more to the Eternal Spear Meditation.

He had been practicing it now for a few months, and as he refined each movement with his new strength, he built the confidence that it was time to take the next step.

Just like the Fivefold Spear Art, the Eternal Spear Meditation was based on movements and forms.

Minghao had confirmed that his mastery of the movements was at a stage where it would need time and experience to improve—he had internalized the fundamentals.

That meant he could move on.

The first form was called Eternal Ray of Sun. The closest equivalent was Piercing Fang. Physically, the biggest difference was that it included a wrist roll at the apex of the move, but Jianyi was confident that he would manage.

What made this, of course, very different from the forms he had learned for the Fivefold Spear Art was that he needed to learn the qi cycling patterns in parallel. Otherwise, it would be a nice new martial art, but not a spirit cultivation technique.

However, the form was, naturally, a product of the movements that went into it, which came with qi flows of their own. So what it boiled down to was learning the new physical form and the connections between the individual cycling patterns.

The movements for the Eternal Ray of Sun were Eternal Sun, Dawn, Veil, and Dusk—or in other words, rise, advance, close, and retreat.

Fortunately, the technique manual described in detail how the form was to be executed, and Minghao helped him get the form itself in decent shape.

Once that was up to a minimum proficient level, he started working on the qi cycling. It was a process that required patience, but he made it work, and after three days of intense practice, he felt for the first time qi surge into his spirit.

It started with the gates on his feet, then legs, then arms, and finally lungs.

The amount he gathered exceeded the kata by a lot, even though that included all eight movements and not just four.

Looking into his lower dantian after having run through the form for an hour, the progress was noticeable.

He settled down to grill a few pieces of spirit beast meat for lunch. Minghao now did small tours of the area on his own while Jianyi was cultivating, and on his last trip, he had found a few herbs that could serve as a salad to accompany the meat. It felt like a proper meal, even if eaten while sitting on uncomfortable rocks—though he had noticed that since his first step into the Body Tempering Realm, the level of discomfort had declined.

While chewing on his steak, Jianyi reflected on the Eternal Ray of Sun and, with the bit of distance that a good meal brought, he wondered whether he had made the same mistake with the form that Minghao had called him out on earlier for his work with the Fivefold Spear Art.

While he used the Eternal Spear Meditation as a cultivation technique, the Eternal Spear Art was, first and foremost, a martial art. As such, he should treat every movement and form with the same focus and intent that he did in his regular spear practice.

The Eternal Ray of Sun had to be executed as if he wanted to kill an opponent, not to have his hair floating in the wind beautifully.

He nodded to himself, put his food to the side, and went into the starting position.

He imagined the beaver he had defeated days ago standing across from him.

His qi was primed.

One deep breath.

He lunged.

His gates roared open, and he could almost sense the turbulence in the qi around the camp as it came streaming into his spirit.

He exhaled forcefully.

It was very powerful—but also exhausting. That level of energy could not be brought to every form for an hour at a time.

Thinking about the trade-off, he heard Minghao clear his throat behind him.

“By far your best attempt. Once you can bring that level of focus and intensity to every form, in practice, while cultivating, or in a fight, you will have achieved a new level of mastery.”

Jianyi felt heat rising up his neck. He had felt his personal progress, and it was nice to be acknowledged for it.

***

Jianyi had made quite a bit of progress on his spirit cultivation since they had entered the wilderness—but now, it surged forward.

The qi in his lower dantian that was accumulating on the bottom like sediment in a lake had grown continuously, and after two more days of rigorous practice, he felt that he was close.

He took a short break to stretch his muscles and eat some of the meat that they had dried—another of Minghao’s lessons.

To get back into the right mindset, he practiced the kata of movements once, which garnered him a bit of qi—he had to grin, remembering how much qi he thought that was when he first completed the kata—bringing him ever closer to the third stage.

The feeling of fullness in his lower dantian hadn’t left him for quite some time now.

Then, he stepped back and centered himself. Sometimes, he still imagined a foe, but he tried to move away from that. He wanted to be able to bring all his focus, all his intent, without a crutch.

His left foot moved forward.

Qi circulated through his spirit.

Hips and shoulders provided the energy for his arms to catapult his spear forward.

Gates opened.

A slight twist of his wrists.

Qi entered his spirit—multiple times more than during the kata before.

He stepped back into the starting position.

Smiling, he looked into his lower dantian. Like a second layer, the motes of qi that had accumulated since he had been able to cultivate again had settled at the bottom of his dantian and had now taken on the same silvery hue that the first layer had. He had advanced to the third stage of the Qi Gathering Realm.

It was debatable whether he was halfway through the realm now, as every stage took more qi than the one before, but he was certainly making progress.

He felt the quality of his pathways, dantians, and even his body improve. The physical impact was less than from reaching the first stage of his Soul-Forged-Body, but it was noticeable nonetheless.

Surprisingly, he felt slightly disappointed that he couldn’t tell his friends about it—and that came with a brief jolt of pain, thinking about Jun—but then he focused on the achievement. Depending on how Minghao would assess his improvements with the spear and in woodcraft, he was pretty close to achieving Elder Sun’s goals.

Previously, he had resolved to write a letter to his mother when he was back in the sect, but now he felt like there was no reason not to write her now. He could then write another one once back and send them both together.

He was sure that she would appreciate being kept abreast of his progress. Even though she had never talked about these things with him, he was pretty sure that she knew a lot more about the challenges that a young cultivator faced.

Now that he thought about it, he realized that he didn’t really know when his mother and father had met. It had never felt that important, and when he had thought of it, he hadn’t wanted to ask, to not make his mother sad.

Relationships between mortals and cultivators were not that uncommon—apparently also something that had changed over the centuries of the war—but there was no denying that they brought challenges with them. The differences in life expectancies first and foremost.

Jianyi was sure that he would never want a relationship like that—he couldn’t imagine a scenario where his wife would die before him, where his children would die before him if they couldn’t pass the Heavenly trial.

No, there was no space for something like that in his life.

He had too many demons to kill.

***

That evening, to celebrate his success, Minghao shared one of the remaining meals that he had prepared back in the sect.

“Minghao, how quickly do disciples usually progress through the stages?”

The man shrugged. “The spectrum is large. Don’t forget, there are some that never make it out of the Qi Gathering Realm—whether that is due to lack of talent or drive. It is a small share, though. It is considered good, and one of the usual requirements to become an inner disciple, to get to Qi Condensation within a year—but some strong cultivators took longer. You should expect one in five or so to be that fast.”

Jianyi’s thoughts raced—he was on the third stage now, and they were still weeks away from the half-year mark. That meant he should be well on track. It felt as if a weight had been lifted from his shoulders. He knew that he shouldn’t be that competitive—the demons were his enemies, not his sect brothers and sisters, after all—but he couldn’t help himself.

Minghao continued, unaware of Jianyi’s thought. “It also depends on resources. If somebody is very talented with the spear, performs well in the mid-year assessment, gets access to more resources, they can buy more cultivation pills and get into a self-reinforcing process of gathering strength. Talent, luck, hard work—just like in the mortal world, it is hard to be successful without all three.”

That resonated with Jianyi, and he was well aware that he had been lucky to have Elder Sun and Minghao help him so much.

Still, that left him positive about his progress.

Minghao clapped his hands. “Enough about all this. Let’s talk about your test.”
