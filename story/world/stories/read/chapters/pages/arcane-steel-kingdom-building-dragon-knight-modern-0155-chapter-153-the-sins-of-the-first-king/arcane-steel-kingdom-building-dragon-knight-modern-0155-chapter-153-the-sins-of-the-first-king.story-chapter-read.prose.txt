While Nigel was having his mid-life crisis—or was it a mid-bloodline crisis?—I couldn't help but notice the resemblance between the Dragon Kings’ blessing and the one Tes had used. Most likely, the Dragon Kings’ blessing was the exact same energy utilized by the Dragon World Tree’s moderator. Speaking of which, in about two years I’ll have another one ready to use.

My thoughts were interrupted by Nigel’s pained question.

"Then why?"

The question was heavy, vibrating with generations of suppressed frustration. He was asking why the royal family would intentionally subject the Acards to centuries of humiliation, knowing the truth the entire time.

"To get the answer to that question, I would have to finish the story," I said, leaning back in my chair and lacing my fingers together. "But it boils down to one simple fact. King Aurelius was a man who hailed from a minor noble family. He managed to crawl up to the top, hailed as a king, wielding the power of a Tier 9. He was fiercely protective of that power."

Nigel settled back down. The defensive posture was gone. He looked at me, the fight drained out of him, replaced by a desperate need to know the truth about his own blood.

"The fact that the young boy couldn't bond with a phoenix was kept strictly under wraps," I began, translating the cramped, furious handwriting of Maghri Vex into a cohesive narrative. "Only King Aurelius and Maghri Vex were privy to the knowledge. In fact, King Aurelius had conveyed to Maghri that he still wished for the child to continue his lineage and be the next king."

I paused, letting the bitter irony of that statement hang in the air.

"The King claimed the people might oppose a flightless ruler. He asked Vex to teach the child magic in secret. The child was kept away from everyone, hidden even from the Vex family and the public. The official reason given was the King's desire to keep his heir safe from assassins. Perhaps there was some truth to that fear. Only Queen Hellena, Maghri Vex, and the most trusted attendants were allowed near the boy."

Nigel frowned, his brow furrowing as he tried to reconcile this hidden prince with his own, very public, very humiliating childhood.

"Meanwhile, in Dragon Valley, something miraculous had occurred," I continued, tracing the edge of the glass table. "The then Dragon King of the Blue Clan had offered his blood to a young boy, allowing him to bond with the next Dragon Prince. The news spread through the lands like wildfire. Another dragon-blood family was born, joining the ranks of the Gold Dragon blood family—the monarchs of Lumina—and the first dragon-blood family, the Green Dragons, the stewards of Dragon Valley. This boy was the young scion of House Wight."

I tapped the ancient journal.

"Dragon contracts must be made before the dragon has hatched, while it is still an egg. This was unprecedented news. Rumors of a boy walking around with a floating egg were spreading everywhere. Everyone was trying to ingratiate themselves with House Wight at the time. When King Aurelius put down the rebellion in the northern territory with brute force, House Wight and House Black had already formed a pseudo-alliance."

I looked directly at Nigel. "King Aurelius knew it was just a matter of time until they came knocking on his door with a young Tier 9 Dragon Knight. Even more frustrating for him was the fundamental difference in our lifecycles."

Nigel looked confused. "Difference in lifecycles?"

"Yes," I explained. "The Dragon Kings produce an heir every five hundred years. The dragons, instead of clinging to power until death, choose to pass on their Tier 9 status to one of their offspring. In the process, they return to Tier 8 and retire, letting the next generation take over. This means every five hundred years, there is a guarantee of a new Tier 9 Dragon Knight contractor."

I leaned forward. "All magical creatures, apart from angels, can only have one contract in their whole lives. Since the Tier 9 Mother Phoenix doesn't die and doesn't pass on her authority as a Tier 9, Cinderfall cannot reliably keep producing a Tier 9 knight every few generations. The King only had his own lifespan."

"Things were looking dire for Aurelius. The current steward of Dragon Valley was training the boy in the ways of a Dragon Knight. The boy would be knocking at his doorstep in about three hundred years, right in his prime. The King, already three hundred years old, would be in his later years, well past his prime. Something needed to be done, and fast, or his kingdom would break apart in two."

Nigel swallowed hard, the political reality of the ancient world finally clicking into place.

"Meanwhile, news that his firstborn had no talent in magic also reached his ears, from his most trusted friend, Maghri Vex. The King put up a front. He told Vex it didn't matter, that the boy would still be his heir apparent."

I stopped, letting out a short, cynical laugh.

"But secretly, he had already taken a mistress. She had given birth to another male heir, entirely unbeknownst to Maghri Vex. King Aurelius might have allowed the firstborn to be king if he had possessed magical talent and built a Mage Tower to secure the capital. In the King’s eyes, without that power, it was just a matter of time before the boy would be dethroned with no power to back him. The King had settled on backing his second son, Titus Flavius. The young boy had already managed to bond with one of the phoenix eggs incubated by the First Flame, meaning he would become a Tier 8 knight."

"Titus Flavius," Nigel whispered, the name of the Second King of Cinderfall tasting like ash in his mouth. "The Usurper."

"Yes," I nodded. "But there was a problem with this plan. The King couldn't yet announce to the world that his second son would be crowned king. Doing so would polarize the Vex family—the absolute backbone of his political power. If he lost the support of the Vex family, the already shaky pillars on which the kingdom was built would crumble. So, he continued the facade of the Vex family heir being the next king, while he secretly built up Titus to be his true heir apparent."

I pointed to the worn leather book. "Even Maghri Vex didn't find out until much later. As you may guess, this is the core behind the Acards' fate."

Nigel’s hands clenched into fists, resting heavy on his thighs. He was staring at the journal, but I knew he was seeing the faces of his ancestors, men who had been lied to and discarded.

"King Aurelius planned out the downfall of the Vex family meticulously. He gave them leniency, allowing them unprecedented powers. Over the years, they became more and more corrupt, exploiting the people and misusing their powers. The discontent began building up. It was a masterclass in manipulation."

"While the King allowed and encouraged the Vex family's behavior, he appeared helpless and at their mercy to the common folk. More and more people began to hate the mages for their oppression. Rumors began to spread that the Crown Prince was also a Vex, and would be vile and oppressive, as he too possessed the Vex blood."

I took a breath. This next part was particularly brutal, even by the standards of a medieval warlord.

"Meanwhile, the dwarves had invited King Aurelius. The High King of the dwarves personally offered to forge him a magical blade, offering their greatest treasure to the People's King. The King saw an opportunity here. A way to seize the First Flame and produce an endless supply of Tier 8 knights, fighting the upcoming young Dragon Knight of House Wight with raw numbers."

"But the dwarves wouldn't give up their greatest treasure so easily," I explained, shifting the narrative to the fall of the mountain kingdom. "The First Flame was one of the two keys behind the dwarves' strength. The first is their impenetrable defense. The second is their magical weapons. More importantly, their living weapons, capable of growing alongside the wielder. The stronger the wielder, the stronger the weapon."

Nigel looked up, confusion cutting through his anger. "Living weapons? I've never heard of such a thing."

"Because they don't exist anymore," I said flatly. "The magical living weapons would also die alongside their wielder. These weapons achieved their miraculous powers upon being tempered by the heat of the First Flame. It gave the dwarves the strength to stand up as one of the great kingdoms."

"The King went to the dwarven nation, taking with him his elite Tier 8 knights. Now, here is where history differs wildly from reality. The legends say the First King besieged the dwarven mountains and broke through their impenetrable defenses. In reality, he was invited in. The High King of the time forged him a living weapon."

I leaned forward, making sure Nigel heard every word clearly.

"The first thing King Aurelius did upon receiving it was drive it through the dwarven monarch's heart. While the Tier 9 Mother Phoenix initiated an attack from the outside, King Aurelius and his Tier 8 knights wreaked havoc from the inside. Without a monarch to lead them, the dwarves fell fast from the attack on two fronts. Their prized First Flame was stolen."

Nigel stared at me, his mouth slightly open. The glorious foundation myth of Cinderfall was nothing but a bloody, treacherous mugging.

"King Aurelius had spat on their hospitality, biting the hand that fed him," I continued. "The dwarves couldn't last for long, as their magical weapon supply was gone. When that generation died alongside their weapons, the knowledge and the power would be lost. So they adapted. They began supplying weapons to all sides, making it so no other great nation would want them to be conquered."

"With a constant supply of Tier 8 combatants secured, King Aurelius could finally do something about the Vex family. The discontent kept growing until an assassination attempt was made on the lives of the young prince and the queen. Maghri Vex was near the area. He couldn't save his beloved sister, barely able to save his nephew."

"The man lost his mind, falling into a deep depression upon not being able to save his sister. He began secluding himself. Aurelius took that opportunity to finally crack down on the Vex family's misgivings, appearing to the people as a just, decisive ruler."

I paused, letting the true horror of the act sink in.

"But the fact was, the assassination was orchestrated by his mistress. And King Aurelius allowed it to happen intentionally, making the security so lax that peasants could storm the castle of the Crown Prince and Queen."

Nigel flinched as if I had struck him.

"With Maghri Vex gone, the Vex family was systematically destroyed. The Crown Prince was stripped of his title on the grounds of not being able to bond with a phoenix. He was given the Acard name. Titus was named Crown Prince. The mages were oppressed, treated as lesser beings compared to the Phoenix Knights."

"Eventually, the day came when the young contractor of the Blue Dragon King came knocking on the doors of King Aurelius. Dragon Valley stepped in to prevent the fight between two Tier 9 combatants from scarring the continent. Maghri Vex was called from his self-exile in the Obsidian Dominion to help broker peace by Dragon Valley."

"When Vex returned, what he saw broke him. He came to understand his sister's death was not his fault, but an orchestrated plan. His nephew, a pet and plaything for the new royal line. His family erased. All of this done by the one he called friend. The man whose life he saved and gave shelter to."

"It is said during the negotiation table, King Aurelius intentionally provoked Vex, whispering the truth of his betrayals just out of earshot of the other mediators. The King wanted to rile the mage up, to make him look unstable and secure more favorable terms for Cinderfall. It worked perfectly. Vex lost control and attempted to strangle Aurelius right there in the summit hall. Dragon Valley was forced to intervene. They physically removed Maghri Vex, officially stripping him of his prestigious Professor status at the Academy, and exiled him."

"Eventually, a treaty was reached, and the great nation was broken down into two nations: the western nation of Cinderfall and the northern nation of Aerthos, comprising the lands of the two major houses of the north, House Wight and House Black."

I gestured toward the window.

"But King Aurelius was not happy with this situation. His World Tree was changing his lands, causing them to become a volcanic oasis. There was no way to grow food. So another treaty was signed. Cinderfall would provide raw ore, and Aerthos would provide them with food. That was the start of the makeshift agreement."

I looked back at Nigel. He was trembling.

"Maghri Vex, on the other hand, had lost it. There was no way he could beat a Tier 9 in combat. So he shed his humanity and became a Lich. The man had lost all hope in humanity, setting his sights on becoming the first Tier 9 mage and eliminating humanity. Done with the greed and selfishness he viewed as inherent to our race, he wanted to end all living things to rid the world of the endless greed of man."

I looked at Nigel, who was gripping the armrests of his chair so tight the metal was bending under his white-knuckled grip. The man might just pull them off in disbelief and rage.

"Well, that is the story portrayed in these pages," I said, my voice quiet in the sterile room. "As I said, I can vouch for Aerthos's side of the story being quite factual. It is fascinating how the truth differs so much from the history taught in Cinderfall. As our history books match up, it is highly probable that is the truth behind the history of the families, the reason behind the Acards' suffering, and the truth behind Cinderfall, Maghri Vex, and the founding of Aerthos and Cinderfall."

I pushed the journal across the glass table toward him.

"You can borrow this if you want to verify the story yourself."
