Chronomancy, in my most humble and unbiased opinion, is the single greatest magical lore one can master. I would dare say it is an essential magical lore. As you pass through the pages of this book, I implore you to wield your Chronomancy and use the spells you learn upon these pages. This is more than just a tome. The fascinating thing about Chronomancy was that it was built on a scant few spells, while other lores had so very many. Yet, even though Transpositioning was one of these few spells, it was, in effect, an omnipotent spell in many respects. Being able to move yourself in relation to time allowed you to move things you were attached to, or interfaced with, across time as well. This act was called Rooting. At a certain point, Kelhaus wanted to consider Rooting a separate spell all of its own, but then she realized it was simply the culmination of the Transpositioning spell itself—after all, one had to breach and shed a portion of their mana to achieve an act of rooting.

And these acts of rooting were essential if one wanted to interact with the world itself, for any contact a Chronomancer made with something moving at a different pace and axis of time demanded a stabilization and a breach in balance of mana derived from their field.

Normally, the text would end here and continue on into the other sub-branch of chronomancy titled Presentism. Much akin to Transpositioning, Presentism was both theory and spell, focused on the lore that the future was not yet manifest and the past was too solidified to be changed. Meaning that the eternal and ever-passing present is the only actual thing the mage can manipulate.

Though that seemed like a useless thing to manipulate at face value, it was actually a grand power unto itself. The ability to alter how one experiences the present causes fluctuations in the pace of time. Thus, this allows a mage to create conflicting flows of time and disruptive currents, to wield time against itself.

This was where most of the offensive Chronomancy spells follow, in which portions of a being's body were rent apart not by physical forces but by temporal displacement. As a limb was trapped in a chronomantic bubble, in one place, the body had to go another. Even should one be tremendous in physicality or force, time abides by its own laws, and once rendered null of magical resistance, dismemberment was less a possibility and more a predetermined outcome.

As he tried to cast the book into the future, it resonated like a bell as it briefly blinked out of his hands with a flash of gold, then rebounded and returned to him, this time with its contents expanded. “Hold,” the woman spoke to him for the first time, her voice mature but light. “I wish to see you. I wish to observe you. Hold, please, and pardon me for this conversation.”
