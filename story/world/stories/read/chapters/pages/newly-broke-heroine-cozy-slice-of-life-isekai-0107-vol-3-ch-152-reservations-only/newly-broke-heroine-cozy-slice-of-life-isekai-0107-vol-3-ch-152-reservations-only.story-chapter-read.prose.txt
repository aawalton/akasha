After they finished with the pop-up cafe and grouped up at the inn, Fiona felt like something was still off. Bianca did, too.

“Is this too easy?” Bianca asked. “From the few interactions I had with him, something felt off. Way off. I think this is a trap, and he knows it’s us. And possibly me, too.” She rubbed her feet. “Oh, gods, I’ll never wait at another table again. The things I do for therapy,” she groaned.

“He said he knows the true worth of things he touches. That sounds like a merchant kind of thing,” Fiona chimed in. “Maybe it was an off-the-cuff comment, but I think there’s more to it. Crap, did we touch hands?”

She remembered they had not, in fact, done so. “Remember, whatever happens tonight, while we’re incognito? I can’t hold his hands. In case his power is some magical bullcrap where he can sense I’m worth a fortune of hearts.”

“Like that is going to happen. Fi, you’re our door kicker, not our stealth expert,” Jake complained, while Nick drew up plans. “So how do we neutralize the pyrogel? Once that’s squared away, we can go to town on his scaly backside.”

“Assuming we can find it, and assuming it’s not too far gone? Bonnie and I can neutralize it so it breaks down into its disparate components. Most of it is slime gel, but the potency comes from the other alchemical compounds in it.” He showed several alchemical grenades. “The fumes in these gas grenades will break down the volatiles safely. It’s the fastest way to get rid of it, without a proper alchemist lab going full tilt.”

“No magic spell to fix this one easily, huh?” Fiona asked. Nick shook his head, feathers fluttering from the motion.

“No. Sometimes there’s no easy way. All we need to do is keep him busy. That’s it.” He frowned when he pointed at Fiona. “You were supposed to lie low, not get yourself invited to this dragon’s lair! That disguise of yours isn’t going to hold!”

“Hey, I got us in the front door,” she fumed. “And I did it without waving around blunt force trauma threats. Look, I can refrain from smashing his face in for a while. Plus there’s…”

“There’s what? What is there to discuss?” Nick asked with a snap of his beak. “This scaly pain in my ass forced the guild to run an internal defense of the kingdom! People got hurt, Fi. Some of them badly. That includes many of the kobolds in Doug’s employ who took damage. Regardless of Glados and her involvement, he still has to answer for that.”

She hadn’t forgotten it. But it wasn’t as easy to hate Karlin since he was trying to do something admittedly altruistic, at a hideous cost.

She shook away the nagging thoughts. “You know what, you’re right. Let’s keep it simple. You and Bonnie dig around, render his explosive stash into nothing more than a kid’s chemistry set, we arrest him, and bring him topside. Simple enough.”

Nick raised a brow at that. “There’s nothing simple about this. Karlin had a small entourage of three, plus his assistant. They weren’t top-tier, but we don’t know the layout of this place, security, or numbers, until we’re inside. That teleport pad under the tavern could go anywhere. We are also assuming he doesn’t have gate access codes.”

That teleport pad likely cost a pretty copper to rig up for private access, in hindsight. Nick continued with his concerns. “What I’m saying is, this could be risky. I want a backup plan, in case the pyrogel isn’t kept where he’s located. You need to find out if he has any other safe houses,” Nick insisted. “He likes you. Gods, if that disguise ever slips, this is going to go tail up, fast.”

Bonnie stepped in. “It should hold up as long as Fiona is using her internal mana draw. Which, for her, will last a day without stopping.”

“What about me? Am I just going to be a wallflower?” Bianca asked.

Fiona thought the answer should be obvious. “Yes, precisely. Bianca, you should head home since you’re back to…”

“Normal?” she demanded, arms folded, shooting down Fiona’s words before she even finished. “Don’t you get it? Varith and I are still one person. I will never be back to ‘normal’ ever again, Fiona. And the best place to anchor me to this weird new reality is with you. And your friends.”

Bianca bit her lip gently. “The best I can hope for is for small moments like this when I’m not torn down the middle. You get to go home to an apartment, to a cat who adores you, to friends who literally jump into a burning building for you, and the other way around. There’s no ‘home’ when your own mind feels like a prison, Fi. Or maybe you’ve forgotten that.”

“I haven’t.” Fiona rubbed her temples gently. “Bee, I’ve never forgotten. I’d never let that happen. I can understand that you want to be a part of this. I do. But, as you are…you don’t have any mark powers. Especially if we get in a pinch, which, given my track record? It’s likely to happen. But probably not for the reason I plan for.”

Bianca raised a brow. “You know I still can cook that dragon with dark energy powers, right? I can still willingly take on my bruiser ego.” Then, she shrugged. “Just saying, it’s an option. I’m also willing to exercise…restraint.” The last word was spoken through gritted teeth, as if she hated saying that.

“Tactically speaking, Bianca?” Jake proposed. “If you do go as Varith, and you’re recognized? It might get really awkward for you. It was a risk even having you down here. But…if you agree to follow my lead on this, you can serve on the backup team. In case blunt force Fionas are insufficient to persuade the dragon.”

“I think you meant my hammer,” Fiona countered. “Alright, it's expected that Bonnie, Nick, I, and Darla are there. We have a lot of unknowns. Whatever happens, be cool.”

Wearing her old face again felt strange. And getting dressed up in the more formal attire that Darla had stashed away in her dimensional kitchen felt stiff. Her armor had more maneuverability than this.

Bonnie expressed her discomfort audibly. “Why do they make it so difficult for tailed Folk to find clothes that are comfortable?” she sighed, rubbing at her lower back. She pulled out her enchantment wand to try to fix it so that the opening for her tail was a little higher. “The next job I take is magical seamstressing. I already do most of the work as it is with runesmithing.”

Karlin’s instructions were to meet with Missy by the tavern. It was a less rowdy crowd tonight, and Missy was dressed all prim and proper like before. Though she wore a feigned smile. Darla led the way, looking proud. “Now, just a fair reminder, no. I can’t willingly give him food poisoning. That would be a sin. And me cooking anything less than my best isn’t going to happen.”

“You have the important job, Darla. Cooking for a prickly dragon.” Doug had volunteered to stay behind on this one, and Nick looked so strange in a chef jacket. “Nick, we might be searched when we go in. Darla’s mark power, too.”

“Planned for it. I have dimensional pockets on my jacket. I need my tools and alchemical neutralizer. I just hope Karlin hasn’t put the stuff into…you know, bombs or something.”

They met Missy outside, and she wordlessly gestured for her to follow them. They didn’t go in the front door, however; they instead came in around the side, with Darla raising a questioning gaze.

“I hope he’s not expecting me to cook…here,” she said uneasily.

The woman wore a faint smile. “My associate has a private venue. Please follow me. You will be searched upon arrival, fair warning.”

Darla tapped at her knife kit slung over her shoulder. “He isn’t going to have to look very hard,” she replied with a coy smile. “Mage steel with a touch of alarite blend. Keeps them sharp practically forever, with minimal maintenance. I could split a feather in half by just dropping it on the cutting edge.”

Missy laughed heartily at that. “Is that so?”

“Hey, you cook for the best, you need the best tools available.” They wasted no more time and approached a side door. Missy pressed a ward stone to a small sensor on the doorway, and Fiona heard several locking mechanisms unwinding. “There is also a magical nullification ward on entry.”

Fiona nearly froze. Don’t panic. Bonnie already anticipated this. Karlin might be arrogant at times, but he’s not so naive as to have gotten away with this much theft and bullying without at least some modicum of preparation.

The heavy door opened to a teleport pad that barely fit in the room, and they all stepped inside onto the platform. Missy inserted a small key into the panel, and the translucent stone material lit up with an intense white light.

Fiona hated that bright flash that always left dazzles in her eyes. She blinked repeatedly, and her surroundings came into focus. Then she gazed at the room they were in.

This put the Lockheed Manor to shame. Doug hadn’t been embellishing when he used the phrase ‘this place is fit for a dragon.’

The room was constructed of smoothly chiseled walls, vaulting up to at least twenty-five feet high. They were in a large foyer, with a coat rack, both humanoid and dragon-sized, on display. Karlin’s security approached; two men wore dark jackets, and wands were holstered on their hips. “Any magical effects?” they asked.

“Uh…true story, I had a little accident when I was younger,” Fiona said bashfully. The guard raised a brow. “It’s a small illusion spell, you’d barely even know it was there.”

“Please disable it,” he asked politely.

Just then, Bonnie waved her tail subtly–and the wand hidden in her fur activated, triggering the deception effect of the spell running over them now like a dazzling grid of lights.

The guard sucked in his breath sharply as Fiona let the first illusion layer fall, to reveal the second one, showing a few burns. “I apologise, Miss. I understand. How come you didn’t get it healed?”

“Injury set in, and my parents were too poor,” she replied casually. After fiddling with the datapad in his hand, the second guard nudged the other and gave him a gentle thumbs-up. “So, are we good?”

“Yep, we’re good. Thank you.” He pointed to Darla’s knife set. “Anything I should worry about here?”

“Nope. These are my tools.” She unfurled the kit to show them off, and he ran his fingers over the handles, letting out a sound of contentment. “Like what you see?”

“Magical materials, but no enchantment. Why’s that?”

“Any weapon ‘enhancement’ spell makes it so my cleaver cuts the table in half,” Darla added with a roll of her eyes. “Never found a mage who could get it just right to balance a cutting edge and still give the right feel for preparing the meals.”

“Please follow me,” Missy instructed. Fiona saw that Karlin’s retreat was…strangely sparse. There were a few decorative busts of famous dragons. She continued to map the interior mentally.

Two floors or more. Five guards total, none of whom looked particularly dangerous. That included the kitsune and the two others she’d seen earlier in the day. Karlin must be working with a small crew. She noted there was a heavy metal door off to one side, and a rather modest living space filled with…

Fiona frowned. Karlin had a library? Doug had mentioned he couldn’t keep a collection that interested him for more than five minutes, only that he read when necessary. “Excuse me, Missy…how many books are in there?”

“About a collection of…two thousand?” she proposed, waving one hand through the air. “Do you read?”

“More than I used to.” She also caught the gaze of a small painting of what she suspected was one of the gods: a draconic woman with feathery hair, wielding a broadsword with a single green gemstone radiating power.

Gaia. No surprise there, I guess, looking like a humanoid dragon. But there was also another image of the Gilded Empress that Doug had mentioned. Tucked away in a deeper recess of the room.

There was a gnawing feeling in the pit of her stomach. She was missing something. Did Karlin know this was a ruse? Did he research Darla and the fact that she was a business partner in the emporium? He seemed to know her father. But not so much about her.

Missy brought them to a set of double doors leading to a large living area, filled with a study desk and various magical devices scattered on the surface. In the room stood Karlin, holding an oversized wine glass. His wings fluttered out broadly, and he smiled at them. “Greetings, and welcome to my…well, they don’t call them lairs anymore. Lair syndrome is a real thing. Dragons get too possessive of stuff sometimes.”

Check that off my list. I am at least half dragon. Except my possessions are adorkable friends. Fiona suppressed the urge to laugh, and Darla bowed politely beside her. She followed suit, as did Nick and Bonnie.

“Greetings, Karlin. I do have a prior event coming up in a few hours, so, shall I get started?” Darla asked. He shook his head.

“Actually, I was wondering if I could get to know you a little better. I know of your father, and I knew he had a daughter who had taken after him. But then you disappeared from Underlune.”

“Impromptu summoning,” Darla said, flicking her favorite cooking pan into existence with a twirl of her fingers and spinning it on that lecherous mage-sized dent. “By the time my dad got topside, I was self-starting myself and doing a little catering with a bit of help from others. I let it be known that I was not cool with being whisked away.”

Karlin raised a brow. “So you’ve spent time topside and bottom side.”

“Hey, I don’t mind being a top,” she said deadpan. Bonnie tried to stifle a laugh, but failed. Karlin leaned into it with a soft chuckle. “You’ve been to the city of Fiefdala?”

“From time to time,” he answered. “Alas, there are some disagreements I had with the current regency. I packed up my business and left. I keep other businesses going in no less than five kingdoms. Small loss for me, big loss for them.”

“That so?” Darla snapped her fingers, pointing at him and peering at him intensely. Then she tapped her chin rhythmically. “I think I can get a feel for your favorite dish. No magic tricks either.”

“Oh? What do you think I fancy?”

“A reminder of home. Nothing fancy. Just something wholesome. I’d say it was the hash tubers and steak from this morning, but no. You were eying the fresh-pressed griddles I had. With the fluffed cream, fresh fruit, and those Zembari berries that grow in Underlune.”

Karlin clapped proudly. “Oh, my. I’m always suppressing my sweet tooth. Plus, it reminds me of my brother.”

Darla glanced around, feigning ignorance. “Family trouble?”

“You could say that,” he said, swirling the wine in his glass. “Can I get you anything? Wine?”

Darla folded her arms, head canted. “As a policy? Never when I’m on the clock or with a client. I took a stab at your preferences and loaded up my dimensional kitchen. Do you mind if I–”

“Right this way,” he said, showing them to a short hallway with an oversized banquet table. Which only had chairs for half the length.

Fiona found that odd. She heard a soft cough from Missy and turned. “Uh…can I help you?”

“Can I meet you in the library? This will be but a moment. I recall you had some interest. Karlin, I’d like to borrow her.”

“Huh? Oh, right. I must show our esteemed arrival that I put stock in a good setup for culinary efforts.”

Fiona peered at Missy as she led her back down the hall. The guards were milling about, relaxing in a lounge chair. One was attempting to play at what Fiona swore was a pool table. She focused on Missy and tried to glean something from her using her weight of hearts ability.

She figured that Missy must be a hardened criminal to associate with the likes of Karlin and his men. She would have thought that after escaping the forge, she'd have rethought her life choices. This clearly indicated she had not.

Alright, Missy. What is the weight of your soul? Let me just do a harmless little check--

The second she started getting a read of something light and airy in Missy, she felt an electric jolt down her spine. A jolt that caused Wingding to recoil on her wrist, and Fiona rubbed at her hand gently.

What the hell was that? Some kind of anti-scrying? She’d never had that happen before. She hadn’t actually used it very often because she didn’t know what, exactly, it was telling her. Only that Barry had a half-filled heart, and Glados soul was as heavy as a lead brick.

Missy waved to the library, smiling proudly. “You like it?”

“I don’t suppose it has anything in the flavor of Walt Whitman, does it?” she posed casually. Whatever had happened just now had never happened before, and she focused on the shelving.

The books were surprisingly well preserved, the room was lit by arcane torches, and there was a small sliding shelf leading to the second level of books. At least Karlin had been courteous to people shorter than him. “Anything by Lewis Carroll?”

Missy gazed at her, looking puzzled. Then, she snapped her fingers, looking enthused. “I think we do have something for Carroll, actually. One second.” She hopped up onto the mobile ladder and, after a minute, pulled down a copy of a book.

Fiona didn’t recognize the cover, but she recognized the title. Alice in Wonderland. “Hey, thanks! I’ve been looking for this one for a while.” The pages felt crisp, and the surface was yellowed with age, but still legible and intact. “Uh, this isn’t a library, though. I’m not sure Karlin would just–”

She pressed the book into her hands. “No, you should keep it. I’ve spent so much time watching Karlin debate burning the whole collection. I’ve spent a lot of time talking him out of it. But at the end of the day, he can’t. Even as much as he wants to, he can’t go through with it.”

Fiona squinted. “Huh? Why? Why would anyone burn a collection of literature?”

“I–”

“Missy? The kitchen awaits,” Karlin called out from down the hall. “C’mon, I think you’ll enjoy this. I’ve never seen anyone juggle knives and make a meal before!”

She thrust her hands up in the air in silent frustration before smoothing her blouse. “Be right there!”

Then, Missy drew close. “Sorry, it's been a rough patch. Pretend you didn’t see anything. This is one rabbit hole I wish I hadn’t fallen into.”

She kept rubbing at that choker, even as she turned and they headed to the kitchen and adjacent dining room, where Darla was setting up.

Wingding? We should be fearful of this one. I’m getting a funky vibe from her. Funkier still was Fiona’s notion that Missy was wearing that collar for a reason.

Funkiest of all was that this mystery woman was keenly aware of a book written half a cosmos away.
