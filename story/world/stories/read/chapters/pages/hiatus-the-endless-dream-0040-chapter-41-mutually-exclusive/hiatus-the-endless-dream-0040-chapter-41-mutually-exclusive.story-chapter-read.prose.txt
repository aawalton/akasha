I could feel myself getting pulled into the situation against my will. Eleven of us stood in the overturned ballroom with varying ranges of fear and discontent. The Survivors flinched away at my gaze, even Alder, who still held his gun, tensed as my eyes passed over him.

One person was missing, likely dead, but all I felt was that it wasn't my problem. The only requirement was that four lived. How or in what state didn't matter.

My eyes landed on Maerin, Krevith, and Paltra, who stood in their own little group towards the edge of the room. They were nearby, but located close to the exit. I felt a pang of guilt at the thought that had passed over me. I liked them. Well, two of them. I knew nothing about Paltra, but she seemed fine, and Maerin trusted the Huntress. I had already decided they would live and not as some sort of parasitic abomination. They deserved that much. Turning over to the Fiendish parasite wasn't on the table for them. For everyone else, though…

The Fiendish statue solved all of my problems here.

I looked at the remaining Survivors, one of which was undoubtedly the third Visitor.

I didn't know who the killer was. That was fine if I just gave the command to keep everyone alive in some state. Unless they could kill the parasite themselves without Jeren or me noticing it would be a win. The third Visitor would have to reveal themselves, and then we could kill them.

I let static take my vision and re-checked the item's description just to make sure.

[Oathbound Parasite**: In an effort to find out what something is, it is—on occasion—better to find out what it isn't. A so-called negative definition. A creature can ingest or otherwise implant the Oathbound Parasite into themselves or another creature and give it a single directive. The parasite will grow and adapt on its own to follow that directive to the letter, regardless of how it has to get there. Once the directive is completed, the parasite will act on its own accord.]

[*: This item is considered Wandering, and is not bound to a specific Visitor. It must be recovered after every use to keep it.]

[*: This parasite is a living thing. Its changes to a host are permanent until it is excised from its host.]

Yeah. It would work. It would have to be Alder. He had the gun.

I was under no illusion about fighting someone when they had a gun, especially with my [Fragile] Restriction. If I had Krevith with me, though, it would be trivial. I just needed to get him alone. I turned my attention to Jeren, who seemed deep in thought.

"Do you have any charge on [Coin Flip]?" I asked.

He shook his head, "I used it before you left to get the other Survivors. I asked if you going would help them."

Huh. "What did it say?"

Jeren nodded, more to himself than me, "Heads, so yes. I'm guessing you wanted me to use it to see if Biancca is still alive?"

"Kind of, more if looking for her will increase our chances of winning." I saw his brow furrow, although he otherwise kept a neutral expression. There was something in the way I said that he didn't like, but I couldn't figure out what.

The image of him standing over me had burned itself into my thoughts. It reminded me that I had also been shunted out of [Wayfarer]. Sure, he had thrown the rope down, but even I would help an enemy if it meant I'd win. From now on, I made a note to be more careful in my wording.

The portly blond man sighed, "It's a good question. I have enough Presence to increase my Lucidity to ten. Then I can use another charge." He grimaced, seeming to roll the thought around before nodding once more. I watched as static took Jeren's vision, and a moment later, he had produced a coin. He whispered a question and sent the coin spiraling.

The coin spun high into the air, the meager light from the lanterns reflecting off its silver surface. I held my breath, everyone did, as we watched its descent. It bounced twice off the ground before tracing a circle on the ground. It grew smaller and smaller until it finally came to rest.

Heads.

Relief shot through my system. The intricacies of [Coin Flip] were lost on me, just that it would answer a vague question with yes, no, or land on its side for everything else. In short, it was divination. It meant that the Boon had a way of estimating the future based on the actions everyone would take. Heads meant the path I'd chosen—isolating Alder and feeding the parasite to him—would increase our chances at winning. It's why I didn't want him to ask if this Biancca girl was alive or not. If the question just had to do with the act of searching itself, then it didn't matter. Maybe we'd find her, maybe not, but whatever the outcome, it would be beneficial.

More than anything, I was glad it didn't land on tails. I was out of plans.

Jeren took a deep breath, "Alright. We'll look for Biancca. The Spirits say it is safe."

"The Spirits? The Spirits, Prophet? One of those attacked us! You prance around with a Fiend as an equal!" It was the spectacled man, Nico, who yelled the questions. His eyes were wide, and the outrage in his tone told me exactly what he thought about Jeren in this moment. More worrying, though, was the subtle agreement from the other Survivors.

They were wary now, and it was no longer just directed at me.

The woman in the three-piece suit looked like she was going to say something, but the heavyset wrinkles of conflicted thoughts forming on her face stalled her long enough that Jeren got the word in instead.

"The wolf?" Jeren asked, deepening his voice to make it echo around the room. "I killed the stone construct. You think that was a true spirit. Something that could be beaten so easily? You saw Sheppard, sitting in the river, doing nothing to harm us, yet you think a random wolf is equal to it?"

"It looked exactly how Rend is described!" Nico cried out.

"A stolen visage is a pale thing. If it were the true spirit trying to shake me from this path, it would have done so by the grace of God, but I— we are still here, and Biancca is alive too. We argue for little reason when another Survivor could need help. I will hear no more of this."

He punctuated his last sentence, meeting all the Survivors' eyes until they looked away. Nico held Jeren's gaze the longest, but gave way eventually. I breathed a small sigh of relief, glad that he had managed to get them under control at least for the time being. Obviously, it wouldn't last, but I didn't need it to.

My smile didn't last long. "The two of us," Jeren gestured at me, "Orlan and Theon will go check things out. Everyone else should stay here." That plan was fucking stupid, and there was no way I was going to leave Maerin in the same room with Alder and Nico when the man had a gun.

"No." I spoke, and everyone froze.

"No?" Jeren seemed genuinely shocked, but I just shook my head.

"It's better if we all look. We don't have much time. The moon is getting brighter." I got mostly confused looks from the Survivors at that, but Jeren paled, understanding my meaning. "We need to get underground and soon. So, the sooner we find … the Survivor the better."

He looked to the others, bit his inner cheek, and then spoke, "He's right. Orlan, Theon. Still come with us, but everyone else look around, stay close to here, we may need to leave soon."

Jeren began walking towards the room's exit before I could argue for more. The older man and the young boy began approaching, a little uncertain. The young boy, who I assumed was Theon—unless Maerin had an issue with a child for some reason—tugged on Orlan's sleeve. "Can Alder come with us too?"

Orlan looked to me with a questioning gaze, but before I could respond, Jeren cut in, "No. It will be just us four."

Something felt off here. Maybe it was the way the boys' question sounded rehearsed, that I was being separated from my group, that I had no way of getting the Fiend into Alder, or maybe it was Jeren bulldozing through this situation, but there was something that I didn't like. Whatever it was sounded every alarm bell I had. I hadn't yet dismissed the Shroud, so I covered my vitals with it as I followed Jeren out of the room.

It was completely silent as the three of us followed Jeren. No one said anything, and the atmosphere was thick with tension. It felt like being near a compressed spring tied together with zip ties. I only noticed that after a minute, none of us were looking for the missing Survivor, not even Jeren, who was walking ahead at a brisk pace. He didn't call out or look in any rooms; he strode with purpose. Then he opened a room at seemingly random and stepped in.

It was one of the lavish sleeping quarters, a full bed with decorations and everything. I stepped in after Jeren, followed by Orlan and lastly Theon.

Then Jeren shut the door. The latch seemed to echo in the small room. My heart thrummed in my chest, completely uncertain about what was happening.

"You know if you kill the Survivors you'll be killed too right?" Jeren said, and all thoughts left me.

I didn't know who he was talking to, and both Survivors looked just as shocked as I did. The words hit me again as I finally processed what he was saying. Was it really that simple? Was it that easy? That the killer would now be in front of me? My eyes narrowed at the two Survivors.

Orlan froze. Theon's eyes were only white. His lips quivered, but no sounds came. It wasn't confusion I saw on their faces, it was fear. The kind that came with being outed.

No one spoke. No one moved.

The silence stretched on as I waited for some sort of confirmation or denial from either party. I leaned slightly, hand inching towards the [Oathbound Parasite**].

Then, before I could move, Orlan grabbed the young Theon. There was a knife to the young boy's throat all of a sudden. He yanked the boy closer and spoke, his voice shaking, "How did you know?" he asked.

Jeren rolled his eyes and pulsed [Wayfarer]. The world blurred into motion as I was launched across the room and into a nearby wall. My Shroud took all of the impact of the now broken wall I had been sent sprawling through. I barely managed to catch Theon getting pushed back and pinned into the corner, scrambling at the golden barrier like he could climb it and escape.

I looked for Orlan, trying to see where he had been launched. Except he hadn't. He had been thrown to the floor but was still inside the barrier. I scrambled to my feet, ready to run or fight, but Jeren's attention was solely on the young Theon.

"I saw the wolf come from your cell," Jeren said, his voice soft and even. "I knew it couldn't be Orlan."

"How?" Orlan was on the floor, his hands shaking as he looked between Jeren and his ward. "How do you know that. You are no Prophet!" Despite the man's age, his voice cracked.

Jeren released the golden film, but none of us made any large moves. The traitor just pressed himself further into the corner.

"Because when I took you two with me, to get Maerin, after she and Paltra said no. You ended up killing yourself. That entire time, that entire run, Theon was aimless, confused, and didn't even react when you died. I thought it was just a kid who was traumatized. It was the same at the start of this one, too, but when he saw Dorian, that changed. He seemed to come alive." Jeren shot me an apologetic look.

"Talin remembering you is when I realized it. It was your presence, just you being there, that changed things. This Instance was designed for you and you alone. So, of course, the extra Visitor, who would no doubt be expecting to see you, would be confused when you were nowhere to be found."

"You know the lose condition, right? You'll be consumed if you actually kill everyone." Jeren reiterated.

The kid said nothing at first, just pressed himself further away from Jeren. Then he began to pray as he clasped his hands together.

Orlan's eyes were wide, and I could see his irises flick back and forth, thinking of something he could do. "Don't hurt him. He doesn't understand what our world is. He doesn't know what he's doing! I only agreed to help him so I could convince him!"

I stared in disbelief. It was the kid? The kid was the traitor? I reached into my pocket, palming the statue of the Fiend.

"I'm not going to hurt him." Jeren's voice was calming, like he was talking to an animal. He shifted his attention back to the boy. "You've been tricked. There is no going back. The only way left is through. No one else needs to die. We can make this right still."

I thought about the wording I could offer the [Oathbound Parasite**].

"You— you wear the mask of a Prophet, but God will use me as a tool to strip away the skin falsely placed there!" The young boy shrank back further. "I will be cast back into water and reshaped by the hand of God! I will not hail him!"

He was a zealot. He had killed Talin. There was no helping him. He was also too risky to keep alive, at least with free will.

I stepped beside Jeren, but before I moved past him, the man side-eyed me and shook his head. I didn't listen.

"Stay away from me, you Dreamspawn! I will not be consumed by you!" the traitor spoke.

Jeren pulsed the golden film, sending me flying through a different wall.

I stared up at the opposing hallway of the ship, stunned. Right. I was his enemy right now.

How long had Jeren known? He said he confirmed it since Rend left their cell, but when had he actually known? When had he suspected strongly enough that he should have acted? Could he have saved Talin?

I gritted my teeth. I forgot the type of person Jeren was. At the end of the day, he wanted to save everyone he could. I just hadn't realized that meant even at the cost of other people dying.

That had to be the reason he saw me as an enemy. Because he knew how I'd react once I knew. He knew I had made some deal with Maerin, too, and could likely easily glean that it was a violent one. It was why I was here, why he was so insistent on my being here instead of where I could get to Alder or Nico, and where he could directly stop me from getting to Orlan.

It was a bitter pill to swallow the first time—that Jeren wanted to help everyone just because he could—now that pill was rancid. He wanted to save everyone? Fine.

Well, I wanted to win, and those two things were looking to be mutually exclusive, because Jeren would let everyone die if it meant saving someone.

I stood up. As careful as the man was to protect everyone, he forgot one thing. When you put holes in a room, nothing stops someone from leaving, even if you locked the door.

"Well. Good luck convincing the kid," I said, stepping further through the hole my body had made and into the halls of the ship.

"Dorian," Jeren called out. "Where are you going?"

I turned and stared the blond man down. "You can't save everyone. I've only been in the Dream briefly, but I can tell it's trying to teach us something. At the end of the day, you can be two of three things: alive, principled, or good. Your plan will never work, but mine will. Good luck, Jeren."
