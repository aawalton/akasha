The small amount of relief that Ezra had experienced from seeing the apparently good relationship between Georg and Aaron was washed away as he was escorted into the sitting room. Georg had given a quick explanation of what Ezra had done, with less detail than Ezra would have liked, to Aaron as they made their way into the room. While they traveled, the Count’s face fell further and further, from amiable, to severe, and a minute or two later, Aaron had led them through a hall that was at least twenty meters long that then led to a staircase upward, and then an additional fifteen meters to the sitting room.

“Lawrence,” Aaron commanded, snapping at one of Brom’s guards as soon as he had gotten the general story, “kindly manacle the accused.”

Ezra opened his mouth to protest, but Georg clapped a hard hand on the Adept’s shoulder.

“Don’t argue with the Count.”

Ezra gritted his teeth, his patience and ability to stand for this injustice running thinner and thinner. He extended his hands in front of himself, and a pair of manacles were quickly locked onto his wrists, and he was commanded to stand in the center of the sitting room. He was away from any chairs, and standing on feet that, despite training and years of hard life, were tired from a day of walking and carrying equipment for the New Dragon’s Toenail.

“Count Villy,” Georg said, his previously familiar address to his brother gone. Once Aaron looked at him and gestured for him to continue, he said, “Another of my subordinates will be coming shortly, along with a witness. Would you please send a guard to allow her entrance?”

Different from before, there was no quick answer. Instead, the ruler of the city, a man that Ezra had always cursed and hated who now held very direct control over his fate, stared at Ezra with hard eyes. The dark irises had seemed so warm and loving when exchanging casual conversation with his brother were now flinty and accusatory.

“Lieutenant Georg,” he eventually answered slowly, “you’ve already brought yourself as a character witness for the accused. As far as I can tell, he doesn’t deny having assaulted the scion of a noble house. I’m not sure what kind of witness you’re going to bring in here that will be able to affect this trial in any way.”

“I do deny—” Ezra started to speak, but Georg stepped forward and put both of his hands heavily on the younger man’s shoulders. They slammed into him, making his twisting stomach lurch even more as Georg’s eyes met Ezra’s with an overwhelming force.

“This is the last time I’m saying it,” Georg said, his voice powerful. “Ezra, stay silent. Every word you say will not help you, it doesn’t matter what you say. If you are asked a question, answer it in single-word sentences, and allow me to say everything else. I’m here to help you, I’m here to protect you. I know what you have at stake, but you don’t know how to protect yourself in this place. Please, stay silent, and allow me to do what I’m doing. Even if you’re not sure what I’m getting at, do not open your mouth unless you are asked specifically to do so by me. Do you understand, soldier?”

“Yes, sir,” Ezra replied, his fear and anger still roiling in his stomach, but his trust for the man whose life he’d saved was still strong enough to fight for him for now.

“Good.”

As soon as Georg had said it, he turned to his brother and said, “You know that this is a case of directly conflicting witness on both sides, and Ezra’s words will mean very little in comparison to those of Rebecca. This additional witness is going to come here to further strengthen our case. If you were to deny our ability to bring an additional witness in, I would go and use every recourse possible to find and acquire justice for this soldier from a greater authority.”

At that, the Count narrowed his eyes and almost seemed like he was going to growl in frustration.

“You would prostrate yourself like that for him?”

“I already said,” Georg stated, holding strong eye contact with Aaron, “he saved my life. I’m not going to let that go unanswered.”

There was no time for any further conversation, as a woman whose voice Ezra didn’t recognize knocked on the door and called out, “Lord Rufus Wainwright and Lady Rebecca Wainwright have arrived.”

“Thank you, Winifred,” Aaron said, his eyes slipping off of Georg and towards the door.

No sooner than he finished speaking did the door fly open, and a man in a finely tailored suit and holding a silver-headed cane swept in. A curling gray mustache adorned his face, and his hair was, similar to Aaron’s, carefully styled. At first glance, he almost looked like a retired soldier. His shoulders were somewhat broad, while his belly did pull against the waistcoat under his suit. Someone who hadn’t spent as much time evaluating people’s builds might have thought of him as barrel-chested, but Ezra could tell by the way he walked and carried himself that he was no fighter. In fact, Ezra was nearly certain that any appearance of strength and power was brought by the clothes, not the man.

Behind him came in who Ezra could only assume was the same detective that he’d faced only an hour or so before. However, instead of an angry face twisted by hatred and judgment, this woman had two massive black eyes, her eyes so swollen that she could barely open them. Her left arm hung in a sling, and she seemed to walk with a slight limp. Ezra’s mouth hung open in amazement at the bald lie they were trying to sell, wanting to protest the lies, but instead, he allowed himself to trust in the words that Georg had said. Before anyone could even give greetings, the rotund nobleman began speaking.

“I see you’ve brought the criminal here.” As soon as he said it, the portly man gestured dismissively at Ezra. “I would appreciate your consideration in allowing the Wainwrights to dispense justice upon the criminal who dared assault your guards while conducting themselves in accordance with your laws, Count.”

Then, without waiting for an answer, the man named Rufus gestured with two fingers and pulled two other, unknown guards in. They started walking directly towards Ezra with purpose.

“I appreciate your worry,” the Count stepped forward, his voice cracking like a whip, “but you would do well to remember where you are, both in my home and in my town, Rufus. You are no judge, you are no jury, and you are certainly no executioner. If you consider yourself capable of dealing with me in such a way, I would advise you to speak with the Duke and receive his permission before you start trying to make commands to my constabulary in my home without my command.”

The other nobleman, though without a title, seemed to find it a surprise that Aaron would stand up to him in this.

“Of course I would never seek to countermand you or overstep my authority. I simply thought that we would be on the same page in this. After all, the law is clear as to what will occur to a criminal who would assault a detective or a noble in the streets. I had thought that the decision was made, not that I was making it. My apologies for considering your work already done.”

Something about what the noble had said seemed to rub Aaron the wrong way, but he didn’t say anything to that effect. Instead, he merely said, “The accused’s commanding officer requested for a hearing to be conducted, so I was going to send for you anyways. As such, we will conduct a hearing here, and determine what the appropriate punishment would be.”

“If you think that it is necessary to perpetuate this farce in the pursuit of your duty, then far be it from me to stand in your way,” Rufus bowed his head, the movement regal and somehow still mocking. Even so, he seemed to look at both Rebecca and Georg in confusion. His daughter also seemed to be taken aback by the current sequence of events, and they settled into the room at the Count’s invitation.

“Now, take a seat, Lord and Lady Wainwright,” Aaron commanded, gesturing at a sofa on the left side. It looked to be made of a soft, scarlet material, and Ezra had desperately wanted to run his hands over it when he entered. Instead of that, though, he watched the lying bitch and her arsehole father sit on the sofa, their eyes not even looking at him as they tried to sentence him to death.

“In this case,” Aaron said as soon as the Wainwrights were settled, “the specific accusation hasn’t been leveled officially against Ezra Soldier as of yet. Since you, Rebecca Wainwright, are present as the purported victim, please present the accusations.”

“The alleged crimes are as follows and in ascending order of severity,” Rebecca said, her voice much weaker and more pathetic than when she was shouting for him to be beaten: “Evasion of legally permitted fines and tariffs, assault of a squad of the constabulary, use of aural attacks in so doing, attempted murder of myself and my squad, and, in that process, lèse-majesté.”

“Then so are noted the accusations,” Aaron said, his face stony as he seemed to glare at Ezra. “And how do you plead?”

Ezra looked at Georg, who stepped forward. “Guilty on the count of evasion of fines, assault of the squad, and use of aural attacks. Not guilty in attempted murder and, of course, lèse-majesté is impossible when said in reference to Detective Wainwright, as she is in no way the ruler of this city.”

“I agree on the case of lèse-majesté, which will be dismissed,” Aaron nodded. “Then, in the cases of evasion of fines and assault of a squad of my constabulary, I sentence you to one week in the stockade and to pay a fine of thirty large silver.”

Ezra felt his eyes bug out at that sentencing. That was so much money! He wouldn’t be able to afford that in so long, so what would happen if he continued to be unable to pay? Before he could continue to think about that, Aaron continued, “In the case of assault of a squad of my constabulary with aura, it constitutes deadly force, and I am inclined to judge in favor of this having been an attack with the intent to cripple or to kill. Is there any reason why I should think otherwise?”

“Of course,” Georg replied.

“And that is?” Rufus scoffed. “Look at my daughter. Her arm’s been broken and her eyes blackened, her leg assaulted and her life so obviously barely retained! It’s a miracle that Baltisse extended her regal mercy to Rebecca and preserved her life. She’s come and declared what Please, explain why the word of some street rat recruit of yours should have his word believed when he’s spent his life working as a hired tough in a tavern that’s nearly a brothel?”

“You think a man working for his living makes him untrustworthy?” Ezra laughed, unable to hold himself back. Then, he clenched his jaw against the insults that wanted to bubble out. He'd already made the mistake of opening his mouth, but maybe a question wouldn't be as bad as actually speaking his mind.

“This blithering fool thinks he can try to kill my daughter and then insult me to my face?” Rufus said, rising up and stepping toward the chained Adept. His words were as if he was furious, but the way that his face twisted in pleasure pounded the lesson home to Ezra that he was supposed to Shut. The. Fuck. Up.

Just as Ezra started to insult himself, the long cane whipped out and slammed into his knee. He grunted and glared at the noble as he sneered at the bound younger man, but Georg stepped forward and interposed himself between Rufus and Ezra.

“There, you both got your hits in,” Georg said, glaring at Rufus in a way that the arrogant fucker seemed much less willing to ignore. “Step back.”

“What will you do, Lieutenant?” Rufus asked, emphasizing the scorn and hatred in the title. Even so, he was obviously more than a little taken aback at Georg stepping in. “You’ve lost your mind, stepping forward for a criminal like this!”

“Prisoner Ezra,” Aaron said, cutting through any continuing argument, “stay silent unless talked to. I will have you gagged if you insult people in this trial.”

Extra could feel his pulse in his temple, but instead of saying anything, he nodded once, continuing to stare at the fat bastard. He wouldn’t allow him to get a cheap shot in like that again. With all that was happening, he’d been on his back foot, but now, he could focus on the enemy in front of him, and keeping himself safe.

“Now, to answer your question, Rufus,” Georg said, “I can tell you how we can tell if Ezra was attempting to murder anyone.”

“Oh?” Rufus asked, his moustache twitching on his upper lip. Ezra imagined ripping it off as Georg shrugged.

“Firstly, the claim that Rebecca’s sustained such terrible wounds is untrue. I’ve seen an injured soldier, and I know what I’m looking at. More importantly, I saw Ezra kill an orc from five meters after fighting on the shield wall for half an hour. If he wanted to kill a single detective? Well, Rebecca wouldn’t be here, unless you think she’s hardier than an orc.”

Georg’s declaration seemed to echo through the room. Ezra wasn’t sure why the declaration that the person already accused of being able to use aura actually using aura was so surprising, but he didn’t say anything. Instead, he watched as Rebecca started to open her mouth, just for the female voice that’d introduced the Wainwrights, Winifred, to interrupt.

“Acolyte Mabel si’Baltisse, accompanied by Soldier Amy Tanner have arrived, and the Acolyte is convinced her presence is absolutely necessary. Should I send them in?”
