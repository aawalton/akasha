I concentrate and a blob rises from the bowl of water in front of me. Slowly, other details are added – eyes, a mouth, sharp fangs, and a scaly texture.

Doing my best to hold onto what I’ve formed from water and magic, I lift it higher out of the bowl, adding a sinuous body to it. A misshapen mass appears dangling from the head, as if I’ve attached a bunch of noodles to it.

I concentrate on smoothing out the texture of the body and the water obeys, flowing into place. Then I focus on arms with sharp claws appearing…and the head falls apart, the water splashing back into the bowl as it’s done too many times before.

I sigh out my frustration and lean back to stare up at the bright blue sky for a moment to relax. I was inspired by Torrent’s use of Shaped water creatures in the battle. If we’re attacked by something that strikes us from below, we may be limited in the kind of attacks we can bring to bear. A serpent Shaped from water would be a useful weapon in that case.

The Water Mages in my crew insist that this is the only way to even hope to replicate the kind of attacks Kyrian was capable of – especially since I only have a single Skill to do them with and no water-based Class. I’m not inclined to argue – I know I need to get a far better understanding of water if I want to use it with any sort of dexterity or flexibility. It’s still hard and frustrating.

Water is just so different from fire in many ways. Where fire is eager to spread, water keeps a kind of bondedness with itself – it’s willing to spill or to form a ball, but to manipulate it into differently-shaped sections takes effort. Fire transitions between forms easily – too easily, sometimes; water is malleable, but there’s an innate resistance within it to remaining in whatever unnatural form I’ve put it in.

Out of curiosity, I lift my hand and summon a ball of fire into it. Working delicately, I shape wings, a tail, a long neck, and four limbs. The fire reacts eagerly to my focus and reaches out tongues of flame to form the shape I’m directing it to. It’s different to water in that it is perfectly happy to change shape – not seeking to collapse back into a ball all the time – and it doesn’t put up the same resistance that the water does when I prevent it from changing.

Of course, the fire dragon is ever shifting – the deeper reds intermingle with oranges and yellows unpredictably. And as soon as it’s fully formed, it takes on something of a personality, twisting and turning in my hand, flaring its wings as if it wants to fly.

Overtaken by a sudden whim, I throw it up in the air. Floating like an ember on the breeze, it flies, not even needing to shift its wings to stay airborne. Aingeal sends me a sense of delight and zooms from where he had been absorbing the fire elemental stone I gave him after dealing with the inferno on these very ships. He dances with the dragon, and the dragon dances joyfully with him.

The sailors around aren’t quite so happy. They might have become more used to Aingeal, but that doesn’t mean they’re keen on having a fire elemental around on a ship that, despite the enchantments reducing its vulnerability, is still made out of wood.

I nudge Aingeal mentally and he flies off to the side of the ship. I’m amused and curious when the dragon flies with the elemental without requiring any direction from me. Reaching out with my fire-magic, I brush against the little dragon. I wouldn’t call it sentient, or even remotely sapient, but there’s a hint of personality there, distinct from the general personality of fire.

Interesting. I’d thought that Kyrian had a dedicated Skill that allowed him to create a water construct with its own personality and will, but maybe it’s just that he’s got his Water-Shaping to Master level. I certainly can’t compare with that right now – though I earned a couple of levels in Water-Shaping during the battle and have earned another one since I started experimenting, my Skill is still only Novice rank.

Since I’m not feeding any more mana to the fire dragon, it doesn't last too long. As its store of fuel runs low, it starts to flicker, and then dies a few seconds later. I’m relieved to see that it didn’t have enough self-awareness to seek out more fuel to burn.

Aingeal is disappointed – I have to promise him that I’ll make him another companion to play with later before he stops bobbing in front of my eyes and returns to absorbing the transformed Energy Heart.

Break over, I return my focus to the bowl of water in front of me. Vellus strongly emphasised that I shouldn’t try to control the sea first off but should focus on a bowl of water until I’m able to control it the way I want consistently. Of course, I couldn’t resist trying to control the sea despite his warning, but I quickly found out what he meant. Like fire, the greater the body of water, the greater its will. Fire resists being extinguished; water resists being contained. And, in true water-fashion, it tends to find any crack in my composure. A bowl is far more manageable – at least I was able to make a head.

But sitting here musing won’t help me get any better at the task, so I bend my head over the bowl again.

“Lord Markus?”

The voice startles me and my control slips. The water serpent’s head and half-formed body splashes back into the bow. I glance up to see Lady Keshaka, a touch of apology colouring her usual intense expression.

Lady Renaye’s eldest daughter doesn’t look much like her. In fact, Lady Keshaka looks more like one of the Nethayar with her dusky skin, amber eyes, and chocolate brown hair. But she’s inherited her mother’s hands – long and delicate. Excellent at fine motor control.

“Lady Keshaka,” I greet with a brief nod that she returns. “Is it that time already?” I ask a little ruefully, glancing up at the sun. Oh, how I wish I had a watch! Keshaka is working on it, but hasn’t created a prototype yet. And I’m about to give her something else to think about since I’ve been impressed by what I have seen of her.

“Yes, my lord,” she replies, as stoically as usual.

I think this is the first time I’ve seen her on deck since she boarded – she spends most of her time squirrelled away in her cabin, tinkering away at various incomprehensible devices. I offered a workroom to her but she rejected it. From what she said, she often wakes up in the middle of the night with an idea and finds it frustrating when she has to travel somewhere to start putting it into practice.

But there’s a good reason for her uncharacteristic appearance: I’ve asked her to be here.

I didn’t, however, invite Tiria. I glance at her with raised eyebrows.

“I thought you were working on a new spell that should help us if we’re attacked from below,” I comment. Tiria had been very interested when bombs that can be dropped and then explode under water after a period of time or once they’ve reached a certain depth came up in one of our conversations. Too interested, perhaps. She was willing to be distracted from her own research – mostly because she doesn’t have any test subjects available to test new theories – and dived into the idea with gusto. I wasn’t expecting her to come up for air for at least a few days.

“Oh, I am,” Tiria responds quickly. “And I think I’m making progress with it, though that remains to be seen. But Kesha mentioned that you were going to talk about magical devices from the other world and I couldn’t resist tagging along. Oh, do please say I can stay?”

Her expression turns limpid and begging. I can’t deny that she’s a beautiful woman, but I resist the impulse to melt under her gaze on principle.

To be fair, her input might be useful. She’s a researcher and comes from a different area of magic from Keshaka who is more of a magical engineer. Together, they might actually manage to help me.

But we do need that spell if Tiria can pull it off.

“Alright,” I say finally. “You can stay.” Tiria’s expression fills with both triumph and satisfaction which makes the contrary part of me grumble. “But,” I add warningly, “I want to see progress on that other spell by the end of the tenday. I don’t want you getting so distracted by this that you don’t continue your research there.”

She pouts prettily and I pointedly look away. What a time to develop a crush, I grouse. Anyway, just because I feel attraction doesn’t mean I have to act on it. Especially given the power differential here.

That thought makes me abruptly feel lonely – even if I never say anything to Tiria, finding a partner who wants me for me is going to be difficult now I’m in line for one of the most powerful lordships in Moriax.

Consciously distracting myself, I move the bowl so there’s more space on the table, I then pull out one of the suitcases that I brought from the other world and rest it in the newly-cleared space.

Unzipping it, I reach in for my placephone and my Kindle – two devices that have languished there untouched for more than a year. I brush my fingertips across their screens, remembering a time when I wouldn’t go a day without engaging with at least one of them. I’ve certainly broken the habit of relying on my phone – no choice when it has no battery. And it’s not like I’ll be able to use it to call anyone or ask questions on the internet – that requires satellites that are definitely not present.

But if it can be charged, I’ll be able to see my pictures again – the ones stored on the device, anyway. I swallow down the sudden surge of longing that results from that thought. I might have made a new family, and be immensely grateful for every one of my friends, but that doesn’t mean I’ve forgotten those who used to be my world.

On a more practical front, I’ll be able to use the timer and stopwatch functions. With Fire-Shaping at my command, I don’t exactly need the torch function anymore – though I suppose it could come in handy at certain moments. The clock will also have limited use since each day in this world has twenty-eight candlemarks, not twenty-four hours. But just seeing my pictures is enough reason to make me ask Keshaka for help. And if she can figure out how to charge my phone, she should be able to charge my kindle – I miss my library of books. Moriax seems to be significantly behind when it comes to fiction stories.

Speaking of the magical engineer, she’s fascinated with the zip on my suitcase.

“Your world uses this to close things?” she asks, moving the metal tab back and forth and seeing how the teeth interlock on one side and separate on the other.

“We do,” I confirm.

“Fascinating!” She peers even closer at it. I wait a little impatiently for a few moments and then, when she still seems enraptured by the zip, I clear my throat.

“If you think that’s interesting, these are even more so.” That, of course, gets her full attention.

I start explaining what I know about both phone and Kindle – what they’re for, what they’re made of, and how they work. Since my own understanding of the last is not much more than that they’re pieces of metal and plastic that somehow use electricity stored in a battery to function, I doubt I’m much use on that front, but both Keshaka and Tiria absorb my words with interest and twitchy fingers – I can tell they’re already eager to take the devices apart.

Plastic takes a bit of explanation – they don’t have it in this world.

“Here,” I decide finally, reaching into the bag and pulling out one of my only printed pictures – my mother. I gently remove the picture from the frame, taking a piece of metal from my Inventory and moulding it with Metal-Shaping to make a new frame for it. Then I pass the old one over to Keshaka. “This is a metal frame with plastic moulded around it. You can explore that – I don’t mind if you break it during your explorations.”

“Thank you, Lord Markus,” Keshaka says, accepting the frame with an unusual amount of excitement in her expression. I rummage through my bag trying to see if there’s anything else I could give them that might help. While searching, I find my wok – a saucepan that was sadly retired when one of the Warriors wasn’t looking where he was going and stepped on it. Fortunately, by that time I was able to use magic to replace it, even if I never managed to fix it.

Digging deeply in the bag, I find something unexpected – an old yoyo. That must have been in there before I packed it and somehow I missed it when I was examining what I had brought with me in the other world. Pity – the string could have come in useful. Still, this might be just the thing to protect my phone from over-eager magical researchers.

“You can take this too,” I tell them, placing the yoyo on the table. Both of them gaze at it with confusion.

“What is it?” asks Tiria, prodding at it with a finger as if it might move. “Some sort of trophy?”

“No,” I smile. “It’s a game for children.” I demonstrate, hooking my finger in the loop and letting it bounce. “But the thing is, this used to flash lights when I played with it, so it must have a circuit board and battery inside that can conduct electricity – a kind of tame form of lightning. I don’t care if this is broken, so perhaps try your experiments on this first. If you can make the lights work again, it’s a good sign that you can help with my phone.”

“That would certainly be useful, Lord Markus,” Keshka agrees, already picking up the yoyo and bringing it closer to inspect it. I quickly unhook my finger from the string and let her.

I continue rummaging through my bag. In the end, I don’t find anything else as potentially useful as that old yoyo, but I do hand over the charging cable, charging plug, and universal adaptor that I brought with me for them to inspect. Ultimately, if they can somehow generate electricity that the charging cable can use to deliver to the phone, that would be perfect. The main question is of power.

For now, I’ll keep my phone and Kindle. They can come and ask to borrow the devices if they want, but I think they need to learn more about electronics from the yoyo before they run any experiments on my more important devices.

Technically, if they fry the electrics accidentally, it won’t lose me anything – the intact phone and kindle aren’t doing me much good as it is. But while the devices are intact, I still have hope. Both Tiria and Keshka are disappointed, but they don’t argue too much. That is to say, Keshka doesn’t argue at all while Tiria attempts to change my mind – fruitlessly.

I watch for a while as they examine the yo-yo, but find both their actions and their murmurs incomprehensible. This is high-level research, magic-style, and I just don’t have the background to make heads nor tails of the symbols or spells they’re using to gain information. I think they even conduct a short ritual that spews out a whole load more symbols. They twitter eagerly about the results, but it’s gibberish to me.

Getting a little bored, I pull up my status screen and check on my progress – I’m curious to see if I’ve made progress even since the battle.
