With a stern stride, Anadis’ father walked past Selene—completely ignoring her, as though seeing an Oupire girl was not noteworthy—and went straight to Anadis, his gaze dark and sharp. It took Anadis too long to realize what was happening.

“Hey, Dad, I have a lot to tell—”

“You have two seconds to explain why you were out in the forest just a couple of days after waking up from an attack that nearly left you immobile in that very same forest.” Father drew his arm back.

“W-wait… What?” Anadis stammered.

“One,” he counted.

“Wait! I can explain!”

Mother and Grandma grabbed his arm and pulled with all their might, but to no avail.

“Hadrian, listen! I haven’t told you the whole story!” Mother shouted, her voice strained as she struggled to pull Father’s arm back.

“Foolish son! Listen to your wife!” Grandma snapped before smacking Father on the back of the neck.

Thoughts scattered like leaves in a storm inside Anadis. Then the world narrowed to a single point—a palm, thick, rugged, and calloused. Was he about to hit him? No. No way.

“Two.” Father snapped his arm forward, aiming at Anadis’s head. In that moment, Anadis felt the world freeze. Father was serious. He was actually going to do it. Anadis shut his eyes, waiting for his inevitable doom when—“Stop!” Lightning crackled furiously around Albus, his white hair standing on end as he planted himself between Anadis and the palm of death. “Anadis did not do anything wrong!” he shouted, arms spread wide. “This is not a reasonable response!”

Father pulled his arm back, but not far enough, because every survival instinct in Anadis’s body screamed. Anadis glanced at Albus. Where had he even come from?

Then Father eyed Albus sharply and asked in a firm voice. “How so?”

Albus said nothing for a long time, but then another voice echoed behind them. “I-I can explain,” Sevran announced, climbing through the shutters and entering the room, huffing. “It’s all my fault. Anadis didn’t go to the forest willingly.”

The room went quiet for a moment, only the crackle of Albus’s lightning splitting the silence. Mother and Grandma sighed wearily. Selene stayed off to the side, eyes wide and darting frantically from person to person. Forcing himself to inhale, Anadis managed to look up at his father. “Dad, I-I can explain everything. It’ll all make sense; please allow me.”

The man nodded solemnly.

It wasn’t easy. Anadis explained how he’d gotten the Boon and lied about setting it up in a cave just in case a Grull attacked him so he could escape. Father didn’t believe him until Anadis summoned it. Of course, being a Luminous, he knew it was a Boon right away. Sevran apologized repeatedly for activating it, but Anadis didn’t blame him—the boy hadn’t known and was just curious.

“Why didn’t you tell me about the Boon when you woke up?” Father said after Anadis finished.

“I honestly forgot about it, and with what you told us at dinner…”

Father ran a hand down his face and sighed. “That’s understandable. Still,” he lifted a finger. “You scared your mother. If there’s one thing a man must never do, it’s worry the people who love him—especially a mother.” Then he took a single step closer and crouched to meet Anadis’s eye level. In a low, sharp voice, he said, “I need you to promise me two things. One, you won’t hide anything from us and will be open about anything you plan to do. And two, you won’t hurt your mother again.”

A lump in his throat made it hard to reply. Anadis glanced at Hel out of the corner of his eye. Uncertain, he managed, “I promise.”

“Good,” Father said. “Now… I understand you helped this young lady?” He pointed toward Selene, who was uncomfortably holding her stuff to the side. She’d made attempts to leave, but Father wouldn’t let her. “For that, I’m proud of you. A man must always help those in need. Keep your chest forward—you did a good thing.”

“Thank you… Father.” Anadis’s voice was cautious, as if he were testing whether it was safe to speak.

Selene cleared her throat, and everyone turned to her. “Ma’am… Thank you for helping me.” Her voice was guarded. She bowed slightly toward Mother. “You didn’t have to, especially knowing I’m not… like you. But I’m grateful nonetheless.” She paused for a moment, then looked at Anadis. “I won’t trouble you any longer. I can walk myself home.” She turned and walked toward the door until—

“Oh no. You’re not leaving, young lady,” Mother’s voice cut in, blocking the exit. “It’s pitch dark outside.”

“I’m a Blessen. I can take care of—”

“I don’t care,” Mother said, resolute, arms crossed and chin held high. “Blessen or not, it’s not safe for you. Stay here. End of story.”

Selene’s eyebrows shot up so fast they nearly escaped her face. Probably nothing would have happened to her if she’d left, but that word was doing a lot of heavy lifting, and in this house, Mother didn’t deal in “probably.” She dealt in “because I said so.”

“Listen to my wife, kid,” Father said to Selene, arms crossed.

“You’ll stay with my daughters tonight,” Mother said. “And tomorrow my husband will take you to your parents.”

Hadrian nodded, then reacted. “Wait, I will?”

“Yes! A problem?”

He shook his head, then said, “No problem, darling. I’ll see it done.”

A loud growl echoed through the sudden silence. Selene’s face flushed crimson as she clutched her stomach, trying to quiet it.

Mother tilted her head and let a smile soften her face. “Oh, you poor thing. You must be starving.” She gestured toward the kitchen. “Why don’t you eat that weird flatbread with cheese and tomato sauce my son made? Honestly, I don’t know where he got the idea, but it’s delicious—especially the meat and herbs. It’s… simple, but amazing. You’ll love it.”

Mother waited for Selene to respond, but she didn’t; she was too lost or too hungry to understand what Mother was saying. “You know he cooked it because he knew you’d be hungry once you woke up.” Mother winked at Selene.

“For me?” Selene met Anadis’s eyes. “…Thank you.”

“Oh yes, that’s my son for you, such a gentleman. What did you call it? Pessa?” she asked.

“Pizza…” Anadis whispered.

“What a weird name. Of course, a kid would name that. Still, you may have a knack for being a chef.”

A cheerful shriek came from behind Mother’s back. Before Selene could protest, Nora darted forward and grabbed her by one arm. “Yes! You’ll eat with us! Come on, let’s get to know each other.” Aelia appeared on Selene’s other side, taking her free arm with equal enthusiasm. Selene didn’t resist much—probably too stunned—and let herself be pulled along by the two sisters.

Selene glanced at Anadis one last time before disappearing into the hallway.

Good luck… you’ll need it.

Anadis fell face-first onto the mattress, which felt like heaven on Gaelora beneath his tired muscles. The gentle patter of rain on the roof, the distant croaking of frogs, and the soft nightly breeze slipping through his bedsheets made this the perfect night to sleep. Well, it would have been, if it weren’t for the loud shrieking erupting from the next room—his sisters’ room. He couldn’t make out every word beyond the constant bickering and laughter, but he had a feeling Selene had become their little Oupire project girl.

Their screeching amplified.

Argh, can’t they shut up? I want to sleep.

“Girls!” Grandma Anna’s voice burst through the hallway. “If I hear one more shriek, you’ll sleep outside!”

Silence followed, then stifled giggles… then silence again.

Anadis smiled. Grandma saves the night.

Hel phased through the wall, illuminating the dark room and prompting Anadis to wonder if the room would look brighter to anyone else, since only he could see her.

“Their rambling was almost entertaining,” she murmured, stifling a yawn. She drifted onto the pillow and curled up to sleep, her glow dimming like a dying ember.

Wait, she sleeps? For a second, he doubted it, but even she couldn’t stay awake indefinitely.

Looking past her to where Albus was sleeping on his side of the room in his bed, Anadis whispered, “Albus. You still awake?”

The white-haired boy turned his head over his shoulder and nodded.

“Thanks a lot for… you know, putting yourself between Dad and me… really, that was nice.”

Albus shrugged. “You would have done the same.” Then he pointed outside. “When you disappeared earlier today, Sevran and I didn’t know what happened. Sevran was going frantic. He thought some kind of invisible Grull must have taken you. We searched the whole town, the mountains, and a bit of the forest looking for you. We eventually stopped because we couldn’t think of anywhere else to look, and came back hoping you’d be back… and you were.”

“Listen,” he said, feeling bad for not responding to his long story, but he was so tired he wanted to go to bed. “I need your help with something important,” Anadis murmured. “It has to do with that girl. You in?”

“Okay,” he said almost immediately.

“I knew I could count on you.”

“Anadis,” Albus called softly, “I’m happy you got yourself a girlfriend.”

Anadis breathed in, almost choking. “She’s just a friend…”

“Oh, I see.”

Tiredly, Anadis lifted a fist into the air. Across the room, Albus raised his in return, sort of a wordless acknowledgment, a gesture that didn’t need to land. With that, Anadis threw a blanket over himself and drifted off to sleep.
