Before they decided how to proceed, Hannah and Penny explained what they knew about the void and how to fight it. A lot of it sounded like they were speaking in metaphors, as if their main goal was to establish a new religion or something with Luke at the helm. What both of them and their messiah had failed to mention before the entire group accepted the deranged plan was how only the two of them knew enough about this stuff to even fight void creatures. To put them down, you needed to destroy their Source channels, apparently. Doing that required an intimate knowledge of and the ability to sense the system itself. That was according to what they knew, at least. Curtis wasn’t so sure. He had yet to meet anything or anyone he couldn’t consume. Why should void creatures be any different? They would try to eat him, and he would in turn eat them. Fair.

To their credit, no one had known the void would show up in the dungeon. That wasn’t supposed to be possible. Luke’s plan was to drain Source from some big bad void monster and put it into the dungeon. That was supposed to make the monsters or whatever inside stronger, but he hadn’t mentioned anything about cosmic death incarnate joining in. Whatever.

Nick: “Hello? Are you getting this?”

Penny: “Yes. It’s working.”

Nick nodded and took center stage. Curtis’s brother always demanded attention, and the natural ease with which he wielded his charisma made him a natural leader. At least that’s what his coach had said once, when explaining one of the many ways he wanted Curtis to be more like his brother. His little brother. That stung at the time. Not anymore. If there was something Curtis didn’t want, it was to be the center of attention.

“Okay, listen up,” he said, raising his hands into the air to get everyone to quiet down. “Like every smart horror movie character ever, we’re going to split up.”

Ray nodded gravely. “Smart.”

“Let me finish, or I’ll start calling you Ray of Sunshine too,” Nick said with a disarming smile.

Narrowing his eyes, Ray chose not to reply.

“Good, great,” Nick continued, shooting Curtis an unreadable look. “Like I said, we’re going to form two parties and split up.” He pointed down to the town by the sea. “One party will go down there and see what’s going on, and the other will go to this king, whatever-his-name-was.”

“Eriksen,” Priyah said. It was the first time she had spoken since coming into the dungeon.

Nick nodded. “Thank you, Priyah. Eriksen. With the quest being corrupted, we’ll have to go find the objectives for ourselves. We’ll all be careful, and if something happens, we have the communication gems.” He looked up into the sky. “Looks like it’s about noon here. If the gems stop working for whatever reason, we’ll meet up back here when it gets dark.”

When no one spoke up with a better idea, Nick nodded to himself and started dividing the group of people into two parties.

Integrated Martin. Phase Blade. Level 34.

Integrated Curtis. Hollow. Level 41.

Integrated Ray. Assassin. Level 36.

Integrated Penny. Tempest. Level 39.

Integrated Priyah. Shepherd. Level 29.

Integrated Kenny. Healer. Level 27.

Integrated Nick. Knight. Level 37.

Integrated Mateo. Lancer. Level 30.

Integrated Elijah. Radiant Templar. Level 31.

Integrated Lemira. Core Caller. Level 33.

Integrated Vicky. Vitalist. Level 30.

Integrated Hannah. Vesper Champion. Level 42.

Curtis had hoped not to get stuck with Kenny. There was just something about the man’s voice, appearance, personality, and everything else that rubbed him the wrong way. But when he saw how Nick and Vicky were looking at one another, almost perfectly mirroring Elijah and Lemira, he knew there was no chance in hell Vicky would be in any other party than Nick’s, and with two healers and two parties, there was nowhere else for the asshat Kenny to go. At least they gathered all the lovebirds in one party.

“There we go,” Nick said. “Martin, you take your group down to the water. If there’s nothing there, you let us know, and we’ll see about meeting back up.”

“Sounds good,” Martin agreed.

With that, they were off. Trudging down toward the small seaside town, Curtis looked down at the much shorter Priyah walking beside him. “Why did you join?”

“Be nice, Curtis,” Penny said without looking back from where she walked up front with Martin.

“I’m just asking a question.”

Priyah’s eyes went wide as soon as he said her name, as if not used to people addressing her. She shot a glance his way and then looked down at the ground, her small fists balled into fists. “I want to help.”

“That’s it?” he asked.

“They’re all dead. Dead or gone.”

“Who are?”

“The Band.”

“More people quit the guild?” Curtis asked. He had done so himself right before agreeing to go into this dungeon. There was no point to it anymore. No point at all.

But Priyah shook her head. “Nash is dead. Mas is dead, and Sam, his sister, left to fight the void. Tom didn’t even come with us, and Savannah just disappeared. I’m all that’s left, but I want to help.”

Curtis nodded. “Fair enough.”

“What about you?” Priyah asked.

He was surprised to have the question thrown back at him. She didn’t seem the type to speak unless spoken to. Taken off guard, he shrugged. “I don’t know.”

“You don’t know why you’re here?”

“I don’t.”

“That’s sad.”

Curtis blinked and gave her a long look. He was about to reply, but unsure what to say, when Ray broke in.

“You guys are depressing me.” He pointed. “Look. We’re almost there. Please hold the tears.”

Penny’s words dripped with warning. “Ray…”

“Let’s focus,” Martin said, his eyes scanning a low wall spanning the length of the town. People were stationed along it at uneven intervals. Most of them clutched spears or bows, but none looked like they wanted a fight.

A party of five somewhat official-looking militiamen strode out of a low wooden gate that looked like it couldn’t even hold pigs in a pen from escaping. They wore red and green tabards over ring-mail. Their uniform had an emblem emblazoned, but it was impossible to make out what it had once looked like, because they’d all cut long gashes in the fabric, destroying it. City guards who had defected, Curtis guessed.

“Who are you?” one of them called after the group had stopped a little too far away for a normal conversation.

“We’re here to help!” Martin called back.

“Who?”

Martin turned around. “What was the Jarl’s name again?”

“Eriksen,” Priyah said.

“Thank you, Priyah,” Martin said before turning back to resume the conversation.

Her face reddened.

Curtis rolled his eyes but didn’t have time for much more, because an arrow streaked through the space between the group of Integrated and the town, slamming into Kenny of all people. Bad luck, considering he was at the back and half-crouched, trying to stay hidden. The Healer let out a shriek, his eyes going wide as he stared at the haft stuck in his forearm.

“Heal yourself,” Curtis said as he grabbed the arm. “This might hurt.”

Breaking the arrow and pushing it through wasn’t possible since it was lodged in bone. Instead, Curtis pulled, tugging it free. It was a mess, but there was no time to do a better job of it. More arrows had joined the first. The Townsfolk wanted a fight. Or did they?

After the first couple of arrows, they had stopped coming. The group up front looked surprised, if anything. A shout rose from behind the wall, but it wasn’t a battle cry. It sounded more like someone crying out in terror. More voices joined in, and it grew into a cacophony. One by one, those manning the walls disappeared, and another voice added to the din.

“Do you think they’re pranking us?” Ray asked. “I think they’re pranking us.”

Lightning crackled in Penny’s eyes, and she rose into the air, hair whipping, caught in a strong wind blowing from the water. Martin turned to Ray. “You can go invisible, man. Why don’t you go check?”

“Fine,” he grumbled and disappeared. “I’ll go investigate the demonic screeching. You guys just hang back and take it easy.”

Curtis still held the arrow and considered using that to get the Healer’s attention, but thought it would be more thoughtful to use something else, like his boot. “Just heal yourself already.”

Rather than calm down, Curtis’s boot in his side seemed to make it worse, because Kenny’s screams grew louder, and then louder still. They turned… weird. It was as if the sound took form, making the air shudder. Kenny had joined in with those on the other side of the wall, and they got first-row seats to a smaller version of what was happening back there.

Hannah descended with an arrow through her leg, her face white with pain. “What’s wrong with Kenny?” she asked, but the others didn’t have an answer for her. Instead, they watched together as Kenny’s skin darkened and his flesh buckled inward, like all his bones had just up and vanished.

“No,” Penny whispered. “He’s turning.” She looked from the arrow in Curtis’s hand to the one still stuck through her leg, eyes full of worry. Curtis didn’t blame her. The arrow had done this to Kenny. That had to be it.

“He’s dead, or will be soon,” Curtis said.

“I’ll make sure the void doesn’t take him,” Penny said.

Curtis held up a hand, stopping the chant before she had a chance to start. “Let me try.”

“You know how?”

“Learning by doing,” he muttered. “Learning by doing. Let’s see what the void can do against my darkness.”

The void, it turned out, experienced the same sort of hunger that had taken over Curtis ever since changing his class from Reaver to Hollow. No, it had been there before that, just hidden away. What a thing to feel that need to consume while being so devoid of emotions and wants. So far, Curtis hadn’t even found what he needed to make part of himself to fill that hole. Monsters didn’t work. Perhaps the void would.

Kenny was turning into something else before their eyes. Since he was their one healer, there wasn’t much anyone else could do to save him, and it was doubtful anything could have been done by any healer, save perhaps Luke, who had that uncanny ability that seemed to rewind time and restore whatever injury someone sustained. But Luke wasn’t there, and Kenny was dead. Or perhaps he was still alive in there, but it wouldn’t stay that way for long.

“Are you sure about this?” Penny asked.

Curtis nodded without looking over at her. “Yes. You should go help the others.”

Her voice sounded again. Smaller this time. Afraid. “What if that happens to me too?”

“It won’t,” Curtis grunted. “You won’t let it. You’re stronger than this one.”

She was silent for a moment, then he saw her nod out of the corner of his eye, and then she was off.

The rest of the group disappeared, leaving him alone with the dying Healer. He stood there for a moment, taking in the transformation. Flesh turned blacker than black and fell in on itself, creating a rift in the air. There was no smell, and he made no sound. If Curtis had to guess, there was a lot going on inside the decaying body, but he didn’t have the sort of expertise necessary to understand that sort of thing. These days, he didn’t need much understanding in any case.

Summoning the darkness inside him and bringing it out into the world was a process as natural as breathing. It was difficult to remember how it had been before the change, because the way he was now was how it was supposed to be. He had been running toward this state ever since being a kid. He just hadn’t known it yet. All those days alone, all that time by himself in the dark. Becoming a Reaver was like coming home and finding himself, but when he became a Hollow, he knew that he needed no home. He needed nothing and no one, except to feed. Had he now, then, perhaps found the sustenance his body cried out for?

Curtis’s arms lost their form to the darkness, and his legs followed. Soon, there was nothing left of the form he took when others were around. The darkness was his natural form, and it was time to pit himself against the nothingness of the void. Something in there beckoned, something he hungered for. After draping himself over Kenny, who was no longer breathing, Curtis wrapped himself around the body, creating a cocoon. Enclosing the thing Kenny was turning into meant it had no way of escape, nowhere to run. A smile reached Curtis’s lips as he got to work. At first, there was nothing there. That soon changed. All it took was a change of perspective. He didn’t need to consume something. He needed to consume nothing. The void rose up to meet him, its hunger almost a match for his own. It was a living thing, a creature of eyes, mouths, and slithering limbs trying to break free while also attempting to end him. This was not the first monster that thought it could feed on Curtis and his darkness, but it made the same mistake as all the others. The darkness was not a separate thing. The darkness was Curtis, and Curtis was the darkness. They were one and the same, and when it came to hunger, not even the infinite void of nothingness could compare.

The struggle of nothing versus darkness was brief but intense. In the end, Curtis’s hunger won out, his will stronger than that of the creature. When it came to hunger, nothing could rise above his need to eat and the will he wielded to accomplish that task. The void lost its integrity and broke apart under the immense pressure of Curtis’s focus. After all, how could nothingness win over anything? That idea was preposterous. Something glimmered deep inside. It was faint and formless, but undeniable. Curtis had found what he hungered for.

Consuming that energy was not enough, but it was a start. He felt that energy strengthen his darkness in a way he hadn’t experienced before. It was far more potent than simply feeding monsters to it. Reforming the old image of himself, that of flesh and bone, Curtis smiled to himself, feeling stronger than ever, and, more importantly, he felt a rush of emotion. Joy filled him. A giddiness of expectation, of things to come. He had found the one thing that could quench his need. Now, he needed more of it. Much, much more.

He was surprised to find this was not what his Seeker profession had been pointing him to. There was something else out there to hunt. All of a sudden, he had rediscovered the excitement of the hunt. The gray tint of the world that had fallen into place with the defeat of the Singers disappeared. Curtis followed the screams, hoping to find more dungeon dwellers turning into void creatures. He still hungered, after all.
