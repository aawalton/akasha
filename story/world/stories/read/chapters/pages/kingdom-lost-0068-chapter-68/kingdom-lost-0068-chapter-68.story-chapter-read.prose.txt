The heavy wooden door of the tower creaked as Valrik pushed it open wider. The night air carried the faint scent of pine smoke from the dying hearths below. Riley stood motionless on the threshold, her fingers still curled around the edge of the frame. The rider’s message hung between them like frost.

Her mouth parted, then closed again without sound. The words Valrik had just spoken settled into her chest like cold iron.

“Call the others,” she said. Her voice came out quieter than she intended, but steady. “First thing in the morning. Everyone who needs to be there. Thorne, Landryn, you, and Nikola. We plan at dawn.”

Valrik nodded once. His broad shoulders shifted as he turned back toward the steps. “I’ll see it done.”

He descended without another word. The sound of his boots on the stone faded quickly. Riley remained where she was. She stared out over the dark shapes of the bulk houses, the sawmill’s silhouette against the moonlit ridge, the faint gleam of the quarry beyond. The settlement looked small from up here, too small for what was coming. She closed the door and the latch clicked into place with a soft finality.

The first light of morning crept over the eastern ridge in thin, pale streaks. Tama stood outside the bunk house door, arms folded against the chill that still clung to the ground. The sky above her shifted from deep indigo to soft gray, no clouds. Clear enough for travel, clear enough to be seen.

Her shoulders felt lighter than they had in months. The choice was no longer hers alone. The people she had led here had made their own decisions. Some stayed behind with the elders and the youngest, some came forward, some still wavered. She carried no guilt for any of them now. The weight had lifted the moment the last cart rolled through the gates yesterday.

A small hand slipped into hers. Tama looked down. Mali stood beside her, barefoot on the cold earth, nightshirt hanging loose around her thin legs. The girl’s dark hair was tangled from sleep.

“We are happy now,” Mali said. Her voice was small, almost swallowed by the morning quiet.

Tama felt the child’s fingers tighten once, then relax. Mali kept her gaze fixed on the sunrise. She did not look up.

“This place is special,” she added.

Tama swallowed. The words landed softly, without demand. She squeezed the small hand in return. From inside the bunk house came Nami’s voice, gentle but firm. “Mali, come back to bed. It’s still early.”

Mali released Tama’s fingers. She turned and padded back through the doorway without protest. The door eased shut behind her with a quiet thud. Tama stood alone again.

The sun cleared the ridge, and its warmth touched her face for the first time that day. She lifted her chin and breathed in the sharp, clean air. This moment would come to an end with the group’s vote on their future, once they were awake.

The meeting chamber inside the tower held a single long table in the center, its surface scarred from the few meetings held at it. Four men sat around it now: Thorne with his arms folded tight across his chest, Valrik leaning forward on his elbows, Nikola making last-minute checks on the HUD, and Landryn deep in thoughts about the mine’s projected production for today.

Riley stood at the head of the table. Sunlight slanted through the narrow window slits, hitting the table.

“The villagers are scheduled to leave this morning,” she said. “We have two important matters to discuss before they go. But first, Valrik.”

Valrik straightened. He let the silence stretch a moment longer than necessary. The others shifted in their seats.

“We sent riders yesterday,” he said. “One uncovered something.”

The pause thickened. Thorne’s jaw tightened. Nikola closed the HUD.

“We know the location of the raiders’ compound.”

A collective breath caught in the room. Thorne’s chair scraped back an inch. Nikola’s eyes narrowed. Landryn’s face hardened.

Thorne leaned forward. “Let’s ride for retribution.”

Nikola nodded sharply. “Yes, we should raid them and take everything.”

Valrik’s voice came low and hard. “Leave none alive. Burn everything to the ground. For our fallen.”

Landryn raised one hand, palm open. “We need a plan. This shouldn’t be taken lightly.”

Riley met each man’s gaze in turn. “I agree with all of you. We need to take them out. Not only for our fallen, but for the general safety of the realm. But we do need to plan—to prepare. I want the four of you to work together on this. Thorne and Valrik, run the drills today. Assess the soldiers and identify who should ride on the mission. Nikola and Landryn, inventory what the forge has produced. Outfit those chosen men with the best gear we have. Adjust resource and mine production for the day and night this operation will happen. We want anyone watching us to think it is business as usual here while we are gone.”

She paused. The men exchanged glances. Thorne gave a single nod, while Valrik’s mouth curved in the barest hint of approval. Nikola rubbed his chin, and Landryn traced a finger along the edge of the table.

“We will also need to gather intelligence on the raiders’ settlement,” Riley continued. “How many are there? What are their weaknesses? We will plan that out later today.”

Murmurs of agreement rose around the table. Thorne thumped a fist once against the wood, and the others followed with quieter affirmations. Riley let the sound settle.

“As for the Hoshin people,” she said. “If they decide to come, then the Clawborn will follow shortly after. And…”

Thorne straightened. “We will stand ready.”

Valrik’s voice overlapped his. “Our soldiers will meet the challenge head on.”

Riley looked at each of them again, slower this time.

“And we aren’t ready.”

The words dropped into the room like stones into still water. No one spoke, and the burning log in the brazier crackled. Outside, distant voices carried on the morning breeze, the sounds of carts being loaded and farewells being exchanged.

The silence held.

Riley let the silence stretch a moment longer. She placed both hands flat on the table, fingers spread against the rough wood.

“I deal in facts,” she said. “We are not ready. We have an army that has spent most of its time gathering. We have defenses to develop and we need more soldiers. Many, many more. We also know very little about the Clawborn, really. All this must change, and for that we need time.”

Nikola leaned back in his chair. The wood creaked under his weight. “So then they can’t come here?”

Riley met his eyes without blinking. “They must. Or they will die.”

Valrik rubbed a hand across his jaw. “So what is the answer? How do we save them and keep the Clawborn at bay so we can be prepared?”

Riley’s mouth curved into a small, deliberate smile. “We replace them,” she said, “with our soldiers.”

The words landed flat in the room. Thorne’s eyebrows drew together. Nikola’s mouth opened halfway, then closed. Landryn’s fingers froze on the map. Valrik stared at her as though waiting for the rest of the sentence.

Riley continued before anyone could speak. “We move most of them and change their clothes. Outfit our soldiers to look like them.”

Landryn exhaled through his nose. “So we produce resources for the enemy?”

“We buy time,” Riley answered. “Our soldiers outproduce the villagers. We leave some of the able-bodied ones behind so there are familiar faces. The Clawborn see the same carts rolling out, the same people in the fields. They see tribute, and nothing changes on their end.”

Nikola’s eyes seemed to defocus, as if he was imagining Riley’s scenario. “We can train enough to replace those soldiers in about two days. And with the villagers here to make up a lot of the gathering shortfall, it would work.”

Thorne crossed his arms again. The muscles in his forearms flexed. “How long would we keep up these appearances? How long would we gather resources for them?”

Riley lifted one shoulder. “That, I don’t know. We would need to plan that out. The key is it gives us flexibility. Time is our most valuable resource. The key is that we can save the Hoshin people.”

Valrik studied her for a long beat. Then he gave a single, slow nod. “We are behind you, Warden. This is a bold plan.”

Thorne glanced at Nikola, then at Landryn. One by one their heads dipped in agreement. The motion was small but certain. No one spoke after that.

Riley pushed away from the table. “Then we start today. Valrik, Thorne, begin the drills and select who can pass for villagers. We want the first group of soldiers to be seasoned gatherers. Nikola, Landryn, stand ready for their decision, then figure out the clothing swap. Keep it quiet outside this room. The less who know the full plan, the better.”

She walked to the window and looked out. Below, carts creaked along the main path. Hoshin villagers congregated outside the bunk house as if they were waiting for something. The sun stood higher now, burning off the last of the morning chill.

Riley turned back to the men. “We offered them a future. If they say yes, let’s make it a bright one.”

The group rose without further discussion. They took their leave. Riley remained by the window a moment longer. The weight of the Hoshin villagers’ decision was in Riley’s mind. They could change this place forever.
