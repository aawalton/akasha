Eternal Regeneration - lvl 42

Without warning, Asher suddenly found himself standing outside in a familiar practice field. He, Samantha, Rulfar, and Moxy had used this very spot countless times for sparring and honing their skills. It was close enough that the capital was still visible off in the distance if only just, but far enough away that they wouldn’t be disturbed. From the looks of things, Samantha was currently in the middle of practicing her archery, the hunter focused on her distant targets as she let fly an arrow that whistled across the field before sinking straight into her bullseye. She was absorbed enough in her practice that she hadn’t noticed his sudden appearance just yet.

“Nice shot,” Asher said, causing her to practically leap out of her skin as she spun around and trained an arrow on his chest. For a single moment, Asher got to experience what it was like being one of Samantha’s targets, before she realized it was him and her eyes widened.

“Asher!”

All but hurling her bow and arrow to the side, Samantha leapt at him, throwing her arms around him and tackling him to the ground. The surprise impact was almost enough to drive the air from his lungs, and Asher chuckled weakly as he returned the desperate hug.

“Sorry for the scare,” he said as he kissed the top of her head. “Are you okay?”

“I should be asking you that,” Samantha said, her grip tightening around him as if afraid he’d vanish again the moment she let him go. “Donvath told us what happened, but I still couldn’t believe it.”

“Oh he did, did he?” Asher asked, frowning as he only just then recalled what he was going to do to Donvath. “What did he say?”

“He told us how he pretended like he was betraying you and killed you in a very specific manner in order to get your Eternal Regeneration to unlock the strongest effect possible for its third-tier evolution,” Samantha explained, a dark look spreading across her face as her grip tightened even further around him. “He had a rough guess as to when you’d come back to us, and I told him if you didn’t reappear by then, I’d become famous for finding a way to assassinate The Immortal.”

“Wow… You’re kind of hot when you’re pissed.”

Snorting, Samantha leaned forward and pulled him into a passionate kiss, letting her lips show him just how much she missed him. The two of them sat like that for a minute, and by the time they pulled away, Asher finally realized a rather important fact about his resurrection.

While he had his flesh and blood body back once again, at the moment, he was naked as the day he was born.

“One second,” he said, getting to his feet as he pulled his back-up set of sea serpent leathers out of his rift and put them on. “That’s better. Anyway, what did I miss? How long was I gone anyway, a day or so?”

“Asher…” Samantha said, her smile fading as she gave him a concerned look. “You’ve been gone for just over three weeks.”

“Weeks?!” he repeated, his heart sinking as he realized the passage of time within that eternal void truly had been impossible to keep track of. While it was certainly better than years, all of his prior resurrections had only taken a few hours or so. That said, he supposed this was the first time he’d had to recreate his body entirely from scratch. A few weeks sounded like a fair price to pay.

“Donvath promised us you’d be back within the month, but he couldn’t give us a more specific timeframe than that,” Samantha continued, looking him up and down as if hunting for any sort of irregularities surrounding his new body. “How do you feel?”

“The same as ever,” he shrugged, flexing his fingers and confirming they still worked. “I’m more than a little annoyed at Donvath for this stunt, but I’m willing to at least hear him out before I stab him in the stomach. The fact that he’s been entirely honest with you guys surrounding what happened is a point in his favor, at the very least.”

After confirming that Samantha and the others were alright, and that his new body seemed identical to the old, Asher’s biggest concern was wondering what exactly had happened to everything he’d been wearing at the time of his death. His clothes had definitely turned to dust along with his body, but he couldn’t remember what had happened to his daggers or artifacts. He’d been a little preoccupied with the most unexpected betrayal of his life.

“It definitely came as a surprise,” Samantha nodded. “Rulfar wasn’t very happy, but it was actually Moxy who seemed the angriest. She punched Donvath in the face when he told us, sending him reeling over his desk and knocking the mountain of paperwork all over the place. I wanted to do the same if not worse, only…”

“Brian is still holed up with Donvath’s truthseekers,” Asher finished, giving her an understanding smile. “Don’t worry, I get it. While that’s definitely the safest place for him, that unfortunately gives Donvath some unpleasant power over you. Is Brian alright?”

“He’s fine. Visiting him has been trickier without your Recall skill, but Donvath has made it work. I’ve still gotten a day with him once a week.”

“That’s good. Oh, the lich!” Asher said, only just then remembering what they’d been in the middle of before his untimely demise. “What’s going on there?”

“Nothing,” Samantha shrugged. “Without your Recall, none of us are exactly capable of getting back to the forest even for some scouting. And without Sever the Thread, it’s not as if we could truly kill Jormon anyway. As far as we know he’s still sitting in his fort behind that colossal monster we felt.”

“Okay. Okay, that works,” Asher decided, letting out a sigh as he ran his fingers through his hair. “In that case, no sense in putting it off any longer. I’m going to go have what might very well end up being a somewhat violent talk with this kingdom’s High Prince. Want to come with?”

“Donvath asked us to let the two of you discuss things privately once you’d returned,” Samantha said, giving him one more kiss before her emerald wings flared out from her back. “I’ll go let the others know you’re back!”

Waving at her as she took off into the sky, Asher cracked his neck and summoned Crescent and Nail to his hands. While he kept his void dagger on his person at all times, his two primary daggers were always safely stored away inside his Personal Rift. Hoping Donvath would have a damn-good explanation prepared for what had just happened, Asher focused on his Eternal Mark linked to his and Samantha’s room in the royal palace, trading the large, empty field for their luxurious accommodations. Despite three weeks having passed, everything was just as he remembered.

Other than Donvath himself sitting at their table, giving him a sheepish grin.

“Welcome ba—”

Following Moxy’s lead, Asher punched the High Prince of the kingdom in the face through a tunnel of compressed space, feeling Donvath’s nose crunch under his fist as the man went sprawling to the ground. As Donvath reeled, Asher stepped forward, ensuring Complete Resilience’s immunity had been switched back to the Time element before he tossed Nail back into his rift and used his free hand to grab Donvath by the collar. Hoisting him up without the aid of his Belt of Strength proved to be impossible, so Asher crouched down instead, his eyes narrowed as he pressed Crescent against Donvath’s exposed throat.

“The fact that this is happening at all means that you’re allowing it to, and that really pisses me off,” Asher admitted, staring hard into the High Prince’s silver eyes. “Not going to rewind time and try for a do over?”

“I think this is more than justified after what I did to you,” Donvath admitted, pressing a finger against his nostril as he tried to clear it of blood. “Also, ow! That hurt! At least my Decaying Touch skill isn’t painful!”

“I’m tempted to leave you with a broken nose for the rest of your life,” Asher admitted, knowing that would be the case so long as he didn’t release Sever the Thread’s hold on Donvath’s wound. “So maybe use that as your motivation to be incredibly thorough in your explanation about why you did what you did.”

“Consider me motivated,” Donvath grinned, glancing down at the gleaming blue blade still held against his throat. “Would you mind?”

Asher held his blade there for a few more seconds before stepping back with a grunt. It was only then that he spotted his bandoleer of daggers and missing artifacts tucked into a neat pile on another chair by the table. Even his void dagger in its abyssal sheath Helpha had forged for him laid there, and he quickly grabbed his missing belongings and suited back up. The only change he made was swapping out his Ring of False Death for the new Ring of Honest Ears Donvath had given him recently.

He was going to make damn sure Donvath was telling the truth.

As Asher got suited up, Donvath got to his feet and brushed himself off before taking a seat at the table. The High Prince looked even more tired than usual, which was really saying something.

“You don’t need a power nap before we get started?” Asher asked, securing his bandoleer across his chest and making sure it sat as always. “I wouldn’t want you to pass out in the middle of your explanation and have to start over again.”

“If I’m going to pass out, it’ll be from blood loss,” Donvath said, rolling his eyes as he pointed at his broken nose that still had blood actively dripping from it. “You’ve already made your point, do you really have to keep your skill active?”

Breaking Donvath’s nose had in fact been very cathartic after the man had killed him, but Asher decided he had a point. Now that he was back, Asher didn’t want to give Donvath any excuse to change his mind and suddenly decide he didn’t owe him that explanation after all. After Asher mentally cancelled his skill, Donvath thanked him before pulling a potion of Lesser Restoration out of one of his pockets and downing the thick liquid. His nose snapped back into place quickly enough, and he let out a sigh of relief as he poked at it.

“It’s been some time since I was hurt like that,” Donvath admitted, sniffing through his nose a few times as if ensuring it still worked. “Can’t say I missed it.”

“Talk,” Asher demanded, taking his own seat across the table from the High Prince as he stared at him. “Why go through all that effort to get me to evolve my skill in a specific way? How were you even able to prevent me from using Complete Resistance to purge your Decaying Touch skill in the first place? And why were you so insistent that killing me was so important? I mean, ‘for the good of the world’? Seems a bit over dramatic, don’t you think?”

“I do tend to have a flair for the theatrical,” Donvath admitted, holding up his hands defensively as Asher placed Crescent down on the table between them in a silent threat. “Sheesh, touchy much? As promised, I’m going to try and answer your questions as best I can. I’ll even answer a handful of questions you haven’t realized you have yet. However, there are still a few restrictions I’m bound by that I can’t do anything about. Things that I can’t tell you because you haven’t hit the Stage of Power yet.”

“I don’t give a damn about your restrictions,” Asher said bluntly. “If you can see the future, you should know the only way I’m going to be happy is if you tell me everything I want to know.”

“In the least annoying way possible, I knew you’d say that,” Donvath said, giving him a crooked grin as he nodded toward the bedroom door. “Which is why I asked for a third party to help mediate this discussion.”

As if waiting for her cue, the bedroom door opened, and Asher’s eyes widened as he took in a warm, grandmotherly face he hadn’t seen in some time.

“Hello again, Asher,” Gran said, the centuries-old healer who predated the Noala Kingdom giving him a warm smile as she closed the door behind her and stepped farther into the room. “I hear you have a few questions.”
