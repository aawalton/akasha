Chapter 124

Thorne and the large Clawborn kept delivering blows to each other. As one advanced, the other would counter. They were at a stalemate.

Valrik watched, but he also turned to watch the gate continuing to close. Several of the allied troops slipped through and were already on the other side, while some of them tried to slow the gate's progress by holding it back. A few of them took arrows from the archers inside and fell to the ground dead.

Finally Valrik sheathed his sword and walked over to the battle. Thorne had just blocked an incoming attack from the Clawborn, twisted his feet in the dirt, and leveraged the opening to step aside and try to deliver the final blow. Before he could complete the swing, Valrik drew his blade and sliced through the Clawborn's head, and it fell to the ground. Dust flew up as it hit. The Clawborn's body dropped to its knees, then slowly tipped over and fell all the way.

Thorne watched the body drop in disbelief. His eyes hardened as they shifted toward Valrik, who stood there with his swing completed and the sword still in his hands. Valrik straightened, ensheathed the sword, and looked at Thorne. Thorne squinted hard, and before he could speak, Valrik said, "We need you. We need to go inside. The gates are closing."

Thorne straightened up, and the two ran for the gates, narrowly slipping between them as they closed all the way.

Once they were through, they assessed the situation. It was the two of them, Thorne and Valrik, and ten other allied troops. Four Clawborn swordsmen drew their swords, and two archers stood behind them with bows drawn.

Five of the allied troops surrounded one of the Clawborn swordsmen, and they all started trading blows. Thorne and Valrik squared off against two of the other swordsmen, and the remaining three allied troops moved in on the final Clawborn swordsman. The Clawborn archers strained, having trouble deciding who to point their arrows at as the melee blurred and turned chaotic.

The five allied troops quickly hacked their Clawborn down dead, then turned their swords to help the three trying to advance on the next Clawborn. But that Clawborn had already impaled one of the three, and in a quick motion pulled its sword free and sliced through the neck of another, leaving one standing, sword up, waiting for the incoming attack. As the five rushed over, they surrounded that Clawborn and took him down, losing only two more of the troops.

Valrik had already tried to stab his Clawborn, but the Clawborn moved quickly and sidestepped the attack, delivering a counter that hit Valrik in the back and knocked him to the ground. Valrik rolled and in one clean motion was back on his feet, raising his sword just in time to defend against the next incoming attack.

The blades crashed together, and the Clawborn and Valrik were almost nose to nose, teeth showing as they ground them together, pushing back and forth.

Thorne had already exchanged two blows with the Clawborn he'd squared off with. That one was much faster than the one he'd fought in the yard, but Thorne matched his speed blow for blow, ducking and rolling, dodging attacks. Finally he landed a strike to the Clawborn's leg, below the knee, and it took damage. The Clawborn was limping now, and Thorne began to circle him.

Across the courtyard, the allied troops started to hack the final Clawborn swordsman to pieces. But as they swung, the Clawborn retaliated and impaled one of the allied swordsmen through the middle. The man dropped to his knees, spit up blood, and fell face forward, dead.

The remaining troops kept hacking at the Clawborn, who finally fell to their blows, cut down by injuries to the head, the arms, and the torso, until a final strike through the neck took him out completely.

Before the remaining five could celebrate, two arrows came in. The first went through the eye of one of the men, who screamed and reached toward his face and fell to the ground, writhing in pain just before he died. The second caught another through the throat. He tried to pull it free, and he did, but the wound spewed blood, splattering one of the swordsmen close by.

As the second man fell, the remaining four turned toward the archers, who were quickly rearming and getting ready to loose another attack. They moved fast, shortening the distance, but the archers managed to loose two more arrows. One clanged off an allied trooper's armor. The other caught a man in the arm, and he dropped his sword and reached for the wound and the blood pouring from it. He fell back, and the three remaining moved forward.

As they got closer, the Clawborn archers couldn't reload, and they drew their own swords to hack and swing. The first archer took a blow to his right arm, which was poorly armored, and the allied sword cut through it, leaving him unable to attack again.

The second Clawborn managed to repel a couple of the incoming attacks from the allied troops. The third he couldn't block, but as the sword came down, it clanged off the Clawborn's armor. The Clawborn raised his sword, smiling, and roared, baring his teeth at the three as they moved in to attack again.

This time one attacked, and the Clawborn repelled it. The second attacked a moment later, and the third came in less than a second after, the sword falling from above. Again the Clawborn's blade was there, repelling the first and second attacks. But this time the third sword caught the Clawborn in the neck. The allied trooper recoiled backward, pulling his blade back toward him, leaving a gaping wound that spewed blood down the Clawborn's front and side.

The Clawborn's free hand instinctively reached up and covered his neck. As he raised his sword to keep defending and improve his position, the first allied trooper attacked again, and the second came closely after. The Clawborn's sword stayed high against the attacks from above, but it left the lower part of his body exposed, and the third soldier lunged in with a stabbing motion for his stomach, below the plated armor where the skin was bare. The sword found the Clawborn's stomach and pierced straight through, front to back, blood pouring from both sides. The trooper twisted the blade and pulled back, leaving an open, gaping wound.

All three allied troops, swords at the ready, took two steps back as the Clawborn dropped to his knees. His sword fell from his hands. He covered the wound in his neck with one hand and the wound in his stomach with the other. Then he fell backward, and moments later he bled out, his eyes still open.

Thorne was still circling his Clawborn. He disengaged and decided to change up his attack, backing up, limping slightly, trying to trick the Clawborn into thinking he was at a disadvantage. The Clawborn took the bait and moved forward. Thorne quickly sidestepped, drove his sword through the Clawborn's stomach, twisted, and pulled it out as he spun around to its backside, readying for the counter.

Thorne was breathing heavily now. He pulled off his helmet and dropped it to the ground as the Clawborn's renewed attack came in. He held his sword above his head to block it and used his left leg to kick the Clawborn in the stomach, driving it back slightly and leaving it off balance. Then Thorne kicked up his right leg, leaping into the air, raising his sword and bringing it down hard on the Clawborn, who couldn't regain his balance in time to defend against the falling blade. The Clawborn's neck took the brunt of the damage and sprayed blood as it cried out in what would be its final moments.

As Thorne landed, he kicked the Clawborn in the face. The Clawborn fell back, dropping to his knees and then onto his back, and Thorne finished him with the tip of his sword, just under the neck. The Clawborn bled out on the ground.

Valrik repelled the last Clawborn sword attack that had come his way and stepped back two steps as the Clawborn advanced. He held his sword in front of him as the Clawborn came in for another assault.

This time the Clawborn swung, and Valrik sidestepped in a twisting motion. The blade missed and hit the ground with a clang. The Clawborn turned his head toward where Valrik now stood at his side, bared his teeth, and roared, lifting his sword for a second attack that Valrik's blade was already waiting to block.

As the Clawborn tried to reposition his stance to continue the battle, Valrik crouched low and swung for the midsection while the Clawborn's sword sailed narrowly over his head. Valrik's sword found the opening in the armor, a small exposed section of stomach and fabric, and landed where he meant it to. The Clawborn's stomach sliced open, and blood poured down the front of his legs as he stumbled back.

Now Valrik stood straight. The Clawborn looked down at where his own hands had met his stomach. He lifted one hand, saw the blood, licked it, and roared, showing his teeth again as he took a two-handed sword position. Then he went in to attack.

Valrik decided once again to sidestep the attack. As he did, Thorne was waiting, and he drove his sword through the Clawborn's open wound, angling up, turned the blade, and pulled it out. Even more blood fell from the wound. The Clawborn tripped and fell face down on the ground.

Thorne stood up and looked at his blade, covered in Clawborn blood, then looked over at Valrik's waiting eyes.

Valrik smirked, but his eyes widened, looking for an explanation. Thorne said, "Well, we have to wrap this battle up. We have things to do."

Valrik's expression turned to disdain as Thorne turned to the remaining allied troops, who were already smiling. A grin crept across Thorne's face.

"Well, this is our day!"

He started to laugh, and the men began to cheer. Through all the celebratory noise, a faint banging was missed, until a voice rang through.

"Hello? Will you let me in?"

Valrik turned suddenly. "The Warden."
