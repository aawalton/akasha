What awaited on the other side of the unstable Gate was not the blighted Ashen Realm Vir had feared, but something far, far worse.

They emerged to a sky that looked burned, as if the gods themselves had scorched it. Darker and more sinister, somehow, than the now-familiar Ash.

Thick, low clouds blotted out the sun, illuminated only by the constant show of a lightning storm, and the thunder was ever-present. The air carried the scent of ozone, dust, and something faintly metallic, even worse than the worst parts of Mahādi.

It was the prana density that gave it away—or rather, the lack of it. Not even the periphery of the Ash had such little prana.

Maiya stopped short, a hand pressed to her heart. “Is this…?”

Vir swallowed hard. “I think it must be.”

Despite the crumbling ruins of buildings, despite the street covered over with soot…

This was not the Ash.

It couldn’t be. For a perfectly circular shimmering wall of light ringed all that remained of the world, and far ahead, half obscured by falling ash, stood what had once been a gleaming white city.

The two-tiered marvel of Imperium construction was now tainted gray with soot.

“The inevitable fate of the realms should we fail in our mission,” Vir murmured.

The last breath of a doomed world.

Even the wolves’ howls sounded forlorn.

For what they saw was not just a city on the very edge of the Boundary, but rather the final bastion of civilization in a universe where the Ash had reclaimed everything else.

In this universe, the Fateweavers had won. Once they crushed Alt Ashani, there would be nothing left.

And worst of all…

“Uh, Vir? Where’s the Vimana?” Maiya asked, frowning at the city’s floating second tier.

For as tragic as this universe was, that was not what made Vir’s mood darken. It was the utter absence of the flying fortress that ought to have hovered above the city.

It was the very Vimana they boarded to begin this journey, and in this universe, it was missing.

But how did one move a Vimana without a genius like Saunak to unlock its secrets? Had humanity come into contact with demons in this universe? Even in that case, Saunak was not so simple to find, what with his tower being deep in the Ash.

There were too many unknowns to even begin to form a conjecture.

“If it’s not here, how in all the Ash are we to get back?” Maiya asked in panic.

“It’s possible they are across the Boundary somewhere,” Vir said, looking at the opaque wall that stretched up to the sky. If the rest of the world had been claimed, then it stood to reason they were out there, floating. “Worst case, we venture there.”

That there weren’t a half-dozen Vimana floating above the city was a good sign—if the Altani did manage to learn how to commandeer those Imperium marvels, they would have been fools not to bring them back before the Ash claimed them.

“Ashani?” Vir asked. “What do you think?”

It was only then, when Ashani failed to respond, that Vir managed to tear his eyes off the terrible spectacle to look at the goddess.

The goddess who, at the moment, stood entirely unmoving, her eyes as blank as glass.

“Ashani!” Vir shouted, rushing to her.

“What’s wrong with her?” Maiya asked frantically. “It’s like she’s…”

“Like her core has been drained, but that’s impossible! I recharged her core not long ago,” Vir said, peering into her.

Only to stop in shock when the sudden reality hit him.

The world had appeared bleak, and it certainly was. But only now did he realize that background color that permeated everything—the air, the ground, and all living things—was suddenly missing.

“Prana Vision,” Vir breathed. “I can no longer use Prana Vision!”

“But that’s impossible!” Maiya cried. “Wait, if that’s true…”

She brought out a precharged C Grade orb and fired into the air, only to sigh in relief when it activated without issue.

“Looks like I panicked prematurely…!?”

Her eyes bulged, and her relief immediately turned into panic.

“You can’t charge it?” Vir guessed.

“No! It’s… not that I’ve suddenly become weaker. It’s like I can’t use magic at all!”

“Janak warned us that the laws of this reality would be different,” Vir said, biting his lip. “I suppose we just found out how different.”

As though corroborating his statement, the Ash Wolves had been howling sadly shrunk closer to Vir, as though fearful of the realm itself.

“But Ashani—”

“Is a construct of Ash prana,” Vir completed. “Prana fuels her very being, from her core to her movement to all of her powers. As much as she resembles us, she is not a being of flesh and blood.

Vir knew that all too well, having seen the snaking metal ropes that formed her muscles and ligaments, back when they’d first met.

“So, what? Ashani’s dead here?”

“More like asleep,” Vir said. “Unless we can find a way to reconfigure her for this realm.”

“Fat chance of that,” Maiya said. “We’ll have to hide her.”

Vir nodded, hefting the goddess over his shoulder. The action took far more effort than it should have, as the vast majority of prana within Vir’s body was now suddenly locked.

“That feeling from earlier. It wasn’t the lack of ambient prana, was it?” Maiya said. “It’s like all of my reserves are just gone.”

“It’s still there,” Vir corrected. “Or, I think it is. We just need to relearn how to use it before it leaks out of our bodies.”

With how much both Vir and Maiya had stored within them, that would take a while. Even still, it was not infinite, and the sooner they untapped the potential within their bodies, the better off they’d be.

That said, if the rules truly were different here, relearning magic improperly could very well backfire, killing them in the process. Anything was possible when the Fateweavers were involved.

“Well, then I suppose we’re in the right place,” Maiya said, thumbing to the city in the distance. “If anyone can teach us the basics, it’s them.”

Vir nodded, noting the irony. If the Altani of this realm were anything like his own, then there wasn’t a single being within those walls who could match their strength or mastery over magic.

And yet, he might as well be a talentless, pranascorned child for all the good his knowledge did here.

“Well, we were planning to keep out of sight, regardless. Seems we'll just have to do this the old-fashioned way,” Maiya said, casting her eyes on the desolate city’s walls. “But you do know what this means, yeah?”

Vir slowly nodded.

“If the Boundary has shrunk this much,” she continued, “then every other city has already fallen. Humans wouldn’t have stood a chance.”

“Nor most demons, I imagine. And that’s if the Ash wasn’t any more powerful than it is in our universe,” Vir replied. “Janak’s recordings said this world was almost consumed, but seeing it like this…”

A sudden thought struck him. One that stopped him cold.

All this time, he had thought the Ash was a unique curse on his universe. A horrible consequence of Janak’s actions there.

But now that he thought about it, the previous universe also had the Ash in it, as did this one. Which meant that this universe’s Janak, or someone equivalent, had made a similar mistake.

“It’s not just ours, is it?” Maiya muttered, coming to the same conclusion.

The thriving Imperium universe didn’t have Janak. They were fine. The conclusion was obvious.

“Every universe that has Janak in it, or anyone close enough to him, ends up like this,” Vir muttered. “How many countless worlds have fallen because he angered them?”

His chest constricted. The weight of it weighed down on him more than the Automaton on his shoulders. More than any worry he’d ever had.

Maiya put a hand on his shoulder, her gentle expression looking even more serene and graceful against the bleak background and the raging thunder. “I know how you must feel, but if Ashani were awake right now, you know she’d tell you that this wasn’t your burden to bear. This is Janak’s mistake, and his alone.”

Vir let out a breath. “Thanks Mai, but whoever is to blame, the question is, if we don’t help them… who will? Can we really turn our backs on these universes?”

“How about we solve our own universe’s issues before thinking of any others, hmm?” Maiya said with a sad smile. “To say nothing of the litany of other issues that plague us. Like, you know, being trapped in another universe without any magic or a means to get back?”

Maiya looked out at the ruined city. “Even if she were awake, Ashani can’t make Gates between universes, and even if she could, they would notice. We’d doom every world we tried to save. And if all that weren’t enough, how do we turn back the Ash when we don’t even know how to do that in ours?”

“I don’t know,” Vir admitted. “But I’m hoping Janak will. At the very least, I’m hoping he’ll have a way for us to close the Ash Tears plaguing the realms.”

If he didn’t, if all of this was for naught… Vir didn’t want to think about it. He couldn’t. How many lives had he condemned simply by embarking on this journey? How many had perished to errant Ash Tears in his and Ashani’s absence?

They continued in silence toward the tall walls of Alt Ashani’s lower city, stopping just out of range of any guards that might be patrolling the ramparts.

“So what’s our plan, then?” Maiya asked. “Ashani’s down and we can't use magic. How are we even supposed to get in? I’m guessing your idea of venturing into the Ash is out too?”

“With how crippled we are? I wouldn’t be surprised if we died on the spot. Besides, if they’re so strong they’ve altered the rules of reality even here, I don’t dare to imagine what it must be like in the Ash. The land reclaimed by them is probably far more lethal than anything we’ve encountered.”

Maiya nodded, cracking her knuckles. “So we sneak in.”

“We sneak in,” Vir echoed. “If there’s an answer to that Vimana’s disappearance, it will likely be in the city. Despite all that is wrong with this universe, it is to our benefit that it is Alt Ashani that remains. If anyone in all the realms knows what happened, it would be the Altani. They must have records or survivors who know where their Vimana went. Such things cannot simply disappear.”

“So we investigate,” Maiya said. “And we relearn how to use magic posthaste.”

Vir nodded.

Now, more than ever before, staring at this pathetic last bastion of civilization, Vir understood with painful clarity.

The prana, the twisted, colorless husk of a world.

This was their warning. The future that awaited their own universe, as well as countless others, should the Ash continue unimpeded.

He’d always known it, of course. Everyone in both realms did. But it had felt so far away. A matter to be dealt with thousands of years in the future, by their distant descendants.

To be shown the end like this, however, made it real in a way nothing else could. That magic had been warped and corrupted somehow made it all the worse. This was not a problem for progeny to solve. By then, it would be far too late.

Vir clenched his fists. If there was any hope at all to be found for this universe, he’d find it.
