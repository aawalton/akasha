Chapter 123

The moon dimmed as the orange light of the burning Clawborn depot grew. The terrible rotten egg smell faded as more of the depot wood fueled the fire. Smoke billowed up into the sky, clouding over the stars that had been visible. The moment was almost frozen in time as Riley's mind raced.

The Clawborn guard held their formation in front of the open gate. The water used to try to control the growing fire dripped from Clawborn shields, armor, and helmets. Behind them, the remaining Clawborn not on the wall or in front of the gate were running around inside, yelling and throwing what water they could bucket up to the walls where the fire was spreading.

Riley shifted the east wall attack to focus on its southernmost end. Taking out guards at that end was critical now. Stopping guards from moving easily from the east wall to the south was the order of the day. Or night, as it were.

She then shifted to the turtle, selecting the cluster of men and having them break formation, instead lining up toe to toe with the superior Clawborn guard, holding their distance. If the Clawborn advanced a step, Riley's soldiers would retreat a step.

Then she selected all the infantry and cavalry already facing the south wall in the tree line. The cavalry saddled up and the infantry readied their long pikes. These were all level 2 with the Riley-certified upgrades from outside the system. They wore heavier armor, carried enhanced weapons, and had special training. Marrying this with Riley's secret sauce, her face hardened and she moved them to the edge of the trees.

"Let's see how the Clawborn's finest measures up."

Riley turned to Thorne. "Remember, once they reach the line, have the cavalry release and circle back a longer loop."

Thorne nodded and half smiled before he mounted his horse, then he unsheathed his sword and raised it. "For the HOSHIN!"

With that, the cavalry rode out of the trees hard, soldiers screaming at the top of their lungs. The horses squealed as they left the tree line at full speed, their hooves sounding of deep, rolling thunder as they galloped toward the south gate. The Clawborn soldiers guarding the front gate took a half step back as they faced the oncoming wrath of Thorne's advance. One of the Clawborn flinched and was quickly drowned out by the others roaring like lions in the face of the new force. Valrik turned his head for a moment and his face softened slightly, seeing the lion's share of the forces flashing forward into the pending battle.

As the cavalry advanced, the infantry emerged from the tree line and started to run toward the south gate. They had much ground to cover as they left their horses behind. Their formation loosened, but that aided them in moving faster with their large pikes held in both hands as they slowed to a steady march. They had made their presence known, and now they were timing their entry into this coming battle.

The cavalry were nearing Valrik's men and slowed their horses while they all reached into their saddlebags. Timed almost perfectly as the cavalry reached Valrik's men, Thorne yelled, "NOW!"

Valrik and his troops dropped down into a crouch, and Thorne's troops revealed what they were holding in the saddlebags and threw them at the waiting Clawborn. The small clay pots flew through the air at the Clawborn, puzzled about what was happening. One Clawborn's head turned slightly sideways like a dog trying to make sense of a robot chicken. The archers held as they too were puzzled with the attack.

The level four Clawborn soldiers raised their shields as the clay pots fell from their arcs in the sky and smashed against the waiting Clawborn shields. A white powdery cloud formed around the entire Clawborn line. It looked like someone had thrown flour at the soldiers.

Thorne's troops turned their horses and started to ride back a distance before stopping and turning back facing the south wall. Valrik's troops came back to their feet, raising their shields and stepping back as the cloud swelled outward. The wind shifted and some of the powder hit a few of Valrik's troops. Then the screaming started.

The powder met Clawborn wet faces and skin and started to react. The white powder had settled and steam started to rise off of them like they had just stepped out of a hot shower. Clawborn and a few of Valrik's troops started to scream and claw at their eyes. As they rubbed violently at their eyes, it just made the problem worse. The victims clawed at their skin, trying desperately to rub the residue off, to no avail.

Valrik turned and moved toward the few of his soldiers that had fallen to the ground in agony and stood over them, jaw set, sweat rolling off his brow. He slowly drew his sword and held it in an inverted grip with the tip pointed toward the ground. The men continued to scream, pleading and clawing at their faces. Valrik pointed his sword at the first casualty and winced as he drove the blade into the soldier's throat, closing his eyes until it hit the ground. He pulled out his blade and turned toward the others, ending each one's miserable desperation with his cold blade.

The Clawborn broke formation and the scene became dizzy and chaotic. Thorne returned with the riders and stopped just short of Valrik's soldiers. Thorne turned and signaled to the treeline. Several flaming arrows were volleyed to a short distance from Thorne, and several of the cavalry dismounted with wick-topped clay pots in hand. Thorne and the others quickly grabbed the still-flaming arrows from the ground and lit the wicks, and in one clean motion threw the clay pots toward the higher and midpoints of the south wall and open depot gate. As the clay pots hit the wall and ground, there was a bang with a large orange flame the height of a man or so, gone in a second.

Some of the Clawborn yelled, "Magic!" Others: "Witchcraft!"

Then the dark grey, almost black smoke started to billow out and low. It was thicker and sootier than the wood-burning smoke, and also heavier. This accelerated the chaos to a new level. Now the archers were climbing down from the wall, trying to avoid the orange flashes that had hit some of them, burning skin where exposed. As they climbed down, they started to cough horribly as they breathed in the toxic smoke that smelled like rotten eggs. Some of them fell off the ladders and hit the ground, unable to get up.

The Clawborn soldiers still on their feet who could partially see backed up or just ran away from the flashes, which ruled out any possibility of them organizing an attack. The others who had fallen to the ground were now choking on the fumes from the smoke. The yelling and screaming only intensified.

Thorne looked to Valrik as the infantry joined them. Valrik gave a nod, and they turned and yelled, "Attack!"

The soldiers moved forward, cutting down the standing compromised Clawborn and ending the agony of the ones rolling around on the ground. Swords met flesh and cut deep into blistering skin, quieting the horrific scene one voice at a time.

Riley, now standing just inside the tree line, opened the HUD and selected the troops in the tree line facing the now empty east wall, redirecting them to just behind her troops at the south wall. The remaining soldiers around Riley were directed toward the depot, where the fighting was starting to pick up momentum. Once the soldiers moved past Riley, she waited a moment and started walking toward the battle.

Now the remaining Clawborn soldiers inside the depot were trying to close the gate as the fighting continued just outside. The gate started to move, but much more slowly, because there were far fewer soldiers to aid the cause. The fires in the three burning guard towers had completely engulfed the structures and would fully light the horror below.

The five remaining level four soldiers who had dodged the earlier assaults were surrounded by Riley's men and were cutting them down at a feverish pace. Riley watched closely as wave after wave of her soldiers tried to cut them down, doing only minimal damage.

Thorne had drawn one of the Clawborn out and was standing toe to toe with him. One of the Clawborn's arms was blistered and burned, and he was favoring his other arm to compensate. The Clawborn raised his sword and roared at Thorne, who raised his blade and lunged forward yelling a bloodcurdling battle cry. Their swords met with a crashing ring and a flash of sparks. The Clawborn managed to push back Thorne's attack. Thorne pushed off the ground, advancing with another. Again, the two swords rang out a battle cry, meeting in another stalemate, neither swordsman advancing nor giving ground. This time the Clawborn pushed off Thorne and took a half step back, pointing his sword at his opponent and advancing forward, trying to drive the sword tip into Thorne's chest. Thorne turned and let the advance pass, and he shoved the Clawborn in the back with his free hand. The Clawborn turned and raised his sword to again meet the incoming blade from Thorne. All the while, behind the two, the gate's two sides inched closer together.
