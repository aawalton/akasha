It took Cass a bit to figure out what had happened. It turned out that the map didn’t show a person everything, no matter how detailed it had become since its upgrade to Command Center.

Each building in the local area was designed to be transformed, as was the wall between the 5th, 4th, and 3rd rings. As they rolled back, the feat of engineering was designed to create an arena-like bowl with a large central area.

All of this took Pellin moments to figure out. For Cassio Vale…well. He was still questioning things.

“See!” Pellin said, pointing at a part of the wall that easily showed it was designed to shift and move. “There’s a locking and unlocking mechanism built into it.”

“Come on! You can see it right there!”

Even Profile agreed with him.

Traitor.

“No!”

“Maggie is a tall, thin woman with glasses. Mmm, and she has brown hair in a tight bun. I think she was wearing a gray dress the last time I saw her. Noah has shaggy blond hair touching his shoulders, and you’ll probably find him leering at some nearby women. He’s also very foul-mouthed.”

“Easy.” Then, in a feat of grace that choked Cass up, she leaped upon several ledges, moving up them in a stair-stepping manner. Somebody called out a complaint, but gave up when she reached the top. The group busied themselves while their Scout worked, talking about what the trip to Liberty was like.

“It was so scary, Cass,” Pellin said with a shake of his head. “Everywhere you looked, monsters.”

“That’s weird because I don’t think we saw any on our trip.”

“Because, as the others tell it, you had a ride in luxury,” Orla complained. “We were stuck riding horses. And I still haven’t showered!”

“I’d hardly call kidnapping a luxury,” Cass replied with a dark look. “Plus, it took me far too long to figure out you were all alive.” Seeing Gary wince, he put a hand on his shoulder, “Sorry, Buddy.”

“Yeah, he’s got a ton of oddball sayings like that,” Cass said with a wistful smile. Then, as Gary looked at a nearby stand selling some kind of meat on a stick, he snuck up quietly. Oh, so very quietly.

The footsteps of a cat. A Bengal tiger! I am silence incarnate. Nobody can hear me coming.

Adya leaped down, “You’re in a much better mood than you were when we got here.”

“Having you and my friends here will do that,” Cass said with a smile. Bella nudged him with her snout, “And of course, my noble steed, Bella the Magnificent.”

She whuffed and stomped the ground.

“True.”

Everyone in the group gave him a strange look before Adya led them to meet Noah and Maggie. How the Scout had located them, abilities or not, was up for debate as the four followed in her footsteps, horses in tow.

The crowd wasn’t too happy that several large animals were brought into their midst. However, anytime someone tried to complain, the tall form of Bella’s shadow would alight on their person. It was hard to act angry when a massive draft-horse stared you down.

God, I missed this. So very much. But I’m not gonna cry. I’m not…

Cass’s innermost monologue relievingly ended when a loud voice shouted over the crowd. He’d probably never have heard them without his enhanced hearing. A part of him wished he hadn’t heard them at all.

“Look at those melons! Yah neva seen such big ones.”

“Stop being stupid, Noah.”

“I’m tellin yah. They’re huge!”

A break in the crowd allowed Adya to surge forward, four humans and horses following quickly in her wake. The scene they came upon gave him a moment’s pause.

Noah and Maggie stood in front of a wide-reaching stall of food. Just before them stood several platforms of large, obviously ripened green melons close to bursting. On the platforms next to them were a collection of grapes, with more assorted fruit on the other side. Noah had a big grin on his face as he hefted two melons, one in each hand.

“So ripe and full. It’s all I can do to simply hold them up.”

“You’re such a child, Noah. When we get back, I’m telling Ren about this.”

“Pfah, he’s on a date with that servant woman. Probably won’t even see him till tomorrow.”

“Oh, hello, Cass. Who are your friends?”

Noah dropped both melons on the ground. He quickly reached to his side and pulled the metallic object always present into his hand, pointing the tip of it at Adya, “It’s the pickle stabber!”

“What?” The Scout asked, then Cass figured Profile must’ve updated for her because the next thing he knew, her spear was in her hand, pointed back the man’s way. “You kidnapped my Partner!”

“Yah tried to stab me pickle!”

“You’re so far over your daily limit that I’m about to start subtracting from the future,” Maggie warned. Smiling, she stepped over and pushed his weapon down while speaking to the group. ‘I’m Maggie, and this idiot is my Guard, Noah. It’s lovely to meet you all.”

Orla, recognizing that she was the true speaker of the Lioran group, stepped forward. Reaching out a hand, she took Maggie’s in her own, “Great to meet you, Maggie. Now, as for the cost associated with any kidnapping, Cass and I will be expecting restitution.”

“Of course. Your kidnapping of him has caused a major collapse in the gloriously powerful enterprise, Common Solutions. We followed a three-prong design, of which your kidnapping has not only taken away one, being Cass, but also the primary, being me. That means, you owe me a large amount of money.”

“Really…” Maggie said with a raised eyebrow.

“I never joke about debt and money,” Orla said so seriously that even Cass believed this wasn’t a ploy for Crests. “Our charter, of which Liora, a member of the Central Guild, has backed, states that any loss of income caused by the Guild must be paid by the Guild. As a de facto representative of those from the Guild who have caused us financial harm, that means you must pay it back.”

“How much we talkin?’ Noah asked as he put away his forgotten weapon.

Orla smartly turned to him, “At the time of the kidnapping, Common Solutions was making 100 Crests a day in profit.”

The Liora branch of Common Solutions is still trying to recover as I speak, with our single remaining prong, Rina, doing her very best to hold everything together. Your actions have almost cost Liora the collapse of one of its brightest and most forward-thinking enterprises since the Reshaping.”

If we assume that rebuilding Liora will cost a significant amount of resources, and that Common Solutions is the premier provider of basic goods used in virtually every construction project, then you’re in a bit of a…” She looked at Noah and spoke real slow, “pickle.”

Gary leaned over towards Cass, “Why do all the weirdos and perverts like Orla so much?”

“Common Solutions charter, rule #17: Should a Founding member of the enterprise find themselves undeniably restrained and kept from their duties, it is required that all haste and resources be spent in their retrieval.”

“Hello everyone, and welcome to this month’s Lottery!”

Everyone in the crowd sat down where they were standing, all at once. It caught the group so off guard that they looked incredibly out of place. Grabbing the halters, they moved the horses to the side and out of the thoroughfare as Orla continued to strenuously argue with Maggie.

“At least 150 Crests per day, that’s a bare minimum unless you want me to move this up to the Capital.”

As they approached the man at the microphone, they carried between them a brass-colored tube with a cranked handle on the end and two platform legs beneath it. A groan went out of the crowd, and Cass clearly heard the melon vendor beside him say, “Of course it’s the Greaves family. They only own half of the construction businesses in Liberty.” He spit on the ground beside him, then looked listlessly down.

“Most of the time, it’s 25. But sometimes, it’s a lot more. I’m not too sure why.”

Beneath Cass’s feet, he felt just the slightest tremor. Looking over at Adya, she nodded to confirm she’d felt it too.

That must be the exercises Alan was talking about.

The vibrations from below are way stronger now. That’s what they do. Give out the upgrades to their favorites first, then, when it’s time to give some to the common people, push the exercises to their limit since nobody will notice them.

Sneaky shits.

Cass would never say he was the most intuitive man around when it came to understanding women. That’s why it was such a grand gift from the System that he could feel Adya’s emotions so very clearly.

And I know we’ve missed seeing each other for a while.”

“Yeah!” He said, the spikey feeling she’d just started to send disappearing into the blaze of a sun. “A real one. Just you and me-”

“And probably Bella.”

He rubbed the side of Bella’s jaw, right where she liked it, promised he’d see them both soon, and then the group was gone after some quick farewells.

Later, in the back of the cart with Maggie, she looked over at him, “Is it nice to have your friends with you again?”

“Far, far more than you know, Maggie.”
