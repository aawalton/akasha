“Menu! Status! Map!” I tried. Thinking it really hard: Menu!! STAT! SYSTEM Activate!!!

Nothing…

It’s so fucking cold. Am I supposed to go into the houses? I sure want to be in one.

I walk up to the first house, ready to knock, while preparing some half-baked story about how I got lost… No answer.

Next house—there’s a light on the second floor, and I swear I can hear human voices. Still no one answers. Damn it! I know you're in there!

I could knock harder, maybe shout. But… no. Probably not a great idea to seem hostile.

Wait… was that music?

Yes! Music meant… something. I follow the sound and soon I find the best damn thing I could hope for: a real, honest-to-god medieval tavern.

I walk inside, and my isekai theory gets confirmed right in front of me: Three dwarves drinking ale at a table, laughing and shouting.An elven bard in the corner, singing and playing some weird guitar-thingy.

More people, some elves sitting at another table.

And the barmaid was pretty— with an impressively ample chest.

My mood does a full 180.

I’m a freaking adventurer in some Skyrim-isekai shit.

This is my territory. I’ve played my fair share of RPGs, and I’m not half bad at them.

First, talk to the barmaid, get some basic info about the world, maybe catch wind of the main conflict. Evil wizard? Dragon? Bring it on.

I’ll pick up some quests—start small, level up, grind weak mobs until I’m a few levels above quest expectations in order to play it safe. Very safe. I’m assuming death here means actual real-life forever death, at least until proven otherwise.

But I can’t plan anything without knowing the first thing about this place. That means talking to the nice barmaid comes first. She’s behind the counter now, wiping it down with a cloth.

I walk up to her, which sounds easy, but it's really not.

Some details about myself:I’m sixteen. Extremely virgin. And large breasts tend to have an effect on my mental state.

If this were a normal RPG, I’d have Confusion or Stun as a status effect.

This is hard for me.

“Hello, nice to meet you…” I stammer. “I’m an adventurer… I come from… somewhere else.”

“Hello, young adventurer,” she says with a wide smile. “I’m Hildi. Something in mind to drink?”

I was thirsty.

But shit! I had no money!

Would Hildi want payment upfront? In Skyrim, if you steal even a wooden spoon by accident, the shopkeeper will try to kill you. Better be careful.

“I’m sorry, I have no money,” I say, immediately catching myself staring at her cleavage.

“That’s fine,” she says. “I can get you water for free. We got some quest posters on the wall over there.”

She gestures. “Quick money for the brave.”

“Water’s good. Thank you.”

At least I wasn’t hungry.

“Can you tell me a little about this… country?” I ask.

That gets her talking. Big time.

She tells me about the kingdom—Aldioli, in the southeast part of the continent Athersu. They’ve been at war for years with a neighboring kingdom, Lithuron, because of some prince who fell in love with the niece of a baron whose peasants were executed by a queen who... yeah, whatever. It goes on and on.

About ten long, boring minutes went by as Hildi talked, and while most of it flew right over my head, I managed to catch the important parts. The kingdom I was in, Aldioli, was in an ongoing dispute with its northern neighbor, Lithuron. Nothing major, just years of half-assed skirmishes that never really changed anything. To the west, there was a rise in bandit activity, and in the capital, Aldiolin (with an “n”), there were reports of growing rebel unrest. Conveniently, our current town, Kamatouu, was close enough to get caught up in that mess. Interestingly, all the conflict seemed to be human-only. No elves or dwarves were involved. No dragons, no cursed kings, no dark lords bent on annihilation. At least, not yet.

“Thanks, Hildi. I really appreciate you telling me all this…”

“…”

She says nothing. Just goes back to wiping the counter.

Wait a second. Hildi was an NPC, right?

She kind of acts like one. A bit robotic, speech slightly monotone. But she did understand what I said about money, and she answered when I asked about the country.

I need to test this.

NPC Test #1: Repetition.I will ask the question multiple times and see how she responds. Worst-case scenario, I will look stupid and apologize.

“How much for a glass of ale?” I ask.

“That’ll be 12 Sigots.”

I wait.

“How much for a glass of ale?”

Same answer: “That’ll be 12 Sigots.” But her tone… maybe a little less patient?

One more time.

“How much for a glass of ale?”

“That’ll be 12 Sigots,” she says curtly, then turns away and goes back to cleaning.

Okay, that was definitely annoyance. She’s not a basic NPC.

But maybe not a real person either? People don’t react like this, at least not unless they’re seriously autistic.

NPC Test #2: Weird Questions.

“Did you see the new GTA6 trailer?”

“I don’t understand what you mean.” She goes back to cleaning.

“Hola, ¿cómo estás?”

“I don’t understand what you mean,” she says again, not even looking at me this time.

So... her responses were textbook NPC. But her mood shifts. That’s really weird. I didn’t want to push too hard, but Hildi was beautiful, and by now my curiosity got the better of me. There was one last experiment I had to try.

NPC Test #3: Touch.

I move closer, slow and unsure, until I’m right in front of her.The counter’s barely half a meter wide; I lean in, heart racing, forcing myself to breathe.

Yeah, I know—total creep move. Way out of character. Touching a girl? Definitely not my norm. But I need to know how these “NPCs” feel… and the dwarves nearby have axes the size of tree trunks.

I reach out, hesitate, then gently touch her arm.

She doesn’t react. Not a glance, not a flinch—just keeps wiping.But I feel her: soft skin, muscles shifting beneath. Unmistakably human.

And yet… she behaves like I barely exist. Like she’s coded only to serve drinks and answer simple questions.

Next experiment: can I move her?

I take her hand, first pulling gently, then harder, until I’m actually leading her a step.

Her face changes—anger flashes across her features—but she still says nothing. The contrast is almost comical.

Sorry, I think. I’ll stop bugging you soon. I just need to know if I can actually walk you out of the tavern. Moving NPCs around town might come in handy.

I try to guide her through the side gate of the counter, ignoring her scowl—

She jerks back.

“How dare you attack me?!” she shouts.

My blood turns to ice.

I mumble something incoherent.

Hildi draws a long knife and shifts into what can only be described as a combat stance.

“Prepare yourself, criminal!”

No. No no no. This can’t be happening.

“I… no… I didn’t—” I cry. “I thought… I thought… it was a mistake—”

“You’ll pay for this.”

She opens the side counter door and starts walking toward me, blade in hand.

“I thought… I…” I stammer, backing away. “This is a game… and you’re an NPC…”

“Prepare yourself, criminal!” she repeats.

Some rational part of my brain realizes she is still an NPC and that I triggered an attack state. But I’m full-on panicking.

I backpedal, trip over a chair, and fall.

“I’m sorry! I’m sorry!” Tears well in my eyes. “Please don’t hurt me!”

But Hildi doesn’t stop. She’s over me now, knife ready.

“You will pay for this.”

Let’s pause and reflect for a moment on the state of our hero:

A brave adventurer, summoned from another world, no doubt to save the realm from evil.

But instead of defending townsfolk from monsters or bandits, he harassed the barmaid…

And now, he’s about to be stabbed to death.

Because he’s a weak little idiot.
