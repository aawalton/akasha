The underbelly of New Camelot is dark, hot and wet, reeking of rust and sulfur. Giant, intertangled pipes delve deep into lower levels hundreds of feet below me, occasional deadly puffs of steam escaping their weak couplings. Precious strips of orange, polluted sunlight sifts through the grates of the city above, giving the chambers a dusty amber glow. I wince in pain as I look at my right foot. Yep, definitely broken.

The clashing sounds of metal on metal and screams of battle above have become distant, only the occasional BOOM heard in the distance. I glance up towards the shaft I had fallen through from Lancelot’s Draconiton, Arondight, but all I can see is smoke and muted sunlight with the occasional dark shadow flying over in a blur.

It’s amazing that I’m even fucking alive. He’ll be worried about me.

With a groan, I roll over and assess what other injuries I might have. Shallow breathing and sharp pain in my lower left side tell me a possible broken rib. Fantastic.

How many bones have I broken at this point? I wonder, morbidly amused at how high that number must be now compared to the average mortal. I laugh darkly under my breath. Also, it’s a good way to distract myself from the pain. I’ve gotten pretty good at that these days.

Sixty..? No….it’s got to be higher at this point..

I use the railings of the haphazard bridge I’ve fallen on to hoist myself up onto one leg, grunting and swearing in several different languages in the process.

“Bliet! Gnaght! Arrrgh….Serða!” Huh…haven’t used that last one in a while. A memory of a certain 6 and a half foot viking jarl with piercing blue eyes smiles at me, eliciting an accompanying stab in my cold, dark heart, and I smile back sadly.

“Ek mun aldri gleyma þér,” I whisper to the amber dark, and to the ghosts of my past. I will never forget you.

Hopping along, I make my way down the bridge before I come to a section that’s begun to rust away from many years of neglect. The railing itself is twisted and ripped away, as if something large fell through this area and took a chunk from the bridge with it. The sound of rushing water can be heard below, and I carefully peek over the edge, only to be greeted with a sulfuric mist from the waterfall created by a ruptured pipe some 50 yards below.

Well, we have a problem now.

The problem is a four-foot gap within the floor of the bridge itself, save for a small border on the right side that looks pretty rusted. So on one side, we have no railing, and a gap of four feet, and on the other, a small rusted border with a railing that looks like it’s just about ready to disintegrate at the touch. And to cross this on an injured foot, no less.

I shrug, and choose the rusted side, hobbling my way over. Let’s face it, we know I’ve endured much worse than a several-hundred-foot fall.

Whatever force that has been moving my bones these past few thousand years spurs me on, and I grunt my way across the haphazard section. Chips of rust are scraped free by my boots shuffling along, and I wince with every step of my right despite barely loading any weight on it. Halfway through, a loud metallic creak sounds beneath me and I freeze.

A few painful moments pass by, with the only sound the rushing water beneath me and the pounding of my heart. Yeah, the adrenaline never really goes away. Because at the end of the day, this shit will still fucking hurt. I may be no stranger to pain, but it doesn’t mean I seek it out or look forward to it.

After nothing happens, I start shuffling again, slower this time. I find myself holding my breath as if that will somehow magically make me lighter. It doesn’t.

Crrrrreeeeaaaaak!

Then suddenly the rusted section and I both drop, the bridge and railing bending at a 90-degree angle at a point further up. I manage to loop my arms around the section I was clinging to, avoiding the drop of a couple hundred feet, and my shoulder is almost jerked out of its socket in the process.

“Umph!” I grasp for better purchase, but my feet dangle into the mist of the falls as it drifts up, drenching my leathers. I peer up towards the bend, trying to gauge how much time I have before it breaks completely and the section and I plummet further into the underbelly. Should I try to climb it? Can I make it with this injury?

And then, the section breaks.

Well, this was a short one, I joke darkly, as I tumble down, down down into the misty abyss. Ice cold needles stab every inch of my body as I hit the water and the breath is knocked out of me. I can’t tell which is up or down as I roll through the current, limbs scrambling. My legs feel so heavy. At least I wasn’t wearing one of those ridiculous dresses. I’d drown for sure with all those layers of skirts and lobster-tail like bustles. And don’t get me started on the hats.

Miraculously, the rapids force me to the surface and I sputter for air, but not long until I’m thrown back under. This process repeats itself as I make my way down this freezing river, in darkness. Days pass, and the current finally slows enough for me to stay above the surface for more than a moment. I drift into a giant chamber, ceilings over a hundred feet high. Sunlight drifts in amber rays through portholes in the city road above, illuminating a mountain of rusted scrap metal in front of me.

As the current takes me to shore, I crawl on my hands and knees until I can stumble to my left leg and make my way out of the water. I collapse on a flat sheet of metal, exhausted, cold and wet, yet somehow, I am still alive. A sharp sting on my arm alerts to me to a slice there that I hadn’t noticed before. It extends about six inches from my elbow towards my wrist.

Rude.

I turn my head back towards the ceiling, right as a mechanical shaft opens, and more scrap tumbles towards the top of the mountain. The new pieces disturb the peak of the mountain, causing others to roll their way down towards the shore. I’m too tired to move. I probably should though. Death by avalanche of rusty scrap metal doesn’t sound pleasant.

But something else stirs me from my sheet metal bed. A mix between a toaster and a lamp making its way down the mountain of scrap, and in its journey, knocks a television/blender out of its original place, revealing the glimpse of sleek, untarnished steel. That catches my eye. It’s towards the bottom, maybe ten feet up from where I lie. So it’s been here a long time.

I push myself up on my elbows and squint at it. It’s hard to say, but I think I can make out some etching on the metal. It’s...a set of runes? Maybe.

With a growl and some more expletives, I push myself up and climb closer. My foot is now EXTREMELY unhappy and is letting me know about it, but now I’m curious and when I’m curious well…we know what happens at this point. I play stupid games and sometimes win stupid prizes.

A rusted hovership part crumbles under my grip and I slide back down the mountain a couple feet.

“Gnaght Kriplin!!” I screech in another world’s language, but I don’t stop. All I can see is that glimmering piece of metal untouched by time and rust. Something… is calling me towards it. A tug from my soul. I have to get to it.

I scramble up the rest of the way like a mad woman, forgetting my pain and tossing scrap metal behind me as I go. A pair of broken goggle spectacles which probably would have fetched at least 5 guineas when they were in their prime, another weird toaster lamp thing, I still don’t know what those are but I saw one in Lancelot’s office. This one was in better shape, still some of the decorative brass piping intact, at least before I tossed it behind me with the rest.

At last, I’m at the steel peeking out at me, beckoning for me to reveal what lies beneath this rubbish. So I do, piece by piece, and what comes together before my eyes is a masterpiece.

The etching I saw are in fact runes, carved in an angular fashion around a metallic eyelid that spans about a foot across. It is shut in silent slumber, but the metal…when I touch it, I can almost swear I feel a slight vibration. Steel scales radiate from that eye, runes painstakingly etched into each one. Soon, I reveal an identical eye, and then, a snout that is longer than my whole body.

I can’t believe that I have found a Draconiton down here, of all places, buried in this scrap. It’s the first time I have seen one inactive.

Lancelot told me about the Draconitons that lost their souls, that they never wake again and their metal becomes part of the metal wasteland of the underbelly, lost to the world. But with that hum I feel, within my bones…I was brought here for a reason.

I’m not a member of Arthur’s round table. It’s forbidden for me to even be this close to one of these things without permission. I’d be incinerated by now if it was Sir Agravaine’s Draconiton, Grimspire. That guy’s such a dick, and his dragon is even worse. Also, I know nothing about the bonding process, that information is especially withheld from newcomers, and certainly someone like me that stumbles into this world out of nowhere in strange clothing no less. It’s ok, I’m used to it. Not my first rodeo.

Another rude sting on my arm interrupts my thoughts.

“Fuck! That hurts!” I glance down. “Oh.” That’s why.

Somehow I missed a leech that had latched itself to my forearm, on the other side of my cut. I grip its slimy, slippery body at the top with my pointer finger and thumb and pull. It makes a slurping noise as I struggle to remove its sucker from my skin.

“Arrgh! Fucking hell.” But I manage to get it off, and throw it towards the river. I check for more, and unfortunately find four of them, on my stomach, back and right leg. Several unpleasant moments later, I’m breathing heavily, significantly more pissed off than how this day started, but I’m free of the devilish things.

I look back towards the Draconiton, still in its slumber. Before realizing what I’m doing, I reach towards the snout, my fingers itching to feel that cool, sleek metal and that otherworldly hum. I’m like a moth to a flame, my eyes wide like saucers on a child in wonder at a candy shop. This has it’s a trap! written all over it, but I can’t help myself.

What could go wrong? Everything.

Once my fingertips brush the metal this time though, the hum intensifies and I feel it travel up my arm in a jolt, but I don’t pull away. It feels wrong to, somehow. So I hold steady, letting the hum crawl its way through my veins until it reaches my heart, where it gives a sudden pound that has me doubling over, clutching it. Once again, and my vision goes blurry, eyes watering. Then, a final time, and my vision darkens completely.

Ah…there you are. I was beginning to wonder whether you would keep me waiting another century or two.

His voice rings in my mind like a bell, deep and resonant. Everything is still dark, but in the back of my mind I see an old forge coming to life. Coals awaken beneath dark ash. Embers skip and dance among the flames amongst the ancient stone hearth.

“Uhhh…hello?” I speak aloud.

I can hear your inner workings perfectly fine, human.

“Oh, right.” Shit.

I mean, hello. I probably shouldn’t be here…doing this. I’m not a knight of the round table. Fuck, the realization of what I have just done suddenly hits me and my heart thunders in response.

You are exactly where you need to be, Beatrix.

The darkness finally fades and my vision returns. Whirring sounds bring my attention back to him, as the many overlapping shutters of his eyes open, and the metallic rings within click and rotate for the first time after centuries of slumber. An amber glow ignites behind the ancient glass lenses, as they shift forward and back, the aperture narrowing as they focus on me.

How do you know my name?

You have awakened me with your lifeblood. I know all of you, Wanderer.

I start at the mention of my kind, retracting my hand, and that is when I notice it. A small smear of my blood, likely from the removal of the leeches, is spread on his snout.

Well, shit.

Fecal matter has nothing to do with it. Humans...Such odd expressions.

I literally facepalm. Well this is going well.

I know of your suffering. Your loss. Your endless journey. We have that in common, human.

A rush of images floods my mind. So. Much. War. A darkness that blots out the suns. Screaming citizens of the citadel running in fear. An abandoned child crying amongst a sea of bodies. A flash of sunlight on metal scales as another Draconiton falls from the sky, landing with a devastating impact that takes out an entire temple. It does not move again and its once amber glowing lenses have gone dark. A man in armor with kind, sad eyes, reaches out with a bloodied hand before it falls, and stills. An earth-shattering roar of agony echoes through the valley where he's fallen before the memory fades.

Loss. Yes, that I know too well.

I shudder, clenching my fists. The memories threaten to surface, to flood and drown me, but I push them back down. I wait a few moments before I find that steady, cool calm that I've learned to master in the last thousand years.

What do I call you my new friend?

I am Aurelian. The First Forged.

My eyes widen at that. First forged. But that must mean...he could know where the Draconitons come from?

Unfortunately, human, that history has been lost even to our kind. Our only purpose is to serve the Realm, and the King. All I know is that I was the first.

Aurelian's massive head rises, as he looks to the chamber's ceiling, as if he can see up to the sky where Arthur fights above us in battle.

My last rider, Sir Aldren Vale, was helping me discover our forgotten origins, before he was killed.

The man in his memory, that must have been Sir Aldren.

I'm so sorry.

Worry not human. As you already know, we cannot let the past hold us captive. We must forge ahead.

Yes, we must.

With the protest of my foot, broken left rib and overall exhaustion, I pull myself up to a stand. I will help continue what Sir Aldren started, Aurelian. We will discover the truth behind your origin. This, I vow.

This time Aurelian rises to his forelegs, causing the mountain of scrap to tumble all around us in a cascade of rusting metal, splashing into the river. I do my best to dodge them as they come flying at me, but Aurelian at least bats away a hovercraft from crushing me like a cat pawing a toy. It flies to the other side of the chamber in a heap of crushed metal and my eyes widen at the power.

Damn.

At his full height, he stands about 5 stories tall. The rays of amber sun glint off his rune-carved scales. He extends his long neck, steel horns twisting towards the sky, as his shoulder shifts, and he lifts his folded metal wings away from his flanks. With a deep mechanical groan, his wing joints extend and long, jointed spars unfurl like fingers, drawing his enormous wings taut. Overlapping plates slide into place along the leading edges of the wings, settling with heavy clunks and revealing more detailed, swirling runes on his wing panels, creating a wingspan of about 120 feet. Then, he flaps them once with frightening speed and agility considering their size, and the resulting gust of wind nearly knocks me off my feet. Centuries of settled dust and discarded scrap go flying.

He's magnificent.

By my eternal soul and flame within, I bind my vow to yours human. I will be your wings, your shield and your fire. I will fight beside you, tooth and claw, and stand with you in the darkness.

I return his vow with a feral grin.

Great. Now let's stir up some trouble. There's a battle above we're overdue to crash.
