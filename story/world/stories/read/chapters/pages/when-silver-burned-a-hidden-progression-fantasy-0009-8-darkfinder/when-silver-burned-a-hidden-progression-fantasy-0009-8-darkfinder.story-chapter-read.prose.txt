When first hearing the term 'underworld', people might be inclined to think of dark tidings. Mind altering, will-bending substances. Knives under the sleeve. Shady figures dealing in shady goods in shady alleyways. They'd be right to a small extent, but only if you consider the bottom of the barrel as the whole and nothing else besides.

The underworld was a catch-all term for the lower part of society, from the Clenn nails and Scarred dirts, roots and rots, up to abysses and hives. They had the numbers, and most of them had class and enough wealth to get by. Proper citizens, farmers, artisans, and entertainers all paying—or pretending to pay—their taxes to fuel the lifeblood of the Republic as the overworld cared for, governed and protected them. You could expect fair treatment as an underworlder even from the Arches themselves. At least, if you were Clenn.

That being said, today Kalsin delved headfirst into the bottom of the barrel.

He started around inns with reputation for trouble, nursing lonely ales as people attended their much needed drinks and brawls, leaving behind bloody teeth and sour gossip. Innkeepers knew much, so he tried winning their favor by refraining from acting like an obnoxious jackass, which wasn't particularly difficult in the given company. He brought up talk of smuggle for people who did not want to be found. He got turned heads and quick retreats.

Next came brothels, though it took Kalsin some effort to keep his lunch in his stomach with men and women trying to drape their scar-ridden bodies onto one another. It would've been a decent place to learn things had he been swimming in coins. But after all that he had spent, his purse was running light. And he still needed to pay for the darkfinder.

A fruitless afternoon past, as torches were being lit along the roads, Kalsin returned to their inn to find Jack tapping her foot impatiently. She made a grasping motion, face impassive but eyes playing the traitor with their spark. She found what they were looking for. And not a moment too soon.

The coming Blindlight was nearly upon them, marking the end of the month and remaining for a duration of one, two, maybe three days. Barring an éj-lantern and adequate knowledge, travelling under Blindlight was foolish at best and suicidal at worst, and so darkfinders took it upon themselves to herd the fools where they needed to go. Their business wasn't illegal, per say, but it definitely raised questions why one would pay for the service. Reasonable, everyday folk would simply delay their travels and wait until Warmlight returned.

No. If one hired a 'finder, they did so because they wanted the eye-stinging radiance to cover their trails. They wished to be gone, and to be gone quickly and unseen.

Jack had instructed to pack everything on Sap and Hollow as she led them to the outermost edge of a dense residential area, an unassuming brickwork home blending in with the rest, down to the tended flower pots on the windowsill. The mahogany entrance had a miniature wooden sign nailed on, proclaiming the owner was one 'Bellé Treyant'. Jack made the grasping motion again, now at the sign. Found it. Or perhaps it was her way of saying 'darkfinder'. Their communication was an ever-evolving learning process.

Kalsin didn't ask how she found the darkfinder. Either she had used their services in the past, or junkies spilled information better than drunks. At the end of the day, it made no difference.

Kalsin banged on the door for a good minute before it opened. A woman, wrinkles mapping her dark skin like thin fractures, squinted at them with her one visible eye. The other was covered by an opalescent eyepatch, texture scale-like. "Whatchwant?" she asked. A barb-wired whip was a gentle instrument in comparison to her voice.

Kalsin went to speak, but Jack preceded him with a rapid flurry of hand signs that he couldn't hope to follow if he had a dozen decades. The woman's squint of suspicion shifted into a squint of consideration as she followed along, clearly more versed in the language than he was.

When Jack finished, the woman looked at Kalsin. "And you?"

Kalsin said, "Yes."

The house from the inside had a militant feel with the sparse furniture only covering the essentials and aligned perfectly with the carpet they stood upon. Devoid from color, nothing but the woman's bright eyepatch and long vibrant fingernails borrowed life to the scenery. After brief introductions, the woman—one Darkfinder Treyant—stayed silent as she led them into an office, though Kalsin caught her glance back and down occasionally, as if sizing up their hands. He had the sudden urge to hide them in his pockets.

She ignored her seat, stopping with a rigid posture behind her desk instead. Kalsin found himself looking for her scar, and realized she didn't have it on display. He frowned, instincts kicking in.

"Candy," she proclaimed without a look at the mentioned bowl on her desk.

Kalsin wasn't partial to sweets, but Jack shrugged, palming half the bowl into her pocket with one move. Something to keep her leaf craving in check, he figured.

"Fee?" Treyant croaked. "You got no money, you get no deal. Not open to charity cases."

As Kalsin counted out the slightly outrageous, though expected amount of payment, he was acutely aware of Treyant's rapt attention hanging on not the coins, but the tip of his fingers. She scooped up the small pile with a that's-that sort of private nod.

"When—"

"My assistant is leading your bahten to the square as we speak," Treyant spoke over him. "You will stay the night. One, because I don't trust you to rat me out and two, because last-minute arrivals get no leeway in say-so. You will obey me under Blindlight, and you will start practicing now. Those éj-lanterns at your belts, do you know how to use them?"

"We get by under the light," Kalsin drawled with a carefully measured tone. As hunters, they had to learn to navigate under Blindlight. Which meant they mostly avoided getting hopelessly lost.

"Not well enough, or you'd not be weighing my purse."

Treyant rifled through a drawer until she got her rainbow-clawed hands on a key. She lobbed it over to Jack. "Room's upstairs, first door you see, shitter on the right. We leave for the square while it's dark and wait for the rest of my clients until Blindlight rises. You sleep overtime, you lag behind, you're done."

A few hours, a quick nap, and a brief test of the shitter later, Kalsin and Jack were tailing the darkfinder toward the central part of the city. Torches were burning low in the pre-morning air as darkness ruled the empty streets. A creeping cold heralded the coming day, and so they had donned warm clothes to prepare.

Treyant wasn't strong on words, but when asked, she gave a rough explanation about the route she'd planned. With the city's heart as a starting point, anyone hunting their trails would have a hard time predicting the direction they'd take, let alone their destination. Once outside the walls, the darkfinder’s primary goal would be one thing; put as much distance between them and the city as possible while Blindlight lasts. The length of which, of course, being a toss of the dice.

In that time, Kalsin had three objectives: discover the identity of the heretics, convince them about his goodwill, and safely part from the group before the lawmen ambushed them. All accomplished with a looming chance of a spy—who likely sent the collector the note—hiding in their midst.

The main square of Remallon stretched out in a long expanse of smooth shellstone, its uninterrupted flow halted only by Midlane, where the dark outline of the wall that separated the two districts seemed small and subdued, as if cowed by the vast openness before it. The square descended into shadow at its center, touched by fading lights that shrank to pinpricks—framing the edges and converging at the far end like frozen fireflies.

They waited under the northmost cluster of torches. Kalsin couldn't see anyone beside their group. It was the same in every city around this time. By now, citizens had their shutters drawn, curtains sewn and reinforced with twice the fabric. The homeless had retreated to find, dig, or kick empty the nearest secluded holes. Guard patrols had been postponed as they huddled in their barracks. Even stray cats knew better than to stay outside as they sought refuge in drainage pipes, abandoned crates, hollow floorboards in a terrace.

Boots echoed on stone, drawing closer.

The first arrival came alone, draped in a cloak with not much to cover, his scrawny figure not doing any favors to the scar that was left breathing on his skeletal collarbone. When he approached, Treyant reached in to draw back his hood, gave that private nod, then resumed ignoring them all. It must've been the assistant, because he had Sap and Hollow behind him. The man handed over their mounts without a word and withdrew.

The cold soon invaded Kalsin's ears, but the fur-lining at his neck stopped it from seeping deeper. He let Sap munch on his hair, the bahten utterly unimpressed by the dropping temperature. Jack had her hands stuffed under her arms, cloak draped over bulky armor plates as Hollow's reins dangled free just an inch from her face. The warlander mount stared attentively into the dark, until his scaled ears went flat and he let out a neigh.

Next came a younger couple with nerves in their gait, keeping close to one another. The man's bald patch gleamed in the flickering orange light and cast a shine to his restless, oaken eyes. The woman, holding strong to her companion, exchanged a glance and what Kalsin could've almost described as a smile with Treyant. It was hard to tell with the grim darkfinder.

Like Treyant, the pair considered themselves worthy enough to cover their scars. Kalsin had frustration circle him not unlike a buzzing insect. He shifted away when Sap's tongue attempted to soak his already frosty ear.

Over the next hour, seven more people joined the procession, and none of them came alone. An old man with, Kalsin figured, son and daughter supporting him. Two rough looking women, their sun-baked faces dotted by battle scars and each leading a horse. And lastly, two figures wearing large silk coats similar to priest robes, their hulking cowls resembling the followers of Vadász, god of mystery, deception, and the necessary unknown.

Each group stood apart, uneasy, in their respective circles as Treyant unhooked her éj-lantern, fiddling with the glass. Kalsin smoothed a hand through his mane, trying to get the disrupted field of would-be-bahten-grass in order. There was a crunch, startlingly loud in the night, as Jack started chewing on candy that was probably not meant for such toothy consumption. She grinned at the scarred women.

While some people were good at breaking the ice, Jack rammed through glacial seas with the subtlety of an iron hulled fleet.

"Between all of us, we've three éj-lanterns," Treyant said without preamble. She jerked her head in Kalsin and Jack's direction. "Those two will separate, form a line behind me, and the rest sticks to a carrier like moths to light. Hmph. Bad analogy." She scratched her jaw, and Kalsin only now noticed that where her fingernails had been the color of rainbows yesterday, she'd exchanged them for short but oddly thick, azure blue claws.

Kalsin ended up with the family tagging alongside him, which suited his purpose just fine. His most tangible lead was the fact that these azTél figures were siblings—if Quin was to be believed. Feeling in control during the torture session, it was unlikely the collector had been deceiving him with his questions, but the tiny chance couldn’t be locked out.

Another variable was the heretics themselves. If discovered, their true identities thrown in their face, how would they react? Outright denial. Resorting to violence. Flight soon as opportunity presents itself. All were possibilities that Kalsin wanted to avoid. He'd have to tap around with caution first, give away as little as possible and see where it takes him.

Jack already had a back-and-forth going with the warriors and they puzzled over her meaning, then cracked smiles she got it across. Ever silent, Jack always appeared to be saying a hundred things as she shifted her stance, the way she grimaced, using body language to tell tales more colorful than any word could. One simply couldn't help but feel like she'd known them for a lifetime. The lone man, the assistant, stood nearby but didn't socialize.

As for the rest, the couple and the cowled priests remained alongside Treyant. The darkfinder was completely still as she kept attentively facing east.

And so they waited.

A thin, white line began to open up and expand toward the horizon. Casting a halo around the backdrop of the city, the light grew by jagged increments in no resemblance whatsoever to Warmlight's slow, steady advance. Its rays were not orange, not yellow, but absolutely and consummately white as it relentlessly washed over the lands, stealing the sky's blue, the roof tiles' rustic, the green of brushes. Colors drained to gray, brightening until the uniform white made them one with itself, bit by bit shrinking their field of vision like a choking glove descending around the throat.

In the year 785, marking the transition from Kis of Summer to Köz of Summer, Blindlight rose above The Ember Republic.
