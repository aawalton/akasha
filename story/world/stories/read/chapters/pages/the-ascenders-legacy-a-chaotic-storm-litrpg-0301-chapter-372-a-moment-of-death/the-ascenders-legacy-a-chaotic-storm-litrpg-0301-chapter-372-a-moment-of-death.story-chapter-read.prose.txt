The number of Calamities present on any battlefield was a delicate balance—one meticulously kept equal to prevent catastrophes such as the Icon War.

The Calamities were not there to fight. Not truly. Any battle between them would render it impossible for anyone else, even Mythics to fight. No, they were there to keep their enemies in check, and prevent the already devastating war from becoming worse.

But the keyword to that balance was delicate. And Zerathis Starless had just broken it.

“Oh, for ascendant’s sake!” High Elder Mystral cursed as she watched the transmigrant’s brain splatter across the battlefield. “Why does this man keep doing this? Is he foolish?”

“Evil would be more accurate,” Zarxan gritted, anger rising within him as silence descended over the battlefield. “This is the second time Zerathis has broken a rule. First the Icon War, and now this. It is almost as if it is—”

“Intentional,” High Elder Tharn finished, glaring at the screen. “This is no mistake. He is sabotaging us. They are—”

The rest of his words were cut off as sound suddenly returned to the battlefield, the sky itself shattering as Calamities descended with fury in their eyes, their intentions unmistakable.

“Go, go!” the First Seat shouted to the Calamities gathered within the hall, his own eyes widening as the screen shifted to show the transmigrants—pale and terrified.

Frozen utterly in shock.

The hall emptied immediately as Calamities rushed toward the battlefield, distorting space, distance, and reality to get there as soon as possible.

If there had ever been a time to oversaturate a battlefield with Calamities, it was now.

But even Calamities could not cross such vast distances in an instant.

No matter how fast they moved, the Calamities already on the battlefield would reach the transmigrants first.

“By the storm,” Velora cursed. “They’re doomed.”

“No.” The First Seat shook his head. “They are not. They only need to hold out for a few seconds.” He swallowed. “They can hold out for a few seconds.”

But even he didn’t believe his own words.

In a battle between lower-tier cultivators, very little happened in seconds. But Calamities were not lower-tier cultivators. They were beings so immensely powerful they could wipe out entire armies in less than a second.

No advanced-class cultivator could hold out a second against one, let alone dozens.

And yet, he dared to hope that his transmigrants were different. That they were powerful enough to survive at least a second.

It was that hope that burned within him when Kaelthorn suddenly asked,

“Where is Zarxan?”

They all turned to the sixth throne, eyes widening when they found it empty.

High Elder Zarxan was gone.

***

Zarxan Al’ruhn had moved even before the First Seat commanded the Calamities to.

With panic unbefitting of any Calamity, he tore through reality itself to appear above the battlefield in an instant, energy erupting from his core as he searched the skies for one person and one alone.

Aodhán Ashoka-Brystion.

As important as the other transmigrants were, Aodhán was most important to him. And he had not saved the boy from Lutia only to let him die here. He had not brought him this far to lose him now.

But after a full second of searching the battlefield, and finding no trace of Aodhán’s unique energy signature, Zarxan realized he had arrived too late.

Aodhán was no longer on the battlefield.

Nymera, however, was.

And despite the clawing instinct to abandon everything in search of the boy his master had given his legacy, Zarxan could not leave one transmigrant behind to chase another.

Gritting his teeth, he stepped through space to stand before the enormous tornado Nymera had conjured, a snarl tugging at his lips as he faced the approaching Calamities, a storm of pure electromagnetic energy billowing around him.

“I advise you to rethink this.” he said quietly, his power rising.

They did not. But Zarxan had never truly expected them to.

Weaving his fingers, he pulled on the governing energies of the world around him—electromagnetism, radiation, light, resonance—and weaponized them, drawing them together into a single point no larger than a seed.

A singularity born for ruin.

And then he unleashed it—a single line of impossible brightness that cut across the heavens in one instant, carving a rift through existence itself, and peeling reality along its path like cloth beneath a blade.

The entire battlefield shook and light vanished as darkness swallowed everything.

But Zarxan had only just begun.

He would carve through every Calamity on this field if he had to.

He would find Aodhán. And nothing alive would hinder him.

***

On the earth below, Mythics ran for their lives, teleporting away from the battlefield as quickly as they could to escape the catastrophe about to unfold.

With so many Calamities rushing in from both sides, a clash—inevitable as it was—would rip the battlefield apart, destroying it even more thoroughly than the Icon War had destroyed the last.

Eager to contain the effects of Zerathis’s foolish mistake, Lordstrom Veylmont created portals to take Mythics far, far away from the battlefield in an instant.

But only so many could escape before the clash began.

The world shook an instant later—space, time, and reality itself cracking as multiple Calamities clashed at once, ripping the very framework of existence apart with their power.

The world went white.

Colors bled.

The earth split.

And through it all, none of the transmigrants had been saved.

***

“How could this happen?” Aspwick despaired as he watched the screen. “How…” His gaze drifted to Zerathis, and he snarled. “That foolish Calamity. He has doomed us all.”

“I wish I could kill him,” Sang-Hoon growled, deep hatred in his eyes.

Only a moment ago, he had been celebrating the victory of his transmigrant. And now, the boy was dead, his brain splattered across the broken earth like discarded pulp.

But the truth was that they had all been doomed from the beginning.

Lysandra had died.

Ariana had died.

Seraphine had been captured.

And had Zerathis not acted when he did, Alaric would have died too.

His actions may have doomed them all, but at least this way, the Storm Sect would taste death as well.

It was a loss for all of them. But if things worked out right, it could be a greater loss for the Storm Sect.

Sighing, Elder Mournwind resigned himself to a bleak fate. If balance wasn’t restored after this, then it was over. He had no more fight left in him.

And judging by the looks of the other elders, neither did they.

This was their last hope for balance.

Even if it ruined them all.

***

Meanwhile, within the cold expanse of the chaotic void, space screamed—not with sound, but with sensation. With pressure. With the nauseating feeling of existence being stretched too thin and snapping somewhere just beyond perception.

Aodhán reeled within it, his entire world spiraling as Master Gyatso dragged him through the void, racing violently against the hand of fate itself.

The void churned and twisted around them—distance, time, light, and direction all losing meaning as they hurtled forward. There was no up or down. No forward or backward.

Shards of shattered space drifted through the darkness like jagged glass, whipping past so fast Aodhán barely registered them. Rivers of distorted light flowed sideways through the void, entire stretches of direction bending inward only to split apart without warning.

One misstep could mean dismemberment.

One hesitation could mean death.

And yet Master Gyatso pulled him through it all with terrifying precision, weaving through the chaos of broken realities, fractured distances, and churning space at a speed Aodhán could neither understand nor follow.

A twist of the old monk’s free hand folded a screaming rift shut before it could touch them.

Another gesture swept aside a rain of spatial fragments so effortlessly Aodhán could not help but think Gyatso had done this a thousand times before.

With every stride, miles vanished beneath their feet as he warped the distance ahead and crushed it flat.

The jagged fabric of chaotic space was not merely being manipulated.

It was obeying.

Subservient.

It was mastery laid bare—a skill far beyond what was ordinary for spatial cultivators. But for all his skill and precision, one truth remained.

No one could outrun a Calamity. Not even Gyatso.

And a second later, the Calamities caught up to them.

There were eight of them, each bearing down with murderous intent.

Aodhán’s heart sank as he desperately searched his mind for some skill he could use to fight back. Some technique.

But there was none.

Gyatso, however, wasn’t quite ready to give up yet.

“Eyes forward.” he snapped as he yanked them sideways, teleporting hundreds of miles in an instant.

But even that was futile.

The eight Calamities were waiting for them the moment they arrived, their presence so immense that the void itself recoiled beneath the weight of it.

“That is enough defiance from you, old man,” one of the Calamities snarled. And before Aodhán could blink, a spear of blood punched straight through Master Gyatso’s sternum.

Aodhán’s world cracked. His heart stopped.

And for one impossible instant, he simply stared. Then horror crashed into him.

“No!”

He lunged forward, hands reaching desperately toward Master Gyatso’s chest as though he could put it back together if he moved fast enough.

“No! No!”

His fingers shook. Blood coated them. Too much blood.

“Aodhán,” Gyatso sputtered, his eyes wide in terror. His grip tight. “Aodhán…”

“I’m here,” Aodhán cried, clutching both of the old monk’s hands. “I’m here. Stay with me. Please stay with me. I just... I just got you back.”

“I’m sorry,” Gyatso whispered, tears pooling in the corners of his eyes. “I couldn’t protect you.”

Aodhán shook his head violently. “No, Master. Please. Please stay.”

His voice broke.

“You just found me.”

Gyatso smiled faintly—a tired smile. A father’s smile.

“I always knew you were special.”

“No!” Aodhán’s grip tightened. “You’re my mother’s legacy. You’re the last tie I have to her. Master, please—”

But Gyatso was already gone.

The man who had taken him as a son for as long as he could remember. The man who had refused to rest even when the other monks gave up searching for him.

The man who would have done anything just to protect him.

Gone.

Aodhán stared at the lifeless body in disbelief.

Then his hands fell limp.

And something within him shattered.

The grief, fear, and desperation he had held at bay since the Calamities descended came crashing through him all at once, leaving nothing behind to hold the rising power back.

And in the wake of that collapse, the power that had stirred unnoticed until that moment finally rose to the surface.

He looked up, the third dream flashing through his mind as eight Calamities stared down with murder in their eyes.

He was going to die here. There was no escaping it anymore.

But if that was the case, Aodhán refused to go down without a fight, without an attempt at vengeance no matter how futile the effort.

He wielded the authority of the storm.

He was a general—a commander fighting a losing battle. But he couldn’t retreat.

Only charge.

And with all the authority he had, Aodhán reached for the storm of power rising within him and screamed, summoning a gigantic storm into chaotic space.

But in that grief-stricken moment, it wasn’t a rainstorm that answered.

It was a storm born from his mother’s bloodline. A storm that rose and fell like the tides of the sea, flowing ever onward, waiting for no man in its pursuit of the future.

It was a storm that encapsulated all existence within itself.

A storm of time.

And as its power surged through him like a beast violently awakened, the dream of the Origin Breach flashed through his mind.

“Brace yourself,” a distant voice cried out. “Another bolt is coming. We need to close that breach.”

But Aodhán did not need to be told. He knew the devastation the coming strike would wreak upon the Raventhorn if it landed.

Because he had relived this nightmare a hundred times before, and it always ended the same way.

Fortunately, he wasn’t alone this time. Daruk was with him, his voice a beacon of calm amidst the chaos.

“It’s just a storm, Aodhán. It doesn’t matter what kind of storm it is. It’ll listen to you. You just have to speak to it.”

Aodhán whispered, “It’s never listened before.”

“No, it never has,” Daruk replied with a smile. “But then you had no authority. Now you do.”

The memory shattered in that instant, ending upon those exact words, and Aodhán lifted his gaze to the roiling storm above.

He had authority now.

Without hesitation, he raised his hands and pushed.

The storm resisted at once, desperate to surge ever onward, but Aodhán refused to let go. He pushed and pushed and pushed until he was utterly and completely spent, reversing the flow of time within his domain.

Energy and willpower bled out of him like a river. Chaos burned through his spirit, and the quintessence of every seal he possessed sputtered and burned away as he spent everything he had to buy himself—

One second.

Aodhán despaired as the Calamities above him laughed.

“Is that all?” a Glass Calamity taunted, looking down at him with a snarl. “I was most certain a transmigrant of your caliber could do more than that.”

“I care little for whatever powers you possess,” another Calamity said, this one dressed in the colors of the Solar Sect. “You killed Ariana Solis. She was our most powerful transmigrant. And for that, you die.”

Aodhán collapsed, too weak to hold himself upright any longer.

This was the end.

The Solar Calamity stretched a hand toward him, radiance swelling brighter and brighter.

Aodhán closed his eyes as death came for him—slowly, surely.

His life flashed before his eyes. And an instant later, darkness swallowed him—a cold embrace of oblivion and eternal rest.

There was no pain. No scream.

And for one eternal instant, Aodhán Ashoka-Brystion was dead.
