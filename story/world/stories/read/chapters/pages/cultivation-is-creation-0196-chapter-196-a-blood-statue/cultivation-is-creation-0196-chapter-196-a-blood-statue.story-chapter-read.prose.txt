The star's power forced itself through my meridians and every part of my body was screaming in protest at the energy that had no business being inside someone at my cultivation level moving through my spiritual pathways like it owned them.

It was like trying to hold a supernova inside a paper cup.

The power exploded outward and became a miniature sun that appeared above my head, and unlike Ke Jun's blood red imitation of a celestial body, this one was pure stellar fire, white hot and furious at having been contained for as long as it had, and Ke Jun's blood red eyes went wide as he felt what the energy actually was.

"That is impossible." It was the first time I had heard genuine shock in his voice. "Stellar Realm energy inside a Qi Condensation cultivator?"

He tried to pull back, the blood red lines forming complex defensive patterns in the air around him as his form started dissolving into mist, but it was already far too late for any of that to matter.

The star above my head released a beam of pure stellar energy that went through Ke Jun's blood domain as if the domain wasn’t there at all, and when it hit him, the result was something that would have been spectacular to watch if I had been in any condition to appreciate it properly.

His spectral form solidified against his will, forced back into a physical shape by the sheer overwhelming pressure of a higher realm's power, and then his semi-transparent skin began to smoke, bubble and then burn. The stellar energy consumed him one molecule at a time, turning his essence into fuel for its cosmic fire.

"No!" There was real fear in his voice now, the first time I had heard that from him either. "This wasn’t how it was meant to end, I should have…"

The stellar light got brighter, and his words disappeared into the torrent of purifying flame, his body broke apart and was consumed. Where before his remains had dissolved into mist that would reform itself, his body was now reduced to scattered drops of blood that fell onto the platform in a pattern that for some reason reminded me of stars spread across a night sky.

When the light finally faded, that was all that remained of a former Civilization Realm cultivator, a few drops of blood slowly spreading across cracked stone.

The star above my head flickered twice and then went out, the sudden absence of its power left me feeling hollow. My legs stopped working. I went down to my knees on the blood soaked stone with every breath feeling like I was inhaling something sharp and broken, my meridians burning, my bones aching deep in the marrow from what the stellar energy had done moving through a body that wasn’t built to carry it.

"That," I managed between breaths, "could have gone better."

"Master," Azure warned, "your vital signs are deteriorating rapidly, the strain of channeling stellar energy has damaged multiple meridians and spiritual pathways, and your physical essence is dropping to dangerous levels."

He wasn’t wrong about any of that, I could feel my consciousness beginning to pull away from me, the edges of my vision darkening, the sounds around me getting muffled and distant. It seemed like I wasn’t going to be conscious for very much longer when something unexpected happened in my inner world.

The Genesis Seed stirred.

It had been quiet through the battle, barely noticeable against the overwhelming presence of the stellar energy, but now it was awake and it was hungry. A deep hunger that reached outward toward the blood essence that still saturated the platform and the air around me. Constructs of pure qi emerged from my body and began absorbing the lingering traces of Ke Jun's power.

I could feel the blood essence being broken down, refined, and transformed by whatever process the Genesis Seed used for these things. Power that had been twisted by centuries of unnatural existence being stripped down to its most basic elements and then rebuilt into something new, and my body began to change along with it.

My meridians were the first thing, the damage from the stellar energy healing and then reforming into something stronger and more expanded than they had been before. My physical essence, which had been at a dangerous low, surged upward from 45 to 48 and then to 53 and finally settled at around 55, which was the level of a Stage 8 Qi Condensation cultivator.

The changes weren’t only internal because I could feel my facial features shifting slightly, becoming more refined and noble, which was probably a result of absorbing so much of Ke Jun's essence. I was likely beginning to look somewhat like him, which wasn’t a thought I was particularly comfortable sitting with.

"Master," Azure said, and he sounded fascinated, "you should look at your inner world, there appears to be an unexpected development."

I looked inward past the red and blue suns to where the Genesis Seed pulsed with its usual power. Sharing that space now was something that hadn’t been there before, a statue made of what appeared to be solidified blood essence, depicting Ke Jun sitting cross legged in meditation with an expression on his face that was more peaceful than anything I had seen on him while he was actually present and threatening to steal my body. But the statue was incomplete, portions of it still unformed as if whoever was making it had run out of material before finishing.

"It appears the Genesis Seed has begun some integration process with the remnants of Ke Jun's power," Azure said.

"Ha! I knew taking you onto my team was the right decision!" Liu Chang's voice came booming across the platform and I looked up to find him climbing slowly back to his feet, his stone covered body cracked and damaged, but his expression undimmed by any of it.

"Senior Brother Liu," I said, managing a weak smile, "I’m glad you’re still alive."

"It’ll take more than a ghost from the ancient past to keep me down," he said, though I noticed the wince when he moved. "But I’ll admit that was closer than I prefer things to get."

Around us the other survivors were beginning to move again, Yan Li pulling himself upright using a broken pillar with his robes torn and bloodied but his bearing somehow still carrying the quality of someone used to being in command. Shen Xuanyu and Zhang Wei were helping each other stand while looking pale and emptied out but alive, and Su Yue was sitting on the platform looking like she had decided that sitting was as far as she needed to get for the moment.

"We lost too many people," Liu Chang said, and his voice had gone quiet and heavy as he looked around at what was left of the battlefield. "Good cultivators and brave disciples who deserved better than this."

Nobody said anything for a moment.

Bing Lan's face came to my mind, her quiet competence, the precision of her sword techniques, how she had kept fighting until the very end, and the other disciples who had simply ceased to exist in the middle of the battle, their remains absorbed into Ke Jun's techniques without anything left behind.

"They died as warriors," Yan Li whispered, "defending against a threat that could have spread far beyond these mountains if it had been left alone, their sacrifices will be remembered."

I noticed Liu Chang's expression go somewhere distant when Yan Li said that, and it reminded me of something I had been curious about since the middle of the battle itself.

"Senior Brother Liu," I said carefully, "those ghost techniques you used were unlike anything I have seen before, what were they exactly?"

He was quiet for long enough that I thought he might not answer at all. "The ghosts were my clan members," he said finally. "My family. The Imprisonment Scripture is a way of preserving those who have fallen and allowing them to continue fighting alongside the people who are still alive." He stopped there, and his tone made it clear that this was all he intended to share about it at this moment and I wasn’t going to push him on it.

Everyone had their own burdens they carried around with them.

"Speaking of unusual techniques," Yan Li said, turning his sharp eyes toward me despite how exhausted he looked, "the demonic energy, and that star above your head..." He didn’t finish the question but he didn’t need to.

"Everyone needs techniques they hold in reserve, Senior Brother Yan," I shrugged.

He looked at me for a long moment and then nodded. "Yeah, they do," he said, and a slight smile appeared on his face. "If you ever find yourself in the capital come and find me, I would be glad to show you around."

From most people that would have been a casual thing to say, but from a member of one of the empire's most powerful clans, it was more than that, and I wasn’t going to be foolish enough to dismiss it.

"I’ll keep that in mind," I said, and meant it.

Yan Ziheng and the other surviving Yan clan member made their way over to us looking somewhat awkward about the whole thing.

"We apologize for our limited contribution to the battle," Yan Ziheng said, bowing slightly. "We should have…"

"Junior Brother Yan Ziheng," I said, "you’re both Stage 7 cultivators and nobody could expect you to participate in something like this, the fact that you both stayed in your positions and didn’t run says a great deal about your characters."

"Senior Brother," Yan Ziheng pointed out with a small smile, "you’re also at Stage 7, and you only broke through to Stage 7 during the battle itself."

I laughed and didn’t have a good answer for that.

The truth was that I tended to think in terms of actual combat ability rather than cultivation stage rankings and sometimes forgot that by any normal measure, the numbers didn’t add up to what I had just been doing, but he had a valid point and I wasn’t going to argue with it.

"Well," I said, looking around at the destroyed shrine, "since we’re here and we technically won, does anyone want to look for anything worth taking?"

The looks I received in response covered a wide range of reactions, but I was already moving toward the remains of the stone coffin so I didn’t spend much time examining them.

I found an ancient jade figurine and put it in my ring, then something that looked like a meditation mat and put that in as well, and then a spirit herb growing between two cracked stones that definitely wasn’t going to survive in the state the shrine was now in, so I helped it into my ring too.

"Are you really looting the shrine?" Su Yue asked, with a tone that couldn’t quite decide whether it was more amused or more offended.

"Resource acquisition," I said, not looking up from a piece of jade I was examining. "And he did try to steal my body, so I think that makes everything here a reasonable claim."

"He has a point," Liu Chang said, but I noticed he made no move to join me.

We spent about an hour going through what remained of the shrine but found very little of real value, either Ke Jun hadn’t accumulated much material possessions, the battle had destroyed most of it, or the good items had been gone from here long before any of us arrived. But what I did find looked like it would be useful enough for developing my inner world, so I wasn’t going to complain about it.

Liu Chang eventually called for everyone. "We need to get this report back to the sect," he said. "A Civilization Realm cultivator using forbidden techniques to preserve his consciousness and attempt a return to life is not something the elders should hear about slowly."

I took one last look at the shrine before we left.

The structure was a total loss, most of it reduced to rubble and scarred stone between Ke Jun's blood domain and what my stellar energy had done to the place, the platform was cracked through in multiple places with the remaining pillars barely holding position.

Walking back toward the village, I became aware that the other disciples kept looking at me and then looking away when I noticed, and I understood what they were thinking about because it was the same list of things I would have been thinking about if I had been watching someone else do what I had just done.

The star above my head, the changes in how I looked, the demonic quality of the energy I had been putting out, they were all suspicious, and they could wonder about it for as long as they wanted. Everyone had their secrets, as Yan Li had said, and I had more than most people did, and I intended to keep it that way for as long as I possibly could.
