Usually, when Asher used Recall to teleport around the Noala Kingdom, he simply popped into existence beside his Eternal Marks like a ghost. The skill was silent, near-instant, and quite a bit jarring, based on the sheer number of times he’d caused Rosh to scream at his sudden appearance at least.

Using Recall to travel from one realm to another, on the other hand, was a bit more noticeable.

“Asher?!” Samantha shouted, jumping back as he suddenly appeared directly before her and immediately collapsed to the ground. The swirling sphere of energy that had protected him from his brief stint in the void realm rapidly collapsed, dispersing in all directions at once and nearly knocking Samantha off her feet from the unexpected push. “Are you okay? Your eyes!”

“Just popped a blood vessel from straining a skill,” he managed, taking a shuddering breath as he gripped the normal, non-changing grass beneath his fingers. Never before had he realized how much he’d taken regular-old grass for granted. Knowing that it wasn’t going to transform into jagged rocks or termites with a taste for human flesh the moment he looked away was quite the relief. Rubbing at the pain in both his eyes, he grinned up at Samantha. “...Possibly two.”

“What happened?” she asked, dropping to a crouch and taking his face in her hands, frowning as she looked him over and immediately began conjuring up some healing water. “What was that weird ball of force that blasted off of you?”

“Great question,” he said, sighing as Soothing Spring got to work repairing his damaged eyes. Once they were fixed, he took a look at the waiting notification. He was a little surprised by the lack of notification informing him he’d achieved the realm wanderer class in truth, but he figured that probably had something to do with his state of mind. Reaching the wild realm hadn’t been something he’d done willingly, and returning to the material realm had been out of desperation more than an excitement for venturing between realms. He could only imagine that he’d earn the class the next time he decided to risk traveling through the void and across realms of his own accord.

Still, even if he hadn’t earned the realm wanderer class just yet, his Recall had benefited from the brief expedition just as much as his Permanence had.

Recall (active) - Lvl 24

Tier 1: Travel between marked locations at will

Tier 2: Journey across the realms themselves

Shards: 12,920,732

Though also just like with Permanence, being in the wild realm had had a strange effect on his skill evolution. Not only had it evolved, taking the shards he’d willingly offered without bothering with a notification, it seemed Recalling from one realm to another had caused a massive jump in his skill level.

Yet despite his little solo adventure resulting in two of his last three skills still in the first tier evolving, and giving them a significant boost to their levels, that didn’t mean Richard was in the clear.

“I’ll explain everything in just a bit, but I need to go have a talk with Richard,” Asher said, thanking her as Samantha helped him to his feet. “Long story short, our friendly wizard threw me into the wild realm without warning, and then left me there. It was only by evolving Recall that I managed to get back here.”

“I’m sorry, you were in another realm?!” Samantha asked, her jaw hanging open as she stared at him. “...What?!”

“Like I said, I’ll explain everything,” he chuckled, giving her a quick kiss before his gaze hardened. “But Richard first.”

“You better go on your own. If you bring me with you, I might just shoot him before he can explain himself,” Samantha growled, her fingers twitching toward her quiver as if she was already planning on it.

Deciding that was probably for the best, Asher left Samantha to her archery practice as he Recalled back to their room in the palace. Part of him was impressed that she was still going, as she’d already been at it for most of the day. Though he supposed that her work ethic most likely explained why she was so good with the bow in the first place.

To Asher’s surprise, just as he was about to storm over to Richard’s room and tear the man a new one, possibly with a very real dagger, there was a flash of violet light and the wizard popped into existence directly next to him.

“Oh thank the realms!” Richard cried, actually falling to the ground at Asher’s feet and wrapping his arms around Asher’s waist, squeezing him like he’d just discovered his long lost grandson. “You’re back! I was so worried!”

“Uh…” Asher started, having been completely caught off guard by Richard’s reaction. He’d honestly expected to find the wizard sleeping somewhere, or in the middle of a magical experiment, having completely forgotten about him and his quest for an egg. Witnessing such relief from the usually snobby and uncaring man was unexpected enough that his initial anger had been doused somewhat.

Though only to a point.

“Where the hell were you?!” Asher snapped, deciding to at least hear the man out before he threatened to kill him like he’d been planning for the past few hours. “I clicked your stupid artifact at least a hundred times, you never showed! And how could you possibly think throwing me into an entirely different realm without warning was okay?!”

“I’m sorry! I’m so sorry!” Richard said, and Asher recoiled as he realized the man was actually crying into his shirt. Richard sniffed as he looked up at him, and only then did Asher notice the state the man was in. Having worked as a mortician's apprentice for the past few years, Asher had seen more than his fair share of corpses even before coming to this world and learning how to fight.

Currently, Richard put most of those bodies to shame.

The wizard didn't just look utterly exhausted, his face was gaunt and his skin as pale as a ghost. His entire body was shivering violently, as though he was experiencing early symptoms of hypothermia, and there was dried blood decorating his ears that looked as though it had only just been dabbed up. Adding onto all that the wizard's advanced age, and Asher feared Richard would collapse at any moment.

“Here, take a seat,” he said, helping Richard over to one of their chairs and offering him some tissues. Nodding his thanks, Richard collected himself for a moment, taking a few deep breaths. His body was still shivering quite a lot, so Asher snagged one of Samantha’s many homemade blankets, wrapping it around the wizard before sitting across from him.

“Are you okay?” he started. The longer he looked at him, the more Asher thought Richard looked like he had more than just one foot in the grave. It was almost to the point where he was debating offering him one of their potions of Restoration. But before he could float the idea, Richard waved off his concern, giving him a sheepish grin as he wiped at his eyes.

“I’m fine. It’s just been some time since I last suffered from tritessence debt.”

“‘Tritessence debt?” Asher asked, the term completely new to him. “Is that the official term for running out of energy?” He’d run out of energy before, and while it wasn’t a pleasant experience, he certainly hadn’t looked like Richard did now.

“No, it is a skill that wizards pick up during our decades of training,” Richard explained, blowing his nose again and wrapping the blanket tighter around himself as he shook. “When we run out of tritessence, rather than waiting for it to regenerate, we are able to functionally borrow more from our soul. It is not a technique used often, as it is extremely dangerous, and quite lethal if we borrow too much. I won’t be able to use any magic for the next few days while I recover, either.”

“So if you had to use such a technique, that means you ran out of energy,” Asher surmised, putting the pieces together. “And based on how happy you were to see me… I take it you were actually trying to bring me back from the wild realm all this time?”

“Yes… You have my deepest apologies for leaving you there longer than I promised,” Richard said, looking like he actually meant it as he bowed his head. “Knowing your skills and how competent you were, I didn’t think surviving in the wild realm for a mere hour would be a problem for you. In fact, I thought it would be excellent training! But at the one hour mark, when I tried to bring you back, only to discover that my spell wasn’t working…”

He shuddered, looking like he was at a loss for words. “...I’ve never had my magic fail like that before. I didn’t know what to do! I tried desperately to bring you back again and again, but it was like something was preventing my spell from completing. Praying it was just some quirk of the wild realm I’d never encountered that would change soon enough, I continued trying to bring you back, even going into tritessence debt when I ran out.”

“You couldn't just make a new spell?” Asher asked, raising an eyebrow. Richard’s current condition and complete change of personality was making it rather hard to be furious with him. That and the fact that at the moment, Asher feared shouting at the man might genuinely cause his heart to give out.

“Making a new spell is something that takes years of effort,” Richard explained, shaking his head. “Even copying another person’s skill is something that can take weeks, or even months, depending on the complexity. I only have one spell for accessing the wild realm, passed down from my own mentor when I was training to be a wizard years ago. The wild realm is actually the simplest of the different realms to travel to, as the spell harnesses the everchanging nature of the realm to convince it to make a portal for you. I swear to you, I’ve never had a problem casting this spell in the past!”

Oh, Asher thought, sighing as he finally realized what must have happened. Well, that explains that. At least Richard didn’t intentionally try to abandon me.

“I'm still mad at you for sending me to another realm without any prior warning,” he started, seeing Richard shrink into his blanket a bit, playing the part of an old, remorseful man quite well. “...But I guess I can’t fault you entirely for not being able to bring me back. That was probably one of my skills.”

“What do you mean?” Richard asked, looking at him in confusion. “I saw your skills. If you’d had one that would interfere with my spell, I never would have sent you over there in the first place.”

“It’s a brand-new effect. I evolved my Permanence skill while I was in the other realm, after you sent me there,” Asher explained. “The tier 2 effect prevents a large chunk of the wild realm surrounding me from changing. If your spell takes advantage of the realm’s own chaotic nature to create a portal, that explains why it wouldn’t work.”

“You can stop the wild realm from changing?” Richard asked, recoiling in shock at the new information. “That’s incredible! Do you have any idea of the applications your skill would have for new expeditions into the realm?!”

“Oh yeah, I got my own taste of how much fun trying to navigate through a constantly-changing realm can be,” Asher laughed. “That wasn’t the only skill evolution I got either. I can use Recall to jump between realms now, as long as I have an Eternal Mark already placed.”

“That certainly explains how you got back here,” Richard said, his brow furrowing as he began muttering to himself something about adventurer logistics and tritessence expenditure. Seeing that the wizard had at least a little bit more color in his face than he had when they began talking, Asher shook his head. With all his big talk and unpleasant attitude, it had been difficult to see Richard as anything other than an annoyance. Now, looking disheveled and huddled up in a blanket, he didn’t look like a powerful wizard. He just looked like a frail, old man.

“Alright, why don’t we level with one another for a moment,” Asher said, sighing as he tried to choose his words carefully. “Seeing as we’re both national assets, and we both work pretty closely with Donvath, we’re probably going to be seeing a decent amount of each other going forward. We don’t have to be friends, but can we at least have something resembling a cordial working relationship? I’m too busy with training and trying to rid the world of serious threats to spend a couple of weeks sitting in your study letting you copy my skills, and I’d rather not have to keep going out of my way to avoid you. In exchange for me not telling Donvath about any of this, and you owing me big, can we do that?”

“That is more than fair,” Richard said, looking like a massive weight had been taken off his shoulders as he sat up straighter and smiled. Only then did Asher realize Richard probably hadn’t been all that worried about him. Most likely, he’d been worried that his cushiony gig as the palace wizard might be in jeopardy after stranding the kingdom’s only other national asset in another realm of existence. “Oh, and here. I did take care of these during that first hour you were away.”

Pulling an arm from the blanket, Richard handed him the two once-empty artifacts that had started this whole ordeal. Nodding his thanks, Asher took a look at them.

[Ring of Realm Sight (Realm of Ages)] - Grants the user the Lesser Realm Sight skill (Tier 1)

“Seeing as I don’t really have any use for it…” Pulling the fuzzy egg out of his rift, Asher handed it over to Richard in exchange. The wizard blinked as he took in the incredibly unappetizing egg, staring at it as if Asher had just dropped a giant diamond onto his lap.

“Is that…?”

“It’s an egg from the wild realm,” Asher shrugged, thinking back to Scritchems and how the weird deer-like creatures had changed before his very eyes. “Though I admit, I have no idea what will grow from it.”

Richard’s eyes went wider than he’d ever seen them before. Gingerly picking up the egg, he turned and rushed out of the room without a word, nearly tripping over Samantha’s blanket as he did so.

Rolling his eyes, Asher sighed as he watched the wizard flee without even closing the door behind him. Despite the horrific shape tritessence debt had put Richard in and how much progress Asher had made during his short stint in the wild realm, he still felt like Richard got off too easy. Still, if he really meant what he said about no longer trying to hound Asher to let him copy his skills, then everything that had happened would be a worthy trade. And not just because the journey had resulted in two of Asher’s last three skills reaching the second tier.

His team had finally received another critical tool for actually taking those greater demons down, and he planned to put them both to good use.
