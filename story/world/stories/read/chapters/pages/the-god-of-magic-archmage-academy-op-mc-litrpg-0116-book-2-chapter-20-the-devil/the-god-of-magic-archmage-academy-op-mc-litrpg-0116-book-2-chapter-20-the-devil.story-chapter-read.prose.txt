Sen

The morning after the uprising, he found a letter from Drussan. It had been slipped under the door of the abandoned shop they’d used as a base of operations. One of the dreadbeast’s other human allies must have delivered it. The message was simple.

It worked.

After splitting up, Drussan’s job had switched from setting up the escape of the various dreadbeasts slaves to coordinating their movements. Dreadbeasts had incredible stamina and were quite fast when they needed to be. In the past, the march of their hordes had been likened to earthquakes because of the loud rumbling they caused for miles when they moved in large groups. Sen smiled and set the letter on a table. There hadn’t been any word from the Green Man. Tutor had monitored the creation for a while, or at least some of its copies. But eventually they all faded away until even the connection finally snapped. It was likely he’d completed his purpose, and the forbidden spell wiped itself from existence.

The only thing keeping Sen from dwelling on alternatives was the fact that Drussan said his people were free. That Drussan had received correspondence from all the various groups. Everything had gone exactly as planned.

So what exactly was bothering Sen?

In the end, he decided to take a walk to clear his head. The people of Vitree had taken to the streets as usual, but instead of shopping and trading goods in the market, the town was abuzz with gossip.

Last night a powerful storm had suddenly swept over the town. Their windows had fogged over, and those who tried to leave their homes found themselves magically shut-in. Not to mention the sounds of explosions from Sen and Solomon’s fight. It was the talk of the town, and everyone wanted answers. He was uninterested in most of the rumors, though one piqued his interest. Many people were talking about the fact that Lord Thessus’ estate had burned to the ground last night. Many more wondered what exactly that meant and who was responsible.

He maneuvered his way through the crowd. He hadn’t slept in a few days, and he thought it would be wise to get something to eat.

[Perhaps we should return so you can rest, Master. I’m not sure delaying rest any further is wise.]

Sen shook his head. “I’ll sleep once everyone has returned.” He’d sent his friends to fight slavers and their guards at a moment’s notice. Sure, he was confident in them, but he wouldn’t be able to sleep peacefully until he knew they were safe. Besides, he needed to feed Malachi. As much as he despised the man, the slaver turned dreadbeast would likely wake from his karma-induced experience soon, and he’d need to eat. He planned to send him to Drussan and the others in a few days once most of the spells’ other lingering effects wore off. The dreadbeast freedom fighter had thought it best that they not bring a convulsing dreadbeast along with them. So, Sen agreed to watch over the old man for a while longer. Despite his personal feelings about it all. It was the least he could do for Drussan.

Dirke had previously mentioned a decent breakfast place he sometimes stopped at while he was in town, and the idea of eggs and possibly a pastry wasn’t the worst at the moment.

The crowd ahead of him split to make way for soldiers hurrying past. They were led by Captain Fulcrae. The red-haired man wore a serious expression and moved quickly, ignoring the questions that the crowd shouted at him.

“What happened to Lord Thessus?”

“Are we safe? Is my family safe, Fulcrae?!”

“What was that last night? Are we at war or something?”

Fulcrae’s men pushed back against the crowd and kept their pace. Fulcrae obviously had somewhere important to be. He wasn’t stopping for anything.

His eyes flicked toward Sen.

Nevermind.

“Sen!” Fulcrae called out in surprise. He hurried over, signaling something to the men in his employ. They formed a circle around the two of them. The surrounding civilians stopped shouting questions, wondering what was going on.

“Captain. How can I help?” Sen asked, feigning innocence. He’d been in disguise last night, at least for most of the fight. He’d also been careful to obscure his actions with the shadow forests power. There was no reason for the captain to suspect him.

Fulcrae frowned, as if considering how to phrase his next question. “I was wondering if you had any information about last night. There were…a number of odd circumstances. We’re investigating the disappearance of Lord Thessus and the destruction of his estate. We’re also hoping to get any information we can on the people responsible for the spells cast last night. The people of Vitree were trapped within their homes. We also believe the storm that passed over was magical in nature, and several buildings were damaged from a fight between two extremely powerful individuals. It ended before we could intervene, but we need to ensure something like this can’t happen again. The people are…well—” He beckoned to the crowd around them.

“Worried,” Sen finished for him. “You don’t think I had anything to do with it? Do you?” Sen asked.

[The innocent act, huh? I don’t know how you sleep at night. Oh, wait, you haven’t been.]

Fulcrae’s eyes widened. “No, I’m sorry. That’s not what I meant. I sensed you were stronger than most when you first entered Vitree. I simply wondered if perhaps you’d sensed anything that might be helpful?”

Sen thought for a moment. “Unfortunately not. I’m a bit of a deep sleeper.”

Fulcrae raised a brow. “You slept through those explosions? And the thunder?” he asked.

Sen chuckled. “Yes, well, I use a spell to ensure I get my full eight hours. I only wake up if my life is in danger.”

“You must be quite powerful indeed if your spell didn’t think you were in danger last night. I’m sorry to bother you, but we should keep moving. If you hear anything, please let me know,” Fulcrae said.

Sen nodded. “Will do. Good luck with your search. I hope you find whoever’s responsible.”

Fulcrae nodded. He and his men pressed onward, deeper into the city.

Sen slipped through the crowd, casting invisibility on himself to ensure no one in the crowd bothered him after seeing him talk to Fulcrae. He cut through an alleyway into an adjacent street and kept moving. This street was calmer, and the pleasant aroma of bacon and baked goods perked him up. He dropped invisibility and continued his stroll.

He thought things had gone surprisingly well until Tutor’s notification.

[We’re being followed.]

Sen didn’t react visibly. He kept his pace and spread his mana senses around him in a net. Nothing. He didn’t sense anyone. That made him frown.

Are you sure? Is it Fulcrae or one of his men?

[Positive, and no. My analysis spell flagged a Prince-class mage a moment ago. That’s far stronger than Fulcrae. I thought it might be a coincidence, but they adjusted their path when we cut through the alley. They’re definitely following us.]

A Prince-Class Mage in Vitree? That was odd. It explained why he hadn’t sensed them, though. Tutor’s analysis spell allowed him to peer through some veils that otherwise would escape even Sen’s notice. If whoever was following them could hide from Sen’s sight, then they were dangerous.

Interesting. Let’s see how it plays out then.

He walked right past the restaurant he’d been planning to eat at, though his stomach protested.

If whoever was chasing him intended to cause him harm, he needed to get out of the city. His fight with Solomon had been difficult enough to contain. If he had to go spell for spell with a prince-class mage, he wouldn’t be able to afford worrying about collateral damage. He only hoped his stalker would be patient enough to wait to strike until he’d escaped the town limits.

Fortunately, they were. He made it to the gate and left town with no problems. Once he was out, he made his move.

“Encapsulation. Float Maxima.”

He shot out of town with a burst of speed, quickly putting as much ground as he could between himself and the town. He sped past farms and their respective crops, veered off the main road, and skidded to a stop in a large grassy plain. Mountains loomed in the distance, and he appreciated the splendor of them for a moment while he waited. His pursuer kept up easily, as he expected of a prince-class mage.

He turned to face them.

[Oh. I stand corrected. Princess-class mage.]

She was tall. She had light-brown skin and dark brown hair. Her eyes were emerald green, and she wore a smug look on her face. Her bare fingers poked through fingerless black gloves, and golden bracers adorned her arms. It wasn’t the only gold on her person, though. She had bands around her biceps, which were massive, a large golden necklace, and a red scarf wrapped around her neck. An impressive six-pack was on full display, and a simple loincloth that matched her scarf covered her waist. She rolled her neck, folded her arms together, and glared at him.

“You’ve got a lot of nerve walking around town like that instead of hiding like the rat you are.” She looked to her right and spat into the grass.

Sen’s mouth fell open. “Uh, what?”

“You know what you did!” She shook her head. “We had a meeting, and you went and stood me up. This is why I can’t stand cultists.”

Ah, a misunderstanding. “I think you’re confusing me with someone else…I’m not in a cult.”

The woman raised a brow. “Uh huh. Zed said you’d try to weasel your way out of our meeting. I told him not to retire on me. But he said it wasn’t up to him. Well, I think I’ve got a solution to that.” She punched her palm, and a wide grin spread across her face. “If I kill you, they’ll have to give Zed his old post back.”

Tutor, why does the name Zed sound familiar?

Tutor imitated the sound of a sigh in Sen’s head. [That was the name of the cultist of the God of Magic responsible for overseeing the kingdom’s ruins before Dirke took over.]

What? Why would she think I’m in the cult of —

He paused and looked down at his robes. All white robes. Exactly like the Cult of the God of Magic wore.

Oh.

“Listen.” He held his hands up to defuse the situation. “It’s not what you think. I do know Dirke, and I’d be happy to introduce you to him. Whatever business you have to settle, and however you wish to settle it, is between the two of you. But I have nothing—” He jumped back, creating distance from her sudden attack.

She hadn’t even cast a spell. She’d simply wrapped her hand in mana, shot forward, and punched the ground where Sen had been standing.

The blow left a crater behind.

“Enough talk. Minds made up. You gonna fight or run?” she asked, grinning again.

Sen chuckled. “So be it. But don’t say I didn’t warn you.”

[May I remind you she’s a princess-class mage? In other words, two entire ranks above your hybrid rank.]

How amusing. “May I remind you I’m the God of Magic?”

She shot forward again, and this time, he flew forward to meet her strike.
