A few seconds prior… Sort of.

“No… Stop!” Asher shouted weakly, his face paling as he realized he’d failed. Ploryn had proven himself to be not just a step above the other liches he’d fought, but an entire staircase ahead in terms of knowledge and power. Rather than spare him a second glance, the fires burning in Ploryn’s eyes went out as he lowered his head and raised his staff.

Before plunging it directly into the center of the glowing ritual.

The moment his staff connected, the ritual began to shine as the wind picked up and started swirling all around them. As if Ploryn was harnessing tritessence from the very world itself, the runes he’d painstakingly put down began to glow and shake. Only after a few seconds, Asher realized it wasn’t the runes that were shaking.

The entire mountain was beginning to tremble and quake, and Asher looked on in horror as cracks began spreading out from Ploryn’s staff across the top of the mountain and massive chunks began falling inward.

Down toward Dormaul and the innocent people living below.

“All of this could have been avoided if you’d just given yourself up!” Ploryn called over the rushing winds and loud explosions of rock impacting rock ringing up from the interior of the mountain. The lich stood amongst the chaos, the flames in his eyes flickering in the harsh winds as he pointed his glowing staff toward him. “I’ll be back for your element later!”

With that, there was a flash of violet light, and Ploryn vanished.

For a few seconds, Asher did nothing but stand there upon the collapsing mountain, barely even able to support his own weight while still recovering from taking a lightning bolt to the chest. The wall of mud that had sucked in Cora and Jameston tumbled down to the cavern below as another chunk of the mountain collapsed in on itself. He might be able to bring back what remained of the Eight Crests’ realm wandering team… but Dormaul?

There were tens of thousands of people living down there who had been relying on him to keep them safe, even if they themselves hadn’t been aware of that until just now. He couldn’t bring them all back.

Sinking to his knees, Asher punched his fists into the shaking ground beneath him, his eyes scrunched shut as he tried to reconcile his failure. Not only was Dormaul and all its citizens going to be destroyed, one of the strongest liches in the entire Council had escaped to wreak havoc somewhere else. Even with Donvath giving the Noala Kingdom a do-over, they’d failed to protect everyone.

Donvath…

Asher’s breath caught, and his eyes shot back open when he was struck by a sudden thought. All might not be lost just yet. Donvath hadn’t given him a clear answer when he’d asked if there was a chance of rewinding things a second time should they slip up, but that didn’t mean he couldn’t. Deciding to hold off on his grief until after he’d exhausted all their options, Asher forced himself back to his feet. He had no idea what would be needed of him, but just in case, he threw down one of his marked daggers to give himself something to Recall back to, before Recalling over to the palace. Based on what Donvath had let slip, roughly ten minutes was the maximum amount of time he could go back.

No matter what, Asher had to find the High Prince and explain what needed to be done within that time limit.

Downing another potion of Lesser Restoration to help speed up his healing, Asher ran through the palace hallways as he headed for the room they’d all strategized in that morning. If there was anywhere Donvath would be, it had to be there. Spotting Lily and a second royal guard standing outside the door, Asher let out a sigh of relief.

“Asher?” Lily asked, blinking at his sudden appearance. “You look awful.”

“No time, have to talk to Donvath!” Asher shouted, not even slowing down as he slipped into the astral and ran straight through the door.

Or at least, he certainly tried to.

Smacking off the enchanted door, Asher fell back into the material realm, cursing up a storm while he rubbed at what felt like a broken nose. Lily stood over him, raising an eyebrow as she watched him struggle to his feet.

“You think we would risk people sneaking into the war room while Donvath was alone? This room is a bunker.”

“Well I need into the bunker!” Asher snarled, each second ticking by feeling like another knife to the gut. “Dormaul has fallen, and I need to talk to the High Prince!”

That seemed to be the secret passphrase, as Lily’s eyes widened and she stepped back. Without so much as sparing her a second glance, Asher shoved his way into the room and let the door slam shut behind him. The moment he laid eyes on the High Prince, head down and fully engrossed in what looked like a series of maps on the table, he ran forward and slammed his hands down. The loud, sudden noise clearly caught the High Prince off guard, as Donvath shrieked and clutched a hand to his heart before discovering it was Asher who had interrupted him.

“Don’t do that!” Donvath snapped, glaring at him before going back to listening to a sending stone. There were six different stones in front of him, and every single one of them appeared to be vibrating as people tried to get in touch with him. “I don’t have the energy for frivolous rewinds right now, and I’d like to maintain at least some semblance of dignity as the High Prince.”

“Dormaul is gone,” Asher said, causing Donvath’s face to blanch as the sending stone tumbled from his fingers. “We failed. The mountain is collapsing as we speak. Can you undo it?”

“The chunk of time I unwound was too large,” Donvath muttered. Biting his thumb, he stared down at a series of maps and diagrams covering the table. “I could do a smaller jump, but it’ll take practically everything I have. I’ll be functionally out of the rest of the battle against the Council.”

“That’s fine, you only need to go back about a minute,” Asher said, a plan suddenly igniting within his mind as he realized Donvath himself was the key to taking down Ploryn. “You need to undo everything… and then help us stop Ploryn.”

“Me?” Donvath asked, barking out a harsh laugh before shaking his head. “Asher, I’m not sure if you noticed, but I’m not a fighter. I can hold my own when I know exactly how someone is going to come at me, but going up against a lich? There’s no chance.”

“Ploryn can undo skills and affect tritessence directly!” Asher shouted, slamming a fist down onto the table and causing Donvath to jump. “As much as I hate to admit it, he’s too skilled. The only way we’re going to stop him is if we catch him completely off guard, but he can view into the astral realm. The only other alternative is jumping him from the future.”

“Asher, if he can undo skills, then that means he can probably prevent my Timeline from automatically kicking in upon my death,” Donvath pointed out, his knuckles whitening as he gripped the edge of the large table. “…He could kill me for real.”

“He just did kill thousands of your citizens!” Asher roared, barely able to believe what he was hearing. “Are you seriously going to just sit back and let everyone else fight your battles for you?! The great High Prince Donvath, beloved ruler and man the citizens of the Noala Kingdom look up to, too cowardly to put his own life on the line for a change? I thought you were better than that.”

Each sentence was like another dagger to Donvath’s chest, and the High Prince collapsed back into his chair, his head hanging low as he placed his face in his hands. After a few seconds, he took a deep, shaky breath. Brushing the hair out of his face, he sat up straight and gave Asher a look he couldn’t quite place.

“There is… are, people relying on me to survive. You don’t look like you’re in such great shape. Can you honestly tell me that the you of a few minutes ago is in good enough condition to take advantage of an opportunity if I buy him one?”

Now it was Asher’s turn to hesitate as he thought back to just how much he’d struggled to move his body after receiving that bolt of lightning to the chest. Donvath couldn’t go too far into the past, and that moment right before Ploryn triggered his ritual, when he closed his eyes to focus, was almost certainly the perfect moment to strike. Debating for a few seconds, Asher nodded.

“…I’ll make it work.”

Suddenly, Asher realized what he was actually doing. Yes, his plan was the only way to go back and save all of Dormaul, but for the first time since arriving on this world…

He was going to die.

The thought struck Asher like a physical blow, and he staggered back for a moment, catching himself against the wall. Sure, there would still be a version of him from only a few minutes ago who would go on to live, hopefully with the relief that they’d managed to save Dormaul and wipe out Ploryn. But this version of him would cease to exist. Asher supposed there had been countless hims who had been clipped from the tree of time whenever Donvath had used Timeline in the past, but of course, from his perspective, he’d never been one of them.

Donvath seemed to recognize the look on his face, because the High Prince sighed.

“Sorry, Asher, we don’t have time for your existential crisis. There’s a reason why I don’t normally utilize Timeline for other people, or even tell them when I’m going to do it, but this is something of an emergency. If I’m about to follow through with this crazy plan of yours and potentially throw my life away, you need to be prepared to do the same. Quickly, I need you to use your second-tier of Incorporate and show me the memories of your encounter with Ploryn. I need to see precisely where I’m to appear when I leap into the past. Also, you can’t tell anyone about what we’re about to do. While I do have the power to go back to a location I wasn’t in at the time, I’ve done everything I can to ensure nobody is aware of that fact. Not my royal guards, not my truthseekers, nobody. You’ll become the first person privy to that secret, do you understand?”

“Yes,” Asher muttered. The unexpected trust Donvath was placing in him shook him out of his mortality-based stupor long enough for him to place a hand on his head and feed him the memories he wanted. “Wait… I’m not going to exist in a moment anyway… Why bother telling me that?”

“Are you kidding? I’ve seen you get ripped in half by greater demons, disintegrated by the power of time, and even eaten alive from the inside out by an almost-sentient clump of mold,” Donvath snorted, tapping a buckle on his belt and surprising Asher when a pair of brilliant blue wings erupted from his back. “Not to mention a thousand other horrific deaths across a million futures that never came to pass. I wouldn’t be surprised in the slightest if you found a way to survive this, and that terrifies me more than you could ever know.”

It took Asher a moment to realize why the wings were necessary, before remembering that Dormaul had already collapsed. If Donvath wanted to get himself into position using the memories he’d been given, he was going to have to fly up to where the top of the mountain had once been. Donvath didn’t see the need to explain any of that to him of course, as the High Prince cleared his throat.

“Now, enough pillow talk. Bring me to what’s left of Dormaul, I’ll take care of the rest. And for what it’s worth…” Donvath paused, laying a gentle hand on his shoulder as he gave him a tired but proud smile. “…I’m honored to call you my friend.”
