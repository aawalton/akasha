The ranchers were ecstatic, congratulating Ferris. Word had gotten around that a cultivator was bonding one of their slimes, which drew a crowd. A cheer broke out when he announced he had succeeded.

Almost everyone grew up training to become a cultivator, only for about nine out of ten to fail. Not everyone was rigorously trained to join a martial tradition like Ferris, but any clan worth the name provided schooling and guidance. Every person here had spent countless hours exercising and meditating, being told how much better their life would be if they could simply unlock their soul. Having more Adepts would make the clan stronger, after all.

The ranchers were no different. Many of them probably still practiced meditation each night, hoping to be a late bloomer. A cultivator bonding to a slime at their ranch was exciting enough to celebrate. Even Foreman Carter, with all his protests, was caught up by the mood—although he never stopped feeding the slime on time.

Speaking of the slime, Ferris felt an odd sensation when looking at them now. He could sense that the bond connected them in an indescribable way. The book on familiar bonds had described this, explaining that it was the basis for cooperation and communication, but words failed to do the feeling justice.

The bond felt like… flexing a muscle he’d never known existed. It had a strange mix of camaraderie and safety. This connection was also why the book stated familiars never attacked their bonded human. Ferris could sort of understand why, he felt that hurting the slime would be like poking himself in the eye.

Confidently, Ferris reached out his right hand to really feel the slime for the first time. They were giving out pleasant ripples, and he could feel contentment from the bond. Before, touching had been stressful and tedious, always vigilant to pull back at the slightest change in behavior.

Now, with the good feelings flowing through him, patting the slime was a pleasant affair… until his hand was engulfed in acid.

Ferris stared in shock for a moment, not believing what he was seeing. Then the pain hit.

He screamed, and Foreman Carter grabbed him by the shirt to pull him back. The rancher holding the rope had set it down to celebrate and hadn’t been warned before Ferris boldly touched the slime again.

The ranchers, while surprised, had plenty of practice drills—and real-life experience—to handle a slime burn. A nearby bucket of water was dumped over his hand. The pain felt worse under the water, and Ferris screamed again. Foreman Carter held his arm steady as at least four more buckets were dumped over the wound, but Ferris lost count.

A cream was applied to his hand, then a bandage was skillfully and painfully wrapped around it. Somehow the wound was now both freezing and burning.

As the pain shifted from blinding to merely harsh, the embarrassment kicked in. I spent all that time being careful, sat through all the warning, and then ignored it all for a pleasant feeling and a note in a book. Ferris admonished himself. I was so smug, testing everything, thinking I was smarter.

“Damned fool.” Foreman Carter’s iron grip released him; joyous mood gone. “I told you, but no one ever really listens until they live it. Slimes are dangerous.” The man sat next to Ferris, placing a hand on his shoulder. “You were bound to get burned eventually, dealing with the things. Just be happy it was a mild case, and it happened around help.”

Ferris gave a ragged nod as he tried to blink away the tears from his eyes. The freezing cream had begun feeling like many sharp needles, but the pain lessened. Whatever the ranchers put on his hand must be effective.

He wanted to protest and say that he had been careful. That the book had lied to him. That a familiar never hurt their bonded partner...

Instead, all he could say was, “Thank you.”

Foreman Carter stood, then began herding the ranchers back to work. There were some grumbles, but they all had their own responsibilities. Can’t spend all day watching the cultivator play with a slime.

Speaking of which, Ferris looked back at where the slime was chained up. They had gone back to rolling around, systematically searching the limits of the area. Every time they passed over the spot Ferris had trained them to stand in, the slime would hesitate for a couple seconds, but then move on.

It was bubbling unpleasantly. Before, Ferris could only guess feelings, but now the bond left no question: the slime was not happy. The feelings went beyond words, and Ferris could only comprehend small pieces. They were hungry, and unhappy the food had stopped. They were trapped, unhappy they couldn’t get the chain off their core, and really wanted to eat the chain. More than anything else, they were… unhappy that Ferris was in pain?

Ferris sat safely outside the slime’s range and watched for a while. The emotions coming through the bond were strange. He thought he understood some, but they felt odd. Foreign. Many thoughts were simple and childish, like pouting that the food had stopped. Others hurt his head with their complexity, like the slime’s memory of where they’d been and their plan to explore. Not just that, but systematically check and recheck everywhere associated within the restraint and the limits around it.

Ferris shook his head, trying to stop the flow of thoughts.

Strangely, the slime immediately stopped rolling. They were swirling in… confusion? They had felt something new and were investigating.

“Roll forward,” thought Ferris, trying to project at the slime.

Slowly the slime started rolling, although not in the direction Ferris intended.

“Stop,” and the slime stopped. Ferris was elated, and the slime started rippling in joy, mirroring his emotion.

What followed was several hours of a new kind of training. Ferris got better at projecting his commands, while the slime got better at understanding. After a while, they even responded with a basic confirmation feeling. Best of all, Ferris could get the slime to ignore food completely. Even the special berry could be placed on top of the slime without being consumed. They weren’t happy, but did listen.

“Eat?” the slime asked, not in words but feelings. But also, kind of in words.

“No eat,” Ferris responded.

“But… eat!“

“No eat.”

“… eat now?”

“No eat.”

The conversations were simple. He thought of them as words, but the actual messages were more like emotions and memories and thoughts and words, all at once. If Ferris tried to relay a complicated idea the slime swirled in confusion. Many times, the slime projected an image that hurt Ferris’ head. Generally, the slime was happy to follow instructions, even when it came to ignoring food. Only the berries tempted them to protest the commands, but even then, the slime did what Ferris asked.

Some commands were easily followed, but many caused confusion. The slime didn’t have a face and had trouble with the idea of ‘forward.’ To them, there was no difference between front, side, or back.

The slime did have an excellent mental map of locations, as well as the ability to know relative location to Ferris through the bond. This mental map was not, however, a visual image. Honestly, Ferris didn’t understand what it was.

Sight and sound were a strange problem, causing a lot of confusion between them. Ferris would think, go over there. The slime would stop and swirl, unable to understand the image Ferris added to the instruction.

In retrospect, the reason was obvious: the slime had no eyes or ears. The idea of a ‘seeing’ something was as foreign to them as swimming to a bird.

This explained why they hadn’t responded to visual or audio signals. They did have an excellent sense of touch everywhere on their body. Or maybe it was better referred to as taste? As far as Ferris could tell, taste and touch were the same thing to the slime.

Whatever the sense was called, it also worked inside their body as they ate things. The slime liked the taste of the chain but was frustrated at being unable to eat it.

Earlier, Ferris had seen that the slime learned fast. Now, with the bond helping, they took it to another level.

While at first sharing what he saw only caused confusion, that didn’t last long. Once the slime understood something once, they had it down perfectly.

Eventually they could move correctly based on images alone, at least if the slime could also see themselves through Ferris’ eyes.

Throughout the training, Ferris was able to get a better understanding of the slime’s physical capabilities. They weren’t fast, with a top speed matching a brisk walk. Anyone running could outpace a slime easily.

Mechanically, the slime moved by gripping onto the ground and rolling their body forward. At some point Ferris would need to test if the slime could move on a sheer surface like glass, or something slick like ice, but nothing at the slime ranch gave any trouble.

To hop, the slime had to squish down and then quickly pull together while releasing the ground. It worked, but not very well.

The slime was adept at climbing, but would fall off any flat surface like a wall. If they had something to surround and grip—like the magical chain—they could easily climb up and hang suspended.

When it came time to try physical contact again, Ferris was apprehensive.

“This me. No eat me. Never eat me,” Ferris told the slime, carefully touching with his unbandaged hand.

“What never?” The slime shifted a bit, confused.

They struggled a lot with abstract concepts, like in this case the idea of time. If Ferris could show something in the real world, the slime would understand. But anything requiring an explanation was hard. Complicating everything, Ferris was also struggling with a feeling of fear, enhanced by the pain in his right hand. He couldn’t help it, and the feelings leaked into the bond, further confusing the slime.

“No eat now. No eat later. Never eat.” Ferris tried to focus his thoughts, removing unnecessary emotion, but it was hard. The slime was beginning to learn words, but they still mattered less than the mental thought behind them. Both of them were getting better, but it was easy for misunderstandings to happen.

“No eat.” The slime responded, more confidently.

As he spent more and more time with his new familiar, Ferris began wondering what to call them. Names were important, but for that reason he also didn’t want to rush it.

At least Ferris felt reasonably confident they would listen for now, but he was worried how long it would last. He thought back to the stories of slimes ignoring training after a day. The slime was smart in a confusing way, but would they forget? Would they wake up in the middle of the night and start eating everything in range? Could Ferris meditate safely, or would the slime climb up and cover his head?

He would never leave the slime at the ranch. Now that they were bonded, destroying the slime’s core would likely kill him. He needed a way to feel safe with the slime.

Ferris inspected the magical chain that tethered the slime to the ground. When Foreman Carter had killed the previous slime, he’d needed to unlatch the cage before he could crush the core.

If he had something like this, he could restrict his familiar away from his bed.

Unhooking the chain from the pole, Ferris began leading the slime around a like a dog on a leash. Foreman Carter would not approve. The slime could just climb up the chain to reach him. Still, Ferris wouldn’t get anywhere without some risk. I just need to watch carefully, he thought, and give a command if needed.

It was interesting, seeing the metal cage floating inside the slime as they rolled after him. It reminded Ferris of the ability Janice Landsworn had developed to protect Gumly’s core. Armor inside the slime’s body.

Ferris wanted it.

Leading the slime up and out of the pit, he set out to find Foreman Carter. It did not take long to find the man, who was not amused by the slime-on-a-leash trick.

“At least give it a day between burns, son.”

“Thank you for the guidance, Foreman Carter.” Ferris tried to show the man respect, but it was hard while blatantly ignoring his advice. “Please, let me show you what I have accomplished.”

At least the man was a bit impressed with Ferris’ ability to command the slime without speaking. He was shocked when Ferris placed a berry on the slime’s head.

“I am trying to be safe.” Ferris put on his negotiation face. “May I ask where the ranch gets equipment like this chain? It would help me take precautions.” His uncle had left him some money, although almost definitely not enough for something forged with magical materials.

“They are specially made for us by the second branch.” The foreman frowned. “I can’t just sell them either, gear like that is provided by the clan.”

“Is there anything you would recommend? I will be sleeping in the same room…” Ferris sighed, a bit exaggerated, but not a lie.

The man’s frown turned into a scowl, which Ferris hoped was driven by concern.

Looking off to the side, Foreman Carter sighed.

“Fine,” he said with a grunt, “the thing would eat you in your sleep. I won’t have that on my conscience. I will write it up as a necessary safety expense.”

Ferris looked up, excited. He didn’t like his familiar being referred to as a ‘thing,’ but now wasn’t the time to argue.

“But,” the man said, “you will have to pay the merit points to replace it.”

Ferris tried not to wince too hard. “How many?” He had just received some from Elder Harper, but he hoped it wouldn’t be too expensive. Those points could directly translate into techniques and resources necessary for delving. The only way to get more would be to spend time working for the clan.

“Blast it, I don’t know. We don’t sell them.” The man rubbed his head in frustration. “Let me check the books.”

Ferris practiced coordination with his slime familiar while the man visited an office off to the side. He would throw small pieces of food and direct the slime to jump up to catch them. No matter how much it looked like playing, it was definitely serious practice.

“Alright, I can let you have it for six merit points.” The foreman returned, holding a small device. “Do you even have that many yet?”

Ferris nodded, a bit stiffly. “Yes, fortunately, I do.” If he worked at one of the lesser clan jobs, it would take two weeks to get that much. Of course, he could also earn more from a single delve… once he was strong enough.

Ferris hadn’t had a chance to visit the merit store yet, and so he didn’t know the standard price for magical items. The foreman was likely giving it to him at cost, which the merit store would never do. Likely the price would start much higher, plus extra for being a specialty item.

Ferris used his identity token on the device Foreman Carter held to pay. Unlike his new magical chain-cage-leash, this device was actually enchanted. Hemon had shown him one when he had gotten his identity token. They were costly to make, but allowed a mundane like the foreman to conduct business. They saved a lot of Adepts' time, shifting many tasks to assistants.

Of course, if any were stolen it would be a major problem. Ferris didn’t know what methods the clan had to track them down, but he doubted they would be simple.

After that, Ferris gave his thanks for the help, then bid farewell. As a parting gift, Foreman Carter gave him more of the burn cream, with instructions to change the bandage daily. Ferris also got to keep the last berry; it would make a good reward for the slime later. He was warned the magical berry would lose potency after a day or so. Ferris didn’t know much yet about how magic within items and resources worked, but he knew some would stay magical forever, while others needed to be refilled with qi from a cultivator.

His new familiar in tow, Ferris left the slime ranch. He had only spent a single day there, but he felt changed forever.
