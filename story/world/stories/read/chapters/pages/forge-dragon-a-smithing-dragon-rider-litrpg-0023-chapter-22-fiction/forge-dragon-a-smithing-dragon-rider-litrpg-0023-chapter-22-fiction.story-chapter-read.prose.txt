The emotions dropped from Kelia’s face as she looked from Caleb to Bog and back, processing the statement.

“What?”

“This is Bog. He’s a dragon.”

“I got that part,” she said, “Explain.”

So he did, repeating his story all over again.

Kelia was far more intrigued by the story than Grisim was. Where he seemed wholly unconcerned for Caleb’s safety in the retelling, since he had very clearly survived, Kelia hung on every word and was visibly stricken each time Caleb recounted himself doing something ill-advised.

Throughout, Grisim kept checking on the meat that refused to cook, while Bog stared daggers at Kelia every time she touched Caleb, and Avis did the same from her perch up above.

“That’s a lot to take in,” Kelia said, once Caleb had finished his recounting.

He nodded in agreement.

“We can talk more about this later, but what are you going to do?”

“What do you mean?” Grisim asked.

Caleb and Kelia turned to him like he was stupid—because that was what they both thought.

“He can’t just bring a dragon back to the village and expect people to not want to kill it. You know the elders would say the potential for more initialized would be infinitely more valuable than whatever value the dragon would have. Also, he can make enchanted equipment. Is he going to tell anyone and risk the emperor finding out, or is he going to hide it?”

“I’m not an idiot,” Grisim shot back, “but… that’s a good point. I hadn’t thought about the elders. I did think about the crafting, though.”

There was an awkward pause after that as they all thought about Grisim and Caleb’s mother, who’d been taken away by the empire under similar circumstances. She’d always been a talented Weaver, but one day something had clicked and she’d begun producing enchanted items much more regularly. She’d tried to keep it hidden, but word had eventually gotten out. When the empire came to take her, they took most of her work with them as well. All the boys had left of their mother was a tapestry that accelerated healing if you slept in the same room.

“I’ve thought about this a lot. I think I’ll just have to hide it all,” Caleb said.

“How?” the other two said in unison.

“Bog can stay out at my forge,” he explained. “It’s far enough out that no one goes there. And as for the enchantments… no one needs to know about that.”

“How is he going to stay hidden?” Kelia said. “Won’t he raid the town’s iron? And what about your class? What are you going to say? Are you going to stop making enchanted equipment?”

“Bog won’t go roaming into town. He’s not stupid or desperate. I’ll give him all the iron he needs. As for my class, I’ll just say I’m a Frost Dragon Hunter. No one in the village can check. If I hide my magic and use my Imperium to simulate enhanced strength, I could easily pass. I’ll tell Dimel I put my points in strength and that I got a enhanced perception skill.”

“How will you get enough iron to keep Bog satisfied? Working off of scraps?” Kelia asked.

“That will be the easy part, I suspect. If I turn in that breastplate and helmet to Master Dimel and say I found it in the mountain, he’ll give me enough iron to experiment out in my private forge. Once I have a good start, I can start selling my mundane work. You wont believe how fast I can work now with my magic. Plus, I can make enchanted equipment for you two, and maybe your dad. After that, I can produce it slowly and act like each was a stroke of brilliance. Mom didn’t try to hide her work. She made it for everyone.”

Another awkward pause ensued as they reflected on their mother’s “flaw.” When she’d learned she could make magical equipment that helped people, nothing could have stopped her from sharing it far and wide.

They talked over the specifics on Caleb’s return and how each would corroborate the story. For one, they decided there had to have only been a single frost dragon hatchling, as Caleb killing three alone while uninitiated would be unbelievable. Secondly, all the equipment Caleb forged would have to have been found in the cavern. They changed the story of the cavern adventure slightly, in that Caleb didn’t find any magical ore, or kill a basilisk. They did, however, keep Caleb’s report of the first troglodytes’ existence mostly intact in case someone felt the need to follow Caleb’s feat. Lastly, the fight with the frost dragon was changed.

To Grisim’s delight, they all agreed it would be best if he’d shown up and saved Caleb, who had been desperately holding off the dragon in the cavern. Grisim—in this version—ambushed the dragon from behind and slew it before it knew he was there

“So… did you level?” Grisim asked once the planning had died down.

“I… uh… don’t know, let me check.”

“How do you not know?!” Grisim asked, affronted.

“I got annoyed with the notifications popping up while I fought, so I started ignoring them.”

Caleb checked his notifications and found that he had not leveled.

Adult Frost Dragon has been killed.

Arcane Power Attack has increased to level 2.

Arcane Power Attack has increased to level 3.

“No, I just leveled my Power Attack,” Caleb said.

“Only that?!” Grisim asked, outraged on Caleb’s behalf. “I gained 2 levels!”

“Really?! But… you hardly did any of the—ow!”

Grisim interrupted Caleb by throwing a rock at his head.

“I don’t know what you’re talking about. You were cornered and I heroically saved your life at the last possible instant,” Grisim said, smiling wide.

Kelia sighed and rubbed her head.

“This is going to get old really fast,” she muttered.

“I agree,” Caleb said, rubbing his head for a different reason.

“What’s going to get old?” Grisim asked. “The fact that I saved your beloved? You know it’s not too late for you to ditch that twig for me? I won’t hold the constant rejections against you.”

“Constant?” Caleb asked, looking from Kelia to his brother. “How many times have you had to reject him?”

“Once or twice,” Kelia said, smiling.

“That’s not a lot,” Caleb said.

“A day,” Grisim and Kelia said in unison.

The conversation broke down into the trio’s regular bickering and joking as they all sat waiting for the dragon meat to cook.

“This is probably the reason we never try to eat the dragons the new initiated kill,” Caleb observed after Bog had pulled the hunk of meat off of the flames.

Using his knife, Caleb carved an outside centimeter off the meat, finding it cooked but the meat further in still raw.

Despite the effort it took to cook the meat, it did taste rather good.

“This is probably what happened when you ate that metal,” Caleb observed to Bog. “It kept leaching your body’s heat, making you sick.”

To test his theory, Caleb pulled out the pouch he’d filled with the metal dust and, using him Imperium, threw a pinch of it into the roaring flames. As soon as the dust neared the fire, the fire retreated from it. Under Caleb’s control, the dust fell into a tight circle a few centimeters across. Around the circle, a ring of black coals formed from the embers that had been white-hot just a moment earlier, and beyond them flickered a weak flame.

Caleb pulled the metal out with his magic before the whole thing went out and watched as the flames slowly retook the ground.

“That’s… really useful,” Kelia said.

“Yeah, I can use a few flecks of the stuff to lower the temperature of the forge,” Caleb said. “I think that might be all it’s good for, though. I can’t see me generating enough heat to forge this stuff. Bog couldn’t even do it when he ate it, it nearly killed him.”

“Speaking of death,” Grisim said, “do you think we should send Avis with a note to Dad telling him you’re not?”

Kelia sighed.

“You boys are hopeless. I did that an hour ago.”

Caleb had noted that the devil bird was gone, but hadn’t seen Kelia write a note. She thought of everything.

He pulled her in closer and enjoyed just being with his two favorite people.

Bog sensed the contentment through the bond and narrowed his eyes at Caleb and Kelia.

“I love you too,” he said to Bog, mollifying the dragon.

Kelia pulled away.

“You say you love the dragon before you say it to me?”

“What do you mean?” Caleb said, knowing he was likely digging himself into a deeper hole. “I’ve said it to you countless times.”

“Yes, but not today. The day we are reunited after I thought you dead for three days.”

“I, uh…”

“He told me he loved me too, right after I saved him from the dragon,” Grisim chimed in.

“I saved you! And that’s not true!”

“We all agreed that the story in which I saved you was the true one, and in that version of the story, you looked longingly into my eyes, told me you loved me, and promised you’d let me marry Kelia in your place.”

Kelia smiled, revealing to Caleb she was only teasing, and sat back down beside him.

“Let’s keep the story believable, Gris,” Kelia said. “He said he loved you, but then he passed out from the exertions. His constitution is only 8, after all.”

“You can’t just go telling people my constitution is 8,” Caleb said.

They continued workshopping the story while making jokes until the sun fully set. Earlier they’d pitched a tent, but with Bog and the fire, they all decided to sleep out beneath the stars.
