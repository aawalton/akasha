It was already late, and Callie had picked all the millefolia leaves Granny Ethel could ever want. Seriously, couldn't she have sent Tristan to gather them? He was the one bragging that he knew where millefolia grew. Yet Callie had to spend hours before she managed to find them. He wouldn't even give a hint!

The sun was already setting when she was done. If Callie got home after dark, Mom would be so mad! She had to hurry back.

However, just as she was about to rush home down the familiar trail, Callie heard a rustling in the nearby bushes. There was plenty of activity in the forest, but only one possibility came to her mind. An arcane beast.

It was far more likely for it to be a squirrel or a bird. But what if this was that rare time when it was an arcane beast? She had to check and make sure.

Why? Because Callie absolutely adored arcane beasts. They could use magic, and they were utterly adorable. All animals were adorable in her opinion, but arcane beasts especially so. Granny Ethel had told Callie so many stories about the ones she had met when she was young and how they had helped her, some directly, some in more subtle ways.

Promising herself to leave after she took a quick look, Callie stepped off the path. Her heart pounded with excitement. If it were a real arcane beast, she would draw it and describe it just like Granny Ethel did with her herbs. The elderly woman had a whole encyclopedia of herbs, and Callie wanted to create something like that, too, but for arcane beasts.

The problem was that they didn't exactly wait around for somebody to draw and study them. The only arcane beast she'd ever seen was a mireling, and even that was from afar. That's why every chance was precious. Once she had a few arcane beasts written down, she would show them to her parents and prove that arcane beasts were good. That fighting and killing them was bad.

Callie followed the noise and, going closer, heard an odd rattling sound. Almost like chains. But why would there be chains in the middle of the forest?

Creeping deeper among the bushes, she saw a rabbit as white as fresh winter snow, half hidden among the bushes. But it wasn't a normal rabbit. From its head, it had two small antlers growing, very similar to those of a young deer. An actual arcane beast!

Callie wanted to scream with joy. How long had it been since she'd seen one? It must have been two years since the skywolf incident, although it felt like it had happened just yesterday.

Her hands had trembled when Callie held the knife, readying to strike a skywolf baby. After all, as the daughter of hunters, she had to do it. But in the end, the knife slipped out of Callie's hands as she gazed at the large and innocent eyes of the creature. She couldn't bring herself to hurt it. Soon, the skywolf mother appeared to retrieve her child, and to Callie's relief, she had been left unhurt.

Since then, Callie had resolved to understand the arcane beast. They surely weren't the vicious killers everyone made them out to be. After all, there had been compassion in the skywolf mother's eyes. It felt as if the creature had said thank you. Callie was certain that everyone in the village had to work a little harder to understand arcane beasts.

But something wasn't right with the rabbit. It wasn't running. Or rather, it was trying to run, thrashing around and occasionally jumping up. But why wasn't it moving forward?

Pushing past the bush, Callie gasped in horror as she understood why. Metallic jaws were clamped shut on the rabbit's hind leg, blood marring its white fur. How could someone do something so horrible?

This wonderful creature was caught in this dreadful, painful trap. It would either starve to death or become someone's trophy. They would likely profit greatly by selling it to a dragonblood.

For a second, Callie wondered if her parents had placed this trap. But no, it couldn't have been them. Although they were hunters, even they wouldn't subject any creature to such a cruel and torturous fate. Whoever had done this was undoubtedly an incredibly cruel person.

Slowly, she approached the deer rabbit while keeping her hands up to show that she didn't mean any harm. "I-It's okay. I'll help you."

The rabbit thrashed more wildly, and Callie stopped, fearing that it might injure its foot even more. The smell of blood was sickening, making her stomach lurch. But it wasn't the time to be queasy. Callie was determined to help.

"Please… I only want to help you," she whispered softly to the rabbit, hoping that it would feel her sincerity.

It paused for a moment, looking at Callie with deep black eyes. As if searching her very soul, it peered at her, unblinking and unmoving.

"I want to help," Callie repeated, hoping it would understand.

The rabbit nodded.

Callie blinked in surprise. It had most definitely nodded at her.

Callie inwardly rejoiced that the rabbit had understood her and approached it slowly. Its nose twitched nervously, but it didn't move anymore. Were the circumstances different, Callie would have squealed at how adorable it was.

But instead, she grabbed both sides of the metallic jaws and tried to pull them apart. They didn't budge. What was that word Dad used when something didn't work out? Dammit. Mom said it was a bad word, but dammit if this wasn't the right time to use it.

This wasn't going to be easy, not for a little kid like her. An adult could likely pull such a small trap apart with ease. Looking up, Callie saw that the sun had almost disappeared, dusk already descending upon the forest. If she remained here for much longer, she wouldn't be able to find the path that led home. What would Callie do in the forest at night? Even adults could get lost in the dark.

But this deer rabbit was stuck. There was no way for it to break free on its own. If she left it here, then it would surely die. Same as those poor creatures that her parents hunted. Callie imagined it lying on the ground, cold and lifeless.

No, she couldn't let that happen! Even if she had to spend the night alone in the dark in the woods. Even if Mom yelled at her afterwards.

"Hold tight. I swear I'll get out even if it's the last thing I do," Callie said to the deer rabbit.

She did everything she could to get the trap to open—pulling with her hands, smashing with rocks, poking with branches, even using her whole body weight. The night settled over the forest as Callie struggled, owls hooting in the distance. Her hands ached from the many bruises she got, but eventually the trap budged open wide enough for the deer rabbit to pull its foot out.

"You're free! You're free!" Callie cheered loudly.

The rabbit hopped a few meters, perhaps a bit spooked by her yelling. But a bloody trail followed it. Callie realized her job was only half done. Granny Ethel always said that the first thing to do with injured patients was to stop the bleeding.

Thankfully, that had been exactly the reason why she had been sent to the forest. To gather millefolia leaves, which were used on cuts or injuries that bled a lot. Callie had seen her apply the crushed leaves on people hundreds of times. She knew what to do.

"Let me treat your injuries," Callie said to the rabbit, and showed it the herbs she had gathered, outstretching them in her palm.

The rabbit sniffed at the herbs curiously and relaxed. It even turned around for Callie to have better access to its injured foot. She was convinced that the rabbit could understand her.

She hurriedly crushed the leaves and tore off a strip of cloth from the bottom of her tunic, not caring about Mom's wrath once she found out. Oh, she would be so mad.

Carefully, Callie applied crushed millefolia leaves on the deer rabbit's injury, then wrapped the cloth around it. It was a bit difficult to tie a proper knot in the dark. The moonlight was barely peeking through the forest canopy. But in the end, with trembling fingers, she managed to secure the makeshift bandage in place.

"This should stop the bleeding," Callie said to the deer rabbit.

It tentatively placed its injured foot on the ground and hopped a small distance away. The rabbit definitely limped a little, but it could place some weight on its injured foot, likely meaning it wasn't broken. She sighed with relief.

"Go on home!" Callie urged.

The deer rabbit looked at her with an expression that felt like a "thank you," and then its antlers began to glow faintly with warm light. She couldn't believe it. Just like candlelight, its antlers illuminated the area around it. The light felt soothing, and seeing it convinced Callie that she had done the right thing.

Her chest filled with pride. For the first time in her life, she had this strong, warm feeling within herself. That this was what she was always meant to do.

But the feeling didn't last for long. Callie needed to get back home, but she didn't have a clue where the path was, no matter how much she looked. It could be in any direction since everything looked the same in the dark.

As her body trembled with fear, the rabbit jumped a short distance away and then stopped, looking at her expectantly. Almost as if it was telling her to follow.

It was the only source of light in the dark forest. Callie had no reason to doubt it, so she followed. Perhaps it could lead her to a safe place where she could stay for the night. Tomorrow, she could search for a way home.

The rabbit kept hopping forward, and Callie did her best to follow. But every noise in the forest suddenly seemed scary. Every hoot of the owl made her jump a little, every rustle of the wind made her heart hammer with fear. Those shadows looked awfully like wolves. Those weren't wolves, were they?

While Callie looked at every weird shadow in panic, the rabbit had jumped a good distance away, its light like a faint flicker somewhere ahead.

"W-Wait for me!" Callie called out and rushed after the deer rabbit. In her haste, she tripped over a tree root and painfully scraped her knee.

A small yelp of pain escaped her lips as Callie reached down to touch her knee. It hurt so much. Was that blood? Warm and wet, there was little doubt that she was bleeding. Callie considered using the millefollia leaves she had gathered, but the rabbit's light was getting farther and farther away. She didn't have time to apply the leaves, so Callie ignored the pain as best as she could and kept moving.

"Wait!"

As Callie ran after it, she accidentally ran into a rocky wall of something kind. After feeling it for a bit, she realized it was a cave.

The deer rabbit's light came from what looked like a cave opening. Thinking about it, a cave wasn't the worst place to spend the night. Callie wouldn't get wet in case it began to rain, and surely if the rabbit went inside, it was safe.

But as Callie took a step forward, her foot slipped on something, and she fell deeper inside the cave, hitting a rock or two along the way. Everything hurt. Her hands and elbows felt raw. Her knees were bleeding so badly that she could smell the blood.

Tears streamed from Callie's eyes, although she tried not to cry. Crying never solved anything, as Dad always said. Pain ran through her body as she looked around, searching for the glow of the rabbit's antlers. But everything was dark. Where did it disappear?

A low growl reverberated through the cave, followed by two loud, heavy footsteps. Callie could feel the heavy vibrations through her whole body.

"AHHHHH!" The girl screamed. Only something very large could create vibrations like that. Was it a bear?! Would it tear her to shreds with its claws?

Another growl reverberated, followed by a more intense thump which shook the whole cave. She feared it would collapse on her head; that's how violently it shook.

But instead, thousands of tiny glowing lights lit up all at once in the cavern, illuminating it. Callie realized that those were fireflies that lit up because of the disturbance. But there was something more impressive in front of her—the large creature that was with her in the cave. It was not a bear.

Dark green scales covered its incredibly long body. And it wasn't just long. It was big as well. Callie couldn't believe that this massive creature, which was undoubtedly bigger than her house, could fit in here. It had huge claws, two large horns on its head, and spikes running all the way down its back to the tip of its tail.

Wait, were those wings folded on its back? It was a bit difficult to tell with fireflies as the only source of light, but those looked like wings. Most incredibly of all were the two deep amber eyes gazing at Callie from a lizard-like head.

It couldn't be. Dragons were extinct.

Yet here one was, alive and massive and staring straight at her. It took every last bit of her willpower not to scream or freak out or start openly gushing over the wondrous creature.

But that was a real, live DRAGON. It might as well swallow her whole, and Callie wouldn't care. She would die happy in its belly.

Dragons weren't extinct! They were very much alive, and one of them was right in front of her. Out of all arcane beasts to meet, Callie met the king of them all. The creatures whose very presence created the magic of this world. Nothing could have made her happier.

Callie took a step closer, wanting to see the dragon better. It let out a low coo. Not a warning. More like a question, although she had no way of knowing what it wanted. But it was trying to say something. Callie's heart beat with excitement, thundering in her ears.

"I-I am C-Calluna," she stammered, feeling a dopey grin form on her face.

The dragon blinked at Callie curiously. She hoped it was curiosity and not hunger in its eyes. It took a deep breath. For a second, she thought it would turn her into a roasted human girl, so she pressed her eyes shut. If this were her end, then Callie would accept it.

Its breath washed over her like warm spring wind, soothing and pleasant. Opening her eyes, Callie saw a leafy sprout emerge from the cave floor, which quickly grew in size until it turned into a flower bud. The flower bud opened, forming a cup-shaped white flower which glowed ever so lightly in the dark.

"Wow…" Callie exhaled, taking a step closer. It was so beautiful. She had never seen a flower as beautiful as this one.

The dragon extended its front paw and touched the flower with one of its claws, making it pop open. Inside was a clear, slightly shimmering liquid. Looking at it made Callie realize how thirsty she was.

The cave rumbled slightly as the dragon took a few steps back and pointed at the flower with its snout. Fireflies lit up with new vigor at the disturbance, and Callie gawked at the scene in disbelief.

"You want me to drink this?" She asked.

Not only had she met a dragon, not only did it not eat her, it even offered Callie this magical water to drink. This was undoubtedly the happiest day of her life.

The dragon inclined its head ever so slightly in what she assumed was confirmation.

Callie didn't have to be told twice. With trembling fingers, she reached out towards the flower and grabbed it. It came off from its stem with surprising ease, and the girl drank the water inside. It was cold and refreshing, and with each sip that she took, she could feel the pains and aches previously plaguing her body disappear. It was healing her.

But as Callie drank, she also felt something else. A strange heat was bubbling somewhere within her chest that grew hotter with each sip. Unbearable almost. But as the dragon observed her drinking with its amber eyes, Callie knew that not finishing all the liquid would be incredibly rude. She didn't want to be rude to a dragon, so she drank it all until the last drop.

The moment she was done, the heat within her reached a boiling point, spreading through her whole body. She clutched her chest as wildfire took over her, making Callie want to scream. It was too much to endure.

The last thing Callie saw before passing out was the bright light shining from her forearm and the amber eyes of the dragon watching.
