The battle for the northern wall slowly settled into a grim, mechanical purgatory.

The initial, explosive panic of the dawn assault had burned itself out, violently replaced by a dull, relentless war of attrition. The writhing ocean of rotting meat simply continued to crash against the solid oak logs, an endless, shrieking tide that felt disconnected from the reality of living, breathing combat.

Standing on the reinforced central platform, Josh watched the grinding rhythm of the siege play out with a heavy, sinking heart.

The volume of defensive fire had dropped precipitously. The sky above the plains was no longer black with falling arrows. The civilian militia, their fingers raw and bleeding from the coarse hemp strings of their bows, were now firing sporadically. They were desperately attempting to ration their rapidly depleting quivers, only letting loose when a ghoul managed to climb within a few feet of the upper parapet.

The magical bombardment had suffered a similar decline. The blinding flashes of elemental fire and the crackling arcs of lightning had dwindled to occasional, desperate bursts.

Josh leaned over the wooden railing, his heavy steel armour groaning. He hoisted a large, jagged piece of shattered masonry to his chest, waiting patiently until a small cluster of zombies had clawed their way twenty feet up the timber wall. He dropped the heavy stone, watching it crush three of the monsters and send them tumbling backward into the mud.

It was effective, but it felt entirely pointless. For every zombie he crushed, two more immediately scrambled over the ruined bodies to take their place.

Further down the line, a hollow tower of interlocking ghouls began to rise against the wall. Josh stared at them for a moment. They were all dressed in similar clothing, a uniform of red and white. He wondered if they had all been performers together when they lived.

Brett stared at the same group, but did not immediately unleash a torrent of plasma. The elemental mage stood perfectly still, his pale face slick with sweat, his jaw clenched tight as he watched the horrifying structure grow. He waited until the tower was mere seconds from spilling over the parapet, maximising the structural vulnerability, before firing a single, highly compressed orb of white heat to shatter the base.

The tower collapsed, and the militia cheered weakly, but Brett simply slumped against the railing, his breath coming in ragged, painful gasps.

"We are bleeding out," Josh murmured, his storm-grey eyes scanning the exhausted, shivering faces of the defenders lining the walkway. "I hate to say it, but Brett was right. If the Bone General sends those heavy skeletal knights forward right now, they will walk over us without breaking a sweat."

He turned away from the parapet, his mind working rapidly. The brute force tactics of the morning were failing. They needed to change the rules of the engagement, or the city was going to fall before the sun even reached its peak.

"Brett, hold this platform with Bhel," Josh commanded quietly, stepping back from the edge of the wall. He turned to the elven healer, gesturing with a heavy, steel-clad hand. "Carcan, come with me. We need to find Caistina immediately."

Carcan did not question the order. She nodded, gripping her amber-topped staff, and followed the towering heavy fighter as he pushed his way through the crowded, blood-slicked walkway.

They moved away from the main gatehouse, navigating the chaotic rear lines of the ramparts. The space directly behind the parapets was a miserable, desperate sight. Exhausted militiamen sat slumped against supply crates, their heads buried in their hands. Minor wounds were being hastily wrapped with torn strips of linen. The air smelled strongly of sweat, fear, and the coppery tang of fresh blood.

They found Caistina Tharistrar near the eastern watchtower.

The elven mage was standing around a large, hastily erected wooden table covered in architectural blueprints of the city walls. She was flanked by Captain Aris and two other heavily armoured commanders of the city guard. They all looked incredibly grim, pointing at various weak points along the defensive perimeter.

"The eastern flank is requesting more pitch, Lady Caistina," one of the guard captains was arguing, his voice hoarse and desperate. "The corpses are stacking too high. We need to burn them out."

"We do not have more pitch," Caistina replied, her melodic voice tight with severe stress. "The central reserves are entirely depleted. You will have to rely on falling masonry and spears."

"Caistina," Josh interrupted, stepping up to the wooden table. His massive, steel-clad presence instantly commanded the attention of the stressed officers.

Caistina looked up, her amethyst eyes flashing with a mixture of relief and deep concern. "Josh. Carcan. Is the central gatehouse secure? Has the portcullis buckled?"

"The gates are holding," Josh confirmed, resting his heavy gauntlets on the edge of the map table. "But the men on the wall are failing. We are playing directly into the Bone General's hands. He is using the lesser zombies as a meat grinder, and we are happily feeding our stamina and mana directly into it."

Captain Aris frowned, his scarred brow furrowing defensively. "We are repelling the boarders, Guardian. What else would you have us do? Let them climb over the parapets and slaughter the militia?"

"I would have us manage our resources," Josh countered firmly, refusing to be intimidated by the senior officer. He pointed back towards the long, winding line of the ramparts. "Look at your people, Captain. They are exhausted. The archers cannot draw their strings, and the mages are suffering severe mana sickness. If the heavies charge, we will have absolutely nothing left in the tank to stop them."

"He is correct," Carcan added, her serene, authoritative voice cutting through the rising tension. She stepped forward, her silver eyes locking onto the guard captains. "From a medical perspective, you are pushing these conscripts beyond the limits of their endurance. Lactic acid buildup and magical depletion will cause a total, systemic collapse of the defensive line within the hour. Fear is keeping them upright, but their bodies are actively failing."

Caistina crossed her arms over her sapphire-blue robes, her sharp mind instantly grasping the horrific tactical reality. "What is your proposal, Josh?"

"We need to completely restructure the defense," Josh stated, outlining the plan he and Brett had been quietly formulating while watching the towers fall. "First, we stop the sporadic, panicked casting. We need to establish a strict, rotating roster of high-tier mages. Their sole purpose will be to conserve their mana pools and watch for the undead flesh towers. They do not fire at individual ghouls. They wait until a tower reaches critical mass, and then they destroy the foundation. One spell, maximum casualties."

"That requires immense discipline," Aris argued, shaking his head. "If the militia sees a tower rising and the mages doing nothing, they will panic and break ranks."

"Then we anchor them with strike squads," Josh replied smoothly. "You have dozens of high-level adventurers pinned down in static positions along this wall, acting like regular spearmen. It is a waste of their mobility and strength. Pull the heavy fighters off the front line. Form roving squads of three or four veterans. When a breach forms, or a tower gets too close, the strike squad sprints to that location, clears the parapet, and moves on."

The three guard captains exchanged a long, deeply conflicted look. The plan went against all traditional siege doctrine. They were essentially proposing to leave the actual front line in the hands of the terrified civilian militia, relying entirely on highly mobile veteran units to plug the inevitable holes.

"It is too risky," the eastern flank captain protested loudly. "If a strike squad is bogged down on the left flank, and a breach occurs on the right, the militia will be slaughtered before help arrives."

"They will be slaughtered anyway if they collapse from exhaustion," Josh growled, his storm-grey eyes burning with uncompromising conviction. He leaned over the table, his imposing size forcing the captain to take a half-step backward. "You are treating this like a normal war. It is not. The enemy does not tire. We do. Which brings me to the final part of the plan."

Josh looked directly at Caistina. "We need to order one half of the current wall defenders to step back from the parapets immediately."

The silence that followed the demand was absolute. Even Captain Aris stared at the human Guardian in profound, open-mouthed disbelief.

"You want us to halve our defensive strength?" Aris finally choked out, his face turning a mottled shade of red. "While ten thousand monsters are actively trying to climb the walls?"

"Yes," Josh confirmed, completely unwavering. "We order them to step back, sit down on the walkway, and rest. They drink water, they bandage their hands, and they let their mana cores slowly regenerate. They stay close enough to grab their weapons if a massive push happens, but they are off duty. In two hours, they swap with the men currently fighting on the edge. A continuous rotation."

"That could work," Caistina breathed, her amethyst eyes widening as the elegance of the brutal logistics settled into her mind.

"It is madness!" Aris argued fiercely. "The walkway is already too crowded. If we pull half the men back, the defenders on the edge will be completely overwhelmed by the sheer volume of the climbing dead!"

"No, they will not," Josh countered, his voice a low, steady rumble of tactical certainty. "Because pulling half the men back creates physical space. Right now, the militia is packed shoulder to shoulder. They cannot swing a sword without hitting the man next to them. If we thin the line, the remaining defenders have room to brace, to breathe, and to actually fight. Furthermore, it creates a clear running lane behind the front ranks for the roving strike squads to move freely."

Caistina looked at the large parchment map, her long, elegant fingers tracing the lines of the timber ramparts. She could see the logic unfolding. It was a terrifying gamble, deliberately weakening the immediate physical barrier to preserve the long-term stamina of the army, but it was the only mathematical path to survival.

"Josh is right," Caistina declared, raising her head. Her voice carried the final, unquestionable authority of the Guild command. "We implement the shift rotation immediately."

Aris looked as though he wanted to argue further, but the sheer, commanding presence of the elven mage silenced him. He offered a stiff, incredibly reluctant salute.

"As you command, Lady Caistina," Aris sighed, turning to his subordinate captains. "You heard the orders. Form the mage rotas. Identify your highest-tier fighters and pull them off the parapets to form the strike squads. And start pulling the odd-numbered militia ranks back for a rest cycle."

The guard captains scattered, running down the length of the wooden walkway to begin shouting the new, highly controversial orders to the exhausted sergeants.

"It was a bold tactical proposal, Josh," Caistina noted softly, a genuine, deeply respectful smile touching her lips. She reached out, grasping her pristine white staff. "You are thinking like a true commander. Not just surviving the next swing of the sword, but planning for the survival of the entire city."

"I am just trying to make sure my friends don't bleed out before the real fight even starts," Josh replied honestly, feeling the heavy, coiled tension in his chest ease slightly now that the commanders had accepted the plan.

"Well, if we are rotating the fresh troops," Caistina said, her sapphire-blue robes swishing as she stepped out from behind the wooden map table. "Then it is time I contributed to the magical rota. I have been hoarding my mana for hours while your young friend Brett melts the front lines. It is time I took a shift on the wall."

Josh and Carcan nodded, turning to lead the elven mage back towards the central gatehouse platform.

As they walked back through the crowded rear lines of the ramparts, the immediate, profound effect of the new tactical orders became incredibly apparent.

The hoarse shouts of the guard sergeants echoed over the wind, ordering specific ranks of the militia to step back. The initial reaction was widespread confusion, quickly followed by absolute, staggering relief.

Exhausted bakers, merchants, and young farm boys practically collapsed onto the wooden floorboards the moment they were permitted to step away from the terrifying edge of the parapet. They leaned against the inner barricades, dropping their crude spears and burying their faces in their soot-stained hands, weeping openly with sheer gratitude for the brief, temporary reprieve.

Crucially, the withdrawal of half the defenders instantly changed the physical dynamic of the wall. The claustrophobic, suffocating crush of bodies evaporated. The fighters remaining on the parapets suddenly had room to plant their boots, to draw their bowstrings fully without elbowing their neighbour, and to breathe the freezing air.

A clear, three-foot-wide lane opened up directly behind the fighting line, running parallel to the wall. It was the perfect, unobstructed highway for the newly formed strike squads to sprint towards any potential breaches.

A ragged, exhausted cheer of genuine relief rippled down the length of the timber ramparts. The morale of the defenders, which had been dangerously close to shattering completely, was suddenly, miraculously bolstered by the simple promise of eventual rest.

Josh walked through the newly created space, feeling a profound sense of satisfaction. The plan was working perfectly.

As they approached the central platform, the first official shift rotation was already taking place. Fresh volunteers, primarily local citizens who had previously been assigned to the secondary street barricades, were being brought up the steep wooden stairs to replace the most heavily injured and exhausted militiamen on the front line.

Josh paused, stepping aside to let a small group of fresh, terrified-looking volunteers move past him toward the parapet.

He watched them go, offering a silent nod of encouragement, when a familiar, broad-shouldered figure caught his eye.

The man was stepping confidently toward the edge of the wooden wall. He was wearing a thick, heavy leather apron over a simple woollen tunic. He carried a heavy, utilitarian iron broadsword in his right hand, resting the blade casually against his shoulder.

But it was the armour on his head that made Josh's breath catch in his throat.

The man was wearing a massive, heavily dented steel greathelm. It was the exact same helmet Josh had worn for months, the one he had returned just hours ago, with a profound apology for the deep scratches near the temple piece.

"Garrik," Josh breathed softly, stepping out of the running lane.

The tavern landlord turned at the sound of his name. Garrik looked older in the harsh, pale light, the deep lines around his eyes completely visible through the narrow T-shaped visor of the steel helm. But his posture was unbroken. He did not shake, and he did not look down at the writhing sea of zombies with terror. He looked at the horde with the cold, assessing gaze of a man who had spent years fighting in the dark.

Garrik offered Josh a slow, deeply respectful nod, adjusting his grip on the heavy iron broadsword.

"I was just going to stay at the inner checkpoints… stay safe," Garrik said, his voice echoing slightly from within the steel helm. He offered a small, grim smile that did not reach his eyes. "But Barb told me to get up here and make sure you boys do not do anything incredibly stupid. And I always listen to my wife."

Josh stared at the tavern keeper, a massive, overwhelming surge of emotion tightening his throat. The sheer, unyielding bravery of the common people of Ashenfall was a terrifying, beautiful thing to witness.

"Hold the line, Garrik," Josh commanded softly, his voice thick with profound respect.

"I always do," Garrik replied firmly with a smile.

The tavern landlord turned away, stepping up to the wooden parapet. He planted his boots shoulder-width apart, raising his iron broadsword, and prepared to face the endless, rotting tide to buy his city another hour of life.
