They didn’t move as a group. That would be foolish.

For as busy as Marcel was firming his grip on the remaining forces of the expedition, it would have been obvious when word began to quietly spread among the ranks. Whispered questions, testing the waters with members, were hurriedly asked. Time was short, and their window of opportunity even shorter. Swift messengers whose loyalty was without question were dispatched across the city, both to people who were stationed as defense for the battle that had never come, and to…other parties.

Supplies were gathered, blades were sharpened…and yet, there was a hope they wouldn’t have to be used against other members of the expedition. They bore no sin. They had committed no crime. The only sin lay on the shoulders of a single, ambitious Gnoll, who saw an opportunity for greater glory…

And grasped for it.

Instead, the forces of those among the expedition who still believed in the mission of Nathaniel Hart…turned their ambitions elsewhere.

To the freedom of their leader.

Sylvia could acknowledge she was the heart of it all. The focal point of this little rebellion of theirs. A mutiny, really. Bella had pointed that out with a muffled laugh, and she hadn’t been able to gainsay her.

It was an appropriate term.

With the help of both the Pirate Captain and the officers of the Polaris Reach…this was possible. They had already deactivated and secured the mobile ward stone that guaranteed safe passage beyond the walls of Rhoscara. Without it, it didn’t matter what glory Marcel sought to secure within the Barren Forest. He wouldn’t be able to reach it without choking to death on the apparently Divinity-poisoned Aether of Vereden.

That gave them enough time to assault the palace and rescue their leader, from wherever it was he had been locked away. After that…

Sylvia couldn’t help but hope that Nathan would have an idea of what to do then. Perhaps with him freed, he could reassert control over the expedition, and then they could do what they came for.

To drive the Velancian forces of the Savoy away from the city of Rhoscara. It was a naïve hope, Sylvia was willing to admit.

But she had never considered herself a cynic.

It was as they were slowly moving supplies and personnel out of the expedition base camp that the reply to their outbound message came.

If not in an…unexpected manner.

The heart of their conspiracy, as Sylvia, Bella, and Maria coordinated matters, had been set up within the small room that had once housed the ward stone. They were even using the flat surface of the plinth as a makeshift table, to rest a map they could plan movements around. The three of them were huddled around it as they did so. Two Polaris guards whose loyalty was not in question had been stationed outside of it, to watch and receive messages as they arrived.

Sylvia hadn’t expected the form this one had come in.

“Where have you been?!” She hissed at the abashed form of Bleddyn, who had been ushered into their little command room. He had the good graces to wince at her tone, but not much. The huge Mynnyd man had to hunch his shoulders to even fit in here.

“Getting’ ready,” Bleddyn whispered back. “As soon as that Savoy fucker had twisted the lot of these stunties around his little finger, I knew we were done here. S’good thing the Florens seemta have forgotten me and me men, which is odd enough as it is. Because our part in the battle was always as the backup plan. We were meant to evacuate people in case the battle all goes to shit. And…,” He looked around almost smugly at the surprised faces of three very different women. “I’d rightly say it has.”

“Of course,” Maria breathed, shaking off her shock. “It all makes sense. The specialty of the Unbound was always rescuing people from a bad situation. But…where can we possibly go, if we have to leave? It’s not like it’s safe outside the wards.”

“Yer right, it ain’t,” Bleddyn nodded easily. He suddenly smirked. “But it is below ‘em. The corruption don’t touch underground, and the thing about these old Dwarven cities? They go deep. They got this whole history of diggin’ tunnels that snake all about the Principality, miles below it. Most of ‘em are collapsed, either on purpose or from the years. But the Unbound?” He shrugged, still looking smug. “How do ye think we’ve been gettin’ around so easily to snatch freed slaves in the first place?”

As Sylvia and Maria exchanged excited glances, Bella leaned forward in sudden interest. “Where do these here tunnels lead?”

Finally, Bleddyn’s enthusiasm dimmed. He scratched his cheek awkwardly. “Well…that’s the problem. Here in Rhoscara, the only paths we’ve got go either south…or east. And I don’t recommend us goin’ south. Them tunnels are tight. So. If we’re getting’ out of the city…we gotta go east, and that leads to the main base of the Unbound here in Velancia.”

Sylvia paused, suddenly. “East?” She murmured. “What’s east of-“ She stopped mid-sentence in realization, her eyes flying open. “No. Your main base is really there?” At Bleddyn’s almost sheepish nod, she couldn’t help but shake her head in amazement. “I…admire the temerity, I suppose. I’m certain Nathan won't object too hard when we rescue him.”

“I don’t know the Principality very well,” Maria furrowed her brow in thought. “What is east of Rhoscara?”

Surprisingly, the answer came from neither Bleddyn nor Sylvia. Instead, a somber Bella tapped a finger on the map, some distance away from the city. The area was unmarked, and yet she seemed to know exactly where to point. “Nate used to talk about it, when I got ‘im drunk enough. It’s destroyed, now,” She said in a strangely wistful tone. “But it used to be a little Dwarven plantation…”

“By the name of Addersfield.”

……………………………………

They masked their movement with those of the Elves. The remaining Captains had expected the Elves to depart with the capture of Alveron, and Marcel had even advocated for it. The meeting where they decided the fate of the expedition seemed to have ended by the time they had finished their preparations. They were lucky that the-

(Sylvia hesitated to call them traitors. The only person she really considered traitorous at all was Marcel. Everyone else, as far as she was concerned, were simply being misled.)

-separatists had yet to discover their theft of the ward stone. Within the wards of Rhoscara, it was sometimes hard to sense the presence of the mobile field provided by the Astray’s stone. But that wouldn’t last forever.

Each of the ring leaders of what Sylvia was ironically thinking of as the ‘Loyalists’ had split up and stashed away into one of the small house carriages of the Elves. Enough of them could speak the common language of Vereden that the Elves understood that they were stowing away to rescue the captured leaders of their people. Of the hundreds of carriages that rattled away into the streets of Rhoscara, led by agents of the Unbound to these underground entrances. From what she understood, the Elves were going to have to abandon many of these mobile homes. There just wasn’t enough room down there.

They had received this information with a level of stoic understanding that Sylvia was frankly uncomfortable with.

But of those carriages that were leaving the safety of the expedition, fully one third of them were carrying either soldiers or supplies that had been…commandeered.

It wasn’t much. Perhaps a few hundred soldiers, mainly from the Polaris Reach, and enough weaponry and sundries to supply them for a few weeks, at best. Sylvia had barely had time to grab a handful of hers and Nathan’s personal effects from their shared room before the Elves had started to depart.

She watched from the concealed window slats of the carriage she had managed to sneak into, eyes on the Captains watching from the Elves leave the safety of the expedition. While Marcel just looked satisfied, Gustave had the good graces to look at least a little ashamed, while Nyx…

It was always hard to read her fellow Sculpted. Even among her people, the onyx woman was famed for her stoicism.

Still…

Long hours spent in her presence let Sylvia detect a hint of discomfort in Nyx’s gaze.

Sylvia sighed, shaking her head and turning away from the slats. Instead, she directed her gaze to the Elven family inside the carriage with her. The father was up on the driver's bench, directing the Chamels through the press of the other carriages and maneuvering through the streets of the city. But his wife and two children were down here with her. “Thank you,” Sylvia whispered, dipping her head to the visibly weary, brown-haired Elven woman. She clutched a babe no more than a few months old to her breast, while a stoic-faced young boy with long, pointed ears deliberately didn’t look at Sylvia. Still, he clung to his mother’s hand nonetheless. “We won’t bother you for long.”

The tired grey eyes of the Elven mother studied her for a moment and then nodded. She didn’t speak, though.

The rest of the ride through the city streets was…mildly uncomfortable, for Sylvia. Not because of the silence from the Elves within the carriage, however.

Instead, it was the infant.

The child both mildly unsettled Sylvia…

And intrigued her.

As if she were in the carriage with her, Sylvia could have sworn she heard Honoka’s voice echoing in her ears.

“You might be able to have natural children of your own,” Sylvia recalled her mentor telling her, in the aftermath of her Second Breakthrough, shocking her half to death. The aged Kawamaran woman had just laughed at Sylvia’s startlement. “Yes, it’s true. We’re not sure, of course. But the way Cultivator Sculpted are panning out, it’s an actual possibility. With your newest Breakthrough, you’ve developed quite a few organs of your own, within that pseudo-flesh shell of yours. Including a womb, and all of the necessary bits and bobs required for the process. However, you’re still barren as of now. Bluntly speaking, you have no eggs. You can be thankful for that.” Honoka had smirked. “I certainly don’t miss my monthlys. But…we’re not certain what will happen once a Cultivator Sculpted, especially a female one, reaches the Third Breakthrough. For all we know, you’ll morph enough then that you’ll start to produce viable eggs. When that happens…there’s a possibility your boy toy could knock you up.”

“Honoka!” Sylvia had laughed, scandalized at her master’s frank assessment.

Honoka had smiled at her, then, with a strangely sad tint to the expression. “If that happens, though…that’ll be your only chance,” A far-off look had entered her fiery eyes then. “If you waste the three to four hundreds stretch of time and hit the Fourth Breakthrough, there’s a near-zero chance you’d be capable of conceiving. It’s…just not possible for higher-level Classers, by that point. Too much of our existence is leveraged into maintaining our metaphysical selves to properly bring new life into the world. Sylvia…when you get to that point, I urge you to give the matter some more serious thought.”

Unsettled by Honoka’s unusually serious speech, Sylvia had given her word that she would.

She had never said a word about the Healer’s advice. Not to anyone.

Not even to Nate. Sylvia was just too…unsettled by the idea.

And so, she tried not to think about it. But both the sight of the young babe at its mother’s breast, and the very real threat to Nathan’s life right now…

It was making Sylvia consider…

Things, perhaps a bit more deeply.

…………………………..

Sylvia barely paid attention to the oddly intricate carvings upon the underground walls, where the Unbound had led the Elves. They had disembarked at a large underground cavern that just barely managed to fit the refugees, the fleeing loyalist expedition members…

And the forces of the Unbound.

There were far more of them than Sylvia had been expecting. But then again…

How else would Bleddyn have been able to harry an entire nation of slave owners, though, unless he had a large force at his beck and call?

There must have been well over a thousand former slaves down here, in this cavern. They might have rivaled the forces of the expedition at its height in sheer numbers, and they had constructed an entire fortified camp here. Tents of all shapes and sizes stretched out as far as Sylvia could see, fulfilling all kinds of functions. Outright barricades blocked off a number of different massive tunnels that stretched off into the distance far out of sight, all the while former slaves bristling with weaponry readied themselves.

Bleddyn had already told her that wasn’t even all of them. There were more waiting back in the cavern under Addersfield. And it was all very interesting, of course…

But she was focused on something else right now.

The gathered forces of her loyalists, who were going to accompany her on her mission to save Nathan.

Two dozen. That was all that Sylvia was willing to bring on an assault of this magnitude. Preferably, they would have done this as a stealth operation, but Sylvia wasn’t sure that was possible. Undoubtedly, Nathan would be well-guarded, and there was only so much that blades in the dark were capable of. There certainly weren’t enough operatives from the bad old days of the Nocturne Division and SED to be able to pull it off on their own.

No…

They had to go in hot, hard, and fast if they wanted to pull this off.

Honestly, two dozen might be too much.

But that was a chance they were just going to have to take.

Sylvia stood around a large planning table in the command tent of the Unbound, a large, canvas affair. Upon the table stretched a surprisingly detailed map of Rhoscara itself, and around it were both the leaders of the Unbound, but also those of her loyalists.

Bleddyn studied the map for a moment. “Alright,” He eventually nodded. “We’ll do it like this. Our scouts have told us that a number of different Savoy platoons have spread out into the city, apparently with the blessin’ of Prince Elysael.”

“Fucking traitor,” One of the Unbound Captains hissed. The woman clamped her mouth shut, though, when Bleddyn shot her a warning look.

Shaking his head, Bleddyn continued. “We’re goin’ to hit them for ye,” He nodded at Sylvia. “My men will ambush ‘em and try to kill as many Savoy dogs as we can, before fadin’ away. At the same time, we’ve got a special surprise for their forces at the docks. Don’t we, boys?”

Gallows chuckles were his answer, and Sylvia could well imagine just what ‘surprise’ was in store for the Savoy forces.

It wasn’t that hard to extrapolate some of the plans that had already been made for the invasion, and guess what he was going to do.

“It’ll be our distraction for ya,” Bleddyn smirked. “And the signal for when ye should start. Ye won’t be able to miss it when it happens, I promise ye. That all sound good to you?”

Sylvia paused for a moment and directed her gaze at the two dozen she intended to bring with her on this mission. All of them had been able to fit in the tent, and she had felt that it was only right that they heard the plans for it.

Renauld, standing off to the side, clutching his staff a burning, determined anger in his eyes.

Maria, quiet and still at her side, ever faithful, ever reliable.

Bella, her pipe lit and held to her lips. She nodded evenly at Sylvia as her gaze passed her by.

Walter, the surprisingly plate-mail-clad Squire of Nathan’s, a stoic, steadfast energy hanging about him like a cloak, a bastard sword slung over his back.

Meia, the Half-Orc woman guardian of the ward stone, her arms crossed over her chest and almost seeming eager for battle.

Fade, the full-grown Spirit Wolf sitting on his haunches directly to Sylvia’s left, a nearly feral look in his emerald eyes.

And seventeen other members of the expedition. People from the Polaris Reach, the former intelligence agencies, the Army, the Pirates…

And even one of the Elves had volunteered to come along.

Kierla, the young scout that had led them to Alveron in the first place. The teenage Elf was nearly vibrating in place as she clutched her bow close to her chest. It wasn’t fear that caused the shaking, though.

It seemed to be excitement.

It…would have to be enough.

Sylvia turned back to Bleddyn…

And nodded.

“Let’s do this.”
