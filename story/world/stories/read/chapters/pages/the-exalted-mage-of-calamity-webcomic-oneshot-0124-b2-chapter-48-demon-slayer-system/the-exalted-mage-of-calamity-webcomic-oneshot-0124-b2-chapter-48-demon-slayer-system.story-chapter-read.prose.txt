“This. This. Is. AMAZING!”

Gwen ran through the inner halls of the airship until she stopped in front of Veronica’s room.

“That’s the fourth time you’ve said that,” Veronica said, her attention still fixed on hanging her clothes in the closet.

The three of them were inside the airship Veronica had bought the day before. After packing all their belongings from the Maristar Gold Inn, they transferred everything onto the airship and set sail. Or, more specifically, set lift. The ship was currently flying a few hundred meters in the air, hovering above the city.

“Yes, but it needs repeating!” Gwen squealed. “Five bedrooms, a shower that’s practically a large pool, a kitchenette with fully stocked pantries, a massive rimebox, automated stoves, and did I forget to mention how comfortable the beds are?!”

Veronica chuckled, closing the closet door. She turned to face her just as Nasha came down the hall.

“This entire airship is very impressive. Everything you’d ever want is up here,” Nasha said.

Gwen turned to face her, nodding enthusiastically. She looked like an excited dog that had finally found its home after being lost for weeks.

“I was honestly against the idea of living up here because I’m really bad with heights, but as long as I don’t look down, this place is paradise,” Gwen said.

“I’m glad you like it,” Veronica said. She took a sweeping glance over her room.

It came fully furnished: a luxurious queen-sized bed with soft covers and an even fluffier collection of pillows. Thick, fuzzy carpet covered the entire floor, broken only by a large circular rug woven in vibrant colors. An abstract design twisted across it, though she couldn’t quite place what it was meant to be. Flowers, perhaps, or maybe clouds. Given where they were, either would have been appropriate.

Several polished dressers occupied the walls with gleaming brass handles, all shining beneath the warm lamplights installed in the ceiling. There were plenty of drawers and even a small personal table in the corner with several tiers, along with recessed shelves and compartments. A cushioned armchair rested near a narrow bookshelf beside a window that peered down at the city below.

The room wasn’t large, but it wasn’t small either. Anything she needed was within arm’s reach.

“Have you finished unpacking?” Nasha asked.

“Yeah,” Veronica said, turning to face them. “What about you girls?”

“Yep! Got everything unpacked and organized!” Gwen said.

“Same here,” Nasha said.

“Good. For the foreseeable future, this’ll be our base of operations. There isn’t much that’ll beat having an airship as our living quarters. We’ll be able to go anywhere we want in Vitian like this. Much quicker, too. And we won’t have to keep paying for stays at an inn.”

“Oh, I’m never leaving. I want to live here forever,” Gwen chirped.

“So? Does it have a name?” Nasha asked.

Veronica’s face shifted into confusion. “A name?”

“Oh! Good idea!” Gwen flicked her gaze to Nasha, balling her fists near her chest with a short jolt. Then she turned back to Veronica. “We have to have a name for this place, right? We can’t just call her ‘base’ or ‘ship.’”

Veronica crossed her arms, tilting her head in thought. “A name, huh…”

In the future, she’d never used an airship before. At least, not personally. She had been on several of them, though. Martin was always enthusiastic about giving his opinion on each and every new model he created.

If she needed a name for an airship, then…

“How about… Mirabelle?”

“Not bad,” Nasha said, tilting her head as she considered it. “Where’d that come from?”

“It’s the name of the first airship Martin ever created.”

“This Martin guy also made airships? What hasn’t he created?” Gwen asked, wide-mouthed.

“He was the Arkavail Empire’s chief inventor. A master when it came to magitech. There probably isn’t anything he hasn’t at least touched on. He did create Sage, after all, and he’s the only reason I’m able to talk to you both.”

“Mirabelle it is then,” Gwen said with a smile. “Ooh, should we have a crew name? Like… ‘The Plunderers!’ Or since we’re saving the world, ‘Saviors X!’”

“What’s with the ‘X?’” Veronica asked.

“Dunno. Sounds cool.”

Nasha chuckled, causing Veronica to do the same, and eventually, the three of them fell into a chorus of laughter.

“How about we leave the crew name for next time,” Veronica said. “I need to finish upgrading Sage’s quest. She’s been bugging me about it since we left the auction yesterday.”

“We’ll leave you to it then,” Nasha said. She turned to Gwen. “Want to help me make breakfast?”

“You know how to cook?” Gwen asked with a head tilt.

“No.”

“Huh?”

“That’s why you’ll be helping me. You worked as a restaurant server before, right? Should mean you know a thing or two,” she said with a smile, linking her arm through Gwen’s and pulling her away.

Veronica watched the two of them head down the hall toward the kitchenette.

She shut the door, crossed to her bedside drawer, and pulled out one of the two corrupted monster cores she had won during the auction. It belonged to some B-rank flying beast, the kind of creature only mages around the sixth tier could reliably fight.

It was spherical, cool to the touch, and roughly the size of an apple, though it carried the density of steel. It resembled a forged glass orb, with murky, translucent frozen mist settled within. Merged through that mist was a darker black sludge, almost like curdled blood. Demonic corruption.

Veronica also pulled out the small gemstone, the one that had been embedded in Hevestra’s head. It was a peculiar thing, unlike anything she had seen before. Even the concept of it was strange: an artifact that could absorb any type of energy? It was unheard of, even in the future.

“Alright, Sage, what do I do with these?” Veronica asked, sitting on the edge of the bed.

[Hold both items in separate palms, and circulate your mana into your hands.]

Veronica did as Sage asked. With the gemstone in her left hand and the monster core in her right, she began moving her mana. The gemstone shimmered, already absorbing her mana as the light within shifted between white and blue. On her other hand, the monster core instead began to rumble.

[Please concentrate and try to imagine linking your mana from the gemstone to the monster core.]

“I’m not going to get corrupted by this, am I?” Veronica asked, tilting her head upward.

[No. According to my calculations, there is a 94% chance you will not be affected by the corruption.]

“…And what about the other six percent?”

[Your arm may be corrupted momentarily, but I can help stop the corruption from spreading past your wrist. You will need to expel the corruption yourself afterward.]

Her face tightened into a frown. “You’re saying that like it’s easy to get rid of demonic corruption. Especially if it’s already corrupted my own hand.”

[Risks for the results of science.]

A sigh slipped from Veronica’s lips. She had heard that phrase before.

Stupid Martin.

Closing her eyes, Veronica channeled more mana from her cores, imbuing her palms completely. She focused on the weight of each object, trying to coat them both in mana while simultaneously feeling the link from one hand to the other. Like two separate streams of water forking together into one pond.

There was a noticeable pressure; her hands slowly felt heavier. Mana continued flowing outward from her cores, but then she felt a slight resistance, as if her mana had struck a dam. She urged more mana out until she finally surpassed it. Then something changed.

Veronica’s brows flattened. The resistance returned, but not in the same way as before. It wasn’t a wall. It was more like she had encountered a crushing wave, one that pushed back against her with far more force than her own flow of mana.

“Sage? What’s happening? Why is…”

In only a few moments, her own stream of mana was overtaken. Her fingers tensed, and the weight in her hands multiplied as if she were holding iron balls. Power surged into her palms and traveled up her wrists.

She immediately panicked, wondering if it was the demonic corruption she had been worried about, but confusion replaced the fear almost at once. The monster core was in her right palm, and yet she felt the surge of power in both hands. She resisted the stream with her mana, but she was slowly losing the fight. It moved past her wrists, up her forearms, then to her elbows and even her shoulders.

Her concentration broke. Both surges shot past her flow of mana and rushed directly back into her core room. Her muscles locked for a moment, as if struck by an electric shock. Her eyes flew open as a gasp tore from her lungs. In that one instant, everything froze. Her perception turned inward involuntarily, leading into her mindscape as her consciousness traveled along her mana veins. She followed it all the way down to her core room until she finally saw it: the anomaly.

A few moments later, her body returned to her control, and she gasped in a breath of air. She heaved forward, trying to regain her composure. Her gaze dropped to her hands, which were both pressed against the edge of her bed.

Her left palm was empty. In her right palm sat the monster core. An uncorrupted monster core.

“Sage?” she said softly, her voice airy as she looked at her hands. “What just happened? Where’s the gemstone? And where did the demonic energy from this monster core go?”

Her status panel appeared as Sage’s voice echoed out.

[You have completed a quest: Defeat the demon Hevestra.]

[You have completed a quest: Acquire demonic energy.]

[Reward: Demon Slayer System Upgrade]

[You have gained a new auxiliary demon core within your core room. This core is able to absorb demonic energies and contain their power. Excess demonic energy can be used in a variety of different ways.]

[New quest assigned: Slay a demon and absorb their demonic energy.]

Veronica stared at the notification for several seconds longer than she normally would have. The more she read, the more her expression shifted from simple confusion into something much harder to place. Confusion, shock, intrigue, and astonishment all tangled together.

None of what she read seemed real. It was as if Sage had simply started spewing nonsense at her. A prank, perhaps. Because there was no possible way she had just gained another mana core—a demon core.

“Sage,” she said carefully. “Explain.”

[The gemstone previously embedded within Hevestra’s head has been absorbed and integrated into your core room as a temporary demonic core.]

Veronica’s face went still, though her complicated expression remained. “You put a foreign object directly inside my core room? One that’s been inside a demon?”

Her voice wasn’t panicked, but there was a faint alarm beneath it, as if she were asking whether she had permission to panic.

[The gemstone possesses many peculiar properties, including the ability to absorb different types of energy. After many weeks of continuous testing and experimentation, I was able to make it recognize your body as a host, similar to how Hevestra used it within their own body. After absorbing your mana, the gem’s outer vessel shattered and traveled along your mana stream until it reached your core room, where it settled inside as an auxiliary core. There should be no serious problems or side effects from this upgrade.]

“Should be?” Veronica asked. “How many tests did you run? Don’t give me some bullshit like there was only a forty percent chance of succeeding.” She looked down and noticed that, indeed, the crystal hadn’t simply disappeared. The remaining shards were scattered across the floor before her.

[I conducted 742 simulations and found the mean success rate of the entire procedure to be above 97%.]

Veronica narrowed her eyes, skeptical. “And what about outliers? What were the lower bounds of your calculations?”

[The lower bounds reached 4% and 7% at the lowest, respectively.]

Veronica’s frown deepened. Even the lines of her face seemed determined to appear. “And if the result happened to be one of those lower outliers?”

[Risks for the results of science.]

“Oh, fuck off,” Veronica said, standing up. She looked down at the monster core in her hand. She couldn’t see the dark sludge inside it anymore; the demonic corruption had been completely purified.

“So? What exactly do I get from this? Immunity to corruption and more mana? Don’t tell me I can access some type of demonic transformation now, like beastkins can with their beast forms.”

[You can now absorb demonic energy and purify it, healing certain forms of demonic corruption to a degree, including corruption affecting others. Absorbed demonic energy may also be converted into mana, replenishing your reserves without straining your mana cores. You may also use the demonic energy directly like mana, but I do not know the exact outcomes for each spell. More importantly, any excess demonic energy you do not need will be converted into energy for me to use, accelerating my processing capabilities.]

“What’s the benefit of that? Besides analyzing the gemstone and the mysterious seed we received from that elder in the Valley of Silence, there hasn’t been anything you’ve done that I would classify as ‘slow.’”

She flexed her fingers and examined her body, but she didn’t feel any different.

[I am referring to different processing capabilities. More specifically, my ability to improve your body and grant you access to greater perks. I no longer need to rely strictly on your tiered growth. Now, based on how many demons you kill, I can grant you corresponding rewards and abilities. The more you kill, the stronger you will become.]
