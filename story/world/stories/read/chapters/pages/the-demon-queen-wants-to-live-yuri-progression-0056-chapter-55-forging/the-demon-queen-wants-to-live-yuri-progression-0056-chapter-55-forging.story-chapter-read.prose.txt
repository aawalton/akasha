The world was white. I moved my hands up to shield my eyes. Heat washed over me in a massive wave. I tried to take a step back right as someone's weight slammed into me from the side. Then I was on the stone floor. It was cold against my back. The weight on top of me was anything but cold.

It was Ash. She was braced over me. One arm lay across my chest, pinning me down. The other was planted on the stone beside my head. Her body covered mine completely. Her hair had fallen forward and the ends of it brushed my cheek. The light slowly faded and my eyes adjusted. It took a few seconds for the ringing in my ears to thin into silence.

The Hero's face was very close. Close enough that I could see the grey in her otherwise blue eyes. It was at the edges, as it had been before. She was close enough that I could feel her breath against my own mouth. It was very warm. Something inside of me was humming very loudly. Was that the Ember Core, or another thing entirely? "Are you okay?" Ash asked.

"Perfectly. This," I said, and my voice came out lower than I intended, "is not one of those times where I require assistance."

The Hero's eyes widened. She looked down at me, as if she herself was only just now seeing where we were and what she had done. Her face colored, and she rose very quickly. She looked away, even as she held out her hand. I did not need the hand just to rise, but I took it anyway. She had shielded me from what she thought was danger. If a danger did arrive, then she would need my help far more than I would need hers. I did not say this. Some of the heat from that white must have lingered, for I felt it in my cheeks now.

Behind us, Edda was pressed flat against the wall. Alisa was crouched with both hands over her face, which was the least dignified pose I had witnessed from the girl.

"I told you the metal isn't ready yet! You cannot force it! And yet you forced it anyway. Tell me, is there soot covering your ears?!" The voice came from somewhere inside the room, and it was unmistakably angry. We peered through the doorway and saw this 'Atelier'.

All of the walls were full of color, and entirely too much of it. Every one of the walls had carvings, most in shapes and forms I did not recognize. Sculptures stood in alcoves along the far wall. Between them hung portraits, though portraits was perhaps generous. Some were paintings, depicting people I did not know. Others were sweeping landscapes I had never seen.

One caught the eye. It was a portrait of a great spiralling city built under a massive skeleton, each bone as long as a hill. That skeleton looked…very familiar.

In the room, there were four sections of stone benches stretched across the floor. On every table sat pale, fist-sized lumps of some material that glimmered in the light. Most of the benches had students, and all of the students were ducking. Several had their arms over their heads. One boy at the front bench had his face pressed flat against the table.

The Inker -for only she could have been the Inker- stood at the center of the room, where the arrangements of the benches had left a circular gap between them. Currently, she was standing close to one of the benches, looming over a child flinching. The Inker was tall. She would have been tall even as a man. She stood more than a full head above me. She was thin. Her height made her look more tree than woman. Her hair was a deep blue, falling past her shoulders.

Blue. A darker shade of blue than Edda’s, but still a color no human would have. This woman was a demonblood.

"You cannot force the form you want," the Inker said. Her voice was soft, and somehow sharp all the same. A pale boy flinched before her, ducking behind the table. "This is the most basic of basics. If you try to force something again, I will have you thrown out of my class."

The boy was nodding furiously. So furiously that it was unclear if he was agreeing, or if his neck had simply broken.

Ash leaned close to me. "Didn't think we'd see an Inker like this. I thought they were all human."

I nodded. I had seen my kind cower in the lower tiers of Koralis. I had seen children with nubs for horns who would not raise their eyes from the dirt. I had dug a grave for a demonblood girl no older than Sara, and the soldiers had cursed me for the asking.

The Inker turned and saw us for the first time. Her gaze moved over Ash briefly, as if she were no more remarkable than anyone else. Then it found me. It stopped on my horns. I almost thought I saw the woman sigh. This one stood a full head above a room of humans, and did not bow to a single one of them. She had loomed over a human boy, and it was the boy who had flinched. I found I did not mind the sight.

"Well," the Inker said. "It seems today is a rather exciting day for us all."

We sat together at one of the benches. My legs protested faintly as I lowered myself onto the seat. Odd. The ache was faint, nothing like the Ember Core, but it was there now. Was this what Ash had warned me about? I would not give her the satisfaction. Edda sat to Ash’s other side. Alisa sat on the opposite bench. She had looked at us as she’d sat down. She remained inscrutable.

The Inker appeared in front of us. She set two of the pale lumps on our table, one before each of us. "Now, what do you two know of forging? This is my first time seeing you, but people often have some idea before they walk in."

Ash answered. "We know very little. Only that you can turn metal into things with Essence."

The Inker's nose curled. She glanced sidelong at Edda as if this were her fault, and sighed. "That description is rather... pedestrian." She tapped the pale lump in front of me. "There is a class of metals and ores that are reactive to Essence. Reactive to Essence beyond just the physical. This is Chalkiron, the most common of these metals." She straightened to her full and considerable height. "Essence Forging is not the art of shaping metal into what one wishes. That is a common and unfortunate myth. When you forge with Essence, it is the metal that forges itself to what a person is."

She looked between us. "The process is simple. You will channel Essence from your marks into a material. You will follow where the mark leads you, not where you wish it to go." She glanced across at the boy she had been berating earlier. The boy looked at her and flinched.

"You will not try to force it. Even if you do succeed -and some people do succeed- the result is never as good as if you'd simply listened instead of demanded. At Line you will only have access to Chalkiron, but there are dozens if not hundreds of materials, some so rare few have ever seen them. They all have their own little quirks, but the fundamental principle remains the same. You may practice. Try not to ruin too many of these pieces, please? And do not forget what I have told you both."

She turned and walked away. It seemed that was all for this instruction, such as it was. She was already moving to another student, one whose pale ore was vibrating so quickly on her desk it almost fell off the edge before she caught it. "What did you d-"

I turned from them and stared at the Chalkiron. I touched it with my fingertips. It was warm, warmer than it should be. On my right, the Requiem hummed cold. On my left, the Cradle hummed warmth. The red and green Lines both pulsed, once. "How curious," I murmured. Something stirred beneath the surface of this thing, a buzzing so faint I half believed I had invented it.

Ash picked up her own piece, turning it in her hands. "There wasn't anything like this before."

She was right. I had known every ore worth knowing in my age. I had razed mining outposts and salted Dwarven veins to keep lesser metals than this from enemy hands. I had never seen this Chalkiron before. And yet it was an evidently useful metal, if it was being used here. Useful in ways that were unique in this new world. Curiosity had always been my sharpest hunger. It woke now, and it was ravenous.

"I already know what I want," I said. Ash looked at me. "I have decided." I sat straighter. "It shall be a blade, one that is a mix of black and white. Perhaps not as long as my former blade, but no less impressive." I paused. "I have even chosen a name for it: Blackstar, the Sundered Dawn."

Ash stared at me. "Did you miss the part where she said you cannot force something?"

"Did you miss the part where another Inker said I cannot have the core I have now?"

The lightness left Ash's face. "No," she said, more carefully. "And... I can feel what that costs you."

The Ember Core ground in answer as if to mock me. I contained the wince that came. "This will be different," I said.

I held the Chalkiron in both hands. I closed my eyes. I knew little of the [Blacksmithing] Classes in my time, but the principle had been simple. One knew what they were forging. One knew the exact materials it took. One knew what their weapon looked like before it ever settled into their hand. This task of forging and hoping one got something useful was ridiculous. Perhaps the people of this time believed this is how things ought to be. I did not.

I pictured Blackstar. In my mind, it was a wicked thing of black and white, running right down the middle of the steel itself. The pommel was a shifting black and white, each one breathing into the other. At the center of its hilt was a gemstone. A red gemstone, just like my eyes. Yes, that is a weapon fit for me. I channeled Bloom and Ruin through my palms as I clutched the Chalkiron.

Ten seconds passed. I opened my eyes. The Chalkiron had not changed. I frowned, channeled harder and for longer, and opened my eyes again. The lump was gone, and in its place was a fine grey dust coating my palms and the table.

Beside me, Ash's piece lay in a thousand tiny severed pieces, cut so fine they looked like metal sand. Ash stared at the pile. I stared at the dust coating my hands.

"I... wondered if maybe I could do it too."

I stared at her. The Hero, who told me not to force something, had just tried to force something. The look on her face was the look of a child I had seen in Hamel, one who had been caught by her mother just as she’d stolen sweets. It should not have made me want to smile. "We will try again," I said.

Five lumps of Chalkiron had passed through my hands, and not one of them had become a blade, or anything else. The second had become dust again. The third had crumbled in my fingers, rather than simply becoming dust. The fourth had slumped in my hands, sagging into a flat grey puddle. The fifth had hummed once, in a tone that was almost hopeful. It had then burst into a thousand tiny shards.

Perhaps the problem was the Requiem. I reached for the sixth lump, the one Inker Linde -someone had called her Linde- had reluctantly set in front of me. And because she was doubtless tired of bringing me more of this Chalkiron, she had set almost a dozen more lumps. Now, there was hardly room to move without knocking one over.

I did not channel Ruin at all this time, focusing only on Cradle. The Requiem was destruction. It made sense that one could not create with destruction. That was the problem. Perhaps I would not have Blackstar, but a gleaming white blade called the ‘Starblade’ would serve just as-

I opened my eyes. Where I had held the lump in my left, there was now only dust. The Cradle had just destroyed something.

These hands had unmade kingdoms. They had killed Gods. They could not make this lump of coal be anything but a lump of coal. I had knelt in river-mud once, trying to make a vase, and Ash had told me to wet my hands. There was no water here that would help.

A Queen did not sigh. Did not slam her fist against a desk because she was annoyed. I wanted to anyway.

I looked to my side. Ash fared little better. There was a pile of cut stone in front of her, each cut so finely it was no thicker than my fingernail. The pile was almost large enough to obscure her from view, if someone were to look at her from the front. Ash reached for more Chalkiron. I did the same.

I scanned the room between attempts. The students at the bench next to ours moulded their Chalkiron with much greater success. A boy produced something that looked like a hammer head. A girl coaxed a curved thing that might one day be a bow. The Inker moved from one student to the next.

I glanced towards the opposite table and saw Alisa. This was the first Forging class she had taken, since she had received the same explanation from the Inker that we had. I had been watching her for some time. Only because she had sat directly in front of me. On the table in front of her were three pieces of forged metal, all of which looked vaguely like daggers. The first was the crudest, so thick and uneven it was more cudgel than blade.

The second had an intricate crossguard, but the blade itself was curved inward at the edges, making it useless. The third, finally, had started to resemble a proper dagger. It carried no proper ornamentation, but it was something that could cut. Alisa stared at it, shook her head, and reached for more Chalkiron.

Edda's results were the opposite. I counted four wildly different shapes abandoned in a row. The first was something long and straight, the second was something curved and hollow, the third was something with links, and the fourth was something strangely round. She was currently clutching a fifth piece of ore. I had little doubt this would be something different still.

Why was I wasting my time watching these children? The small hill of my failures was turning into a mountain, and here I was watching children make their own meager attempts. I had never bothered to watch anyone else before, and now I gave their attempts almost the same attention I did my own.

I focused only on the task in front of me. Between the seventh and eighth attempt, I felt it. A pulse from somewhere outside of me, and yet not so far. It was that…faint bond between me and Sol. A chirp I could not hear but somehow knew had happened. I did not know how I was certain. How strange. This did not feel like my bond with Echidna once had. I reached for that connection. Did she suffer some indignity? If a single one of her feathers was out of place I would-

Another pulse came, and with it came an impression. Sol was…satisfied. What she was satisfied by, I did not know. I wanted to. My hand went to my shoulder, to a satchel strap that was no longer there. The egg was gone from it now. She was fine. How strange.

I had felt this thing between me and her settle when she had hatched, but never before had I gotten an impression from it. It shouldn't have been possible. It wasn't possible. The impression was faint. Perhaps it was my own wanting reflected back at me. It must be so.

I picked up another piece of Chalkiron. My hand had trembled, and I forced it still. I would be with her soon. Now was the time to focus. At some point, I had stopped counting how many I had ruined.

During one of these stretches, when the dust had thoroughly coated my entire table, I heard them. They came from the table on my left. A few feet away, but close enough.

"-believe she calls herself Lysanthia. As if that makes her anything more than-"

"-been a fluke. Mistress Selen said the thing was cracking anyway. I mean, just look at her. Heard she collapsed in conditioning too from a friend. Does that sound-"

There were more whispers. They had been picking up as my failure had grown. I ignored most of them. I was above the biting of insects.

"-halves like her are bad enough. What about the one sitting next to her? The filthy traitor-"

I looked up from the ingot I held. The words had come from the opposite bench, from a boy that sat three seats to the left of Alisa. The boy was looking at Ash. A girl next to him pointed in our direction.

The Requiem answered before I called it. Black spread from my right hand into the Chalkiron beneath my palm. The metal aged in an instant. Before, it had turned to dust. This time it ceased. My vision narrowed. The boy was only fifteen paces away. I could reach him in two strides. He thought he could say whatever he wished without consequence? I would show him what it meant to sit beside the Demon Queen, and what happened to those who spoke against the one who chose it. I could-

Ash's hand was on my knee. It was warm and calloused. I stopped.

I could feel the ridges of old sword-work against my leg through the fabric. Her pulse was far steadier than mine. She was looking at an ingot of Chalkiron, not at the boy she must have heard. "It’s okay," she murmured.

Slowly, I took a breath. The Requiem still stirred, and I forced the Essence back from it. The black lines curled around my fingers slowly sank back into the red Line. Then I looked at her hand, still on my knee.

Someone had called this woman a traitor for sitting beside me. One of her own kind. The thought had never occurred to me before. Insults against my own person were meaningless and beyond notice. Since coming to this Academy, I had heard my share of them. Insults against Ash were not meaningless, no matter how small. I did not know why, only that it was true.

The Ember Core hummed, and Ash's hand was warm, and the room was very loud and very quiet at the same time. She withdrew her hand. I slowly reached for a new piece of Chalkiron. This one was the second to last.

I tried again. I was not thinking about Blackstar anymore. I had not in some time. I was not thinking about any particular form at all. If I was thinking about anything, then perhaps I was thinking about the hand that had just been on my knee. The hand that was no longer there, but the place where it had been was still warm.

I held the metal and did nothing. The Chalkiron was warm and faintly buzzing. Each piece had been that way. I had noticed it before, but now, without the pressure of my Essence pushing against it, I could feel something I had missed before. A something. This something felt like a question.

What are you?

Something like an answer came. It came from both of my marks at once. The stone I held seemed to hum in answer. Then my marks hummed again, and some part of me felt like they were…waiting. I stared at the Chalkiron. Surely this was not my imagination.

I took a very deep breath, and let the Essence flow. The Requiem Essence and the Cradle Essence. I channeled bits of them into this ore and simply waited. Nothing happened for the first seconds.

Then the stone warmed. It started to shift in my hands. One edge of the lump started to expand and lengthen. The pale surface darkened on one side and brightened on the other. This was unlike anything that had happened before. This thing was becoming something. I did not know what. Even at this early stage, it was almost certainly not becoming a sword. I was sure of that much.

The room had gone quiet around me. I did not notice. Ash had gone still beside me. I did notice that.

I felt this strange stone strain and tremble. Hairline fractures webbed across the surface. The thing shuddered. I held my breath and tried to pull the Essence away. It was too late. The whole thing was one whole for a single breath, and then it snapped cleanly in two. One half fell on the table. I still held the other half. I let it drop.

I stared at the two broken halves. Whatever it had been, it had lasted only seconds. But for those seconds, the thing had been becoming…something. I had wanted to see it. I had wanted to see what it became, and now I never would. Every other piece, I had destroyed. This one, I had only failed to keep.

The Inker appeared at our bench. Her shadow fell over me. She examined the broken halves. She studied the fracture point for some time.

"Do not despair," she said. Her voice was almost gentle this time. "You finally understand the process. That alone is more than most achieve in their first week, let alone their first class." She paused. Studied the break more closely. Her brow furrowed.

"The Chalkiron was not strong enough to hold whatever your marks envisioned. Normally, the end result is feeble and brittle, but it does form." She looked at my forearms, at the fake red and green Lines. "A Line should be able to mould any sensitive metal available to them. Chalkiron can break from excess, but how could a Line…."

Her voice trailed off. She held the fracture up to the light. Whatever she was examining, she did not share. She set the pieces down, but her gaze lingered on my Lines for one second too many before she turned to Ash.

Her gaze flicked over the now hill of metal pieces. "Your mark is something to do with Cutting, correct? A strange Primal." Ash nodded. "Your other mark is…a Cypher. Ah, I see. I did not realize." She pointed. "Your marks are at different stages," the Inker said. "The ore hears two voices, but one is shouting and the other is whispering. The forging must come from both. Until they are closer in strength, the metal will not settle for you."

"I see." Ash nodded. Her jaw was set.

"You should come back when you have reached the same stage for both. It is my mistake. I should have noticed earlier." Then, the woman had moved on.

I watched Ash. She was looking at her right hand, at the Veil. It was just a Spark. Ash had made no attempt to make it more. I had not asked her about it, nor pressed her. Even I, who knew little of these humans, knew Ash well enough not to press.

I looked at the table before me. At the almost dozen misshapen lumps. Piles of dust. I did not feel as I had expected. I had failed a dozen times, in front of a room full of children who whispered about me. I should have felt furious. Or humiliated. Or both.

I felt neither. The metal had listened. Just for a moment, just before the end, it had listened. The metal had been shaping into a poor thing. It had been clearly lopsided even as it was forming. Whatever would have come from it would have been unsightly. It might have still been adequate.

"Your failure is not as significant as you believe," I said. The quiet words were aimed at Ash, though I had not turned to face her. "Many have failed far more spectacularly than you today. I, for instance-" I gestured at the table. "-have thoroughly surpassed you in failure. You should be proud. You have wasted less than I."

Ash looked at the table. She looked at me. She looked at the table again. Then, she laughed. Her shoulders shook with it. The sound was warm. I did not know what I had said that was so amusing. I would not discourage her laughter.

The Inker announced the end of class. She said it had gotten late, though there were no windows here to judge by. Students filed out around us. Edda was one of the first to leave. The girl murmured something about Corin and Ren and left. Her face was very red. Had been very red since I’d spoken to Ash.

Alisa lingered at her bench. Her gaze flicked at the stains on my table, and then at the pile of rubble in front of Ash. Our eyes met. She nodded. I saw her right arm rise, and then slowly pause. The other humans made their whispered comments. None of them were about Ash. That was good. For them. Alisa glared at their backs, nodded at me again, and then she too left.

I took one last look at the broken halves and stood. My legs ached more fiercely than when I had first sat down. I would not acknowledge this. I grabbed Ash's arm.

"Ash, come. Let's check on Sol." The words came out faster than dignity should have allowed.

Ash looked at the point where my hand still held her arm, though it had no reason to. "Thank you," she said.

"I did not do anything worth thanking. But if I did…it is only your right, as my retainer. Nothing more."

She smiled, and her blue eyes were very bright, and the room was very warm. I focused on the two halves again. I did not let go of her arm. Perhaps I held it tighter.
