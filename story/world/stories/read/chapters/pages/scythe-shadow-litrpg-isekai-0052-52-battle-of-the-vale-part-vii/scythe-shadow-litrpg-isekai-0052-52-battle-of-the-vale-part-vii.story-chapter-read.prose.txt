While Pirhani's Reapers and the Unchained battled in the stands, Lancelot and his comrades defended the edges of the arena, picking off Sins that took interest in expanding the fight.

Jericho came up to me and placed hand on my shoulder. "Glad to see you're still alive, my boy. Really pushing the limits, though. Don't you think?"

"It's certainly not my strongest suit."

"No," he chuckled. "Certainly not."

Nuzzle popped his head out from in the folds of Jericho's hood and scampered down his arm to me. He leapt onto my neck and latched on.

"I missed you too, buddy!" I laughed, struggling to breath from his grip.

I pet the little lizard and he relaxed, perching on my shoulder like he belonged there.

"And you…" I looked down at the ground beneath Jericho's feet. Shadowless. "Why'd you do it?"

Jericho shrugged. "Oh you know… Solidarity and all that."

"That's not a good enough reason to throw your life away like I may have."

"No, it's not."

"Then why?"

"That's not a question for me to answer."

Jericho stepped aside, revealing Calliope behind him. She avoided eye contact and brushed her short blonde-pink hair behind an ear. Was she… being shy? Bashful? It seemed so foreign on her.

But after what I'd put her through, why should I be surprised she felt weird around me.

"You seem different," I said.

"You too." She finally looked up and met my eyes. There was something there. An ice that needed to be broken. Words that needed to be exchanged.

"What? You mean this?" I grabbed a corner of my Emerald cloak and swished it about.

She laughed, though her heart wasn't in it. "That. Among other things."

"Listen," I started, the guilt welling up in my chest. "About Allium…"

"There are more important things right now."

"Like the oath you swore to Valciferous?"

"Like why I swore the oath, Zephyr. We… We need to talk." Her eyes were welling up now. I'd never seen her like this, normally so fierce and headstrong. What the fuck is going on?

"So, let's talk."

"Not here," she said.

As if on cue, Valciferous appeared beside us, glowing runes beneath his feet, breaking the moment.

"I hate to interrupt this little celebration," he said, "but I need a word with Zephyr."

"It can wait," I said, brushing him to the side.

"No. It cannot."

Valciferous' cold, pointed fingers grasped my arm.

"What are you—"

Five more gods zapped into existence around me, blocking me from Calliope and Jericho. Each one was faceless, just two glowing sockets for eyes, and clothed in white and gold. Different ornamentations crowned their heads, the only distinguishing feature besides the patterns of the intricate gold embossments on their gowns.

"What's going on?" I asked, nearly shouting now. My scythe trickled down my arm.

Other Reapers had turned to see what the fuss was about. Calliope tried to fight through the gods, reaching out toward me, but some invisible barrier kept her at bay. She yelled something, but I couldn't hear, as if all sound had been muted.

Nuzzle hissed from my shoulder, spitting at the gods, smoke seething from his nostrils. But a white hand plucked him from behind and tossed him away.

I used my Second Sight, observing the threads that bound these gods. I could cut them, like I cut Pirhani's. If they threatened me or my friends. I could—

Valciferous put and hand to my forehead and suddenly I was falling backwards into darkness.

I woke in a black room.

Kneeling and naked. Arms restrained to the sides by an invisible force.

No Emerald cloak. No scythe. My right hand was normal, not coated in the carapace armor.

A white light shined down on me in a circle.

I tried to stand, but my arms and legs wouldn't move.

This wasn't Penumbra, was it? Then is it Vitria? Am I home?

Additional lights flickered on, forming a semi-circle before me. The only things visible in the darkness of the room. Seven of them.

Runes formed on the floor within the first circle of light.

Valciferous appeared, making it clear we weren't in Vitria. Where then? The same place Pirhani had taken me on the way to the colosseum? I'd assumed that had just been a different part of Penumbra. But maybe the gods existed somewhere else. A third plane of existence?

Valciferous didn't say a word to provide explanation or appease my confusion. Simply crossed his arms and stood there ignoring me.

I would've yelled at him, but my mouth wouldn't obey me any more than my arms and legs.

The other gods appeared in order, skipping the second spot—where Pirhani presumably would've been. The god with the crown of thorns who'd seen Pirhani carry me through the runic portal was the fourth to arrive. He didn't look at me, simply closed his eyes.

None said a word for a long time, the others only staring at me. Waiting.

My eyes darted between them. What is this? Some sort of interrogation?

I'd killed one of them. I'd killed a god. Oh fuck… Ohhhhhh fuck. But I'd had to. It was self defense! Did gods give a shit about self defense?

I was about to find out…

"Shall we begin?" the last in line finally said. He wore an actual crown, unlike the more obscure ornamentations upon the others' heads.

Their leader? God of the gods?

"Indeed," another said, her fingers tapping together impatiently.

"Valciferous, you have the floor," the crowned one said.

"Thank you, Azperidel," Valciferous said, unfurling his crossed arms. "We're here because one of my charges"—he opened a hand toward me—"Zephyr, Emerald Reaper of Astral and Death Affinity, committed an act of treason against the gods."

Yup. This is it. I'm a dead man.

"His guilt in this matter is not up for debate," he continued. "Zephyr killed the Jungle Goddess Pirhani and harvested her Will. I bore witness to this event, as well as a number of other Reapers. We gather in this council of the gods, not to judge the validity of the truth laid before us, but to determine recompense for these actions."

"Let's just kill him and be done with it," a goddess with a winged tiara interjected.

Several other gods spoke at once, a mix of affirmation, anger, and dismay.

"Silence," the leader—Azperidel—demanded.

They listened immediately. Though these were all powerful beings, clearly this particular god had the respect and possibly fear of the others. That was noteworthy, assuming I survived.

"Pirhani was one of us, yes. But she was also a damn fool who violated the Pact. Which one of you was prepared to release your ties and fully descend to the Umbral Plane to confront her?"

My eyes scanned the faceless gods, the rest of my body still unable to move. None of them spoke up, until Valciferous finally cleared his throat.

"Exactly," he said. "She took an extreme approach. It required an extreme solution that none of us was willing to enact. Zephyr did us a favor."

"No," a muscular god with a flame crown said. His voice was gruff and firm. "He did not do us a favor. He did you a favor."

Valciferous fumbled for words at that. "I… Yes. You… erm… have a point there."

"Pirhani's approach was misguided, sure," the flame god continued. "But no one can deny the validity of the concerns that led to her actions. You," he pointed to Valciferous, "have been running the Meadow like your own little kingdom, encouraging Reapers to stay and build lives. That leaves far fewer in our subsequent zones to handle the more powerful Sins. Every day it becomes more and more difficult to defeat Sins in the Arctic and Volcanic zones before they become Unchained. We need more Reapers to advance."

"Yet you want to remove one of the most promising new Reapers who is already Emerald rank," Valciferous retorted. He looked over at the god with the crown of thorns for some reason, but he was simply standing still, eyes closed.

"Don't even get me started on the fact that you brought this boy in without consulting any of us," the flame god said. "This Death Affinity boy."

"Magnademus, you know I don't have the power to simply pluck whomever I want from Vitria."

"We all know there are ways around that, Valciferous. Don't be coy."

Another outburst began, but it was quickly dispelled by Azperidel. He looked to the other gods. "Nostra. Zonicus. You've been relatively quiet. I'd like to hear your thoughts."

A goddess with a crown of snowflakes straightened and turned to their leader. "Regardless of how Zephyr came to be here, he's shown great promise. He acted in self-preservation and in doing so saved us all an even bigger mess to clean up later. I know someone like him could eventually make a great difference for me in the Arctic Zone. I say we let him live."

Well, at least someone wants me alive. I made a mental note to thank Nostra if I made it out of this alive.

Azperidel nodded. "Zonicus?"

The god wearing the crown of thorns opened his eyes—maybe for the first time since he'd arrived. I hadn't been paying attention to him the entire time, but he'd yet to participate in any of the discussion or even the more unhinged exchanges.

"I'm conflicted, to be honest." He still would not make eye contact with me, gaze looking straight ahead, seemingly at nothing. That brief visage of sympathy or sadness I'd seen before was completely gone leaving an impassive, emotionless statue. Maybe I'd imagined it. "All perspectives expressed here are valid. This Reaper is a danger to us, but a boon against the Sins. Valciferous has been lenient in his duties and a change is necessary. Perhaps a temporary suspension will provide the incentive necessary."

Valciferous stared at his fellow god, eyes wide as if shocked.

"As for the boy, certain restrictions can be put in place. We can—"

"What are we even doing here?" the winged tiara goddess asked. "I say again. We kill him. A god slayer cannot be allowed to roam around the Umbral Plane unchecked. This has never happened in our history. Not even a Death Reaper has dared to reach their hand this far before. Think of what this will do as word spreads among the Reapers. We're looking at an unprecedented change to the status quo. Not one of you can predict the outcome."

"This is true," Azperidel said. "We cannot know what will come from this. But Pirhani is the one who set us down this path. There is no going back. I agree with Zonicus' proposal of a suspension." The god turned to Valciferous. "Valciferous, you are dismissed from your post as god of the Meadow until a time of my choosing. Magnademus, I cede temporary regency over the Meadow to you. Nostra, the Jungle zone will be yours until I find a new god to replace Pirhani."

"And the boy?" Magnademus asked.

"A vote," Azperidel said. "Do we let him live?"

My mouth went dry. Of course, it'd been dry this entire time, but it was even more dry now as my fate was being decided.

"Valciferous," Azperidel said. "As you're currently suspended, your vote will not count."

The former god of the Meadow hung his head, but did not argue. He'd seemed so high and mighty when I first me him. How things had shifted, shamed among his peers.

"Scaria?"

"No, we kill him." The goddess with the winged tiara spat the words.

"Zonicus?"

The thorn-crowned god closed his eyes thoughtfully again. He took several deep breaths, apparently still deciding.

I silently tallied the votes based on what'd been said so far. Scaria and Magnademus wanted me dead. Nostra wanted me alive. I had no idea what Azperidel or Zonicus wanted. That was two for death and one for life, that I knew of so far. If either Zonicus or Azperidel voted for me to die that would settle it…

I needed this vote.

Zonius finally sighed. "Let him live, for now."

Azperidel nodded. "Magnademus?"

"Kill him."

"Very well," Azperidel said. "The vote is tied, two apiece. You leave me no choice but to make the decision myself."

Azperidel, god of the gods looked at me.

I stared back, heart pounding in my chest.

He didn't say a word. Simply lifted a finger.

Then the floor beneath me fell away.
