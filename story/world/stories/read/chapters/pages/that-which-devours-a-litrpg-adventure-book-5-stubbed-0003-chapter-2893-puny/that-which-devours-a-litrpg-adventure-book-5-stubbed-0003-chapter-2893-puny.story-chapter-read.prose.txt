The low growl filled the pitch black cavern, but I didn’t let it slow me down as I continued to walk forward. The level differences between us made it clear the croc should back away slowly, except they weren’t.

Two other larger crocs remained nearby, but not growling. Instead, they watched, though all of them were under level 100. I wasn’t worried.

The air grew warmer as I walked deeper into the cavern and closer to the croc.

Level 97 wasn't a challenge for me at all, and honestly, I just wanted to dry off a bit.

If they wanted to start trouble, I’d finish and get a meal out of the mess.

The croc held his ground, but as I approached, it suddenly cut off. The whites of its eyes glimmered like it’d finally spotted me.

The acidic scent of fear drifted up from the creature, and I rolled my eyes. I’d been very clear I wasn’t starting trouble.

The room warmed enough that I didn’t feel as wet from the frantic swim.

“How warm does this area get at the edges?” I asked quietly into the room.

“Too warm for some of them,” whispered the Spinosaurus next to me. “We will all huddle around the pool.”

“So the eel keeps the water cool, which cools down this cavern?” I asked in a roundabout way.

“Yes. She winds through the deepest parts of the river, staying as close as possible to the mana ice.” The shy spinosaurous didn’t feel so shy to me.

“Good to know that no one wants to mess with her.”

A gruff noise rose from the croc that still hadn’t moved. The Spinosaurus answered me. “She is rank 3. No one here could take her.”

“Not my problem.” I shrugged. “I just want to get through the Bright.”

Plus, make sure this was a safe space to eat the rock candy that Noseen had sent to me. Not that it was actual rock candy, but it seemed close enough.

Rock Candy from a devourer.

A dead one.

Given how it made my fingers feel, a powerful devourer.

A small whisper in the back of my mind wondered how they were taken down. The same feeling had come when I’d seen the massive skeleton that nothing really wanted to grow around back in the Sanctuary.

It made the war feel so much more real, less like a note in a history book. Not that I had a history book. I’d kinda love a history book at this point, just to keep some of these people straight.

That’s when it happened.

A slight movement of air.

I twisted, slamming my spear tip through the top of the Nimble Croc’s head. The rock underneath it chipped.

The two others froze, having barely moved before their leader was dead.

“That was dumb, but now I have a lovely snack.” My voice came out rather cold, and bits of my aura leaked into my surroundings.

The Shy one backed away slowly.

The other crocs, including the two that had inched forward, all pressed as far away from me as possible.

[You have gained no experience from combat.]

Bullshit. He attacked me first.

I should totally get experience from that, even though it’d taken me no time at all and a single hit had killed the thing.

Alright, maybe I shouldn’t.

I yanked my spear out of the carcass and paused, staring around into the darkness. The tip glowed a strange blue, giving it the look of a much bigger spear tip.

Nothing moved.

I rotated around then flipped the croc over. My spear shortened and I dug into the beast’s chest, already knowing where the heart hid. Thankfully, it wasn’t too messy.

Then I popped the small object in my mouth.

Cold rippled through my stomach, then up my throat, before warming steadily. A soft spice made my eyes water a little. Spicier than the last couple had been.

[You have devoured a Nimble Purussaurus and gained 5 stat points in Quickness.]

I cut off a sizable chunk of meat from the shoulder area and munched on it. Energy flowed through me so quickly it almost made me pause. While I could always feel my stomach getting to work, healing whatever needed it, this was a step above that.

It made me hesitate before finishing the hunk. My stomach bulged a little, but I didn’t feel stuffed. Still, that was enough for me.

Eyes stared at me from all directions, and at the carcass in front of me. When I glanced up, they all glanced away except for the two sets behind me.

Stepping back from the carcass, I turned to Grizzle. “Are you hungry?”

His eyes practically glowed in the darkness as he approached.

“Shy, what about you?” I asked, turning to the much smaller creature.

She shivered. “Not my kill.”

“I share with my friends.” I tilted my head, trying to get a feel for her. “Grizzle can share if you are hungry.”

The meat in my inventory would tide me over, but the rest of the creatures in the cavern might not have a stock pile like I did. Depending on how long the Bright lasted, everyone would get hungry and tempted.

I left the two Spinos to it and headed closer to the water entrance. Not too close, though. I used to watch horror movies like everyone, and that eel was Rank 3. Still, I got close enough that the temperature dropped a little.

Crunching came from Grizzle as he tore the carcass into pieces. He tossed one to Shy, who took it carefully, then swallowed most of it.

“How long does the Bright last?” I asked, after she finished her food.

“Long. Most try to find a place to hide and sleep during it. The same for the Dark.” She stepped closer to me, and when I didn’t react she moved even closer before laying on the ground. “You came through the portal.”

“I did,” I answered carefully. “We are civilized folk.”

Cold seeped through the group, but didn’t have the same chill with my armor up. I appreciated the gains I’d made with improving the skill.

“I am as well, but the beasts near the portal make it hard to leave.” She lay her head on her front limbs. “I want to leave here. Find somewhere where we don’t have to be moving all the time.”

“You are welcome at our home. It’s a small community where we take care of each other. Folks like you, and folks like me.”

“Grizzle made it sound like something out of a story,” she said carefully, before adding, “You own it?”

“Yes. I have family that takes care of the day to day since I tend to… travel.” That was the best word I had, and it sounded better than ‘rush after my friend who had been kidnapped.’

“Travel is needed for growth.” She shook her snout. “It’s hard. There aren’t many here who make it close to level 100…”

Something about how she said it felt like a warning.

Grizzle stomped back in our direction, not leaving much behind. He hit the scant remains with his tail, batting them in the direction of the other crocs.

A small fight broke out, which grew a little larger as the creatures fought over the scraps.

I couldn’t look away.

The two higher level creatures beat the snot out of the smaller ones, until nothing else approached them. A few who limped away might not make it. Others eyed them as they slumped to the ground in the far reaches of the cave.

One let out a shuttering breath, and two others jumped on them, restarting the brawl again. This time, several crocs rolled in our direction while fighting the others. One suddenly went airborne and flew over my head, then landed with a splash into the water.

No one else really paid attention, but I focused on the pitch black water.

Something waited there.

I tapped Shy on the side, and backed farther away from the pool. She followed, but kept her eyes on me.

Grizzle followed as well.

Something stretched out of the water, reminding me of the Kraken from a few dungeons ago. It inched along the ground, touching things here and there, but generally moving closer to the fighting. Grunts and growls echoed around the cavern.

We stayed quiet.

It took a few seconds, but finally my insight triggered.

[Obe, Nightmare Thread, Unknown, Predator, Unknown.]

The thin thread that stretched across the smooth ground stayed almost invisible. The worst part was that voice inside me that warned me not to mess with it at all. Not to even approach it. I knew the lack of level information, or really much information at all, was a sign of just how far above me this Obe was.

The three of us inched closer to the wall off to the right. Bigger rocks and boulders covered the area. I sat on top of the closet boulder.

Grizzle moved into the rocks, and Shy followed. Neither of them noticed the thread, or the second one that joined the first. Both were heading directly for the mess of a fight.

One croc snapped at another, but then the thread touched its foot.

The croc froze for a split second, giving another the opportunity to bite at its side. There was no reaction from the attack.

Then the crock just dissolved into dust.

My mouth went dry.

I knew of one person who did that. Noseen.

Panic rippled through the cavern at the sight of something turning to dust. Crocs stamped in every direction, but they started dropping like flies as they tripped over or touched the threads that were now stretched across the floor.

As quickly as it started, everything went silent.

Only a few crocs remained, staying in the farthest reaches of the cave, also perched on boulders.

No one moved.

The threads slowly seeped back into the pool.

I didn’t dare breathe until the feeling of danger receded.

“What was that?” I barely whispered.

“Death,” mumbled Shy. “It stalks the deepest waters, near the Eel, but rarely comes this close to the surface. It lives in the kelp forest.”

I shivered, thinking of tall streams of kelp and threads of that winding between them.

Death forest for sure.

“Why aren’t there many creatures above level 100?” I asked.

“They get picked off by things like that,” she whispered, before pressing herself closer to the rocks. “It’s why I need to leave before I gain too many levels. While I could go into the dungeon, I’d grow too fast in there, and not be able to flee fast enough.”

The dungeon was a trap.

That made the gaps in the herds of beasts a lot more reasonable.

Noseen's comment about finding a safe space to eat the damn rock candy was laughable at best. Maybe if I entered the dungeon again, but as far as I knew I didn’t have that option.

“We’ll get out of here in one piece, though Grizzle wants to hit up the dungeon again, right?” I turned to him.

“Yes, Alpha. I want to do at least one more try, and gain the levels I can…” his voice trailed off, then he nodded almost to himself. “With Whomper leaving soon, I want to fill in the gap.”

“The insects are no joke,” I agreed. Keeping the town protected during the storms, which included the massive insect clouds, was a noble goal.

Though, one thought remained in the back of my mind. What would happen to the town as people's levels grew? Especially with access to the other dungeon on Kabi’s world? I pushed the thought away, since it didn’t really matter right at this moment.

Instead, I pushed my senses out in all directions, and triple checked my radar.

The crocs were all still huddled on various rocks pretty dang far away, and nothing rippled under the water. At least nothing that appeared on my radar.

The Nightmare Thread hadn’t shown up on it, though.

This was the best I’d be able to get right now. I pulled the heavy rock out of my inventory, and it immediately made my fingers tingle. The tingle continued inching its way up my arm.

I switched into my second sight, and the rock thing glowed like a mini sun.

Pure mana concentrated in the heavy rock at a level that seemed almost impossible. It glittered like the mana pool from the sixth wave of the dungeon.

And I was supposed to eat it?
