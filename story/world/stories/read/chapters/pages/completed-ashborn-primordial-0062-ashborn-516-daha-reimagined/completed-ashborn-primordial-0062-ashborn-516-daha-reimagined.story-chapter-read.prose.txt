Vir and Maiya descended through corridors choked with soot and centuries of neglect, the wolves scouting silently in front of them. Vaulted ceilings that Vir once thought gaudy had fallen into disrepair, and the gold that lined the walls had tarnished to black in the face of time’s march.

The slow erosion of a civilization that had withered away.

“How far down do you think it is?” Maiya asked, her voice echoing off the walls.

“Far,” Vir said, trying to peer beneath their feet with Prana Vision. “In our universe, I stumbled upon it from a sewer access, and even then, I had to venture deep underground.”

After some searching, Vir located the entrance to the catacombs behind a section of wall that had partially collapsed, descending into darkness.

“Of course it has to be down there,” Maiya muttered as Vir pulled Maiya into the shadow realm, bypassing the broken stairs.

The wolves bounded over the gaps and met back up below.

The catacombs were older than the castle, which had been rebuilt and renovated multiple times over the centuries.

Alcoves lined the walls, each holding the cremated remains of the wealthiest Sawai. King Rayid had been known to charge an arm and a leg for the privilege, which many of the aristocracy had only been too happy to pay.

Ironic, since the royals’ ashes were always dispersed at sacred places.

Vir cycled prana constantly as they walked, channeling it through his recycler tattoo, tricking this universe’s rules to make it his own.

It was annoying and ultimately inefficient—introducing a middle step—but it was necessary. Vir had poured much of what he’d regained into that last battle, and should they face anything similar, he wanted to be ready. For the same reasons, Maiya was busy recharging her S grade orbs as well.

The shadows were ubiquitous, and so, rather than wasting hours navigating the labyrinthine passages, Vir shuttled Maiya and the wolves deep underground, to places not set foot in for millennia.

To the ruins of the Imperium outpost he’d once stumbled upon years ago. Or its counterpart in this universe, anyway.

Even without the immaculate blue-and-white architecture, unblemished despite the passage of time, the beautiful interplay between the structures and the prana that wreathed them formed a sort of art that only Vir and the Iksana with Sight could appreciate. Relics from a civilization the world would never again match.

“Still hard to believe Daha’s built on top of all this,” Maiya said, running her hand along an Imperium wall.

“This was probably all buried after the Fall,” Vir said. “They likely had no idea.”

“I suppose if they had, they’d have turned it into a temple and announced it to the world like the Altani did,” Maiya replied.

It was interesting to Vir. A single person discovering these ruins early in Hiranya’s history could have changed it forever. With access to an Imperium outpost, they would’ve attracted more talent from across the world. How different would their economy have been? How different would their power be, and the culture that developed?

He’d always wondered why King Rayid had never made public the existence of the Imperium ruin… though he supposed doing so would also mean revealing the existence of their superweapon to the world. Given that the Altani knew of it—they’d even flown in to prevent its outbreak once Vir had accidentally set it loose—Hiranya must have realized some political benefits from its existence. Maybe that was the real reason why the Altani had offered Hiranya as much attention as they had.

There is one entity who’d know, Vir mused, wondering if the Fateweavers truly could see all possible timelines and outcomes. How did a being such as that process all that information? Vir struggled with the few seconds Clarity showed him. To project events days or weeks into the future, let alone years…

If there was any evidence that their foe existed beyond time and space, it was this.

They continued deeper, following the beautiful corridors into the dormant settlement. Too small to be considered a town, Ashani had said these sorts of places were used for Thaumaturgical research.

As they descended level after level, Vir’s concern grew. Prana Vision showed nothing—no nexus of Ash prana that should have blazed as brightly as any Vimana.

“Vir?” Maiya noticed his expression. “What’s wrong?”

“I’m not seeing it,” he admitted. “I should have picked up a prana signature by now.”

“You think it escaped?”

Vir had no other explanation, as much as he was loath to admit it.

They pressed on until finally, at the deepest part of the outpost, Prana Vision picked up something.

Not the dazzlingly bright signature he’d expected, but a dim haze, barely noticeable against the background.

“It’s… weak,” Vir blurted, guiding Maiya to the source. “It’s nearly dead.”

The Prana Swarm hung suspended in a vast chamber, but where most swarms manifested as a black cloud, this one was invisible to the naked eye, and even this close, Vir had to wonder if it was a swarm at all.

“You could probably go right through it without it harming you,” Vir muttered, frowning.

“How, though?” Maiya asked. “Prana Swarms are invincible. Don’t they just grow over time as they consume stuff?” She gestured to the area. “Not like this realm’s lacking for prana.”

“It’s that,” Vir said, pointing to four pure white towers that stretched from the floor to the ceiling of the cavern and blazed brightly to Prana Vision. “It’s diverting all ambient prana away from this chamber, creating a dead zone. I’m guessing if either of us walked in there, it’d feel pretty similar to the Voidlands.”

“The Imperium starved it,” Maiya muttered, peering at the center of the space, trying to make out the swarm. “How long has it been in here like this?”

“Likely since the collapse of the Imperium,” Vir replied.

“So they can be killed after all,” Maiya chuckled.

Vir snorted. “Sure. If you have the right Imperium facility and several thousand years to wait. On mortal timescales, Swarms are invulnerable.”

The Swarm pulsed weakly, a tendril of light reaching toward them before dissipating.

It’s hungry, Vir thought. Perfect.

He stepped forward, cycling prana through his tattoo at maximum capacity. Black flames erupted around him as he drew on everything he had.

“Er, Vir?” Maiya said nervously. “Are you sure about this?”

“Not at all,” Vir admitted. “But unless you have a better solution…”

Maiya sighed and shook her head. If there had been any other options, they’d have taken it long ago.

This was a move born of desperation. But one, Vir thought, that might just work.

It had better, he thought, extending his arm into the subjugation field. Or else we’re trapped here forever.

A torrent of Ash prana flowed toward the Swarm. The creature surged forward, wrapping around the prana like a starving animal, consuming everything he put out in seconds, forcing Vir to increase the flow.

The Swarm began to grow, and soon, even Maiya could see it.

“It’s like fostering some horrific pet,” Maiya muttered, taking an involuntary step back.

Vir poured more prana in, letting it grow and grow.

The Swarm doubled in size, then doubled again, and soon it filled half the chamber, its black form sucking the light out of the nearby surroundings.

Maiya backed away. “Vir, if it gets any bigger, it’s going to overwhelm the Artifact.”

“That’s exactly what we want,” Vir said, sweat beading on his forehead.

“This is insane,” Maiya muttered. “Actually insane.”

Vir had no reply to that. It was insane.

Vir kept feeding it until his reserves ran low and he was forced to stop to cycle more prana through his tattoo before continuing again.

The Swarm grew massive, filling the entire chamber and bulging against the containment towers that began to creak and groan under the stress.

And then, the Artifact constructed by a race so advanced they might as well be gods, a tower that had stood for thousands of years… cracked.

Hairline fractures spread across its perfectly white surface.

“Vir!” Maiya shouted.

“Almost there,” Vir said through gritted teeth.

The Swarm was enormous now, and unquestionably larger than it had been since its imprisonment.

It writhed against the towers, waging a war of pranic might. The inscriptions on the Artifacts flared brighter and brighter, but it was of no use. The inscriptions unraveled and the nearest tower shattered.

With it gone, the load on the remaining towers spiked, and the second shattered. It was soon followed by the third, and finally the last.

No longer suppressed, the Swarm surged outward, filling the chamber, rushing Vir and Maiya, enveloping them…

And then the world went white.

Vir felt the universe twist, felt reality unwrite itself. Time flowed backward and then forward—a sensation that made him want to throw up. Suddenly, he was falling and rising at the same time, dying and being born, existing in all moments and none.

The sensation ended almost before it had begun, and he found himself standing on solid ground. A cobblestone road, to be precise.

Around him were the sounds of passersby living their lives.

This was, without a doubt, Alt Ashani, and Maiya stood beside him, as disoriented as he was. The wolves tucked their tails and brushed up against their legs, whimpering in fear.

“It… worked?” Maiya asked. “It worked!” Maiya repeated, laughing in relief. “We did it, Vir! We threatened to destroy everything and they blinked.”

Vir pulled her into an embrace. They’d gambled everything on a theory and won. The Fateweavers had seen their intent and decided the previous timeline was preferable to annihilation.

“I can’t believe it—!?”

Maiya’s words were cut off as the street shook, sending several people tumbling and screaming.

In the distance, the source of the quake made itself known. Like a leviathan breaking through ice, a conical megastructure burst from the underground complex that housed it with a world-splitting sound, sending tremors throughout the city.

And then, as if things weren’t bad enough, Prana Vision picked up something that made Vir’s blood run cold.

A familiar signature of pure Ash prana, and it was right behind him.

“No,” Vir whispered, spinning around.

“What’s wrong?” Maiya began before spotting it immediately.

For the quake wasn’t the only reason people were running for their lives, screaming. It wasn’t even the main reason.

“The Swarm,” Vir said hollowly. “It came back with us.”

Freshly freed and ravenous for more prana. In a city filled with innocent people.

They’d unleashed an uncontrollable monster on the very people they’d hoped to save.

“Vir,” Maiya said quietly, face ashen. “What have we done?”
