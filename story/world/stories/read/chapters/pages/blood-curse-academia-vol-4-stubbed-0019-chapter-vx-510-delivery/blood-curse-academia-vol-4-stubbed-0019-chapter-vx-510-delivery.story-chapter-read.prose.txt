Chapter V.X (5.10) - Delivery

Roba never elaborated on the contents of the sack. And the knot at the end sealed it closed via magic as to only allow the receiver access to what lay within.

Kizu half expected the border security to search the bag as he entered Port Kallis, but they simply glanced at his normal paperwork and waved him through after he jumped to the city’s beacon.

The city’s guards were on edge and focused. Just not on him. Instead, all of their attention was on other people from Tross. It took Kizu a minute to figure out how they knew he was from Hon and not a fellow countryman of theirs. It was his accent. Whenever he spoke, tension visibly eased out of them. No Tross loyalist would speak the way he did.

Having visited the port city a dozen times before, Kizu knew his way through the winding streets as he followed Roba’s directions to her contact’s home.

The streets were wet, with a drizzle overhead. And the people equally dreary. For a city on the brink of revolution, they certainly all looked depressed and suspicious rather than the fiery and gung ho attitudes he expected from revolutionists. Thankfully the bland, unenchanted outfit Roba had supplied him with passed the scrutiny of any eyes that examined him. Unfortunately, unenchanted meant subject to the whims of nature. A drizzle added up, and after only a few minutes of walking, Kizu was drenched. Strands of his black wig clung to his forehead and he had to keep pushing it out of his eyes.

He blushed and kept his head low as he passed a pub that he and Ione had gotten particularly sloshed in, suddenly extremely grateful for the wet wig.

Even with Ione’s family paying off the damages, the owner had more than a few threats for them if he ever spotted either of them again. Kizu was certainly not welcome back. Though he did wonder if her bear’s claw marks still marred the walls. She had been trying to show off her incredible control by creating a carved image of the two of them. That had only been the start of that disaster of an evening.

He resisted the urge to peek through a newly repaired window and hurried past.

The address Roba had given him led him to a silver gate at the edge of the city. A brick wall stretched out to either direction, several meters tall. Through the gate, he saw rows of trees lining the driveway. However, from his vantage point, Kizu couldn’t even see the house itself.

“This…is an expensive property,” he muttered to himself. He glanced at the old burlap sack full of lumpy objects. It wouldn’t look out of place amongst the belongings of a homeless vagabond.

Following the instructions given to him by Roba, Kizu walked over to the pillar beside the gate. He set the flat of his hand against the cold brick.

“Who’s this?” a scratchy disembodied voice called out. “How’d you know about this?”

“Uh…Roba sent me?” Kizu replied. “I have a sack for the guy who lives here.”

“Roba?” A shuffling of papers that sounded odd without any visual indicator. “Oh. The secretary of Shinzou Academy. Fine. Get in here, kid.”

The gate creaked open, as if pushed by invisible servants.

Kizu stepped forward, intending to walk up the driveway. But then the stones beneath his feet moved. The sudden sensation nearly tipped him over as the driveway gained speed. By the time regained his balance, he sped along the driveway at a breakneck speed. The neatly organized forest to either side of him passed by in a blur.

And then everything stopped. He stood in front of a simple cottage. Scenic and serene. Little puffs of white smoke exited the chimney and loaves of fresh bread cooled on the windowsill. The front door hung ajar, exposing a warm interior with a crackling fire.

Kizu looked back over his shoulder at the elaborately manicured forest and caught sight of a maze of hedges behind him to the right and a garden with tunnels of flowers to his left. Everything about this property screamed opulence and wealth. Everything but this humble cottage.

Without knowing what else to do, Kizu shouldered the sack and entered the home.

“Wait,” the scratchy voice from earlier called out as Kizu stepped through the threshold. The speaker was still nowhere to be seen. “I need your name. It looks like ‘errand boy for Shinzou Academy’ won’t fit in the allotted space on the visitor records.”

“Kaga Kizu.” The words left his lips instinctually, but the moment they were out, it occurred to him that stealth might be key for this part of his journey as well.

“Kaga Kizu!” a jovial voice called out. “What a lovely surprise!”

A familiar older man stood up from the wellworn couch in front of the fireplace. He beamed at Kizu. He wore a monocle over one eye, a bowler hat over his salt and peppered hair, and a well-pressed suit over a lean body.

“Do I…know you?” Kizu asked.

The man’s smile only grew. “Vincent J. Winthropen! We met a few months back when you first jumped into the city. You didn’t have that dead rag on your head back then though. Spoke to the chap, Albert, on your behalf. I apologize for the current house. If I had known I would have company I would have arranged for something more grand for the day.”

“Your…house?” Now with more context, Kizu did indeed recognize and place Vincent as the man who’d assisted him with the border security on his first arrival to Port Kallis.

“The entire building is malleable to my whims. Though I need to give it a night to restructure itself. It’s a bit shy and likes to change in the dark.” Vincent gave Kizu a wink behind his monocle. “I prefer a more relaxed and compact setting for when I am on my own. I often find the more grandiose the building, the emptier it feels without company.”

“What about the person who spoke earlier?” Kizu asked.

“Oh, Yvonne? That’s just the ghost that haunts the property. She’s my secretary and harmless.”

“Not harmless,” Yvonne’s voice responded.

“Helped me buy the place at a severe discount too!” Vincent replied. “We’ve been together for what, thirty years now?”

“Thirty-eight.”

“Some fools simply don’t understand the value of a resident ghost haunting your home.” Vincent t’sked and shook his head. “Had to chase off the exorcists the previous owner arranged.”

“They posed no threat,” Yvonne said dismissively. “This property would need to be razed and salted to dispel me.”

“You’re really that powerful of an undead?” Kizu asked.

“It depends on your definition of powerful. My soul is bound to this land for eternity. And violence does not come easy to me. But I do control and manipulate the property as I see fit.”

Kizu looked around the cozy cottage. It looked weathered and well used. The bookshelves held books. He even spotted a bit of dust in the rafters above. “You created this place?”

“It is one of many I cycle through. Vincent prefers it.” The ghost remained invisible while it spoke to him. And since Kizu lacked any sort of soul perception ability, he had no means of detecting it.

Kizu made a note to tell Aoi about this place. She’d doubtlessly charge over here as soon as possible to quiz the undead. Then he wondered if she’d bother to use her connections to get access to this place or would attempt to trespass instead. Actually… maybe he wouldn’t give her all the details. Withholding the address might not be a terrible idea.

“Oh! Look at me! I’m prattling on and being a terrible host!” Vincent declared. “Please take a seat! I’m afraid I’m not accustomed to sudden company. Can I fetch you anything to snack on? Drinks? I believe we have some wine shipped in from Edgeland before the Dragon invasion and trade embargo.”

“Water is fine,” Kizu quickly said. The thought of Edgeland wine put his stomach in knots.

Vincent raised an eyebrow at his swift reply.

“I, uh, dated the daughter of an Edgeland wine merchant,” Kizu sheepishly explained. “Kind of ruined the drink for me.”

“Ahh, the exploits of the youth!” Vincent laughed heartily and snapped his fingers. A moment later a clear glass of liquid appeared on the tea table. “Now sit and relax!”

“When he offered to serve you, he was actually offering my services,” Yvonne said. “Vincent’s only magical power is vast wealth.”

“You can’t cast any spells?” Kizu asked, taking a seat on the couch. He raised the glass of water to his lips and took a sip. It was extraordinarily refreshing as it trickled down his throat. Despite the steady drizzle of rain outside, Kizu found himself parched. He took another sip.

“A bit of a personal question,” Vincent chastised. “But yes. My affinities are lacking in everything save for enhancement spells. And I’ve never bothered to train those skills. Oh, and of course I know how to jump. Transportation is vital to good business.” He tacked on the last bit as if knowing spatial spells was an after thought.

With the implied capabilities of this house changing shape, Kizu wondered if there was anything he could learn about spatial magic here. There must be some enchantments at play. Or perhaps Yvonne knew a few tricks.

But as he was examining the room for the hundredth time, his eyes fell on the burlap sack at his feet. His half formed question died as he recalled his actual reason for arriving here.

“Roba asked me to bring you this.” Kizu heaved the sack up and presented it to Vincent.

The rich old man leaned forward and fiddled with his monocle. “Interesting. What sort of package did the secretary of Gizrim Ballarfulur deem necessary to send to me via courier?”

The sack floated out of Kizu’s hands unbidden and hovered in front of Vincent. He reached in, his arm sinking nearly to his shoulder, then pulled out a spherical object.

Kizu’s first assumption that it felt like porridge wasn’t far off. Instead, it was a massive wad of gray, wet sculpting clay. Vincent plopped it down on the tea table and extracted the others. He lined them all up next to the first, soon filling all available space on the table.

Rather than questioning it verbally, Kizu used his spellsense to try to detect the clay’s purpose. It definitely was enchanted material. And, he suspected it was laced with divination spells.

“Excellent!” Vincent clapped his hands together. A wine glass with blue liquid materialized in front of him. He snatched it out of the air and swirled the liquid. Then he raised it in a toast.

“To your excellent work as a delivery boy!”

“Thanks?” Kizu raised his water and clinked it against the wine glass. Then, following his host’s example, took a gulp of his drink.

Downing his blue drink, Vincent tossed the glass over his shoulder and rubbed his hands together. “Now, to get to work.”

He slapped one of the wads of clay. It wobbled under his touch. Then it slowly molded itself into the image of a human head. Vincent repeated the action with each clay wad. Soon, there was a table full of heads in front of him.

“Is that…the headmaster?” Kizu asked, staring at one of the first smiling heads. Then his eyes drifted over to the others. “And…Sasaki?”

“Oh? You’re acquainted with the Hon Emperor?”

Vincent might have said more, but Kizu’s ability to listen vanished. At the back corner of the table was an all too familiar head. One that he’d promised himself he’d never forget.

Kaga Anna. His sister.
