Xin Wei followed Lee Jia’s gaze and smirked.

“Ah, the beast cores? They certainly do catch the eye, don’t they? We were quite fortunate that they were all undamaged. Four normal cores, and one perfect quality core—all of them third grade. A reward fitting to the trial, don’t you think?”

Lee Jia honestly had no idea, but that was the furthest thing from her mind right now. Glancing at Eui, she saw her staring at the cores with a troubled expression. She was probably thinking the same thing as Jia—what does this mean?

They had somehow formed a beast core within Eui, but neither of them had any clue of the implications of that. Jia thought she could vaguely recall hearing something about it once, but she didn’t remember where.

Unaware of Lee Jia’s inner turmoil, Xin Wei continued speaking.

“Normally we wouldn’t have harvested anything other than the cores—far too much effort for too little gain—but since you were indisposed we took the time to butcher them all properly. Besides, I figured you could carry the bulkier materials in your storage artifact.”

Lee Jia nodded distractedly.

“Sure that’s fi—”

She stopped suddenly and shot Xin an angry glare. He held up his hands defensively.

“I can’t be blamed for revealing this particular secret, Miss Lee. Those medicines had to come from somewhere. I assume we have Yan Zhihao to thank for that particular boon?”

Lee Jia let out a frustrated sigh, but nodded.

“Ugh, yes. I’m constantly looking over my shoulder worried that he’s going to come to retrieve it.”

“A valid concern, Miss Lee—that artifact is likely nearly as valuable to his clan as he is. They are not common. This expedition would be a perfect time for him to strike, so we should proceed with additional caution.”

Lee Jia was about to agree, when Xin’s words gave her pause.

“Wait, you want to keep going? We nearly died on the first day, shouldn’t we turn back?”

Looking around at the group, she saw that most of them were shaking their heads or shrugging. Takeda spoke up to explain.

“We knew it was going to be dangerous, Lee Jia. We took some injuries, sure, but we’re fine now, in large part thanks to you. Now that we know the dangers, we’ll be better prepared for the next fight!”

Looking around, Lee Jia saw that most of the group was nodding in agreement. She turned to Eui, who had suffered the closest brush with death, and met her unearthly red gaze—a grim reminder of what had happened. To her surprise, Eui’s expression was a sly grin, the same she often used when teasing Jia about something.

“What was it you said to me before? You’d fight for every scrap they took for granted? Show them what it means to be hungry? We can’t back down now just because it’s a little more risky than expected. Yan and Hayakawa are probably assuming it’s a race against each other for the rewards, and I don’t know about you, but I relish the opportunity to put those arrogant brats in their place!”

Jia couldn’t help but laugh at the reversal of roles. Usually she was the one trying to encourage Eui with impassioned speeches. It felt kind of nice to be on the receiving end for once.

“Alright! Let’s discuss our strategy while we rest, then. I noticed a few gaps in that fight…”

After a heated discussion about combat tactics, they agree to spend the remaining night resting and set out at first light. Since her dimensional ring had already been revealed, Jia decided to treat herself while they rested, and summoned a bamboo skewer filled with chunks of glistening meat, as hot as it had been when she stored it. Before she could take a bite, her eyes widened in shock as she recalled the voice of the old street vendor.

“A core’s the same thing, ‘cept it were formed inside a youkai … heard some people can do it too, but that’s how you get oni—demons…”

She nearly dropped the skewer as her hands began to tremble. A cold sweat began to form as she glanced edgewise at Eui, who was eyeing the skewer enviously. She tried to hide her nerves behind a smile as she passed it to Eui and summoned another.

What in the names of all the ancestors had they done?

Lee Jia didn’t have much time to contemplate her troubles, nor to privately discuss them with Eui as they continued to explore the wilderness. Though they hadn’t encountered anything as dangerous as the Ash-furred Obsidian Wolves, they frequently ran into magic beasts. The density of the mana—as well as the beasts—only grew as they got closer and closer to the mountain.

“Frankly, I’m surprised they built the academy down on the plains. Most sects prefer to build in the mountains where there’s more spiritual energy. Supposedly because it’s closer to heaven.”

Xin Wei turned out to be quite the conversationalist. When he wasn’t plying Lee Jia for secrets, he always seemed to find something to discuss, and someone to discuss it with. In this case, Dae was the one to speak up in defense of the academy’s locale.

“While it’s true that mana density is naturally greater at high altitudes, it makes very little difference in cultivation efficiency. Building on flat ground allows for more compact campus layouts, allowing for efficient mana-gathering formations.”

“I’ve heard about the Goryeon mage colleges and their tall, single-building constructions. I don’t see the point if your mana gathering formations are so big that they encompass entire mountain ranges.”

Dae chuckled awkwardly and scratched the back of his head.

“Hehe, this one is uh, special. It’s the largest and most powerful formation my master has ever created. A combination shield and mana gathering formation that dwarfs even the great formation that protects the capital, Songdo.”

“Hmm, I see. Still, could the academy not have been built on the mountain? You’d get the best of both worlds, and we could separate ourselves from those outer disciples.”

Lee Jia’s ears twitched slightly as she overheard Xin use an unfamiliar term.

“Outer disciple?”

Dae scoffed and shook his head.

“The academy does not have inner and outer disciples. We are all students of equal standing and opportunity.”

Xin shot him a smug grin and raised one eyebrow.

“Oh, certainly! That’s why eighty five percent of the students are back on campus while we go out on a mission. A true wonder of Goryeon progressiveness! Ow!”

Lee Jia kicked a rock so that it bounced off of Xin Wei’s head.

“You didn’t answer my question. What are inner and outer disciples?”

Xin grumbled as he rubbed at the back of his head.

“You could have gotten my attention another way, you know. You see how they treat me, Stoneface? Aren’t you supposed to step in and defend me?”

“My apologies, Xin. I had mistakenly thought your words were a thinly veiled request for Miss Lee to strike you with a rock. I will be more vigilant in the future.”

Xin Wei narrowed his eyes at Guan Yi’s deadpan retort.

“I hate you all. To answer your question, Miss Lee, in Qin a sect is generally split between the outer sect—which houses those of lesser talent and peerage—and the inner sect, where those of higher talent can cultivate in peace.

“In rare cases, a particularly talented outer sect disciple might gain the attention of the elders and promote to the inner sect, and anyone could be taken in by a sect elder to become a core disciple, though that’s very rare.”

Lee Jia pursed her lips as she tried to imagine it.

“Wouldn’t that be awkward? Why keep it all split up like that? Besides, how does peerage play into it? It’s not like someone is going to be more talented based on birth alone.”

Several strange looks came her way as she finished speaking, including Xin, Kim, and Guan. Even Eunae seemed to have a troubled expression at the direction in which the conversation had gone.

“Well first, the great sects have thousands of disciples, and it’s a logistical nightmare to keep them all in one place, so splitting them up is necessary. Some sects are so large that they split further into smaller branch sects.”

Guan nodded at Xin’s words.

“For example, Xin and I are from the Heavenly Teardrop Peak. It’s split between the Xin family’s Spiritual Flowing Purewater sect and the Earth Rending Sword branch, which is maintained by my own Guan clan.”

Earth Rending Sword? That sounded familiar.

“Like the sword art technique? The one that lets you slash things that are far away?”

Guan nodded, his expression remained placid but Lee Jia thought she saw the slightest hint of pride shining in his eyes.

“The very same. It has been developed and refined by my family for centuries, and has been adopted by many of the great sects as a core technique.”

Xin Wei waved off Guan Yi’s interruption.

“Yes, yes, it’s a lovely technique. As for peerage...well there’s no simple way to say this, Miss Lee, but talent is absolutely hereditary. You and Miss An are the exceptions, not the rule. Qin has its own ways of rearing talented children, and I doubt it’s a coincidence that the great houses of Goryeo all consistently reproduce the same spiritual traits.”

Lee Jia frowned and glanced at Eunae, who was remaining remarkably silent despite her obvious discomfort, then at Kim who seemed to wear his high spiritual affinity with great pride.

For the first time, it occurred to her that it was normally impossible to predict the spiritual traits that would be expressed in a half-spirit child, but the same was not true of the noble houses, who seemed to consistently express traits from a specific common ancestor. The Kumiho in Seong’s case, and the Great White Tiger Baekho for Kim.

“Ok, I don’t want to hear about the inbreeding habits of nobles.”

Eui interrupted in her typically crude fashion.

“Besides, we’re getting close to the mountain and I think we’re about to have company.”

Sure enough, Lee Jia sensed several auras approaching. Having auras meant that they couldn’t be magical beasts, but she didn’t let her guard down. They hadn’t met any yet, but elementals had auras and were just as dangerous or more than beasts. Her fears weren’t exactly assuaged when it was Yan Zhihao’s group that appeared.

“What have we here? I wondered who we might encounter so close to the base of the mountain, but it was Xin Wei! I’d say it’s unbecoming of you to be leading a group of beasts, but actually it seems to suit you well.”

The boys surrounding Yan covered their mouths with their sleeves and snickered at his words. Xin Wei took it in stride, placing a fist into his palm in front of his chest and bowing formally.

“This junior greets senior brother-in-craft. You honor me with your compliments Senior Yan Zhihao.”

Lee Jia looked askance at Xin as Yan scoffed and waved his hand.

“Nonsense, I don’t recall recognizing you as a member of my sect, Xin.”

Xin Wei tilted his head with an expression of mock confusion.

“Are we not members of the same school, practicing our craft under the tutelage of the same elders? If not sect-brothers, then what are we?”

Yan rolled his eyes and shook his head.

“Fine. This senior recognizes Junior Brother Xin Wei. Happy now? That’s as close to being a member of a great sect your worthless family will ever achieve again.”

Lee Jia glanced between Xin and Yan with a look of utter confusion. Clearly there were some deep layers of Qin etiquette at play here that she didn’t understand in the slightest. For some reason, Xin Wei was looking very pleased with himself.

“Only naturally! This junior thanks senior for giving me face. Only, I should clarify that I am not the leader of my group. Our team is led by Senior Sister Miss Lee Jia.”

Yan Zhihao’s eyebrow twitched and Lee Jia belatedly realized what Xin Wei was doing. What a devious snake he was! She silently resolved herself to never let her guard down around Xin Wei.

The silence stretched uncomfortably and Lee Jia wondered if she was supposed to offer a greeting here. She didn’t know the first thing about social etiquette in her own nation, let alone Qin! Xin, oblivious to—or perhaps thoroughly enjoying—the tension, continued to stoke the flames of the situation.

“What’s the matter, senior brother? Will you not offer greetings to senior sister?”

Yan ground his teeth so tightly that Lee Jia could hear it from where she stood, and the other boys around him exchanged nervous glances.

“You know that it is customary for the junior to offer their greetings first, junior brother.”

Lee Jia began to sweat slightly as all eyes suddenly fell on her. The ball was in her court, and from Yan’s smug expression he was expecting her to miss. Well he was right, so she decided not to disappoint. She offered Yan Zhihao her sweetest smile and waved in greeting, channeling her inner Eunae.

“Hi, Yan!”

The attack seemed to inflict critical damage as Yan Zhihao choked on his response and stared at her incredulously. Unfortunately, Xin Wei had fallen to friendly fire and was now doubled over on the ground, laughing as hard she had ever seen anyone laugh. She said a silent prayer for her fallen brother-in-craft and continued her offense.

“I haven’t seen you since our duel! I hope you’re well. Has your expedition been going well?”

Yes! This was the social skill she had refined over months of tea parties with Eunae and Takeda. The ultimate icebreaker—small talk! She regarded Yan Zhihao curiously as he grew increasingly flustered. What was wrong? Had he not spoken to her informally and even rudely laid hands on her on their first meeting? Surely he could handle this much.

“What’s wrong, Yan? Your face is pale, are you injured? I have some medicines you can borrow.”

Yan’s face darkened immediately, and a vein on his forehead seemed to bulge as he glared balefully at her. Oops! Had she pushed it a bit too far with that one? Yan Zhihao recovered his senses enough to speak through gritted teeth.

“I am quite well, Miss Lee, thank you for asking. Might I inquire what brought your party to this place? The mountain is wide, and surely our fates aren’t so linked as to have met by mere coincidence.”

Lee Jia shuddered at the thought of Yan Zhihao as her fated one and shook her head.

“No, we’ve been tracing the strongest flows of mana coming from the mountain in hopes of finding the source of the mana disturbance.”

Yan clicked his tongue and frowned.

“Tsk, and here I was hoping that everyone would be as idiotic as that red-haired savage. I think she plans on simply flattening every beast on this mountain until she either stumbles on the source of the disturbance or runs out of monsters.”

“If it’s Hayakawa, she could probably do it. Her strength is unreal—she could probably take on everybody here by herself.”

Yan’s eyes widened slightly at Lee Jia’s words. She knew she was exaggerating a bit, but only a bit.

“If you say so. In any case, if you’ve been following the spiritual energy then we’re likely to end up going in the same direction. Rather than fight over it, what do you say to a truce?”

Lee Jia raised an eyebrow skeptically.

“You want a truce? With us? You literally just called us beasts a few minutes ago.”

Yan shrugged carelessly.

“I meant no insult. I can’t be blamed if you take offense with reality.”

Lee Jia stared slack-jawed for a moment before shaking her head. This guy lived in his own world.

“I would have thought you’d be more competitive. I don’t know what the reward is, but I doubt it splits twenty ways.”

“A snake will die if it tries to swallow an elephant. We’ve already encountered a number of third rank beasts on the way here. Whatever’s at the source of the disturbance shouldn’t be less dangerous than that. In the worst case, there may even be a xiantian ranked monster. I’m not too proud to recognize danger when I see it.”

He could have fooled Lee Jia, but she couldn’t argue with his logic. Whatever they found was likely to be dangerous, and it would be good to have more allies. She exchanged a glance with Eui and received a begrudging nod, which the rest of the team echoed.

“Alright, fine. But how will we share the spoils?”

“No need to fret about it. Treasure will fall into the hands of those destined to obtain it.”

Lee Jia stared blankly at Yan Zhihao.

“Finders keepers, then?”

Yan’s smile became slightly strained as he responded.

“Just so, Miss Lee.”

With their truce arranged, the two groups moved on, keeping each other at a slight remove as they followed the increasingly dense leylines. Lee Jia walked next to Xin Wei and spoke in a low whisper.

“Xin Wei, if you ever put me on the spot as part of your stupid social games—or whatever that was—again, I’ll let Eui decide what to do with you.”

Xin glanced nervously at Eui and saw red eyes and the least friendly smile he’d ever witnessed staring back at him. He turned back to Lee Jia with a shiver.

“Understood, Miss Lee.”
