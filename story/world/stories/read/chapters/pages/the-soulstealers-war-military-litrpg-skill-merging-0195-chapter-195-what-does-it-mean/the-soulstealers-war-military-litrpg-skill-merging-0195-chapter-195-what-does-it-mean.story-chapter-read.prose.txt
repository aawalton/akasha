“The Dun Altir Order has accepted our offer,” Hardric told me, excitement audible even through the leaves of the plant and the static of the line. Today, the interference was especially intense. It was raining somewhere between Castle Urcia and Hyrdford, making the leaves of the intermediary plants shake unnecessarily.

“All of the offer?” I asked, leaning closer to the plant to make my voice heard, while holding tight to the lower leaves to reduce any losses of vibrations. A late summer wind washed over the castle—fallout from the gusts on the nearby hills—and the roofs rattled. It was getting darker sooner, and inside our room in the barracks, the candles flickered. Brawl and Anchor prepared for bed while I held my weekly meeting.

“They gave us the money, and they said that if our supplies did what we said they did, they would continue paying for it,” Hardric said.

I sighed in relief.

“Now, even under your new tax breakdown,” Cuthward added, “some of the guild’s income will be taken as tax.”

“Some, but not enough to break us,” I said. “Now that we have a paying customer, it frees us up to actually start spreading and spending.”

“If our supplies are as effective as we say,” Hardric warned, “It’s no guarantee that the Order will come back to us.”

“Our potion bases will help them brew stronger potions of their own, correct?” I asked. “There’s no risk about this. We know what it does, and so do they.”

“No, sir, but I like to be cautious,” Hardric said. “So far, this is just a one time purchase. They’ve sealed no contract.”

I nodded, even though they wouldn’t see it, then asked, “What about sending anyone to train their alchemy at the source of all the resources? Did they accept that part of the deal? Did you bring it up?”

“We brought it up. Their negotiator—some Adept Brother of their order named Malin—was hesitant, but he says he might send a party of their young learners along with a few teachers, if we are willing, to experiment with some of our supplies. They could always use more experience in brewing potions. And yes, healing potions specifically.”

“Good work,” I said. “Any other progress to report, Cuthward?”

“Almost everything is in order,” the man said. “Much of it will require your seal to be truly official, but the people are respecting the law as if it was official. You should, however, consider getting a seal of your own. A unique seal.”

“Are there any local artists who would like a commission?” I asked. “Is it a…coat of arms?”

“What’s that?” Hardric asked.

“I am not sure what you mean by ‘coat of arms,’ my boy,” Cuthward said. “But your seal should be the symbol on your shield. Thegns have a unique symbol painted on their shield—or they should.”

“What if I like the raven?” I asked. “It kinda fits…with me being a Dupe and all, to just use the standard flag that all Dupes march under.”

Cuthward snorted. “I suppose we could make a raven seal. But you might wish for it to be slightly different, to help identify you better in a crowd and during a battle. And, perhaps, to make it less presumptuous. You are claiming the flag of that nation as your primary symbol, you realize?”

Once I do come up with a symbol, I’ll let you guys know, and we can imprint it on a signet ring.” I knew they used signet rings, even if they didn’t have coats of arms.

“Yes, sir,” Cuthward said. “It will be done.”

But you could make a monster’s blood more like sap by combining it with other alchemical resources. Since we weren’t likely to find any un-burnt arcane trees down here, it was the closest I’d get.
