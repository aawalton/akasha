Luke awoke the next morning after a restless sleep. His dreams had been soaked in carnage—visions of the Lhair soldiers being torn apart by Thunder Bombs replayed in an endless loop.

Their screams echoed in his ears, dragging pain through his skull and manifesting as a throbbing headache. He sat up, wincing, only to find Victoria seated at the foot of his bed, watching him with quiet concern.

“Here, take this,” she said, offering him a cup.

He took it and peered inside, immediately recoiling as a pungent odor assaulted his senses. His eyes watered.

“Victoria… what is this concoction?” he asked warily.

She smiled sweetly. “A tincture my mother used to make. Drink it in one gulp—it should help you relax before you head out today.”

Luke’s heart sank. Even without tasting it, he knew it would be vile. And her advice to down it all at once only confirmed that suspicion.

But how could he refuse her? She was clearly showing care in the only way she knew how.

Steeling himself, Luke pinched his nose and threw his head back, swallowing the contents in one go. A fiery burn ravaged his throat, forcing a violent coughing fit.

It took nearly a minute for him to recover.

“Wait… was there alcohol in that?” he croaked, incredulous.

Victoria tilted her head. “I don’t think so,” she said innocently, though her vague tone did little to reassure him.

Luke shook his head and swung his legs off the bed. Whatever it was, the tincture had certainly woken him up.

“You ready?” he asked offhandedly.

“Mmm. I just need to infiltrate the crown prince’s forces, right? That’s too easy.”

Her confidence didn’t surprise him. He considered asking how she planned to do it, but quickly decided against it.

He knew what kind of person Victoria was. The less he knew, the easier it would be to sleep at night.

After packing his things, Luke bid her farewell and stepped out of the dormitory. The sun had just begun to rise, its golden rays stretching across the horizon.

When he reached the academy gates, a familiar figure awaited him. Tristan Gale, the head librarian, gave him a silent nod. His expression was unreadable.

“This is who we’ve been waiting for? A teenager?” a deep voice sneered.

Luke turned toward the speaker—a towering man clad in gleaming armor, the Marxx family crest emblazoned on his breastplate. He stood over six and a half feet tall, a greatsword strapped across his back nearly as long as he was tall.

Beneath his plumed helmet, a scarred face sneered with clear disdain.

Even if Luke hadn’t caught the derision in his tone, it was written all over his face.

Rather than take offense, Luke chuckled inwardly.

Another fool who doesn’t know his place, he thought, letting his gaze bore into the man.

“Name yourself, warrior,” Luke said, hands clasped casually behind his back.

Even standing tall, Luke was still at least a head shorter than the burly knight before him. Yet there wasn’t a flicker of fear in his eyes—not even in the face of a man who looked capable of cleaving him in two with a single swing of that monstrous greatsword.

The knight’s face darkened, blood rushing to his head as his anger surged. It wasn’t often that a man of his station was spoken to so brazenly.

“You dare disrespect me, boy?” he growled, his voice low and dangerous.

Luke jutted out his chin, stubbornness flaring in his gaze. “I’ll speak to you however I damn well please.”

The scene would’ve been comical—like a fox baring its teeth at a bear—if not for the suffocating tension in the air. Slowly, deliberately, the knight reached for the hilt of his greatsword, the intent behind his movements unmistakable.

“Hold on,” Tristan stepped forward, positioning himself between the two. His voice was calm, but his face was weary. “Let’s not get into a pissing contest before we’ve even left the capital.”

“Tristan, why are you protecting this brat?” the knight snapped. “This pompous son of a whore just insulted me—me, the second prince’s personal knight. I have every right to reclaim my honor!”

“Aye, you do,” Tristan nodded, unfazed. “But what do you think the king will say when he finds out you killed General Drakon over a bruised ego?”

Silence fell like a guillotine.

Luke watched the knight’s face twist—anger giving way to confusion, then horror. It was clear now that the man had no idea who he was speaking to.

“My... my apologies, General!” the knight stammered. He cupped his fists and bowed deeply. “This humble knight’s name is Richard Boyd.”

Luke’s brows lifted slightly. The name stirred a memory.

“What’s your relation to Master Boyd of Clayton City?” he asked cautiously.

Richard raised his head, eyes narrowing with surprise. “You know my father?”

Luke felt a chill ripple through his body. His posture straightened as he studied the man more closely. Now that the fire of anger had faded, the resemblance was unmistakable. Same broad build. Same steely gaze. Only the beard was missing.

“Your father is a dear friend,” Luke said, his tone softening. “We fought side by side at the siege of Clayton City—thirty thousand Lhair soldiers against our walls. He was my teacher… and the one who appointed me as his deputy general.”

As the words sank in, Richard’s expression shifted once more—from surprise to awe. He bowed deeply again, this time not just to Luke’s rank, but to the man behind it.

“General Drakon,” he said with reverence, “my father spoke very highly of you. Please, punish me for my ignorance!”

His words drew gasps from the other soldiers nearby.

Luke raised an eyebrow, clearly not expecting such a dramatic shift in tone.

Could it be that Master Boyd told his son about my role in the siege? About the Thunder Bombs? Luke wondered. Though the thought piqued his curiosity, this wasn’t the time for such questions.

“What are you doing?! Bow to the general!” Richard barked at his entourage, who had been frozen in stunned silence.

“We greet the General!”

The ten or so soldiers accompanying him shouted in unison, cupping their fists and bowing in deference. Not one dared to disobey.

Luke waved a hand dismissively. “Raise your heads. It’s forgiven,” he said calmly, choosing not to dwell on the incident. “Let’s move—we’ve delayed long enough. I doubt the second prince will be pleased.”

Fortunately, Richard and his retinue obeyed without hesitation, and the group soon set off toward the meeting point.

This time, as they made their way through the streets of Ralis City, people stepped aside. Vendors, passersby, even beggars moved quickly from their path. Between the Marxx family crest on Richard’s gleaming armor and the escort of elite soldiers, not even the Black Tiger Gang dared interfere.

Tristan, Richard, and Luke walked at the front, with Luke positioned between the two larger men. Though he looked like a boy beside them, he carried himself with a quiet authority, his back straight and steps measured.

“How many men do we have?” Luke asked, clasping his hands behind his back like a man taking a stroll rather than leading a military unit.

“Fifty royal guards, General,” Richard replied, dipping his head respectfully. “It may not seem like much, but these are elite warriors—each one personally trained by the Master of Arms.”

Luke gave a small nod, his expression unreadable. “And the crown prince? How many follow him?”

There was a beat of silence before Richard answered.

“The crown prince has been given twenty men...” His voice faltered slightly. “But... he’ll be accompanied by the Blood General’s descendant.”

Luke’s eyes widened before he caught himself, quickly restoring his composure.

Hayden is coming here?

A flicker of unease tightened in his chest.

He had entrusted Victoria with infiltrating the crown prince’s escort, but with only twenty soldiers, protecting Leo would be difficult. And now, Hayden Hart—the Blood General’s heir—was joining the expedition.

A chill prickled the back of his neck.

Hayden wasn’t just a formidable tactician—he was a bloodthirsty madman. Worse still, he was under the impression that Victoria belonged to him. At least, that’s what Luke had been led to believe.

Would she be alright? What if he finds her? What would he do?

“General Drakon,” Tristan’s voice broke through his thoughts. “I understand you assisted General Hart during the capture of Xiu Fortress. What’s your impression of him?”

Luke exhaled slowly. “He’s capable,” he admitted. “Though his methods... would be considered extreme by orthodox standards.”

“I’ve heard the same,” Tristan said, nodding in agreement. “As long as he protects the crown prince and completes the mission, there’s nothing to worry about.”

Luke nodded along, offering a neutral smile—but inwardly, he disagreed.

Hayden Hart was ruthless. Bloodthirsty. And worst of all… interested in him.

The memory of those cold, piercing blue eyes came unbidden—eyes that seemed to see straight through his skin, burrowing into the soul beneath. Luke shivered, remembering the almost effeminate grace with which Hayden carried himself, a deceptive elegance that masked something far darker.

I really don’t want to see that guy again, Luke thought grimly.

It was one thing to be analyzed by a general. It was another to be read, laid bare by a man with a twisted smile and too many questions.

Convincing Hayden of how he’d "coincidentally" discovered the gunpowder recipe had already taken all of Luke’s cunning. And even then, the general’s knowing grin had made it clear—he wasn’t convinced.

In fact, the more Luke had tried to deflect, the more intrigued Hayden had become. Interest had turned into infatuation. And Luke knew—the more exposure, the greater the danger.

“We’re coming up on the meeting point now,” Richard announced.

Ahead, the massive city gate loomed, and just beyond it, a cluster of armored men and distinguished officials waited.

Luke's shoulders tensed as his eyes scanned the crowd. Please… don’t let him be here. His gaze swept across the group, only easing when he confirmed Hayden's absence.

Thank the gods.

As the group approached, those near the gate turned to acknowledge them. Judging by their expressions, it was clear that Luke’s party was the last to arrive.

He spotted the crown prince standing at the front, regal and composed. Their eyes met.

“Viscount Drakon,” Prince Leo greeted, a polite smile on his lips. Yet beneath the surface, Luke detected a hint of irritation. “It is good to see you again.”

Luke cupped his fist and bowed slightly. “Likewise, Your Highness.”

“I don’t understand why Father assigned you to my brother’s party,” Leo said, his voice sharp despite the calm exterior. His gaze shifted toward the opposite group. “It’s unfortunate. I would have preferred you at my side during this mission.”

So that’s why he’s displeased, Luke realized.

Their first meeting had made quite an impression. Leo had outright asked him to join his faction—even though the royal academy strictly forbade students from involving themselves in political factions.

If Luke had been appointed to the crown prince’s retinue, Leo likely would have tried again to recruit him, perhaps even more aggressively this time.

Maybe it’s for the best, Luke thought, allowing himself a quiet breath of relief.

Suddenly, a loud, slurred voice shattered the tension.

“Bring me more wine!”

Heads turned.

A young man in ostentatious robes stood wobbling near a carriage, an empty cup slipping from his fingers and clattering to the ground. Before he could fall, Richard rushed forward and caught him.

“Unhand me, you bastard!” the man snarled, shoving at the knight with drunken clumsiness.

Luke narrowed his eyes, the last sliver of patience draining from him.

So this is the second prince... the one I have to babysit.

The man was already drunk before the expedition had even begun. Luke glanced back at Prince Leo, who offered a wry, knowing smile.

Suppressing a sigh, Luke straightened his posture and turned toward the crown prince.

“My apologies, Your Highness,” he said, cupping his fist again. “I should get going. I wish you luck.”
