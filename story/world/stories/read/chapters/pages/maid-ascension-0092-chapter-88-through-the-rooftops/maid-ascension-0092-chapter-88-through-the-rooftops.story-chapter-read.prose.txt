By the time the dinner bell echoed across Castle Elthram, Lira was already leaving through the outer gate.

She carried a satchel containing a novel, several folded sheets of paper, and a small bottle of ink wrapped in cloth. She also had a new paper rope wound tightly around her torso beneath her maid's uniform. Everything had been carefully prepared hours beforehand.

One of the gate guards glanced at her as she approached.

"We'll be closing the gates soon."

"I'll be spending the night visiting a friend."

The man looked her up and down before a knowing grin spread across his face.

"Have fun."

Lira flushed, offended by his lewd insinuation. For a brief moment, she considered correcting him.

Then she simply nodded and continued walking.

It wasn't worth making a fuss. Maids left the palace all the time, but arguing with a guard would only attract attention.

Rather than heading directly toward the slums, Lira took the long route through the merchant district until she found a small tea house overlooking one of the busier streets.

She ordered a glass of freshly pressed pear juice and spent nearly two hours quietly reading while the city gradually settled into evening.

As laborers started returning home and merchants closed their stalls, Lira watched the owner begin stacking chairs onto the empty tables.

"We're closing."

Lira thanked him, finished the last of her drink, paid, and stepped back into the street, with night having fully set in.

The city looked entirely different after dark. Without the endless crowds, every footstep echoed farther, while lantern light illuminated only small circles of stone, leaving the spaces between them dissolved in shadow.

Lira made her way toward the poorer districts before slipping into a narrow alley hidden between two rundown houses.

Only once she was certain she was alone did she remove the bottle of ink from her satchel. She poured a careful puddle onto one of the prepared sheets.

"Asatan. Tir. Kisar."

The liquid stirred like dozens of tiny black insects. The ink crawled across the paper, twisting into perfect runes under her control.

One after another, she began preparing her protections.

"Kisar. Maja. Tureth. Tarasana. Kurur."

Mana Stabilization.

"Kisar. Maja. Veshar. Tarasana. Kurur."

Mana Obfuscation.

Any magical disturbances surrounding her should now be invisible to mage sight, appearing as nothing more than ordinary ambient mana.

Satisfied, Lira prepared the remaining sheets.

"Kisar. Riad. Luneth. Tarasana. Kurur."

Air Purification.

The air surrounding her immediately smelled considerably fresher, carrying away the nearby scent of sewage as well as her own scent.

"Kisar. Picos. Hishar. Tarasana. Kurur."

Her modified sound-dampening rune activated, and unlike Prince Aric's original design, the rune muted only sounds leaving the spell's area.

"Kisar. Yorad. Veshar. Tarasana. Kurur."

Body Obfuscation.

The spell didn't seem to do much from her perspective, but she knew from experience that it made her harder to notice, like a mirage in the desert.

Satisfied, she activated the final layer of protection.

"Paldina. Ladar. Pristana. Turat. Oshkan."

Danger Sense.

A divination spell that would activate if she found herself in danger.

Now that all her defenses were up, only one final spell remained.

"Ladar. Oshkan. Pika."

Her Place divination stretched outward before locking onto its target, tugging gently to the east, where Sir Salvek was.

Normally, the spell would have been unreliable without prior information, but Lira had already absorbed his personal journal file and knew the general description of the building where the "mud fishing" operation took place.

Lira walked through the sleeping city like a ghost. The combination of spells proved astonishing. As long as she avoided walking directly beneath one of the few lanterns, she was nearly invisible.

Several drunken youths stumbled past her laughing, and none of them even glanced in her direction. She was even able to sneak past stray cats, normally very wary of humans in neighborhoods like these.

Lira couldn't help feeling slightly impressed by her own progress. At the start of winter, she barely had any idea what magic was. But after months of independent research, she could now do this.

Eventually, the divination led her to the edge of the slums.

Lira stopped and examined the area. The neighborhood certainly wasn't wealthy, but neither was it the miserable collection of collapsing shacks described in Salvek's journal.

While it was true that some of the houses lacked proper exterior finishes, with unvarnished raw wood exposed to the elements, most had proper roofs, and some even had glass windows. The streets were also reasonably clean.

Lira would have called it middle class, perhaps...

She looked around again.

...or maybe this was simply what a noble like Salvek considered scandalously poor.

The building in question was three stories tall and had a noticeably newer appearance than the surrounding homes.

Lira wasn't about to enter through the front door, magical stealth or not. Instead, she crossed to the building opposite before looking upward.

She should be able to climb it reasonably well. It was nowhere near as high as the palace walls.

"Pika. Asatan. Farshum."

She unwound the paper rope from beneath her dress and had it slither upward until it latched onto one of the structure's supporting beams.

Lira really hadn't expected climbing buildings to become such an important skill after accepting work as a palace maid.

Even with magic assisting her, reaching the rooftop left her breathing heavily.

She collapsed onto the tiles with an exhausted sigh.

"I really need more exercise," she muttered. "Maybe I should practice the exercises in the Sword Saint manual. Or maybe I could create a spell that mimics the effects of exercise."

After allowing herself a minute to recover, she slowly crawled toward the edge. Only her eyes peeked over the roofline.

"Pika. Cipos. Syneth."

Her vision sharpened, making everything appear closer.

"Pika. Cipos. Hishar. Kaldris. Velar."

Focused Hearing.

"Pika. Selan. Tureth... Pika. Velar. Syneth."

Meditation and Enhanced Perception.

The world became wonderfully clear as she began absorbing information and sorting it into an orderly whole.

The first floor was a lobby area. Through one of the rooms, Lira spotted a seating couch where a nobleman was drinking with a scantily clad young woman.

The second floor held the bedrooms, judging from the sounds. Lira heard heavy masculine breathing, loud feminine moans, and the rhythmic sound of a bed striking the wall. Most of the curtains were closed, and the only room that wasn't made Lira wish it had been.

Ordinarily, she might have blushed at the sight. Instead, her enhanced perception quietly assembled details she wouldn't even have known to look for.

The woman was pretending to enjoy herself. Her reactions came a fraction of a second too late, and every gasp sounded rehearsed.

Meanwhile, the older man's breathing was labored and uneven.

Lira recognized him as Lord Taren, Master of Coin, and a distant cousin of the king. He was technically in charge of Harwin and the bookkeeping tower, as such a lofty position could only be "trusted" to a noble. In reality, everyone knew he was worthless, so they simply dealt directly with Harwin instead.

Here he was, a married man having relations with a girl the same age as his daughter.

Lira immediately shifted elsewhere.

The uppermost floor held cramped rooms for the girls and the few guards. From the brief glimpses she caught through the boarded-up windows, the rooms were dark and barely furnished.

As she continued scanning the building, she heard crying.

"Don't cry, Yolanda."

"I can't help it..." The girl's voice shook. "He punched me."

Another woman sighed tiredly.

"I know."

"When I told Captain Salvek..." Yolanda sniffled. "He thanked me for informing him and said he'd make the customer pay extra for damaging the goods."

"I know..." the older woman repeated softly.

"I want to go home."

"Don't!" the older woman said sharply.

"I want to go back to the farm."

"No. If you run away, they'll find you. You need to trust me."

"They won't."

"They will."

The older woman's voice became frighteningly calm.

"And if they do..." She stopped. "They'll make sure everyone else watches when they punish you."

Silence.

"They might let you leave after a year or two," said the older woman, "once the nobles get bored with you."

"Thadwick promised..." Yolanda whispered. "He said I'd earn silver. He said I might even become some noble's mistress..." Another sob escaped her. "If I came with him."

"Thadwick is a liar." She paused. "Was a liar."

"But now that they've got you," the older woman said, sounding utterly exhausted, "they don't need to lie anymore."

"I should've stayed home harvesting turnips..."

Even through meditation, Lira felt something twist inside her chest.

She quietly moved on.

This time she found Salvek's office back on the first floor. Only part of the room was visible through the shutters, but the voice was unmistakable.

"Dario, how's the new girl doing?" said the old knight.

"She is still crying, sir," answered a younger man. "Mina went to comfort her."

"Pathetic." Salvek snorted. "She couldn't even handle one punch."

Lira's fingers slowly curled against the rooftop tiles.

"When Lord Cohen visits tomorrow," Salvek continued casually, "send the new one to him."

The younger man, Dario, hesitated.

"...Sir?"

"He likes them frightened," said Salvek.

Silence followed, then a hesitant, "Yes, sir."

"As for Mina?" He let out a cruel chuckle.

"Send her to me after she's finished. I'll teach her why she shouldn't take the initiative without being ordered."

"Yes, sir."

Lira was so angry that her meditation spell shattered. Bile rose into her throat, and for one horrifying moment, she thought she was going to vomit.

She swallowed hard and took a deep breath. She couldn't go in without a plan. Salvek was a noble. There would be an extensive investigation if she simply started throwing fireballs into his office.

Tonight had only been about reconnaissance. She couldn't afford to become reckless just because she was angry.

As she stared down at the rooftop beneath her hands, only one thought remained.

Salvek couldn't be allowed to continue. The sooner, the better.
