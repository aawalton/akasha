Bernard started to float.

The bastard actually started to float, his massive frame rising into the air as the Soulkeeper’s Crown settled onto his head. Cade smacked his forehead with an audible slap, not bothering to hide his exasperation despite the imminent danger.

“Are you—” Cade threw his hands up. “Who puts on a creepy artifact in a place literally called a crypt? Did your mother drop you on your head as a child?”

Bernard’s triumphant smile faltered as the crown’s dark metal began to pulse with sickly green energy. Its central gem flared with blinding intensity, bathing the chamber in emerald light. His eyes widened in sudden comprehension—too late, much too late. Veins of emerald light spread from the crown across his face, tracing intricate patterns beneath his skin.

“Something’s wrong,” Nora whispered beside Cade, her greatsword steady despite her weakened state from protecting the unconscious Evie. “That crown... it’s alive.”

Bernard’s crew formed a protective semicircle below their hovering leader, weapons drawn. Augustus Vale, the Stone Britches’ second-in-command, pointed his barbed spear at Cade, the intricate tattoos on his arms seeming to writhe in the eerie light.

“Stay back!” Augustus shouted, his voice edged with uncertainty. “This is our prize. You’ve lost, Stormhollow.”

Cade might have responded with something clever if Bernard hadn’t started screaming—a sound so primal and agonized it turned Cade’s blood to ice. The gold-ranker’s body convulsed mid-air, his massive frame contorting as tendrils of sickly light erupted from his skin.

“Get back!” Cade shouted, pushing Elena behind him as he crouched into a defensive stance. “Everyone, get ready to—”

A cocoon of swirling magic enveloped Bernard, cutting off his screams and plunging the chamber into eerie silence. For three heartbeats, nothing moved. Then the cocoon shattered like glass, sending prismatic shards of energy flying across the chamber.

What emerged was no longer Bernard.

The body was his—the massive frame, the bearded face—but everything else had changed. His eyes glowed with ancient malice, pupils replaced by burning emerald slits. The crown had melded with his skull, its dark metal now seemingly part of his flesh, adorned with pulsing veins of eldritch power. When he moved, he left spectral afterimages trailing behind him, as if he existed partially in the spirit realm.

“My lord?” Augustus Vale stepped forward uncertainly, his massive frame dwarfed by Bernard’s hovering form. “Are you alright?”

The thing wearing Bernard’s body regarded Augustus with alien curiosity. Its movements were fluid yet wrong, like a puppet controlled by inexperienced hands. It descended until it stood before Augustus, and when it spoke, Bernard’s deep voice was layered with another, older voice that echoed with the whispers of countless graves.

“Augustus Vale,” it said, the name spoken with ancient knowledge. “Earth dominion. Loyal since childhood.”

Augustus took a half-step back, confusion written across his scarred face. “Bernard? What’s happened to—”

The corrupted Bernard moved with impossible speed, plunging his hand into Augustus’s chest. Unlike a physical attack, his arm phased through armor and flesh alike, leaving no wound but causing Augustus to convulse in agony. When Bernard withdrew his hand, he held something pulsing and luminous—Augustus’s core, glowing with earth magic.

The crown’s central gem brightened hungrily, and Bernard held the core before it. Tendrils of dark energy wrapped around the core, corrupting its natural amber glow with sickly green veins. When the process finished, the crown’s gem dimmed slightly, its light fractionally less intense.

Bernard returned the transformed core to Augustus’s chest with the same phasing motion. Augustus collapsed to his knees, his body wracked with spasms as the corrupted core reintegrated with his essence. When he rose again, his eyes glowed with the same emerald light as his master’s, and faint spectral afterimages followed his movements.

“Witness my gift,” the thing that was Bernard announced to his remaining crew. “Witness true power.”

One by one, he transformed them all.

Elizabeth Conroy, the crew’s assassin, didn’t even try to flee. Her pale features remained impassive as Bernard extracted her shadow-infused core, though her body trembled violently during the corruption process. When she rose with glowing eyes, her shadow dominion had taken on properties that bent light around her in unnatural ways.

Nash Redthorn, the demolitions expert, attempted to fight, hurling explosive runes that dissipated harmlessly against Bernard’s aura. His fire core, when extracted, burned with particular intensity before being smothered by the crown’s corruption.

Rufus Caldwell, their financier and occasional torturer, actually begged, his usually impeccable appearance disheveled as he groveled. The corrupted Bernard seemed amused by this, taking particular time with his transformation, drawing out his suffering before returning his corrupted water core.

Lastly came Thorne, their tracker, who simply closed his eyes in resignation as Bernard approached. His wind core, when corrupted, caused his body to occasionally flicker like a mirage when he moved afterward.

With each transformation, the crown’s central gem grew dimmer, depleted by the exertion of corruption, though it still burned with frightening intensity.

Cade’s mind raced through options, each worse than the last. This thing—whatever had possessed Bernard—radiated power beyond anything he’d ever felt. Beyond silver rank. Beyond gold rank. Perhaps beyond anything mortal.

Still, he had to try.

“Stay behind me,” he commanded, his voice steady despite the fear clawing at his throat. His destruction magic manifested around his hands, silver flames laced with tiny threads of gold as he focused his will into a protective ward. Ancient symbols carved themselves into the stone before them, Cade pouring every scrap of magical knowledge he possessed into the barrier.

The corrupted Bernard turned to him, head tilting with reptilian curiosity as his transformed crew took positions behind him.

“A ward?” The thing laughed, the sound like broken glass ground underfoot. “How... primitive.”

He gestured lazily, and Cade’s painstakingly crafted ward shattered like spun sugar, magical fragments dissipating into nothing. The backlash sent Cade staggering, pain lancing through his core as his magic rebounded.

“Shit,” Cade hissed, the word wholly inadequate for their situation. He recovered his stance, hands still wreathed in silver-gold flames, though they seemed pathetically dim compared to the power radiating from the thing before them.

The creature’s attention shifted, its gaze moving past Cade to where Gavin stood protectively holding Chubbs. The transformation on its face was immediate—rage, pure and ancient, twisted Bernard’s features into something barely recognizable.

“You,” the thing snarled, taking a step forward that left ghostly afterimages in the air. “Creation.”

Chubbs’s small body went rigid in Gavin’s arms, her eyes widening with something beyond fear—recognition. She began to tremble, tiny spasms racking her small frame as memories too vast for her childish mind tried to surface.

“No,” she whimpered, burying her face against Gavin’s chest. “No, I’m Chubbs. Just Chubbs.”

“Chubbs,” Gav affirmed, backing away as his eyes flashed amber. “She’s just a little girl. Whatever you think—”

“SILENCE!” The thing that was once Bernard roared, the sound shaking dust from the ancient ceiling. “Do you think me blind, Creation? Do you think me fooled by this... this infantile vessel? After all this time, after what you did to me, to my brothers?”

Cade moved to intercept, destruction magic blazing. “Leave her alone! She’s just a child!”

The corrupted Bernard smiled, the expression all wrong on those twisted features. “More lies, thief? How many have you told her? That she’s human? That she’s mortal?” He laughed again, the sound like bones breaking. “That the powers awakening inside her are normal?”

Chubbs’s head snapped up, her gaze fixing on Cade with a new awareness that squeezed his heart like a vise. Betrayal, confusion, and dawning understanding warred across her cherubic features.

“Cade?” she asked, voice small and suddenly older. “What is he talking about?”

“Don’t listen to him, Chubbs,” Cade urged, desperate. “He’s lying. He’s—”

“I’m lying?” The creature’s laughter cut through the chamber. “Oh, that is rich, coming from you. Tell me, thief, did you think you could hide her forever? Did you truly believe you could keep what she is from her? What do you intend to do with her?”

Chubbs stared at Cade, tears welling in her eyes. “Cade? What did you do?”

The moment stretched between them—brittle, fragile, filled with all the unspoken truths Cade had buried beneath smiles and reassurances. He opened his mouth, though whether to lie again or finally tell the truth, he’d never know.

The corrupted Bernard struck.

A wave of power slammed into them all, sending everyone but Cade flying backward. He stood his ground only through sheer stubborn will, destruction magic flaring in a desperate shield. Through watering eyes, he saw Bernard gesture, and Chubbs was yanked from Gavin’s arms by invisible force, suspended in air before floating quickly to the dais.

“NO!” Cade lunged forward, but the transformed Stone Britches intercepted him, their movements unnaturally fast, leaving spectral trails as they formed a wall between him and the dais. He blasted Augustus with a concentrated beam of destruction energy that should have incinerated him on the spot—but the corrupted man merely staggered back, his body momentarily destabilizing before reforming, eyes glowing with renewed malice.

Elizabeth materialized behind Cade, her corrupted shadow dominion allowing her to move through the darkness itself. Her daggers would have found his spine if Elena hadn’t yanked him aside, her whip dagger catching Elizabeth’s wrist. The blades still carved a shallow gash across Cade’s shoulder.

Chubbs screamed as she was pulled through the air, her small hands reaching for Cade. “Help me! CADE!”

The corrupted Bernard caught her with one massive hand, holding her aloft like a trophy as the dais beneath him began to rise toward a previously hidden aperture in the ceiling. His transformed crew moved with jerky precision toward the platform, converging to form a protective circle.

“I thank you for delivering her to me,” the thing wearing Bernard’s face called down. “After millennia imprisoned, I shall finally complete what I began. The ritual calls for the blood of Creation—how fitting that it shall be you, dear aunt, who ushers in our new age.”

Cade fought with desperate ferocity, his destruction magic manifesting as arcs of silver-gold flame that illuminated the ancient chamber in strobing flashes. He carved a path toward the rising dais, each step a battle, each breath a prayer.

“Elena, left flank!” he shouted, spotting Elizabeth’s shadow-warped form materializing from darkness.

Elena responded instantly, her whip dagger whistling through the air with lethal precision. The blade caught Elizabeth’s arm mid-strike, opening a gash that leaked corrupted essence instead of blood. The assassin hissed—an inhuman sound—but barely slowed.

Gav, still reeling from Bernard’s initial assault, struggled to his feet. His eyes flashed amber as partial transformation rippled across his features—claws extending, teeth sharpening—but the lycanthrope couldn’t complete the shift. Blood trickled from his nose, the psychic backlash from trying to reach Chubbs’ mind still reverberating through his consciousness.

“I can’t—” he gasped, staggering forward. “Something’s wrong with my—”

Nash’s corrupted runes interrupted him, sizzling through the air in emerald arcs. They detonated in cascading explosions, the concussive force sending Cade tumbling across the stone floor. His skin blistered where the corrupted fire touched him, pain unlike anything he’d experienced from normal flames.

“Cade!” Rayka’s voice cut through the chaos. She darted between two acid pools Rufus had conjured, tossing Cade a small blade that gleamed with familiar enchantments. “Get her back!”

He caught the blade mid-roll and immediately understood. With a flick of his wrist, Cade sent the enchanted blade spinning into the heart of the next rune cluster. The resulting detonation created a momentary gap in the barrage.

Bunny seized the opportunity, launching his small white form through the opening with surprising ferocity. The dragonling’s claws left phosphorescent trails in the air as he latched onto Thorne’s face, preventing the wind-corrupted tracker from maintaining his near-invisibility.

Cade sprinted through the opening, destruction magic condensing around his fist into a concentrated spear of superheated flames.

Twenty feet to the dais.

Fifteen.

Every muscle burned with effort, every sense heightened with desperate focus.

Augustus Vale intercepted him, the corrupted warrior’s massive frame moving with impossible speed. Earth dominion erupted from his hands in twisted spikes that burst from the floor like obsidian teeth. Cade launched himself into a controlled slide, the spikes missing him by inches as he channeled destruction magic down into his palm, shattering the stone beneath Augustus’s feet.

But while Augustus stumbled, Rufus struck from behind. Water—blackened and viscous with corruption—coalesced into whips that lashed across Cade’s back, the impact driving him to his knees. Each droplet that touched his skin burned like acid, eating through his armor.

“Ten feet!” Elena shouted, her whip dagger a silver blur as she fought to reach him. “Cade, you’re almost—”

A wall of corrupted runes erupted between them, cutting Elena off mid-sentence. Through the sizzling barrier, Cade glimpsed her dispatching Elizabeth with a vicious counterstrike, but four more corrupted thralls had materialized from the shadows to replace the assassin.

Nora remained at the chamber’s edge, her massive blade creating a protective perimeter around Evie’s unconscious form. Her eyes met Cade’s across the battlefield—a silent apology. She couldn’t abandon her ward, not even for this.

Cade dragged himself upright, his vision blurring as he forced his destruction magic into a final, desperate surge. The dais was rising faster now, nearly at the aperture, Chubbs’s terrified face a pale oval against the darkness.

Bunny, sensing his master’s intent, disengaged from Thorne and made a wild leap toward the dais. For one breathtaking moment, it seemed the little dragonling might make it—his claws extended, just inches from the platform’s edge.

Then Bernard gestured almost casually. A cage of sickly green energy manifested around Bunny mid-leap, suspending the dragonling in air. Bunny thrashed against the barrier, his small form radiating defiance, but the corrupted magic held firm.

“So much effort,” Bernard’s layered voice mocked, “for something so inevitable.”

With a final burst of destruction magic, Cade launched himself toward the dais. For one suspended moment, his fingers actually brushed its stone edge—close enough to see the ancient runes carved into its surface, close enough to feel the cold emanating from its core. Close enough for Chubbs to reach toward him, her small fingers stretching desperately for his.

“Cadey—!” Her voice cracked with terror and betrayal.

Their fingertips almost touched.

Then Augustus struck from below as the large man ascended, a pillar of corrupted earth magic slamming into Cade’s chest with bone-crushing force. The impact sent him hurtling backward, destruction magic dispersing in useless sparks around him. He crashed into a stone column, the impact reverberating through his entire body as darkness edged his vision.

“Gavin!” Cade shouted over the pain and the chaos. “Can you reach her mind?”

The lycanthrope’s eyes blazed amber as he concentrated, one hand extended toward Chubbs. “I—I can’t! Something’s blocking me—something ancient and cold. It’s like trying to push through solid stone!”

The dais continued its inexorable ascent, carrying Bernard, Augustus, and most devastatingly, Chubbs, toward the ceiling. The aperture dilated wider to receive them, ancient mechanisms grinding as the entire structure trembled.

“I wish you a satisfying death,” the thing that had been Bernard called down, its voice echoing through the chamber as the dais neared the opening. “Though I doubt it will be quick.”

As if triggered by his words, the channels surrounding the dais began to overflow with glowing liquid, spilling across the stone floor and eating through it like acid. The corrupted Stone Britches members still battling Cade’s crew leapt impossibly high, landing on the rising dais with their master.

Chubbs’s terrified face disappeared into the darkness above as the aperture began to close.

“CHUBBS!” Cade’s scream tore from his throat, raw with desperation and rage. Destruction magic exploded outward from his core in a shockwave that cracked the stone beneath their feet—but it was too late. The ceiling sealed shut with a final, damning rumble of stone.

Cade stood amid the devastation of his own making, chest heaving, magic crackling around his clenched fists. The faces of his remaining crew reflected his own devastation—Elena’s tearstained fury, Nora’s grim determination as she cradled Evie, Gavin’s helpless rage, Rayka’s quiet despair.

Jer, sacrificed to the crypt’s hunger. Orro, lost in the flood. And now Chubbs, stolen by something ancient and terrible wearing Bernard’s skin.

Cade turned to what remained of his family, his eyes burning with a promise that transcended words. “We’re getting them back,” he vowed, voice like gravel over glass. “All of them.”

The stone beneath their feet began to crack.
