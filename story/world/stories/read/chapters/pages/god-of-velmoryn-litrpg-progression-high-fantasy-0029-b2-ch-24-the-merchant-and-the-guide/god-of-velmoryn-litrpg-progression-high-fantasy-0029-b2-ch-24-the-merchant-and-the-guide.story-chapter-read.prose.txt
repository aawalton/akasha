Even though I had already decided to enter the Pantheon, first I needed answers to two crucial questions. Was it truly safe, and how was I supposed to enter it in the first place?

Rather than attempting to speculate on my own, I activated Guidance.

[Pantheon - General Information]

Entering the Pantheon is completely safe, for no god’s life may ever be endangered within its boundaries. However, safety does not mean freedom from risk. While within, a deity may be pressured into an unfavorable pact, manipulated through hidden terms, or, on rare occasions, challenged to a duel of gods. Other dangers also exist, though they occur even less frequently. Regardless of circumstance, the Pantheon guarantees that no divine being may suffer true death. If any deity violates laws, you may seek judgment from the five founder deities who established the Pantheon.

Entry requires nothing but the god’s will, provided all conditions have been met.

Your Angel may accompany you, though doing so will increase the entry cost proportionally to its strength.

This information is a bit vague. My life cannot be in danger, but I could be forced into forming a pact? And who knows what kind of pact that would be. And what about the duel challenge?

I was already considering abandoning the idea of entering the Pantheon when another blue notification appeared before me.

[A Message]

Congratulations on raising your Rank, mighty God of Velmoryn!

May I, the humble avatar of the God of Merchants, V, be allowed to enter your kingdom?

Soon, kneeling V appeared in my realm.

“Your presence is even more overwhelming now, Your Lordship,” he said, rising slowly. His eyes wandered across my dominion as though checking what had changed. I even noticed a faint smile on his face once he confirmed that everything looked exactly as it had before.

“How did you know that I’ve raised my Rank?” I asked. I had not planned to hide it, but the fact that V already knew left me confused.

“Your kingdom is no longer in the mortal world,” V replied without hesitation, then bowed deeply. “And only a senseless person would fail to notice how your presence has grown.”

The problem with V was that I never knew when he meant what he said. For all I knew, he could have been trying to flatter me. Yet I still wanted to know whether my presence had truly changed. V could not see my actual form. He only saw the puppet seated on the throne, the image I used to represent myself. So if he had genuinely sensed a difference, then something about my divinity had indeed changed because everything he could actually see was exactly the same as before.

“My King, why are you not speaking to this creature directly?” Aurelion asked after silently observing us for a while.

I hesitated. Even though I knew Aurelion would remain loyal no matter what, I still did not want to admit that I had spent this entire time acting like a mighty deity while hiding my true, pitiful form from everyone’s eyes.

“No one but you has seen my true form,” I said at last, choosing to reveal only part of the truth.

Aurelion’s face brightened instantly. He lifted his head and struck his fist against his chest. “I understand. Only the most loyal should be allowed to witness your greatness directly.”

I nodded and turned back to V. He had shaped a contract and was adjusting it by gliding his glowing index finger across the parchment. Once satisfied, he sent it drifting toward the puppet figure of the God of Velmoryn seated on the throne.

“Your Lordship, information about you has been purchased by a few dozen more deities, and now that the restriction has vanished after your kingdom left the mortal world, I will gladly deliver your share,” he said.

I made my manifestation nod and quickly checked the contract.

After confirming that the part about conditions and restrictions was correct, I read the last line specifying the amount of Divinity Points I would receive.

12,724.

It was far more than I could have hoped for. It might even have been more than the total divinity I had accumulated since becoming a god.

Unwilling to waste even a second more than needed, I quickly confirmed the contract by touching the parchment with my divinity at the place where my mark was required. The moment I did, dozens of silver tentacles rushed outward from V. They shot toward the God of Velmoryn on the throne and shifted in color midway, turning crimson.

[Congratulations, Verde, you’ve gained 12,723 Divinity Points!]

The sensation was deeply satisfying, and more than that, I genuinely felt stronger. Even the mist in my realm thickened and began to shimmer faintly.

“I did not expect so many gods to take interest in me.” My voice carried through the entire realm, strong enough that even the projection of the Crimson Guardian trembled.

V grinned and bowed slightly.

“Once the rumors spread about a new god ascending from the mortal realm, everyone grew curious. And because the demand was high, we charged everyone a premium. Well… almost everyone.”

I made the manifestation on the throne nod again. My mind was already busy planning the things I could attempt with such power, and only now was I beginning to understand how pitiful low Rank deities must appear to those above them.

If every Rank threshold truly grows tenfold, that means deities a single Rank above me have more than one hundred thousand divinity, and some might even approach a million.

The idea alone made me feel insignificant. For the first time, my initial Rank, Insignificant God, felt completely accurate. Compared to who I was now, my past self truly had been insignificant. But compared to the five deities who created the Pantheon…

I need to be very careful when dealing with gods. The gap between Ranks feels like an insurmountable barrier. Unless divine combat works very differently from how I imagine it, defeating someone above your Rank should be nearly impossible.

Entering the Pantheon seemed even less desirable now. The thought that someone of a higher Rank could challenge me to a duel was enough to make me hesitate, and I was literally the lowest Rank among them. The system had told me earlier that I would be forcefully removed from the mortal realm upon reaching the Rank Awakened Divinity instead of Ascendant Sovereign. And I was fairly confident that Awakened Divinity was the lower of the two. So in a way, I was forced to prematurely leave the mortal realm, which was likely a much safer place than where I was now.

“Your Lordship,” V said suddenly. He seemed ready to depart, so I assumed he wanted to bid farewell. “Are you planning to visit the Pantheon? If so, it would be my honor to guide you.”

I nearly cursed him.

Despite my fear of the unknown, I understood that eventually I would have to enter the Pantheon. But I wanted time to prepare. At the very least, I wanted to begin a few projects to accelerate Velmoryn's growth just in case my stay in the Pantheon dragged longer than I wanted to.

I knew it was all excuses, but the thought of stepping into a world where everyone was stronger than me, a world where deities were openly hostile toward one another, was enough to drain any enthusiasm I might have had. I was not looking forward to it, to say the least.

Yet even gods did not seem to possess complete freedom. I understood that entering the Pantheon alone would be far worse than going with V, who was prohibited from harming anyone. And no deity was allowed to harm him either.

Perhaps his company will make things safer for me.

“I have never been there,” I said, making my manifestation tap on the armrest. V already knew I understood nothing about the world of gods, so by showing that vulnerability, I was hoping to gain even a little of his trust.

“Naturally, Your Lordship. None of the deities who have just ascended from the mortal world have visited the Pantheon. Let me think what information I can offer you free of charge,” he said as he straightened and scratched a scale near his chin with his sharp fingernail. “First, I would advise using an astral body instead of your true form. Even if your life cannot be harmed there, revealing your appearance might give clues to those who wish to target you.”

I nodded, both with the puppet on the throne and my actual head. V’s advice eased some of my concerns, and for once, I believed he was being sincere.

“If you do not have an astral body, we can sell you one, Your Lordship. And we will even offer a 1% discount. Only because you are our valuable customer.”

1% discount… now I see why he is volunteering information. He wants to sell his product.

Even so, I was going to buy it. Not only would it hide my mist-born form, it would be far safer than entering the Pantheon myself.

“Also, you should bring your Angel with you. No deity enters the Pantheon without their guardian. Unless they send an avatar of themselves.”

He knows even about Aurelion…

V grinned and produced a new parchment, then began writing another contract.

“I will buy the astral body as long as the price is reasonable.”

“It is only one hundred Divinity Points, Your Lordship,” V said as he bowed and added a few final lines before sending the parchment toward my manifestation.

The moment I signed it, a strange formless figure appeared in my dominion. It was a cloud of black and dark red, hovering just above the ground. To be honest, I had expected a faceless shell or something along those lines, but the astral body looked far closer to my previous form than I’d have liked to.

[You’ve paid 99 Divinity Points to God of Merchants!]

“Now, Your Lordship, simply will the gate to appear,” V said. He stepped back and pointed at the place where I was expected to shape it.

It took only a thought, and in an instant, a silver spark appeared in my dominion. It grew quickly until it resembled a full, giant whirlpool with the silver and gray lines turning within it. Then it suddenly flared, bright enough to disrupt even my vision for a moment.

The glow faded just as quickly, leaving only the steady rotation of swirling energy behind.

“Shall we?” V asked, gesturing toward the silver portal.
