Viktor swept his gaze across the organized chaos unfolding before him.

The whole place rang with the chorus of hammers striking iron and wood, the groan of timber under strain, and the overlapping stream of gremlins yelling in each other’s faces as though volume alone could settle every dispute. All of this commotion revolved around the single structure that dominated the entire landscape, the one and only reason for every noise and curse coming from the many disparate creatures that had been gathered here.

The trebuchet.

Its massive frame rose high above the workers swarming under it, lashed together with thick bands that had been forged in the workshop of the dungeon. The throwing arm already leaned skyward, hungry and patient, while the unfinished counterweight box hung empty at its base, waiting for the stones that would eventually be poured into its belly and give the whole ugly business its point.

Gremlins crawled over the beams like insects across a fallen log. Some balanced effortlessly along the soaring lattice, tightening bolts with their nimble hands. Others huddled below, measuring lengths of rope with knotted cords or adjusting pulleys that threatened to jam at the slightest tension.

And they were far from the only creatures laboring here today.

A pack of gnolls hauled against thick ropes to hoist a massive support beam into place, moving in fits and starts as they followed the instructions of a gremlin standing nearby. Dozens of goblins darted back and forth across the entire site, fetching materials from the supply stores and depositing them wherever the shouting seemed the loudest. Near the rear of the machine, two Ettins did the job of twenty ordinary workers and produced twice as many complaints, their six heads bickering incessantly with one another the entire time. Yet, despite the constant arguing, or perhaps powered by it, somehow the timber came down exactly where the waiting gremlins wanted it. Viktor decided not to examine that miracle too closely. Some things worked better when left alone.

“You came to inspect, Master,” came a voice beside him, cutting cleanly through the surrounding din.

Viktor turned without any real surprise, already knowing full well whose voice that was before he even laid his eyes upon the source, and found himself looking down, as expected, at his chief gremlin.

“She’s coming together beautifully,” Kazyk said, his eyes glittering with unmistakable pride.

“I can see that. Now, give me a report on the progress.”

The gremlin boss’s grin widened until it seemed to stretch clear across his narrow face.

“The main frame is finished. Strong as fortress walls, Master. Cross-bracing is all done too. Axles are mounted, though we’re still polishing the bearings. Throwing arms are balanced within half a finger’s width. And the sling... it’s braided from the Dread Spiders’ silk, and that means it has more strength and durability than any rope we could have twisted ourselves.”

Viktor understood perhaps half of it. He nodded anyway.

“Everyone seems to be working with remarkable coordination,” he said.

“I bit the heads off those who don’t.”

As he chuckled at that, an Ettin lumbered past them. One head inclined toward him, its right hand rising to press against its chest as it offered an exaggerated, polite bow. At the very same time, a different head let out a deeply satisfied groan as its left hand reached back to scratch its butt with enthusiasm.

“So,” Viktor said, turning to face Kazyk directly, “when will it be ready?”

“One day if nothing goes wrong.” The gremlin boss gave a casual shrug. “Two if something inevitably does.”

“Good. But don’t forget that we need to run test shots as well, before we can trust it in an actual battle.”

Kazyk brought his hands together, rubbing them with barely contained excitement. “I’ve already had three ideas for the demonstration.”

Viktor suspected that at least two of those violated several basic principles of safety, and a couple more rules that hadn’t been written yet because nobody had considered anyone crazy enough to need them.

As though reading the skepticism written across his master’s face, the gremlin offered up an easy, disarming smile before anything was voiced aloud.

“Everything will be ready before the enemy arrives, Master. You have my word on that.”

“Well, you haven’t disappointed me so far, so I’ll take it.” Viktor turned away from the siege engine. “Keep at it, then. I’ve got other things to inspect.”

“Yes, Master.”

And so, with the matter settled for the time being, he had Celeste teleport him onward to the next destination.

He found himself standing once more in the Great Hallway inside the tomb of the second floor. The last time he walked through this place, he was accompanied by that spider-woman Akane, who carried a single torch, its flickering flame the only source of illumination in an otherwise vast and suffocating darkness. Now, however, the passage was flooded with a bright white light that poured in from both sides. On the ramp between them, silver-armored Templars marched alongside the tomb guards, moving up and down as they drilled their formations over and over again.

Heavy, metallic footsteps announced company before he turned. A hulking figure came to a halt at his side, an enormous warhammer resting across one armored shoulder as though it weighed nothing at all.

Viktor let out a low chuckle at the sight of it. “When was the last time I saw you looking like this? I almost forgot this was your true appearance.”

“Both are my true appearance, Master,” said the skull housed within the jar.

Well, one form was how she actually, physically existed, while the other was merely a projection she had chosen to create for herself, one whose appearance could be reshaped however she pleased. Then again, if she insisted that both forms were equally true, equally hers, then who was he to argue the point any further? There was, after all, no real reason to debate the matter. As long as she kept swinging that hammer of hers in the right direction, it was all that actually mattered.

He returned his gaze to the ramp, where the soldiers continued their drilling with the tireless, joyless efficiency of things that had never needed to eat, sleep, or complain.

“Is everyone ready?”

“Yes, Master,” Galatea said. “You still intend for this place to be where our decisive battle happens?”

Viktor nodded.

Even though he now knew that the invaders would come for the Core Room, which meant they would inevitably push their way through the fourth floor, he had decided not to change the plan. After all, the cathedral was the dungeon’s final line of defense, and he had no intention of staking everything on one single engagement. Contingencies existed for a reason. Hence, his forces would still meet the invaders here, on this second floor, and if the battle didn’t go his way, he would have them fall back to the fourth floor and make their last stand there.

He let his gaze drift toward the rows of Wisps lining the hallway, providing the illumination.

“They’re the ones who will weave the net of light to trap that bastard?”

“Yes. They hover before the walls now only because this is merely a drill. In the actual battle, they would instead take up positions inside them. The net of light, combined with the stone of the walls, would form an impenetrable barrier to both corporeal and incorporeal beings, sealing this passage against anything that might try to force its way through.”

Viktor studied them, each one a glowing orb roughly the size of a human head. Normally, they were just weak monsters with barely any offensive capability to speak of, which meant in most dungeons that had them, their roles were to distract the adventurers while other monsters did the actual work of killing. But here, in this hallway, they were about to play a very important part of his plan.

“What are they, exactly?” he asked.

“When someone dies,” Galatea said, “their soul lingers in the Realm Beyond only for the briefest of moments. And during that fleeting window, if nothing intervenes to preserve that soul—as mine was by the grace of my Lord, for example—then it will inevitably be drawn toward Oblivion and lost forever. Yet, even a lost soul leaves a mark on the spirit world behind it. A residue. An echo. Faint traces of a life that was once lived. And it is from those lingering remnants that a ghost of the deceased eventually takes shape.”

Viktor chuckled. “So ghosts are not actual souls, huh?”

The skull in the jar shook itself slowly from side to side. “No. The soul itself is already gone. What is left is nothing more than an echo of what once was. It may carry fragments of a person’s memories, or hints of their personality, but it is not that person themselves.”

“These are them, right? The echoes?” Viktor said as he glanced at the ranks of silver-armored Templars.

“Yes. When they were still alive, they were devoted worshippers of the Higher Will, and after death, their ghosts continued that same devotion, wandering the spirit world in service to a faith that had outlasted their own bodies. By the grace of my Lord, they have been bound to those suits of armor, so that they may serve Him once more.”

If they had been such devoted worshippers of the Lord of Light, then why hadn’t their souls been preserved the way Galatea’s had? Was it because they were not deemed valuable enough to warrant the effort? So instead, they were left to be consumed by Oblivion, their remaining traces in the spirit world gathered up and repurposed. And just like that, what had once been faithful souls became nothing more than obedient constructs, hollow armors marching in formation without a shred of free will to call their own.

Of course, Viktor was not going to voice such thoughts to Galatea’s face. “A very nice story,” he simply said, “but I’m asking about the Wisps.”

“The ghosts wander the spirit world for as long as their echoes hold together. Well, some of them do manage to slip into the physical world and roam among the living, though since they are incorporeal beings, they don’t really interact with anything, and people who don’t have special perception won’t even notice them. Either way, those ghosts do not endure forever. Piece by piece, they break down, gradually losing whatever personality and memory they once carried. After they’ve lost most of what made them who they were, they no longer wander according to their original nature, but instead drift as nothing more than raw energy, instinctively drawn toward the brightest source nearby. In the spirit world, the Light is one such source. And the remnants that found their way to it, basking in the grace of the Higher Will, were transformed into what we call the Wisps.”

“And that’s why they can use light magic?”

“Yes, Master.”

An interesting bit of trivia, Viktor thought, storing it away in the back of his mind for when it might prove useful, if such a moment ever came to pass.

“Looks like everything is in place here. I’ll leave the rest to you and Khenemhotep. Celeste, teleport me to the Core Room.”

An instant later, he was back at her side.

[Everything seems to be in good order.]

“Well, yes.” Viktor dropped into the nearby chair. “Everything for Xander’s welcome party is coming together nicely. But, as I said, he’s the least of our problems.”

[You are right, Master. There is still the matter of the Forbidden Words, about which we’ve learned nothing at all, and the Apostle, who we now know is a living, human Reliquary, thanks to the information provided to us by the Eternal Flame.]

“Which doesn’t help much honestly,” Viktor said with a sigh. “Knowing that something is a Reliquary doesn’t tell us anything about what it can actually do.”

Of course, he knew he couldn’t realistically have expected the Eternal Flame to hand him everything he wanted on a silver platter, but even so, he couldn’t quite shake the disappointment that settled over him at the thought. After all, knowing that the enemy was powerful and extremely hard to kill did absolutely nothing for a man’s peace of mind.

“No point in complaining about it, though. The best we can do now is hold as many cards as possible and improvise when the game eventually starts.”

Even though he had already made up his mind to have the decisive battle take place in the great tomb, that didn’t mean he intended to sit on his hands until then. He was going to send out probing attacks well before that, in order to gather information about what exactly he was dealing with. However, he suspected Xander and the Apostle would simply hang back and let the other adventurers absorb the brunt of those early hits, which meant even the probing attacks needed to have enough bite to clear the fodder standing between him and any useful information.

“Now, let’s move on to a less important matter. What should we do about the Daelin team? Have you thought of anything better than just dropping a rock to block their way?”

[My apologies, Master, but this has proven a difficult problem. Both teams are heading toward the same destination, and I see no clean way to separate them.]

Same destination, huh?

Wait.

An idea suddenly dawned upon his mind, unfolding itself with a clarity that made him sit up a little straighter in his chair.

“Have Galatea pay Ekon another visit,” Viktor said as a smile spread across his face. “Tell him it’s time to finally pay off his debt.”
