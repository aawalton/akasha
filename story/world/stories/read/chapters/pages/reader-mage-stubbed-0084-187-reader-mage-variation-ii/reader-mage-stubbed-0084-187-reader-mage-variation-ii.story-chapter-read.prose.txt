The world stretched out before Merlin. His eyes pulsed like they each had hearts of their own, and the color of everything before him shifted into a warped blue. The murmurs raining down from the crowd were both muffled and clear to him in a strange manner, like he was listening to their words from a pipe stretching across rooms. The whole feeling was bizarre and disorienting, and yet he felt quite accustomed to it. Most of all, he felt significantly more powerful than he had been just a second ago.

Merlin locked eyes with Marcus, and the boy smiled, his stern expression breaking about like a mud wall rammed through by a tractor.

Any other person might have been confused as to why Marcus seemed excited, but Merlin came to a quick inference. The boy was obsessed with his abilities, and now that Merlin had just showcased something even more fascinating, it excited him.

Regardless of how they both saw each other at this moment, though, they were in a duel.

Marcus raised his hand and snapped his fingers. Merlin felt the arena quake, but no one else did. He noticed that Marcus had manipulated sound once again, but unlike before when he didn’t notice it until it hit him, Merlin saw the waves move through the air as it approached him. He took a deep breath and calmed his heart, waiting patiently to see if his newest ability functioned in the exact way he hoped it would.

He had not had the time to test it out previously, as the whole sequence that had led to its existence wasn’t a straightforward one.

After his training duel with Lee Jaehyun, he had come to a realization that trying to channel out his mana to deconstruct spells while manipulating it at the same time to use his other Perks or skills, was almost impossible.

He had likened the process to how Mages manipulated more than a couple spells in quick succession, but last night as he went through the whole process in his mind once again, he realized that he had been going about it the wrong way.

He was not like the others. He wasn’t a traditional mage like they were. He was different. And that meant the way he used his abilities would differ too. For the mages, even though the spells they were casting were technically different, they were cut from the same cloth. Chima, for example, no matter the spells he cast, it would be a fire spell in general. It always would. Basically, he was manipulating the same element, just giving it different forms.

If there was anything the System had made clear to Merlin ever since its inception to him, it was that his Perks differed. His Reader Mage ability was not the same as his Dungeon Artist Perk. They functioned differently and were bound by different and distinct rules. This was also true in Singularity Mage. Even though Kieran had never been burdened by the same problems as Merlin, specifically being restricted by the time Reader Mage needed to be activated, he had never managed to use it at the same time as he used his other abilities. It was one or the other. But Merlin knew he wasn’t as powerful as the protagonist, which meant he had to find a way to bypass the restrictions that came with Reader Mage. A cheat.

His saving grace had come in the form of Maid Eun-Wol’s brief lecture on attunement. He shouldn’t focus on his mana as a whole, but each molecule and how they were bonded. Which brought Merlin to a question. What if he could manipulate those bonds? Tweak them to fit his own ideas?

At first glance, it seemed risky, but he had lived his whole life taking risks, so Merlin didn’t hesitate. He fell into a trance immediately, and unlike the first and consecutive times he had tinkered with his ability to attune with his mana, he took a different approach. Instead of heading straight for the nucleus of his mana and boring into it, he instead focused on the particle chain around it. They were numbered in the same amount as the Perks and Skills he possessed. The molecular structures bearing the knowledge of his Perks were of a lustrous blue and green, while those possessing the knowledge of his skills were of a dull blue and red.

Merlin made his way to the one linked to Reader Mage. It was of a more complex structure than the rest, even Dungeon Artist, and for a moment, he wondered if he would be able to find a way to manipulate it to fit his ideas without destroying himself in the process.

Which was why he made a decision to be certain of what he wanted before he tinkered with the structure so as to not cause a negative nuclear reaction.

After a long while of floating in the lukewarm darkness within himself, he came to an enlightenment when he realized that the way he had been thinking about using Reader Mage with his other abilities was dysfunctional.

Spreading out his mana and trying to manipulate it so that he did more than a single thing at once was like trying to drive two steer two wheels at the same time; dividing one's attention in such a manner was a recipe for failure—and that was considering it worked.

But what if one of those wheels was left on automatic? That would give the driver ample time to focus on the other wheel and steer it in whatever way he wanted.

That gave Merlin the idea he so desperately sought.

Instead of Reader Mage to only function when his mana came in contact with his opponent’s, he decided to tinker it so it functioned in a similar manner to a circuit breaker.

Reader Mage didn’t need to come in contact with his opponent’s spell to deconstruct it any longer, all it had to do was simply exist. And that was proven effective as the sound waves produced from Marcus’s spell fizzled out halfway through the arena as it rushed towards Merlin.

[Be glad it worked, but do remain time conscious]

Merlin’s brows pulled in at the System’s note, and he didn’t need to be told twice that the thing had just warned him about being overly lackluster.

He had no intention of being that way.

Immediately, Merlin leaned back and dashed forward, a blue blur to the onlookers as he quickly closed the gap between himself and the momentarily dazed Marcus. As soon as he popped up before the boy, he tightened his right fist, the glove of mana wrapped around it bubbling with intense energy as he targeted Marcus’s midsection.

Marcus took a step back with gritted teeth, pulled his arms apart, and clapped—hard.

An ominous feeling fell upon Merlin as a heavy gust of wind blew him a few steps backwards, giving Marcus the chance to nimbly dance away from him.

For a moment, Merlin was confused, sweat gushing out of his forehead as he was caught off-guard by Marcus just making use of a wind spell, and one that had triggered a severe fright in him that had made him almost think he would die.

His confusion was immediately vaporized by the System as it appeared before his eyes.

[Spell: Cavitation Bang.Category: Advanced, Tier ISpell Wielder: A-Class Mage.Spell Description: A spell formed by shaping the nucleotide sequences present in the Mage’s modified deoxyribonucleic acid to correspond with a certain aspect of the knowledge their mana possesses. Honestly, he had used more than a hundred decibel in this instance]

[Deconstruction Complete]

[Deconstruction Process: Disrupt the bonding between the weakest of the rung base pairs of the Mage’s deoxyribonucleic acid, which is the adenine to the thymine pair, by channeling your mana into the construct of their hydrogen bond.]

[Progress to leveling up Intelligence: 78.5%/100%] → [98.5%/100%]

[Free Attribute Points received: 20]

[End this fight quickly!]

Merlin didn’t need to be told twice. One glance at the countdown hovering to the edge of his eyes, noting that just a few seconds over thirty seconds had elapsed, he sought out Marcus once again, putting aside the nagging thought that Marcus had just tried to pulverize both him and his hearing with over a hundred decibels of sound level.

He found the boy to his side with a great surge of mana bursting out of him as he prepared to cast what seemed to be another dangerous spell.

A-Class Mages sure had it nice.

Merlin decided to switch initiatives. He retreated a few steps back as the mana gloves around his arms changed forms into his [Mana Whip]. However, they didn’t take the form of the usual kind, but warped into chains with hooks at their ends.

Marcus’s brows pulled in at the sight. His earlier smile was nowhere to be seen now.

Merlin hurled the chains at the boy, and Marcus rushed out of the way, cautious of being hit.

But the chains hadn’t been heading for him.

They swerved as they got to where Marcus had been standing a second ago, changing their direction as they homed in on the Grimoire hovering in the sky.

Marcus noticed this far quicker than Merlin had hoped for, and was able to draw his Grimoire close to himself just before the chains could rip it apart.

Merlin didn’t see it as a complete loss, however, after all, his chains had ripped out one of its pages, which rapidly disintegrated into ashes in the air.

Merlin was curious if that meant Marcus had lost one of his spells—which he was most likely certain wasn’t the case—but he didn’t have the time to dwell on such thoughts. After all, his actions had annoyed Marcus.

Marcus immediately jumped on his feet and condensed the surge of mana he had gathered earlier, and an intensified fright of that which Merlin had felt a moment ago crept upon him.

Before Marcus could release the spell, Merlin clenched his fist—hard—and punched the arena. Cracks spread out from his area of impact like fissures, destroying the surface of the arena as they snaked towards Marcus.

Marcus gritted his teeth, disoriented, and had no choice but to move away from where he was standing before he sunk into the floor.

Merlin followed him. He activated [Mana Spring] and blasted himself to Marcus’s new location.

Marcus swerved on his feet and slammed his hands together, throwing a massive wave of his cavitation bang spell at Merlin. But Merlin had no intention of letting the spell make contact with him this time.

Unable to move his body in the air, Merlin resolved to rely on his [Mana Whip] skill. He shot one out towards a protruding concrete jutting out of the arena from his previous strike, and pulled himself from Marcus’s line of sight.

The spell missed.

He was not necessarily afraid of if the spell would bypass Reader Mage and affect him due to its greater intensity compared to before, what he was more afraid of was being blown several steps backward due to the aftermath once again.

As soon as his feet made contact with the floor of the arena, Merlin flashed behind Marcus, clenched his fist, and punched Marcus in the side.

The attack threw the boy all the way across the arena, rolling on the ground. He came to a stop just before he rolled off the edge and coughed up a mouthful of blood as he held his stomach tightly.

Merlin drew in a sharp gasp after that attack and fell to his knees, breathing heavily.

Reader Mage Variation II took a lot out of him stamina-wise. It was like the exhaustion of several days of working 24 hours at an oil rig slammed into him at once, and his muscles quaked in response.

His eyes shifted to what was left of the time hovering by the side of his eyes, and noted that only thirty seconds was left.

Merlin gritted his teeth. “I have to move fast.”

He pushed himself up and approached Marcus where the boy was still lying on the floor, trying to catch his breath. Then he clenched his fist once more and intensified the surge of mana wrapping it in the form of a glove.

“Give up,” he muttered.

Marcus’s dark brown eyes shifted to him, angry and filled with pain.

Merlin had made sure to hold back as much as he could, but it seemed it had still been far too much.

Marcus scoffed in Merlin’s face. “Like hell I will.”

Merlin pressed his lips together; with twenty seconds to go, he had no other choice but to knock Marcus out.

Without saying a single word more, he sent his fist down at Marcus, who just lay there, accepting his fate. However, before the attack could connect, the glove of mana vanished and so did the cloak covering Merlin. His fist froze just inches away from Marcus’s stomach, and immediately a severe pain coursed through his body. The exhaustion he had felt a few seconds ago was amplified.

Screaming, Merlin fell to the ground, startling the crowd and the instructors around. His innards felt like they were on fire, forcing him to rub his hands across every part of his body as he rolled on the floor.

Terrified by what was going on, Marcus sat up despite the pain he was feeling and pushed himself several paces backwards as his chin quivered.

“What the…?” he mumbled.

Instructor Park wasted no time flashing onto the stage as she signalled for the paramedics to hurry over.

Merlin, on the other hand, had his mind scrambled.

What’s happening to me? he thought through the pain. I had ten seconds left. Why did it vanish? What’s going on? The System. Answers! I need answers!

He got no reply from the System, however, it was completely silent.

This threw Merlin’s mind into further disorientation as tears crawled out of his eyes. The pain wasn’t reducing, it was worsening. And with it came a severe disorientation.

He felt his throat scratch and burn, like he had gulped down a cup of boiling water. His palms, however, were very cold, like they had been dipped into a bucket of ice. It was the same with every other part of his body. The temperature differed, and it felt like each part of him wasn’t his.

Merlin screamed out in pain and he saw it. A burst of flame escaped from his mouth.

He didn’t believe it. It was the pain. It was making him hallucinate. It needed to stop.

Then he felt the icy cold that had taken over his hands seep out and onto the arena, relieving him, just like his scream earlier had relieved the itch in his throat.

The murmurs from the crowd traded places for gasps and then the whole arena fell silent.

All of a sudden, the pain that had taken full control over Merlin’s body subsided, like he had released it all during his display.

He remained on the floor of the arena for a moment, taking several deep breaths as he tried to calm his raging heart. After a moment, he sat up.

His heavy eyes wandered to the crowd at first. Every single person was staring at him, pale like they had seen a ghost. Then he shifted his attention towards the people standing by his side. Paramedics were just at the edge of the arena, stunned, and Marcus was standing beside Instructor Park, both of them looking dazed.

Merlin’s heart skipped a beat, assuming that his display had forced Instructor Park to declare him unfit to continue and had given Marcus the win.

“No!” Merlin voiced, recalling the instance with Man-Shik during his own fight. As long as he proclaimed he could still do it, they would let the fight go on. “I can still fight,” he argued. “I don’t know what happened now, but I can still fight.”

However, Instructor Park didn’t reply. Her eyes simply moved from locking with him and towards something behind his back. Merlin noticed that whatever it was, it was what Marcus had been looking at too.

He turned around and froze.

Behind him was a thin layer of ice that spread all the way from his palm towards the end of the arena.

Merlin drew a sharp gasp as an incredulous stare slapped his face.

“What…” he began. “What the fuck is this?”
