Sui Duang stepped into the arena under loud cheers. He raised a hand—they cheered harder.

“Young Master Sui! Young Master Sui!”

The chanting was deafening, and well coordinated.

Sui Duang was a big man. When they first met, Gary had jokingly compared him to an NBA player. He was well over six feet tall, with a lean but powerful musculature. Though he wore the long cyan robes of a junior disciple, Gary imagined corded muscles underneath—an image only enhanced by his confident, piercing gaze. This man was no slouch. He’d seen combat, he’d trained hard, and he was ready to fight to the death if needed.

Facing him was the somewhat tall woman, Caramel. She was one of the people Gary had introduced to Bob the Cultist, their Muscle Sap distributor, meaning she was stronger than she used to be. That didn’t make her equal to Sui Duang. Even by just standing there, the difference in their aura was stark. She was like a torch before a bonfire. Her nerves were plain to see.

“You can do it, Honey Bunny!” came a shout from the disciple seats. It was Pop Ear—the man with big forearms who’d just won his own duel.

Caramel shook her head but grinned.

“Fight!” the deacon announced, and Caramel bounded forth. The orange spear she wielded left its sheath, swinging smoothly towards Sui Duang.

Wait. Where’s his spear? Gary realized.

At the last moment, Caramel changed her trajectory, the spear jerked up as if pulled by someone. Instead of Sui Duang’s chest, it was now heading straight for his head. His eyes tracked it coldly. He still hadn’t drawn his spear. There came the sound of metal slapping flesh.

Sui Duang’s hand gripped the shaft of Caramel’s spear. It had been stopped just shy of his head, yet he hadn’t even looked away.

The crowd gasped. Caramel, however, only grinned.

“I got you now!” she yelled. As she pulled away her spear, a thick, slimy substance still connected it to Sui Duang’s hand, attempting to pull her along. He resisted. “Oyaaa!” Caramel yelled, muscles bulging visibly on her arms as she pulled with all her strength.

It was like trying to pull a tree. Sui Duang gave a little, then wrapped his fingers around the substance and yanked back. Caramel lost her balance. Her eyes widened. Sui Duang lowered his body, pulled back harder to send her flying towards him, then stabbed out with the two fingers on his other hand. A blast of blue aura crashed into her, bending her at the waist, sending her flying back and crashing into the arena wall. Sand flew. Caramel collapsed face-first.

“Winner, Sui Duang!” the deacon announced, rushing to check her condition.

Sui Duang looked at his hand, which still gripped Caramel’s substance. His aura activated. A moment later, he let her spear drop, then raised his hand in victory. The crowd thundered.

“Sui Duang! Sui Duang!”

“Damn it! Caramel!” Pop Ear yelled, jumping on stage. She’d already regained consciousness, thankfully. “Don’t speak,” Pop Ear told her, picking her up in a princess carry. He turned to glare at Sui Duang, but he’d already turned his back and jumped to his seat.

“I’m sorry…” Caramel whispered, burying her head in his chest.

“It’s okay, Honey Bunny. There was nothing you could do. You fought well,” he replied tenderly.

“Awww!” the crowd said.

Pop Ear jumped back to their seats and deposited Caramel, who was immediately swarmed by her friends. She waved them away, indicating she was fine.

Meanwhile, Gary was glued to his seat. He replayed the battle in his mind.

He defeated her so easily… Without even using his spear…

His eyes traveled to the spot in the sand where they’d fought. Despite not using his spear, Sui Duang hadn’t taken a single hit, nor had he moved a step. A single, casual attack had ended the match.

How could there be such a big difference between them? Gary wondered. Could I have done the same? No way…

He wanted to go train, but the clan’s instructions had been explicit. On the first day only, they needed to be present for the whole thing.

“I wonder how they know it will take nine days,” Alicia wondered. “They said each participant fights once per day and gets eliminated at two losses, right? It sounds unpredictable.”

“It’s basic math,” Cog replied.

“How so, genius?”

“It would rattle your little battle-oriented head. To keep it short, I simulated the tournament a thousand times, assuming high variance in the abilities of the participants. The cutoff of sixteen participants occurred almost always sometime on the ninth day.”

“If you need to simulate it, it isn’t basic math,” Gary said absent-mindedly.

“It’s basic for me,” Cog said, raising his nose up high.

Alicia crossed her arms. “Well, I don’t think that’s right. I’ve been here every year, and it’s always seven days. Since we have double the number of disciples this year because of the second clan selection, shouldn’t it be double the days?”

“The participants increase, but so do the daily duels. Don’t bog yourself in the details. Look, Feng Shen is going up. Just enjoy the show.”

“Feng Shen?” Gary’s eyes snapped open. The number one disciple had just walked on stage. His name thundered the audience, the chanting stronger but less coordinated than when Sui Duang had appeared.

Feng Shen stood humbly, greeting his unlucky opponent with a nod of the head. Said opponent—a short but stout man—cupped his fists. “Please give me guidance,” he said.

“Fight!” the deacon said, and the short man charged first. He gathered enough aura to make his spear glow. Knowing he could never win, he used his strongest strike from the start.

“Farmhand Toils the Land!” he roared, falling like a silver meteor.

Feng Shen stabbed two fingers into the attack. There came a clash of aura—a mixture of black and white, swiftly drowning out the silver. The world snapped when the colors mixed. When Gary could understand what he was seeing again, the short man stood silent, while his spear span through the air to land at the edge of their arena. He brought his palms together and bowed.

“Thank you for your mercy,” he said.

“It was a decent strike. You possess strength, but your speed and stability are lacking. If you practice a technique like Fan the Spear, your grip over your weapon will be harder to break,” Feng Shen said.

“I will follow your advice. Thank you, senior,” the man said, bowing again. Clapping filled the stands. The other three battles were still ongoing, but nobody was watching them when Feng Shen was on the stage.

“He’s a good guy, right Gary?” Cog asked, getting no reply. “Gary?”

Gary’s eyes were wide and glued to the stage. “I couldn’t see it,” he said.

“What?”

“I couldn’t see that strike. Whatever Feng Shen did, if he used it against me…I would just lose. There is no contest.”

Cog turned the gear at the back of his head. It didn’t do anything, but he did the motion when he felt nervous. “That’s natural, right? He’s the number one disciple, and he’s so far ahead of everybody.”

“He’s a true genius,” Alicia added.

Gary shook his head. “I don’t want to just admit defeat. To anyone. I wanted to train hard and struggle for first place, even if I lose eventually, but this… What can I even do? How am I supposed to face him if I can’t even see his moves?”

“Maybe it’s just hard to see from the side?” Cog tried.

“Maybe…”

Gary’s mind turned foggy from that point on. First Sui Duang, then Feng Shen… The tournament was awash with powerful cultivators, and they were just the peak of one year’s worth of disciples of one clan out of many.

If just this tournament is so hard…how am I supposed to reach the top? What monster lie in the tower?

The first day matches went on. Lin Xin took to the stage eventually, also not using her spear. She’d grown stronger since the expedition—Gary could tell. He didn’t know whether using spear fingers to show off was something that happened every year, but other strong cultivators followed Sui Duang’s example. By the end of the first day, around ten people had used only their fingers to win. None did it as casually as Sui Duang or Feng Shen. Twenty more people had tried to do it but failed, forced to draw their spear in the end. A single person tried to show off but received a solid blow for it. He ended up losing even when he did draw his spear.

The clan’s competitive spirit was high. So many people had used their fingers, declaring their intent to fight for the top spots. They weren’t cowed by Feng Shen’s display of power. They were ready to give it their all.

Right. I can’t be discouraged. I already faced Sui Duang once, and though I was weaker, the difference wasn’t that great. I’m strong, too. They were just showing off.

“We all won! How amazing is that?” Cog celebrated as he, Gary, and Alicia exited the Empyrean Steps.

“We should celebrate,” Alicia said, glancing at Gary. “Maybe dinner?”

“Chloe and Asim have invited us to the Bad Babes,” he said.

“Oh. When?”

“Right now. The entire audience is leaving with us. Let’s go catch a table before it gets too crowded.”

“Crap! You’re right!” Cog sprinted ahead, scaring a few passers-by and leaving Gary and Alicia stunned behind him.

“Why is he running?” she asked.

“I think he likes being early,” Gary replied. “On the plus side, if he rushes, we don’t need to.”

The two of them strolled leisurely through the Empyren plaza, heading for the main street that housed the Bad Babes.

“I didn’t see you much since the expedition,” Alicia said.

“I was training a lot.”

“You’re always so busy… I admire that, but sometimes I wish you’d slow down a bit. Rising high means little if you never stop to enjoy the view.”

“My goal is not to enjoy anything. It’s vengeance. For myself, my family, my friends, my planet… Bad people did this to us, Alicia, and I can’t let them get away. Not while I live.”

“If you don’t slow down, you’ll break yourself.”

“That’s the point. Breaking myself makes me stronger.”

“No, it doesn’t, Gary.” She grabbed his hand with both of hers and stopped to stare him in the eyes. “Breaking yourself doesn’t mean becoming stronger. You can do both. Grandma always told me that climbing is a marathon, not a sprint, and that the people who don’t realize it end up broken along the way.”

Gary frowned. “I can take care of myself,” he said.

“I know, but… Look, I’m sorry, I just worry about you. Can you promise me that you’re taking care of yourself? That you’re not just desperately pushing forward?”

Gary shook his head and smiled. She pouted.

“I’m your friend, Gary. Taking care of each other is what people do. You can train as hard as you want, but promise me you’ll take breaks every once in a while. We can find things to do. You still owe me that dinner from when I took you to the food market, right? We can do that when you have time. Relaxing occasionally is good for training, too.”

“You know I have a Duel of Honor coming up in two weeks or less.”

“I know.”

“And you still want me to skip on training.”

“It’s good for you.”

Gary sighed. “Fine. I’ll train as hard as I can, and when I feel overwhelmed, we can grab dinner once. How does that sound?”

She raised a brow. “It’s reasonable.”

“Great.”

Instead of thinning, the crowd grew as they approached the Bad Babes. A massive line had formed in front of the restaurant-bar-strip club-brothel. Most people headed for the ground floor. Gary and Alicia pushed their way to the reception.

“Sorry, we have friends inside,” Gary said.

“Name?” the doorman asked.

“Gary Steel.”

The doorman, a burly, brown-skinned humanoid wearing a turban, glared him down. “Not your name. Whoever holds the table.”

“Oh. Right. Sean Cog? Asim El’Shenawy? Chloe Santana?”

The humanoid grunted and stepped aside, letting them enter.

The Bad Babes was used to crowds. Despite the hectic rush outside, the interior was pleasant and calm, echoing with jazz music over the din of laughter and conversation. Aromas filled their noses, while the beautiful decor of flowers and lamps satiated the eye.

Gary quickly spotted Asim, Chloe, and Cog sitting at a table for five and walked over.

“Hey guys,” he said.

“Here come the fighters!” Chloe said. “We got you beers already! Drink, drink, drink! We need to celebrate!”

“Well fought,” Asim said, nodding sharply.

“Thanks,” Gary replied. He and Alicia took their seats.

“What did you think of our clan?” Alicia asked.

“You have some strong people,” Asim said.

“Your fighting is a little too straight-forward, though,” Chloe said.

“Can’t help it if we all use spears,” Gary replied.

“Don’t worry, I’m sure you’ll grow variety as you climb.”

“That Feng Shen person was the most impressive,” Asim said, sipping calmly on his beer. “I couldn’t see his strike. Chloe couldn’t, either.”

“Yeah,” Gary said.

They went quiet for a moment. The conversation of the nearest table wafted over. “And then, all his hair fell off! Can you believe it? That’s the funniest cultivator I’ve seen in a while!”

Gary cringed. Chloe burst out laughing. “It really was hilarious,” she said. “Do you think your future opponents will forfeit to keep their hair?”

“Please no. I need the experience. Isn’t there some medicine that can regrow hair?” Gary asked.

“Is there medicine that makes children grow up faster?” Asim replied.

“I don’t know.”

“No, there isn’t. Some aura abilities can do it, but most will just need to wait it out.”

Gary covered his face with his hands. “I hate my life,” he said.

“There are perks to being a star,” Chloe said.

“I’m not a star. I’m a clown.”

“Your opponents are clowns. You’re the star. Even after Feng Shen’s mysterious strike, everyone was talking about you on the way out. We need to find a way to monetize that.”

“Haven’t we monetized enough already?”

“Speaking of,” Chloe said, leaning closer to the table. Everyone did the same. “Ahem,” Chloe said, glancing at Cog and Alicia.

“We know already,” Cog said.

“I know you know, but isn’t it better for you to know less?”

“We should follow the law, but if we don’t, it’s more fun to know. We already have enough information to be executed if anyone mind-searches us, anyway.”

“I don’t mind,” Alicia said.

“Suit yourselves. Well, Gary, you did an amazing job bringing customers. Spear cultivators are the ideal customers—they’ve been saving up to buy spears, and they rely heavily on Strength. I also understand that your clan’s Treasury Hall is corrupted, giving them less Muscle Sap than other clans. That has to sting.”

“The Treasury Hall is headed by Elder Rat,” Alicia said. Everyone nodded like that explained everything.

Chloe continued. “Between the spear disciples and the customers Bob and I brought in, our supply is insufficient. We must increase production to keep up with the demand. Can we do that, Asim?”

“I can get a second lab under a different name and double my production. The problem is, we’re running low on balls. I only have ten left. If I accelerate, they will run out within the week.”

“Isn’t that good?” Gary said. “We finished our balls, made some aura stones and Muscle Sap, and now we get to walk away before the authorities realize what’s happening.”

“No, Gary, it’s not good. We have a great thing going. Stopping now would leave so much on the table,” Chloe said.

“It would also leave our lives intact,” he replied.

“I agree with Gary. We’ve already completed our goal. Risking it anymore is pointless,” Asim said.

Chloe pursed her lips. “Bob and the cultists brought me in contact with some people who claim to have more boss balls. It’s not much, just ten or twenty balls. If we accelerate our production now, we can finish melting and selling everything by the time of the invasion event in two weeks. It’s too short for anyone to catch onto us. And, to be frank, our operation is so small that nobody can notice unless we’re ratted out. A couple thousand drops of Muscle Sap, running for just a month, is nothing to the million-people Shores of Glory.”

Gary and Asim glanced at each other. “It’s risky,” they said.

“I’m still the one with the greatest risk. The only one who knows about you guys is me. Even Gary’s clansmen don’t know he’s part of the operation,” Chloe said.

“It’s just a little more money,” Gary said.

“It was never about the money,” Asim said.

“Exactly,” Chloe said. “Some good people won their matches today due to the extra Muscle Sap. They showed them who’s boss. If we stop now, the situation just reverts to what it was before, and the corrupted clans and families can carry on abusing their authority to keep almost all the Muscle Sap to themselves. What we’ve done so far is a drop in the ocean. And yes, I know it’s risky and it will only get riskier, but I don’t think it’s too dangerous. If it is, we’ll pull back. Until then, however, I want to double or triple our output and spread those drops to the people who need them. Do you remember how those assholes ruined our planet? Yeah. Nobody can punish them while they have the entire Tower’s power structure behind their backs. Upsetting the balance is the first step. That will ruin their foundation—then we can strike.”

Gary stayed silent.

“I agree with Chloe now,” Asim said. “We can’t let them get away with it, Gary. Even if this is tiny, even if we end up getting caught, it’s the best we can do to give them trouble. Let’s create chaos and help the poor escape the thumb of the clans. Maybe we’ll be executed, but at least, we’ll have done something to fight back.”

Gary mulled it over.

“Uh, guys? You do understand you make no sense,” Cog said.

“This is way too dangerous,” Alicia said.

“Fine. I’m in. Let’s give them hell,” Gary said.

“Alright!” Chloe pumped a fist.

“Gary!” Alicia said.

“Sorry, Alicia. They’re right. We have a goal, and this is what we must do. If we die, we die, but at least we’ll have given them something to cry about. I’d probably die to Sui Duang or some monster on the fourth floor anyway.”

“No, Gary! You said you’d reach the top or die trying!”

“My goal is not to reach the top. It’s to make those assholes pay. That’s what I’ll die for, and right now, the best way to do it is to create more Muscle Sap, both to keep and to sell. Fuck them and fuck their laws. This is war.”
