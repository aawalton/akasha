Loud, rhythmic clangs echoed throughout the room Archie was using as his smithy, thanks to his Basic Workshop – Blacksmith, set up in the space Bralmir had his nanite shift into. He was curious to see how much his bare smithing technique had improved during the two days he spent inside the Trials for his newly gained Profession: Spiritforging Engineer, without being forced to use his Spirit Fusion with Vul to create an item.

While he knew his forging had undoubtedly improved, his skill, Mana Smithing Mastery, remained at Uncommon rarity – even after he had consciously created a Rare-rarity item during his second trial for his D-Grade Profession.

When asking such a question to Bralmir, his first reaction was to shift the lounge into a bare, well-lit room where he could take out his Basic Workshop – Blacksmith and start forging right away, so he could see how he forged right in front of him.

And so, he did.

Currently, he was shaping a molten red Steel ingot, courtesy of Bralmir, who also gave him a couple more crates of metals to work with, considering he’d used up all the Iron and Steel he’d had on him building the second teleporter.

The steel was glowing at its critical point, the vivid orange-red color pulsing faintly as he adjusted his stance and brought the hammer down. A solid, even clang rang out within the smithy.

The force of impact pushed the metal out in the direction he wanted it to go in. Each strike of his hammer was deliberate and controlled. Not too soft, so the metal would take shape without flaring inconsistently, and not too hard, so it wouldn’t create unnecessary internal stress. His strikes had to strike a balance between them in order to infuse his unattuned mana into the steel.

He was focused.

But that wasn’t to say that he wasn’t chatting.

“So, anything big happen in the Multiverse lately?” Archie asked as the steel was slowly thinning and lengthening into the basic profile of a longsword.

“Last thing I remember seeing online on my Guild Membership Card was everyone finding out that the next batch of Tutorial Participants from my universe arrived... But that was a while ago, and I hadn’t really found the time to look through it again due to being cursed and all and wanting to spend whatever remaining energy I had to create that teleporter and getting as close to D-Grade as I could without evolving.” Archie said as he picked up the semi-cooled Steel off the Obsidiansteel Anvil with his tongs and dipped its edge back into the Forgesmith’s Flame within his Inchoate Titansteel Forge to maintain its critical temperature.

“Recently?...” Bralmir drawled from the sofa he sat on as he watched Archie forge. “I assume you mean in terms of major news and not in entertainment.”

“… The Auction House hosted by the Merchants Guild will be opening back up in two years after their 48 millennium hiatus they took in order to repair the damage caused at their last opening.”

“48 millennia is insane,” Archie chuckled as he then returned the steel to his anvil to keep shaping it while the metal was still pliable. “What happened that caused them to do all of… that?”

“Some dumbass young goddess thought that she and her faction were cheated out of an item, I think it was something for making a piano, I don’t really remember what was said because I was blackout drunk at the time, but they and another faction went at it while inside the Auction House and they ended up destroying the left wing of it.”

“Oh, who won?” Archie blinked, eyebrows climbing slightly as his hammer paused mid-swing. “… crap, I messed up a hammer strike there,” he muttered, before exhaling through his nose and setting back into rhythm, the metal ringing in harmony beneath his strikes.

The longsword was coming along cleanly, and unlike his earlier works, where it took him much longer to forge a dagger than now, where in less than half the time it would take him to do that, he was already almost halfway done with forging a longsword.

“Neither,” Bralmir snorted, beginning to recall what happened from that time at the Auction House where he attended with Balgid to see if there were any good materials they could use for their forging, and not totally because Balgid whined about going with him to the Auction House for a Cycle and a half.

“By the time the fight just got good, they were all restrained by the Auction House’s security and slaughtered for disturbing the peace of the Auction House,” Bralmir said before smirking. “Honestly, I benefited quite well from that, as the Auction House just gave away apology gifts to everyone who had to wait for the issue to be resolved.”

He flipped the blade with his tongs, striking along the spine this time. The sounds of his hammering that followed echoed slightly differently than before, but Archie adjusted quickly, recognizing the way the resonance was shifting with each strike as he imbued it with his own mana.

“Would someone not try to game it to where you’d trick a couple of people to do that every time they open up to get free stuff?” Archie asked as he kept his eyes locked on the steel he was currently shaping.

“No, absolutely not,” Bralmir dismissed, snapping out a part of his wooden beard and placing it in his mouth, and lighting it like a cigar.

“Getting banned from the Auction House is incredibly horrible news due to how beneficial it is to everyone who visits it,” Bralmir said as he exhaled smoke, which immediately evaporated away in order not to accidentally kill Archie if he inhaled the smoke.

“Anyone who’s able to attend it benefits from it immensely, and I’m not just referring to the Records you gain from visiting it. Within the Auction House, they have everything and anything you could ever want; items perfect for your Profession, Class, Race, anything you could ever think of,” Bralmir said, creating a second mouth on his cheek to speak out of in order to continue inhaling the smoke with his main mouth.

Archie didn’t respond right away, too busy adjusting the pressure of his strikes. The blade had reached its ideal length, but the edge alignment still needed work.

Another clean strike along the edge – ting.

Another, lighter this time – tink.

“Perfect on this side,” he muttered, nudging the blade an inch left along the anvil face before giving it a measured tap with the rounded side of his hammer.

“What’s the price range for a new D-Grade like me?” Archie asked, bringing himself back to the present. “Not to brag, but I got like 20 Gold Helix Coins,” Archie smirked, already knowing the response Bralmir was going to give him.

“Quite a bit of things, actually,” Bralmir shrugged, smirking at Archie’s confused expression. “The Auction House is a branch of the Merchants Guild, and they have many merchants of varying Grades and contract workers working for them to sell their work, so you’d be able to find something you can purchase.”

“They are merchants, and limiting their clientele based on Grade is stupid, because in true money-grubber fashion, if they can make money or benefit off of it, they’ll do it without a second thought.”

With a soft grunt, Archie pulled the longsword free from the anvil’s surface and turned toward the oil vat nearby, held the longsword inside it, uncaring of the hot oil that sputtered and hit his visible skin and clothes.

“Huh, I guess, I’ll have to check it out and see what I can buy from it the next time they open their doors. Do you know when’s that?” Archie asked as he turned to Bralmir, who had already subconsciously begun adjusting the ambient settings of the room with a simple thought. The nanites of the room they were in began to inaudibly hum in the air as the walls subtly rippled and the temperature stabilized to something ideal for the tempering process, all of which was being done without Archie noticing.

“They plan on opening the Auction House sometime soon, your kind of soon, so it should be open in less than a decade,” Bralmir answered.

Ten years? That sounds a bit… Oh, right, I evolved. Ten years is basically like a year to me now, since as a D-Grade Human I can live up to a thousand years now… damn, that's weird to think about, Archie mused to himself.

I’ll see if I can convince Henry to check it out with me and see if we can find something cool to buy.

The quenched blade hissed faintly as it cooled, steam rising off the surface in tendrils that curled and vanished into the controlled air of the room. Archie set the blade gently across his anvil and took out two leather straps and wrapped them around the grip of the blade. After using Forgesmith’s Flame to dry the wet leather onto the hilt of the longsword, he deactivated Forgesmith’s Flame in his Inchoate Titansteel Forge.

He rolled his shoulders, the lingering heat of the forge clinging to his skin like a second layer of skin. “It’s weird how much I’ve gotten used to the feeling of heat and how much I appreciate it now, after hating it for so long while back on Earth,” Archie said aloud.

After letting the blade rest for a few minutes, he gave it a once-over and Identified it.

Steel Longsword (Uncommon):A steel longsword forged by Archie Gracefield, a D-Grade Spiritforging Engineer. The horizontal cross guard provides protection for the wielder’s hands as well as various other effects. Status: Excellent. Requirements: Humanoid Race & Lv 100+.

Not bad, Archie mused as he flipped the blade around and offered the hilt to Bralmir so he could give it a look over.

“You’ve improved quite well in the past two days that you spent in your second trial,” Bralmir nodded as he examined the blade Archie just finished forging. “Your Mana Smithing Mastery is at Uncommon rarity, correct?”

“How did you know I have that skill?” Archie asked, surprised that Bralmir knew that he had it. “I thought you couldn’t look at skills on another person’s Status Page.”

“I didn’t look at your Status Page, I can just tell that that’s what you were using the skill for as you were forging,” Bralmir answered.

“And to answer your next question, I am the Forgefather,” Bralmir smirked. “It would be quite the accomplishment if you had a forging skill that I’d never seen, done, or heard of.”

“… Yes, my Mana Smithing Mastery is Uncommon rarity,” Archie confirmed. “I’ve been meaning to ask you about it and why it's still like that. Even after improving my forging by leaps and bounds, it’s still at Uncommon rarity.”

“Yes, your forging has improved in these past two days compared to how you were forging back on Fractal, it still is within Uncommon quality of work,” Bralmir nodded, tossing his all-natural root cigar behind him as he stood up from his nanite sofa and took out a flat sheet of metal from his spatial storage.

“As you know, E-Grade, well, F and E-Grade in your case, are the fundamental building blocks on your Journey to Godhood. Think of your time in them as the equivalent of your infant and toddler years. Becoming a D-Grade is like becoming a teenager: you can do a lot more than you could as a child, have the potential to achieve far more with much higher limits, and are now being compared to everyone else who has reached that stage and pushed it to their limits.”

Tapping the metal sheet with an extended root finger, Archie watched as two runes suddenly engraved themselves atop the sheet.

“Are you able to see the quality of runes?” Bralmir asked, who received a nod from Archie. “Good, so when you Identify these two runes, what do you see?”

“I see two Fire Resistant runes, one is of Minor – Minor quality and the other is a Minor – Lesser quality,” Archie said as he Identified both runes individually.

“Correct, those are the lowest and second lowest quality of runes you can etch as a D-Grade, and to make a line, currently your forging skill alone is at a Low – Low quality when compared to every blacksmith in existence’s skill back when they were in D-Grade or currently are in D-Grade,” Bralmir said, putting away the metal sheet. “And I’m not saying this to you to discourage or insult you as you just entered D-Grade-”

“No, it makes sense entirely,” Archie nodded with a shake of his head, interrupting Bralmir. “I’ve only just started forging just about three months ago, and I’ve learned so much about forging in just over two days.”

“If anything, I’m actually really happy to hear that skill-wise I am so far behind. As it means I have so much to look forward to when I push my forging skills to their absolute limits.”
