What followed was not something Luke would describe as a fight. The room, pitch-black, grew darker, and that darkness seemed alive with malice. Screams erupted but cut off an instant later as Weaver’s Perception told Luke what happened. It was like watching a horrific accident in slow motion, where you’re unable to look away. Five Intruders found themselves wrapped up by Curtis before they knew what hit them. In that dark room, the Hollow was in his element, and the Singers struggled like small insects trapped in a web where escape was a hopeless dream.

”What’s happening?” Hannah whispered, her shadows extending out in front of them, forming a shield around the bed. ”Is Curtis alright?”

Luke swallowed hard. “It’s better not to know, trust me. Curtis is fine.”

Darkness squeezed the life out of the Singers, not unlike how a huge snake would deal with its prey. Throughout the agonizingly slow ordeal, the Singers did everything in their power to flee. Not fight back, because how would they? Running was the only option, but Curtis suppressed even that through a combination of will and some skill that ate whatever mana they spent trying to use skills. It was like watching a monster doing its best work.

“Dude,” Luke said once it was over and there was no trace of the Singers left.

Curtis, still in his intangible form, waited a beat before giving a response. “…That was… disappointing.”

“Curtis?” Hannah asked.

“Sorry for the intrusion,” Curtis spoke in a monotone. “I’ll leave you to it.”

“Wait!”

“He’s gone,” Luke said, feeling a bit relieved to have that presence gone. Then, ashamed of that emotion, he changed the subject. "What now?"

Retracting her shadows, Hannah stretched out on the bed. "After seeing if there are any security add-ons for the Shelter, I don't know. We can do whatever we want, I guess?" She yawned. "Maybe another couple of hours of sleep first, but then..." She shrugged. "We've got six months."

"Six months to get stronger," Luke said with a nod.

Groaning, she tugged on his arm. "I was going to say six months to relax, but maybe that's too much to hope for."

"Yeah... I think relaxing won't be what's in store for us," he said, allowing her to pull him down.

Once morning came, Luke lay there, staring up into the gray, bare ceiling. After everything that had happened, he felt empty, like he was running on an empty tank of gas. It wasn't physical exhaustion. There wasn't anything wrong with him in that regard. This tiredness was connected to his mind and, even more so, his will. Having jumped around from Singer to Singer, then portal to portal, and worked the weave more than ever before, he now found himself lacking the drive to even get out of bed.

"What are you thinking about?" Hannah murmured. She had woken up without him noticing, another point to show how spent he was.

"Nothing," Luke said. "I'm just exhausted."

Her thin, long finger traced a line from his neck down across his chest and to his stomach. "Too tired?"

That one thing wasn't quite enough to snap him out of the haze, but what followed went a long way towards accomplishing just that, even if it took him a while longer to get out of bed. A little later, Luke got dressed and left the Shelter. An invigorating chill clung to the air. Walking down the street without a clear aim, just to get his body moving, he couldn't help but feel portals opening and people moving to enter them. Entering a dungeon was the last thing he wanted at that point, and he declined a few messages from his friends urging him to join them. Life was continuing as if the Singers hadn't been taking over Earth less than twenty-four hours ago. That was good. Everyone needed to get stronger, after all. That included him, but he needed a little time to find his way back. Billions of Singers had died by his hand, or thread, but try as he might, Luke couldn't make himself feel responsible for it. Their extinction just filled him with weariness. How pointless. What he needed was something to take his mind off it all.

Stumbling forward, Luke cursed as a pebble rolled away after almost tripping him. He stopped and stared at it for the longest time, then a smile spread across his face, motivation worming its way back into him. Luke took a few steps forward and bent down, picking up the small rock. He held it up to his face, turning it this way and that. Nothing remarkable about it at all. Not yet. Focusing, he settled in to change that.

Pebble of Crushing Weight: Weight added to object.

Chuckling, Luke stood and threw the rock at a wrecked car. It flew true and punched through the side of it, the increased weight adding to its effective damage. Nodding to himself, he looked around, searching for more stuff to use.

"Enchanting," he said. "Now that's something to focus on." He looked over his shoulder. "Isn't that right, Smudge?"

The not-so-little voidling let out a yelp of agreement and wagged his tail so hard that bits of void-matter splattered in every direction before being pulled back into his bulk. This time, the companion even produced a sound. That was new.

"Can you help me find rocks?"

Smudge barked, jumped in place, and spun around before setting off, racing up the street. Ignoring the devastation around him, Luke sat down in front of a small pile of rubble and picked up the first item he saw. It was a small piece of dented metal, green with flecks of rust. Looking around, he couldn't see where it might have originated, but it didn't matter, not for his purposes.

Metal Piece of Strength: +3 Strength.

Once enchanted, the Integrated Shop accepted it as an item and paid a whopping one credit for it. It wasn't much, but once he got going, wouldn't he be able to enchant thousands of items every day? That added up to a decent sum. It wouldn't rival what others got from dungeons, but it was still far better than nothing.

Torn Fabric of Intelligence: +3 Intelligence.

Bottle Cap of Intelligence: +3 Intelligence.

Enchantment failed: Item destroyed.

Medium Stone of Strength: +3 Strength.

Glass Shard of Speed: +3 Agility.

Enchanting the items was a soothing process that calmed Luke's nerves and sparked a small measure of joy. It required quite a bit of focus and a tugging on his will as he worked told him that using Willpower would yield greater results, but even so, it was relaxing in a way that even meditation wasn't. The minor enchantments didn't eat much mana either, so, at least at this point, he would be able to keep going forever if he wanted to. That was likely to change once he got to more advanced work, but that was a worry for later.

Settling into a comfortable pace of enchanting and then selling the useless items, the hours bled away. Smudge returned after a while and unloaded a sizeable pile of miscellaneous trash right next to where Luke sat, almost as if he had regurgitated it from within his void. As a thank you for the help, Luke tossed his companion a couple of Meta-Hearts. He'd stockpiled quite a few from the Singers and was happy to be rid of them.

Toy Car of Recovery: A slight boost in stamina regeneration.

Empty Cigarette Pack of Vitality: +3 Vitality.

Ruined T-shirt of Self-Repair: This enchanted piece of armor will repair itself as long as a single scrap remains.

Mana flowed through him as item after item got the enchanting treatment. Working through the many options, Luke saw the profession experience bar climb higher and higher.

Cracked Glasses of Wisdom: +3 Wisdom.

Punctured Soccer Ball of Speed: Provides a minor speed boost.

Broken Game Console of Self-Repair: This enchanted item will repair itself as long as a single scrap remains.

Staring at that latest item, Luke deduced that the enchantment didn't work for non-clothing or armor-type items. The console didn't repair itself. A shame, since it would have been appreciated in the main hall of guild headquarters. Either way, he was getting so close to leveling up the Enchanting profession that just a few more items would do the trick. While going from Level 3 to Level 4 wasn't a huge accomplishment or anything, Luke still couldn’t wait. Perhaps that jump would reward him with more interesting and powerful enchantments? Even if it didn't, he didn't intend to stop. He was just having too good a time for that. Six months was a long time. How far would he be able to go in that time? If nothing got in his way, he'd become the best damn Enchanter on Earth!

Apologies for the interruption, but you are needed for a mission of the utmost importance.

Luke's head fell forward as he sighed. "Well, damn."
