“You’re going to do what?!” Tara asked incredulously.

Ace shrugged. “It’s the only way in.”

As the team huddled together, processing what he had told them all, Ace paused to let the gravity of what had to be done sink in.

The jet-black waterfall thundered in the distance, its roar rolling across the lakeshore with a constant, oppressive drone. Ace stood at the edge of the lake, his reflection absorbed by its unnaturally still surface. Not a single ripple disturbed the water despite the violent cascade at its far end. The bloody mist rising from where the falls crashed against rocks painted the air with a fine red haze.

Ace felt his team’s eyes on him, their silence heavy with questions after what he’d shared about the altar and the shadow-bound guardian. The blood sacrifice. The battle royale that was coming. The risk that, given the Shadow Realm’s limitations, he might have to fight off this sentinel on his own.

“So let me get this straight.” Marcus finally broke the silence. “We need to perform some kind of blood ritual on an ancient altar to wake up a guy who’s basically been turned into a lawn ornament, and he’s probably going to try to kill us the second he wakes up?”

“That about covers it.” Ace nodded, scanning the edge of the forest behind them. The skin at the back of his neck prickled. They were being watched—he was certain of it.

“What if we find another way in?” Marcus suggested, his eyes darting nervously toward the falls. “There’s got to be a back door or something, right? Evil vampire lairs always have secret entrances.”

“Been in many, have you?” Ace asked with a smirk.

Olivia studied the waterfall with cold calculation. “The guardian is clearly there for a reason. Perhaps we should simply overwhelm him with a coordinated assault. Four against one should provide favorable odds, regardless of his abilities.”

The way she said it—clinical, detached—sent a chill through Ace. Every day, Olivia seemed less and less human. He didn’t know if this was the world’s corruption or something darker. For all he knew, this was the real Olivia, with the world slowly chipping away at her mask.

This wasn’t a simple museum curator.

She was something more.

Tara frowned, and her eyes never left Ace’s features. She was processing, analyzing—not just his words but the tension in his shoulders, the set of his jaw, the way he carried himself when he spoke. She saw more than the others. Always had.

“We need what’s in the Crucible,” Ace said finally, his voice firm. “Everything depends on it—getting stronger, understanding this world, maybe even finding a way home.” He turned to face each of them individually. “But before we do this, I need to know something.”

“And what’s that?” Tara asked.

“If you’re ready,” he said simply. “What we’re about to do—there’s no turning back once we start. I need to know we’re solid. That when things inevitably go sideways, we’ve got each other’s backs.”

“Fuck yeah, we do,” Marcus said with a mischievous grin. “You saw what we did to those bandits.”

“This is going to be harder,” Ace pointed out.

“How?” Olivia shrugged. “It’s four against one, from the sound of it.”

“There’s something wrong with this guy,” Ace corrected. “He’s tapped into the Shadow Realm even more than Victor was. Hell, more than I am.”

At that, everyone went silent.

The wind whispered through the ancient pines surrounding the lake. Shadows stretched long and thin across the eerily still surface, breaking only where blood-tinged spray rose from the base of the distant waterfall. The forest seemed to hold its breath around them—not the peaceful quiet of a restful woodland, but the watchful silence of a killer before they strike. Leaves trembled at the forest’s edge, their undersides flashing silver in the lilac twilight, while birds launched from the canopy in sudden, nervous bursts. Even the constant thunder of the falls seemed muted now, as though the very landscape anticipated what was about to unfold.

Ace broke the silence by roughly clearing his throat. “Like I said—this is probably going to suck. We’re going to have to think on our feet, coordinate, and push past the boundaries we’ve had thus far. Can you all do that?”

“Look, we’ve followed you this far.” Marcus swallowed hard, then attempted a smile that didn’t reach his eyes. “You haven’t gotten us killed yet, so I guess that’s something.”

“It’s a win,” Tara said with a weak smile.

With a deep and settling breath, Marcus nodded. “Yeah, man. We trust you. We can do this.”

In unison, Tara and Olivia nodded.

Good.

They had a chance at surviving this, then.

A high-pitched giggle shattered the moment and echoed across the lake’s surface. It was haunting, like an echo in a cave, and it blended with the gentle rush of wind that wove its way through the trees beyond the lakeshore.

Ace sighed.

He knew that voice.

Sure enough, the System materialized beside them, her childlike form perched casually on a low-hanging branch that jutted out over the blackened grass of the lakeshore. She kicked her legs back and forth, like she was on a swing, and she looked for all the world like an innocent girl enjoying a day by the water.

If you ignored the malice in her crimson eyes, of course.

“I’m so proud!” She clapped her tiny hands together. “Look at all my baby vampires, all grown up and ready for their big adventure! This is going to be so much fun to watch!”

“Where have you been?” Ace demanded, instantly on guard.

She sat up a little taller. “Aw, did you miss me?”

“Nope,” the entire team said in unison.

“I just assumed you were out there, making our lives harder,” Ace confessed.

“Who says I didn’t?” The System examined her fingernails with feigned boredom, and moments later, she waved a dismissive hand. “I’ve been bopping around here and there, putting out fires you drama queen mortals so desperately love to start.”

That piqued Ace’s interest. They hadn’t seen much of the others who inhabited Floor 1—save for the vamps who tried to kill them.

It wasn’t a great sign of what was to come when they met more of their kind.

“Oh, it’s those silly dragons.” The System rolled her eyes. “The dragon triumvirate is acting up again—such narcissists, I swear.” Her smile widened, revealing teeth too sharp for her childish face. “But I wouldn’t miss this for anything. The Crucible entrance hasn’t been activated in ages!”

“You could have warned us about the guardian,” Tara said, her voice tight with controlled anger.

The System’s eyes widened with false innocence. “And ruin the surprise? What kind of host would I be?” She leaned forward, her voice dropping to a theatrical whisper.

Ace’s expression remained carefully neutral, but his hands clenched at his sides. “So you knew? Even if you can’t get into the Shadow Realm?”

“Of course, silly goose,” the little girl said matter-of-factly. “I’ve sent enough teams to their dooms that I picked up at least a few details.”

“Jackass,” Tara said under her breath.

If the System had heard that, she thankfully didn’t say anything.

“Well?” the little girl prompted, her eyes gleaming with malicious delight. “Are we doing this or not? I’ve got popcorn and everything!”

She snapped her fingers, and a paper bucket the size of her head poofed into existence. She coiled her arm around it and dug for a fistful of popcorn, only for half of it to tumble out of her palm and onto the grass.

“Fuck’s sake,” Marcus whispered, “I really hate that kid.”

“We all do,” Ace muttered back.

Ace turned his back on the childish sadist sitting on the branch, his focus returning to the still waters of the lake. The waterfall thundered at the far end, its dark waters a stark contrast to the purple sky beyond.

“Everyone stay back,” Ace ordered, stretching out his arms to release his tension. “I’m going to cross into the Shadow Realm and activate the altar. When I do, the guardian will appear—hopefully here, in our reality.”

“If it doesn’t?” Tara pressed. “Is he someone you can face alone?”

“I don’t know,” he confessed with a wary glance over his shoulder at her. “At the very least, I can try to phase him back with me.”

“Oooh!” the System chirped. “I wonder if that’s possible?”

“Will you shut up?” Ace asked.

In answer, she simply grinned with those razor-sharp teeth.

“And then what?” Marcus asked, nervousness creeping into his voice. “What happens if you get him into this world? We just… attack?”

“Be ready to,” Ace warned. “But if he’s a prisoner of this place, maybe we can reason with him. He looks tormented. I doubt he chose this.”

“And if not,” Olivia interjected with a twirl of her makeshift staff, “we beat the shit out of him.”

Everyone paused, watching her in a blend of surprise and concern, before Ace reluctantly nodded.

“Eloquently put,” he said dryly.

Tara crossed her arms over her chest. “Doesn’t Shadow Realm Immersion drain your Soul Meter fast? How long can you stay?”

“The veil between worlds is thin here,” Ace answered. “Maybe that works in our favor. I think I can stay longer without hemorrhaging SM.”

“Maybe,” she echoed with a nervous twinge in her voice. “You think. Perhaps. There are a lot of unknowns here, Ace.”

He shrugged. “That’s what we’ve got to work with.”

Olivia watched with clinical interest, and she tilted her head in curiosity. “What’s it like when you bleed in the Shadow Realm?”

“Can you not be weird for, like, two seconds?” Marcus asked.

The Blood Dancer shot him a sidelong glare, but she didn’t respond.

“Be ready for anything,” Ace cautioned, trying to regain control of the conversation. “We don’t know what state this guardian will be in. Maybe he’ll be rational, or maybe he’ll come out swinging. Be ready for anything.”

“Right,” all three of his team members said in unison.

“You all are lucky.” The System floated down from the tree, her popcorn forgotten on the branch as she studied Ace with unnerving intensity. “Sergeant Grumpy Pants sure is special. If he wasn’t here, you wouldn’t even be able to enter.”

“Why’s that?” Tara asked.

“You need Shadow Realm Immersion to even activate the sentinel, silly,” the System pointed out, rising on her tiptoes to boop Tara on the nose.

“Seems like a flawed design,” Marcus said. “I mean, if we can’t even enter, what’s the point?”

“Maybe the only ones they want to enter are those with access to the Shadow Realm,” Tara suggested. “Maybe those are the kind of people they want to trap, like this sentinel guy by the altar. Or worse.”

Everyone went silent—except for the System, who suppressed a chipper little giggle at the thought.

Ace ignored her, and closed his eyes instead. He reached for that cold, familiar place within. Toward the doorway to the Shadow Realm that had become a part of him. His skin prickled as reality shifted once again. Colors bled away. The constant roar of the black waterfall muted to a distant whisper.

The altar stood before him now, fully materialized in this half-world. Ancient stone, weathered by forces beyond time and carved with runes that pulsed with faint light even in this colorless realm. And, as before, the man whose body had been overgrown with writhing shadow vines knelt at its base, his stone fingers digging deep into the shadow-drenched dirt.

Ace approached cautiously, studying the curved channels in the stone. They formed an intricate pattern that converged at the center of the pedestal, and a small needle-like protrusion stuck from the center of the circle.

They had provided a way to easily sacrifice his blood.

How thoughtful.

Without hesitation, he pressed his palm against the needle. It pierced his palm with a jolt of pain, and his blood immediately began to spill through the channels and runes carved across the altar’s surface. The wound burned cold, and his blood was darker here—almost black.

The shadow-infused silhouette beside him twitched, decades of stillness giving way to the first tremors of movement. Whatever came of this, Ace knew with cold certainty: if it came for his people, he would rip its heart out if he had to.

No hesitation.

No mercy.

Just bloodshed and violence—which meant giving the System exactly what she wanted.
