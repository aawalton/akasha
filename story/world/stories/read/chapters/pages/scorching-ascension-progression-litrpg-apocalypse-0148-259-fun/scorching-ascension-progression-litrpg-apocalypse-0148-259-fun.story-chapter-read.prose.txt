While a new upgrade had been made available, a new option that would give his Arcanums more versatility to fall back on, Ethan opted to put the selection on hold. He had considered it as he flew over the dunes of the rift, the sun warming up his back and wings, and in the end, he preferred to focus on why he was actually here. Now was not the time to spread himself thinner. He was here to shore up some of his weaknesses and get better, To really get familiar with the building blocks of his skillset so that no weird worm magic or anything like it could threaten to rip them out of his grasp.

So new toys would have to wait. At least this way, if he were to go up against some dispelling magic, he’d have an edge and so for that, as he made his way toward the first of the side structures—just because they were closest—he kept his eyes on any other worm encounters. Just so he could get used to their odd magic. Unfortunately, by now, it seemed like whomever managed survive the initial rush knew the score, so there weren’t that many opportunities for him to swoop in with the save. Which meant that he had to create his own opportunities and what better way to call for the worms that to knock on their doors?

Once more, he reduced his spellforms to alleviate the strain he would be put under, leaving this time the scales, skeleton, and the wings. It was a little more than the last time, but he had to continue pushing himself forward and so, hovering above a suspiciously flat stretch of the desert, he started lobbing fireballs down at the sand. By the time the surface was glowing with molten glass, less than a minute after he had started, he saw the creature’s arrival before he could feel its pulse of magic, and he braced.

From up high, it was much easier to see the subtle shift of the sand as the creature approached so when the it made its move, Ethan was ready. The worm burst out of the ground with a discordant screech, erupting like an explosion of sand and molten glass, and for the third time today, Ethan felt his magic tremble like a candle in the wind. But only for a short second.

A moment later, he reaffirmed control and lining up his trajectory, he dropped out of the sky. Whether his resistance was thanks to his multiple encounter with the magic or thanks to the worm having overtaxed itself, he couldn’t tell. Maybe it was because he hadn’t been directly on the sand and had been nearly a hundred feet above ground. The result was the same. It hadn’t been enough to rip his magic from his grasp and this time, once the worm lay bleeding and broken, Ethan flew away with a prize in his grasp.

Assimilate [Primagem of Suppression (Mid-grade)] ?

“So that’s how it’s doing its thing? Not by dispelling, but by suppressing?” he muttered over the wind that whistled in his ears.

Suppression. Maybe there were some extra aspects to the ability, but there was no denying that this aspect was a main component. This also told him that this suppression—while maybe not a Prime Manifestation—was an important aspect of the creature’s ability, considering all of them could use it.

Just like water with the Water Monitor, he thought to himself. Maybe once this trial was over, he could hunt some more of the worms. This primagem ought to be valuable.

Gaining altitude, he observed his surroundings as he approached one of the side temples, and while he was tempted with challenging a few more worms—while being closer to the ground this time—he refrained. After all, he wasn’t alone in this rift, and he didn’t want to fall too far behind.

Landing on the weathered, sandy yellow stone, Ethan found himself standing on top of some sort of stout, wide tower with a somewhat smaller temple at its center. He hadn’t been able to see stairs or ladders on the side of the building and now that he was upon it, he was a little taken aback.

It felt… much more majestic than it had looked from above.

The structure reminded him of pyramids, and just like those monuments, they were much larger when one stood right next to them. Ethan would say the structure was at least 200 yards wide. Maybe more. But he couldn’t dwell on its dimensions or style for long, as now that he was close, he could tell he wasn’t alone up here considering the flashes of light that blasted out of the two-gated entrance to the central building.

“This place is massive,” he muttered as he made his way toward the action, eyes scanning his surroundings for a short moment before they narrowed on the central, temple-like structure. One by one, his spellforms were summoned, while the sounds of ringing and explosion reached his ears over the gusts of wind.

He might be late to the party, but better late than never and seeing the scorch marks and the puddle of blood he stepped around, a few might have already been sent home. So this ought to be interesting.

Still, no matter how excited he was, he couldn’t afford to drop his guard, and soon enough, his wariness paid off.

As soon as Ethan passed the double-door’s threshold and noted the two individual slinging spells at each other, he sensed movement in the corner of his eyes and reacted, barely dodging away from the dagger that nearly skewered his right kidney. Ethan’s counter came within the same beat, but when he kicked, he only felt cloth against his scaled foot as the dark cloak swished away and as he prepared to rush in and pay the ambusher back, the cloak dropped to the ground, revealing empty air where his opponent should have been.

Well, that’s not alarming, Ethan mused while a grin tugged on his lips as he slowly lowered himself into a fighting stance. His eyes roved about slowly as he listened, while under his scales, he prepared his flames to blast his surroundings at a moment’s notice, and even when the voice intruded on his concentration, it barely registered.

“Keep him off of me,” calmly said the bare-chested caster. To the hidden rogue, Ethan presumed, and while he couldn’t spare the attention to peer into the chamber, he picked up a few details as he waited for the stealthy bugger to make their move.

First, the center of the room. The glowing pedestal was hard to miss, especially with the golden sheen that the light-well was emphasizing. As for his fellow visitors, there was the one who just spoke, a short man that seemed right out of a monastery. He wore thick, wooden beads around his neck, and was bare-footed, with only a pair of airy, orange pants held around his waist with a black waistband. He stood with his hands held behind the small of his back, while three swords levitated above him, periodically flashing forward toward the second darting figure.

The second man’s attire looked more modern, with sleeveless—

Ethan whirled, his scales igniting as he detonated like a fireball, letting out all the heat and flames [Emberborn Heart] had accumulated in time to hear the curse as a shadowy figure was sent blasting away. Fire trailed after them, latching on to the dark sleeves and face-wrap, which the stranger quickly patted away as she landed in a crouch and ripped her face-wrap off before giving him an annoyed glare that quickly turned to shock when she realized Ethan was already half-way to her.

As if he would have given her time to recover.

[Human - Level 88 (II)]

The woman dodged the first swipe easily enough, slipping under his arm and to the side with her blade flashing toward his armpit. Ethan couldn’t help but notice how she kept herself away from a stray kick so clearly, this wasn’t her first time fighting from up close. He also didn’t miss how she let him get close, even after he had detonated himself. Did she think he couldn’t do that again, or did she trust she could mitigate such an attack?

Taking a step back, he let the blade swipe past him and threw a punch that she barely dodged before he followed it up with a knee, only for her to catch it with her leg and use the momentum to throw herself back and when she landed, she took a moment to straighten up and gave him a sly grin that Ethan could only return.

“This rift is a lot of fun,” he simply said, then he exploded forward.
