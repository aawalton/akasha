[Adult Lightning Dragon - lvl 999]

The storm dragon slammed to the ground in a fighting fury, striking out at Tahira quick as lightning.

She reacted in the same instant, teleporting in a Voidburst from where he’d struck to above and behind him, leaving behind a flare of energy that warped and tore at the fabric of the plane.

The dragon pulled up short, halting its attack before it could collide with her trap. Well, that was fine. She tagged the back of its head with a Voidmark instead, then added Suppression and Void’s Eye for good measure.

The dragon’s tail lashed out at her and she teleported back to the front of the beast, leaving behind another patch of disrupted reality. It tried to teleport in turn, but Voidseal was already in place. The monster’s own attack slashed into its body, weakening its protective spells and even knocking free a few scales.

She shook her head. “And here I hoped you’d give me an interesting fight.”

The dragon didn’t answer. Lightning boiled around it, forcing Tahira to jump away.

“So you’d prefer to do this at a distance? Fine by me.” She drew Hungerfang, took aim, and fired a bolt of pure Void Annihilation into the beast’s chest.

At this, oddly, the dragon seemed amused. She saw why a second later, when the ability splashed off a barrier just beyond the creature’s scales and faded instead of burning into it.

“Too close to lightning to overcome your innate resistances, is it? We’ll see.” Tahira activated one of her lesser used abilities and latched onto the protective barrier, draining it into her outstretched hand.

The dragon fired a focused barrage of lightning from its mouth, one which Tahira would have had a hard time evading without giving up some of her ongoing abilities.

Fortunately for her, she wasn’t alone. Geron threw his crystal body between her and the dragon just in time to intercept, and the storm disappeared into his chest without a trace. All that happened was the crystals across his body turned from a faint pink tinge to a pale cyan.

“Be careful,” Eileth called from the sidelines. “He’s not as helpless as he wants you to think.”

The dragon turned to snort a massive ball of lightning at the seer, which Geron again intercepted. The cyan of his armor grew stronger. “He is playing a dangerous game if he wishes to not take us seriously.”

“It’s a normal storm dragon,” Tahira argued irritably. “Not an ancient, not an ascendant, nothing to get worked up about.” She tagged it with another layer of binding, turning its mana into a direct siphon to the Void rather than permitting it to merely damage itself. Between locking it in her domain, suppressing, binding, and sealing its strength and abilities, it was practically dead already. It just didn’t have the decency to acknowledge it.

And that was all before Geron got his claws into it. Where the Void Huntress would lock down and prevent abilities from being used, the Crimson Cataclysm would consume them and turn them back against their originator. When he wasn’t busy turning enemies into new accessories or rot vectors, that is.

All in all, the dragon was a disappointing adversary all around. It didn’t try to counter any of her abilities, simply gave up on using any mana-based attacks as soon as it realized what her seal did, and stuck with its own generated lightning, its fangs, and its tail. Once it tried to knock them over with a gust of wind from its wings, but when that failed to move them it went back to mainly evading and throwing in the occasional attack when it got bored of crouching and growling.

However, in addition to being disappointing on the assault front, it turned out to be a frustrating nightmare on the defence front. While Tahira could evade and Geron could absorb pretty much anything it threw at them, the same was true in reverse. Their attacks bounced off or sank in harmlessly. Apart from her highly specific abilities, it resisted any standard form of damage or debuff.

What should have been a quick end was dragged out by the beast’s infuriating reliance on tactical attrition. It was spending no mana, so the fact that Geron had created a mana void for them to fight in was irrelevant. It wasn’t trying to escape, so the fact that Tahira held it in her domain was pointless. If anything…

Tahira narrowed her eyes. It seemed perfectly content to remain indefinitely in a pointless punching match where neither side could gain any advantage.

“Sev? Can you regress this thing for me?” Tahira growled. She’d been having an irritable enough day already without having to deal with an uppity A-Rank beast like this.

“He’s otherwise occupied,” Eileth said calmly.

“What?” Tahira whipped around to search the area they’d chosen for their fight, and indeed saw no sign of their fourth member. “Where did he go?”

“Where he needed to be. Now, stay focused. Things are going to get interesting in another few minutes.”

“This isn’t just another one of your tricks?”

The seer smiled. “If you’d rather die, go ahead and ignore me.”

Tahira didn’t justify that with a response. She turned back to the angry storm containing an even angrier dragon and got back to firing Void Annihilation into its flank. Maybe she’d get lucky eventually. Or maybe it’d give up on this huge beast nonsense and revert to something she could play with properly.

Either way, its wards remained frustratingly impermeable.

This was going to be another long, long day.

“Hold on tight! This is going to be interesting.” Noah careened toward the ground at well beyond lethal velocity, his momentum only growing as they streaked across the sky and down toward the heart of the gravity field.

He gathered in all the mana he had remaining and, a split second before impact, triggered Arcane Step with a trajectory upward and out along the ravine.

He wasn’t sure how much momentum would be preserved by the teleportation, as he was normally moving at a normal superhuman speed. The answer, it turned out, was almost all of it.

Thankfully, standard gravity had a chance to slow them down a little as they emerged from the Arcane Step and into the real world outside the gravity field, but that was still not enough to save them.

They crashed into the far edge of the ravine where the path curved, leaving a perfect half-sphere imprint where Aurelia’s globe shield struck the stone.

“Sorry!” she yelped as the shield’s overcharge feature activated, exploding the dome and throwing them to the ground in a tangled heap.

For a long moment they just lay there, gasping for air. Their rapid flights and sudden shifts had left them both flushed, and Noah could hear Aurelia’s frantic heartbeats against his own from where she lay atop him.

Then Noah was laughing again, and Aurelia joined in without really knowing why. “That was insane,” she said between breaths.

Bun Bun suggests we hurry to find shelter, the dragonspawn proposed with a faint wobble to his voice. This is not a safe place to wait.

“Bun Bun wants us to get up.” Noah didn’t get up.

“That was insane,” Aurelia repeated, laughing helplessly.

The Old One would not be happy that you are remaining in the open, Bun Bun tried again.

Noah looked up at the sky, which was conspicuously free of Ascendants shooting across the valley toward them. “I think we can spare a few minutes.”

Bun Bun thumped his rear leg aggressively. Get up, Noah. We don’t have time for this.

Noah blinked and sat up. “Bun Bun?”

We are being hunted. His long ears twitched, tightly upright, and he bunched up his body as if to spring into the air at a moment’s notice. We need a plan. The Old One gave us a starting point but it is up to us to move beyond cowering in a cave.

“Zax said to get to the base camp.”

“He’ll come for us once he finishes with the Ascendants,” Aurelia said confidently. But she did climb off Noah’s lap and dust herself off. “Won’t he?”

Noah wanted to agree. He couldn’t bring himself to. Either answer felt like a lie. “I don’t know. But Bun Bun is right, we need to get to the—”

No. The cave is a trap. We need to find a way to win, not cower like prey.

“Uh… not sure if you’ve noticed, but we’re all below level 420. We’re struggling against things two hundred levels above us, let alone six hundred. Without Zax, we’d be dead already, and we haven’t met anything else close to an Ascendant.”

“What’s he saying?”

Noah shook his head. “He doesn’t want to hide.”

We are dragons! Have you no pride? We fight!

“Apparently his vocabulary also improves when he gets worked up,” Noah added with a laugh.

Bun Bun is not in the mood to play cute right now, the familiar said scathingly. Noah is being a rock-skulled nimwit. The Old One is not invincible and he is not omniscient. If we are going to win we need to make a plan and we need to make a plan before it’s too late.

Noah sighed. “He says we need to make a plan to fight back.”

“Fight back? Against three Ascendants?” Aurelia looked at the defiant dragonspawn rabbit aghast. “I’m not suicidal.”

None of us is. But we need a better plan than hide in a pile of rocks and hope we’re saved before the wards run out of power.

“Either way, we can start by getting to shelter.” Noah stood and stretched, shaking out his arms after the crash landing. He transformed into dragon form again, thankful to find that it worked. He helped Aurelia up onto his back and took off, Bun Bun flapping along beside him.

The familiar kept up a constant stream of questions and suggestions, picking through everything Noah had in his inventory, any ability he had, anyplace they had ever visited, as if he was suddenly going to find a combination that let them defeat someone at level 1000.

Noah answered the questions absently, his own mind racing through possibilities. What they needed was a force multiplier, or some way to limit their adversary’s power in turn. Ideally both. Unfortunately, he couldn’t think of anything aside from ‘tag them with Life Siphon and run far enough away they can’t kill us before it takes effect.’ But from what Zax had said about him being able to drain for a lifetime before making a dent, he couldn’t help but feel even that would be an exercise in futility.

It has been a long time since you used your weapons and portals together, Bun Bun remarked. You were very effective with them back in Drakonias.

“My abilities are stronger now, but that also means they take more mana. In Drakonias, my regeneration was much higher than here. Also, we had a steady supply of mana crystals to replenish with. Here, we’ve had a limited amount.”

“And we’re down to no amount by now,” Aurelia said, checking her inventory band with a sigh. “Only a couple D-Rank ones but that wouldn’t be enough for even a basic firebolt any more.”

Bun Bun thumped his leg irritably, which looked very amusing in midair. There is so much mana here. Why can’t you use it?

“Can you use A-Rank mana?” Noah asked incredulously. “You’re barely over level 300.”

Of course I can. Bun Bun drew in a breath, then his entire body turned into lightning and he streaked ahead faster than Noah’s vision could track.

He brought up the familiar’s status sheet, and was surprised to find that he wasn’t exaggerating.

Bun Bun

Active effects: Elemental Body

Health: 100%

Mana: 100%

“You’re channeling A-Rank mana directly?” Noah asked blankly as the dragon-rabbit landed beside him with a dignified huff.

Of course. I am a dragon.

Noah glanced at Aurelia, then at his own status sheet. “Can you teach me?”

You do not teach a dragon to breathe fire. Are you a dragon, or aren’t you?

“I don’t breathe fire.”

Have you tried? Bun Bun challenged. You humans. Limiting yourselves to the bounds of your Astral Scripts. They are a tool, not a cage.

“I don’t know how to breathe fire.”

Breathe in. No, through your mouth. Taste the mana in the air. Feel it on your tongue. Bun Bun flicked his tongue out, short but forked, eyes half closing as his chest inflated. Feel the power in your lungs, the ache it has to rejoin the world. Hold it until it surrenders, and only release it once it is yours. He breathed out, a cloud of sparking lightning-laced fire.

Noah exhaled, then coughed at how long he’d been holding his breath.

Bun Bun growled irritably. It is a war. You must prove yourself superior. Subjugate the mana and the air, force it to transform as you will it to.

“Is this really the time to be trying weird breathing exercises?”

Would you rather wait until you have been captured?

Noah shook his head. “If Zax isn’t strong enough, why do you think I would be? Shouldn’t we be making plans for shields or escape routes?”

We are dragons! We do not cower and we do not run! If Noah wants to be a coward, then Bun Bun will fight alone.

“I think maybe you need to rest and cool down a bit.” Noah reached for him, and Bun Bun flew out of reach with a hiss.

Bun Bun is not going back. We fight.

“Sometimes, retreat is the only way to win.”

When has retreat ever led to victory?

“Lots of times. You should read more history.”

Dragons write our own history.

Thankfully, before the argument could escalate further, they reached the protected cave Zax had given them back when they first entered the area. Noah exhaled with relief, then sat down to meditate. Zax’s wards helped keep the mana thin enough he could breathe freely and didn’t have to struggle so much to integrate it.

Bun Bun snorted in derision, but he let the matter drop and went off to sharpen his claws against the floor on the far side of the cavern.
