“Remember, if any of you have any skills nearing evolution, evolve them in the wild realm at your own risk,” Asher said as the team did one last check of their gear. “I only have two examples, but that was enough to tell that skills don’t evolve normally in the wild realm. There’s a chance you might hurt your future growth.”

“Noted and ignored,” Moxy said, doing little more than actively stretching while Rulfar and Samantha finished securing their armor and checked their weapons. “You got the thingamajig?”

“Yes, I’ve got the thingamajig,” Asher said, rolling his eyes as he lifted his new artifact and shook it in front of her. It looked almost like a blank compass, complete with a needle pointing at nothing with no markings. “Assuming this works correctly, we shouldn’t have any problems finding what we need.”

While Richard hadn’t been able to create an artifact with the intense death energy that they needed to keep Smiles going, the wizard had at the very least been able to create an artifact capable of detecting such sources of death energy. The artifact wouldn’t be very useful against the Council, due to the fact that the liches were smart enough to hide themselves from such devices. But if they were hunting for some sort of monster, it was exactly what they needed.

“I’ll take that,” Samantha said, removing her Ring of Lesser Realm Sight and snatching the artifact from him. “The ring is pointless in the wild realm, so better I swap out an artifact than you. You’re our ticket home, so we want you fighting at maximum capacity.”

“Good point.” Tossing the ring into his rift, Asher clapped his hands as he checked on Rulfar. “You ready?”

“I’m just hoping I don’t need to trigger my blended skill and stay in battle form for hours on end again,” Rulfar admitted. “Spending so long hibernating to recover my energy is tiring, and I dislike doing it back to back.”

“We’ll do our best to make sure that doesn’t happen,” Asher promised. “Alright, if everyone’s set—”

“Wait!” Donvath said, the High Prince actually kicking open the door and stopping Asher before he could throw up a Spatial Lock and yank them all into the wild realm. “Just a moment!”

“Did you seriously use your Time element to pick the perfect time for a dramatic entrance?” Asher asked, frowning at the grinning prince. “We’re sort of in the middle of something.”

“Maybe I just have really good timing,” Donvath shrugged, waving at the rest of the team before clearing his throat. “I stopped you because I thought you could use one more pair of eyes on your journey. Or, you know… a pair of flames? Damn it, I lost the bit and I’m too lazy to rewind time, just get in here.”

Asher had already figured there was only one of two reasons why Donvath would stop them. If it wasn’t to warn them that they were all going to die and to not go at all, then that only left one possibility.

Kroult walked through the doorway, the centuries-old lich actually looking far better than he had a few days ago. The hundreds of holes dotting his body from the spikes within the soulsteel coffin had been healed up somehow, and he wore a dark green robe that matched the fires burning in his eye sockets. He walked with a gnarled staff that was completely mundane according to Asher’s Identify, and he gave a hesitant nod to the four of them.

“Kroult here has been officially cleared of any suspected evil background,” Donvath said, clapping the lich on the back. “I gave some of my best truthseekers a go at him, and he checked out. Beyond a few petty thefts and some killing in self-defense, he’s clean.”

Asher wasn’t certain if it was Secret Seeker drawing his eyes, but he didn’t miss the ever so subtle instantaneous shift in Donvath’s expression that he’d come to recognize as the High Prince’s tell for when he used Timeline to undo recent events. That meant Donvath had said or done something he realized was a mistake, and rather than live with his errors like the rest of the world was forced to, he decided to just do it over again. Before Asher could decide whether or not he wanted to press the issue, Moxy butted in.

“Glad to hear! Guess that means welcome to the team,” Moxy grinned, looking the lich up and down. “Today is a bit of a weird one with us going to the wild realm and all, but I’ll show you how sparring practice works later.”

“The wild realm…?” Kroult asked, the fires in his eyes dimming slightly as he looked between each of them. “…What?”

“Asher is a realm wanderer, and we need a source of incredibly dense death energy in order to keep the severed head of one of the King of Death’s creations alive,” Rulfar explained. “We can’t find what we need here, so we are heading to the wild realm to try our luck. We could use your assistance if you’re willing to join us. You are aware of the other realms, aren’t you?”

“Yes, I know of the major realms and a few of the minor ones,” Kroult said, quickly getting over his shock. “I just had not expected to hear that my first day of assisting you would involve heading to an entirely new realm. Is this sort of thing normal?”

“Maybe for Asher, but not for us,” Samantha said, smiling kindly at the concerned lich. “None of us have been there either, so don’t worry. And Asher has a skill that will make the realm much safer for us than most people.”

“Enough talk you five, get going now,” Donvath said, walking out and going to close the door before poking his head in and giving them all one last grin. “Make sure you’re home by nine, or no dessert for a week!” With that, the High Prince of the Noala Kingdom slammed the door. After a moment, Kroult turned to give them an even more concerned look.

“…That is the man in charge of the country?”

“Try not to think too much about it,” Asher said as he threw up Spatial Lock around all of them and focused on his Eternal Mark. “Also, try not to scream. This is a bit… jarring.”

A swirling sphere of energy formed around all of them before they were ripped off the face of the realm, sent hurtling through space as they shot like a rocket straight for Asher’s mark. Moxy was the only one who managed to follow his directions and laugh along for the ride. Rulfar, Samantha, and even Kroult all screamed as they shot between realms, passing through the void in a split second before landing on the wild realm. The moment they did so, Asher made sure Permanence’s second-tier effect was active, locking down their surroundings for a quarter mile in every direction.

“Look alive guys, make sure we’re safe!” he ordered, doing a quick scan of their surroundings. It looked like they were in a snowy tundra with large icicles jutting out of the ground here and there. He didn’t spot any immediate threats, but he did see what reminded him of an excited puffin the size of a soccer ball hopping up and down as it chirped happily up at him.

“Hey there, Scritchems,” Asher grinned, tucking his daggers away and picking up the ball of fluff as he gave the wild beast a big hug. “Glad you’re still in one piece!”

“That’s Scritchems?” Moxy asked, her eyes only landing on the beast for a second before they went back to searching for something for her to punch. “I thought you said it was a giant lizard that towered over you.”

“I thought it was a deer,” Rulfar said, furrowing his brow.

“Animals in the wild realm are just as beholden to its rules as the terrain is,” Asher explained, giving the fat puffin a scratch on the head before holding it out. “Anyone else want a go?”

To Asher’s amusement, it was actually Kroult who raised his hand first, the lich’s flaming eyes and undead fingers not seeming to bother the wild beast in the slightest as it closed its eyes and shook from the pats. While Kroult took care of Scritchems, Asher took in the rapidly shifting landscape off in the distance. Already, a chunk of land that none of them had been looking at for a few seconds had turned into a glowing pyramid of orange rock that reached up to the heavens with what looked like eels with wings flapping around it. Rulfar and Samantha’s jaws were appropriately dropped, and each of them gripped their chosen weapons all the tighter.

Moxy just looked excited.

“Everyone close your eyes, I want to see what else we can make appear!” she laughed, cracking her knuckles in anticipation.

“The wild realm is dangerous, so we’re going to do this very carefully and methodically,” Asher instructed. “Luckily for us, our immediate surroundings are going to remain stable because of my skill. However, we actually are going to close our eyes for a few seconds at a time and give the wild realm a chance to shuffle and shift. Then, we’ll observe the realm again, with each of us looking in a different direction to keep it from changing too much. Samantha will check Richard’s artifact and notify us if the new landscape contains the death energy we need. If it does, we rush over there as fast as we can and try to lock it down before it changes. If it doesn’t, we close our eyes again and rinse and repeat. Make sense?”

“When do we throw in the towel if we don’t get any readings?” Rulfar asked, finally tearing his eyes away from the distant pyramid that was somehow slowly inverting before their very eyes. Observing the wild realm kept it from going through drastic changes, but only Asher’s Permanence skill kept things perfectly stable. “This is all one giant gamble to begin with.”

“Let’s just give it a couple of hours and see what we can find,” Asher suggested. “We might even run into other interesting stuff beyond a source of death energy that we’ll want to check out. During my trip here with the realm wanderer team from the Eight Crests we found a field of crystals, some of which contained tritessence that could be absorbed in an emergency by shattering the crystal. If they hadn’t given me one as a bribe, I would have died facing off against the King of Death.”

“If I may,” Kroult said, interrupting him as he stepped forward. The lich still held the ridiculously fluffy Scritchems in his arms, which made for a hilarious contrast. “Can I cast a spell on you four? It is taken from a tier-1 Mind skill, and it should help you all coordinate your movements better. I used to use it to help arguing couples see eye-to-eye with one another.”

“That’s actually a great idea, if everyone is fine with it,” Asher said, looking at the others and getting a handful of nods in return. “The realm wanderer team had someone with the Mind element connecting all their thoughts to help keep them all on the same page. Obviously this sounds like a far lesser version of that, but that’s probably for the best.”

Having gotten their approval, Kroult walked around and placed a hand gently on each of their shoulders. Every time he did so, the green fires in his eyes flared for a moment. When the lich got to him, Asher could feel the skill settle into place over his mind. While he couldn’t do anything as crazy as hear his teammates thoughts, he somehow just knew what they were about to do.

“That’s trippy,” Moxy snorted, pretending to throw a punch at Rulfar and making the bloodhound flinch before she’d even moved.

“Don’t do that!” he snapped, causing her to laugh and immediately do it again.

“Thanks, Kroult, this will help us coordinate opening and closing our eyes in sync with one another,” Asher said.

“I am just glad to be helping people again,” the lich admitted, going back to scratching Scritchems as the puffin crooned.

“Focus, everyone, it’s time to get to work!” Asher said, stepping between Moxy and Rulfar before they could start fighting for real. “Everyone pick a direction and turn toward it. When you feel me go to close my eyes, you do the same, and vice-versa when I open them. Let’s find what we’re looking for and head on home before something bigger than we can handle shows up and tries to eat us.”

“Don’t threaten me with a good time,” Moxy said, grinning as she closed her eyes. “Ready!”

“Ready,” Samantha nodded, nocking an arrow as she lowered her head.

“Realms above, I’m ready,” Rulfar grumbled, gripping his axe as he did the same. “Never thought this would be where I died.”

“That’s the spirit,” Asher grinned, looking in the one remaining direction before closing his eyes as well. “Let’s see what we can find!”
