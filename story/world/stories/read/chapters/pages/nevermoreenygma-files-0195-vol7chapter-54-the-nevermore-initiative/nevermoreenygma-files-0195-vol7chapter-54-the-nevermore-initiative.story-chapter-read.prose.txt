Chapter 54

The Nevermore Initiative

In recent years, governments had tried to strike a balance between chaos and order, and although at times it seemed as futile as trying to stop the rain by spreading one’s arms wide, the truth was that the situation had not spiraled out of control, even though everyone was already talking about the “Dark Events.”

Still the existence of the feys was a secret guarded fiercely, a truth wrapped in lies designed to maintain a fragile balance.

But Mai was no longer sure that balance was real. If the existence of the feys were revealed, perhaps the world would finally change.

Perhaps humans, feys, and those who existed between the two could put aside subterfuge and find a path to peace.

Yet, every time she allowed that hope to seep into her mind, reality struck her hard.

Humanity did not easily accept the unknown —no more proof was needed than opening a history book to see the pretty results. Since time immemorial, it had feared what it could not control. What assurance was there that this time would be different? What guarantee existed that feys would not be seen as threats, as errors to be corrected? Even blamed for Dark Events or natural disasters in recent decades?

Yes, part of her wanted to be foolishly optimistic about human nature and the occult world, but more likely than not, it could very well end in a fight with sticks and stones, or with one side launching nuclear bombs from catapults. Although those were more like the absurd nightmares she used to have in the early hours of Wednesday mornings.

Mai wondered if perhaps she too was part of the problem. Of course she was. She had spent so many years in the shadows, operating within a system that perpetuated the same cycle of lies and violence. Though her intentions had always been to help, she could not deny that, in some way, she had also contributed to keeping the truth hidden.

Not to mention the political leaders she’d taken out over the course of her career. They’d all been bad people, but that depended on the point of view. To the judicial system, she’d practically be a cold-blooded killer.

Think too much, and she might end up searching for a light that could very well burn the moth antennae hidden in her hair.

Time alone had allowed her to reflect, but it had also shown her how difficult it was to find an answer.

Governments had already made the reality of Dark Events known.

Perhaps the existence of the entire hidden world would no longer be a secret, though not without an expiration date. At any moment, the truth could surface—not through the conscious decision of those who protected it, but through the simple accumulation of anomalies that could no longer be ignored on the fragile edge.

These aberrations in the laws of physics had begun as rumors in dark forums, then as half-censored news, and finally as phenomena even conventional science could not explain. Spaces where gravity inverted for seconds. Fragments of streets that disappeared and reappeared kilometers away. Standing in the wrong place could start with buying a hot dog in any metropolis and the next moment be teleported into a satellite orbit. Echoes of human voices in places where no people had ever been, Although this was good news for ENT specialists and psychiatrists, whose offices had been flooded with patients coming in to have their ears checked because they were hearing things, or because they thought they had a mental disorder.

Each of these events was another crack in the fragile structure of reality.

For Mai, these incidents were not only warnings of a deteriorating balance, but also a reflection of an undeniable truth for even blind Martians: the world could no longer afford to ignore what lay beyond its comprehension.

That was what she thought.

But she also knew that the truth was not a double-edged sword to be wielded lightly. In the current state of the world, revealing the feys would not only be an awakening for humanity, it could also become a new witch hunt.

History had shown this again and again. In times of crisis, humans sought scapegoats, and with scarce resources, political tensions, and environmental collapse in several regions, it was all too easy for someone to point at the feys as the cause of it all. That had already happened in the Middle Ages: Hey! Blame the fairies and magic for all the mischief in the world.

After all, it was not the first time the emergence of a new had ignited the flames of fanaticism.

A few years back, when the Aeons appeared, part of the world considered them the greatest aberration in human history. It did not matter that their existence was merely a consequence of evolution and biotechnology, to many, they were a mistake, a man’s attempt to play God.

Conspiracy theories arose instantly. Some claimed that the arrival of the Aeons was behind the droughts, earthquakes, and the collapse of world order. Others went the opposite direction: they saw them as prophets, heralds of a new era.

Messianic beliefs emerged quickly.

Entire sects rose, claiming that the Aeons were a sign of the end times or, worse, deities themselves. Some of these beliefs faded over time, others became religions, and a few took a more radical path, with groups willing to impose their vision on the world with blood if necessary.

Mai was clear that if the feys were revealed, history might repeat itself.

Some would see them as monsters. Others as gods. Others might see them as perverts who had come into the world to lead humanity into a new era of decadence. Yeah...there was no need for the Feys to do that, humanity had done half fuck-spectacular work on its own, and the other half was down to the Dark Events.

But few would try to see them for what they truly were: beings who only wanted to live.

Years with Miho and later with Wishper had taught her the dichotomy between magic, religion, and science. In a world she had once believed to be governed solely by reason, the existence of the hidden world and Dark Events contradicted that with every anomaly.

But people did not change easily. Even with evidence, even with reality collapsing before their eyes, most would continue seeking answers within the same limits where they had always taken refuge. Some would search for scientific logic in the inexplicable. Others would call it the work of gods. Others might seek advice from the elves of the lysergic realms to help them choose a side.

And between the two extremes, feys would be trapped in crossfire.

Mai sighed.

Mai remembered those discussions with Miho and the classes, hours of conversation where her mentor, explained how humanity had always tried to categorize the inexplicable into concepts it could manage. If something had no scientific explanation, it became magic. If magic was insufficient, it became a divine miracle. And when all else failed, its existence was denied or ignored entirely, such as tax returns. But denying it did not mean it ceased to exist.

Perhaps that was why Miho did not fear the eventual revelation of the feys. For her, it was only a matter of time before they came to light.

Mai, however, did not share that optimism after her morning coffee. Decades had passed since those conversations with Miho, and while some world leaders were trying to address environmental or social issues, others were busy picking lint out of their belly buttons.

No matter how logical that perspective of peach might seem, the reality was that fear and ignorance were far more powerful forces than any truth. It did not matter if feys, espers, and humans shared the same origin, what mattered was how they would be perceived.

And if the espers—technically human—were still subject to debate and secrecy, what fate awaited the feys when the truth was revealed?

Mai had already heard of initiatives to expose the existence of espers to the world. Closed-door conversations, discussions among scientific and political leaders over whether it was the right time to admit the truth. But years remained before that could happen. Fear of public reaction was still a major obstacle.

If revealing the espers was already a complicated battle, exposing the feys would be a war in itself.

And deep down, Mai was not sure humanity was ready for that truth.

She doubted that would be solved by an agreement to give milk to the hairy ones, more likely, they’d end up throwing beans at them—or something worse.

2064

The spring night in Saitama was quiet.

Mai was used to the solitude of her home on the outskirts of the city in that season, where the only sounds were the chirping of crickets, cicadas and the whisper of the wind through the trees.

She was in the back with the wolves, a beer, and the moon shining down. The background music was just there to compete with the cicadas.

That was why the sound and the sight of a luxury vehicle approaching along the road immediately put her on alert.

It was unusual for anyone to visit her at that place. Only a few knew her whereabouts, and none of them would arrive in a car so elegant and silent. It couldn’t be Miria or Nerea either—they had taken a few days off in the south. She moved toward the entrance of her house, watching carefully as the vehicle stopped in front of her. Two of the wolves were also alert.

She had to be cautious. Just a few years earlier, Wishpers had executed an almost suicidal mission to end resource conflicts in South America. They had carried out a massive infiltration operation that ended in 2060 with the destruction of several multinational corporations. Mai had experience with this, having been involved in a similar operation during Hydra.

Although she hadn’t been asked to collaborate in 2060, there could always be retaliation from someone who had discovered her past affiliation with the group. Any stranger appearing near her would trigger her moth-like senses, much like the nocturnal bats in the forest behind her house.

The doors opened, and three figures stepped out.

The first was a human man in a dark suit, light brown hair, with a professional posture and a controlled expression. He didn’t seem ordinary, the way he moved suggested delicate, yet fluid movements. His hands showed that they were delicate, with no scars or overly prominent knuckles. He had a short beard.

Beside him was a slender fey, dressed in modern but functional clothing, with slightly elongated ears and long blond hair. He seemed to be the most laid-back of the three.

But it was the third figure who immediately captured her attention.

It was a short woman with blonde hair, blue eyes wearing a suit. The first thing Mai noticed were the small horns protruding from her head, barely visible under the moonlight. Then her gaze traveled down to the thin tail swaying gently behind her and finally to the delicate, translucent wings emerging from her hips.

There was no doubt she was a fey, though Mai couldn’t precisely identify her species.

The young woman stepped forward calmly, her movements measured but without arrogance. She stopped a few feet from Mai and looked at her with an expression blending courtesy and smiling.

“She's really tiny,” Mai thought. If it weren't for her anatomy, someone might mistake her for a little girl, but she definitely belonged to one of the shorter species of feys. Well… if she hadn't been five centimeters taller, Mai would have been classified as short, too. Shorty-Mothy—it was better not to think about how much that would have made Miria and Nerea laugh.

“Good evening, Miss Izumi,” she said. “My name is Rein Aogami. It’s a pleasure to finally meet you in person.”

Rein tilted her head slightly and held out her hand. Mai took it. It was really small, but warm.

Then the human in the dark suit spoke.

“Travis Cornwall,” he said with a slight nod. His voice was deep, with an accent betraying foreign origins. “And this is my colleague, Leon Mustang.”

The fey nodded with a half-smile, projecting a more relaxed air than Travis.

“Yoh!” Leon Mustang greeted casually. Mai squinted at him. Had she seen him before? Where?

She then turned her attention to the girl—Rein Aogami. That name… it sounded familiar.

Rein Aogami. She had appeared in reports during Mai’s work with Wishper. If she remembered correctly, she had been involved in Dark Event research teams, just like her father.

Yeah…now she remembered.

Aogami. Aogami Shin. Aogami Rein. That guy Shin was also the fey that Milena and Sari had a connection with—though Mai couldn’t recall the other name. There was other name… Liz...Lizard? Elisa? She tried to remember, but it didn’t come to mind. Still, she was certain there was nothing negative associated with her. At least on the surface.

“Rein Aogami…” Mai repeated, keeping her gaze fixed on her.

“I see you’ve heard my name before,” Rein commented with a slight smile.

“How can I help you?” Mai finally asked, crossing her arms.

Rein remained calm, giving a slight bow, almost like a diplomatic gesture. “We came to speak with you. Do you have a moment?”

Mai exhaled slowly. Stepping aside. “Please. Let’s talk.”

Mai guided them into her living room. The lighting was dim, more out of habit than necessity, Mai had always preferred the warmth of soft lights to the impersonal glare of overly bright lamps that distracted her too much. One of the quirks of having a moth side.

Without wasting time, she put water on to boil and prepared tea for her guests. As she served it in dark ceramic cups, her mind continued analyzing the situation. A recruitment visit. She had already imagined it.

Mai served the tea politely while Leon watched the wolves gliding through the air above their heads.

“We’ve been traveling across Japan these past few days, visiting several people of interest… oh, thank you,” Travis continued, taking the teacup with both hands. “You are one of them.”

After serving the tea, Mai sat in front of them and interlaced her fingers. She glanced sideways at Leon, who surreptitiously slips a few drops from a flask into his tea.

“What type of people of interest?” she asked.

It was Rein who answered.

“To join the Nevermore Initiative,” she said, pronouncing the name with a tone of importance.

Mai raised an eyebrow.

“Nevermore Initiative? I haven’t heard of it before.”

“That’s normal,” Leon Mustang interjected. “Although it was founded in 2050, we’ve operated in the shadows since then. The initiative was born with a single purpose: to gather as many feys as possible globally to study the Dark Events.”

Mai said nothing immediately. She took a moment to process the words.

“So you’re looking for me because I’m a fey,” she deduced.

Rein shook her head.

“Not only for that. We are interested in your experience, your history with Wishper, and your knowledge of the Dark Events. We’ve received references from former comrades. We know you have skills that would be very useful to us.”

Mai took a sip of tea, watching the three with an inscrutable expression.

“Who funds this organization?” she asked finally.

This time, Rein took a couple of seconds before answering.

“Someone from the hidden world with significant influence in human finance and among the Aeons,” she explained. “We cannot freely give the name, but I assure you their interest aligns with ours. We seek not power, nor control, only knowledge.”

Mai set her cup on the table with a soft clink.

“Interesting,” she murmured. “And what exactly do you expect from me?”

Travis leaned slightly forward.

“We want you to be one of our special agents. We’ve never had a rigid hierarchy, just a team of highly skilled individuals, each with the freedom to operate according to their own strengths. We believe you would fit perfectly into that framework.”

Mai rested her chin on her hand.

“Sounds good in theory,” she said, noncommittal. “But I’m not interested in being a mere tool in an organization I don’t know well. I’ve worked for Wishper, and I know what it means to get involved in matters that are too big.”

Rein smiled faintly.

“That’s why we’re here,” she said. “To answer all your questions. And to show you this is different.”

Mai studied her intently, measuring her words.

Silence fell over the room for a moment, broken only by the soft clinking of the cups.

Finally, Mai sighed and leaned back in her seat.

“Alright,” she said. “You have my attention. I don’t doubt your organization has good intentions,” she added. “But so far, everything I hear is the same thing other agencies have tried before. Wishpers, some governments, even certain private groups… all wanted to study the Dark Events, but no one has reached anything conclusive.”

“Because they approached it with the wrong mindset,” Rein replied calmly. “We don’t just want to study them. We want to prepare.”

Mai furrowed her brow slightly.

“Prepare?”

Rein nodded.

“You know as well as we do that the Dark Events have not diminished. On the contrary, every year they increase in frequency and intensity. The veil separating the hidden from the visible has grown thinner. If this continues, there will come a point when it will be impossible to hide the existence of the hidden world. It could happen in a few years or several decades, but sooner or later, it will happen.”

Mai couldn’t help but sigh. She had thought about it many times before. The number of unexplainable occurrences had grown so high that keeping everything secret was a titanic task. Even with the best cover-up strategies, there were too many leaks, rumors, and internet theories… Humanity was now discovering answers they weren’t even ready to accept, or questions that did not have any.

“And what do you intend to do about it?” she asked neutrally.

Leon spoke this time.

“Build a bridge,” he said seriously. “The Nevermore Initiative isn’t just a research group. It’s an advance force. If the world is going to learn the truth, we better be ready for what comes next. If we don’t control the process, it will be chaos. Think of what happened when the Aeons appeared: conflicts, extremism, sects that took them as gods, purists who tried to exterminate them… The world wasn’t ready. And if the existence of the the occult world becomes public all at once, history will repeat itself.”

Mai watched him in silence.

“Society is already coming to terms with the Dark Events.”

“Yes. But what if, when the existence of the rest is revealed in the future, it catches us off guard and leads to something even worse?”

Travis, who had remained reserved until now, finally spoke.

“That’s why there’s a global effort,” he explained. “A tripartite organization is being created with feys, humans, and Aeons. An entity that will manage the gradual revelation of the feys’ existence, ensuring the transition is as smooth as possible for all species.”

Mai raised an eyebrow. “Are you telling me that, besides this Initiative, there’s another group whose purpose is to organize the revelation?”

“Exactly,” Leon replied. “For now, the project is in its infancy. We’re just laying the foundations, establishing alliances. Travis is one of the representatives working on it from an external position.”

Mai averted her gaze, watching the shadows cast on the wall. The wolves had grown bored of the two men, but they seemed interested in Rein’s tail, which was wagging nonstop. All three must have keen senses of perception—the human included—because she had already realized that they could see them clearly.

Mai sighed.

She had spent years thinking about the inevitability of the truth. She had asked herself more than once what would happen when the world stopped being blind.

And now, it seemed others were already preparing the ground for when that day came.

Finally, her gaze returned to the three visitors.

“You’re asking a lot more than just for me to join an organization, I guess,” she said in a measured voice.

Rein smiled faintly. “There’s no rush, Miss Izumi,” she said. “Take your time to think it over. But if you accept… you could be part of something much bigger than you ever imagined.”

Mai inhaled slowly and picked up her teacup.

Rein observed Mai with a calm but expectant look. “So, what do you say? Are you interested?”

Mai didn’t answer immediately. She took a moment to gather her thoughts, letting the weight of the question settle in her mind. Then, in a serene voice, she responded: “I’ll think about it. This isn’t something I can decide lightly, so I’ll need some time.”

Leon nodded in understanding. “That’s completely fair. We’re not here to pressure you.”

“We can give you as much time as you need,” added Travis. “But eventually, we’ll need an answer. This isn’t just a job, Mai. It’s a decision that will shape the future.”

Mai couldn’t help but let out a faint, humorless smile. And who doesn’t have their future marked these days?

They spoke a bit more about the international situation: rumors of human factions wanting to accelerate the feys’ revelation, the skepticism of some groups, and the ways certain traditional organizations resisted change.

They also mentioned some unexpected alliances that had emerged, and how the Nevermore Initiative was trying to move cautiously among these latent conflicts. Nevermore functioned primarily as a center for paranormal studies, but it had plans for future expansion, aiming to become a research and development institute with multiple branches. The organization had been founded years ago, and the trial period had ended.

Mai listened attentively, memorizing important names and details. Although she hadn’t given a definitive answer yet, the information they were providing was valuable.

Finally, Rein, Leon, and Travis stood. They didn’t insist further, which Mai appreciated.

“We’ll contact you in a few weeks,” said Rein, adjusting her jacket. “But if you decide sooner, you know where to find us. You can ask any details to the Inoue Section.”

“I’ll keep that in mind,” Mai replied, accompanying them to the door.

As she watched them get into the vehicle and disappear into the distance, Mai closed her eyes for a moment, letting the night breeze caress her face. The wolves gathered around her, and Mai petted Kuro's head.

The Nevermore Initiative didn’t seem all that different from Wishper in terms of structure and operations. However, its long-term goals were another story. Wishpers had been a wall between humans and the hidden world. The Initiative, on the other hand, seemed to want to open a door.

The difference between the two approaches wasn’t small. It was the difference between hiding the truth and preparing to reveal it.

Mai sighed, closing the door behind her.

If she accepted, what would it mean for her?

“They wanted to recruit me,” Mai said, leaning against her desk.

A very tanned Nerea, sitting on the armchair, crossed her arms after hearing Mai’s story.“I’ve heard that name… lately it’s been coming up in research circles. TF people have joined them too, according to some leaked reports.”

Mai looked at her with curiosity. “TF? The ones from the legends?”

“Yes. Some former agents, some researchers. Nothing official, of course. Publicly, they investigate the paranormal. But they say that girl, Rein Aogami, has been studying ancient ruins related to Earth’s prehuman past and the OOPArts for years. Wishper had files on her. Apparently, she was trying to separate myth from history. Good luck with that.”

Miria frowned. “And what did they discover?”

“Enough to annoy more than one historian,” Nerea replied with a half-smile. “Rumor has it they found evidence of non-human civilizations hundreds of thousands of years ago, some even millions of years ago. Basically what we already know, but they’re gathering proof.”

Mai rested her chin on her hand. “Sounds like recycled myth.”

“Maybe,” Nerea said, “but the people at Miskatonic University are pushing for certain findings to be officially published. They want it recognized that the human ‘canon’ history is a childish version of something much more complex. I mean, for humans, feys are ancient legends. Imagine if they actually came from something far older. For example, aside from the legends, there are things we don’t know about, like dragons… or you, Miria. What kind of fey are you? Centaur, unicorn, or Pegasus? You have three myths associated with you, yet there’s no record in history of a creature like you ever existing. ‘Helicorne’ is just a reference to your horn and something you made up.”

Miria let out a short laugh. “And you think the revelation of the feys depends on that?”

“I wouldn’t rule it out,” Nerea replied. “If the world accepts that intelligent races existed before humans, that there were one or more civilizations prior, including feys or other creatures… then admitting that feys exist wouldn’t be so traumatic. It would be the logical next step.”

Mai raised an eyebrow. “So basically, they want to rewrite history so people don’t panic. Clever.”

“Or manipulative,” said Miria.

“One doesn’t exclude the other,” Mai responded.

Nerea shrugged. “It’s also said that Nevermore has evidence the planet already harbored intelligent life hundreds of millions of years ago. Something about ‘primeval species’ mentioned in ancient grimoires.”

“Grimoires?” Mai repeated. “Well, that does sound like Wishper.”

“I once heard that someone in the last century discovered an entire city in Antarctica that was millions of years old, but those stories also come from grimoires,” Miria joked.

Mai remained silent for a moment, watching the coffee steam dissipate into the air.

Deep down, she knew all of it made sense in a way. If Nevermore was digging into the oldest secrets, if they were truly trying to piece together the visible and the hidden, then that group was involved in something big.

And big always attracted danger.

“What if you join just to spy a little?” Miria said suddenly, smiling mischievously. “It wouldn’t be the first time you feigned interest just to gather information.”

“That was a long time ago,” Mai replied, though a small smile escaped her for a moment. “And it almost cost me my head.”

“But you survived,” Nerea replied. “And besides, you’re the perfect person for that. Nobody distrusts you… at least, not until it’s too late.”

Mai let out a dry laugh. “Thanks, I guess.”

“Think about it,” Nerea insisted. “If they’re really linked to TF or have access to Miskatonic’s secret data, you could find out what they’re planning. Maybe even understand their true purpose.”

Miria brought a hand to her chin thoughtfully. “After all, this involves all of us.”

Mai stared out the window.

The idea wasn’t absurd. If she had learned anything at Wishper, it was that organizations claiming to seek “the truth” almost never shared it fully. Joining Nevermore, even temporarily, could give her answers—or at least useful information.

“Maybe I will,” she said finally.

Miria and Nerea looked at her, surprised.

“Are you serious?” Nerea asked.

“I don’t know,” Mai shrugged. “But if they want to prepare for the future, maybe it’s time to see what kind of future they’re building.”

Miria smiled. “And if it all goes wrong, you can always say you were a spy.”

Mai looked at her with a half-smile. “Yes. As always.”

“But what are you going to do with the office?”

Mai sighed. It was better to start organizing everything and delegating tasks again if she was really thinking about joining Nevermore.

Maybe a few months inside would be enough to find out. She wouldn’t stay long. She was only interested in making sure there was nothing dangerous that could put everyone at risk.
