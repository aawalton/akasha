Merren’s shout seemed to rip the arena out of its daze. Everything moved at once. The guards in the stands and by the exits rushed down, some people recoiling, not even understanding how to react to the beast that a second ago had been supposed to tear the girl apart and was now lying at her feet like a loyal hound. People began rising from their seats, leaning over the railings, shoving each other.

Sakura snapped back to herself unexpectedly. The shock vanished sharply, and in its place came one clear thought: they were about to be crushed by numbers. Pampli might be fast and terrifying, but there were too many guards. If they stayed here for even half a minute longer, they would be boxed in from three sides, and it would all end very quickly.

Pampli had already risen too. He sprang up so fast it was as if he had been waiting for the command to move, turned toward the rushing guards, and growled. Now he was protecting Sakura, ready to attack anyone who posed even the slightest threat. His enormous obsidian muzzle jerked toward the nearest guard, and the man instinctively flinched back, nearly dropping his spear.

Sakura looked around quickly. Fight here? No chance.

“We need to run,” she whispered.

She had no idea how insane this must look from the outside, but there was no other way. Sakura darted toward Pampli, grabbed the rough, hot armor along his side, and, gritting her teeth, started climbing up. It was much harder than it looked. He was too tall, too wide, and of course there was no proper saddle. Her hands slipped over the smooth black plates, her boot slid off his wet side, her heart lurched for a second, but Pampli seemed to understand what she was trying to do: he crouched lower, held still for a fraction of a second, and that was enough. Sakura hauled herself onto his back almost on her stomach, seized the ridges of obsidian plating, and barely had time to press herself down when the beast launched forward.

He surged ahead. Pampli charged straight toward the nearest stretch of stands, where people had not yet fully understood that what was happening had stopped being a performance.

“Move!” Sakura screamed, not expecting her own voice to come out that loud. “Out of the way!”

People threw themselves aside because an obsidian beast the size of a tiger was charging straight at them. Pampli slammed into the lower edge of the stands like a battering ram and simply went through them. Boards cracked, a crossbeam split, and Sakura had to shield herself with her sleeve from the flying wooden splinters. Someone screamed right by Sakura’s ear, and for one moment she thought they were both about to go down in that mess of wood, people, and panic. But Pampli only sprang higher, vaulted over the next section, landed heavily near the edge, and leaped again without hesitation.

For one brief, absurd second, Sakura remembered the runes carved around the arena and realized they must have been meant to protect the spectators from magic. Apparently, nobody had bothered to make them protect the spectators from an escaped obsidian beast.

Another jolt hit so hard Sakura’s teeth clicked together, and she nearly bit her tongue. She clung to the beast with both arms, pressed herself to him with her chest, and only managed to breathe out:

“Good. Come on. Come on, Pampli, again!”

He heard her. Or maybe he already knew what he had to do.

Clearing the last rows, the beast hit the upper platform with his paws, skidded over the planks, and rushed for the outer wall of the arena. Guards were already shouting there, some trying to block the way, some leveling spears, but Pampli simply smashed the first one aside with his chest, clipped the second with his shoulder hard enough to send him flying, and then leaped out, away from the arena.

The stones shone with rain, mud spread across the road in dark streaks, and people in the street began turning toward the noise. First they saw only a blur of black movement, then the red flashes across the beast’s body, then the girl in a black cloak on his back, and only after that did they start screaming and hurling themselves out of the way.

“Move!” Sakura shouted over the noise of the pursuit. “Out of the way!”

This time they obeyed her more readily. A woman with a basket dumped everything straight into the mud and threw herself against the wall of a house. Some boy froze where he stood until someone yanked him back by the collar under an awning. Two men arguing by a cart split apart so abruptly it was as if a blade had passed between them. Pampli tore down the street, scattering the city by his mere presence, and behind them Sakura could already hear the guards shouting, boots pounding, orders barked in fury.

She glanced back over her shoulder and felt the back of her neck go cold. Too many were chasing them. The first guards had already come pouring out of the arena, and now more were joining them from side streets, from the square, from the gates.

The thought came instantly: the main gates. They had to get there before they were shut, before Merren fully came to his senses and raised everything he had. Sakura had almost opened her mouth to turn Pampli toward the wider street when something inside her tightened sharply and unpleasantly.

No. If she ran now and vanished back into the forest, she would end up alone with a foreign power, a beast, and a million questions no one could answer. That would not be an escape, only a delay. She needed answers. She needed someone who understood more than she did. She needed Gilbert.

Sakura whipped her head around, calculating direction.

The scaffold. The dungeon beneath it. If they had not taken him away, he was still there. And if she moved fast enough, she could make it in time.

“Not the gates,” she breathed, not even sure whether she had said it to Pampli or to herself. “The scaffold. Come on. Go there.”

The beast jerked his head as if he had caught her intention through the wind and abruptly changed direction. They skidded hard through the turn, his claws screeching over the wet stone, Sakura nearly flying off his back before she managed to cling on tighter. They tore back the other way, into the old quarter where the streets grew narrower and darker.

She recognized the scaffold before she saw it whole: by the wooden platform, by the block, and by the executioner in the black hood. A beheading was in progress. The condemned old man knelt trembling near the block, the executioner was already raising his sword, and the crowd around them wore the same hungry expression as the people in the arena.

It lasted exactly until Pampli burst onto the platform with a roar. The execution collapsed instantly. The executioner did not even have time to understand what was happening. He turned at the crash, saw the red eyes, saw Sakura above them, jerked backward, and lost his footing at once. One foot slid on the wet plank, his heavy body went over sideways, and he fell off the platform with a scream, straight into the mud beneath the scattering crowd.

The old man who had been about to lose his head opened his mouth in delight at first, almost laughing in relief that death had changed its mind, but then he noticed Pampli two steps away, the chaos all around, and seemed to decide gratitude would be wildly inappropriate. He sprang up, nearly tangling his own legs, and bolted away laughing, unable to believe his luck.

“Well, at least someone’s happy,” Sakura threw out as she jumped down.

A heavy execution axe remained on the platform. Sakura grabbed it with both hands and nearly swore aloud from the weight. For her, it was almost unmanageable, the haft long and slick with rain. But she had no choice.

“Pampli!” she shouted, turning toward the entrance to the underground level. “Stay outside. Don’t let anyone in. Understand?”

Pampli growled, and that was enough. The first guard who came rushing up to the platform recoiled at once when the beast sprang to the edge and slammed one paw down so hard the boards cracked. Two more tried to come around from the right, but Pampli bared his teeth and knocked one aside while the other dropped to his knees from the force of the roar alone.

That was enough for Sakura. She ran down the stairs into the dungeon, dragging the axe behind her, and shouted before she even reached the cells:

“Everyone away from the bars! Now! Back!”

Voices, cursing, frightened shouting, everything blended into one ugly chaos. Sakura had already spotted the cell she needed. Gilbert was there. He stood by the bars and looked at her with that same strange calm that would have been infuriating in any other situation.

“You,” was all he said, stepping back.

“Later,” Sakura breathed. “Everybody back!”

She raised the axe and struck the lock and the frame as hard as she could. The blow came out crooked. Pain shot all the way into her shoulders. Metal clanged, the handle vibrated in her palms, and the grate only shuddered.

“Come on, girl, hit it again!” Gilbert said with an unexpected smirk.

Sakura lifted the axe again. This time she adjusted her grip, turned her body harder into it, and struck again, more accurately. Metal cracked, the fastening bent, and one bar shifted aside with a screech.

“Again!” someone from the rabble farther down shouted.

“Everybody shut up!”

The third blow was the one that did it. The lock broke, the twisted grate jerked inward, and the prisoners crammed inside Gilbert’s cell rushed for the opening at once. For one second Sakura even regretted causing this release in the first place. The rabble locked up with him had no intention of waiting politely or leaving in any kind of order, and the men in the neighboring cells started shouting at once, rattling their bars and cursing when they realized the breach was not for them. But there was no time to fix that now.

Gilbert slipped out among the others. He moved quickly and smoothly, more than one would expect from someone who had just been locked in a dungeon. As he passed Sakura, his eyes flicked over her face, the axe in her hands, and then, inevitably, up toward the crashing and growling above.

“How interesting,” he remarked.

“Questions later,” Sakura snapped. “Run.”

They burst back outside just as Pampli hurled another guard aside. The man skidded across the wet boards before tumbling off the platform. Gilbert froze for a second, and this time something like genuine shock finally showed on his face.

“That is...” he began.

“Yes!” Sakura barked. “I know! Later!”

More guards were already pouring down the street. Too many of them.

“Get on!” Sakura shouted.

She grabbed the plating on Pampli’s side and climbed up again. This time it was a little easier, though her whole body already ached from the arena. Gilbert did not waste time trying to look dignified. He simply jumped, caught hold, pulled himself up behind her, and settled far too close, but there was no room for propriety now.

“Where?” Sakura shouted.

“Not the gates,” he answered at once. “They’ve already sealed them. Over there!”

He pointed left, toward a narrow alley between two houses.

“What’s there?”

“They’re rebuilding that district. Scaffolds, construction sites. We can get higher from there and jump the wall.”

“I hope you’re right!”

“We’re about to find out!”

Pampli, as if he had understood the direction, bolted there without needing any further command. They shot into the alley, nearly scraping both sides with their shoulders. Behind them the shouting was already growing louder:

“There!” “Don’t let them get away!” “Block the street!” “Crossbowmen, forward!”

The alley opened into a half-dismantled quarter where houses stood inside scaffolding, beams stuck out at odd angles, and boards, stone blocks, lime carts, and heaps of rubble lay everywhere. The perfect place for an escape, provided you had completely lost your mind.

“There, up!” Gilbert shouted.

Pampli shot onto the first wooden platform as if he weighed half what he did. The whole structure groaned under him. One beam cracked at once, another swayed, Sakura felt everything beneath them shake, and instinctively flattened herself against the beast.

“Don’t fall,” she whispered.

“Are you saying that to him or to me?” Gilbert asked darkly.

“Both!”

They kept climbing, leaping from platform to platform, from scaffolds to stone ledges. Below them helmets were already flashing into view, some of the guards beginning to climb after them, others trying to swing around the district from the far side. One crossbow bolt whistled past and buried itself in a beam a handspan from Sakura’s face. She did not even have time to be properly scared.

The higher they went, the shakier the structures became. Boards bent under them, ropes pulled taut, and Pampli kept climbing anyway. The rain had made the wood slick. Once one of his hind paws slipped off the edge, Sakura cried out, Gilbert grabbed onto her harder, but the beast recovered, found his footing again, and kept going.

At last they climbed high enough for the city wall to open up in front of them. There were still a few yards of open space and stone between them and the top. Not a chasm, no, but not the sort of gap anyone sane would call easy.

Pampli stopped. Sakura looked down and regretted it at once. Everything swirled beneath them: wet beams, people, spears, shouting. Then she looked ahead. The top edge of the wall seemed both close and impossible.

“He can’t...” she started.

“He can,” Gilbert said.

“How do you know?”

“At some point, you just have to trust the moment.”

“That sounds naive.”

Pampli crouched. His whole body drew itself tight into one terrible spring.

“Oh gods,” Sakura breathed, and locked both arms around him.

The beast jumped.

For one wild, endless moment there was nothing beneath them. No support. Only wind in her ears and the stone wall in front of them.

Pampli’s forepaws slammed into the edge of the masonry, his claws screeched across the wet stone, his entire body jolted downward, Sakura felt them being pulled back into empty air, and she had almost decided they were going to fall...

But Pampli held.

With a harsh, furious growl, he drove his claws deeper, hauled himself upward with his whole body, and rolled onto the wall. Gilbert let out a short, disbelieving laugh of relief. Sakura only shut her eyes for a second because her hands were shaking too badly.

“We made it,” she whispered.

“So far,” Gilbert said. “Don’t relax.”

They ran along the top of the wall a little longer until they found a stretch where the outer face had uneven stone projections and old cracks. The descent was hideous, far too steep for common sense, but there was no choice. Pampli started climbing down, hooking with his paws, jumping from one ledge to another, knocking loose small stones. Once Sakura banged her knee painfully against a jut of rock, another time she almost lost her grip, but in the end they made it down.

Beyond the wall stretched a dark, wet strip of ground, and beyond that, the forest. A huge dark forest.

They had escaped Mebrel.

Pampli kept running without stopping. Sakura looked back only briefly at the high wall of Mebrel. Now they would hunt her. Not only Merren. Anyone who heard about the girl who had brought an obsidian beast to heel in the arena. Or anyone who stood to gain from turning her in, studying her, killing her, or selling her.

She would never have peace.
