Aira tested the Splinter against different sections of the treehouse, moving with deliberate care across the seamless wooden walls. Unlike her earlier forceful approach, she now held the crystalline fragment with a lighter touch, letting her fingertips barely graze the surface as she sought any point of weakness or resonance. She focused on it not with the whole might of her Energy Manipulation skill but only softly touched it with her arcane powers.

"The Splinter reacts differently depending on where I place it," she observed, watching the subtle variations in its emerald glow. "It's almost like... a conversation. The wood responds, but then pulls back when I push too hard."

Lila crouched beside her, studying the interaction with keen interest. "It reminds me of training a wild animal. Push too forcefully, and it retreats. Offer your hand gently, and it might approach."

"I'm trying to reach the Source through these walls, through the tree itself," Aira said, closing her eyes to better focus on the energy patterns. "If I could just establish that connection again..."

But the mountain's heart remained distant and silent. Whatever bond had formed in the cavern's depths seemed muted by the layers of living wood that imprisoned them—intentionally so, she suspected. The treehouse itself was resisting to be a conduit in this communication process.

"It's too… distant," she finally admitted, lowering the Splinter with a frustrated sigh. "I can sense echoes, but nothing strong enough to work with. It's like trying to hear a whisper from across a valley."

Al darted between them, his claws making tiny scratching sounds against the floor as he investigated their progress. His whiskers twitched with interest at the small indentations Aira had created, but even his tiny form couldn't squeeze through the minuscule gaps that quickly sealed themselves behind her efforts.

"We should be careful," Alliot warned, his voice unusually subdued. "If the shamans detect your manipulations, it could worsen our situation. They're almost certainly monitoring this prison."

The group fell into an uneasy silence, each lost in their own thoughts as the reality of their confinement pressed in around them. They didn't dare attempt meditation, fearing that the amplified energy might alert Khanorrh and his followers to their continued efforts at escape.

Time passed, the trio was disconnected from the outer world. Their arcane feelings dimmed, and their sense of time wavering. Morning came, announced only by the faint change in the amount of light filtering through the thinnest sections of the wooden walls. The space remained sealed, the air growing increasingly stale as hours passed without enough fresh circulation. Their natural resilience as undead meant they didn't need food or water. Still, the psychological weight of confinement bore down on them nonetheless.

Then, without warning, the wall near where the door had once been began to ripple and part. The transformation was no less remarkable for being familiar—living wood peeling back layer by layer to create an opening. Through this new breach stepped Khanorrh, his weathered face set in lines of cold determination.

For a heart-stopping moment, Aira feared he had detected her attempts with the Splinter, but his immediate words revealed a different purpose altogether.

"The shamans have convened and agreed with me," he announced without preamble, his voice rumbling like stones settling after an avalanche. "Stone shifts, but it doesn't break for just anyone. We've deliberated on your transgressions against the mountain's sovereignty."

His eyes swept over them, passing judgment with each glance. "You," he pointed at Aira and Lila, "are to be dissolved within the Roots of the Mountain. The earth doesn't weep—it swallows. As for you, blightborn," he turned to Alliot, "as an official envoy of Wentouk, you shall be allowed to leave and return home with a warning message from our council. Tell Ainorrh that the deepest cracks hold the oldest stories, and we have not forgotten hers."

The proclamation hung in the air like a physical weight, pressing down on them with undeniable gravity. Khanorrh offered no further explanation, no opportunity for appeal. With the same abrupt efficiency that had marked his entrance, he turned and departed, the living wall sealing behind him as if eager to enforce his decree.

Alliot remained frozen for several moments after Khanorrh's departure, his face a mask of disbelief. Then, as the full implication of the shaman's words registered, his expression transformed into one of unbridled fury.

"But that means they want to kill you!" he burst out, his voice strangled with outrage. "It goes against all our basic principles!"

Aira nodded, maintaining a surprising calm despite the sentence pronounced upon them. "I know, Alliot. That's definitely not the best outcome for us," she smiled wryly. "They didn't offer us any timeline, but we have to think fast. There should be a way to escape."

"Dissolution within the Source?" Lila's voice cut through the tension, her eyes wide. "What exactly does that mean? Is that some enlightened execution method I've never heard of?"

Alliot paced the confined space, his agitation palpable in every step. "It's not just execution," he explained, his voice tight with suppressed emotion. "It's... erasure. Complete. Final." He stopped, turning to face them. "The Source will absorb all energy from you, leaving nothing behind. Your essence, your consciousness—everything that makes you you will be consumed. Nobody survives that."

"Well, that sounds rather permanent," Aira said with forced lightness. "Not my preferred exit strategy."

Lila leaned against the wall, her fingers drumming a nervous rhythm against the wood. "By the twisted branches, that's extreme even for these root-loving ancients."

Alliot's shoulders slumped as the weight of the situation fully descended upon him. His internal conflict played out visibly across his features—loyalty to his kind warring with his growing attachment to his companions.

"This is wrong," he muttered, almost to himself. "This goes beyond punishment. Beyond justice. It's... erasure of those who challenged their authority." He looked up, meeting first Aira's gaze, then Lila's. "I can't stand by and let this happen."

"Does this mean you're finally choosing a side?" Lila asked, her tone gentler than her words might suggest.

Alliot nodded, a decision crystallizing in his eyes. "If the shamans are willing to commit such an act, then everything I thought I knew about our principles is flawed. Everything I was taught to defend..." He trailed off, struggling to articulate the paradigm shift occurring within him. "And… There's more… There's something I noticed a while ago, but didn't think was prudent to share with you."

The conversation lapsed into silence, each of them contemplating the dire circumstances they faced. As the quiet stretched, Aira found herself recalling their many encounters with the enlightened, their methods of advancement, their approach to power. Something had always seemed... off. The pieces finally began to align in her mind.

"Alliot," Aira began carefully, "is this about some aspects of the System—the Nexus? For example how it provides clear paths and objectives. Quests, power progression, levels." She gestured to encompass them all. "You've seen us level up, gain new abilities. But you enlightened... after centuries of existence, your progression seems remarkably slow by comparison."

Alliot's expression shifted, discomfort evident in the set of his jaw. His fingers twisted the hem of his shirt—that nervous tell they'd come to recognize. "I... this isn't..." He exhaled sharply, resignation replacing resistance. "Fine. Yes. You're right."

"Right about what?" Lila pressed.

"We don't have access to the quest system like you do," Alliot admitted, the words seeming to physically pain him. "The Nexus responds differently to us. We can sense it, interact with it to some degree, but we don't receive clear guidance or rewards the way you describe."

Aira's eyes widened with realization. "That's why your levels remain relatively low even after centuries of training and exploration."

"Exactly," Alliot confirmed, unable to meet their gaze. "We've been trying to understand the Nexus for generations. Developed our own methods. Our own systems of advancement. But it's slow. We gain power through meditation. Through communion with the Sources. Through years of disciplined practice. Though, before I met you, I never thought this pace was… inadequate."

"And then someone like Aira arrives," Lila said, understanding dawning in her voice, "gaining in weeks what might take you decades."

"It's more than that," Alliot continued, the dam of secrecy finally breaking. "The Nexus seems to... favor her somehow. Respond to her in ways it never has to us. And now with you, Lila—changed as you were—the same patterns emerge."

He looked directly at Aira now, his expression a mixture of awe and trepidation. "I think Khanorrh realized what your existence might mean for us. Even if he doesn't know about the Splinter. I'm sure he felt the power of your connection with the Source. For him… It probably felt like violation. A direct connection to the Source that bypasses centuries of our careful cultivation. It threatens everything the shamans have built their authority upon."

Aira turned the Splinter in her hand, watching how its emerald light played across her fingertips. "So they'd rather eliminate us than risk what we represent."

"That makes their decision much clearer," Lila said grimly. "But it doesn't solve our immediate problem of escaping this wooden prison before they dissolve us into the cosmic soup."

Alliot straightened his shoulders, a new resolve hardening his features. "I may have a solution. It's risky, but it might be our only chance."

"We're listening," Aira said, hope kindling in her eyes.

"We decided not to use meditation before," Alliot began. "Because we didn't want to attract attention. I don't think we should care about it anymore."

"Well, root me sideways!" Lila exclaimed. "Alliot is becoming bold? That's a scene I'd really like to see!"

"What other choice do we have?" Alliot asked, his usual stoicism cracking under the weight of their circumstances. "If we do nothing, you two will be dissolved. At least this way, we have a chance."

Aira nodded, her decision made. "Let's do it. We'll focus on the Source through the Splinter. If we can establish a connection strong enough..."

"It might respond," Lila finished. "After all, it seemed to have a mind of its own down in that cavern."

They arranged themselves in a circle at the center of the treehouse, the Splinter placed between them. Aira took a deep breath, centering herself before reaching out to both Lila and Alliot through their party bond.

"Follow my lead," she instructed. "Let your energy flow through me to the Splinter. Don't hold back—we have nothing to lose now."

The meditation began as it always had—the slow, steady rhythm of breath, the gradual shifting of attention from physical to ethereal. But this time, there was a desperate intensity to their focus, a wild abandon that had been absent from their previous careful exercises.

The Splinter's glow intensified, emerald light spilling between Aira's fingers and casting strange, shifting shadows across their faces. Minutes passed with no discernible change beyond the strengthening radiance, and Aira felt a flicker of doubt. Perhaps the treehouse's dampening effect was too strong, the distance to the Source too great.

Then, almost imperceptibly at first, the wood grain of the floor beneath them began to shift. Tiny ripples spread outward from where the Splinter touched the surface, like a stone dropped into still water. The movement accelerated, the patterns of the wood swirling and realigning themselves in ways that defied natural growth.

"It's working," Lila whispered, her voice tight with excitement and fear.

The change rippled beyond the floor. All around them, the walls writhed as if possessed, ancient wood creaking and shifting in protest. The formerly seamless surface bulged and contracted in sickening waves, like the chest of some massive beast taking labored breaths. A chorus of groans—not quite wood, not quite living—filled the space, rising in pitch until it vibrated through their bones. The very structure they'd called home now twisted against them, fighting the foreign influence that commanded its transformation from within.

Aira's arcane senses flared suddenly, alerting her to movement beyond their prison. "Someone's coming," she warned, never breaking her concentration. "Multiple signatures. Moving fast."

"Khanorrh," Alliot confirmed grimly. "He must have sensed our attempt."

"Push harder," Aira urged, channeling more of her power through the Splinter. The emerald light blazed now, so bright it hurt to look at it directly.

The wall facing the direction of the approaching energy signatures began to part, wood peeling back in that now-familiar transformation. But simultaneously, something else happened—the floor beneath them shuddered violently, then simply ceased to exist.

One moment, they were sitting on solid wood; the next, they were falling.

The trio plummeted downward through sudden darkness, the opened floor having revealed a yawning chasm beneath their prison. Aira clutched the Splinter to her chest with one hand, her other arm flailing uselessly against the rush of air. She caught brief glimpses of Lila and Alliot tumbling nearby, their forms silhouetted against the rapidly diminishing light above.

The fall lasted seconds that stretched like minutes, the wind howling past Aira's ears as darkness swallowed them whole. Their descent ended with a bone-jarring impact as they crashed onto a sloped surface. Earth and stone scraped against Aira's skin, the impact driving air from her lungs in a painful rush. She tumbled helplessly down the incline, rocks and debris cutting into her as she rolled, until her shoulder slammed against something solid, halting her momentum with brutal finality.

Dazed, she struggled to orient herself in the near-total darkness. Only the Splinter's glow provided any illumination, revealing a vast cavern stretching away into shadows. The ceiling far above showed the ragged hole through which they'd fallen—a perfectly circular opening in what appeared to be the underside of Ziemrot itself.

"Lila? Alliot? Al?" Aira called, her voice echoing strangely in the cavernous space.

"Here," came Lila's pained response from nearby. "Bashed up but breathing. Well, figuratively speaking."

Alliot appeared at the edge of the Splinter's light, limping slightly but otherwise intact. Al scurried beside him, somehow having managed the fall with the least injury of them all.

"The Source," Alliot whispered, looking around in awe. "It listened to you. It opened a way."

As if to confirm his words, they heard distant noises from above—furious actions of the enlightened entering their treehouse to see there was no one there. The circular opening through which they'd fallen began to seal itself, living wood growing across the gap like accelerated healing over a wound.

"But it opened for them too," Aira realized, understanding dawning. "The Source doesn't distinguish between us and them. It's responding to both, acting on different requests simultaneously."

"The Source has its own will," Alliot said, a note of reverence in his voice despite their circumstances. "Its own purpose. Separate from the shamans who claim to speak for it."

"Well, I'd love to philosophize about the mountain's mysterious ways," Lila interrupted, "but those voices are getting closer, and I'd rather not stick around to become one with the cosmic tree roots, if it's all the same to you."

She was right. Even as the opening above sealed itself, they couldn't be sure there wasn't an entrance near them that the pursuing guards and shamans could use to get them.

"Aira, can you sense the right way somehow?" asked Alliot.

Aira looked around, extending her senses, trying to feel once again the pulsating might of the Roots of the Mountain. "This way," she said, after a brief hesitation, pointing down one of the many tunnels branching from the cavern. "This path should lead us away from Ziemrot. If we move quickly, we might escape before they can figure out where we are."

They set off at a hurried pace, the Splinter's glow lighting their way through the winding passages. The tunnel system was vast and complex, with junctions and forks appearing every few dozen meters. But Aira led confidently, seeming to sense which paths would lead outward rather than deeper into the mountain's embrace.

After what felt like hours but was likely only minutes, they emerged into a narrow ravine, moonlight washing over them like a blessing. The sides of the ravine rose steeply but were climbable, the surface rough enough to provide handholds.

"Up," Alliot directed, feeling himself again in his own element. Tracking, defending, hiding. That was what Alliot was trained to do. That also meant he knew how to avoid the unwanted attention. "Once we reach the forest, we'll have a better chance of evading pursuit."

They scaled the ravine's wall with desperate energy, helping each other over the steepest sections. Al rode on Aira's shoulder, his tiny claws digging into the fabric of her clothing for purchase. When they finally crested the edge, they found themselves in a dense forest, the trees providing immediate cover.

"We need to put as much distance between us and Ziemrot as possible," Alliot said, already moving. "They won't give up easily."

"Where to?" Aira asked, jogging to keep pace.

"Lamcada," Alliot replied without hesitation. "It's our best option. Another enlightened city, but one not as closely aligned with Ziemrot. The domain of the Fire element."

"Another elemental Source," Aira realized. "Another potential Shard."

"Exactly," Alliot confirmed. "And with luck, a place where we might find allies rather than executioners."

As they pushed deeper into the forest, leaving the mountain and its secrets behind, Aira felt the Splinter warm against her skin where she'd tucked it securely. Its glow had dimmed to a subtle pulse, but its presence was undeniable—a fragment of an ancient power now bound to her, the first piece of a greater puzzle that was only beginning to take shape.

Behind them, the Rooted Mountain loomed like a dark sentinel against the night sky, its secrets still mostly intact despite their brief communion with its heart. Ahead lay uncertainty, danger, and the promise of discovery.

Lamcada awaited.
