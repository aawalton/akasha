“Forgive me, mediator, but you have not identified yourself or presented credentials establishing your authority to intervene at this level. May I have your name, and know what rank you hold in the Mediator MacKenzie Clan, please?” she said slowly, each word measured.

I felt myself freeze for a moment as panic at having to identify myself flooded through my system, until I heard her request to know the position I held in my own clan, and a smile slowly spread across my face.

I was about to answer when I noticed people stepping off my ship–like literally walking off it, as if they were taking a stroll through a meadow–and beginning to surround the Third Expeditionary Fleet.

“What is the meaning of this,” she yelled as she glanced away from me.

“No idea,” I replied as I relaxed and watched the panic slide onto her face for a moment before going in for the killing blow. “However, since you desire to know who I am, I shall at least tell you. I am Kenna Mackenzie, ancestor of the Mackenzie Clan.”

I watched the rest of the colour drain from her face before she whispered a single word, “Fuck.” The next moment, all the cultivators surrounding her fleet unleashed qi attacks, shredding the vessels and killing thousands.

I watched silently as the cultivators moved in to finish off anyone they had missed. It was a moment later, when one of them grabbed a box and slid it under their arm, that I realised no one was actually here, and they had all been kept safe in the data banks, letting their clone bodies die.

I was about to let them go by telling the cultivator to toss the data bank back to be collected or remotely retrieved when he flexed his arm and crushed the box against his torso. The next moment, it was floating with the rest of the debris field, and other cultivators were breaking the data banks for the other ships, ensuring the deaths of everyone.

I felt my mouth fall open as I watched. I felt a brief moment, in the back of my mind, when I wondered what would happen to the real body now the mind had been destroyed.

It took me a few moments, but I slowly turned my head to look at Commander Tollen Kreth, only for him to flinch away from my attention. I could see the panic on his face. I had only watched him for a heartbeat before I noticed the cultivators outside were returning to the ship.

“Please inform Zeo, that I have returned and will be awaiting her arrival in the Dwarven Ghost City.” I had thought up the reference on the spot but hoped it was enough for Zeo to understand where I meant.

I immediately ended the call and waited a few more moments for all the cultivators to be back on board before I pushed power to the engines and began to pull away from what was left of the battlefield. I glanced at the debris field and crushed data banks before engaging the warp drive and sinking into subspace.
