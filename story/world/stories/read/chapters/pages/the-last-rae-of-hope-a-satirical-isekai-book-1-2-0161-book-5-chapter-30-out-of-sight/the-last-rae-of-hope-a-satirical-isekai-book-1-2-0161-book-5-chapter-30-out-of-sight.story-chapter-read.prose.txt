The carriage ride to the castle gave me exactly enough time to question almost everything that had led me up to this moment.

Olethros sat across from me with his eyes closed and hands folded in his lap, appearing entirely at peace with the evening’s prospects. I had been watching the city pass by the window for the last five minutes and had cataloged approximately a dozen red flags before we even reached the gate.

And that was before I had visual of the knights.

Silver. All of them bore the markings of the Order of Silver. My gaze first lingered on their markings, combing over each one as they had lined themselves in neat rows.

No one I recognize.

Then I closed my eyes.

I couldn’t see their auras. They were human and hybrid, to be sure, but there was no sense of their aura, no flicker of amity, no way for me to tell if they were truly part of the Order or just people wearing the colors. There was only darkness.

Was it me? Did I lose sight in my mind’s eye for swapping deities?

Or was it them?

I opened my eyes and stared hard out the carriage window. Their armor was polished until it gleamed. But some parts didn’t sparkle, either. The joints, like the articulated pieces at the elbow and knee, had a dull, gritty look of slapdash scrubbing—something I was so sure about, I conceded that I had probably also participated in such laziness back in the day.

Two knights near the castle gate snapped to attention as our carriage rolled past, then relaxed the moment we were by. A third was standing and rocking his feet back and forth, the telltale signs of not being used to standing for so long.

Fresh recruits!

“No one seems particularly skilled,” Olethros said, without opening his eyes. “They have amity, but it’s thin and inconsistent at best.”

“Normally, I’d be okay with that, given what we’re up to, but I have to admit it makes me uneasy.”

He nodded. “Nora is similarly out of sorts. There are patrols on the outskirts of the city, but their abilities are laughable.”

The carriage slowed.

I leaned to look out the window, noting that an older knight was approaching. By the time I turned, Olethros was already gone, his seat empty and the brooch at my collar faintly warm where he had retreated into it.

Guess I’m up.

The door opened.

The knight who opened it wore a rank insignia and moved with the kind of ease and attentive posture that came from actual training. He took my invitation, carefully, and then paused.

“Will you be attending alone this evening, my lady?”

I cleared my throat and straightened my shoulders, as if I had been caught but would never let on to that fact. In what I hoped was a voice beyond my years, I explained, “It would hardly be wise to arrive with a lesser one on my arm if I'm hoping to catch a better one, would it?"

He laughed as he offered me a hand down the awaiting steps, surprised out of his professionalism. “Happy hunting, my lady.”

I inclined my head and took up his offer, stepping down.

I couldn’t see any silver on him with closed eyes, but I could feel his aura through our brief touch.

My sight beyond sight no longer works right.

***

The castle interior was different. The last time, staff had been peppered with crimson livery, the colors of King Saulus’s private guard. But now, again, only silver could be seen, the majority of whom were novices.

They were also upsetting the staff.

One knight, a fair-haired girl with pointed and twitching rat ears, kept questioning every servant in close range.

“How long until we start?”

“Where’s the sitting lounge?”

“How many nobles are we expecting?”

I did my best to ignore her despite my sympathy for being placed in a role without any preparation. Instead, I pretended to be calculating with my fake, male-hungry gaze. My acting was probably poor, which made it all the better—no one went out of their way to strike up a conversation with me. Before long, a young man I didn’t recognize addressed the throng I was in, bidding us welcome and leading us to file to the sanctuary.

I guess Lord Reginald, Royal Chamberlain to King Saulus, is also gone.

“Don’t head to the front,” Olethros murmured from my serpent brooch. “You’re not important enough to approach.”

I filed into one of the back rows of pews. Prince Viktor, looking very full of himself, stood at the altar, but I spent most of my time trying to decide if I recognized the simple-smocked and balding priest standing next to him.

Nope.

He held an orb in both hands, which began to glow as he raised it. Silence followed, just before Relias’s voice filled the sanctuary. It was both clipped and rather uninterested; the tone of a man who had better things to do. He introduced the priest as Johan, offered a few words about the glorious new age of Vitkor’s reign, and then handed over the procession to his subordinate.

Father Johan placed the orb upon the altar while Vitkor knelt, awaiting his crown. I bit my lip to keep from laughing as he winced, clearly still tender from the tumble off Aurum’s golden backside.

Serves you right!

One of the knights had been prepared to start a salute, and the rest in attendance sluggishly followed. Cheers also rose from the pews as nobles stood in celebration, but the effect was less than satisfactory. When I was onstage and revealed here, the room was at capacity, with standing room only in the back. Now, the pews were only half filled.

Viktor seemed to sense something as well, for just a fraction of a second, just before shaking his head and pointing towards the exit.

“Let us all celebrate!” he cried, claiming his royally unearned victory.

***

They had fixed the wall that Nora blew out when we were fighting the demon Anhinga. I noticed it when we filed into the banquet hall—the stonework was exquisite, but the mortar was gleaming a little more elegantly than the adjoining corner.

King Vitkor was introduced with full ceremony at the center of the room, immediately gaining several orbiting noblewomen angling for a first dance despite the probability that it had been pre-determined.

Gotta admire the hustle, though.

I was smart enough to stay away. Once again, I pretended to seek easier prey, all while watching for an exit as music swelled from the corner orchestra.

“You keep forgetting to look behind you,” Olethros murmured into my ear.

I whirled around, startled.

“Make a show of taking my hand,” he said without moving his lips, offering up his palm.

I snatched it as he grinned.

Olethros swept me onto the dance floor with a small flourish, his graceful steps slow but precise. I followed, finding myself focusing on trying to match his confidence. My dress was a bit limiting because of another layer underneath, but not so much that we couldn’t have a little fun.

Fun?! Did I think this was fun?

As we danced, I’m sure I scowled a bit, slightly disappointed in myself for liking an activity that was in any way related to what Relias had said was a hobby he enjoyed.

But that was probably just another lie, too.

“You’re not focusing on me,” Olethros said, pouting slightly as he pulled me close.

“Sorry,” I murmured, blushing a bit at the loss of distance. “This trap is definitely unnerving me. I want to avoid triggering anything, but—”

“That seems impossible, doesn’t it?”

I nodded. “This grand trap isn’t to stop us... It’s to frame us, isn’t it? Something really bad is going to happen to everyone here.”

“Not if we don’t take initiative.” He leaned into my ear. “You’ll be happy to know we have about two minutes,” he said pleasantly, “before the servants realize the castle is on fire.”

I pulled back to stare at him incredulously.

He probably got Nora's approval and just "forgot" about mine!

He grinned, then went in for one last playful twirl. “It worked so well for Relias the first time. As you’ve said before, why reinvent the wheel?”

***

I had to admit, the smoke was useful, if not cough-inducing.

Nobody watched the servant corridors when the first shouts went up near the kitchen wing. The side passage we took was unguarded because the evacuation process was a mess. Since so many of the assigned knights had no idea about the specifics of the castle’s layout, we managed to dip and weave into ignored offshoots whenever we wanted.

I paused at one set of stairs long enough to strip off the gown, exposing the light trousers and fitted top beneath it. It was by no means armor, but it was the best Cybill and I could come up with.

The fact that my armor wasn’t enchanted sure pointed to ensuring I died by demonic animus, didn’t it?

Relias could have dropped his amity barrier at the right moment, and poof, I’m just a tin can!

I kicked my dress down several stairs in angst before passing it.

Olethros handed me Will and Faith at the bottom of the stairwell. After a few zig-zags through the darker hallways, we found the first dungeon.

It had been a political prison where sons and daughters of noblemen who demonstrated against their fathers were sent for a day or two to play at being of the people. From there, they would be released to manage their own campaigns, all while still being funded by those in power to draw attention to other things that didn’t matter as much.

King Saulus, however, had decided that such demonstrations were no longer in style, opting to send the guilty to the same public prison, regardless of their status. It made sense not to keep your enemies so close in the castle, too.

We walked the dusty halls of the abandoned cells, noting several sets of footsteps leading toward the back wall but never returning. Olethros pushed on the false panel, revealing another set of stairs.

The sub-basement was not on any map we had looked at. Its ceiling was low, with several pillars holding it up, while the outer walls sealed in the stale, sour air. Olethros’s staff cast a dim, purplish light just far enough to illuminate several shapes on the floor—shapes that had ceased being people some time ago.

I didn’t look at them for any longer than I had to.

There were no individual cells here, just sets of chains along pillars. At the far end of the basement were three figures. Olethros lit up his staff further so that I could see better. Two were sealed inside armor with no gaps and no insignia. They both, however, had black jewels embedded in the center of their visors. Their swords were in their hands, the tips resting on the ground as their shields were held out in front of them. Between them, I could see Prince Mito, chained, beaten, but still breathing.

“Go back,” he croaked. His hand lifted toward the closest Erebortite guardian without quite making it all the way. “Don’t wake my brothers up again.”
