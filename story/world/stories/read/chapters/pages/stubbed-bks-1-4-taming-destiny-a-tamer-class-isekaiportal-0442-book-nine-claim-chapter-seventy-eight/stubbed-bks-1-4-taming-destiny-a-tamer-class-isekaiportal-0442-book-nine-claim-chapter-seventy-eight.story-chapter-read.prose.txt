On my way back to the healing rooms, I reach out to my companions. They can't have encountered the beasts in the forest – they would have told me – but I don't want them returning to the settlement and walking into an army camped outside without warning.

They meet the news with immediate concern.

We're coming back, Bastet replies without any need to deliberate, making the decision for all of them.

Actually, I'd prefer it if you could try to find out where the beasts are coming from, I suggest. We need to know why so many different species are here together, and whether more are on the way.

But they're attacking the settlement, Bastet protests, and several of the others send wordless agreement. You're trapped in there.

There are plenty of fighters here. As long as the walls hold, we'll be fine – there are no Tier threes in the army, as far as I know. What I really need is information. I've sent Valence's scouts to do the same, but they won't see things the way you do.

Still, Bastet doesn’t seem convinced. Her protectiveness has only increased in strength ever since she Evolved to Tier three. Ninja breaks in, sounding slightly hesitant.

Matriarch, if we just attack the ones threatening our den, they could run away. If we find their den first, then we can stop them from running away and attacking us again later, she points out.

Bastet hesitates, then I feel her capitulation, begrudging as it is.

Fine. We’ll find the enemies’ den so this is the last time they threaten us. Her tone is more than a little menacing, but it’s not directed at me so I’m appreciative rather than concerned.

Thank you, I tell her. And be careful – we don’t know what might be waiting in the den. She replies with a wordless wave that expresses her dissatisfaction and love all at once. I send my own love to all of them, then pull away from the Bond and focus on my own task.

Inside the healing rooms, I move directly to the room in which the first attacker is residing. It’s an unexpected hive of activity and I have to press myself to the wall to allow two healers past with a bed. A third follows with a mattress and sheets.

Several voices snarling meet my ears before I’m able to see inside the room, and when I do, my eyebrows twitch upwards. The woman I had been inspecting before is still there, and she’s predictably growling and tugging at her restraints to try to get at those closest to her. Given the crowd present, her fingernails are actually successfully grasping at one of the healers’ robes, though they don’t seem to be able to piece the cloth at all. I don’t think the healer has even noticed.

As well as the three healers – those who had been carrying objects – are the four guards and two attackers. One of the attackers has been pinned into the corner to the far right of the door on the mattress at the foot of the woman’s bed; the other is being wrestled into the bed that’s been placed in the empty space in front of the door.

Despite being Warriors, the non-Classer man is giving them a run for their money. Fortunately, he’s gagged so they only need to worry about his nails. Nonetheless, several bear bleeding scratches.

Finally, they manage to pin him in place. A moment later, he’s chained to the bed like the woman is.

As they step away, I hear several sighs of relief.

“These ferals are awfully vicious,” one of the healers comments to one of the guards who grunts in acknowledgement. The same healer glances up and notices me there. His eyes light up. “Lord Markus! Can we impose on you to install some metal rings in the wall? There isn’t enough space for another bed so we’ve had to put the third one on a rush mattress in the corner there.”

The two guards now relieved of their charge and the same number of healers step out of the room, giving me enough space to enter.

"Healer Limar,” I greet him, “Is it wise to put them all in the same room? What if they break free and start attacking each other?" I ask doubtfully.

The healer grimaces.

“It isn’t my preference,” he admits, “however we simply don’t have the space to house them separately. We are rather full at the moment due to recent events, and Healer Thessalanor wishes to keep some space available for potential casualties from the attack on the wall.”

I eye the positions of the three attackers carefully, then sigh and nod in acknowledgement – Thessalanor has a point about likely future casualties so it’s good thinking to save space where possible. This room will be pretty crowded when all three are in place, but they shouldn’t be able to reach each other.

“I understand. You wanted some rings set in the wall?”

“Yes, my lord – to chain this patient’s hands and feet. Here, and here,” Healer Limar confirms, pointing out the two spots, one on the side wall, one close to the door. “Do you need me to send for metal rings?”

“It’s fine,” I dismiss. I have lots of things in my Inventory; I’m sure I can find something suitable. Sure enough I have several slots filled with billets of metal. I pull out a steel billet and feed my magic into it, encouraging it to soften. When it’s almost as soft as clay, I begin reshaping it into two rings, each with an anchor-shaped attachment point.

“I’ll need to get at the wall,” I indicate mildly to the guards holding the third attacker. They quickly shift him out of the way – fighting all the while – and it’s only a matter of moments to embed the anchors into the wall, reforming the stone around them. Those shouldn’t be coming free any time soon.

It only takes a few more minutes until the second man is installed on his new bed – again fighting all the way. One of the guards retreats with a new bleeding scratch on his face while the healer is winded from a kick to his gut.

“What do you wish us to do now, Lord Markus?” one of the guards who had been waiting outside the room asks as we file out.

My eyes flick over the several scratch wounds they’re bearing.

"Have those seen to – who knows what might be on their nails. Then please go to the wall – they’ll need your help there."

“Come this way and I’ll treat the scratches,” Healer Limar wheezes, before sending me a quick glance. “If that’s acceptable with you, my lord.”

“Of course,” I quickly agree. After several quick bows, the group of five hurry down the corridor, leaving quiet in their wake.

Actually, it’s almost too quiet – and it takes me a moment to realise why: the three patients are no longer growling. Curious, I activate Fade and Stealth, then peer in through the door unbeknownst to the residents within.

What intrigues me most is the absence of aggression between them. In complete opposition to the way they had been with anyone else in the room, they’re currently almost quiescent, and seem to be ignoring each other’s existence.

If I needed further evidence that these cases are linked, here it is.

Footsteps sound in the corridor. The quiet exchange from within the room shifts immediately, the tone becoming aggressive once more. I turn to see Healer Thessalanor approaching. He jumps as I deactivate my concealment.

“Lord Markus!” he greets in a voice that’s just a little too breathless to be normal. “Healer Limar said you sent for me," he continues, more calmly. “I take it you wish me to inspect the two newest patients?”

“I would,” I agree. “The situations are just too similar to be coincidences.” I grimace.

Not detailing any of my theories, I precede the healer into the room, and watch as he sits first next to the man on the bed, and then kneels near the feet of the man on the floor. Suspecting that I know what he’ll find, I wait until he resurfaces, the expression in his eyes grave.

“They all have those internal scars,” he comments.

“And no build-up of toxins?” I check.

He hesitates for a moment, then shakes his head.

“Not that I could tell immediately.”

I nod, resigned to what I must do next.

"Do you remember what we discussed earlier – the means of investigating more closely that I prefer not to use unless necessary?" He inclines his head warily. "I think we can now say it’s necessary," I say grimly.

Healer Thessalanor looks at me with speculation.

“I take it this is something to do with your Class?” he asks shrewdly.

I nod shortly.

“It is. Though, at least on the woman and Ker Bolis, it is legal.”

Healer Thessalanor’s eyes flicker in understanding as they dance over the collar on the neck of the man lying on the rush mattress – and the lack of collar on the man lying on the bed.

“Then I will make space for you. Would you like me to leave or stay?”

“Stay,” I request. “Continue working on Ker Himlan. Hopefully, if I can find out enough about the issue, you’ll be able to solve it for him.” Because if not, I’m going to have another issue on my hands.

"Of course, my lord." He moves directly to the man currently restrained on the bed. In the meantime, I perch on the mattress next to the woman – I still don’t know her name as none of the witnesses gave it, unlike the witnesses to the more recent attack.

"I'm sorry," I tell her quietly, not knowing whether there's still something inside her that can hear and understand despite the unseeing, uncomprehending cast to her eyes. “I wish there were another way.”

Then, meeting her gaze, I trigger the Dominate.

The familiar space forms around me, an almost laughable resistance separating me and the woman standing opposite – it’s more like walking through warm, humid air than any sort of current. I stride through it easily.

Where am I? What's happening to me? a voice asks, tremulous.

My eyebrows go up. I wasn't expecting her to be capable of speech, given the state of her body.

“We're in a Battle of Wills,” I answer, getting closer. “What do you remember?”

I remember being at the marketplace, she replies slowly. And then my headache got very bad. And suddenly everything went black.

“You don't remember anything after that?”

No. Her distress is rising. Where am I? Why am I here?

“You attacked the shopkeeper and everyone else around you who you could reach. You don't remember that?”

The shock she feels is unmistakable. I pause just in front of her.

No…no, I would never attack anyone! I swear! Please believe me!

In this space, the sincerity of her emotions is confirmation enough. Fear and desperation taints everything – understandably. A violent slave can expect an even shorter and more unpleasant life than a normal slave.

“It’s alright, I believe you,” I soothe.

Who…who are you? she asks shakily. I’m a little surprised that she doesn’t recognise me – I suppose I’ve got used to being recognised by everyone I meet.

“I’m Markus,” I reply, oddly unwilling to put the title on that.

She recognises me nonetheless and a gut-wrenching pang of horror goes through her at the realisation.

Master…please, please believe me, I didn’t hurt anyone!

“I believe that you didn’t want to,” I agree easily. “But the evidence is there that your body did.” I sense her preparing to beg again, and instead hold up my hand. “You’re clearly not in control of your body right now, and I want to find out what is. That you’re still aware is fortuitous. I would like to Bind you – it will allow me to investigate your body in far more detail, hopefully finding out exactly what is controlling you. But I would prefer to have your consent. Do I have it?”

I feel her confusion at having a choice at all, and hesitation to give me even more control over her. I cross my fingers discretely. If she says no….

You hold my contract, you can do what you like, she replies, on the surface compliantly, but I feel the resentment hidden below.

“And yet I would like your consent. Though I admit that I don’t know if we will be able to help you without a Bond – the head healer and I have both investigated, with no success. And we cannot allow threats to the settlement to continue….” I don’t have any moral issues with stacking the deck in my favour.

I feel her war with herself, most of her wanting to comply, either because she doesn’t want to make me angry or because she’s frightened about what I’ve told her of her body attacking people without her awareness. However, there’s still a small fraction of her that wants to test whether I mean it – whether I will actually wait for her consent.

Honestly, I’m not even sure what I will do if it comes down to it – this is the good of the settlement at stake, after all. But then, do the ends truly justify the means?

Fortunately for me, I am not tested in this moment.

Please help me, master, she says finally. I am…I am…willing. Her tone is reluctant, but I’ll take it.

No longer needing to touch the person to apply the Bond, I simply allow it to take root in her soul.

Even as the Battle of Wills shatters around me, I dive deeply into the woman’s body. The innate resistance that was there before has fallen away completely.

Everywhere, but one small spot in her brain.
