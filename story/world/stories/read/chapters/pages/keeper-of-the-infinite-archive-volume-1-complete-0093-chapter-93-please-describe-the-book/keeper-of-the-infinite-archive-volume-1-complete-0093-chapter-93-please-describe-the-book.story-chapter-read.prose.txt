A couple of days later, Bart sat behind the Patron Services desk, reviewing the three shortlisted applications he had laid out on the brass counter.

He had conducted two serious interviews already that morning. The process had been illuminating, on a variety of candidates and what he needed for Archive and if he needed to compromise somewhere.

Candidate One had been Cecily Webb, a Fae Senior Archival Specialist from the Silver Spire. She had seen the vacancy almost as soon as the Silver Spire's professional repository circular picked up the posting. Cecily possessed immaculate credentials and superb catalogue knowledge, arriving under the assumption that this was effectively a Special Collections role at the most prestigious repository in existence.

During the interview, Bart had mentioned the regular Patron Services rotation.

“How soon do you anticipate appointing dedicated public service staff?” Cecily had asked, completely seriously.

Bart had looked at her. “This is the dedicated public service position.”

Cecily had gone very still. “Oh I see.”

She was not pompous or incompetent. She was a first rate archivist who had simply applied to the wrong job.

Candidate Two had been Edwin Holt, a Minotaur Institutional Librarian with the Tessera Municipal Library Service. Tessera's employment registry had automatically matched him against Bart's vacancy. Edwin arrived extraordinarily prepared, with his questions already numbered in an organised folder.

“Current departmental organisation chart?” Edwin had asked.

“We do not have one,” Bart had replied.

Edwin had made a note.

“Personnel Services representative?”

Bart had looked briefly toward Gregor. “We do not have one of those either.”

Edwin had made a longer note.

“Pension transfer agreement?”

“Not yet.”

Another note.

“The employment registry assessed me as a 94% match,” Edwin observed.

“What was the missing 6%?” Bart asked.

Edwin opened another page in the folder. “I believe I have a list of reasons.”

Bart began to feel that the interview was taking place in the wrong direction. Edwin was a genuinely excellent librarian. He simply required an institution mature enough to employ him properly.

Then Bart looked at the application for Candidate Three.

NAME: NELL MERCER

CURRENT POSITION: ASSISTANT LIBRARIAN

INSTITUTION: TALVEN CIVIC LIBRARY

JURISDICTION: NORTH MERE MUNICIPAL CONSORTIUM

That had secured Bart’s attention immediately. She was not a prestigious scholar from an academic spire or an administrator from a vast city bureaucracy. She was from a busy, thoroughly ordinary municipal consortium Bart had never encountered.

The most interesting part of the application, however, was the cover note detailing how she had discovered the position.

HOW DID YOU LEARN OF THIS VACANCY?

Your notice appeared on Talven Civic Library's legacy consortium terminal during morning returns. Our Head Librarian believed it was a delayed pre-Ruin posting. I pointed out that delayed postings generally do not contain current funding disclosures. We verified the institutional seal. You appeared to actually be open. I applied.

“I like her already,” Gregor said, reading over Bart’s shoulder.

“You have not even met her yet,” Bart pointed out.

“She noticed you are in trouble.”

An hour later, the Staff Restroom door opened with its familiar WHOOSH GURGLE !. Mabel checked the temporary interview authorisation, then stepped aside. An orc woman emerged, carrying an overfilled canvas satchel.

Nell Mercer stood half a head taller than Bart, broad shouldered, with olive green skin, two short lower tusks, and thick black hair pulled into a loose knot. She wore a faded brown cardigan over a waistcoat, the sleeves pushed up to her elbows.

“Nell Mercer,” she said, offering Bart her hand. “Assistant Librarian. Talven Civic.”

The formal interview began with a noticeable, slightly awkward cultural friction. Bart asked a question about circulation policy, listened to her answer, and then went completely quiet while he mentally reconstructed how her method might apply to the Archive's damaged catalogue.

After several seconds, Nell frowned.

“Was that a rejection?” Nell asked.

“It was not a rejection,” Bart said.

“Then what was it?”

“I was thinking about your answer.”

“You become extremely still when you think.”

“That is my habit.”

“Where I'm from, if someone goes that still after you've answered a question, you start apologising.”

Bart considered this. He stood completely silent for another five seconds, processing the cultural information.

Nell watched him become completely still again. “You're doing it again right now.”

“I suppose I am,” Bart agreed.

Despite the minor communication gap, the interview continued. Bart looked down at his clipboard and asked a carefully prepared question. “A patron requests a work but provides an incomplete citation. How would you proceed?”

“I would ask them what they are actually looking for,” Nell said immediately.

He had expected a detailed discussion of catalogue search techniques, keyword triangulation, and bibliographic verification methods.

Before he could redirect the question, a real test instead presented itself.

A patron approached the counter, interrupting the interview. He was a visiting scholar, looking slightly bewildered by the vast scale of the main hall.

“Excuse me,” the patron said, looking at Bart. “I am looking for a red book which I had read long back on a king.”

Bart had been about to search the catalogue. Nell had searched the patron instead.

She turned away from Bart and faced the scholar directly.

“Red title or red cover?” Nell asked.

“Cover,” the patron replied, surprised.

“History, law, biography, or something else?”

“History.”

“A king you admire, or one you are trying to prove was incompetent?”

The patron hesitated, looking slightly guilty. “Incompetent.”

“Excellent,” Nell said brightly. “Which kingdom?”

Within thirty seconds, Nell had narrowed the request to a kingdom, a century, and a specific failed military campaign.

Bart entered the details.

Thousands of possible records collapsed to four.

Nell turned the screen toward the patron. “Any of those names familiar?”

The scholar pointed to one immediately.

Bart opened the holding record and advised the patron that he will request retrieval and inform them when ready.

He quietly also made a note on her assessment form.Then he decided to test the hardest part of the job. He deliberately gave her the most difficult scenario the current Archive could produce.

“A patron requests a verified book,” Bart said, outlining the parameters. “The catalogue confirms that the Archive owns it. However, the physical access route to that sector is offline. What do you do?”

Nell crossed her arms, leaning slightly against the counter. “I tell the patron it is currently unavailable.”

“You would not attempt to retrieve it?”

“Absolutely not,” Nell said firmly. “I would tell them it is unavailable, and I would not promise a date when it might be fixed, because I do not have one. Then I would look for alternate editions, related texts, or partner holdings that might serve the same purpose. I would register the patron for a notification if it ever becomes accessible, and I would record the unmet demand. I absolutely do not enter an uncertified sector.”

She gestured toward the massive stone pillars. “I am a librarian. I do not repair corridors.”

From somewhere high up on a nearby scaffolding, Ada’s voice drifted down.

“Good, someone with common sense”

Nell blinked, looking up at the ceiling, then looked back at Bart. She leaned slightly closer to the counter.

“How much of your overall collection is currently inaccessible?” Nell asked.

Bart couldn't give her a reliable number. Nobody had yet produced a complete, verified access map of the facility.He told her exactly that.

She processed the information playing with her satchel strap.

“So you do not actually know which inaccessible sections are costing you the most service,” she concluded.

Bart looked at her. It was the same principle he had used when drafting the position: restoration priorities should reflect actual unmet demand. Nell had reached it independently after less than an hour inside the Archive.

More importantly, she immediately turned the principle into a working process.

“You should keep a blocked demand register,” Nell suggested, treating the interview as a live operational problem. “Group it by holding, subject, and collection or sector. Track the number of active requests against the availability of viable substitutes.”

Ada descended the scaffolding ladder and approached the desk. “Then I can add structural safety and inspection feasibility to the register.”

Gregor leaned and added. “And I can add retrieval feasibility.”

COLLECTION ACCESS COORDINATION had stopped being a line in a job advertisement. It was becoming an actual service.

Their underlying personality clash, however, appeared almost immediately afterward.

Bart looked down at the interview assessment form she had handed back to him earlier. He noticed she had written extensive notes in the narrow margins, looping her handwriting around the rigid printed boxes.

“There is a designated notes section at the bottom,” Bart pointed out.

“There was not enough room there,” Nell replied.

“There are three pages.”

“There was not enough useful room.”

Bart examined the margins again. The notes were relevant. The handwriting was perfectly legible. Every annotation referred clearly to the appropriate question.

They were also in entirely the wrong places.

Somehow, this made it worse.

Bart set the form down. He asked the most important question.

“Why do you want this position?”

He did not want a heroic speech about the destiny of the Infinite Archive or the preservation of cosmic knowledge.

She didn't give him one. She looked around at the partly repaired desk, the glowing interlibrary queue on the interface, Mabel standing guard at the massive doors, and the faint rattle of an empty carrier moving overhead.

“Your advertisement says you are open,” Nell said simply.

“We are open,” Bart confirmed.

“It also says your services are still being rebuilt.”

“They are as you can clearly see”

She looked at him. “Then somebody should probably work the desk while you find out how much is broken.”

Bart disclosed the funding limitation clearly. He told her directly that her current compensation was guaranteed by their existing reserves, but permanent funding beyond that operational window was not yet secured.

She appreciated the disclosure.

“So the job is real, but permanence isn't,” she summarized.

“That is the situation,” Bart said.

“Good. Your advertisement was at least not lying.”

Bart made the offer. The System generated the provisional employment terms under the Archive's adopted compensation framework.

APPOINTMENT: ASSISTANT LIBRARIAN - PROVISIONAL

COMPENSATION: FUNDED FROM CURRENT OPERATIONAL RESERVE

DUTIES: AS PUBLISHED

ROLE EXPANSION: REQUIRES CONSENT

Nell read the document rather than merely scrolling to the bottom.

Bart approved of that.

“These are the actual terms?” she asked.

“They are,” Bart said.

Nell signed.

For the first time since Bart had arrived, he was no longer the only professional librarian in the Archive.

The System immediately updated the interface.

INTERLIBRARY / REFERENCE SERVICES

DEDICATED PROFESSIONAL STAFF: 1

Then Nell immediately placed her hands on the counter. “Where is the inaccessible request list?”

“Orientation comes first,” Bart said firmly.

She looked around at the staggering, multi leveled expanse of the Infinite Archive.

“How long is orientation?” she asked.

Gregor did not even look up from his ledger. “Do not ask questions you are not prepared to hear the answer to.”

“Orientation first,” Bart repeated.

She looked at him for a long moment. “You are an orientation first person, aren't you?”

“I am precisely that kind of person,” Bart said.

“I suspected that.”
