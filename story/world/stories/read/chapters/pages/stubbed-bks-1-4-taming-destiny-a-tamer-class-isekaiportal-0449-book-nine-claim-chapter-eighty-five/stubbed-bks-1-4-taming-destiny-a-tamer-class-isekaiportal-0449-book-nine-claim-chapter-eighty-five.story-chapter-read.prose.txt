When I reenter the settlement, my heart heavier in my chest than the bodies of the two samuran hatchlings are in my arms, the first thing I see is Trouble.

Anger rises inside me, hot and powerful.

"You were supposed to be watching them," I accuse, storming over to him. "What happened?"

Trouble flinches and lowers himself further to the ground, his wings spreading wide in the raptorcat gesture of submission. I barely notice, too consumed by grief-driven anger. "Why were they out there? Eight of them – how did you not notice?"

Iandee steps forward and puts his hand on my shoulder.

Markus, he says, gently but firmly. It wasn't his fault.

I realise I'm practically panting, my eyes burning. I straighten and try to take hold of my emotions. As the red recedes from my vision, I see how many people have actually come with Trouble. All the samurans bar Catch, who’s out with Bastet’s group. That includes all the hatchlings, who have crept forward to surround me, the closest ones touching and prodding at the two bodies in my arms, making heart-wrenching little whimpers of distress.

My anger subsides, leaving only the grief it was masking.

I do my best to hold back the tears. It's not only my Bonded who are present – those on the wall are watching, some overtly, others pretending not to. I have an image as the settlement leader to uphold. And even besides the need to reassure them by staying calm and collected, what would it look like if I show such visible grief for baby samurans, and not for the people injured or killed in the battle with the beasts?

I take a slow breath and lay the bodies down, allowing their siblings to come and investigate. The six hatchlings we managed to save hang back, heads low, their spikes flickering in mottled dark green and blue – shame and grief made visible.

"I'm sorry," I tell the adult samurans quietly. "I wasn't able to save them."

"It's okay, Markus," Hunter says softly, though the same colours flicker through her spikes. "We know you did your best.”

“And without you," Happy adds, "half these hatchlings would probably already be dead. It's a miracle that these are the first casualties we've had."

River just steps forward and puts her arms around me.

My eyes fly open at that – hugs are not something samurans do – but she's clearly spent enough time among humans to pick up on some of our habits. After a moment's hesitation, I put my arms around her shoulders and find myself holding on more tightly than I intended. I bury my face in her shoulder briefly, permitting myself to feel what I can't display in public.

Then I pull myself back together and release her. She relaxes her arms and steps back at my silent prompting. Of everything, the hug has helped the most – a wordless expression of faith and sympathy from one who should have been most angry. The Pathwalkers are the ones to have carried and laid the eggs, after all.

I look at Trouble, crouching on the ground looking utterly miserable, and sigh. I move to crouch beside him and run my hand over his head and behind his ears.

"It was wrong of me to accuse you before I knew the whole story," I say quietly. "I'm sorry. Will you tell me what happened?"

He doesn't tell me. But he shows me – a series of images, his memory made visible. First, Trouble, Blaze, and Spot with Iandee in the schoolroom, doing their best to keep the hatchlings occupied despite how increasingly restless they were becoming. A figure arriving with food. Then the awful moment as the parasite seized control, amiable expression twisting into something vicious. The attack on Iandee. Iandee forcing the feral person out of the building – and then them realising some hatchlings had disappeared. The fear when he returned after having dealt with the feral person, no hatchlings with him. The frantic search through the settlement. The discovery, too late, that they had left it entirely and were fighting the beasts outside.

Trouble follows the images with a wave of plaintive apology. I suspect he'd already been drowning in guilt before, but my accusations clearly made things worse.

I dredge up a smile – tremulous as it is – and try to reassure him, as the others had reassured me earlier.

"You did your best. I wish you'd told me sooner, but you did your best." Though, even as I say it, I'm not sure earlier knowledge would have changed anything. Everyone was already either occupied or in lockdown, and we didn't have anyone spare to go scouring the settlement for samuran hatchlings who had chosen the worst possible moment to exercise their rebellious tendencies.

Maybe if I’d had Bonds with them…. Hindsight is always twenty-twenty. I’ve never had Bonds with babies when I can avoid it – I don’t want to influence their development too much. I didn’t consider changing that policy with these ones. Maybe I should have.

At least Trouble looks a little better at my reassurance, his wings lifting slightly from the ground. He perks up further as the adult samurans approach and quietly make it clear they don't hold it against him either.

Hunter comes to stand beside me as I watch the hatchlings surround the two bodies and the six survivors, clearly at a loss about what to do, but grateful that six of them survived what had killed two.

"This might not be entirely a bad thing," Hunter muses quietly.

I look at her incredulously.

"Two of them died."

The ripple of multiple shades through her spikes tells me little, but thoughtfulness comes through the Bond.

"Smith was right when she said that, normally, half the hatchlings would be dead by now. Even in the last hatching, several of the most adventurous ones were killed, if you recall." I nod, remembering those occasions, each one difficult to bear, each one feeling like I’d failed the village. "Perhaps this will help the others be wiser, now that they understand that their recklessness has consequences."

I sigh.

"Perhaps. I wish they didn't have to learn it this way, though."

Hunter hums in quiet acknowledgement. We watch the hatchlings together in silence for a moment.

"Markus."

I turn to find Valence himself coming to stand beside me. He glances briefly at the samurans, then gives me his attention. "I'm sorry to interrupt, but the beast wave my scouts warned us about is now visible. Two Tier Threes are confirmed. We'll need your support."

His tone is neutral. I'm not certain whether the faint note of chiding I hear beneath it is real or just my own guilt speaking. Either way, he's right. I can't spend time mourning hatchlings who put themselves in danger. Not in the middle of this, at least. That will have to come later, when the crises are over.

I send a flicker of apology to the adult samurans. They reply with the emotional equivalent of shooing me away – they know the situation as well as I do.

"Right," I say, nodding firmly at Valence and pushing my emotions aside. "Let's go."

Valence leads the way back up to the wall and points toward the advancing line of beasts.

"Those two have been confirmed as Tier Threes by the scouts," he says, indicating one that looks rather like a large, dark brown lion and another that resembles a mostly-brown giraffe with a significantly shorter neck. Not the kind of creature I would have expected to reach Tier Three – predators have an obvious advantage there, and this one doesn't look like a carnivore.

"So far we've been letting the beasts come to us, using the wall's defensive enchantments to whittle down numbers," Valence continues. "Would you prefer to maintain that strategy, or change it?"

I consider it quickly – the wave is closing fast.

"There are enough of them that we should try to take some out before they hit. You were warning us earlier about the wall's integrity and I don’t think we should test it on this lot."

"Then what's your strategy, Markus?"

"The Earth Mages have joined us?" I check.

Valence nods.

"They were effective in the end of the last battle – they destabilised the ground and made the beasts easier targets for our archers and generalist mages."

"Good. Then here's what I think we should…do…." I trail off as I see a patch of sky acting very unnaturally.

It’s been cloudy all day, but one patch of clouds suddenly darkens drastically – a patch that is directly over the rapidly-approaching mass of beasts.

Valence and I exchange glances as torrential rain falls in a tight column onto the advancing beast wave and almost nowhere else. Then, as the downpour fades after only a few moments, vines of ice stretch out through the sodden beasts, gaining easy purchase on the quickly-frozen water.

The beast wave grinds to a halt. Even the Tier threes are slowed, though I can see their greater strength is already ripping through the ice. Real vines join the mix, creeping over the frozen beasts to target the Tier threes and wrap them up in cocoons.

"It looks like Josiah and Elarion found each other after all," Layton remarks, stepping up beside us.

I glance at him questioningly.

“Did I miss something?”

He gives me an enigmatic smile.

"Nothing the rest of us didn't also miss. Though, either their timing is excellent, or they have been communicating."

Then I realise we're standing here watching when we should be moving. I look to either side at the two other lords and give them a small smile, the scent of victory prodding my tired sense of enthusiasm to resurface once more. Two of the hatchlings might have died, but that doesn’t mean anyone else has to. And with the beasts stuck in the ice, they’re easy targets – for now.

"What are we waiting for? Valence – lead a charge, would you? Deal with those beasts while they're slowed. Layton – Ivor can carry you closer, and you can help keep the ice in place and drive the temperature down. Make them as uncomfortable as possible. Kill some of them if you can"

"And you?" Layton asks, one eyebrow raised, even as Valence nods and strides away to organise the warriors.

I grin at him.

"I'll be working with the Earth Mages."

The battle is barely even worthy of the name in the end. Even though it’s the biggest force so far, and has twice as many Tier threes as the previous wave, Josiah’s and Elarion’s opening strikes put the beasts on the back foot.

The weakest beasts succumb to the ice – especially when Layton gets in and drives the temperatures well below zero. The Water and Plant Mages keep the Tier threes so bundled up that they’re unable to help in any way. And the Earth Mages and I create spikes from the ground, directed with almost surgical precision, taking out the attempted attackers one by one.

I do get a little distracted when a very distinctive owl-bear lumbers across my vision, vines sprouting from everywhere he walks to entangle the beasts further, wrapping around necks and piercing chests.

“Artio?” I find myself whispering. He turns his head briefly towards me, then looks away again to concentrate on his task.

I shake myself – he has the right idea. I can catch up with him afterwards – find out exactly what happened and how he woke up from a coma that I feared he might never escape.

Finally, the only two living beasts are the two Tier threes.

“Hold!” I call, my wings lifting me up in the air. A fireball lands on the side of the lion-like creature and I turn to glare at the mage who had released it. She looks sheepish.

“For what, Markus?” Valence asks, looking disappointed as he levels his enchanted sword at the herbivore-like beast.

“He’s going to Bind them,” Elarion answers, his gaze cool as he steps out from within the group of mages and farmers who had been sheltering behind a wall of ice. “Aren’t you?” he asks, looking directly at me.

I shrug.

“We need information,” I reply more nonchalantly than I feel – tied up or not, they are Tier threes. It would be embarrassing to fail in public. But I steel myself for the Battle. No point in putting this off.
