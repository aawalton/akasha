Having woken up past noon and with nothing besides work on his domain scheduled for the day, Gio sat at the foot of his bed and stared down at the key to his future.  Removed from the Banshee’s Shroud-silk bag, the greater sigil became a fountain of water and mirror mana, lighting up his room in a shimmering cascade of blue and silver.

I have just over two weeks until the mid-term examination. Fifteen days. It’s going to be… a close finish.

The timeline hadn’t been perfectly planned, but Gio was going to forgive himself for that, on account of just how many factors had to perfectly align to get him to this point.

​His misadventure in the ‘Stacks’ under the ill-advised direction of the Owl had earned him books that he had read to develop the first seed of his talent for creating spells. From these books, he had taught himself how to interpret spell blueprints and could identify some common components of modern spellforms on sight.

​After defeating the dragon and being shattered, Saint Baphelus had accepted Gio’s request for tutelage, taking that seed and further nurturing it with a custom, mirror-focused training plan. Gio still hadn’t mastered all of the spells that Baphelus had him learn how to scribe, which was a testament to how thorough his mentor had been, and how busy Gio had kept since then. Somewhere in the near future, he promised himself that he would finally sit down to figure out the [Scrying Glass] spell that had sat unused in his spellbook for all this time.

​And then, Gio had quickly improved. He used his tenacity and the unique advantage of having a sentient reflection to double his research speed, which, when coupled with the revolutionary ideas present in the framework of Angelica Hart’s teleportation spell, had let Gio craft his first totally unique spell, [Mirrorwalker]. This had earned him recognition from the spirits in the form of his spell-crafting skill improving.

​And now, Gio faced his greatest challenge yet. He had, with the help of his friends, successfully retrieved a unique core sigil, and it was finally time for him to begin work on his domain- a domain that would be fully unique to him.

​While someone else might be daunted by the thought of having to conceptualize, draft, finalize, and then learn how to cast a domain spell in such a short period of time, Gio felt liberated.

​After all, he was back in his element.

​Now that he had all (or at least most) of the resources he needed to make his domain, the hard part was over. All that he had left to do was a few rounds of grueling research and laborious testing within a condensed timeframe… while still attending classes.

​Professor Anaximenes was being merciful and had assured the class that the weeks preceding the domain test would be mainly focused on allowing the student body to prepare for their first major milestone (AKA no assignments). This did not, however, exempt Gio from needing to show up to class.

​But that was a ‘tomorrow’ problem. Gio still had the last day of his weekend to recover from his trip to the dungeon while breaking ground on his domain.

​And so, he began his little ceremonies. He sorted and put away most (but not all) of the materials he had gathered while he was working on his [Mirrorwalker] spell, and laid out fresh inkwells and clean parchment on his ‘research station’. Gio eagerly set about the task of consolidating all of the relevant data and notes that he already had for the domain, placing them artfully around the core sigil, which sat propped up on a pedestal that befitted its station as the core of this project, a throne comprised of a re-purposed desk picture frame that would keep it safe from unintentional spills.

​With his academic altar prepared, Gio leisurely got dressed so that he could complete the last task before getting down to business- the task of filling his much-beloved [Spatial Bento] with a backup meal.

​“What do you think would be the best thing to ask Marie for? I’m thinking something with a low risk of making a mess, maybe a sandwich. Thoughts?” Gio asked.

​He found himself staring at his inert reflection in the scrying plane.

​“Oh, right.”

_____

​[Welcome to: The House of Torches. Exhibition Matches are held multiple times a day. Please see a Fighting Club Representative to sign up.]

​Gio speed-walked through soot-stained pathways, darting between stone buildings to avoid knocking into students and faculty alike. The ever-present smoke haze of the House of Torches mixed with the scent of sweat and heavily used athletic equipment to create a disharmonious, inescapable wall of odor that suffused seemingly the whole area, a feat in and of itself.

​Please, let the room be empty.

​As he looked up to the sky, and beheld a vaulting cave ceiling interspaced with large hanging braziers hundreds of feet in the air, Gio marveled, not for the first time, at just how big the school was. The House of Torches was just one area within the Crystal Ring Academy, and it was like a small underground city. It was dappled with arenas, coliseums, and training areas of all shapes and sizes, interwoven with the odd student-run business or watering hole. Gio even passed by a ‘seedy’ bar that Marie had fashioned out of the ruins of what appeared to be an old excavation machine. Just by walking past it, Gio caught a whiff of an unpleasant, crude-oil-esque smell.

​The combat-focused area was abuzz with activity on weekends, but had been especially busy with first-year students trying to make use of the testing arenas to practice their burgeoning domains.

Gio ducked down a back alley path that Jean had shown him months ago. It was an unassuming cobblestone corridor between squat buildings, the sounds of students utilizing nearby training rooms audible nearby. In between two anemic torches that failed to properly light the hall, a haggard wooden door bore a metal chain attached to a sign that read:

"OUT OF ORDER.SOMEONE BROKE MIRROR…The story has been taken without consent; if you see it on Amazon, report the incident.PROLLY RESPAWN IN 2 MONTHS?COME SEE FRONT DESK IF U NEED A CLONE 4 TRNG. -PROF. OLIGANTE."

A professor wrote this? I suppose the more… combat-oriented staff members aren’t necessarily known for their eloquence.

​Gio went to move the chain aside, sighing as he noticed a clearly magical lock that secured it to the door.

Resisting the urge to sigh, Gio instead searched for a way around the obstacle.

I wonder…

He conjured two thin sheets of mirror under his feet, kicking one under the door, and using the other to smoothly dive through the Between with [Mirror Jaunt]. He appeared in front of the magic mirror without a hitch.

If only everything could be so easy. Am I… turning into a breaking and entering specialist? Surely not, right?

Gio turned to face himself in the mysterious mirror, noting the scar he had left on it in the middle that had already healed a bit since the last time he had visited. As Gio looked himself in the eyes, he felt… a knot of energies. It felt almost as if there were several competing keys, all aiming to slot themselves into an overcrowded lock.

We still don’t really understand what is going on with this. Another thing that I would love to return to at some point, when I get a chance to learn enchanting.

Rio stretched and smiled.

“Sorry for the wait.” Gio signed, frowning slightly. “I hope it wasn’t uncomfortable.”

His reflection rolled his eyes at him, using sign language to convey a sense of incredulousness to the best of his abilities. “It’s not like that, I told you. It’s kind of like… a dream. Without the mirror, I’m just… you. If anything, it was kind of relaxing to not be the Aspect of Chaos for a while.”

“I can’t really wrap my head around that,” Gio replied. “How could having your agency taken from you feel like anything except imprisonment?”

Rio shrugged. “I don’t know how to explain it to you. You saw my memories when we fused, right? Did you see anything that felt like what you’re thinking of?”

Gio paused. “Well… no.”

“Then… what’s the issue? I think this is one of those moments where our innate sense of compassion is not doing you any favors. I’m telling you that it’s not a problem. You know what is a problem? That you skipped breakfast before coming here, so now we’re both hungry.”

_____

Gio and his reflection sat on opposite sides (and technically opposite dimensions) of a table in his dorm that he had propped up against a full-length mirror. They each ate their breakfast burrito while skimming over their materials as they formulated a plan.

“The first thing I’m going to do is go check on Trio. I’m sure that he’s going to want in on this, and we owe him some furniture.” Rio signed.

“That’s a good idea… but the problem is that I don’t have any money, and I’d rather not spend any more academic points on furniture. I don’t know what I might need them for in the future.” Gio said as he wiped burrito from his fingers, the sign-language equivalent of talking with your mouth full.

“That’s why I’m going to steal reflections of stuff from the stacks,” Rio said.

Gio raised an eyebrow. “Is that… viable? Don’t they fade over time?”

Rio polished off the last of his iced coffee before responding. “Well, for starters, I promised to be the personal courier for our third copy for the time being, and he’s probably sitting on the wet ground right now, plotting on how to make my life miserable. I’ll get him as many recliners and chaise lounges as I can get my hands on, if necessary. Secondly… I’m kind of hoping that he can learn how to use his new dungeon powers, whatever those may be, to learn how to fix stuff like this… but like with everything else we have going on, that is going to take time.”

“Ugh. So many things to work on.” Gio said, massaging his temples.

“Nah.” Rio replied. “For now, we’re just going to work on one thing only. Just get started on the domain… Trio and I will-”

Rio jumped, looking like he’d seen a ghost.

“What’s wrong?” Gio asked, struggling to understand what his reflection was seeing.

“Can you pop over to my side for a sec? You need to see this.”

“Ooookay…” Gio said, preparing his spellbook. Rio had already marked the targeting circle on the floor, so all that needed to be done was cast the spell.

Walking through the mirror, Gio smoothly cast [Mirrorwalker] as he transferred into the Between, gliding across the different phases of the mirror dimension until he reached the reflection of his dorm room.

He was greeted by the sight of Rio looking into… a hole. A shimmering hole mid-air that leaked light and mana into the reflection of his bedroom.

“Hey there,” Trio said, adjusting something on the other side of the hole.

“Trio? What… how are you doing this?!” Gio demanded.

“So, fun fact, I think some mirror spells do weird stuff when you use them inside the mirror dimension. This was supposed to be [Scrying Glass].” Trio replied.

Gio stood statue-still, trying and failing to process what he was seeing and hearing.

The hole in the mirror dimension had feathered edges, thin like melted ice and refracting light in an odd way. Gio looked out into the sanctuary of Telchine’s garden, marveling at the gentle flow of high-density water and mirror magic that was cascading through the hole.

“I… don’t know what to say. Nice to see you, I guess?” Gio said, baffled.

“Yeah… I wasn’t expecting this either. It seems to work just fine on the real world, but the second I used the spell on Rio… this happened. Anyways… have you guys gotten started on the domain yet? How can I help?” Trio cheerily asked.

“Oh. uh… we were actually just going to come check on you, and bring you some food, maybe a table and some chairs.” Rio answered.

“Oooh, that would be nice. Can you hand me those notes?” Trio asked.

Gio limply handed Rio’s copy of their notes to his third reflection. His mind raced as he tried to factor in this change to his plans.

Hold on…

“This… doesn’t this… triple our research speed?” Gio mumbled.

“I mean, yeah,” Rio replied. “I figured that I’d have to ferry notes back and forth between the garden and our room… but this works, too. Saves me the trip through the Between, which is nice.”

Rio summoned a plane of mirror, angling it at the table and chairs. He then split himself into multiple copies, which used [Mirror Jaunt] to travel through the fractal reflection. His copies emerged from the split image, carrying another perfect copy of Gio’s table.

“Can you make that portal bigger?” a Rio copy asked.

“I’ve been trying. I’m not sure.” Trio answered.

“Hold on. Prepare to have to re-make the connection if this doesn’t work.” Rio said.

His reflection tugged at the edge of the hole, and a cracking sound rang out as the edges widened. Rio’s hand came away mangled and full of glassy shards, which he laughed off without a care, his unique constitution already healing the upsetting wound.

Rio’s fragments walked the tables through the portal, setting up Trio’s copy of the workstation as one of them updated Trio on their plans. One of the Rio clones left, returning with another burrito for their newest research fellow.

“This is getting very strange, very quickly,” Gio said.
