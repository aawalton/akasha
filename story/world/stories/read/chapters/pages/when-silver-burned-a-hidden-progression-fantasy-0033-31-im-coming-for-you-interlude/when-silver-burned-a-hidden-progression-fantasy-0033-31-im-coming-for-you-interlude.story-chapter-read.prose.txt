A second was an eternity in her mind. For but a fleeting moment, thoughts swirled within the confines of her head, looking for routes and connecting pathways quicker than the conscious could follow.

Half a second: the map of the Eastern Expanse spread out before her. From her vantage point up in the skyline of aVonal, she could see a row of endless forts that marked the Inner Line of Defense. Further out, the red tundra got more and more desolate as the Old appeared with a barely perceivable line in the far-away mud. With a mental command, she dismissed the jagged border lines and the bold letters that marked the landmarks with their names on the map.

One second: She sailed forward, letting the Inner Line approach and then pass below her. The Old zoomed in, giving her a better look at the little earthen wall and the details the map-makers had deemed worthy to include. The clay huts where the soldiers slept. The lookout towers above the red grass, and a few of the faded lines that the cartographers had failed to fully erase from the model as the frontline shifted over the decades.

One and a half second: She looked north first, toward the Olna. The lake was large enough to split apart both the Old and the Inner Line of Defense—but several forts encircled it around the shores, too many to make landfall a realistic scenario. It might be done, with few people and during the night. Or perhaps during Blindlight.

A bit closer and toward the south, the Maroon Gulf broke into the borderzone of the continent, flowing out into the ocean of Kel Waters. A large, many-armed depiction of a monster’s head peeked out from the middle of the inked waves. The Leviathan.

Nobody guarded that place, for obvious reasons. But maybe, she thought. Maybe a man and a woman could cross unnoticed on a slim little barge.

Two seconds: She flew out above the ocean, rapidly approaching the Republic’s southernmost region, Seven Fallter. She summoned back the letters and numbers with a wave of her hand, and began storing the calculations and coordinates that a captain might be able to make sense of. Storm patterns and mythos nests. Rocky islands for a fleet to shake off a pursuer.

She eyed the settlements along the shore, smaller villages that might’ve been part of the most civilized region of the Republic, but civility always came with a dulling in the claws. Nobody would expect trouble around these parts. Whereas going up north, toward the Olna—well, nothing but the coldest extension of Újtavak resided there.

Two and a half seconds, and she began to feel the strain of her still-fresh surge.

A plan—that’s what she was building.

Jack opened her eyes with a silent gasp, her root’s surge running out. The grass didn't help much to soften the ground underneath her where she knelt, steel greaves digging in. She glanced to the side where her tent lay in a coiled bundle. She hadn’t bothered to set it up.

“Your unit will be setting up that-a way, miss.”

It was the head assistant of the nails’ division who spoke, having approached the little circle of solitude Jack had retreated to. Only a few recruits watched from the sidelines of the camp, their hands raised to shade their eyes from the glaring sun.

Jack rose, casually towering over the man even as she began to dust off her knees. She squinted at the sun, debating whether to try and squeeze in some more planning an hour from now.

“There,” the man cleared his throat, pointing back toward the camp, at the next section closer to the front. She was about to be transferred from the nail section forward to the roots. “The flag with the bent oak?”

Jack absently nodded, still not looking at him. She hadn’t bothered to learn his name. Or the name of any of her fellow nails.

Grabbing her bundle from the ground, she began stomping her way toward the area designated for roots. The rattle of her armor drowned out whatever the nail penpusher said to her back.

Even from a distance, she could see there were fewer people loitering about in this section of the Clenn division. That was good—less people meant less obstacles in her way to fend off. Though unbothered for the most part, some figures had needed a fist in their face to realize she wasn’t interested in anything but advancement.

That wasn’t too rare, among the Clenn. Some people just pushed day and night, striving to go farther and harder than anyone else. She was glad to play one of the stereotypes.

Not two steps inside the perimeter outlined with purple stakes, a gruff woman stepped up to her. “‘Scuse for a moment.” She shook out a document, finger trailing down the list of names. “Formerly Nail Janette, now advanced? Please sign here.”

The process that followed was brief, yet still longer than Jack would have liked. They asked questions, and she nodded and signed where necessary. This whole affair of soldiering was a lot more lax than she had expected. Nobody, not even their lieutenants ever did so much as raise their voice. The Clenn treated this whole affair more like a vacation, a spectacle for the elite. Practice drills were optional, and the average Clenn recruit used the mandatory surge training to simply show off. No obedience and no sense of duty was forged here.

“Wait here for a spell, please.” The woman scratched something out, then quickly disappeared through the flaps of her tent.

Standing there beneath the glaring sun and surrounded by people she didn’t care for, Jack closed her eyes, and willed her mind to surge.

The construction of the blocks resumed, outlines becoming more structured, future possibilities steadily arranged on the board. Many pieces would be knocked down, and many more end up with fates unknown.

She gave them no thought. Only two pieces mattered.

Hold on, Kal. I’m coming for you.
