Chapter 84 - Aric's Prediction

The hallway outside the dorms still hummed faintly with talk of the ending to the colosseum’s chaos. Distant voices echoing through the stone walls accompanied by hurried footsteps from students returning to their rooms. Zephyr barely registered any of it. His strides were quick, almost frantic, boots striking the marble floor with sharp, impatient clicks.

Lena’s dorm was on the third floor, she had a corner room, her window facing the gardens. She was happy that she’d been given such a nice room, said the mornings made her plants “happy.” Normally, that thought would’ve softened him. But right now it only made his worry more intense.

He reached her door and didn’t bother smoothing down his hair or catching his breath. He knocked three times, and waited for an answer.

“Lena.”

His voice echoed down the hall.

Nothing.

He knocked again, louder this time.

“Lena, open up. It’s me.”

Still nothing.

Zephyr tilted his head and placed his ear to the door, listening. He expected the soft scuffle of slippers on wood, the sound of her stumbling out of bed or shuffling books aside. Anything.

But the room behind the door was silent.

A cold pressure crept up the back of his neck.

He placed his palm flat against the wood. “Lena, seriously. This isn’t funny. Open the door.”

He was answered with silence.

His jaw tightened.

“Fine.” He stepped back. “You asked for it.”

One sharp kick slammed against the lock, not enough to break it, but almost. The frame rattled. Another kick, and the metal latch gave way with a crack.

He pushed the door open.

The room was dim, curtains still drawn, only a thin band of sun sneaking through the slit between them. Dust drifted lazily in that faint beam of light. The air smelled faintly of lavender, parchment, and… salt?

Something was wrong.

Zephyr froze on the threshold, chest tight.

Her bed was untouched.

Blankets still tucked neatly.

Pillow perfectly centred.

But her desk… was a disaster.

Workbooks lay sprawled in every direction, open and overlapping, pages bent as if they’d been turned too quickly. Notes were scattered across the surface, some fluttering in the breeze from the cracked window. Pens lay uncapped. Her mug of herbal tea had spilled, staining a stack of assignments still halfway complete.

Zephyr’s stomach dropped.

Lena was naturally a very tidy person, she never left anything out of place, her organisation bordered on obsessive. Even then, usually this wouldn’t be enough to cause Zephyr to worry. But after the events at the colosseum combined with Aric’s constant reminders about Lena’s future, he couldn’t help but panic.

The only explanation he could muster was that Kaelin came, and they’d left in a hurry.

He stepped farther in, slowly, as though he expected her to be hiding behind the wardrobe or crouched under her desk, or more that he hoped. But the room was completely empty.

Zephyr reached out and touched the edge of one of her notebooks. It was open to a page of sketches, diagrams and drawings of interesting plants, ideas for new spells, and random notes in her neat handwriting.

His hand hovered there, fingertips barely grazing the ink.

His eyes moved across the mess: the torn scrap of parchment on the floor, the ink bottle knocked on its side—luckily with the cap still on, the drawer left half-open, the chair pushed aside so that it was facing the door.

A tight, sick dread coiled in Zephyr’s chest.

He turned once more to scan the room carefully—trying to piece together the last moments before she disappeared. Her cloak was gone. Her boots too. But everything else, everything she usually had with her had remained.

Zephyr’s hands balled into fists.

“Kaelin…” he whispered under his breath. “What have you done…”

He stepped back out into the hallway, leaving the door half-hanging off its frame, eyes dark with a determination he hadn’t ever felt before.

Kaelin was leading Lena into a deathtrap, and Zephyr wasn’t going to sit back and watch while she does.

***

As Aria led them down the sloping passageway, the air grew heavier. It’s was stuffy and felt dirty just to breathe in. Dust fluttered around in the dim light from the few torches which were present.

Their footsteps echoed unnaturally, bouncing back toward them as if they were completely surrounded at all times.

Aria walked in the front, she walked with purpose, careful, but not so much that she seemed scared. Her braid swung sharply with each step. She did not look back, not even once. Kaelin recognised this version of Aria, she wasn’t being irrational, she looked prepared to fight, despite how weak she’d always been.

Kaelin wasn’t ready.

Or at least, she feared she wasn’t.

Her heart hammered in short bursts, each beat pulsing at her temples. Her breath came unevenly, though she tried to steady it. The tunnels felt claustrophobic, their walls too close, their shadows too deep. She had felt confined before. While she’d been inside Ari— Ezra’s body, she was physically petrified. At least this time she had some control over her movements.

But somewhere ahead, Kana was alone.

Kaelin swallowed hard, forcing her legs to keep pace. She could not fall behind. She wouldn’t.

Lena walked behind her, the sole voice daring to break the suffocating silence.

“So,” Lena whispered, her tone light, “remind me again why I let you drag me all the way down here?”

Kaelin exhaled shakily, a ghost of a smile tugging at her lips despite everything. She glanced back at her friend.

Lena’s eyes were already on her, bright despite the dimness, her expression half-amused, half-nervous.

“Seriously though,” Lena went on, lowering her voice as though someone might hear her, “You couldn’t have stopped for five minutes to explain? All I know is that we’re here for Kana, but what actually happened?”

Aria didn’t respond, nor did her pace waver. To be honest, it wasn’t clear if she’d even heard what Lena had to say.

Lena continued, “I know it’s important and all, but my life is waaay more important, I know she could die, but so could I!”

Kaelin shook her head. “You didn’t have to come. I shouldn’t have—”

“Kaelin,” Lena interrupted, tapping her lightly on the shoulder, “I’m sorry, it was meant to be a joke, it must have come out wrong, I’m just… a bit stressed is all.”

Kaelin’s chest tightened at that, the thought that she’d be willing to risk her life, Lena is putting all her trust in Kaelin, and ultimately, she might actually die. That wasn’t some baseless fear.

The tunnels branched again. Aria paused only long enough to glance at two forked corridors, then veered right. Her voice drifted back, clipped and firm:

“Stick close, it’s easy to get lost down here.”

Kaelin’s stomach clenched tighter. Each turn looked identical to her: the same coarse stone, faint water stains along the ceiling, small alcoves filled with nothing but dust. The place was a maze. Without Aria’s lead, Kaelin doubted she could retrace even two corridors back.

And Kana was down here somewhere. Alone. Or not alone. Kaelin wasn’t sure which possibility frightened her more.

After minutes that felt like hours, Lena broke the silence again, but this time she sounded serious.

“What exactly are we expecting to find?”

Kaelin inhaled slowly, choosing her words. “I’m… not sure.”

Lena raised an eyebrow. “That’s not comforting.”

“She’s in danger,” Kaelin said, voice trembling with urgency. “And I know Aric is involved. I just—” The words tangled in her throat. Fear and certainty warred inside her, neither fully winning. “I just need to get to her before something happens.”

Aria glanced back briefly, her expression unreadable. “Whatever we find,” she said, “we need to stay focused. Don’t split up, and keep your eyes peeled.”

Lena nodded. “Trust me, I am gluing myself to you two. Whatever’s down here can have the rest of the academy, but I won’t let it get to me.”

Kaelin forced a steady breath. She needed to be strong. She needed to be calm. She needed to put all their cards on the table.

“Worst case, I have a trump card.”

Both Aria and Lena stopped. Turned. Looked right at her.

The tone in the group quickly shifted into strategy mode.

“A trump card?” Aria repeated, tone low. “Explain.”

Lena frowned. “Kaelin, what are you planning to do?”

Kaelin held their gazes for only a heartbeat before looking away. The lamps flickered above her, shadows dancing across her hands.

“It has always been too dangerous to attempt before, but I’ll just have to commit if things get bad.” she said quietly.

“Define ‘bad’ for me,” Lena pressed.

Kaelin’s throat tightened. The truth clawed at her chest, wanting to be spoken but unable to escape.

Instead, she forced a fragile smile. “Just trust me. I won’t let anything happen to either of you.”

Aria watched her, studying her expression as though searching for fractures. Her lips turned upwards slightly, a smirk forming on her face.

Then she turned and kept walking.

Lena lingered a second longer, uncertainty and concern etched into her face. But she followed too.

Kaelin trailed them, falling a step behind. Her thoughts swirled like storm winds, harsh and relentless.

I’ll only use it as a last resort. If someone’s about to die, or there’s nothing else left up my sleeve.I just… I can’t let them down here, not now. Anything but that.

Ahead of them, the tunnel darkened, the path narrowing into a jagged, twisting throat of stone.

And they walked on.

The tunnel widened gradually, the ceiling lifting until Kaelin no longer felt the need to hunch her shoulders. The air changed too, it felt less stagnant likely due to the faint breeze which wafted past them. Their footsteps echoed farther now, each sound bouncing ahead and returning warped and hollow.

Aria slowed first.

Her hand lifted slightly, signalling caution, and the three of them eased forward until the corridor simply… ended.

They stepped out into a vast chamber, and Kaelin stopped dead.

The room was enormous — a hollowed-out cavern carved into smooth planes rather than the natural, jagged stone which they had seen in the tunnels before. The ceiling arched high overhead, disappearing into shadow where faint crystals embedded in the rock gave off a dull, grey-blue glow. The floor stretched wide and empty, broken only by the various cracks which seemed to have formed over time from both natural wear and earthquakes.

Three other entrances branched from the chamber — one directly opposite them and two more set perpendicular, running off into the left and right walls. Each corridor was wide enough for several people to walk abreast, yet they seemed rarely used.

There was no furniture, no markings on the ground, just empty space.

Lena stepped forward first, boots clicking lightly against the stone. She turned slowly, eyes scanning the empty expanse.

“Okay,” she said, voice echoing far louder than intended, “this has got to be either a really strange training room, or the creepiest meeting hall ever.”

Her attempt at levity bounced off the walls and returned to them thinner, more hollow.

Aria moved ahead cautiously, her gaze flicking from entrance to entrance.

Something about the room felt wrong — not dangerous in an obvious way, but as if it was designed for a purpose, one they couldn’t figure out. Like a stage set for characters in a play.

“What do you think this place is for?” Lena asked quietly now, her earlier humour fading.

Kaelin shook her head. “I don’t—”

A soft scrape echoed from the entrance to their right.

All three of them spun toward it.

A figure stepped into the light.

Aric.

He emerged casually, one hand tucked behind his back as though he were strolling into a lecture hall rather than a hidden chamber beneath the academy. His coat brushed against the stone as he walked, the faint glow from the ceiling catching in his eyes, making them seem almost ethereal.

He stopped several paces away, smiling faintly.

“Well,” he said lightly, voice carrying effortlessly across the cavern, “you got here faster than I expected.”

Kaelin felt her pulse spike, every muscle tightening.

Aria shifted into a defensive stance immediately. Lena’s shoulders squared, her hands already flexing as she prepared to use her magic.

Aric tilted his head, studying them like a scientist observing ants in a terrarium

“I have to admit,” he continued conversationally, “I expected more resistance along the way. But everything has fallen into place so beautifully.” His smile widened slightly. “It’s almost disappointing how easy it was.”

Lena’s jaw tightened. “You’re delusional if you think—”

“Oh, Lena,” Aric interrupted gently. “How you’ve changed! Straight to confrontation this time, I remember when you were oh so passive, wouldn’t even hurt a fly. Look what she’s made you into.”

His gaze slid past her and settled on Kaelin.

“You, however,” he said softly, “are why we’re here.”

Kaelin felt his words press against her, sharp, like a knife.

“You don’t have to do this,” he went on. “None of you do. Especially not you.” His eyes softened, almost sympathetic. “You can save me. You can save all of them too. With your power… we don’t have to fight at all.”

A sharp laugh broke the tension.

Lena stepped forward, shaking her head. “Yeah, that’s not happening.”

She slammed her foot down.

The ground trembled.

Stone surged upward in a violent ripple, forming a massive wall between them and Aric. It rose in a single sweeping motion, lifting the three girls with it until they stood atop a broad ridge several metres above the chamber floor. Jagged edges formed along the sides, creating natural cover, while layers of compacted earth reinforced the structure from within.

Dust drifted slowly through the air as the wall settled into place.

From their new vantage point, Kaelin could see the entire chamber — all four entrances, Aric below them, the empty expanse now feeling more like a battlefield than before.

Lena planted her feet firmly at the front edge of the wall, arms spread slightly as she reinforced the structure with additional layers of stone and hardened soil.

“There,” she muttered. “High ground. Defensive position. Let’s see him talk his way through that.”

Below, Aric exhaled slowly.

For a moment, he said nothing. He simply looked up at them, eyes narrowing slightly as he assessed the barrier.

Then a faint, scowl made its place on his face.

“I knew you’d be troublesome,” he muttered under his breath.

Aria shifted closer to Kaelin, her voice low and steady. “We hold position. Don’t let him dictate the pace.”

Kaelin nodded, though her eyes remained fixed on Aric.

His earlier calm had not wavered. If anything, the wall seemed to have confirmed something for him — as though every move they made was another piece sliding perfectly into place.

The chamber fell into a tense silence.

No one moved.

Aric’s gaze flicked between them once more before settling back on Kaelin.

“Last chance,” he said quietly.

Kaelin didn’t answer.
