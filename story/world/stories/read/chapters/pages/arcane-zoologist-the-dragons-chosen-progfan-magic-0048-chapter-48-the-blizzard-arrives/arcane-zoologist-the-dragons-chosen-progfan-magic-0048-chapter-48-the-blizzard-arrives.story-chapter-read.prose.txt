"Brother…" Callie whispered, fearing that Callen might get caught in the snowstorm on his way home.

"Don't just gape like a fish, carrothead! We need to warn everyone," Tristan pulled on her arm.

Callie numbly allowed the boy to pull her away. But as they ran from house to house, warning the villagers of an impending snowstorm, Callie realized that no matter how much she worried for her brother, there was nothing she could do for him.

The best thing she could do was warn the adults, who actually might be able to do something.

"I'm going to warn Gerda's family. Tell the chief, okay?" Tristan said and disappeared into the house before Callie could object.

She knew that the boy just wanted to avoid talking to the chief; he left the most unpleasant task to her. Had he done some mischief again? It didn't matter at the moment. Callie decisively knocked on the door of the chief's house.

After a moment, his wife opened the door. When she saw that it was Callie standing there, she frowned. "What is it?"

Callie felt uncomfortable under her gaze, but forced herself to speak. "A snowstorm is coming. I came to warn you and the chief."

The woman peered outside, looking at the sky. A thick layer of grey clouds covered it, as it mostly did during winter. However, there were no signs of snow just yet, much less a snowstorm.

"It doesn't look like it," the chief's wife said skeptically.

"What's the matter?" The chief himself called from deeper within the house. "Somebody asking for me?"

"Calluna here is playing a prank, claiming that a snowstorm is coming," the chief's wife said, narrowing her eyes at Callie.

"I-It's not a prank!" Callie insisted.

The chief came to the door, his eyebrows furrowed. "Come inside and explain exactly what makes you believe that a snowstorm is coming."

Callie followed the chief, feeling that she was going inside somewhere she shouldn't. Aurelia also appeared from upstairs, likely to check who had come. When she saw it was Callie, her expression twisted into one Callie didn't quite understand. Disgust?Fear? She wasn't sure.

Nonetheless, Aurelia went to her mother, while the chief urged Callie into his office. She sat meekly at the table. It felt like she was being scrutinized again, but to Callie's surprise, the chief offered Callie a handful of raisins.

"I-Is it alright for me to eat these?" Callie gasped in surprise. Grapes were hard to grow in these parts. Most of them were made into spirits, with only a small portion being made into raisins. It was a treat, almost of the same value as sugar, because of how delightfully sweet they were.

"That's why I am giving them to you. So tell me about the snowstorm. Don't hide anything, okay?"

Callie nodded, putting one of the raisins in her mouth. So sweet! So tasty!

She told the chief that she noticed the frost caterpillars crawling to the ground and breaking out of the cocoon to get out of high places. She did hesitate to reveal that these little bugs were actually arcane beasts, but ultimately decided to admit this truth, alongside Granny Ethel's conviction that their behaviour meant an impending snowstorm.

"I never would have thought that those little bugs are arcane beasts… Are you sure about that?" Chief Aemon asked.

"My mark has never reacted to regular animals or bugs before," Callie said.

The chief nodded. "Very well. Thank you for the warning. Don't yet tell everyone that frost caterpillars are arcane beasts. Take the raisins with you. Think of it as a payment of sorts."

Callie left happily, clutching at the tasty treats. Once she left the chief's house, Tristan was instantly on her, pestering her about the raisins. She didn't give him any. After all, he had cowardly left her behind.

But the boy didn't relent, so in the end, she shared two with him and brought the rest home to share with her family. Obviously, she also warned Mom and Dad of the incoming snowstorm.

Mom held the raisins thoughtfully in her hands. "A snowstorm… What if Callen gets caught in it?"

"That's what I'm worried about!" Callie said.

Dad looked deep in thought as well. After a minute of deep silence, he said. "I'll go with Gale. Maybe Silas's wagon is stuck and needs to be dug out."

Mom opened her mouth. It looked very much like she wanted to go with Dad, too. But her eyes landed on Callie. Callie realized that Mom couldn't leave her alone.

"I'm going to stay at home. We'll bring extra firewood from the shed inside. Draw extra water. Cover any corners that might let the cold through," Mom said.

Dad nodded. Half an hour later, he had packed everything he might need in Gale's saddlebags and set off on the way to Drymon. The first snowflakes appeared just as he left.

The whole village sprang into action after that. Not because of Callie and Tristan. Many villagers didn't want to believe two children, especially the biggest troublemakers. But when the chief announced the incoming snowstorm, people took things seriously.

Firewood was gathered, water drawn to fill every spare bucket, pot, or bottle that could be found. Barns and coops were filled with water and food for the animals, should they be trapped inside for several days without people to replenish it.

Mom mostly took care of the things at home, while Callie, just like Tristan, was sent to help Granny Ethel. At her age, she wasn't able to carry firewood or buckets of water for long. Neither could she get into nooks and crannies to make sure that the wind wasn't blowing inside.

The whole time Callie worked, she was worried about Callen. She prayed with all her might that he would return home safely before the storm.

"They'll be back," Tristan said, likely having noticed her unease.

"How do you know?" Callie asked.

"Because your brother is strong. A little snow isn't going to hurt him!"

But as if to spite him, the snow began falling more and more, and the wind too grew stronger by the hour. By dinnertime, when it was dark outside, Callie could barely see anything beyond the thick flurry of snowflakes.

Mom and Callie both stood at the village entrance, holding each other's hands and straining to see into the distance. Neither of them spoke, but they didn't need words to know how much they were both worried about Callen. If Dad didn't bring him back today…

Dark figures appeared somewhere in the distance. Callie's heart filled with hope. The figures grew larger. It was hard to tell the shape among the snowflakes, but it appeared like two horses next to each other. Soon, Callie could make out something akin to a wagon.

Before long, she saw that it was Dad sitting at the bench of Silas's wagon and steering two horses towards the village. He must have tied Gale to the wagon, too, so it would be easier to get it through the thick layer of snow that had formed on the road.

"DAD!" Callie called cheerfully.

He waved, so Mom and Callie both rushed to the wagon. Callen and Silas were both in the back, their cheeks red from the cold.

"Are you alright, brother?" Callie asked as Callen got out of the wagon.

"We're alright. It was just very difficult through the snowed-in roads," Callen noted.

"We were basically running from this storm since Drymon, but it was always a bit ahead of us, making it difficult for the horse to move forward. Good thing your dad came with Gale. Turns out the horse I bought in autumn is very young and not yet able to pull a wagon full of merchandise in snow," Silas chuckled.

Callie looked at the horse next to Gale. It indeed looked smaller and very tired to boot, gasping heavily through its nostrils. She patted the poor creature on its neck. "You did well bringing my brother back safely. Thank you!"

Gale neighed in dissatisfaction.

"You did well, too, Gale! Thank you!"

"Let's hurry and get out of this cold," Mom urged everyone.

"Silas, what about the wagon?" Callen turned to the merchant.

"Don't worry. We'll deal with it once this storm settles down," Silas said.

After Silas's wagon was taken to its designated place near the village center, Callie and her family headed home to hunker down for the storm, just like the other villagers.

At the dinner table, Callen hurried to tell the family what he'd seen during his time away. "You know, thanks to all the swamp lantern mushrooms Callie got from the glimmerhares, I was able to sell the extra."

"Really?" Mom asked in surprise.

"Yes! Silas said it would be a good experience. Although…" Callen scratched the back of his head awkwardly. "I did… sell them too cheap. Silas let me sell them on my own, and I tried to drive the price up, and it felt like I had done just that. But he later told me that I could have sold it for twice the price, especially with a blizzard coming in."

"You'll do better next time. Don't worry, son! It's a learning experience," Dad put a hand on Callen's shoulder.

Callen nodded. "Yeah, and I think I still have a fair amount of money. We could use it to buy things we need for winter. Maybe some warm underclothes?"

Mom shook her head. "This is the first money you have earned on your own. We will touch it, unless there is absolutely no other way."

Callen frowned. "It doesn't feel fair that I keep all of it…"

Callen rummaged through his pockets and then handed a few coins to Callie.

"For me?" Callie asked, looking at the money in disbelief. She has never been given money before. Not for her own spending, at least.

"Yes! If it wasn't for you, we never would have gotten those mushrooms. I think it's fair that you have half of it," Callen said.

"I… I wouldn't even know what to do with it…" Callie pushed the coins away. It felt too much for her to have them.

"Keep it, Callie," Dad said. "Perhaps you can buy yourself some nice pencils for your notebook. The charcoal one isn't going to last forever."

Callie blinked in surprise, then took the coins. New pencils sounded wonderful. Perhaps she could even buy ink and a quill. She'd seen it only a few times in her life, but she knew that it was used to make real books.

That evening, she went to sleep hopeful. She was intent on studying the frost caterpillars more closely tomorrow, despite the strong howls of the winds and the cold that pushed its way through the holes in the corners. Nonetheless, Callie was warm, tucked away under her thick woolen blanket.

The same couldn't be said the next morning. When she woke up, Callie could barely force herself out of bed. The wind was still howling, and the snowstorm was well and truly raging. All she could see out of her bedroom window was white.

In the end, Callie wrapped herself in her blanket and waddled her way out of the room. She found Dad near the fireplace, moving his hands in gestures she had come to recognize as those necessary for fire magic. A burst of small flames came from Dad's outstretched palm and flew into the fireplace, engulfing the firewood.

But a moment later, the fire seemed to sputter out, leaving barely any embers behind.

"Why isn't it burning?" Callie asked.

"I wish I knew. At some point during the night, the fire went out. I had been getting up to light it up every hour or so, but the wood just refused to burn," Dad said with no small amount of frustration.

"Honey, come here!" Mom called from the storage room.

Dad hurried over to her, and Callie followed. They both gasped when they saw that all of the firewood was covered with a thin layer of frost.

"I-Is it really that cold?" Callie gaped.

"No… It shouldn't be impossible. The storage room is properly sealed up. There shouldn't be any snow coming in," Dad said.

Mom and Dad tried to search for the mysterious hole in the storage room, and soon Callen joined the fray. Callie didn't understand how this could be happening. She had never seen anything like this before, and this certainly wasn't the first blizzard in Greyfen.

She scratched at her forearm and realized how uncomfortable it felt. Rolling up her sleeve, she saw that the mark was flickering. Like a candle in the wind, it glowed and dimmed as if it were broken.
