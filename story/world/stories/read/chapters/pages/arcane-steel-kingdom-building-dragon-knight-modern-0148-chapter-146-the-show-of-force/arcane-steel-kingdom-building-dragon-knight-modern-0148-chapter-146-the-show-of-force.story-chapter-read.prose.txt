The Cinderfall knights charged, a desperate sprint across the ash-choked valley floor, hoping to silence the hovering plasma katanas before they could carve through their rear echelons.

Bob didn't let them close the distance easily. He stepped into the path of two advancing knights. He infused the ancient silver blade with raw, concussive magic and swung it in a wide, horizontal arc.

A crescent of highly pressurized water erupted from the edge of the steel. It was a physical blade of fluid moving fast enough to cut iron. The water-slash struck both knights across the chest simultaneously. The sheer kinetic impact lifted them off their feet and threw them backward into the churned mud.

One of the knights didn't get up. He hit the ground and instantly erupted into a sphere of golden flame.

Neither Bob nor I had touched the man with a lethal strike. The water had dented his armor, certainly broken ribs, but it wasn't a killing blow.

Is this what they call dying from extreme emotions? I wondered, sidestepping a spear thrust from a third knight.

[No, Master. Kaelus has killed a Tier 8 Phoenix in the upper atmosphere, forcing the contracted knight to also lose their life through the severed bond.]

The other two phoenix knights engaging Bob realized the same terrifying truth. The battle in the sky was not going in their favor. The two smaller birds perched on their shoulders shrieked, launching themselves upward. They expanded rapidly in size as they ascended, abandoning their knights to join the desperate dogfight in the cloud layer.

The battlefield arithmetic was clear. It was a two-on-one situation for all of us. Bob had his two targets, and I had mine. The dragons up above must be facing the exact same odds.

...

High in the bruised twilight sky, Kaelus flapped his massive, starlit wings, arresting his momentum.

He hovered in the freezing air, looking down with profound, unadulterated disappointment. A pile of grey ash was scattering in the wind where a magnificent, fiery bird had been a second ago.

How am I supposed to eat its flesh if it turns to ash after death? he grumbled mentally, his tail lashing in annoyance.

He looked behind him. Aquarius, a swirling vortex of deep ocean currents and freezing mist, was currently tangling with two massive phoenixes. The water dragon was holding his own, his elemental advantage evident as his frost-breath hissed against their golden flames.

Surely Aquarius won't mind if I take one of them, right? Kaelus mused, a predatory gleam in his cosmic eyes.

One of the lower-tier phoenixes, a smaller bird circling the main fray looking for an opening, made a sudden, aggressive dash for Kaelus's blind spot.

Kaelus didn't even turn his head. He simply tracked the thermal signature and the magical intent. As the bird closed the final distance, claws extended, Kaelus snapped his left wing out with devastating, localized force.

The heavy, starlit scales slapped the burning bird squarely in the chest. The impact sounded like a thunderclap. The phoenix shrieked in agony, its wings buckling, and plummeted rapidly toward the ground.

As I was saying before I was so rudely interrupted, Kaelus continued his internal train of thought. It is his fault for not telling me the flesh turns to ash if I kill them. It needs to be eaten fresh. But do they expect me to eat raw flesh? Disgusting. I am a dragon with standards.

He noticed two more phoenixes, massive Tier 8 beasts, charging directly toward him.

Kaelus smiled, revealing rows of razor-sharp teeth.

Let's see if I can capture one of them alive. I bet Brother could find a way to cook them.

...

I met the heavy, downward swing of a Tier 8 knight with the edge of a plasma katana.

I expected the superheated azure blade to shear the iron weapon completely in half. Instead, the two weapons collided with a dull, heavy clang. The plasma sparked and sputtered, locking against the metal as if striking a solid block of insulated lead.

Tes, analyze.

[Master, it appears a Tier 8 knight can imbue enough kinetic and thermal energy into their weapons to render the material density akin to plasma.]

Well, shit. There goes my little hack to deal with the heavy hitters. I suppose I shouldn't be surprised. Historically, Tier 8 Phoenix Knights were said to be able to cut through any weapon or armor forged by conventional means.

I still had ways to cut his blade.

The knight roared, pushing his weight against the lock, then pulled back for a wide, horizontal swing aimed at my ribs.

I didn't block. I created a miniature, localized wormhole directly in the path of his swing.

The knight’s eyes widened in confusion as his blade entered the white, shimmering portal and vanished. A fraction of a second later, the tip of his broadsword erupted from a corresponding exit portal fifty yards away, impaling a distant, lower-tier phoenix knight through the back.

The sensation of cutting through flesh fed back through the hilt of his weapon, but he hadn't touched me. Before the man could fully process the bizarre physics of the situation, I snapped the portal shut.

The spatial collapse severed the broadsword in two, leaving the knight staring at a useless, jagged stump of iron.

There was something deeply satisfying about beating someone at their own game. Some would even call it therapeutic.

The knight, however, did not share my amusement. Seeing his weapon destroyed, he lost whatever tactical discipline he had left. He went completely ape-shit.

Flames spewed from his gauntlets and the joints of his armor with every movement. He abandoned weaponry entirely, launching himself at me with a flurry of punches and kicks, creating tidal waves of golden fire that scorched the ash around us.

All I had to do was blink and weave. I folded space, shifting three feet to the left, then two feet back, dodging his strikes at the absolute last second. He was doing all the hard work for me, his wild, uncontrolled bursts of fire incinerating the smaller fry who got too close to our duel. Why waste my own mana reserves when I could have him do it for me?

His partner, the other Tier 8 knight who had been engaging me, realized the futility of the attack. He broke off from his guarded stance and moved to restrain his comrade, grabbing him by the shoulder and shouting something lost in the roar of the flames, trying to calm the rage.

The second knight turned and looked directly at me.

He was clearly trying to figure out who I was. Cinderfall intelligence didn't know of any other Dragon Knights in my army besides myself and Bob. The broadcast I had made years ago, right after saving my family, featured me in the Reaper armor, but I hadn't worn the helmet. I am wearing it now. Is this the classic 'can't recognize a person with and without glasses' situation?

Is it really that hard for them to figure out my identity? Was the starlit dragon not a dead giveaway? Or are they simply not willing to accept that a man in his twenties can reach Tier 8?

Technically speaking, achieving this level of power usually occurred when a knight was deep into their second century. In this world, my rapid growth—and Kaelus's—was an anomaly, accelerated by Tes’s biological optimization protocols.

Speaking of Tes, she chimed in.

[Master, Cinderfall cavalry is currently charging. They are utilizing the dwindling numbers here in the frontline in an attempt to bolster their troops and overwhelm the position.]

Assign Ivela to deal with it, I projected back. Unlike this vanguard, that force is comprised completely of regular knights. It will be easy enough to deal with.

My attention drifted back to the immediate battle. I saw Bob driving his silver blade through the chest of a reforming phoenix knight, about to secure another kill. It was sad to admit, but to deal with these heavy hitters efficiently and end this skirmish before more reinforcements arrived, I was going to have to harmonize with Kaelus.

I reached deep into my soul. The bridge snapped into place.

My power levels surged violently, punching through the barrier of a pseudo-Tier 8 and stabilizing as a full-fledged Tier 8 entity. The air around me crackled, heavy with the scent of ozone.

I pulled at the static energy vibrating in the atmosphere and the charge building in the bruised sky above.

I used the eleven plasma katanas hovering around me as grounding points to channel the lightning.

The battlefield descended into chaos. My lightning cut through everything without mercy. Even my own automata became collateral damage, their chassis sparking as the erratic arcs of energy struck them.

Tes, understanding the situation, didn't try to move the machines out of the way. Instead, she used them to pin the enemy combatants in place. The automata lunged forward, grabbing the struggling knights in mechanical bear hugs, ensuring they couldn't dodge the incoming strikes.

The two Tier 8 Phoenix Knights weren't faring any better. The lightning was following them like a relentless, sentient serpent, giving them no room to maneuver or breathe. There was also the terrifying factor that I could teleport anywhere in the immediate vicinity, making their defensive positioning completely useless.

Their caution became their downfall. As they focused entirely on my shifting location, they missed the secondary arcs of lightning gathering in the sky above them. The bolts slammed down, zapping both of them simultaneously.

The battle was over. I saw Bob finish off his two targets, the heavy, silver broadsword ending their final resurrection cycles.

This localized victory forced me to realize a massive problem. Estimates showed Cinderfall possessed a few hundreds of these Tier 8 combatants. I couldn't reliably deal with them all at the same time if they ever coordinated a mass assault. I needed to come up with a way to deal with such large numbers without consuming so much of my personal mana reserves. Next time, they might send fifty, or a hundred. That would be a problem.

I needed the Crawlers operational before the enemy realized the optimal solution to their current predicament was their age-old, favorite tactic of an all-out, frontal charge.

The cloak of the unknown was keeping me safe in this fight. As a famous strategist from my old world said: Appear weak when you are strong, and strong when you are weak.

That final show of power was exactly that. I needed the enemy to think I had overwhelming, limitless strength, making them cautious and hesitant to commit their full forces.

High above, the air force dealt with the charging cavalry reinforcements, the Wyverns executing precise strafing runs that turned the advancing column into a burning trench.

On the ground, the engineering automata flooded the frontlines, scaling the berms and beginning to restore the damaged defense grid.

As I began my walk back toward the command post, a dragon dropped from the cloud layer. He shrank rapidly during his descent and landed heavily on my shoulder.

Brother, I couldn't eat a single one, Kaelus’s voice was whining and sad. They… they… turn to ash right after you kill them.

He sniffled mentally. I couldn't just eat them raw. I needed to catch them. My plan was to trap them in ice and have you cook them for me. I even managed to catch one in ice, but it, for some reason, also turned to ash.

Why did I have a sinking feeling I was entirely responsible for that? My massive, indiscriminate lightning strike had likely fried the knights below, causing the trapped birds to use more charges than intended and finally dissolve into ash right in his claws.

Oh well. Time to gaslight him into forgetting this tragedy.

I made my voice as empathetic as possible. "Well, when you think about it, how does one cook a phoenix? Their body temperature is higher than regular fire. You can't exactly roast the meat."

Kaelus looked at me, a look of profound shock crossing his draconic features as realization dawned on him. So you're telling me it was already pre-cooked? he asked, his head tilting to the side.

While holding back a chuckle, I confirmed his theory. "Yes. It is a special delicacy. It comes to you fully cooked and alive at the exact same time."

Kaelus put his paws over his head, whining and grumbling. All I needed to do was take a bite.
