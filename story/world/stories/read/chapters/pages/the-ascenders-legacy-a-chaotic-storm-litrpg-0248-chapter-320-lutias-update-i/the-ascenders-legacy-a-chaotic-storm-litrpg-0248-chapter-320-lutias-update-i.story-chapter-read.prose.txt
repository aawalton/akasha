Back in Lutia, what could only be described as a continental shift was happening.

With the cult of the watch now successfully destroyed, and all known cultists either killed or on their way to death row, people no longer had to live in fear. Those who had been kidnapped returned to their families, while those who hadn't made it out alive were mourned in a grand procession that stretched across all four kingdoms.

The mourning had given way to celebration pretty quickly.

It was the celebration of a new era, they had said. A celebration of freedom. And people had celebrated with all their hearts, coming out in waves to dance in the streets—not realizing that they had just killed the last known member of the bloodline that would have united Lutia and saved it from the curse of the Limit.

They may have been free from the cult, but they were still deeply entrenched under Attilan's boots.

Those who knew neither celebrated nor cursed, satisfied with things simply returning to the way they once were. Their prime argument? Better to remain in bondage than under the rule of a descendant of Sárán Beither.

Whether they were right or not was not a matter they bothered to speculate about. What mattered was for things to return to normal.

And for the most part, it did.

The celebrations died down, and the kingdoms went back to hating each other—only this time with less bloodshed and more ink.

After coming together to fight against the children of the watch, fighting against one another seemed almost petty, and so, grudgingly, they formed a truce.

No more wars. No more bloodshed.

It wasn't an easy bill to pass, but with emotions running high after such a massive victory, the people were more than happy to let bygones be bygones. They wouldn't instantly become friends—especially Sunstone—but apathy was more than appreciated.

The people couldn't have been happier.

Soldiers returned home to their families. The academies reopened, and talk of the academy tournaments resumed, getting people excited for the future. They proposed making the tournaments even grander than before—doing it as a continent, not just as kingdoms—and the citizens of Lutia grew even more excited.

But for a select few individuals, the opposite was the case.

In a colorful bedroom in the Brystion household, Daruk stood before his mirror on the very first day of resumption, noting how pale he looked against the brightness surrounding him—a ghost haunting a room too cheerful for grief.

A sigh escaped his lips in resignation.

After the whole Abyssos event and Aodhán's disappearance, his mother had taken to decorating like a dying man to water, and no one in the house could bring themselves to say no to her enthusiasm—not after how devastated she'd been in that first week.

And so, they had endured as she'd turned the house into a rainbow of 'happy colors,' stating that the colors would help them all cheer up. And to be honest, it had worked. Only not quite in the way his mother might have hoped.

Sighing once more, he switched out his pale blue robe for a deep black tunic, only to realize that the deeper color only made him look more washed out than before.

"Pale blue it is then," he muttered to himself and switched it back, ignoring the slight gauntness in his face. Today was the first day of resumption after the whole Abyssos incident, and Daruk couldn't bring himself to care—him, who had once dreamed of becoming a noble scholar.

It was a shame.

Daruk had nothing against the academy, but after all that had happened, it just seemed so… trivial. Unimportant in the grand scheme of things. But since his alternatives were either staying at home with his parents or joining a guild, Daruk figured the academy was the better option.

If nothing else, it was his best chance at meeting a Calamity and enacting his plan to join Aodhán. It was also the only place he could hope to get some concrete information about Gordon—if there was any at all.

Exhaling deeply, he cinched his robes tightly and stared at himself for a moment longer before stepping out of his room. The sweet-smelling scent of pastries greeted his nostrils, and he realized his mother was already hard at work.

Mood rising subtly, he headed down to the living room, where he found the rest of his family setting up for breakfast. He smiled, ignoring the part of himself that pointed out with no small amount of bitterness that a certain storm awakened was missing.

"Daruk," his father smiled at him over a news magazine. "How'd you sleep?"

"Very well, thank you," Daruk replied, shooting Aldric a glare that warned him against revealing that he had, in fact, not slept at all. "How about you, Dad? A nice rest?"

Unrid grunted. "It would have been much nicer if it wasn't so cold." He raised a knowing brow. "You wouldn't know anything about that now, would you?"

Aldric smirked. "Must be a draft. I mean, it's the height of summer."

Daruk ignored both of them and turned to assist Yue as she set the table. "I didn't feel any chill in the air. Maybe you two are just getting old."

Unrid laughed and pointed a finger at him. "I am a fire awakened, boy. If I'm to feel the effects of my age, it would be heat, not cold."

"Who's growing old?" his mother asked as she stepped out of the kitchen, looking much better than she had in the last few weeks. When her gaze landed on Daruk, she smiled widely. "You're finally awake. Aldric wanted to wake you up, but I thought you could use the extra hours of rest to prepare for the new day."

Daruk smiled and moved to assist her. "Thanks, Ma. I did need the rest."

Aldric grunted from his position at the table, and his father simply shook his head. The motion caused Yue, who hadn't said anything since his arrival, to chuckle. "I thought stronger awakeneds shouldn't need quite as much sleep as before."

Daruk paused and turned to glare at her. "Chatty now, are we?"

"Leave her be, Daruk," Synové chuckled, quickly coming to Yue's aid as she sat down. "Unrid, would you say a prayer, and let us eat?"

They all sat down, his father said the prayer, and a few seconds later, they all dug in.

The food was good—his mother's cooking always was—but Daruk found himself picking at it more than eating until Aldric kicked him under the table. He glared at his brother, but before he could say anything, his father did, nodding satisfactorily as he downed a cup of warm milk.

"First day of resumption, huh? I hope you're ready for it."

Daruk looked down at his plate and shrugged. "It's not a big deal, Dad. I'm simply going back to school. It's like resuming from a half-decent holiday."

"Still," his mother chimed in from the other end of the table. "You'll be seeing all your friends for the first time after all that happened. That's something you have to be emotionally prepared for."

Daruk sighed. "I've seen all my friends, Mum. They were here when Aldric and I arrived."

"But those were only a few people," his mother pressed. "Surely, you must have more friends than that."

"He'll be fine, Mum," Aldric chipped in, an awkward chuckle escaping his lips as he came to Daruk's rescue. "I'm guessing it's his friends who need to be emotionally prepared for the new Daruk."

Daruk glanced at his elder brother and passed him an extra helping of pastries in thanks. Aldric had grown from the smirky, rakish individual he used to be, and now that he worked in Fort Dominion with the champions themselves, he had become a sort of family head, despite their father's presence.

His mother stared at Aldric for a long moment before nodding. "Well, if you say so."

"Trust me," Aldric smiled. "I'm sure."

He turned his gaze to Yue and nudged her gently. "If there's anyone we need to worry about, it's Yue. She joins the adventurer's guild today."

Yue looked up from her food as if the mention of her name had pulled her out of deep, dark thoughts.

"Oh no," she finally replied, shaking her head. "You don't have to worry about me at all. I'll be a fly on the wall. Literally. I won't be in any danger."

Daruk chuckled at that. "I don't know why you're joining the guild at all when you could simply enter the dungeons without anyone noticing."

Yue shrugged. "I'd rather not be hunted like that Sunstonian changeling, Seo-yeon. Besides, I wouldn't be proud of it."

"Aww," Synové cooed, beaming widely. "Look how proper our Yue is turning out to be."

Yue blushed brightly, and they all laughed.

After the laughter died down, Unrid smiled at her. "I'm happy you're finally taking the step. Aodhán would have been so happy to see you thrive."

The mention of Aodhán's name sent everyone into a deep, dark place, and against his natural instincts and the Skalk's urgings, Aldric left them to pull themselves out.

Daruk was the first to do so, a hint of emotion evident in his features as he smiled at Yue. "He would be so proud."

Yue smiled back. "He would be." She pulled out the voltrite ore Aodhán had left behind and began discussing how she intended to fashion it into some sort of weapon.

The conversation flowed to lighter topics after that, and Aldric sighed, a wry smile coming to his lips as he realized that Aodhán's shoes weren't something he was ever going to fill.

The thought should have brought sadness. Instead, it made him smile. He had no intention of filling Aodhán's shoes. He simply intended to keep them warm until Aodhán returned.

He glanced up at Daruk, and his smile soured. Unfortunately, he doubted Daruk had such patience.

The rest of breakfast passed without incident, and finally, it was time for Daruk and Yue to leave.

"Oh, my babies," Synové smiled sadly as she pulled Daruk and Yue into a tight hug, clutching them to herself with a strength she shouldn't have possessed. Daruk and Yue went willingly, knowing just how hard this departure was for her and how brave she was being to let them out of her sight after all that had happened.

"Promise you'll call every day," she asked, and they both nodded.

"I'll call," Daruk stated, while Yue grunted. "I'll literally be back in like five hours."

"Just take it," Aldric grunted from where he stood, sensing how terribly close to erupting his mother's emotions were. "No talking back."

The struggling girl grew pliant once more, and after a few seconds, Synové finally let them go. Unrid hugged them next, and then finally, it was Aldric's turn.

Expression somber, Aldric smiled down at his brother and pulled him into a hug.

"You can do this without Aodhán," he whispered. "It won't be easy, but you can do it."

Daruk stood stiffly in his arms for a moment, and then he nodded once. "I can do it."

Aldric smiled, feeling the tight mix of emotions surging within his brother and wishing he could take them away. But stripping people of their emotions wasn't empathy. It was a violation.

And Aldric had no intention of turning his family into thralls.

Daruk and Yue left the house a few minutes later, flying away on a construct of ice. Aldric watched them go with his mother and father by his side, swallowing thickly as the emotions of his parents weighed heavily on him.

"Will they be alright?" Synové asked in a whisper as they finally left her line of sight, and Aldric glanced at his mother, forcing himself to offer her no false assurance.

"We can only hope, Mother. Hope and pray."

She nodded, and after another moment of silence, asked, "What about Aodhán? Is he alright? Can you still tell?"

Aldric turned his gaze to the distance, feeling the link between him and Aodhán still humming in his spirit. He tugged on it and smiled when he felt it ripple with life.

"Yes," he replied. "I can still tell, and he's alright."

His mother's eyes gleamed as she wrapped her arms around Unrid. "Good. That's very good to hear."

She walked into the house a few minutes later, and Aldric glanced up at his father. "She's doing well."

His father stayed silent for a long moment before he too nodded. "Thank you."

He too left, and finally, only Aldric remained. He stared into the distance where Daruk and Yue had disappeared and smiled sadly.

The world may have gone back to the way things were, but it seemed it had forgotten to take several parts of itself along. Parts yet unhealed. Parts yet aching.

Cyrus Valerion sat within the vastness of his cultivation chamber, eyes closed as space undulated gently around him.

It was barely dawn, much earlier than his usual waking time, but today was not a day to sleep in. It was the first day of resumption, the day to prove to the world that the events of Abyssos hadn't traumatized him.

He was a noble heir after all. Noble heirs didn't have the luxury of trauma.

Exhaling deeply, Cyrus held the image of the academy in his mind, forcing himself to walk into every training room, including the one where the kidnapping had happened. He was confronting his fears, as his father had unceasingly stated ever since his arrival from Abyssos.

"You are my heir, Cyrus," his father would say. "Fear is for bastards, not heirs."

It was stupid and wrong, but at least his father was finally using his real name. Small mercies and silver linings, right?

Shaking his head, Cyrus focused his mind on the mental image, breathing rhythmically to settle his core after his most recent advancement.

The past few days had been grueling but interesting. He'd had the rare opportunity to spar against his brother, Artemis, and had gained so much from the experience—creating new skills and sharpening the ones he already had.

All that he'd learned had finally condensed into an inspiration the night before, granting him a seal and pushing his core into the thirtieth tier—an unusual rank for a student in the first-year bracket, but his case was obviously very different.

Unfortunately, the only two people who could truly relate to what he had gone through weren't people who liked him.

Daruk Brystion had made his dislike for him quite obvious after he'd betrayed them in Abyssos, and Aodhán…

His thoughts stuttered as a memory of Aodhán's icon flashed in his mind, and Cyrus shook his head. He'd been so foolish to compare himself. It made so much sense now why Mirith had treated him with such sympathy.

She had known. She'd seen the foolishness of his rage.

He, an ordinary awakened, comparing his fate to that of a transmigrant.

Exhaling, he shook his head again.

How he wished he had known earlier, long before he created his ideal and centered it around competition and rivalry, because the truth was that as much as he hated Aodhán, the transmigrant had been a very, very good rival—a mountain peak that remained always just a little out of sight.

And for him to progress, Cyrus knew he needed someone else like that.

Daruk would have been a great substitute—he certainly trumped him in raw strength and willpower—but Daruk lacked the necessary passion to be a good rival. He was too cold. Too precise. Too clean.

No matter how much he thought about it, Cyrus didn't see Daruk rising to defend his honor or fighting to keep his position on the leaderboard. Making him a rival was like fighting against a river that barely noticed his existence.

Cyrus grimaced. Hopefully, the exchange students the academy had gained as a result of the truce would be more receptive to a rivalry. Or perhaps his former classmates might have grown stronger than him.

But it wasn't likely.

That thought occupied his mind until a knock resounded in his chamber and Virgil's voice echoed a moment later.

"Young Master Valerion. It is time."

Cyrus opened his eyes then and pushed himself to his feet.

It was time. With Aodhán no longer in the picture, he had a crown to claim and rivals to make. He needed to grow stronger quickly. With his father now calling him by his name, the last thing Cyrus wanted was to disappoint him.

A wild chatter filled the academy entrance as the light of dawn bathed its massive walls in liquid gold.

Students and their guardians crowded the grounds, forming clusters of cheerful laughter as they waited for the great gates to open. Many embraced after long months apart, and some cried openly. But what they did not do was speak of Abyssos—or of the war that had only just ended.

There was no need to poison such a beautiful morning with terrible memories.

Instead, conversation drifted to safer ground: the coming tournament, the peace treaty, what the new academic year might bring—and finally, the most tantalizing topic of all: the exchange students.

To foster peace, the four kingdoms had agreed to a student exchange program. Each kingdom would distribute fifteen of its most promising students across the other three kingdoms to study. Ragnarok, for instance, had sent five elites each to Calodan, Unoros, and Sunstone—and received the same in return.

The goal was simple: encourage interpersonal ties by placing students from rival nations in one another's finest academies. And despite everything Abyssos had shaken loose, the 5th academy was still one of the best academies in Ragnarok, which meant they got three new exchange students—one from each kingdom.

No one was crass enough to point or whisper outright, but many eyes lingered on the small cluster standing apart near the entrance.

One was a telekinetic from Sunstone, with platinum-gold hair and eyes that looked as though they had stared into the abyss of evil itself. Another was an ice-awakened from Unoros, radiating a ghastly chill despite her warm, welcoming expression. The last was a quiet-looking telepath, raven-dark hair framing eyes that revealed little.

Not much was known about these exchange students other than their kingdoms and affinities, but that changed half an hour later when a figure appeared high above the ground, standing on a platform of chilling ice, frost-white hair flapping in the wind and blue irises as piercing as daggers.

Daruk Brystion.

Gasps of awe and admiration erupted from the crowd as they all turned to stare at him—a double inheritor of immense power and the brother to Aodhán Brystion, the transmigrant.

Camera shutters clicked, eyes developed stars, and potential rivals assessed with taunting smiles, but Daruk barely spared any of them a glance. Instead, his gaze was fixed on the exchange students, his cold demeanor cracking slightly as he recognized two of them.

Seojun and Destra.

Slowly, he descended to stand before them, his lips stretching into a smile as he pulled them into a hug. He hadn't known them as well as Aodhán had, but he'd known them enough that seeing them now caused a surge of emotions he couldn't name.

In the end, when he finally pulled away, he smiled. "I'm happy to see you."

"We are too," Seojun said, offering a slight bow. "And… we're sorry about your brother. About the continent we live in."

Destra nodded. "I choose to believe in change. One day, I think this world will truly change—and certain barbaric actions will be outgrown."

Daruk's smile turned wry. He wasn't sure how much hope he had left for Lutia, but he nodded all the same. "Thank you."

They smiled, and Daruk finally shifted his gaze to the last student, noting how familiar she looked.

"I'm sorry," he said slowly. "Do I know you?"

The girl smiled. "I don't think you do, Daruk Brystion. But I know you. My elder sister was very… thorough when she learned I'd be sent here."

His brow furrowed. "Your elder sister?"

Her smile widened. "My name is Cruella Vaelys. Nice to make your acquaintance."
