The trap door sat heavy in the floorboards. Rayan’s fingers hovered just above the iron handle, his hand trembling slightly as he prepared to pull it open.

Riley jolted suddenly, as if someone had poured cold water down her back. She crossed the room in three quick strides and planted her boot firmly on the wooden panel, pinning the trap door shut. Rayan jerked his hand back as if burned.

“Send for the raider prisoners,” Riley said, her voice flat and cold.

One of the soldiers snapped a sharp nod and dashed out of the half-burned building. The sound of his boots faded quickly across the compound. A few moments later he returned, leading the two original raider prisoners between him and another guard. Both men had their wrists tightly bound in front of them.

Riley pointed at the larger, more defiant-looking prisoner. “Bring the mouthy one here and remove his bindings.”

The soldier shoved the man forward. Another guard stepped in and cut through the ropes with a quick slice of his knife. The raider rubbed his raw wrists and glared at Riley, his mouth already twisting into a sneer as he waited for whatever came next.

The soldier finished cutting the raider’s wrist bindings with a quick slice of his knife. Another soldier stepped up beside him. Together they seized the man by each arm and marched him forward until he stood directly in front of the trap door.

Riley shoved Rayan aside with her shoulder. She pointed at the wooden panel on the floor and looked straight at the raider prisoner. “Open it.”

The raider stood motionless, eyes narrowed in defiance. One of the guarding soldiers pressed the edge of his sword against the man’s throat. The blade rested just hard enough to draw a thin line of blood.

The raider raised both hands slowly in surrender and gave Riley a single nod. He squatted down, gripped the iron handle, and yanked the trap door upward. The hinges creaked loudly in the quiet room.

Nothing happened; no explosion and no traps. No sudden rush of guards from below. Only darkness and the faint smell of damp earth rose from the opening.

Riley stared down into the black hole for a long moment while the raider remained crouched beside the open door, breathing hard.

Riley turned her gaze to the raider. “Go down there,” she ordered.

The raider paused, his eyes flicking between Riley and the black hole. He took a hesitant step onto the first wooden stair. A sharp twang echoed from below. A crossbow bolt shot upward and buried itself deep into the side of his neck.

The remaining two raiders gasped. The wounded man dropped to his knees, clutching at the bolt as blood poured down his chest. He toppled forward and collapsed across the open trap door, his body twitching once before going still.

Thorne crouched beside the stairs and examined the frame. “There is a trip wire on the first step.”

Riley pointed at the second raider prisoner, the one originally captured near her own base. “Remove it and you enter.”

Thorne’s head snapped up from the trap door. His eyes locked onto Riley, narrowed slightly, the lines around them tightening. For a long second, he simply stared at her and his expression hardened.

A confused look crossed the raider’s face. “What? It?”

Riley stared at the raider hard. “What’s what! Remove him and enter!”

The soldier guarding the remaining raider dragged the dead man’s body off the stairs and shoved it aside. The second prisoner swallowed hard, his face pale. He stepped forward slowly, eyes fixed on the dark opening as he prepared to descend.

The raider prisoner stared into the dark opening, his face slick with sweat. “No please, I don’t know if there are any more traps down there.”

Thorne’s voice stayed hard. “Either you die here or chance for life by going down there.”

“I can’t please don’t…” the raider begged, his voice cracking.

“Kill him,” Riley said.

A soldier drew his sword and stepped forward. The raider backed away quickly, hands raised. “Ok! Ok!”

He turned toward the trap door and paused, studying the first step with wide eyes. After a long moment he carefully stepped over it and placed his foot on the second step. Nothing happened. He repeated the careful process, testing each stair before committing his weight. Step by step he descended into the darkness until his head disappeared below the floor and the sound of his cautious footsteps faded away.

The room fell silent except for the faint crackle of settling embers in the burned timbers above. Riley, Thorne, and the remaining soldiers waited, eyes fixed on the open trap door.

The raider’s voice drifted up from the darkness below. “I can’t see much down here…”

Thorne reached out and took a lit torch from the nearest soldier. He dropped it through the open trap door. The flaming brand tumbled downward and landed with a clatter somewhere in the basement.

Riley leaned closer to the opening. “What do you see?”

“Shelves and chests,” the raider called back, his voice echoing slightly.

“Open one of the chests,” Riley ordered.

“Which one?”

“Pick one!”

“Okay, I will try this big one.”

A moment later a heavy thud sounded from below, followed by desperate choking and the dull sound of a body collapsing onto the floor.

Thorne glanced at Riley. “I think he’s dead.”

Riley exhaled slowly through her nose. “More traps. Great.”

The basement remained silent except for the faint crackle of the dropped torch still burning somewhere in the darkness. Smoke began to curl lazily up through the trap door as the group stood around the opening, staring down into the black space where two men had already died.

Riley gave a sharp nod toward the door and stepped outside. Thorne followed her into the open air of the compound.

“Grab a bunch of soldiers and have them clear the room,” Riley said. “If they are injured, transport them immediately back to the hospital.”

Thorne’s face tightened. He looked at her with open suspicion. “Why not use the last raider?”

“I need him alive,” Riley replied.

Thorne hesitated, his jaw working silently for a moment. Then he gave a single nod and turned to walk toward the gate.

“That raider doesn’t need to know I need him alive,” Riley added.

Thorne stopped mid-stride. He did not turn around. After a brief pause he continued walking without another word, his boots crunching over charred debris as he headed for the soldiers waiting near the gate. Riley remained standing outside the half-burned building, arms crossed, watching him go.

Thorne soon returned with five more soldiers. He led them past Riley without a word. As each man walked by, Riley looked away until the last soldier had entered the half-burned building. She hesitated for a moment, then reluctantly followed them inside.

The last living raider stood sweating in the corner. His eyes darted toward Riley several times, but each time she sensed the look and turned toward him, he quickly glanced away.

Thorne watched the entire process with a hardened expression. His eyes moved across the room while his head stayed still. He kept his gaze away from Riley.

One by one the soldiers entered the basement. As each man triggered a trap, the next soldier moved in to clear it. They followed their orders without hesitation and kept stoic expressions on their faces. After a half dozen attempts the room stood clear of dangers. The dead soldiers were collected and carried out for immediate transport back to the hospital at the base.

The basement now lay open and quiet below the trap door. Faint torchlight flickered from the space beneath, illuminating rows of shelves and the outlines of several large chests. Riley stood at the edge of the opening and stared down into the cleared chamber.

Riley turned to Thorne. “I have summoned all remaining available soldiers to return that are not traveling back to base. Once all our dead have been transported back, start transporting everything in this storehouse back to base. Load the valuables into the tower. I will have Landryn prepare the construction of a storehouse for the remaining items. Put any ore, wood or food in the silos. Have all the items accounted for by Nikola. Have this raider help under guard.”

Thorne stood at attention with an emotionless face. “Understood.”

Riley paused for a moment. Her eyes hardened slightly. Then she turned uncertainly and walked out the door, heading back toward the main gate.

The sky hung overcast and grey. A cool wind blew through the courtyard of the raiders’ compound. Riley shivered as the breeze cut through her clothes and sent a chill racing up her spine. She stopped and scanned the area. We won, she thought as she stood in silence. After a long moment she began walking slowly back toward the camp.
