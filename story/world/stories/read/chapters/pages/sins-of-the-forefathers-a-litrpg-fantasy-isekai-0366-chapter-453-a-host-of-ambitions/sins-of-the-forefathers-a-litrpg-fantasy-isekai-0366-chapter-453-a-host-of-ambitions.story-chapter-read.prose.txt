‘The Elders and their lines were not only favored by this external force, but they were being given instructions by it,’ Hermus continued. ‘This ‘System’, whatever it truly was, was not known to the greater Netherim people. They had no idea that their grand, mysterious Purpose was being done at the behest of an existence that was neither Man nor Spirit.’

I sighed, another one of my long-held suspicions essentially confirmed. I had always been suspicious about how Aveline could actually speak and understand the common languages of Vereden. There was precisely zero chance that my daughter spoke anything but whatever language had been common parlance among the Netherim. And yet, from the moment I had met her, Aveline had not only been able to understand me, but all of the other peoples of this planet.

The rest of us had to rely on a Skill given to us the moment we were Awoken in the System, Language Adaptation.

Aveline could just instinctively understand people.

And here Hermus was saying that the System had ‘favored’ these Elders and their lines. I wonder, had Cecily, Aveline’s mother, counted as one of those Elders? She had been the Chief Engineer of that bunker, after all…

I remained silent as Hermus continued his explanations. These were answers to questions I had long searched for, found in a place I really hadn’t expected.

‘Whoever it was that discovered this knowledge, they did not keep it to themselves,’ The disembodied, deceased Spirit sighed. ‘If it were one of my students, they did not say. Whoever it was, they spread the knowledge far and wide. The Netherim people were not their own, it was whispered in dark corners. Their labors were for a mysterious entity who did naught but bless the already most privileged amongst them. All the while, they huddled in holes in the ground, denied the sight of the sun and even a single breath of fresh air. Across the breadth of Vereden, discontent began to arise, despite the efforts of the Elders to quell it. Earlier, you told me that you believed those who would become gods had slaughtered their fellows to the last. No. I tell you this now, that is a falsehood. Instead…they became the leaders of a revolution.’

Sylvia cocked her head in consideration. “The Netherim descended into a civil war?”

‘Eventually,’ Hermus confirmed. ‘But it was many years of discontent within the bunkers before even the first fist was thrown, and the first blade bared. However, it was within these halls, far, far away from the eyes of the Elders, that an idea born from resentment began to circulate. How dare this System bestow only its gifts upon the bloodlines of the affluent? Power should be shared freely, so that the downtrodden may rise above the station created for them at their birth. If this System will not share these gifts…’

“Then they’ll be taken,” I finished for him, in a quiet tone. I could already tell where this was going, and I…had some very mixed feelings about it.

I did not need sympathy filling my heart for these people in particular.

‘Just so. Please, understand this. Perhaps their deeds would, in the future, become something other than virtuous. Perhaps a lust for conquest and dominance would overtake them. But…at the start? Those you know as the gods began their endeavors with a righteous indignation within their hearts, at the inequality that had afflicted their people for so long. I welcomed such a thing. In mine eyes, and through my contemplations upon the nature of Death, all are equal when it came to the end. Death is not malicious. It is not a punishment, nor a curse. Rather…all should be thankful that no existence is truly unending.’

“All things end,” I echoed Rafael’s words from earlier. It had struck me as almost a catechism then. Now I was sure it was.

It was odd to realize that maybe Necromancy of all things…had risen from what sounded like an almost religious sect.

Though I couldn’t see him, Hermus’s voice when he spoke next was almost…diminished, as if he had wilted. ‘And yet, things changed. Within these walls, the endeavor to study and understand what this System was took many a year. If you seek to steal fire from the gods, first you must understand what that god is. My students started from nothing, and so their work was the work of decades. In truth, often the magics worked within these halls deviated greatly from what I had built with Necromancy. Many things were discovered, many paths explored. The first, great breakthrough that occurred was when limited contact was made with the System itself. What they discovered about it was…not what was expected.’

A bolt of lightning raced up and down my spine, and I could tell I wasn’t the only one startled by the implications there. Sylvia had leaned forward at his words, just as intently interested in what Hermus was saying as I was. She may have been content to let me carry this conversation, but that didn’t mean she was passive about it.

But Rafael, though…

I’m positive this was the first time the Lich was hearing this as well. If Sylvia was interested, then the Lich was enraptured. I think, in his burning eyes, Fade, Sylvia, and I might as well not exist from how focused he was on the physical remains of his apparent Necromantic mentor.

‘It was almost…procedural. Mechanical in design. The hint, it seems, was in the name,’ Hermus chuckled dryly. ‘It was almost like a machine. No…not almost. It was exactly like a form of the computational engines that drove Netherim society. And yet, it was so much more. Impossibly vast and immeasurably complex in scale, the difference in capability between the devices the Netherim possessed and what the System was…it was, according to one of my students, akin to the difference between a campfire and a star. And all of it, as far as my disciples could tell, was written into the very fabric of reality itself. The apparatus itself had no discernible source they could detect. It was…,’ He paused, as if to search for the words. ‘It was almost as if they had discovered a universal, fundamental force, not unlike gravity or magnetism, that had heretofore been unknown to them. It had always existed…and yet it was beyond their reach, running in the background. But there was a difference. This force was being directed. There was, apparently, something or someone that was controlling it. This existence, as they learned, was known to the Netherim Elders…as the Administrator.’

The Administrator…I remember I heard something about whatever that was, in my time in the bunker. From Travers, I believe? It had been an almost offhand comment from the combative Lich, something meant to taunt me. He’s just mentioned them and moved on, amused at my frustration.

I was starting to think that not only had Cecily been one of these Elders…but Travers as well.

Which made my Aveline the daughter of two Netherim Elders.

Hmm…

‘My disciples came to believe that this Administrator was the driving mind behind the System. The existence who was directing the Elders to some nebulous, secret goal. And perhaps…a source of power as well. In their furtive explorations of the hidden workings of the System, they gleaned knowledge of the unpinning mechanics that defined it. From long study, they translated these concepts into the realm of the physical as best they could, as a language. This language, this system of writing, would come to be known as Runes.’

Turns out, I had been both right and wrong when I was examining the chamber above. The gods had essentially stolen the knowledge of Runes from another source, that being the System itself. But, to give them credit, they might have actually created the language itself if they had been translating what might well have been universal concepts into a form of language.

It made sense, when I thought about it, that the Runic system had originated from the System itself. What I had seen in the above chamber might well have been the first, original syntax of the language the gods had mapped out. That explained its almost raw nature. They must have refined it over time until they had eventually passed down what I had learned, as the modern version.

Streamlined. Easy to understand…

And I’m beginning to think less potent.

‘But they were not as surreptitious as they believed. Not long after completing their work upon the Runic system, they were abruptly locked out of all System access. They had disguised their access into the outer workings of the System as coming from other, more senior Netherim. However, who you knew as the gods underestimated the reach of the Administrator. Though it took them years to notice the inconsistencies, it was eventually discovered. They were shut out, and the Netherim Elders were informed of my disciples' overreach. Suddenly, the halls of my home were not merely a place where they studied and furthered their understanding of Mind, Body, and Soul. Instead, it was the only home they were welcome within. All of them were exiled from their bunkers, their homes. This, I believe…was when the spark that would become their grand plan, their own Purpose, was lit.’

I closed my eyes, suddenly understanding the motivations of the gods far more than I ever had. “I see,” I said heavily. “If they couldn’t return to their homes, they would make a new one. But to do that…”

“They’d need to be strong enough to defy the Elders,” Sylvia said quietly. I noticed she had looked away from the mole skeleton.

I think she might have been as uncomfortable as I was, with how much more…human this was making the gods seem.

‘And the greatest possible source of strength…was the System itself,’ Hermus agreed. ‘Suddenly, my students were no longer content in researching the System. They wanted its power. But they had been deliberately locked out from it, cast from the warmth that was the secret benefactor of the Netherim. It did not deter them. If they were denied legitimate access to the System…then they would just have to take from it what they wanted. And so, a new plan was created. Instead of subtlety, they chose to brute force matters. They would smash open a connection to the System, something far more potent than anything they had dreamed of up to that point. Something with a very specific purpose in mind. They had theorized that, perhaps, it was the Administrator themselves who was the power behind the veritable throne. If they could but wrench whatever power they needed from that creature, then they could fulfill the promise that decades of unrest within Netherim society had foretold. They could wrest control over their people from the Elders, whom they viewed as nothing but the puppets of the Administrator.’

I was really not enjoying the parallels I was seeing in the gods motivations right now, to everything I had experienced.

Not at all.

‘To that end, the above chamber was prepared. I…apologize, Rafael,’ Hermus addressed the raptly attentive Lich in an apologetic tone. Said Lich’s burning eyes flickered, as if he had blinked rapidly in confusion. ‘I told you that the chamber was where the ascension itself occurred. This is, in truth, a misdirection. Instead of a Chamber of Ascension…it is a Chamber of Transportation. When it was finally activated, my students did not suddenly gain a measure of Divinity. No…instead, they were transported directly to wherever it is that the Administrator exists.’

My jaw hung open. Almost involuntarily, my eyes drifted upwards to stare at the ceiling above.

They had physically gone to where this Administrator was…

Suddenly, I really wanted to go back upstairs and spend the next year doing nothing but picking apart the runes within. Who cares if it was all written in the equivalent of old English to what I understood? With enough time, I could puzzle it out, I was sure of that.

‘Riding waves of purest Aether, they vanished. I did not see them again for nigh upon a fortnight. I do not know the particulars of what happened whilst they were gone, but I do know this. They came back different. In many, many ways. Some fortunate, and some…less so. Their personalities had shifted, as had their appearances. Even I, who had spent decades within their presences, struggled to tell them apart. One thing was for certain, though. They had seized the power they had desired. Oh yes, they were now mighty indeed. Strong enough that the Elders who they had once so feared were little more than insects now. With this…we are rapidly approaching the limit of my knowledge.’

I jerked, blinking rapidly. “What do you mean, the limits of your knowledge? What happened with the Administrator? What about the Netherim and the Elders? What did they take from the Administrator to ascend?!”

It was only when I felt a familiar, cool hand on my shoulder that I realized I had started shouting at the large mole skull.

Hermus wasn’t phased, though. ‘I do not know,’ He answered hollowly. ‘Those students whom I had nurtured so closely, whom I had grown to love as my own children…left, not long after. Never again would I see them, nor would I receive knowledge of the outside world for a long, long time. It took over several centuries before a new disciple would find this place, and I learned of the…aftermath. However, he was not one of the Netherim. Rather, that student was a Genirim. A Dwarf, to be precise, who stumbled upon my home in his underground wanderings. Once, he was a prospector, marking out locations for a new project by the name of the Long Road…at the behest of his people’s new god. Yorgun…it was not his birth name, but I recognized it still. Once, it was nothing more than a silly little nickname. A practice that my disciples took upon themselves to separate their present from their past within the bunkers.’

A large, regretful sigh filled the air, whispy and light upon the ear. ‘They had taken those names and chosen to rule not only Vereden, but other worlds as well. Places I had barely even known existed, they now governed with an iron fist. Of the Netherim, my new student knew nothing. He had never heard either the word Netherim, nor Genirim. My only guess…is that they succeeded in destroying the Elders. But as for the rest of the Netherim? There was something curious that my student told me. According to him, around the same time that the gods conquered the known worlds, a great host of angels descended with them. Powerful warriors, who wielded strange artifacts, and held great knowledge. The gods did not build an empire alone. I believe…that many among the Netherim sided with the freshly ascended Divinity, and chose to join them.’

‘After all…the gods had promised them freedom.’
