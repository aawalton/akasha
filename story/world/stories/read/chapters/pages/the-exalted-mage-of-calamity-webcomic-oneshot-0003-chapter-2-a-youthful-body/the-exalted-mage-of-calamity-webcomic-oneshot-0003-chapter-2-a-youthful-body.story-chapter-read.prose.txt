Veronica groaned, waving her hand at something furry tickling her face. She almost drifted back to sleep once she heard it scurry away.

A few moments passed as she lie in bliss.

Then, a sharp gasp tore from her lips as her eyes flew open a moment later; her chest heaved as if she had just taken her first breath in years.

Her eyes went wide, despite the haze and throbbing headache. She sat up and scanned the forest around her.

Enemies? Demons?

Wait... Forest?

Conscious again, she was greeted by a dense canopy of green; the sunlight peeked through in fractured beams through branches and leaves. She caught a glimpse of a squirrel climbing up a nearby tree.

“Why am I…” she murmured.

The next thing Veronica felt was the chill. A biting, invasive chill that prickled her skin and sent a shiver down her spine. She stirred, groaning softly, the damp forest floor beneath her digging into her arms and legs. The dirt was hard and packed, as if she’d been lying there for a while.

Her head throbbed as fragments of her memory surged forward: the device, the crackling energy, and the last surge of mana as she activated it. She remembered the demon’s laughter, her own blood pooling at her feet, and then—nothing.

Her fingers clawed into the hard, cool dirt beneath her. Her hands trembled as she stared at them, smaller and softer than they should have been, free of any calluses, scars, or wrinkles. She looked down at herself, her strangely younger, unmarred body.

She placed her hand cautiously but delicately on her chest, feeling for the wound left by the demon Xertal, in case her mind was playing tricks on her.

But no. It was gone.

Her chest was whole and complete, skin unbothered, as if she had never been impaled in the first place.

She leaned back. A smile formed on her face. A small chuckle came before turning into a series of laughs. It worked. It actually worked, she thought, echoing in her mind.

At only thirty-four, the burden of her twin mana cores had aged her drastically. Her body mirrored that of a woman thirty to forty years her senior. But at this moment? That wasn’t the case at all.

“Martin, you lunatic. You actually did it.” She chuckled under her breath. “No. I guess I’m the lunatic for relying on such a device.”

All the hope she had placed in his invention—the gamble had paid off.

Xertal’s ugly, demonic form came to her mind. She grinned, clenching her hand slowly before raising a fist to the sky. Her arm didn’t crack. “Serves you right, stupid bastard. Just you wait until I wipe out your army.”

Regressing to her younger body was a surprise. Perhaps it was even better that she had. It gave her more years of youth to work with. However, there was something else of concern she needed to deal with.

Her clothes were missing. No robes, no undergarments, nothing. The device had sent her hurtling back in time and placed her naked, sitting in a forest.

She shivered before swinging an arm across her chest, covering herself up. Her gaze darted between the trees for any sign of movement. No one seemed to be nearby; there was no rustling, no voices, no wandering eyes. Her eyes scanned the forest floor, trying to find if her clothes had been scattered anywhere. There was nothing but twigs and leaves.

“Great,” she muttered, her voice trembling from the cold and disbelief. “What the hell is going on…? Where are my robes? And…”

She took another quick scan around the ground. “Seriously… my staff, too?” She scowled. Had she seriously lost everything on her person?

Why am I in the forest and not in the middle of Annesheim? she thought.

“Though I should be glad I didn’t wake up in the middle of the city square…” she said, chiming in on her own thoughts.

She stilled for a moment, pressing a fist to her head. Her headache was killing her. Wincing, her eyes trailed down her body again, and she noticed something odd. She let go of her chest and probed around her body again. Pressing her thumbs to the top of her thighs.

“No… not here,” she murmured. For an entire minute, she pressed down hard on her skin: neck, shoulders, chest, stomach, hips, thighs, calves, even her feet.

“Where...?”

Medusa’s curse had flared when she was a young child. Back then, they started as small spots, like pebbles buried beneath her veins. But it was obvious she wasn’t that young currently. She must’ve been in her late teens or early twenties by appearance.

Surely… surely it’s…

Had she forgotten?

Little by little, the realization crept in.

“No… there’s no…” she whispered, scarcely able to speak from disbelief. Her eyes watered, but she refused to let a tear fall.

It made sense now why she was so at peace while sleeping. She hadn’t noticed it due to the mental fog pounding in her brain. But now she realized it—the constant pain she suffered beneath Medusa’s curse—it was gone.

A wide, genuine smile spread across her face. She laughed. A long, genuine laugh. Her mind reeled at the absurdity of it all. The curse that had ruined her life was gone. Cured. All the suffering—all the effort she’d endured just to survive, erased in an instant.

Then something interrupted her thoughts.

A voice. It echoed everywhere around her.

[Veronica Everwells.]

She jolted, heart leaping up in her chest. Eyes wide, she spun around instinctively, scanning the trees, the underbrush, and the canopy above. But the forest remained quiet and empty.

Veronica pulled her arms to her sides, elbows bent, pulling on the mana within her core and—

What?! My… my mana!

It was… gone. Not completely gone—but it was barely a drop of the reserves she had as a 10th-tier mage.

“Who’s there?!” she called out, voice anxious, tinged with irritation. “What did you do to me?!”

She called on whatever small reserves she did have, completely alert.

[Do not be alarmed, Veronica.]

The voice was calm, precise, but more importantly, mechanical. It seemed unbothered by her flustered state. The voice was also strangely reminiscent. It reminded her of someone. Of something—resonating deep within her head.

“Wait… this voice. You’re…” she muttered, slowly lowering her arms.

She recognized the voice. But it sounded different. It wasn’t spoken aloud, but directly into her mind.

[Your assumption is correct. I am a Sentient Arcane Guidance Engine. You may refer to me as SAGE.]

“…Why are you in my head?” And actually—where’s the device you were in?”

[Following synchronization during temporal displacement, my core matrix was integrated directly into your mana lattice. Physical housing is no longer required.]

She blinked. It took her a second to even begin unpacking that.

“You’re… in me?” she asked slowly. “Like… part of me now? What type of joke is that?”

[Affirmative. I now operate as a fused magical-intelligence entity.][Current status: passive support mode.][Awaiting directives.]

Veronica’s breath stopped before letting out a sigh of relief. She dropped her arms and relaxed the mana coiling within her body.

So, the device had activated. Not only that, but the process had bound it to her. This… Sage.

Of course. Of course it had. Because surviving a spear to the chest and waking up in the past with a restored body wasn’t complicated enough.

She covered her chest with her arm once more. “You couldn’t have at least kept my clothes intact?”

[Martin designed the device to only send organic matter back in time. Temporal reversion did not preserve non-biological materials. Clothing, accessories, and external possessions were not deemed essential for continuity. This includes the previous housing unit.]

“Not deemed essential?” she muttered. “My staff was worth as much as the royal treasury.” There was a pause. The forest rustled slightly with the passing wind.

Veronica shuddered. With a small amount of mana, she summoned a small flame between her hands to keep warm. It wasn’t exactly cold, but not having clothes made everything feel chilly.

“What exactly happened to me?” she asked. “Did I actually go back in time?”

[That is correct. Before your body died due to Xertal’s fatal blow, your soul and consciousness were successfully transferred to the past.]

She looked down at her palm. “That’s fine and all, but… Sage—what happened to my mana? Why… why is it all gone?”

She could feel her mana core. Her reserves were pitifully small. At her current state, she wouldn’t even be able to cast a tier-2 spell without concentrating.

[Magical attributes may have been reset to match this timeline's current body. Further analysis will be needed to confirm.]

“What? But I had more mana than this even when I was a child! I feel like this body hasn’t even been attuned to the first tier yet?”

She looked back at her arms, chest, and then the rest of her body. She wasn’t that young. Late teens, perhaps. Twenty, maybe? At the very least, she should have been a fifth-tier mage by now. But that obviously wasn’t the case given her minuscule mana reserves.

[Apologies, but there is insufficient data for me to offer a precise explanation. Temporal reversion has caused various anomalies within your body. This includes the absence of Medusa’s curse. The exact application of how time-travel affected you is still unknown, but may be the result of several combined phenomena.]

Her jaw clenched. “Insufficient? That just sounds like an excuse,” she muttered. “And of course. Why wouldn’t that be the trade? Strip away the curse—fantastic. But while you’re at it, just erase over thirty years of growth and training, too.”

She paused. “What about the ‘me’ of this time? Did I just disappear from the capital? Or am I a different person here—considering I don’t have Medusa’s curse?”

[I am unable to confirm how your arrival has affected this timeline’s Veronica Everwells. I cannot prove nor disprove any theories at this point in time without more information.]

“So… there could be two of me—the me of this timeline and I took over her body—or maybe I never even existed in the first place. Great. You don’t know this, don’t know that—is there anything you do know that can help me?

[Due to the loss of my previous housing unit, I have also lost the primary power source sustaining me. I am currently being powered by your own mana system and operating in low-power mode. Many of my functions are currently limited. I can, however, display your current status and attributes, if you would like.]

“Status?”

At the mention of the word, something jumped out at her. A transparent panel, hovering with information right before her eyes.

Name: Veronica EverwellsCurrent Tier: Tier-0 Mage-Primary Path: UnattunedSecondary Path: Unattuned-Mana: 200/200 MUMana Regeneration: 20 MU/hour-Quests:Attune to a path and achieve Tier-1Form your first set of mana rings

“Sage? What is this?” Veronica looked at the screen. It moved with her vision. Her hand even went through the projection without interrupting it. There wasn’t any magitech device powering the display—it was astonishing.

[These are your current stats that I have compiled, showing information I deem relevant for your knowledge.]

Her eyes narrowed as she read each line. Her brow creased as she read the portion about her mana. “What is MU?”

[MU stands for Mana Unit.]

“That metric doesn’t tell me anything,” she said.

[Apologies.]

And that was it. Just a simple apology, and no further elaboration. An MU was probably some fake metric that it had come up with.

She continued reading. “What are these quests you have listed?”

[These are what I suggest you work towards if your goal is to prevent the demon invasion.Martin designed my primary function to assist, and these quests, as outlined, will prove to be the best goals to strive towards so you can reach Tier-10 once more.]

Martin… always doing unnecessary things, she thought.

“Seriously? You’re telling me my life now comes with objectives? I don’t need something like that.”

[These quests will prove very valuable to you, as I have rewards available to you after their completion.]

Veronica arched an eyebrow. “What kind of rewards?”

[There are many quest rewards. These include new spell formulas, methods, or magical techniques. Such as triple casting or buffering spells.]

Veronica’s eyes went wide with shock. “Wait… you can help me triple cast? That’s an advanced technique that even I never learned how to do. People who can triple cast say it takes intense focus and abnormal mental strength. Much more than double-casting. Martin was actually holding out on me? Why can’t you just give that stuff to me now?”

[Many functions require more energy than is currently available in low-power mode. I am mostly an incomplete magitech intelligence that Martin integrated into the time reversion capsule at the last moment, along with the Innovation Society’s database of information. I was not originally supposed to assist you in this manner. Many of my assistance protocols were designed hastily.]

[As for the quest rewards: most quests will require me to stress-test your capabilities before they can be unlocked. Consider these not as quests, but more as prerequisites. I need sufficient data collection on your current growth state to perform the necessary upgrades safely and without issue.]

“Alright… if you say so.”

From what she gathered from that explanation—Sage was almost like a parasite. Or maybe… a symbiotic existence. Something that fed on her mana core while also providing benefits at the same time to its host.

She had several people who assisted her in the future. A personal chef who managed all of her meals. An assistant who reminded her of daily tasks and took care of finances. Most things she didn’t really need to think about. And now—she even received a helper in magical advancement? It seemed almost too good to be true. Perhaps she could indulge and tolerate it for the time being.

“Alright. Then what kind of rewards can you give me once I reach the 10th tier again?”

[The rewards are calculated as you progress and are not determined beforehand, except for a few select options. The 10th-tier is too far for an accurate reward estimation at this stage.][However, with 8% confidence, one such late-stage reward may include a new synthetic attunement path. One besides the main and secondary paths you choose. A tertiary path.]

“What?!” she exclaimed. “You can just make up a third attunement path?!”

[Hypothetically. Further growth and data are necessary to raise the confidence coefficient.]

Veronica’s mouth stayed wide open for a few moments longer, stunned at the statement. But it didn’t stay that way for long.

[Veronica, there appears to be a group of humans being attacked by a monster 400 meters east.]

“What?” Her eyes scanned the area again. She attempted to levitate and fly—but then clicked her tongue. She wasn’t high enough tier yet.

[Likelihood coefficient is at 62%. It appears they are losing and may require immediate assistance.]

Veronica stood instead, feet on the ground. Her expression turned serious. Her clothes could wait until later.

It had been so long since she’d seen survivors. She couldn’t just let people die purposely if she could help it.

“Lead the way.”
