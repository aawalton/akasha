I cross my arms over my chest, deeply unimpressed by the Tier four’s arrogance.

“I don’t appreciate being ordered around like I’m one of your minions. How about a thank you for bringing your mate back to you?” My tone is caustic.

The nunda gives a dismissive grunt.

You brought my mate back because I threatened you. I hardly think it deserves thanks. And you have brought her back in such poor condition that I should be taking it from your hide. The least you can do is to ensure she has no human bindings soiling her fur.

This arrogant cat.

“Do not overestimate yourself,” I tell him coldly. “The only reason I decided to go along with your demands was because I didn’t want to hurt Kalanthia’s mate while she was away – and I was concerned about Kalanthia herself.”

The nunda gives another dismissive grunt.

You were fearful of me. You did not wish to fight me. You are too weak to defeat me.

“I didn’t want to fight you,” I agree. “And I still don’t – though your attitude is certainly making that an increasingly tempting option. But if we must, then I will give the order. And you wouldn’t be the first Immortal we kill.”

The cowardly blob was hardly a good example of an Immortal, the nunda scoffs. Getting past its beasts was a challenge, I admit, but then once you were through its final defences, it was weak. He looks contemplative for a moment. Though it was surprisingly good at pretending to be dead. I thought I’d already killed it once before.

“Which by itself indicates the difference between us,” I tell him pointedly. He pulls up his lip and shows his teeth as the barb lands. I decide to twist the knife more firmly. “Anyway, the parasite controller isn’t the only Immortal we’ve killed. I’m sure Kalanthia could give you more details of that one since she was in the fight.”

The nunda looks at his mate in surprise. I sense how troubled she feels at the current conversation. Still, I’m gratified when she flicks her ears and sends an acknowledgement.

It was a challenging battle, but, indeed, Markus Wolfe and his companions fought an Immortal Wind Roc in its own territory – and won.

Only because you were there to help, the male nunda replied with confidence.

Another of the pack has become Enlightened since then, and she is powerful, Kalanthia replies, then hesitates uncharacteristically. And that means there would be at least two Enlightened fighting on his side, should you choose to attack.

The male nunda steps away from his mate in shock as her implications register.

You would fight against me…with him?

So would I, Lathani says defiantly, stepping forwards. She, along with the rest of my companions have been waiting quietly but attentively for the issue to be resolved – or deteriorate. I feel several of them itching to come forward and greet Kalanthia, but they’re holding themselves back for now. This is my pack – our pack – and sire or not, if you threaten it, we will fight.

Stay out of this, cub, the male nunda snaps at her – literally: his teeth click together loudly. This is not your business.

Kalanthia snarls and steps between him and Lathani, her lips pulled back.

Do not threaten my cub!

The male nunda snarls back at her.

Your cub is interfering in matters beyond her comprehension.

She is defending those she holds dear. As will I.

The male actually takes a step back. Because the emotional impression Kalanthia communicated is even clearer than the words I hear. Lathani isn’t the only one Kalanthia’s protecting, and the implication has rocked the more powerful nunda.

And then, that moment of shock passes. The nunda’s eyes begin to glow white again, and he lowers himself into a crouched position, his muscles tense and ears pinned back. He looks to be moments away from pouncing.

You protect a human? You have changed, Kalanthia, and not for the better.

I feel the words hit Kalanthia like knives and abruptly decide that enough is enough. The nunda said it himself – he believes that we’re too weak to be a threat to him. And it seems that even his mate and daughter aren’t enough to talk him around.

Let’s try something else.

While the conversation has been going on, I’ve been feeding mana into the ground little by little, preferring to take precautions that might not be needed than to be caught without them. Kalanthia may know what I’ve been doing, but if she does, she’s ignoring it – not helping, but not hindering, either.

It’s easy – the earth around here is already familiar with my signature. Now, I press a large chunk of my mana very quickly into the earth below the Tier four’s paws, oversaturating the area.

I feel it as the Tier four presses a touch more firmly on the earth in preparation to move. That's the moment I trigger my trap.

As if I’d previously dug a pit out from below the elephant-sized nunda, the earth disappears from beneath his feet. He drops with a startled snarl.

Not far – the pit is only as deep as his shoulders. But before he can leap out again, I direct the earth that was displaced to crash down on him like a wave, essentially burying him from the neck downwards.

He snarls furiously and his domain explodes outwards with his outrage.

All but Kalanthia and Bastet back away out of immediate range, the Tier ones actually driven to flee by the power of the domain. I’m knocked back several paces myself before I force my feet to stop moving.

Kalanthia! Release me! he roars. The sound echoes in my ears painfully.

I didn’t do anything, Kalanthia tells him, sounding vindicated. She looks pointedly at me.

Human? How dare you attack me! Release me immediately or suffer my wrath!

“Yeah. Not until you apologise to Kalanthia and Lathani for being an enormous arse, and agree to dropping the arrogance so we can have a decent conversation,” I tell him flatly.

He just snarls at me menacingly.

A human? Dictating terms to me?

It’s clearly a rhetorical question as his eyes brighten and abruptly the ground around him begins letting out sharp cracks and hisses of steam. Connected to it as I am, I feel how the sand within it turns to glass even as fragments of organic matter are charred to ash.

But the earth accepts the electrical charge with little change.

When he starts struggling, however, I feel the difference. The earth is more fragile, easier to break. Left to himself, he would escape in little time.

But I’m here. Pressing in more mana, I turn his efforts against him. Glass and rock combine to make even stronger formations, the holes left by escaping steam close up and become solid once more.

He roars with frustration again, but this time it’s more than just sound. A wave of something rattles through me like the most powerful bass speakers that I’ve ever listened to.

I clutch at my ears that have burst with pain, and, for a moment, I don’t know which side is up. Dizziness assails me, nausea crawling up my throat for a moment.

Stop it! I hear, even though I’m sure my eardrums have burst. I don’t want you to hurt him!

He has you under some sort of spell – or the Bond is affecting you more than you believe!

His ‘spell’ is that of trust. And affection. Nothing nefarious!

You cannot judge. I will free you, and then you will see.

Oh boy. This sounds familiar. Is leaping to conclusions and being unwilling to accept any other interpretation a nunda trait?

And just as back then with Lathani and Kalanthia, I figure there’s only one way to get through to him.

Hopefully he’s trapped enough to make it possible.

Forcing my eyes open, I find I’m lying on the ground near where I’d been standing. My view of the male nunda is blocked by a wall of spotted black and yellow fur. It takes a moment for that to resolve into Kalanthia.

Much as I appreciate her defence, it’s not what I need right now.

Pressing magic into my ears sees them heal quickly. With that, my sense of balance returns and I quickly push myself to my feet.

While I was out, the nunda has managed to get one paw free and is scrabbling at the stone around, trying to claw away the earth.

Concentrating, I urge the earth to recapture the paw. I’m very tempted to pull an inferno down on him – and I know Aingeal would be happy to help me – but I don’t actually want to hurt the nunda. Permanently, anyway.

The nunda snarls in frustration as the next time he scrabbles at the stone around his neck, his paw sinks straight into the stone – and then is covered by more.

His glowing white gaze meets mine.

Dominate.

The pressure that hits me like a tidal wave is unsurprising. In his usual state, I would need around fourteen more levels with all stats dedicated to Willpower to have a chance. But the nunda is blindly enraged, and trapped – both harm his Willpower.

As a result, I’m able to stay in the Battle of Wills. Just.

Like Kalanthia before him, Tilathis is able to communicate with me even though I haven’t taken a single step forward.

And now you show your true colours, human! he accuses. I feel the emotions swirling around him even from here – outrage, fury, betrayal, and the faintest touch of fear. Not content with entrapping two of my family, you must have us all. He presses at me with sudden vigour. It’s precise, and very deliberate. I suppose I shouldn’t be surprised that a Tier four is able to direct his soul even in this kind of space.

“Not at all,” I reply, digging my hands and feet into the ground to stubbornly stay in place. “I’m not intending on Binding you.” I shove my sincerity at him.

The nunda scoffs, but I hear the hint of uncertainty in his tone, my blatant emotions proving my words.

Then why pull me into this space? There are only two ends – either you defeat me or, far more likely, I defeat you. And then we are back to where we were before – and I will kill you for what you have done to my family.

“Those are not the only options,” I contradict him. His emotions swirl angrily in response.

Then enlighten me, he challenges.

“This is a conversation, nothing more. And by the end of it, perhaps you will have changed your mind and no longer wish to kill me.”

His amusement is harsh and savage.

And what makes you think that is possible?

“Because here, you can feel my emotions far more clearly than on the outside. You’ll know when I speak the truth or when I lie,” I tell him simply.

The emotions around me slow their powerful pulsing for a moment. Then they heighten again.

Then speak. What truth do you wish me to believe?

“Two truths. First, Kalanthia and Lathani are both here entirely of their free will,” I start. Of everything I wish to say, this seems to be the most important. “I have not knowingly manipulated them to make or maintain our Bonds. Although I might direct them in battle, out of it, anything they do is of their choice.”

Of course, they might choose to do something to please me, or to not displease me, but since that’s just part and parcel of having an amicable relationship with someone, I don’t consider that remotely as something limiting their free will.

And your other truth? The male nunda asks, his emotions rippling wildly, but not giving me any indication of what he’s thinking except that it’s tumultuous.

“That we could beat you if it comes to open battle.”

This one earns a bright flash of disbelief tinged with contempt.

You are too weak. Is that not why you pulled me here? Your bindings shattered under my strength; you were yourself sent crumpling to the ground before my roar. Yet you believe you can defeat me? You are deluded.

“No. That isn’t why I brought you here,” I tell him coldly and deliberately. “It’s because I care what Kalanthia and Lathani think, and, right now, I don’t believe they want you dead, for all their annoyance with you. Because that is what would most likely happen if we faced you.”

My sheer confidence seems to have dented his own, finally. I press on, needing to hammer home that this Tier four is no longer the biggest threat in this neighbourhood before it comes to open warfare that will hurt all of us.

“You think we’re weak because I couldn’t go head to head with you alone and win. But you forget the strongest part of being human – unlike nundas, we don’t hunt alone. Even your strength would give way under a group of Earth Mages working together. Even your health would run out under hundreds of poison-laced blades and arrows. Even your magic would fail if put to the test against multiple, layered shields.

“You indicated that getting through the parasite controller’s hordes was challenging for you. We slaughtered them with barely any casualties.”

I pause to gauge how my lecture has been received. For the first time, the hesitance overpowers the rage in his aura, and he’s stopped actively trying to wash me out of the Battle of Wills space.

“I don’t want to fight you,” I tell him more quietly, shoving my sincerity at him. “I would much rather have an amicable relationship of equals – I don’t expect you to be a subordinate, even. But if I can’t have that, then I’ll make sure that I finish whatever fight we start together – making use of all the resources I have available to me.”

For a long moment, the Battle of Wills is silent. I can feel it beginning to fracture – we don’t have much time left. But there’s time enough for this important thought process.

Finally, I feel the emotions swirl around me once more, and then the nunda sends his response. It’s not quite what I was expecting.
