Twelve days until the tournament.Twenty-nine days until the invasion.

The Shores of Glory were as Gary remembered, which was astutely natural since it had been less than a week since his last time here. It just felt like a year.

People congested the streets, swimming around carts, carriages, and mounts. The first two were pulled by glyans, the Tower’s equivalent of oxen. When a powerful climber rode on his mount, however, they weren’t using glyans. Gary saw a man dressed like a roman centurion on top of a black-maned horse with fiery red eyes. There was an ascetic monk wearing nothing but a loincloth who rode a turtle. A lion-human warrior wore golden armor and rode a spectacular lion through the busy streets.

A mount was as much as a sign of strength as it was of status. All pedestrians gave these climbers a wide berth. Gary did the same. They were definitely at a higher realm than him, but he wondered how they stacked up against the Heaven Reaching deacons. Would Branch Elders ride mounts?

Unlike regular floors, which high-level cultivators had no reason to roam, the city floors were melting pots of different species, levels, and clans. The average pedestrian was at the late Energy Harnessing realm. Many were at the early Birth of Aura, while those above that were usually clan disciples or rogue climbers. However, while Gary could finally be considered strong among pedestrians, there was no shortage of stronger climbers walking around. They blazed in his perception like beacons of aura. Every street held someone above Birth of Aura. Occasionally, Gary saw people he suspected as being at an even higher level. These people were mostly on mounts or inside carriages. When they traveled on foot, passers-by gave them a wide berth, creating islands of emptiness in the busy streets.

Occasionally, flying ships of all makes and kinds passed overhead, and Gary could only marvel.

City floors are a wonder… he thought, sighing inwardly. There are so many strong people… Then again, who knows how many clans have branches around the city, and each should have several Branch Elders. Maybe climbers above the third realm aren’t such a rare sight here.

He stuck to the main streets as he approached the colossal building in the center of the city—the Empyrean Steps, a massive skyscraper with ninety-three floors. Most skyships anchored on docks that stretched out of the tower as balconies. Gary could barely see its base through the massive plaza around it, which was positively swarming with people. Dozens walked through the tower’s wide gates every second.

Thankfully, that wasn’t his destination. Gary watched the sun set behind the Empyrean Steps for a moment, then turned right into the Bad Babes: an atmospheric bar with two floors of brothel and stripping over it. Gary stuck to the ground floor, quickly locating his table on the garden outside.

A guard gave him a cold glance as he walked by—he must have been present when the Brothers almost tore down the place to get Gary.

“Chloe! Asim!” he exclaimed, rushing to greet his friends.

“Gary!” they replied. Everyone hugged. They held him tight.

“I’m so glad you’re okay,” Chloe said after they sat down.

“I told you I’m bulletproof.”

“But monsters don’t use bullets,” Asim said.

“Yet,” Chloe said. “How was the expedition, Gary? Did anything happen because of the invasion quest?”

“The monsters went amok. There was a hundred-foot titan sprinting our way. We barely managed to get out. Also, we were attacked by the floor’s strongest boss the moment we appeared.”

“That’s harsh,” Asim said.

“I’m just glad you’re okay. Do you know what prompted the invasion? The disciples at my clan are burning their midnight oil to figure it out,” Chloe said.

Gary winked at her. “No idea,” he said.

She raised her brows. Asim rolled his eyes. “I knew it,” he said.

“How’s the thing going?” Gary asked.

That made them serious. Though they’d picked an isolated table and the music ensured they wouldn’t be overheard, everyone leaned in.

“It’s going fantastic,” Chloe said. She broke a wide smile. “For the first time, I actually believe we can make them pay.”

“What do you mean?” Gary asked.

“We sold, Gary. We sold big,” Asim said.

Gary’s eyes lit up. “How much?”

“Guess.”

“Fifty drops?”

“More like a hundred fifty.”

“What!?” Gary struggled to keep his voice down. “Are you serious?”

“Asim managed to melt six of the boss balls you gave him,” Chloe said. “At thirty drops per ball, that’s a hundred and eighty drops. I sold one-fifty at thirty stones per drop, so we made four thousand five hundred aura stones. Your share is one thousand, three hundred, and fifty. Asim keeps forty percent to cover his expenses and we get thirty each.”

“Plus ten drops out of the thirty we didn’t sell,” Asim said. “We’ll give you your share in an alley after.”

Gary took some time to form words. “Guys, this… This is a lot! How did you even get people to pay thirty stones per drop?”

“I decided to start high and lower the price as needed, but people bought,” Chloe said with a shrug.

“Who bought!?”

“Some friends and acquaintances in the Academy. To be honest, over half went to Marla.”

“Marla?”

“Yeah.”

“Marla Jackson, the drafter?”

“That’s her.”

“Where did she find the money?”

“No idea. She’s trying to build the Earth Alliance she spoke of, and apparently she’s doing well. She wants those drops as rewards to incentivize her members to perform.”

“Tens of thousands of drafters survived the Floor of Trials. If she managed to recruit even part of them, and she taxes their job income or accepts contributions, it wouldn’t be hard to gather the three thousand aura stones she gave us,” Asim said.

“She must be running low on funds, though. She said I shouldn’t consider her for the next batch,” Chloe said.

Gary scratched his head. “Well, I like making money, but it feels like we’re skinning the drafters. Can’t we give them a discount?”

“That’s what I told her! Starting from the next batch, I can sell to her for twenty stones per drop. I wanted the first transaction at full price to establish goodwill,” Chloe said.

“She really needs to be careful. Distributing Muscle Sap is illegal,” Gary said.

“She’s sharp, Gary. I’m sure she’ll make it work. Besides, even if they catch her, the only person who knows who we are is Marla herself, and she only knows me. They’d need to get through her alliance, then through her, then through me to get you.”

“Thank you. You’re taking a risk,” Gary said, but Chloe shrugged.

“It comes with handling distribution. Truth be told, I never thought I’d become a drug dealer, but I’ve seen Breaking Bad.”

“It’s not drugs. It’s perfectly safe, beneficial pills that are only illegal to sell because the clans want to hoard them. We’re doing a good thing,” Gary said.

“I still have plenty of balls to melt. Production won’t be an issue. Distribution, on the other hand…” Asim said.

Chloe bit her lip. “It’s not easy to find customers. Besides Marla, I only sold to seven people, demanding that each buys a minimum of five stones. It’s not worth the risk otherwise. It’s hard to scale while maintaining secrecy, and I need to study, too. I just don’t have the time. I want to bring some friends into the game.”

“Friends?”

“Yeah. They’re students at the Deep Blue Academy, like me. They belong to a cult, but don’t let that scare you—they’re just a bunch of hippies who’ve found the Tower’s equivalent of weed and are smoking the shit out of it.”

“Excuse me?” Gary said.

She laughed. “It’s called the Cult of the Broken Compass. Their holy texts are doodles, and they follow the vibes to see what the cult wants them to do next. They basically sit around all day and claim that relaxing helps them harmonize with fate.”

“Wait. What do you mean they follow the vibes?” Gary asked.

“They literally just go where they feel like it. Bob stopped a robbery once because the vibes told him to go for a walk. They’re fucking crazy.”

“And you want to trust those guys with our potentially lethal operation?” Gary asked in disbelief.

“I know how it sounds, but I trust them. They’re good people. Their cult wants them to do good deeds for others, and I can see they enjoy it. They volunteer and stuff. Also, their confidentiality is made of steel. I’ve been asking them about the cult’s initiation rites and who introduced them, and it’s like talking to a wall. They just smile and nod. They also know all kinds of people.”

“You know that sounds completely nonsensical.”

“I know. This is where you need to trust me, Gary. I’ve done my homework.”

He scratched his head, still hesitant. Asim spoke up.

“I don’t mind. Chloe is handling the distribution. She should do whatever she likes.”

“What if we get caught?”

“If we die, we die. You can’t partner with people and handle their end of the business.”

Gary sighed. “You’re right. I trust you, Chloe. Do whatever you think is best.”

“Thanks!” she beamed at him. “I’ll just use one guy for starters. His name is Bob.”

“I was almost relieved before you mentioned his name.”

“What’s wrong with Bob? Gary isn’t any better.”

“Gary Steel. What’s his last name, Cultist? Bob the Cultist?”

“I have no idea actually. His friends just call him Boooob.”

“The weeded like it’s 1969 friends?”

“Yes.”

“Oh God,” Gary said. “By the way, those doodles you mentioned remind me of something. Can you take a look at this?”

He reached into his pouch to retrieve a booklet. It was about the size of his palm and only an inch wide, with no visible author name. The title was, “Rebellion from Below.”

“I took this from the cultivator I killed in the Floor of Trials,” Gary said, handing Chloe the booklet. She flipped through it quickly.

“Yep, these are the doodles. Your victim was Cult of the Broken Compass, or he killed one of them and stole his belongings. I haven’t seen this book before, though. I don’t think it’s part of the cult.”

“So a cult member had this random book and was scribbling at the edges. Either your dude, or someone who got killed by your dude,” Asim said.

“He did seem weird, but he was nothing like the cultists you described. He was a sadistic, lunatic piece of shit,” Gary said.

“Then he probably killed someone from the cult and kept the book because he found it funny,” Asim said.

“Or, the cult goes deeper than we believe,” Gary said.

“I don’t think so,” Chloe said. “Can I keep this? I’ll show it to Bob, see what he thinks.”

“Sure. It’s not like I was reading it.”

“That’s all for work. Tell us about the expedition, Gary!” Asim exclaimed.

Gary finally relaxed. He went into the expedition details, hiding nothing from his friends. If anyone was listening in, they were doomed anyway.
