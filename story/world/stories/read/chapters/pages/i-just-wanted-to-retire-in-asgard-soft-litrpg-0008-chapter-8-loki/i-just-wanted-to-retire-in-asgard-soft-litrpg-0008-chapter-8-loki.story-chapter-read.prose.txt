"NO!"

Odin jerked awake, eyes wide, searching the dark while the nightmare still clung to the edges of his mind. He could still see it, still feel it. Roots of gold choking the sky. A throne cracking open. A voice, echoing and broken.

Freya's hand was already on his chest. She had sat up beside him, her straight blond hair tousled from being jolted out of sleep.

"Breathe," she said, low and steady in the dark. "You're here. You're still here, with me."

"Freya." His voice cracked. "My love. You're still alive."

"Yes, my love, I am." She looked at him, the mightiest of the gods, still shaking with fear.

She eased him back against the pillows and used a little magic to settle him. Then she checked his pulse, his throat, his temperature, and when she finished, she knew where this was heading. Her face didn't change, but the skin around her eyes tightened.

"That bad, huh?" Odin said.

"Yes, love. You're getting weaker, a little more every day. At this rate..."

"Thor needs to stop playing errand boy for that mortal." His voice was rough. "He has to take the throne before..." He broke off, one hand pressed to his heavy chest.

"No." Freya answered right away, and quietly. "After thousands of years, he finally looks happy. Like he's found his equal. I won't take that from him. Neither of us should."

He turned to her. "I see. You'll always be a mother first and a queen second," he said, his fingers brushing her cheek.

She dropped her gaze. "I'm sorry, dear. I just can't."

"I know." Odin stared at the ceiling. Then he snapped his fingers, and the bedroom vanished.

They stood in the space between realms, where Yggdrasil grew, the great tree that bound everything together. It rose out of the deep dark and climbed through every realm, golden light pulsing along each branch and root, nine threads of light stretching outward.

Freya had never entered this place, not once in all her eons of life.

"This is..."

"The beginning and the end," Odin said. "Everything starts from nothing. Do you feel it? That power? It's the source of mine. The Odinforce."

She did. The air hummed with belief turning into power.

Odin lifted his hand. At the knuckle of one finger, the skin was no longer flesh. It was the living gold of the tree, smooth and bright, spreading a little more every time he looked away.

"It's power, and it's responsibility. And it's consuming me," he said, without drama. Just the truth. "So I'm asking you, my love. Prepare Thor to rule."

Freya's eyes stayed on the finger. "And Loki? You know what she'll do when that seat is empty."

Odin didn't answer. He could only look at the tree. He wanted to tell her everything would be all right. He even wanted to pray. But who could he pray to, when everyone prayed to him?

Loki stood outside the workshop door in black leather armor, dressed like she was ready for battle. Her hair was dark and curly, her skin pale as fresh snow.

A few dwarves who'd come to visit took one look at the goddess and hurried off the other way. Loki couldn't help laughing.

She waved her hand, and the door should have simply opened. It didn't. No matter what she tried, it wouldn't budge.

"Hmm. Interesting..."

She knocked once. Polite. Almost playful.

No answer.

She knocked again, slower this time, and let a thread of her dark magic slip into the wood to listen. It crept through the grain and slithered across the floor, and there he was: Barry, ears covered, bent over his table, working.

"Let's see how well you protect yourself, engineer."

Her green eyes flared brighter. The thread hardened into a bullet and shot toward Barry's back. Even at that microscopic distance, the jacket's field answered instantly, with surgical precision.

Loki felt it. A sudden darkness, like a blade drawn an inch from her mind. Before it could reach her, she severed the thread and pulled herself back.

Thor's warning had been clear: further contact would be lethal. But this was something else. It was as if Barry had commanded death itself and given it one simple order: touch him, and die.

She'd never been especially interested in playing fair, anyway.

So she came at it from two angles. She shaped a construct out of Surface's voice, down to the dwarf's nervous cadence, and sent it around to knock at the service entrance. The main door she would take herself.

Inside, Barry sat at the console. Faint portal residue still shimmered near the wall where Thor had last come through.

He tinkered with the remote, adjusting the System's settings. He knew he was playing with something bigger than himself, but he didn't like a system that asked questions. Questions meant it was building a model of him, and he hadn't decided yet whether he wanted to be modeled.

Knock, knock.

"Sir Barry, it's me. Surface!"

Barry ignored it completely.

A second knock came from the service entrance in the wall, and Surface's voice with it, thin and nervous. "Sir Barry? You there? Brought the calibration rods. The ones from the forge."

He hadn't asked for any calibration rods. That was strange.

He pulled up the feed. The service entrance showed nothing but the corridor. The main door showed a woman in dark clothes, using Surface's voice.

Loki...

"Ha. Cute." Barry reached for something inside his jacket. He could already feel her invisible threads spreading everywhere.

Outside, Loki felt a thread snap.

"Oh, interesting."

She dismissed the Surface voice with a flick of her fingers. If she used her Odinforce, she could force the door open, but she knew facing the man while he wore that jacket would be the wrong move. And Thor would be here within minutes. The confrontation would turn ugly.

She preferred games to fights.

"I'll give up for now."

She walked toward the Bifrost access point, leaving something like a golden microscope behind her, along with a note of just two words: A gift.

She wouldn't force the door today. She'd wait for the day it opened on its own and welcomed her in.

Thor arrived twenty minutes later, carrying a case of materials from the forge and a list of questions about structural tolerances under prolonged System load. Sci-fi words, but he was starting to understand them.

As he approached the door, something caught his eye: a golden object that looked a lot like the things inside Barry's workshop. He picked it up and knocked on the main door.

It slid open on its own and shut right behind him.

"You left one of your things outside..."

"Someone tried the door." Barry didn't turn around, still busy with the remote. "Cleared every barrier you placed and slipped right through."

"Who could do that? Those enchantments were woven by the finest witches under my own mother's command."

"Right. Also, your sister does a great Surface impression. Give her that much."

"Loki."

"Yeah."

Thor set the microscope on the table and pressed a hand down on its stand. The table groaned under his palm like soft metal as low, steady currents hummed under his fingers.

"She'll try again."

"Hey, hey, go easy. I'm the one doing the fixing around here, not you." Barry finally turned, spotted the golden scope, and took it. "And what do we have here? A microscope?"

"Don't. It might be cursed. She's starting her games again, and I even warned her not to."

Barry ignored him, pulled out his scanner, and swapped its magic chips for his new crystal ones.

"Hmm, let's see," he said, running it over the scope.

"What are you doing?"

"Good and evil are just feelings we decide to trust."

"Yeah?"

Barry looked over the readings and smiled a little. He pulled the crystal chips out and slotted them into a second, much bigger scanner with several smaller ones attached.

"Do you think anyone could build something that lets us see evil, or goodness, with our own eyes?" he asked, converting the machine's insides into something else.

"Huh? That would be imposs—"

"Just a rhetorical question, Thor. And yes, it can be built. You just have to be a genius."

He finished the new scanner, set the golden microscope beneath it, and switched on the whole rig. All seven scanners swept over the microscope at once.

A thin sheet of paper printed out. Barry took it and read.

"Oh, look at this. Your sister gave me a scope that could, in theory, let me see all the way into the nanoverse, while slowly driving me insane every time I used it. Crafty."

Thor snatched up the golden scope and crushed it on the spot.

"I'm sorry."

"Don't be. Not worth it. Say sorry to your sister. Must have taken her some effort to make something like this..."

Thor walked back to the sofa and sat down heavily. "You don't get it. All she wants is chaos. Nothing but chaos. And I'm sure she'll stop at nothing to break you now..."

Barry smiled. "Then tell her to bring her A game. Not this curse bullshit."
