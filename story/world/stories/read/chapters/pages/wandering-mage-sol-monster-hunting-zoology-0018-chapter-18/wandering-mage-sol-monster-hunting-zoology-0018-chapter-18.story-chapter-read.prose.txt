Tressa shrieked with delight as she zipped through the cavern tunnel, a horde of giant scorpions just behind them.

“Hahahahaha!” she cried, her feet levitating several inches off the ground as she clutched a sticky orb beneath one arm, and wind whipped past her face. “Isn’t this exciting?”

“Exciting?” Briadon shouted. “Are you insane, elf?”

His hooves hammered against the stone as he skidded around a sharp bend, nearly slamming shoulder-first into the wall. Lisha followed close behind, claws clicking against the ground, two milky-white orbs tucked awkwardly beneath each arm.

Tressa’s light spell, Lumas, darted ahead of them, with several small orbs bouncing along the tunnel walls and throwing frantic shadows in every direction.

Behind them, the Rock Scorpions screamed.

There were several of them, each nearly as large as a wagon, their pale brown exoskeletons streaked with black and gray to blend with the underground stone. Their many legs scraped and pounded against the tunnel floor as they charged, pincers snapping, tails raised high.

Tressa hugged her stolen prize tighter, the orb squishing against her ribs.

She laughed even harder. “Come on! This is so much fun!”

“You got a messed up sense of fun!” Briadon snapped as he huffed, running forward. He ducked under a rock jutting from the ceiling so his horns wouldn't catch on it.

Tressa rotated her body and thrust a hand out, casting a spell without her staff. A blast of air hit the scorpion that was coming up close behind Lisha. The monster was thrown back by the spell, and it slammed into the one running behind it. Several shrieks of agitation came from the monsters as the two bottlenecked the tunnel, but soon the chase was on again, though Tressa’s spell bought them some time.

“There!” Lisha yelled.

Up ahead of them was rays of sunlight coming through the entrance of the scorpion den.

They burst out of the cave and back into the sunlight. Lisha and Briadon slid to a stop as Tressa softly touched down on the grass. They turned to see the scorpions stop at the mouth of the dark cave, staring at them, clashing their pincers together in anger but daring not to come into the light of the sun, as it would burn their soft exoskeletons. They clicked their massive pincers several more times in rage, then turned around and headed back into the tunnel, knowing the eggs were lost.

“Haahaha, take that!” Tressa said, raising a middle finger at the scorpions. She had learned that little gesture from Briadon.

Lisha set her eggs down and wheezed. “I'm so glad… you can cast spells without a catalyst,” she huffed.

“Thanks, but I only do small spells. Others, I have to use staff. But wasn't that fun?” she asked, setting her own eggs down on the ground.

“Once again, how the hell was that fun!?” Briadon snapped as he tried to breathe.

Tressa fell back into the grass and burst out laughing as she spread her arms out, a wide smile spread across her face. She could never have this much fun back home. If she were still there, she would have spent hours on her knees in the temple or trying to restore burned books and studying to be a lady of the council, none of which suited her at all.

“You're insane, Tressa. You actually are insane,” Briadon gasped. “But if you're having this much fun, the elven capital must be boring.”

Tressa looked over at him. “You have no clue. If I was home, my nose would get buried in study,” she said in a thick accent while speaking in the common tongue. “Father would never approve of what I do now.”

“Your pronunciation is getting better,” Lisha commented. “And I'm glad to know that you are having this much fun.”

“Not the time, Lisha,” Briadon said. “How about the fact she almost got us killed?” He finally turned to Tressa.

“I did not.” Tressa shot up, crossing her legs, trying to defend herself.

“Yes, you did! If you hadn't gotten distracted by the damn scorpions, we would have been in and out quickly!” He jabbed his finger at her and snorted through his snout.

“I do nothing to wake them! You the one stomping ‘round like a damn storm. Your hooves were far louder than sketching scorpions. You walk ‘round like boom boom boom!” Tressa snapped, forgetting some of the common tongue in her anger at his accusation.

“Enough!” Lisha yelled, cutting off their argument. “What's done is done. We can sit and blame each other all day, or we can get these back to town and collect our bounty.”

Briadon huffed at Tressa, and she flipped him off. He growled and flipped her off back, and soon the small elf was practically nose-to-nose with the massive minotaur as they both growled with a middle finger in each other’s faces.

Lisha rolled her eyes. Sometimes Tressa and Briadon seemed to butt heads more than necessary. But it was still entertaining as much as it was annoying.

Tressa turned to Lisha. “Thank you.”

Lisha blinked. “For what?”

“For saving me.”

“You saved us back there by blasting that scorpion just in time. The fact you can use magic without a catalyst once again surprises me.”

“I can only do small spells I am most familiar with. Wind magic comes easiest, and my light spell I create. Lumas. It's hard sun times.”

“You mean sometimes,” Briadon said.

“That's what I said,” Tressa said.

He shook his head, giving up, as Lisha was the better teacher when helping Tressa speak the common tongue.

They picked up the eggs and headed back to town. It had been a little over six months since Tressa left home, and Lisha had been regularly teaching her the common tongue.

“Wait! I just realized something!” Lisha said. “Tressa. You do still study.”

“What?”

“You know I have you learning the common tongue. And even out here, you're still studying as if you are in the elven capital.”

Briadon suddenly burst out laughing as Tressa made a face in disgust.

“She got you there, elf. Not to mention constantly studying random things out here.”

“That research, not study!”

She stuck her tongue out at Briadon, annoyed, and flipped him off again. He was starting to regret teaching her that, as it had quickly become her favorite gesture, especially when aimed at him.

“But I'm better than before,” she said, ignoring Briadon's glare and turning to look at Lisha.

“Fair, but you still have a ways to go. When you get angry or too excited, you mix elvish and the common tongue together. We'll need to work on that.”

Tressa groaned as now she was going to have to do even more work.

“I’m trying best.”

“No, it's ‘I'm trying my best,’” Lisha corrected.

“Must I say ‘my’?”

“Yes. It's proper speech. The more you learn, the easier it will get. It's important so that you can talk to merchants, the guild, and negotiate with clients effectively.”

“Hmph.” She made a face that showed she was annoyed by the fact and she wanted to cut corners but knew Lisha was right.

After a few minutes of each of them resting from the life-or-death situation, they stood, picked up the eggs, and started walking back to town through the trees and thick brush, as most of the area was heavily wooded.

“Damn, I can't tell what's worse, this forest or the one around Falidorn.” Briadon groaned as he tried to carefully maneuver around the roots and thick undergrowth, ducking his head as he did so his horns wouldn't get caught on any branches.

“You know, it does seem a bit more overgrown,” Lisha agreed.

Tressa looked around to see tons of ferns, moss, vines, and roots. A twig snapped under her foot, and she jumped. The other two looked at her and laughed.

“Guess we still on edge,” Tressa chuckled nervously.

“And you call me the stomper.” Briadon burst out laughing.

They all laughed.

After about an hour, they made it to the main road and headed toward the city of Ligis.

Lisha let out a sigh of relief as her clawed feet touched the rough dirt road and not the tangled undergrowth of the forest. Ahead, they could see the city gates far in the distance.

Her tongue flicked as she said, “We're lucky there weren't any monsters on the way back. That would…”

“Lisha?” Briadon asked, looking at her.

A low growl came from their left.

“You've got to be kidding me,” Lisha groaned.

“Just had to say something, didn't you?” Briadon said as he set the eggs down. He slung his club off his back and held it in both hands. The monster hadn’t appeared yet but they could hear the low growl from the bushes.

Lisha set her eggs down next to his and pulled out her curved scimitar, as she had left her main weapon, her massive halberd, back at the inn thinking it'd be too cumbersome in the scorpion caves.

Tressa stepped back, placed her eggs next to Lisha's and Briadon's, and used the staff calling spell to summon her ashy white staff. “Hehe, so, um, what is that sound?” she asked.

“Not sure yet. Just stay behind me,” Lisha said as she readied her sword. Each had their back to the others as they created a small triangle to protect one another, as they had no idea what had made the sound or where the monster would come from.

Tressa's eyes darted around the forest, looking for anything that might stand out. She swallowed, gripping her staff tightly, when suddenly—

A rabbit with a horn on its head jumped from the bushes. It stopped and scratched its ear, then looked up at the group, its red eyes staring at them like it couldn't care less.

“Geh!” Tressa jumped.

“Horned rabbit,” said Lisha. “Not too big of a threat.”

“Unless it's in groups,” Briadon corrected.

Tressa set her staff down, quickly pulled out her notebook, and began sketching.

“You really shouldn't be doing that right now,” Briadon said as he looked down at her, his club in his hands.

“Shut up. This might be only chance,” she snapped, crouching down and looking from the rabbit to her book as fast as she could, hoping it wouldn't run off before she could finish at least a rough sketch.

It jumped a few feet to the side and stopped to sniff, then looked back over at them. Tressa had heard of these but hadn't seen one yet. Its fur was light brown and gray, its eyes were red, with a single silver horn extended from its forehead. The buck tooth rabbit teeth were a little sharper than normal, almost like little fangs. Compared to a normal rabbit, it was a bit larger, about the size of a small dog.

“So what should we do with it?” Briadon asked, leaning on his club as he stared at the little monster. He was tired of Tressa and her antics of stopping to sketch every little thing. But he guessed that was to be expected, as she had never really been outside the forests around Falidorn.

“The question is, are there more nearby, and was it attracted to the eggs?” Lisha motioned to the six large eggs next to Tressa, who was now down on her on her knees closely observing the horned rabbit as she sketched.

“Hmmm. You think it's a scout?”

“Possibly.”

“Wait a scout?” Tressa asked, looking up.

“Yeah, sometimes horned rabbits travel in groups of about ten to twenty-five. And they sometimes send out scouts, probably like this one here, to find food,” Lisha explained, as the rabbit inched closer and closer with its nose twitching.

Tressa quickly scribbled down notes of what Lisha just said.

“They eat meat?”

“Yeah. They eat meat and plants.”

“Omnivore,” Tressa said as she wrote it down. “Sends out scouts.” A nod came from Tressa as she wrote, then tapped her pen under her chin and muttered in elvish, “They must be quite clever, and perhaps have a hierarchy…”

“Mind not doing that?” Briadon said.

“I'm going to, as we still have a day left to get the eggs back and town is there. So no, I'm going to take notes. What happens if we kill the scout before it get back?” Tressa looked up at Lisha, a terrifying glimmer now in her eyes.

“Not sure. Probably think that it was killed by something bigger.”

The rabbit's nose twitched, and it opened its mouth and let out an ear-piercing screech as it figured out the eggs were food.

Briadon instantly smashed the rabbit, sending guts and the horn rolling across the ground. Blood splashed across Tressa's face, and she lurched back onto her butt, her nose curling in disgust as her notebook dropped with a thud. She wiped some of the gore from her face and gagged.

“You going to throw up?” Briadon teased.

“No,” she groaned in disgust. “Do you have to smash so close to me!?”

“Well, yeah. It was just trying to alert its pack.”

Tressa grabbed her book and quill and stuffed them back into her satchel, and Lisha came up and grabbed some of the guts from Tressa's hair, tossed them aside, and picked at the bits of fur that had landed on Tressa's shoulder.

“You okay?”

“No, I'm covered in guts. How can I be okay?”

Lisha chuckled. “Well, it's part of the job.”

“Only because Briadon!” she snapped and stood up.

Multiple creatures chittering could be heard from all around them. “Great…” Lisha muttered. “They heard the call. I really didn’t want to have to fight a pack of horned rabbits.”

“Well, we can sell the horns to an alchemist or blacksmith,” Briadon said. “Make a little extra from this expedition.”

“True, and they tend to pay better than the guild for the horns.”

“We could also sell the pelts.”

“Didn't think of that,” Lisha said as she stared at the pile of mush on the ground. “We'll have to be careful when we kill them so that they are in good enough shape, though.”

“Right, no smashing.” Briadon grinned.

Tressa looked at him, catching a word that grabbed her interest. “Alchemist? They actually exist?”

“Yeah. They make potions and other enchanted trinkets. Most are not super powerful, but good enough that it's worth a silver here and there. Though the closest shop is far west. Maybe even more northwest.”

“I see,” she said as she picked up her staff. “Hope we can meet one soon. I'd like to ask them questions.”

“Maybe when we get back to town, depending on how good a shape the rabbits are in. So, guys, get ready, and try not to damage the bodies too badly. We need to make a profit,” Lisha said.

“Understood,” both Tressa and Briadon said in unison.

Tressa quickly thought through some of the spells she had learned from the library. She could use most basic elemental spells—fire, wind, earth, electricity. Probably earth spear would be best if there were a lot.

“Welp, here they come,” Briadon said as they heard the thumping of feet and the cries of the rabbits as they grew closer and closer.

Tressa gripped her staff tighter as the three took formation, ready to battle the oncoming rabbits.

Suddenly, several lunged from the bushes, letting out chittering screams, only to be met by Briadon's club as he swung. Their bones crunched under the force as they were thrown back with limp bodies. He avoided smashing them against the ground as that might damage them too much.

Tressa wrinkled her nose at the sound and used earth magic to pull rocks and shape them into little spears the length of throwing knives, then launched them in spray at the rabbits. They pierced the bodies, impaling some to the ground, merely wounding others, but many were killed easily.

One made it through and bit into Lisha's leg, bypassing her scales. She let out a surprised yelp and swung her blade, decapitating the small monster.

“Ow!”

“Are you okay?”

“Yeah, I'm fine! The little shit bit me!” she responded, using yet another phrase she’d learned from Briadon.

The two larger monster hunters charged in, swinging and slaying rabbits while Tressa stood behind them, taking up the backup position by firing earth darts and protecting the eggs.

After several minutes of fighting, they had blood-stained ground around them full of the now-dead rabbits. Some were sliced in half, others punctured like pincushions with earth darts, and a few smashed into gooey piles of meat when Briadon failed to restrain himself.

Behind Tressa, Briadon scoffed as his club rested on the ground, covered in blood and gore and bits of fur. “That was barely a workout.”

“That's what you always say whenever a battle ends too quickly,” Lisha said as she kicked a corpse. Her tongue flicked out of her mouth, and she made a face at the stench she picked up.

Tressa rolled her eyes and bent down to the nearest rabbit. As this was her first time ever seeing them, she was truly fascinated. Her hand reached out and grabbed the horn, finding it smooth, unlike what she had first thought, as the small ridges would have made her think that it would be coarser, similar to a rock. But no, she found it smooth, and it made her think of the antlers left by the Corson Deer that traveled around Falidorn. She steadied the head with one hand and twisted the horn with the other. It took some force but she found it detached surprisingly easily. “Hmm,” she muttered. “How much time does it take to grow?”

“I’m not sure,” Lisha said.

She considered why they would be so easy to break off. Perhaps so after attacking they don’t get stuck? That would make sense.

“Tressa, mind breaking off the horns while we gather them up?” Lisha asked.

“Yes! I can do that,” she said, pulling her eyes away from the horn. She then moved to each rabbit, taking some horns as Lisha and Briadon moved about, gathering the least destroyed and mangled bodies of the rabbits and placing them into piles.

Lisha pulled a knife from her belt and started to cut off the back feet and ears, stuffing them into an enchanted bag that prevented rotting and blood stains so they could carry back the ears and tails for bounty rewards.

Tressa looked around at the bodies strewn about and wondered just how many they had killed. She would have to guess there were at least fifty or so. Maybe less counting the bodies of mush that mixed together in one big mass.

Pulling the other bag off, Lisha started stuffing the rabbits into it while Tressa pulled off the horns and placed them into a separate pile.

“Also, I don't think we should sell all the loot at once. Considering how far north we are, we might get blamed for something stupid, like overhunting or poaching,” Lisha said.

“True. Or blame us for killing hunters to take their harvest,” Briadon added.

Lisha let out an annoyed groan. “Fuck, I hate it. Just because we are different.”

Briadon grunted in agreement. He hated the prejudice as much as she did, and he glanced at Tressa, who was deeply focused, looking between the two different horns in her hand.

“It got a lot worse now that she is with us. We are just lucky to find any work.”

“Briadon,” Lisha snapped, though she knew he meant well. She looked back, but luckily it seemed Tressa didn’t overhear as she was far too interested in examining a horned rabbit corpse, then reached for her notebook and jotted something down while quietly muttering to herself in Elvish as she often would when she got focused.

The treatment toward them when they entered Ligis was a lot less welcoming than before, when they had registered Tressa as a guild member and part of their team. There had been some verbal comments toward them at the start, but those had been dismissed by the guild so long as she followed regulation. And Lisha had been grateful Tressa didn't understand the common tongue well in that moment, considering a few things said about her.

“So we need to be careful, right? About letting others know what we have?” Tressa asked.

“That's correct,” Lisha said, being pulled out of her thoughts.

“Hmmmm,” Tressa said, as they had told her about hunters who would kill others for loot or whatever high-paying contract was given to another team. And so it was best to keep contracts under silent oath when signing. The Hunters Guild wasn't exempt from the internal struggle all of them faced to bring in coin and fame. It wasn’t unheard of that a team would kill another team and make it look like a monster had done it so they could steal their bounty. There really wasn’t a good way for the guild to investigate such occurrences since hunter’s dying while on an expedition was common.

“So what you're saying is we sell between towns?” Tressa asked.

“Yes.”

“What about bigger cities? Will we be able to slip the loot in?”

“Depends on how strong their detection magic is,” Lisha explained. “If it's not strong enough, we can fudge the numbers of our haul. So if it's poor, instead of saying twenty-five horns, we can say eight or lower.”

“We could also bribe the guards,” Briadon added.

“I'd rather not, as you never know if you'll get the guard that accepts bribes.”

Briadon grunted. “We are in a bit of a mess here.”

Tressa suddenly burst out laughing.

“What?” Briadon asked.

“You said mess,” Tressa said as she picked up one of the smooshed rabbits by its ear with two fingers. “Its literal mess and uh…”

“Figurative?” Lisha offered.

“Figra-tive mess!”

“Ah, I get it!” Briadon said and joined Tressa in a burst of laughter while Lisha only smirked with her tongue flicking at them.
