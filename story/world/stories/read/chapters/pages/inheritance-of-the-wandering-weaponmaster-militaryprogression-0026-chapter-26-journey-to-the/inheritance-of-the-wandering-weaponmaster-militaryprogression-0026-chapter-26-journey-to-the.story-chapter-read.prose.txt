Once night had truly fallen, the tents were prepared and Ezra was settling into his tent when he heard an argument outside. His feet ached and he wanted nothing more than to just settle into his sleeping roll and just ignore whatever it was, but he thought there was something familiar in one of those voices. Instead of pulling his woolen blanket over his head and losing himself in unconsciousness, he groaned, rolled over, and walked out of the tent.

There was some method to the way that the tents were all set up, but he couldn’t remember what they were. Captain Riyal’s tent was in the center, as were all the wagons, but beyond that? Ezra had just done what he’d been told to. He strode in the direction of the argument, and the closer he got, the better he understood why he’d felt like he needed to come.

“—he knows me. Just say that it’s Adam, and he’ll come here.”

“I’mnm sure he knows who you are,” the soldier replied, Spoon, funnily enough, “but we’ve been marching all day and set up the camp for you as well. Talk to him in the morning, I’m sure he fell asleep as soon as he got his dinner. That’s what you should do as well, because we have another six days of this, and the morning will come real damn early.”

“Thanks, Spoon,” Ezra said as he came forward, “but I’ll talk to him. Thanks for keeping watch.”

“Sure,” he nodded back before stepping away. Spoon, the man who’d once been known as Adam, had been selected as one of the guards for the caravan, which meant that he got to ride in the wagon, but also meant that he was in charge of the night watch and still needed to march tomorrow. Ezra didn’t envy the position, but the other soldier had come a long way since he’d been terrified at the idea of fighting with a spear. Petr’s question about what would happen when the goblinoids were actually there brought no small amount of fear to Ezra’s mind, but he pushed that from his mind.

“What’s up, Adam?” Ezra asked, noting that Spoon twitched towards him at the use of the name.

“I wanted to talk,” Adam shrugged. “Today sucked, huh?”

“Not as bad as the days that came before it,” Ezra laughed. “Only walking? That’s not so hard compared to what they’ve had us doing.”

Adam blinked hard a couple times. “Really? They’ve been working you that hard?”

“From sunup to sundown basically every day,” Ezra confirmed.

“What about worship? Or breaks?” Adam gasped.

“Oh, yeah, you’re a follower of the Watcher, huh?” Ezra remembered aloud.

“Inkosuza bless me with eyes to see,” Adam confirmed. “They’re not going to let worshippers take even a half day of rest, are they?”

“No way in hell,” Ezra laughed. “And Spoon wasn’t wrong, I’m really damn tired. If you just want to talk, we can do that in the morning. Did you get dinner?”

“Yeah,” Adam said, “but it doesn’t compare to anything at the Toenail. Maybe that’ll be what I miss most of all…”

“Well, you should be back home in just a couple weeks,” Ezra comforted him. “After all, they’re thinking six more days’ marching, three days’ waiting and training, then at most a couple days of fighting. Then, you’ll get to go back home, get a nice little payment, and take it easy.”

“I guess,” came the response. “And when will you be home, Ezra?”

“I don’t know,” he shrugged. “I've signed up for two years, at least. I’m not sure how much more freedom I might have once the extermination’s done, though. I want to stop by The Toenail when I can, though.”

“Two years?” Adam let out a long sigh. “I wonder if it’ll be worth it for you. I mean, you’re losing so much time for training. You said yourself that it’ll be more dangerous for you than for me anyways, so maybe you shouldn’t have taken the Madam’s advice.”

“No,” Ezra snapped back, surprised at the strength of his feelings on the matter. He forced himself to calm as he saw Adam’s face and explained, “Look, I’ve got some great opportunities for me. I don’t want to be a thug my whole life, even if I’m working as a bouncer for the Madam. If I ever fell out of favor there, then I’d be left having to find work for Knifefingers or maybe the Shrikes. I don’t want that.”

“The Madam loves you,” Adam scoffed. “You’d always have work for her.”

“Eh,” Ezra replied. “I don’t want it, though. This is an opportunity for me to move up in the world. I’ve always wanted that, and this is more than just a time for me to fight things. I met a Pseudo-Knight, and she’s given me training, says that the Duke might want to hire me if I’m as good as she thinks.”

A heavy silence settled over the pair of men at that, with Adam’s eyes clouding as he thought about what Ezra said. After about fifteen seconds, Ezra couldn’t stand it any more and asked, “What?”

“It’s just…” Adam trailed off. “You’re going somewhere. I’ve never minded your dreams of being a Knight. I still don’t. It just feels bad to have you saying that you’ll leave everyone behind so easy. What’ll Matilda think?”

“Tilly’s got nothing to do with it!” Ezra barked. “If she doesn’t want me to become something more than just a bouncer in a bar, then she’s suddenly become someone different in the past month.”

“That’s not what I’m saying—” Adam protested.

“Yeah, sure,” Ezra shook his head. “I’m not leaving y’all behind. I’m moving along, and I’m hoping I can help you out too while I’m at it, and if that pisses you off, then move the fuck along.”

“Ezra,” Adam’s chiding voice grated on Ezra’s soul and he waved the older man off.

“Adam, I’ll try to keep you safe, but I need some sleep. I’ll be seeing you.”

He didn’t say anything more as he turned on his heel and returned to his tent. Adam called out behind him, but he was turned away from entering the soldiers’ side of the camp, and Ezra felt that separation more than ever, as childish as he felt for focusing on that.

“I feel my feet,” Ezra replied, trying to allow a measure of humor to color his tone. Since he’d argued with Adam the night before, he’d felt mostly like an idiot. His old friend’s point made a bit more sense to him in the morning than it had last night, though he still didn’t understand why he was acting like Ezra was just turning his back on everyone. Near as he could figure, Adam was a bit jealous to see Ezra leaving the slums behind. He knew he’d felt that same way himself, so he couldn’t argue with the feeling entirely. Anyways, he was focusing on helping the other members of his squad to draw aura into themselves as they walked. Boyd still hadn’t even sensed aura, while Leah and Cord had felt it and had managed to pull a small amount into themselves.

“I don’t feel anything either,” Cord shrugged back. “I don’t know how you do it so easy, Ezra.”

“I just do,” he said helplessly. “I’ll help you how I can, but I can’t do it for you.”

Leah was largely silent, walking forward with half-closed eyes as Amy tried to follow suit. Petr didn’t even try, and when asked, he had a simple answer.

“I’ve been trying to feel it for a decade. Never have. I doubt today, while I’m marching, will be the day that changes. We need to have at least one of us have their eyes open anyways.”

There was a current of envy in his words, but he kept his face controlled and his body language neutral as he walked along. Ezra hadn’t said anything more to that, instead continuing to fill his inner world as best as he could manage. The kilometers passed underfoot without much difficulty, though they stopped journeying over hills as much, instead sticking to a path that wound between hills that were steadily growing into mountains. Ezra’d never been outside of Villy, and though he’d seen the mountains as a smudge on the horizon, now that they were close, he couldn’t help but find himself getting lost in the mountains that looked down on him. They reached the sky, the peaks totally white except for scars of gray stone where the ice had failed to stick.

The second and third days of the journey passed much like this, with the brief stops being barely notable, except for the clearer view of the mountains as they approached and then walked between them. When Ezra asked Petr what was going on, Georg’s second-in-command had an easy answer.

“It’s nearly impossible to cross over the mountains, especially for the army of goblinoids. Maybe a Dragonkin’ll be able to slip through that way, but that’s not our problem. The more ordinary enemies, the ones we’re in charge of, will have to come through this pass. Everyone else’s already set up at the mouth of the pass, so we’re getting there so the goblinoids don’t have nearly so much opportunity to spread out.”

Like the first day, Ezra only really spoke when spoken to, and when he’d tried to find Adam to reconcile, he’d failed to find him. Among the levies, it was easy to disappear, and Ezra couldn't dedicate hours a day to trying to find his friend and mend broken bridges. Jormun just let him stew in “the damned teenaged angst”, and it wasn’t long before Ezra gave up on finding Adam, especially after the third evening. That day, Chloe found him.

“Sorry for the delay! I can’t give you quite so much instruction as before, but I’ll give you what I can,” she said without further explanation as she dragged him out of the tent and towards Captain Riyal’s tent.

It was the largest “building” among the entire camp, and she pushed her way into the tent with Ezra getting dragged along behind. The captain was there, and he looked like he’d expected the interruption.

“Good evening,” Riyal nodded.

“Yes, sir, good evening,” Ezra replied, as caught up in the whirlwind of a woman as ever.

“I hope you don’t mind me witnessing your training,” Riyal continued. “As someone who’s stalled out at Adept, I’ll benefit from witnessing the abilities of a Pseudo-Knight.”

“Of course, sir.”

“Continue,” Riyal commanded as Chloe made Ezra stand in the center of the room.

“I’ll go much easier on you today,” she explained, “because I can’t afford to spend so much of my internal aura with the fights coming any time now. But, I wonder how close you are to the borders of your tier, so I figure I can help you out this way.”

Before he could answer, her aura pressed heavily on him, and Ezra flared his inner world as much as he could manage in response. His knees wobbled and threatened to buckle, but he kept himself upright as his vision swam. What felt like the weight of the mountains overhead pressed on his shoulders, and his upper body dipped lower. With a groan, though, he stayed upright as he fought to keep his breathing steady.

Just as he thought he couldn’t go on any longer, the pressure disappeared and he could finally breathe easy.

“You’ve come a long way!” Chloe congratulated him. “That’s all for tonight, but tomorrow, it’ll be shorter and more intense.” Then, before he could even groan, she pulled a spear and shield out of the corner of the tent and tossed them over and grinned widely.

The brief victory that he’d found in standing up to the pressure of Chloe’s aura faded from his mind after that.
