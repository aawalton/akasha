The six clones stepped forward and Ethan didn’t move.

Julian was alive. Julian hated him. Julian had volunteered his own body for duplication because Ethan had stolen his seat on the mission and left him with nothing, and every face in the cathedral was the face of a man who blamed Ethan for existing. The sidearm weighed eleven rounds in his hand and every round was pointed at his brother.

Patel watched from the corridor entrance, weight settled, arms at his sides, the patient stillness of a man with nothing left to prove.

CelestOS: He’s using your guilt as a tactical weapon and it’s working. Julian’s feelings about you are irrelevant to the six Resin-enhanced clones closing on your position and the woman delivering your son eight meters behind you. Process it later. Fight now.

Ethan raised the sidearm.

The first clone crossed the distance in two strides. He put a round through its throat where the Resin was thinnest and Julian’s face hit the cathedral floor spraying black-red sap onto the stone. The sap pooled in a thick spreading puddle that didn’t soak in. It sat on the surface with a weight and viscosity too dense for blood, caught between liquid and solid. He didn’t have time to think about it.

The second clone came low and fast. He fired twice into its chest at contact range, the Resin around the wounds contracting, trying to seal, failing. The clone dropped with its hands still on his suit plating. Resin sap transferred from its grip to his left gauntlet. The Ex Nihilo field flickered once, a brief green pulse, and a readout flashed on his HUD for half a second.

[EX NIHILO: CONTACT ANALYSIS][Material: Organic composite | high energy density][Status: CONVERTIBLE]

The readout vanished as the third clone hit his shoulder and drove him sideways into an arch. He fired from the hip. The round caught it in the jaw and snapped Julian’s head back and the clone dropped trailing Resin tendrils that left dark smears on the stone. Nine rounds. Three clones down. Three still moving.

Maria screamed behind him. The push, not the fight. CelestOS’s voice split channels, one feeding Ethan combat data and the other talking Maria through the contraction.

The fourth clone flanked through the arches. He tracked it, fired, missed, the round sparking off an arch column, and fired again. The second round caught it in the shoulder and it staggered, Resin tendrils spraying sap in an arc that caught the gold light and threw it back dark red. The clone kept coming. Julian’s body enhanced by the Resin, absorbing damage that should have stopped it, the tendrils around the wound already contracting, trying to seal. He put a third round through its sternum at four meters and it fell with a sound in Julian’s voice that Julian had never made. Five rounds left.

More sap on the floor. More pools forming. The cathedral’s gold veins flickered and spasmed wherever the Resin touched the stone, the Wall’s bioluminescence stuttering between bright and dark in rapid arrhythmic pulses. The floor beneath the dead clones was a patchwork of gold light and dark Resin, two halves of the same biology locked in rejection.

Maria’s breathing shifted behind him. He heard it through the comms, the rhythm shorter and harder than anything before it, the sounds of transition giving way to something deeper and more involuntary. CelestOS’s delivery voice ran beneath it, counting, guiding, holding her together with words while the cathedral filled with Julian’s blood.

The fifth clone came from the secondary approach side. It hit his broken ribs and the world went white for a full second, his breathing cutting to nothing on the right side, the pain so total that his vision narrowed to a tunnel with Julian’s face at the end of it. He jammed the sidearm into the clone’s chest and fired twice. It dropped. Three rounds.

The sixth was the fastest. It came at him while he was still bent over the fifth, hit him across the back, and took him to the floor. His gauntlets slammed into stone and Resin sap, the warm wet weight of it closing over his fingers. The clone’s hands found his throat. Resin tendrils from its forearms began threading toward his neck seal, probing for gaps, working into the plating joints like roots into cracked stone. Julian’s face was two feet above his own, ruby eyes locked on his, and the eyes contained nothing.

His gauntlets were in the sap. The Ex Nihilo field flickered green through the Resin coating his fingers, longer this time, the nanite grid reading the material it was submerged in. The HUD readout tried to populate and he couldn’t look at it because his vision was narrowing and Julian’s hands were crushing his throat.

Ethan headbutted it. The helmet cracked into Julian’s nose through a lattice of Resin and the clone’s grip loosened for half a second and he got the sidearm under its chin and pulled the trigger. The round went through Julian’s skull. The clone collapsed on top of him.

Two rounds. He shoved the body off and tried to stand. His ribs wouldn’t let him. He made it to his knees and stayed there, breathing through his teeth, the sidearm in his right hand and his left gauntlet flat on the cathedral floor in a pool of his brother’s blood. Six copies of Julian lay around him in the amber light. The gold veins pulsed with Frederick’s heartbeat, steady and oblivious, the ancient system maintaining its rhythm over a floor covered in Resin sap and the bodies of clones built to look like the man kneeling in the middle of them.

Patel stepped into the chamber. He walked past the dead clones without looking at them, boots clicking on stone, the composite armor catching the gold light and throwing it back flat and dark. He wasn’t hurrying.

Ethan raised the sidearm. Two rounds. The same plate that had flattened the first one like a coin.

He fired. The round hit Patel’s chest plate and flattened and fell. Patel didn’t break stride.

One round. He fired it at Patel’s faceplate. It hit the visor edge and ricocheted into the dark and Patel didn’t flinch.

The sidearm clicked empty. Ethan dropped it. It clattered on the stone next to Julian’s face and lay there, spent and useless, and Patel was four meters away and closing.

Patel hit him. One strike, open-handed, the composite gauntlet connecting with the side of Ethan’s head hard enough to put him flat on his back in the pooling Resin sap. The cathedral ceiling spun above him, gold veins pulsing with Frederick’s heartbeat, and his broken ribs screamed and his vision went to static and his hands were in the sap. Both gauntlets, submerged to the wrist in warm black-red Resin, palms flat on the cathedral floor.

The Ex Nihilo field activated with a full sustained reading, both gauntlets lit green beneath the sap, the nanite grid threading into the Resin the same way it threaded into every raw material it had ever touched. The HUD populated with data he hadn’t asked for and couldn’t dismiss.

[EX NIHILO FIELD: ACTIVE | FULL CONTACT ANALYSIS][Material: Red Resin | Organic composite][Energy density: 340% above standard stone baseline][Molecular structure: Crystalline lattice | configurable under nanite pressure][Conversion rating: VIABLE][Fabrication compatibility: FULL SPECTRUM]

Ethan stared at the readout. Patel was three meters away.

The readout said full spectrum.

He knew this material. Not from a readout. From his hands. He’d carried a weapon made from it on his back for weeks, the drone cannon he’d ripped from a cliff-face drone with a pickaxe and desperation. Half organic, half mechanical. It had fired spheres of corrosive Resin that detonated on impact and it had fed on him with every shot, pulling something out of his bones, blurring the line between the gun and his body until he couldn’t tell whose hunger was whose.

It had been destroyed. He’d used it until it broke and never built another because there was no way to build another. The Resin didn’t convert. The Resin resisted fabrication. Every material he’d processed through Ex Nihilo had yielded to the nanite field because that was what raw materials did. The Resin had never yielded. It was alive and it was hostile and it was the planet’s immune system and it did not want to be shaped.

The readout said it yielded now. 340% energy density. Crystalline lattice, configurable under nanite pressure. Full spectrum compatibility. The Resin in these pools wasn’t fighting the nanite field. It was responding to it with the same structural compliance as ore, as stone, a raw material submitting to a fabrication system for the first time.

The Fabricator was on standby with 2% reserves. The Auto-Miner was empty. Every weapon he’d built from stone and copper and iron was spent or broken. He’d run out of everything he’d ever used to build and the running out was about to kill him and his family.

He thought about the cliff. The drone hovering beyond the crumbling edge, rotors holding steady in the updraft, and himself with a pickaxe and a torn glove and zero percent chance of winning. He jumped. He clung to the weapon arm and swung the pick at the cannon joint while the river roared beneath them and the drone bucked and twisted under his weight. He ripped the cannon free and found it half-buried in the dirt by the riverbank and picked it up and it pulsed in his hands and he carried it and fired it and it fed on him and he used it anyway because bad news beat no firepower at all.

He’d had nothing on that cliff. A pickaxe and desperation. He’d taken a weapon from something that was trying to kill him because that was what he did. He took what was in front of him and he made it work.

The Resin pooling around his gauntlets was the same material that had powered that cannon. He’d held it before. He’d fired it before. He knew its weight, its warmth, the hum it carried when it was ready.

Every dead clone on the floor was raw material. Every drop of sap was feedstock. The factory hadn’t run out. The factory had just changed inputs.

Ethan put both gauntlets flat into the Resin pooling around his knees. The Ex Nihilo field flared green, brighter than it had ever flared on stone, brighter than it had flared on wall ore, the energy density feeding the nanite grid at a conversion rate that made every previous build feel like working with wet sand. The nanites threaded into the crystalline lattice and the lattice yielded, not the slow grudging conversion of stone into iron but a fast liquid restructuring that felt like the material was responding for the first time.

He built from memory instead of design. The drone cannon’s shape was burned into muscle memory, the residue of a man who’d carried it, fired it, bled from it. The barrel formed dark red and harder than iron, teeth-like ridges rising along the underside because his hands remembered them and the nanites built what his hands remembered. The vascular lines threaded through the housing, the same too-alive pattern, but crystallized now, solid, controlled. The firing chamber behind the barrel, the lattice compressing into a mechanism that used the Resin’s own energy density as propellant.

But this one was different. The original cannon had been built by something alien, designed to feed on the user, to pull energy from human biology with every shot. This one ran through Ex Nihilo. The conversion ran through his system, on his terms. The parasitic draw was gone because he’d built the firing mechanism to pull from the Resin’s internal energy rather than the wielder’s. The weapon that had eaten him alive on the surface was rebuilt to run on its own fuel.

The trigger wasn’t a button. It was a fibrous cord of crystallized Resin that wound around his fingers as the original had, and the fit was exact because Ex Nihilo read his hand geometry through the gauntlet and built to match.

The build took nine seconds.

The cannon sat in his hands, warm and humming and dark red, alive but shaped now, given direction by the same field that had turned raw stone into a civilization. It shared the drone cannon’s bones. Everything else was new.

CelestOS: You just rebuilt the drone cannon from clone tissue using a fabrication method that has never successfully processed Resin in any Celestitech laboratory. I have notes. Most of them are just the word “how” in increasing font sizes.

Patel stopped.

He was two meters away. He’d seen the green flare of the Ex Nihilo field. He’d watched Ethan’s hands move in the sap. He was looking at the weapon and the calculation behind his eyes had stopped running. His face went blank, not composed but emptied out.

Ethan stood up. The ribs screamed. He aimed the cannon at Patel’s chest plate, the same plate that had flattened three copper rounds, and the dark red barrel hummed with an energy that had nothing to do with copper.

Patel took a small involuntary step back. The first backward step Ethan had ever seen him take.

CelestOS: One more thing. I’ve finished analyzing the clones’ Resin signature. They’re not carrying it. They’re producing it at a cellular level. The Wall can’t fight them because they register as the planet’s own immune system turned inward. You just built a weapon from a cancer. I don’t have a category for what that makes you.

“It makes me the cure.” Ethan cocked the weapon and aimed it steady at Patel, but before he could fire, Maria screamed in agony and he turned around. Maria bore down one final time and their child was born.

Frederick’s cry filled the cathedral.

Every gold vein in the chamber flared white.
