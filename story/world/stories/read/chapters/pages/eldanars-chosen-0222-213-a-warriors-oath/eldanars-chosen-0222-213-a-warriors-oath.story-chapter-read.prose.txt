The doors of the Ashenfall Guildhall groaned loudly as Josh, Brett, and Carcan pushed their way inside from the freezing twilight of the city streets. The atmosphere within the grand hall had shifted dramatically during their time in the Artisan District and their emotional stop at the Bull's Head tavern. The exhausted, traumatised silence of the morning retreat had been replaced by a frantic, buzzing, militaristic energy. The Guildhall was no longer a sanctuary. It was a war room.

Adventurers of every rank and class were frantically preparing for the inevitable siege. The sharp, rhythmic scraping of whetstones against steel echoed from every corner. The heavy smell of oiled leather, bubbling alchemical pitch, and nervous sweat hung thick in the warm air. Clerks were running between the long wooden tables, shouting out defensive grid assignments and handing out heavy canvas sacks of emergency rations.

Josh led the way, his new, massive heater shield secured comfortably to his back and his armour clanking with each step. He scanned the crowded room, his storm-grey eyes searching for their missing party member.

He found Perberos near the massive roaring hearth at the far end of the hall.

The elven scout was not alone. He was standing around a large, scarred wooden table with Ronald and half a dozen other lightly armoured adventurers. They were all wearing muted leathers in shades of dark charcoal, deep forest green, and pitch black. They carried no heavy shields or longswords. They were armed entirely with recurve bows, silent throwing knives, and short, curved blades designed for swift, lethal efficiency. They were the eyes in the dark, the elite stealth operatives of Ashenfall.

Ronald was leaning heavily over a massive, unfurled parchment map of the northern plains. The feline beastkin was pointing a sharp claw at various topographical markers, his voice a low, urgent murmur that was drowned out by the ambient noise of the hall. Perberos was nodding slowly, his luminous silver eyes tracking Ronald's movements with absolute, chilling focus.

"They are finalising the route," Brett observed quietly, his voice lacking any trace of his usual sarcastic wit. He pulled his new crimson Ember-silk cloak tighter around his shoulders. "We should not interrupt them. Let’s find a table."

They claimed a small, empty booth tucked away in the shadows near the stairs leading to the upper dormitories.

As soon as they sat down, the emotional toll of the day finally seemed to crash down upon Carcan. The elven healer, who usually radiated a profound, unshakeable aura of serene calm, looked grey with worry. She sat rigidly on the wooden bench, her pale, aristocratic hands clasped so tightly together on the table that her knuckles were completely white. She did not look at Josh or Brett. Her silver eyes were fixed entirely on the distant figure of her twin brother.

"He is one of the best scouts in the entire province, Carcan," Josh offered softly, leaning his massive arms on the table. He wanted to offer comfort, to ease the terrifying tension radiating from the healer. "If anyone can slip past that horde and make it back without being seen, it is Perberos."

"And Ronald is with him," Brett added, trying to inject a note of logical confidence into the conversation. "The beastkin has survived a load of dungeon breaks. They are not going out there to fight. They are just gathering intelligence."

Carcan did not smile. She slowly unclasped her hands, rubbing her elegant fingers together as if trying to generate warmth.

"You do not understand the scale of the corruption leaking from that forest," Carcan whispered, her melodic voice trembling slightly. "The dark mana is suffocating. It actively seeks out the living. Stealth requires more than just silent footsteps when the very air you breathe is trying to consume your life force. If they are spotted by a Crypt Weaver on the open plains, there is absolutely no cover. There are no trees to hide behind. It is a flat, frozen expanse of death."

Josh and Brett exchanged a heavy, helpless look. There was nothing more they could say to reassure her. The mathematics of the scouting mission were undeniably horrific, and empty platitudes would only insult her intelligence.

They sat in tense, oppressive silence for another twenty minutes, watching the frantic preparations of the guild around them.

Finally, the tight group of scouts around the map broke apart. Ronald began handing out small, tightly sealed glass vials of dark liquid to the rogues and rangers. Perberos took his vials, tucking them securely into the leather pouches on his belt, before turning away from the table.

The elven ranger walked smoothly through the crowded hall, his movements silent despite the chaotic noise surrounding him. He approached their shadowed booth, pulling out a wooden chair and sitting down opposite his sister.

Perberos looked rested, having clearly taken the time to sleep in the upper dormitories during the afternoon, but his face was set into an expression of uncompromising, stony resolve. He looked at Josh's new greathelm resting on the table, and the shimmering crimson fabric of Brett's new cloak, giving a small, approving nod.

"Your gear looks significantly better," Perberos noted quietly. "You look like you are ready to hold a wall."

"We are ready," Josh confirmed, meeting the scout's eyes. "What is the plan?"

Perberos leaned forward, keeping his voice incredibly low. "Ronald is leading us directly up the old northern trade road. We are going to push past the abandoned forward operating base and attempt to locate the leading edge of their marching column. We need to ascertain if the boss monster we saw was an isolated heavy unit, and if they have managed to salvage actual siege engines from the ruined northern keeps."

"Siege engines?" Brett repeated, his pale face turning a shade paler. "You think the undead can operate catapults and battering rams?"

"The lesser ghouls cannot," Perberos replied, his silver eyes flashing in the dim light. "But the Crypt Weavers possess a terrifying level of intelligence, and the monster in charge is clearly an entity of high tactical intellect. If they have battering rams, the heavy timber gates of Ashenfall will not hold for more than a few hours. The city council needs to know exactly what is going to hit them so they can position the heavy fighters accordingly."

Carcan reached across the table, her trembling hand gently grasping her brother's wrist.

"How far north are you pushing?" Carcan asked, her voice cracking slightly.

"As far as the shadows allow, sister," Perberos answered softly. He turned his hand over, intertwining his long fingers with hers, offering her a rare, physical gesture of deep familial affection. "We will not take unnecessary risks. If the horde is too dense, we will fall back immediately. I have no intention of dying in the mud."

Before Carcan could reply, a sharp, piercing whistle cut cleanly through the deafening noise of the Guildhall.

Ronald was standing near the main entrance, his muted green cloak fastened tightly, his twin hunting daggers strapped securely to his thighs. The beastkin did not shout an order. He simply looked at the gathered scouts, gave a single, sharp nod, and pushed the thick wooden doors open, stepping out into the freezing night.

The other rogues and rangers instantly fell in behind him, moving with grim, silent purpose.

"It is time," Perberos said, slowly pulling his hand away from his sister's grasp.

The elven scout stood up from the wooden chair. He checked the string of his beautiful elven longbow one final time, ensuring the tension was perfect. He then turned to Carcan.

He spoke to her entirely in fluid, melodic Elvish. "Cala síla erin lû e-govaded 'wîn, muindiel," he murmured.

The light shines on the hour of our meeting, sister.

The language was incredibly beautiful, sounding like wind rustling through ancient leaves and water flowing over smooth stones, but his tone was heartbreakingly sorrowful. "Daro i-vorn. Uuma dela."

Keep back the dark. Do not fear.

Carcan closed her eyes, a single, silent tear slipping down her pale cheek as she listened to her twin's final words. She replied with a single, trembling phrase in the same language, placing her hand gently over her heart. "Nai elenath sílar erin thrad lín. Tol-acharn."

May the stars shine upon your path. Return to me.

Perberos nodded slowly, absorbing her blessing.

Then, the elven ranger turned away from his sister. He stepped around the wooden table, stopping directly in front of Josh.

Perberos looked up at the towering human. The ethereal, aristocratic beauty of the elf's face was completely hardened into a mask of deadly, cold steel. He did not look at Josh as a friend or a travelling companion in that moment. He looked at him as a fellow warrior, measuring the exact depth of the human's soul.

"Josh," Perberos said, his voice dropping into a harsh, uncompromising whisper. "I need you to listen to me very carefully."

Josh stood up, his heavy steel boots planting firmly on the floorboards. He towered over the lithe ranger, but he gave Perberos his absolute, undivided attention. "I am listening."

"You and Brett are anomalies," Perberos stated, his silver eyes locking onto Josh's storm-grey gaze. "You possess a strength that defies the natural order of this world. I have watched you stand your ground against horrors that would break veteran soldiers. You are a Guardian in the truest sense of the word."

Perberos took a half-step closer, his voice dropping even lower, meant only for Josh's ears.

"If the timber walls fall," Perberos commanded fiercely, "if the undead breach the gates and the city streets become a slaughterhouse, you do not stand and die for a doomed city. You do not throw your life away trying to be a heroic martyr for strangers."

The ranger pointed a long, pale finger directly at Carcan, who was sitting quietly behind them.

"You take my sister, and you run," Perberos demanded, his eyes burning with a fierce, desperate light. "You use every single ounce of your unnatural strength, you use that heavy steel shield, and you carve a path through the horde to get her out of this city. You do not let the dark touch her."

Josh felt the immense, crushing weight of the demand settle heavily onto his broad shoulders. It was not a request for a tactical retreat. It was a brother handing over the ultimate responsibility for the only family he had left in the world.

Josh did not hesitate. He did not look away from the piercing silver eyes of the elven scout. He raised his right hand, heavily clad in dark steel, and placed it firmly over his own chest piece, right above his heart.

"I swear it," Josh vowed, his voice a deep, resonant rumble of absolute, unbreakable conviction. "On my life, on my shield, and on my honour. I will protect her with my last breath, Perberos. You have my word."

Perberos stared at the human for three long, tense seconds, searching for any trace of hesitation or doubt. Finding absolutely none, the terrifying tension in the elf's posture finally broke.

Perberos let out a slow, quiet breath. He reached out, clasping Josh's thick steel forearm in a strong, warrior's grip.

"Thank you, Josh," Perberos whispered, the cold steel in his voice completely replaced by profound, genuine gratitude.

He released Josh's arm and gave Brett a final, solemn nod of farewell.

Without looking back at his sister, Perberos turned and walked swiftly towards the main entrance of the Guildhall. He melted seamlessly into the crowd of rushing adventurers, slipping out through the heavy oak doors and vanishing entirely into the freezing, pitch-black dark of the night.

Josh slowly sat back down on the wooden bench beside Brett. The deafening noise of the chaotic preparations in the grand hall seemed to wash over them, muted and distant.

They looked at Carcan. The elven healer was staring blankly at the closed oak doors, her face a mask of silent, profound sorrow.

The shopping was done. The goodbyes were said. The scouts were out in the dark. There was nothing left for them to do but wait for the inevitable, and the apocalyptic army that would undoubtedly follow.
