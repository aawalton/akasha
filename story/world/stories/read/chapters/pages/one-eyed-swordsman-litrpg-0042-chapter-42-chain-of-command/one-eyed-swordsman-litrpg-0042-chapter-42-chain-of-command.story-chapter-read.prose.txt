Heat swelled between Oswald and the thugs. Sweat clung to skin, and smoke hung low, curling in lazy spirals.

Oswald caught every detail: the crusted blood on one man’s knuckles, the oily sheen on another’s brow, the way laughter still floated through the tavern.

The one nearest to him stepped forward. This thug had broad shoulders strained against a threadbare coat, and a half-healed scar split across his jaw like a fault line. “Well, you dropped the boys at the door. Good for you. But you really think you’re walkin’ outta here after that? Place is packed to the gills with Dragoons, you don't stand a chance."

Another voice came from behind, a chuckle that carried all the intelligence of a crow picking at trash. “Yeah, one swing from us and you’ll be pissin’ teeth. Ain’t no way you’re makin’ it out."

Oswald shifted his weight slightly to one leg. They think numbers make them safe. Think that just ‘cause there’s more of ‘em, it’s already over.

“I can beat the daylights outta every single one of you, best not to piss me off." Oswald said.

The thugs burst out laughing. A chorus of rough voices, slapping the table, one nearly doubling over. “Listen to this brat! Thinkin’ he’s some kind o’ hero!”

In a split second, Oswald moved faster than they could finish their wheeze, he grabbed the nearest Dragoon by the front of his shirt. The man barely had time to blink before Oswald lifted him up.

Then Oswald threw him with full force. The thug’s body crashed through the wall like it was made of paper and shot out into the street with a violent crunch. Dust burst outward, followed by the distant thud of something heavy hitting cobblestone.

The tavern froze. Oswald straightened, the remnants of the man’s shirt still clenched in his fist. He let the fabric drop, then looked back at the rest. Let’s see if they’re still laughin’.

The Rat’s Nest went silent. Mugs stopped clinking. Dice stopped rolling. Every eye in the place locked onto Oswald.

The man he’d thrown hadn’t come back in. Probably couldn’t.

The hole in the wall still gaped behind him, splintered wood and crumbled mortar trailing dust in the air. Outside, dogs barked. Somewhere far off, a bottle shattered.

Oswald stood his ground, he knew that places like this didn’t listen to reason. Places like this only spoke one language: power. And he was fluent enough to make himself heard.

Behind him, a boot shifted. Then came the whistle of steel.

Oswald turned reflexively. The thug was mid-swing, a jagged blade arcing for the space between the boy's ribs.

Yet, Oswald caught the man’s arm just above the elbow, twisted, and drove his forearm into the thug’s chest. The impact sent the man stumbling back, wind knocked clear from his lungs. Before he could recover, Oswald stepped in and drove his boot squarely between the man’s legs.

The thug made a noise halfway between a gasp and a squeal before crumpling to the floor.

Oswald looked down at him. Should’ve stayed quiet.

He turned back to the rest. The smoke from half-dead pipes coiled around him like fog, but no one dared move through it now.

A single drop of beer slid down the side of a mug, catching the lamplight as it traced a slow path along the stained glass. A boot scuffed quietly against the wooden floor.

Oswald’s voice cut through the silence. “Alright, who’s next?”

No one answered. Mugs were suddenly fascinating, several Dragoons stared into theirs as if hoping they’d forgotten something at the bottom. One man cleared his throat with a dry rasp. Another shifted on his stool, the wood groaning beneath him.

Thought so. Talk tough ‘til someone hits back. Then it’s all sweat and sideways glances.

Eventually, one of them raised his head. He looked younger than the rest, with a leaner frame and dark hair that fell messily over one eye. His fingers tapped a quiet rhythm on the tabletop, quick and light, but his hands stayed clear of any weapon. “What do you want?”

Oswald lowered his hand from where it hovered near his belt. “I want a word with your boss.”

From off to the side, another voice snapped, rough, older, edged with suspicion. “Who the fuck are you workin’ for?”

Oswald didn’t answer. He turned.

The man who’d spoken sat wide in his chair, a squat brute with a gut pressing hard against his belt and forearms like mallets. His nose had clearly been broken more than once, and his knuckles bore the calluses of someone too familiar with other people’s pain.

Without a word, Oswald closed the space between them. One step, then two. The big man barely had time to shift in his seat before—

Crack.

Oswald’s fist drove into his stomach with full force. A clean, brutal shot to the gut. The impact hit with a dull, meaty thump, followed by the rush of air fleeing the man’s lungs. He folded forward with a wet grunt, coughing hard, one arm sprawled across the table, the other clutching his ribs.

Oswald didn’t move back. He stood over the man, looking across the room as the rest of the Dragoons stiffened. “Anyone else got a bright idea? Or are we done wastin’ time?”

The room stayed silent, until finally, another Dragoon stood. A tall man with a shaved head and a jagged scar down the side of his neck. He looked Oswald up and down, not impressed, but not foolish either. “I’ll take you, Boss won’t like bein’ bothered, but I’d rather that than you wreck the whole damn place.”

“Smart choice.” Oswald said.

The man moved toward the door, stepping over the groaning bodies on the floor without a glance. Oswald followed closely. Now let’s see who’s actually runnin’ this place.

Stairs groaned beneath their boots, old wood creaking constantly. Oswald kept his eyes forward, steps steady, but his fingers flexed lightly at his sides. Doesn't seem like a trap so far.

When they reached the landing, a narrow hall stretched left and right, but the thug stopped in front of a plain wooden door with a battered handle and a long scratch down the center like someone had once tried to claw their way out.

The Dragoon jerked a thumb toward it. “Boss is inside.”

“Yeah? Then tell me this, if your boss is such a big deal, why didn’t he drag his ass downstairs when I was tossin’ his boys around?” Oswald asked.

The thug rubbed at the back of his neck, exhaling through his nose. “Best you just… see for yourself.”

Oswald’s expression didn’t change. “No. You open it.”

The Dragoon hesitated, fingers twitching near the handle. He looked like a man trying to decide whether it was safer to argue or obey.

He chose wisely. With a reluctant breath, he reached out and pushed the door open. When Oswald stepped inside, it was difficult to ignore how the room smelled like booze, sweat, and something faintly sour. Not rot exactly, just the kind of staleness that came from too many long nights with the windows shut.

Bottles were strewn across the floor and table, some upright, most on their sides, with amber liquid pooled beneath them. At the center, stretched across a ratty couch, was a man built like a warhorse.

Shaggy blonde hair spilled in messy clumps across his face and shoulders, and a beard of equal roughness covered his jaw. Muscles stood out like stone beneath sunburned skin, his chest rising and falling with each thunderous snore. He wore only pants and a heavy belt.

Oswald blinked. Then he chuckled. Well… that explains a lot.

Behind him, the Dragoon hovered in the doorway like he didn’t want to be too close. “You gonna kill him?”

Oswald didn’t even turn around. “What I do ain’t your damn business, now scram.”

The thug didn’t wait to be told twice. His footsteps echoed down the hall, faster with each step, until they disappeared back toward the tavern below.

Oswald stepped inside and closed the door behind him. The latch clicked. The snoring wavered.

Then, slowly, the man on the couch stirred. He groaned once before rubbing a hand across his face and cracked one eye open. It was bloodshot and unfocused, the other still lost beneath a mop of tangled hair. “Who're you?”

Oswald pulled a chair out from the far wall and dropped into it. “Who do ya think?"

The boss snorted, coughed, then let out a low, satisfied burp that filled the room. He smacked his lips, blinked a few times, then looked Oswald over more closely. “You don’t look familiar. So either you’re new… or you’re the bastard who beat the piss outta everyone downstairs.”

Oswald leaned back slightly in the chair. “I’ll let you figure that out.”

The man on the couch threw his head back and let out a thunderous laugh that rattled the bottles on the floor.

“Name’s Ulric,” he said between wheezing chuckles, wiping his nose with the back of his arm. “One of the ‘team leaders’ here in the Red Dragoons. That’s what they call it, anyway. Fancy word for ‘guy who yells really loud.’

Oswald leaned forward, elbows braced on his knees. “Name's Oswald, so, how high up does that put you?”

Ulric scratched at his chest, thoughtful for a second. “Well… there’s a handful of us like me. Team leaders, y’know. Then there’s Senna above the team leaders, and above her is Aldric. Pretty simple."

Oswald nodded, processing it. “Didn’t expect you to be so open about it.”

Ulric snorted and waved a dismissive hand. “I’m not in this business to die, kid. My idiot boys downstairs might puff their chests and act hard, but I ain’t stupid. You ain’t normal.”

Oswald opened his mouth to toss back a reply, but didn’t get the chance. A bottle came flying straight at his face.

Without thinking, he caught it. He stared at it for a second, then lowered it and set it on the table between them.

“You got a death wish?” Oswald asked.

Ulric just grinned. “Nah. Just makin’ a point.”

He sat back, smirking like a kid who’d just pulled off a harmless prank. “Truth is, I ain’t got many talents left. But bottle-throwin’? I’m damn near an artist.”

Oswald let out a short snort through his nose. “If nothin’ else, I respect your honesty.”

“Cheers to that,” Ulric said, reaching for another bottle, this one already empty, and tossing it aside with a dull clunk.

He straightened a little, posture less slouched now, and tilted his head as he looked Oswald over again. “Speakin’ of, though… still don’t know why you came all this way to see me. Ain’t often a kid kicks in my door just to stare at me.”

Oswald smirked faintly and exhaled. “I want to see Senna, either her or Aldric."

“Aldric’s run off to who knows fuckin where. And Senna… I know where she’s at, but gettin’ to her ain’t gonna be simple.”

"What's the problem?"

Ulric scratched at the edge of his jaw with one dirty fingernail, sighing through his teeth. “She's  busy. Underground arena’s runnin’ hot right now, so she’s got her hands full.”

“There's an underground arena?”

Ulric leaned in slightly. “How bad d’you wanna know?”

Oswald’s smile faded as he leaned forward. “If you’re jerkin’ me around, I’ll put you through that wall just like the last guy who crossed me.”

Ulric blinked. Then he threw his head back and howled with laughter, slapping his bare thigh hard enough to make the bottles rattle again. “You’ve got bite kid, I’ll give you that. But maybe you oughta think twice before throwin’ out threats.”

He jabbed a thumb toward himself. “Because I’m your best shot at gettin’ into the arena without a hundred eyes on you. I get you through the gates without anyone askin’ too many questions… and that means you’ll have a straight path to Senna, maybe Aldric too if yer lucky.”

Oswald narrowed his eyes slightly. “I’m listenin’."

Ulric leaned back into the couch “Here's my proposition, you fight in the arena as a participant.

“I’ll sponsor you. That gets you on the list without fuss. You win enough matches, stay in the game long enough... chances are real good Senna’ll come to you. She usually meets the finalists in person before the last round.”

“So you rake in coin while I take the hits. Real generous of you.”

“Course I profit. But so do you. You’ll get half of whatever I pull in from your matches. House always takes a cut, but even after that... it’ll be enough to keep you drinkin’ for a month.”

Ain’t after coin right now. And roughin’ up a few scumbags to get closer to Senna or Aldric? Could be worth the bruises.

“You better get me to Senna if I fight for you. If I find out you been lyin’? I’ll make damn sure to come back and knock your teeth in.”

Ulric held up both hands, that crooked smile never slipping. “You’re welcome to try, sure. But I know what Senna can do. You don’t.”

“And why should I trust you?”

“You shouldn’t, but I’ll sweeten the deal. You fight for me in the arena, I’ll tell you what she can do. And… I’ll make sure your mess at the Rat’s Nest disappears. No one’ll come knockin’. Not tonight, not tomorrow.”

Oswald’s jaw shifted slightly. He glanced to the side, silent. Normally I’d scoff at a setup like this. Fight for a cut, play the thug in someone else’s little ring? But it ain’t about the coin this time. Places like that draw people from all over the place. Might be the fastest way to learn who’s pullin’ the strings here in Old March.

He looked back at Ulric. "I’ll do it. On one condition.”

Ulric tilted his head. "Speak up."

“You don’t tell anyone why I’m fightin’. Far as they know, I’m just here for the payday.”

Ulric’s smile widened into something almost proud. “That’s easy."
