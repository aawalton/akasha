“There. That should do it,” said a small voice from behind an access panel. A voice Vir had gotten to know well, and it seemed that reviving long-dead Imperium laboratories was just another of her countable skills.

“Yes, the lights are now on,” Vir called back. “You can come out now.”

Siya came crawling out of the access port, black marks on her face, her long black hair a tangled mess.

A wave of her hand and all that went back to normal as she activated her Imperium magic.

It was magic, in the truest sense, for even after all this time, Vir had not a single inkling as to how it worked.

“Excellent work as always, Siya,” Vir said with a smile, ruffling the girl's hair, much to her annoyance, though she didn't fail to puff her chest out in pride.

“A mere 5,000 years and everything is in such disarray. Father would have been so disappointed.”

Vir ran a finger along the terminal of Janak's long lost lab, wiping away one of the many cobwebs that covered most surfaces.

“I'm more amazed that anything is working. Now, let's see if it was worth all this effort.”

His hands moved over the console, bringing up projections of screens. What had once been gibberish now felt like second nature to him after Siya's training. That, and the translation device she had given him.

“How is the core?” she asked, frowning.

“Dormant for now,” Vir replied. “Let us finish our business here before it flares up again. I would not want to risk an outbreak in a place as important as this.”

Siya nodded, activating a console beside Vir's.

They fell into silence as they brought up Janak's many, many logs and began reading through.

“Studies into the nature of chakra...” Vir muttered. “More studies into the nature of Chakra... Your father truly was obsessed with it, wasn't he?”

“Can you blame him?” Siya asked. “If something as new and marvelous as Chakra appeared in the world, I know I'd demand a dedicated lab to study it. For a civilization such as ours, the world long ago ceased to hold any secrets. You can't imagine how exciting something like this is.”

Exciting was one word for it. The rupture of the realms and the creation of the Ash had killed more people than anyone could count, ruining plenty more lives in the process. Like Janak's. Like Siya's.

The two trudged through log after log in silence, in what had become routine for them.

With each read entry, Vir's hope dimmed just a little. By the time he got to the end, he knew they had, once again, hit a wall.

“Nothing,” he said disgustedly, waving a hand to dismiss the projected screen. “All his theories, all his frantic work, and all he managed was to catalog the nature of Chakra and its effects. Nothing on our enemy. Nothing about this prana storage artifact he'd been working on. Nothing that could help us.”

When Siya failed to reply, Vir moved closer, taking a look at the projection she was engrossed in.

“What have you found?” Vir asked, seeing a full set of new logs he'd missed appear.

“There's more,” Siya said in a hushed voice. “So much more!”

“I don't understand,” Vir replied, scanning the text. “What is all this?”

“Vir, he didn't stop his research into prana storage because it was a dead end,” Siya said excitedly. “He simply altered course because he learned it was the wrong thing to study. The very tip of a far more profound connection. A connection... between prana and chakra.”

Vir raised his eyebrows. “But that's impossible.”

“Maybe not,” Siya replied.

“More. We need to learn more.”

Princess Tiyana Matali flew at Vir with all the hopes and hatred of all of humankind.

Haste was the only first to activate, and only because it bought her time to bring to bear all the others. Tia cycled every mote of prana in her body to empower it far beyond its normal, slowing time far beyond what was normally possible.

Empower, Bulwark, Preternatural Grace, Warrior’s Aegis. One after another, each of the many Talents she'd learned activated in a cascade, each hungry for her prana, burning through her reserves far faster than she could hope to cycle.

But prana conservation mattered little when one was up against a god.

Even without Aspect of the Demon God. Even without any of the slew of Artifacts said to have been bequeathed to him living Imperium deities in another universe, the Akh Nara was beyond any human, and she would not be the one to underestimate him.

Tia roared, lunging at him with such force that the rock beneath her boots cracked.

She became a golden blur, her muscles screaming in protest. She moved faster than she ever had in her life.

Phantom copies of her manifested, striking Vir from every direction. Each with their Artifact Talwars.

Her own original Talent, developed from the Mythic Blade Swarm, and forged from those blighted years in the Ash.

Blade Swarm, with its dozens of weapon copies, was, while impressive, ultimately less lethal than the original blade. Exceedingly effective against masses of weaker Ash Beasts, but against a powerful foe? Useful for diversions and little else.

Yet Tia’s Phantom Swarm was so much more. Not only did it copy her in full, when fed as much prana as Tia was giving it, the Talent turned into something far, far more devastating.

Each phantom feinted and each phantom struck with deadly might. B Rank Lightning Bolts fired, while not one but two precharged A rank Lightning Storms manifested, spearing Vir with enough energy to turn most beings into a charred mess.

The Akh Nara was, of course, not most beings.

The living deity saw all of her vast magic, all her Talents and immense skill... and avoided it all.

He did not attack. He did not block. He did not even move from where he stood, simply leaning, imperceptibly adjusting his body such that her every strike missed and her every magic fizzled into nothingness.

It was then that Tia realized just how horrible she'd underestimated her opponent.

It wasn't that Vir needed his armor or his weapons. He very clearly didn't.

If he'd wanted her dead, she would've died well before she'd even launched her first strike.

No, Tia realized with crushing hopelessness... Vir wasn't probing her or taking it easy...

He wasn't participating at all.

Tia bellowed a roar with every shred of her soul... and redoubled her attacks.

“There!” Siya declared. “That should do it!”

“The panel is now active,” Vir replied. “Starting the projection.”

“Always – always – always thought they were beings that operated outside the b-bounds bounds of our knowledge. Not of m-ma-matter of prana or chakra.”

Siya frowned, her hands on her hips. “It's corrupted.”

“More than enough for us to follow,” Vir said, squeezing her shoulder. “Now shush, and let us listen.”

“The truth is so much more complex... and yet simpler at the same time. It was obvious when when I ran the simulations. Really, when I just stopped and thought about it. Their efforts impact reality, do they not? Indeed, they seem able to affect everything from living beings to something as esoteric as chance itself. There must have been a connection. Some means by which they converted or manifested their vast, unknowable power into something real. It was this moment I became fixated with, and for good reason.”

Janak turned to the screen, exhausted, haggard, and yet, the pride in his eyes was undeniable.

“At long last, I believe I have found it.”

“If...” Siya whispered, eyes as wide as saucers. “If it's true...”

“If it's true,” Vir repeated. “This changes everything.”

“Why!” Tia screamed, her voice full of anguish. “Why won't you fight me!?”

Gone were the projections and the orbs. She'd spent the prana in those, and not even she could recharge A rank magic mid-combat.

For all the good it did. Her blows never landed and her magic dissipated harmlessly without touching a single hair on his skin. She might as well hurl insults instead. Damn the projections displaying her fight to humans and demons alike. Damn what others saw or heard.

She was beyond all that now. Far beyond.

“I wanted to apologize, you know?” she shouted between attacks. “Wanted... for years! To tell you how ashamed I was of how I treated you back then. To show you how I'd matured. Venturing into the Ash as penance. Surviving for... for ten long years!”

Tia Empowered her blade arm, bringing her Artifact talwar within a hair of Vir's robe, unleashing a Blade Launch at the last possible moment.

She needn't have bothered. Whatever magic Vir had was clearly beyond her. The magic was gone an instant later and he stood exactly where he had just a moment prior.

“I wanted peace between our people, you know. As badly as anyone. Everything I have ever done since you reunited the realms has been in that service. I... I know Maiya and I had our disagreements but we wanted the same end. And then... you killed her! You murdered her!”

If Blade Launch wasn't enough, she'd give him an entire barrage of them. A dozen pranic blades surged at Vir as Launch Barrage, draining her reserves further than in years.

The blades all coalesced at Vir... and Tia didn't know whether to laugh or to cry when they managed to elicit a response.

For the first time since the battle started, Vir extended his arm.

And with that outstretched arm, he absorbed every one of those pranic blades. Not just blocked or thwarted. Tia could see her prana become his.

“Why...” she whispered, trembling as her pent up emotions came flowing out. “Why won't you say something!” she screamed. “Why don't you defend yourself? Why don't you tell me there was some grand reason for why you killed her!?... why won't you say her death wasn't in vain?”

The tears flowed freely down her cheeks, on full display for her army, but Tia cared not. Let them see how much she cared for Maiya. The woman this demon murdered in cold blood. A woman who stood so high above her. So dazzlingly bright.

“I admired her...” Tia said, sinking to her knees. “I never told her. She thought I hated her. Now I'll never be able to, and her own husband can't because his mind is broken.”

She turned to the sky, where she knew her face was being projected to the gathered armies. “Do you see?” she cried. “Do you see what has become of your god?”

Silence was her only answer.

Tiyana Matali couldn't say what tormented her more. That, for all her training and experience, she was as powerless as an ant before the Akh Nara? Or that, through the whole of her heartfelt plea, the Akh Nara stood unmoving. Not saying a word. Not betraying a single emotion.
