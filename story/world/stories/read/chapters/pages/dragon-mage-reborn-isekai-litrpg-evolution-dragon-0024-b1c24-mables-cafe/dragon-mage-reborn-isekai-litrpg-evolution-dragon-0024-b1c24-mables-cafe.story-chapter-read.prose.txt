A dainty little café shop—ironic choice for a bandit club. Nolan had no problem walking these streets without guys like Varg trying to claim that juicy bounty on his head. So I had to ask why he was a free man in these beastkin parts, to which he said, as bluntly as possible, ‘They owe me.’ For the people of Ebor, he’d done plenty of services and acts of protection on their behalf, so he was on their good side. From clearing outlaws to clearing monster outbreaks, he was that guy who kept the streets clean.

Nolan also mentioned that his title involved more than just thieving from the rich and feeding the poor like some twisted badass Robin Hood. He seemed to enjoy taking on these dangerous assignments, special quests, and missions that had him uprooting corrupt businesses and exposing criminal operations. Aaannnd the kind of work that got him in the sights of powerful figures, like King Khnum.  It was his way of restoring balance, albeit the Nolan way.

A ‘Robin Hood with a high risk appetite’, as I put it.

‘You sure are a magnet for trouble,’ I commented. ‘When you told me that you were a wanted mercenary, a bandit, and all of the above, I wasn’t thinking that you were a vigilante’

“A vigilante goes out of his way to stop crime and punish criminals,” he clarified. “I’m not about the business of wanting to be a wannabe superhero.”

‘So then, thrill comes first, then everything else?’

He smiled. “Because life without risk is boring.”

We arrived at the cutesy café, a contrast to the rugged man standing right next to me. The café was a whimsical artistic enclave of pastel colors where the air smelled of fresh-brewed coffee and sugar-glazed pastries. The building stood out against the backdrop of gray and brown establishments lining the street, its exterior adorned with intricate floral patterns that gave it an almost otherworldly charm. For a second, I felt like I was walking along the streets of Tokyo with the bubbly vibes I was getting from this little slice of heaven.

A small, cobbled path led us to the entrance, miniature cherry blossom trees outlining our way. A hand-painted sign hung above the door, reading: ‘Mable’s Delights.’

The bell attached above the frame of the door chimed as soon as we walked in. The café was filled with a few patrons, some enjoying their brews while others engaged in light conversation. My eyes continued to roam, noticing the cheery wallpaper with little animated sketches of chibi baristas holding trays brimming with cakes, muffins, and steamed milk-cups covered the walls. The floor was a polished white canvas of marble tiles, reflecting the colorful pendant lamps suspended from the ceiling. I looked up as they emitted a soft glow over my head, where I realized the pendant lights were decorative bears.

Wow, Mable went with a theme and stuck to it.

A long counter stretched from one side of the wall to the other, with a glass window showcasing an array of delicious pastries and desserts. Behind it, an older man in a crisp white apron was busily arranging an array of what looked like macarons. He was tall and lean, a build I wasn’t expecting for someone his age. The man wasn’t a beastkin but a human, with hair a snowy white mane that curled at the ends, giving him a dignified, almost regal air. His skin was slightly tan, most likely from some type of outdoor work, with fine wrinkles and folds, but his eyes were vibrant—a brilliant ember that sparkled with unyielding vitality.

His face lit up when he saw Nolan, with a warm smile spreading across his creased face.

“Ah, Black! It’s been ages,” he greeted enthusiastically, leaving his work to move toward the center of the back counter where the register was.

“If by ages, you mean a few weeks, then yes,” Nolan quipped, his lips curling into a small smile. “It’s good to see you, Elefre.”

As we drew closer, his smile began to wither away. I could tell that he was staring at Nolan’s face, more specifically, the wrap he had around his missing eye.

His thin fingers pointed to his temple as he asked, “What happened there?”

“Tribal goblins got to it.”

“Got to it? You mean it’s—”

“It’s in my inventory,” Nolan intercepted. “I’m planning to replace it when I have time.”

“My god Black… The trouble you get yourself into,” Elefre sighed, returning to his cheerful demeanor. “Never a dull day. But I see here you got a new friend in the mix of things.”

“You lost an eye, but you got a dragon!” he jested.

“A bargain of a trade, right?” Nolan teased.

He turned his attention to me from behind the counter. “Ahh, she’s beautiful. A Frost Dragon. Did you really head to the Northlands to fetch her?”

“No, actually. I found her in the badlands of goblin country. And she isn’t a Northlands dragon.”

“Oh, then, what kind of a dragon is she?”

“She’s special, that’s all we have to know for now,” Nolan said behind a simple smile, not wanting to get into the details of what I was with Elefre. Which was understandable. Nolan’s subtle look over his shoulder told me that he didn’t want the customers getting into my business. He’d find the right time to tell Elefre; for now, Elefre would just have to speculate on my mysterious identity.

The old man scratched his white beard with a smirk on his face. “Special aye?” he said, then proceeded to [Inspect] me. I could tell that was what he did when his face dropped.

“Level 19? But she’s still a Dracapod?!” he muttered under his shaky voice.

“Heh, like I said, special.”

He chuckled. “Boy, I think special is putting it mildly!”

It was clear that the barista didn’t have a way to talk to me telepathically. When he asked me for my name, and I said it internally, Nolan had to repeat it for me.

“Freya, sounds like royalty!” he complimented. “Here, let me get you both something hot to drink.”

“Maybe after,” Nolan said as Elefre reached for the coffee maker. “I need to talk to Mable first.”

“Well, you know where to find her!” he beamed. “She’s out back.”

“Thanks.”

“Ah, Black, before you go,” Elefre said as he stepped to the side and pulled out a mason jar from the under-counter mini fridge. It was a third way filled with white liquid, sharing the same appearance as milk.

He slid it slowly across the white marble and stared at Nolan. “She insists I offer her specialty everytime you pay her a visit.”

Nolan flat-eyed him, and Elefre shrugged. “I haven’t tried it myself, but–”

“I’m walking out of this one before you get yourself in trouble, old man,” Nolan teased, but I didn’t get the inside joke.

Elefre tittered softly to himself as Nolan led the way, and we went through the small kitchen and reached the steel door. At first glance, it looked like one of those commercial walk-in freezers, but when Nolan approached it, I wasn’t sensing frost. He gave the metal door a series of distinct knocks, and then I noticed a pair of gold cat eyes poking from the rectangular glass window.

She looked down at Nolan like he was a stranger, and then turned her curious stare toward me.

“Quit messing around, Thea, and open up.”

“You know the drill,” her dark and raspy voice ordered. “What’s the password?”

Nolan slouched his shoulders and sighed. “We’re going to go through this every time?”

“Password,” she said again, bluntly.

“Milk-shake,” Nolan grunted.

“And the dance of entry.”

“I’m not doing that shit.”

“Dance, or you’re not allowed inside.”

“Thea!” Nolan retorted.

She looked very gothesque, and I could tell that I was going to like her already! But she wasn’t Mable. Possibly, another bandit part of the guild?

So I mosied on in front of Nolan’s feet and prepared to give the raven-haired girl a show.

I started to bob my head and sway my tail rhythmically, and then, as if I’d been doing it for years, I began to dance. A silly dragon dance with tiny steps, enthusiastic head bobs, and even a little twirl or two. Nolan watched on like this performance was commonplace for me at this point, while I rendered Thea completely speechless.

Through the narrow window in the door, I could see her golden eyes snap wide open. After a moment of stunned silence, she finally opened the door, her perky cat ears twitching at my performance. It was as if she’d never seen a dancing dragon before. And honestly, now that I thought about it, she probably never had. Given the record dragons had of being furious and rampaging. I didn’t exactly fit the bill. The door was open now, revealing a giant tank of a woman inside.

“Aye, Mable,” Nolan greeted her, but when her eyes met mine, she dropped all of her paperwork and dashed toward me, taking her desk out in the process. My innate reaction to a woman her size charging at me was to defend, but as soon as she squealed with glee, I dropped my guard and braced myself for the inevitable. It was clear that the redhead was some type of cow beastkin, with hooves, a long spotted wool tail, and short horns on the top of her head. Her and her huge jugs rushed toward me, completely disregarding Nolan. In fact, he stepped aside so he could stand clear of her opening arms, seeming to have expected this turn of events.

The closer she got, the harder the ground beneath me shook, until she grabbed me in her arms, giving me a tight squeeze that could have been mistaken as a death grip.

“Oh, she’s absolutely precious!” Mable gushed, her perky and sweet accent reminding me of the countryside. She doted on me hard, all of that high-pitched squealing suddenly turning into a rumbling purr.

“Don’t go suffocating the poor girl,” Nolan teased, while my face was buried between her breasts.

They reminded me of that one time Captain Reginald got that water bed from the flea market….

“Ain’t she just the cutest cuddly bug I ever did see!” she cooed, her finger stroking the top of my head.

Mable made me feel like a baby for the first time since I got here, being pampered with love, words of affection, and lots and lots of snuggles.

I squirmed in her iron grip, meeting the amused gaze of Nolan who looked on with a grin. It was clear that, despite his exasperation, he enjoyed this. Maybe it was the sight of me, a dragon, being cuddled like some pet poodle, or maybe it was just Mable’s unbridled enthusiasm that amused him.

“This is the most precious gift you’ve ever given me, Nolan!”

I squawked!

“Gift?” Nolan repeated as Mable walked over to the desk she knocked to the side, sitting on top of it while she continued to caress me.

“After all this time we’ve been together, you’ve finally decided to show me how much you care!” she sang as she crossed her smooth leg over the other. “Oh Nolan, you shouldn’t have,” she said as she batted her lashes at him with those bedroom eyes.

“I shouldn’t. And I didn’t. She’s no gift. And Freya is not a pet.”

“And you named her, too?!” she tweeted.

‘Hey, can you loosen your grip on me for a sec?!’ I chimed in, Mable instantly dropping me on the floor with a sharp yelp.

I stumbled back, shaking my head to clear my vision and trying to regain my demeanor. My scales were ruffled, and I definitely felt like I’d stunned her. For a minute, I’d forgotten that I was advanced for my young age, and talking in coherent sentences wasn’t something baby dragons did.

More than that, she understood me. Did everyone in this guild have a dragon scale crystal?

“Oh my goddess, I’m so sorry!” she apologized as she picked me up and settled me on the desk next to her. “I just wasn’t expecting you to talk!”

‘And I wasn’t expecting you to understand me. I guess we are both in for some surprises this morning.’

“Well, aren-chu a chatty one!” she complimented, her eyes filled with undeniable wonder. “A smart Frost Dragon.”

“Not from Northlands,” Nolan corrected. “But I could see why you’d think so.”

“She’s so pretty, Nolan…” Mable drooled, fascinated with me. Her eyes were getting all glassy again, and she was curling up. I felt like she was two seconds from scooping me up and smothering me again.

“What happened to your face?” Thea asked Nolan bluntly.

“I could ask you the same thing,” Nolan quipped, tossing a jab right back at her.

Thea crossed her arms over her chest and fed Nolan a sarcastic glare. “Funny.”

Mable proceeded to do what everyone else had the habit of doing, and [Inspect] me. The reaction was always the same, and I took the time to follow her lead.

My jaw instantly dropped:
