I stood. My body tried to object, I stood anyway. I stepped forward, barely steady. My ribs stabbed me with every breath. Martha's dress hung off me in scorched strips. Most of my skin felt raw. I had taken worse, in a body that could have shrugged off worse.

I looked down at my arms. There were two lines now. One black, the other white. Each wanted to drag me somewhere the other refused to go. I looked back up at the phoenix, then at the villagers.

We had not bought enough time. They still pressed against the small exits, their shouts drowning each other. Then there was Sara, somewhere behind me. We still needed to fight. I took two more steps forward, and then I was standing next to the Hero.

The Hero looked at me, then she looked at my arms. "I'll buy time," Ash said. "I can last for a while." She raised her sword and charged. There was no aura coating the Hero's Sword. I suspected there never would be anymore.

The beast fired at her. Ash cut balls of flame with her sword as she pushed in close. The beast tried to strike her with one of its four wings. Ash ducked beneath it, and carved a line on the joint with the tip of her blade. The wing severed and fell. As before, it did not stay on the ground. It rose, and when the wing reformed, it was thicker than it had been.

Ash was buying time for me to do something. Against a beast that could so easily kill her. I had done the same for her, once. Against an Elk thrice as tall as I was. The phoenix launched a burst of flame that caught Ash's side. She rolled and came back up to her feet, and then she was charging again.

I stepped forward and extended my right hand. Black flame extended from my hand and struck the phoenix's side. The scales greyed and then cracked. The feathers curled and blackened as the phoenix screamed. Then the fire burst across its body, burning the rot away.

I had not used the Requiem this way before, and yet it felt natural. Instinctively, I knew the range -five meters. I knew the cost -a not insignificant amount of mana with my reserves. The Requiem’s black flame only had the appearance of fire. It was a darker color than the one the phoenix used. It was…an absence in the world. A cessation of all that was and could have ever been.

The phoenix screamed again. Ash swung at it before it could turn to face me. I extended my hand again as more skin blackened under the darkness, cleaned a few seconds later by golden flame. In the moment of contact, I saw something that came through the mark. It was an impression, though it would not have been wrong to call it a vision instead.

There was a sky, impossibly wide and blue in all directions. The wind was clean. The sun was bright and soft. Then something seeped into the blue. Black. It started from one point, and it spread outwards. It poured across the horizon the way ink pours into water.

The sky that was blue became dark. The wind that had been clean tasted of acid. The sun that had been soft became scalding. And the creature that had been whole became something that burned and burned and could not stop burning.

The vision made me stagger. I almost dropped to my knees. The black flame flickered before I threw the Requiem back against the beast once more. The flames struck the creature and decayed its shoulder. The phoenix burst in golden fire again, and the decay was wiped clean.

And through the black flame of the Requiem, I could feel the nature of the phoenix. This corruption, those mismatched eyes, those wrong joins, all of those were something forced about the phoenix. They were all chains. Just like my own Role had been.

The creature had been only gold. I could still feel some of it, faint and guttering underneath all of the black, like embers buried under smoke. Like a small, pathetic campfire refusing to go out. Something of what the creature had been was still in there, and it was still fighting. It had been fighting for centuries. Perhaps longer than that.

It was that part of the phoenix that held the rest back from killing us. That was why we were still alive. My right arm trembled. I knew this creature. I knew it the way I knew my own face. For centuries, I had raged against a cage no one else could see. Through a mad gambit and a madder Hero I had broken free.

This creature would never break free. It had no mad gambit and no hero to be its unwitting key. It would burn and burn and forever burn.

The Hero was getting slower. I could see it in the way she stood. In the way her sword trembled when she raised it. She did not have much time left. She rushed for the phoenix's right leg, but the beast had seen her too. Its beak opened, and I saw a great gout of flame build in its throat.

"Move!" I shouted. Ash threw herself sideways. The blast scorched the earth where she had been. She hit the ground on her shoulder and picked herself up slowly. Then Ash backed away. The phoenix did not pursue her, though it could have.

I looked back at my hands. I had two powers. Warmth and cold. The Requiem could kill this creature. I was certain of it now. If I poured everything I had left into the Requiem, the phoenix would die. The wrongness that was this creature…and the gold that still struggled beneath.

It would be an ending. An ending for a creature that had suffered for far too long. It would be clean. I had given clean endings before. To cities. To races. To Gods. Clean ending were all I had ever known.  I almost did it.

But a Hero had sat across a pathetic campfire from me, and she had given me food. An old woman had filled my bowl without being asked. A girl had made me a vase from mud. One I had broken, and then spent an afternoon on my knees trying to make another one that was worse in every way, and the girl had held it to her chest and smiled.

Those were all beginnings. Of what, I did not know. I chose not to do as I had only ever done. Not because some higher power stayed my hand.

I was tired of only bringing endings. I did not yet know if I, the most despicable Demon Queen in history, deserved it.

"Lysanthia!" Ash's voice, ragged. "What are you doing? Stop!"

I walked towards it. I would have run, if my legs had let me. Each slow and agonizing step closed the gap between it and me. And the Phoenix watched me come. I saw it twitch in place. It could have so easily killed me.

I reached for the beast's chest. The beast's feathers were on fire, and it burned through whatever strips of the dress remained. It hurt. I did not care. I took a breath and pressed both hands against its chest. And then I used both of my powers.

I used the Requiem first and black flame spread through the creature. I aimed it at corruption and black feathers withered beneath my hand. And beneath the black, just like I had felt, there was only gold. A fading gold, but a gold all the same.

I followed with the Cradle's white. I aimed it at the golden feathers and it spread over them with warmth. The gold did not overpower the black. The dull gold stayed a dull gold, and I understood.

The Cradle was not enough to heal this creature. It had been burning this way for too long. I could not save this thing. But there was something left beneath the black, and beneath even the fading gold. A single spark, deep in the creature's chest that was only brilliant. I reached for it.

Perhaps some part of the phoenix felt it, for its whole body went still. Then its golden eyes found mine. And in their reflections, I could see myself. Small and burnt, I looked nothing like a Queen. The phoenix pressed its weight against my palms. Some of the weight was its size. Most of it was the weight of centuries lived. I knew that second weight well. The phoenix wanted an ending.

Only an ending.

"I know," I said. My voice came out very soft. My hands shook. "I will give you what I can."

And I used everything I had. All of the mana still left to me. The Requiem took the corruption. The Cradle caught what fell. "I'm sorry," I whispered again, feeling something warm trail down my cheeks.

If I knew this power better, I could have done more. Could have preserved it as it had once been. The phoenix's eyes dimmed. First the black ones, then the gold. Its massive body began to crack and dissolve. And then the last of its power, trapped inside the feathers, spiralled outwards.

Gold and black flame rushed over me. The fire was in my lungs, in my eyes. It covered the whole of me. My skin screamed and every nerve I had told me to let go. I held on. The last thing I saw was gold.

Then, there was nothing at all.

Ash saw Lysanthia walk into the fire and screamed.

It was a pained cry, one that Ash had used so many times on her journey, for people she had tried and failed to protect. Tried and failed to protect from the one walking into the flames.

The flames grew and spiralled inward, gold and black twisting together in a column that pierced the clouds. The heat drove Ash back five steps. She dug her heels in and tried to push forward. She could not reach her.

Ash screamed again and charged. She channeled the last of her old mana, everything she had left. It did not matter. The greatest monster in history burned in front of her, and Ash could do no more than barely keep her place.

The vortex of flame suddenly collapsed, and the light that had been blinding dimmed and winked out. The roaring of the flames stopped, leaving behind absolute silence. Ash ran over ground that cracked beneath her boots. It was still hot enough that she could feel it through her soles. Embers of gold and dark drifted and fell in every direction. They almost looked like rain.

Not again. Not again. Ash thought. Please, not again.

She found Lysanthia at the impacts' center.

The Demon Queen was curled on her side, her body drawn tight around something pressed to her chest. She was naked, Ash saw that now. Most of her skin was burned and blistered. Even her horns were cracked. One of them had lost its tip. Her hair was gone in patches, singed to nothing.

She looked nothing like the woman who had killed Ronan. Nothing like the one who had tried to fight and destroy the world by herself. A distant part of Ash realized Lysanthia had not looked like that for some time.

For a moment, Ash almost thought she was dead. Then she noticed it -slow and rhythmic breathing. Ash finally breathed, she did not know how long it had been since she last had.

Then Ash saw what Lysanthia was clutching. She saw a golden egg -small enough to cradle in two hands. Veins of black ran through the shell, pulsing as Ash watched.

Ash dropped to her knees on the scorched earth. On Lysanthia's left arm, through the ruin of burned skin, the white Line still glowed. On her right, the black Line drank in the light.

Ash, the Last Hero, wept for Lysanthia, the Last Demon Queen.
