Reality could be disappointing sometimes.Kevin learned rather quickly that normal animals were not exactly great conversationalists. After his third or fourth attempted conversation with a chipmunk or a rabbit while he was in chipmunk form, he realized it wasn't very surprising either. After all, these were not exactly the smartest creatures in the world.

The first chipmunk he tried to talk to had just repeated, "Spot the danger and food, don't have time," over and over again. The rabbit had been equal parts hungry and horny. Then the second chipmunk he tried to talk to was thinking about water but worried about predators. When Kevin spent a few minutes observing a deer near a barking squirrel that it was totally ignoring, he decided to try another conversation.

When he shimmied down the tree and said, "Hello, how are you today?" as politely as he could, using [Soul Speak], the deer's head whipped around. Although it did not possess the same facial muscles or intelligence as a person, if the deer could have raised its eyebrows, it would have.

"A small tree thing is talking to me," said the deer. "This is the first time that has happened."

Kevin said, "Yes, I'm just trying to make friends today and chat with the other animals."

The deer blinked at him with big watery eyes. "How are you talking to me, small tree thing? I have never spoken to a small tree thing before."

"Uh, yes," said Kevin. "Well, I saw you eating here and I decided to say hello."

"But you are a small tree thing," said the deer, "and you are talking to me. How can this be?"

Kevin stared at the deer, which stared back, and he accepted the reality that most animals were too stupid to have a real conversation with. Part of him was disappointed about that. The part of him that regularly ate animals was not. If he had suddenly found out that all the animals in the forest could talk and think just like he could, he might have to adopt a vegetarian with monster meat-only diet. That would be inconvenient, to say the least.

Since Kevin was a goblin now, even considering such a thing made him shudder. Not only that, if he had found out the animals had personalities as deep as people and had changed his own diet, he would have felt morally obligated to tell others too.

Wow, dodged a huge bullet there, he thought to himself. For now, he decided that speaking to animals had limited usefulness.

Back to the drawing board, thought Kevin. He sat on a tree limb and pondered deeply what he could do during his journey to the goblin country in order to get stronger. The irony that the longer he sat around thinking about this, the longer it would actually take to get to Tallridge Hollow, was not lost on him. But he wasn't in a huge rush, and now that he'd actually decided that he liked having Lydia around, it didn't seem like the worst thing in the world to take a little longer before getting to his destination. Besides, having his dragon cousin around would definitely be good for his prospects of a prolonged life.

Kevin didn't know what to expect from Tallridge Hollow, but he couldn't imagine goblins being less respectful as humans when it came to polymorphed dragons. He amended that he was making a great many assumptions, but for the time being, this is what he was going to accept as truth going forward. Nothing he'd read in all of his books contradicted anything that he was assuming, anyway.

Finally, as Kevin thought about how to increase his control over spirit magic, he realized that there was a relatively simple solution to all of this.
