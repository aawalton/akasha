Interlude IX

Dual Requirements

Department of Healing – Unity University

(Head Healing Professor: Florence Livanathan)

Communication is one of the most important methods for countries to remain both functional and relevant in the now and in the future. That is why the University that has been charted as by far and away the best establishment for teaching new and promising minds of all the empires left standing. Given how important the strength and value of knowledge and cutting-edge techniques have become. That is why selection into the different departments can make or break an individual and indeed a team that will survive on the front lines. Given the importance of a true healer on a team, admission into the Healer’s department of the University has become the most important, which also has caused the selection process to be that much more stringent.

When selections inevitably got to Florence’s desk, it was typically the last chance at rejection. The system would have acted as the initial arbitrators to see if all components of a signed contract were met. Meaning the last piece that Matriarch Livanathan could do was deny an applicant based on their personality or morality concerns. Something that was not unheard of but had been used in the past to weed out some of the more sadistic healer candidates that first applied to the University.

“What is that?” Portia, Florence’s top secretary and well known informant to the other department heads asks, indicating the message that has been transcribed, sealed, and now presented to her by Portia herself. Seeing her, Florence holds her tongue, knowing that anything she says can and will be told in triplicate to the other departments and worse to the President of the University. Which is why Florence takes a moment to breathe, before delivering her clear and concise answer.

“We have received a review request from one of the South-Western territories,” Florence declares, keeping her answer vague so that others can’t conspire to reach out and try to interfere with her operations.

“South-Western?” Portia confirms, though it is clear that she would like more information.

“Yes, it seems that according to the system, a group has signed and already fulfilled the full conditions for a Self-Created Teams Doctrine contract.”

At that comment, Portia looked shocked, a look alone that was worth divulging even this much information to the informant. Seeing her in such a state, Florence almost thought this was worth the cost of information she was readily giving out.

Better still, knowing that there was a healer capable of easily fulfilling all of the terms and conditions required for a Self-Created Teams Doctrine contract was worth the price of admission. Added to that was the fact that she had already been planning to go to that very same province thanks to an earlier missive requiring her immediate attention.

“Oh this is big, should I begin scheduling travel plans for you to personally inspect this individual? For the morality clause?” Portia asked, clearly trying to weed out the wanted information.

“No need,” Florence replied, a faint smile forming on her lips as she saw the look of confusion and masked irritation flutter across Portia’s face before her mask of indifference was firmly set back in place.

“No need?”

“No, for we are already planning to go there this afternoon.”

“We are?”

“Yes, remember the urgent missive for the Purging Trial provided by General Brack who found a nest of Death Eater smugglers hiding in an arena?” Florence asked, her words causing a myriad of emotions to float across Portia’s face.

“Wait, you mean it is in General Brack’s territory, but I thought he—” Portia asked, but her words were cut off as Florence just nodded.

“Yes, his territory has long since been consumed by the Z-Rot, and other forces. In fact, for a time everyone thought the General and his army were destroyed, but they came back, battle hardened and angry. Angry at the state of what their country had become in their absence and now angry at the fact that the people that stole his granddaughter kept her as a circus performer.” Florence noted.

“So the daughter is the healer?” Portia asked. Hearing the confusion in her words, Florence only smiled, knowing that she was leading around the informant by the nose, and enjoying every minute of her confusion.

“No,” Florence offered. Then with a pause, she continued, “but she is one of the confirmed individuals to receive training here thanks to her completion of the Self-Created Teams Doctrine contract.”

Now Portia just stood there mouth agape as she tried to comprehend all the different ways that this conversation had gone.

“So who is this new Healer?” Portia asked.

“I don’t know.”

“You must know, it is part of the contract.”

“Well, it seems that the person who completed the contract for General Brack’s grandchild and friend did so as a guarantor.”

“A guarantor? Is such a thing even allowed?”

“Yes, actually it is, and it seems that a well-known Healer who never actually attended these halls as a student was able to sign for herself, leaving room for her apprentice to take her place if needed.”

“Wait? Who signed?”

“One General Anastasia Bloom, but you might know her better by her code name, Death Defier.”

“Death Defier? Wait, I thought she was dead?”

“Retired, went to some back woods town to live out the rest of her life in seclusion, but has apparently become the heart of not one but two different but seemingly conjoined events. What with having taken an active role in General Brock’s return, and forced trial, and now signing up for said General’s grandchildren to receive elite education. And you know what that means,” Florence paused as she let the unspoken thought linger in the air. A thought that she was almost certain that Portia had already bandied about once in her mind.

“What?”

“That the morality clause will not matter. That regardless of what dirt we might find on this Apprentice that the General clearly wants to receive this education for. That even if we fail her, there is no way we can fail General Bloom on morality,” Florence concluded, and as she did she saw the wind get sucked right out of her secretary as if she had been gut punched. For that was a common trend that Portia and the other department heads had concocted, void the initial contract for a team, and then have a retrial for the healer, one that the healer would pass. Then have that healer be forced to group with the best and brightest that the other department heads either personally chose, or owed favors to, or both. Basically, this signing with General Bloom as the guarantor made one less headache that Florence would have to deal with. One less chance of political sabotage, as anyone willing to get on the General’s bad side by forcing her to take the place of her Apprentice would be asking for trouble. For that very General would have to be here on campus everyday, and she would find out who sabotaged her Apprentice.

“When are you going to meet with this new Apprentice Healer?” Portia asked, holding out a document and seeming to take notes.

“Right now, the train leaves in a few moments, and as usual I will be taking the entire Healing Faculty, along with all of the enrolled students with me.”

“Wait, the entire department?” Portia asked.

“Yes, didn’t you already schedule our departure times for later today anyways?” Florence asked.

“Wait for what again?”

“For us to attend the trial and for us to show the new generation of healers what to look for and how to spot Death Eater smugglers, along with showing what their punishments will be for anyone caught smuggling Death Eaters.”

“Right, should I come with you?” Portia asked, a note of nervous concern on her face as it was clear she needed to find out who this apparent apprentice would be. To get some details other than she is General Bloom’s new Apprentice.

“No, while I am gone, I will need you to take over all Healing Department related duties in my stead.”

“Right—” Portia stammered out, and before she could say or do anything else, Florence used that exact moment to walk out and head to the train yard, her overnight bag, and her black healer’s garment already packed away neatly.  Then stopping, she paused and turned to Portia as if just remembering something.

“Actually, there is something you can do for me,” Florence stated.

“There is?”

“Yes, gather all the house banners, I don’t know if where we are going will have all of them,” Florence stated and then began moving before Portia could reply.

“Right,” was the one-word response that a clearly disappointed Portia offered.

Just hearing her pain filled response caused a jolt of excitement to surge through Florence. It was sad that she had been reduced to enjoying the pain of the spy that had been thrust upon her by the University, but by now she had learned to take the joys in life where she could. Feeling suddenly lighter, Florence felt herself gracefully gliding to the train station where she was pleased to see that all of the department students and faculty were already loaded and accounted for.

With a quick check, she also nodded to herself that all house banners had already been gathered, cleaned and assembled, just in case. Seeing that everything was in order, and that Portia might be running around wildly looking for items that no longer existed, she gave a gesture to the conductor who nodded and began preparations for their train to take off, before anyone else could weasel their way onto her trip. A trip that now required her to proceed over a trial, and to evaluate a potential new recruit, simple and best of all, it got her out of the stuffy university.

There was also the benefit that she would finally be able to see her friend and mentor when she arrived. All in all, a good day.
