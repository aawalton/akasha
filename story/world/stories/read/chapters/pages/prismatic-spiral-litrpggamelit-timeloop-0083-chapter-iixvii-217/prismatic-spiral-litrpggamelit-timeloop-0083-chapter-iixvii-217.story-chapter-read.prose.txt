Chapter II.XVII (2.17)

Jacqueline guided them up a switchbacking path along a snow covered mountain. More than once, Mitsuko had to Mend the soft snow to keep the landscape from collapsing into a full avalanche. It likely hadn’t been this consistently warm in centuries. The stretches of mountain where snow had already fallen away revealed brown rocks and dirt. This high up, there wasn’t a plant in sight.

“Are you certain you do not require assistance?” Jacqueline asked.

Mitsuko, shouldering Holly on her back, waved away the offer. “Don’t worry about me. If Holly clings to your back she might end up frostbitten from exposure to you.”

“But is your physical body fine? You don’t need to rest?” Jacqueline asked. “Flesh and blood wears easier than my structure.”

“I have magic assisting me,” Mitsuko explained. The sculpt might interpret that as some sort of enhancement spell or she might realize it was another example of the limited temporal spellwork she’d already witnessed. Mitsuko didn’t particularly care at the moment. If Jacqueline was under the guardian’s control, then she would already know that either Mitsuko or Holly were their opponent. And if not, then the loop was coming to a close soon regardless so it wasn’t as if more information leaking meant much at this stage. She doubted the sculpt could even make it back into town in time.

Jacqueline, despite being chosen for the role as their guide, had a slight hobble to her step. That wobble acted as another deterrent to keep Mitsuko from handing Holly over to her. Mitsuko could react faster to disaster if Holly was with her.

“Is your leg fine?” Mitsuko finally asked as they pulled off to a bluff overlooking their side of the mountain for a break. An unnecessary stop for Mitsuko who continued to keep herself in peak condition, but a likely a welcome respite for the sculpt woman.

Jaqueline scrunched up her snowy face in discomfort. “It melted as we retreated into the mountains. I was dealt some of the least concerning injuries compared to many of my brethren, as I had my coat; however, my leg still suffered damage. Many others experienced far worse.”

Mitsuko recalled King Lars’ melted face. And she’d heard the population of sculpts on the whole had dwindled significantly as they took casualties. A double digit percentage drop in their species’ overall numbers.

She sat on the snowy cliff with her legs dangling over the edge. Far below was another forest of pine trees. And beyond that, Mitsuko could spot the small mountain village they’d come from in the distance. A cluster of wooden buildings shoved together. Not a proper village at all. More like a hunting outpost or mining colony. But it was all the people had as an option to retreat to.

“The children were fine because their size allowed them to need less juhyo fur?” Holly asked.

“Yes. And they were prioritized by many parents. I have no children and no younger siblings, which worked to my fortune in this turn of events.”

“Can I see your leg?” Mitsuko asked. “I might have a method of healing the damage.”

The sculpt woman pulled back and eyed her with suspicion. “You know something about sculpt healing? Where did you learn? Our bodies might have the same base structure as a standard humanoid, but I promise they are not like yours in the slightest.”

“It heals every species,” Holly said. “Let her give it a look.”

While the frown remained on Jacqueline’s face, she relented and rolled up her left pant leg.

Snow white with veins of icy blue. Mid-thigh, the frozen material jutted out, creating a jagged area right above the sculpt’s knee. Mitsuko ran a finger over the rough thigh.

“So do you think you can repair the damage?” Jaqueline asked. “Refreeze it properly?”

Despite her hesitation, Mitsuko noticed a glint of hope in the sculpt’s eye. Mitsuko glanced in Sterling’s direction. He watched her but said nothing. Surely he’d speak up if this was an impossibility? Mend Others almost certainly included sculpts. She saw no reason it would be locked solely to humans.

“Yes,” Mitsuko said confidently. She pressed her hand against the leg. The snow of her leg was surprisingly stiff under her touch, but she felt it melt slightly as her body heat invaded the woman’s fragile body. Small wisps of steam leaked from their contact. That hardly mattered though. Misuko cast Mend.

She felt the will of the leg. She denied it the movements it experienced over the last few days, instead focusing on the essence of what it was. Solely the leg, not the rest of Jaqueline’s body. It still jerked a bit, but the impression her hand left on the snow popped back into place.

Jaqueline cried out in fear as the leg’s damaged area melted. But in a matter of seconds, her leg reformed into a perfectly smooth stretch of snow and ice.

Mitsuko removed her hand before her body heat could reinflict damage to the exposed leg.

Speechless, the sculpt ran her fingers down her now perfect leg. Silence stretched and Mitsuko exchanged a look with Holly.

“It was well done,” Sterling proclaimed from behind her. The cat, while his voice remained a bit of the grump from having to climb a mountain earlier, nodded his head in approval. “You should do your best to seek out other unique species to test my spell on. It will further your experience and knowledge in regards to Mend. The final level will be a difficult hurdle to clear, so you need to put in a lot of effort to achieve it.”

Mitsuko considered all the different species she’d seen on Mauve Island. There should be a pretty wide variety. It was simply a matter of finding people in need of healing. It wasn’t as if she had any intention of hurting and then Mending them simply for experience with the spell. She wasn’t a sociopathic sadist.

Wiping her stoney eyes, Jaqueline concealed her leg behind her fur pants and stood. Then she bowed deeply to Mitsuko. “Thank you. A thousand times over. Thank you.”

Mitsuko made a little wave, a bit embarrassed. “It’s not any trouble. In fact, I feel like I learned a lot helping you. And you’re already doing us a favor by acting as our guide.”

“Let us continue then. If there are people on this island who did this to us, then they should pay with interest for the death and pain they’ve inflicted.”

“That’s the spirit!” Holly cheered. She hopped to her feet and dusted off her jacket.

Mitsuko joined them, but as they left the bluff behind and journeyed further into the mountains, Mitsuko looked back one more time at the serenity they departed from. The little brown cabins in the distance, tucked away between the mountains. Even still, with mountains on all sides, the sun beat down on the village from directly overhead. Unless Mitsuko did something, this entire island would melt.

She journeyed on, resolute.

After another hour of travel, Mitsuko stood on the rim of a drop off, this one far less steep than the bluff from earlier, but still not easily hikable. Breaking an ankle was basically a given. Getting buried in an avalanche just as likely.

“The most easily accessible ice tunnel is at the bottom of this basin,” Jacqueline said.

Mitsuko blinked, then bit down the inside of her cheek hard enough to make it bleed. Of course it would be down a steep and dangerous slope. Nothing could ever be simple and easy for Mitsuko. Next island probably had its temple in the center of a lava filled volcano.

“Any ideas on how to get down?” Mitsuko asked. She could probably throw herself down and let her body tumble and break. Repairing her physical body wasn’t an issue for her. But getting buried three meters deep in an avalanche might prove more difficult of a hurdle. She wondered if she could Mend the snow back to where it came from without it carrying her off with it.

“We have some equipment,” Jacqueline said, her tone chipper, as if excited. “Follow me, the shed is over here.”

Mitsuko and Holly exchanged a curious look. What sort of equipment did Jacqueline have in mind? Mitsuko doubted snow shoes would cut it for what they needed. They followed after the sculpt and found a small wooden building, half buried in the wet snow.

Her questions remained as she examined a shed full of sleds and slabs of wood.

“These are enchanted?” Holly asked as she picked up one of the slabs of wood. It was longer than she was.

For the first time, Jacqueline dropped back her fur hood and genuinely smiled. She beamed at the equipment around her. She licked her frozen lips. “I thought I would never again… but now, with my leg healed. Back in perfect condition.”

“Jacqueline?” Mitsuko waved a hand in front of the sculpt to get her attention.

She blinked and finally tore her gaze away from the shed’s contents.

“These are skis! The enchantments will allow them to stick to your feet. They take a fair bit of skill and practice to get used to, but with a little training, a slope like this will pose no hurdle to you.”

“How much practice are we talking?” Mitsuko asked. She lifted one of the skis and turned it in her hand.

“Depends on the person. Most outsiders don’t learn. I’m used to seeing children learn over several years.”

Mitsuko did not have several years to spend. But she also wasn’t a child. Not right now, at least.

“Let’s see what we’re working with.” She brought the pair of skis out with her and set them down in the snow outside.

Both Holly and Jacqueline followed her, the latter with a bit of a spring in her step. She slapped down her skis next to Mitsuko and slipped her boots into place. The bindings glowed slightly as an enchantment activated.

“Is there a way back up?” Mitsuko asked, examining the slope carefully.

“An enchanted rope is designed to pull us back up.”

“Maybe it’s better to use that to take us down as well?” Holly said nervously. The pair of skis she held were clearly designed for children, fun little shapes carved into the wood. “I mean, it looks fun… but if I fall, it also looks really, really cold.”

“The snow is soft,” Jacqueline reassured her.

Mitsuko glanced back at the soft snow below her and noted dozens of not soft boulders strewn about.

“Besides,” Jacqueline continued, “your skis are meant for children. They’ve been enchanted to stick a bit more to the snow so as to not travel quite as quickly. That will help you maintain control.”

“Wait. I want those!” Mitsuko said. “Give me a pair of children’s skis too!”

“Your weight would put too much stress on them and break them.”

Holly snickered. “She just called you fat, Mitsuko.”

“She did not call me fat,” Mitsuko shot back. “She’s saying that I’m not child-sized.”

Mitsuko stared down the slope. She might not be a diviner, but she foresaw a lot of bruises in her near future.

Sterling edged away from Mitsuko in the direction of Jacqueline. He had the shame to at least slink over with guilt-ridden posture. The sculpt scooped the cat up.

“Traitor,” Mitsuko muttered. “All that talk of believing in me and you flee at the slightest sign of danger.”

If Sterling heard her, he showed no sign of it.

“We can start over here first,” Jaqueline suggested. Rather than move in the direction of the slope down to the ice caves, the sculpt gestured the opposite way. A slight incline that ended with a massive mound of snow. “Just an hour or so of training to get you used to the equipment.”

“Half an hour,” Mitsuko bartered. She knew that more time getting used to the equipment would help her, but she didn’t have the time to waste if she wanted even a bit of time to explore the ice caverns.

Jacqueline reluctantly agreed half her original time and immediately launched into instructions on controlling the skis.

While the sun remained stationary overhead, Mitsuko still felt the seconds creep by as she face planted repeatedly on the gentle slope.

“You’re both performing excellently!” Jacqueline said. Ever since arriving at the shed full of skis, her entire personality seemed to have shifted into that of an excitable and encouraging teacher.

Mitsuko resisted the urge to snap back as she trudged back up the slope, skis tucked under an arm. She wasn’t certain if Mending her body would undo all the experience she’d gained learning how to control and balance on the skis. So her knees ached. Her feet hurt. Her fingers were numb from the cold. But improvements were slowly making themselves known.

Despite her hesitance, Holly seemed to actually be enjoying the exercise. She fell a fraction of the amount of Mitsuko. Likely due to the cheating enchantments placed on her skis and not Mitsuko’s.

“I think you’re both good enough to get down without dying,” Jacqueline proclaimed as they returned to the crest of the slope. “Just remember wide turns. Keep your knees bent. And don’t be afraid to fall to the mountainside if you feel yourself losing control.”

Even knowing that she could repair her body on a whim, Mitsuko still felt a kernel of dread as she stared down at the other side of the mountain. There must be a dozen safer and easier ways down this steep slope, and yet Jacqueline was insistent they ski.

“Well, let’s get this over with,” she muttered, then shuffled off the edge.

At first everything was fine. Just long stretches of switchbacks with awkward turns. Mitsuko fell, but always toward the mountain. No wounds worse than light bruising. Jaqueline, with Sterling safely perched on her shoulders, led them down a safe and secure path. Holly followed behind Mitsuko, only crashing into her once. A surprisingly successful descent for the first half of the slope.

Then Mitsuko spotted movement. A swelling of snow. She jerked her skis in an attempt to slow herself. The movement toppled her to the side and she lost track of where she’d seen the motion. She scanned the slope carefully as Holly passed her by.

Sterling shifted on Jaqueline’s shoulders, causing the sculpt to turn her head back and spot Mitsuko pressed against the slope. She cupped her hands together around her mouth. “Everything okay back—”

A blast cut the sentence off and a flurry of snow cut off Mitsuko’s vision of the sculpt woman. A sickening crunch followed by Sterling’s yowl. Noise from the cat ceased after only a second.

From within the cloud of snow, several massive bright blue eyes glowed.
