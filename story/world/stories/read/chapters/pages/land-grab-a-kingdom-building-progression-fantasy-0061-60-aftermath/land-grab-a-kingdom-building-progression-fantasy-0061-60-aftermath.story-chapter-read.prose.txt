“Tavian? Tavian, wake up!”

Tavian blinked his eyes open; fires still burned in the hall around him. He hadn’t been out for long.

“Lea? Is something wrong? Is the fortress safe?”

“All is well here, I was just worried about you.”

“I’m alive,” Tavian sent, scanning his surroundings.

Lord Anarvia lay unconscious across the room, shards of the shattered Witherstone over him.

Tavian groaned, dragging himself to his feet. His whole body screamed and ached, like he’d been dragged through a maelstrom on a rope behind a clipper.

He reached for his sword, realized it wasn’t there, and then saw the Core. Or what remained of it.

It had detonated, sending sharp metal shards around the room, embedding themselves into the stone. The explosion had knocked down all the ghouls, too. Tavian hoped that without the Witherstone, they would not rise again.

Cormath’s sword lay on the ground nearby, cracked and glowing.

Where was Cormath?

Tavian spotted him across the room. He couldn’t make out the man’s condition, beneath the thick robe he wore, but he was moving.

He stumbled over to the man. “Cormath,” he gasped. “Cormath, you’re still alive!”

“Still undead,” the man said, grimly, turning to face Tavian. “No thanks to you.”

Several gouges had damaged his face, raw, bloodless wounds that would never heal. His cloak was torn and slashed in several places, revealing more wounds.

The horror of what his former sword master had become gripped Tavian. Cormath pressed a palm to his forehead. “What did you do to my sword?” he asked.

“I’m sorry,” Tavian gasped. “I had too… It was the only way…”

“I…” Cormath looked around. “I don’t remember anything. Anything from before I died,” he said.

“How did you know to come here?” Tavian asked.

“I felt the same pull of power that attracted the ghouls,” Cormath said. “When I felt the Core go miasmic, I guessed you might have had something to do with it.”

“Come on,” Tavian said, as Cormath staggered toward the center of the room and picked up his blade. He examined the crack in the sword.

“That’ll teach me,” Cormath muttered. “I never should have used this as a weapon. Or trusted a boy with it.” He slid the sword back into its sheath, rolling out his shoulders and scanning the room.

“I’m sorry,” Tavian said. “I needed something to destroy the Core.” He crossed over to where his Aegis had fallen, and picked it up. He needed to do something about Lord Anarvia before he woke up. The man looked like a deflated waterskin; the miasmic power that had swollen his body had left, leaving loose flaps of flesh sagging to the floor.

Tavian struggled across the room and knelt beside the man. He recovered belts from nearby corpses, and used them to bind the man’s arms and legs. Lord Anarvia would face justice, publicly.

Braun. What had happened to Braun? He’d sent his brother to link up with Donath.

“Cormath,” Tavian said, turning. The sword master had been with Donath, hadn’t he? But the lich was gone. Tavian stood alone, the only living being in the audience hall, surrounded by bodies.

“Lea, is Braun still alive?”

“Give me a minute, since he’s not within your borders,” she said. “Let me check.”

Tavian moved toward the door. His men needed his support. There were still ghouls out there.

He paused. There was one thing he could do right away, to help the survivors. It was time to extend his kingdom. Tavian opened his system interface and selected all the tiles in a straight line between himself and Tavirost fortress.

These he claimed. They began flipping, his domain reaching out toward the battlefield that was Anarvia’s compound. He couldn’t claim a tile that didn’t currently border his kingdom, so the process took some time.

He claimed more tiles, everywhere he thought some of his men might be. Anarvia’s compound. The warehouse.

With each new tile claimed, he felt a rush; a sensation of strength and power building inside.

Euphoria.

His kingdom grew. The kingdom must grow.

He wanted to claim enough tiles to go to Tier IV. He wanted to claim the whole city; which would take him to Tier V.

Fighting the urge that would certainly lead to overextension, Tavian waited until the claims completed.

He felt a rush of strength as the tile where he stood was annexed into his kingdom, and he began to benefit from its influence.

⬣ Kingdom Status ⬣

A thousand more tiles to get to Tier IV. It was enough for now; he’d get there soon enough, especially with the treasures that Lord Anarvia had failed to sift so far.

Then there were perhaps twenty-five thousand people living in the city. With an army of Karath Amon fast approaching, they’d come to swear fealty to Tavian. And they’d pay a tax.

For the first time since Ilara had tried to kill him in his bedchambers, he felt something besides desperation.

“Braun’s alive,” Lea reported. “You just claimed the tiles where he is.”

So Braun was close.

“Is he injured?”

“I can’t say. I just know he’s still connected to theCore system. But Tavian, people are dying.”

Tavian needed to go find him. He needed a weapon, there were still ghouls out there…

His sword. Tavian looked back to the dais, where Ilara lay.

Where Ilara had lain. A flash of movement caught his attention, the hem of a gown disappearing through a doorway.

Tavian cursed himself. The woman had a knack for slipping out of places. Halin, the coresmith, had disappeared too.

He took a few steps toward the exit near the dais. Ilara couldn’t get far. He might be able to catch her.

Dragging himself forward, his body ached. Blood dribbled from open wounds onto the floor. His enhanced constitution supported him, and he jogged toward the door.

Braun could be dying out there.

Tavian reached the door in time to catch a glimpse of Ilara disappearing around a corner. She was heading away from the main gate. Away from Braun, and Donath.

To the west, where the Karath Amon were. The pieces fell into place for Tavian, and he understood that Ilara, not Lord Anarvia, had employed the Karath Amon. In fact, he was starting to recognize her fingerprints all over the coup.

Tavian heard the distant shouts of men fighting.

If he caught Ilara, would that stop the Karath Amon?

If he could even catch up with her—might he not be running into another one of her traps?

Tavian cut off his pursuit. He needed to find Braun.

Turning back, he jogged toward the entrance of the hall. A fist of Anarvian men-at-arms surrendered to him as he passed, but he ignored them. The enemy was not his concern right now; all of Lord Anarvia’s men would be giving up now. What soldier kept fighting after the destruction of his Kingdom’s Core?

Tavian felt a rush of emotion for the men and women who had joined with him the night of the coup. They should have surrendered. But they refused to abandon Tavian.

He would not abandon them.

Tavian emerged from the hall, blinking in the pale morning twilight. A battlefield greeted him there.

Bodies littered the ground. Men fought, moving about in small units. Ghouls died.

Tavian found a squad captain and flagged him down. “My brother!” Tavian asked. “Where is Braun?”

“I don’t know, my lord,” the man said.

“Donath?”

“He’s at the gate.” The man pointed.

“Keep fighting,” Tavian said. “These ghouls will not rise again. We’ve destroyed the Witherstone.”

The captain saluted, and Tavian moved on toward the gate.

He found Braun, battered and beaten, but still on his feet. He looked like he’d been run over by a cart.

“Braun?” Tavian called to his brother.

“Tavian? You look worse than death,” Braun exclaimed, sheathing his sword and jogging over to embrace his brother.

“Praise Ishtoran,” Tavian said, smiling. The sun had crested the horizon, and the bright crepuscular rays illuminated his brother. The ghouls would be fleeing the sunlight, now. Without a Witherstone to control them, they’d seek out dark holes and hide away, until they were eventually rousted out and destroyed.

Tavian turned, and faced toward the ocean. The city sloped away from them here, down to the docks in the harbor. Rooftops shone in the morning light.

Tavian’s interface pinged with a notification.

⬣ Perk Upgraded: Map Maker II (passive, togglable) ⬣

⬣ Any hextiles you see are automatically mapped. Any subjects you see have their positions automatically added to the map. ⬣

Tavian checked his map. He activated the population overlay. Little green dots clustered around his location, marking the positions of his men in real time. When they moved out of his sight, the dots slowly faded toward gray, marking outdated information.

Tavian breathed a sigh of relief. He’d desperately missed this ability, since his father’s Core was destroyed. With his growing kingdom, having the ability to see troop movements was going to be huge.

They were going to need this against the Karath Amon.

“System update?” Braun asked.

“Yeah,” Tavian said. “I’m going to give your Class a new Perk, but be warned: I’m always going to know where you are.”

Braun cocked an eyebrow. “If I don’t want it?”

“You can turn it off,” Tavian said. “Don’t stress.”

He added the Map Maker II Perk to Braun’s [Regent III] Class. Now anyone Braun saw would also show up on Tavian’s map. If he gave the perk to all his officers, he’d always have an up to date map of where his forces were positioned.

“You didn’t get the Witherstone?” Braun asked after examining the Perk.

“Destroyed,” Tavian replied. “Along with three Karath Amon Scarabs. Halin the coresmith was in league with Lord Anarvia. Ilara escaped with him. And she took my sword.”

“Lord Anarvia?”

“Bound,” Tavian said. “Unconscious.”

“We should feed him to his ghouls,” Braun snarled.

“We’ll see that justice is done.”
