Avenging Leviathan’s tidal wave saw its last remnants collapsing well shy of the walls. The form of the dragon within let out a howl, a war cry, and shot himself above it all, flying like a bat out of hell for the final fortress ahead. The enemy unleashed volley after volley of magic and ranged fire of all kinds, making any explosions of water and endless torrents of steam… but he kept coming.

“Yes!” Samantha cheered with a fist held up. “Go, Levi, go!”

Just in front of the walls of the central fortress, an ominous aura congealed, and like reality had grown spots, multitudinous voids opened. From out of these, material seemed to explode outward. It was white in contrast, and it grew as fast as Redberry’s trees or brambles, and twisted itself all together, forming an interconnected mesh of tight knots in an expanding wall.

It was bone. Samantha felt something no less ominous about the material than the initial aura. It was no natural bone at all, but rather like the skeleton of an eldritch thing which never belonged in a natural world. Reality screamed at its touch, moved away from it, and was repelled.

That’s definitely not good.

Levi did not stop — in fact, he charged even harder, right for the woven wall of bone. Levi’s war cry became all the louder, an almost feral thing that blanketed the battlefield in a rumbling bass tone.

He hit it like the ocean crashing, like a storm cloud raining itself down all at once, with force enough to feel like another tidal wave had hit the battlefield.

Unfortunately, the wall didn’t give way. Levi splashed into it, water exploding in every direction and landing on the enemy soldiers too near, causing no small amount of collateral casualties.

Oh, shit!

Levi more or less came apart, as water rebounded off of the wall and continued vibrating in the air. Seemingly with wooziness, Levi reformed, significantly diminished and rapidly weakening. He was… almost literally evaporating into thin air.

“I see you’re not without mighty countermeasures, sages!” Levi called as he huffed and retreated from the wall, his surface reforming until he was almost not like water at all, but solid. “Hmmph. So be it. Water flows where the world wills, I am far from my source, and this is no natural hollow.” His gaze swept over the soldiers still trying to attack him, and he formed into an aggressive serpentine curl, his scales and top fur bristling menacingly.

Levi’s form began to steam and become chaotic, more and more, as he floated over a mass of terrified soldiers. Soon, most of his body was absolutely roiling, like boiling water. “A parting gift then, to remember me by!”

The majority of his body exploded into hundreds of different globes of violently boiling water, each collapsing down on a tower. The final towers near the walls of the city had the bone-growth walls materialize and block the globes, but it was obvious very few could be saved this way.

The vast majority were doused in the water, which found its way through every hole, crack, and crevice as the water disappeared, leaving heavily reduced, smoothed-out, shiny walls still steaming upward. Otherwise, those towers were quiet. Samantha didn’t need to investigate to know everyone inside was dead.

Levi’s eyes were the last thing to linger in the sky, as his summoning came to an end. The eyes locked with her third eye as his voice echoed in her mind. “I have done all I can, Endbringer. The rest I leave to you.”

“You did incredibly well, Levi,” Samantha replied. “Thank you. Rest now.”

“I am not so old as to need a nap this soon after action! Aside from that, the new memory state of the world invigorates me. Would that it had been an aeon earlier, but it’s still nice. I will observe the end, in any case. Good luck to you.”

“Thanks again, Levi.”

The army was still surging through the muddy remainders of the water left in Levi’s wake, crossing the enormous gap he’d made in the battlelines. Flyers boldly went up again in full numbers, having little fear of the sorry remnants of towers anywhere near them.

Meanwhile, the unyielding bone structures seemed to be lazily shifting around in the air near the city walls, unhurried.

“I don’t like the looks of that,” Dax said balefully into the global gestalt. “Samantha, is there any additional intelligence? Not getting much from others, but it’s plain to see that it's obscenely strong.”

“It’s something from Below,” Samantha replied. “Has to be. Many attacks will have trouble due to matter not wanting to touch it. There needs to be spiritual, conceptual advantage. Water can’t do the trick, obviously. Things that break bone are better, perhaps. Hard, physical things.”

Samantha made a flight of her Firebirds dive for a bone wall, to breathe fire on it. As expected, absolutely nothing happened. The bone didn’t even scorch.

Dax made a sound of blowing air out. “Great. Your explosion magic might work, at least?”

“I think so. I have a firm conceptual advantage. I already break reality. I’m sure Six Moons Falling will do well against it, too.”

“I can think of nothing I break more efficiently than bone,” Six offered confidently. “Coordinating minotaurs will likely do well, too.”

“I agree.” Samantha recalled their minotaur champions’ coordinated combat problem-solving quite well during the Glae ritual demon battles.

Far before the alliance foot soldiers engaged the enemy again, the front ranks of the Dominion suddenly pitched to their knees and began screaming, dropping their weapons to hold their hands to their heads. Samantha vaguely sensed something out of her specialty: it was psychic.

“Dear heavens,” Ash muttered in the gestalt. “This is horrific…”

“Ash, we need intel, damn it!” Dax commanded. “What is happening?!”

“It’s-”

Before she could say anything more, the vanguard line of the enemy exploded in a mass of gore, and their bones grew out in great expansion, into a horrifying blood-coated mass like a sick mockery of a bramble wall — one that extended around the entire circumference of the city like a circle. The mass was much more than merely their bones could have facilitated, and the height was obscene, perhaps twenty meters high. It shifted and writhed, active and alive, as void-pitted skulls could be seen watching from around and within.

Despite everything she’d seen, Samantha was still stunned once more by the lengths the Dominion would go to.

The voice of the Director chimed in her head once more, like an echo in the distance. ‘Can you even acknowledge how depraved you became? How those you taught even trumped your own depths of degradation? Why would you ever want to do this again?! Tell me. Explain it to me!’

“Their minds were dominated and then sacrificed,” Ash said. “I’ve never seen anything like it…”

Most were silent in their horror at the event, but Dax seemed completely calm. “What are the odds this can be done to our ranks?”

“Zero. This had some sort of imprint in them that was exploited. It… it’s whom they serve that allowed this.”

“Why am I not surprised? Okay. We’ll need to form a concentrated wedge to breach. Hardest hitting right in the bloody front. Samantha, is this wall the same strength as-”

“No,” Samantha answered immediately. “It’s inferior comparatively to the backline walls; I can see that even at a glance. I’m not sure how much that will help us collectively, though. We’re talking about a dilution of potency for an expansion of coverage. I feel like we’re staring into a grinder for our men.”

“Fighting and dying is what our soldiers are here for, Samantha. If they can help, they must, and we have to try.”

Samantha took a deep breath, knowing it was true. Unlike half the damn people the Dominion ‘employed,’ her entire army was composed of volunteers. They’d mitigated casualties almost miraculously so far in the battle. “Fine. But we’re definitely gonna unleash Contempt For the End to bolster the vanguard, and we need to seriously consider activating the crown if things go to shit.”

“Agreed,” Dax said quickly.

“You lot should see the other side right now! Phew.” It was a voice Samantha hadn’t heard in a while: Lucky, one-time underground boss of what was once Caneboro, and now a similar unofficial boss for the underground ‘in general.’

“Wait, you’re here?!” Samantha exclaimed. “You don’t need to be in this-”

“No way!” Lucky interrupted with a laugh. “War gives me indigestion, Beautiful! I’m just watching for entertainment purposes. Broke out the best spirits for this, no less. Keep at it, lovelies!”

Rolling her eyes, Samantha didn’t reply, but did look at the mirrors capturing the other side of the bone wall, as everyone else had fallen silent.

On the other side was a mix of chaos from panicked, running soldiers, and the utter calm of others rising from fallen states, as they stood upright, stock still and empty-faced. Score by score, the men were falling down and screaming, as the mental domination seemed to be hitting them in waves.

Samantha muttered into the gestalt, “So much for the Dominion wanting to preserve some trust in their people for a hypothetical after-victory state.”

“Yes,” Ash said, voice a bit shaky. More calmly, she continued, “But they won’t sacrifice order. The domination appears permanent, minimally degenerative, and their bodies remain alive, like an evolution of the mind-slave punishment for criminals. I’m sure these will make grand keepers of the peace.” Her voice dripped with bitter irony.
