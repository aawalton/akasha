I spent a long time focused on how bitter I was. Even longer feeling sorry for myself. I taught the young, my family, even if the connection was so distant that no one would actually believe it existed. I didn't give up, but I didn't care.

Every one of my old friends, except for one, had moved on. They'd stopped caring, they'd died, they didn't want to be reminded of what once was. I was supposed to vanish, to do the respectable thing and give up.

I didn't. I lingered. I wasn't sure why, I was broken and had nothing.

Now though? I've worked on a Seed. I can see what they're building, what they could become.

Do they?

~ * ~

All Fay wanted was to have a good night's sleep and not wake up worrying. Well no, that wasn't all she wanted, not even close, but it was what she wanted most right this moment. She'd managed to distract herself by worrying about Seb and Anya, she'd kept herself busy planning the surprise celebration dinner, she'd even tried exhausting herself focusing on work for Valene that didn't need be finished for another week or more. None of it helped. None of it gave her answers to the question she had yet to openly acknowledge.

What did she plan to do.

It seemed like such a simple question, such a small thing to answer. A few years ago it was simple. A few years ago she was alone, she was focused on her work and all she cared about was proving she was a pre-eminent researcher, that her dedication to her Path was the right choice.

Then she got sent to a backwater, she made friends, found family. She was accepted, she had people who saw her for who she was, who loved her. And all this before she found actual love, before she realized how much Briana meant to her.

One person at that dinner had known, one person had heard her say she'd hit level 29 and given the words the weight they actually deserved. She couldn't blame the others, half of them didn't know better. Briana knew, at least on some level, but she was in no hurry, she was decades away from the answer. Alex was, she wasn't sure about Alex. He was focused, he was talented, he wasn't someone she could talk to about this.

Yshe knew, Yshe somehow understood. She hadn't looked into the details of Yshe's past. She knew that there was a connection with Valene, that they had known each other years ago, but how long ago, where and why weren't anything she'd pried into. It hadn't mattered at the time, it might matter now, but the middle of the night was a bad time to start digging. Especially since it would mean waking Briana up.

She rolled over in bed, trying, failing, to get comfortable. Trying, and failing, to keep from being a disturbance.

"Fay?" Briana mumbled.

"Sorry, go back to sleep."

"What's wrong?"

No, this wasn't right. She didn't want Briana to have to deal with this, she didn't want to ruin her night too.

"Nothing. Just thinking," she said.

She felt the bed shift, felt Briana roll over onto her elbow to look down at her. There was just enough light to see her concern, to realize that there was no escaping the conversation. She tried to smile, she could tell it looked forced even as she did. She sighed, maybe talking would actually help.

"Level 29 Bri."

"That was nearly a month ago."

"I don't know what I want to do," she tried to explain.

"Sure you do. You want to research, you want to be part of the Holding, you want to roll over so I can-"

"Bri!" she interrupted, momentarily flustered despite everything. "I'm serious. I don't know how I want to go forward."

"Oh."

One word. They were on the same page now, at least mostly. She'd admitted to her concern, to the issue. She had to explain the rest, she couldn't simply leave it there. She had to.

"I can keep pushing, advance as fast as I can from the work I get through Valene and for the Holding. I can keep being Elite, keep the pace. I can stop, I can slow down and just be Fay. I don't have to push, I don't have to prove anything to anyone. I can-"

"You don't have to prove anything, not to me, not to Valene, not to anyone," Briana said gently. "You're wonderful, you're exactly who I want you to be. You don't have to push."

"But what if I miss it? What if I try to slow down and I just… can't? What if it changes me?"

There. She'd said it. She didn't want to be someone else, she didn't want to try something and fail, to not still be Fay, the woman who could do whatever she chose.

"It won't change you. And you don't have to rush the choice. You can take time, you can sleep on it."

"I can't sleep on it," Fay said. "I've tried. I tried distracting myself, tried being busy, tried everything I can think of. It works for the moment, maybe even for some time after, but the thoughts keep coming back."

"What do you want to do?" Briana asked. "Not what do you feel you should do, not what do you think someone else wants you to do. What do you want to do."

"I want to be here, I want to be part of my Holding, part of my family. I want to be with you. But I want to reach the next wall, I want to see where it takes me."

"I don't want to be alone," she added after a moment, her voice failing her.

"Have you talked to them? Told them?"

"When?" she said as she tried not to laugh, not to cry. "At the party? When their awarenesses were blurring together? When everyone was having a good time? They haven't been back since."

"They get back in what, two, three days?"

She nodded. She could talk to them, she wasn't sure what they'd say, if they'd have answers. Part of her wanted them to have an answer, to tell her what to do, part of her hoped they wouldn't, that they'd be as uncertain as she was, and she'd be able to figure it out, reassure them with her ability to find answers.

"Then how about you help me fall back asleep?" Briana said as she settled against her. "You woke me up so it's the least you can do."

Fay smiled and pulled her closer. That. That she could do.

~ * ~

Fay held her breath as she watched them take in her worries. She'd told them everything, mostly. Briana had been perfect. Making sure coffee was ready, making sure everyone would have what they needed then rushing off to work to deal with inventory.

Seb had paused as she hurried off, she'd seen him start putting things together. From the look on his face he'd been wrong about his assumptions, that he'd been sure this was going to be about them? What had they done this time… No, this wasn't the time for that.

"I didn't know," Anya said finally. "No one ever mentioned anything other than the wall."

"You couldn't have known," Fay said. "Why would you?"

"But you said Briana understood, that she knew. She's from Earth too."

"Deep Earth," she clarified. "Passing the wall isn't seen as strange, as exceptional."

"Do you have to decide now?" Seb asked.

"Not deciding is pausing. I either pause or I push. I'm an Elite or I'm just another Seer."

"Fay!" Anya said as she put down her coffee. "You are not just another Seer. We both know you know that."

"Fine. But the rest applies. If I push I'm Elite, I'm still the Seer who made it this far. If I don't push, then who am I?"

"You're still Fay," Seb answered. "You haven't pushed since you leveled up, have you?"

"No..?"

"Are you less yourself?"

"No…"

She frowned. He wasn't wrong. She hadn't pushed. She'd spent a whole month worrying about changing, and hadn't changed. She couldn't make up her mind, but that didn't change who she was. She still looked for details, still looked for connections, still put things together-

She blinked. She hadn't felt her Affinity flare up so obviously in a long time. She had been sure she'd managed to incorporate it, to make it part of who she was, what she did. She found the connections, she put them together as she found the information, she cleaned them up behind herself when they were no longer needed.

"Fay?"

"Are you okay?" Seb asked. "That was-"

"Yes. Yes," she answered. "That was. There's something I'm missing, that we're missing."

Anya glanced between them before understanding filled her face. Seb must have filled her in. That bond between them must make things so convenient, especially with as clear a band as they had when they communicated. To be able to understand, to explain without words, without needing to elaborate, simply share.

"What's the connection," she mumbled. "What are we putting together."

"The Holding?" Anya asked. "The land? Our futures?"

"Future."

There it was, the first thread, the first step along the line.

"Yours? Ours? All of ours?" Seb asked.

"Yours, ours. One, then both."

Their future, that was the starting point, that was where she had to look. She was helping them, they were the connection point. Their future leading into one for all of them? But they were heading to school, she wasn't.

"It's not school," she said. "We won't be together, not there."

She frowned. They were close. School wasn't it, wasn't where the connection led. But school was the path, they would do well at school, she knew they would. How couldn't they.

"But we're going to school," Anya said. "We're going to make the deadline, we're going to get there and learn what we need to."

"And then we get to go to-"

"Tournament!" Fay all but shouted. "The Progression Invitational."

"You told us there were prizes," Anya said hesitantly, clearly trying to make the leap. "That doing well could change everything."

"Maybe not change, you're already doing so much. But yes, the prizes for Combat Path are legendary. The prizes for Non-Combat aren't any less."

"Non-Combat tournaments?" Seb asked.

"Ways for Engineers to show they can design and build. Healers can help the injured. That Seers can discover details, or analyze Awakened potential."

"You'd do amazingly," Anya said.

Fay blushed. Anya might frustrate her at times, might tease her mercilessly, but she was there for her, she cared.

"I'd have to get invited. Before I hit level 30."

"How do you get invited?" Seb asked.

"Non-Combat is trickier. Have to be nominated, have to prove you're already at that level. You need a sponsor."

"Valene?" he said.

"If I were still part of the council. He'd have to justify and explain. I'd have to prove it."

"Is that the only way?" Anya asked.

"Combat Path can bring along support Pathers. Non-Combat automatically qualifies that way, but doing badly reflects poorly on everyone if you get in that way."

She hesitated. There was one criteria she hadn't mentioned. A slight complication. She'd be able to go with them, she'd be able to be part of the tournament with them, they could be together, except.

"I'd have to wait," she said softly.

"Wait?" Anya asked.

"Tournament is only for levels 26 through 29."

She felt Seb look at her, not questioning, not deciding anything. She saw Anya open her mouth then bite her lip, not interrupting the silence. This was her choice, the choice she hadn't known she could make. She could push now, she could decide to stop, or she could wait. They would catch up.

She smiled, chuckling softly at the thought. Anya would catch up, especially if it was a challenge. Seb would be right there beside her.

"You decided," Seb said.

"I'll wait," she agreed. "But if you take your time, if you make me wait even a day longer than I need to…"
