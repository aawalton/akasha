Nerisse of House Siren accepts the hand of her guard as she steps onto the ship. She doesn’t need it, but she’s learned that, for a woman, displays of vulnerability work as well as displays of dominance for a man. If in a different way.

A man waits for them, his posture straight and only slightly deferential. Someone of high rank on the ship. But not the captain or head of the warriors – his neat outfit denotes something different.

“Heir Siren? I am Sarran, Expedition Steward. Lord Markus is waiting for you on the quarterdeck. I will accompany you there.”

Nerisse doesn’t bat an eyelid at the introduction that, while polite, nonetheless makes her position very clear.

“Ser Sarran,” she replies, noticing by the lack of reaction that she is right in guessing that this man is a Classer. “I thank you for your consideration.”

He inclines his head briefly but says nothing more. Leo, standing at Nerisse’s shoulder, tenses at the lack of acknowledgement. She sends him a warning glance. They are the defeated party, at the mercy of the one they so foolishly attacked.

If their strike had worked the way Lord Kyrian had wanted it to, they would have left their opponents floating on the shards of wood that would have been the only remains of their ships. The survivors would have been left only so Lord Kyrian could honestly say he hadn’t murdered another Great House Heir in cold blood, but this far from land, their fate would have been inevitable nonetheless.

That Lord Markus has given them the opportunity to surrender is more than Lord Kyrian would have.

She, and the single guard she was permitted, make their way up to the quarterdeck. Nerisse doesn’t make her observation obvious, but she notes the few signs of battle. This ship wasn’t taken by force of arms, but force of magic. Fire magic, to be precise. Much as she had expected – a massive ball of flames suddenly appearing and falling upon Lord Kyrian’s ship was hard to miss.

Given the strength required to overcome the enchantments she knows were there, Lord Markus must have a Master Fire Mage in his retinue. She hopes, for their sake, that it wasn’t Lord Markus himself who wielded it – she was in the audience when Lord Markus fought that duel with Heir Fell and saw how he wielded earth to gain his victory. An enemy who is a dual elementalist as well as a Tamer would be…concerning.

Rijordan is already on the quarterdeck and she exchanges a brief glance with him. She does not verbally chide him, but by the way he quickly drops his eyes, she suspects that he hears the message loud and clear. He thought that they might stand a chance against Lord Markus’s forces. Thought that a Beast Tamer wouldn’t have any idea of how to sail a fleet against one thronged with Water Mages, even if he held the advantage of numbers.

From what Nerisse saw, he did even worse in the battle than she did.

But she spends little time on someone who is, ultimately, as powerless as she herself. Instead, her eyes skip over the tamed beasts standing tensely around, and land on the two main characters of this drama.

Lord Kyrian appears to be unconscious, his head in his manservant’s lap. Nerisse wonders whether that is of Lord Markus’ making, or his own. It could be either. She saw the tidal wave that Lord Kyrian wielded against his enemy – exceedingly impressive in size and momentum, but ultimately a failure in execution. It would make sense for Kyrian to have collapsed if he cast it without a circle of other Water Mages providing magic. Such a working would be utterly beyond any individual in Nerisse’s retinue; it would take a Grandmaster Water Mage to complete without ill effects.

Lord Markus is crouched over him, his hand on Lord Kyrian’s shoulder, his eyes closed. Kyrian’s manservant, who mothers him and is fanatically loyal to him regardless of how dismissive and frankly cruel he is to the man, is tense but does not attempt to protest. Lord Markus is not overtly attempting to harm him, then.

Nerisse allows herself to flick her eyes over Lord Markus. Even his style of dress is a complete contrast to Lord Kyrian. Instead of Kyrian’s ostentatious robes that scream wealth and power, Lord Markus’ tight-fitting armour is dull and monochromatic red. Yet Nerisse’s sharp eyes are not easily fooled and she can see the quality in its creation. Where Kyrian almost always appears to be dressed for a palace ball, Lord Markus clearly takes a more practical approach.

Lord Markus opens his eyes and then murmurs something to Kyrian’s servant. The man’s face relaxes and a hint of gratitude enters his eyes. Good news of some sort, clearly.

The Great House Heir stands and steps back, crossing his arms over his chest. He does not turn to greet either Nerisse or Rijordan. An intentional or unintentional slight? Nerisse would usually take it as the first, but she has heard of the maladroitness of this heir in the palace. She is not in a position to take offence either way.

He waits. And the rest of them are forced to wait with him. Without recognition of their presence, Nerisse does not dare bring his attention to herself ahead of time. From all appearances, neither does Rijordan.

Nerisse wonders what is holding Lord Markus back from giving his judgement, what is motivating this theatre of bringing them all here. Cruelty? Or a sense of justice? She would give a lot to know – for her future as well as for her present.

Once more, she curses Lord Kyrian for the fool that he was in forcing this issue. And her mother for not being strong enough to tell Lord Torrent ‘no’ when he came searching for companions for his son.

Nerisse pulls herself out of her thoughts – Lord Kyrian shows signs of waking.

He is not obvious about it. He must know instinctively that something is wrong. But a twitch of a finger, a flutter of an eyelid are all easy signs to read when they are staring so intently at him.

His movements still as he comes to full awareness. Nerisse imagines that he is taking in all that his senses offer. Oddly, there is none of the stirring of magic that she is accustomed to in his presence. House Siren is known for their magic sensitivity – it has served them well over the generations.

But here, no humid air brushes at her cheek; no droplet of sweat trickles down her back. Instead, there is a dryness to the sea breeze that is noticeable in its rarity; a heat to the wind that speaks of more than sun.

Nerisse wants to follow it to its source, but dares not spare the focus right now. Not when Lord Kyrian has just opened his eyes.

For a moment, he pays no attention to those surrounding him though he must know they’re there. Instead, he lifts his arms and examines them, the wide sleeves of his robes falling back to reveal dull metal cuffs around his wrists.

Even without the way his lips curl and his eyes flash, Nerisse would know that the almost-bronze cuffs could never be his – they’d be far more ostentatious.

His mouth opens, but before Lord Kyrian can speak, Nerisse notices his servant’s hand tightening on his shoulder. Nerisse has never seen Lord Kyrian take direction from anyone, but for once, he seems to reconsider what he will say.

His eyes move from the cuffs to the figure of Lord Markus. Heir Titanbend stands a few paces away from him, his hands clasped behind his back, posture straight, expression unreadable.

Though Nerisse suspects that she spots satisfaction in his eyes, he does not gloat. Nor does he rush to speak. Eyes narrowed, Nerisse carefully weighs what he appears like. A predator, like one of his Bonded? No. More like a judicator. Which, Nerisse supposes, is exactly what he’s acting as here, though Lord Kyrian has more protection from his judgement than Nerisse and Rijordan do.

She can only hope that he has more mercy than the average city law-enforcer.

“What is this?” Lord Kyrian asks, his voice surprisingly free of bluster. Perhaps some of the arrogance was knocked out of him when he saw how easily his opponent broke through this strongest attack. But anger? Oh, that is there – in buckets. And perhaps Nerisse is not mistaken in seeing a flicker of fear there, though it is well-hidden.

“Consequences,” Lord Markus replies calmly. He doesn’t speak loudly, but his voice carries in the silence around them. “You attacked my people. You endangered my companions. You acted without provocation and after I had already given you a warning. You cannot think that you could sail away freely after all of that.”

His voice has a touch of incredulity at the thought. Nerisse wonders whether he is perhaps being a little too optimistic – Lord Kyrian has spent the whole of his life escaping consequences thanks to his rank and his father. What would give him any reason to think that that might have changed now?

“Cutting off my magic is going beyond any reasonable response, Titanbend!” Lord Kyrian hisses, leaning forward and wrenching himself away from his servant when the man attempts to hold him back. “Remove these! Now!”

His voice rises in unmistakable command – someone used to his word being law for all around him. Nerisse knows that tone all too well.

“No,” Lord Markus replies. He does not raise his voice one jot, and yet somehow it seems stronger and more impactful than Lord Kyrian’s stentorous tones. “I have chosen not to execute you in cold blood. I have also chosen not to take you hostage. Instead, I will give you another opportunity to make better choices. But I will not allow you to keep the magic that would have destroyed my ship and drowned everyone aboard if I had not intervened.”

Lord Kyrian clenches his teeth.

“You are not the King! You have no right to cast judgement on me!”

Nerisse wonders if this is a hint of political awareness surfacing at last – too late.

“No, I am not,” Lord Markus agrees, side-stepping the clumsy trap Lord Kyrian laid for him. Well. It’s not as if Lord Kyrian has any practice with matters of subtlety. “Therefore, regretfully, we will not meet today in a second battle – one that would end with you being Bound to my will and forced to make up for your attempt on our lives through service.” His tone is very pointed.

Finally, Nerisse sees Lord Kyrian falter a touch, his expression of arrogant certainty dropping briefly from his face. Perhaps he felt the same shiver down his spine at the scenario Lord Markus presented that Nerisse did.

She is abruptly glad that Lord Markus appears to be holding to Moriaxar law even if they are already days away from its shores. If not, then perhaps she would have to fear the same fate. Many sins can be covered by the ocean; even more when separated by one.

“Nonetheless, I am not giving you a choice,” Lord Markus continues, breaking the lingering silence. “As recompense for your unprovoked attack, I am taking your ship, all resources aboard it, and your ability to use magic.”

He takes a deep breath and his eyes begin to flash. Nerisse feels electricity gathering in the air and can’t help but shiver. The dryness is still there, but it has become bitingly cold, like the air inland on a frosty morning.

“Kyrian of House Torrent. You have attacked me and attempted to kill me. You have attacked my companions and attempted to kill them. You have attacked those sworn to me and attempted to kill them. For this, I sentence you to wearing those cuffs. I curse you to be unable to remove them yourself. I curse you to be unable to ask others to help you remove them. I curse you to be unable to allow others to attempt to remove them. Yet, if you can repent of your folly and come to me to offer a sincere apology and intention to change your ways before five years and a day have passed, they will fall off your wrists at my touch and you will be free.”

Nerisse’s eyebrows rise without her permission as she hears the formulaic invocation of a Curse. She was not expecting that, though, in retrospect, it seems logical.

The magic gathers and intensifies to the point where Lord Kyrian, the target of the magic, begins to shiver. Nerisse, sensitive to the magical movements of a Curse, feels a moment when it wavers, and wonders whether it will fail like so many others. Lack of knowledge or foundation are the usual culprits, and as far as she knows, Titanbend has never dug deeply into Curses. Not like her own family, whose vocal magic lends itself well to such mediums.

Yet then she feels it steady, as if an iron will had clamped around it and forced it into alignment with his desires. At that moment, Nerisse knows that this one will succeed.

And it makes sense. The most powerful Curses have a strong, negative emotion at their base – resentment, anger, grief, desire for vengeance. Given the situation, that is clearly easily supplied.

Then, though the caster does not need to be a powerful mage – or even a mage at all – their will must be strong enough to impress their intentions over their target or targets. From a family well-known for focussing on their Willpower attribute, Lord Markus must have that.

And finally, they must know how to word the Curse. In this, Lord Markus has been well-tutored. He evoked the base emotion of the Curse: protective fury and desire for vengeance, the reason for the frigid feel of the magic. He laid out the consequences of the curse – and made sure to remain within the bounds of what the target can control, allowing it to exist independently. He repeated both of these elements three times each. And then, crucially, and where most Curses fail, he offered a way to break the Curse.

Many think that creating a Curse that can never be broken will make it stronger, equating it to a ward with no exit. Yet, in doing so, they prevent it from working at all. Even the wording was perfect – five years and a day.

Nerisse is not at all surprised when she feels the magic wrap around Lord Kyrian and then slide down to concentrate itself in his cuffs. It locks into place, Lord Kyrian yelping and clawing at the bands as they probably turn colder even than ice. The effect is temporary – soon, Nerisse knows, they will return to their usual temperature. But those bands will not be coming off. Not unless someone pours in enough magic to drown it out, or has a will strong enough to subdue the living will of the Curse – which will increase the more it is fed with its base emotions. Given Lord Kyrian’s usual lack of emotional control, outrage, desire for vengeance, and anger are likely to be in large supply.

Nerise should be dismayed – Heir Torrent’s magic was a large part of why their three ships faced the ocean without fear. Yet she can’t help but feel a curl of satisfaction. Well played, Lord Markus, she thinks with a touch of admiration.

“What have you done to me?” Lord Kyrian demands, his voice full of fear and understanding he does not wish to accept.

His hands twitch towards and then away from the cuffs – Nerisse suspects that now the Curse has settled, he can’t even claw at them physically.

“Undo this! Now!” His tone is the last, desperate grasps of a drowning man. He knows in his heart that it will be useless, but he has to try anyway.

Lord Markus ignores him entirely.

“As I said, I am taking your ship and everything on it. Your crew have been allowed to swim to your allies’ ships. I will now speak to your allies to determine whether you’ll be allowed to keep anything more than the emergency rowboats.”

With that, he turns away from Lord Kyrian, dismissing him without even a nod like he’s a servant.

But Nerisse cares little of that. Not when those sharp cerulean eyes have come to rest on her. She swallows, unable to keep from flinching.

My turn.
