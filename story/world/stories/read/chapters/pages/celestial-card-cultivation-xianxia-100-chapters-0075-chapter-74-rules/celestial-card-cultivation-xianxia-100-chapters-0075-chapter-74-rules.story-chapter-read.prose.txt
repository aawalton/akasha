They had left the sect when the winter sun was already low on the horizon, and it didn’t take long for darkness to creep out of the forest that lined the road.

“Can you tell me where we are going?”

“Of course, we are going into the wilderness,” Minghao chuckled.

To Jianyi’s relief, he continued quickly after. “We don’t have time to go very far; we just have a month after all.

“We will follow the road for one day, and then we will head east into the forest. This close to the city and the sect, the dangers should be very manageable. And let me be clear, it is up to you to handle all dangers—I am just a housekeeper, after all.”

Jianyi snorted. He didn’t know specifically when Minghao had abandoned life as an active cultivator, but he certainly wasn’t a ‘just’ anything.

“We will make a small base camp, which will be one of your first lessons. From then on, I will show you a few things in the mornings, and the rest of the day is up to you—that will leave you with plenty of time to hunt for your food, find the ingredients for your body cultivation, train, and cultivate.”

That there was a clear plan, even if a lot of it depended on him, made his shoulders loosen up quite a bit. He was not so confident that he could actually progress his body cultivation under these circumstances, but he would know better once he had a chance to read the manual, hopefully later that night.

They stopped at a fortified rest site not too long after, which Minghao commented on as Jianyi’s final comfort of civilization for quite a while. It was easy to find a bit of dry wood for their campfire, which Minghao showed Jianyi how to light without anything but a bit of physical energy.

Once the fire was lit, Minghao pulled out two wooden bowls with stew and handed one to Jianyi, who, in turn, handed one of his two steamed buns to the retainer with a grin and started eating the meal that was indeed as hot as if it had just been pulled from the stove.

There was nothing Jianyi would have liked to do more than start the integration process for the Cinnamon Tree Sprout card, but given that he didn’t know how his Celestial Core Card would react, he decided to wait until they were a bit further into the wilderness.

Instead, he held the jade slip with the Soul-Forged-Body Path to his temple and infused a tiny bit of qi.

The technique unraveled into his head, and he started to process the manual.

Body cultivation differentiated five realms, just like spirit cultivation: Body Tempering, Body Refining, Body Forging, Body Transformation, and, lastly, Body Perfection, which was often also called Jade Body.

There were five stages within each realm, aligned with the strengthening of different body parts: bones, organs, blood, muscles, skin, and different techniques differed in the order in which they approached them.

The alignment of the different stages with the elements was clear, though: bones required Earth affinity, organs Wood, blood Water, muscles Fire, and skin Metal.

Jianyi’s first order of business would therefore be to procure—make?—a potion that primarily consisted of an Earth essence and some components from an Earth-aligned spirit beast. Apparently, there was some flexibility on the specifics, but something like the Stone Boar Jianyi and his friends had encountered on their gathering mission would be perfect. Assuming he could defeat such a strong creature.

Once he had the potion, he would need to press the qi into his bones and follow a simple circulation method—he was confident in succeeding once he had the potion, but getting there seemed unrealistic.

“Minghao?”

“Yes?” The cultivator seemed lost in thought, staring into the fire.

“To create essences, I need to be able to control three threads of qi—I am getting there, but I am not confident that I can do it in a month…” he voiced his biggest concern.

“One step at a time, Jianyi. Don’t worry so much. Once you have an Earth-affinity card, we will see what we can do.”

It was not easy not to worry, but again, Jianyi decided to trust his teachers. If they believed it could be done, he would do it.

“Should I take the first watch, Minghao?”

“When you're out alone, or even with a small team, in the wilderness, it is critical to assess whether you need a watch or not. In the lower cultivation realms, sleep still matters as tiredness kills as surely as a rampaging spirit beast. Ideally, you have a watch; the second-best option is to have formations that protect or at least warn you—and sometimes, you just have to take a risk and hope for the best.”

That all made sense to Jianyi. “How much do formations like that cost? They sound very useful.”

“They are useful. Now, regarding the cost… I couldn’t say how many contribution points they cost in the Treasure Hall, however,” he paused to rub his hand with a greedy smile. “I might be willing to part with a set of basic formation flags, well-suited for a young cultivator, for the low, low price of one common card plus a few nice spirit beast parts.”

Jianyi snorted at Minghao’s performance. He suspected that such a formation would be quite a bit more expensive in the sect—and slowly it dawned on him how this month would go. He wondered how many beasts he realistically could slay on his own—typically they were stronger than a single cultivator in the same realm.

But he started to understand that if he could do it, Elder Sun and Minghao would reward him greatly. Something in his chest, something that had been sitting there since Elder Sun had basically kicked him out into the wild, loosened a bit.

“Also, if you didn’t know, this rest place has an integrated formation—so for tonight we should be safe,” Minghao said with a wink.

***

It was already late afternoon the next day when Minghao pointed towards the dense birch forest on the east side of the road.

“We will move deeper into the forest here. While we do that, I want you to ponder on the concept of presence. I am not talking about anything cultivation-related. It is a concept that my father used to explain to me how one becomes a successful hunter. It is all about awareness of presence. The presence of others, and your own. Can you sense other beings? Can they sense you? I also want you to note that I said sense, not see. You can sense someone's presence with your ears, with your nose… with all your senses. Do you understand?”

Managing his presence had never been relevant to Jianyi’s life so far. He was sure that there were parts of the city where you needed to be at least as careful about your presence as out in the wild, but fortunately, that hadn’t been the case in their neighborhood.

But of course, it made sense. “Just like in the story of Ye Qian—the Silent Fox?” he asked, just to catch himself and blush. He wasn’t sure what had made him bring up a children’s story now.

Minghao laughed out loud. “Exactly, like the Silent Fox. She tracks her targets and is a master of stealth. If it helps you to think in those terms instead of presence, that is also perfectly fine," the man said, still chuckling.

Jianyi tried to move on from that embarrassing episode. “Do I have to look out for tracks of spirit beasts?”

“Yes and no. First of all, this is not just about spirit beasts; it applies to mortal beasts and humans equally. Second, it is not just about tracks in the dirt; you need a much more holistic awareness—remember to sense, not just see. It is about broken stalks, scratch marks in bark, and of course scents and sounds. Sometimes, it is also about the absence of those. If you hear no birds singing, your neck hair should be raised. Lastly, spirit beasts specifically can also be tracked with your soul senses, but that is something for much later.”

Jianyi nodded slowly. There were a lot of things to pay attention to. He was sure that he wouldn’t get it right from the start, but it seemed like something that could be achieved with hard work. That made it immediately attractive to him—especially if it meant that he would find food.

“I assume then that managing my own presence is essentially the opposite of that. Meaning, making sure that one does not leave broken stalks behind and taking baths so that one does not leave a stench.”

“Cleanliness is a virtue, but for this specific topic, the answer might also be the opposite. Instead of trying not to smell, it might be easier and more efficient to try to smell exactly like your environment.”

That sounded unpleasant but logical.

With all senses sharpened, he took the first step into the forest—and had to twitch when a small dry branch broke under his weight. It sounded as if a thunderclap had come down upon them.

Minghao chuckled behind him. “I couldn’t have made this up. Excellent teaching moment, Jianyi, thank you.”

He felt heat rising up his neck, but didn’t let this discourage him at all. He was here to learn, so learn he would.

***

They had spent many hours walking through the forest until, already deep into the night, Minghao had found a place that he thought could serve as a base camp.

Kindly, he had explained in detail why he had chosen the specific location—protection from wind, access to a water source, multiple smaller predators present, making it less likely that a major threat was present, and others.

After a quick meal, which had consisted of a few berries that Jianyi had collected along the way for him and a delicious-smelling dish for Minghao, the retainer had withdrawn into his tent and informed Jianyi that he was on his own until the next morning.

The first question he had to ponder was one of a watch. In the absence of a formation—he had already committed that he would use the first card he could harvest to buy one of those from Minghao—his choice was to stay awake or risk it and go to sleep. A full day of hiking had left him slightly tired, but it was bearable. The motes of qi coursing through his body, as few as there were on the second step of the Qi Gathering Realm, strengthened him. Slightly, but noticeably. He could deal with a night without sleep.

He would see what the next night brought—either a formation, or at least a better understanding of the environment and its risks.

After having decided on that, he turned to the Eternal Spear Meditation. Very mindful of the need for consistency in this cultivation art, he had already spent a few minutes in the morning, but now was the perfect time to replenish his reserves and make sure his qi stayed smooth and flexible.

He was used to the pain by now and built only a slight sheen of sweat while performing the kata of movements multiple times.

That out of the way, he settled down to make sure he was mentally and physically calm, then he produced the Cinnamon Tree Sprout card out of his storage ring. It was finally time to absorb the card and see how the Celestial Core Card would react.
