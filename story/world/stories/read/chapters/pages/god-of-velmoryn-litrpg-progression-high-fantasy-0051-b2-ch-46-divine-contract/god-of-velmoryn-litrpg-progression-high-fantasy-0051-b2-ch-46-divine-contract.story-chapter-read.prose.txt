“Your Radiance…” she muttered as she raised her head. Her eyes settled on the God of Velmoryn. “Please… please help me against the God of Justice…”

She might have looked vulnerable, but I wasn’t convinced. She had no idea who I truly was, or whether I could even be trusted. How was it reasonable for her to beg me for help so easily?

I might have overlooked her testing my divinity. I might have told myself she was simply that desperate. But help against the God of Justice? That was the authority the Night God held in the Goddess’s final memory.

Was I really supposed to believe this was all nothing but coincidence? That Athena sought help against the very god I was destined to confront within months?

“Who is the God of Justice?” I asked, partly to confirm and partly to break the silence that had stretched a little too long.

“One of the nine human gods,” she replied.

“Nine? Not ten?” I asked instinctively.

“Oh… so the rumors about the God of Elves distancing himself from the human world were true,” I heard Athena’s voice.

Though her lips did not move.

Could she perhaps communicate without speaking? Or were those her thoughts? Until now, I hadn’t been able to perceive her mind.

The next moment, more of her thoughts reached me.

“Damn it… this dominion is too powerful… I need to guard my thoughts… shit, my shield already collapsed… wait, can he hear me? Oh no… happy thoughts… I must think about anything but… NO, SHIT… crickets, butterflies, velvet, fire…”

What the fuck?

Hearing her thoughts was confusing enough. But when they stopped making any sense, it became something else entirely. Still, my confusion didn’t last long.

A blue window appeared before my eyes.

[God’s Kingdom]

Within your Kingdom, your power is absolute - even against deities who possess more divinity. Only gods whose Radiance surpasses yours in purity may withstand the pressure of your dominion’s aura. All others must expend significant divinity to resist… or face the consequences.

The Goddess of Motherhood and Fertility failed to shield herself with sufficient divinity. Her defenses have collapsed.

She is currently in a vulnerable state. Her thoughts may be read. A divine contract that does not impose excessive restriction may be forced upon her, or she may be attacked.

After reading the window, all kinds of thoughts and plans flashed through my mind. Attacking her would not only be risky, but wasteful. It wouldn’t benefit me at all. It would only earn me another enemy, and that was the last thing I needed.

But forcing a contract? Now that was tempting.

Perhaps Athena felt that the silence was working against her, or perhaps she simply wanted to distract herself so I wouldn’t hear more of her unfiltered thoughts. Either way, she began speaking again.

“There were indeed ten deities humans worshipped. Some stronger, some weaker.” She spoke far too quickly. “But one of us was deceived into a contract that was unfair toward her and ultimately cost her her Authority. And a god without Authority… well, I’m sure a great deity such as Yourself understands what that means.”

Well, I didn’t. But I could guess. She likely withered and perished. Some new god would then destroy her last statue and absorb what remained of her power…

“The one who obtained her Authority was the God of Justice,” Athena continued. “I also have a contract with him. In fact, only two human gods do not. One is Bahenna, the Goddess of Fire and Rebirth. She is far too powerful and ancient to involve herself in mortal affairs unless someone kills one of her apostles or… I apologize, I got carried away…”

“Why is his divinity so oppressive?!” Her thoughts reached me again. “I’ve been in dozens of other gods’ dominions, but this is the first time I feel like this. Even Noah’s divinity was nowhere close…”

Noah… yes. It’s that bastard.

The moment Athena mentioned the name, it confirmed everything. There was no way two human gods named Noah, with the exact same Authority, could exist. And the unjust contracts matched what I already knew about him. Hadn’t he obtained Lilith’s Authority - God of Moons - in the same way?

“Continue,” I said, my voice perhaps a little too confident. With her in such a vulnerable state, I felt strangely powerful.

“Ah? Right,” Athena stammered. “The second deity not bound by contract to Noah is the God of Merchants. But they are protected by the Pantheon itself and have sworn to remain neutral. I cannot seek their help.”

“Why me? Why seek me instead of any other deity?”

“Because…” Athena whispered and paused. She kept staring at the God of Velmoryn on the throne, hesitating. “I know Your Lordship has partially lost Authority over the elves and… the God of Justice is trying to obtain it.”

She knows? How could she know when even the God of Merchants doesn’t seem to?

Her words explained her unnatural trust in me and why she had risked provoking me despite believing I was far more powerful than she was. From her perspective, I must have appeared as someone immensely strong, yet weakened - and in desperate need of capable allies.

“The contract I have with Noah… cursed be the day I agreed to it…” she hissed. Her face twisted with anger. “Unless I help him obtain Authority over the elves before the end of this year, he will steal mine!”

“Please trust me… please trust me… please trust me…” she kept repeating in her mind.

If she believes I possess what the Night God needs to complete his Authority, then she could be working for him.

Accepting Athena as an ally would be a double-edged sword. I would never be able to fully trust someone whose problems could be solved by betraying me. But her services could also prove extremely useful - be it information about Noah’s possible weaknesses or maybe even his plans.

I made up my mind.

“I will consider your request. But first, you must prove your worth. If you believe the God of Justice is our shared enemy, then by providing something that could aid me against him, you would be serving your own goal as well.”

I paused, waiting for her reaction.

“If Noah finds out… can I really trust him? Wouldn’t it be easier to tell Noah some of the information I’ve learned and try to free myself…”

That was exactly what I had been looking for. If she hadn’t even considered betraying me, I would have assumed she was acting. At the very least, she would have been too stupid for me to even consider working with her.

Now she sounded real. Desperate.

But that didn’t mean I was going to form an alliance with her. Nor did it mean I had any intention of trusting her and letting her leave with whatever she had learned about me.

My realm trembled as I stirred all the divinity I possessed. I didn’t know how much power would be required to subdue a god, and I had no desire to take risks.

“Your Lordship?” Athena mumbled as the wave of crimson energy surged toward her, drowning her entire figure.

“I must leave,” she thought and tried to channel her power - but that was the very first thing I had secured.

The faintest layer of pink energy that briefly covered her body was devoured by violent red almost instantly.

“Had you not considered betraying me, I would have let you leave,” I lied. Even though I wasn’t doing anything unjust, I felt the need for justification. Just in case the Pantheon could intervene in matters such as this.

“What are you planning to do?” Athena pleaded, voice trembling.

“Be at ease. I have no intention of harming you. Not unless I come to see you as my enemy.”

The time for words was over. I needed to concentrate fully on what I was about to do.

[Warning: You are attempting to subdue a god!][Warning: Divine Contract is being formed!][Your divinity has suppressed the Goddess of Motherhood and Fertility!]

Even without the blue windows, I already knew. I could physically feel it. This was a new sensation - one of complete control and overwhelming power. I felt her futile attempts to resist. I felt her shields collapse. And then…

Her consciousness.

I felt what a god’s true body was. Not the exterior shell that served as a vessel, but the core. The true Athena.

“I will only impose conditions I deem just, for I do not harm unless deserved.”

I had no idea where those words came from, yet I still spoke them. They rose from instinct.

[Divine Contract]

A divine contract has been successfully imposed upon Athena, Goddess of Motherhood and Fertility.

Due to its structure, the required cost has been reduced. 742 Divinity Points consumed.

If either party violates the contract, their Authority will be transferred to the other.

Note: Because this contract does not compel a deity into action and serves primarily as a protective measure, the Pantheon will not be alerted.

“You may leave now,” I said, watching Athena’s trembling figure return to its natural color as my divinity withdrew.

“The God of Velmoryn?!” she thought, taking a closer look at my manifestation on the throne. “Of course… the dark elves, the violent demonic divinity, and the ruthless angel… I signed a contract with a devil… no, another devil…”

Despite those thoughts, her spoken words carried an entirely different tone.

“Thank you, Your Lordship,” she said with a slight bow. “I am grateful that the contract is of a protective nature.”

I made the God of Velmoryn nod and waited for her to leave. Even if she believed I had acted unjustly, I didn’t care. I genuinely believed I had been kinder than most deities would have been in my position. I could have enslaved her if I had wanted to. But I didn’t. I had only ensured she would not feed the Night God information about me.

“I…” she muttered. “Please take this. If you decide to join forces with me, summon me into your kingdom.”

Her figure blurred and vanished before I could properly examine the swan-shaped coin lying on the floor where she had stood only moments ago.
