Chapter V.XIX (5.19) - Genie

Power swirled around Kizu, so thick that it physically spun the sand at his feet around him. He stood stationary amongst the surge of magic while he clutched his spear and did his best to imitate Lahel’s stoic nature. His partner stood to his side, utterly unimpressed by the powerful magical display. Perhaps that was a result of her personality or maybe the attitude of indifference came from her experience working with the magical artifacts allotted to humankind. Regardless, she remained cool as they addressed the being of pure magic that existed all around them.

“For what reason have you descended into my lair?” a booming voice said all around them. “A suicidal attempt to take what is mine? Or do you wish to appeal to me? Fall to your knees. Speak your oath of servitude and perhaps I will forgive this slight.”

“Neither,” Lahel said. “We’re not here in an attempt at suicide nor seeking to become servants chained to a mythical being. We’re here to bargain. If you decline our offer, we will leave you in peace to resume your rest.”

“A bargain? Hah. You mortals have nothing of value. Even your souls are little more than a drop of energy. The only resource you hold in abundance is a stiff sense of self-importance. And arrogance if you believe you can speak of your departure. The moment you stepped foot in my home, you forfeited your future to me.”

The entirety of the cavern they’d entered glowed an ominous white, as if the unseen being that existed in here threatened to blow apart their very souls in a blast of radiant energy.

Lahel smiled, though Kizu knew her well enough to know the expression held no humor. Only a mad fury. “We have a gift from the stars. Kill us and sentence yourself to your continuous existence hounded by Sekai’s children. Resume hiding away in your hole, trapped in solitude.”

The walls dimmed and the power relented. There were a few moments of silence before the power surged again, this time condensed in the space in front of the humans. When it subsided, a man stood in its place.

Magnificent eyebrows, a pointed beard, and perfect skin. Kizu knew enough about Genie to know it was nothing more than a guise. The gown of purple with golden stars it wore was as much part of its essence as its skin and hair.

“You recovered a gift from the stars?” the Genie asked. It extended a hand. “Show it to me.”

“Zayne,” Lahel said. She didn’t take her eyes from the Genie as she spoke his name.

Kizu removed a golden oil lamp from within his inner vest pocket. The weight of the metal object had been awkward as they’d traveled across the dunes to this hidden sanctuary, but he’d decided that the minor discomfort assured him of its existence at all times.

“Gorgeous.” The Genie reached out and stroked the metalwork with a single finger, from handle to spout. His hand trembled as he pulled it back. “You offer this to me? Why? What boon do you ask in return?”

“We want power. Magic for our species.”

The Genie chuckled but his eyes remained affixed to the golden lamp. “How many lamps like this do you possess?”

“Over a hundred. More than enough for each of your siblings. No Genie will be without one.”

The Genie’s attention never left the golden lamp. “You ask for much. And you lack the capacity to command the power you wield here. This is near useless in your hands. What makes you believe this is a worthwhile bargain?”

“But you do know how to use it?” Kizu asked, he stood steadfast but his heart rate rapidly increased. If the Genie understood….

“Of course.” Finally, the being of power tore his eyes from the lamp to glare at Kizu. “You dare imply incompetence of me?”

“Peace, great one,” Lahel said, placatingly. “My companion is simply ignorant of the extent of your greatness. It is as you say, we lack the capacity to control the power that rests in his hands. It is a strength beyond this world and not a creation developed from our Titans’ might. Far beyond what our feeble minds can comprehend.”

The Genie considered the lamp. “You humans already have some control over magic. I can sense the ripples emitted from your jewelry."

Lahel’s smile slipped for a moment as she shifted her footing. But she recovered quickly. “Those are artifacts. Not true magic. A borrowed power that doesn’t belong to humankind.”

“Innate magic.” The Genie crossed his arms and considered them both with a frown. “A great demand to change the entirety of a species. I will need to consult with my brethren and sisters.”

“We will return,” Lahel promised.

“Give me the lamp, so I can better convince the naysayers.”

“No.”

“You dare deny me?” Power rippled off the Genie, threatening to crush both Zayne and Lahel.

Lahel folded under the extreme pressure, her legs buckling and body collapsing to the stone floor. However, Kizu remained on his feet. His knuckles turned white as he gripped his spear for support and his frame quivered, but he kept his footing and met the Genie’s gaze. The lamp remained clutched in his other hand.

“This is one lamp,” Kizu said, definitely keeping his head raised while he spoke. “We offer you a hundred. The rest remain hidden away in a place you’ll never find without our aid. Kill us and your greed forfeits the spoils.”

The Genie glided forward, his legs not even moving as he repositioned. Kizu fought the innate urge to step back. Every survival instinct inside him begged him to flee. Only years of combat experience kept him from fleeing in the presence of this powerful being of unfathomable power.

The Genie stood with only a centimeter of space between them. A rainbow of color swirled in the being’s eyes. Colors Kizu had never even fathomed before this moment. The gaze pressed its will down on him.

And yet, Kizu refused to buckle. He met the gaze with a glare of his own. His feet held steady. His grip on his spear tightened.

“Very well,” the Genie said, its unblinking eyes never wavering. “On the night of the autumn equinox, where the canyons converge, we will meet again. Consider this deal complete and the contract signed in blood and magic.”

With a sharp spike of pain, Kizu’s cheek stung as blood dribbled down from the new wound. The blood evaporated into a whirlwind of power.

A blink of the eye, and Kizu stood out on a desert dune, overlooking the city. The pressure disappeared. He let out a long breath and a shaky smile formed.

“We actually did it. This will change the world.” He turned to embrace Lahel.

But no one was there. He whirled around and spun in circles while he searched the dunes for any sign of her. Nothing at all.

Then he looked down at his hands. One held his spear and the other the oil lamp.

“Collateral,” he whispered. The word faded into the empty desert as understanding took root in him. The Genie wanted assurance that Kizu would return and hand over the promised power.

And this action guaranteed he would get what he wanted. Kizu would certainly return with Lahel’s life in the balance.

Heart pounding in his ears, Kizu’s eyes slipped from the lamp and settled on his spear.
