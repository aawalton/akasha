Minor Divinity Evolution?

What was this? My levels weren’t high enough to rank up. I needed to grind out more than a few of them for both my class and my profession. Yet, the last time the word divinity had come up was when I ate the Rank 3 Rock Breaker in the dungeon. Now, here it was again.

A disconnect between my body and my mind formed.

I tried to blink, but nothing happened.

Grizzle’s breathing slowed down, then paused. The cold vapors hovering over the water in the distance seemed to freeze.

[Do you wish to proceed to Minor Divinity Evolution?]

I didn’t know. What did that mean?

This wasn’t the first time that I didn’t know what most normal people in the various worlds seemed to know, especially about the next steps with the system. In this case, I thought I’d had plenty of time to learn, except now I suddenly didn’t.

A deeper darkness crept along the edges of my vision. One I’d seen before, high in the skies. One Noseen didn’t want to talk too much about it.

[Starting your Minor Divinity Evolution is a one way path. Either you succeed, or fail. You lose your citizenship and the protection it provides once you evolve, and start down the path of Divinity.]

Concern rose in my mind as I noticed these messages were different. While visually they looked the same, mentally it felt like a softer voice, a different person speaking. Someone personable, who cared, not the impersonal system.

It threw me before I reread what the notification said, then my concern vanished. I needed to know this. Losing my citizenship meant all the rules surrounding it, all the rules protecting me right now, were out the window. What did that mean, exactly?

[You will lose the following: Protection from higher level beings, ease of reproduction, and bonus experience for quests, crafting, and battles.]

[You will retain your stats for being a citizen, access to the quest board, portal access, Megacity access, and language translation. Divine beings who injure or kill citizens who do not attack them first, otherwise give them reasonable cause, or qualify as suitable prey, will be penalized.]

Noseen had eaten creatures that were substantially lower leveled than them. Then again, from what I’d personally seen, none of them had been citizens. And, for all I knew, the others had qualified as giving reasonable cause or suitable prey, I guessed.

The rest of this really created a different tier for divine beings, though.

What would being divine mean?

[Divinity is the next step on the path to the stars. Not everyone wants to travel that path, and citizenship provides protection to those who do not wish to, or cannot, succeed on that path.]

For the first time, the System made it clear that I could fail. That others, likely, had failed.

That spooked me a little.

[Failure is possible with evolution. If you fail, you will remain a citizen, and walk a different path. Opportunities are available for those who fail, even opportunities to earn additional chances, though that is difficult.]

Failure was not an option, then. Not for me. Especially not with the mana coursing through my body at this moment, though I couldn’t feel it. I only had one question left.

Is this ranking up?

[Yes, this will be what you would think of as Rank 3. Make a choice, little one.]

Little one? This wasn’t the system explaining this to me. The darkness at the edges of my vision flickered, then vanished.

[Do you wish to proceed to Minor Divinity Evolution?]

The notification popped back up, just like nothing else had happened, which highlighted how strange it had been that someone had just answered a bunch of questions for me.

Mentally, I took a deep breath.

Yes, I do.

The darkness flashed a bright white, and I found myself in a familiar room. Sterile, all-white walls with a single empty table were all I saw. I sat in the single chair, completely comfortable.

Back to class selection, or at least the same virtual space where it took place. Remembering, I’d been here during my Rank-up to Rank 2, too.

I slowly let out a breath as a soft voice started speaking.

[Welcome to your Minor Divinity Evolution Trial.]

[Before we can begin, you must walk the same path that most of the Firsts before you have tread.]

I didn’t like the sound of that. The initial title had come from reaching level 50 and ranking up before any other humans. Others had spoken of being First, like it meant something, but Noseen hadn’t given me much information on it. Now, here it was again.

[You are the First of your kind to reach for the stars, and those following in your footsteps need a guide. You will fill that role, along with helping to define the path that your people will walk.]

I was not the person for this. Not at all. My fingers tapped on the table but didn’t make a sound in the space.

A humanoid figure formed across the table from me, sitting in another chair.

“Is this easier for you?” asked the being. Its voice sounded the same as the notifications that popped up in this room.

“This works, but I don’t think I’m the right person for this,” I added.

“You are who is sitting here. For good or bad, you will be this person.”

I swallowed and decided to go with the flow. From the very beginning, when I decided the golden glittery box was the way to go, I’d just pushed forward. I needed to stick with that type of energy.

“Then, let’s do this,” I said carefully, trying to force my shoulders to relax.

They nodded, and somehow seemed more human.

“Should humanity be judged on its past, or on its future?”

The question smacked into my chest like the mana well had. Immense pressure pushed at me from all sides. Images fluttered through my mind, first of the fight with Grent, then of other dungeons, before sitting near the lake in the Sanctuary with the monument for those that didn’t survive the shuttle crash.

Other moments probed at the back of my mind, including the fear during the crash, and then the whiff of the sweat from the physical training I’d endured, barely, to even get onto the ship.

Before anything else could pop inside my head, I spoke. “We’ve made mistakes in the past, but each of us had reasons to get onto that colony ship. A reason to reach for the stars, and for the potential future it might offer us. We’ve struggled to move beyond where we were born.”

The humanoid nodded, and the impression of ears formed on either side of their head, along with hints of hair.

“You want humanity to struggle,” it said.

I held up a hand, interrupting them. “No. Humanity should be proactive, growth oriented, and ambitious. It doesn’t matter what they strive for, however. They should be judged on what they actually do.”

“A battle ready species then, focused on being the strongest.”

“Or making the best booze,” I said, suddenly thinking of Doc. Abby popped into my head, standing next to the cooking pot. “Or focusing on the growth of the community. We aren’t just a fighting group of people. We are multi-dimensional.”

“Multi-dimensional? Describe what you mean.” The figure shifted in their seat, almost like they were uncomfortable, as hints of clothing formed.

The change to the being bothered me, but I couldn’t put a finger on why.

“Humanity strives. Some strive to fight, but not everyone. We also appreciate those who want to heal, or make new things like my brother, and Hammy.”

“Those who create weapons,” they said, interrupting me this time, pivoting the conversation back to fighting. “Guns, mech armor, that sort of thing.”

“Not just weapons though. Houses and clothing, art and growing things, like Benny. We build more than war machines. Look at Lakeside. We built a community of beings. Not just humans, but beasts, and dinosaurs with access to safe spaces to grow up.” While I focused on fighting and getting stronger, not everyone did.

Then they brought that up. I closed my eyes as they spoke.

“Yet, you yourself focus on getting stronger. Grinding in the next dungeon, hitting the next level. Stretching to levels beyond what you should be limited to.” Again, their form shifted, and this time hints of a nose and eye sockets appeared.

“That is me, but that isn’t every human.” I let out a sigh and opened my eyes. “We have those who heal, like Sang, and those who hunt, like Hawk. Both can exist at the same time, because we are multi-dimensional beings. We are adaptable, we are not just one thing.” Kabi’s people came to mind. How those who fought were prized, and honor was more important than most anything else.

We weren’t like that, and I didn’t want that to change.

It shouldn’t change.

We liked the freedom to be what we wanted. Freedom. That was key.

“We need to be able to change if we really want to, and if we put in the work. Like learning runes, or how something works. We are curious and love to learn,” I said, trying to get my point across.

“Ambitious, growth oriented, creative, and curious,” they said.

“Yes.” Hopefully, that answered the question.

“Humans heavily rely on technology.”

Images of the shuttle, rifles, and Hammy’s mech suit came to mind, along with the prefabricated housing.

“Yes, but we also use mana, like the crystals from the planet we are on, and potentially runes like I learned to use them. Both have their place. The freedom to experiment with it all.” More words dripped from my lips in a rush.

“Freedom?” It asked, as again its form changed. This time its shoulders widened and they placed both hands on the table. They leaned closer.

“Freedom to become what they want.” I almost snorted. That level of freedom was the exact opposite of what the question originally alluded to.

“Limitations exist for a reason, to protect a species from destroying itself.”

Images popped into my mind of leaving Earth, and the whispers and rumors about how much time the planet might have left.

“I’m not saying remove all limitations, just give better options to those who really work hard to accomplish something for a good cause. Those who strive, whatever it is they strive for. Like, the person who wants to learn how to heal people, but doesn’t have a teacher. Why shouldn’t they be able to get a class to make them a better healer?”

The person leaned back in their chair and ran a hand through their dark hair, which hadn’t been there before.

“Look at me. I am wearing clothing from Kabi’s people, using a spear from my people, teaching myself pure mana work after several other professions, and I’m climbing up the ranks. I’m friends with different beings from various worlds, and even now I’m on a new planet discovering so much. Yet, I started without a class, then a profession that was great, until I couldn’t level it, but now this is me.”

I couldn’t seem to stop arguing with this person. No matter what I said, it didn't seem to understand. Honestly, I wasn’t sure if there was a way to make them understand what it meant to be human.

“You earned where you are.”

“Exactly!” This time I pointed at them. “I struggled to get here, but I just kept going. Everyone should have that option.”

“Humanity should have the freedom to earn what they are…” it trailed off.

“Yes.” This time I nodded. That was a better answer to the question about judgment.

Light flared over the person, and then, suddenly, there was me, sitting across the table.

Same eyes, hair, and outfit. I hadn’t had a chance to really look at myself recently, and the changes were rather drastic.

My teeth were slightly pointed, and my eyes had darkened a little. A vibe hung over me, of power and danger.

“So, you are me?” I asked, trying to figure this out.

“I am a reflection of you, a ghost of sorts that the rest of your kind will meet when they get to this point. I am the one they must answer to.” Her voice was on point. She sounded exactly like me.

“That’s going to be awkward when you meet my Dad and Brothers,” I said.

She smiled, her teeth standing out all the more. “They’ll understand, and can handle it. Don’t you agree?”

I didn’t have an answer for that. Not really.

“The bigger question is, can you live up to your own ideals?” she asked, her eyes suddenly going darker. “Can you pass your own trial?”
