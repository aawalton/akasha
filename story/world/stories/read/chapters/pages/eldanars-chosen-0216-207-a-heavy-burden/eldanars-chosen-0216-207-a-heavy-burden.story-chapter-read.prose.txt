The final ten minutes before the abandonment of the forward operating base were absolute chaos. The rescue squad did not have the luxury of resting their aching muscles or celebrating their survival. They immediately fell into the rushing tide of the evacuation. The dirt road was completely choked with heavy wooden supply wagons, groaning draft beasts, and terrified civilian labourers frantically lashing down the final crates of essential rations and iron armaments.

Josh pushed his way through the surging crowd, his heavy steel boots clanking tiredly against the frosted earth. He was looking for the part of the convoy designated for the critically wounded. Brett, Carcan, and Perberos followed closely behind him, dodging rushing city guards and panicked porters.

They finally located the heavy, canvas-covered cart near the very centre of the departing column.

Josh pulled back the thick canvas flap, peering into the dim, cramped interior of the wagon. The space smelled strongly of bitter medicinal herbs, old blood, and clean linen. Lying on a bed of tightly packed straw, completely swathed in thick woollen blankets, was Bhel.

The dwarven fighter looked terrible. His skin was a sickly, pale shade of grey, completely lacking its usual healthy, wind-chapped leather hue. A massive, pristine white bandage was tightly strapped across his broad chest, covering the terrible wound where the black sludge had nearly eaten through to his heart.

Despite his horrific condition, the dwarf was awake, and he was absolutely furious.

"I am telling you, I can walk!" Bhel grumbled loudly, his deep, gravelly voice echoing within the cramped wagon. He was weakly attempting to push himself up on his one good elbow, glaring at a terrified young acolyte who was trying to press him back down into the straw. "I can swing a bloody axe with my left arm! Give me my armour! You are treating me like a fragile, crystal goblet!"

"You look more like a cracked clay pot, my friend," Josh noted dryly, stepping up to the back of the wagon.

Bhel stopped struggling instantly, his dark eyes snapping towards the entrance. A massive, genuine grin broke through the thick, intricate braids of his beard, completely transforming his pale face.

"Josh! Brett!" the dwarf coughed, a wet, rattling sound that made Josh wince. "The light preserves! I heard the alarm horns sounding. The panicked cowards loading this cart told me you had marched straight back into the rotting trees. I thought I was going to have to get up and drag you both out of the mud myself."

"We appreciate the sentiment, Bhel, but we managed to find our own way out," Brett replied, leaning against the wooden frame of the wagon. The mage offered a weary, exhausted smile. "Though it did involve entirely, entirely too much running for my personal taste. You missed my spectacular display of cardio though."

"I missed a fight, is what I missed," Bhel complained bitterly, his thick fingers picking restlessly at the edge of his heavy woollen blanket. "Lying here on my back while the rest of the party is out cracking skulls feels just wrong. Just point me toward the back of the cart. I can act as a stationary turret. Hand me a throwing axe."

Before Josh could offer a diplomatic refusal, Carcan stepped up to the edge of the wagon.

The elven healer did not look amused. Her luminous silver eyes were narrowed into sharp, uncompromising slits, and her pristine pale green robes seemed to radiate a cold, dangerous authority. She looked directly down at the stubborn dwarven fighter.

"Bheldur Giantguard," Carcan stated, her melodic voice dropping an entire octave into a tone of chilling menace. "You have suffered catastrophic tissue damage and severe magical corruption. If you attempt to sit up, if you attempt to lift a weapon, or if you even think about leaving this wagon before we reach the safety of the city walls, I will personally break your other arm."

The terrified young acolyte in the wagon nodded vigorously, looking at Carcan with reverence.

Bhel stared at the elven healer for a long, calculating moment. He possessed the stubborn, unyielding pride of the mountain folk, but he also possessed basic survival instincts. He recognized the terrifying promise of violence hiding behind her serene, aristocratic face.

"Right. Fine," Bhel grunted, slowly sliding back down into the straw and pulling the heavy woollen blanket up to his chin like a scolded child. He refused to look Carcan in the eye, muttering darkly into his beard. "Just resting my eyes, then. Conserving energy for the siege. Perfectly reasonable decision that I’ve come to of my own volition."

"See that you do," Carcan replied crisply, turning her back on the wagon.

"Move out!" Torin’s gravelly voice roared from the very front of the massive column, echoing clearly over the din of the panicked camp. "Maintain a tight formation! Do not let the wagons drift apart!"

The massive retreat finally began. The heavy wooden wheels of the supply carts groaned in protest as the draft beasts leaned into their harnesses. The column slowly lurched into motion, heading south down the wide, frost-covered dirt road toward the distant safety of Ashenfall.

The weaker fighters, the exhausted civilian labourers, and the heavily injured were placed near the front and centre of the convoy, shielded by the bulk of the heavy wooden wagons. Ronald, refusing to be contained within a static marching line, took half a dozen of the fastest, most experienced rogues and rangers and pushed far ahead. They vanished silently into the freezing darkness bordering the road, acting as the advance scouts to ensure the path to the city was completely clear of ambushes.

Perberos, however, did not join the scouting party this time. The elven ranger chose to remain at the very rear of the column, falling into step right beside Josh, Brett, and Carcan. He kept his beautiful elven longbow unslung and rested easily across his shoulder, his silver eyes constantly scanning the dark, treeline behind them.

Those of the rescue squad that could still stand had been assigned to the rearguard, the final line of defence between the slow-moving civilian wagons and the encroaching horrors of the corrupted forest.

As the convoy slowly pulled away from the forward operating base, Josh turned his head to look back at the encampment they had built. It was a deeply unsettling sight. The dark, unnatural miasma of the forest was no longer creeping. It was flowing. The thick, foul-smelling black sludge and the suffocating necrotic fog were spilling out from beneath the dead pine canopy like a physical tidal wave.

The heavy, iron braziers they had been forced to abandon were snuffed out one by one as the fog rolled over them. The solid, wooden palisades and defensive trenches were rotting before their very eyes, the timber turning black and crumbling into foul-smelling dust. Within the swirling darkness of the fog, Josh could see thousands of glowing green eyes, a terrifying, endless ocean of malevolence watching the humans retreat.

The undead did not immediately charge. They simply absorbed the abandoned camp, reclaiming the territory with terrifying, silent inevitability.

"Keep your eyes forward," Josh ordered quietly, turning his back on the horrific spectacle. "If they decide to push the pace, we will deal with it then."

The march back to Ashenfall was incredibly tense.

The freezing winter wind howled mournfully across the open plains, biting through their heavy cloaks and chilling the sweat on their skin. Every single snapped twig, every sudden gust of wind rattling the bare branches of the sparse roadside trees, caused weapons to be drawn and defensive shields to be raised. The sheer psychological weight of knowing an army of thousands was marching just a few miles behind them was suffocating.

However, the attack never came. The undead horde seemed perfectly content to consolidate their hold on the northern forest and the abandoned camp, allowing the retreating column to march through the dark plains completely unbothered.

After an hour of walking in tight, nervous silence, the party finally felt safe enough to let their attention drift, and they all heard a familiar chime echo softly in the back of Josh's mind.

Josh blinked, a small, translucent blue system notification screen materialising in his peripheral vision. He let out a sudden, involuntary whoop of absolute relief, the sound startling the nearby city guards marching in the line.

"What is it?" Brett asked, his hand instantly glowing with a small, defensive spark of white-hot plasma.

"Check your status," Josh grinned tiredly, his storm-grey eyes locked on the blue text floating before him.

Brett frowned, his eyes losing focus for a fraction of a second as he summoned his own internal interface. A massive, genuine smile broke across the mage's pale, exhausted face. He let out a loud, highly undignified cheer, throwing his hands in the air.

"Level twenty-seven," Brett announced to the dark road, his voice filled with profound satisfaction. He pushed his imaginary spectacles up his nose, looking at Josh. "The experience points from the sinkhole. Or maybe from the run? My physical endurance stat has surely crossed the threshold into the realm of 'slightly below average.' I might actually survive a brisk walk without collapsing."

"I hit twenty-seven as well," Josh confirmed, feeling the subtle, welcoming rush of fresh stamina flooding his aching muscles, a passive reward from the system for crossing the threshold. He scrolled down his notification screen, his brow furrowing slightly. "Hold on. I unlocked a new passive skill during the retreat."

Carcan, walking a few paces to their left, looked over with mild curiosity. "What is the nature of the skill?" Carcan asked, her voice calm and melodic. "Is it an offensive enhancement for your blade, or a new defensive aura for your shield?"

"Neither, actually," Josh replied, reading the glowing blue text carefully. He could not help the sudden, amused smirk that tugged at the corner of his mouth. "It is a utility skill. It is called Encumbered March."

"That sounds incredibly boring," Brett noted, leaning closer to peer at Josh as he wasreading. "What does it do?"

"It says here," Josh read aloud, his tone perfectly flat and serious, "that it passively increases my stamina regeneration by twenty percent whenever I am carrying an excessive load."

Perberos, who rarely showed any emotion whatsoever, let out a single, sharp snort of amusement from the shadows, instantly realising the exact trigger for the new ability.

Carcan stopped walking completely.

She stood frozen in the middle of the freezing dirt road, her luminous silver eyes blinking rapidly as her brilliant, analytical mind connected the dots. The system had actively rewarded Josh for scooping her out of the freezing mud and physically carrying her through the corrupted forest like a sack of grain.

The furious, vibrant crimson blush returned to her pale cheeks with a vengeance, turning the tips of her pointed elven ears a bright, glowing red in the darkness.

"Wait," Josh added, looking up from the screen, his smirk widening into a terrible, teasing grin. "The system log says I acquired this specific skill because I successfully carried a 'heavy load' out of the danger zone."

Brett completely lost his composure. The exhausted mage burst into loud, wheezing laughter, clutching his aching sides as he stumbled down the road.

"I am not heavy!" Carcan hissed, her serene composure completely shattering.

She marched swiftly up to Josh, her muddy boots stomping angrily against the frost. She did not use her glowing amber staff. She simply raised her hand and delivered a sharp, stinging smack directly against the heavy steel plating of his shoulder guard. It did absolutely no physical damage, but the message was entirely clear.

"I weigh significantly less than the dwarf you regularly haul around, Josh," Carcan huffed indignantly, refusing to meet his highly amused storm-grey eyes. "The system is clearly flawed, and its phrasing is entirely offensive."

"I do not write the rules, Carcan," Josh replied smoothly, offering a helpless shrug, though his eyes were dancing with levity. "I just carry the burdens the universe places upon me."

Carcan let out a highly uncharacteristic, frustrated growl. She turned on her heel, her pale green robes swishing angrily in the cold wind, and marched swiftly ahead to walk beside her quietly chuckling twin brother, putting a solid five yards of distance between herself and the grinning shield-bearer.

"Oh, that was a mistake," Brett wheezed, wiping a tear of mirth from his eye as he fell back into step beside Josh. "She is the only person keeping your mangled ribs in one piece. The next time a ghoul bites you, she might just decide you need to build character through natural healing."

"Yeah… I think you’re right," Josh admitted, the genuine smile lingering on his face.

The brief moment of levity cut through the suffocating tension of the march, providing a much-needed mental break from the horrors they had just survived. However, the lighthearted atmosphere did not last long.

The rhythmic, heavy crunch of boots on the frost behind them announced the arrival of Caistina Tharistrar.

The elven mage had been walking the entire length of the retreating column, checking the perimeter and speaking with the guard captains. She fell into step right beside Josh and Brett. Her sapphire-blue robes were perfectly immaculate, and her posture was rigidly straight, but the deep, exhausted lines around her amethyst eyes betrayed her immense stress.

"Enjoy the high spirits while you can," Caistina advised quietly, her melodic voice carrying an uncompromising, grim weight. She did not look at them, her gaze fixed entirely on the dark road ahead.

"We survived the night," Josh pointed out respectfully, his tone shifting instantly back to business. "The entire missing party was recovered. That is a victory."

"It is a successful tactical retreat," Caistina corrected sharply. "Do not confuse survival with victory, Josh. You saw the scale of the corruption bleeding out of the treeline. You saw the size of the horde they are massing."

"Are they going to march on the city immediately?" Brett asked, his sharp, analytical mind instantly jumping to the defensive logistics.

"The undead do not require sleep, rations, or supply lines. They have no reason to wait," Caistina explained, her grip tightening firmly around her pristine white staff. "We have retreated, which means they now have total, uncontested control of the northern plains. They will likely follow our trail of panic directly to the gates of Ashenfall."

"We have high, solid walls," Josh countered, thinking of the imposing, heavy fortifications of the city. "And we have hundreds of veteran adventurers, plus the city guard. We can hold a siege."

"A siege implies a war of attrition," Caistina said softly, looking over at the heavy fighter. "A normal army will eventually break if they run out of food, or if the winter cold freezes them in their tents. The dead do not freeze. They do not starve. They will simply throw bodies against our walls until the pile is high enough for them to walk over the ramparts."

A heavy, incredibly oppressive silence fell over the small group walking in the dark.

"We need reinforcements," Brett concluded, his pale face tight with worry. "The local Guildhall is not equipped for a full-scale, apocalyptic invasion. Has word been sent to the capital?"

"The local magistrate sent a message the moment the warning horns sounded," Caistina confirmed, though her tone offered absolutely no comfort. "They carry urgent requests for the deployment of the Royal Guard and high-tier military mages."

"How long until they arrive?" Josh asked.

"The capital is entirely too far away," Caistina stated bluntly, delivering the final, terrifying piece of the puzzle. "Even if the the crown mobilises the army the exact moment the message is received, it will take them a minimum of seven days to march a relief force of that size to our gates."

She stopped walking for a fraction of a second, letting the devastating reality of the timeline sink in.

"We do not have seven days," Caistina finished softly. "We likely do not even have two. When the horde hits the walls of Ashenfall, we will be entirely alone."

The elven mage trainer did not offer any further words of comfort. She simply increased her pace, moving smoothly ahead to speak with the commander of the city guard leading the centre of the column, leaving Josh and Brett alone in the freezing dark.

They walked in silence for another hour. The freezing wind continued to bite, and the heavy groaning of the wooden supply wagons filled the air.

Slowly, the oppressive, suffocating darkness of the night began to break.

Josh looked up. Cresting a massive, rolling hill in the distance, the imposing silhouette of Ashenfall finally came into view against the pale, pre-dawn sky. The massive, dark walls looked strong and unyielding, but the sight did not offer the usual sense of profound relief or safety.

Sprawled massively outside the heavy iron gates, and undoubtedly filling every single available street and alleyway within the city itself, was a chaotic sea of flickering campfires and temporary canvas tents. Thousands upon thousands of refugees, commoners from the outlying farming villages who had fled the initial undead incursions, were packed tightly together, seeking shelter beneath the shadow of the walls.

They were families, children, and elderly civilians who had absolutely nowhere else to go.

Josh stopped walking, staring at the sprawling, vulnerable sea of humanity. He was not just fighting for his own survival anymore. He was not just fighting to protect Brett, or Bhel, or Carcan. When the undead general finally brought his rotting army out of the darkness and threw them against the timber walls of Ashenfall, Josh and the exhausted adventurers walking beside him were going to be the only physical barrier standing between the undead horde and tens of thousands of innocent, terrified lives.

He gripped the leather handle of his heavy steel shield tightly, feeling the cold, uncompromising bite of the metal.

He took a deep breath of the freezing air, forced his aching legs to move, and began the final, long march down the hill towards the city gates.
