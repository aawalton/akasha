Varuna suppressed a sigh, forcing her breathing to remain slow and silent beneath the optical camouflage. The man she was tailing—Bishop Malachi, a supposed pillar of the Lumina Imperium's morality—was pathetic.

He leaned heavily against the trunk of a frozen pine tree, wheezing like a punctured bellows. He had barely walked half a mile from the monastery, and already he was sweating through his rough-spun disguise, his face a mottled, unhealthy red.

Lumina really needs to institute mandatory physical training for their clergy, Varuna thought, adjusting her position in the shadows.

The man's physical state stood in stark contrast to the rest of the Imperium's forces. According to the strategic briefings pounded into the heads of every Aegis operative, Lumina’s military was a terrifying, multi-tiered monolith divided into five distinct Orders.

At the bottom of the hierarchy lay the Inquisition and the General Army. The Inquisition functioned as a penal legion—a meat grinder built from criminals, heretics, and the desperate, commanded by disgraced officers seeking redemption through blood. They were branded slaves, thrown into the worst fighting to soften up the enemy. Above them sat the Knights of the Cloth, the Paladins attached to the sprawling network of churches and bishops like Malachi. They acted as regional enforcers, heavily armored and fanatical.

Then came the true power. The All-Seeing Light managed its intelligence apparatus with terrifying efficiency. The Royal Knights formed the elite standing army directly under the Emperor’s command. At the absolute pinnacle, answering only to the Golden Sovereign himself, were the Angels and the Dragon Knights of the Golden Flight.

Bishops like Malachi were supposed to be the strategic minds commanding the Paladins and the General Army. If this wheezing, soft man represented their regional leadership, the Imperium was rotting beneath its golden veneer.

Malachi finally pushed himself off the tree. He wiped his brow with a silk handkerchief he had foolishly brought along, and lumbered the last few hundred yards to the secluded hunting lodge.

Varuna crept closer. The lodge sat dark and seemingly abandoned, nestled deep in a thicket of snow-covered pines.

She slipped into the shadows near a side window and engaged her thermal optics. She anticipated the warm glow of two bodies intertwining—the classic clandestine meeting of a corrupt official and his mistress.

The scene unfolding inside made her elven blood run cold.

She disabled her optical camouflage and slipped a specialized glass-cutting tool from her belt. With silent precision, she removed a small pane of the frosted window to hear the voices inside clearly.

"...it's barely enough to feed my children for a week, Your Eminence," a woman was saying. Her voice was thin, reedy, and vibrating with a desperate, suppressed terror.

Varuna peered through the gap. A young woman, dressed in rags that barely held together, stood in the center of the dusty, unheated room. She was looking down at a small leather pouch of coins Malachi had just tossed onto a rotting wooden table.

"You should be grateful for my generosity, girl," the Bishop sneered, unfastening the heavy clasp of his merchant’s cloak. "With your husband conscripted to the front lines, you would be starving in the gutters of Oakhaven by now. I am providing for the needy, as the Light commands."

He let out a wet, greasy chuckle and began to unbutton his tunic. "Now. Earn your charity."

The woman squeezed her eyes shut. A single tear escaped, tracking a clean line through the dirt on her cheek. She reached for the hem of her tattered dress, her hands shaking violently.

Varuna had seen enough. The Warlord had ordered her to find leverage, leaving the specific methods entirely to her discretion.

Relying on her training as a deep-cover operative, she bypassed the heavy wooden door completely. She slipped through the window she had already compromised, moving with a fluid, silent grace that didn't disturb a speck of dust.

She landed directly behind Malachi. Before he could even register the shift in air pressure, she flicked her wrists. Two specialized, pneumatically driven needles shot from the concealed launchers on her gauntlets.

Thwip. Thwip.

One caught Malachi in the fleshy part of his neck. The other struck the cowering woman in the shoulder.

A highly concentrated, fast-acting paralytic developed by the Aegis medical corps flooded their systems. The woman slumped to the floor immediately, falling into a deep unconsciousness. Malachi, possessing a bit more mass, managed a gurgling, confused squeak before his eyes rolled back and he crashed onto the dusty floorboards like a felled oak.

Varuna walked over to the incapacitated Bishop. She knelt, her face a mask of contempt beneath her visor.

For a moment, the cold detachment required of a spy evaporated. A surge of icy disgust washed over her. She drew her foot back and kicked him, delivering a solid, satisfying thud against his ribs.

"You preach that your churches are built to help the needy," she whispered to the unhearing man. "Is this your charity? Exploiting the starving wives of the men sent to die on the battlefields?"

She took a slow breath, forcing her heart rate down to regain her professional composure. Losing control was dangerous, a direct violation of every protocol she had been taught.

She quickly stripped him of the heavy, golden chain bearing the sunburst crest of the Imperium. Her scanners confirmed it radiated a complex, localized mana signature. It served as a master key, designed to bypass the specific wards of his own church.

She turned to the woman and checked her pulse. Strong and steady. She would wake up in a few hours with a mild headache and a pouch of gold she wouldn't have to suffer for.

"Specter-One," Varuna commented over the encrypted channel. "Keep an overwatch on this cabin. The target is incapacitated. Specter-Two, maintain perimeter scan at the church. I'm going in."

Varuna slipped back out the window, re-engaging her optical camouflage. The heavy golden necklace hung around her neck, granting her a stolen passkey to the heart of the enemy.

She retraced her steps, following the subterranean tunnel back to the monastery. The decoy spell was still kneeling dutifully in the chapel. She slipped past it, using the Bishop’s key to bypass the heavy stone altar without triggering the localized alarms, and ascended into the pristine, silent halls of the church.

It was late. The patrols were sparse, and most of the acolytes were asleep in their cells. She moved like a shadow across the polished marble floors, heading straight for the central nave.

The main prayer hall was massive, lined with towering statues of angelic figures carved from white stone. At the far end, behind a veil of shimmering, consecrated light, sat the high altar. This was where the teleportation network had to be hidden.

She approached the veil of light. It hummed with defensive magic intended to turn an unauthorized intruder to ash. As she stepped forward, the golden sunburst around her neck flared with a sympathetic resonance. The veil parted like a curtain, granting her safe passage.

She slipped through, emerging at the edge of a deep, circular shaft that plunged straight down into the bedrock.

A spiral staircase hugged the wall. She descended. The air grew colder, heavier, tasting strongly of ozone and ancient, stale magic.

At the bottom, she stepped into a vast, subterranean cavern lit by glowing, fist-sized crystals embedded in the walls. The floor featured a complex, mind-bending mosaic of interlocking runes etched into the stone and filled with silver and gold.

Her suit’s analytical scanners flared to life. Small, spider-like drones detached from her back, scuttling across the floor and walls to map the chamber and analyze the runic arrays.

The scans revealed an entirely different origin for the magic. Unlike the grandiose, prayer-reliant aesthetics of the Imperium, this architecture was mathematical and brutally efficient. The runes were archaic, belonging to a syntax that predated the Seven Kingdoms.

"The tip was accurate," Varuna murmured, observing the drones’ data stream. "They found this network."

The Lumina Imperium hadn't created the global teleportation network. They had discovered the ruins of a lost civilization and repurposed them, building their 'Churches of Light' directly over the ancient hubs to control the travel lanes.

She explored the cavern further. To one side, behind a set of heavy iron bars, sat a secondary chamber. It operated as a vault. Inside, neatly cataloged on stone shelves, were dozens of artifacts. Strange, geometric shapes made of unknown metals, glowing spheres, and heavily modified crystalline matrices lined the walls.

She scanned them. Every single one bore the unmistakable, cloying mana signature of Angelic magic. The Imperium had taken the ancient technology and brutally grafted their own divine enchantments onto it, jury-rigging the ancient systems to serve their needs. One of the artifacts, a large, bowl-shaped device, operated exactly like a modified scrying pool, repurposed for long-range, instantaneous communication.

She recalled her drones once their memory banks were full of structural and magical data. The Lord Commander would need weeks to decipher the mechanics of this ancient network, but he finally possessed the raw data to do so.

Her mission was technically complete. Looking up toward the high, vaulted ceiling, she noticed a narrow catwalk leading to a small, glass-enclosed office overlooking the entire cavern. The Bishop's private sanctum.

She scaled the wall, bypassing the stairs completely, and landed silently on the catwalk. The office door was locked. A quick pulse of energy from her gauntlet fried the mechanism.

The office was Spartan. It contained a desk, a chair, and a heavy, iron-bound safe built into the wall.

It took her less than two minutes to crack the mechanical lock on the safe. Inside lay three leather-bound ledgers.

Varuna pulled them out and laid them on the desk. She opened the most recent one. It detailed troop movements, supply transfers, and the mundane logistics of running a regional garrison. It confirmed exactly what they already suspected: the churches operated as military hubs.

She picked up the second ledger. It was older, its pages yellowed and brittle. She flipped through it, discovering a transit log that recorded every entity entering or exiting the Oakhaven portal.

She skimmed the entries, her eyes catching on a date from sixteen years ago.

Twenty units. Designation: All-Seeing Light Enforcer Unit 51.

In the margins, written in a cramped, hurried script, was a personal note from the previous Bishop: 'They smelled of the sewers. A foulness that lingered for days.'

Varuna froze. The description clicked into place with terrifying precision.

Every operative in the Aegis intelligence division had to study the historical case files. The most prominent file covered the assassination attempt on the Lord Commander when he was an infant. The reports detailed twenty attackers, cloaked in black, wielding an angelic isolation barrier. They had reeked of the sewers.

House Wight had never known how the assassins bypassed the border defenses and appeared deep within their heavily guarded territory.

Now she knew. They had stepped through a door.

Her hands trembling slightly, she picked up the third and final ledger. This one was heavily sealed with wax. She broke it open, revealing a private journal passed down from the previous Bishop.

She scanned the pages, utilizing her suit's rapid-capture optics to photograph every entry. She stopped on a page dated shortly after the assassination attempt.

The Hidden Light has failed, the entry read. The operation in Aerthos was a disaster. The contractor of the Platinum Dragon survived. The threat remains. The Sovereign is displeased.

The journal confirmed the darkest, most paranoid theory Alarion had harbored. The Lumina Imperium hadn't just supplied the weapons. They had orchestrated the hit.

They had done it because of Kaelus. They wanted to eliminate the possibility of a Master of Space before the dragon even hatched.

Varuna carefully placed the ledgers back in the safe and locked it tight. She wiped the room of any physical trace of her presence.

She exited the church the way she came, slipping back through the subterranean tunnel and into the freezing night air. She returned to the lodge, placing the heavy golden chain back around the neck of the still-unconscious Bishop Malachi.

"Mission accomplished," she whispered into her comms. She engaged her optical camouflage and melted back into the forest. "Requesting extraction."

Thousands of miles away, on the command bridge of The Aegis, the data packets arrived in a burst of heavily encrypted static.

I stood at the central console, watching as Tes rapidly decrypted and sorted the information Varuna had stolen from the heart of the Imperium. The structural scans of the ancient portal network provided a technological treasure trove that would take months to fully understand.

But the translated text from the Bishop's ledgers held my full, undivided attention.

I read the words over and over, the cold, sterile glow of the holographic display reflecting in my eyes.

The hidden light has failed... The contractor of the Platinum Dragon survived.

The truth was absolute, laid bare in ink and parchment. The Lumina Imperium had tried to murder my family. They had sent the assassins into my nursery when I was a baby, all because they feared the cosmic potential resting in an unhatched egg.

A glacial anger took hold, settling deep in my chest with the unyielding certainty of a collapsing star. It was entirely different from the frantic rage I had felt toward Ignis. This was a quiet, methodical fury.

Their target had always been Kaelus. They had set their sights on my little brother before he had even drawn his first breath.

"They want a war," I whispered to the empty air of the bridge, my voice devoid of any inflection. "They've wanted it since the day he was born."

I looked up at the tactical map. My gaze bypassed the burning coastlines of Cinderfall, moving past the shattered forests of Sylvanheim, and settled firmly on the glowing, golden borders of the Lumina Imperium.

I had lost my home once to the fires of an arrogant king. I would not let these self-proclaimed angels touch a single scale on my brother's hide.

They thought they were the hidden light, pulling the strings of the world from the safety of their holy towers. They were about to find out what happens when you anger the dark.
