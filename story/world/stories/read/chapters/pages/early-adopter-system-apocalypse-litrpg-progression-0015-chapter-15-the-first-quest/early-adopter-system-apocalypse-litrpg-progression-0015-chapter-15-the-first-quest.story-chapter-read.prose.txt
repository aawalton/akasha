Scarlet knew the past few hours had been harrowing because she was barely fazed by the notification.

“Let’s see the fine print, then,” Scarlet muttered as she focused on the prompt.

[Quest: Establish a Safe Zone]

[Description: During the post-Integration ‘Adaptation Phase’, increasing mana saturation will give rise to progressively higher-tier entities. Establish and secure a settlement zone to impose regional stability and suppress hostile incursion.][Time Limit: 7 ‘Earth’ Days]

[Objectives:

Optional sub-objectives may appear][Base Rewards:

Additional Rewards: Variable based on performance]

[Would you like to accept this Quest?][Yes | No]

She fully read through it, her gaze moving steadily from line to line. She could feel her mind picking apart everything she had just read. Briefly, she glanced over at Maus who had already curled himself into a ball on his fuzzy, little pillow. His eyes: half-closed. His mind: quite empty.

“Good for you buddy,” she said, and genuinely meant it. There was no point in both of them having terribly high blood pressure. She idly wondered if that was something VIT could fix. Then her eyes flicked back to the Quest.

“Okay,” she said, drawing the word out.

She rubbed her knuckles against the bridge of her nose. Then she stood and crossed the short distance to her chaise lounge, picking up a new pad of paper and a different pen on her way, and feeling quite vindicated about her small stationary-purchasing vice.

“Okay,” she said again.

She read the Quest a second time, making notes about some of the terms and objectives.

What is this ‘Adaptation Phase’? She queried the Guide.

[The Adaptation Phase follows world Initialization. During this period, the newly integrated world undergoes continued systemic adjustment. Mana distribution remains unstable as saturation rapidly increases, while environmental and biological changes continue to develop. Outcomes during this phase may significantly influence long-term development]

“Okay…” She squeezed her eyes shut and clenched her fists before releasing the tension with a deep breath. She made several more notes on her writing pad before she queried the Guide again.

What was post-Integration seclusion?

[Newly integrated worlds receive a 12-year seclusion period during which external Factions cannot directly interfere]

Scarlet went still.

And what did it mean by ‘cannot directly interfere’? Could they indirectly interfere? The Guide did not respond.

Still.

“Only twelve years,” she murmured. Whatever protection ‘seclusion’ offered would end after only twelve years.

She straightened slightly as the implications settled in. It was likely these Factions were made up of other sapients. The idea that Earth was not alone had been a theory for her even pre-Initialization.

The thought was not comforting.

Twelve years wasn’t a long time. Not on a historical scale, or a planetary one.

The pen hovering over the page stilled as another thought clicked into place. That would explain why she’d received such a daunting Quest so early.

She felt her eyelid twitch, and she took a deep breath.

She wanted more information, she always wanted more information, but security was the first priority, and those rewards would help her immensely.

So, she asked the more immediate question. What constituted a settlement zone?

[A minimum viable settlement zone must be at least a five-kilometre radius from designated anchor point.]

A five-kilometre radius. She went to her bookshelf and pulled out a map book. Her INT Stat gave her a remarkable memory, but the physical map made that tangible.

5km was not small.

She would be establishing a settlement zone. That was more than just a single building, or even a block. The minimum viable size was an area the size of a town. A proper one. Something that would encompass roads, structures, terrain, and landmarks. Things she could not control with a locked door, a drone camera, and a bit of foresight.

“All right,” she said quietly, tapping on her leg with her fingers. All of those were problems for later. She moved on to the rewards section.

Pillar of Establishment.

A quick query told her the pillar was what legitimised a Safe Zone. She’d need to establish the pillar before she could claim the Administrative rights.

Then there was the last item. A Token of Ascendance. When she queried the Guide, she received a curious response.

[Information restricted to token holders]

At least it hadn’t told her she needed to advance before it said anything.

Scarlet’s lips pressed together, before relaxing. She wanted to know, to understand. That tiny spark of curiosity flared. Burned a little brighter; a little hotter.

“Well,” she said, turning her head just enough to glance at Maus, “you up for the challenge?” she asked Maus. Her feelings were passing through the bond unfiltered, carrying that spark with them.

Maus responded immediately.

A wash of warmth, a flicker of excitement, a quiet, steady pulse of determination that had nothing to do with ambition, and everything to do with wanting to help.

Scarlet let out a small breath, some of the tension easing from her shoulders. “Thank you, dear,” she murmured. The team had decided.

[Would you like to accept this Quest?][Yes | No]

[Yes]

The Quest was accepted, slipping into place as part of her Interface, accessible now in the same way as her Status or her Log. She flicked through it briefly, then dismissed it.

She was preparing to make another note when–

Thump–

A heavy, familiar impact slammed against the bunker door.

Scarlet’s jaw tightened as the sound reverberated through the space.

“Right, right,” she muttered, grimacing as she turned her attention toward the source of the noise. The door held, as expected, but the reminder was unpleasant.

The Quest was a medium-term problem. The much more immediate problem was that it was not currently safe for her and Maus to leave the bunker.

She leaned forward slightly, elbows resting on her knees as she worked through the problem. She and Maus could not start the quest, access more supplies, or grab their loot until the safety issue was solved.

Another, lighter tremor ran through the door, as though whatever was outside was probing it.

“All right, I have an idea,” she said to Maus, “and I’m going to need your help.”

Her first idea came easily enough. She already had a sensory advantage, and she already had a partner who could respond to threats much more quickly than she could.

The issue was that while she could sense the shadow bat, she was struggling to coordinate with Maus.

She reached for the bond while spreading her awareness outward, searching for that flicker of presence she had felt before. When she found it, she pushed the impression towards Maus, trying to share what she was experiencing with him.

Maus looked up and sent back a wave of confusion.

So that didn’t work. She could somewhat share sensory impressions with him; however, he had no ability to interpret what she was sending.

She leaned back, letting her thoughts flow.

“You’re definitely receiving something,” she continued. Bond Communication worked at the speed of thought. The difficulty was trying to translate what she was feeling into something useful to Maus.

Because that was the real constraint.

Time.

The bat didn’t give them the luxury of explanation, or of interpretation. It would be there, then gone, and if they hesitated, that would be the end of it. The end of them. It was fast enough to just smash them. With its cloaking, they wouldn’t even see it coming.

Her fingers stilled as something clicked into place.

Mana.

The answer was mana… The question was: how?
