Every party of the Locksmith's body hurt. Well, at least the important ones. Did I die? Still on the first floors? But Harry quickly dismissed the absurd idea; he was too proud to believe it. What the young man remembered, however, was having landed a definitive attack on Dracno, or how the cultists referred to him: the Shepherd.

Was the collision powerful enough to take down a man that size? He questioned himself, wondering if he had perhaps killed the man. But before he could ruminate more on the idea, a sharp pain came from his back as he looked around. Harry was in one of the pews, right in front of the church.

The bones in his leg? Shattered like shards of glass after a robbery. The grinding of his bones could be heard with every subtle movement. The Locksmith tried to move his right hand and discovered the muscle missing. With all the pain circling his body, for a second, he thought it would be calmer to be dead, but he was alive. Without a hand? Yes. Destroyed? Absolutely. But alive at last.

It was late afternoon, judging by the twilight coming from the entrance of the church, which was opened. Despite the pain, Harry discovered the left side of his body almost clean of serious damage. Bruise pains were plenty, certainly, but nothing compared to the agony of a broken bone.

He then felt his entire body slowly sliding down. The Locksmith desperately tried to hold onto the chair with his right hand, but found the grasp non-existent. In a swift motion, he fell.

"FUCKIN' HELL!" He yelled and groaned.

With a jump, Madeline woke up terrified, right in the pew in front of Harry. Discussing behind the ambo of the church, Vela and Brun soon realized what was going on.

"By the gods, Harry!" Madeline said. "You just woke me up from a bad dream!"

"I just landed on my broken hand...!" Harry replied between groans. "...And isn't it a good thing that I woke you from a bad dream?"

"I..." The young woman paused, then added. "Actually, you're right. Thank you, Harry!"

"...You're welcome."

"Mr. Locksmith!" Vela said with a calm smile as he carefully helped him sit on the chair. "You're awake, finally. So nice to see you talkative again. We missed you."

"Okay, now I know you're lying." The young thief chuckled. "And thanks, but I can do this on my—Argh!"

“Please, compose yourself, Mr. Harry.” The former monk said. “We don’t want to see you almost dead again.”

"...You don't?" That was a... weird sentence. It shared a sentiment Harry hadn't heard in a long time. Maybe it was sarcasm? But he looked at his right hand once again and all the stitches and white wraps around it. All around his body, careful bandages and pieces of mattresses held his blood from coming out. They had... taken care of him. Huh.

"Of course not." Brun was the one to reply, coming near him and staying on her knees. "I thank you, in behalf of the entire group. I know we didn't get off on the right foot, but I genuinely thank you for the sacrifice you've done."

"...I only tried to stop Dracno." He added, hurriedly. "I wasn't trying to save you guys."

"Either way," She insisted. "You did. That's all that matters, okay?"

Sometimes the mind had a hard time trying to catch up to the body's actions, and that was one of them. Now thinking more clearly, Harry understood the severity of his actions. He almost killed himself only to stop Dracno. This wasn't on the plans. I should've just left... Why didn't I? And the people around him seemed answer enough. They could've killed me in my sleep... And, although it was a rather disturbing thought to have, Harry had forgotten that what made life important was companions. Trust is a two way bridge, right? The Locksmith smiled to himself as he avoided the old woman's gaze.

"...Are you okay?" Madeline asked, in a more serious tone.

“You should’ve seen the other guy.” He replied quietly, struggling not to add a laugh. “...He’s worse than me, right?”

"We do not know, Mr. Locksmith." Vela said, grasping both his hands like a priest. "By the time we reached to save you, his Emblem had already covered all the damage you did to him. We can't even check if he's still alive. And although his Emblem's still working, which would indicate sign of life, we are not sure; two Emblem users can act differently."

If he were dead, the Emblem would've just left his body, leaving an empty husk... Harry added inwardly.

"Shit..." Harry said. "How much time have I missed? Actually, now that I'm here; why is my right hand missing? Who did all these stitches?"

"My dad." Brun said, getting up and taking a step back.

Wait, what?

"Mr. Daniel did the best he could, even though he acted in a more... brute way." Vela seconded.

"He was very brute." Madeline added, a wide smile in her face. "Like, specially when he broke your right hand. It snapped out so fast! It was like a farmer ripping the head out of a chicken, so—"

"Okay, okay!" Harry closed his eyes and shook his head. "I don't need to know it all."

Daniel did this? The viking? The one who tried to kill me when we literally first met? It was a shock, not only to him, but for the majority of the group, except for Brun. First he had saved Harry from the grasp of Dracno with his yelling, and then took care of the young thief, even as he didn't respect him. As Harry thought more about this, the stranger Daniel became.

"He worked fast." Brun said. "And took care of all the major issues. I took care of the rest as he took down the cultists as the loop restarted, seeing as it would be a better use of his time. He may not seem like it, but he's a good man. He's the same way as when he left me."

"...He didn't leave you, Brun." Harry said, empathetically. "The world just took him away."

"I guess that's a perception." Brun said with a weak smile. "Either way, could you please give us the room, Madeline? Or, in this case, the church?"

The woman almost argued, but, like she'd remembered something, simply got up and gave Harry a smile. "Of course. Get better, Harry. You were really brave."

Thump! Thump! Harry's heart seemed to beat faster for some reason as it then slowed down when she left the building. Soon, there was only Vela, Brun, and Harry on the church.

“What happened?” He asked as soon as she left. “...Did anyone else die? Did I do something with the attack? Is it something about the loop?”

He assumed the worst, even if it didn’t make much sense.

“N-No, Mr. Locksmith...” Vela said, “It’s about something else...”

They looked at each other. Brun scratched her arms while Vela looked at the empty ambo for a moment. It certainly was something they weren’t keen on talking about. The embarrassed Brun then composed herself and frowned.

“We don’t know where your journal is.” Vela cringed upon hearing that.

What do you mean? It's with me... Harry thought as he touched his body and looked around. No. No, it can't be. Why? How? How did I miss it? Was it during the fight or before it?

“That doesn’t make sense.” Harry contested the affirmation. “If the loop restarted, and it was with me from the beginning, it should have appeared now.”

“Yes, but...” Brun then pulled a C-Coin from her pocket. “If the person really wanted to, they could place the journal in their body and spend a C-Coin. The journal would then be assigned to that person, and before the loop restarted, someone probably did that and—”

“You can do that!?” He shouted for the first time, holding back another cough. “What the fuck!... Sigh. That was probably while we were all fighting Dracno. But why? it isn't special or anything.”

And then the young thief remembered a strange happening during his battle with Dracno in the last loop. Although the logs and the Armor Emblem could pose dozens of issues on their own, none of them had the capacity to blind him. At the moment, Harry understood it as a failed attempt from Vela to once again melt the Armor Emblem from Dracno. That conjecture was losing its forces by the minute the more Harry thought about it. Especially when he focused on a missing member.

“...Only our group knew where it was.” Harry concluded in a serious and severe tone.

"Before we point fingers," Vela raised his hands and took a step back, looking around. "Know that dozens of cultists and possibly other members with Emblems lived here for who knows how many years. See it this way, Mr. Locksmith: some cultists saw your journal and, in the middle of the brawl, took it with them. Simple and effective. If it were valuable, they'd gain information, but if it weren't, they'd piss you off. In their perspectives, this was a worthy risk. I understand if you distrust any of us, but please understand the other possibility."

"...Where's Prism?" Harry asked for the displeasure of Vela. "She's the one who has an Emblem that can turn her fucking invisible. Besides that, I was blinded for some unknown reason while fighting Dracno. I bet you a year of earnings that she was the one who robbed me. And then took off, for some reason."

"That is the same conclusion I reached." Brun replied, finally. "It is not optimal to believe in treachery in a group, but all the evidence points to Prism being a robber."

After a moment, Harry chuckled. "Now I understand why people hated me so much."

"Sorry?" She said.

"Nothing... Nothing." Harry replied as he slowly got up, with Vela promptly helping him as soon as the Locksmith threatened to get up. "There's nothing that can be done now, is there? Where's everyone, by the way?"

"...A type of mutual collaboration between members involving—"

"They're having a party." Brun cut Vela off with a grin. "Nothing too serious, just having a few watered beers and uncooked steaks. For morale, of course."

"...For morale." Harry repeated.

"For morale." She concluded. "Of course, if you're feeling well, you could help in the... morale."

"...Of course." He said.

"Either way, I need to check on the hatch to the next floor, and how much time left do we have until the next loop. If you two excuse me..."

And as she was leaving, Harry looked at Vela, who gave him a reassuring smile.

"You're going to the party?" Harry asked. "I mean, should you be going to the party? Seeing as you're a monk and all..."

"I tend to the candles." He said as Harry stabilized himself and grabbed his candelabrum. "And they seem to... Yes. Oh, yes. Right. They tend to like it very much."

"...That's a joke, right?" Harry asked. "You talking to candles and stuff."

"Candles?" Vela chuckled. "No, no... That is a joke. I actually talk to the fire."

"Oh, I see." Harry said, but added inwardly. That's going to be a long night. "So, would you care to join me as I pretend to be frail all the time?"

"Oh, come on Mr. Locksmith! You don't need to pretend; we all know you're true nature."

"Are you perhaps... insulting me?" Harry asked with a smile as they both turned to the entrance.

"I would never." Vela paused and then added. "It's just that, sometimes, the flames talk through me."

"Oh, for fuck's sake..." He gave a slight bump in the monk's shoulder. "Shut up, man..."

In the distance, a few pews and chairs circled around the mother flame.
