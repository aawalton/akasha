Rayne moved through the army camp, knowing that in just a few hours, everyone would know about his class advancement.

Henry and the clerks inside the command tent would make sure of it. News like that never stayed contained in an army camp, and certainly not when it involved something like breaking past the first wall.

Rayne hadn’t told anyone about it till now. Not Commander Evans nor his own party.

Part of it had been because he was still trying to come to terms with everything around him, and he had wanted to tell them once he had made a decision.

But he didn't have the proper understanding of the class options given to him.

The books he had read back in Fort Algar had barely scratched the surface. They spoke of classes in passing, but none of it had gone into the kind of detail he needed right now.

Rayne had been given his first class before he had transmigrated, and even his memories lacked any details other than some stuff about the common classes.

That was why he had decided to speak with Captain Edran. Of course in private after sending the clerks including Henry out.

Talking to someone who had already gone through it had helped more than he had expected. The captain hadn’t given him all the answers, but he had given him enough to avoid making a blind decision.

If Casper had been around, Rayne would have gone to her instead. But they had barely seen each other since he had woken up, and he didn't want to wait anymore.

So he asked the next person he could, and in the end, he didn’t think it was a bad one.

If anything, the talks about his advancement would only improve his standing within the army. Breaking past the first wall wasn’t something common, and people paid attention to it. Commander Evans would see it. Captain Edran already had. Others would follow.

In the eyes of many, it meant that the gods were favoring him despite his background, and in a world where magic existed as a tangible force, it was a good perception to have, especially when he would soon be in front of the truthsayers.

Rayne understood that much.

With those thoughts settling into place, he continued walking through the camp, passing by soldiers who paid him little attention.

It didn’t take long before he reached his tent.

There was nothing else left for him to do today, so it was the perfect day to finally break past the level 30 mark.

Rayne stepped inside, letting the flap fall closed behind him as his expression grew more focused, then he immediately sat down.

Rayne took a slow breath, steadying himself before focusing inward.

The notification had been sitting at the back of his mind for days now, lingering there like a quiet weight that refused to go away. Now, it was time to put an end to it.

With a thought, he pulled it forward, and a faint translucent screen formed before him.

Class upgrade options available. Please find a suitable place to undergo your advancement.

Just below it, three options were listed.

Death Knight (Uncommon).

Arcane Duelist (Uncommon).

War Weaver (Rare).

Rayne’s gaze lingered on them, his expression calm, though his thoughts had already begun to move.

He had gone over these options every day since the notification had appeared. At first, he had expected more—some form of explanation, a description, anything that could guide his decision.

There had been nothing.

The system had simply presented him with the choices and left the rest to him.

Which meant he had to rely on his own judgment. Or whatever information he could gather.

Fortunately, speaking with Captain Edran had helped.

The captain had recognized two of the classes—the first two—and had been able to tell him enough to at least understand what they offered.

Rayne’s focus shifted to the first option.

[Death Knight].

Even without explanation, the name itself told him enough. But what Edran had told him had helped him evaluate it better.

Basically, it was a class centered around death mana and swordsmanship, but more than that, it leaned heavily toward destructive output. According to the captain, there were [Death Knights] capable of releasing attacks powerful enough to bring down entire walls in a single strike.

But it came with its own cost. The class was heavily mana-dependent. Every mana strike and class skill would demand more from him than what he was used to.

His attacks would become stronger, far stronger, but sustaining them would be another matter entirely. And more importantly, it wasn’t truly a swordsman’s class.

Captain Edran had made that clear. A [Death Knight] fought more like a mage than a warrior, using the blade as a medium. It would push him toward a more ranged style of combat, relying on bursts of destructive mana rather than controlled, close-range fighting.

Rayne wasn’t sure how he felt about that, and his gaze shifted to the second option.

[Arcane Duelist].

This was the one Edran had advised him against.

It wasn’t a weak class. In fact, it was highly sought after in certain parts of the world, especially the Rakasham Empire. The captain had mentioned duelists valued it greatly, especially those who fought in arenas.

And for that, it was exceptional.

It enhanced the user’s stats and abilities during duels, sharpening their focus, increasing their speed, their reactions, and their overall effectiveness when facing a single opponent. But that was also its limitation.

Outside of duels, it was an average advancement class to have. Not weak, but not exceptional either.

It didn’t offer the kind of adaptability Rayne needed, especially in battlefields where threats didn’t come one at a time. It was a class designed for a very specific purpose, and while it would make him deadly in that scenario, it wouldn’t help much elsewhere.

Rayne let out a quiet breath, his eyes lingering on the two options for a moment longer before slowly shifting to the third.

[War Weaver].

The one the captain hadn’t recognized, and also the one that gave him really little information by name.

The only thing the name really told him was that it had something to do with war.

The second part of it—weaver—made him pause a little longer. It sounded closer to a mage class rather than direct combat. But even that wasn’t certain. [War Weaver] could mean a lot of things, and none of them were clear enough for him to make a proper decision.

That uncertainty was what bothered him the most. The fact that it was a rare class only made it more complicated.

Captain Edran had explained that much to him. Rare classes weren’t focused in just one direction like most common or uncommon ones. Instead, they combined different aspects, often pulling from multiple paths and turning them into something more flexible.

Hence, only one in a hundred thousand class holders received one from the gods. Captain Edran had even looked at him with envy for a few seconds when he had told him the class grade.

Rayne’s gaze remained fixed on the option as his thoughts continued to move.

For him, stats didn’t matter as much.

He could always gain more by stealing them. All three classes would surely give him Arcane as he progressed, and that alone was already enough for him to keep growing stronger over time.

What mattered more were the skills.

[Death Knight] would give him destructive mana-based abilities that could end fights quickly if used properly. [Arcane Duelist], on the other hand, would refine his body, and give him skills based on mana reinforcement. That much he had learnt from the captain.

Both were clear, but [War Weaver] wasn't. Rayne frowned slightly. What would it even give him?

Would it grant him spells? Something more tactical? Would it strengthen him only in large-scale battles, or was it something completely different that he couldn’t even begin to guess?

There was no way to tell, and that made it feel like a gamble.

It could be the best option out of the three, something rare enough to completely change his path for the better. Or it could be the worst, something too situational, too complicated, or simply not suited for him at all.

There was also the possibility that it would fall somewhere in between. Not bad, not exceptional, just another class that worked well enough.

But Rayne doubted that.

Rare classes didn’t tend to be average which meant whatever [War Weaver] was, it wouldn’t be simple.

Rayne exhaled quietly, lifting a hand to scratch the side of his head as he stared at the three options again.

He didn’t know what to choose. A few weeks ago, the answer would have been simple.

He would have picked Death Knight without hesitation. The raw power alone would have been enough to make the decision for him. Even with the higher mana cost, it would have made him far stronger than he was now.

And at the time, that would have been all that mattered, but his mindset had changed after the dungeon.

He wasn't just fighting alone. He had a party to take care of, and his quest already pushed him towards handling more people under him, and focusing only on his own power might not be the best way to survive in the army.

If anything, it could corner him.

It would push him into a specific style, one that relied heavily on his own output. And while he could adapt with his skill and stat stealing, that wasn’t something he could depend on consistently. Too much of it came down to chance.

Because of that, he didn’t want [Death Knight] anymore. [Arcane Duelist[ was already out of the question.

Captain Edran had advised against it, and Rayne understood why. He wasn’t a duelist, and he wouldn't be anytime soon.

Which only left one option.

Rayne’s gaze settled once more on the final name. A part of him wanted to just choose it and be done with it.

The thought had been sitting at the edge of his mind for a while now, growing stronger the longer he stared at the option. But another part of him held back, reminding him that this wasn’t a small decision he could rush through.

This would shape how he fought from here on. Still, was he really rushing?

He had already gone through the other options more times than he could count. He knew what they offered, what they lacked, and why they didn’t fit him. [War Weaver] might have been unknown, but it was also the rarest among them.

And more importantly, it was clearly a combat-oriented class.

The more he focused on it, the more it pulled at him. There was a quiet certainty building inside him, something he couldn’t quite explain but couldn’t ignore either. It felt right.

Yet the hesitation remained, holding him back just enough to keep him thinking in circles until, eventually, he realized he was overdoing it.

He might not know everything about the class, but it was still a rare combat class. That should have been enough to make his decision.

Rayne let out a quiet sigh, his shoulders relaxing slightly as he made up his mind.

“Here goes nothing,” he muttered.

Then, without giving himself another chance to hesitate, he selected it. The moment he did, the panel in front of him faded, replaced almost instantly by a notification.

Congratulations for selecting the class [War Weaver]. You have advanced your class past the first wall and reached Level 31.

Rayne smiled seeing that, and the next second, another series of notifications appeared.

War Weaver (Rare)

A lot of people fight in wars, but not everyone can weave through them like experts whose fate had been written to rule the battlefields since the day they were born. Only [War Weavers] can.

The class blends mana, battlefield awareness, and combat skills into a unified fighting style. [War Weavers] do not simply fight battles, they shape them. They can move through any battlefield, and emerge victorious.

Gives balanced growth across Vitality and Arcane with one stat point each every level. Also grants the hidden stat Charisma with one stat point in it every three levels.

You have gained the following class skills due to [War Weaver].

War Pulse (Rare).

Weapon Mastery (Uncommon)

Weaver’s Protection (Uncommon)

Rayne’s eyes moved quickly across the notifications, and as the meaning settled in, a wave of relief washed over him.

It was combat-focused. Not just that, it was exactly the kind of class he needed. Leadership focused, but clearly also prioritising mana and combat equally.

Though, he didn't understand what this new stat was? He never even knew there were hidden stats, but before he could pull his status up, and look further—

A sudden jolt ran through his body.

Rayne stiffened slightly, blinking as the sensation passed, only for another one to follow immediately after, and just then, a new notification flashed into his mind.

Body undergoing adjustment to accommodate new class. Please remain patient.

He frowned faintly, confusion rising just as another jolt hit him, stronger this time.

“What—”

The word barely left his lips before the sensation twisted sharply, as if something inside him had been pulled tight and then wrenched out of place.

Pain surged through him, not at a single point, but everywhere. His muscles tensed violently, his breath catching as the feeling spread, digging deep into his bones, his nerves, his very core.

It felt as if his entire body was being reshaped from within, every part of him forced to adjust at once.

Rayne’s balance gave out in seconds. He dropped to the ground, his body hitting the floor of the tent as another wave of pain crashed over him, stronger than the last.

He couldn’t speak, couldn’t move. All he could do was endure as the sensation overwhelmed everything else, tearing through his body and mind, leaving nothing behind but raw, blinding agony.

And then, as he moved his hand towards the potions at his waist, another jolt hit him, and this time, everything went dark.
