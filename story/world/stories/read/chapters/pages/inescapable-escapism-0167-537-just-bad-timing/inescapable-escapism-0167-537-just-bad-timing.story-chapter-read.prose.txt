I’m pretty sure there was a smile on my face before I even woke up. I didn’t really become aware of it until I was semiconscious, and my cheeks already hurt. The grin was still plastered on my face, though, and despite the pain, I couldn’t stop.

Not that I wanted to. I didn’t. I was too happy, and that was a strange change for me. I liked it, though. I wasn’t used to feeling so good first thing in the morning, or ever really, and I wanted to feel that way forever. It seemed impossible. I knew that it was, and part of me didn’t really trust the emotion, but I longed for it nonetheless.

Also, I felt weirdly well-rested, which was unusual for me. Generally, I was still kind of tired when I woke up in the morning. It wasn’t the same exhaustion that I tended to feel later on in the day, but a deep fog of tiredness that seemed to penetrate deep into my very bones and was impossible to shake.

It had been a good night. I’d spent the entire time at the Academy, and every moment had been fun. I’d hung out with Seth after class, gone to bed and woken up before having an entire day there. Classes had been good. I felt like I’d learned a lot, and Seth had told me all about his attempt at beating the current-generating machine over lunch. Like me and everyone else, he hadn’t succeeded, but he hadn’t drowned either, so that was good.

Getting to spend so much time with him had been great. I really enjoyed it, and each moment I spent there just made me feel more and more comfortable around him. I already felt pretty comfortable with him, but it was getting easier to just talk to him without feeling anywhere near as much anxiety as I normally did when speaking to people. Seth just made me feel at ease. I wasn’t sure how he managed to do it, but he did.

And spending a whole day with him meant that when I went to Duncan’s house later, I wouldn’t have to feel too bad about not being at the Academy. I’d spent good quality time with Seth, and so I could spend time with Duncan.

I’d still feel a bit guilty about it, though. I knew that I would. It was hard not to, but I was pretty sure it would be easier. Perhaps I wouldn’t be bothered by the feeling that I should check in on the Academy or worries that Seth was concerned because I was acting differently. I could just… focus on whatever Duncan and I were doing.

Nervousness bubbled in my stomach, and I chewed on my bottom lip. What would we be doing when I got to his? I’d already turned the question over and over in my mind, but I’d yet to come up with enough of an answer. I wanted to know for sure, but it would be impossible to do that until I was there.

I was excited, though. I was looking forward to seeing Duncan again, to hanging out with him and hopefully kissing him again. Maybe we’d even kiss a few times. That would be nice. More than nice, actually. I’d like that a lot.

My mind flitted towards my wardrobe. What should I wear, I mused as I rolled over, pulling my duvet in tighter. Obviously, I wanted to look nice, but I wasn’t sure whether I could get away with wearing a dress again. Mom hadn’t questioned it yesterday, but I wasn’t sure whether I’d get lucky twice in a row.

She would probably be awake when I went out, I realised. From what I could tell, she’d spent all of yesterday unconscious, and I doubted that would happen again. That meant I needed to wear something that matched whatever lie I was going to tell her. Otherwise, she’d get suspicious.

I hadn’t worked out what I was going to say to her yet, though. I knew I couldn’t use the gym again. Dad had mentioned that he’d picked me up from there when we got home the day before, and there was no way I could pretend to spend two full days back to back there. Mom would never buy that.

Phoebe did suggest I pretend to spend the day at hers, and that could work. It might not be that convincing. Normally, if I went to Phoebe’s during the holidays, I’d spend the night, and I couldn’t exactly do that at Duncan’s. I’d done it before, obviously, but it felt different now.

My face began to heat up at the thought of that, and I felt my stomach tighten. Part of me kind of did want to sleep over at his, but it was too much. Even though I knew nothing could happen, maybe we’d just spend the night gaming and talking like we had before, it still scared me. It just felt too fast.

But I could say Phoebe had plans later, I considered. Maybe if Mom asked, I could pretend she had family over or something, so I couldn’t stay at hers. It probably wouldn’t even occur to Mom to ask about that, though. I was pretty sure I was just overthinking it.

That might work, though. It was a bit risky. I was worried about somehow being caught out, but it felt unlikely. Phoebe had said her mom was away on business, so there was no chance of my mom running into her, and I doubted it would come up in the future at all. Mom and Phoebe’s mom didn’t really talk much. Phoebe’s mom tried, but my mom didn’t.

She didn’t like Phoebe’s mom. She didn’t approve of her, and I wasn’t sure why. Mom had given me her reasons before, but none really made sense to me, and I tended to zone out whenever she started complaining about her.

The smile returned to my face as I grabbed my phone and unlocked it, blinking as the bright screen blinded me. My vision cleared quickly, and I tapped on Phoebe’s messages, deleting the ones where she suggested the lie in case Mom started snooping.

I hesitated before clicking on Duncan’s name. I had to get rid of the texts from him too. Anything that mentioned going to his house had to go. I couldn’t leave any trace of it for Mom to find. It would ruin the whole plan.

A sigh slipped from my lips as I read through his messages before sadly deleting them. I didn’t want to do it. It was sweet to see how excited he seemed to see me again. I’d already read the messages multiple times, needing to convince myself that he’d actually said those things, so I was pretty sure I’d be able to remember them, but I still wanted to keep them even though I knew I couldn’t.

Dropping my phone on the duvet, I yawned widely before starting to sit up. I reached my arms up above my head, savouring the feeling of stretching my back out, before freezing suddenly. My eyes widened, and I grabbed my phone again.

There was more I needed to get rid of.

I’d completely forgotten about it, but I’d taken screenshots of a few of the messages Duncan had sent me to send to Phoebe. I needed her help knowing how to respond to some of them, and I just wanted to see her lose her mind about a couple of his other texts. She had, obviously. She was so excited for us.

Quickly, I deleted the pictures before finding the recently deleted folder and emptying that too. I wasn’t sure whether Mom would know to look there, I thought as I found Phoebe’s texts again, but I wasn’t willing to risk it.

Once I was satisfied that the messages between Phoebe and me had been cleared of any mention of Duncan, I locked my phone again and threw my duvet back. The carpet felt soft beneath my feet as I stood, and I found myself humming as I walked towards the door, but I fell silent as I threw it open.

“Good morning,” my mom called as I stepped out into the hall.

Immediately, my eyes snapped towards her, and I froze. She had just reached the top of the stairs, a basket of laundry in her hand, and I had no idea how long she’d been there. I wasn’t sure whether she just happened to reach the landing as I emerged from my room, or whether she’d been waiting there for a while.

That seemed ridiculous, though. It would have been so weird for Mom to have done that, but I was pretty sure she’d done it before. She’d used the excuse of putting laundry away then too, and that was part of why I didn’t believe her. That wasn’t something she really did. Dad was the one who did all of the washing.

“Morning,” I replied, trying to keep the caution out of my voice.

“You’re finally awake,” she continued. “I thought you were going to sleep away the entire day!”

Her tone was light, but there was a judgemental undercurrent to her words, and I couldn’t help but glance at my phone. I knew she hated me checking it when she was talking to me, despite her doing that all the time, but I needed to know what time it was.

“It’s only just ten?”

“Exactly!”

It wasn’t exactly late. If anything, it was still pretty early, but Mom made it sound like most of the day was already gone. I still had the majority of the day to do stuff if I wanted to, and she couldn’t judge me. Sometimes, she didn’t get up until the early afternoon.

I didn’t say any of that to her, though. There was no reason to point it out because I knew it would just make Mom mad. It seemed like she wanted me to say something, though. The silence stretched out between us, and she continued to look at me expectantly.

“Well… I’m just going to the bathroom,” I said eventually.

Mom sighed, moving the basket to her other hip.

“Fine, but hurry up,” she told me. “I don’t want to be out all day.”

Confusion crashed into me, and I stared at my mom blankly, but she just stared back. It seemed as though she had no idea why I was so baffled.

“What?”

The word slipped out before I could stop it, and immediately, my mom scoffed.

“What do you mean ‘what’?” she asked, doing an intentionally poor imitation of my voice.

Irritation rose within me, but I forced it down as I gripped my phone tighter, trying not to let myself get worked up by her. She enjoyed it when she knew she’d gotten under my skin, and I wouldn’t give her that satisfaction.

“Why are you going out?” I replied, carefully rephrasing my question.

Mom blinked at me slowly before letting out a laugh.

“We’re going shopping,” she explained. “You need things for next year, don’t you?”

My heart started to sink.

“I thought we were going next week?”

I shouldn’t have said that. I knew that I should have just agreed with her and not asked any questions, but I couldn’t help it. I was really looking forward to seeing Duncan, and I didn’t want to miss out on seeing him.

Annoyance flashed across Mom’s face, and I had to hold back a wince. I’d made a mistake. I already knew I had, but her reaction made that even more obvious.

“No,” she snapped. “I told you yesterday that we’re going today. God, you never listen to me, do you? You’re almost as bad as your father. He never listens to me either.”

The urge to argue with her rose within me. I was certain she had not told me yesterday. We had barely spoken all day. She’d been passed out when I’d gone out, and we only saw each other for a couple of minutes when I got back from Duncan’s, but she didn’t mention it then. Even though I hadn’t been in my world in the evening, I was sure she hadn’t come to see me, so there was no way she could have told me that we were going shopping today.

“I have plans.”

As soon as the words left my lips, I wished that I could take them back. Mom’s annoyed expression turned to anger for just a moment before becoming cold. She pressed her lips together, and I saw her jaw clench.

“Oh really?” she asked, her tone challenging. “What’s more important than having a uniform to wear to school next year? Or have you decided you don’t need it this year? You’re dropping out? Well, you could have told me that sooner!”

The edge of my phone case cut into my hand as I gripped it even tighter. Part of me was so tempted to agree with her, to say I didn’t actually need to go shopping. I hadn’t grown too much in the last year, and I could have probably gotten away with wearing what I already had for a bit longer. Then, I could have just asked Dad to take me shopping, and I knew he’d do it.

But it wasn’t worth it. As much as I wanted to see Duncan, I knew the arguments and fury from my mom would be too horrible to deal with. It wouldn’t just affect me. If it had, I might have been able to take it, but it affected my dad too, and I didn’t want to put him through that.

I had to give in, I realised as disappointment soared within me. I didn’t want to, but it was the best option. It was the only one, really.

“Nothing,” I muttered. “I was just going to see Phoebe, but I need to get stuff for next year, so I can see her another day. Sorry.”

A smug smile appeared on Mom’s face, and I looked away quickly. I didn’t want to see it. It only made the sting of my disappointment more painful, and it already hurt enough.

“That’s what I thought,” Mom said, not managing to keep the triumph out of her voice. I don’t think she even tried. “Now, hurry up and get ready. I wanted to go out at nine.”

I didn’t bother replying to that. There was nothing I wanted to say. I’d been so happy minutes ago, but that joy had shrivelled up like a dying flower, and I was miserable. I couldn’t help but drag my feet along the floor as I made my way towards the bathroom and locked the door behind me.

She was already in a horrible mood, I thought as I turned the tap on so Mom wouldn’t tell me off for wasting time in the bathroom and not getting ready if she pressed an ear to the door. We hadn’t even left yet, and I knew that did not bode well for how things were going to go when we were shopping. She would probably be even worse than she usually was, and I was dreading it.

Leaning my hip against the sink, I took a deep breath before lifting my phone and staring at it for a moment. I had to text Duncan and tell him, but I didn’t want to. I was still desperately clinging to the hope I’d be able to think of a way to fix things with Mom or get out of going shopping with her, but there was nothing I could do.

Hey, sorry, I typed before pausing. It was hard to keep going, but I forced myself to continue. Turns out I can’t do today. Mom’s decided we’re going shopping for school stuff. Sorry. Are you free tomorrow?

Tears prickled behind my eyes. I was so disappointed, but part of me was also worried about how Duncan would reply. I knew him well. It was unlikely he’d get annoyed at me, but the thought still pulled at me.

Maybe he’d think it was intentional, a small voice in the back of my head whispered. Perhaps he might suspect I just didn’t want to see him or that I was cancelling on him at the last minute for no reason. I was just flaky, and he wouldn’t like someone like that. He wouldn’t want to be with a person who messed him around like that.

A tear slipped down my cheek, and I squeezed my eyes shut to hold back the rest. I couldn’t let another fall. If I did, more would follow, and I needed to leave the bathroom at some point and face Mom again. I didn’t want her to know I’d been crying. It would just make her more smug.

My phone vibrated, and my eyes snapped open again. Reluctance and anxiety spiked within me, but I forced myself to read the message.

Oh no, that sucks. No worries though. I have training tomorrow, and I’m meant to be going to the pub with the team after, but I can always go next time. I should be done with training around 2?

Guilt speared my heart. I didn’t want Duncan to cancel on his friends just to see me. That made me feel even worse, and I hesitated, trying to work out how to phrase that in a way that didn’t make it sound like I wasn’t looking forward to seeing him.

Nah, don’t be silly! I wrote, trying to sound less miserable than I felt. Can you do Thursday?

I read the message a couple of times to make sure it sounded okay before sending it. It wasn’t perfect, and I kind of wanted to apologise again, but I resisted the urge. Sometimes people told me I apologised too much, so I tried to stop. Phoebe did it, and Duncan had a couple of times too. They weren’t mean about it, obviously. They just said there was no need, but that always made me uncomfortable.

That works! Duncan’s text read.

A small smile pulled at my lips, but my lingering sadness chased it away before long.

Great, what time do you want to do?

Once more, Duncan replied almost immediately.

Mmmm wanna come over around midday?

That sounds good!

Awesome! See you then :)

Can’t wait, I replied before deleting the messages, locking my phone and using the toilet.

He didn’t seem too annoyed that I couldn’t go to his like I’d planned, and that was good. Logically, I knew he wouldn’t be, but his response helped. And we’d rescheduled. It wasn’t going to be too long until I saw him again, which made me feel a bit better, but disappointment still clung to me as I washed my hands and dried them quickly before unlocking my phone again.

I needed to let Phoebe know what had happened. Otherwise, she’d text me later, expecting me to be with Duncan, and I’d have to explain then. It would be harder with Mom there. She always judged me when I was on my phone, and if I was texting too much, she’d demand I hand it over for her to read.

Change of plans. Mom’s decided we’re going shopping today, so I’m seeing Duncan on Thursday instead, I wrote to Phoebe.

She was meant to be out with her cousins for lunch, so I didn’t expect her to reply straight away, but my phone buzzed before I even had time to get the cap off the toothpaste.

Oh boooooo! That’s so annoying. Do you think your mom knew you were going to go to Duncan’s or is it just bad timing? Phoebe asked.

Panic fluttered in my heart. I hadn’t considered that, but I was pretty sure she didn’t know. The only way she would have found out was if she’d followed me to Duncan’s house yesterday, which I didn’t think she had, or if she’d seen my texts. I’d been careful to delete any potentially incriminating messages from my phone recently, so I doubted she’d read anything I didn’t want her to, and I wasn’t sure when she would have even had a chance to do it. She could have snuck into my room whilst I was asleep, but I’d got rid of the messages before then.

I think it’s just bad timing, I replied, typing with one hand as I brushed my teeth with the other.

Okay, phew. Well, good luck. I hope it isn’t too bad this time?

Oh, it will be, but thanks, I typed. What are you doing today?

Mmmm not sure. Marie’s been saying we should go water skiing for ages, and I don’t want to go because it does not sound fun at all, but I think she’s booked it anyway, sooo…. That should be awful.

I snorted softly before spitting my toothpaste out and taking a swig of mouthwash.

Ooof. Well… have fun? I wrote.

I won’t, Phoebe replied, adding a heart emoji to the message.

You got this!
