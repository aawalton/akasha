Dear Diary,

Y'know, there are definitely some disadvantages to the calendar in use here and now.

Okay, there's definitely one huge disadvantage, which is that nobody sat down and taught me what the months are, what order they come in, any of that. It's one of those 'everybody knows', 'common sense' things. Which I guess isn't the core of the disadvantage. That would be that while I can kinda keep track of what day of the week it is most of the time, and apparently I'm not half bad at figuring out when the Equinoxes and Solstices are, what with my connection to the sun, I don't fuckin' know when anything else is.

Seriously, I kinda remember Conrad's High Holy Day, but I think that's because there's a Mana shift big enough that even I can feel it if I pay attention. I know Saffron's birthday because I can multiply by three hundred and sixty-five; never thought I'd be able to do that math in my head, but here we are. I can remember Siobhan's birthday because it's literally New Year's Day, which is like, the Spring Equinox by another name. I guess Tallulah and Marie aren't really big on birthdays, what with any given one not meaning all that much after the first thousand. Not to mention them predating the modern concept of calendars.

I get that people have always had a basic idea of the passing of seasons, but if you go far enough back, I don't think they really assigned a particular number of days to each season; Summer lasted from when the Spring rains stopped until the leaves began to Fall.

So yeah, my point here is that I might have actually been too hard on myself when it came to missing birthdays. Not that I shouldn't try or any shit like that, and I'm still gonna feel some kinda way if I miss one entirely, but I need to acknowledge that I'm doing that shit on Hard Mode, because not only am I tryna keep track of a calendar without a phone or computer or any of the shit I used to use to keep track of that shit back in the day, there aren't really printed calendars here and now, not that I've seen.

Might have something to do with their printing presses being shit I'd expect to see in a museum about the Renaissance, and prior to the next generation of kids, most of the people couldn't read them anyhow.

At any rate, after Saffron and I did some practice diplomacy on the conference room table, we spent the rest of the day talking with Swanson and Marie. Okay, Saffron chatted with Swanson about the state of supplies aboard the Black Dragon, because her Cold Iron plating made it pretty damn inefficient to resupply her via Scrying Portal. Thing is, the Tentacles had no such issues, and with the number holding station nearby, if things got tight all they needed to do was bring a couple of them over and route supplies through them.

What with his focus on logistics as most of strategy, the Marshall must have creamed his shorts when he found out about Scrying Portals and how they could keep our ships supplied. Not to mention how an army in the field could do the same. Not that we really needed an army in the field at this point, what with everyone in Atlantis being part of the Alliance, a close friend, or smart enough to pretend to be friendly.

Marie was using the deep fat fryers to tinker with her fried stuff recipes. I'm not sure exactly how she did it without panko bread crumbs, but when the Captains returned for dinner I realized she'd managed some really good tempura. Weirdest thing had to be realizing as I ate the tempura shrimp, scallops, and veggies that tempura didn't use panko or any other kind of bread crumbs. Weirdest thing, having Skills that let me figure out how something was made just by eating it. I mean, I'm pretty sure there's some fancy technique or some shit like that, but the crunch profile fit basic light batter dipping rather than bread crumbs or flour dredge.

The Captains did return for dinner, and if they found it weird that Marie served all six of us, at least until I pulled her into my lap and fed her tempura, they didn't say anything. They did compliment her cooking, which was both polite and just facts, and I appreciated both.

When we finished eating, Saffron pulled Marie and I to our feet, then nodded to the Captains. "You're welcome to join Admiral Swanson for dessert, but Tabitha has somewhere to be this evening." At their questioning looks, she smiled and said, "a religious ceremony. We'll see you in the morning."
