“Before we get started, I figured we could do a quick round of introductions. Maybe explain what each kingdom represented here is willing to bring to the table,” Donvath said, scanning the room as he spoke. “I’ll go first. You all know me already as both Donvath the High Prince as well as by my moniker of The Immortal. The Noala Kingdom is prepared to devote my highly-trained truthseekers, and a handful of our more-powerful nobles if necessary to the cause.”

“I’ve heard of your truthseekers,” Groln admitted, scratching at his chin with a claw as he gave Donvath a curious look. “They will be interesting to see in action. Regardless, I am Groln, a royal slayer for the Hojon Empire. Though most know me as Hellspawn. The Hojon Empire is willing to lend the assistance of one of its greater powers, myself, as well as that of my personal team of royal slayers. We are an elite fighting force capable of putting down just about anything.”

“I’m Lanra, a wizard from the Eight Crests,” Lanra said, somehow managing to ignore the soft scoff that came from the Lambit Republic representative as he glared at her with his arms crossed. “The Eight Crests decided offering up its skilled team of realm wanderers was a smart move, as there was no telling if any of the liches were hiding in another realm. Though rest assured, we are more than capable of fighting here on the material realm if the need arises.”

“I’m Amanda, also known as Falling Sun,” Amanda said, her entire body seeming to glow for a moment before she dimmed back to normal. “Nunait is offering up as many members of our Order as are needed. We will put our entire fighting force on the line to wipe out the Council of Death, we just need to know when and where to go.”

“Hounth. Representing the Lambit Republic,” Hounth said, keeping his arms crossed as his lips formed a thin line. “I will admit I was against the idea of joining the coalition in the first place, but I was outvoted, and I was selected to come here. We are offering a portion of our military to the cause, as well as a couple of mercenary groups.”

“Your military?” Lanra repeated, scowling at Hounth from across the table. “What good does that do us? I don’t suppose you are planning to pay for a fleet of pegasi to transport your common soldiers halfway across the continent to where they are needed, are you?”

“Take it up with the rest of the representatives,” Hounth said, leaning back in his chair as he frowned. “I already made clear I didn’t want to come at all.”

“We appreciate the show of support, at the very least,” Donvath said, being strangely diplomatic for a change before he raised an eyebrow toward Asher, who blinked and pointed at himself.

“Oh, me too? Alright. I’m Asher, also known as The Reaper. I’ve got an elite team, and the five of us have already taken down four of the fourteen liches, as well as turned one of them to our side. I think that speaks for itself as to just how capable we are.”

“I wanted to ask about that,” Groln admitted, giving him a curious look. “You really have a lich working with you? Donvath mentioned it in his message, but it is still hard to believe.”

“I’m with Groln,” Amanda said, a deep frown on her face as she turned toward Asher. “There is no such thing as a good undead. They need to be wiped out, all of them.”

“No need to worry about that, promising to lay Kroult to rest was the only way I could get him to help us,” Asher admitted, getting surprised looks from around the table. “I know Donvath told you about his situation, but spending six hundred years sealed away underground… Kroult just wishes to pass on.”

“I don’t like it… but if what you say is true, we will turn a blind eye for the time being,” Amanda decided, looking like she was undergoing quite the internal struggle.

“I hope you’re not expecting to receive the bounty for this Kroult you speak of before actually killing him,” Hounth said coldly.

“Not at all,” Asher said, shooting Donvath a curious look. “What did you end up deciding on for the bounty anyway?”

“Ten million shards per lich,” Donvath said, grinning at the look of utter shock on Asher’s face. “I’ll admit the Hojon Empire is floating most of the cost, but the rest of us are chipping in as well. A few other countries who are not willing to openly join the coalition have anonymously donated to the bounty pool as well, showing their support for what we are trying to do. I’ll give you the forty million shards you’re owed once we’re done here. Try not to pass out before then.”

It took Asher a moment to realize he’d stopped breathing. Forty million shards was an astronomical number that he could barely wrap his head around. Hell, that was enough to evolve all of his and Samantha’s skills to the third tier. Even after splitting it fairly with Moxy and Rulfar, that was an obscene amount of wealth.

“Admittedly, there is a reason why my Queen has not only agreed to the idea, but contributed so heavily to the bounty pool,” Groln said, drawing everyone’s attention as he raised a claw. “…One of the members of the Council resides within the Hojon Empire.”

Groln’s declaration got the appropriate number of gasps from around the table, including from Asher as well. Not a single one of the journals Zygrul had left them had included any mention of the Hojon Empire. Which meant the lich Groln was talking about had to be one of the five liches that Zygrul didn’t know the location of.

“That’s great news!” Donvath said, coughing into his hand as Groln frowned at him. “I mean, not about a lich being there. But that you know of their location! Our inside source has given us leads toward a few of the remaining liches, but we didn’t know about one being in the Hojon Empire.”

“I don’t mean any disrespect, but if you already know of one in your own empire, why has it not been dealt with?” Lanra asked. “The Hojon Empire has nearly a dozen greater powers to its name, and is regarded as one of the three superpowers on our continent. I would have imagined it would deal with a lich that dared to stay within its borders.”

“It is… a touch more complicated than that,” Groln said with a sigh, glancing around the room cautiously. “What I am about to tell you is top secret, though I suppose it will be discovered soon enough once we begin fighting. The Hojon Empire is in a stalemate with the lich. We are unable to wipe them out.”

“How so?” Hounth asked, looking intrigued for a change. It seemed the news of the superpower that was the Hojon Empire being on the back foot had been enough to draw the man from his annoyed mulling.

“As you all know, the Hojon Empire is famous for our focus on the individual,” Groln explained, getting nods from around the room. “Because of that, while our armies are smaller than the other superpowers, we boast more warriors in the Stage of Power than any other country. However… that also means we have more corpses of such as well, as old age comes for people regardless of how strong they were in life.”

“Oh no,” Amanda said, clearly picking up on what Groln was alluding to before anyone else as her eyes widened. “How many?”

“The lich has raised almost a full dozen minions. Each one of which was once in the Stage of Power when they were alive,” Groln admitted. “Three of whom were even former greater powers themselves from centuries past.”

“That… sounds problematic,” Donvath said, drumming his fingers along the table. “And you tried dealing with it yourself already?”

“No. With such a powerful force under the lich’s command, our Queen is not willing to risk losing any of our living forces,” Groln admitted. “For each one of us who falls, it would only grow even stronger in power. We have managed to quarantine it deep within our kingdom, but we are at a stalemate. Their minions are even stronger in death than they were while they were alive. We’d actually debated reaching out to Nunait at one point, as your Order is famous for its ability to fight the undead, but we didn’t want to risk the other superpowers learning of the threat within our own land.”

“I would have been willing to help, but the majority of my Order is too weak for something of that magnitude,” Amanda admitted. “We will need quite the force to strike back against something like that.”

“Thankfully, we have quite the force right here,” Donvath said, grinning at all of them. “This is exactly what the coalition was founded for. However, we should discuss what we know of the other liches as well and decide where we want to attack first. It sounds as if that lich is at least locked down for the time being. It might be wise to go after one of the others before that one.”

“So long as we don’t hold off for too long, my Queen is willing to wait,” Groln nodded.

“In which case, let’s go over what we know,” Donvath said, grabbing the stacks of journals and handing them out to everyone. “Here are copies of what little intel we have to go on. Prior to Groln’s announcement, the location of five of the remaining eight liches were unknown to us. Now, we know where to find four of them. Some of them are more dangerous than others, and some we know almost nothing about. Go ahead and go through those, then we can make a plan.”

“Donvath…” Lanra said, her face paling as she looked up from what she was reading. “What is this about a ‘ritual of extraction?’”

“The Council of Death has devised a way to rip a person’s element from their very soul,” Donvath said, eliciting gasps from most of the others.

“Impossible,” Hounth scoffed, the representative being the odd man out as the others all looked thoroughly terrified. “Come now, you’re not just going to believe him, are you? That’s absurd.”

“It’s true. I haven’t encountered that lich yet, but one of the ones I took down also dabbled in some horrific modification practices,” Asher admitted, thinking back to The Lost Lady and the poor men she’d kidnapped over the years to turn into her freaky minions. “Good rule of thumb, don’t rule anything out as ‘impossible’ when it comes to the Council. I mean, Zygrul has a Void element. That alone should tell you just what they’re capable of.”

Donvath gave him a subtle thumbs up as Hounth’s face paled and the representative began reading through the journals more carefully.

As everyone read up about the liches, Asher looked around the room, wondering if he’d really be able to keep his Eternity element a secret with what was to come. Thus far, he and Donvath had kept the Council’s entire plot to create the Eternity element under wraps. It had been tricky convincing everyone that Asher had a secret skill capable of putting a lich down for good, but seeing was believing, after all. Taking down the King of Death and allowing people to freely enter the Deadlands again for the first time in fifteen-hundred years had been all the proof people needed that Asher’s skill was the real deal.

Asher was sure every single member of the coalition figured it was his unique Spatial element that had somehow provided him with a skill capable of taking down the liches, and he was more than happy to let them think that.

Once everyone finished reading, they began planning. Seeing as Asher was the only person actually capable of putting the liches down, logistics got a bit tricky. They were willing to go after two different liches at the same time in multi-pronged attacks, but they needed him there to finish the job. Thankfully, so long as people were willing to bring something with them that he’d placed an Eternal Mark on beforehand, Asher was always only a single Recall away. He was a bit nervous about splitting their forces in such a way, but the rest of the coalition seemed to be in agreement that speed was of the essence at this point. The Council wasn’t used to making decisions quickly, and they’d already been dealt a terrifying blow. If given enough time, they might come together and decide to go on the offensive, and none of the people gathered there wanted their kingdom to be the one the liches made an example of.

“Then it’s decided,” Donvath declared, picking up two of the eight journals and grinning as he looked around the room. “We split up as agreed upon and go after these two liches first. Get some rest everyone, and make sure your warriors are prepared. Tomorrow, we go to war.”
