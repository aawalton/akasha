ONE HUNDRED YEARS LATERLoch walked out the wooden doors, they had long replaced the glass, and down the concrete steps. Kelly squeezed his hand. He turned to her and smiled. She hadn’t aged a day in the last hundred years. Still so beautiful. His rock. He squeezed back. They stepped onto the yard, both stopping and looking back at the Brady Clanhold.

Coe-Brown had changed, no longer recognizable. Parts had been destroyed, most by the dragons over fifty years ago, some rebuilt by choice as the functions of the building changed. It was still the Clan Brady Clanhold but no longer the seat of power for the territories controlled by Loch.Not by Loch, but by the Brady Republic. He hated that name.

The once school looked more like a castle and viking longhouse mixed together. Loch loved it. No longer a place where hundreds lived, it was now home to just his immediate family. A growing family.He turned to look at the small gathering further down the hill. His family, friends and closest allies. All his bannermen, the twenty of them, and their families. Led by Elora Seedspear and Brian Jefferson, the two standing in front of the others. Drew stood near Elora. He had never taken a position as a bannerman, instead serving the Clan as a guild leader, coordinating dungeon runs across all their territory. Jake Warrick and his family, two daughters and a son, stood with his wife. Casian and Mike Turner, representing two of the wandering guilds, directed with spreading out into the still wild lands, searching for dungeons and other groups of survivors or invaders. Ed and Susan Turner, with their youngest, a twenty year old daughter. No longer just Magistrate of Clan Brady, Ed was in charge of the entire Brady Republic, serving as the President of the Congress. A position that he was very good at.

Darren Holmberg and Kristin Conway stood together, hands clasped. Their responsibilities had increased in the last hundred years. Loch had never pushed his people, the ones that had formed Clan Brady those many years ago, into positions of power. They had all earned it.Lisa and Carla, Kelly’s two swordguards, her version of the bannermen, stood with others from the Valkyries. Around them were Ankorik of the Anura and Treli from the Hasic, two of the neighboring tribes. Sinat Halfsolace talked with Gorigo Moonfalls of the Grassstalks tribe of elves.

Dignitaries from the other Clans in the Republic, and some of the further territories were there. Diana Brekhov, the Clanchief for a new United Russia. Thomas Bledsoe, representing his Clan Donovan. Kate Barton from Clan McAllister. Two of the Clans that had allied with Loch in uniting most of what had been the United States and Canada. Others from Clans across the world. He had hoped Oroku Yoshi would have been there. He had not seen the man in around two decades. No longer the leader of Clan Yoshi, he had spent many years undoing the damage he had done, then had journeyed into the world to help rescue those oppressed. Loch knew he was still alive, as his name appeared in the Ranking Board still in the number two spot.

There would be more waiting. It was a momentous occasion.

To the side, in their own little group was his family. Harper and her husband Davis, their ten year old daughter standing between them. Harper held her son in her hands. Only three months old. Piper was next to her older sister, holding the hand of her five year old son. Her husband, Chris, was somewhere in South America with an advanced team. There had been a dragon’s nest and he was scouting it out with his party. Loch was proud of his daughters and sons-in-law. In that department, he knew he’d been lucky. He looked at his daughters. So much had changed. They both looked twenty-five or so. They had aged normally through their teen years but then it had slowed. Loch and Kelly still looked mid-forties, a little more gray in his beard. Next to Piper, leaning down and talking to her daughter, was Jamison.Loch had never expected to have another child. Jamison was the splitting image of Kelly, but ask her and she’d say their son looked like Loch. Twenty years old, and already strong for his age. He stood up, smiling at his parents as they came to a stop in front of the group.“Wow,” Loch said, looking them all over. “Thanks for coming.”“This is a big day,” Ed said. “Not just for you but for Earth.”

“True. So let’s not wait,” Loch said, waving at the group to get going.Down the slope, a collection of horses and other mounts waited, along with a couple of carriages. He led Kelly to Jarl and Light, the horses somehow granted extended lifespans. Larry pawed at the ground, walking over to nuzzle at Harper’s baby. Swift bent down, letting Piper’s daughter climb onto his back. Piper walked toward one of the carriages, rubbing at her pregnant belly.

Loch looked back at the Clanhold, his family’s home for a hundred years and hopefully a thousand more.

“You ready for this?” Kelly asked.

“Yeah,” Loch said, looking away from the Clanhold, smiling at Kelly. “Ready for another adventure with you? Of course.”***Loch pulled Jarl to a stop, the long caravan continuing past. He looked over Bow Village, the first village he had ever been involved in building. It hadn’t grown much over the years, the villagers preferring to keep it small. There was no need for it to be big, even though it was in the shadow of Brady Keep. The area no longer got as much traffic as it once had.

With the increased size of the Brady Republic, things had moved to more centralized locations. There were few unique Resources or Dungeons in the area, the Northwood Territory mostly getting visitors to the various museums that had been constructed. There were Dungeons that needed clearing, so adventuring teams still traveled the region. But merchants rarely came out this far. Which suited the people calling the village home just fine.Smiling, Loch patted Jarl on the head, the horse trotting to catch up to his family.

The world had grown in such a short time, not just the planet itself, but the people. Cities had been rebuilt, land reclaimed from monsters and invaders. The people of Earth had not just survived but they were thriving.

He looked down at the baby, poking her in the nose. He rubbed the hair of his grandson, which annoyed the kid, so Loch did it again.“Grandpa,” he muttered, making Loch laugh.

“You’ll be here,” Piper asked, looking at both parents but mostly Kelly, she held her growing belly.“Of course,” Kelly said. “We won’t miss it.”

Loch hugged her, giving her daughter a hug. The little girl had to be pried off Loch. He stood in front of his son, clasping the younger man’s shoulder. Jamison pulled his father’s hand off his shoulder, clasping it warrior to warrior. Loch smiled.

It had been very different raising a child fully in the Connection. He’d found that a lot of his old worries had faded. He still worried, and Jamison had a bit of a wild streak, but it wasn’t the same as it had been with Harper and Piper.

Like Harper and Piper, Jamison had gotten a Rare Class. His Leafblade Class didn’t sound strong but it was extremely deadly. He was a solo force to be reckoned with, the command of vines and nature giving him control over the battlefield. Loch had seen his son fight and it was a thing to behold.“We won’t be gone that long,” Loch said, releasing his son’s hand.“Yeah, you owe me a run of the Wyvern’s Nest Dungeon.”Loch laughed. It was a newly discovered Dungeon on the edges of their personal Territory.“Lochlan,” Little River Stone said, his deep voice drawing all their attention. “It is time.”

Loch and Kelly stepped back from their family. He gave his grandchildren a quick wave, smiling at his children. Kelly had tears in her eyes. It was the first time since the very beginning of the Connection that the Bradys had been separated for more than a couple of days. But this time would be voluntary.

He looked out over his family, friends and allies. People that had been fighting for the survival of Earth, its growth and the strength of its people, for a hundred years. The Connected System had come and they had survived, they had thrived, but Loch had not forgotten his promise from those first days. He had gotten many quests through the years, given out hundreds, but there was one that had stayed in his Log, never changing. The last few months, in preparation for the Hunt, Loch had spent time clearing out his quest log but the one remained. He knew it would be decades, maybe centuries, before anything changed with it.
