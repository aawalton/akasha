I woke up. Opened my eyes.

I was in a room. On a bed.

That meant—not dead.Which was good. Very good.

The events of the last two days came flashing back all at once.Oh god.Oh god, I was so close to dying. My eyes started to tear up.

I remembered my right leg and checked on it.Looked like someone had shoved the bone back in, stitched me up, then bound it with a splint and strips of cloth. Crude, but it worked. Thanks, mystery medic.

I looked around the room. Probably the rest area of the town's small infirmary.

I spotted Samar sleeping on a couch. They were probably taking turns, making sure someone was always here with me.Nice of them!

I also saw crutches leaning beside the bed.Cool, I thought. I'm mobile again.

I wanted to try walking a bit.I probably shouldn’t.But it was important—to feel alive. To know everything other than my leg was still functional.

I was slow and careful, really.I took both crutches and slowly steadied myself on my good foot while still leaning on the bed.Then I crippled my way to the door and opened it.

It was probably the middle of the night.I stepped out onto the porch. All was dark and quiet in town.

I looked up.

The stars were beautiful.

I caught myself tearing up again.I was so happy. I’m alive, and I was so happy to be so.

I walked back to bed.

Lori was fine.I was sure of it.I’d ask about her tomorrow.

Come morning, I woke up and saw Juliette sitting beside the bed, reading a book.The Dark Emperor – Volume 5.

“Morning…” I said with a smile.

Juliette looked up, wide-eyed, then threw the book aside, rushed at me, and hugged me hard.

That reaction totally surprised me.

“Geshem, you fucking idiot!” she yelled at me. “What was that trying-to-die nonsense good for?!”

“Sorry,” I said. “I was… I was stupid again.”

She let go. I saw tears glistening in her eyes.

“You know,” she said, in a low, shaking voice, “this whole strong leader thing… our Stormcrows thing… it’s all you. You made this. It couldn’t work without you. So you can’t just go and fucking die!”

I smiled. My eyes were wet too.

“I’ll try and be more careful. I swear it.” I said. “But you’re wrong about the Stormcrows. We’re an amazing band with an amazing leader—and you’ll be just fine even without me.”

“Maybe. Maybe not,” she sniffled.

“How’s Lori?” I asked.

“Lori’s fine,” Juliette said. “I mean, when we found you, she was a bleeding mess, but most of the injuries weren’t very deep. We cleaned and bandaged her well. And yesterday afternoon, she woke up looking like she was ready to wrestle a bear. Insisted on going back home.”

That was good to hear. “You said… yesterday?”

“Oh yes,” said Juliette. “We found you the day before yesterday. You’ve been sleeping like a babe since then.”

Oh. I see.

“Camila?” I asked.

Juliette shook her head.

“After you disappeared, we only focused on finding you,” she said. “I hope she’s fine, but… odds are… you know.”

“Yeah…” I said.

“So,” Juliette said finally, “are you going to tell me what happened?”

Right. Lori hadn’t talked much, so they had no idea what happened to us.

“We were looking for Camila. The sun started to set. Nayeem wanted to head back, like we agreed… but I was stupid. Told them to go back alone. Said I’d catch up. It was… a bad day for me. I was really messed up in my head.”

“I slipped into the stream. The water was fast. Took a lot of pounding from rocks. Couldn’t get out. Then I heard it...”

Another pause. This one for dramatic effect.

“It was the sound of a waterfall,” I said. “The sound of inevitability. That was the sound of my death.”

“Okay, I get it,” Juliette smiled, “Please continue the story.”

“When I fell, I probably shattered my shinbone,” I said. “I crawled to solid ground, but couldn’t move. Just sat there. In huge pain. For hours.”

“That’s horrific!” Juliette covered her mouth. “I can’t even imagine…”

“Then came the horned rat pack.”

Juliette audibly gasped. This was the juicy part.

“There were fifteen of them. Large as cats and twice as mean! They came carefully, cautiously. And about a second before they tore my face off… Lori showed up.”

“Thank God!” said Juliette, completely immersed in the story.

“She skewered the first from afar with arrows. Then she descended with a knife. She fought like a demon—already bleeding from numerous wounds, scratches, bites. They were all on her, biting and clawing. But she kept fighting to protect me.”

“But the rat pack wouldn’t back down,” I continued. “She was also spent from walking all night. They were winning.”

“So what did you do?” Juliette asked, eyes wide, sitting on the edge of her seat.

“I did magic,” I said. “Had one CO bottle on me. I used it to help Lori. Somehow… it was enough. We chased them away.”

“That’s amazing!” Juliette said. “Both of you! This was your first time using it in battle, and it saved your life. And Lori… wow. She probably loves you deeply.”

That made me blush.

“Lori is amazing,” I agreed. “I actually wanted to visit her.”

“What? No, Geshem!” Juliette said. “The physician said you should rest in bed for a couple of days, then use the crutches. Honestly, your wound looked horrible. They had to operate to set the bone. I was afraid you’d die of infection. Though Charlotte said they had some kind of paste that works like an antibiotic.”

“I actually thought about that,” I said with a smile. “You have to remember we’re on an alien world. With alien pathogens.”

“Is that a good thing?” she asked. “Your immune system is tailored for Earth.”

“True. But the immune system is incredibly versatile. It attacks anything unfamiliar—organic or not. And on the other side, pathogens are highly specialized to their hosts. Most Earth microbes can’t infect humans—only a few evolved to breach our defenses. We’re brand new to this planet’s microbiome.”

“You sure know a lot about this stuff.”

“I used to internet,” I explained flatly.

“Well, what about toxins? Weird chemicals that got into your blood while you were lying with an open wound for a whole day in the forest? Also, that super-versatile immune system you’re so proud of—it can also overreact. Autoimmune responses, inflammation… stuff like that can kill you. They don’t have steroids here to calm it down.”

“You know a lot too,” I admitted. Good points.

“I used to listen in school,” Juliette said.

“But I feel good.”

She came close and felt my forehead. “Gash, you do have a fever. You’re staying in bed. I’ll try to get Lori to visit you.”

“Fine.”

“Are you hungry?”

“Like you wouldn’t fucking believe.”

“Good. I’ll go get you something.” She smiled. “It’s really good to have you back.”

It was really good to be back.

The rest of the day was actually fun. All the Stormcrows came to visit, and every time I got better at telling the courageous (and dumb) tale of my short, painful adventure.

With Santiago and Charlotte, it went the best—they were gasping, shouting, and I swear, almost crying.

Hyun-woo, on the other hand, was obsessed with the horned rat pack's tribal behavior. Kept asking me questions like I was their biographer.Hey! They were the bad guys here!

Still, it was nice being sick. Everyone worried about me. Brought me food, books, company.It’s nice, when people take care of you.

But Lori didn’t come.

Juliette tried to ask her, but wasn’t sure why she wouldn’t.It’s hard to know why NPCs do or don’t do things.

I hoped it wasn’t because of my rejection, before our ordeal.

In the evening, I sat with Juliette and Hyun-woo.Juliette wanted to talk about the attack we seemed to be expecting from the enemy kingdom—and from that group of snub players, the Order of the Phoenix.

It was something we needed to talk about, but never had the time.Right after the king’s messenger, we got that call from Farhad’s group at the keep. Then we went to the goblins' party, got drunk, Camila went missing, then I went missing.

We were a busy band, like that.

“It’s no coincidence,” Hyun-woo said. “The message from the king, a few days after the Order of the Phoenix surveyed the area, and talked about an attack. It could happen any day now.”

I gave a short chuckle at the mention of name Order of the Phoenix.Juliette caught it, squinted her eyes at me.

“The Order of the Phoenix is a cool name,” she said.

“No it’s not,” I said. “It’s lame.”

“Didn’t you like Harry Potter?”

“Yeah. So? Still lame name.”

“The Stormcrows is sooo much better,” she retorted.

“Okay, guys,” said Hyun-woo, cutting us off. “What do we do if an attack does happen?”

“We sit it out,” said Juliette. “That’s not our fight. We helped Kamatou against Farhad’s group—we had our reasons. But this is a different story. We have no reason to believe they’ll mistreat the NPCs. And every reason to believe they’ll outmatch us.”

She paused, then added, more softly:“Look, when I turned down the king’s messenger, Camila asked why we weren’t taking a vote. I joked and told her this is a dictatorship. What do you guys think? Should I have taken a vote?”

“Oh, that was okay,” I said. “You decide things. Or you decide to consult us. That’s the point of being the leader. If we voted every time, being leader would mean nothing. So yes, Juliette, you are a dictator. But we like you anyway.”

“Thanks,” she chuckled.

“I agree,” said Hyun-woo. “If someone’s not okay with how you lead, they can suggest a vote for a new leader. For now, our system works. If we grow, say to fifty members, we might need more structure. Roles. Procedures.”

“That sounds awful,” said Juliette.

“If that happens, I call dibs on Minister of Arcane Arts.”

“Sure,” Juliette said. “It’s yours.”

“Yes!”

“So, back to the topic,” Juliette said. “We sit out the battle and hope there’s not too much violence. If they win and take over, we’ll work with the new government. Hopefully, like Felix said, it won’t really matter. If they end up being evil rulers running a reign of terror, we’ll consider our options.”

“That makes sense,” Hyun-woo nodded. “During the attack, they'll be at their strongest. But after they establish control, the main army will move on, and if they fear uprisings, we’ll have more leverage. Best-case scenario: we position ourselves to influence the new government.”

“We wait, then,” I said. “And we still have to plan for attack on Farhad’s keep.”

“About that…” Juliette said. “Earlier today, I got another phone call from Ananya. She said Farhad’s group never came back. Their search parties didn’t find anything either. She wanted to know if we knew something.”

“It doesn’t make sense,” I said. “It’s been, what, three days since the battle?”

“Four,” Juliette corrected.

They were level two,” I said. “They also knew the routes back. Even if some were injured, they should arrived by now easily. Maybe they lied—just a trick to make us lower our guard.

“I don’t think so,” Juliette replied. “When I talked to her, She sounded… resigned. She talked about running low on food. The ones who usually participate in the food raiding parties— they were all in the attack. I told her to contact us daily. We’ll tell her if we learn anything. I know we’re technically enemies, but we don’t want them to starve.”

“But what is stopping Farhad’s group from reaching back to the keep?” I asked.

“Something happened to them,” she said. “That’s for sure. Anyway, let’s put our assault on hold. If Farhad’s group doesn’t return, there’ll be only five people at the keep. None of them warriors. And they’re starving. We won’t need a catapult for that.”

“In that case,” I said, “maybe we should send someone to Kuriolis. Give them food. Actually rescue them.”

“We’ll see,” said Juliette.

“But we can still design the siege engine and have the goblins build it,” said Hyun-woo. The guy was actually excited. “If not for the keep, we might find another use.”

Yes. Having catapults is awesome. Even if it's just for fun.

Still, something about Farhad’s group disappearing bugged me. Not because I feared for their lives—just because I hated the idea of something unknown in the forest strong enough to take out strong players.

The next morning, I woke up happy.

I ate tasty oatmeal and soup that my dear friend Santiago brought me.

Then, after he left to bring me another bowl of soup, I made my grand escape.

What? Two days is basically “a couple of days.”

And the point was to not stress the injured leg—so as long as I was slow, using the crutches, and jumping only on my good leg, I’d be fine.

First stop: the market. I had something to buy.

I slowly crippled my way through town. It was a slow process, but I managed.

Then came the long walk to the forester’s cabin. That took a long time, but I wasn’t in a rush.I was just happy to finally be out of bed, basking in the sunlight, feeling like a person again.

When I entered the cabin, I saw Lori standing behind the counter.When she saw me, she came around and walked toward me.Then stopped—not too close. Just… a normal people, respectful distance.

She smiled brightly.Then the smile faded.

“It’s good to see a familiar face out here,” she said.

This statement was… little cold.

I got it.She was happy to see me alive, but she remembered what I said before. The rejection.It hurt her.

I walked up to her anyway and gave her a hug—or more like, awkwardly leaned into her with a one-handed half-hug.

“Thank you, Lori,” I said. “You saved my life.”

She said nothing.

I released her and reached into my satchel.

“This is a gift,” I said with a smile, handing her a small leather packet. “Your father said that sharing is how the forest keeps its balance.”

I had bought Lori a skinner’s knife.

Not the best gift for your average lady.But your average lady probably didn’t skin animals daily.

This one was a beautiful piece—made from Motheror steel (whatever that was), with elegant engravings. It had cost me a staggering 20 silvers—half my personal savings.

Lori opened the leather case, pulled out the knife—and her eyes lit up.She gave me a huge, unfiltered smile.

In that moment, that smile was worth twenty gold. Easily.

“Just so you know,” I said, “this gift has a selfish motive. I want you to make me nice pelts. And gift them to me. I want a lot of gifts from you.”

She looked me in the eyes.

“I also want us to go to that restaurant again,” I said. “And I want you to teach me archery, because that looked really cool. And I want to hear you sing. From now on… I want us to do tons of things together.”

Then Lori did something she had never done before.

She hugged me.

It was a super strong hug—she was basically crushing me.But I liked it a lot.

I wanted to date Lori. It was a decision I made.

And it was the easiest decision in the world.

We then sat and talked nonsense for maybe an hour.

Then Santiago arrived at the cabin.

“Dude!” he said. “You were like, dead, not two days ago. You should be in bed!”

“I’m fine,” I smiled. “And Lori said the spirit of the forest will heal me anyway.”

“Juliette knew you were here and told me to drag you back to bed,” he said. “I think it was like… a leader order. So I must obey!”

“Sure you will,” I said. “But first, I need your help with something really small.”

“Ah… okay?” he said, clearly suspicious. Rightly so.

Honestly, I was glad Santiago showed up.

While stuck in bed, I had time to think. I’d come up with a new magic ideas.

And I needed a guinea pig.

“Come,” I said. “Bye, Lori. I’ll see you later.”

I led Santiago to the small hunting cabin outside.

“Wait…” said Santiago. “Isn’t this where you practice your poison gas magic?”

“Yes, but not today,” I said. “Today I just want to make you stop breathing.”

“You what?!”

“Don’t worry,” I promised. “Nothing poisonous. I’m just going to try to interrupt your breathing with some magic. If you feel like you’re really struggling, raise your hand and I’ll stop. It’s 100% not dangerous.”Probably.

“Sit here.” I pointed to a chair. He sat. I sat opposite him, a few feet away.

“I’ll start with general gas magic,” I explained. “I’ll try to create an anti-mixing sphere around the air in front of your face. See if it interrupts the airflow to your lungs.”

“Okay…?” He looked afraid. But also kind of excited—he thought magic was cool. So it balanced out. I was counting on that.

Unfortunately, for air I had to use the general gas circle. No specific circle for ‘air’ in Miguel’s books. So it was less effective.

“I feel a little wind,” Santiago said. “You’re doing it, right?”

“Yes…” I said.Yeah. It didn’t do much.

When Santiago breathed—like through a straw—it created low-pressure zones that pulled in new air from the sides. My anti-mixing sphere wasn’t strong enough to prevent that.

“Next, I’ll try something similar,” I said. “Just using a circle customized for pure oxygen. The idea is, when you breathe and the oxygen level drops, the circle will prevent new oxygen from mixing in.”

I cast the O₂ circle. It wasn’t my specialty like CO, but they were similar enough, and this one was stronger than the general air circle.

“Did it work?” I asked. “Feel any different?”

“No…” Santiago said, taking a deep breath. “Air feels normal.”

“Okay, I’ll try something else.”

I kept the oxygen circle active but increased the size of its influence.I also shifted it—from in front of his face into his lungs.

“How about now?”

“No, still not—wait, I…”He started hyperventilating.

“OKAY STOP!”

I stopped immediately. He gasped and breathed normally again.

“That was scary as shit, man,” Santiago said, eyes wide. “What the hell did you do?”

I smiled."Scary as shit" was exactly the response I was aiming for.

“I’m not really sure how it works,” I said. “I moved the anti-mixing oxygen influence sphere into your lungs. And it seemed to interrupt oxygen absorption by your lungs.

“So… you can make people stop breathing now?” he asked.

“No, no,” I said quickly. “It’s not that powerful. You probably could’ve handled it. And it was more scary than dangerous. Plus, if someone simply moves, I can’t make the sphere follow quick enough. And it’s not a poison like CO—so after a moment, they’ll be fine.”

“But your enemy doesn’t know all that,” Santiago said.

“Exactly,” I said. “Now I want to try it on myself. I’ll hold on as much as I can. Please stand close and catch me in case I faint.”

He did. I cast the circle again—on myself, this time.

The first few seconds were normal.

And then I felt it—like each breath wasn’t fully doing its work, like there wasn’t enough oxygen in the room. It was scary.

But I kept going.

Unfortunately, as I hyperventilated, I still remained conscious, feeling only dizziness.

I couldn’t make people faint—not yet—but I could scare the hell out of them, without needing gas bottles or setup.This was another great tool in my arsenal.Well… maybe after some more practice.

“Thanks,” I said. “That was a huge help.”

Then the door slammed open.

Juliette was standing there, looking furious.

“Santiago, what the fuck?!” she yelled. “I told you to drag him back to bed, not help him do gas magic!”

And just like that, Santiago was in serious trouble.

I smiled to myself.I was a cripple now—so no one was shouting at me.

Three more days passed in blissful calm.

I was getting better each day—stronger, more stable. I could cripple-walk around town without Juliette yelling at me every five minutes.

Each day, I spent hours with Lori.

She taught me things I could do even in my current state—some gross animal stuff, sure, but also useful things. Like how to identify herbs, which mushrooms were edible, which ones would make you trip balls, and which ones would murder your entire bloodline.

In return, I taught her how to sew with a needle.

She picked it up absurdly fast. Like, terrifyingly fast. Lori was a natural at everything she tried.

And yes—by now, we were dating. For real.

I think… Maybe the others saw it. And guessed?And I truly didn’t care even one bit.

I didn’t feel lame for dating a non-human NPC.The opposite—I felt proud.

She was sweet, strong, clever and deadly with a bow. Honestly, every guy in the kingdom should envy me.

We kept in daily contact with Ananya, up at Farhad’s keep.

Still no sign of Farhad’s group.

Their situation had gotten worse. They were running really low on food. We were starting to seriously consider helping them—maybe inviting them to camp closer to Kamatou, so we could bring them supplies.

But the problem was obvious:Whatever made Farhad’s group disappear… could still be out there.And anyone we sent might be next.

Besides, I wasn’t in any shape for forest travel anyway.

But that was other people’s problem.

As I said—my days were relaxing and happy.

Not that it would continue like that.

Not in this world.

Because in this world… things always happen.

This time, it started with two goblin scouts, running breathless into the forester’s cabin.

They’d spotted a massive human army, not half a day’s march to the north.
