When Alexander asked the giant wolf what they should call him in the contract, it simply turned to him and said, “A name for mortals I have never had, though if you like you may call me Big Bad.” The wolf turned slowly toward Judah and showed all its teeth in a near-maniacal grin. Judah felt the hairs on his arm stand on end as he looked quickly away from the wolf.

In the contract, Big Bad agreed to an extended non-aggression pact and to guide them, make them aware of threats, and lead them to the location of the girl. They began by informing the group that the person who currently held possession of her was called “The Widow of the Wood.”

“That is a rather ambiguous name, even compared to Big Bad,” Alexander pointed out. “It seems more like a title.”

“The first of names is to give control, or for those you are intimate with so you might be whole. The second of names is for reputation—be you friend or foe, this is information.”

“So The Widow of the Woods would be the ‘second of names,’ as you called it?” Alexander asked.

The wolf nodded in agreement.

“Is she a spider!?” Judah exclaimed as he shivered. “She sounds like a spider.”

Judah and the others had lagged a few yards behind, still wary of the wolf, walking as if braced for an attack. Alexander found this caution foolish; Big Bad had signed an extended non-aggression agreement. He continued to probe the wolf about the potential dangers they might face as he walked beside it.

“The Widow makes her home in your mind. Like it or not, she makes you be kind. Recovering the girl might not be dire, but the girl may not have the needed desire.”

Alexander glanced back at the group to ensure they had heard the wolf properly. Brian nodded, and Alexander began considering all the possible implications of what had been said.

As they followed the wolf, Alexander noticed subtle changes in the wood. The erratic, bunched-up tree roots began to weave together more harmoniously. They aligned and pointed in the same direction—which, he was certain, was no coincidence: the direction they were walking. He found himself appreciating the pattern the roots formed. It also made the walk easier in the dim light provided by his headlamp and the flashlights the others carried. Several members of the party were prone to stumbling on the roots, unaccustomed to uneven terrain. Fortunately, there were no sprained ankles. Alexander had made sure the group tightened their laces near the top of their boots so that ankle support was adequate.

Then, subtly, a small pulse of power rolled over him like a massage that struck his nervous system directly. He felt a glow of warmth in his chest and a general sense of serenity. After a few seconds, he sensed the source of the feeling just over a stone’s throw away.

“Hey,” Brian called.

Alexander looked back to see Johnny whispering in Brian’s ear. Big Bad had stopped and sat on its haunches as Alexander walked back to the collected party.

Brian continued once Alexander was close enough to hear a whisper. “I was just informed that there is something ahead.”

“I felt it as well…” Alexander said, trailing off. His mind felt oddly fuzzy.

Johnny walked over to him and squinted.

“Yes?”

“Are you okay?”

Alexander had to pause and really think for a moment. Something was certainly off, but it did not seem detrimental.

“I feel goooood,” Cassidy said, rubbing her hands slowly over her face.

Alexander saw Linda sniffing her diffuser necklace and giggling. Brian looked at them, then he too began to laugh. The entire party seemed to be enjoying themselves. Johnny, in contrast, began to look concerned. Alexander tried to think why Johnny would be concerned, but couldn’t quite pin it down. Instead, he became fascinated with the mosquito mesh rolled up on the brim of his hat. The texture both prickled and tickled as he rubbed it. He found it amusing and started saying it aloud.

“Prickle and tickle and prickle and tickle and—”

“Ey! I think y’all are being affected by magic like that sleeping spell again!” Johnny said, more urgently this time.

The others seemed unfazed by his words, but what he said reached Alexander’s subconscious enough to make him focus.

“Um, I think I am experiencing intoxication,” Alexander announced.

“We should back away from whatever that is ahead of us,” Johnny suggested.

Brian and Judah were now laughing unreservedly as they began poking each other’s cheeks.

“I would be amenable to that suggestion,” Alexander said. “Though you may be required to help me ambulate, as my appendages seem to be capricious.” As he spoke, he felt as though he were rambling plebeian drivel.

Johnny herded them like sheep, nudging them back in the direction they had come from. After a few dozen yards, Alexander felt his mind clearing, and the rest of the group’s giggling gradually stopped.

Cassidy still wore a smile as she asked, “What on earth was that? It was a lot like when I took… well… you all don’t need to know.”

“Like a certain narcotic that makes all of your sensations and emotions enjoyable?” Brian asked flatly.

“Well, yes.”

“So what are we going to do about it?” Judah asked. “I assume the source of this enchantment is that source of power you felt ahead. It seems Johnny is the only one who can resist it because of his wisdom levels.”

“I think I may be able to do something minor to help buffer myself,” Alexander said to the group.

Alexander reached into his pocket and pulled out his small tackle box. Opening it, he found a feather-shaped hairpin. Pulling it out, he removed his hat and placed the pin in his hair.

^

Hairpin of Minor Resolve

A hairpin that moderately strengthens the user’s mental resistance to magical influence.

“This hairpin helps me resist magical influence over my mind,” he explained to the group.

“Is that the only one?” Brian asked.

“Unfortunately, yes. I have had to ration my resources as I am saving up for something else.”

Brian nodded approvingly.

Cassidy, on the other hand, decided to continue being belligerent. “Well, good for you, but how on earth does that help us?”

“It doesn’t,” Brian said simply. “I think Johnny and Alexander should approach the source of the magic while the rest of us stay here, outside of its effects…” He looked over the group. “Where is Linda?”

As they all looked around, Alexander’s headlamp caught the frolicking motions of Linda passing the wolf as she shouted, “The most essential oil in the world!” with her hands waving above her head.

“Well, crap,” Brian sighed, frustrated. Then, more urgently, “We three can’t go after her. Johnny and Alexander, do your best to get her before she gets into any real trouble. Go!”

Johnny, Bubba, and Alexander jogged after her, careful not to slip on the roots that still blanketed the ground as if drawn to the power source. As they neared Big Bad, Alexander felt the pulse of power again. This time, instead of his nerves responding, he felt as if his body were covered in a thin sheet of saran wrap while a wave of water washed over him. He could sense it, but he knew it wasn’t actually touching him. This boosted his confidence, and he picked up the pace.

As they passed the wolf, Alexander noticed an unpleasant sneer on its face. Given that he was not the best at reading people, it was entirely possible he was misinterpreting the talking wolf’s expression. Still, it did not sit well with him.

Just another two dozen yards past the wolf, the trees ended abruptly. Alexander could make out a soft blue glow ahead, emanating from the ground. They were catching up to Linda, but it became apparent she would reach the source of the glow before them. The features of the glowing source turned out to be a small pool of liquid—almost perfectly circular. The liquid emitted an ethereal glow that gently lit the area. The roots of the trees went right up to the edge and bent ninety degrees downward, forming the walls of the pool. Linda had stopped at the edge and begun to bend down, cupping some of the liquid in her hand.

“Stop, Linda!” Johnny shouted.

She didn’t even flinch as she brought it to her lips and drank several mouthfuls seconds before they grabbed her shoulders and pulled her away. She went limp in their arms as they backed away from the pool.

“Oh, hun,” the Cajun said sympathetically to the passed-out woman. “I don’t imagine this won’t have far-reaching consequences.”

Alexander nodded, thinking that what Johnny said was likely the best use of a double negative he had ever heard in conversation. He knelt beside Linda and considered engaging her gag reflex by pushing a finger down her throat. However, without her consent, he felt he would be in a precarious legal position. His human resources training in sexual harassment had long overwritten his basic first-aid training from his Boy Scout days.

“We oughta find out what this pool is,” Johnny said, nodding toward it.

Alexander turned to the wolf, who was strolling up behind them.

“Excuse me, Big Bad, if you would please inform us what this pool is?”

The wolf just smiled.

“Under our agreement, you must tell us of threats to our safety and freedom.”

Big Bad chuckled and said, “The Linda is safe as safe can be. She has no need to be made free. Mortals make choices and choices mean freedom. Bargains have been made although you cannot see them.”

“Ah. Guess this is why boyo said not to bargain,” Johnny observed. “False sense of security.”

Alexander turned to the glowing pool and felt the small waves of energy coming off it. “Detect Magic.” Looking at the pool as he used the spell, he shifted his senses—like seeing something two-dimensional take shape and come to life before his eyes. His enhanced arcane levels had helped him see threads and patterns in spells last time he focused, but this was like a third-grader looking at an astrophysicist’s equations on dark matter.

Yes/No

Alexander hesitated, recalling what had happened earlier with the wolf’s magic. A foolish phrase came to mind: No pain, no gain. While true for muscle-obsessed jocks, it was far from a universal truth. Still, he saw the need for some risk of pain in this matter.

With a queasy feeling in his stomach, he said, “Yes.”

A display of lights that would put every firework or laser show to shame blinded him with complexity and power. Pain struck as if a needle had been shoved through his eye and pierced his brain. He tried to push through, concentrating on one braid rather than the whole tapestry. From there he focused on a single tiny thread of the magic before he felt the equivalent of someone kicking him between the legs and slamming his mind shut.

Identification failed. You have gained a level in Arcana! Arcana level 10: You have 5 points to distribute to your Arcana skill tree.

“You alright?”

Alexander turned his head and looked up at Johnny, only now realizing he had fallen to his knees and laid his head awkwardly on the ground.

“I’ll be okay,” he said, getting up slowly.

Alexander wondered why his Arcana skill had leveled despite failing his attempt.

Sitting up to regain his balance, he reached down and unzipped his fanny pack of holding. He moved a plastic water bottle aside. Opening it, he brought it to his mouth and drank deeply until he finished it. Feeling slightly waterlogged, he approached the edge of the pool. Kneeling, he moved to dip the bottle in to collect a sample. He was interrupted by an airy female voice.

“To fill a chalice from my pool, do you give yourself over to the weavings of the wandering so easily, mortal?”

Startled, Alexander rose to his feet. He scanned the dark tree line for the voice. At the edge of the trees, moving toward them, he made out a childlike silhouette. The being glided forward in an odd manner. He adjusted the angle of his headlamp to see more clearly.

The roots beneath the figure rolled upward, lifting and pushing her forward. Her frame was small like that of a child. Looking at her face, she appeared to be around eight years old. She had pale skin on her face and torso, but from her knees and elbows her pale smooth skin turned into bark like a tree. Her hair was made of tiny roots that trailed over her shoulders and down her back all the way to the ground behind her. It was as if she glided over the roots using her hair.

As she neared, Alexander felt a powerful pressure pushing down on his shoulders, as if compelling him to kneel. Her eyes and lips were as black as night, and she wore what looked to be woven Spanish moss as a gown.

As she reached the opposite side of the pool from him, she raised a hand to cover her eyes and grimaced.

Squinting her eyes at him, she said with irritation, “Mortal, the light you emit is bothersome. If you wish to engage in civil discourse, I advise you to extinguish it.”

“Oh, my apologies,” Alexander said, turning the headlamp off.

“Better,” she replied, lowering her hand with an annoyed expression. “I too shall lower the power of my presence as a gesture of my goodwill.”

The pressure on his body lifted, and his general sense of danger calmed as a result.

“This pool belongs to you, you said? Am I correct to assume you are The Widow of the Woods?” Alexander asked, unconvinced. While it was plain to see she was no spider, she was also far too young—in appearance at least—to have been married.

“You would be correct in this. What might I call you mortals?”

“No names,” Johnny whispered.

Alexander was about to begin his modified introductions when Big Bad cut in.

“Oh fair Widow of the Wood, I shall name them if I could. If I could.”

The Widow simply nodded with a knowing smile.

“The one who radiates the light is Alexander of great magical might. The one who kneels next to the mutt is Johnny whom has much wisdom to strut…” The Widow nodded slightly to Johnny and Alexander as the wolf announced them. “…and the one whom lays upon the ground, and taken the magic that she has found, is called Linda whom to you be bound. Be bound.”

As he spoke, The Widow of the Woods’ eyes brightened. “I am delighted by so many visitors this day. Welcome, Sir Johnny and Sir Alexander.” Alexander shivered as she uttered his name.

He felt a chill in the air as she welcomed them. Johnny stood and shivered slightly before returning the bow the Widow had made. “Excuse me, young lady. We came to recover a young woman who had the misfortune of getting lost in these woods. Would you by chance have encountered her?”

The girl laughed like the sound of wind chimes. A wave of delight hit Alexander’s ears, causing him an inexplicable sense of joy. “To call this one young is amusing. Such perceptions the mortals have. As for the young miss, do you refer to one Megan Dyer?” She spoke Megan’s name with an odd hint of command. In the distance they heard the creaking sound a tree makes under too much pressure from the wind. A few seconds later, they could make out a new gliding figure. The roots carried a woman’s figure, seated this time, and it resolved into the familiar face from the photo they had seen the day before—or was it two days now? Alexander needed to check his phone for the time.

Megan Dyer’s clothes were a bit dirty, but otherwise there was no evidence of mistreatment. Her face was glazed over in a slight stupor, and she seemed unaware of their presence.

“Yes, that would be her,” Johnny swallowed. “Her parents do miss her dearly, ma’am. Would you allow us to escort her home?”

The Widow of the Woods smiled openly at Johnny. “What a noble quest you find yourself on. Most certainly would I be happy to let you escort her home to her own kin! I shouldn’t dream to entrap or overrule one’s free will.”

Well, that seemed rather easy. Maybe Alexander could get home in time to not miss laundry day.

“That is of course if she is willing to accompany you home.” The Widow moved closer and knelt next to the young girl. She pushed a piece of Megan’s hair back over her ear paternally and said gently, “Megan Dyer, do you wish to accompany these men to your home?”

The glazed expression on the girl’s face finally snapped to attention. She whipped her head toward the Widow with a concerned expression.

“I would not want to leave this wood. To be separated from the peace and joy of the pool would be tragic. I do not believe I would survive the day without dying of heartbreak!” She said this as if she were still on stage performing a play. Her arms moved grandiosely, and her face exuded far too much sincerity. There was some kind of ruse at play here.

“Oh, dear of mine. I would not wish that harm to befall you. Good sirs, there is no mere mortal that can take from the pool and still desire to depart from it… save for a powerful intervention.”

Johnny looked at Alexander, who uncharacteristically felt like rolling his eyes. He pulled out his pen and a sheet of paper.

“Is there anything we can do to procure a powerful intervention?” Alexander asked, pulling out a new contract.
