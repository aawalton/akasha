Two voices echoed from the shadowed staircase. The first was masculine, with a rich timbre. The second held a dulcet, almost musical cadence.

I recognized them only a moment before the speakers came into view.

Embriel had a narrow build that, much like Velidan’s, exuded a mixture of coiled strength and lithe grace. His face was relaxed planes of ivory skin offset by sharp cheekbones and a dimple at one corner of a too-red mouth. He wore a shimmering blue vest wrapped over creamy satin, with tall brown boots that clicked against the stone.

The woman beside him had soft features and short hair that shone like sunshine behind a cloud. Her eyes were so pale they almost seemed colorless. Shimmering, nearly-sheer tiers of gauze cascaded down her body.

I’d seen the pair once before, through swollen eyes and a numbing fog of pain. And I remembered vividly how they’d viewed my distress; with mocking laughter. As they climbed into view now, the memory came back in fierce detail and I found myself clutching both sides of my chair.

I’m surrounded by monsters. Literally. But they probably don’t care about me at all, so I should just… sit here and be quiet. And it will be fine. Right?

I flicked my gaze towards Velidan, but he wore an easy smile and was paying me no mind.

“Vel!” Embriel crested the top step, face splitting into a wide grin. “Fancy seeing you here!”

“Embriel. Saffine. This is a surprise.”

“A good one, I hope.” The woman drew near, gaze flicking to me and nose wrinkling.

Embriel swept one hand through his already slicked-back bronze hair, then settled it to rest on the back of his neck. A muscle popped with the movement. “We’ve been dropping by more and more. Trying to… oh, what was your phrase? Spread the wealth?” He chuckled, then slid uninvited into the far chair and picked up my glass of wine. “And I bet you’re here buying me a gift, aren’t you?”

Velidan snorted. “I’ve better ways to spend my money, and I gave you several just the other night.”

The other man clucked his tongue. “Vel! I’m hurt! l bring you something nice every single time you host.”

“‘Nice’ is relative,” Saffine said under her breath, earning a mock-scowl. She gave a syrupy smile in response, then drifted to gaze down at the field.

“Harpy,” her companion chuckled. “And you said those were tokens of good faith.”

Velidan inclined his head. “Consider them both. Speaking of which,” his tone fell to a dry pitch, “shouldn’t you be off scheming somewhere? Plotting an invasion while bathing in blood?”

“Dawn, now there’s a picture.” Embriel’s face went blank, then twisted into a grin. “Tomorrow’s not an invasion. It’s just a little… taste. Whetting some appetites.” After a short silence, he straightened and turned a more serious look towards the black-haired Fae. “You’re still coming, right? I want you there. More than most of the others, if I’m being honest.”

Velidan stretched back in his chair. “You’ve been going on for months about how you’ve finally figured out how to hold a portal open. Which is impossible, of course-”

“It’s very possible.”

“So either I get to watch you make a fool of yourself or I get to witness a miracle.” He smirked, then raised his half-drunk glass in a toast. “I wouldn’t miss it.”

“Excellent!” The other Fae beamed, clinking cups. “We begin in the morning.”

“Your messenger already told me. And it begs one of my many questions; why are we launching an attack over breakfast?”

He chortled. “Don’t be ridiculous, Vel. We’re going in at night. And ‘attack’ is a harsh word. Think of it more like a… tour. The day’s for convincing everyone else to get on board. Besides, I promised everyone a good time! There’s entertainment lined up. Moon knows we’re all due for some fun.”

Velidan swirled his drink, offering no response.

“Speaking of which…” an appraising look was cast in my direction. “Will your pretty pet be coming?”

I’d been staring at him, fists white in my lap as I imagined shadowed blades and blood pooling on cobblestones. He killed people. Just earlier today, this creature had innocent people slaughtered in the streets.

I’d imagined Embriel to be… I wasn’t sure, exactly. Dressed head to toe in jagged steel, dripping in blood and lurking deep in some shadowy recess of the Night Court. That he was simply here, wandering the same place we were, with an easy smile and a carefree laugh… it brought shock, fear, and anger together in a fierce swell. It made me want to stand up and demand justice.

Salt, imagine if I’d brought the shard along! Could we have ended all this right here? Could it have been that simple?

But I hadn’t brought it. And now a predatory gaze was sliding down my throat and lower still, leaving behind the sensation of oil on my skin. I wrapped both arms around my middle and turned towards Velidan, who watched the other man unreadably. I wondered if he was having the same murderous thought as me.

If he was, he gave no indication. “She will.”

“Good! That’s great for the image, you know; you being so on board. Might help sway some of the others. She looks a lot better this time, too. Must’ve mouthed off right before that last party, huh?” He leaned forwards with the statement, nudging me with a conspiratorial wink.

I flinched back as violently as if he’d struck me. Acrid bile flooded my throat.

“No judgement, of course! And you’ve always had good taste, so I’m sure she’s great fun.” The claim fell so calmly, so politely, that it made me want to scream.

“Good taste?” Saffine’s titter came from just behind my head. “Are we ignoring all the mud? The state of her hair? Night, the odor?”

I was growing more nauseous with every word. I turned my face down, trying my hardest to sink into the chair. Just breathe. Don’t even look at them. He said to be quiet, surely they’ll leave soon and-

“You’ll clean her up before tomorrow, Vel? I don’t want my carpets smelling like this. And she’d better not track dirt in.” One immaculate white shoe tapped near my mud-caked boots.

“Don’t listen, little lamb.” Embriel’s rich voice came alongside another snicker. “You look adorable under all that dust. Where did you even find her, Vel?”

My heart nearly stopped. I didn’t want these creatures to even know that Fellbrook existed, much less have any interest in going there. I cast a panicked look towards Velidan.

He just shrugged. “Some little village. I actually thought she was one of yours, at first.”

That earned a puzzled glance.

“A glamour failed. Quickly, too. I assumed it was you or one of the others pushing back, but… well. As it turns out,” he leaned forwards, giving me a malicious look and voice dripping with all the weight of a delicious secret being spilled, “she’s a runekeeper.”

Heavy silence descended on the tower. The very air seemed to thicken.

Then Embriel slammed a hand down and threw his head back, roaring with laughter. “So that’s what did it! Dawn, Vel, I wondered why you came around so suddenly! After years of snapping at me!” The Fae lunged so fast I shrieked. He crouched before me with both hands braced along the armrests, face so close to mine that hot breath mingled with my own. “I bet it was cathartic, Vel. Wasn’t it? Hurting something so precious, and no one lifted a finger to help.” He tucked a strand of hair behind my ear, purring, “Did they, little lamb?”

“W-what?” I cringed back, curling both knees up as if to put a barrier between me and him.

Velidan watched us calmly, sipping his wine. “They did not.”

Just as abruptly, Embriel returned to his seat. “A runekeeper. Good for you, Vel! I found some runekeepers a few years ago. In… what’s that city with the white spires?” His face puckered. “In the north. It’s got libraries. What’s it called?”

It took a long moment to realize he was addressing me. My mouth went bone dry. “L-Lighthold?”

He snapped his fingers. “That’s the one. Charming name. Garruk and I were sniffing the place out, and it turned out they had a few dozen runekeepers holed up there. All of them huddled around books and statues, trying to pray their way back to magic. And Garruk… well. He likes to have his fun, and I was feeling bold, so I told him to be as vicious as he liked. He made me sick.” He made a face. “And of course he kept drawing the dead back up, sending them wandering off to terrorize the city… honestly, the whole thing got really out of hand. He had a great time, though.”

I pressed numb hands to my mouth. The world seemed to spin around me.

One glinting, bronze eye closed in a wink. “Don’t fret, lamb. Most of us are better behaved. Garruk’s just ancient and angry. He remembers the good old days, before we all got locked away. Blames the flock for the shepherds’ protection.” Embriel leaned close again, holding me with eyes that glinted like a drawn blade. “And you’re clearly not a happy pet, so I bet you know exactly who came to save those other runekeepers. The loved ones, with their feeble little sparks and puffs of wind.” He slammed a fist down so hard I jumped. “No one. Dying sheep. Screaming for help.” He grabbed his glass and raised it high, as if toasting the sky itself. “And not a shepherd in sight.”

“What do you m-mean?” My heartbeat swelled to roar as I realized I’d spoken the question aloud.

“Brin,” Velidan gave me a thoughtful look, “has little context for most of this, and no understanding of her own meager powers. She’s very curious about magic.”

“Is she?” Embriel pointed a sweet smile my way. “Good for her! Perhaps she deserves to see something more exciting than your little healing trick, or whatever sparks she can call up.” He cocked his head to the side, pointed two fingers at me, then curled them down.

The shadows beneath our table twisted up, closing around my wrists with shocking weight. I jerked upright with a cry. It rose to a scream as my hips met marble and I was yanked forward and down, bent taut across the marble.

A pale hand slammed Embriel’s down inches from my face. The shadows snapped free and I staggered away until one palm found the wall.

“Mind your own rules.” Velidan released the other Fae with a shove. Dark wine pooled into a spiderweb of black lines between them.

“Oh, she’s fine. That didn’t hurt in the slightest. Did it, lamb?” Embriel’s tongue swept along his lower lip. “And did you see the look on her face?”

“I saw you spill perfectly good wine. Brin, go get us more.”

“W-what?” My ears were ringing. My legs were trembling.

“Ask for a pitcher of black vyrian and another glass.” Velidan drawled, nodding towards the stairs. Then, so sharply it jolted me into motion, “Now.”

Their voices echoed off the stone steps behind me. When they faded for silent solitude, I sagged against the wall in a dizzy, breathless daze. I couldn’t seem to stop shaking, nor chase away the remnant of cold pressure around my wrists.

I wasn’t sure which part of the violence had upset me more; its suddenness, or how calm they all were in the aftermath. As if throwing someone facedown onto a table, then nearly breaking the table, was a perfectly normal activity.

They think frightening me is funny. Salt, it’s probably a game to them. But there are… surely there are lines. Rules. Velidan promised I wouldn’t be hurt, and he needs me to not be hurt. They’ll scare me, but they won’t go too far.

The thought was meager comfort.

So I found a better one.

He hasn’t been lying to me. Not about everything, at least; not about Embriel or Sedgewick Vale, not about Fae planning to attack the realm. He didn’t make it up, it’s actually happening and…

And we’re going to stop it. We are. We have to.

I steadied myself against the wall. Pressed my palms to the stone, breathing in and out as I imagined sunlit steel and a voice like thunder.

Everything can die.

I clung to that ember, fanning it furiously as I made my way downstairs and then back up with a large pitcher of sin-black liquid. The smells of burnt nuts and bitter fruit stung my nostrils with every breath. When I reached the top step, all three Fae were sitting and I was a ramrod pillar of coiled heat.

“Lord Velidan has informed me,” Embriel took the pitcher, voice oozing dark amusement, “that my joke was not funny. Apparently you’re quite fragile.” He refilled the glasses with a deep inhale. “Mm. Strong stuff. Trying to get me drunk, Vel?”

“You’re more fun when you’re drunk.”

“Hah! I’m always fun. Anyways, lamb, have a seat. You asked about magic, and Vel says you actually do have a few nifty little tricks.” His eyes sharpened to a shrewd slant above the wineglass. “Which is… surprising.”

“Brin can call up fire, light, and rain,” Velidan said smoothly as I sank back into my chair. He gave me a warning look, then finished, “And bees.”

“Bees?” Saffine choked on her drink. “Dawn, why? What’s the point of that?”

“Bees. Cute.” Embriel leaned back, all languid grace and wry mirth. “Show us.”

My pulse jumped. “I… it doesn’t work here.”

“Do as you’re told.”

After an uncertain glance towards Velidan, who nodded, I traced the zigzag pattern of zellen with a whisper. My skin tingled and my chest constricted, but nothing else happened.

Embriel’s fingers mirrored the gesture. “Mm. Beautiful. You learned more of the bright tongue than me, Vel. What’s zellen?”

“Hive, perhaps? Swarm?” He lifted one broad shoulder. “Something to that effect.”

“What’s the bright tongue?” I found myself scooting forwards, looking between the two with baited breath.

Velidan took in my expression with a little smile. “A language from before the realms were sealed away, Brin. One devoted to the shaping of magic, crafted by noble Fae from the Day and Gilded Courts.”

“Then adulterated by lesser Courts,” Embriel took a sip of wine. “And gifted in fragments to mortal fools.”

My forehead scrunched and my mind roiled. Just as before, the baffling twist on history and what I knew to be true was making me dizzy.

The bronze-haired Fae tilted his head, eyeing me slyly. “The gifts of your shepherds, lamb. And when these innocent fingers,” he clasped my hand, pressing his thumb up the length of my palm, “trace a rune, that’s you pleading for their help.”

I tugged free, viscerally aware that the freedom was given instead of won. “You’re saying… what? When I use a rune, I’m speaking a Fae tongue? And I’m asking for a Fae’s help?” My wide eyes found Velidan’s.

“Yes,” he said simply, and the answer shook me to my core.

“Some bleeding heart,” Saffine hissed after downing her glass, then pouring another, “hears your mewling mortal cry from across the realms and decides to drip a little magic in. As if they don’t have anything better to do!”

“Clearly they do, these days,” Embriel snickered. “Ever since some fool decided to face down a living terror. Thought he’d be a beacon of hope, vanquish the Nightmare and return to his realm a glorious hero.”

“And instead he died,” the sing-song lilt was laced with laughter. “It’s like poetry, isn’t it? I have a theory, you know. I think he was someone important. Noble, powerful, loved… and now they’re all in mourning. Too blinded by their own tears to care what happens anywhere else.”

“See, I think she’s wrong.” Embriel drummed his fingers on the cracked table, voice dropping to a vicious pitch. “I think Andruil’s finally snapped. I think he’s furious. Stewing up in the Day Court sky, watching all his children bleed power. I think he lost his oldest, favorite son ages ago, and then twenty years ago one of the other little brats got himself killed trying to save the poor humans.”

“He thinks dad finally closed the door for good,” Saffine nudged Velidan with a giggle, sloshing wine onto the table. It seeped into the cracks, leaving dark stains behind. “Decided he’s done letting Day Court Fae sacrifice anything for another realm. Poetry, like I said.”

“Two of my guesses, as well.” The soft, steady voice beside me held none of the other two Fae’s obvious glee. His endless eyes held me as the night sky spun above and I tried to wrap my mind around the reality of all this. “And the humans think magic is dying, because they simply don’t understand where it comes from.”

“Poor things,” Embriel clinked his glass to Saffine’s. “Now there’s a whole realm ripe for the taking, with scarcely more than a self-stylized holy order defending it.”

“But where…” I could scarcely find my voice. I pressed both hands to my temples, sucking in mouthfuls of fragrant air and trying to find shreds of reason within their words. “Some of our magic still works! You said the other runekeepers could still do small things, and my runes work!”

Embriel raised one shoulder leisurely. “So? All that means is that one or two Day Fae are still trying. But,” he snickered, “from all I’ve seen the past decade, they’re obviously not doing very well.”

“It’s a few weak sympathizers,” Saffine took another gulp. Wine dribbled down her chin. “That’s all. One or two bleeding hearts left in their broken Court, throwing scraps in answer to your prayers.”

“Someone likes you, little lamb.” The oily purr crawled beneath my skin. It came ahead of a mocking smile. “Isn’t that sweet? They’re not powerful enough to really help, and they obviously can’t come save you from anything. But someone tries to answer whenever you bleat.” Embriel lifted his glass high, as if toasting the very stars.

Frost crawled across my skin. I shook my head. It was too much. Too mad, too unbelievable, too overwhelming.

Just when it threatened to press the very breath from my lungs, Velidan leaned forwards to pour himself a fresh cup.

“And perhaps,” he murmured, holding my gaze as the words sank straight into my bones, “someone’s very confused.”
