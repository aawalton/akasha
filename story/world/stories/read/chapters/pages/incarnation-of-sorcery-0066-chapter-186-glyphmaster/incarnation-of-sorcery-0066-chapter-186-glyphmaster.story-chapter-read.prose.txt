Ryland didn’t wait for anything. Now that he had more or less confirmed what had happened to the Ephemeral Colossus, he could move on. Finding another one wasn’t beyond him. But he had other plans for the moment. Letting the Order get away and assault the next Colossus wasn’t something he’d abide by, so he was going to stop them.

Stop them right from his current location to boot.

Sadly, the magic required for something of the sort wasn’t something he could cast easily. It was no mere spell creating an instantaneous effect. His next endeavour would be something that took time and effort, that would be so complex, he’d need Glyphs he didn’t have.

So, for that reason, he needed to visit the one person in the universe who had an even vaster collection of magic than Ryland himself did.

“I will return,” he said. “Before long. [Translocate].”

The rift only needed him to step through the curtain of darkness before he arrived at his destination. And what a destination it was. For all that Ryland had been here dozens of times over his long life, he never really got tired of the wonder surrounding him.

Xyke had constructed her pocket Realm herself. And true to her proclivities, everything around Ryland was made of Conceptual Glyphs. There was no reality present. None of the Glyphs ended up representing anything palpable or visible or somehow interactable with a physical sense.

Instead, it was all magic. It was all written. For Glyphmaster Xyke was the Incarnation of Inscribing.

Ryland took it all in for a moment. Here was a column of Glyphs towering into the sky that was just a congestion of Conceptual Glyphs about the sky. There was a sea of Conceptual Glyphs about the ocean, creating crests and troughs as more Glyphs regarding the wind blew across them.

It was as though Ryland had entered a world where reality was replaced with writing. The vastness of the spread of written magic outshone even the range with which his Incarnation form turned everything to Conceptual Glyphs.

Ryland soon located an Encapsulated Glyph of Teleport. Touching it took him straight to his target—the Glyphmaster of the Cosmos, Xyke.

Normally, entering someone else’s pocket Realm was practically impossible if the creator didn’t choose to share the method of entry. But Xyke had created a magical backdoor that would allow Ryland to enter her abode in one specific location. She had placed a handy little Glyph for Ryland to teleport as well, because her pocket Realm suppressed most spatial magic.

At his next destination, Ryland found himself inside a giant sphere of condensed magic. There were so many Conceptual Glyphs forced into one location and one strange shape, that the density reminded him of an Incarnation’s body.

“Ryland?” Xyke’s voice echoed in from everywhere. Some of the Glyphs pulled away from the sphere before joining together to form a vaguely humanoid figure. “You’re disturbing my beauty sleep?”

“Not without good reason.” Ryland raised an eyebrow. “Also, why would you need beauty sleep? You exist in this disembodied state where you’re nothing but a collection of Glyphs.”

“What? You don’t think Glyphs can be beautiful?”

Ryland knew Xyke well enough to know that he was almost on the verge of insulting her magic, which was something the Glyphmaster was very sensitive to. One would think that one of the strongest beings in all of the cosmos with more knowledge on Glyphs than anyone else would remain confident about her own capabilities. And she was, no doubt about it.

Yet her sensitivity to anyone or anything throwing even perceived insults at her Glyphs made her a dangerous person to talk to at the best of times.

“Not at all,” Ryland said. “In fact, I have some Glyphs of my own that I think are quite gorgeous.”

Xyke leaned forward in interest. It felt like a lightning bolt was standing very close to him. “Glyphs of your own, you say?”

“I hate to disappoint you, but I merely meant Encapsulated Glyphs.”

Xyke drew away in disappointment. “I should have expected as such. Anyway, since you’re here, you might as well spill why you’ve come this time. I refuse to go on another walk. I did that three decades ago, and I still haven’t recovered from the ordeal.”

“Ah, yes. Fresh air and the great outdoors. Such an ordeal.”

Xyke might not have had a face to express herself anymore, but her glare was still palpable.

“Anyway,” Ryland said. “I need a few different Glyphs specifically to set a curse circle.”

“Oh,” Xyke’s Glyphs glimmered like a rainbow. “The Great Marionys Ryland resorting to curses and hexes like the witches of Realm 15. What has the cosmos come to, I wonder?”

“Indeed. Things are astir in ways I’d rather they hadn’t. But one cannot choose the times they encounter, only deal with them as best as they can.”

“And your choice is to curse everyone involved.”

“Not everyone. Just those who deserve it.”

“Hmph. I have no reason to doubt your judgement. It’s even more accurate than mine at times.”

“Yes, I’d like to think my judgment about such things is better than a decades-long recluse.”

“Come, come. You want those Glyphs or not?”

Ryland stopped teasing and told Xyke just what he needed. She pored over his list and called forth every Glyph Ryland requested, taking no time at all to find them. It seemed she had grown her Glyphic capabilities while remaining locked within her pocket Realm. Maybe the fact that she had once considered Ryland a rival had spurred her to even greater heights.

When she reached the very end of the Glyphs, Xyke’s magic glowed a frowning green.

“There is no Glyph,” she said. “Called and come with me to a Transient Realm.”

“Well, I never said that was a Glyph,” Ryland said.

Xyke brandished the list at him. “Well, this says otherwise. It’s written there. Right at the top. List of Glyphs.”

Ryland sighed. “I promise I didn’t come here to convince you to take a walk and enjoy a relaxing breeze. I just want to rescue my protégé.”

For once, Xyke didn’t have an immediate reply. She could be harsh and biting and occasionally more eccentric than him, but that didn’t mean she was fundamentally a bad person.

“Explain what happened, please,” she said.

So, Ryland did. He explained his goal of recapturing the pieces of himself, how that had led him to Arcoryx Academy, everything with the Living Mana, his encounters with the Flourinus Order, and how they had kidnapped Acrypha into the Overrealm.

“Where the Great Gods,” Xyke said, her Glyphs glowing a crimson of distaste. “As they like to call themselves, are perched on their thrones of hate and malicious ambition.”

“Hate that is mostly directed at me, yes,” Ryland said.

Xyke’s Glyphs glimmered again, changing colours rapidly as she considered different thoughts. As always, Ryland was patient. She was deliberating. Her decision would be sound. As was proven when the Glyphs settled on a soft yellow colour.

“Alright, then,” she said. “I will come with you. If you intend to face the Great Gods, then even you will need all the help you can get.”

It was perhaps the graveness of the situation that had Xyke agreeing to come along with nary a complaint. Ryland expressed his gratitude. She waved it off, though the shiny silvery sheen of her Glyphic form confirmed that she was pleased.

In minutes, they were ready to head out. Ryland pointed out exactly where the Transient Realm was supposed to be. Its location ought to have remained static. The Ephemeral Colossus had passed away, and as the main source of magic empowering the Transient Realm was gone, it no longer had the ability to shift or move on its own.

This was proven true when Ryland and Xyke appeared in the Transient Realm through another rift.

“Incredible,” she said. Her Glyphic form was now gone, replaced by that of a dragonkin. She favoured dragonkin bodies, though this wasn’t the original one she had been born with. “They actually murdered the poor soul.”

“Indeed. Thus the curse.”

Xyke’s golden eyes hardened like a sun in a drought. “Let’s get started, then. I will lay the foundations, and you can empower the Glyphs.”

Their combined experience meant the curse circle was placed in less than half an hour, despite Ryland’s unfamiliarity with the Glyphs. Magic was flowing into the Inscription with greater and greater power. One of the Subglyphs was the Glyph of Receptacle, which allowed one to condense a far greater amount of mana into the curse or spell than one could cast instantly.

Xyke clapped her scaled hands when done. “It should be ready now. Your idea is rather brilliant.”

“Thank you,” Ryland said. “It’s merely using time as a vector.”

She shook her head, the jewels dangling from her curling black horns glinting and tinkling together. “You used your Soul Glyph of Time, yes. But you’ve also used your Soul Glyph of Genesis to reach the Orders’ very souls, and then my Glyphs come into play to establish guilt and ensure the right mages are targeted. Are you ready to activate it?”

“I suspect the Transient Realm will be somewhat stressed when we start,” Ryland said. “It’s unlikely the Order didn’t think of this countermeasure and then plan accordingly.”

“I hardly care. They’ll get what they deserve, and that’s what matters.”

Ryland wasn’t so sure of that. But he went on with the curse anyway. It was high time they started.

The mountains around the Transient Realm started rumbling. All those depthless pits shifted and coalesced, quickly coming together to form a figure of darkness, though it didn’t remain that way for long. Far above them, those endless waterfalls plunging from the sky turned into streams of liquid light, filling up the giant-shaped pit until there was a figure in truth.

An enormous, titanic figure that could once have been an Ephemeral Colossus. One that housed an Incarnation.

But of course, the Colossus was long gone. There was no Incarnation. So instead, the glimmering liquid energy started shooting off its gargantuan body, plunging through the air with crimson bursts and shooting right into the perimeter of the Transient Realm.

“Off they go,” Xyke said. “How long do you think they’ll need to find their targets?”

“I’m not sure,” Ryland said. “Depends entirely on what precautions the Order has taken against curses such as these.”

This whole thing qualified as a curse rather than a regular spell circle because Ryland really wasn’t powering those deadly scarlet streams. Instead, he had merely called down the essence of the Colossus that was infused with the Transient Realm, seeking to help it reconstruct its body.

But said body was dead. Whatever kind of soul it had possessed was long gone. Upon finding its body in such a state, the essence had turned violently aggressive.

So, the next part of the curse, the one that allowed Ryland to direct that devastating aggression productively, came as a result of the Glyphs Xyke had provided. Her powers had allowed Ryland to pinpoint just where the culprits lay, and directed the remaining essence of the Ephemeral Colossus to attack the Order.

Everything shook. The tremor was so harsh, so unending, that Ryland was forced to tear his attention away from the curse Inscription.

“Looks like the Overrealm doesn’t appreciate being invaded by foreign energy,” Xyke said.

Her statement was astute. The edges of the Transient Realm were closing in on them, as though an unfathomably titanic beast was crushing the whole region in its grip. The pressure was going from palpable to unignorable, quickly starting to compress upon them.

“Ryland,” Xyke said. There was the slightest of edges to her voice. “Do you think they’ve trapped you here?”

What an odd question. Yet, once again, it was astute. Ryland tried casting [Translocate] yet again, but found himself blocked from doing so. Mad. To think they had cast an Inscription that kept aggressors within the Transient Realm locked inside until its destruction.

Now, the question remained whether the “they” was the Flourinus Order or the Great Gods.

“Don’t worry,” Ryland said. “Their mistake is in assuming I have only one means of moving from one location to another.”

Xyke had to raise her voice over the grinding noise of the entire Transient Realm being crushed. “I should think the Great Gods would cover their bases well.”

Ah, so she was positive this was the work of the Great Gods. It did make sense. They were being crushed by the Overrealm, which was the Plane of the Great Gods. And their divine magic was a level beyond what even Ryland was capable of, so it stood to reason that they’d be the ones blocking his rifts.

But Ryland’s point remained. They had blocked one avenue of traversal. Ryland had a great deal more.

Though, it turned out Xyke was right again. The Great Gods were covering their bases well. No matter what sort of Subglyph Ryland changed or how he modified his [Translocate] spell circle, he couldn’t get it to work.

“[Rewind],” Ryland incanted. “[Relative Existence].”

It was getting harder and harder just casting spells. The trembling was now shaking everything, including the air that Ryland and Xyke were floating in, which caused their spells to come out with stops and stutters. Ryland just made sure he didn’t accidentally bite off his own tongue.

Meanwhile, Xyke was casting her own Glyphs everywhere. Not just from her own manasoul, but also by turning the world into Glyphs.

When the Transient Realm tried hemming them in and closing upon them, a wall of inescapable Glyphs created a barrier that none of even the Great Gods could break through. Not yet, at least. They would force their way in eventually. But until then, Xyke had gifted Ryland some time.

Time he used to its best potential by [Rewind] eventually reaching the exact spell Ryland was looking for.

“Really?” Xyke asked. “That’s your big idea? To use their own magic against them through those abominable Wound Corridors?”

“You mistake the gist, Xyke,” Ryland said. “I’m not seeking to use [Wound Corridor] as my escape route. Not technically, and not spatially. Instead, we are traversing through the Wound Corridor because it was here a while ago, well before the current situation took place.”

“Oh!” Xyke’s draconic eyes became excited slits. “We’ll be travelling through time rather than space.”

“In a sense, yes. We aren’t going into the drastic future nor are we affecting incidents in the past. Rather, we’re using the echo of a spell’s manifestation to escape. And I’m quite certain the Order would never be expecting something like this.”

Xyke’s Glyphs started shattering entirely. However strong they might have been, the force crushing the entire pocket Realm wasn’t going to let the Glyphs last long.

It was a good thing they had an escape route, then.

“Let’s go, Xyke,” Ryland said.

“Do you know where this will drop us?” she asked. Despite the question, she was clearly ready to jump into the Wound Corridor.

“Not really. But I think we’ll find where the Order is supposed to be. Or rather, where they were before they absconded into the Overrealm, which might provide some very interesting clues.”
