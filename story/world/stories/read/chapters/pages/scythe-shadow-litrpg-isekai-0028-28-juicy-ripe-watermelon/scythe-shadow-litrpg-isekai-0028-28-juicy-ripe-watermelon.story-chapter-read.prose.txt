The white stallion’s powerful legs stampeded below, as I held tight to the reigns and squeezed my calves against his torso.

Wind swept back my unnatural teal hair and my Amber cloak thrashed behind me like a flag in a hurricane bearing the unwanted sigil of Valciferous.

I was no rider, but this was glorious. There was a weightlessness to it, yet also a strength. I felt like I could fly. Free from the bounds of the earth.

I felt a little guilty commandeering Calliope's horse, but it was just for a little while. To get away. A one way ticket to solitude.

And I had no doubt that Allium would be able to find his way back to Calliope the same way Nuzzle always seemed to find me.

It had been a challenge, at first, to convince Allium to even let me mount onto his saddle, but the creature clearly remembered me. I’d first approached slowly, pet his face and gave him some grass from my hand. He truly was a beautiful horse—despite being zombified and all. His pure white coat glistened in the light, almost silver. Several patches of skin and muscle were missing, exposing the skeleton within, yet he had no drapes of hanging flesh or signs of blood and gore. He smelled like a farm, of course, but not of decay and rot like some of the other undead beasts.

He allowed me to approach easy enough, but every time I got a boot positioned in one of the stirrups he would take a step or two forward, pulling my leg so I’d lose my balance. Almost like he was toying with me. Clearly, the horse learned from his master. Fucking Calliope. Several passersby had laughed at my failures until I'd led him to a more secluded alley and finally hauled myself successfully up into the saddle.

It took me several minutes to figure out what the hell I was doing, but I’d actually paid attention when I rode with Calliope, and I recalled the basics from that one riding lesson I had as a child.

I’d never rode this fast, though. Hopefully I’d be able to convince Allium to stop once the time came. No need to think about that just yet, though. That was a problem for future Zephyr. For now, I’d just enjoy the wind. Revel in the freedom. Feel the—

An orange blob exploded from beneath the stallion’s white mane and attached itself to my face.

“Holy fu—”

I fell backwards, completely off the saddle, and my feet flung into the air. The ground slammed against my back, knocking the air out of me. Flashes of light danced around my vision like stars.

And a little orange lizard slathered its sandpaper tongue all over my face.

“Fucking Nuzzle,” I hissed, breath returning to my lungs.

He licked up inside my nostril one last time, before hopping back onto my chest and sitting his bum down.

I balled my fist and wound up to slap him away, but stopped.

“Damnit Nuzzle. Why are you so fucking adorable?”

He hopped twice, his smokey trail mimicking his movement, then sat again, wagging his tail all expectant-like. I cupped a hand around his head and he nuzzled into it. I let my head fall back into the grass and stared up at the cloudless azure sky. A few of the misty blue birds flew by singing their lament.

Why did I always seem to end up on the ground?

Eventually, I sat up. To my surprise, Allium was only a short ways ahead, grazing on a patch of red flowers. I was again amazed at how well trained he was. But maybe that wasn't it at all. She'd indicated he was more like Nuzzle than a normal horse. Bonded. Intelligent—though that last part didn't really apply to Nuzzle, who was now chasing his own tail among a cluster of pink flowers.

I got back up in the saddle—without trouble this time—Nuzzle surfing my shoulder. We continued on, following my instinct based on my memory of the tavern’s map and the direction of the invisible light source in the sky. I rested a hand on Allium's neck as we rode along, feeling his coarse hair, and whispered a word of thanks for letting me borrow him.

It took nearly three full hours, but eventually the distinct scent of a sea breeze danced through the air. I pushed the stallion harder, and we soon came upon a rise in the land. The horse trotted me to the top.

The sight took my breath away nearly as much as Nuzzle's surprise attack.

A steep dune sloped down from the edge of the Meadow, leading to a thin beach that stretched north and south until it disappeared around a bend in the coast. Beyond it, a crystalline blue ocean stretched out as far as the eye could see. Unending. The water was glass, not a single wave breaking the tension, and light twinkled across the surface like a billion galaxies.

Fuck me… In a place so hopeless and heartless, how could something so beautiful exist?

Nuzzle chirped and scuttled down the dune until he reached the edge of the water.

I urged Allium to follow down a more gradual decline of the dune. We trotted along the coastline a ways. Eventually, a pair of white glowing cotton ball like entities appeared over the water. They danced and twirled above the sparkling surface, keeping pace with us, before zipping off. Some other unknown critter of this world, like Nuzzle and the blue birds I'd seen.

After a while, I dismounted and allowed Nuzzle to splash around in the shallows.

“You like that, huh?” I asked the little salamander.

He chirped and dunked under the water, then resurfaced a second later.

I removed my boots and tied them together to hang over the horse’s saddle. It had been so long since I felt the sand between my toes. Avery would've loved this. The sea always had a magic to it, and this one was no different, despite being in the Umbral Plane.

With Calliope’s stallion in tow and Nuzzle proudly stomping in front of me, we travelled north along the water’s edge. I allowed the sea to tickle my feet, the breeze to mess my hair, and I forgot—for a time—that I was in the land of Sins and Reapers. My responsibilities slipped effortlessly from my mind and I simply enjoyed myself.

Every once in a while, out over the water, another of those cotton balls rose from the ocean and glided over the dunes toward the Meadow, like basketball-sized dandelion fuzz. I tried to get close to one to examine it, but it moved too quick and avoided me.

I'd noticed a few like them in my travels so far, but thought they were simply a large Penumbran version of those floating white fluffy seeds we had back home. Milkweed or something. But that clearly wasn't the case. These weren't just blowing in the wind. They moved with intention, sentient at some basic level.

As I made my way around a sharp curve in the coastline, a small cliff came into view ahead, slicing through the beach. Carved stairs curled up the ruddy stone face leading to a tower that must have stood over a hundred feet tall. A lighthouse.

I didn’t see any boats out in the water and there was nothing on the tavern’s map I could recall. No islands, no other continents. So what purpose would a lighthouse serve?

Unless there was something out there far beyond the sea.

I squinted at the dark horizon, as if that would help me see any better into the void.

Maybe… I focused on what might have been a splotch of something, but it was difficult to see with the faded gray sky above it.

Allium pulled on the reins in my hand and kicked at the sand.

I let go of the reins and patted the horse’s nose, trying to settle him, but he kept nodding his head up and down, braying. His eyes were wide, almost afraid.

But of what?

I looked back out at the ocean. The spot I'd seen was gone now, if it had ever been there, indistinguishable from everything else.

Nuzzle scurried up my leg. His little toes shivered nervously on my shoulder.

“You alright, buddy?” I held out a hand for him to hop onto.

Allium whinnied and pitched back on hind legs. I reached out for the reins, but fell to my hands and knees as he twisted away. He bolted back in the direction we’d come, sand flying behind his hooves.

“Oh, come on!” I shouted. “So much for being well-trained!”

How stupid did I have to be to let go of the reins? This is what I deserved. Fucking horse messing with me again.

I started jogging after the asshole of a creature. He’d have to stop eventually and still hadn't made it back to the curve of the coast. But damn was he fast when set free.

Then, out of nowhere, a giant black gorilla beast launched over the top of the dunes next to Allium. It flew through the air like some monster out of a Michael Bay film, oversized fists clenched together over its head like a hammer. A blue aura radiated around it. A cracked skull hung overhead.

Unchained.

Its fists smashed down directly on top of Allium.

The horse exploded into a stain of gore on the beach like a juicy, ripe watermelon dropped from three stories up.
