Ixion tore reality apart with a loud CRACK.

He emerged from with violent lurch, hanging motionless in the sky as the scent of ozone and frozen time dissipated around him. Below, an archipelago lay scattered across the endless blue like shards of emerald on a sapphire cloth. The Tidesong Isles. They looked deceptively peaceful from this height, the morning sun painting the waves in strokes of brilliant gold.

He ignored the beauty. His senses, honed by centuries of walking the hidden paths between worlds, reached out, not for sight or sound, but for the very texture of reality.

And there it was.

It was a sour note in the cosmic song, a splinter under the fingernail of existence. A deep and resonant fracture, not of one discipline, but two. Time and space had both been violently manipulated here—bent, broken, then brutally snapped back into a semblance of order. The echo was vast, washing over the entire chain of islands, too diffuse to pinpoint an exact source. But it was recent. And it was powerful.

The one you seek is here, a voice whispered in the hollows of his mind. It was a silken, insidious sound, like honey mixed with poison. I can feel the resonance. So much power…

Ixion frowned. Why speak now, when it had been silent for months? He huffed. A question for another time.

Are you sure you can handle—

Silence, Ixion commanded, his own thought a blade of ice. He felt the entity within recoil. He did not need its counsel. He did not want its hunger.

For months, he’d been scouring the oceans of this world, chasing whispers and legends. Two of the five Divine Vestments were his: the Aegis of the Void, the armor that now served as his second skin and his god’s cage; and the Star-Strider’s Greaves, which allowed him to tread the paths of translocation. But the others remained lost. The Crown of Whispers, the Gauntlets of Unmaking…and the sword. Thal’ruun’s Relic, the Blade of Collapsing Stars. A weapon so potent, so dangerous, he’d believed it lost to the ages.

Until it had been used.

He’d felt its slumbering power being manipulated—a spatial distortion here, a tear in reality there. Minor tricks, but enough to prove the sword was on this world. And now, this new, massive fracture—a wound torn and reset by a master of time.

This Wielder’s power is immense, the god, Thal’ruun, whispered again, unable to contain its excitement. Imagine it… The sword, and this new wellspring of temporal might. We could finally ascend. We could join the Trinity themselves.

Ixion clenched his jaw, the metal of his helm grinding softly. The Trinity. The thought of them made the armor feel tight, restrictive. For all their posturing as cosmic arbiters, they were just gods like any other, content to watch the lesser worlds burn as long as their own power remained absolute.

“I have no desire to join them,” Ixion murmured, his voice a low vibration in the empty air. “I intend to unseat them.”

His goal was not to climb the ladder of divinity. It was to kick it over. To sever the ties that let gods feed on mortal worlds, to cauterize the wounds before they could fester into another war. He’d read about one unholy war between the gods. And he had no desire to see one for himself. The cost was too high, the devastation too absolute.

And the growing number of god-bonds on this world…it was a problem. A beacon. Every new Wielder, every surge of divine power, was a flare in the dark, risking the attention of things that swam in the deeper voids of the cyberverse. Things that made gods like Thal’ruun look like fledglings.

You say that, but you hunt my Vestments, Thal’ruun goaded. You use my power. You cannot deny what I am. What we could be.

“You are a tool,” Ixion stated flatly. “A means to an end. Nothing more.” He let a cold wash of emotion flow through his veins, silencing the parasitic god. Let them see him as a monster, a rogue who used a god’s power to hunt his own kind. It was a small price to pay for a universe that continued to exist.

His gaze swept across the islands again, settling on the largest, the one at the heart of the archipelago. Twilight Peak. The disturbance was strongest around there, the echoes of time and space layered thickest. He would start there. He would scour every island, every reef, every underwater canyon if he had to. He would find the one responsible for this temporal scar.

And he would find his sword.

He gathered the navy mist around him, a cloak of translocation and intent. Then, with a loud SNAP, the world dissolved.
