“Oh, well,” she smiled. “It probably may be quite private.”

“Still, it seems to align with our plans,” Lila said. “Isn’t it?”

“Alright, let’s return to our house,” Aira said. “We all have things to explore and discuss. I’m getting to the 105th level soon and I’ll get another skill. Lila skipped through the 20th level recently, she has to have a new skill. You, Alliot, are at fourty right now. So, as I said, lots of things to discuss.”

The walk back to their quarters was silent, each lost in thought as they processed what had transpired. The Smoldering Ember pulsed warmly in Aira's pocket, its heat a constant reminder of what they'd accomplished—and what it might mean for Alliot's improved connection to the System, for the whole group, and for the world as a whole. The crowning achievement of their month in Lamcada had raised as many questions as it answered. And when they came back to the house, Ronorrh was waiting there.

“So, it seems it was a success,” she exclaimed rubbing her hands together. “Will you show it to me? It has been a while since an artifact not related to the old human tech was created here in Lamcada!”

Aira took the new shard from the pouch. It glowed with a steady, internal heat, apperaing as a small, cracked piece of obsidian. It shone with a molten orange light through all the minute cracks, as though the fire within never truly died. Warm to the touch, it radiated the energy of a fire that once raged but now simmers quietly, waiting to be rekindled.

“Oh, so pretty!” Ronorrh said. “May I see the other one as well?”

“Sure,” said Aira. “Why not.”

She didn’t have a chance to hold both of them together in her hands until now. Everything happened so fast. They seemed to pulsate in accord, sharing the same frequency with the power of the Rune embedded inside of her during the transition to this world.

"Ooooww! How peculiar!" Ronorrh's eyes sparkled with interest, the flames within them dancing faster as she leaned in to study the Shards. "No wonder Ainorrh's been keeping her flames so tightly controlled over this little project of hers. The old wind-witch always did love her secrets." She tapped her chin thoughtfully. "But you need two more, yes? Veskai shouldn't be a problem—they're practical sorts. But Ainorrh's city?" A knowing smile crept across her face. "She orchestrated everything specifically so that you'll have to come back to her! Oh, she's clever as fire finding the path of least resistance. Always has been."

“What do you know about her plans?” asked Alliot. “We were trying to figure things out for quite some time, but it’s a tough puzzle.”

"Nah, it's not my secret to share," Ronorrh said with a theatrical wink, flames dancing in her eyes as she leaned forward conspiratorially. "But I'll let you peek behind the curtain, just a tiny glimpse." She traced a pattern in the air, her finger leaving a momentary trail of sparks. "In my opinion, we've struck the perfect balance with humans here in Lamcada—a dance of mutual benefit. You know we have to rely on them to keep our population intact, yes?" She arched an eyebrow, her expression suddenly innocent as a child's while her eyes held ancient mischief. "Or did your precious Ainorrh conveniently forget to mention that part of our existence?"

“Yes, sure, we all are dependent on the natural flow of converted,” said Alliot. “Spontaneous manifestations prompted by the Nexus, all of that.”

“Oh, sweet summer child,” said Ronorrh. “This will be massive, I can feel it! No, I mean constantly converting humans into enlightened. Sort of like Aira did it with Lila. Without that, we would never be able to preserve our civilization. We don’t breed. And sometimes we die. We live hundreds of years. And natural flow of transformation just isn’t enough to compensate for all the losses.”

"So what," Lila snapped, amber eyes flaring with the fire of memories still too painful to touch, "you mass-snatch humans like forest thieves and transform them against their will?" Her hands clenched into fists, each finger rigid with the echo of her own death and unwanted rebirth. "By the twisted branches, you all are just the same! I've been hearing whispers my whole life—tales from the outer settlements of people vanishing on moonless nights, offered up like sacrifices to appease the undead! Was I just another quota to be filled?"

“Oh, no!” Ronorrh exclaimed. “Nothing brutal like that. That’s Ainorrh’s way. And most of your main allies, Alliot, choose the same approach. But us, you see humans here. We trade. It their free choice to become enlightened. Maybe with a little persuasion from their leaders. But they profit quite a lot from that arrangement, don’t worry.”

“But what are you saying about Ainorrh?” said Alliot. “I thought Wentouk doesn’t have any diplomatic relations with them.”

“I really shouldn’t tell you,” Ronorrh said coyly. “But you three were very helpful here. Taught us a lot. Showed us a lot. Alright, I’ll tell you. But promise not to tell anyone how you figured that out. You know that we produce and upgrade human gadgets. The whole trade with them depends on that. We are not alone, of course. Other earth-affinity cities have their own developments in that area. So, we supply this stuff to other cities and they trade with humans. All of us trade with humans.”

Ronorrh paused for a moment as if considering something.

“Well, most of us,” she said. “Ziemrot has their values. They don’t want to turn humans. So, they are dying out. That’s why Kha-no-rr is so spooked. They see enemies under every tree.”

“So, you see,” she continued, “It’s a balanced system. But each of us chooses their own path to the future. I think our wait is more… humane, if you wish.”

“Why should I believe you?” asked Alliot. “It goes against everything we’ve been taught.”

“You don’t have to believe me,” Ronorrh said. “Moreover, I don’t need you to believe me. I don’t even need you to create the fourth Shard. It’s Ainorrh’s project, not mine. But then, talking about what you’ve been taught. How did they teach you to call humans?”

Alliot took a step back, not sure what to say.

“Please, we are all friends here,” Ronorrh smiled. “Nobody would be angry. Am I right, Lila?”

“We call them ‘barbarians’,” said Alliot.

“Not very friendly, right?” said Ronorrh. “I’d even say a bit racist, isn’t it? They have technology, they have governance. They just don’t have magic. And, in the end, we need them. But I’ve said enough. I hope this helps you when you need to make some tough decisions.”

“And Aira,” she continued. “I hope to see you back here at some moment in the future!”

“Sure,” Aira was a bit confused by this speech. “Why not, it would be nice to see you again.”
