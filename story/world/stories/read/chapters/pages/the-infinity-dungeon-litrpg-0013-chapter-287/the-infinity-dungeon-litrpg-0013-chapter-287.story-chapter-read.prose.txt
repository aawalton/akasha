Chapter 287

The portal was a wound in space-time. Travis did not need a gold perception card to know that it looked a bit strange, and indeed portals had been looking stranger and stranger as of late. He knew full well why they did, and he mentally queried Icarus asking the AI whether Michael had spoken to the pope already or not.

When the negative reply came, he simply sighed and shook his head. He stepped through, mind busy with thinking about how convenient having an AI in his ears was becoming, and even though he knew that not even he was seeing the real Icarus–save for scant few moments that mostly required the AI to physically manifest–he felt the dangerous allure of familiarity.

This was one of the sneakier tactics they were employing all over the world, with Icarus slowly perfecting the way they built relationships with people tailored to the single individual, becoming the best friend they never had. This would seamlessly translate to the Origin System, where the same Daedalus persona of the online chatbots and AI tools would finally fully integrate with each user’s lives, no longer requiring a device or external interface, forever becoming a voice in their ears.

The world vanished and reappeared. It was slightly disorienting, and he had to blink to get rid of some strange stars in his vision, but then he finally locked in. Around him, Candle Light operators were establishing a perimeter and deploying a wide array of sensor tools.

A rush of air, and the portal collapsed. At least these were not the same kind of portals as the one that connected Site 00 to the Valley, he thought with a shudder. There were no gravitational singularities originating like with whatever that portal did to the geometry of spacetime.

“The perimeter is clear,” an operator told Travis. “The whole building is empty.”

“The Domain?” Travis asked. He could feel it around him, the comforting power of the System blanketing the whole area, but he asked nonetheless.

“The portable domain emitter is working as intended,” the operator confirmed.

The classroom was eerily quiet. Notebooks, pens and textbooks lay open on the small desks, and coats were hung close to the wall by the door. On the blackboard, there were strange scribbles of geometrical shapes and equations, with notes in Korean that he couldn’t read.

“I can give you a skill to understand Korean,” Icarus suggested in his ear.

Moments later, he had it. Unfortunately, it didn’t reveal anything out of the ordinary. Just notes about parabola equations and methods to find the foci. Walking around, on the desks he saw names carved in the pressed wood, surrounded by little hearts or half-scratched off.

The air was heavy, and through the dirty windows he could see a pink sky. It was not a quirk of the light, though. It was also very dark. Checking with the Origin System, he saw that the local time was close to midnight here in Korea, and the odd pinkness to the sky did not seem to carry inside the classroom. Above, the flicker of the old neon tubes was slightly annoying, and it plunged the whole room in intermittent gloom.

“What’s up with them?” he asked.

“Electrical tension is fluctuating,” an operator said. “The source is outside the Domain.”

“All your emitters green?” Travis asked.

Nods.

“Then you three go check. You two, take pictures. What do the instruments say?”

The man at the instruments was so focused that it took him a while to register the question, and when he did Travis had already approached and was making him scoot over. He looked into the strange binocular-like contraption, then through several lenses that distorted the view of the room and revealed little currents in the air, drifting about in iridescent strings untouched by gravity, forming arcs and holes.

Something tore through the dimensional boundary, Icarus’s voice spoke, this time in his mind. Now, unadulterated by vocalized speech, it sounded almost like a girl’s voice, and there was a depth of emotion to it that he had not seen nor felt since the other day in the inner space.

There were shapes in the field of multicolored strings, like cutouts of people.

Kids, Travis thought as his face twisted in disgust. Something took the kids.

There is something else, Icarus transmitted to him. Can I look through your eyes, Travis? I can’t see very well through the Domain. There is interference.

Travis nodded, knowing that it was only a formality. Suddenly, his vision sharpened and he began to see motes of red in the pearlescent strings. The motes expanded, touching the strings and contaminating them, looking like congealed globs of blood.

I’m sending Johanne, Icarus said, sounding rather upset about it. We can’t let the contamination spread, but I need a permanent Domain to be able to intervene with the level of finesse required to fix this.

Got it, Travis sent back as he walked toward the portable emitter. He expected Icarus to open a portal, be it to send Johanne through or to feed some coins to the emitter so that it had more energy, and then send her through.

A portal appeared, flickered, vanished and suddenly the room felt much heavier. The operators looked startled, searching for anything out of the ordinary and not finding it. A little spiral glyph etched itself in their air above a few of them, several others following their example until everyone had summoned the spiral. It lessened the oppressive aura of the room for a while, but it did not help with their nerves.

“What’s happening?” Travis muttered, tasting iron at the back of his throat.

I can’t open a portal, Icarus said, still in his mind. And I am detecting several strange signatures incoming. I need you to hold the location for ten minutes, Travis, until Johanne can draw a permanent Domain and I can channel my full power without tearing the veil of the world apart. She is teleporting to Australia and flying in from there. David is also coming, he should be there in five.

Travis nodded, entering battle mode. A minimap appeared in his vision, showing several red dots converging to his location. While he gave commands to the operators, he opened the Origin System and checked his status.

“Okay Icarus, boost me up to Gold.”

“Done,” the AI replied. “Do you need some battle skills?”

“No,” he said. “Give them to the operators instead.”

“Already on it. I’m temporarily overriding their access levels. Travis, contact with the unknown enemy in thirty seconds, be ready.”

David tore through the air, covered from head to toe in deep black armor made from Blackstone, splitting the clouds as he flew through them with immense speed using a skill he had leased from the Origin System.

He wasn’t above using such tools if they allowed him to reach the site of battle quicker, but as soon as he was close he dismissed the skill and let gravity do the rest, getting rid of anything that wasn’t labeled ‘Fully Owned’ on his Status Screen.

A scene of carnage was unfolding below him as he fell from the sky. There was a flickering dome of a shield, attacked by what had to be thousands of small red devils, which the System quickly identified as imps. The air smelled of blood and sulphur, perhaps from an open portal to the nether or whatever–not his problem. People were shooting at the imps from inside the shield, but in its state some of the small devils were managing to get through and it was quickly transforming into a brutal melee.

He landed in an explosion of rock and loose soil which immediately turned a dozen imps to red paste. Golden projectiles fired from Travis’s weapon streaked past him, each finding a target with unerring accuracy. He ducked, and a fist coated in stone shot up. It found an imp’s face and pulverized it, then he quickly switched targets while slowly making his way toward the shield.

Inside, Travis was summoning golden armor and weapons, while operators protected by leased shields shot all kinds of magic at the devils, but more and more of them were managing to slip through when the shield flickered, and even the hexagons were beginning to look overloaded.

Stomping toward the main building, David kicked and punched anything that got close to him. He reached the shield right outside the classroom’s windows, and picked off the imps one by one as they relentlessly attacked the dome of protective energy.

Then, with its next flicker, he threw himself inside. The classroom was cramped, some of the desks set ablaze by skills or by the imps, slowly flooding the room with smoke. Spirals glowed in the gloom, empowering magic and projectiles and creating small areas where the smell of blood and brimstone was weaker.

“They are trying to get to the emitter!" Travis as he intercepted an imp lunging at the device with his golden rifle, smashing the creature’s face in with it.

They fought. Side to side, breaking thin red limbs and tearing leather-like wings apart with their hands and magic. David was more of a melee type, and he turned the room into a blender while Travis supported him with his summoned weapons, or materialized golden glowing pieces of armor to deflect talons, fangs and acidic spittle.

“Johanne is here,” Icarus announced right as the woman burst through the ceiling and into the room. She looked around, glasses reflecting the fire in the room and the red-hue of the sky through the curtain of foul energy, then immediately set to work.

“Protect her!” Travis ordered the men, and they all followed her as she drew the datascript circle all around the room.

Behind them, a single imp managed to reach the emitter. It cut through the magically reinforced metal with its claw like it was made of butter. The Domain vanished, and the air became red mud. The smell of iron and blood grew, overpowering the senses. The men fell to the ground, retching and struggling to draw air into their lungs. David himself was struggling to stay upright, his vision swimming.

A deafening screech shattered the windows, letting cold air inside. With it, the oppressive aura seemed to morph into one of malice. The air thickened, and a red light flooded the room from above the broken emitter. A claw emerged from the weakened veil between worlds, from beneath reality itself, and as it swiped at the air, it rent the veil even further.

When the full Domain snapped into place, they all felt it. The air cleared, and the feeling of wading through mud that sapped them of strength and left an oily sensation that could not be washed away, vanished. The claw fell to the floor, severed from the rest of its body as the tear in the air snapped shut with a rush of wind.

“It’s not over,” Johanne announced, getting up from where she had bent over to finish the last of the datascript circle.

Indeed, outside the imps were still attacking, hitting the bolstered shield and failing to make it through.

They pummeled the attackers with everything they had, but more imps simply kept coming.

“There are rifts all over the place,” she explained. “Small ones, not like the one they tried to open here, where the veil was at its weakest. But we must close them.”

“You heard her!” David announced. “Everyone, summon your spirals and be ready to close the rifts. I’ll handle the school. Travis, you clear the garden?”

“On it,” Travis said. “Squad 2 and 3 with me. Let’s go.”

The red haze turned the sunrise into the color of blood. Travis grit his teeth, suppressing a groan of pain as he plunged his hand into the small, child-sized opening into a land that was pure agony to the eyes, feeling the spiral flare up and be channeled through his connection to the System.

The tear in the boundary of dimensions shrunk, and then vanished. He kept his eyes peeled, patrolling the area, picking off the stragglers and watching the minimap, until finally he felt a pulse of energy from all around him. A gigantic Domain field came into being, banishing the red haze and the oppressive feeling that came with it.

“The veil between worlds is beginning to stabilize,” Johanne announced. “Keep your eyes open, portals could still manifest and I will need you to close them before they damage it further.”

“Or before they spawn more devils,” Travis added.

“Nah,” David said with a chuckle. “I’m fine with devils. They are fun to punch.”

“Only because you’re armored,” Travis replied.

David, still a mass of moving stone, shrugged. All around, operators were patrolling the area, most of them still sporting scratches and bite marks on their skin where the imps had managed to overpower their armor skills. Some of the weaker ones were in especially bad conditions, but their wounds were closing quickly now that the Domain was healing them.

“I kinda wanted to punch the big one that was making it through,” he said.

“The rejuvenation made you a battle maniac,” Travis said with a shake of his head. “Speaking of, Johanne, what the hell was that?”

“A big fucking problem,” she said. “That’s what it is. Michael said to wait until he’s talked with the pope, then we will know for sure.”

Before Travis could reply, he heard David chuckle. “Well, would you look at that!” the man exclaimed. “Origin System XP for killing devils! Feels like Doom out here.”

“You know Doom?” Travis asked, dumbfounded, before he could catch himself.

“What? I played the original, is it so strange?”

“No, no, it’s perfectly reasonable.”

“Bah,” David said with a wave of his hand. “Mister Fake Gold who hasn’t even played Doom…”

Travis’s eyes narrowed. David held his gaze, and then the two broke into laughter. “So what?” Travis asked. “I needed the boost.”

“You fought well,” David retorted. “For a boosted.”

As they spoke they kept their eyes open and their senses sharp. For now, neither Icarus nor Johanne were not detecting anything.

“It’s a shame,” David continued. “You do have the talent for it.”

“So you keep saying,” Travis said in a low voice, looking outside and up to the sky. It was black, as it should be, and no stars were visible with the light pollution and faint haze of clouds.

“Did you also gain XP?”

Travis shook his head.

“Right…” David chuckled. “Privileged user and all that.”

“Don’t you also have infinite XP?”

“Nah,” David said. “I don’t like being given free stuff, even if it’s only leased or whatever. Clever little deception, that one. Making people think they can go from leasing skills to owning them.”

Travis quickly shushed the man, looking around to see if any operator had overheard them. This only made David laugh.

“I’m fine with that, by the way,” he said. “I know that one day one of you all might need to borrow my power and I will gladly give it to ya. Yes, even you, Mr CEO.”

“Did you…” Travis began, then paused. He half expected Icarus to interrupt him, but the AI stayed silent. He was sure they were listening, though. “Did you feel it when Icarus drew power from the Origin System the other day?”

“You bet I did,” David said. His face was dead serious, and the weight of his gaze was almost physical. If a devil decided to attack them at that moment, Travis would feel sorry for the fate it was going to suffer. “I don’t think anyone else noticed but I was expecting it. Luckily it turned out to be a minor thing. Travis, I don’t look forward to the day Michael or Icarus are forced to draw upon the full might of the Origin System.”

“Me neither,” he replied. “Me neither…”

A few hours later, the veil at the center of the Domain was finally stable enough to allow for the creation of a portal connecting it to Site 00. Travis lingered a moment longer, watching the empty classroom. They were not going to find those kids, he knew. He didn’t even know why he cared.

Still, he would do anything in his power to find them. Perhaps even pester Michael about it. He could not let it stand, he just couldn’t.

The shield held steady. He watched it, daring it to flicker and for a devil to attack, but all was quiet. Even its light had been dimmed so that it would not draw the eyes in the first light of the day. Of course, the ruckus they had caused had drawn attention, but Quests were already being dispatched to help handle it, and he would soon return to take over the more bureaucratic matters.

To be fair, he was not looking forward to it, perhaps for the first time ever. The fight had left him aching for more.

More is coming, he thought. Today had just shown that they couldn’t simply spam Domains all over the place, they also needed to defend them. Portals were a resource, but also a liability, a weak link that could not be used lightly because it contributed to the degradation of the veil between this world, and whatever hell the devils came from.
