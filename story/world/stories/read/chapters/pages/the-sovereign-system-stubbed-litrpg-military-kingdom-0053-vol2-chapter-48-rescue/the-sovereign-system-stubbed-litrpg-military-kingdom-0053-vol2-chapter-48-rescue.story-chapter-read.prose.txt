Rain poured down in sheets over the forested terrain, the canopy above doing little to shield those below from the storm’s relentless assault. The campfire hissed and sputtered, barely audible over the roar of the downpour—before it flickered out entirely.

A large figure groaned.

Bound at the wrists behind his back, he struggled to sit up, water streaming down his face. His eyes squinted against the stinging rain.

Nearby, curses cut through the air. As he turned his head, he saw the guards scrambling for shelter beneath the trees, leaving him exposed in the open. He wasn’t surprised—but that didn’t stop him from shooting them a searing glare.

Richard Boyd let out a weary breath, a wave of helplessness washing over him.

It had only been four days since they’d parted ways with General Luke Drakon, and Richard had been tasked with escorting the second prince to Yunxi.

As the prince’s personal guard, this wasn’t the first time he’d been assigned to accompany him—but it was the first mission of real consequence. The prince had changed in the short ten days spent alongside the young general.

He was no longer impetuous or quick to anger. It was as if something had awakened within him, leaving Richard reeling.

This was the same man who once paraded his status with pride, shirking all responsibilities in favor of wine, women, and lazy afternoons at the Jade Pavilion chasing after Fairy Qin.

And yet—for a moment—Richard had hope. A fragile, fleeting hope that the second prince had finally begun to change. If they could just reach Yunxi and return with their men to the capital, it would signal a turning point not just for the prince, but for the entire kingdom.

But that hope was shattered on the second morning after their departure.

They were ambushed.

The slaughter came swift and brutal. Richard barely had time to grab the prince and flee. The fifty men under his command fought valiantly, buying them precious moments—but it wasn’t enough.

They were up against a force five thousand strong. The fact that Richard had managed to escape at all, dragging the prince alongside him, bordered on miraculous.

Even then, he held onto the faint hope that they might be allowed to leave unharmed. After all, they were only two men. Their pursuers shouldn’t have known who they were.

He was wrong.

The enemy chased them with ruthless precision. Not a single arrow was fired from horseback. They weren’t trying to kill them—they were trying to capture.

That chilling detail made it clear: they either wanted answers, or they knew exactly who he was protecting.

The chase didn’t last long. The gelding they rode on simply couldn’t handle the weight of both men over long distances. Exhausted and cornered, they were soon captured.

And Richard would never forget the words that followed.

“Kill that big bastard. We only need the second prince alive,”

A lithe man growled, his voice like gravel, as he pointed straight at Richard.

The command had made Richard shudder—but it was the implication behind it that struck him the hardest.

They had known who he was.

Someone had told the enemy that the second prince of the Marxx Kingdom was out there.

They’d been betrayed.

Richard’s jaw had tightened. Fury simmered beneath his skin as his eyes narrowed. His hands moved on instinct, reaching up behind his back to grip the hilt of his greatsword.

A low, metallic groan had followed as the blade slid free—an ominous sound that marked the beginning of a hopeless fight.

He had scanned his surroundings. Ten enemies nearby. He might’ve stood a chance.

But then he had seen them—cavalry in the distance, over a hundred strong, racing toward them.

His hope had died right then and there.

But what happened next… he would never forget.

“P-Prince Lyon!?”

His eyes had widened.

The second prince had stepped in front of him, planting his body between Richard and the encroaching soldiers. His face was dirty, his jaw clenched with resolve. In one swift motion, he had drawn a dagger and pressed the blade to his own throat.

“If you so much as lay a finger on him, I’ll slit my own throat.”

Lyon’s voice had rung out—steady, commanding, and utterly serious.

Richard had blinked. “What are you doing, Your Highness?”

But the prince hadn’t responded.

For a long, breathless moment, no one had moved. Tension hung thick in the air like smoke before battle.

Then the cavalry had arrived.

A massive figure led them—a man with a crimson-dyed beard and a tunic to match, half-hidden beneath heavy armor. His iron helm concealed most of his face, save for a narrow, cross-shaped slit through which sharp eyes glinted.

He had looked over the scene and burst out laughing.

“To think the cowardly second prince would grow some balls! I figured you’d be pissing yourself when I caught you.”

He had twirled the end of his beard as he spoke, amusement dripping from his voice.

The men beside him had shifted uneasily but remained still, clearly awaiting his signal.

After a pause, the man had sighed.

“It’d be a pain if you died now. Bind them both—we’ve wasted enough time.”

At his order, several soldiers had approached cautiously. Richard had still held his greatsword aloft, but his eyes had stayed fixed on the man in front of him.

Lyon.

The prince stood tall, his dirtied robes clinging to his body, his posture unshaken.

Richard had stared at him for a long moment.

Then, with slow finality, he had let his blade fall.

The greatsword had struck the dirt with a heavy thud, sending up a puff of dust.

Lyon had flinched at the sound, turning with wide eyes as his dagger dipped.

And Richard—who had once been ready to die fighting—had gone still.

His vision blurred.

The memory faded.

Now, Lyon huddled in the mud beside him, soaked and trembling, his clothes stuck to his skin. The prince's once-pristine image was long gone—replaced by a figure covered in bruises and grime, worn down by days of captivity.

But when Richard looked at him now, he didn’t see a pampered noble or a political burden.

He saw someone else entirely.

A man.

A prince worth dying for.

“Fuck, they could’ve at least given us a tent…” the young man muttered, his curses barely audible over the thunderous downpour. His expression darkened further as he shot a venomous glare toward the sentinels huddled beneath a nearby tree.

Prince Lyon, like Richard, was bound at the wrists behind his back. Despite his royal blood, he was receiving the same treatment as a common prisoner—a fact that gnawed at Richard’s thoughts.

Why capture them only to treat them this way?

He didn’t have a map, but Richard knew they had been taken southwest. Judging by the route they'd used to reach Xiandong just ten days prior, they were almost certainly in Lhair territory now.

That, combined with the enemy’s knowledge of the prince’s location, painted a grim picture.

Someone from within had betrayed them. And that someone was working closely with Lhair.

More troubling still—they didn’t want Lyon dead.

They wanted him alive.

Richard turned toward the prince. “Can you stand?”

Lyon gave a faint nod and struggled to his knees in the thickening mud, gritting his teeth as he slowly pushed himself upright. He teetered for a moment before staggering toward a tree, leaning heavily against its trunk.

Richard wasn’t as lucky.

Their captors had bound his feet as well, clearly deeming him the greater threat. With no other option, he began to squirm through the mud, the cold seeping into his bones as his soaked robes dragged through the muck.

Eventually, he collapsed beneath the same tree as Lyon, panting, shoulders slumped. Every limb ached. The meager rations and frequent beatings had left him feeling as weak as a newborn calf.

By now, the fire had long been snuffed out, leaving them cloaked in near-total darkness.

Richard let out a heavy yawn and sagged against the tree. Sleeping bound was miserable on its own—doing it drenched and caked in mud bordered on torture.

It was going to be a long night.

He exhaled slowly, turning toward the sentinels’ last position, expecting to see their usual smug faces as they enjoyed his suffering.

But there was no one there.

Richard’s brow furrowed.

“Where did the guards go?” he muttered, eyes scanning the shadows.

“They probably found a tent to crawl into. Bastards,” Lyon growled through gritted teeth.

But Richard didn’t look convinced. “No,” he said, voice tightening. “There’s no way they’d leave us unattended…”

His body tensed, instincts flaring.

Then—he felt it.

A heavy hand clamped down on his shoulder.

He flinched violently. That couldn’t be Lyon—his hands were still tied.

Before panic could set in, a voice whispered in his ear:

“Relax your body. I’m undoing your bindings now,” a voice whispered in Richard’s ear.

His heart surged.

They were being rescued.

“Take the second prince north from here. The General is waiting,” the voice instructed—just before Richard felt the rope around his wrists go slack.

Something was pressed into his hand.

A dagger—its hilt wet and sticky.

He didn’t hesitate.

With his arms finally free, Richard brought the blade down and sawed through the thick rope around his ankles, eyes darting through the darkness, half-expecting enemies to leap out at any moment.

But no one came.

He surged to his feet, spinning toward Prince Lyon—who stared at him in disbelief, mouth agape.

“R-Richard? How—”

Richard silenced him with a hand over his mouth, then swiftly sliced through the prince’s bindings. Without a word, he pointed northward, grabbed the young man by his drenched robes, and hauled him along.

They barely made it twenty feet before Richard spotted them.

Three sentinels—slumped in a heap at the base of a tree. Their necks twisted at grotesque angles, as though something had torn through them without mercy.

He inhaled sharply, rainwater sliding across his lips.

He recognized that voice earlier. Tristan. The vice general.

But never—not once—had he imagined the man possessed the raw, savage strength to nearly rip a man’s head clean off.

And yet, even more shocking than that…

How was he here? Had he already climb the unassailable mountain that adjoined Xiandong and retreated?

It had only been four days since they last saw each other, how could such a thing be possible?

But now wasn’t the time to marvel. Not in the middle of an escape.

They pushed through the dense underbrush, boots squelching through the rain-churned mud. Trees loomed around them like silent sentinels, the storm masking the sound of their hurried breaths.

Richard froze.

Movement.

Figures stood in the shadows ahead.

He gripped the dagger, his muscles coiled to strike.

But the men didn’t move.

No weapons raised. No alarms shouted.

And then he saw it—the faint glint of familiar armor beneath the cloaks.

Marxx soldiers. Allies.

Relief flooded through him like a crashing wave. He didn’t know how this rescue had been orchestrated—and he didn’t care.

All that mattered was that it was working.

They moved further north, slipping away from the enemy encampment. The trees began to thin, and that’s when he saw him.

Even through the veil of darkness and pouring rain, he recognized the silhouette immediately.

That slim frame. That quiet composure.

The boy he had seen off just four days ago.

“General…” Richard breathed.

But before he could say more, Prince Lyon tore free from his grasp and stumbled forward.

Richard instinctively reached out to stop him—but froze when the prince collapsed into Luke’s arms, embracing him tightly.

In the rain-soaked gloom, all Richard could see was the prince’s chest rising and falling, his entire body trembling as he clung to the general.

The storm swallowed the sounds of his sobs.

Richard stiffened. The prince was filthy—mud-caked, bruised, still shaking from the ordeal. What if the general took offense to such an outburst? This was, after all, a man who had just risked so much to pull them out of the fire.

But those fears melted away the moment he saw it.

Luke’s arm wrapped around the prince’s back, steady and comforting. He gave a few gentle pats—nothing extravagant, but enough.

Enough to say: You're safe now.

Then, Luke’s voice rose—clear, calm, and commanding, slicing through the curtain of rain.

“Prince Lyon. Richard. We need to talk.”
