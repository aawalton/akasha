Elynara stood on a low rise just off the road, the wind tugging lightly at her cloak as she surveyed the farmland ahead.

The sprawl stretched wide across the shallow valley, with fenced pasture patches, rows of unfamiliar crops, and narrow footpaths that wound between them like faded ribbons.

A few distant figures moved slowly across the land, far enough away to look like colored dots. Aside from the occasional squawk or creak of a turning windpost, the place was quiet.

Tall grass cropped low, bare patches where hooves had rubbed the soil, and seemed like most of the open land was suitable for grazing. Some areas were tilled and planted, but not with anything she recognized. No wheat. No cabbage. The rows looked too fine, the leaves wrong. One field had long, crimson-tipped stalks that swayed like reeds in the breeze.

She watched them for a moment, frowning slightly.

Definitely not carrots.

Adjusting her satchel, she started down the slope, following a foot-worn path that curved between rows of stakes and low bushes. Every few minutes she’d spot another fence or shed, but they were scattered, with no real center to the place. Just land, and distance.

She walked for longer than she expected before finally seeing a pair of men working near a leaning stretch of fencing both bent over a collapsed post. She approached at a respectful angle, waiting until she was clearly visible before lifting her hand.

“Excuse me. I’m looking for the Esharil household? I was told they run a farm near the forest edge.”

The two looked up from their work, squinting slightly in the light. With a soft grunt, the man leaned on the post. The woman, wearing a dust-stained tunic, pointed down a narrow branch off the main path.

“Keep heading that way till you see the old windbreak tree. Then swing left. They’re the last place before the forest. Big open pens. You’ll know when you see it.”

Elynara nodded in thanks.

“Appreciate it.”

The walk stretched longer than she’d hoped. The path twisted around the back of another fenced field, then dipped down past a dry creek bed.

It took almost another hour of steady walking and re-checking her directions before she spotted the signs of homesteading, with the typical tight cluster of fencing, low wooden pens, and open sheds built into the slope.

Beyond it, the forest stood like a deep green wall, distant but looming. She could almost smell it now: sharp bark, moss, and something faintly sweet riding the air.

She spotted movement near one of the pens. A woman with rolled sleeves and a sun-faded straw hat was tending to a group of strange, chicken-like birds. They were leaner though, with long legs and feathers that resembled smooth scales, while their heads were topped with single, soft crests that twitched at every sound.

A boy, no older than eight, helped nearby, scooping feed into shallow troughs.

Starpuff, who had been flickering lazily behind her for most of the walk, suddenly darted ahead like a missile.

“Oh no you don’t–”

Too late.

The spirit zipped past the fence, swooping low and weaving between the birds, startling them. Feathers flew. The creatures squawked in alarm, scattering into messy, flapping chaos.

Elynara has long figured out that, though invisible, Starpuff was very much able to nibble on and ‘touch’ things in a way.

And the mischievous spirit used that trick with reckless abandon!

The woman straightened, laughing as she wiped her hands on her apron.

“Well well, a visitor! And a noisy one, too. Who might you be, little missy?”

Elynara couldn’t help it; her steps slowed, and shoulders relaxed. She let the corners of her mouth turn up as she stepped forward.

“My name is Elynara, and Alicia mentioned I could find a place to sleep here?”

The woman blinked, then grinned so broadly it nearly stretched her whole face.

“Alicia sent you? Little Alicia from the guild? Stars above, she remembers us!”

She stepped forward, hand wiping feed dust from her tunic before planting one hand squarely on Elynara’s shoulder.

“Rina Esharil, matron of this small kingdom of dirt and feathers. And this one’s Finnie, youngest of the lot.”

The boy gave a quick wave before resuming his mission of chasing spilled grain into a corner.

“I–thank you. I’m here on a couple of guild jobs, actually–” Elynara tried.

“Oh, you’re working? Wonderful! Helping hands are always welcome! Mmm, a guild lassie that helps. Perfect. Just perfect.”

And with that, Rina turned, waving for her to follow as she marched down the dirt path without waiting for a reply.

Elynara blinked, then followed, staff tapping lightly behind her. Starpuff floated low near her heels, practically unnoticed for once.

“Is she always this fast?”

But her question fell on deaf ears, Finnie too busy to care and the matron just prattling on.

“Kaela and Theren are down in the rows past the shed. Aren should be with them too; hard worker, that one. Quiet, though. Bit broody sometimes, but it’s the age. Kaela keeps him in line.”

They passed between pens and troughs, the land slightly sloping. Off to the side, wooden posts held bundles of drying herbs she didn’t recognize, and further ahead, an open shed creaked in the breeze.

Elynara caught herself staring at Rina. While she was quite an animated chatterbox, her wolf-like ears and fluffy tail made for quite a spectacle, moving in tandem with her words.

Her ears were long and covered in short fur, soon twitching slightly as she turned to call out toward the shed. Her tail, a deep brown with faded black bands, swayed behind her in lazy arcs. It was the first time Elynara had truly interacted with a Forest Folk family up close. So far she only caught some quick glances in town, and in passing from a few orphanage children.

She made a mental note not to stare. Not too much, anyway.

They crested a small rise and reached a flatter work area. Bundled tools were being loaded into a cart by a tall, broad-shouldered man; Theren, no doubt. Nearby, a girl no older than twelve was hauling a coil of rope with exaggerated effort, while a teenage boy stood beside her, sorting through small sacks.

“Kaela, Aren!” Rina called. “This is Elynara! Alicia sent her!”

Kaela waved immediately, her cheeks flushed from work. Aren gave her a glance and the barest nod before returning to his task.

Theren turned, giving Elynara a small nod and nothing more. She appreciated the simplicity.

“Lodging and meals in exchange for help, or modest coin if you’re busy with requests,” Rina explained, clapping her hands once. “Or both, honestly. There’s always something to do.”

Elynara gave a small laugh, unable to argue.

“Sounds fair.”

She stood back as the family eased into motion again. Kaela was dragging the rope over with mock theatrical grunts, Rina chatting lightly with Theren about the cart’s wheel, and Finnie now trailing behind Starpuff as if trying to catch it even though he couldn’t see it.

It was a lot. But it felt oddly normal. Like everybody in this world preferred work done over simple coin. It almost felt expected now.

Elynara folded her arms loosely across her chest, watching the rhythm of the farm shift around her. Then, when she finally caught a breath between all the movement and introductions, she spoke again.

“Actually… There’s another reason I’m here.”

She saw Rina pause mid-step, the others slowing slightly too.

“I wanted to ask about the spirits. To learn more about them… because I can see them.”

The moment the words left her mouth, the world around her stopped.

“You can see spirits?!”

Kaela froze mid-step, rope dangling from her hands. Finnie nearly dropped the tool he was carrying. Even Aren’s head snapped up, eyes narrowing slightly; they were just sharply focused, not skeptical. Theren slowly, methodically, lowered his shovel to the ground.

Rina’s ears twitched upright, tail going stiff behind her. Then her eyes went wide, mouth parting in something between shock and joy.

“Lass,” she said, voice rising with barely contained energy, “why didn’tcha tell us sooner!?”

Elynara opened her mouth, but it was already too late.

“What do they look like?” “Where’d you see them?” “Can they talk?” “Do they float?” “Do they eat the bread or just smell it?” “Are there only forest spirits or house ones too?” “Did they like our charms?” “Do they live in the woods?” “Do they follow you? Can they hear me right now?”

The noise hit like a wave. Kaela was bouncing on her heels. Finn was half-lunging forward with both hands raised like he could grab answers out of the air. Even Aren’s gaze lingered with more than a little interest.

Elynara took one cautious half-step back as Starpuff flared in her peripheral vision, flickering into full view behind her with its form puffed out in visible alarm. It drifted upward and slightly behind her head, peeking down like a nervous fluff peering over a shield.

“I–, uh–”

Rina clapped her hands, waving frantically as if swatting flies.

“Alright, alright, enough… poor girl’s gonna go deaf with all that racket. Kaela! Finn! Take her out, show her the shrines.”

Kaela lit up like someone had tossed her a festival prize.

“C’mon! I’ll show you where we leave the offerings!”

She darted forward, grabbing Elynara’s wrist with both hands.

Finnie tugged on her sleeve from the other side, practically vibrating.

“We’ll show you all three! They’re real old!”

Elynara barely managed, “Alright, alright–I’m coming–!” before being pulled forward at a brisk trot.

Starpuff zipped overhead, still a little puffed up but no longer hiding, flickering just enough to peek into animal pens and over fencing as they passed.

The chatter didn’t stop.

“Some of our farm shrines are older than the house,” Kaela said proudly as they moved through the back path. “My grandpa said one of them has stood since his great-grandfather’s time!”

“We leave bread and fruit and stuff, ” Finnie added.

“We carve charms too,” Kaela said, tugging her braid over her shoulder. “Wooden ones, mostly. Mama makes the clay ones on cold days.”

The farmland thinned as they walked, the path weaving toward the outer edge. Small clusters of trees began to appear. Less tamed, less worked. The sort of places that looked like they were left alone on purpose.

Elynara listened, nodding where she could, answering questions when she managed to get a word in.

“Is the spirit with you now?”

“Yes.”

“Do they eat fruit?”

“I don’t think so.”

“Does it talk?”

“No.”

“Can it see me?”

“Definitely.”

Finnie made a small squeaking noise and flailed one arm at the empty space where Starpuff had just flicked past.

It was chaos. But it was warm chaos.

The edge of the farm was less groomed than the rest; the grass was a little higher, the fences were more weathered, and the brush was left untended rather than trimmed. They passed an old wooden wheelbarrow half-swallowed by weeds, then a leaning post tied with braided cloth and beads faded from sun and rain.

“Do all the farms around here have shrines like this?” Elynara asked, partly to redirect the flood of questions.

“Most,” Kaela answered. “Some don’t keep up with them, but Mama always said we’d be stupid not to. She says it’s like forgetting your neighbors are alive.”

Finnie nodded emphatically.

“And if you forget them, they might forget you. Then they don’t help the crops, and you get bad luck.”

“Or wild spirits move in,” Kaela added. “That’s worse. The quiet ones that don’t like people.”

Elynara raised a brow.

“That actually happens?”

“Once,” Finnie said. “Before I was born. The creek rose and the root cellar flooded. Mama said that was the sign.”

Elynara didn’t know what to say to that, but took solace in the fact they likely just didn’t know any better.

Starpuff floated lazily ahead of them, occasionally pausing in midair to examine fence posts or bob near half-buried stone markers for reasons only it understood.

Or didn’t. Elynara was pretty sure her crazy spirit companion just went with whatever crossed its mind, no consideration given.

They passed a pair of tall, curved trees that arched over the path like a gate. Beyond them, the earth sloped gently downward, where patches of moss and clover spread beneath the trees.

Kaela pointed ahead.

“Shrines are through there. One’s in the trees, one’s near the stream, and one’s under a big root. That one’s my favorite.”

Finnie tugged at Elynara’s sleeve again.

“Do they follow you all the time? The spirits?”

“Um, only one does. Starpuff. That’s what I call it.”

“Is it floating somewhere around here?”

“Mhm.”

“Can I touch it?”

Elynara smiled faintly.

“Probably not. I could ask, but it does as it pleases.”

Finnie frowned but kept looking anyway, eyes scanning the air above her shoulder as if sheer willpower might make it visible.

“Do you think the other spirits know it?” Kaela asked.

“I don’t know. I’m hoping to find out.”

That earned a beaming smile from Kaela.

“Good,” she said. “You should ask the one at the root shrine, they might know!”

“You said they don’t talk back.”

Kaela shrugged.

“They don’t have to talk to feel loud.”

Elynara didn’t know how to argue with that, so instead she focused on where they were going.

And ahead, just visible between a trio of moss-covered trees, Elynara spotted the first shrine.

It stood nestled in the roots of a massive tree, scorched black and half-hollow from a long-past lightning strike. Its bark curled in places like old paper, and the hollowed section yawned like a gaping wound. Around it, the ground had grown wild, with vines creeping up from below and moss unstintingly thick over the stone base.

Elynara slowed as they approached. A small ceramic bowl rested in a shallow niche, half-filled with dried flowers and what might’ve once been a few slices of dried fruit, now little more than a leathery curl.

Kaela’s voice was quieter here.

“We used to think there was one here. A spirit, I mean. Before the tree got struck.”

Finnie was already prodding the edge of the stump with a stick, eyes downcast.

“Mama says they moved on.”

Elynara studied the stone for a moment, then glanced at the children.

“Could I have a little quiet?” she asked, gently.

They nodded. Kaela tugged Finn back a few steps.

Elynara sat down, staff resting across her lap, and closed her eyes. She let her breath settle. Let her awareness stretch.

Nothing.

No tug. No hum. No flicker of presence.

Just the old wood creaking. The faint rustle of grass. The hollow emptiness of a place that had once held something, and now did not.

She opened her eyes slowly and placed her hand on the stone.

“Even if it’s gone now… maybe it watched over this place once.”

Kaela’s expression softened. Finn sat on a low root, head tilted.

They moved on without much more talk.

The second shrine sat near a wide pond, reeds curling along the bank and flowering bushes crowding the edges. Dragonflies buzzed lazily above the surface. Someone had recently placed a few bright petals at the base of the shrine, which itself was low and simple, made of weathered wood and stacked stone.

The moment Elynara stepped into the clearing, she froze.

“They’re here.”

Two presences moved gently in her senses, one of which was large and steady, pulsing with deep, slow waves. The other was light, twitching and dancing like a wind-chime spirit, flicking between reeds and skimming the pond’s surface.

The children gasped.

“You can really see them?”

“They’re actually here?”

Elynara nodded, a quiet smile growing.

“Clearer than anything so far.”

Starpuff surged ahead, doing tight spirals above the shrine like a smug little parade marshal, flickering brightly as if to say, See? I brought her.

Kaela and Finn edged closer to the outer stones, watching wide-eyed but keeping a respectful distance. Kaela crouched and whispered prayers to the spirits, while Finnie waved at the water like it might wave back.

“Mama told me they like the wind and warmth.”

Elynara chuckled and stood, letting the moment rest before turning toward the trail again.

“Let’s go see the last one.”

The third shrine waited farther out, near the treeline at the edge of the family’s land. Here, the trees pressed in a little tighter, shade pooling across the roots like a quiet veil.

The shrine was newer, with a less weathered wooden structure that was clearly maintained more recently. A carved bowl sat at its base, filled with small seeds, a few pieces of fruit, and what looked like a freshly plucked sprig of mint.

Just above the bowl floated a single spirit: slow-moving, round, and dimly glowing. It hovered in a lazy spiral, tilting slightly as Elynara stepped near.

It saw her. She felt it.

“This one gets the first fruit from every harvest,” Kaela said, hushed again. “Always.”

“It’s the quiet one,” Finn added. “Mama says it’s like the land watching.”

Elynara let the quiet stretch for a moment.

These spirits weren’t like Starpuff. They didn’t flicker or bounce. They didn’t hover over her shoulder or react to jokes. However, they were aware; like old neighbors who, steady and distant as ever, watched from their porch and did not mind your presence so long as you were courteous.

She knelt briefly beside the offering bowl, letting her sense linger. The spirit hovered, content to exist just beyond her full understanding.

“I want to try something,” she said eventually.

Kaela perked up.

“Here?”

“No… back at the pond.”

They returned the way they came, this time with less chatter. Starpuff hovered lower again, dimming slightly, slipping between branches like it didn’t want to intrude.

At the second shrine, Elynara knelt once more, placing her staff across her lap and grounding herself. The wind-spirited one flicked near, trailing a ripple across the pond’s surface.

Kaela and Finn stepped back, whispering to each other until even that faded. Elynara was left to her focus as they quietly wandered off to the side, Finnie skipping stones and Kaela picking reeds.

She closed her eyes.

And turned inward.

Elynara sat cross-legged near the second shrine, brushing aside a loose stem of grass before settling her staff across her knees. Eyes half-lidded, she let her awareness spread

The spirits still hovered: one near the reeds, drifting like a wind-eddy and the other farther back, slower and more centered.

She closed her eyes briefly, centering herself. Then opened them again and lifted one hand, drawing a controlled thread of mana into the air. No shape, no flare. Just a stable line of energy extending from her palm.

No immediate reaction.

They watched.

She held the thread in place for several breaths. Still nothing.

Then, finally, the smaller spirit shifted forward. Its path wobbled slightly as it drifted closer, then straightened out. It passed through the mana, and Elynara felt the subtle pull as the energy left her control.

She adjusted, maintaining flow.

The second spirit came in slower, but just as deliberate. The strand was thinner now, so she let it unravel and refreshed it: same stability, wider dispersal.

Both spirits accepted it without hesitation.

Elynara watched closely. Counted the time between approach and response. Checked their movement. Noted the pace of consumption. There was no tension in the way they drew mana in. No sign of stress or urgency. Just steady intake.

Starpuff hovered to the side, out of the way but observant, its glow low and even. It didn’t interrupt. For once, it simply mirrored her quiet focus.

She let the mana trail off and waited.

Several seconds passed. Then there was a faint, barely perceptible pulse from the smaller spirit, but it was distinct. A directed flicker of mana, pressed downward into the soil beneath it.

Elynara’s gaze shifted.

A flower by the shrine’s base opened slightly further.

She didn’t react outwardly.

Instead, she drew more mana and reshaped it, this time making it more mist-like, scattered and slower to fade. The spirits moved in again. Another pulse followed.

She made no sound. Just sat with the result. Watched.

Then she began thinking it through, piece by piece.

The spirits were clearly consuming mana given to them, much like Starpuff. But unlike it they used it to help the plants in their… domain? Was it just the excess they used? She wasn’t sure.

“But… why don’t they just feed from the ambient mana around them?”

The spirits floated calmly near the shrine, ignoring the drifting flecks of ambient energy like it wasn’t even there. They had only responded when she offered something directly.

Same as Starpuff. It never tried to absorb loose mana from the air either. Only fed from what she formed and controlled.

She shaped another offering, dispersing it loosely but still structured, in an even finer of a mist this time. The spirits came again without hesitation. Drifted close. Absorbed it, calm and smooth.

Then the pulse.

A flicker of mana released, subtle and outward. The flower by the base of the shrine brightened slightly. Another unfolded, just a little more.

Elynara’s fingers stilled. Her brow creased.

That wasn’t just a reaction. That was feedback. Intentional.

She sat with the observation for a moment, then shaped another flow of mana and repeated the offering.

Same response. Drawn in. Calm. And again, the faint pulse outward. A soft brightening in the foliage around them.

So.

They took mana from others.Didn’t react to anything ambient.And returned some of that mana outward, into things. Into growth.

It felt a little different too, but how? She couldn’t really tell.

Her mind began lining the pieces up.

They can consume mana, but can’t process it themselves from ambient mana.Like they lack the necessary biology for it.And when fed, they reinforce the environment.Which in turn can feed them mana back as it grows.

It felt like an ecosystem, a cycle of dependence.

One thing influencing the other.

She looked toward the reeds again. Then back at the spirits. Then toward the trail, recalling the scorched stump, and the stillness at the dead shrine.

The comparison was obvious now.

“The shrines with healthy land still can support spirits. But once the spirit is gone, the land slowly withers”

It wasn’t just the mana level of the world degrading.

If there were fewer people, or beings in general expelling mana for spirits to consume, spirits wouldn’t get what they needed. And once spirits faded, so would the land they tended. No growth. No healing. Nothing to anchor life back into damaged places.

She folded the conclusion into her mind, calm and methodical.

The fewer mana producing beings, the fewer spirits could survive.And without spirits the growth and recovery slowed. And fewer mana beings were born. Every time a cycle repeated, things got worse.

Until now.

Just how many cycles like those passed, for there to be just a few spirits in a land that big?

She shaped another light thread of mana and released it again, watching the little ones respond without hesitation.

And a little later they returned to their slow drifting patterns, circling the pond in lazy arcs like leaves caught in an unseen current.

Elynara remained seated.

“I’m already still,” she muttered. “Might as well put in a little more training.”

Starpuff hovered nearby, for once not interrupting. Like it was pretending to be a quiet sentry on patrol.

She focused inward, letting her mana cycle.

Drawing mana into herself, then letting it recede.

Over and over, in a quiet rhythm.

Her breathing stayed even, arms resting loosely in her lap. The cycles came easily at first, minutes trickling by. Then gradually, if not predictably, resistance set in. That slight internal friction. The pull and drag of a core approaching its current limit.

She let the fatigue settle in her limbs before she checked her new max.

Fifty-seven. A clean increase of five.

She nodded to herself and let her hands fall to her sides before slowly rising.

It was a good stopping point.

Kaela and Finn were still nearby, lounging under a tree and watching dragonflies. They looked up as she approached, and Kaela hopped to her feet without needing to be asked.

“Done?”

“For now, yes.”

They walked the path back together, a little less energetic than earlier, but still happy to badger her with questions.

Back at the farmstead, she joined in a few small tasks, though nothing too heavy. Just moving tool bundles, stacking feed, checking fence lines. The kind of work that didn’t require guidance, just hands and patience.

The others didn’t say much at first, but they watched. Even Aren gave her a nod when she helped lift the crate he’d been quietly struggling with.

Later, they invited her to eat with them. Rina set a pot on the table with mock ceremony, announcing it was “humble fare with proud vegetables,” which earned a snort from Theren and giggles from Kaela.

Elynara took a spot at the edge. The meal was warm and simple: root stew, bread still soft in the center, and something pickled that made her blink on the first bite.

The children couldn’t hold it in for long.

“She fed the spirits,” Finnie blurted. “They ate her magic. And then a flower bloomed.”

Rina raised an eyebrow, interested. Elynara simply nodded now and again, and only responded when asked, but Kaela was quick and bright as she added more details.

“I still need to study them more. I have seen some signs. But I still know very little.”

That was good enough. Rina smiled and offered a short tale about her grandmother’s mother, who once left sweet herbs in the wind just before a flood changed course.

The story was entertaining, though Elynara didn’t know how much of it was true, if anything.

Still, it passed the time.

Afterward, they showed her to a small room at the back of the house. Straw mattress. Folded blanket. A shuttered window that looked out toward the edge of the forest.

Lantern light flickered low as Elynara sat cross-legged one last time, notebook in her lap.

She wrote out her thoughts on the day and what little she found out about the spirits.

Growth is returned to the land when spirits are fed mana.They are the caretakers of the natural world.They seem to be mostly dormant for now, as if conserving their energy until an offering or other source of mana comes along, since it seems they cannot feed on ambient environment mana themselves. Might lack the ability or function to do so? . And they’re kind of… sleepy? They don’t seem to feel stress or anything in particular. Just… float there. Feeding them seems viable, though clearly it would take many mages doing so all the time to have a big effect. Must think on how to scale it.Fewer spirits = fewer healthy ecosystems. Time is not on our side…

She reread the notes once, then closed the notebook and tucked it beside her bag.

The blanket was rougher than she was used to, but warm. Exhaling, she laid back and gazed at Starpuff, where it drifted still and dim just above the wooden beam of the ceiling.

She placed the notebook down and blew out the lantern.

The room settled into quiet as she stretched her legs under the blanket.

Starpuff dimmed near the ceiling, flickering just enough to be seen if she squinted, before suddenly plopping down onto the pillow right next to her face.

Elynara shifted under the blanket, adjusting the corner by her shoulder. The straw mattress creaked once.

Although there was still much to learn… it seemed she had found the first puzzle piece for the grand quest Celestia hinted at.

A soft smile spread on her lips as she looked at her glowy companion.

Things were looking up, after all.
