Like a kicked wasp’s nest, the swarm rippled outwardly as the shockwave spread out, then they boiled out in waves, breaking formation as their leaders were suddenly engulfed in the Avatars’ toxic fog and now that he wasn’t paying so much attention to his bait Avatar, Henry could take in his surroundings a little better. From the point of view of a scout half a mile away, their numbers were much easier to visualize and account for. Unlike his previous clashes with them, Henry couldn’t help but note that there were a lot of them out there, to the point that his hundred-foot-wide sphere of pale, yellow toxic fog had only engulfed a tiny portion of them. Maybe five percent of the whole, Henry would say, and naturally, a moment later, the queens stepped out of the fog, antennas flicking back and forth as they surveyed the agitated swarm.

Order was established immediately.

Clenching an arm in annoyance, Henry smothered his disappointment. He hadn’t expected his neurotoxic fumes to be too effective against those two, but he had hoped for at least something. Unfortunately, only weaker cores rushed into his soul from the explosion. A few of the B-rank got caught in the attack, and maybe in a few minutes, some elites would join them. Though while that was easy enough of a harvest, he wasn’t too impressed. His toxins’ effectiveness definitely had some room to improve, which was… fine. The skill that created them was still only B-rank. Same as the Aspect that housed it. It would eventually get better. Though hopefully sooner rather than later.

“Decent coverage,” commented Velistraine to his side, her steps echoing behind him against the bone floor, and Henry nodded distractedly. Adding a nebulizing component to Spiteful Detonation hadn’t been too hard when he had Toxic Shroud in his repertoire. Merely the work of a few hours while he hounded the invaders’ ships over the last week. But speaking of ships…

“There’s more and more of them,” he muttered. While he could keep track of most of what was happening, Henry had been very busy with the bait situation and the two queens, so he’d only been somewhat aware of the ships, large and small, that kept appearing and joining the rest around the massive, mysterious door. In fact, compared to the numbers the space-mantises had brought forward a few days ago when he’d led them on that short-lived chase, there were at least ten times as many ships now. They littered the area, orbiting the doorway like some sort of dark, unholy, insectile halo.

Velistraine stopped pacing next to him, arms still crossed as her glimmering red eyes took in the scene. “So, what’s going on? And what’s the plan now?”

Maurice looked up at that, though he kept quiet. Velistraine was clearly itching to fight herself, but they’d already discussed that none of them would go out there with their real bodies. Especially not now with two queens around.

Thinking of the two, Henry replayed the last couple seconds once more.

I didn’t even see the war queen move, he thought. Even when he knew she was coming. That was worrying. He didn’t know what that one was capable of, and the only thing that had saved him was how easy it was for him to detonate his clones. If the process had taken a single extra step, she might have been able to stop him.

Henry eyed the projection quietly for a moment as he considered his next steps. The lack of knowledge of what this new individual was capable of made him uneasy. Especially how this new queen seemed to be capable of cowing the architect. For now though, Henry quickly relayed what he’d heard the queens say to each other, and in another section of his mind, Henry was trying to understand how the architect had cracked that doorway to Attal’s basement open, and how long it’d be before it could do it again.

In fact, that might be the question they had to figure out. This, right here, was the sword hovering above all of their heads. Seeing the numbers of the invaders arrayed in front of them, the pit in Henry’s stomach was growing wider and deeper, and the goal of going home as soon as possible grew a little bolder and shinier. As for attacking this swarm right now, he was considering shelving it. The bait-Avatar he’d just detonated had been equipped with multiple Avatars tucked within to ensure it could be destroyed, and Henry already had plenty of Avatars around to make sure none were taken. But now, after the exchange he had had with them, the situation had changed a bit.

Now, the two queens knew he could still self-destruct while his skills were suppressed. They might not know exactly how he’d done it, but they knew it was a possibility. One they might be able to plan around. Not to mention, there was a third queen somewhere, still unaccounted for. He had to tread carefully now. The queens might have some other means to lock him tight, and while the architect queen might not be capable of co-opting his Avatars, it wasn’t worth risking the worst for just a few cores.

“We’ll need to always make sure the queens aren’t around when we nip at their heels,” Henry finally said, once he finished filling them in. Velistraine and Maurice were still digesting the news.

“The Graft is the System, right?” Maurice asked, eyes still set on the wildfire that was the swarm. By now, the chaos was gone, and in its stead, impeccable order reigned as lines upon lines of soldiers and elites filled their ships before they took off to join the greater armada. “So the System is a key for that bigger door? How are they sure of that, exactly? Also, doesn’t that mean the System… has like a home, somewhere? And a body? It can’t just be in our heads if it can be used as a key. Right?”

Henry frowned, while Velistraine hummed, scratching her chin. The captain was looking a lot better than how they’d found her. With plenty of food, water, and exercise, Velistraine looked as sharp as ever. Well. Aside from her clothes, that was. She’d managed to wash them, but that was all she could do for them. Between all three of them, they had the tailoring skills of a piece of dry driftwood.

“I’m thinking that’ll have to wait for when we have all the orbs on hand. At least, I hope so,” Velistraine muttered, and just like Maurice and Henry, she never stopped watching the scene ahead of them. There was an undeniable sense of unease hovering about. Especially considering that every few seconds, more ships were showing up.

“We’ll need to warn everyone as soon as possible,” Henry said, reaching a decision. With a thought, he began moving his deployed Avatars. It had already been a week out here, which meant they’d been gone for close to a month already. Ideally, they’d be out here for another week at most, harvest all they could before heading back and from then on, they could use time dilation to their benefit. “As for whether I’m attacking them right now or not… I’ll wait. Maybe we’ll nibble at the edges once the queens are gone. I’d say… let’s give them an hour or two. We can’t delay much more, and we need all the help we can get,” he added, muttering the last bit mostly to himself. While no one disagreed, Henry was still wary.

Slowly, he had his Avatars pull away before they started tucking each other into his Maw to be deployed elsewhere until, after a few minutes, he was only left with a couple dozen that he then pulled away from the door and after hesitating for a brief moment, he decided to have a pair constantly watching over the queens. Each would have two Lifeblood Avatars watching from a distance, camouflaged, and hopefully, he’d be able to keep track of them. To minimize future encounters, as the more he tangoed with them, the higher the risks were for him and for everyone back in their hidden world. One mistake was all it’d take. Plus, Henry had recently experienced the effects of anti-teleportation magic. He didn’t know if these creatures had something like that, but he tried minimizing its uses, just in case they tried to trap him that way. Thankfully, Henry could always self-destruct himself fast enough that nothing they used could stop him, but if he gave them enough chances and opportunities to try, he’d bet they could come up with something. He didn’t want one of his clones in their hands. Right now, the damage might be limited. But once Henry, Maurice, and Velistraine made it back in their world, he’d rather not leave a thread leading the mantises straight to him and to everyone else. Even if Attal and Zerathstra knew there was some sort of threats waiting from the other side of the veil, Henry was willing to bet that even in their worst-case scenario, they weren’t picturing something of this scale.

So Henry pulled his reserves and prepared himself for his next attack. They had to go home as soon as possible. They had to prepare. They had to be ready. And the next couple of days only added to their urgency.

Apparently, Henry had also underestimated the scale of this enemy.
