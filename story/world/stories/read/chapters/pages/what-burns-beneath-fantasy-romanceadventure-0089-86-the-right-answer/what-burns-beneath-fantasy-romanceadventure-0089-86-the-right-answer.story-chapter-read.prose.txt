I followed the Thalessian with slow, uneven steps. He led me into the barracks interior, which was now occupied by a handful of unfamiliar faces. Men and women were scattered throughout the main room, chatting as they oiled bits of armor or rifled through packs. They all wore what I was beginning to recognize as the Sedgewick Vale Watch uniform; supple brown jerkins worn over creamy linen, with steel-gray cloaks and iron broaches carved in the shape of a sickle and sword tilted crosswise.

“I’ll be using your office for a time, captain,” the Thalessian murmured to an older man engaged in conversation. It was not phrased as a request.

The captain had cropped salt-and-pepper hair and weary eyes. He gave me a curious look, then dipped his head. “Of course. First door on the left.”

“No interruptions.” He beckoned me towards the room, then added over one shoulder, “And some of my men will be reconvening here for supper. See that food and water are provided in plenty.”

“Yes, sir.”

All noise was swallowed when we stepped into an adjoining office and my guide shut the door. It was a small, poorly-lit space with a single rectangular window. An oak desk sat in the center with a flickering candle on top and two dense wooden chairs on either side. The Thalessian took the further chair, beckoned me to sit across, and clasped both gauntleted hands atop the desk.

So closely confined, I found myself unable to look away from the smears of blood on his glove. And I realized with a shudder that it wasn’t on the fabric itself. The leather had an odd metallic fixture tightly clasped around the knuckles and back of the hand; it looked like a thin veneer of backwards, slightly raised snake scales. Fresh blood had pooled beneath each divot.

I imagined the feel of such jagged metal against flesh. Bile crept up the back of my throat.

He noticed my attention and flexed his hand. Slick metal gleamed like fire in the candlelight. “Don’t fear, my lady. I assure you, both prisoners are well restrained. You’re perfectly safe.”

The bile thickened. “Sir, I-”

“Vhaik Brisimere.” His lips thinned into a tight line, one corner quirking up. “I prefer transparency in discussions of this nature.”

My pulse jumped. Discussions of this nature. Saints.

“And you are one Brin Damelle.” His clean hand slid a piece of parchment from one vest pocket. He unfolded it atop the desk with a crinkle, then scanned the writing. “I’ve read your account.”

“And you had… questions?”

“Several.” He offered no more, simply turned his chin downwards and spent a long while reading what was, presumably, Joselia’s cramped handwriting.

I began to fidget. “Sir… if I may ask… the prisoners. The pacted. Don’t Fae bargains sometimes make it… well, impossible for them to speak of some things?”

He didn’t look up.

“I met a man in Snowmelt. One who was bound to a hag and couldn’t tell anyone about her. I imagine Joselia wrote it all down.”

“Baltha. I see the name referenced.”

“Yes. He… this man, when he tried to tell us things it was like… like he couldn’t breathe. Like the words were choking him.” I winced, looking at the bloody glove. “Surely your prisoners are forbidden from giving away their master’s secrets, so… it… it just… I don’t understand why you would-”

“Torture them?” He said it so casually that I caught a breath, certain I’d misheard. He finally looked up and gave a dry chuckle. “An unpleasant necessity. You’re correct about the… limitations at play. But,” that soulless smile returned, and the sight of it made my gut churn, “sometimes a fruitful interrogation doesn’t necessitate asking the obvious questions, but the right ones.”

The right questions. I closed my eyes. Inhaled slowly. Right. Breathe. I don’t have to convince him of any lies, I just need to give the right answers. I can do this.

“It just seems cruel. Sir.”

“Your clothes are very fine.”

“My… thank you?”

“It was an observation, my lady, not a compliment.” Vhaik leaned forwards, fixing me with that piercing stare. “I was not aware that Fellbrook held any nobility.”

My skin flushed with heat. “They were… part of my inheritance, I suppose. My mother left them for me.”

“Ah. She was wealthy?”

“Yes. She died when I was very young.”

He said nothing. Just searched my face.

I shifted in the chair. The wood was uncomfortably hard, digging into my bottom and spine in all the wrong places. “You had questions about Fellbrook? About the attack?”

The ghost of a smile graced his mouth. “Not at all. Dame Joselia was quite thorough in her report. My questions are about you.”

Me. Right. Breathe. I fought to keep my expression neutral.

“It is rare,” he stood, hands clasped behind his back, and turned to face the window, “that we hear accounts of… what sounded like a conversation with a monster. A lengthy and detailed conversation, in which this creature saw fit to divulge plans of an attack on Sedgewick Vale and speak of cryptic rituals.”

My mind raced. I gnawed my lip for a moment, then offered in a shaky voice, “I don’t know if conversation is the right word. More like… he gloated while I cowered in a corner.”

He didn’t turn. “Mm. I’m sure it was very frightening. Did anyone else witness this… gloating?”

I swallowed. I had, of course, pointedly not told Joselia about Royce and his injuries. He was unconscious. You can’t be a witness if you’re unconscious. “No.”

“Are you a maiden?”

I started. “I… beg your pardon?”

Vhaik slowly pulled his gloves off, set both atop the desk, and lounged beside them. He propped an elbow on his thigh and leaned forwards. A perfectly casual pose, and the look he leveled at me was perfectly casual, too. “It’s a very simple question.”

My cheeks burned. I found myself glancing around, as if an escape lay hidden on the floor. “It is, but I don’t see-”

“I see no band of promise on your wrists. No husband’s garnish on your fingers. You’re… in your early twenties?”

“Twenty-two, but-”

“Uncommonly pretty, and with an inheritance. Yet no young men have sought courtship?”

“They have,” I ground out, hands curling tightly, “but my guardian left the choice to me. What does that have to do-”

“Some Fae offer power, my lady. Some offer vainer things. Beauty and wealth, among others. I can’t help but wonder if your… status as an unpromised, wealthy, and beautiful young woman is a new development.”

My jaw dropped. “Salt, you think I bargained with a monster for good looks? For clothes? Sir-”

“Were you attracted to him?”

“What?”

“Did you find this creature desirable? Alluring?”

“Alluring?” I nearly choked on the word. “I found him frightening.”

His smile didn’t falter. “The two are not always incongruent.”

‘You very nearly enjoyed yourself.’

My eyelids slammed shut. “They are for me.”

Brittle silence settled through the air. Then, in a very mild tone, he said, “You didn’t answer the question.”

My nails dug into the armrests. “I… I suppose under different circumstances, yes. If he had been a normal man, I would have considered him… h-handsome. But it wasn’t a man who walked into my home and terrorized my village, it was a monster! I found him terrifying and cruel and… and… vile!”

“Hm. I must say, my lady, this is the most impassioned I’ve heard you, yet.” He rasped a wintry little laugh. “So either you feel very strongly about this, or… well. Shall I quote the poets about women who protest too-”

“How dare you?” I stood, shaking with… I wasn’t even sure what. Anger and fear and the overwhelming urge to run. Not just from this man, but from the cold ivory digging into my hips. From the smell of roses and the mocking rumble of laughter and the awful pressure as he closed in-

“I dare many things. You may sit.”

I remained standing. It was probably very foolish, but my defiance wasn’t born from any kind of strength; rather, my legs wouldn’t move.

‘You weren’t the only one enjoying yourself, Brin.’

I bit the inside of my cheek. Hard enough to taste blood, and hard enough to ground me. “What is the point of these questions?”

He examined his fingernails. They were very clean and clipped to even, polished edges. “It’s chilling, my lady. Truly. The horrors one hears from survivors of Fae attacks. Tales of the most heinous tortures; of flesh and minds fed upon, of spirits broken and souls defiled.” He inclined his head, gaze resettling on me as one slowly traced along the bloody scales. “I wonder what terrible fates nearly befell our guests in the other room. What price they paid for their power… and their safety.”

I sank back into the chair. My chest felt like a fist was closing over it.

“The fact that a monster was in your home, and for quite some time, yet you emerged unscathed…” The skull-like grin re-emerged. “Truthfully, I find it… impossible.”

My tongue tasted like ashes. “Perhaps he wasn’t in the mood for torture.”

“Perhaps.” One too-clean hand stroked his jaw. He searched my face. “Did you exchange anything for your safety?”

My pulse jumped. “I… D-did I-”

“When this creature came to your house, was there an exchange made for your safety?”

When he came to Fellbrook? No. Not that night. I sucked in a steadying breath. “No. He… I was frightened when he came to the house, but he didn’t seem interested in hurting me.”

His eyes narrowed, as if the phrasing had piqued a keen interest, and his next question slid out like heavy oil. “Ah. Was he interested in anything else?”

At my lack of an immediate response, Vhaik shifted forwards. He dropped slowly to one knee, bracing an arm against it as if kneeling before me. A position of near-subservience, but it felt quite the opposite; it felt as if he was laying claim to the very space around me.

The feeling only grew when one hand settled atop my lower thigh. Narrow fingers splayed wide, palm resting just above my knee.

Every muscle in me coiled. I straightened, watching him with all the wariness of a trapped animal. “Take your hand-”

“You needn’t fear me. Not if that is the explanation.” He rasped a little laugh. His fingers tightened ever so slightly. “Even if you were willing. These creatures can be very charming, my lady. Seductive. You wouldn’t be the first blushing maiden lulled in the night.”

The blood drained from my face as I seethed, too furious at the insinuation- and too terrified of the empty calm on his face- to answer.

Vhaik continued, searching my eyes, “And there would be no punishment for an exchange of that sort. Not beyond your own shame, of course. And whatever misfortune your belly might show.”

I clenched both hands in my lap, spine ramrod straight, biting down a tide of frantic vitriol just as the door swung open with a shattering crack.

“Sorry to interrupt, sir.”

I nearly fainted. Renner somehow managed to sound snide and smug and vicious all at once, and it was the most wonderful thing I’d ever heard.

“Sir, I tried to stop him.” Heavy footsteps and the Watch captain’s out-of-breath voice snarled from behind.

“Get out.” Vhaik remained kneeling. His unsettling eyes traced the lines of my face, searching for answers. For guilt. “Both of you.”

“Nah.” Renner’s smug tone sharpened to a pointed edge. “Signal smoke’s up. You’re expected, right?”

A muscle at the corner of Vhaik’s silvering temple throbbed. The cold gaze shifted away from me, finally, and towards the man doubtlessly smirking in the doorway.

The ensuing silence felt like a noose around my throat. I closed my eyes, head turning to one side. Leave. Listen to him. Leave. Please, please, please-

Leather creaked in front of me and the pressure of his hand slowly lifted. Cloth rustled, then metal clinked just ahead.

I dared to look up. The Thalessian stood beside the desk, expression neutral. He reaffixed his gloves as I hurriedly stepped towards the open door, not waiting to be dismissed.

“One last question, my lady.”

I froze with one foot out of the room. My mouth went dry. “Y-yes?”

He waited for what seemed like an eternity. Then, still in that casual murmur, asked, “Have you seen the Fae since you left Fellbrook?”

Renner stiffened beside me.

The right answers. The right answers. That’s all it is. I drew a deep breath, turned, and met the Thalessian’s stare evenly. “Yes. Every nightmare.”

I turned and left.
