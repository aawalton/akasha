Her hand was back. A hand that wasn’t hers. And that was the least of the weirdness Acrypha was dealing with just then.

The moment she had been swallowed up by whatever weirdness had eaten her, the pain had stopped. A part of her wanted to attribute that to the spirit of her parents. There had been those hands. Strange, but familiar. Twisted, different, hardly anything like what they had been in life, yet there all the same.

They had come for her, after all. Even now, even after everything that had happened, they were protecting their daughter.

Or at least, they had left that impression in the Living Mana, which was now carrying that duty out with faithful dedication. For the new hand that had replaced the one eaten by the link to the demiurge made of Living Mana entirely.

If only Acrypha could take the time to appreciate the wonder of it and get over the trauma of what had happened to her original hand.

Sadly, the weirdness dragged away her focus.

The world she was in now was a riot of colours. Reds crested and troughed above her like an ocean in the sky. Blues and greens cratered and sank beneath her like a forest being struck with invisible meteors. Through it all, virulent molten gold cobwebbed and fractured across it as though she was seeing everything through broken glass.

And why was she floating? Or was she floating? Where was she? How was she surviving?

So many questions crowded Acrypha’s head, she could almost forget her circumstances. Almost. But after seeing what Mavar had done, after experiencing the hardships he had dealt her and the tyranny he had inflicted on Rhel and Viren, her mind kept returning to the battle. She had to get back. She had to help. She had to…

The colours wobbled. Strands of opalescence broke away from unseen space to swim around her in fine filaments. They were enchanting. Mesmerizing.

She touched one strand with her new hand, and new power flowed through her.

“What is this…?” Acrypha gasped out.

This magic. She had never felt anything of the sort. Her soul was shivering. Her manasoul, her Soul Glyph, every bit of magic and sorcery she contained, including all her Inscriptions, everything shivered. She was turning into something. The hand was just the beginning.

It should have felt frightening instead of enlivening, worrying instead of empowering.

But then, what was someone like her supposed to do against a mage who could supposedly ascend to become a Great God? What was Acrypha capable of against someone like that without greater and greater power?

“This is your potential,” someone answered.

Acrypha started. It felt like the words were vibrating through the Living Mana itself. The voice was both familiar and possessed a tinge of wrongness to it she couldn’t really understand.

“And your curse…” it continued.

Acrypha swallowed. “Who are you? What do you want with me?”

It was reverberating with a slight echo in her head. Some sort of telepathy? Or was there a being inside her spirit somehow?

“I am a part of you,” the voice said.

“What does that mean?”

Her own words had taken on an edge of harshness. This was no time for riddles. If this was power, then she’d take it and use it as she saw fit. If not, then she’d discard it. She didn’t know if she had saved Viren and she intended to find out, and if this power assisted her in doing so, then she’d wield it like the weapon it was.

“Do you know where you are, child?” the voice asked.

That made her falter. Acrypha had been so focused on her goal that she had stopped thinking about anything besides it. Her current location should have been an important question, but her priorities had made her push it down.

“No…” she admitted. “Where?”

“The Overrealm.”

It sounded vaguely familiar. “Where is that?”

“In the Plane of the Great Gods.”

Acrypha’s thoughts screeched to a halt. Great Gods. Ascension. Had whatever that pulled her in actually been aiming for the Demiurge instead? Had she somehow stolen his opportunity? Maybe his power too?

The thought left her feeling both giddy and faint at the same time. Bastard deserved nothing less.

Yet, if she was about to be flooded by the power that should have gone to a new god…

Acrypha would probably die.

The thought was sobering, but not actually frightening or disheartening in any way. Her goal hadn’t changed. If anything, she was even more determined to accomplish it.

“How did I get here?” she asked.

“I know little,” the voice said. “Only what I am to you, and what you can be.”

“What I can be?” The harshness returned. “Please, I don’t have time. Are you the Living Mana? If you’re part me, cursed or blessed or whatever, then can you lend me aid? I need it to save a friend, and I need it now.”

“You fail to understand much, child, through no fault of your own. Where lies this friend of yours?”

“Not here. Outside this… Overrealm.”

“As I feared. One cannot simply pass in and out of the Overrealm at one’s will. Not even the Great Gods.”

That was true. For all that magic ruled the Accorded Realms, for all the fantastic events and beings that existed in the cosmos, the presence of the Great Gods was not one of them. Most people didn’t even think they were real. Not in the cosmos, at least. Just entities that existed in some other dimension, who could grant blessings to rare individuals.

“There has to be a way,” Acrypha said. “If someone can break in, then surely I can get out.”

She knew she was supposed to be able to count on Ryland, but she couldn’t help it. If she didn’t find a way to get to Viren and ensure his safety, if not beat down that utter prick of a Demiurge, then she’d never be able to live with herself.

“I know no such procedure,” the voice said, sounding almost sorrowful. “I am you, after all. I am a part of you.”

Acrypha shook her head. “A new part of me.” She raised her new hand. “You’re this. And if you can exist here and there, surely you can establish some link. Some connection.”

The voice was silent for a while. Maybe Acrypha had finally scared it off. Or maybe she needed to accept that she had gone mad and then work accordingly. Who in the blasted Realms talked to people in their heads?

“There may be a way,” the voice finally said. “A way to establish contact and shift existences between the Planes.”

Acrypha latched onto it like she was drowning and it was all that would save her. “What do I need to do?”

Because surely a disembodied voice couldn’t exactly do anything. Though, there was this new arm she had gained. Someone or something must have given it to her. Someone or something capable of active acts of magic.

“Close your eyes and prepare yourself,” the voice said. “You are not alone. But know this, when the time comes, you yourself won’t be able to return to your old universe. Only the intention of your will.”

Acrypha’s first impulse was to complain. What did the voice mean she wouldn’t be able to get back to where she belonged? Surely this was merely an extension of her kidnapping, and she’d be able to return to Arcoryx Academy once this was all done. That thought was what had kept her going so far.

But she shoved away the feelings harshly. Her eyes teared up and her breaths came out faster than before, but she forced herself to focus. She’d worry about her own circumstances once she was done with all this. Once she had saved Viren.

“I’m ready,” Acrypha whispered.

The voice heard her anyway. Then it sent her forth.

Mavar Val Arona existed everywhere, and it left him feeling incomparably alive.

He was in the rushing wind, in the drowning rain, in the blistering lightning, in every iota of Rhel he had painstakingly enforced with his own will over centuries. And more than Rhel, he was among the divine too. His ascension was nearly there.

Incarnation of Control? Hardly. This was just an intermediary step, a middle stage before his real objective. Mavar would become a Great God even if it killed him.

For he was never going to be satisfied remaining in the same stage that Ryland kept himself to.

“You inconsiderate fool!” Mavar cried. A gargantuan iceberg, almost a simulacrum of the Continental Icedrift, formed high over Ryland’s head before slamming at him like the spear of a giant. “Do you have any idea how long I’ve been awaiting my ascension? Do you know how long and hard I’ve toiled to achieve my crowning glory? My rightful place amongst them?”

There was no answer. Instead, Ryland shouted another spell. “[Reality Scar].”

Space cracked open, swallowing up the gargantuan iceberg as though it had never even been there to begin with.

Mavar scowled as he was forced to repair reality again. His Soul Glyph of Unity flared bright hot, allowing him to wrap his manipulation around the vector and force it to adhere to a semblance of normalcy, closing the breach Ryland had clawed open.

“[Sun Rain]!” Mavar cried.

Pillars of scorching light broke through the clouds of storm and fiery fume, beaming down to the earth to let loose mountainous explosions. None of them actually hit Ryland for he evaded deftly. He was constructing a spell. A complicated one.

An enormous rift cracked open above Mavar, swallowing all the pillars of light.

A moment later, more of Ryland’s accursed rifts cracked open all around Mavar, forcing him to start dodging the enormous lasers he himself had crafted. This was one of the main annoyances of facing Ryland. One could never be certain when one’s own power would be reflected back at them with such unerring accuracy.

Yet, like Ryland himself, Mavar was channelling mana and casting magic even as he evaded. Light lengthened. Colours warped. The universe stretched out as Mavar started controlling time itself.

There. Now Ryland was trapped. His figure was frozen. No matter what barriers and protections he enjoyed, Mavar the Eternal would use eternity itself to take him out.

Ryland appeared right in front of Mavar with a dark gold crackle of his rifts, his arm transformed into that spectral skeleton variant. He punched Mavar, who was attempting to overcome his shock, but still managed to just barely evade the blow.

“You—” Mavar had been about to ask how he had evaded the trap of time itself, but he was familiar with Ryland’s magic. This had the taste of [Timescape]. “You god’s cursed trickster. You’ve forgotten what it means to be an Incarnation, so I’ll show you what a newborn god can truly do.”

“All you’ve shown me thus far, Mavar,” Ryland said. “Is how the centuries have given you power, but no real wisdom.”

Mavar growled. The sky burned beyond them, red and black and ablaze. Farther below, Rhel was a wasteland of broken magic, titanic behemoths breaking and reforming while devastating the surface of the planet. Shattered mountains rained down to make more blood of Rhel’s innards gout in massive eruptions.

“You have no idea, Ryland,” Mavar said. “None at all.”

“Don’t I?” His eyes glowed like the pinpricks of the night sky. “I know full well how you struggled under my shadow while we worked together, eventually reaching a decision that you manipulated the world to accept, but one which forced me to leave for I could not abide by it.”

“Do not dare rewrite history!”

“You knew that I stood head and shoulders above you, so you spent the next few centuries devising a way you could trump me in your misguided notions of what power meant.”

“You—”

“You knew you yourself would always fail to catch up to me, so you didn’t even bother trying. Instead, you stole the power of all the Realms. You tapped into the phenomenon of Living Mana, gathered and condensed it until it would amplify your magic and grant you access to reserves of might hitherto unforeseen in all of the cosmos.”

Mavar screamed. “And I will—”

“And you’ll fall regardless.”

His form glimmered. Mavar found himself frozen. It was as though he was the one now petrified in time. No. No. He knew this trick. Calamity-cursed Ryland was merely manipulating Conceptual Glyphs.

So, he blasted his own will against the fiend of an Incarnation. This world was his. Rhel was his domain, his property, his very self. His Soul Glyph of Unity ignited with fury, crashing against the imposition Ryland had enforced against the world. This reality wasn’t Marionys Ryland’s to manipulate. Impossible.

“Too late,” Ryland said. “[End—”

Mavar didn’t care how late it was. He simply set his soul on fire and unleashed everything.

A star’s core bloomed in the skies of Rhel. Everything became ignited to such a degree, half the world of Rhel was bathed with burning, conflagrating light.

Mavar laughed in the midst of the blinding, eradicating brilliance. His mana ejected out of him with violent intensity, and his Incarnation form was forming a burning link with the Living Mana injecting into the atmosphere of Rhel from where it was left behind in the cosmos. Together, they amplified his power.

Together, they enforced his unstoppable control.

For Mavar was breaking the world. It didn’t matter. Once this was done, once Ryland was beaten, he could remake it all. A Great God could do as he willed.

And a Great God could call down the wrath of the Overrealm itself. For that was how he was fuelling this starlike explosion. By channelling an impossibly overwhelming amount of mana, so much so that even his Incarnation form struggled to pull upon the magical energy, Mavar was deliberately drawing on a very different source of power.

The Overrealm itself.

Cracks and splinters sent golden beams of the divine shooting through Rhel’s atmosphere, causing further disruption and destruction. But Mavar had set all that he controlled with his Soul Glyph of Unity to ignite and eradicate itself, and by impressing his will upon the cracks of the Overrealm, he was causing divinity itself to fracture and break and burn.

“Where are you now, Ryland?” Mavar asked. His very words were rippling like fuel set aflame, blazing like a conflagration. “Where…?”

Oh, Mavar wasn’t a fool. He had set the atmosphere on star-core fire for a very wide distance, and even then, Ryland had most likely escaped to a safe point. Perhaps he was even preparing some sort of countermeasure. A counter spell to drill through overheated plasma.

And kill Mavar.

But he was preparing his own next move too. This heat was merely the beginning. This warped mixture of matter and energy was only the first step.

All of which now paused because something was already making its way through the energy.

Someone had broken out of a rent of the Overrealm that Mavar himself hadn’t called forth. For just a heartbeat, Mavar wondered if Ryland had opted for a more devastating direct attack. But no. The figure was very different, had a completely separate profile and taste of magic.

It was—

The girl!

But not her. Not truly. This felt like an apparition given solidity, a ghostly form that allowed her to phase through Mavar’s obliterating brilliance, brimming with her own foul magic to annoy him just as her master was doing.

That cur Ryland. All his high and mighty spiel about saving children and now he was sending one of them as sacrificial pawns to defeat Mavar where he alone couldn’t. When Mavar got his hands on that feckless bastard, he was going to eradicate every iota of Ryland’s being and wipe out his memory from across the Accorded Realms.

“I don’t care how you’re evading my prowess, girl,” Mavar said. “Depart now before I focus my ire upon you.”

The girl continued approaching. Her face was twisted with detestation. “I’m here to stop you, Mavar Val Arona.”

“[Dead Aura Embrace],” Mavar incanted. “You will die. We’re both aware you won’t leave a scratch upon me.”

The girl grinned at him. “It’s a good thing I’m not targeting you then, is it?”

Mavar scowled as his magic added another layer of annihilating fury. The aura he had called on was manifesting with incredible power. Crystalline panes were dividing reality, before cracking and crushing everything they held, light itself screaming as it was ripped apart.

But the girl was fine. And she really wasn’t attacking him. Instead, her assault, her strange magic that was flecked with both the divine energy of the Overrealm and something that was intimately familiar to Mavar now, was heading someplace else. Someplace close.

“Wait,” Mavar ordered. “Where are you—how are you channelling Living Mana and divine energy? Living Mana…”

His eyes widened. Then he roared.
