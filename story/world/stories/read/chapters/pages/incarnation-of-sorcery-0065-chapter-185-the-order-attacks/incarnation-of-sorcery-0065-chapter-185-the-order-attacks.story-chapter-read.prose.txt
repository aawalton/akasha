Viren’s heart thudded like it was part of the storm high above him. He was having a hard time believing it, but then, unbelievable stuff kept happening.

In this case, he was facing a literal invasion. That too just days after having dealt with one himself. Well, not himself, technically. That was all Ryland. As usual. Which was probably why he was all alone this time when his own temporary base was about to be assaulted by powerful foreign entities.

Viren glanced back down at the wide field stretching out before him. Out in the open, he was nothing more than a gnat. He would probably die at the first clash of powers. Just one look at the sky above him confirmed that the attacker was significantly higher-ranked than Viren.

Which meant he’d need to get under Ryland’s fortifying spells any way he could.

Honestly, he was surprised he had remained calm enough to figure his goals out so quickly. Maybe hanging around Ryland and watching impossibilities fly around like nothing had hardened him somewhat.

The invader in question soon came into view. All of Rhel in Viren’s field of view had sunk into a dark gloom, thanks to how far that enormous smoky cloud brimming with lava had spread. But the darkness didn’t hide the features of his enemy, who had brazenly chosen to show themselves.

Not that one needed much bravery to stand against someone like Viren.

He frowned. For a second, he thought that one of the Astral Raiders that Ryland had repelled from Abarosk had found their way to Rhel somehow. The being floating far above Viren had the same ashen-metallic skin, the same burning coals for eyes, and the same powerful build as the raiders.

But that was where the similarities ended. Aside from the difference in their sorceries and how they had chosen to invade, Viren’s adversary had a very different bearing compared to the Astral Raiders.

Those half-robes, half-armour lent the invader a regal air. Their manasoul was so strong, it was making the air ripple around them like Viren was watching a mirage. The sceptre in the invader’s hand glowed like its top was filled with lava too, wreathed with burning orange lightning.

And then there was the insignia too. The one with the talons around a Realm. Great. The Flourinus Order had come to pay a visit, and Viren was the only one who was home.

“Ah, I seem to have come across a rat!” she said.

Her voice was loud enough to drown out the lightning of her lava storm. Viren could almost feel her regard settling on him with all the power she was capable of summoning, like he was an actual rat noticed by a soaring hawk.

“Takes one to know one!” Viren shouted back. He wasn’t sure it was enough for her to hear him. In fact, the thunder of his own heart felt louder than his voice, despite the increase in volume.

But the invading forgean Order mage heard him very clearly. “The rat speaks. How entertaining! Do you know what’s more entertaining than a talking rat, rat?”

Viren was not about to rise to the bait, even though he was actually quite tempted to ask what.

“It’s a scurrying rat, of course!” the Order mage said.

Then she proceeded to fling her magic.

“[Volcanic Rain],” she shouted.

The storm of lava broke. It shattered out from the sky, burning and breaking and blasting through everything as it descended towards Viren like an avalanche of utter annihilation.

Viren was already running. There was no way he’d normally be able to outrun such a gigantic spell on his own, but he was boosting himself with quick applications of his Tier [7] Glyph of Ignition. Little jets of compressed flames were pushing him forward ever faster. He just needed to get to the fortification. He just needed to be a little faster.

The landslide of lava and half-molten debris crashed behind him and shattered the land. A spike of worry and dismay stabbed into Viren’s heart. Ryland’s Inscription. It was getting assaulted and there wasn’t a single thing Viren could do to stop it.

Except run. Except distract. The woman was laughing. The Order operative was taking more pleasure in treating Viren like a cat with its prey than in destroying the Inscription.

He could use that, right?

Well, part of him intended to. The rest of him was screaming as he desperately attempted to outrun the tidal wave of lava rushing along right behind him. He felt like his hindquarters surely had to be on fire. Which was a very ironic statement considering what his Soul Glyph was supposed to be.

A thundering crack behind him nearly threw him off his feet. Had that just been—

Viren looked up and saw the Order mage raising a hand high, lightning flickering in her upraised fist like a javelin about to be thrown. Curse the Calamities. As if the tide of lava wasn’t bad enough, now she was throwing lightning down upon him too.

He now had to time his dodges so that he wasn’t blasted by literal bolts from the blue—or black, rather, in this case.

Viren couldn’t stop screaming as bits of him caught fire while he just barely managed to keep dodging. Just a little farther. Just a little more…

“[Overburn]!” Viren shrieked in desperation.

A wall of flames exploded behind him, and combined with the addition of the Subglyph of Ignition, Viren finally gained the momentum he needed to throw himself right into the blessed safety of that complicated barrier he himself was trying to reproduce. Thankfully, this was Ryland’s well-established one, not the amateur copy Viren had attempted.

He was just in time. The fiery explosion of volcanic power burst across the fortification Ryland had left, crackling in a spray of lurid, orange lightning that embedded glowing lava right into the barriers.

Viren’s heart felt like it was trying to climb into his mouth. Barely. Just barely he had managed to save himself.

“Aw,” the Order mage moaned. “Did the rat manage to reach its little hole?”

Despite how his legs were trembling, like he was a Calamity-cursed newborn foal, there was no time for him to lie shaking in fear and terror. That wasn’t how heroes faced their foes, regardless of the odds. Viren would be no different. This wasn’t a battle he could run from, so he had to stop fleeing.

“You talk a lot,” Viren shouted back up. “For a scaredy-cat throwing down lightning and fire from far above and far away.”

Like Viren himself, the Order mage didn’t rise to the bait either. “Such paltry scratching and clawing and squeaking will hardly affect Bhusine of the Flourinus Order!”

For a time, the mage was content to just sit back and blast Viren with her spells and firepower. And at the same time, Viren just sat back and only partially worried. He still hadn’t lost faith in Ryland, of course. Nevertheless, he couldn’t stop fretting.

The blasts were powerful.

Bhusine’s magic was incredible. Thundering bolts struck with ever greater frequency, five or six of them landing within the space of a heartbeat. Blasts shattered like wartime bombing. Viren’s whole body kept shaking and trembling with every impact.

Underneath him, the Inscription remained steady and unaffected. Almost like it had even more faith in Ryland than Viren himself. Which was annoying.

Or maybe he was just getting frustrated at himself for not doing anything.

Viren forced his fear and panic down and tried to think. He looked around. Thanks to all the firelight, he found the Glyph of Communication Ryland had left behind for emergencies just like this.

Throwing himself to the Inscribed Glyph, Viren began activating it.

The Glyph failed.

Viren stared. The light from Bhusine’s endless blasts cast everything in a hellish glare, as if Viren had been transported to a Realm where even Ryland's Glyph wouldn't work.

The Order mage. It had to be her. Somehow, she was blocking communication.

Of course she was. Of course she wouldn't want Ryland to return instantly with his rifts, especially after she had gone to such lengths to ensure she attacked when he wasn’t present.

Once again, Viren was struggling against anxiety. The fortifying spell was taking a terrible fiery hammering, and though Viren could place his trust in Ryland, was his only option to just wait it out inside the spell until Ryland arrived? Was he supposed to remain trapped inside like a chicken in a storm-beset coop?

Sweat started beading down his face. The air and the ground were warming up like an oven.

Yet again came a frightful amount of fear, and yet again, Viren pushed through it. There was a way he could control how things went. The heat was his friend. An Archdragon had breathed its essence into Viren. This fire, this heat...

This was supposed to be his.

“[Fuel Soul],” Viren incanted. “[Immolation Immunity]. [Overburn].”

With the help of his Soul Glyph of Inferno, Viren set fire to everything within the fortification spell besides the Inscription itself. That was the only thing that needed protection, thanks to his newly acquired [Immolation Immunity] spell. As such, he didn’t hesitate to set himself alight as well.

The heat pressed in on Viren like a heavy weight, but unlike with the insurrectionists attacking when the Prime Minister had visited Arcoryx Academy, Viren wasn’t burning. He wasn’t actually harming himself. His new spell was working wonders.

Especially when his plan came to fruition. Bhusine’s attention flickered away, apparently satisfied that she had dealt with her rat and the Inscription that was supposed to be her main target.

Viren remained motionless anyway. She’d start bombarding the next area soon, and in fact, if Viren strained his senses, he was pretty sure he was already hearing her assaulting other locations.

Which was good. For the first time, Viren could breathe easy. In a mental sense.

Physically, every breath was searing his lungs. He needed to focus past the pain, because burning his whole body would have been significantly worse. So, for now, he attempted to crawl towards the edge of the fortification spell without giving anything away. If he managed to set fire to other fortification sites from a distance, then he could indirectly chase Bhusine away.

Of course, the problem with that plan was that the other sites of the fortification spell were a little too far away. But Viren refused to let that daunt him.

Spells had ranges that were increased and enhanced by increasing their Tiers. If he was incapable of reaching the other sites currently, he’d find a way to increase his own capabilities on short notice, if need be. He’d sneak out of the protective barrier of the current fortification spell he was under too.

That turned out to be unnecessary, however.

Viren only managed to crawl to the edge of the fortification spell before he spotted something very weird. Space was warping around Bhusine. It almost looked familiar, yet unfamiliar too. There was something strange about it.

Especially because he was starting to realize that Bhusine herself wasn’t causing that. She appeared to be fighting against the spatial warping. Or rather, at the moment, she was merely evading it. Bhusine was quickly flying across the face of her smoky cloud of lava-lightning, trying to see if there was some location the warping wouldn’t follow her.

But it did. It remained hot on her heels, caging her whenever it caught its target. And Viren was pretty sure it would be fatal if that thing caught her.

“Enough!” the Order mage screamed all of a sudden. A variety of spells shot forth, but Viren only managed to catch the last one. “[Universal Heat Expansion].”

The space around her glowed, the air and the light within the region now swimming so much that just looking at it gave Viren a headache. He still stared. Bhusine was literally controlling space itself through the use of heat.

Was it possible for Viren to reach such a level? What kind of spell even was that?

But maybe this was his chance. Now, Viren needed to get out and attempt a sneak attack. Ryland had proven that differences in power could be overcome with the right application of surprise.

“No, no,” a familiar voice said. “You stay in there, Honoured Disciple. Your heroics thus far have been enough. I’ll extricate you shortly. Just hold for the time being.”

Viren gawked as Devas appeared from another warp in the air. His one was edged with blue, a cerulean portal closing behind the Turnalian.

“You!” Viren said. “What are you doing here?”

“I’ve come to deliver an important message to Master Ryland, of course,” Devas said. He was peering closely at the flames, his manasoul brimming with sorcery. “Now, let’s see…”

“Don’t worry.” Viren quickly got to his feet, surprising the Turnalian. “These flames are mine, and I’m perfectly fine.”

Devas blinked a few times at Viren before nodding. Then he looked up where the Order mage was dancing to his tune because of his spatial magic. “I wasn’t expecting to arrive upon an abandoned planet, where a worm from the Order is brazenly assaulting you, Honoured Disciple.”

Viren’s mind reeled. He was supposed to be feeling relief, and he was, but he was just left wondering how in the world the somewhat insane Turnalian had tracked them down. Had Ryland left a message about where they were going or something? Admittedly, it wouldn’t be difficult to track them. It wasn’t like they had taken especial care to hide their location.

“Fear not, Honoured Disciple,” Devas said. His wooden armour started rustling and creaking, like the wood was starting to shift and grow. “With me present, no foul member of the Flourinus Order will ever touch the divine work of Marionys Ryland.”

Viren believed that wholeheartedly. The Turnalian was impressively strong, enough to have annoyed Ryland once not that long ago.

That said, Viren had no intention of simply remaining inside the fortification spell. With a little exertion of his will, the [Overburn] that was roasting everything inside the fortification spell now came to a stop, including the harmless flames upon Viren himself. Then he stepped out from safety. “I don’t intend to sit back without making Bhusine pay.”

The Turnalian looked visibly impressed at the way the flames had just disappeared without a mark. “Then let us counterattack that Order madwoman together!”
