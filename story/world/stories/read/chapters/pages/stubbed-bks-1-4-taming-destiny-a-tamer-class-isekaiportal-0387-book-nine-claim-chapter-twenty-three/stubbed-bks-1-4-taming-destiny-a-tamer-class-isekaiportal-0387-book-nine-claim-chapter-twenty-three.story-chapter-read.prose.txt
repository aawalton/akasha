Ninja snarls angrily and digs in her claws as the wooden den rocks wildly, a loud boom echoing from above. She’s been pacing back and forth ever since the battle started, desperate to be up there, helping. But this is her post – protecting the samuran cubs.

The boom from above is echoed by a boom from next to her. She jumps again – this wooden den is very alarming sometimes.

Suddenly, she hears chirps of distress coming from within the room she is guarding. She hesitates. The adult defender is inside. He should be able to come to their aid. Yet the chirps continue, intensifying.

Growling to herself, the raptorcat leaps for the door. She will not let the cubs be hurt. The handle is more awkward for her to open than it is for Packleader, but she succeeds nonetheless. As soon as the lock has disengaged, a wash of cold water forces the door fully open and streams past her and into the corridor.

Considering they are floating in water, that is concerning enough. The sight before Ninja’s eyes, however, is even more so.

The adult defender is down, his head bleeding and his eyes closed. He’s still breathing; still alive. The source of the blow is apparent – there is a massive chunk of the cold semi-clear stuff Packleader calls ‘ice’ that sticks through the wall. It is around this that water is leaking inside the wooden den. Every moment that passes sees more water enter and at a faster rate as the ice chunk shifts. The samuran cubs are already wet up to their hips from before Ninja opened the door.

Ninja’s thoughts move fast. That the samuran cubs and the adult defender need to leave is certain. She cannot think that water entering the wooden den is a good idea either – they will all get wet, and so will all their nests.

However, the water didn’t leave the room until Ninja opened the door. Perhaps if she closes it, she will trap the water inside again. It makes sense.

But first, the cubs and defender.

Ninja runs further into the room and, while keeping a wary eye on the ice chunk in case it decides to attack her like it did the defender, she nudges and chivvies the cubs out of the room.

When the last is gone, she bites the scruff of the defender’s neck and starts dragging him. Samuran adults apparently do not have much of a scruff, so Ninja’s teeth dig in rather deeper than she’d prefer, but she figures it is better than being trapped inside a room that is filling with water.

It takes a good deal of effort to close the door, but Ninja is determined and succeeds. The flow of water from the room halts entirely when a loud click rings out.

She then takes a moment to contact the rest of the pack to tell them what happened. Packleader is concentrating hard on something else, so Ninja doesn’t disturb him. Instead, she reaches out for Matriarch.

Matriarch sends a sense of alarm at her news, and pride for how Ninja reacted. She also indicates that others will be there to help the defender and to deal with the hole and water in the wooden den. She charges Ninja with looking after the samuran cubs until then.

Ninja accepts the duty – it is what she would have done even had Matriarch not asked her.

However, when she pays attention to the cubs in question, she snarls angrily. Half of them have taken advantage of the lack of supervision to vanish. Sniffing at the wooden boards and eyeing the wet pawprints, Ninja easily picks up their traces.

Thoughts once again moving quickly, she herds the cubs in the direction that their fellows took. She leaves the adult defender – Matriarch has sent someone to help so there is no point in Ninja struggling to bring him with her. It will only slow her down.

The cubs are easy to herd when they are trapped in the narrow wooden tunnels, but harder when there are different routes forward to take. Ninja has to continually leap back and forth to corral the inquisitive cubs. They have quickly overcome the fear of the cold water and their too-still caretaker, and are possessed once more by a spirit of curiosity.

Ninja wishes there were two of her – this is exhausting. Old grief weighs on her. If Storm were here– She cuts her thought off, focusing back on her task. They still have not seen tail nor claw of the missing cubs.

They climb towards the highest level of the wooden den. Ninja feels fear creep through her. She has paid enough attention to the Packbonds to know that it is dangerous above there. Yet that is where the trail leads.

She cannot take the cubs out there knowingly. Going after the ones who have emerged already is bad enough, especially when it’s due to her lack of attention that they escaped her in the first place. Taking others out there…no. She can’t.

But perhaps she doesn’t have to. There’s a group of humans standing near one of the exits onto the outside of the wooden den. They’re tense, afraid. And several of them have Bonds.

She focuses on the one Packleader often speaks to, sending a picture of the samuran cubs and a question.

He responds with words that mean little to Ninja – the thoughts he sends over the Bond are far more useful.

She relaxes very slightly – they have caught some of the cubs. At her demand, they bring the cubs forward. The raptorcat growls a little at the appearance – four of the cubs have their claws and snouts bound shut; two more are in a bag. But she can understand – if the humans just grabbed at the cubs, they probably tried to defend themselves. The faint, appealing scent of blood hangs in the air. Human blood. She feels a moment of pride for the cubs’ fierceness before dismay takes over: there are not enough here.

Ninja has learned to count by watching Packleader and there should be twenty-six cubs. With the six the humans caught and the twelve Ninja has been herding, that makes only eighteen. There are still eight cubs missing.

She sends another thought at the human, this one very pointed. The human replies with regret and embarrassment. His humans were not fast enough to catch all the cubs – the missing ones escaped onto the top layer of the wooden den. The human wasn’t willing to put his humans at risk by taking them out in pursuit, and so far, the wind beast doesn’t seem too interested in the small cubs. But Ninja knows how quickly that can change – a rous is not appealing when there’s a helven carcass, but if the rous is all there is to eat, it is a different matter.

Ninja gives a very firm order to the human – to guard these cubs and prevent them from leaving until the adult packmates come – and impresses upon the cubs that they must wait here for their caretakers. These ones are the less troublesome of the group – they will most likely obey. She also reaches out to the four adult samurans, letting them know where the cubs are.

Matriarch hears of her plans to go out onto the top layer of the wooden den, and urges her not to, but in this case, Ninja ignores her. Matriarch means well, but Ninja refuses to just let the cubs die. Because they will. Even if the wind beast does not attempt to eat them, they will probably fall off the ship.

And if Ninja dies in the attempt? Well, her sister taught her that sacrifice was only a bad thing for those left behind.

The human tries to stop her too, but when she snarls at him and bares her teeth, pressing her determination to do this – through him, if necessary – he steps back and bows his head in submission.

The other humans do not dare block her way, and she quickly makes it out into the tumultuous world beyond.

Wind rips across the wooden ground, making it hard for Ninja to walk upright. Instead, she crouches close to the wooden lengths as if she’s stalking and digs her claws into them for purchase. She tucks her wings close to her back – they will only cause her to be more unstable.

Ahead of her, she spots one cub, being pushed this way and that by playfully menacing wind. Ninja is strongly reminded of how she used to like to play with leaves and insects.

Digging her claws into the wooden ground, she leaps forwards and snatches the samuran cub up in her mouth. She’s gentle with her teeth until it squirms, and then she bites down a little in warning. Not enough to pierce its fragile scales, but enough to make it clear she could if she chose. It goes still.

She takes that one back to the tunnel from which she emerged and spits the cub out, nudging it so it stumbles forward. One of the humans quickly grabs it and she purrs in approval. Turning around, she starts searching for the next.

She finds two more in similar situations – one is pinned in a corner of tumble-down crates and the other is staggering close to another tunnel. Quickly depositing those with the humans, she searches for the last five.

She spots three of them and a snarl builds in her chest. They are on opposite sides of the ship, two together, and all are in immediate peril. One is far too close to the edges of the wooden den, and is about to tumble off it entirely. The other two are playing tug-of-war with a piece of rope, unaware that the rope is attached to a wooden box that is only just balanced – if they pull even a fraction harder or the wind blows a gust in the wrong direction, the box will land right on them.

Yet there is only one of her. Ninja has to choose who to help, and in doing so condemn the other or others.

No. No! She refuses to accept that.

She failed her sister when she was still barely more than a cub. She will not fail even a single one of these cubs. Even if it takes everything she has to give.

For a moment, she almost feels her sister’s weight press against her side, a nudge at her jaw. A presence despite her long absence. She longs to press back, yearns to just bathe in the sense of someone dear nearby who was lost too soon.

Yet she cannot. There are those who need her more than one who is beyond her help.

Along with her determination,something else gathers inside her, a sense of immense pressure. The world seems to pause around her. The wind stops howling and tugging at her wings; the cubs freeze in place. The sense of pressure builds and builds and builds until Ninja can barely breathe with its strength.

And then she feels a sudden pain deep within, like something has forced itself into a space too small for it. The pain doesn’t last for long, and turns into a deep sense of satisfaction and rightness.

Yes, this is what I was always meant to be, Ninja finds herself thinking.

The next moment, all that pressure explodes out of her and the world comes alive again. Her vision is blocked by silvery sparks, as if she were one of Packleader’s fires.

No! she cries. I need to see!

And, as if responding to her thoughts, the curtain of sparks shifts so she can see through them.

She knows that this is the moment of choosing, that she’s supposed to concentrate now on what she wants to be. This is her Evolution – failure here may mean she never achieves it in the future. But the world continues moving and the cubs are in danger.

Following her instinct, she runs towards the closest of the cubs – the one that has climbed onto the railing and is but a small gust of wind away from the water far below – and…projects herself towards the other two, a slip of shadow flying away from her.

Her teeth close around the cub just as it misses its step and tilts precariously outwards. At the same time, she just knows that she has growled at the samuran cubs playing with the rope and frightened them into running for the tunnel. She pursues them and snaps at their heels even as she digs in her claws and leaps for the tunnel with a cub in her mouth.

The humans look pale as she delivers the three cubs to them. Ninja knows not if it is the shadowy double that chases two of the cubs, or the silvery sparks that continue to encircle her. Evolution continues.

A cub’s distressed chirp rings through the air, a gust of wind bringing it to Ninja’s ears. She glances around to see that the wind beast is playing with the last two – tossing them up in the air and catching them only just before they hit the wooden ground.

Snarling angrily, Ninja gouges the wooden ground as she tears towards them, her strides feeling more powerful and faster with every step.

Yet even so, she is at a disadvantage. The wind beast perhaps senses her arrival and does not wish to lose its playthings so quickly. It pulls them up higher, tossing them around in the air like the round, inflated bladder that Packleader introduced to the cubs in the other village.

The chirps increase in distress, though one is worrisomely silent – limp.

Ninja snarls. This beast may be an Immortal, but it will not take her cubs!

Her shadowy double soars up in the air towards the cubs and in a flash of silvery light and a pull, Ninja finds that she has replaced it. Snatching one cub with her jaws, she grabs the other between her front claws.

Extending her wings, she slows down their descent, the wings appearing to increase in size and strength even as she moves.

The silvery sparks are almost gone by the time she lands heavily on her back paws, letting the cub in her front claws fall gently to the wooden ground so she can catch herself before she crushes it.

Yet, before she can cram it too in her jaws, the wind beast takes hold of it again, a tentacle of wind around it so dense that she can see it. It tightens bit by bit, the beast or its magic taking pleasure in squeezing the life out of the cub.

Ninja snarls around the cub held carefully in her jaws, and strikes out at the wind with her claws.

They will cut! she determines fiercely, somehow utterly certain that they will have the effect she wants.

The last of the silvery sparks around her blink out, but their glow seems to have been transferred to her claws. They leave silvery after-images as they slice through the tentacle of wind. And, as she knew they would, they slice right through it.

The cub falls free and Ninja wastes no time in grabbing it with her shadowy double’s jaws. The wind beast is very powerful, and now it is probably also very angry.

Yet, as she runs as fast as she can – which is a good deal faster than before her Evolution – she feels a warm sort of satisfaction that seems to loosen the chains that have been around her heart ever since she let her sister be killed.

I saved them. I failed you, Storm, but…I saved them.
