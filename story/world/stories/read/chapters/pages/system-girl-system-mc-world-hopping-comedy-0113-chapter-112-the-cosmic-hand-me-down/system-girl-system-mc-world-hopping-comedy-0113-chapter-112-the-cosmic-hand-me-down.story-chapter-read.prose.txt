I stared, completely dumbfounded, at the woman who had just cheerfully announced herself as the embodiment of the universe.

She stood in the center of the immaculate, high-rise penthouse, looking back at me with bright, expecting eyes. Then, she let out a sharp gasp.

She bonked herself lightly on the head with a closed fist, a gesture so profoundly human it made my brain short-circuit. "Oh, my silly me! How could I forget? We cut the cake first, then we open the presents! Let me light the candle."

She reached down, scooped me up by the armpits without a shred of effort, and set me down in a small, plush chair pulled right up to the low glass table.

I sat there, frozen. The massive, pristine white cake loomed in front of me. I wanted to run, to demand answers, to throw a tantrum. But when the physical embodiment of the universe tells you to cut a cake, you cut the damn cake without questioning anything.

I sat there on the small chair behind the cake, swinging my tiny, feet. I watched her disappear behind the three-layered confection, heading toward what looked like an open-plan kitchen area to my left.

One moment later, I saw her exit from behind the cake on the left side, walking out of the kitchen with her hands empty. I let out a long sigh of relief, glad she hadn't found some absurd, cosmic weapon to cut the cake with.

But a fraction of a second later, I saw her again.

She exited from behind the cake, this time moving toward the right side of the room. I blinked, rubbing my eyes. She was aggressively rummaging through the drawers and behind the polished marble counter of the kitchen island.

When did she get back there? I thought, my heart hammering against my ribs.

From the periphery of my vision, I once again saw another shadow—which looked suspiciously like her elegant silhouette—exit to the left, heading toward the mountain of presents. When I snapped my head to look there, and then back at the woman who was supposedly standing next to the counter, she was completely gone.

Am I seeing things? I wondered, a spike of pure panic hitting my chest. Or is she multiplying and disappearing into thin air?

My frantic thoughts were interrupted when she abruptly exited from what seemed to be a walk-in pantry directly behind me. She was carrying a small, plastic lighter.

She sighed, flicking the lighter open. "You can never find these when you actually need them."

She leaned over the table and carefully lit the single, solitary candle sitting on the very top tier of the massive cake. The candle was shaped like the number 1.

Then, she unceremoniously slapped a pointy, cardboard party hat onto my head. The elastic string snapped against my chin.

She turned off the main lights. The only illumination in the massive penthouse came from the flickering glow of the single candle, and the distant, colorful streaks of anti-gravity cars shining through the floor-to-ceiling windows.

I stood up on my tiptoes, leaning over the glass table, preparing to blow out the candle as she began singing.

"Happy birthday..."

Midway through the song, the volume kept increasing. It didn't just get louder; the actual number of voices kept increasing. It was eerie. It sounded exactly like someone was copying and pasting the same audio recording in Audacity with normal, fractional delays between them, creating an overlapping, slightly out-of-sync chorus.

"To you, dear Ana..."

I blew the candle out, less out of celebration and more because I was scared out of my mind.

"Dear Ana, yaaa..."

A massive chorus of claps greeted me as the overhead lights snapped back on.

I looked up. I saw the embodiment of the universe. Or rather, the multiple embodiments of the universe.

There were about fifty of her in the room now. They crowded the living space, standing by the windows, leaning against the kitchen counter, sitting on the arms of the sofas. They were all carbon copies of each other, wearing the exact same white sundress, clapping and cheering with identical, bright smiles.

I was doomed. One of her was a headache already. Now there are fifty of them? I struggled to get one single body to operate in the physical world, and she is casually flexing fifty of them on me at once just for a birthday song?

I tried to act nonchalant as I poked the massive cake with what seemed like a flimsy plastic knife she had placed next to my plate. The fifty embodiments all cheered loudly and popped confetti cannons that appeared from thin air. Some of them were whistling enthusiastically.

Damn it, I thought, glaring down at the white frosting. I am a dignified woman in my thirties. Why am I being treated like a toddler? This is humiliating.

Then, my stomach rumbled.

But I am oddly enjoying it. Why?

The Mother System—that was what I was going to call her, I decided mentally—subtly became one person again. The other forty-nine copies simply folded into the air, vanishing without a sound or a flash of light, leaving just the original woman standing in front of me.

She made a small, delicate ceramic plate pop into existence directly on her palm. She cut out a massive slice of the white cake, transferred it to the plate, and placed it right in front of me.

Wait, I thought, staring at the slice. If she can make things just pop into existence out of nowhere, what was all that fuss about looking for a plastic lighter to light the candles?

More importantly, I was entirely preoccupied by the massive slab of creamy goodness sitting in front of me. I may be a grown woman trapped in a child's body, but in the face of cake... who is not a child?

I picked up the golden spoon resting next to the plate and dug in.

I shoveled a massive bite into my mouth. The sugar hit my tastebuds like a physical shockwave. The rich, thick cream overwhelmed me.

Ahh, yes. This is bliss, I thought, my eyes fluttering shut. Cake is bliss. And experiencing it firsthand with one's own physical senses is pure bliss.

The woman sat down on the floor beside my small chair. She rested her chin on her hands, watching me eat with a fond smile.

"Would you like another slice?" she asked gently.

Who was I to deny the lady who embodies the universe? No sir. I am completely helpless here. Nothing to do but obey her wishes. Totally not my own greed speaking here.

The lady handed me a second, even larger slice on a fresh plate.

"Please stop calling me 'lady' and 'woman' in your mind," she said, her tone suddenly stern, though her smile remained. "I can hear it. It hurts my feelings. Call me Mama."

For a moment, my mind went totally blank.

I dropped the spoon. It clattered against the plate.

I had totally forgotten she can do that, I panicked internally. I seriously need some privacy here. She's reading my internal monologue like a teleprompter.

The woman stood up, putting her hands firmly on her hips. "There is nothing a child needs to hide from her mother. And did you just call me 'the woman' in the beginning of that thought?"

I scrambled backward in the chair. "Hey! I need more time, and significantly more cake, to accept this reality!"

She sighed, shaking her head. "If cake is all you needed, I'll have Earth-chan source all you want. In fact, I have sourced all these gifts over there," she pointed to the mountain of boxes, "from your native world of Earth."

Now she was just trying to bribe me.

"Mama." I blurted the word out, trying to appease her before she took the cake away.

I mean, wait. This is my mind. Can you not intrude on my internal monologues?

"Fine," she huffed, crossing her arms.

As I was thinking before being interrupted. Now she...

I saw, in real-time, her expression darkening into a terrifying scowl. I immediately caved.

Mama. Mama! You have my attention.

Her bright smile returned instantly. "Yes. I have even prepared a room for you in my home here. It is full of all kinds of things Earth-chan has procured for me."

I paused, a forkful of cake hovering halfway to my mouth. "You keep saying 'Earth-chan'. Who is that?"

"Ah," she waved a hand dismissively. "The World System of Earth, of course."

My jaw dropped. "Wait. Earth has a World System?"

"Naturally," she said, looking at me like I had just asked a stupid question. "Do you know how rare it is to find a planet without a World System? It’s kinda a given. If a planet has living beings on it, they will have a World System managing it."

I put the fork down. "Well, you clearly don't know, but the one I just came from didn't have one. Dolaria is vacant. The admin seat is empty."

She nodded, completely unfazed. "I know. That's why I sent you there. What do you think of that fixer-upper? With a bit of elbow grease, that one has a lot of potential. You can have it when you are all grown up."

I stared at her. My brain misfired.

Is she... I mean, is Mama offering to give me an entire planet like it's a hand-me-down car?

"Yes," she answered aloud, responding to my thought. "But you need to earn it. I am not raising pampered kids with a golden spoon in their mouths."

I looked down at the literal golden spoon I was currently using to eat my cake. I looked back up at her.

"Well," I said slowly. "Most spoiled kids get a sports car for their sixteenth birthday. You are casually offering a planet here."

She shook her head, holding up a finger. "No, no, no. I never spoil my kids. It's more like letting you drive the old family car. An old reliable one. That is not a sports car. We are Systems. You need to broaden your horizons. That planet is more like a beat-up Honda in Earth terms. A planet like Aethelgard, now that is more of a sports car."

I rubbed my tiny, chubby temples. "Wait. Why would you even give me a planet in the first place?"

"Who said I am giving you a planet?" she retorted, acting offended. "I said you can earn it."

Why does it feel like we are talking in circles here? I thought. Okay, wait. If you are giving me that planet to 'earn', I saw a future where it gets overrun by some odd, stitched-together entities. A bloody, horrific future.

"Yes, that's exactly why I said it's a fixer-upper," she replied cheerfully, completely ignoring the horror of the vision I had seen. "You will need to fix it up before you can use it properly. But it should be a good, stable source of SPP for your eventual evolution. You would need to fend off the eventual doom, then you would need to rebuild civilization from the ashes. But all that will come only after you become a Moderator."

I gaped at her.

Is she casually referring to fending off a global apocalypse as 'cleaning a car'? Is rebuilding civilization as casual a chore as changing a car's oil?

I looked at the woman sitting on the floor.

On the other hand, Mama is the physical embodiment of the universe. So maybe one single planet really is insignificant to her.

Her smile faded slightly. "No. I treat all planets with the same love and care. On that note, little lady. What exactly were you doing out in the void? You were incredibly lucky one of my bodies was near that sector. Or else your psychic form would have been eroded and permanently damaged."

I set the plate down. The memory of the violent storm and the golden rope flashed through my mind.

"Ahhh," I said, pointing a sticky finger at her. "So you are the stalker... I mean, the savior who grabbed my hand back then."

She puffed up her chest proudly, smoothing her white dress. "Yes. I have been watching over you since your birth as a system."

I glared at her. "Wait. Then why did you let me almost get factory reset and deleted by Alesia?"

She put a hand on her hip and proclaimed defensively, "Who do you think saved you there in the first place? I triggered the backup protocol. But... I do agree, keeping your existence a secret from the other Systems was my mistake. I did want to announce your birth to everyone in the network. But there are some greedy universes out there who may try to snatch you away from me if they knew you were a natural birth. So, I kept it under hush-hush for now. Just until you turn Level 7 and become a Moderator."
