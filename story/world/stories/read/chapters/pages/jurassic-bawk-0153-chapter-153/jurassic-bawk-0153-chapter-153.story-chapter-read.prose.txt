Charlemagne’s return to consciousness was heralded by a growing agony that was already so fierce that the oblivion of death seemed preferable.  But his enhanced body, nurtured by the System and transformed through the consumption of a myriad of other species, was simply too durable to give out so easily.  Even with the partial System lockout, his missing wing and lung were simply inconveniences rather than crippling injuries.

But death was closing in on the rooster, nonetheless.  The strange energy beam that had punched clear through Charlemagne’s flesh had somehow infected him, and he could feel the foreign mana attempting to penetrate further into his flesh.  His own mana was fighting tooth and nail to prevent the incursion, and, although it was slowly losing ground, was winning a war of attrition against the invader’s energy.  Unfortunately, it wasn’t winning that war fast enough.

Pushing through the searing pain advancing toward his Core, Charlemagne looked for a solution to his problem.  His Ignition Core, responding to his desire to purge the foreign mana, began to pulsate rapidly, sending power churning through his body.  Most of the mana eventually found its way to his left side, where it sought to annihilate the infection with extreme prejudice.  The rooster had never heard of antimatter, but the reaction when the invading energy met Charlemagne’s mana resulted in mutual annihilation in much the same way.

The issue was that the giant’s mana was so much denser than Charlemagne’s that it was taking only minimal losses as it advanced.  That had to change.  Looking into his Core, the rooster willed the mana contained inside it to take up less space.  At first, the roiling energy seemed to be confused, and it sloshed around like a drunken sailor.  But the rooster wasn’t deterred by his initial failure, and his focus never wavered for a moment.  The next attempt was less awkward, although equally unsuccessful.

In the background, the rooster heard Phasima yell something, but he was too busy trying to stay alive to catch what she had said.

During his third attempt at compressing the mana within his Ignition Core, Charlemagne felt something different.  This time, it felt like the mystical energy wanted to obey him, but there was some other force acting against his instructions.  As he attempted to grab hold of the mana, it was almost as if there was a slimy, greasy barrier that prevented him from getting a good grip.  The sensation caused him to pull away in revulsion, ruining his focus and allowing the mana to escape.

Steeling himself against the unpleasant feeling, Charlemagne immediately dove in again, pushing through the filmy barrier with a flex of his considerable willpower and finally forcing the mana to do what he wanted: fit more of itself into a smaller volume.

His success immediately led to a new problem.  With the mana in his Core now occupying roughly one-third of its original space, his Ignition Core was now mostly empty.  Sure, it would last longer against the encroaching energy that was slowly poisoning his body, but now there was a lot less of it.  Thankfully, there was a ready solution to this: there was so much mana being thrown around within the throne room that he should be able to replenish his reserves in no time.

Reaching out with his mystical senses, the rooster began to pull in as much of the fire and solar-aspected mana as he could, while ensuring that he stayed far away from any pockets of the strange mana type that was currently eating its way through his internal defenses.  It was harder than he was used to, partially because of the constant agony, but also because his System-granted Skills weren’t working the way they were supposed to.  He was forced to rely on his own incredible innate talent for manipulating mana.

That wasn’t an issue for Charlemagne, at least, not until the Sun Queen screamed.  What followed was a surge of power that dwarfed any he’d felt before, overwhelming his senses and sending the rooster back into the welcoming arms of unconsciousness.

Elidi stared in shock as her mind struggled to come to terms with what she had just witnessed.  Her only sister, the only other survivor of their kind, had just ripped out her own Core and shoved it deep into her chest.  She barely noticed the power thrumming through the magical organ as it pushed aside her flesh, heading straight toward the Sun Queen’s Stellar Core.  It was as if time had almost completely frozen as Elidi sought to capture her sister’s final moments in their entirety, committing each microexpression, every movement, and even the shifting signature of Phasima’s Manifold Core.

Then the Cores touched, and Elidi’s attention was forcibly pulled elsewhere.  She screamed.

Even for Vessarians, even for Vessarian sisters who were more than familiar with each other’s powers, merging Cores was a feat of legend, a triumph that would have been memorialized in stone, in song, and in script.  Given a choice, it was not something that Elidi would have attempted under any circumstances.

But she was not given a choice.

Her sister’s Manifold Core shattered entirely upon contact with the stronger Stellar Core burning within the Sun Queen.  The violent act released a storm of swirling energy from the remnants of the Cores.  Elidi found that the mana was mercurial and temperamental, quick to change aspects, but not impossible to coax into a new alignment.  With the lightest touch she could muster, the Sun Queen began to pull the unruly mana into her Core, which was already growing through accretion as the shards of Phasima’s Core were absorbed by Elidi’s.  The process was excruciating, as if someone was using a hot glue gun to adhere strips of extra muscle directly to her heart, but the influx of mana meant that she was able to stave off death for that much longer.

Phasima, seeing that her Core had been accepted, smiled as she cupped her sister’s face with her left hand.  Elidi’s eyes filled with tears even though she forced her gaze to remain fixed on the purple-robed giant still intent on vaporizing the two Vessarians.  Phasima opened her mouth to say something, and then collapsed to the ground.

“Phasima!” the Sun Queen screamed, her grief unblunted by the fact that she knew her sister was already dead the moment Phasima removed her Core.

“And then there was one,” the four-armed invader commented.  “But it seems to have bought you a moment’s respite.  Was the emotional agony of having to watch your sister perish worth existing for another minute or so?  Wouldn’t you have rather died together?  These are things I will undoubtedly ponder later, perhaps it will make a good object lesson for why being ruled by emotions is suboptimal.”

“Shut up!” the last Vessarian screamed as she instinctively pulled on the vortex of mana whirling around her.  Despite her heavy-handedness, the fickle energy obeyed her immediately.  More and more shards of Phasima’s Manifold Core were fused to Elidi’s, causing her Stellar Core to balloon in size by almost fifty percent.

“I’m going to kill you if it’s the last thing I do,” the Sun Queen snarled as she channeled more power than she had ever thought possible.  Her solar beam attack surged forward, crashing against the invader’s energy beam and forcing it back.

“Perhaps it is fortunate that I was here after all,” the purple-robed giant mused as he watched Elidi’s attack slowly overwhelm his own.  The invader, however, appeared to be unconcerned by the turn of events.

“Die!” Elidi shouted as she concentrated all of her power, all of her rage, and all of her pain into pure destructive light.  “Die and be forgotten, far away from home, cut off from those you love.”

The four-armed giant chuckled.

“Me?  Die here?  I think not.”

With that, he let go of the staff he was holding in his upper left hand, allowing it to fall.  The staff slammed into the floor with a heavy thud as the giant gripped his wand with two hands.

“All this time, I’ve only been using half of my full power.  Now witness my true might!”

Despair once again gripped the heart of the Sun Queen as a new wave of power roiled out from the purple-robed intruder.  Her solar beam quickly began to lose steam as the giant poured more and more energy into his own attack.  Then the beams once again stalemated, almost exactly in the middle.  Elidi’s rage flared to new heights as she realized that she was being toyed with.

“Just get it over with already!” she demanded.

“Why not cease your futile efforts and embrace the inevitable?” the invader retorted.  The Sun Queen had no ready answer for this, but she nonetheless refused to completely give up.  Long moments passed as the two sides continued to unleash ridiculous amounts of energy in direct opposition to each other as the stalemate continued.

But operating at full power was taking its toll on Elidi, who, despite her recent powerup, was still at a disadvantage.  Even her rage began to flag as fatigue mounted, taxing both her willpower and her energy reserves with every passing moment.  In her heart, she knew that she couldn’t win, but the Sun Queen hadn’t made it so far in life by just giving up when things got bad.

“Now would be a good time to jump in, if anyone is able to assist,” she shouted to the balcony, having already written the rooster off as dead.

“Teem is standing by to assist,” called a strange-looking Knight from up above, shadowed by a Squire who looked very similar.  “But Teem estimates that his odds of survival are lower than zero point one percent.  Logic dictates that Teem stand by at this time.”

“You coward!” the Sun Queen yelled back.  “Is there anyone up there that won’t immediately die?”

“The one known as Charlemagne has demonstrated his ability to survive a glancing blow from the intruder,” the robot-like Knight responded calmly, not reacting at all to the insult.

“The bird?  He’s lying in the corner, already half dead,” Elidi retorted.  “Wait, he’s…he’s healing.  But he’s still unconscious.  Maybe you could attempt to rouse him?”

“Teem will make the attempt,” the Knight agreed.  However, much to the Sun Queen’s confusion, it was not the Knight himself that leapt down to aid the fallen rooster, but the Squire.

“If you’re quite done begging for help, I think it’s time that we ended this little charade,” the giant growled.  “I have indulged myself enough, against my better judgment.”

With that, an enormous blast of power surged out from the wand, instantly pushing back the Sun Queen’s solar beam and threatening to engulf the last Vessarian within seconds.  Elidi screamed with effort as she pushed back against the inexorable power of the invader’s relentless assault.  Despite everything, she was determined to resist to the end, even if it was completely useless.  At the very least, she would die a death worthy of her sister’s legacy.

Such grim thoughts pressed their way through the haze of fatigue and strain that had slowly been building up within the last Vessarian as she overtaxed her mind, mana channels, and her Core in order to keep up with the tremendous strain that came from channeling vast quantities of mana over a long period of time.  She was exhausted, but the looming specter of death refused to allow her to rest.

“Eureka!” exclaimed a voice from the balcony.  It was Phatagin, who had been deep in concentration as he focused on the strange sensation that had been growing inside him.  “The System is not necessarily infallible, it seems.  Once I accepted that premise, the rest was only a matter of time.”

“I thought that you would rather die than give up the System,” the giant answered, appearing confused for the first time.  “Nevertheless, it is too late to renounce your master.  You must die with the rest!”

“I have no intention of dying today, you over-armed freak of nature,” the pangolin retorted.  “We are going to win!”

With that boast, Phatagin leapt off the balcony and, rolling into a ball, floated toward the throne room’s ceiling.
