The mud at the base of the Ashenfall timber walls could only now be described as a graveyard.

Josh knelt there, his heavy steel boots sinking into the dark, churned sludge. He pressed his left gauntlet firmly against the jagged, bleeding gap in his side where the Wight's greatsword had bitten through his armour. The necrotic cold radiating from the wound was agonising, actively fighting against the hot, fresh blood pouring down his ribs. He breathed in ragged, shallow gasps, his storm-grey eyes tracking the retreating backs of the elite skeletal knights.

They were gone. The flawless, terrifying phalanx of blackened steel had marched rapidly into the swirling morning fog, entirely abandoning the victory they had so nearly secured.

"I do not understand it," Bhel wheezed, standing a few feet away. The dwarven fighter leaned heavily on the haft of his iron axe, his thick chest rising and falling beneath his blood-soaked bandages. "They had us. We were completely spent, Josh. Another minute, and they would have cracked our skulls open like walnuts."

"I am not going to complain about the timing, Bhel," Josh replied, his voice a harsh, breathless rumble.

He slowly turned his heavy steel greathelm, looking out over the wider battlefield.

The contrast between the apocalypse of five minutes ago and the current reality was entirely jarring. The massive, shrieking ocean of rotting meat that had been relentlessly pounding against the walls had completely lost its singular, focused drive. Without the oppressive, dark magical tether of the Bone General forcing them forward, the thousands of zombies, Crypt Ghouls and skeletons had reverted to their most basic, primal instincts.

They were no longer an army. They were a pathetic, disorganised flock of dead sheep.

Thousands of the monsters simply stopped walking. They milled about aimlessly in the freezing mist. Some of the ghouls screeched confusedly at the pale morning sky, while others began to wander in tight, pointless circles. A few of the rotting villagers bumped blindly into the smouldering remains of the pitch trenches, recoiling from the heat without any aggressive intent.

However, a handful of the lesser zombies standing closest to the iron portcullis had not forgotten the scent of fresh blood.

A bloated, purple-skinned ghoul missing its lower jaw stumbled out of the milling crowd. It locked its milky, lifeless eyes onto the kneeling human and let out a wet, gurgling hiss. It lurched forward, raising its jagged claws.

Josh did not even have the energy to raise his longsword.

Before the ghoul could close the distance, Bhel let out a tired, annoyed grunt. The dwarf did not step forward to meet the charge. He simply planted his heavy iron boot, twisted his broad shoulders, and hurled one of his throwing axes with a casual, lethargic flick of his wrist.

The heavy iron blade struck the ghoul directly in the centre of its forehead with a dull thud. The monster collapsed backward into the sludge, its limbs twitching uselessly before going still. Bhel trudged forward, ripping his axe free from the skull with a wet squelch, and walked back to Josh's side.

"Right," Bhel sighed, wiping the black gore from his axe on his leather trousers. "That is quite enough heroics for one morning. Call for a rope or something, lad."

Josh nodded, letting his heavy electrified longsword fall from his grip. The blade hissed as it sank into the freezing mud. He grabbed the rim of his dark heater shield, using it as a crude, heavy crutch to force himself painfully upright. His legs trembled violently under his own immense weight, threatening to buckle, but he locked his knees and looked up at the towering timber wall.

"Can someone please send down a bloody rope?" Josh roared hoarsely, his voice cracking from the freezing wind and the smoke. He leaned down to grab his sword and sheath it, a task he found extremely difficult.

The faces of the defenders lining the upper parapet stared down at them in stunned disbelief. For a long, breathless moment, nobody moved. The militiamen and the city guards were still trying to process the miraculous retreat of the Wight elite guard and the sudden pacification of the horde.

Then, a flurry of movement broke the spell.

A thick, heavy coil of hemp rope came tumbling over the wooden railing, unspooling rapidly until the frayed end slapped heavily into the mud right beside Josh's boots. A second rope followed instantly, landing near Bhel.

"Tie it securely beneath your arms!" Torin's gravelly voice shouted down from the platform. "We will haul you up!"

Josh reached down with shaking, blood-stained gauntlets. His thick, steel-clad fingers felt entirely numb, making the simple task of knotting a rope feel like an impossible puzzle. He managed to loop the thick hemp securely around his chest, passing it beneath his armpits and over his breastplate, pulling the knot as tight as his failing strength allowed. He looked over and saw Bhel doing the same, the dwarf gritting his teeth in agony as the rough rope pressed against his wounded ribs.

"Pull!" Josh yelled weakly.

The slack in the rope instantly vanished. High above, a dozen desperate militiamen and veteran adventurers grabbed the hemp line, planting their boots against the wooden floorboards, and heaved with all their remaining strength.

Josh was physically ripped from the freezing mud.

The ascent was excruciating. The rough bark of the solid oak logs scraped harshly against his dark steel plate armour as he was dragged vertically up the thirty-foot wall. His full body weight, combined with the extreme density of his Adamantite-infused bones and his heavy gear, made him an incredibly heavy burden. He heard the men on the wall groaning and straining with the effort, their boots slipping on the blood-slicked wood.

His wounded side screamed in agony as the rope dug into the overlapping metal faulds near his waist, putting severe pressure directly on the jagged laceration. Josh squeezed his eyes shut behind his visor, grinding his teeth together to stop himself from crying out.

Finally, a pair of strong hands grabbed the thick iron collar of his breastplate.

"I have him!" Garrik's familiar voice yelled.

The tavern landlord, still wearing the dented greathelm, hauled with a massive surge of adrenaline. Josh was dragged unceremoniously over the wooden lip of the parapet, crashing heavily onto the blood-stained floorboards of the central platform. Bhel was hauled over the railing a second later, the dwarf letting out a loud, breathless groan as he hit the deck beside his friend.

For a single second, Josh lay flat on his back, staring up at the pale, smoke-filled winter sky. He had survived. The gates had held.

Then, a blur of pristine pale green and brilliant white violently entered his field of vision.

Carcan did not walk over to him. She practically dived.

The elven healer dropped to her knees on the filthy, blood-slicked wooden deck right beside him. She did not bother with a clinical assessment, and she did not ask him where it hurt. She threw her slender arms entirely around his heavy steel breastplate, burying her face into the thick, padded gambeson at the crook of his neck.

Josh gasped, completely caught off guard by the sheer, desperate force of the embrace.

She hugged him with an unyielding intensity. It was the frantic, clinging grip of someone who had spent the last twenty minutes utterly convinced they were watching the person they cared about most bleed to death in the mud. Her shoulders trembled violently, and he could feel the hot, wet tears soaking right through the padding of his armour.

"You idiot," Carcan whispered fiercely against his neck, her melodic voice completely breaking. "You absolute, reckless idiot."

Josh slowly raised his heavy, exhausted arms, wrapping his thick gauntlets gently around her slender back. He closed his eyes, completely surrendering to the embrace. The smell of burning pitch and rotting meat was instantly replaced by the clean, comforting scent of expensive incense and dried lavender radiating from her robes.

As she held him, Carcan did not neglect her duty. Her hands, pressed firmly against his backplate, flared with a brilliant, blinding golden light.

The healing magic poured from her palms, completely bypassing the cold steel of his armour. It flooded directly into his veins like a river of warm, liquid sunlight. The intense, holy energy aggressively hunted down the dark, necrotic frostbite left by the Wight's greatsword, burning the corruption away in a split second. Josh felt the deep, jagged laceration in his side forcefully knit itself back together. The severed muscle fibres reconnected, the skin fused flawlessly, and the crippling agony evaporated, replaced by a soothing, profound warmth.

He let out a long, shuddering sigh of absolute relief as the pain vanished.

However, the healing process only took a few seconds. The wound was completely closed, the bleeding had stopped entirely, and the dark magic was purged.

But Carcan did not pull away.

She remained kneeling on the floorboards, her arms wrapped tightly around his neck, her face buried in his shoulder. The frantic, desperate energy of the hug slowly morphed into something significantly softer, and undeniably more intimate. She was simply holding him, clinging to the solid, physical proof that he was alive.

Josh kept his arms wrapped around her, his heart hammering a frantic, heavy rhythm against his repaired ribs. He was acutely aware of the hundreds of militiamen and city guards standing in a wide circle around them, watching the quiet, highly emotional reunion.

The lingering embrace stretched on for five seconds. Then ten. The tension on the bloody wooden platform slowly shifted from absolute relief to a very quiet, very palpable awkwardness.

A loud, deliberate, and highly exaggerated clearing of a throat shattered the silence.

"Er, right," Bhel grumbled loudly from the floorboards a few feet away. The dwarven fighter was sitting up, leaning heavily against a wooden supply crate and clutching his own freshly bleeding bandages. "I am incredibly happy you two are having a moment. Truly, it warms my old, stony heart. But some of the rest of us could really do with a bit of that golden light."

Carcan froze.

She pulled back from Josh instantly, scrambling backward on her knees across the wooden deck. Her pale face was flushed, painted a brilliant, vibrant shade of deep crimson that extended all the way to the tips of her pointed elven ears. She refused to meet Josh's eyes, her gaze suddenly fixed intently on the blood-stained wood beneath her robes.

"Yes. Of course," Carcan stammered uncharacteristically, completely losing her serene, dignified composure. She scrambled to her feet, grabbing her amber-topped staff, and rushed over to the complaining dwarf. She pointed a trembling finger at him. "You do not need to hug me, Bhel. Just hold still."

"Oh. I thought I was special," Bhel chuckled, a harsh, wheezing sound, as he allowed her to press her glowing hands against his torn chest.

Josh pushed himself slowly up from the floorboards, his repaired muscles holding his immense weight flawlessly. He stood to his full height, rolling his broad shoulders to test the freshly knitted skin.

The moment he stood upright, the stunned silence on the wall completely broke.

A massive, deafening roar of absolute, unadulterated elation erupted from the defenders lining the ramparts. The civilian militia, the veteran adventurers, and the heavily armoured city guards threw their spears and swords into the pale morning sky, screaming themselves hoarse.

They were not cheering for a generic victory. They were cheering for the human Guardian who had literally leaped off a thirty-foot wall to crush a Bone Goliath, and who had stood alone in the mud against the elite guard to buy them time.

Garrik stepped forward, pushing the dented visor of his greathelm up. The tavern landlord had tears streaming down his soot-stained face. He did not speak. He simply reached out and clasped Josh's thick steel forearm in a warrior's grip, offering a deep, profound nod of absolute gratitude.

Several other militiamen rushed forward, clapping Josh on the back, offering breathless thanks and praises.

Josh offered them tired, genuine smiles, accepting the thanks with humble nods. But as the crowd surged around him, his storm-grey eyes scanned the wooden platform, looking for the familiar flash of crimson.

He had not seen his best friend since the massive pillar of plasma had descended from the sky.

He pushed his way gently through a group of celebrating bakers, stepping towards the inner railing of the platform.

His heart plummeted entirely into his stomach.

Lying completely motionless on the rough wooden floorboards, partially obscured by a stack of heavy wooden crates, was Brett. His friend was sprawled on his back, his limbs splayed out at unnatural angles. His new crimson Ember-silk cloak was draped over him like a burial shroud. His skin was the colour of old parchment, entirely devoid of any healthy hue, and his long fingers were curled into tight, rigid claws.

"Brett!" Josh yelled, the sound tearing violently from his throat.

The heavy fighter lunged forward, his massive steel boots thudding loudly against the deck. The pure, unadulterated panic completely erased the relief of the victory. He dropped heavily to his knees beside the fallen mage, his gauntlets reaching out frantically to grasp his friend's shoulders.

Before Josh could shake him, a slender hand clamped firmly onto his wrist.

"Do not move him, Josh," Carcan ordered.

The elven healer had abandoned Bhel the moment she heard Josh's panicked shout. She knelt gracefully beside Brett, her face entirely serious and clinical, completely stripped of her previous embarrassment. She pressed two fingers gently against the side of Brett's pale neck, locating his pulse.

"Is he...?" Josh started, his voice cracking, unable to finish the horrific question.

"He is alive," Carcan confirmed instantly, her melodic voice completely calm and reassuring. She moved her hand, resting her glowing palm flat against the centre of Brett's chest. "His heart is beating, though the rhythm is incredibly shallow and erratic."

Josh let out a long, ragged breath, resting his forehead against the cool steel of his own gauntlet. "Then what is wrong with him? Why won't he wake up?"

"He is suffering from mana exhaustion," Carcan explained quietly, her silver eyes scanning the mage's rigid features. "The spell he cast to save you and Bhel was entirely beyond his current limits. He completely emptied his core, and then he forced his physical body to generate the remaining mana."

She gently uncurled one of Brett's rigid, claw-like hands. "His evolution allows him to channel massive amounts of thermal energy, but it requires a constant, passive flow of mana to protect his own internal organs from the heat. When his core ran completely dry, he essentially cooked himself from the inside out."

"Can you heal him?" Josh asked, his storm-grey eyes filled with desperate pleading.

"I can repair the minor internal tissue damage," Carcan replied, weaving a slow, delicate thread of golden light into the mage's chest. "But I cannot artificially refill a depleted mana core. Healing magic requires a living spark to latch onto. Brett's spark is currently nothing more than a dying ember. He simply needs time, complete rest, and absolute safety to naturally regenerate his reserves. He will likely be unconscious for a fair while."

Josh nodded slowly, the heavy tension in his shoulders easing significantly. Brett was out of the fight, but he was going to survive.

He carefully gathered the unconscious mage into his arms, lifting Brett with exquisite care. The elemental mage weighed practically nothing, his lean frame feeling entirely fragile compared to the heavy steel plating Josh wore.

"Find a stretcher," Josh commanded a nearby guardsman. "Get him down to the deepest, safest cellar in the temple district and do not let anyone disturb him."

The guardsman offered a crisp salute, immediately running off to find the requested medical supplies.

With Brett safe, Josh finally turned his attention back to the wider battlefield. He walked to the edge of the parapet, resting his heavy, dark steel shield against the wooden railing, and looked out over the northern plains.

The sun had fully crested the eastern hills, burning away the worst of the bruised purple twilight. The harsh, bright light illuminated the true, impossible scale of the slaughter.

The area immediately surrounding the base of the timber walls was a horrific, charred wasteland. The alchemical pitch trenches were completely smothered by massive, smouldering mountains of blackened corpses. Tens of thousands of heavy iron arrows pinned shattered skeletons to the freezing mud. The massive crater Brett had blown into the centre of the lines was filled to the brim with dark sludge and dissolved bone.

But the true spectacle lay further out.

The undead horde, which had numbered roughly ten thousand at the break of dawn, had not retreated. They had not fled back into the corrupted forest.

They were simply standing there.

The vast majority of the lesser infantry, the zombies and the rotting ghouls, were milling about aimlessly on the open plains. They bumped into one another, completely devoid of any aggressive intent. Some were wandering slowly in wide, erratic circles, while others simply stood perfectly still, staring blankly at the frost-covered grass beneath their rotting feet.

Without the commanding intelligence of the Bone General forcing them forward, the ambient dark magic animating them provided no tactical direction. They were a massive, terrifying biological hazard, but they were no longer a coordinated military threat.

"It is over," a baker whispered, leaning over the railing beside Josh. The man dropped his crude iron spear, tears streaming freely down his soot-stained face. "The Light be praised, it is actually over."

All down the line of the ramparts, the feeling of profound, exhausted celebration began to truly take hold. Militiamen were embracing each other, weeping openly. The guard captains were shouting hoarse orders to stand down, allowing the exhausted fighters to finally drop their heavy weapons.

The joyous noise drifted down into the city below. The frantic, desperate wailing of the refugees packed into the plazas slowly shifted. The terrifying screams of panic morphed into a massive, rolling chorus of cheers and absolute relief. The heavy bronze warning bells in the central watchtower ceased their frantic, rhythmic tolling, replaced by the slow, triumphant ringing of the victory peal.

But standing on the wooden platform, Josh did not cheer.

He looked at the massive, disorganised ocean of monsters, a heavy, cold knot of unease settling firmly into his stomach. The battle for the walls had undeniably been won, but the victory felt entirely incomplete.

"Do not let them lower their guard," Caistina Tharistrar’s voice sounded from behind him.

The elven mage trainer stepped up to the parapet. Her sapphire-blue robes were uncharacteristically stained with soot, and the brilliant white light of her staff had dimmed to a faint, exhausted glow, but her posture remained rigidly straight.

She turned her head, looking out towards the distant, corrupted treeline on the northern horizon.

"Perberos and Ronald must have succeeded," Caistina stated, her melodic voice filled with quiet, profound respect. "They found the commanding entity in the dark, and they executed it. They saved this city."

Carcan stepped up beside her, her hands gripping the wooden railing tightly. Her silver eyes were fixed entirely on the distant, dark forest, searching desperately for any sign of movement.

"But where are they?" Carcan asked, her voice trembling slightly.

The terrifying reality of the situation crashed over the small group standing on the platform.

The elite Wight knights had not stayed to fight, and they had not scattered aimlessly like the lesser zombies. They had broken off the engagement at the gates and marched with desperate, terrifying speed directly back towards the northern horizon.

"Then the battle is not over," Josh stated, his storm-grey eyes burning with uncompromising, fierce loyalty. He looked at Carcan, remembering the heavy oath he had sworn to her brother just hours ago. "We need to get them back."
