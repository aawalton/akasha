The first artificial Myrmur Host that came charging at them met its match against four six-armed gangsters. Three of them boxed the creature in against the wet corridor wall while a fourth circled behind it. The Myrmur itself was a lobster-type with hooked forelimbs, giant claws, and overlapping plates that clicked every time it moved, but the Saint’s Hands never aimed for the body trapped inside. Two men hacked shallow cuts into the outer carapace with broad sawblades, deliberately shaving armor away rather than burying steel deep enough to touch flesh. A third caught one claw between two of his arms, another claw between two more, then used the final pair to wrench the creature off-balance. The fourth gangster slammed shoulder-first into its side, bringing the Myrmur down, and as its shells immediately began pulling back into its Host to regenerate, all four of them jumped on the Host to bind his torso, arms, and legs together with thick black chains.

Bioarcanic chains, of course, meant to restrain Myrmur Hosts and prevent the Myrmurs inside them from coming back out for a time. They were special bioarcanic equipment Arnell had made for all of them some time ago, but Gael had never seen them in action before.

“Done!” one of the gangsters shouted.

“Next!” another gangster shouted.

And then all four men were already moving onto their next target.

Gael stared after them—and the rest of the sixty or so Saint’s Hands battling the swarms of Myrmurs before him—as he continued jogging through the shallow water filling the hallway.

Huh.

They’re actually… pretty efficient at this.

He’d known the Saint’s Hands could fight, obviously. There was no becoming one of Bharncair’s largest gangs by handing out soup and politely asking rival extortionists to reconsider their career choices. Suffice it to say he’d occasionally seen Fergal tear no-good upstarts apart, and he’d occasionally seen Saint’s Hands veterans brawl in alleys, storm into buildings, and overwhelm single-digit Myrmurs on the Wraithpier barges, but this was the first time he’d really seen them fight as an organized unit. Fergal had apparently been teaching them proper anti-Myrmur formations while he’d been busy running the clinic, and these were all F-Rank to D-Rank Blight-Class Myrmurs—not at all the usual Wretch-Class he’d seen them fight in Blightmarch.

They’re really, really strong now, huh?

Another crustacean Host came skittering around a corner ahead, armored legs scraping sparks from the floor. Six Saint’s Hands converged without needing Fergal to bark a single order. Two formed the front and took the charge with crossed chitin blades. Another pair flanked. One man hooked a sawblade under the edge of the creature’s dorsal shell and peeled it up without cutting through, while the last punched repeatedly into the exposed joint beneath until the Myrmur spasmed and withdrew around its Host. Less than fifteen seconds later, another bound body was lying safely against the wall.

“Don’t cut deep!” Fergal roared from somewhere farther ahead. “Outer armor only! If the parasite retracts, restrain the Host and move on!”

A chorus answered him from several corridors. “Yes, sir!”

More Saint’s Hands rushed past Gael, six arms full of blades, hooks, ropes, and harpoons. Their formation changed constantly to match the corridor width. Three men for smaller targets. Four for larger ones. Five if the Myrmur had particularly tough armor. They used their extra arms intelligently too, one pair maintaining guards while another pair controlled limbs and the final pair attacked, and whenever one man had to disengage, someone else stepped into his position before the Myrmur could exploit the gap.

And truth be told? Gael wasn’t contributing much. He was mostly just running behind the Saint’s Hands, Maeve, and Jin, letting the fighting specialists do their thing. That wasn’t to say he wasn’t doing anything at all—he and Vivi were ‘navigating’ them through the laboratory.

Fighting might actually be easier, though.

“Left?” Vivi asked from beside him.

Gael glanced down one branching passage, then the other. Both were equally wet, equally red-lit, and equally decorated with pipes that all looked horribly similar. “I think we go… right?”

“You’re not very confident about it!” Maeve shouted back, ripping a Myrmur’s head off before him before throwing the Host back for several gangsters to tie up. “We’re looking for the main surgical chamber, aren’t we? So it looks like a big dome with… red lights and… something else, right?”

“In case you haven’t noticed, there aren’t any signboards around here!” he shouted back.

So they continued their rampage deeper and deeper into the laboratory, which grew denser the farther they went. Narrow corridors gave way to intersections, stairwells, pressure doors, service galleries, and metal bridges crossing over machinery pits. Artificial Myrmur Hosts came at them in waves from side chambers and access tunnels, sometimes alone, but most times in groups of five or six at once. The Saint’s Hands simply folded around them every time.

At the fourth major crossroads, Fergal stopped so suddenly that three men behind him nearly ran into his back. He looked down each branching corridor, then up towards the overhead platforms disappearing deeper into the laboratory.

“Enough moving as one big death ball!” he shouted. “We’re wasting time clearing one route while the rest of the lab stays active. Fingers! Split into six teams, incapacitate every artificial Myrmur Host you can find, and start moving them back to the diving bells! Let the Exorcists find the Breeder! Our priority is the safety of the Hosts!”

“Yes, sir!”

Their death ball broke apart immediately, and Fergal nodded at Gael before he peeled away from the main group with a smaller battalion of his own, disappearing down a different corridor. Before long, it was just Gael, Maeve, Jin, and Vivi—facing off against yet another swarm of artificial Myrmur Hosts before them, of course.

Now even Gael had to put in a little legwork.

The four of them hacked, slashed, punched, kicked, and shot their way through what seemed to be the main corridor. The distant sounds of battle echoed through the walls, and the constant mechanical pulse beneath the floors didn’t help make the lab any quieter. They passed operating rooms with blood drains cut into the tiles, storage chambers full of empty restrains, and several reinforced doors already hanging open. Occasionally they ran into a group of Saint’s Hands, forcing them to double back and look for a different corridor. It was tedious. It was tiring.

But eventually, after what seemed like the hundredth artificial Myrmur Host defeated, the corridor ahead widened and opened into an enormous chamber, allowing them to barge in through the heavy metal doors.

A glass dome arched overhead, black seawater pressing against it from every direction. Rings of steel platforms encircled the upper levels, connected by ladders and narrow bridges. The entire floor was grated metal, and Gael could see bundles of pipes, pumps, and pressure lines running beneath their feet. Dim red lamps burned around the circumference of the room as well, just bright enough to illuminate ten giant cylindrical tanks arranged in a broad ring around the dome. Steam hissed from valves along each one.

Gael couldn’t help but squint at how eerily accurate his initial drawing of the dome was.

This is it.

This is where all the artificial Myrmur Hosts remembered getting cut open in.

But the one thing none of them ever mentioned in their vague recollections was hunched over a massive control panel at the far end of the dome, hands moving frantically across a hundred small levers, valves, and buttons.

You’d think they’d remember a man hunched so far forward he looks like a shrimp in a lab coat, but…

Oh well.

He reached under his coat for a scalpel. The dome was only about fifty meters wide, and he’d hit the cap off a bottle with a sewing needle from seventy meters away before—though Cara had given him a pretty violent scolding and told him not to do it again in the prayer hall—so hitting the Breeder at this distance was going to be a piece of cake. He drew his scalpel, aimed with his tongue stuck out, and—

Vivi fired first. The Breeder’s left leg jerked sideways as the bioarcanic round from her rifle punched through his thigh. He cried out and nearly collapsed against the console, one hand slamming down to catch himself.

“... Nice shot.” Gael whistled, giving Vivi an impressed glance as he stashed his scalpel.

Vivi lowered her smoking rifle slightly, seemingly startled by her own speed. “Thanks…?”

But the Breeder was still moving. Bleeding heavily, he dragged himself upright, reached across the console, and pulled one final lever.

Every alarm in the chamber started ringing, and Gael’s grin vanished as the entire laboratory shuddered beneath them. Around the dome, all ten giant tanks vented clouds of steam at once. Pressure gauges spun. Pipes hammered beneath the floor. More and more red lights brightened and started spinning until the whole dome looked feverish.

Jin began storming forward, clenching his fists. “What did you do, old man?”

The Breeder turned to face them slowly. Blood was still trickling down his leg, and he leaned against the control panel with one hand, breathing hard, but somehow he was confident enough to smile.

“What I must,” he answered steadily. “You’re all right on time, by the way.”

Something moved above them. Gael looked up just as ten artificial Myrmur Hosts dropped from the ringed platforms overhead, landing across the grates between them and the Breeder C-Rank to B-Rank Blight-Classes, all of them. One had a torso wrapped in mantis armor with long folding forelimbs. Another wore thick beetle plating across their back and shoulders. Another huge Myrmur carried four enormous crab claws where his arms should’ve been.

Then Gael saw the ant-like Myrmur with four crimson-chitined arms and flames crawling across their entire body, and he immediately recognized her as the fire ant Myrmur he’d fought back in the catacombs of Bleakhearth. But now he knew who the person beneath the Myrmur was: Miss Umbrage of the Wraithpier clinic.

Back then, he’d been able to talk to her, but now she was silent as a statue. The other artificial Myrmur Hosts beside her showed no fragments of personality, either. Was it because the Breeder had modified and ‘improved’ his Myrmurs even more in the nine months since, making it so they couldn’t even talk back to anyone with their heads enveloped in their living armors?

Gael felt irritation crawl up the back of his neck.

“Stand down,” he called out across the dome. “You’re shot, surrounded, and approximately twenty more minutes from having several hundred six-armed gangsters remove this entire laboratory from the sea. Give up now, and maybe we don’t kill you.”

The Breeder stared at him.

Then the old man laughed softly.

“Maybe you won’t kill me?” The Breeder’s smile widened despite the pain twisting his face. “Why even offer me that?” Out of some personal creed? Some physician’s sentimentality?” His eyes sharpened, glinting behind his bronze-rimmed glasses. “You’re soft. Just like your father.”

That did it. Anger, hot and unpleasant, moved through Gael’s chest, but before he could take out his scalpel again, the Breeder turned back to the control panel.

“Kill them,” the old man said plainly.

And as every artificial Myrmur Host moved at once—the mantis springing up to high ground, the crabs splitting left and right, and Miss Umbrage’s fire ant staying back to lob fiery projectiles while the rest charged straight down the center—the Breeder resumed operating the levers and valves across his control panel like there was nothing more important in the entire world.

Gael cracked his knuckles and glanced at Jin and Vivi. He would like to be the one to beat the Breeder’s ass, but he had to admit, Jin still had a better claim on the old man.

He’d have his turn later.

“Stop the old bastard,” he said. “Whatever he’s doing with those tanks, cancel it. Don’t let him finish.” Then he stepped forward beside Maeve as she opened Mistrender, charging it with toxic blood. “We’ll deal with the Myrmur Hosts and join you in a bit.”
