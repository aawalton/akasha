I used to wonder what it must have been like living before the internet. Not being able to look something, anything up and getting a dozen different answers right away. Of course plenty of them weren't the truth. They weren't lies, they were just mistakes.

But not being able to simply find out about something? I wondered how people managed to survive that. Yes I know there were libraries, that there were schools and universities that had information, but they didn't have everything.

I've spent the last three years, not days, not months, years, trying to find evidence that this clause in a contract is valid. I've asked the other Arbiters, they said look in our records or the EDN. I already had. So they said hire a Seer, have them go look. The Seer found nearly nothing.

Unfortunately nearly was the worst possible answer. If they'd found nothing I would have been able to say no, it wasn't valid. If they'd found something definite, I'd have an answer. But they found something. Not enough to give answer, not even enough for anyone to be sure it was applicable.

My boss, sorry, the Senior Arbiter responsible for this department, told me that I wouldn't be free of this case until I'd spent a decade working on it, longer if the client wouldn't accept no answer.

A decade.

Seb sat up as he felt Gwen and Cara finally return. They'd gotten back to their tent slightly more than an hour ago and Anya had suggested they use the time to rest. They'd only gotten a little rest, not that he was going to complain.

They're back, he sent. Walking quickly.

He felt Anya's focus shift, stretching through his aura, leveraging his awareness. After a moment both their thoughts filled with shock, a new shared sense filling their minds.

Broken arm? he asked.

Shield arm, fractured but set and already healing.

How are we-?

Your aura touches them, is aware of them. I can identify injuries, we both know that. Maybe?

He nodded. There was no way to be sure, at least not yet. They'd have to test, not that he wanted any of them to be injured. But if she could sense state of being through his aura, could she stabilize?

Maybe, she sent. I think I've stabilized you through our bond, at least partially. But if you get hurt just so we can test…

I know, I know.

Pausing at the edge of the tent he bent and grabbed two of the four platters they'd gathered from the kitchen tent as Anya grabbed the others. They'd been hungry, but not so hungry that they hadn't been willing to wait for their friends.

They stepped out of the tent just in time to see Gwen come storming around the far tent and nearly stumble to a halt as she caught sight of them. Cara backed into her a moment later, sending them crashing to the ground. She'd been walking backwards as though watching their rear despite the angle she was holding her arm at, the high-pitched sound passing through her lips confirming Anya's earlier diagnosis.

"Don't. Startle. Us," Gwen gasped before spinning to stare down at Cara, her hands running along the injury as her eyes closed.

"You knew he'd spot us," Cara said. "You hoped they were here."

"Be quiet, you're hurt."

"It's not that bad," Cara said as she pushed herself off the ground. "We've both fought with worse."

"I said be quiet," Gwen snapped, her tone sharpening. "Let me focus."

Seb watched as Cara's mouth shut abruptly as she held her arm out. He could hear the sound of Gwen muttering as Energy wrapped around and through the injury. Anya stepped over to them, platters left at his feet, and placed a single finger on Cara's shoulder. He could tell she was trying to learn from Gwen's work, to watch someone other than George at work.

Don't-

Seb! I know better than to interfere.

He didn't bother answering, they both knew he'd been teasing.

Gwen let out a heavy breath as she let go of Cara's arm. Her eyes jumped between the three of them, panic he hadn't noticed until then fading with every leap.

"We're back," she said before crashing face first onto the ground.

She sat up, spinning to glare at Cara.

"What was that f-"

She paused and Seb saw the back of her neck turn red.

"Oh, Cara, I'm sorry," she said, her words taking on the same tone as a few minutes earlier. "I'm done focusing, you can speak."

"Anya, Seb," Cara said as she stood and walked over to pick up one of the platters. "Tell Gwen I'm happy my arm is healed. And thank you for dinner, we had a long day."

"Cara?" Gwen asked, only to have Anya rest a hand on her shoulder.

"Did you have to?" Seb asked, carrying platters to the two of them before turning back for his own.

"No," Gwen said. "I didn't. I just… Bern… It all…"

"Eat," Anya said.

Seb felt the slightest impact of her Affinity in the words. Nothing like what he'd noticed certain Awakened doing by infusing Energy into their words, nothing close to what she did on occasion in combat, but enough that both Gwen and Cara seemed to lose interest in anything but the food in front of them.

Was that on purpose? he sent.

Was… Maybe. They need to eat, especially after healing. They needed to be distracted from that.

Interesting. Are you trying to distract me by doing all these new things?

"Anya," Gwen said, her platter already visibly emptier as she spoke between bites. "How did you? Not that I don't want to eat, not that Cara doesn't need food."

"Seb just asked the same thing," Anya said with a smile. "It wasn't something I knew I could do, at least not in conversation."

"You've forced me to defy you before," Cara said as she put visible effort into not picking up more food mid sentence. "Much easier when I don't want to agree with you."

"Cara, I-"

"Gwen," Anya said. "Eat or I'll request Cara help me make sure you eat your fill."

He saw Gwen start to glare at his wife before blanching and turning all her attention to her plate.

Threats?

Look at Cara, Anya sent back.

Glancing at Cara before picking at his own food he saw her hunching over her food, eating as quickly as possible while sneaking looks at them. He thought he caught her grinning whenever her eyes shifted towards Gwen, only for her hair to shift towards white when she looked at Anya.

You scared her too, he sent.

I… That wasn't what I-

I know. And I'm sure she'll realize that, after they're done eating.

I hope so.

Seb turned his focus to his own food, knowing Anya was doing the same as they all sat in near silence. He had his own questions, about Cara's injury, about the panic he had seen in Gwen's eyes. He didn't bother asking Anya about what she'd done, their shared thoughts were dwelling on the implications, on the benefits it might lead to.

She'd found a way to apply her Affinity in a more subtle way, to use a lighter touch. All he'd been learning was to increase his touch, to focus and strengthen what he did. He hadn't even started learning anything about Bladesong, about how to get it to do anything he hadn't figured out instinctively.

Stop that.

What?

Stop judging yourself, comparing yourself, Anya sent. We're in this together. You know as well as I do that in a few days or weeks you'll have some idea, figure out how to connect things together, and I'll be the one grumbling.

You will not. You'll tease me for taking my time.

Probably, but I'll also complain about how it isn't fair.

Before he could answer, she flooded their thoughts with memories. Examples of their staggered progress, of one of them finding a path forward while the other didn't. Times where she'd complained, where her thoughts had been following the same path he was now. And most importantly, all the times they'd celebrated together, where her success was his, and his hers. It wasn't a challenge, it wasn't a competition, it was a partnership.

"Do any of you want more?" Cara asked. "I'm still hungry."

"We're good," Anya said.

"I'll get you another platter," Gwen said softly, holding her hand out for Cara's.

"Mistress, you don't need to."

"Yes I do," Gwen said. "Please?"
