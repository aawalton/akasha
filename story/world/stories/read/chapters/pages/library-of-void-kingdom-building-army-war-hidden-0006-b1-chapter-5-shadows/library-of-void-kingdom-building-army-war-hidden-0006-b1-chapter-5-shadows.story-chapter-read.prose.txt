The door to the infirmary opened with a soft creak as Vector stepped into the corridor.

Instantly, the air seemed to tighten around him.

A faint prickle crawled across his skin, raising the hairs on his arms—as if every breath, every presence, had abruptly turned toward him.

And when he lifted his gaze, he understood why.

Dozens of eyes were fixed on him.

Men and women who had been waiting outside since dawn. Sebastian stood among them, along with the other three. Worry was etched deep into their faces. Shoulders were held stiff with tension. Hands clenched unconsciously at their sides. Some looked as though they hadn’t breathed properly since the moment Sebastian had carried him in.

“Young Master—”

The words rose together from every mouth. Restrained. Controlled. Yet heavy with panic.

Before they could even finish speaking, Vector felt it clearly. The Anxiety.

He raised a hand gently to stop them—a calming gesture born of habit after coming to Carnatic, rather than thought.

“Everybody… no need to worry,” he said, his voice steady, deliberately soothing. “I’m fit and fine. I only lost consciousness because of the shock.”

The tension in the corridor eased—just a little. Enough for shoulders to drop. Enough for held breaths to loosen and air to flow in chests again.

But Vector knew better than most: if words alone could erase worry, then fear would never have ruled this world.

“Alright,” he continued, offering a faint smile. “Let the healers finish checking me once more. After that, all of you should return to your duties. So, that you all can work without disturbed thoughts.”

There was hesitation. Lingering doubt. Still, discipline prevailed. The healers stepped forward again, performing their examinations with renewed care. When they finally nodded—confirming what they had already said inside, previously—the relief became visible.

Vector watched them go.

He knew their concern was genuine. Not because of his position. Not because of his status as an heir of the Astrum.

But because they cared.

…

Not long after, Vector found himself seated at a round wooden table inside the strategic meeting room of the Resistance base.

Sebastian. Marcus. Tristan. Rhea.

All four sat around the table.

The four people he trusted most—those who had stood by him since the time he had reached the age of reason. Some as guardians. Some as companions. All as pillars.

Vector’s voice broke the lingering silence of the room.

“I don’t need to ask about the attacker’s condition,” he said plainly. “So, I’ll ask the real question. Did you find anything… anything at all—that could hint at his origin?”

Sebastian answered without delay. “No, Young Master. Nothing suspicious. No marks. No identifiers.”

Vector nodded. Trusting his words completely without a slightest doubt.

As Sebastian had been the one to rescue him. The one who had returned to the scene afterward. And the only one who could do so without drawing unnecessary attention. Sending others would have raised suspicion among the patrolling soldiers.

“Young Master,” Marcus spoke up, leaning slightly forward. “Do you have any idea why the attacker would turn his blade on you so openly? Especially when we all know he was with you?”

Vector met his gaze. Marcus had voiced exactly what he himself had intended to raise.

Despite his fondness for food, Marcus was the sharpest strategist among them, second only to Sebastian. As for Sebastian… age had carved experience into him, lessons paid for with blood and survival. Comparing Marcus to Sebastian would be unfair, but his insight was invaluable all the same.

“I can’t say I’ve discerned his motive with certainty,” Vector replied slowly. “But I’m fairly sure it was related to the Estate. He emphasized it repeatedly during the assault.”

He then recounted the encounter, again—how he had pretended fear, how he had drawn the attacker into revealing information, and how, just before things turned fatal, an unknown force had intervened—the shield and the wind, not the mysterious Energy.

When he finished, a heavy silence settled over the room.

The unknown force. None of them commented on it.

He knew they couldn’t. There was no proof. No clear explanation. It could have been a hidden expert. A coincidence. Even something as mundane as sudden environmental pressure.

So, he let it set aside—for now. Because the very idea of someone controlling wind like a supernatural force was absurd.

Still, Vector briefly connected it with Origin Energy. That he recently came to know about through his inheritance. He would ask the family later. He already knew the people sitting before him had no understanding of it.

He interlocked his fingers, leaned forward, and rested his forehead against the back of his hands.

“If such a deeply rooted pawn in the Resistance was willing to gamble exposure, even risking his life” Vector asked, breaking the silence, “then whatever is about to happen… must be significant. Decisive.”

“What do you think,” he continued, “that Varent Kaelthorn is planning?”

At the mention of the name, expressions hardened.

Varent Kaelthorn—City Lord of Carnatic, an Earl of the Lionheart Empire.

Rhea’s fingers tightened around the edge of the table. Tristan’s eyes narrowed, as if calculating possibilities in silence. Marcus frowned deeply.

Sebastian was the one who finally spoke.

“Then we must assume,” he said calmly, “that the move against you was not the objective—but a precaution.”

Vector felt the same conclusion solidify in his mind. Sebastian’s words gave form to the unease he had been circling since the attack.

“To remove uncertainty,” Sebastian continued. “Before something larger unfolds.”

“That is the most probable reason,” Vector agreed.

Then his tone hardened—no longer speculative, but commanding.

“Caution all members to remain vigilant. Gather everything possible. But no life-taking risks.” He lifted his head slightly. “And increase surveillance around the base. If the Estate has even caught a whisper of our presence, we will change the entrance location immediately.”

“Yes, Young Master,” Tristan replied without hesitation. “I will make the arrangements.”

He was responsible for intelligence operations—and for maintaining the security surrounding the base.

Vector nodded in approval, granting him full authority to act swiftly.

This was not the first time.

In the past, they had altered only the entrance—never the base itself, to avoid being found by Lionheart forces. Constructing a new base in a short time was impossible, and once the enemy learned how to search, repeating the process became easier for them.

The entrance could be sacrificed, but not the base.

Even the slightest doubt, the smallest fragment of hostile intelligence, was enough to warrant immediate change. Such was the nature of their work. Secrecy was not a precaution. It was survival.

Vector’s gaze drifted slightly upward as he thought of all this.

The Light shaft.

In the underground chamber, narrow light shafts pierced through layers of stone and earth, channeling natural illumination into the base below. Pale beams spread across the chamber, soft and restrained, revealing an architecture as ingenious as it was discreet.

Aethel's light filtered through those shafts—pale and gentle, low in intensity. All of this indicated it was still morning.

That… Not much time had passed in all the events. Attack. Memory. Awakening. All of it had unfolded within a short span.

Which meant—

He could still go to the Estate today.

By mid-morning, he was expected to report for his duties as usual. Even if there was a delay, he could use hundreds of excuses—illness, inspections, assignments. None would stand out. And such a small position didn't warrant strict regulation. At most, he would be scolded.

“While… I’ll try to find out what I can about this incident,” Vector added with determination. “If there’s any movement or unusual activity within the Earl’s Estate.”

Though he served as the Head of Intelligence and Espionage for the Resistance in Carnatic, Vector had never abandoned fieldwork entirely.

His role as a gardener within the City Lord’s Estate—under Earl Varent Kaelthorn—granted him access no spy network could replicate. Tedious as the work was, it allowed him to observe routines, overhear fragments of conversation, and note subtle changes in security and movement.

“But they may already suspect you, Young Master,” Sebastian warned carefully. “The attacker could have revealed—”

Vector raised a hand, stopping him mid-sentence.

“No,” he said calmly. “He didn’t know. As I told you before—he only attacked after he suspected I worked in the Estate. Before that, he didn’t even have an inkling.”

Rhea opened her mouth to object.

“Young Master…”

“Sebastian will be there,” Vector said, cutting her off gently but firmly. “If anything happens, he’ll reach me instantly. Let’s not waste time arguing over this.”

Whenever Vector worked within the Estate, Sebastian remained nearby—never visible, never intrusive, yet always close enough to intervene within moments. By sheer fortune, such intervention had never been necessary.

“I will be there, Young Master,” Sebastian said after a brief pause. Then his tone hardened. “But if you sense even the slightest danger… any suspicion at all, you call me immediately. Don’t risk your life again for information.”

With a heavy tone, he added. “Intelligence can only be gathered if there is a life to gather it.”

“If there’s even an ounce of suspicion,” Vector replied, meeting his gaze, “I’ll call your name directly.”

Only then did Sebastian nod, reluctantly satisfied.

This time, Vector had no intention of gambling with his life. Had he drawn Sebastian’s attention earlier, the line between life and death would never have been crossed.

The others, still not convinced, started a round of safety remarks. Rhea especially began lecturing him—joined swiftly by Marcus.

“Yes… yes… I understand completely—STOP,” Vector said, raising both hands in surrender.

A moment later—

Gr… growl… growl.

The sound echoed clearly.

Vector froze.

So did everyone else.

His stomach had chosen the perfect moment to announce its dissatisfaction.

Sebastian sighed, shaking his head helplessly.

“Let’s go for breakfast,” Vector said dryly. “Otherwise, I’ll actually be late reporting to the Earl’s Estate.”

Sebastian followed a step behind him.

The others did not.

Moving together in public would attract unnecessary attention. And attention was the one thing the Resistance could not afford.

Vector squinted, raising a hand to shield his eyes as Aethel’s rays struck his face directly.

He had just exited the mud-and-stone house—its roof stitched together with brittle thatch, unremarkable in every way. To any observer, it was merely another decaying shelter in the slums.

Only he and a handful of Resistance members knew the truth. That… Beneath it lay the concealed entrance to the Resistance’s base.

As his vision adjusted, the surrounding scenery came into focus.

Houses of mud and stone lined the narrow paths; their cracked walls patched together with broken bricks scavenged from ruins. Some structures were barely houses at all—thick, weathered cloth and scraps of leather stretched over crooked frames, tied tightly to form crude barriers against wind and rain.

The slums.

They had never existed in this city before.

They had taken root only after occupation by the Lionheart Empire.

Six months ago, the Lionheart Empire had seized Carnatic, the final city of Elaris—marking Lionheart’s complete control over the state. Carnatic, a border city of Elaris, lay adjacent to Ironfang Hill City of Redfield.

This devastation was not isolated.

It was the consequence of a larger war.

Vector’s homeland, the Novastra Empire, was under invasion. One state after another had fallen to the advancing Lionheart forces—an empire from a distant continent, not even a neighboring nation, yet one that marched relentlessly across Novastra’s lands.

In response, the Novastra Liberation Front—commonly called the Resistance—had risen. Sustained and fueled from the shadows by the Astrum Family, they were all that stood between Novastra and complete subjugation.

Once, the Novastra Empire consisted of seven states: Elaris. Ashmont. Solara. Arya. Redfield. Avalon. Aurora.

Now—

Only three remained free.

Redfield. Avalon. Aurora.

The last strongholds. The final bastions of the Novastra Empire.

Vector lowered his hand slowly, his gaze steady as he looked over the slums bathed in soft morning light.
