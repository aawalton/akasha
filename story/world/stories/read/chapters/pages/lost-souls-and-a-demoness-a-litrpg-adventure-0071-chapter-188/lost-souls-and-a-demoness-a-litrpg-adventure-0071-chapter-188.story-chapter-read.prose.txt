A sweet fragrance drifted through the warm, sunlit clearing. Jade lay on the grassy ground, breathing in the pleasant scent of flowers, unwilling to move an inch further. She had no idea how long she remained like that, exhausted and filled with a complicated mixture of emotions that she had yet to begin to process. Eventually, her breathing slowed and she mustered the strength to rise to her knees.

Jade was in the goddess Elaria's sacred grove once more. Though she had only visited once, it wasn't a place one could mistake. The blooming flowers scattered throughout the meadow were possessed of a vibrance one could not find in the wider cosmos, and the air itself tasted of divine essence. It was an energizing sensation, and Jade's strength continued to grow as she waited, sorting through the mess of grief and triumph that warred in her heart.

"You did well. Both you and your friends."

Jade turned towards the sound of Elaria's voice and was surprised to find her seated beside her. The goddess lounged in a pool of sunlight, which rippled and shimmered as it played across her olive skin. She wore a simple green dress, a light and airy garment with a muted floral pattern.

"Small comfort to the dead." Jade muttered, unable to keep the bitterness from her voice.

Elaria's reply was a pained half-smile.

"I am sorry for your loss, Jade."

Jade didn't respond immediately. What was there to say? Her friends were gone, and nothing would bring them back. The knowledge that their sacrifice ensured her home's protection blunted the pain, but it didn't remove it. Nothing could.

"Thanks." She grunted finally, breaking a silence that had stretched on for minutes. The goddess hadn't spoken or done anything, appearing content to wait for her to be ready. Jade appreciated that. "And thanks for getting me out of there."

"I wish I could have done more, but my power grows weak. Even now, I am starting to fade."

"What do you mean?"

Jade tore her gaze from the soothing spectacle of the small waterfall and inspected the goddess. Now that she took a closer look, was her skin paler than before? And her eyes… They no longer shone with the same luster.

"Closing the dozens of portals Vastrozun managed to open took no small piece of my power, and I was already weak when I woke from my slumber."

"Will you be alright?" Did that question even make sense to a goddess? Jade wondered if she was being foolish even asking, but Elaria's help was the reason the plan had succeeded. A little kindness couldn't hurt. And yet, this wasn't bad news. Not considering the secret desire that simmered in the depths of her soul.

"Perhaps."

She didn't elaborate, and another long silence fell between them. After a short while, Jade felt that she was strong enough to stand. She did so and took a few tentative steps. Her legs were sore, but functional. Good enough.

"Where are my friends?"

"Sharing the good news with all who came to do battle with the demons, I believe."

"You believe?"

"I'm not omniscient, Jade." Elaria laughed, standing as well. "Especially not in this state."

The goddess returned to her hammock and nearly collapsed into it. She looked utterly exhausted, even more so than Jade had been.

"You have another question for me." Elaria said, settling into her hammock. Jade couldn't take her eyes off the goddess, who looked like a being torn straight from the pages of ancient myth. Dark brown hair splayed out beneath her back as she sighed, gently rocking the hammock back and forth. It was a serene, entrancing sight.

"I do." With effort, Jade focused on the conversation. There was indeed something bothering her. Something that she needed to confirm. "Are the Labyrinth monsters really converted celestial spirits?"

"Ah, I see you ran into Ira." Elaria sighed, closing her eyes. "I should've known.""Well?" Jade crossed her arms and stood in front of the goddess, waiting.

"Very well. I would not normally explain to someone the inner workings of the Labyrinth, but you are a special case. In light of everything you've done, I don't mind making an exception." The goddess waved her hand and a stool of twisted roots sprung up from the ground behind Jade, providing her a seat. Jade took it reluctantly. As much as she wanted to leave and confirm for herself that her friends were okay, she needed to be here for this.

"When we created the Labyrinth, we asked our followers for any willing to lend their strength and will into making this new world function as designed. In order to create creatures and monsters that could challenge and train prospective Wayfarers, we needed spiritual templates upon which to base them. For the sake of preserving peace in the cosmos, many of our celestial servants offered their spirits for this purpose. And as we crafted the Labyrinth, they were integrated into its mechanisms.""You say many, but Ira made it sound like it the Labyrinth tried to draw in every celestial, not just the ones who wanted to be a part of it."

"That… is not how it was intended to function." The goddess frowned. "Other celestials born from my power still roam the wider cosmos, I know for a fact that it has not drawn in each and every one of them. And yet, so much has changed since I slipped into slumber following the birth of this world. I cannot say for certain that everything went as we planned.""Your Labyrinth has been eating its way through worlds for centuries, I think it's pretty safe to say things didn't go according to plan."That came out more harshly than Jade intended, but she didn't have it in her to care. The wounds in her heart were still too raw. Was lashing out at a deity a smart thing to do? Probably not. But it made her feel better, as immature as she knew that was.

As if reading her thoughts, Elaria smiled softly at her.

"I have made my share of mistakes in this matter, it is true. And in the end, I have you and your companions to thank for realizing an outcome as good as this. The demon threat is gone. With Vastrozun defeated, his remaining people in their home world will be unable to breach the dimensional barriers that seal them away. I hope that one day, their descendants will learn to live in harmony with the divine essences that still linger there. As for the rest of the cosmos, they are now free from a threat that could have darkened the skies of too many worlds to count."

Jade grunted in agreement. In reality, she was barely listening anymore. She had made up her mind. There was something she very much wanted to ask of the goddess. Jade squeezed her hands into fists and then relaxed them, letting the tension drain from her body as she did.

"Lady Elaria, there is something I would ask of you. As a favor, for having done this for you."

Previously, the goddess had promised to send a healer to help her cure her brother of his illness. That had already been seen to, apparently, but they both knew such a request was practically nothing in the face of what Jade and her friends had accomplished. No, she had something much bigger in mind. It was time to fulfil a promise she'd made, all the way back at the beginning of this journey.

"Speak, Jade. You have more than earned the right to do so." The goddess gestured, stifling a yawn. Jade could sense her growing weariness. Just how much power had it taken to close those portals?

Jade took a deep breath.

"I want you to shut down the Labyrinth."

She wasn't sure what response she expected. Shock? Ridicule? Amusement at the absurdity of her request, perhaps? If so, she was disappointed. Elaria simply turned her head, regarding Jade with the deep, cerulean pools that were her eyes. Jade could glimpse a hint of infinity behind that gaze, and the reminder of the force she was dealing with sent a shudder down her spine.

"What do you mean by that?" Elaria asked.

"The demons are safely imprisoned again, and you just said that they will not be able to escape without Vastrozun's help. So, there is no need for the Labyrinth to keep expanding, right? It doesn't need the resources, and it doesn't need to train people for a future war that will no longer happen. It has served its purpose."

"I see." Elaria's tone was unreadable, and neither could Jade glean anything from her emotions. The radiant spark in her spirit stirred. She remembered Seraphine, lost in the depths of her anger. And Ira, who had resisted her fate to the very end.

When the goddess didn't say anything else, she forged ahead.

"I want you to release all the celestial spirits from the Labyrinth. The attendants, the ones who take on the forms of monsters, any of them who wish to be free of it."

From the very beginning, Jade had felt that the Labyrinth was backed by a malevolent force. She may not have been right about everything, but the fact remained that its existence caused no small amount of suffering. If there were more people like Seraphine and Ira, she couldn't leave them trapped in an endless cycle that fueled a machine whose purpose was already complete.

"Doing what you ask would be a great disruption to the cosmos." Elaria said, regarding Jade closely. "Many worlds now depend on the Labyrinth for resources, training, and more. Without the spirits to maintain it, its functions will grind to a halt. Even the system will falter, or even collapse."

"If that's what it takes to ensure no one is trapped and in pain, than that's just a sacrifice the rest of us will have to make." Jade was a little surprised by the strength of her own conviction. She wasn't entirely sure where it came from. No, that was a lie. She did know. This is what Luis would have said, had he been there with her. But he wasn't, and it was her duty as his friend to keep his voice alive. "I refuse to allow others to prosper from the suffering of others. When the Labyrinth kept Vastrozun sealed away, maybe that was different. I don't know. It doesn't sound like any of this has gone the way you expected it to. But this is the situation now, and we can do something about it. That's what I want. That's what my friend would have wanted, were he still here."

Elaria slumped back, reclining in her hammock. The goddess looked drained, and for the first time, Jade sensed the true depth of her age.

"I suppose… that you are right. Nothing can stay the same forever. That is my very nature, after all. We must grow and adapt, both people and societies. As one age draws to a close, another begins. Still, there are many, especially among the Labyrinth attendants, who embrace their task with dignity and pride. I believe you know one such man.""Damos." Jade nodded. "I wouldn't have made it this far without him. Wouldn't have made it past my first day."

"There are many caretakers like him. Those who seek to help newcomers adjust to this world. No matter what I do, the Labyrinth will still exist. It will still need people like him to act as wardens."

"But not all of them need to stay. The spirits who take the form of monsters don't need to stay trapped in an endless cycle of killing and being killed. They should be released."

"I agree."

Jade breathed a sigh of relief. The exhaustion was starting to hit her again, and she slumped forward in her seat.

"Then… you'll do it?"

"I will."

"What will happen to them?"

"Any who choose to go will have their spirits returned to their creators. The other gods remain asleep, so they will rest alongside them."

"Will they wake up, one day?" Jade asked. "The other gods?"

"Eventually. Over time, they will regain enough of their essence to act again. Especially once the Labyrinth stops drawing upon them to sustain its growth. They entrusted their power to me, allowed me to shape it and give it form. I'm afraid that I have not done as well as I ought."

"The Labyrinth prevented the demons from conquering their way through the stars. It could have been a lot worse." Those were Jade's honest feelings. She didn't like the Labyrinth. It had caused her home too much damage, taken too many of her friends for her to believe it was just. Surely there had been a better way to protect the cosmos than this. And yet, it had done its job. What was done was done. She couldn't change that. What she could do was influence what came next.

"You've grown, Jade." The goddess's smile was tinged with sadness.

"I suppose you would know."

Elaria laughed softly.

"Well, there is no sense in delaying. I've used much of my power already and cannot wait for it to wane further."

A golden glow surrounded Elaria, so bright that Jade was forced to cover her eyes and look away. There was a blinding flash, a sharp crack, and then a wave of heat. Then, everything fell still.

Blinking away spots, Jade saw a new notification waiting for her. It had jumped to the front of the queue, overriding all the combat related messages she hadn't looked at yet.

System Notice

The Labyrinth System is entering dormancy. Certain system functions will be unavailable for an indefinite period. All Wayfarer trials have been suspended. The quest system has been suspended. Monster spawns have been greatly reduced. For more information, please see a Labyrinth attendant.

Wow. Just like that, everything changed. Jade didn't know how to feel, or even if her mind was capable of feeling anything anymore. This was more than she'd expected.

"That's it? It's done?" she asked.

"It is.""I didn't realize it would affect the Wayfarer trials, too.""Many Labyrinth functions are closely connected. When one thing changes, so does everything else. Most of the spirits that had formed the monsters and guardians of this world are departing now, and the system must compensate for that. Even I don't know what it will look like in the end. The system has grown to be its own entity, as is its nature as something that rose from my power. I can only influence it, not control it."

The Wayfarer trials were over. Did… did that mean that she could go home? Without the trial or quests, the system wouldn't force her and her friends to remain in the Labyrinth. They were free.

Do I even want to go home?

The thought surprised her, although it probably shouldn't have. She'd changed so much these past several months, there was no way that she could simply go back to living a normal life on Earth now. But she didn't have to figure that out right away. Besides, there was still one last thing she had to do.

The goddess, or rather, her avatar, lay back on her hammock. Her eyelids fluttered, as if trying to ward off sleep. Enacting such an all-encompassing change on something the size of the Labyrinth had sapped almost all her remaining power, and Jade could sense that she was running on fumes. But she hung on, refusing to slip away.

"Thank you, Jade. That… was the right thing to do."

Elaria gestured again and a portal arch rose from the ground. White mist swirled within, glimmering in the evening sunlight.

"That… will take you home."

"Thank you, Lady Elaria." Jade said. She rose from her seat, but instead of making her way towards the portal, she approached the goddess. Her steps were light on the soft grass, which barely rustled beneath her feet. Elaria yawned, her eyelids fluttering shut again.

The spark in Jade's spirit flickered again. Some remnant of Ira's will remained, whispering to her in the language of souls. The gods had precipitated this entire disaster. They had gifted the demons with the power that made them a threat and then created the Labyrinth to contain them. A world powered by captured spirits of their own followers, and one that scoured other planets for resources and magic. While that may not have been the intention, that's what had happened. And as far as Jade was concerned, the return of such beings was a risk.

She reached Elaria's side. The goddess didn't react to her presence. She looked serene and peaceful, reclining in a soft hammock suspended between two beautiful trees, surrounded by colorful flowers and the scent of woodland life. Jade paused, taking in the sight. Was this the smart thing to do? Probably not. The smart thing to do would be to leave. But... no. At that thought, something inside her gave her another nudge, urging her to act.

Slowly, moving as if in a dream, Jade leaned in and kissed the goddess. Only a tiny, fading spark of power remained in the goddess's avatar, and it easily flowed from her body into Jade's through the draining kiss. Jade didn't try to capture that flicker of divine essence, she knew from experience that her body wouldn't be able to withstand it. Instead, she channeled it into the ground, using her body as a conduit to siphon it away. Even that was almost too much for her. The magnitude of energy flowing through her spirit set her body alight with searing pain. A powerful force erupted between them and Jade was blasted off her feet, sent hurtling through the air to land in a heap in front of the newly erected portal.

"Urghhh…"

Jade groaned, rolling onto her side and curling into a ball. She hadn't expected that to be pleasant, but it had still caught her off guard. She blinked several times, trying to bring her swimming vision back into focus. Her skin still bore the burns she'd endured by absorbing Ira's essence, but now they were smoking. If she'd tried to keep any of the goddess's power, Jade had no doubt she would have exploded like an overfilled balloon.

Wait, the goddess…

For what felt like the dozenth time that day Jade painfully pulled herself to her feet. The regeneration icon still blinked in her vision, reminding her that she couldn't safely take any potions. How strong of an elixir had Siora given her? The thought of her dead friend sent another pang through her heart, a kind of pain entirely different than the superficial sensations crawling over her body.

Elaria's hammock was empty. It swayed lightly in the soft breeze, as if waiting to be filled. But no one came. Jade thought she could feel a distant conciousness receding into the ether, the last vestiges of Elaria's slumbering mind. Had… had she done it? The goddess had been so close to resuming her slumber. Jade hadn't wanted to hurt her, it's not like she could've done so anyway, but the fragment of Ira's spirit nestled in her soul had shown her the way. By draining the last vestiges of conscious awareness from the deity, Jade had tried to put her back to sleep with the others of her kind. And, somehow, it appeared to have worked.

Jade instinctively checked her notification stack, only to find that there were no new entries. Was this due to the changes sweeping across the Labyrinth? No doubt the magnitude of them would throw civilizations throughout the cosmos into chaos for a time. Jade regretted that, but she stood by her request. No more worlds needed to be plundered, no more Wayfarers would be sent into desperate battles, and the people of the Labyrinth could avoid unnecessary battles against powerful and aggressive monsters. No doubt there were still many dangerous creatures and valuable treasure scattered throughout the mysterious levels of the Labyrinth, but assuming the goddess's changes worked, the Universe had just become a much safer place.

All it had cost were the lives of two of the kindest people Jade had ever known.

Jade took one last look around the tranquil clearing, recalling the day that she and her friends had decided to embark on this final quest. Now she was back, their mission accomplished. She tried to remember the resolve she'd held, the conviction that this was something that they couldn't turn away from. The feeling eluded her. If she were back there again, knowing what she did now, Jade wasn't sure if she could make the same decision. She took a moment to reflect on that thought, considering if those feelings made her a bad person. No, she decided. It simply made her human.

With a thought, Jade cast off her demonic appearance and adopted her old, human form. It felt right, now, in a way that it hadn't in a long time.

It was time to go home.
