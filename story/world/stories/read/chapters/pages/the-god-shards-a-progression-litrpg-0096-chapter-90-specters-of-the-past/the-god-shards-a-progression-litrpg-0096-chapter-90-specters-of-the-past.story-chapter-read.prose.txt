Chapter 90: Specters of the Past

“Where the hell’ve you been? I’ve been looking everywhere!”

Leo grunted and didn’t move from where he currently lay atop a thick tree branch. It was a warm day, the sky blue and cloudless in a way that was rare for Adrya at this time of year. If he closed his eyes, he could feel the sun beaming down on his skin, could hear the distant churning of waves just beyond the trees. Ever since he’d found this spot in Lord Mercox’s orchard, it had become his own secret hiding spot. A place he could relax away from the rest of the world.

Or, well, it had been.

After another solid minute of scolding, Leo finally sighed and looked down. Below him, his brother stood with his arms crossed and eyes slightly narrowed. The mix of concern and disapproval on his face was so familiar at this point that he could see it with his eyes closed.

“Yeah yeah, sorry. No one noticed me missing so it’s fine.” Leo idly dusted some dirt off his plain grey servant’s uniform. The cloth was so rough that it collected damn near anything that fell onto it.

“But what if they did? Lord Mercox would be furious!” His brother’s voice was doing that thing where it went higher at the end, a sure sign of an incoming stress-induced panic attack. On closer inspection, the perpetual dark circles under his eyes looked even deeper than usual.

Leo frowned and hopped down from the branch, landing with a light thump on the ground. He winced as his arm brushed against the tree on the way down. The healing burn sitting under his sleeve was still tender to the touch. He bit his lip and schooled his expression before his brother could notice.

“Frazer, chill out. We’ll head back now, okay? No one’s gonna know a thing.”

Frazer’s eyebrow twitched. He looked like he wanted to argue more, but before he did, his eyes darted up to the sky. There, the sun had reached its apex and would begin descending soon. He pursed his lips and finally nodded with an exasperated sigh.

“Fine, but I’m serious. You can’t keep running off all willy nilly. If you’re not careful—”

Frazer was cut off by the pile of grass suddenly pelted at his face. He sputtered, spitting out pieces of dirt while Leo snickered and sprinted away. He could hear Frazer yelling and chasing him from behind, a pebble narrowly missing him. Leo twisted around just enough to stick his tongue out, then continued dashing away.

Soon they’d return to the stifling walls of the manor, but for now, laughing and running under the warm sun, he felt more alive than ever.

—

An icy chill crawled up Leo’s spine.

Slowly, he spun around and came face to face with hazel eyes that were exactly the same shade as his own. Eyes that looked too large on the young face they sat on. That face, smeared with dirt and grime, was far more bony and sunken-in than any teenager should look. It had been gut-wrenching back then, and it was even moreso now that he was staring at it in his twenties.

Leo took in the plain servant’s uniform, the mussed brown hair, the slight slouch in the shoulders—every detail was exactly as he remembered.

He closed his eyes and forced himself to take a deep breath. “It’s an illusion,” he reminded himself. “It’s not real.”

It didn’t matter that every single detail looked real, that the voice and inflections were exactly the same. Leo gritted his teeth and forcibly spun around from the apparition, eyes still squeezed shut. He just needed to ignore it, find Allan and Spade, and deal with the fucker in the manor.

“Not real,” he continued to whisper to himself. He heard that voice calling behind him, but he forced himself to keep walking forward. He knew that the moment he looked back, he’d only drown in the memories. “Not real.”

Something crackled behind him. A second later, the distinct smell of smoke filled his nostrils.

Leo’s eyes snapped open. All around him, roaring fire stretched towards the skies, smoke and mist mixing into a single haze while that voice continued to call his name.

The former [Thief]’s heartrate picked up. This was still an illusion, it had to be—no natural fire spread this quickly, and [Judgement] would’ve notified him if someone had used a spell. But the heat from the flames felt so tangible. Was this what an advanced illusion could do?

Leo looked down and immediately recoiled when he saw not the hand he’d glanced at that morning, but a thinner one framed by dull metal shackles and covered in a perpetual layer of grime. He blinked, and there his usual hand was again. Leo slowly moved his fingers, verifying that they were, in fact, real. His heart was pounding in his chest, and at some point his breathing had picked up. The heat from the fire—the fake fire, he reminded himself—burned hotter than ever.

He closed his eyes and exhaled, attempting to calm down. This wasn’t good. He could already feel that old fear rising, and the last thing he needed right now was a panic attack. He needed to get out of range of the illusion before he saw something else. Something worse.

Opening his eyes again, Leo took a steadfast step forward, ignoring the wall of fire now surrounding him. If he dared focus too long, he might see figures, the dark silhouette of a burning manor, but he forced himself to look ahead as he moved through the magic.

Behind him, a shrill scream pierced through the crackling flames. The sound was slightly muffled, as though it was being filtered through the cold stone walls of a prison—just beyond reach. The hairs on the back of Leo’s neck rose, and before he could think twice, he activated [Sprint] and ran.

“Shit shit shit!”

The screaming didn’t stop. He raised his hands to block his ears. It didn’t do anything. Of course not. This was magic, not reality.

“Sick bastard,” Leo cursed as he continued to run through the illusion, mentally placing himself in relation to the manor he’d seen earlier. The fucker wasn’t just trapping people in illusions, he was purposely pulling out their darkest memories from the recesses of their minds. Leo’s fingers clenched into fists, fury boiling through his veins.

His feet pounded against the earth as he continued to sprint. He had to be nearing the manor soon, he thought. Just how far did this illusion go?

A wall of fire burst forth in front of him, and Leo had to resist the instinctive urge to slow down. Another scream rang out. He gritted his teeth. Without thinking twice, he threw himself into the crackling flames.

All at once, everything stopped.

As he crossed over some invisible boundary, the images and sounds disappeared in the blink of an eye. The roaring flames, the burning mansion, the screaming, his brother. All of it gone in an instant.

Leo gasped and hunched over, gripping his knees for more stability as he caught his breath. Beads of sweat dripped down his forehead, and in the cool mist, the air was suddenly almost unbearably cold.

Swallowing, the [Fragmentholder] straightened again and gazed up. In front of him, a massive, thick stone wall stretched upwards towards the fog-covered sky. He couldn’t even see the top of the manor from here, and a quick scan of his surroundings suggested that the wall must wrap all the way around the manor.

Leo’s eyes narrowed. He was willing to bet that Lord Lowthell was inside, safe beyond the wall as he slowly chipped away at his “guests’” minds.

Leo activated [Judgement] to check if this wall was also an illusion, but nothing appeared. This was real.

He bit his lip. Allan and Spade were still nowhere to be found. Where had they gone? Were they still in the forest? Beyond the mist? Leo quickly pulled up the party map, mentally berating himself for not doing so earlier. In his panic, he hadn’t even thought of checking it.

It was difficult to discern details, but he was able to make out two blinking dots. He sighed in relief when he scanned their stat sheets and saw that neither of them were hurt. That was good, at least.

Based on what he could make out, Spade was somewhere north, likely just outside the manor. Allan’s dot, on the other hand, placed him squarely inside the building.

Leo cursed and closed the map, mind churning. Had that bastard already found Allan? Was he captured? Unconscious? A deluge of worst-case scenarios flooded his mind, and he had to remind himself that according to the [Healer]’s stat sheet, he wasn’t injured. Not yet, at least.

He needed to get inside. Spade was too far away to reach without traveling through the illusion again, and currently Allan was in the more dangerous position. He didn’t feel good about it, but he’d have to trust that the [Executioner] would be fine on her own for a while longer. Right now, he needed to hurry into the manor.

Leo placed a hand against the wall, tapping it. It was rock solid and much too thick to break through. He hadn’t seen an entrance when he’d jumped earlier, and given the sheer size of the building, going around looking for the door would take far too long. Leo took a few steps back and squinted up at the wall, scrutinizing it. Could he make the jump? He frowned. Either way, he was going to try.

Leo took a few more steps back, stopping just outside the range of the illusion. He took a deep breath, mentally braced himself, and double-checked that he had [Acrobatics] equipped. Then, with one final inhale, he dashed forward with the aid of [Sprint] and ran up the sides of the wall.

He made it up about halfway before he felt himself begin to lose momentum. He activated [Sprint] again and attempted to push further, but he was in a losing fight against gravity. Leo cursed. He could just barely make out the top of the wall; he only needed to reach a little farther.

Leo activated [Air Walk] as his foot slipped, jumping off the solid platform of air and propelling himself upwards. He stretched a hand out to grip the ledge, straining his arm. He was so close.

His fingers just barely missed the ledge. His body began to plunge downwards, and Leo’s eyes widened, heart racing as the wind rushed past his ears. In a split second decision, he thrust his arm behind him and activated [Firebolt].

A jet of fire burst out from his palm, the air crackling from the heat. The momentum flung him upwards towards the top of the wall. Leo just barely managed to catch himself on the ledge and heave himself over, adrenaline still pumping through his veins.

He took a deep breath and rose to his feet. The wall was at least three feet thick, giving him ample space to stand on. Up here, the ground below was cloaked in silvery mist, and the forest beyond seemed to stretch endlessly. It was so cloudy that he couldn’t even see the wall of light.

Frowning, Leo focused on the manor. It was a massive building constructed of solid wood and stone with tall windows and ornate carvings running along the walls as trim. The manor itself was actually fairly close to the wall, with about a five foot gap separating them in the narrowest places. Leo scanned his surroundings, trying to find any hint of movement through the softly glowing windows. There was none.

He pulled up the party map again, but the dots weren’t detailed enough to tell him where in the manor Allan was.

Leo turned slowly, gaze drawn to a particularly tall, round tower situated near the center of the manor. His eyes narrowed. If someone needed a vantage point to cast a wide-range spell, that would be the place to do it. He was willing to bet he’d find Lord Lowthell there.

Leo turned and carefully moved further along the wall towards one of the narrower gaps. He should be able to jump over, grab the window ledge, and force it open. Once he was inside, he’d go for the tower. If he dealt with Lord Lowthell, the illusions would fade and he’d be able to find Allan and Spade without issue. Not to mention, Leo thought to himself, fingers clenching as he recalled the sounds of screaming and crackling flames—this had gone beyond the fragments.

Taking a deep breath, the [Fragmentholder] used [Judgement] one more time to verify that the manor and window were real. Then he bent his legs, took aim, and launched himself over to the window. Compared to climbing the wall, this was much easier to grab onto. From there, he supported himself with one arm and used his other to pull out his lockpick. It took some fiddling around to get the window open, but once it did, he was able to push it up and slip inside.

Leo landed on the floor silently. Soft, plush carpet bent below him, and he carefully straightened.

He now stood in a long hallway that seemed to extend around the manor. Candles lit up the dark wallpaper patterned with blooming flowers and petals. A few oil paintings were hung intermittently along the hallway, each one depicting a noble dressed in finery who he assumed were members of the Lowthell family.

No one was around. Save for the whistle of the wind outside, the area was dead silent.

Leo activated [Fade into Background] while he crept along the perimeter of the hall. He soon found a door that opened into a second, wider corridor. This one should take him closer to the center of the building, where the tower was.

Clenching his fingers, Leo ducked his head and proceeded deeper into the manor.
