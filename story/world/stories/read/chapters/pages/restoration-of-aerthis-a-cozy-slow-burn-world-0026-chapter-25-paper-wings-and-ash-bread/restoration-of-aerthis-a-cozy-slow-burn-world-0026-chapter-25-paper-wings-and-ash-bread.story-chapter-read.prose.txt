The day started with Elynara slowly coming back to consciousness. There was no hurry or pressure, so she just let her body lazily catch up to her waking mind. With eyes closed and stretching lightly, it was a lovely start to a morning.

Up until she received a thwack to the forehead.

Nowhere near enough to hurt, but just firm enough to startle her into opening one of her eyes. It worked as an announcement that Starpuff had once again appointed itself official wake-up specialist. It bobbed in the air above her, glowing green and faint yellow at the tips, trilling with far too much pride.

Elynara glared at it with her still-too-sleepy eye.

“You,” she said, voice still stuck in the middle of a yawn, “are very lucky you’re cute.”

Starpuff answered with a pirouette that ended in another light bump, this time square to her temple. Pufflet snorted from the foot of the bed, reacting to their morning antics, then curled tighter into the blanket pile.

Her bedmate made a muffled noise.

“No,” Mina groaned.

Elynara stretched both arms up, felt the familiar ache settle in her shoulders, then rolled them loose, then once again looked at the floating puff.

“You’re consistent. I’ll give you that.”

“Five more minutes.” Mina sleepily muttered

“That’s what you said yesterday.”

“No proof.”

The blanket tugged up in protest. Pufflet, who had apparently claimed that blanket as sovereign territory, grumbled and flopped sideways onto Mina’s legs.

Elynara stood, found her staff, and lowered herself back onto the floorboards with practiced ease. The grain of the wood pressed warm under her shins. She took a breath, lined the rhythm, and sank into the pulse of her core.

She hardly even thought what she was doing anymore, the routine so ingrained into her.

Mana in.

Mana out.

The cycles had changed since her very first attempts. They still pulled, still pressed, but without the resistance of before. Flowed easily with her breathing, following along as she meditated the sleepiness away.

Her center felt a little broader now too, and perhaps a little clearer? Even as she trained her mana sense, the feeling remained just that. Still, she imagined it was a good thing, as each loop wound tighter, more efficient. Every draw held just a little more than they initially did.

She found the point where it started to burn and pressed past it. Gently, deliberately, with just enough pressure, like leaning into fabric until it stretched.

When she stopped, her breath had gone long and even. Her core buzzed warm and steady.

One hundred and seven.

Not bad.

She stood, shook out her arms, and turned toward the bed.

Mina had propped herself up just enough to squint at the sunlight creeping through the window. Her hair stuck to one cheek. Her eyes didn’t quite match.

“You look like you fought a windmill.”

“I fought Pufflet’s whining.”

Elynara glanced around.

Pufflet sneezed and rolled off the bed entirely, while Starpuff looked far too innocent of that crime.

Meaning it probably had to do something with the whines.

“Breakfast?” Elynara offered.

“Will standing kill me?”

“Only a little.”

They moved slowly downstairs. The scent of stew from the previous night still lingered faintly beneath fresh bread and something sizzling in oil.

Rina was already elbow-deep in something unidentifiable and speaking with enough energy to make the cabinets rattle. Finnie leaned halfway over the kitchen counter, arms flailing as he described something “feathery and aggressive.” All while Theren grunted behind a stack of clean bowls, passing judgment on the world one spoon at a time.

The girls slipped in with a greeting, one loud, the other still sleepy.

Mina took up slicing bread. Elynara handled the plates. Starpuff buzzed a little too close to the butter dish and earned a soft bap from Elynara’s staff.

She answered unprompted at the questioning gaze of Rina.

“I’ve tried teaching Starpuff some manners,” Elynara muttered, “ but he’s immune to rules.”

“So you gave it a whack? Good going, works for my boys too!”

They sat together once everything was ready, the table already half-claimed by Finnie’s retelling of what was definitely not a hawk and absolutely not a kidnapping.

Mina’s chewing slowed as the story grew more dramatic. Pufflet stared at her plate until she slipped him a bite of egg.

Elynara leaned on one elbow, warm bread in hand, and watched the story grow wings. Rina laughed at the punchline. Theren made a sound like a man being pained by exaggeration. Aren shouted from outside something about goats.

In the lull between bites, Elynara tried again.

“We should pay for staying. Even just a little.”

Rina didn’t even look up from her chopping.

“Help feed the goats.”

“That’s not the same.”

“Sounds like it is.”

Mina snorted into her tea.

Elynara held her ground for all of three seconds, then sighed and reached for another slice of bread. No use arguing with the woman who could have outdone a literal avalanche in volume if she tried.

The kitchen stayed bright, full of motion. Without rush or schedules, they enjoyed the quiet rhythm of chores and conversation.

Her staff leaned against the wall, and her mana core hummed steady beneath her ribs.

And the morning, loud and lively as it was, still felt like something she could hold onto.

It only took a little more nagging, helping with the goats, as promised, and a few promises to ‘do well out there’ before the girls were finally free to continue their little ‘adventure’.

Though really, they only managed to leave the farm after the second breakfast, or at least that’s how Elynara called it.

Not because it was a second breakfast per se, but because Rina had caught Elynara trying to sneak out after the meal and responded by ladling a second helping directly into her bowl.

Broking no argument, just a spoon, bread, and the firm declaration that “you don’t walk fields on an empty stomach.”

So they left after second breakfast.

Starpuff zipped ahead like it had somewhere important to be, then immediately forgot and began orbiting a passing dragonfly with great intensity.

Aren walked in front, posture relaxed, tail flicking every few steps like a metronome that only half-cared about time. He didn’t say much, just raised a hand occasionally to point toward paths or gates as they passed.

Elynara kept pace without trouble. The morning had been pleasant. Warm without baking, breezy without chill. Just enough to carry the scent of turned soil and crushed grass.

The fields, though. That was a surprise.

She had seen farmland before. Passed it. Walked through it. But this wasn’t rows tucked between roads. These were whole patches of land that swallowed sightlines. Though she didn’t recognize much from the plants being grown, some looked quite close to wheat. And beans. Endless beans. Someone here loved beans entirely too much.

Aren stopped just shy of a fork in the path, gestured to the right with his thumb.

“That’ll take you to Malen’s grove. If he’s out, check by the shade tree.”

“Thanks,” Elynara said.

“You’ll need a full day for his lot. Maybe more.”

“Great.”

“Happy to help.” He turned immediately and headed back the way they’d come.

Mina blinked.

“That’s it?”

“Yep, Aren’s not the most talkative. Takes more after his dad than Rina.”

Pufflet barked at a butterfly and charged into the tall grass.

Elynara watched Aren’s tail disappear behind a hedge and sighed.

The next three hours were work.

Not hard, exactly, but there was no speeding things up. Fence to fence, row to row. Each property had its own quirks. Some had shrines tucked neatly by a pond or under a tree. Others had none at all. A few had what might have been once, now buried beneath moss and roots.

She logged each location in her book, made marks whenever she sensed anything unusual, and checked for any signs of lingering spirit presence.

Only a few spirits answered.

It wasn’t surprising, and entirely the point of her survey. Not every shrine had any spirits left, maybe never had. Offerings alone not enough to attract any more. And clearly a few fell into disuse, the land owners not tending to them for any reason.

In the end, not every field had a spirit left to tend it.

Every empty shrine just hammering home what she already knew.

But at least with a map made, she would know - they all would know - where to focus their efforts.

And so it was worth doing regardless.

Even Starpuff seemed to trill in agreement.

…Or was just announcing its success in bouncing away a flying ladybug.

She would never know.

As for Mina, she didn’t really complain. Just trailed behind, one eye on Pufflet and one hand always on the mana wood tucked under her strap. Occasionally, she would hand Elynara a water flask without prompting, or stop to tie up her boot before it caught on a root.

They chatted a little, but the girl seemed content focusing on her training - and playing with Pufflet.

Starpuff buzzed overhead like a lazy wisp, occasionally dropping low enough to poke Elynara’s shoulder or get in the way of her map.

“Not helpful,” she said, brushing it aside again.

It chirped, unrepentant, and made a loop that ended with a squeaky bonk into her left cheek, making her stumble.

Mina giggled under her breath at her reaction.

By the time the sun dipped toward afternoon, they had crossed two farms and most of a third. Sweat clung to the edge of Elynara’s collar, and her legs had started to protest every time she bent to examine a shrine or note things down.

Still, she kept going.

Every visit tightened the path. Every record made the network stronger. Even if the spirits weren’t there now, they might show up later, if things are restored enough.

Or so she hoped.

They stopped at another place. A ridge this time. But there was no spirit in sight again. She did feel slightly stronger ambient mana though.

And so she noted it down, another mark on her growing map.

Behind her, Mina sat cross-legged in the grass, Pufflet curled in her lap. The girl’s eyes weren’t on Elynara. They tracked something to the left, just past a patch of reeds, where Starpuff had taken to spinning in quick circles above something.

Elynara followed her gaze and frowned.

“See something?”

Mina hesitated, then nodded slowly.

“Maybe.”

Elynara rose and stepped over carefully. Starpuff didn’t stop its loop.

It was a herb patch. Except it held a lot more mana than Elynara would expect.

It wasn’t one she recognized, however.

“You have a good eye,” she said.

Mina looked startled, then pleased. Pufflet rolled over dramatically and sneezed.

They walked two more fields before deciding the day had earned its close. They didn’t find too many spirits, but Elynara made sure to offer some mana to every single one they found. Mina following in her steps.

And so the cycle of feeding and recharging the wooden piece went.

Elynara flipped open her map once more as they leaned against a low fence, the late afternoon wind tugging gently at the corners.

Only five confirmed sites.

Still, it was a good start, and as good a place as any to turn back.

They had just one more shrine to get to before heading back, and Elynara was glad they did.

Five more minutes of walking to confirm another spirit presence seemed worth it, and soon they went about their routine offerings again.

Mina had done this a dozen times now. The few first with guidance, most without. There was no expectation of instant response, but still she persisted.

And something was beginning to show for that effort.

Elynara exhaled softly and let her attention drift to how the girl was doing. And a smile spread on her face when she noted the little ways her hands reacted, sensing something before Mina herself did.

The spirit didn’t rush. It floated toward the charm in a gentle curve, the edges of its light pulling faint streaks from the air. When it touched the mana, the glow thinned for a breath and then brightened again, stronger. The exchange only lasted a few seconds.

Then Mina flinched.

Barely a little bit, a breath of recoil. A twitch behind the shoulders. Her eyes went wide.

When the spirit finally finished feeding and fled back into the trees, Elynara tapped her staff once against the stone and rose.

Mina stood with her, brushing dirt from her knees with one hand while the other cradled the wooden charm close.

But before she had a chance to move, Elynara reached the girl in two strides and wrapped an arm around her side, drawing her in close.

“You felt it,” she said, voice low, grin wide. “You actually felt it.”

Mina blinked.

“I-maybe? It felt weird. Sort of like-” She paused, frowning slightly. “Like something skimmed my hand. But it didn’t touch my hand. It was inside.”

Elynara nodded.

“Yes, and that’s quite enough of a start.”

The girl went still again. Then slowly, oh so slowly, a proud smile curled at the edge of her mouth. One she was clearly trying not to show too much of, and completely failing.

Elynara stepped back enough to meet her eyes.

“That’s your mana sense beginning to open. It's small right now. But it means you’re getting a hang of the flow around you.”

Mina stared at the charm in her hands like it had betrayed a secret. Then she looked up.

“So I’m… actually doing it?”

“You’re starting.”

Her cheeks flushed instantly. She ducked her head, tried to compose herself, and didn’t quite succeed.

Pufflet snored.

Starpuff looped above, then dipped low enough to bump both of them on the head with a clumsy spin. Mina laughed, half a squeak, half a cough, and leaned briefly into Elynara’s side.

“Do I get to brag about this?”

“Only once,” Elynara said. “Then we make you do it again.”

Mina beamed.

“Deal.”

They left the grove together, steps lighter now despite the climb.

The late afternoon light broke across the trail in warm bands. The wind tugged loose strands of hair across her forehead, and the air smelled faintly of apples and turned soil. Starpuff flew low between the farm plants, smacking some grain loose and chasing the flutter before zooming upward again.

Mina laughed once as a swirl of leaf dust caught Pufflet full in the face.

Elynara grinned, then pointed at the spirit.

“You’re encouraging it.”

“I didn’t do anything.”

“Exactly.”

Starpuff trilled in triumph and looped again.

The farmstead crept into view just as the light slipped toward gold. Shadows stretched across the path in long, soft lines, and the roof tiles caught the glow like they’d been waiting for it.

Elynara gave one final stretch overhead, spine popping in two places, then let her arms fall with a huff.

Behind her, Mina trudged along at a determined tilt, eyes forward, feet very much over it. Pufflet had long since retreated into the travel pouch, the gentle sway of his snoring adding a sleepy rhythm to her walk.

Still upright and moving. Victory for the ages.

They reached the porch without comment. The scent of warm spice, and something sweet tucked under it, hit them immediately. Their mood rose at the expectations of sweets to come.

Elynara stepped through the doorway and didn’t even make it past the kitchen threshold.

Rina spotted them instantly.

“There you are! You look like wilted lettuce-” Her eyes flicked from Elynara’s hair to Mina’s shoes to the quiet, suspiciously smug silence between them.

“Wait. Something happened.”

Elynara nudged Mina forward with an elbow.

“Ask her.”

Mina glanced sideways.

“I mean… it’s probably nothing big-”

Rina raised a single brow. That was all.

“I just… felt something. When the spirit fed. Kind of like mana brushing past. I think. Maybe.”

The spoon in Rina’s hand hit the counter with a decisive thunk.

“Finnie!”

From the hallway:

“I didn’t break it!”

“No, child. The good cookies.”

There was a pause.

Then feet scrambled off somewhere at speed.

Mina blinked.

“Wait, I didn’t say it was official, the System didn’t even–”

“You have taken your first step forward” Rina said, voice sharp with conviction. “System or no System. That’s worth celebrating. What’s next? You going to say sunrise isn’t worth noticing unless someone announces it?”

“I—”

“Cookies, Mina.”

Elynara leaned against the counter and tried not to look too pleased.

“She gets like this sometimes.”

“I see that now.”

Finnie returned at a full sprint, holding the jar like a priceless artifact.

“They’re still warm! I guarded them from Aren!”

Aren’s voice echoed from the far room, faint and betrayed:

“You tackled me!”

Mina was quickly armed with a plate and flanked by multiple congratulations, most of them overlapping. Even Pufflet got involved, pulled from the pouch and subjected to what could only be described as overwhelming affection. He lasted four full pats before going limp with dramatic resignation.

The sugar haze took over quickly.

Elynara found her seat beside Mina and helped herself to a second cookie while the others talked over each other.

Rina’s retelling of “the magical breakthrough” was already climbing into the realm of myth. Finnie provided sound effects. Someone mentioned birds, though it was unclear whether they were part of the story or just passing by the window.

Elynara leaned closer and dropped her voice.

“You did good.”

Mina’s ears went a shade pinker than before.

“I didn’t do much.”

She just ruffled her hair in response, quieting her for a breath.

Yet it put a smile on Mina’s face nonetheless.

Just one more thread in the big Plan, tied down clean.

She found footing in this strange new world. Learned about magic and practiced her skills. Her own training aside, Mina’s progress was good to see, one more step towards… hopeful future. And with shrine mapping underway and Mina’s progress no longer hypothetical, all of it was steadily knitting into shape.

Elynara leaned back and caught Rina’s eye.

“That thing we talked about earlier. Is it still alright?”

Rina didn’t pause.

“Of course.”

“Thank you.”

That was all she needed, next part of the Plan to go!

Momentum was important, and every small win worth building on.

Eventually, the results would show.

Unfortunately, her moment of tactical joy was promptly interrupted by a certain little girl, not too happy with all the attention being directed her way.

“Hey,” Mina said, far too casually. “Did you know Elynara can make butterflies out of fire?”

The room stilled.

Rina straightened like she had just been offered treasure.

“Excuse me?”

“No no no–” Elynara sat forward too fast. “Mina.”

“You did it at the orphanage,” Mina added with the innocent precision of a guilty mastermind. “They flew. One even turned into a sparkling phoenix!”

“That was just a small magic show.”

“Oh,” Rina said, already clearing a space near the hearth. “So you really can do it.”

“I, um, I suppose.. yes.”

Starpuff zipped through the air, trailing a celebratory loop like a child demanding a show.

Pufflet yawned, rolled onto his back, and did not move again.

Elynara closed her eyes, breathed in, and resigned herself to the consequences of past magical flair.

Rina clapped once.

“We expect artistry.”

“Your cookies are flammable.”

“We live dangerously.”

Elynara stood, rolled up her sleeves, and summoned a small, perfectly controlled spark to her fingertips.

“Fine. But if anything catches fire, it’s your fault.”

Mina beamed.

The others gathered.

And the first flickers danced into a simple round shape. Then a flowing flame, a butterfly, a bird, and soon even more followed.

The scent of sugar still lingered, but somehow the family was too engrossed to care. Only Finnie used the chance to steal a cookie under the guise of a yawn.

Elynara stifled her laugh.

It wasn’t so bad, after all.

And a small price to pay to bring a little joy out.

The show lasted only fifteen minutes, but their happy chatter followed for nearly an hour more. Both girls managing to extricate themselves out of there sometime in the middle.

Not that the chatterbox of a family minded them being there or not, not once they got going!

It wasn’t long until Elynara lay with one arm tucked under her head, staring up at the wooden ceiling.

The voices nearly overpowering the distant sounds of crickets filtering in through the open window. The bed creaked gently beside her as Mina shifted, one foot kicking off the blanket in her sleep. Pufflet had flopped onto his back again, snout twitching like he was chasing a dream snack. And Starpuff floated in a lazy drift above them all, dim and drowsy, glow pulsing once every few seconds like a heartbeat winding down.

The day had wrung them out in the best way.

At least, Elynara noted, Mina was far more relaxed now. First successes really had that power over people.

Her attention turned back to herself, to the warmth she could still feel in her hands, her impromptu unexpected show rekindling something in her.

Not just from her spellwork, but from the rhythm of it too. Fire shaped into butterflies, into birds, into whatever nonsense Rina and Finnie shouted for next. The moment the little phoenix tail sparked and nearly set the fireplace broom alight, Mina had laughed so hard she almost fell off the bench.

Elynara grinned into the dark. No regrets.

Well, she had one.

The way the flames had moved, it stuck with her.

Her thoughts from the day and the show just now were a stark reminder that she still had a long way to go. And even the things she knew could be made better.

She shifted slowly, slipped her feet to the floor, and pulled herself upright in the low light. Her staff stayed by the wall, untouched. This didn’t need tools.

The boards were cool against her ankles as she crossed to the small corner between the window and the dresser. Enough space to sit. Think. Work.

Her knees folded under easily now as she prepared to meditate. That old stiffness was nearly gone.

Breath first.

Mana followed, rising with each inhale, folding with each exhale. It settled fast. Familiar. Comfortable. Her hands rested palm-up over her thighs, fingers loose.

She pulled gently from her core, shaped the flow without urgency. Let it coil. Then pulled again.

The room whispered softly around her. Nothing sharp, it was just the sense of presence, of mana clinging to beams, collecting in the corners, flowing in its own little ways.

She reached farther. Tapped the stored mana tucked into the charged pieces beside Mina’s pack. Fed it through the cycle.

Then paused.

She wasn’t testing control anymore. That had become routine. This was about curiosity, and learning.

Fire had already become her go-to. Quick to answer, eager to leap. But why? What about the other elements? And perhaps even what the spirits did?

She tried it again, just a little bit, barely enough to shape that first tongue of flame. Fold inward. Tension. Compression.

But instead of lighting it, she held the build.

Waited.

The shape pulsed once, but didn’t change yet. No fire, just a spell ready to go off.

Her brow furrowed.

She eased off and did it again. This time, she let her core mirror the motion. Just the edge of ignition. Not enough to burn. Just to stir.

And watched it with growing curiosity. How it just floated there, responding to her will.

She leaned back on her hands, letting the energy fade without release. Not everything needed to finish. Some shapes were worth studying mid-form.

A quiet thrill crept in under her ribs at the questions this sparked.

The idea that this was a different kind of something. It wasn’t merely just fire waiting to happen, but a hinge. A point mana turned from passive to active. The first edge of something wider.

She didn’t know how to describe it yet, but words seemed unnecessary now. The possibility was all that mattered.

And she still had more to test. Her mana sensing and mana shaping. Then mana breathing and cycling again. The way she pushed and pulled mana from objects.

She thought back to how she learned the fire spells. To rubbing the mana together. To pushing it together. To the ‘spark’ that made the fire, and pressure that made the explosion. And she arrived at a conclusion.

One she would have to test tomorrow. But perhaps… Perhaps she had been looking at things the wrong way all this time.

The yawn took her without warning, blinked hard, and rubbed a palm across her eyes. Starpuff, apparently bored with her meditations, dipped low and landed on her shoulder like a plush scarf that had given up.

“Alright, alright,” she whispered. “I get it.”

She stood, stretched once, then padded back to the bed and crawled under the blanket again. Mina mumbled something into her pillow. Pufflet sneezed.

The inspiration would wait.

But tomorrow?

Tomorrow, she would try holding it longer, test things out. See if it really behaved the way she thought it did.

New questions. New paths.

And all of them worth chasing.
