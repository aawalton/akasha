I stood where the Arbiter had been. Crystal shards glittered around my bare feet. I had broken a thing, just like I had broken so many things before.

There was only silence in the measuring hall. A silence that I knew well, matched with expressions I knew even better. There was one look all people had always made when I showed them what I truly was. It did not matter if they were Humans, Elves, Dwarves, Lizardfolk or even Beastkin. They all had that same look. That strange mix of fear and awe. The fear came from seeing overwhelming power, so overwhelming it was almost beyond comprehension. The awe came from finding the beauty in it anyway.

That look I had seen ten thousand times before. A hundred thousand. It had stopped being interesting a long time ago. I saw that same expression staring back at me now, from several hundred faces. My hands clenched tight, too tight. A Demon Queen was supposed to revel in these gazes. I wanted them to look at anything else.

The Inker who had been next to the crystal was the first to recover. He rose from the floor where the shockwave had put him, brushing crystal dust from his robes with trembling hands. His face twisted into a sneer when he found me. "What did you do, half-demon?" He stared at the empty space where the pillar had stood. In the silence, his voice filled the hall. "Clearly this measurement is compromised. The Arbiter must have been-"

"In forty-one years," the Archon said, "I have seen only one Arbiter ever crack." The old man had not raised his voice. He stood at the rear with his hands clasped. "I had thought that was as far as they could go," he said, slowly approaching. "I never imagined I would see one shatter entirely. It will need to be inspected. This one has been here since the founding of this Academy. Perhaps age has worn away at it. But for now, well done."

The Inker’s mouth opened and closed. He slowly nodded to himself. “That’s right…it was just…old. Had to be…no way a half-”

The Archon did not look at him. "Lysanthia," the Archon said. "Follow me. I wish to speak with you privately."

Ash stepped forward before I could move. She was already between me and the hall, and now she’d taken one step towards the Archon. "She is not going alone."

The Archon regarded her. His face didn’t so much as twitch. "I only wish to speak with her."

Ash's jaw set. "It is no sin to be beyond measurement. I will not let you-"

"You misunderstand, child." His voice was gentle. "She is not in any trouble. I simply wish to speak with her. That is all. You have my word that nothing will happen to her."

Ash looked back at me. I held her gaze for a moment, then nodded. "I will be fine," I said, though I did not believe it. Ash looked stricken. Slowly, she stepped out of the way. I had expected her to still argue. Perhaps the Hero was not entirely a fool.

I moved toward the door in silence. Every face in the hall turned as I passed. I had seen those eyes before. My gaze found the demonbloods, the dozen or so I saw. They were easy to pick out. They looked terrified. Something in my chest ached. I did not examine it.

Ren stood near the massive door. The last time I had seen her, she had been in the row of seats. When she had stepped here, blocking the way, I did not know. I moved past her. "Why the hell do you look like that?" Ren asked. Her voice was quiet. It was the first time I had heard her be quiet.

I stopped and looked at her. "You wanted me to prove myself, did you not?"

The words came to me easily. But something inside of me was uneasy as I said them. Ren opened her mouth. I did not wait for what followed. I stepped through the door and into the corridor, where the Archon was already walking.

We walked in silence. The Archon walked two steps in front of me, his hands clasped behind his back. He seemed perfectly at ease, as well he would be. I was the one in danger, after all. There were only two scenarios here, as far as I could see. The first: this man was going to kill me. He had the power. I had just revealed myself as a threat that could not be measured. Eliminating threats before they grew was a simple and effective strategy. I would have done it myself.

The second: he was going to somehow chain me to him. This Consecration had always been a leash. A leash with a long rope perhaps, but still a leash. Now it would tighten around my neck. How that would happen did not matter. There must be some method for it.

Either option seemed likely and both were familiar. It was exactly as Isabelle had said. The payment will come. It always comes. It was a good thing that my face hadn’t betrayed me in front of Ash.

Occasionally we would walk past students. There were many of them. People who obviously hadn’t been there at the measuring. They glanced at me, glanced at the Archon, and then were the picture of civility. They had not looked at me so when I had been on my own.

We arrived in that familiar hallway. The Archon opened the door to his study and stepped inside. I followed. The room was as I remembered it. A room too small for the power this old man so obviously had. I waited. The Archon moved around his desk. He opened a drawer. He came back to me. This was it. It was going to be some device that-

He had a pair of bracers. Mirrors to the one I already wore, if I was not mistaken. He held them out. I stared at them. Stared at him. "Have you not looked at your own hands?" he asked.

I looked down. My marks were flickering, slowly. Black and white bled through the red and green disguise, surfacing and retreating and surfacing again. I had not looked.

"My apologies for the haste," the Archon said. "I wished to remove you from that hall before anyone else noticed. I do not believe they did. Your display was a rather…apt distraction, even if it did overwhelm the first pair. For now, it seems your secret holds."

I took off the old bracers. It was as simple as drawing all the power away from my arms, and then they fell away. I took the new bracers and fitted them. Red and green once again perfectly hid Black and White. "Is this why you brought me here?" I asked.

The Archon looked at me. Then he shook his head. I braced myself. "The bracers were meant to be enough," he said, slowly. He moved to the window, clasping his hands behind his back. "I had expected you to be extraordinary, with two Sovereigns. That was the purpose of Consecration. I had expected a result that would impress, perhaps shock. Either would have been enough to justify the investment I am making."

He looked out at the Academy grounds below. I followed his gaze. Students crossed the yard in their grey uniforms. "I had not expected what you showed me." He turned back. There was something in his expression I had not seen on this man's face before. It was neither fear nor anything else I could have named. Only that it was a strange expression on someone with power.

"I will try to contain what happened today within these walls," he said. "I will spread word that the Arbiter was malfunctioning and overdue for replacement. I will say that your result was excellent, but the crystal was already compromised." He paused. "I am afraid it will not hold for long. There are people who will hear of this. People even I will have trouble dealing with."

"Do you truly believe that story will do anything?" I asked. Why was he making this nonsensical offer at all?

"Perhaps not. But at the very least, it may buy you time."

"Time for what?"

"To grow stronger. You should have had years. After that…I fear even a year may be optimistic. You may only have months. Use them wisely." He looked at me. “There is a resonance chamber, one that only the Crests are allowed to use. One that I use myself. Being a resonance chamber is perhaps just…a small part of what it is, but it is a good training ground. You may use it. I believe you might be able to handle the strain. You may bring your friend. When you wish to use it, come to me. Before that, you should try the classes we offer. Perhaps those will be enough to get to Sigil.” He looked at me with an intensity that might have made someone else squirm. “Your goal should be to advance to the Crest, and quickly. It is…asking for much, but it might be possible. For you.”

I studied him. Every instinct I possessed told me this was the moment the price was named. I knew I should not ask. I should not tell the hunter that the prey knew it was being stalked. I asked anyway. "Just what do you want from me in return?” I demanded. “Unless you are giving all of this out of the goodness of your heart."

"No," the Archon said. "I am not." He said it quickly, as if it required no thought at all.

"What, then?"

The Archon was quiet for a long time. He looked out the window again, at the students below. When he spoke, his voice was barely above a whisper. "I have buried too many students who looked like you, Lysanthia." It was perhaps the single answer I had least expected. I had demanded it, and now I did not know what to do with it. I said nothing.

The Archon turned around. "May I?" the Archon asked. "I wish to see what kind of Essence Core could have done what I witnessed in that hall. It is academic curiosity."

I hesitated. Slowly, reluctantly, I extended my hand. The Archon moved forward and took it. Something heavy flowed inside me. He closed his eyes. It lasted two seconds. The Archon took a step back. He stared at me. "How?" he asked.

"It seems many people will be asking me that." I had never understood the question. What answer did they all expect?

He shook his head. "That is not what I mean." His voice had changed. "How are you enduring that?" I did not smile. I did not answer. The thing inside me hummed and ached. The Archon held my gaze for a moment longer, then turned away. "You are free to go." Just as he said it, a great bell rang. It was loud even here.

The Archon paused and something lighter crossed his face. "You might want to find the mess hall. If you cannot locate it, just ask a student or two." I looked at him for one more moment. Then I left without another word.

I had half expected Ash to be waiting right outside the door. She was not, and I had looked. Where had that fool Hero gone? Perhaps I would go to this mess hall after all. It was her who should come to me. A retainer did not simply vanish when her Queen had need of her. Why was she not already here? I would go to the mess hall then. I was not annoyed. That was beneath me.

It was a simple plan, one that was immediately made more complicated. Every hallway looked like the last. The same stone, the same-looking doors, the occasional portrait of someone supposedly important. I turned corners absently, and this led me through passages I had not seen before. I was certain that Ren had shown me these places, though what she had said about them, I could not say.

I received many looks during my wandering. I could tell who had been in the measuring hall and who had not. The ones who cowered as I passed -who pressed against the walls or changed direction mid-stride- those ones had heard or been there. The ones who merely glared, those ones hadn't. How would this ratio change in the near future, I wonder?

I took three turns, and almost surely all three of them were wrong. They led me into a courtyard, where I spied two students on a bench near the entrance. They were talking to one another and stopped talking when they noticed me. They looked at my horns, my eyes, the grey uniform. Their gazes lingered before they moved away. I could have asked them for directions. The words were simple. Where is the mess hall? They were just five words. A child could manage them.

I walked right past the two without saying anything at all. I took more turns, and these were wrong too. I walked up a staircase that led to a locked door. I walked up another staircase that somehow led into a wall and nothing else. I walked down a corridor that somehow looped back on itself. I saw more students as I walked, and I asked none of them for directions.

I turned a corner and collided with someone small. The impact was nothing to me. The boy yelped as he hit the floor. The many papers he had been carrying flew through the air and scattered all across the floor. The boy himself was small, very small, with dark hair that fell over his eyes and the tiniest nubs of bone rising from his temples.

I thought I had seen him before, though where and when remained a mystery. Not that it mattered. "Watch where you are going," I said. My voice was sharp.

The boy scrambled backward on his hands, his eyes wide. "S-sorry! I'm sorry, I didn't- I wasn't looking, I just-" He was gathering his papers with shaking hands, shoving them into a messy pile against his chest.

I watched him and a thought occurred to me. One that had occurred to me several times during my…walk. It was a distasteful thought, but the alternative was wandering these strange corridors until I expired of either old age or starvation.

"Boy," I said. He froze. "The mess hall. Where is it?"

He stared up at me. His mouth opened to say something and closed without saying anything. It opened again, and when he spoke, he still stammered over every other word. "T-the- you go past the arch, the big one with the carvings, and then- no wait, from here you'd need to go left first and then past the p-portrait of the dog until you see the big doors. Y-you can't miss them."

I closed my eyes. The directions were a mess, but they were still directions. I memorized them slowly. I opened my eyes. "Thank-"

The corridor was empty. The boy's footsteps were already fading around the far corner. I only had time to see his back before he turned out of sight.

The mess hall was, blessedly, far less loud than it had been last time. Even the large queue near the front was now only a handful of humans. I glanced around, and did not see Ash. I crushed the feeling that came. I saw the line again and moved to join it. Whispers followed me here too. There were very few of those fearful looks here.

I took a tray. The woman behind the counter gave me stew and bread. It seemed they did not only offer porridge. I would remember that. I turned and looked around the hall. Most of the tables were completely full, and so I chose  the third table. The closest table that still had space to sit.

I sat down, set the satchel beside me on the bench between myself and the wall, and began to eat. The stew was acceptable. I had certainly had better. The bread was dense. Not quite hard enough to kill, but closer than I thought bread should be. The stew had a sweetness I had not expected. I was halfway through when a shadow fell across my tray.

"You're sitting in our spot," someone said. I did not turn to see who. "Hey. I'm talking to you half-demon."

I took another bite of the bread. A girl slid onto the bench across from me. She had red hair and a Sigil on her left arm that branched in a bright blue pattern. Two more girls flanked hers. They only had lines.

"Hey, you must be stupid," the lead girl said. "You must not know how things work around here. I'm going to be nice enough to tell you, just this once. This table is ours. You’re not even a Sigil. So why don't you take your little tray and those horns and find somewhere more…appropriate for your kind?"

I ate my stew. It really was not as good as Martha's. "Did you not hear me?" The girl leaned forward. "Or are those horns blocking your ears?" One of the flanking girls snickered.

"I know who you are. You're the new little Consecrated student, aren't you? Heard you go around calling yourself Lysanthia," the girl continued in a voice that carried far. "I heard about your little testing today. Some of those commons are saying you broke the Arbiter?" She snorted. "There must have been some kind of trick. There's no way a filthy half-demon could-"

I ate. She said many more things, and none of them were worth more attention than the stew. At some point, she gave her name. It was Lena Eckhart. She said it with great emphasis. It meant nothing to me, though I believe Ren had mentioned the name to me before. Just where was Ash?

The girl was not very interesting because I had seen all of this before. The girl certainly had pomp, but it was a lie. Beneath it, she was afraid. I could see it just behind her eyes. In the way her shoulders were tight, or the way her fingers pressed too hard against the table. No matter how different this strange new world was, some things would remain the same. I did not think this was fear of me, exactly. That did not make it any less unpleasant.

Lena read my silence as defiance. "You're just like that wretch. Fine," she said. "Think you're above us, don't you? You stupid half-demon bitch, you think you can-" Her gaze snagged on the satchel beside me. "What's in the bag?" I ignored her. Lena's hand moved and seized the strap of the satchel.

The table beneath me cracked as black flame bloomed from my hand. I had not even called for the Requiem, but it had answered. Lena's face was white. Her two companions took a step back. "Remove your hand or lose it. You may choose." My voice was very quiet. I had given her a choice.

There were gasps from the nearest table, and I knew that every eye in the hall was watching. The Archon had just said that he was trying to hide me. I did not care. If this girl thought she could touch what was mine, then she would pay for it.

"You really think you can threaten me?" The paleness left the girl's face, and red took its place. The red of anger. The girl glowered, and blue lightning cracked once across her skin, over her uniform. It cracked again, and for a moment, I thought I saw her entire body glow. The Sigil on her arm was blazing.

A column of flame struck the air between Lena and me. "What the hell do you think you're doing to her?!"

Ren stood at the end of the table. Where she had come from, I did not know. Her right arm was extended towards us, and her Sigil was glowing too. Her red hair was wild and her eyes were wider. She stepped forward. Placed herself between the three girls and me. I stared at the back of her head.

A girl with a crooked collar and soot-covered nose had just stepped between me and the world.
