Chapter 67

One Battle After Another

Stepnye Volki

As the year progressed, waves of armored fractus seemed to be moving westward, crossing the plains and hills of Ukraine.

Mai was assigned to join a mixed team from the organization Veria, one of the continent’s most powerful and enigmatic resistance forces, composed of mages, feys, and other individuals with supernatural abilities. The mission was to halt the fractus advance in the cities of Donetsk and Rostov, just south of the Urals.

Upon arriving at Veria’s headquarters in Russia, Mai met several members of the organization. Among them were Dmitri, a captain and sorcerer—not the heavy-team Dmitri—and Ilya, a fey who could manipulate time on a short scale with certain restrictions. Mai quickly adapted to the group’s dynamics, though the differences in their abilities offered both advantages and challenges. In Mai's case, after a quick check and a fitting test, she ended up on Dmitri's team.

The mission began on the outskirts of Rostov, where fractus had breached the city through a portal, allowing them to reach it within hours. The city was in ruins already, and Mai immediately sensed that something was off from the beginning: the fractus were not just attacking conventionally, there was intelligence behind their movements. As they advanced, the team was ambushed by a legion of fractus using almost primitive guerrilla tactics, seemingly knowing exactly how to move to avoid attacks.

Veria’s intervention was crucial. Dmitri used his ability to create shadows and disorient the fractus, allowing Mai and the others to take the lead. However, when they thought they had gained ground, the true challenge appeared: a giant scorpion-like fractus, brown and red, with several geometric humanoid faces fused into its body, rose among the ruined buildings. It was a monstrosity—smarter than any they had faced before, capable of coordinating attacks through a form of telekinesis or chemical signaling.

At that moment, Mai was contacted by Veria’s commander, who had called in a powerful ally for the operation.

Through encrypted communication, Mai heard the voice of Rein, summoned for the joint operation. Rein’s ability to manipulate the weather and generate devastating storms with her magic was key to defeating the creature.

Mai and the others withdrew as Rein unleashed her power. Within minutes, the sky darkened, and a lightning storm struck the city. The storm’s fury, combined with Rein’s powerful bolts, annihilated the giant fractus and the waves of attacking creatures. Another team handled closing the portal, demolishing a building and thereby providing it with matter to disrupt its stability. Veria truly seemed to have more experience with dimensional phenomena and portals—apparently, this portal was easy to close, but it would open and close at various points along a straight line.

The key was to place decoys at various points along the path until contact with matter disrupted its stabilization. Mai had closed a few herself during the war by saturating them with electricity, but this was the first time she’d seen soldiers demolish entire sections of a city to do so after the usual techniques had failed.

Rein’s intervention was decisive, but Mai noticed something in the dragon fey’s eyes: a sadness and fatigue she couldn’t hide, as if the weight of the war bore more heavily on her than she wanted to admit.

***

By late spring 2097, her battlefield shifted to the Kaluga region, near Moscow. The attack on the city had been violent and swift.

The creature that had invaded the city was one of the rare ones. They could be described as a kind of plasmoid creature with bodies capable of shifting between various states. Their bodies resembled long-legged spiders, but on an enormous scale, reaching up to two and a half meters in length. The central part of the body housed the core, but the real danger lay at the tips of their slender legs. Upon contact with the ground, the surface took on a water-like texture, producing and sending out waves that could affect biological matter. These waves formed interference patterns that seemed to enable a form of communication across the entire network as it advanced.

Because of their shape, they were called “long legs.”

The squad of Dmitri was sent to intercept the threat and dismantle the fractus network before they could reach Moscow.

Upon arrival, they found the ruined city under siege. The buildings, though old, had been reinforced to withstand attacks, but the local forces could no longer hold control. Mai and the team infiltrated the battlefield, using the shadows and the terrain to push forward. As they advanced, they began to notice strange patterns in the fractus’ movements: they weren’t just attacking with ferocity—they were following a far more organized strategy.

And it almost felt as if they were fighting against a unit made up of people rather than fractus.

Mai and Dmitri, along with a small group, descended into the city’s sewers, where the magic users tracked the signals. What they found was a secret underground facility where a group of mages was manipulating the fractus. There were artifacts reinforcing the connection between the fractus and the humans controlling them through a complex, dangerous spell using fractium cores.

The confrontation was violent. While Mai and Dmitri fought the mages, the rest of the teams faced the fractus above. Rein joined the battle, covering the sky with storms of lightning to eradicate the remaining fractus on the surface. The battle ended with the destruction of the facility and the capture of several of the mages responsible. However, Mai knew there was still much to do, and the enemies hiding in the shadows were still at work.

At the end of the mission, Mai met once more with Rein and old comrades from Nevermore, who offered her a brief moment of quiet—silent glances that seemed to acknowledge exactly what Mai felt: the war was far from over, and the worst might still be waiting just beyond the horizon.

After that battle, which ended with the discovery of an entire clandestine research network conducting experiments in Russia and neighboring countries, Mai grew accustomed to the rhythm of the Russian units.

That encounter with Rein was the last one for a long time. In fact, for months she didn’t see anyone from Nevermore. She received news, yes, and the situation was the same everywhere. That’s how the days began to feel almost routine: equipment changes, training for new types of enemies and weapons, and deployments to various parts of Russia.

She had gotten used to it, and that seemed to be all there was to it.

The situation in Russia was far more chaotic due to the open portal in Siberia.News was constantly coming in from that front, and all the reports spoke of battlefields in the steppes and mountains that had turned into fields of corpses—regardless of whether they were humans, feys, or espers.

It was a constant back-and-forth. The terrain was far too vast to cover, and the idea that perhaps, by closing that enormous portal, the situation could spread to the entire world.

That idea wasn’t just a figment of hope. There seemed to be some truth to it.

In the years since the conflict began, analyses conducted by magicians had shown that the ley lines in Siberian territory had changed after decades of remaining the same. These lines extended further, and many of them had direct connections to others thousands of kilometers away, where portals had appeared. Therefore, it wasn’t far-fetched to think that the central core might actually be a sort of central nerve through which certain species—unable to exit through the central portal—created a kind of bottleneck between dimensions. This bottleneck could be what caused portals connecting the ley lines to open. Since the Fractus couldn’t exit through the central portal, they did so through nearby locations using dimensional jumps. As she listened to those theories at the meeting tables of various squadrons, Mai could only wonder if those theories were already being put to the test. Too many lives had been lost around the world, and that war had already claimed more than a billion lives.

If that continued for too long, no one even wanted to imagine what the world would be like afterward.

***

By the end of March, 2098, Mai received word that her orders had changed: she would not be returning to eastern Russia as she had anticipated. Instead, she was being assigned to a new mixed squadron on the Siberian front, a unit called Stepnye Volki, tasked with operating in the frozen valleys and desolate Russian steppes.

The bone-deep cold was relentless, and while the terrain felt familiar, the threats looming over that region were far more dangerous than any front she had faced before—because the heart of the problem lay near there.

In April, upon arriving at the base, Mai was greeted by an unusual scene: Stepnye Volki consisted of individuals with wildly varied abilities, but what stood out most was the camaraderie they shared. They weren’t merely a group of soldiers—they were an improvised family held together by the pressure of war.

Their commander, Sasha, a Ukrainian man with dark hair and a well-trimmed beard, greeted her with warmth and high spirits, always ready to share a funny anecdote whenever the situation allowed. Despite the harshness of war, his humor kept morale steady.

“I don’t care if you’re a fey, a mage, or just a plain human,” he told Mai during their introduction. “What matters is how we fight together. And trust me—we do it well here.”

Natalya, the first lieutenant, a snow-leopard fey with a thick, plush tail, was in charge of tactics and direct battlefield coordination. Her agility allowed her to move effortlessly across the frozen steppes, sometimes vanishing into the shadows only to reappear beside an enemy with a lethal strike, combining firearms with blades forged from fractium. She was always willing to take responsibility during the hardest moments, and her resolute gaze inspired respect in everyone around her—even though she used to have the strange habit of biting her own tail.

Ludovic, the sergeant from Kargopol, was a sturdy man, with scars covering what must once have been a rather handsome face. His demeanor radiated strict discipline, and he spoke in a blunt, unblemished manner. “Emotions stay outside,” he would say, but Mai soon discovered that he possessed an unshakable sense of justice and a deep loyalty to his squad.

Eun-ji, the fey medical officer, always carried a gentle smile. Her healing abilities were more than welcome on every mission. Though her face showed serenity, her eyes revealed the suffering she carried with her, and it didn’t take long for Mai to understand why—years ago, before the war began, Eun-ji had built a family, only to lose it in the first waves along the Korea–China border, her young daughter taken from her.

Jiho, the mage capable of summoning giant elemental constructs through paper-folding, was one of the most enigmatic members of the team. She seemed fragile at first glance, but when she unfolded her intricate origami, wind and earth transformed into monsters that obeyed her command. Her skill would have remained weak if it weren't for the fact that what she used in battle was paper that had been treated with fractium, which gave her the ability to be a front-line fighter. Her power was immense, yet her lack of confidence made her more vulnerable than she appeared.

Finally, there was Soo-ah, a young but determined corporal, with eyes that always sparkled with curiosity and passion for learning. Sometimes Mai caught her studying every movement of her teammates as if each gesture were a lesson, something that reminded Mai of herself when she had been younger, before she understood what it truly meant to live through a war. Soo-ah was the last one to join before Mai arrived.

A group of individuals with such different personalities, yet united by the same goal. In Stepnye Volki, there was no room for individualism, everything had to be done as a team, and each member depended on the others. Mai adapted quickly, earning everyone’s trust, but it was Sasha who gave her the final welcome.

“Here, Captain Mai, you’ll be one of us—but not just another shadow. We all have a role to play, and yours will be important. I trust you, and everyone here will be putting their asses in your hands. Keep that well in mind.”

Mai puffed out her cheeks, showing her obvious displeasure.

“…Please don’t call me captain.” For a brief moment, no one said anything. Only Natalya’s tail swayed from side to side. “Mai is fine. Or Private Izumi, if we’re on duty.”

The squad members exchanged bewildered glances.

“Uh… that’s a surprise,” said Natalya.

“I expected something different from the Silver Witch,” said Ludovic.

Ugh… that nickname was Jinat’s fault. At least it was better than “Mothy.”

“That’s something they gave me without my permission.”

“We’ve heard some stories about you.”

“I’m almost certain many of them are lies and gossip, just like that nickname. I’m not a witch—that’s something that was given to me without my permission. Although I do have silver hair.”

Sasha nodded. “All nicknames are given without permission… generally speaking. Still, welcome.”

“With hair that long, a witch’s hat would look good,” Natalya agreed.

Mai took Sasha’s hand, and Sasha gave her a warm, hearty welcome.

They had been expecting the arrival of an experienced captain from Nevermore. Instead, standing before them was a petite girl with silver hair, just over a meter and a half tall, pouting because someone had addressed her by her rank.

Sasha blinked twice before bursting into laughter.

“You’re interesting. I like that.”

“But why don’t you want your rank?”

Mai nodded without the slightest hint of embarrassment.

And so, nearing her 94th birthday, Mai continued forward with a new team.

***

The first missions went relatively smoothly in April and allowed her to get along with the members.

It was time for her to settle down with a permanent team, so the news that she would be with them until she was told to move on was a relief. Although she would continue to fight, she would not be jumping from country to country. That continental area would be where she would be for the foreseeable future.

Over the past year or so, many of the feys who had been moving around the world had finally settled into permanent teams and units. Mai didn’t know if it was true, but rumor had it that this, too, was the work of the Aeon and their prediction programs. Perhaps the plan of the joint experimental forces had already come to an end after years of conflict.

The first major mission Mai undertook with Stepnye Volki was in Omsk at the beginning of May with other squads, where fractus of different types had been closing in on refugee zones and spreading like a plague across the rural areas. The operation consisted of securing the perimeter along with local units and neutralizing the fractus before they could cause further harm.

The team deployed swiftly.

Ludovic, as always, directed the soldiers with precision. Eun-ji handled the medical logistics, making sure everyone had what they needed to survive the extreme conditions. Meanwhile, Jiho and Natalya moved across the battlefield. Jiho raised walls of earth reinforced by sheets of paper laced with fractium particles. Every fragment had been charged with magic through the local ley lines, turning the barriers into exceptional magical conductors that blocked the fractus' advance. Behind them, the snow-leopard fey picked off enemies one by one with her electric rifle.

Despite being an excellent shooter, Natalya was having some trouble because the fractus they faced came in three varieties.

The first, known as Grivens, were long and thin, almost like serpents made of fractal filaments that writhed and slipped through any crack or gap. Their bodies could coil around human or mechanical targets, tearing at them with tiny spear-like blades that sprouted from their fractal bodies. Their touch also interfered with nearby technology, causing weapons and devices to malfunction, forcing Mai and the team to maintain constant vigilance, distance and precise strikes.

The second type, the Clyx, were bulky and segmented, capable of extending limb-like appendages that stretched like tentacles. Their form confused the senses: shots that seemed certain would veer away, and every attack multiplied at impossible angles. Their mere presence generated a disturbing echo in the air, disrupting the soldiers’ sense of direction and the flow of magic. To fight them, Stepnye Volki had to work in strict coordination, using every piece of cover and firing in synchronized bursts.

The last type were the Aurex, airborne creatures with golden fractal wings or appendages that allowed them to glide and dive at frightening speed. They could shift shape mid-flight, from an almost invisible blur to an imposing threat. They left behind a trail of warped air that disoriented the soldiers and produced freezing gusts, hindering movement and weapon stability. Anticipating their movements was essential, and Mai knew that watching the skies would be critical for survival.

The first wave of fractus was ferocious—but the worst was yet to come.

Using her echolocation, Mai detected a rise in activity in a distant section of the field: a faint crackling of shifting earth beneath the movement of the light-bodied fractus, subtle yet unmistakable. What she didn’t expect was the ambush waiting on the nearby slopes.

Amid the chaos, Sasha quickly ordered a retreat into a tighter formation, their lines covered by coordinated suppressive fire. Soo-ah, young as she was, commanded a support squad, shooting with remarkable precision. Mai felt the battle’s pace intensify, adrenaline coursing through her as she ran from one flank to another, ensuring warning signals were picked up in time.

Following Sasha’s orders, Mai organized the retreat while every member of the squad took on a specialized role depending on which fractus threatened them. Ludovic anchored a defensive wall at the center, shielding the younger soldiers and preventing the Clyx from flanking them. Natalya used her speed and stealth to harass the Grivens from the sides, firing carefully placed shots and cutting their filaments with fractium blades before they could reach the line. Jiho deployed earth golems reinforced with fractium paper, creating mobile barriers that confused the Aurex and protected the rear from descending attacks.

Soo-ah led the support platoon, coordinating crossfire to keep the fractus from closing in, while Mai ran the length of the formation, identifying weak points and predicting enemy movement through echolocation. Every action synced together—shots, barriers, flanking strikes, tactical signals—forming a lethal dance that, despite the chaos, held the squad together long enough to halt the first wave’s advance with the arrival of the ships with portable Magnun Railguns, capable of sweep more longer areas.

The sweep raged for hours, and as the fractus finally withdrew, the squad regrouped to secure the area. As they caught their breath and exchanged words of encouragement, Mai watched Natalya and Soo-ah teasing each other in an attempt to ease the lingering tension. Eun-ji tended to the wounded in other teams with her usual calm expression.

That night, after the mission, the team sat around an improvised campfire, sharing stories and enjoying a fleeting break in the war.

Ludovic, in true leader fashion, gave them a brief talk. “This is the job,” he said as he drank his coffee with a few drops of vodka. “No heroics, no glory. Just survive and keep moving forward.”

“Whenever you’re drunk, you start spouting off,” Natalya said, tapping him on the head with her soft tail.

“I’m not drunk!” the big guy roared with a red nose.

“Yeah, yeah, yeah,” Natalya said, then looked at Mai. “Make sure this guy never drinks too much. He really can’t hold his water.”

“At least I don’t bite my own tail like you do.”

“You want to fight again? I’ll roll you around in the snow.”

“Whenever you want, little cat.”

“Ah! Please, don’t fight,” Soo-ah tried to calm them down.

The two girls and Sasha watched, amused, with their cups of hot chocolate and coffee.

As she watched them at that moment, she wondered how many times she had seen the same thing in recent years. No, rather, throughout her entire life.

Sharing a meal while sitting in front of a campfire in so many places now distant in space and time.She had met many people throughout her life. But she had also lost many.

She didn’t want to ruin the moment the others were having, but she really wondered if her whole life would be like this.

Just flowing along as the years passed.

But in itself, the fact that so many encounters repeated themselves—and the same went for so many losses—was a consequence of living so long.

“Is something wrong?” Sasha asked when he saw her watching them with an expression that was hard to read.

Mai just shook her head. “No, nothing. Can I have some vodka?”

Jiho handed her Sasha’s flask and poured a few drops into her coffee.

It didn’t taste all that great, but the warmth soothed her throat just as much as the scene unfolding before her eyes.

A group of strangers brought together by circumstance.

And Mai, with her laughter, joined the others as she watched Natalya and Ludovic wrestle, rolling around in the snow and grass thawed by the warmth of the fire.

Natalya was the winner and celebrated with a shot of straight vodka, emptying the flask—much to Sasha’s dismay, since he’d have to refill it somehow.

Maybe he later would ask around among the other squads and trade some black chocolate for a full flask.

It was the day after that engagement that Mai heard about the disaster at a linear collider in Tokyo. Oxy and several acquaintances of hers had been there. Fortunately, the Inoue section had suffered very few casualties, thanks to several espers, who used their powers to protect the population centers nearby.

Most of her acquaintances were fine, including Aika, even though the temple had been destroyed. Even so, many had died. Oxy, despite having been dangerously close, had survived. Mai could only lament for her friend, knowing she wouldn’t be able to visit her.
