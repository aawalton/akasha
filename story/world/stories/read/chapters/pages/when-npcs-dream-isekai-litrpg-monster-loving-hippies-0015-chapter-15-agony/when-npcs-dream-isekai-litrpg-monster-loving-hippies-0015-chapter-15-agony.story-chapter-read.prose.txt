Me and Juliette were back at the busy market again. We finally had all the money we needed—it was just a matter of buying and delivering the gear and supplies to the tribe. But there was a lot: spears, food, textiles... It would probably take at least three trips. Also, this was obviously a date. So obvious, I didn’t even need to confirm it with Juliette.

First, we needed large backpacks. They weren’t cheap, but it would be more efficient this way.Should we… buy the fruit cake again?

“Hi John the guide, John the guide!” someone shouted.

Shit. The Korean dude. We’d been spotted.

Ah, but that meant he was still alive. That was a good thing.

“Ah, Juliette, what was his name again?”

“Lee Hyun-woo,” she said. Thank god she remembered.

“Hello, young adventurer Lee Hyun-woo,” I said in my perfect NPC tone. “Is this humble town to your liking?”

“Yes, actually,” he replied. “I did what you said and got a job at the local bakery. I earn money, and they even let me keep the leftovers at the end of the day. Oh, and please call me Hyun-woo, respected guide.”

He worked at a bakery! Got money and free food. Genius. Why didn’t I think of that?

“I’m glad to hear that all is turning out for the best, Hyun-woo.”

“And who are you, respected lady?” he turned to Juliette. I think the system translate Korean politeness weird.

“I’m, uh… Julia, young adventurer. I’m also a… respected guide.”She tried to do the NPC thing too and wow, it was bad. Robotic in a totally non-NPC way.

“Look, respected guides,” he said politely. “I didn’t play a lot of video games, but I now understand that this place is… something like that. Everyone here speaks with simple, pre-made sentences. I believe they’re called... NPCs? I still don’t know where this is, or why I’m here. But… you two… you're not like the rest. I didn’t notice it the first time we spoke, but after some time here, now that I’ve gotten used to how NPCs behave… I’m sure of it. Are you… maybe…”

“You are correct,” I said, improvising. “We are not like the rest. We are here as part of a special quest. In time, we will guide you through your mission. For now, you must be patient.”

“...Okay,” he said, clearly skeptical. But then he bowed. “Thank you, respected guide.”

We bowed back. Not very NPC of us, but come on — it's hard not to bow back.

As we walked away, Juliette whispered, “I don’t think he’s buying it. Probably thinks we’re just assholes.”

“Yes, probably,” I agreed. “But damn — working at the bakery… he gets baked goods for free!”

I wondered if I could convince my employer to give me free underwear. Had to look into that.

That day we bought mostly spears and full leather armor.We already had the cuirass — the torso armor — from our first horrible forest trip. Now we were adding leather caps for the head, faulds for hips and lower torso, cuisses for thighs, and spaulders for the shoulders. We're going to skip the parts for the legs, hands, and arms — they were extremely uncomfortable and probably reduced our maneuverability with the spear, which wasn’t something to begin with.But yeah, leather armor is complicated stuff — and expensive, too.

Bob wasn’t with us today. We’d left him at the tribe during our last visit. Not the first time either—we were fine making forest trips without him now. There wasn’t a single dangerous creature we didn’t know how to detect, avoid, or deal with. We knew the paths. Even Juliette had the route down.

She started to like the forest, even said we were like Bear Grylls from Man vs. Wild.

And she was right. We were badasses.

We walked the mile outside town and entered the forest at the safest point, far from White Stripe territory.

We circled around two horned scaled hippo territories. We avoided the "suddenly snake" trees — little furry snakes that jumped from branches. We stayed far from the smoke roses, which released sleep gas when touched.

A wild armadillo care bear did approach us once, but we raised our spears and shouted, chasing him away easily.Like I said — badasses.

We were completely used to these trips by now.

But nothing could prepare us for what we saw when we arrived.

The first sign was the missing care bear sentries. Then I noticed broken twigs, ruined plants. Tons of footprints.And then... the smell.

"Something's wrong," I told Juliette, panic already in my voice.

We moved toward the cave, behind the usual brush cover, and then we saw it.

Death.

Dozens of bodies - armadillo care bears, scorpion surikatas, furry horned snakes… even cow-deers—were scattered around the entrance, inside the cave, everywhere. “No,” Juliette whispered. Then, louder, “No!”This was a battlefield. The stench of blood and death hit me like a hammer. I doubled over, coughing.The White Stripe tribe had launched a surprise attack — probably not many hours ago.We were too fucking late.

I stared at the bodies, trying to make sense of it.

"I think… most of the dead are White Stripes,” I said. “And it doesn’t look like any of the kids were in the fight.”

“Our tribe too,” Juliette whispered, voice cracking. “I knew these two care bears. And… oh no… this is Alice’s brother.”

It was him. A good scorpion surikata. Always playing with the kids.He was lying sprawled on the dirt. Huge slash across his belly, ripped open by a care bear claw.

Me and Juliette looked at each other. Realization hit at the exact same moment.

Bob.

We both started searching frantically. It wasn’t easy — so many bodies.

Two more surikatas. I saw them and my heart stopped. but they were enemy ones.Not him.

I don’t know how much time passed. Then I heard Juliette gasp — like the air had been ripped from her lungs.

Then came the sobbing, that choking, helpless kind.

I ran over.

There he was.

Bob.

Both legs torn. Two deep punctures in his chest. A horned furry snake bite. Its venom could kill a suricata size animal in minutes.

He was dead.

Juliette lifted one of his little arms, gently, like hoping he would move. But he didn’t.

We just sat there. Crying.

And we cried a lot.
