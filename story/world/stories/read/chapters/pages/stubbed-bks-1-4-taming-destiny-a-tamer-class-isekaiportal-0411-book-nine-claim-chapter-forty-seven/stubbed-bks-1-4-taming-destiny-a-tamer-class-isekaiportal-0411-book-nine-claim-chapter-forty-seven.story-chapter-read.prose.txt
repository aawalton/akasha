Alright, Kalanthia – ready?

More than, the nunda replies a moment before she emerges from my tattoo. As her paws press themselves into the sand, I feel an almost overwhelming sense of happiness and relief pulse through the Bond between us. And also a hint of grateful familiarity that I put down to her connecting properly with the earth once more.

Glad to be back on solid ground? I ask her a little teasingly, rubbing at her chin the way she likes.

You have no idea, she replies, rumbling contentedly while kneading her paws into the ground as if it’s cotton rather than sand.

I notice the Earth Mage who joined us from Layton’s ship edging away, his face pale. Whether it’s the obvious predator or the way she’s rumbling, I don’t know, but that he’s intimidated is clear. Some of the Classers from the other ships tense too; those from my ship are accustomed enough to my companions that they don’t react.

“Right, everyone,” I say, stepping forwards and speaking briskly. “Scouting groups, you’re dismissed unless you have any questions. Earth Mages, step closer to me. Combat Classers,” I give a thin smile. “Prepare for anything.”

Nods and a quiet chorus of ‘yes, my lord’ break the quiet of this deserted shoreline. Two groups of Classers break away, one focusing on investigating the beach and coastline; the other heading up the beach and towards the forest. At the same time, Bastet also appears on the sand, shortly followed by Ninja, Lathani, Sirocco, Artemis, Orion, Artio, Catch, and Pride.

Be careful, I urge my own little group of scouts. I hate sending them out there, but I’m self-aware enough to know that keeping them back would not only be unproductive, but actively detrimental to them. Anyway, everyone in the party volunteered. If anything, I had too many volunteers – I did draw the line at Tier ones joining this initial scouting party. Once we know better what we’re dealing with, they will hopefully be able to join groups going out – if they want to advance into Tier two, they need to hunt. But not now, when, for all I know, there could be Tier threes around every other tree trunk.

They respond with a mixture of appreciation for my concern and exasperation at what they perceive as lacking confidence in their abilities, and then move off, rapidly approaching the forest line.

That leaves the rest of us to use different Skills to investigate the area and to make a safe docking place for our ships.

“Do you have the materials for the ritual, Mage Ain?” I ask the Earth Mage from Valence’s ship. He’s the most experienced of the Earth Mages at level forty-one, and knows some tricks that will help us all work better together.

“I do, my lord,” Ain replies, stepping forward with a bow. “Will your lordship be the centre of the circle?”

“No,” I reply, seeing his immediate surprise. It deepens further when I continue. “Kalanthia will be.” I nod at the nunda in question.

The other mages around us blink, make soft sounds of surprise, and even, in one case, take a step back.

Ain is one of those who blinks.

“Ah…my lord…a beast?” He seems suddenly unsure. “I do not know if the ritual will work as intended with…Kalanthia taking the centremost position.”

“I don’t see why not,” I reply nonchalantly. “You indicated previously that the ritual depends on the strength of the director’s earth-magic, that the being at the centre of the circle is the one who determines just how far reaching and delicate the group as a whole can be. It cannot depend on the centre of the circle having an Earth Mage Class or I wouldn’t be able to take the position. And if it depended on having a certain Skill, then it would be even more restricted, because I’m sure we don’t all have the same Earth-Shaping Skills. Can you see any practical reason for why Kalanthia might not be able to direct the ritual?”

Mage Ain looks more than a little taken aback. He stutters and stammers a couple of responses, but nothing that’s actually relevant.

“Lord Markus, if I may?” another mage asks, a no-nonsense woman from Layton’s ship. I nod at her to go ahead. “Why, might I ask, do you wish your Bonded to be the centre of the ritual? Mage Ain is correct in that it is usually the most powerful mage in the group.”

Which begs the question of why they expected me to do it, I comment privately to myself. My Class isn’t a mage-Class – as Nicholas has demonstrated, not all of my family are as focused on magic as I am. I do have Master Earth-Shaping, but even so, I expect that most of those here are stronger in earth-magic than I am because it’s their focus whereas for me, it’s just one of my Skills. Perhaps they were just trying to stroke my ego. Or it was a test – to see if I would let my ego be stroked over taking the most advantageous course of action.

Still, it’s a valid question that Mage Lillian has posed.

“By sheer dint of her age, I suspect that Kalanthia is the most practiced earth-magic user among us,” I tell them bluntly. “In addition, I’ve seen how she wields her magic – with a finesse and power that is far beyond me. Considering my Earth-Shaping is at Master-level, that should give you a good idea of her capabilities.”

My revelation of exactly what my Skill level is distracts and shocks them – as I hoped. That’s not to say that I would be comparable to a Master Earth Mage, of course – quiet periods during the journey allowed me to study with some of the mages on the ship, including Tiria, and I’ve learned a great deal. More relevant to the situation at hand, I’ve learned far more about what separates a dedicated elemental mage from a dabbler like myself. To have multiple Skills, both passive and active, working to support and empower each other renders a general Skill like Earth-Shaping far more powerful, and specific Skills even more so.

Kalanthia might not have many abilities, but she makes excellent use of the ones she does have. And, as well as my confidence in her abilities, there’s another reason I want her to be the centre of the circle: it allows her to be in control. When I raised this with her, she was uneasy enough at the thought of being a participant even with me as the director. If anyone else took the centre position, I’m certain that she would refuse to participate. She just has too much distrust for humans overall. And, given the scale of what we’re going to attempt here, I’m certain we need her.

The mages confer for a moment, sometimes speaking quietly enough that I can’t hear them, though I see Kalanthia’s ears twitch and affront pass through the Bond a few times. From what I catch of the derisive commentary about what a beast is capable of, I don’t blame her.

They’re not coming up with any theoretically sound ideas for why you shouldn’t be the director, I point out to the nunda whose rumble has become more threatening than content. Rather than trying to defeat them with words, let’s show them that they’re utterly mistaken.

I wouldn’t use words to teach them some respect, Kalanathia replies with a hint of a snarl.

We need them, I remind her. Unless you want to try doing this all by yourself? She grumbles, her ears pinning themselves back for a long moment before they relax.

Finally, the mages step out of their little huddle.

“We have debated and…cannot find any reason for why the ritual should not work with a beast as the director,” Ain admits reluctantly. I know that the conversation held a lot more than just that, but I diplomatically don’t point that out.

“Then let’s get started,” I say instead.

Mage Ain nods sharply and then starts preparing the ritual area. Mage Lillian moves to help him along with two other Earth Mages. Within a few minutes, they’ve drawn the ritual circle in the sand, coated it with an alchemical concoction that Valence’s Alchemist created, and laid out several rune stones at important places.

Unlike some rituals, like the one that brought me to this world in the first place, there are no Cores. That’s for a very important reason – we are the batteries. Once the mages give us the nod, we remove our shoes and take our places, equidistant around the outer circle.

As I step into my position, I feel a slight buzz go through my body. It intensifies as mage after mage joins the circle: eight in total. And then Kalanthia steps into the middle, and that buzz turns into a current of electricity that energises but doesn’t hurt.

“Since our director is unable to speak–”

I can speak, Kalanthia corrects. The way Ain’s voice falters briefly and several others flinch make it clear that she projected it to everyone.

“...Then, since our director is unable to speak aloud…” he pauses for a moment as if to give Kalanthia the chance to correct him again. She doesn’t. “I will invoke the incantation.”

He pauses for another moment. No one objects.

What follows is a brief request for a blessing from Lady Earth, Lord Explorer, and Lady Luck. I wonder wryly for a moment whether they should not be praying instead for Chaos to turn their eyes elsewhere for the next couple of hours – from what the Soothsayer said, that might be more effective since I’m part of the group.

Ain then continues by using some of that odd language that I cannot understand but that makes my ears throb and the magic tingle in my channels.

It’s not long after that when, like during the ritual with Nicholas when I was adopted into the Titanbend family, I feel my magic actually being drawn from me without my permission. The difference is that this is far less foolproof – this is a mage ritual for mages, not a layman’s ritual for the ungifted. The ritual pulls and tugs at my magic, but I have to make sure that it only takes the right kind.

If I allow it my normal mana, unaspected – or, possibly, slightly fire-aspected by nature – it could cause the ritual to fail. Probably with explosive consequences. Instead, I have to make sure it takes only earth-magic, and concentrate on giving it to Kalanthia to use for our shared endeavour.

When I do so, I find myself sinking into the kind of trance that only happens when I’m really extending myself; all I can see are the lines of magic leading from me and everyone else to Kalanthia.

For what feels like an extended period of time, she gathers the magic within herself. I feel a hint of surprise from my fellow ‘batteries’ – and can’t help but feel a hint of smugness myself. It’s not something that featured in the debate about whether to allow Kalanthia this position or not, but her massive mana pool – and, I suspect, her Tier three physiology – allows her to hold far more mana than anyone else here would be capable of.

Then, finally, when she’s glowing a luminous chestnut almost visible to normal eyes, she acts.

A network of magic spreads through the earth beneath our feet. It’s exceedingly fine and delicate, yet detailed – like a spider’s web that is invisible until it reflects the sunlight, but sends every twitch and vibration to the spider in the centre. The surprise and consternation of several of those around at her skill increases my sense of smugness.

Yet, even with her skill and power, I sense the resistance. This is a new continent, and the earth does not know us. It almost questions our presence here, uncertain whether we belong.

Though, oddly, the sense of question vanishes with a speed I wouldn’t have expected from slow-to-change earth. When it has gone, the web spreads far more quickly and fluidly.

It reaches for an extended distance. From where we stand, it moves out into the sea beyond where our ships are, and just as far in every other direction. I pay careful attention to what we feel – connecting it to what our scouts have reported. I particularly note the position of the river that cuts through to the sea, the streams that feed it keeping the land damp but not boggy in most places. The land is covered in gigantic elder trees with thick roots that dig deep into the underground water supply. And on the edge of our awareness, in the area where the trees are thickest a tantalising hint of power piques my interest – and a strong sense of danger warns me away simultaneously.

Kalanthia sends me a sense of a question, the other mages I’m connected with seconding it. Here? they seem to ask.

I agree. Here.

And then the awareness collapses again, our focus pulled back to the centre – and to the space immediately in front of us.

Slowly, the land starts to shudder, yet I can feel that the shakes don’t shift even a grain of the sand within our circle. Many hands working together, all directed by Kalanthia’s firm will, we lift some sections of the seabed, lowering others. I am vaguely aware of the dissatisfaction of various creatures, and shouts throb vaguely through my ears as if I’m underwater myself.

It’s cooperation on a large scale. Normally mages can only cooperate like this if they are master and apprentice – and even then it’s rare to be so aligned that one can use the magic of the other. But that is what this ritual is for – combining the magic of many mages and yoking it to the will of one. Creating miracles.

And, when we are finally done, feeling more than a little fatigued – but not quite exhausted – I don’t even need to open my eyes to know what we’ve achieved: I can feel it.

No longer just a semi-protected bay, encircling arms of rock now offer a protective harbour to our ships. The space within the harbour has been smoothed and lowered to create a safe draught for their keels. Piers stretch out in well-spaced, even rows, ready for the ships to come to berth. We’ve even lifted columns of rock that will offer an attachment point to hold the ships fast. They’ll still need their fenders – rock is rock, after all – but otherwise, no one could ask for a better mooring point. And, working together, it only took us a couple of hours to make.

Even without opening my eyes, I have the confidence to touch the Alliance Bonds I have with the other lords.

It’s ready. Bring them in.
