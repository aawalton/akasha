We saw it in the distance, at first. Slowly, as the minutes passed and the Chamels drove us closer and closer to the endpoint of the Long Road, a black speck in the distance grew closer and closer.

By this point, everyone I had brought north with me, all five hundred warriors of the new expedition, had armed and armored themselves. It felt like more than half of them were up on the decks, while the rest were waiting ready below, but that wasn’t possible.

Still, the sight of those neat rows of soldiers standing at the ready reassured me. I hadn’t known just what kind of reception was waiting for us in the ancient Hold of Karag Varla, but I’d made the assumption that at the very least, the Hold Dwarves would respect strength.

And we were certainly coming in it.

But as the end of the tunnel grew near, and the shape looming in the distance began to come into focus, my unease started to grow.

Something was wrong. Something was very, very wrong.

And as we finally reached our destination, after days of hard driving, my suspicion only grew. As the Final Line slowly crawled to a halt, I cast a gaze at what lay before us…and felt a pit start to grow in my stomach.

In normal times, I was sure I would have been awed by the sight before my ship, for the gates of Karag Varla loomed large. The space before the gates themselves was not only taller than the already monumentally massive height of the Long Road itself, but it was wider too. The result was a kind of cavernous plaza where I was not only sure that the titanic form of Rhazal himself could have stood at full height, but he would have had room to stretch as well. I…genuinely hadn’t realized we were that far below the surface, because the gates themselves?

They were dominated by two statues of a size to dwarf most castles I had ever witnessed.

Two colossal Dwarves stood on either side of the gates, intricately carved from what looked to be a form of black granite, shot through with white inclusions. Stately, with both impressive beards and prominent, glaring frowns peaking through them, they cut stern visages, with every fold upon their ancient cheeks painstakingly chiseled with lifelike precision. For the Dwarf to the left of the gates, he wore stately, intricately embroidered robes, while in his left hand he bore a scepter with a diamond-shaped stone upon the apex, and upon his brow there appeared to be both a crown and a circlet, one above the other. Perhaps an ancient King of some kind?

But for the Dwarf to the right of the gates, this one was unmistakably a warrior. Instead of robes, he was carved to bear layered plate mail of thickness and depth I’m not sure a human could even move in it, much less fight. The result was the figure of a warrior that could have taken blows to fell mountainsides, and do so without flinching. To top it all off, in his left hand, he held a long-handled, double-bladed battle-axe with both edges trailing off into long, bearded edges, the haft of it reaching down to the floor of the Long Road.

Both of them had their free hands held outstretched with the palm facing flat, in an unmistakable message of refusal. The King with his left hand, and the Warrior with his right.

In normal times, the sight of these two grand statues would have filled me with awe. But it was what lay between them that filled me with trepidation.

The gates of Karag Varla were vast, as large as the great Dwarven monuments to either side of them, carved from the same granite…and open. They were open and unguarded before us, a long tunnel on the other side of them. The city of the Hold itself was somewhere on the other side of that tunnel, and yet there wasn’t a soul around.

The silence in the air before the titanic gates of Karag Varla was oppressive in its dread. This was not what any of us had expected.

Nobody? Not a single soldier or warrior or even a diplomat was there to watch the ancient gates that granted entrance to the equally historic Hold of the Dwarves? That…

“…ain’t right,” Bleddyn murmured to my left, his broad palms clenching on the railing before us. “This ain’t right, Nate. I got a bad feeling about this.”

“As you should,” Olag said somberly, approaching the empty space to my right. “Karag Varla is not known to be quite as closed off as some of the other Holds are, but they do have a reputation. The Gates of Anar Sur are meant to be guarded by an entire legion of warriors, ready to lay down their lives at the slightest incursion. They have never been breached, to the best of my knowledge, in all the years that they have stood guard over Karag Varla. Not during the War in Heaven, and not during the Great Schism, were they ever truly surmounted. The only way for them to be entirely deserted like this…”

“Is if these legions were badly needed elsewhere,” I finished with a whisper.

Olag looked away with a frown. “Indeed.”

“Then we need to find out what has happened,” I heard Sylvia say from behind. All three of us turned to see the Sculpted woman standing with her arms crossed, gazing off into the tunnel beyond the gates with a frown on her silvered features. Behind her, here at the nearly superfluous wheel of the Final Line, stood the rest of the Captainry of the New Expedition. “We cannot stay here. We have a job to do.”

I briefly bowed my head and shook it. “She’s right. This isn’t the time to be timid, but we can’t risk the Ward Stone. I…didn’t expect this, but we’ll just have to expect the worst and adapt. We make camp here, fortify the position, and then investigate what’s going on. If this is all a big misunderstanding, and the Varlayans complain about it, then we’ll deal with that when it happens. I’m going to need a volunteer to stay behind and maintain our fallback position. Who’s up for it?”

Under my gaze, the gathered leadership of the new expedition exchanged glances. Some of them I just couldn’t risk going into a potentially hostile situation without their strength, like Bleddyn. Some of them I knew I couldn’t risk the danger of not going with them, just because of their abilities, like Renauld and his Healing. Others, still, I knew would flat out refuse not venturing into the depths of Karag Varla with me, like Sylvia and Olag. Sylvia, because of how much she cared for me, and Olag, because the Dwarf was actually grinding his teeth in impatience from how much he seemed to hunger to go. That left precious few people I would trust to maintain our rear lines, and thankfully, someone did volunteer.

Ragnvald, the war Chief of the Elves, stepped forward from the crowd and nodded at me. “I will stay and command our defense, Hart. I must anyway, to protect the Chamels. They are…precious to us.”

Internally, I breathed a sigh of relief. I barely knew Ragnvald, and had never seen him fight, but from what I’d heard, he was supposed to be competent enough. Somewhere in the low three hundreds, I thought, just from the feeling he gave off to my passive senses.

He would do.

I returned the nod. “Alright. I’ll be leaving…lets say a hundred and fifty of our men here with you, to fortify this position and act as our reserve. Set up some barricades while we’re gone, because I’m taking everyone else with me. We’ll divide our forces into seven squads, with fifty men each. Sylvia, Olag, Maria, Bella, Bleddyn, and myself will command six of those, while the seventh Captain will be…” I trailed off, letting my gaze fall on my first pick.

Renauld gave me a look. The Gnoll was not a fan of command and had barely acquiesced to represent the Gnolls in the first place. I think he might actually maul me if I tried to make him a platoon commander.

Smoothly, I skipped over him like I hadn’t considered it for a moment…and let it fall on my second pick.

Hmm. I may not be the man’s biggest fan right now, but he did have genuine military command experience. For all of his poor judgment skills, that counted for something.

Plus, I could just tell that he was desperate to prove himself after his betrayal. And I was not one to throw away a tool if it wanted to do the work.

I nodded firmly. “Gustave. Will you lead the seventh platoon?”

A ripple of surprise went through the watching, gathered crowd, and all eyes turned to the disheveled, frozen Herztalian officer lingering near the edges. I could see a bead of sweat grow on his forehead at the regard, and he almost involuntarily swallowed before he spoke the first words he’d said to me in weeks, in a distinctly hoarse voice. “Are you…sure, Marshal?”

“I wouldn’t have offered if I wasn’t,” I said evenly. “Now, will you do it or not?”

The younger man closed his eyes painfully for a moment before opening them once more. In their depths, I could see a resolve and determination that had been missing in the officer for some time now. He actually clicked his heels together and offered me a full arm bar salute. “I would be honored, Marshal! I swear on my life I will not disappoint you!”

I hummed neutrally in response. “See that you don’t.” I dismissed him in favor of regarding the rest of the gathered crowd, both the officers and the soldiers. “You all have your orders. Everyone here is already armed and armored, and everyone knows their assignments. Expeditionary forces, those of you with bravery in your hearts, gather around the seven Captains before the ship. Those with determination, harken to Chief Ragnvald. We move out in thirty. Get going!”

The deck of the Final Line exploded into movement as the members of the new expedition scrambled to fulfill their orders. People began to pour out of the lower decks and onto the stone of the Long Road below. Both to gather into their platoons, and to begin the fortification work. In moments, every member of the expedition was finding themselves suddenly very busy. I was no different.

There were a few things I still had to do.

I waded through the crowd until I reached the tall, thin form of the watching Rafael. Like always, the Lich was lingering at the edges, observing matters on with a slightly disinterested air. The old bag of bones actually sighed at my approach. “Don’t tell me, you want me to accompany you.”

Rafael actually cocked his head in surprise when I shook my own in the negative. “No, actually. I want to keep you in reserve. Stay here. Assist with the defense if it becomes necessary, but I don’t want you going into the city just yet. You’re the strongest person here, and I want to keep you in reserve.”

The Lich hummed, an odd sound for someone with no functioning lungs. “Just in case, I see.”

“Just in case.”

“Very well,” Rafael nodded slowly. “That suits me just fine. I shall…resume my investigation of your curious little Ward Stone in the meanwhile.”

I returned his nod and walked away. The mention of the Ward Stone was prescient, because that’s where I was going next.

I found my next odd pick lingering near the mobile ward stone of the Astray, in the below-decks room built specifically for its use. She had taken it upon herself to become the stone’s guardian and keeper, but I had a better task for her.

Meia Itzelan cocked her tusked, Half-Orcen head at me in surprise. “Aren’t you busy, Hart? I thought you had a Hold to investigate.”

I nodded at her. “I do, and I want you with me. I have someone else who’ll keep an eye on the stone.”

A slow blink of surprise was the only reaction from the Sorceress. “Is that so? Wouldn’t it be a better idea for me to remain behind, perhaps set up some Wards as part of the defense?”

I snorted. “Good joke. You and I both know there’s nothing more that you could add here that would be helpful. Besides, I want us both on hand in case we run into any troubles with wards while inside.”

“Well…” Meia drawled with a smirk. “It’s a good thing you haven’t forgotten. I was wondering if you still knew your Abjuration, after all this battle. Speaking of fighting…I suppose a little war would do me good. I’ve felt a bit cooped up, recently. Very well, Hart. I’ll accompany you.”

Two down, and the last two of my personal picks…

Well, they fell into step with me as Meia and I set foot on the deck. I wasn’t terribly surprised.

At this point, it felt natural for both Renauld and Walter to fight at my side. We didn’t even need to exchange words.

We all knew our places, and as we ventured out onto the stone of the Long Road, I firmed my grip on my staff.

Whatever was going on inside Karag Varla, I made a promise to myself.

This wouldn’t be a repeat of Rhoscara. I wouldn’t let us lose another city.

Not on my goddamned life.
