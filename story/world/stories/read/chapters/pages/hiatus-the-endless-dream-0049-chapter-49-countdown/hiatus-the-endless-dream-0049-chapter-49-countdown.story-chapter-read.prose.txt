Moonlight

Krevith lived. He hadn't even been unconscious when Dorian shoved the Oathbound Parasite in his skull. The bullet had slammed into the part of his brain responsible for motor functions and speech—liquefying its contents. He then watched, powerless to stop anything, as Dorian proceeded to mentally break down, and in a desperate, misguided attempt to stay with the group of Survivors, claimed they needed him. Not directly, not in such words, but Krevith understood the meaning and was helpless to correct Dorian or interpret for Maerin.

The consequences of that were still rippling, and had started when Maerin executed Nico. It was not a pleasant scene. Krevith would not have stopped it if he could. Shortened, perhaps.

Maerin did not train in weapons of violent joy and so had to take careful aim when she shot a bolt through Nico's neck, intentionally missing the spine to pin him further to the ship wall. She then repeatedly slammed her heel into the bolt. It tore at his throat, his crying and coughing doing just as much damage as the widening shaft. She missed a few times, shoving and twisting his head. It gave her the idea to kick his head directly, but only after the feathered end was through layers of broken teeth.

Krevith wasn't sure when the man died, just that he was alive when Maerin started and dead when she stopped. Alder might have been next if it weren't for the ship's shaking. A temporary truce, and just like that, Jeren was back, and Orlan had left with Dorian. It was a bitter thing to see someone make so many unfortunate decisions.

Now, there he was, watching his body make another such unfortunate decision. Alder, Paltra, and Jeren had gone into separate tunnels. Time had passed; how much, he wasn't sure. Himself, Maerin, and Yesemire hunkered in the cavern, lit torches strewn about. The forest spirit, now Great, failed to push its way in with the sheer volume of torches. He worried about oxygen.

The spirit seemed distracted, only making cursory attempts at breaking through the swaths of light. Krevith wished to turn his head and look out unto the tunnels where the others had gone, but couldn't.

The Fiend heard something, and Krevith didn't know what, but it began moving. It walked up to Maerin, pointed down the tunnel Paltra was in, and grabbed a torch, motioning for Maerin to do the same. She did so, and with no other option, Yesemire joined.

Faster than they should have, the group ran through the tunnels. The Forest Spirit seemed surprised by their decision. It sent probing attacks, but they were a half-hearted thing—its attention likely focused elsewhere. The Fiend still acted. Krevith could feel his stomach roil. His arm jerked to his mouth, which opened wide. The man's jaw cracked and unhinged, fingertips elongating and throat expanding as the Fiend snaked its host's hand down to his stomach. It grabbed onto something, cold and squirming. It pulled.

Unlike his hand, this cold thing did not change size. The Fiend tore at his insides and broke Krevith's teeth to pull it from his mouth. Krevith caught the barest glimpse of the disgusting creature before it was covered in Void. Never before had he felt revulsion so strong. He couldn't even throw up.

It turned out to be a good decision, as after another few minutes the attacks tripled in intensity, but more often than not were teleported straight through the Void and dispersed into the light.

Yesemire dragged behind at one point, and the Void teleported the dark smoke right in front of her. It surged forward, grabbing her face before Maerin swung the torch around, the shadows dispersing.

Krevith saw the mist flow down Yesemire's nostrils. He mentally screamed, willed his body to move and his mouth to open, to even so much as twitch a finger to warn them. Maybe nothing could be done, but maybe there was; it was worth trying at least, but no matter how much he tried, his body wouldn't listen. The Parasite just stared. Knowing and acknowledging what happened, but not caring about its outcome.

He resigned himself to another unfortunate decision. They continued running through the cave, the Parasite sticking much closer to Maerin with his torch now. Soon they reached Paltra, who had set up a field of torches much like they had.

"What are you doing here?" she asked, worried to see everyone.

Maerin gestured weakly to Krevith, "The Parasite decided to. I don't know why."

The Huntress glanced between the different tunnels before coming to a decision. "We should take the warning for what it is. We'll treat it as if it were a yell. Let's go," and the four of them began making their way through the caverns. They were a winding maze, with many of them looping into one another.

They had to stay on guard at all times. As even a single slip-up could allow the black mist to lash out with violence. More than once, they had to backtrack as the intense red of the moon lit up deep portions of the cavern, causing wavy distortions in the air and shifting sections of stone to their sight.

Twenty minutes of winding tunnels occurred before they were met with another torch source. They held their breath, and from around a bend appeared a woman none of them had ever met. She was covered in blood that went from red to black in the flickering orange lights. She smiled as she saw the group, "Hey Dorian. Have to go. Call you right back."

The unknown woman pocketed the smooth stone. "You were really close you know. Only ten minutes."

They all stared at the Visitor, unsure what she was talking about or the context for anything, but knowing instantly that she was dangerous and not an ally. She gave off the same feeling Dorian had, except she doubled down on that viciousness instead of redirecting or hiding it. Frightening, as if they had wandered into the arms of a waiting creature in the caves.

The strange woman considered them for a moment before biting her thumb and drawing blood. "I'm good at fighting, but one on four is unfair. She started to step behind the bend of the cave, but a thunderous gunshot rang out. Blood spurted from the woman's shoulder as she jerked back against the wall. Paltra clicked her tongue, having missed the head.

Recovering from no doubt ringing ears, Maerin raised her crossbow and fired. The shot went wide.

The Parasite raised its torch and charged forward, having been told what would happen if the Stalker was able to turn itself to stone, but it didn't do so before grabbing Yesemire and pushing her into the waiting arms of the dark mist.

Krevith didn't see the result, but he heard it. It sounded like the popping of a pressurized tin. It was yet another stain of tar upon his soul, for his hands to have been the ones to push her to her death.

His body barreled around the corner, and he was immediately smacked across the head with a rock. His vision went, vertigo set in, but his body reacted as if nothing had happened. The Parasite didn't care about the hit, swinging Krevith's fist wildly and catching the woman in the chest.

She dispersed the impact, going along with the wild slam and grabbing the arm. She slid under and grabbed for Krevith's torch. Krevith's grip on the light was firm, forcing the woman to change tacks, scoop up her own torch, and run for Maerin and Paltra. Neither of them had reloaded, too shocked by the gore of Yesemire and the sudden breakdown of events. Paltra got the worst of it, having been closer to the woman in the three-piece suit.

The Stalker bounded forward. She was almost upon them when the world shook. Debris and dirt cascaded from the ceiling, followed by stalactites. Rocks fell in waves as fissures formed, and the sound of cave-ins echoed from far off. Everyone was knocked off their feet.

Paltra, with years of discipline, held on to her weapon. She was the first to react. She fired, the bullet sailed, and went straight through the Stalker's spine.

With a twitch, the woman stopped moving, only for a moment. A second later, flesh grew, knit itself back together, pushing the bullet out, repairing her shoulder and the nick she had given herself earlier on the thumb. It was like she had never been hurt in the first place. She rolled to the side and sprinted away from everyone.

Krevith watched hopelessly, body speeding full force after the woman. He could see the stone assimilating her neck and shoulders. She slowed, but it wasn't enough. She had abandoned her torch, and Krevith held onto the small hope that the dark mist would take her. That hope was quickly dashed as he noticed everything in the cavern was of mundane darkness. The mist had left.

He couldn't even close his eyes and peacefully accept the Rivers' embrace. He watched as, midstride, the Stalker became a statue. It would only be a moment before God used his blessing of [Stones Throw Away], to transport the moon's burning crimson light and kill them all.

Except, nothing came. Nothing at all.

The Communication Stone crackled to life, while the woman had turned to stone, her clothes had not.

Dorian's voice came through, "Whoever is there listen to me very closely. You need to find water, deep water and you need to find it now. Jump into it, and don't come up until the Instance is over. Even if you're drowning. Your brain won't die for at least five minutes after you run out of oxygen. GO! NOW!"

None of the three survivors needed to be told again. While there was no trust between them now, they understood two things: Dorian wanted to win, and the Parasite was supposed to protect them, so when Krevith's body began jogging through the cave, Maerin and Paltra followed. A thought did briefly cross their minds. Dorian had commanded the Parasite to protect them and one other person of its choice. So, where was the fourth Survivor it was supposed to protect?

Their jog turned into a run, into a sprint, and only briefly slowed when they reached a fork or split. The Parasite would reach forward, traveling through Krevith's arms before clapping as loud as they could. Whatever it was doing must have worked, as the Parasite never picked a path that led to a cave-in. Three minutes passed when they heard water. Another minute passed when the entire underground went freezing cold in an instant. It felt like the pressure of the world beat down on them. Their life played on repeat, skipping to the most twisted moments that could be pulled from. It beat them with the memories as if they were a blunt instrument.

Physically, their joints became sluggish, their teeth chattered against each other, and the world screamed to them of their mistakes.

Krevith only caught glimpses of his surroundings now, neither in control of his mind nor his body. Maerin brought a crossbow bolt to her eye, and Paltra had the rifle barrel in her mouth. She pulled the trigger. She hadn't reloaded after her last shot. Krevith would have bitten his tongue if he could, but his body dragged them along. Then they were in the water. It was so sudden, he didn't notice they arrived until the frigid cold subsided. Krevith's body held Paltra and Maerin in the water as they thrashed. His hands covered their mouths, but he could tell they were swallowing water.

He didn't allow either of them to rise to the surface. They kicked and slammed and bit, but his hands never left them. Their movements grew weak until they became nothing. Their eyes still twitched and moved, so they weren't dead.

Krevith just kept repeating the words in his head over and over again. 'Their brains wouldn't die for at least five minutes."

Dorian

I was sinking into the never-ending lake, as I had been for almost two hours. I was wreathed in Void, which somehow allowed me to breathe despite the lack of air. Heavy stones were tied to me, dragging me deeper than I could've swam without getting crushed by pressure.

"Hey Dorian. Have to go. Call you right back."

That was my cue. In my shell of Void, I untied the stones. Since Jeren had died, I needed a way to tell when to put everything into motion; it just took talking to that madwoman for half an hour. She had found people, enough of them to warrant a pause in the conversation. It wasn't last-minute, but it would have to do.

I peered as far as I could in the pitch black water and activated [Open the Path]. I set one end under the water, 7.5m by 7.5m. The other I set in the sky, facing directly at the moon 1mm by 1mm. When Ruby had stepped through the portal, it had been instantaneous. The second they touched the barrier, they were through. The water should be the same.

I was no expert, but I knew that 56 square meters being instantaneously compressed down to 1 millimeter squared would cause a fuck ton of damage.

The portal appeared. Red light pouring through. It started as a dribble, but quickly turned into a whirlpool as water was dragged through.

I was no exception. My ball of nothing touched the portal, and I could feel myself elongate, the Void stretching with me as I was crushed down to a string of flesh and reformed as a person on the other side, hurtling through the air at incomprehensible speeds. The Void protected me from the immense G-Force as everything around me disappeared. I expected to impact the moon directly and fall back to the ground with its debris.

I continued to sail through the air. I caught the barest glimpse of a cylindrical tower with mountains on every side before I was launched into nothingness.

I didn't die.

I didn't hit a barrier.

Just sailed through empty space, the Instance grounds no longer in sight.

I let myself enjoy a small scream of triumph before reality slammed into me. This wasn't over. Not yet. The forest spirit would steal the power of the moon. I just hoped that the mist couldn't pierce water.

I scrambled for the Communication Stone and turned it on. I yelled over it my directions.

I got no response. I kept repeating them over and over again until the words made me sick.

There was nothing left to do but watch the countdown. Second by second. Minute by minute.
