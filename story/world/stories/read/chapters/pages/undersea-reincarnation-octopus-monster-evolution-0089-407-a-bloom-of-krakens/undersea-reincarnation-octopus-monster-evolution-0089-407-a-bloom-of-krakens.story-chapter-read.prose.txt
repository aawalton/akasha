It turned out the old man hadn’t been seeing things, which, at the end of the day, didn’t really come as a surprise to Hammond. His own senses were good, but the man he was shadowing had spent more years than Hammond had been alive in A-rank. Omar also had at least twenty or thirty levels on him, which weren’t as easy to come by in this rank, so it went without saying that the veteran would have a sharper eye. On the other hand, the white light they’d both seen as they made their way toward the source wasn’t exactly what it had initially seemed.

“This is poison,” Omar said, wrinkling his nose away from the smoke still rising from the smoldering soil. One of the rays had burned through a few branches and a tree trunk before it reached the spot they’d just stopped to examine. Both the wood and the soil around it were discolored, and the acrid, but somewhat sweet smell confirmed it.

“Think it’s safe for us to keep approaching?” Hammond asked, glancing up toward the swooping birds in the distance. Being this far from the city, getting poisoned could be dangerous. Hammond only had the basic resistances. He could shrug off common toxins easily enough, but if this was an A-rank using A-rank venoms, that’d be different.

Omar seemed to think for a bit, then he gestured forward, and the two broke out in a run once more. “[Probably safe. It doesn’t seem too potent,]” Omar sent telepathically while Hammond did his utmost to keep up, a bit relieved. Now that a big fight might be taking place, the easy-going mood of his senior evaporated and he had to admit, so did most of Hammond’s annoyance. This could be an opportunity, and Omar was thinking the same as he continued speaking. “[We just want eyes on the fight to be sure of what we’re dealing with. I can see the eagles and knowing them, they should come out ahead. Considering they rarely go after something stronger than them. But the fight’s taking a while. I’m interested to see what it is that’s giving them such a hard time and maybe, if we’re lucky, we’ll clean up whatever’s left.]”

Hammond didn’t answer and just focused on not walking through another tree again. Stealth was important, in the Reach. It was likely that they weren’t the only ones approaching the fight. The smart monsters didn’t fight fair, and neither did the humans. Everyone recognized this for the opportunity that it was, though he wondered, between himself and Omar, who would get to down whatever was left? The rule said Omar would be the one to decide, but if there were two monsters still alive by the time they got there…

That made Hammond blink, and he almost tore another root by mistake.

“[We don’t have anyone patrolling over there, right?]”

It’d suck if they had to share with another patrol. But if this happened to be a patrol in trouble, then Hammond wouldn’t mind being owed a favor.

“[Nope. We should be the furthest ones patrolling west. If we find people, they wouldn’t be one of ours.]”

Hammond’s brows lifted, and as he processed the response, the first smile of the week pulled on his lips. He’d been having doubts about joining the scouts and frankly, Hammond had been thinking of requesting a reassignment. And he still might, considering he’d gone a full week without much of anything to hunt. But he wouldn’t mind ending the experience on a good note.

Still. He’d prefer to deal with monsters. They were less complicated than people. And as much as he wanted a good fight, he’d rather not be the cause of a war between settlements.

“[One of the birds is down, I think,]” Omar said, before he ran faster. “[Let’s hurry.]”

Hammond was forced to use his Dash ability over and over to keep up, which wasn’t amazing for his mana if a fight were to break out. But he couldn’t allow himself to fall behind. Thankfully they soon made it to the site, but as Hammond took in the scene and tried to make sense of what was happening, he found himself blinking a couple of times. Especially considering they weren’t the first that had gotten here.

On the other side of the wrecked, criss-crossed valley full of craters and torn vegetation, a small sounder of boars were huffing and posturing, led by a scarred, muscled level 9 [Tuskbreaker of the Ravaged Field (A)]. The monster was as tall as a small building, and maybe three buildings long. But none of its entourage dared approach the fallen two eagles, which now were stacked on top of each other. And Hammond could tell why. Because even he could feel the creepy aura that permeated the scene. Not to mention, the literal swarm of floating kraken staring down at him and Omar, which somehow made the aura feel worse, even if none of it was directed at them.

It was like in every shadow, under every leaf, behind every tree-trun was an eye watching him. It felt that if he were to turn, he’d find himself face to face with something with too many eyes staring into his soul.

What had they walked into?

The clearing was… crowded. To say the least. There was a swarm of floating, human-sized krakens, like a bloom of jellyfish. Fifty or so of them. Two kinds, mostly floating around two humans that levitated a couple dozen feet above the ground, and in a quick succession, Hammond shot a few Identifies to know what he was dealing with. And just so he didn’t poke the beehive, as it were, he made sure to cast them through Silent Casting.

[Arcane Kraken (B) - Lvl 92]

[Lifeblood Kraken (B) - Lvl 92]

[Human (B) - Lvl 84]

[Human (B) - Lvl 95]

With every line, Hammond’s blood ran colder. If he had been alone, the krakens by themselves would have been a death sentence to him. He’d win any 1-on-1 but fifty of them? He wouldn’t make it. The weirdest part though was the humans. Most likely, it was the man, and not the child who was controlling the monsters. Not to mention, now that Hammond was looking at him, he was pretty sure the aura was his. It felt as if the man’s gaze was coming from every kraken. Hammond felt… watched. Scrutinized.

Was he being affected by an intimidation skill? Also, was that a teenager about to reach A-rank?

Again, what had they walked into?

Glancing to the side, he met Omar’s gaze, and Hammond was surprised to see the white of his eyes. The man was stone-faced, but before Hammond could ask or say anything, Omar shook his head.

Hammond forgot about the clearing and the humans for a moment. In all of the week he’d spent with Omar, he’d never seen him this intense.

“I got some people to talk to. So off you go,” the voice rang in the clearing, loud and clear, and Hammond looked up. The man was wearing shorts and white shirt, while the kid was too busy floating around and tumbling head over heels in the air. The massive boar heard and understood just fine, but it still chuffed and lowered his head, as if to charge.

“I said, go.”

Hammond nearly jumped out of his own skin as all of the floating krakens suddenly spoke in unison. He could feel the hairs on the back of his neck stand up, and he almost turned away and ran before he caught himself. Why was he so anxious around two B-rank humans?

As soon as he asked himself the question, he knew the answer. Because the two B-ranks just killed two A-ranks, and because the pressure he was feeling right this moment rivaled the aura of the strongest A-ranks he’d encountered. Humans or otherwise.

The boar stood still for a moment, then it took a step back. Then another. Then it turned and left. From the left, something else moved, and Hammond realized he hadn’t even known what it was. He just saw scales, but whatever it was, it wanted none of what was happening.

Finally, the two humans turned their attention toward him and Omar, and so did all the eyes of the swarm of krakens that floated up there. None of the hostile aura was pointed down at them, but the human with the eerie kraken eyes didn’t even need to bother, with how many of the damn thing were staring down at him.

Fifty independent, high B-rank krakens would have been a nightmare to fight. Fifty coordinated ones? There was no winning that, and considering the gouges and torn flesh of the two eagles that lay dead at their feet, it was obvious why.

That thought was like a slap to Hammond, and he swallowed, mouth suddenly dry.

What hadn’t Omar stopped them before they got here? This whole situation felt… dangerous.

Then, before he could do or say much, one by one, the krakens faded away into nothingness, disappearing. Hammond considered they’d gone invisible, but shooting a couple of dozen identifies all around netted him nothing.

Summons, then, he thought. But the fact that the two humans willingly dismissed them meant they could likely be brought back at a moment’s notice.

“Hey there, folks,” the man said, smiling. “I’m Henry. This is Maurice. To what do we owe the pleasure?”
