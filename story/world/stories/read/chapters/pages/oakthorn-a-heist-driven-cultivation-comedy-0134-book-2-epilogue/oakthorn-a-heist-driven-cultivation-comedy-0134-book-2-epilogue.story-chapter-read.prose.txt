Decay stood motionless on the precipice of her sanctum, where moldering vegetation formed a grotesque carpet beneath her bare feet. The scent of sweet putrefaction—her perfume, her essence, her joy—filled her nostrils as she surveyed the sprawling view of her domain. Twisted trees stretched arthritic limbs toward a sky perpetually choked with miasma, while fungi of impossible colors blossomed across the landscape like ghastly stars.

Off in the distance, Baloran was curled up around the gnarled roots of an oak tree coated in thorns. She’d yet to make the convergence submit to her, but she knew she was close to a breakthrough. The corrupted dragon seemed to sense her apathetic gaze, and lifted a scarred eyelid to peer at her.

Hatred—intoxicating hatred—filled the Cloudrift king’s gaze. Oh, how she bathed in his vitriol.

She sensed her master’s presence before there was any physical manifestation. Reality itself seemed to recoil, creating a pocket of near-absolute absence into which Death stepped. Not entered—stepped, as though the distance between realms was merely a formality for beings of his caliber.

Decay did not turn. She couldn’t. Her limbs had betrayed her, as they always did in his presence.

“It is done, then?” Death’s voice wasn’t cold to her ears. Cold implied temperature. His words simply were, like the space between heartbeats or the moment consciousness leaves a dying body.

“Almost, my lord.” Decay kept her eyes fixed on her altar. “The final preparations—”

“Look at me.”

She turned because she had no choice, her body responding to his command before her mind could process it. Death stood motionless, a perfect absence against the fetid backdrop of her domain. Beneath his hood lay carved into her nightmares, for indeed her dreams had been plagued with them since her first meeting with the god before her.

“Your failure to anticipate my arrival suggests a preoccupation,” Death observed. “Perhaps you’ve reconsidered our arrangement?”

“Never,” Decay whispered, the word bitter on her tongue. “The nodes are placed exactly as you instructed. All sixteen catalysts are placed at the convergence points you’ve shown me. The ritual only awaits your involvement.”

Death’s silence stretched until it became its own form of torture.

“The other gods suspect nothing,” she continued, desperation bleeding into her voice. “Fertility hasn’t left her harem in months. Prosperity is busy working on contacting the stars, and Honor wars with the unrest caused by Life’s defeat. I’ve even managed to manipulate my sister.” A nervous smile twisted her lips. “Scorn chases shadows in the eastern provinces now—convinced that the thief you mentioned might be found there.”

The temperature didn’t drop. The air didn’t chill. Decay tried to breathe, but the normally inconsequential action slipped past her. She tried harder, calling upon her Remnants to force her lungs to work.

Sweat began to bead across her pockmarked forehead, and she raked her talons across her throat in a vain attempt to let air in through her wounds. Nothing worked.

“Which thief?” The question hung between them, deceptively gentle.

The pressure around her eased ever so slightly.

“The one… with… strange magic,” she managed, each word a supreme effort. “Stormhollow.”

Death didn’t move, yet suddenly he loomed directly before her, the void within his eyes expanding to encompass her entire field of vision.

“You speak of matters beyond your understanding,” Death said, voice unchanged despite the cosmic violence roiling beneath his words. “That name belongs to a calculation you cannot comprehend.”

Decay tried to apologize, but her voice had been unmade. She felt herself coming apart—every single Remnant she possessed was getting squeezed by a grip so far beyond her power it sent a shudder down her spine.

Then, as suddenly as it began, the pressure dissipated. Decay collapsed, falling prostrate at Death’s feet.

“Your usefulness outweighs your presumption,” Death observed, as one might note the weather. “For now.”

She remained on the floor, hatred and adoration mixing indistinguishably in her chest.

“The Remnant,” Death said.

Decay crawled to a small obsidian box on her altar, hands trembling as she removed the fragment of divine essence. It pulsed in her palm like a shard of negative existence, drinking the meager light of her sanctum. There was a part of her that screamed to absorb its might, but she’d mastered that insatiable hunger long ago. She closed the box, her fingers slipping on the lid.

“Are you ready to begin?” Death asked, though it wasn’t truly a question.

She inserted the Remnant into the center of her carefully constructed ritual circle. Web-like tendrils of anti-light shot outward, connecting to her hidden nodes across the continent. Where they touched, her influence spread.

Across the land, life ended in tsunamis of entropy and rot. She could feel them, oh fuck she could feel them. Their agony. Their confusion. Their horror as flesh dripped off their bones.

She drank it all in.

In villages and cities, mortals reached for loved ones only to find their hands infected stumps. And where her aura amplification widened, Death took his tithe. Thousands upon thousands would die within the next few days and weeks. By the time she was done, she would rule over this continent.

Death observed this unmaking with the passionless attention of an accountant reviewing ledgers. A part of her was hurt that even in the dawn of their victory, he did not react, nor did he give her the praise she so craved.

When he finally spoke, his words exposed just a fraction of that inferno inside of him. It filled her with enough longing that she had to restrain a moan from escaping her lips.

His hands cracked the railing as he gripped the ancient stone and leaned forward.

“Let them all burn.”
