The Realm of Paradise was a Domain-class spell that replicated a virtual space modeled after the Garden of the Nameless God. Whether it was the volume of Spirit Essence or the authority to control and manage the space, Jinyo could do everything within it that he could do inside the Garden of the Nameless God.

When he first obtained this spell, aside from using it to boost the Spirit Essence supply so that Nature Synthesis could operate at full efficiency and to empower his agricultural equipment, Jinyo hadn’t found any other practical applications. It wasn’t an offensive spell—it was a support spell.

But a support spell of the Domain variety—its mere existence was enough to overwhelm an opponent’s morale. Jinyo wasn’t experienced enough and had never possessed any magical talent, so he didn’t realize this. But from the perspective of someone like Esther or Nephrin, who were intimately familiar with high-level mages, Domain-type casters were the most terrifying class of power user.

Domain magic sat near the absolute pinnacle of spellcraft. No one knew what a deployed Domain could do except its owner. Even Jinyo himself had to spend days and nights poring over the Tome of Botanical Arts to fully comprehend it.

The reason Jinyo successfully unlocked this skill wasn’t simply because he finished reading the book. He needed to understand Domain magic itself. Fortunately, the foundation of Domain magic was the fusion of topographical measurement formulas with magical equations. Jinyo was a botanist, true—but he had also been a math teacher for years. All he had to do was look up the relevant magical equations online and work through them repeatedly until Domain magic clicked.

For Jinyo, it was no different from doing calculus problems for a university entrance exam. For everyone else, it was like a monkey trying to write the formula for volume—length times width times height. They couldn’t grasp it because it was far too complex. Only the mind of a genius could comprehend Domain magic.

And now, the “demon” in Esther’s eyes had just demonstrated Domain magic right in front of him. Intimidation came in two flavors: either the target was so terrified they abandoned any will to fight—or it stoked the flames of their rage, doubling their killing intent.

Esther stomped the ground so hard his footprint cratered the earth. The flames on his blade blazed a vivid scarlet.

Solar Flame Sword Art — First Form: Roaring Blaze

A roar erupted from the burning blade. It was a full-powered strike that made Jinyo’s skin crawl. Just from the wind-up alone, the sheer weight of it was palpable. The gap in raw physical power meant there was no way Jinyo could block it with the Sonneteer.

Esther brought his sword down with everything he had. It was so fast that he was certain Jinyo couldn’t dodge. What Jinyo did was swing his right arm—his empty hand—and from thin air, an axe materialized as though conjured by magic. A small hatchet that transformed into a full-sized felling axe in the blink of an eye.

“Copper Axe—Combat Mode, two minutes.” Sword Spirit seized control of the Copper Axe. He swung it with all his might to meet the blow. The Copper Axe’s power granted its wielder immense physical strength and destructive force. The red Ether Blade collided against the axe edge unleashing its full potential.

Sparks flew from the Ether-ore blade. Esther furrowed his brow—he had underestimated Jinyo’s strength. This axe was undoubtedly a magical weapon. It had enough power to withstand his Ether Blade. But so what? Esther pressed on, hammering Jinyo with relentless strikes. Which was faster—a sword or an axe—was plain to see.

An axe weighed far more than a sword. It was durable and hit hard, but without sufficient physical strength, it was nothing more than a large chunk of iron. Jinyo wasn’t weak by any means, but the Copper Axe demanded far too much from his Strength stat. Besides, even though Jinyo had activated the Copper Axe’s skill using Planar, Esther’s weapon remained completely undamaged.

Because weapons forged from Ether ore were the hardest in the world. Ether was a mineral harder than diamond. Even a system-granted magical weapon like the Copper Axe couldn’t so much as scratch an enemy’s Ether Blade. Only another Ether weapon could.

Jinyo already knew the Copper Axe couldn’t defeat an Ether Blade. But the system’s agricultural equipment still had its uses—and he was also testing the Realm of Paradise’s capabilities. He was well aware that Domain magic ranked among the highest tiers of spellcraft, so he never expected the Realm of Paradise to be limited to merely replicating the Spirit Essence levels of the Garden of the Nameless God.

A Domain spell hailed as one of the most difficult disciplines in the world couldn’t possibly be that underwhelming! Jinyo had spent sleepless nights devouring the tome. If the Realm of Paradise truly interfaced with the system’s power, then he should be able to do everything within it that the Garden of the Nameless God allowed.

Jinyo tried summoning agricultural equipment—and it appeared at his call, just as he’d hoped. Because the agricultural equipment was part of the Garden of the Nameless God. The reason Jinyo could think outside the box like this was thanks to Nature’s Creation. The Rice-Grain Bullet and the Carrot Weapon were supposedly useless spells that dealt no damage—but system descriptions weren’t always literal. They required interpretation.

Magic was imagination. This was the first time in Jinyo’s entire life that he felt he could adapt and apply anything he wanted. The beauty of magic lay in its emphasis on knowledge and invention; the way of the sword and martial arts, by contrast, focused on repetition and refinement.

“Tch. Annoying.” Esther clicked his tongue in irritation. His intense, aggressive Planar expressed his unyielding nature. He bent his knees slightly, then detonated all his power into his feet in a single burst—like igniting a rocket to force his body forward.

The red Ether Blade came slashing down, but Jinyo used Trampling Blossom to leap away just in time. Esther pivoted abruptly and launched a second burst with his left foot—a terrifying sudden change of direction. How his ankles could withstand such tremendous force with that kind of flexibility was beyond reason.

‘No way I can dodge this.’ Who would have guessed Esther could launch two consecutive bursts and change direction on a dime? Jinyo was in no position to evade again. The distance between them was barely two body-lengths. If he could double-burst the same way, he’d escape—but Jinyo possessed no such technique.

However… there was something he wanted to test.

‘Open the Structures window.’ Jinyo connected to the system and brought up the full Structures interface.

Esther was hurtling forward at tremendous speed in a straight line. Not even Esther himself could stop. In the split second he was certain his blade would reach Jinyo—a technique brimming with the same power that had killed dozens of Eltharok—nothing could stop him. That’s what Esther believed. Until—

A house wall materialized out of thin air. Esther slammed into the solid wall without decelerating even slightly. His body absorbed the full recoil of the impact—no different from a motorcycle at full throttle crashing into a parked truck.

‘What the—?! Why is there a wall here?!’ Esther’s exceptionally sturdy body still allowed him to keep moving despite the damage to his bones and internal organs. He gripped his sword and staggered back, only to discover this wasn’t just a wall—it was an entire single-story wooden house.

“!” Esther twisted aside just in time as something pierced through the wooden wall. A sharp Copper Spear nearly impaled him—one second slower and it would have run him through. Esther leapt away from the wall. A wooden window flew open, and a potato poked its head out, aiming a water gun and spraying a barrage of Hydro Bullets like a machine gun.

Esther sprinted to escape the volley before it riddled him full of holes like a honeycomb. His breathing was ragged and weak. Since the fight began, Jinyo hadn’t managed to land a single wound on Esther—and yet Esther looked exhausted and drained far too quickly. His heart nearly dropped when the front door slammed open and he saw Jinyo standing there, Copper Spear in hand, aimed straight at him.

The Copper Spear was hurled directly at Esther, who could no longer run as fast as before. Detonating his power meant burning through energy in a short span—it demanded enormous reserves and placed a heavy toll on the body. There was no way Esther could sprint at full tilt or swing his sword at maximum force indefinitely.

“Damn it—you’ve got too many tricks up your sleeve, demon.” Esther spat through gritted teeth. Two power detonations weren’t his limit. He could still fire off one more—but afterward, he’d be severely weakened for several minutes. And that wasn’t worth it if he couldn’t take the demon’s head off cleanly. Because if his enemy survived, Esther would be the one losing his head instead.

A white wooden fence sprouted from the ground. Esther nearly tripped over it but noticed just in time. He vaulted over it and used the fence as cover instead. The Copper Spear embedded itself in the fence without leaving a scratch on Esther. Without the fence as cover, he might not have dodged it at all without burning his third detonation.

‘Why, though?’ Esther thought. If this wasn’t because the demon was clumsy with its powers or had made a tactical blunder—then it was a deliberate choice not to hurt him. Esther ducked behind the fence to avoid the stream of Hydro Bullets that Salty was spraying everywhere. There were no trees in this area to hide behind—it was open ground, and the nearest tree was over two hundred meters away.

While Esther considered how to break from the fence to another point of cover, yet another white wooden fence materialized from thin air, blocking his path. Esther sprang to his feet, but his back struck something solid. His breath caught. He couldn’t move—he pushed, but barely budged.

The plain white fence looked like nothing special. With the physical strength of a trained fighter, a simple twist of the body should have blown them apart easily. And yet they held Esther fast, refusing to release him from their grip. Jinyo held a Copper Spear—which had warped back to its owner at some point Esther couldn’t identify. In Esther’s eyes, it looked exactly like a demon striding forward to take his life.

“I don’t know why you think I’m a demon or whatever it is you want to kill. But I have no intention of killing you. If I did, you’d already be dead.”

“…”

“Your techniques have flaws. They’re powerful, but they burn through energy just as fast as they perform. Your swordsmanship hasn’t reached its full potential. Your strengths lie in the excellence of your martial arts and a well-tempered body. But if you become my disciple, I’ll help you fix all of it—both your sword technique and your combat arts.”

“Heh.” Esther let out a sound from his throat.

“Something funny?”

“A master… I have no need for one. I have no intention of bowing to anyone as a teacher. Especially not a hellspawn like you!” The gaze Esther fixed on Jinyo was pure hatred and malice—unwavering and absolute, as though there was one conviction he had held his entire life that he would never yield to anyone. The vivid scarlet blade in his hand trembled. Intricate circuits hidden within the sword began responding to his wildly surging Planar.

“Calm down! I just want to talk this out!” Seeing the hidden mechanism inside the blade activating, Jinyo panicked. But it was already too late. It was his own mistake—he should have knocked Esther unconscious instead of trying to persuade him.

Ether weapons concealed one secret mechanism.

It activated only for a chosen wielder whose compatibility with that specific weapon was absolute. A system designed to combat catastrophic threats too powerful for any trained warrior to handle alone. The very thing that allowed humanity to end the Age of Collapse and usher in the era of civilizational revival that flourished to this day.

The Divine Punishment System.

“Release—Heretic Inquisitor.”
