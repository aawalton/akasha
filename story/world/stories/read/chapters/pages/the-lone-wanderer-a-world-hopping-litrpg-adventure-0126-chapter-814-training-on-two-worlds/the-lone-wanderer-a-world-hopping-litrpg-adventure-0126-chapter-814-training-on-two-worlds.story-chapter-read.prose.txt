Percy thanked his lucky stars that his sentries were surrounded by Yellow and Orange apes. The demigods were forced to lower the output of the formation to a more manageable level for their weaker patients; otherwise, the magical puppets would never have survived for half an hour within the ring-shaped structure.

Under those circumstances, the biggest challenge was emulating the villagers’ damaged bodies. Green and pale-blue light washed over the platform in alternating waves, making the air ripple with heat and turning the rising smoke into a murky haze. Percy and Micky made patches of projected fur curl and blacken, exposed angry blisters beneath them, and punctuated the act with the occasional hiss or involuntary twitch.

While Percy and Micky were confident in the optical and auditory fidelity of their illusions, neither the Mimicry trait nor the Huehuan’s mutation gave them any way to replicate the smell of burning hair or cooked flesh.

This was one of the main reasons why they had waited until most of the residents had been cleansed. Between the twenty-five people undergoing purification at the same time as the fake merchants and the hundreds of apes who had already endured the painful procedure, the village square stank to the high heavens, making it impossible for anyone to notice that a handful of patients were entirely odourless. The fact that the constructs weren’t corrupted wasn’t much of an issue either, because the majority of the natives had been clean too.

Despite his thorough preparations, Percy remained tense until his minions were allowed out of the formation and disappeared among a crowd of similarly singed villagers.

After purifying the final batch of peasants, the demigod with the light affinity tossed his spare storage device at a teenage girl. She was a pink-furred Yellow-born with a steam affinity – or at least had been, before having her hair burnt – and the tails sprouting from her waist, lower back, and upper thighs were woven into the shape of a short skirt. Her relatively high birth grade and composite element made her one of the most talented people in the village. Everyone – Percy included – had felt a little bad seeing how much she had suffered under the formation. That was probably why the demigod had chosen to gift her the precious artifact rather than anyone else.

“To be perfectly honest, I wasn’t planning to give anyone my spatial ring. As many of you have guessed, it was just a lie meant to provoke the corruption,” the Clear mage admitted. “But I must say that I’m quite impressed by how well your village has managed the infestation over the past year. If every settlement was half as conscientious as yours, our work would be a lot easier. Consider this gift a token of my appreciation.”

His companion added, “I’ll report this to my superiors and float the idea of introducing some sort of global competition between our towns and villages. Those who do the best job of limiting the spread of the corruption deserve to be rewarded for their efforts. Make sure you keep up the good work until our next visit.”

After voicing their praise and warnings, the demigods shrank the platform back into a ring and took off, the gust from their departure scattering loose tufts of scorched fur across the square. A few villagers were already splashing blobs of water over the blackened ground, while others covered the worst patches with a layer of fresh dirt.

Percy wasn’t paying them much attention, as he was too busy mulling over the demigods’ words. While he was relieved to have survived the ordeal, listening as the oblivious apes received all the credit for his efforts had made him want to slam his head against a wall. Then again, this was honestly what he deserved for having snooped on and micromanaged the natives’ lives for several months like a creep.

‘What now?’ Micky asked. ‘It’s great that our sentries can trick even demigods, but our plans for this village are completely ruined.’

‘I want to try cleansing another settlement,’ Percy reiterated. ‘Between the experience we’ve accumulated, our upgraded mindsets, and the minor adjustments we can make to our next formation, I’m sure that we can purify a village of this size much faster.’

They could have called it a day then and there, but Percy felt that their spells were on the verge of an upgrade, so he didn’t want to give up on this trip just yet.

‘Let’s look for a bigger town this time,’ the Huehuan suggested with a mental shrug. ‘We won’t be able to push ourselves to the next level if it’s too easy.’

Percy nodded. His merchants announced that they had exhausted their stock and would be leaving the following morning, giving the villagers a mundane explanation for their departure. It wasn’t even entirely false, since his supplies had been running low for a while, and he couldn’t plausibly replenish them without having at least one of the sentries pretend to leave the settlement.

In the meantime, he scattered the remaining constructs in every direction and commenced his search for a more suitable training ground. Some of the fist-sized sentries skimmed above the broad forest canopy in search of smoke and cleared land, while the rest slipped through the undergrowth or followed the narrow roads leading away from the village.

While Percy’s scouts were busy scouring the lesser spring for half-corrupted towns, most of his mental pockets continued working on the mindsets. The next series of upgrades had nothing to do with the operation on Mapel, but they were exactly what Nephthys and the clone sharing her body needed to manage her subordinates more efficiently.

Thankfully, the mindsets were relatively simple, so Percy was able to finish and register them in quick succession.

[Congratulations! Your familiar has acquired a new mindset: Rapid Cognition!]

[Congratulations! Your familiar has acquired a new mindset: Synchronized Transmission!]

[Congratulations! You have acquired a new mindset: Synchronized Transmission!]

Rapid Cognition was the most potent version of Percy’s Supercharged Cognition that he could currently create for his familiar. It occupied a hundred percent of her mental capacity and passively accelerated her thoughts by thirty percent, making it significantly stronger than Enhanced Cognition.

Percy was still too inexperienced to call himself an expert in mindset creation, and whoever had created the plaque was likely more skilled than he was. However, a mindset personally tailored to its recipient enjoyed an enormous advantage over one that had been mass-produced and granted through an object.

Similarly, creating a mindset for somebody else was harder than designing one for personal use, which was why Nephthys’ passive boost was weaker than Percy’s. Furthermore, the wasp lacked the mind mana to supercharge the mindset, and Percy’s own mind mana was best reserved for his clones, so they hadn’t bothered building that functionality into it either.

Synchronized Transmission, on the other hand, was the mindset responsible for sending Percy’s and Nephthys’ signals to the Starry Commanders possessed by the awakened Amenthei. They each had a copy, which manifested inside their respective mindscapes as a collection of constellations made up of indigo stars. Each constellation took the shape of one of their proxies – all of them wasps – because every possessed Commander was a separate conduit through which Percy and his familiar could feed instructions into a different cluster of bugs.

Percy’s version of the mindset included an extra silver star that represented the Command trait. It was connected to all the constellations and helped him filter his orders through the instincts granted by the trait, though the slight modification hadn’t differentiated either the mindset’s name or its description from Nephthys’ version. Whenever Percy formed a new order, the silver star pulsed, causing thin strands of light to bounce back and forth between itself and the relevant constellations.

Like most of Percy’s mindsets, Synchronized Transmission didn’t directly consume any of his cognitive capacity. It still required processing power to work, but it drew that from Percy’s accelerated mental pockets instead of weighing on his mindscape.

According to Nephthys, other mindset experts would have preferred to scale Supercharged Cognition down slightly to make space for some of Percy’s most demanding mindsets, such as Automatic Motor Control and Synchronized Transmission. Feeding them second-hand resources was less efficient – it was like building a treehouse instead of constructing a regular hut on solid ground. The treehouse had less room to begin with, while also straining the tree supporting it, leaving both mindsets weaker.

However, Percy’s situation was unique because of the synergy between Supercharged Cognition and his Parallel Minds. In his case, feeding his entire capacity into those two mindsets generated several times as many mental resources to use for everything else.

As for Nephthys… well, a different arrangement might have been marginally better, but the benefits were too small for a non-mind-user like her to justify designing a separately optimized version of Synchronized Transmission.

‘I guess I can go a little easier on mindset creation for now,’ Percy thought while wiping the sweat off his forehead. ‘The three of us have everything we need to pair with our spells, and raising the boost from Supercharged Cognition to fifty percent would take over a decade.’

With some dream mana now accumulated, Percy and Nephthys could finally begin practicing with the awakened Amenthei. Percy would split his freed-up mental pockets between expanding his control over the hive and continuing the operation on Mapel, while his physical body remained busy granting the Moirais’ Decree to the Starry Knights.
