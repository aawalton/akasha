Cradling a cup of tea, Ravia Wren watched Cale through a managlass screen. Cale was on the floor of the Gray Lotus HQ dojo, in his underwear, convulsing violently. The kid was relentless. Only weeks since he had awakened and already advancing to Upper Body Tempering.

The argument that he was catching up to levels children attain was starting to ring hollow…

“Little monster…” she muttered and sipped her tea.

Cale Bishop was slowly but with absolute certainty, turning from a liability into an asset worth investing into.

Ravia smiled to herself and flicked at a managlass tablet on her table, rereading Darius’s report for the third time.

Gray Lotus syndicate had increased their standing massively. Darius becoming Core Crystallization meant that new opportunities would open. Ravia already had plans, but now it seemed there was a new variable that also warranted plans.

“Cale Bishop could reach Mana Circuitry in mere months at this pace.”

That would be unheard of. Even scary. It took years upon years from even the most talented cultivators. And on top of that the young man’s True Integra already allowed for use below Mana Circuitry, something that should be physically impossible.

Everything about the damn kid was impossible. He might be an opportunity, but he was also a risk. Her operatives had reported at least three factions watching Darius’s mission. Arcane Veil, Gilded Gear Consortium and the Whispers. Did they want a piece of the pie or to simply eliminate Cale.

The Arcane Veil operative had tried to acquire Cale, but it had been an opportunistic attempt. Still, she was glad Cale had not taken it. It was a point in his favor as an asset.

“But what do I do with him now?”

There was a flutter in the ambient mana of the room. The faintest shift, perceptible to Ravia only because she had a Soul Core. Her floating crystals immediately created an invisible barrier of mana around her form. It was her True Integra, one found in a Nevani ruins long ago. The Gilded Gear could make beautiful mana shield lattices, but the real deal was unsurpassed.

No attack came.

Ravia turned to the intruder.

He was a man of diminutive stature, wearing white robes and white cloak with a white hood which obscured his shaven face. A soft smile was playing on the man’s lips.

Ravia bowed her head. “Master.”

“I believe you have found something interesting,” Master spoke softly, in a gentle whisper which carried through the room from the power of this man’s vast mana. He did not move from where he had appeared, for why would he?

“I was about to inform you—”

“Yet you didn’t,” the man said softly.

A chill ran down Ravia’s spine. She relaxed the defensive mana barrier her floating crystals generated. It would be as useful as a sheet of paper against this man.

Ravia removed her high heels and bowed. “My apologies, master.”

A single flick of a finger, and the managlass tablet from Ravia’s table flew into the man’s grasp. He flicked down, reading incomprehensibly fast.

“Ah,” the man said. “He is growing fast.”

“At an unbelievable rate,” Ravia said.

Master took a few steps towards the screen, hands placidly behind his back, the deep hood still shrouding his features. He watched Cale silently. Black sludge started pouring out of his body. The whole body was a deep crimson, as if burnt. Ravia remembered her own advancement to Upper Body Tempering. The heat and the fire had been unbearable. But she had only been thirteen.

The convulsing had relaxed, but Cale was still breathing rapidly. His back was arched as if in pain and tension, but with closed eyes and gritted teeth, Cale endured. Suddenly he relaxed completely, the rictus snarl on his face easing. The inflamed red of his skin started returning to normal. He was mostly covered in the black impure goop that his now refined body had expelled. A smile spread on Cale’s face and he whooped. “We did it, Aura.”

Ravia shook his head. Stage seven. The boy had reached Upper Body Tempering.

“Little monster,” she whispered.

“We like monsters,” the master softly whispered.

“We do if we can control them,” Ravia said.

“Is this one showing signs that he can’t be?”

“I’m starting to like him, actually,” Ravia said and sipped her tea. She was fairly certain her hand didn’t shake.

“Do not grow too attached…” Master said and scrolled through the managlass tablet. “What is this mention of an Integra upgrade?”

“He apparently found some hidden room left by the Nevani specifically for him.”

The man’s voice darkened just a hint. “That so… And he could access the information?”

“It appears so.”

“Fascinating,” the man said, his voice evening out. “It must be because of this AI, Aura.”

“Would you like for us to extract the Integra?” Ravia asked.

“No, no,” the white robed man said. “He is more valuable alive… That being said…”

“Your command, master?”

“Hire assassins. Just a handful. Lower Mana Circuitry. And arrange an offer from another faction for him. We must push him to learn more. I will be waiting…”

The man vanished and Ravia’s unconsciously tensed muscles relaxed. Relief passed over her. What her master’s machinations for Cale Bishop were, was beyond her. But she would do as ordered. She picked up a spare managlass tablet from her drawers. Fortunately it had all the copies of all the documents her master had just taken.

But questions bothered her. And she had not dared to ask. Cale was special, but to attract the interest of her master…

What does he know about Cale that we don’t?
