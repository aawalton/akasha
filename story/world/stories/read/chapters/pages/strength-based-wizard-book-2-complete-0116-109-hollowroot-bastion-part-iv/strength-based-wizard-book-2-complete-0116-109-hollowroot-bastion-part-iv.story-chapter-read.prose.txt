Chapter 109

Hollowroot Bastion, Part IV

Jelly Boy is a flash of burnt orange, and hits me like a jiggling freight train in stilettos. I don’t even see him move—one second he’s being “kept at bay” by my weaker spectral fists, the next he’s roaring past them, heels clicking, and body soaring through the air, a kick slicing towards my head fast and hard enough to decapitate me if it lands. I mentally unequip my staff and get a forearm up to block just in time. The left leg of his enchanted prosthetics slam into my arm, sending a shockwave through the air. The shin slices me to the bone. I grit my teeth, and send a couple hooks with my free arm, pounding the leg.

The enthralled slime leaps backward, landing in a dramatic pose. I glance at my right forearm. The wound is healing, though slowly. My summoning spell ate a lot of my Health, and my regeneration isn’t working as quickly as it should. Blood is pouring down to my elbow, already forming a puddle on the ground beneath me.

There’s another flash in the corner of my eye. But this time, it’s not Jelly Boy. Yomhalde reacts on instinct. His tentacles flare, bristling with anger as he sweeps a hand through the air like he’s pulling a curtain. Water appears, two circling torrents dragged out of the air itself. The cephalopod warrior rockets forward and the water follows, condensed from moisture and will into a roaring, spiraling lance. It slams into Jelly Boy.

The water hits and instantly freezes. Jelly Boy doesn’t even have a chance to react. It’s a flash-freeze. A cruel, perfect crystalline cage snaps around Jelly Boy’s legs and torso in an instant. The enthralled slime is still vibrating with furious intent. A fire’s burning behind those zombie-like eyes floating in the oddly-colored autumn goo.

“Sorry!” I shout, hoping the lil’ guy will understand once we’re all through this. My attention is immediately snatched by my summoned companion, who has his trident raised, ready to strike the frozen slime. “No!” I bark. “We don’t want to hurt the slime or the girl.”

Yomhalde turns towards me, confused and righteous anger in his eyes like a furious sea. He launches his trident in my direction. The three-pronged spear rips through the air, missing me by a hair’s breadth. The thud of an impact fills the air.

I look over my shoulder to see the trident sticking out of the chest of the Russian, its three teeth sinking deep into the corpse, who is unsurprisingly unfazed by being speared through. I wheel on the Russian, sidestepping towards the World Seed’s altar while keeping my distance from the reanimated portal-hopper.

[Perception] flares, something is coming from my right. I glance to the side and see Liv charging me.

“Deal with her,” I shout to Yomhalde. “Just don’t hurt her! Then help me deal with this masked asshole!”

Yomhalde nods. Suddenly, the second torrent of water circling over his head shoots forward towards the charging Liv. A black portal tears itself into the torrent’s path, swallowing the water. Which slams into my back, flooding from a portal that forms in my blind spot. The freezing water sends me sprawling onto my stomach. It freezes, pinning me to the ground.

“Fucking hell,” I growl, cheek glued to the stone floor of the courtyard.

My mind is racing, but it’s not about the fight. Not really. It’s about the World Seed. I have no idea what ‘insufficient Will’ means. And don’t have time to figure it out. What I need is a container to hold the Seed. I need to check my Inventory… After I get out from under this sheet of ice.

I try to stand, but strain against the blanket of thick ice. I activate [Muscle Mastery] and flex every muscle in my body as hard as I can. The result is… Insane! My mind instantly understands how to maximize the pump and I feel the aura around my body surge. The ice explodes off of me from the force of my flex. Unfortunately, so does my cape. The blue novice wizard’s cape rips itself off of my shoulders, shreds of it soaring into the air. I also feel my Ring of Freedom shatter around my lefthand ring finger.

The System message flashes across my vision.

[Ring of Freedom (Common)] destroyed!

[Cape of the Arcane Student (Common)] destroyed!

Well, shit. I forgot that [Muscle Mastery] destroyed items that the System didn’t label as [Indestructable]. The good news is, nothing too important was destroyed. I’ll miss the cape. It came in clutch and bailed me out of several situations. But, honestly, I forgot about the ring.

No time to mourn articles of clothing. I roll onto my feet.

The Russian corpse is also getting to its feet. It appears that the Russian had attempted to attack me while I was pinned, only to get blown back by my substantial flex. Nice. Yomhalde’s bloodied trident lays on the ground in the distance.

My muscles tense, ready for the Russian’s attack. But my opponent shifts tactics.

He doesn’t come for me. He turns his attention to Yomhalde and Liv, who are currently in a dance of dodging each other’s blows. Though it’s obvious Yomhalde is doing his best to be careful about not accidentally injuring the enthralled human.

The Russian jets forward, opening another portal and teleporting to the two combatants. I open my mouth to alert Yomhalde, who’s about to be on a two-on-one fight until I can dash over. Then, I realize, the Russian isn’t targeting Yomhalde. He’s going for Liv. The masked corpse appears over Liv’s head and comes down with an axe kick.

My body moves before my brain finishes screaming.

[Bulwark Body]

The Skill yanks me forward like a hook in my spine, teleporting me into the path of the incoming strike. I appear between them—half a breath of disorientation—and then impact. This time, I don’t have the agility or speed to block.

The blow slams into me like a sledgehammer made of deadly intent. My vision whites out as the heel of his red rain boot tears a deep gash across my chest, from right shoulder to left hip. My temporary boost in Health and Stamina evaporates instantly, deleted like it never existed. The bars in the corner of my HUD stroke out for an instant. I fly backward a step, feet skidding, pain blooming everywhere.

“UGHH—” It hurts. A lot. I’m losing a lot of blood. My head begins to swim. Shit, I think. I grit my teeth, the iron tang of blood on my tongue.

Yomhalde roars something in his own language and unleashes another wave of summoned water.  Freezing water explodes outward, a rippling sheet across the stone. It catches Liv and coats her in ice, locking her in place—vines and all—like she’s been encased in a cursed sculpture.

It splashes the Russian too, but the masked corpse thankfully still within range of Yomhalde’s attack. But the water doesn’t freeze. Instead, it hits him and slides off like rain off oiled leather.

“What the fuck,” I groan, staggering, trying to breathe through the pain.

Yomhalde’s tentacles flare in frustration. “He resisted that attack. Magic does not cling. He has protection. A landling trick, no doubt!”

I force my [Aura Sense] on for half a second, even though it makes my skull throb.

The Russian’s aura is wrong. It’s threaded, reinforced, like something is anchoring a shield around him. Then, I see it: the nipple ring. The shift in my [Aura Sense] is subtle. The nipple piercing glints faintly, and around it is a subtle, shimmering field. Like a coin-sized barrier generator stapled to his chest.

“It’s the ring,” I gasp. “His nipple ring—it's doing it! I think!”

Yomhalde’s eyes narrow. “Then we remove it.”

“You make it sound easy,” I snarl.

I glance at the chaos: Jelly Boy encased in ice, still thrashing against his frozen prison but doesn’t look like he’s going anywhere soon. Liv frozen. The World Seed rumbling and flashing dangerously. The Russian facing us down. It moves its head from side to side, wet cracking noises popping each time it does so.

I look at Yomhalde. “Can you slow him down at all? Anything?”

Yomhalde’s tentacles curl into a grin that is somehow both charming and terrifying. “Yes.” He charges. Small jets of water explode from his heels with each step. He clears the distance between himself and the Russian in an instant.

The sea monster forms a gesture with his hand. His trident lifts off the ground and flies towards the Russian’s back. His other hand swipes a claw towards the corpse’s head.

The trident spins, twisting like a high-powered drill straight toward the Russian’s shoulder blades. The Russian opens a portal with a lazy flick of his wrist, forming a small tear in space over his back. The trident vanishes into black.

My stomach drops. A portal tears open behind me. Luckily, I’m getting used to this guy’s little tricks. I twist on instinct and the trident comes screaming out of the darkness, aimed at my spine. Despite expecting it, I still barely dodge—throwing myself sideways, hitting the ground so hard my shoulder screams—and the trident slams into the stone where I was, embedding with a vibrating THUNK.

“YO!” I shout. “Friendly fire!”

Yomhalde doesn’t react. He’s matching blows with the Russian now, their fists moving like pistons. Each blow sends water spraying over the surrounding area, like the Russian is in a boxing match with a wet sponge. Eventually, there are several sizeable puddles around the two, including a couple of puddles that were already there from the failed freezing spell.

Yomhalde steps into one of the puddles. At first, it appears purely incidental.

But then he’s gone.

He slides through the water like it’s a trap doorway, reappearing out of another puddle behind the Russian, tentacle mustache flaring, eyes bright.  He wraps an arm around the Russian’s chest and locks him in a grapple so tight it makes my ribs ache in sympathy.

The Russian thrashes. Portals burst to life around the two like black firecrackers in the air, but the hold disrupts him, just enough.

Now. This is my moment. Yomhalde gave me the exact opening I need.

I struggle to my feet, blood still pouring from my chest. The wound is barely healing and I’m not sure how much more blood loss I can manage.

I cast [Force Grab].

A beam of force shoots out from my navel, and at its end forms a spectral hand—smaller than Lefty and Righty, but precise, fingers curled like pliers.

I narrow my eyes, aiming for the ring and pushing all of my mental focus into that single intention, hoping the spell delivers.

The spectral hand clamps onto the nipple ring. And I pull. With all my might, I retract the spell, silently commanding it to hold onto its target. To not let go, no matter how much resistance we face.

The Russian lets out a muffled scream through the ski mask—part rage, part surprise, part oh no my enchanted titty jewelry—as the ring rips free with a wet pop.

I focus on my [Aura Sense], seeing the shimmer around his body flicker. Then, it dies.

Yomhalde sees the recognition in my eyes and doesn’t hesitate.

The water under their feet surges up again, freezing in an instant into a solid block of ice that traps the Russian from boots to waist to chest, locking his arms at his side. Yomhalde phases through the ice, stepping away from the trapped Russian.

The corpse shudders in the ice, red eyes blazing.

I breathe, ragged, staring at the frozen Russian, the pulsing World Seed at the altar behind him, my sister encased, my slime friend iced like a cocktail garnish.

“Okay,” I whisper. “Great. Fantastic.”

I withdraw another one of Liv’s enhanced healing potions and knock it back. My body is instantly covered in a burning sensation, like termites made of fire were crawling under my skin. My Health bar appears in the bottom corner of my vision, rocketing up.

Then my mind snaps back to the only problem that matters now.

Container. I need a container!

“Any chance you’ve got a super strong container on you?” I ask Yomhalde.

“Uhhh…” He scratches at his face confusedly.

“Never mind.” I turn towards the altar. “One shot,” I mutter.

And I sprint for the World Seed.

I hit the altar steps at a dead sprint, lungs burning, brain screaming CONTAINER CONTAINER CONTAINER like I’m in a grocery store scavenger game where the penalty for losing is cosmic annihilation.

I summon my Inventory, and the System menu appears before me.

“Okay—okay—what do I have?” I hiss under my breath, scrolling through mental slots. Potions. Cookies. Random monster parts. Some elemental shards.

The bubble above the altar shudders. A crack crawls across its surface like spiderweb glass. The World Seed inside pulses hard—so hard the air ripples, its power washing over me like static electricity. The bubble warps outward, swelling like it’s about to pop.

“That can’t be good,” I whisper.

The Seed presses against the inside of the bubble like a heart trying to claw out of a ribcage. Behind me, Yomhalde shouts something.

“Landling! The ice won’t hold much longer!”

I spare a glance towards the Russian. The pulse from the World Seed must have weakened the ice. Cracks lace the frozen surface. The corpse actively struggles against the hold the frozen water has on it.

“Contain it in—” Yomhalde pauses, then adds, unhelpfully, “—something sturdy!”

“THANK YOU,” I shout back, voice cracking. “VERY SPECIFIC!” I turn my attention back towards the World Seed. “Very helpful.”

The bubble cracks again.

A thin seam splits. A hiss of energy leaks out, like lightning trapped in a freezer. My eyes water and my teeth ache. I don’t want to imagine what this thing feels like outside of its imprisonment.

Then there’s a wet sound behind me. A rip.

I whip my head around in time to see the Russian—free of the ice—moving like a nightmare in a tutu. He portals, blinking across stone in jagged leaps, each little tear in space letting him cheat distance like it’s a coupon.

Yomhalde intercepts the Russian.

The corpse spears a hand forward. Another exchange of rapid blows, but this time it ends with the Russian’s hand stabbing straight through Yomhalde’s chest.

The cephalopod-headed warrior stiffens. Tentacles bristle again, then go slack. His gray-blue eyes widen in surprise, then dim as if someone turned off a lamp.

“No—!” I shout.

Yomhalde collapses, dissolving into mist and sea-spray.

Ping!

Interplanar Ally Yomhalde has been defeated by Enthralled Rift Ravager!

I feel the loss like a tug in my gut, like a rope lashed to my very core had been cut in that moment.

The Russian turns toward me, red eyes smoldering like twin flames.

Panic does something funny to the human brain. It makes it creative. Or stupid. Usually both.

My Inventory scrolls past my eyes—random junk, mostly—and my gaze catches on something that makes my stomach flip.

Severed Human Hand (x1)

The severed hand. My severed hand. The one the giant gator had snapped off. The one Liv regrew. The one I had kept, like a morbid receipt.

My fingers shake as I withdraw it. It appears in my palm—pale, lifeless, but largely preserved by my Inventory.

“What the—” I gasp, because this is insane.

But insane is all I have left.

The World Seed bulges through the crack in the bubble. The bubble tears. The sound of a portal opening rings at the edges of my [Perception]. The Russian is upon me.

The Seed begins to breach containment. It pushes out like a storm cloud forcing itself through a pinhole.

I reach up with the severed hand.

“PLEASE,” I plead, not even sure who I’m begging. The System? Whatever gods oversee these ridiculous Games we find ourselves in.

The severed hand closes around the World Seed. It grabs the pulsing fragment and shuts, fingers squeezing themselves into a fist around it.

For a heartbeat, everything goes quiet. Everything freezes in motion.

Then, the Russian unravels. The vines threaded through him snap and peel away like rotten rope. His body collapses into loose meat and cloth, the ski mask sliding off a face I never even get to see. The tutu flutters to the ground like a dying joke.

Across the courtyard, the vines wrapped around Liv and Jelly Boy loosen, unraveling and breaking away at their icy prisons. The vines retreat into nothingness.

Ping.

SYSTEM MESSAGE: You have Claimed [Fractured World Seed Fragment]!

QUEST UPDATE: Cut the Thorns

OBJECTIVE: Fend off the Briar Thralls and Claim the World Seed.

OBJECTIVE: Complete.

ALL BRIAR THRALLS HAVE BEEN RELEASED.

BONUS OBJECTIVE: Eliminate all Briar Thralls.

BONUS OBJECTIVE: Failed.

The World Seed, now captured, screams without sound. A wave of energy blasts outward from the severed hand’s closed fist.

It hits the courtyard like a god slamming a door shut.

The walls of the Bastion crack—deep fractures racing up pale stone. Root arches groan. Dust rains from above. And I go flying.

I’m launched off the altar steps and onto my back, slamming into stone hard enough to make my vision spark. I sit up.

The Severed Human Hand is floating above the altar. But it’s no longer a simple human hand. It’s changed. It’s… something else. A fist-shaped item, smooth and dense, like petrified bone wrapped around a star. It glows with a silver and blue light. My HUD flickers, trying to identify it, but the description that’s summoned is vague—almost coy.

Item Identified: [???]

Description: A container that remembers what it held.

“Cool,” I wheeze. I get back onto my feet, this time more slowly.

Then the System hits me with the message that makes my chest loosen, just a fraction.

QUEST COMPLETE: Echoes of a Dead World

You have received a Return Ticket (x1)!

Note: This Return Ticket has no limit on number of Returnees using the Gate summoned by this Return Ticket.

Note: This Return Ticket has an expiration time of one hour. Once expired, this Return Ticket will be downgraded.

You have received the Gratitude of the Undead.

A feel a strange mental weight settle into my Inventory.

I hobble my way to the item that was once my severed hand, and tentatively take it. Nothing happens. I deposit it into my Inventory. Then, I stand there for a sweet, glorious moment, panting and staring up at fractured stone and drifting dust, and for the first time in what feels like forever—I feel… Good?

Behind me, the barrier at the courtyard entrance ripples.

Walter, Preston, and Grush rush in, accompanied by about fifty other skeletons. Walter’s top hat is slightly askew. Preston’s fishbowl glows brighter, scanning the courtyard. Grush stomps in like a tank, looking around as if disappointed there isn’t more to shoot.

“Secure the area,” Walter commands. “Can’t be too sure the trouble is over!” The skeletons immediately fan out. A group of them immediately close in on the strange remains of the Russian. They begin rummaging through the remains, poking at and picking up the enchanted equipment left behind. Let them have it. The exhausted thought crosses my mind.

“Check on Liv and Jelly!” I call to the skeleton accountant. He looks up at me and nods. They rush to Liv and Jelly Boy, helping pry away any residual ice, breaking chunks free.

Liv is sitting on the ground, teeth chattering. Walter summons a cloak from thin air and  throws it over her shoulders, wrapping her in it. Her cheeks are pale.

“I’m okay,” she manages, though she doesn’t sound convinced.

Jelly Boy shakes off melting ice like a dog, then vibrates so hard he almost liquefies from relief.

Walter strolls over to the altar and looks up at me. For a skeleton, he manages to convey about twelve emotions at once.

“You did it,” he says, voice rough.

Preston’s bodysuit join him. The zombie goldfish floats closer in his helmey-bowl. “In the most alarming way imaginable.”

Grush, who’s still looking after the recovering Liv, grunts. Which I choose to interpret as respect.

I make my way back down the steps of the altar, every inch of me protesting.

“Yeah,” I croak. “I improvised. A little.”

Walter lets out a brittle laugh. “That you did.”

There’s no time for long goodbyes. After getting Jelly Boy and Liv back on their feet, I’m eager to get back home. I tell everyone about the expiring Return Ticket. I really don’t want to find out what ‘downgraded’ means when it comes to our only way back to Earth. The last thing I need is one or more of us being trapped here.

We gather close—me, Liv, Jelly Boy.

Walter steps forward, shoulders squaring.

“Joseph,” he says, and there’s a weight to his voice. “Thank you.”

I swallow around the lump in my throat. “Take care of your world.”

Walter’s jaw works. “We will try.”

Preston inclines his fishbowl slightly. “And should you ever return, you will have friends at Castle Dinescu.”

A smile splits my face. I open my mouth, but Liv beats me to it.

“Likewise, if you ever find yourselves back on Earth. I’ll keep working on my alchemy.”

Preston does a little, happy flip in his bowl.

Grush lumbers forward and places a heavy hand on my shoulder—gentle, for him. He still smells like cold cuts and smoke. Then he steps back, saying nothing.

“Er… Okay. You too, pal,” I say.

Jelly Boy blorps and loudly buzzes. A pseudopod extends from his body, waving farewell.

I pull the Return Ticket from my Inventory.

It’s a slip of light, warm between my fingers. It unfolds into a portal with a soft whoomph, a doorway of familiar color and distant noise.

Home.

I look at Walter, Preston, Grush—at the weird undead family that dragged me into this mess and somehow became… mine. Liv sniffles, and I glance down to see tears welling in the corner of her eyes. Ours, I think.

“One last time,” I say quietly. “Good luck.”

Walter nods. “Go.”

I grab Liv’s hand.

Jelly Boy unequips his legs in a flash of pixelated light, leaping into the crook of my other arm. I gently squeeze him, pressing him close to my body. Glad to have my friend back, brief as that fight may have been.

Together, we step through the portal.

And the Hollowroot Bastion, the undead realm, the skeletons and zombie goldfish cleric. All of it.

Falls away behind us.
