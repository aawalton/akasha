Ji Ning was not a genius.

At least, not in the traditional sense. That was something Ling Xin had begun to realize after teaching her new disciple for several days.

The Crimson Flame Control Technique was one of the more accessible flame-control arts she had mastered. It had been developed specifically for alchemy, emphasizing stability, precision, and the ability to regulate spiritual fire during refinement. Although it lacked the destructive power of some combat-oriented fire techniques, its versatility made it an excellent foundation for aspiring alchemists.

For Ling Xin, learning it had been effortless. With her Pure Fire Spirit Body, she'd grasped the basic principles within a single day and could already use the technique smoothly by the second.

Her little disciple, however, was a different story.

It had taken Ning five full days just to produce a stable ember.

Of course, five days was still an impressive achievement. Ordinary cultivators often spent several weeks attempting to reach the same point, and some required even longer. But considering Ning's previous accomplishments, particularly his performance in the Outer Sect examination and his remarkable understanding of cultivation theory, Ling Xin had expected him to learn much faster.

She'd initially wondered whether she'd overestimated his talent, but after observing him for several more days, she began to notice something rather unusual.

Once Ning successfully established the foundation of the technique, his progress suddenly accelerated. The difference was almost ridiculous, as though he'd spent several days wandering through a dense forest, carefully examining every possible path, only to discover a perfectly paved road that allowed him to advance without hesitation.

Naturally, Ling Xin became curious, and her curiosity was finally satisfied when she happened to examine the notebook Ning had been using to record his practice.

The first thing she noticed was just how much he'd written.

Page after page was filled with detailed observations, diagrams, and corrections. From his very first attempt to the hundredth, Ning had recorded practically everything he could think of, including the circulation of qi through his meridians, the timing of ignition, the stability of the flame seed, and the effects of even the smallest adjustments.

Some passages had been crossed out and rewritten several times, while others contained arrows connecting different observations. There were even diagrams comparing how the flame behaved under slightly different conditions.

Ling Xin stared at the notebook for a while.

...Does he really need to write down every single failure?

As someone who had always relied on her natural affinity for Fire qi, she found the entire process rather excessive. When she learned a fire technique, she could usually tell how the qi should circulate after reading the instructions a few times. The necessary adjustments came naturally to her, and even when she made mistakes, she could often correct them through intuition alone.

Her body simply seemed to know what to do.

Ning, however, clearly lacked that instinct, and as Ling Xin continued reading, she gradually began to understand what he was doing.

Whenever an attempt failed, Ning would record the circumstances and compare them with his previous results. If a particular adjustment improved the flame's stability, he would repeat the experiment several times to confirm it.

Once he was satisfied, he would incorporate the improvement into his next attempt. And if the results contradicted his expectations, he would return to his earlier observations and reconsider his assumptions.

What had initially looked like a ridiculous collection of failures was actually a surprisingly systematic process. Rather than relying on intuition, Ning was gradually building his own understanding of the technique through repeated experiments.

That explained his unusual progress. He spent far more time than most talented cultivators establishing the foundation, but once he understood how the technique worked, he could improve with remarkable consistency.

Ling Xin closed the notebook thoughtfully.

So that's how he learns...

It was certainly different from her own approach, and although she still thought he was making things unnecessarily complicated, she couldn't deny that the results were impressive. After all, being able to identify one's mistakes and improve upon them was a valuable talent in its own right, especially for an alchemist.

At that moment, Ning was standing a short distance away, concentrating on the small crimson flame hovering above his palm.

Under his guidance, the flame slowly drifted through the air, changing direction whenever he adjusted the flow of qi. Its movements were still somewhat stiff, and he occasionally needed to pause to stabilize it, but the flame itself remained steady.

Ling Xin watched with satisfaction. This was precisely the kind of control she wanted him to develop before attempting actual pill refinement. Simply producing a flame was easy enough once a cultivator understood the technique, but maintaining its stability while manipulating it required considerably more practice.

Ning was already beginning to manage both.

For his part, Ning was quite pleased with his progress. Five days was how long it had taken him to properly grasp Ember Awakening, compared to the seven days he'd needed to learn his very first fire spell. Although the two techniques weren't directly comparable, he could still feel the difference.

As his mastery of other fire techniques improved, particularly his two fire spells, he found himself becoming increasingly familiar with the behavior of Fire qi. Certain principles carried over from one technique to another, making it easier to understand new methods without having to begin entirely from scratch.

Of course, his relentless practice also played a big role. Thanks to his relatively abundant qi reserves and access to recoverable spirit stones, he could afford to practice repeatedly. And with his panel helping him track his progress, he had an even clearer idea of whether his efforts were producing results.

Still, it was encouraging to see that the foundation he'd been building was beginning to pay off.

Good, good. At this rate, learning new fire techniques should become easier and easier.

Just as Ning was congratulating himself, Ling Xin crossed her arms and nodded approvingly.

“As expected,” she declared confidently, “my teaching must have played an important role in your progress.”

Ning turned toward her, his lips twitching slightly.

Well, he certainly couldn't deny that his master had provided him with an excellent technique and the resources necessary to practice it, but as for the actual teaching, that was another matter entirely.

Whenever he'd asked Ling Xin for detailed guidance, her explanations had generally been along the lines of “It should feel natural,” or “You'll understand once you try it a few more times.”

Which, admittedly, was probably perfectly reasonable advice for someone who possessed the Pure Fire Spirit Body. Unfortunately, Ning did not possess the Pure Fire Spirit Body.

Master, your natural talent is showing.

Still, after spending several days together, he'd become familiar enough with Ling Xin's personality to speak a little more casually.

“Master,” Ning asked, “what exactly did you do to help me learn the technique?”

Ling Xin then fell silent, apparently considering the question with unusual seriousness. Ning waited patiently, and after a few moments, she looked at him with a rather hopeful expression.

“...Does moral support count?”

Ning stared at her, while Ling Xin blinked innocently.

Finally, he sighed.

“Well... I suppose it does.”

“See? I knew I was helping!” Ling Xin smiled triumphantly.

Ning could only shake his head.

Well, at least she's happy.

And so, another peaceful day of master-disciple interaction came to an end.

Golden Bell Armory

“Brother Ning! Over here!”

Ning had barely stepped inside the Golden Bell Armory when he heard a familiar voice calling out to him. Turning toward the sound, he spotted Sun Jing approaching from behind the counter.

Or at least, he assumed it was Sun Jing.

The young man's face was unusually pale, with dark circles beneath his eyes and an expression that suggested he hadn't enjoyed a proper night's sleep in quite some time.

Ning stared at him for several seconds.

“...What happened to you?”

Sun Jing let out a long, miserable sigh.

“Don't even ask, Brother Ning. My family has decided that since I failed to qualify for the Inner Sect during the competition, I should focus more seriously on weapon refining.”

He gestured toward the workshop behind him.

“They've been making me work here every single day! Ten whole hours, Brother Ning! Ten!” His voice trembled with indignation. “Do you remember how carefree my life used to be? I could go wherever I wanted, do whatever I pleased, and spend my days enjoying myself. Now look at me!”

He pointed dramatically at his own face.

“Just look at what they've done to me!”

Ning examined him thoughtfully before nodding.

“Your family really cares about you.”

“...Huh?” Sun Jing blinked.

Ning was being perfectly sincere. Considering Sun Jing's mediocre spiritual roots and his failure to enter the Inner Sect, it made sense for his family to encourage him to develop his weapon-refining skills.

And judging by what Ning knew of the Sun family's background, they were probably providing him with proper instruction, materials, and access to their inherited techniques.

For an ordinary cultivator, such an opportunity would be worth a fortune.

As for the complaint about working ten hours a day, Ning could only shake his head inwardly.

What a sheltered young master.

Ning himself spent nearly twenty hours a day studying, cultivating, practicing techniques, or tending his fields. If not for the benefits of his Jade Skin Technique, which helped maintain his appearance despite his exhausting routine, he suspected he'd look even more miserable than Sun Jing.

Some people really don't know how fortunate they are.

Sun Jing, apparently realizing that he wasn't going to receive the sympathy he'd expected, sighed again.

“Anyway, I already told my cousin to prepare your things. She should be bringing them over soon.”

“Ah, thank you.”

Ning had previously entrusted his bow and several arrows to Ling Yao for upgrades. Now that he'd accumulated a large amount of spirit stones, he'd decided it was finally time to improve some of his equipment.

“Actually, I'm also here to purchase an alchemy cauldron,” he added.

“Oh, right!” Sun Jing suddenly remembered something. “I heard Senior Ling Xin accepted you as her disciple. Congratulations, Brother Ning!”

His smile was genuine. Although Sun Jing could be rather dramatic, he was sincerely happy to see his friends doing well.

“Thank you. My master suggested that I purchase a proper cauldron now that I've begun learning to control spiritual flames.”

“I see, I see. Then you've come to the right place!” Sun Jing's exhaustion seemed to vanish almost instantly. “Follow me!”

He led Ning toward the inner section of the armory.

Compared to the rows of weapons displayed near the entrance, this part of the shop was much quieter. Several alchemy cauldrons rested upon carved stone platforms, their surfaces engraved with delicate inscriptions that emitted faint spiritual fluctuations.

Sun Jing stopped in the middle of the room and spread his arms dramatically.

“Welcome, esteemed customer, to the Alchemy Cauldron Room of the Golden Bell Armory! Here, you'll find everything from affordable beginner cauldrons to exquisite treasures capable of accompanying an alchemist throughout their entire career!”

Ning looked at him. “...You suddenly sound like a salesman.”

“Of course!” Sun Jing replied proudly. “That's another thing I've learned during my training. Someone has to deal with the customers, after all.”

He lowered his voice slightly.

“You know how my cousin is. She barely talks to anyone. In fact, she even created puppets with personalities completely opposite to her own so she wouldn't have to interact with customers personally.”

Ning nodded. He could certainly imagine that.

“Honestly, she's practically a shut-in,” Sun Jing continued. “If it weren't for someone as sociable and hardworking as me, I don't know how this shop would manage.”

As he spoke, Ning noticed a familiar figure approaching from behind. A young woman stood there silently, watching Sun Jing with an expressionless face. Although her expression hadn't changed, Ning could almost imagine the dark aura gathering around her.

“Um... Brother Sun Jing,” Ning said carefully, “you might want to look behind you.”

“Hmm? What do you mea-”

Sun Jing turned around and froze.

“...”

Ling Yao stared at him.

“...”

For several seconds, neither of them spoke. Then Sun Jing straightened his back so quickly that Ning almost heard it crack.

“Ah! Cousin Ling Yao! I was just telling Brother Ning how fortunate this shop is to have such a hardworking and talented weapon refiner managing everything!”

Ling Yao continued staring at him, and Sun Jing's smile grew increasingly strained.

“Really, without you, I doubt the Golden Bell Armory would have achieved even half its current success!”

Ning watched the exchange with amusement.

Unfortunately, Ling Yao didn't appear particularly impressed. After another moment of silence, she turned toward Ning.

“Your bow has been upgraded. The special arrows are finished as well.”

She handed him the items she'd been carrying.

“Thank you, Senior Sister.”

Ling Yao nodded once. Then, just as she was about to leave, she glanced back at Sun Jing.

“I'll check your progress later.”

“...Yes, Cousin.”

Sun Jing's shoulders slumped as Ling Yao departed without another word. Ning waited until she was out of earshot before looking at his friend.

“Your cousin seems quite diligent.”

Sun Jing slowly turned toward him.

“Brother Ning.”

“Yes?”

“Why didn't you warn me sooner?”

Ning blinked innocently. “Because it was funny.”

“...”

Sun Jing stared at him before sighing. “You know, Brother Ning, people who do things like that don't get discounts.”

Ning chuckled.

“Come now. Surely our friendship is worth more than a few spirit stones.”

“That's exactly what someone asking for a discount would say!”

After exchanging a few more words, Sun Jing noticed the arrows in Ning's hands.

“Oh, are those the explosive arrows you asked my cousin to make?”

“That's right.”

Apart from upgrading his bow, Love, from Low Grade to High Grade, Ning had commissioned several special arrows incorporating different inscriptions and materials.

He'd been considering the idea for quite some time. After all, ordinary arrows were useful enough, but there was no reason to limit himself to simply piercing his opponents when he could also make the arrows explode.

These particular arrows contained compressed spirit powder and explosive talisman inscriptions, allowing them to release a burst of destructive energy upon impact.

Ning examined one with considerable satisfaction.

Excellent.

Then another thought occurred to him.

I should call these the 'fuck everything in that direction' arrows.

He nodded approvingly.

Truly, my naming sense is impeccable.

Sun Jing glanced at the rather wicked smile spreading across his friend's face. For some reason, he suddenly felt sorry for whoever would eventually find themselves on the receiving end of those arrows.

“...Anyway,” he said, deciding that some things were better left unasked, “shall we continue looking at the cauldrons?”

“Of course.”

Sun Jing led him toward the first display.

“This is the Green Smoke Herb Cauldron.”

The vessel was made from a dark green alloy, with delicate vine-like patterns engraved along its surface.

“Low-Grade, First-Rank,” Sun Jing explained. “It's quite reliable and doesn't require much qi to operate, so it's particularly suitable for beginners.”

“How much?”

“Five hundred spirit stones.”

Ning nodded, but didn't appear particularly interested. Sun Jing noticed immediately.

“Then perhaps you'd prefer something a little better.”

He moved to the next platform.

“This beauty is the Flameheart Tempering Cauldron. Mid-Grade, First-Rank.”

The cauldron was forged from reddish metal, with faintly glowing flame patterns running across its surface. Sun Jing tapped one of the inscriptions.

“It has a Heat Stabilization Array, which helps maintain a consistent flame temperature during refinement. Very useful for beginners who haven't developed precise control over their spiritual fire.”

Ning examined the inscriptions for a moment before shaking his head.

“Do you have anything higher-grade?”

Sun Jing raised an eyebrow. “Planning to spend quite a bit today, Brother Ning?”

“My master recommended purchasing a cauldron that I could use for many years,” Ning explained. “Preferably something that can be upgraded later.”

In truth, he'd initially asked Ling Xin whether she had a spare cauldron he could borrow. Unfortunately, she'd simply shaken her head.

Throughout her entire career as an alchemist, she'd only ever used a single cauldron. Apparently, she'd never needed another.

According to her, an alchemist needed to become thoroughly familiar with their cauldron, from the way spiritual qi circulated through its inscriptions to how its internal temperature changed during refinement. Even small differences could affect the outcome of a delicate pill-refining process.

And constantly switching between cauldrons meant having to familiarize oneself with those differences all over again.

That was why she'd advised him to purchase a Top-Grade cauldron with the potential for future advancement.

Ning found her reasoning quite convincing. Besides, he intended to pursue alchemy seriously. If he was going to spend money on a cauldron, he might as well purchase something that would remain useful for a long time.

“I see.” Sun Jing nodded. “In that case, I have just the thing.”

He led Ning toward another platform. Resting upon it was a relatively plain-looking cauldron, its dark metallic surface covered in several layers of delicate inscriptions.

“This is the Origin Spirit Cauldron. Top-Grade, First-Rank.”

Sun Jing gestured toward the patterns engraved along its body.

“It has three major inscription arrays. The first is the Heat Stabilization Array, which suppresses fluctuations in the flame and helps maintain a steady temperature.”

He pointed toward another set of markings.

“The second is the Spirit Circulation Array. It distributes spiritual qi evenly throughout the cauldron, reducing unnecessary consumption and making the refinement process easier to control.”

“And the third?”

“Essence Condensation Array,” Sun Jing replied. “It helps gather and condense medicinal essence during refinement, reducing waste and improving the purity of the resulting pills.”

Ning nodded.

“For a beginner, these three arrays can improve the success rate of pill refinement by nearly ten percent,” Sun Jing continued. “More importantly, the cauldron was designed with future upgrades in mind. Once you reach Foundation Building, you can strengthen its materials and inscriptions without having to replace the entire vessel.”

Ning slowly walked around the platform, examining the cauldron from different angles. Its construction appeared sturdy, and the inscriptions were arranged neatly across its surface. Its appearance was also rather understated, which he appreciated.

Simple was the best. He liked it.

“How much?” he asked.

Sun Jing glanced at the price tag. “Eight thousand two hundred spirit stones.”

Ning paused.

Then Sun Jing smiled.

“But since it's you, Brother Ning, I'll make it eight thousand.”

Eight thousand...

That was quite a lot of spirit stones. Even with his recent earnings, Ning couldn't help feeling a little reluctant to part with so much money at once.

Sun Jing seemed to recognize his hesitation.

“I know what you're thinking. It's expensive, but consider how long you'll be using it. A Top-Grade cauldron like this can accompany an alchemist for decades.” He tapped its side. “And there's another advantage. The structure is sturdy enough to withstand the occasional failed refinement without immediately breaking apart. For a beginner, that alone can save you a fortune.”

Ning glanced at him. “...Are explosions really that common?”

Sun Jing shrugged. “Depends on the alchemist.”

Ning thought about his master.

Then he nodded solemnly.

Fair enough.

He examined the inscriptions one last time before making his decision.

“I'll take it.”

Sun Jing blinked. He'd expected Ning to bargain for a while longer, especially considering how careful his friend usually was with spirit stones.

“You've really earned quite a bit recently, haven't you?”

Ning shrugged.

“It's an investment. If I'm going to learn alchemy, I might as well start properly.”

“Haha! Fair enough.”

Sun Jing grinned and began calculating the total.

“Eight thousand for the cauldron, and another four thousand for upgrading your bow and preparing those special arrows. That comes to twelve thousand spirit stones.”

Ning retrieved twelve mid-grade spirit stones from his storage pouch and handed them over. As the stones left his hand, he felt a faint ache in his heart.

There goes my money...

He sighed. “Alas, who would have thought that money disappears once you spend it?”

Sun Jing looked at him strangely.

Ning shook his head with profound melancholy.

“Mysterious... How bizarre.”

At this rate, he was going to be broke again before long.
