Chapter V.LXX (5.70) - Bloodspawn Lair

Escaping Otochi’s lair proved far simpler than Kizu feared. The very few bloodspawn they encountered very quickly dismissed themselves after seeing Kizu, his doppelgänger having clearly made a reputation for himself. He never got any clear answers as to where Otochi had departed to, though Kizu knew wherever it was, it was likely bad news. However, it proved extremely convenient for their escape.

Deciding that retreating to safety was their primary objective, Kizu had elected not to delve into the new knowledge and power at his disposal. Though the temptation certainly ate at him as they navigated the lair.

Kizu, Ione, and Basil traveled on foot as they slunk through the corridors. One of Ione’s summons trailed behind them, with Kumiho safely secured under a skin flap. And, of course, Mort rode on top of Kizu’s head. Occasionally, he’d leap over to Basil or Ione when Kizu encountered one of the patrolling bloodspawn and needed to act like his insane soul sliver, but those stragglers were few and far between.

“Isn’t this the room where they separated us back on our first visit?” Ione commented as they barged through another door.

A bloodspawn looked up from where he sat, on black metal box, approximately two meters across.

“You’re not supposed—”

Kizu cut off his sentence with an overwhelming blast of flames. The bloodspawn likely would have screamed, if not for the flames rushing down his open mouth and devouring the air inside his lungs.

A few seconds later, the bloodspawn’s charred corpse fell off the black box. Kizu felt a brief moment of pity, but smothered it. Ione’s observation had been correct. They visited this room in the past. That box was the same one that imprisoned Anata for all those years. A time dilation chamber.

He pried it open and looked down into the spatially stretched room within. Weirdly, a wave of nostalgia hit him as he examined the space. Not much had changed. It was still pretty barren, with algae growing on the walls. Shuddering, he recalled witnessing Anata lick the green sludge off the wall for sustenance.

As he closed the box’s lid, Kizu wondered briefly about the challenges stealing the time dilation chamber might present. After a few seconds of consideration, he dismissed the thought. Uprooting and running off with the box would be a massive endeavor. Even just stopping to study the temporal and spatial glyphs carved into the box would require too much of his precious time.

Instead, Kizu decided to allot a few minutes to another project.

Recalling the wall where he’d arrived from when taken prisoner, Kizu pressed a hand against it and channeled earth elemental magic through it. While far from his strongest branch in the elemental magic tree, he still knew enough on the subject to strip the enchantments in the wall and bust it open. Rubble and dust sprayed out into the room.

They stepped into the next room and Kizu’s eyes immediately darted to the spatial beacon, now extremely familiar with the design. This beacon was in the shape of a fist-sized emerald that floated in the center of the empty stone room. Completely sealed off from the rest of the palace, Kizu had no doubt it was meant to be equal parts trap and secret entrance.

Flames once again leapt from his fingers as switched which element he channeled. This time, he directed the fire into a spatial funnel, sending out a nasty beam the size of his finger at a temperature that rivaled the magma tunnels. It struck the beacon and the gemstone glowed for a moment. In a matter of seconds, the fire ate a hole into the magical stone and pierced through the other side. The emerald lost its hue as he felt its enchantments fade.

For good measure, Kizu then sent out half a dozen spatial blades and chopped into the remaining gemstone.

Broken and devoid of magic, the pieces of the beacon clattered to the stone floor.

To test the effectiveness of his destruction, Kizu performed a short ranged jump to the other side of the room. No pull. A success.

He smiled. At the very least, the bloodspawn no longer possessed the ability to pull in spatial mages. And if Otochi relied on the beacon for his own transportation, then Kizu had just crippled the Blood Lord’s infrastructure.

“We’re lucky we stumbled on this room,” Basil commented as they walked away. “I wasn’t allowed access to this side of the palace during my stay here. I didn’t even realize it existed until you told me about it.”

It made sense, as Otochi likely intended to keep Anata a secret from her cousin at the time.

They returned to the normal path out of the palace. Kizu vaguely recalled walking these same corridors in his mad dash for freedom with Anata. As the next major landmark, they encountered the room of ice.

Kizu mentally prepared himself for the upcoming confrontation. With the beacon destroyed, he felt confident in his escape route. Jumping all of them across the room to the exit would take a fair bit of blood, but he deemed it far less of a toll than what an honest fight with Otochi would charge.

However, as they stepped into the room, a wave of freezing temperature accompanying their arrival, he spotted no one. In the past, Otochi slumbered, frozen under the ice. No such figure emerged.

Each step carefully placed, they crossed the room. At one point, Basil slipped and smacked into the icy floor. He sprang back to his feet, his nose flattened to his face as he prepared himself for battle. They all expected his blunder to cost them their stealth, for some trap to trigger or a slumbering enemy to awaken. And yet, no nefarious entity reacted.

They reached the passageway on the other side unhindered.

“Worrying,” Basil said as they exited Otochi’s home and returned to the World Dungeon’s sprawling caverns.

“The bloodspawn we encountered did note Otochi’s departure,” Ione pointed out. “Maybe he’s just out in the World Dungeon, scouring it for materials. With the Dragons no longer occupying their region, they could be looting their old home.”

“Maybe,” Kizu said, unconvinced. He recalled what his doppelgänger had said about Otochi’s absence. “I’m worried about what’s happening back at the academy. I wish I’d left Mort above ground. I feel blind and helpless.”

“You have divinations set up, why not use them?” Ione suggested.

Kizu blinked. He juggled so many projects that occasionally things like this completely slipped his mind.

The crone’s bowl still in hand, he connected it with the viewing spots he’d set up on Owl’s Respite. There, he saw Anata sitting on the deck, reading a book with Comet by her side. That made him irritated. The hippogriff was not allowed on the ship and Anata knew that. However, her rule breaking aside, all seemed well enough. She likely just completed her riding lesson with the instructor Vincent sent over. Though, Kizu found it a bit difficult to tell exactly what day it was.

His mushroom induced slumber wasn’t supposed to have lasted more than half a day according to Zayne in the dreamscape. But it had been at least enough time for his doppelgänger to cross the World Dungeon and abduct him. That seemed like more than half a day of effort, based on roughly where Kizu knew they’d triggered the trap. So, maybe it took them a bit longer.

Regardless, Anata appeared safe for now. He was lucky she happened to be on the ship when he checked. It put several fears to rest.

He relayed the information to his companions.

“Now that we’re clear of the bloodspawn, how about we chat about what exactly happened in there?” Ione finished sketching a second summoning circle and the giant lizard mount that carried Kumiho was soon accompanied by a twin for them to ride.

While they loaded up the mount, Kizu explained the dreamscape and his subsequent fight with his doppelgänger. He opted to stroll alongside the mount as they traveled, finding the movement relaxing and helpful. Ione and Basil listened carefully, not interrupting until his story finished.

“So, you are capable of summoning now?” Ione asked.

“There’s a lot more than that to talk about,” Basil said. “Like, the whole immortal sapient soul piece sacrificing its existence to transfer your old broken soul piece into your body.”

“Yes, yes.” Ione waved a hand dismissively. “But, that’s a subject to take up with Aoi, not us. From what it sounds like, Kizu has successfully subdued evil Kizu. Not much more we can help him with there. Summoning, however, I can help him with. What can you do?”

Kizu stopped walking and closed his eyes, sorting through his locked away memories. From what he skimmed over, there was barely any summoning education there. His doppelgänger seemed to have purged the majority of the memories from his mind right before being fully absorbed. Kizu knew enough about summoning to know how essential understanding the beasts was.

He winced as he passed over the most recent memory available. The doppelgänger had rather vindictively left behind their final clash, so he recalled the horrific pain in crisp detail. He was about to dismiss the memories when he pushed the sequence of events back a bit and found a nugget left behind there. That memory cut in right after summoning the elemental hydra, so he didn’t have access to it. However, his doppelgänger had formed a second summon before engaging in battle. He scoured his memories for more of that summon and found it in snippets throughout the locked memories.

Opening his eyes, Kizu noticed Ione standing before him, her enchanted chalk held out to him. He took it and started sketching his summoning circle.

His hand moved nearly with next to no conscious effort. Less than a minute later, he slammed hand down on the circle’s rim and channeled into it, the exact same way as he had in Conjuring F a hundred times over. This time, it blazed to life with ease, greedily sucking in his magic.

Basil and Ione both tensed. They’d heard him tell stories about his broken and uncontrollable summons in the past.

A creature stuck its nose through the summoning circle. Then its head popped through, followed by the rest of its long body.

A beagle, with ears several times a normal dog’s length, sat in front of them, wagging its butt and panting up at them.

“You did it!” Ione clapped her hands together. “Wait! You realize what this means?”

“That I can get a higher conjuring rank and force the headmaster to give me info on my sister?”

“It means that I get to tutor you! First lesson right now.” Ione knelt down by the little dog and poked at it. She lifted an ear and let it flop back down. “Damn. You remember when I first taught you to summon a dog in our first semester?”

“Vaguely. I recall it being some sort of monstrosity with only a single limb and several mouths.” Kizu wasn’t particularly proud of that summon, but before this very moment, it was the only time he could say he summoned something with even marginal success.

“There’s a great deal of info you can gather simply by examining the circle the summon originated from. With a base of knowledge accompanied by a keen eye can know a summon’s strengths and weaknesses before the summoner even completes the spell. Your basic circle here is nearly identical to the one I taught you. However, there are a thousand more intricacies with the design, especially as you approach the center. What does that tell you about the summoned creature?”

“That… it’s far more specific?” Kizu glanced over his shoulder at the empty corridor. It had shifted since they passed through it. “Is now a good time to lecture me? We’re only an hour walk outside of Otochi’s lair.”

“He raises a good point,” Basil said. “As interesting as summoning might be, another time might—”

“We’re fine. They don’t even know we escaped their dungeon yet. Plus, you can kill them if anyone shows up,” Ione said, dismissing their concerns. “And, Kizu, your answer is so vague as to be correct. Fascinating that you can summon the creature without full comprehension of what you’ve done. It likely is a result of your subconscious creating the creature while the memories are sealed in your meditation enhancement technique. What you have here is a creature with a magical lineage."

“Like that two-tailed cat we went on a hunt for when we found the hebijikan?” Kizu asked.

Ione snapped her fingers and pointed at him. “Exactly like that cat! Your other-self must have found and studied this dog. It has an extremely unique heritage and that allows it to be near impossible to kill with magic. The ancestor is known as a nullifier. They’re kind of dog-shaped, so it’s not too surprising one bred with one in the past.”

Kizu knelt down and scratched the top of the nullifier dog’s head. It wagged its tail more furiously and closed its eyes under the scritches.

“Not that useful, since I have antimagic barriers,” Kizu said. “But happy to finally have something to summon.”

“I also suspect that it has a keen sense of smell, though that comes from the dog side of its family tree more than the magical beast side.”

A sudden realization made Kizu want to whack his forehead. Smell. That must have been how his doppelgänger tracked him back when he escaped the prison cell. He’d located him, despite the illusions, because the dog could smell him.

All he had to do to avoid detection was cast a scent illusion to cover his tracks. The exact spell he’d spent the last four months studying.
