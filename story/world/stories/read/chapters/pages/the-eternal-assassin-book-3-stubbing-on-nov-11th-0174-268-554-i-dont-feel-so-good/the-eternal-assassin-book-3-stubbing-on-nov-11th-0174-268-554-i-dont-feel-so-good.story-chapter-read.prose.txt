The good news was that Asher hadn’t actually had to burn all that much of his energy in order to take down Watcher. Other than blocking a few attacks with Spatial Lock and two uses of Distortion’s second-tier effect, he hadn’t really used any of his skills. The bad news was that Watcher had managed to stab him through the chest, and that meant he’d been forced to wait even longer to heal up within his Personal Rift.

Asher had sat there, impatiently waiting for his flesh to close and his injured lung to start working properly again. He couldn’t even use a potion of Lesser Restoration to speed things up because Watcher had broken all of them, along with the rest of their recently acquired potions. By the time he was healed and ready to go, he was down to a little less than half of his tritessence reserves, and he hadn’t even found the King of Death yet.

He briefly debated dropping a million shards to grab a basic resistance to death and maybe buy himself a bit more time, before quickly deciding against it. Not only would his next resistance purchase most likely be his last due to the exponential cost associated with them, he didn't have the ten million shards on hand that would be needed to evolve it. Complete Resilience and Eternal Regeneration were already incredibly powerful tools on their own. Giving up his final choice of resistance would probably only buy him a few extra seconds at most. The Death Aura was simply that powerful.

“We need to move, and we need to move fast,” Asher instructed Smiles as he prepared to leave his rift and submerge himself in the powerful Death Aura again. “If we need to retreat back here again, it’s not going to be good. By the time we find the King of Death, I’ll probably be running on fumes. If I can’t take him out in a single strike and this turns into an actual fight, we’re going to be in trouble. I do still have one last resort that miraculously survived Watcher’s rampage if it comes down to it, but it’s definitely for emergency use only.”

“It’s not too much farther, and I think you’ve got good odds of taking him out quickly,” Smiles admitted. “The entire reason he created so many powerful minions in the first place and devised the massive Death Aura is because he’s terrible at fighting. A genius when it comes to crafting and enchanting, but about as threatening in a fight as a regular corpse.”

“Let’s hope you’re right,” Asher said, taking a deep breath before slipping back out of his Personal Rift. Going from regular space to the dense, heavy Death Aura of the center of the Deadlands was like being slapped in the face. Practically suffocating, Asher tried to focus on just breathing as he took off down the hall once more, following Smiles’ directions to the letter. He didn’t have time to worry about anything beyond putting one foot in front of the other and making it to the lich before he ran out of energy and succumbed to the Death Aura.

Which naturally, was when all the skeletons decorating the halls came to life.

“They’re awake!” Smiles laughed, watching Asher as he cursed and narrowly dodged a jagged bone claw to the throat. “Well that’s remarkably bad timing!”

“It’s annoying, but also a good sign!” Asher decided, lashing out with Crescent and chopping through the spinal cord of the nearest skeleton before racing past the next two. “The lich is getting desperate! He’s run out of powerful minions and is doing anything he can to slow us down and buy time! It’s a race at this point, and a race I intend to win!”

The skeletons seemed intelligent enough to spread out and try to fill up the hallway, which meant Asher was forced to deal with most of them rather than run past them with Distortion. Still, it was more cost efficient from an energy standpoint to fight them without using any of his skills, and Smiles laughed as he was jostled around and even tossed to the floor a few times whenever Asher needed to utilize both his hands to deal with the attacking skeletons.

At the very least, his energy reserves did tick up slowly while he was fighting and dying to the Death Aura. Passive skills like Eternal Regeneration didn’t seem to use up energy for whatever reason, which meant even as his body desperately fought a losing battle against the Death Aura, so long as he didn’t use any active skills, his energy continued to recover just a bit at a time.

The problem with that was the final skeleton guardians seemed to be getting larger and deadlier the closer they got to the center of the workshop.

“Are these actual people?!” Asher asked, cursing as he was forced to slip into the astral to dodge a massive bony fist from a skeleton that would have looked down at Rulfar. Moving closer, he returned to the material realm, using Distortion to reach the skeleton’s spine and slice through it with a thrust that would have made Mornamol proud.

“No, they’re the structures of potential future Servants!” Smiles explained, the severed head currently lying on its cheek and facing away from the battle where Asher had tossed it. “They don’t have any of the magic that makes them unique carved into them yet, but the bones are there! Hah! Get it?”

“Yes, I get it! You’re hilarious!” Asher snapped, forcing a skeleton’s lunge to fall short by expanding the space between him and it, before reversing it and stabbing through the compressed space, slicing clean through its skull. “Are we at least getting close?!”

“I think so!”

“What do you mean, you think so?!” Asher shouted, leaping over a skeleton’s bony tail that attempted to take him out at the ankles. “This is sort of important, Smiles!”

“Just because I know where the center of the workshop is doesn’t mean I know the King of Death is going to be there!” Smiles argued. “I mean, I think that’s where he’d be, because it’s where he always is, but I can’t say for certain!”

“For both our sakes, you’d better be right!” Asher said, hurling a dagger behind him at a smaller skeleton attempting to catch him by surprise before turning and slicing through the tail-skeleton’s spine. The pommel of his dagger cracked into the ambushing skeleton’s skull, shattering it and sending pieces everywhere at the same moment the one with a tail clattered to the ground, its body once again lifeless.

Asher leaned over, clutching at his heart as it skipped a beat. They’d been outside of his rift for barely two minutes and already he could feel his body slowing down as it desperately fought to stay alive. The fact that Smiles’ head looked healthier than ever was icing on the cake to his current condition, and Asher grunted as he grabbed Smiles and continued running.

Skeletons fell one after another, each partially-constructed Servant no match to his twin blades and burning desire to see another lich dead before him. Deader? More dead, he decided as he shattered another skull and ran through the bits of bone before they could even hit the ground.

“You sure you don’t want another break inside your special room?” Smiles asked, actually looking slightly concerned for once as he watched Asher’s ragged breathing. “You’re looking a little pale. And dying.”

“I don’t want to give the King of Death any more time to prepare than he’s already gotten,” Asher said, his body aching as he pushed it to keep moving. “He’s a genius, right? What if the ten minutes I need to recover gives him time to throw together another Servant for me to fight? He’s got all these skeletons already prepared, maybe he just needs to slap some flesh on them and call it a day. I’m in no shape for any more hard battles.”

“I get that, but you’re not exactly in any shape to fight right now either,” Smiles laughed. The severed head let out an impressed noise as Asher used Distortion to duck under the powerful and shockingly fast swipe of a skeleton as he turned the corner, before rocketing back up and slicing through the top of its spine. “Which makes all the skeleton killing far more impressive!”

“I’ve got help,” Asher managed, trying to ignore his heart skipping another few beats as he ran. Once the skeletons came to life and confirmed that the King of Death knew he was coming, Asher had given up his assassin class, trading it for the brand-new gravekeeper class he’d recently received. He’d hoped it would involve making him better at fighting undead, and it had, in a way. It didn’t give him any additional strength or power against them like the demon hunter class did against demons, but it did give him a sixth sense as to where the undead actually were. As far as he could tell, any undead that got even remotely close to him lit up in his senses like they were on fire, which made ambushing him next to impossible.

The class ability was probably incredibly convenient for sensing corpses underground that were gradually turning into undead monsters, but it was also great at detecting skeletons rushing him from behind or waiting for him around bends in the hall.

This lich better be as weak and helpless as Smiles says, Asher thought, stumbling a bit and hitting the wall hard with his shoulder as he failed to make a quick turn. Shaking his head, he righted himself and kept running, practically forcing his feet to step one after the other with nothing but sheer willpower.

“Why are there so many rooms and hallways?” he argued weakly, grunting as he failed to move fast enough to dodge a bony fist and felt it rake across his sea serpent hide armor. He dispatched the skeleton with a thrust to the spine, Crescent’s remarkable sharpness helping overcome his rapidly decreasing strength.

“Most of them hold experiments that have to be isolated, or new Servants in various stages of creation,” Smiles explained. “I’ve probably poked my head into just about every room in this place over the centuries out of curiosity, and some of the ideas the King of Death comes up with are wild. You ever seen what it looks like when someone’s skeleton grows on the outside of their body? Or a zombie with six brains? Crazy stuff.”

“I’m surprised you didn’t get into trouble sooner,” Asher said, spitting out the blood that had trickled from his nose into his mouth as he kept going. “I would have dismantled you years ago.”

“Oh, he threatened it a few times,” Smiles laughed. “Especially after my peeking ruined one or two experiments. But I’m the last of the Plagues. The final wielder of the soulsteel blades. I think he’s got a soft spot for me.”

“He probably won’t for much longer,” Asher coughed, turning one last corner and nearly collapsing in relief at the change in view. Rather than an identical hallway filled with sealed rooms and guardian skeletons attempting to stop him from getting further into the workshop, this hallway ended in a large, heavily reinforced door made from what looked like solid bone.

“That’s the center!” Smiles shouted. “Come on, you’re so close!”

Leaning heavily against the wall to support his shaking legs, Asher ignored the signs of his body shutting down as he lost feeling in his fingertips and the world began wavering. As much as he desperately needed to slip into his Personal Rift and recover, doing so would be the end of him. He’d burned through too much energy fighting his way through increasingly powerful skeletons, and if that wasn’t bad enough, something was happening beyond that closed door. His gravekeeper class was flashing bright warning lights in his mind.

A new undead was about to arise.

An impossibly powerful one.

“He’s about to finish constructing another Servant. Or something worse,” Asher muttered, shaking his head again as he approached the door, wondering why his evolved darkness resistance wasn’t helping with how dark the hallway was getting. “Can’t sense the lich… but I can sense his experiment…”

“Asher, not that I doubt your abilities, but I’m pretty certain I could defeat you in the state you’re in. As just a head,” Smiles clarified.

“If he finishes that undead, it won’t matter,” Asher argued, suddenly finding himself before the door. “Smiles… stay safe.”

“Aw, come on! I want to watch you get turned into paste by whatever—”

Smiles’ cries were cut off as Asher shoved him into his rift and made sure his weapons were at the ready. Mithral dagger in one hand and living wood in the next, he nodded to himself. Taking a deep breath, he slipped into the astral and entered the room at the very center of the entire Deadlands.

Coming face to face with the King of Death and his latest creation.
