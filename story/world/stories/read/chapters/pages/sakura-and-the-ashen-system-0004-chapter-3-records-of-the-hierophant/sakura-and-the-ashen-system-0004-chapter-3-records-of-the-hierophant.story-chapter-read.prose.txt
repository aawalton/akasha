Sakura remained sitting on the floor by the altar for a few more seconds without moving, as if she were afraid none of this was truly over, as if it had only gone still somewhere nearby and was waiting for her to lift her head again. The basement had gone quiet.

Sakura breathed heavily, with short pauses, like after a long run. Her gaze moved from her hand to the barely visible ash-obsidian mark on her left wrist, then to the dark dust sunk into the floor where a living, hungry monster had stood only a minute ago. It had vanished completely. Nothing remained to prove the fight had been real except the ache in her muscles, the blood on her palm, and the feeling that something foreign now lived inside her.

That familiar pale glow flashed before her eyes again. This time Sakura did not flinch. She only lifted her gaze wearily, as if she no longer had the strength to be surprised by another blow from the System.

TARGET ABSORPTION COMPLETE.LEVEL INCREASED.CURRENT LEVEL: 2.TALENT: UNDETERMINED.NEW SKILLS ACQUIRED:— SCARLET ASH TAMER— DEVOURER

PHYSICAL ATTRIBUTES UPDATED:Strength: 3Endurance: 4Agility: 6Constitution: 3Perception: 6Overall Condition: Weakened

Chance of survival: 9.8%

Sakura blinked slowly.

“What the hell,” she breathed out hoarsely.

The System, as always, did not bother to answer. The message hung before her for a few seconds, then dissolved into the air. Level two. She was no longer quite the helpless prey that had just woken up on wet ground among ruins.

Her attributes had changed too. Not all of them, and not enough to make her feel safe, but enough for the numbers to look a little less hopeless. Agility had risen the most, which almost made a bitter kind of sense, considering that most of her heroic achievements so far had involved running, falling, dodging, and trying not to be eaten.

Still weakened, though. Sakura let out a dry, humorless breath. So the System had not magically fixed her.

Her talent was still undetermined. Which meant something else was still ahead. Something the System had not named yet.

“And what other surprise are you preparing for me?” Sakura asked the empty air quietly, with almost no irritation now, more with tired, grim anger. “What else am I supposed to find inside myself?”

No one answered. Only then did Sakura notice that the dry flowers at the base of the altar had changed. Not long ago they had been dead, but now it looked as if the petals had absorbed the glow that had flooded the basement at the moment of her strike. They had come alive. There was something troubling in them now, almost predatory, and Sakura crouched down closer without meaning to so she could get a better look. The narrow petals trembled slightly. Dark veins ran along the edges of some of them, like thin cracks in glass. They were beautiful in a way that made them even more unsettling.

“I need to rest a little. I definitely need to...”

Sakura straightened and took a step forward, about to go around the altar from the other side, when the whisper of the wind passed through the basement again.

This time it did not fade into meaningless rustling.

It was still that same many-voiced swarm of foreign whispers that seemed to come not from one place, but from everywhere at once. And this time Sakura listened more carefully, truly tried to understand, and heard:

“Look down...” the wind whispered to her.

She froze.

There was more than one voice. No, that was not right. There were many. Far too many. They whispered almost at the same time, overlapping each other, but not arguing or tangling together, more as if they were picking up the same thought from different sides.

“Below, under the stone...”“Under the stone, Sakura...”“Look down...”“Under the stone...”

Sakura slowly turned, feeling tension wake up inside her again, though this time it was different: not the animal fear of claws and teeth, but a familiar, wary alertness. She swallowed.

“I wonder if people ever get used to this,” she muttered to herself, looking at the floor around the altar.

But she still crouched down to get a better look.

Sakura ran her palm over one slab, then another, listening not so much to the sound as to instinct, to that strange new sensation that came to her in moments of danger and sudden clarity. The whisper moved over the stone again.

“Lower...”“A little to the left, Sakura.”

“Okay,” Sakura said quietly. “I got it. Probably.”

One of the slabs right at the base of the altar seemed a little thinner than the others and did not sit as tightly in place. A narrow crack ran along its edge, packed with dust. Sakura dug her fingers under the slab and began pushing it carefully aside. The slab was lighter than it looked, as if something really had been hidden beneath it and no one had wanted to seal it away forever. When it finally gave, Sakura found a shallow hollow beneath it. Inside lay a book.

She lifted it out carefully with both hands, as if she were afraid it would fall apart in her fingers. The cover had once been dark, maybe leather, but now it had faded, cracked, and darkened in places with stains that looked far too much like dried blood. The edges of the pages had yellowed and stuck together in places, but the book was still holding. Letters had been pressed into the cover. Letters she had never seen before. Sakura stared at them, ready to admit that she could not understand a single symbol, and in that same moment realized that she was already reading.

Records of D. J. Crowlis.

She drew in a sharp breath and frowned at once.

“How can I read this?”

Sakura turned the book over, looked at the letters again, and slowly ran her finger over the imprint. The language was unknown to her. The shapes of the letters were strange, unfamiliar. Some of the symbols had too many angles, others too few. She did not know how it was written, did not know how it might sound out loud, could not have repeated the alphabet or named a single rule. And yet she was reading it. Simply reading. The meaning appeared in her mind before she even had time to be surprised.

Sakura slowly sat down right there by the altar, placed the book on her knees, and stared at it for a while, feeling the whisper in the basement fade, as if the voices had gotten what they wanted and were satisfied now. Then she carefully opened the first page. Several sheets were stuck together with old blood. Some lines were completely smeared. In some places the ink had run, in others it had faded away. But farther in, the text became clearer.

The handwriting changed from page to page, as if the book had been written by different versions of the same person. Sometimes the lines were even, dry, almost pedantic. Sometimes the symbols sagged and broke apart, as if the writer had been writing on the move or could no longer keep a steady hand.

Sakura began reading the only clear phrase.

There is no way back.

Below it ran a long smeared streak drowned in a dark stain, and then the text continued on the next page.

The Obsidian Hierophant is a mistake. It must never happen again.

Sakura straightened without meaning to. For a moment she wanted to close the book, but curiosity and the desperate need to know won out. She kept reading.

I thought I understood this magic. I thought I would be able to limit it, to direct it. That was a lie. It belongs to no one. It only pretends to obey.

Part of the next page had been torn away, and what remained of the words drowned in dark stains. Sakura could make out only separate words: Power, Scarlet flowers, Ash.

Sakura slowly lowered the book onto her knees, then lifted it again at once. Her hand brushed the mark on her wrist for a second. Everything inside her tightened. Obsidian Hierophant. Her class. The very same one someone named Crowlis had written about. Someone who had already been here. Someone who, judging by everything, had not ended well.

She looked at the text again.

They are hunting me. And they will keep hunting me until they kill me. They fear me, and I cannot blame them. Soon EVERYONE will fear me...

Below that, the handwriting turned harsher.

No one must become the second Obsidian Hierophant. This magic will die with me.

Sakura’s mouth went dry. This magic had not died with Crowlis. It had remained alive in this chamber, and somehow Sakura had been able to activate it and take it into herself. She had become the second Obsidian Hierophant.

Sakura turned more pages. Several of them were badly damaged, but here and there words had still survived.

My students are dead. This magic killed them. Only Nobus and Riana escaped. May the gods stand by them. May they live on without my teaching.

On the last page that could still be read, the text had nearly been pressed into the paper, as if the writer could no longer control the pressure of his hand.

Damn it, what else is hidden inside this magic? I cannot stop my research.

And that was all.

“There has to be something else. I need to know,” Sakura whispered, biting her lip. “I can’t... end like this too. I want to stay alive.”

Sakura kept staring at the last line for a while, hoping that if she looked long enough, something more would appear, some instruction, some explanation, some way to refuse it, some way to turn back, some way to stop being what the System had just called her. But paper stayed paper.

She did not close the book right away. When she finally did, she did it slowly and carefully, as if she were afraid of waking something that had already awakened more than enough in this place.

The wind stirred beside her again, and the barely audible whisper said:

“Sakura must keep going.”

Sakura frowned.

“But where am I supposed to go?”

“To where it is safer.”

After that, the wind said nothing more.

And Sakura understood three things with absolute clarity. First, she had landed herself in a catastrophic mess. Second, she had to hide her class and her abilities if she did not want to end up like Crowlis. Third, she needed to find his students, because they might know the answers... They might be able to help.

Sakura had no intention of dying again. If she had been given a second life, even in a world this strange and dangerous, she would not give up. She would fight for that life.
