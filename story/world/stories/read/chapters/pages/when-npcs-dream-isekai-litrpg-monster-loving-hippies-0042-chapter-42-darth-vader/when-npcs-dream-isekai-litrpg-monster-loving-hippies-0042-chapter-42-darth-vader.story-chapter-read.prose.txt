He explained that anyone could keep their current roles in the town if they chose, and anyone who wished to leave could do so freely and without consequence.

The Baron, his captain of the guard, the master of coin, the chief advisor, and the high priest—all of them would have to step down and leave Kamatouu. They would be replaced by new officials.

The new governor would be Hina, now promoted to the title of Baroness.

As governor, Hina would have full authority. She could appoint or remove officials, change roles, and make decisions as she pleased.

As for taxes, it would be as follows:

Then Felix stepped back, and it was Hina’s turn to speak.

That was... a weird way to introduce yourself. Oh—I get it. She probably said "Yoroshiku onegaishimasu!" in japanese and the system translated it that way. I’ve watched enough anime to know.

Personally, I would’ve taken Rash’gul the Goblin Chief over this sparkly-eyed princess.

“All changes will take effect by tomorrow,” Felix added. “For now, everyone may return to their duties.”

The NPCs understood that part well enough. Their expressions didn’t change as they quietly returned to work. Only the players—on both sides—remained in the common room.

“This is all well and good,” Juliette said loudly, “but we’re not done here.”

That got the attention of the Order of the Phoenix.

“This is our town,” she continued. “We’ll allow you to govern it, but we have conditions.”

“First condition.” Juliette spoke with sharp clarity. “We treat NPCs with the utmost respect. Any harm or harassment directed toward them will be considered a serious crime and punished harshly.”

That drew a snort of laughter from a tall, pale-skinned player with blond hair, but Felix silenced him with a quick wave of his hand.

“Continue,” Felix said.

“Second condition,” Juliette went on. “There’s a goblin tribe in the forest. They are our allies. You will not harm them.”

That one raised more than a few eyebrows among the Order. I could already tell this would be a problem.

“Third and final condition: no XP farming through mass monster killing in our forest. No extermination campaigns against the Scorpion Dog Tribe without consulting me first.”

“No,” Juliette cut him off. “These aren’t suggestions. These are our conditions. You’ll comply now.”

“Look now,” said the blond player, clearly annoyed, “Felix is trying to be reasonable. You should be more respectful.”

“It’s fine, Oliver,” Felix said calmly, trying to keep things level.

But Oliver wasn’t done. “I just don’t think she understands her position,” he continued, turn to Juliette, “You think you’re playing some game here, but it’s obvious to everyone that you’re low-level newbies. Your threats mean nothing. You should be thankful we’re even entertaining your opinions.”

This time, Felix didn’t interrupt. He just looked to Juliette, waiting to see what she’d say.

“You shouldn’t underestimate us,” Juliette said calmly. “Farhad’s group did. And now? They’re half-dead narcs in a cage—at least, the ones still breathing.” She gestured to me. “My crippled mage here, for example, could kill all of you in your sleep easily.”

“Come on,” Oliver chuckled. “That’s adorable. You’re not scaring anyone, little missy.”

Okay—now I need to pause the story for a second.

You see, in all my life—past or future—and I say this with certainty:I will never, ever, be as cool as I was in the next moment.

My oxygen magic circle had been in place ever since Oliver started running his mouth. Hidden beneath the wooden floorboards, invisible. My oxygen spheres were already in position.

So now, slowly, I turned my head toward Oliver.

I raised my right hand—dramatically—and as I placed the oxygen sphere inside Oliver’s lungs, I made a small clenching gesture.

And I said, loud and clear:

“I find your lack of faith disturbing.”

Oliver smiled at first.

Then his smile faded.

He began gasping, hyperventilating—his eyes wide, clutching at his throat.

“I can’t... I can’t breathe!”

“Enough!” Felix shouted, a flicker of panic in his voice.

But I didn’t let go—not yet. My eyes stayed on Juliette.

She took her sweet time, and finally, with a casual flick of her hand, said, “Release him.”

So I did.

“So cool,” Santiago chimed in.

Honestly, I was kind of hoping someone would step in. Even a regular human could kick my crippled ass—let alone a Super Saiyan jerk like Oliver.

I’ll agree not to actively hunt their tribe. But I can’t tell other players not to kill wild ones on sight. Like it or not, they’re extremely dangerous.”

You’ll be in charge of all the maids and servants. You know the NPCs already—it’s perfect! What do you say?”

“Twenty silvers,” Hina countered, still smiling.

“Deal,” Juliette said.

“Awesome!” said Hina. “Now come on—you have to show me the place!”

And with that, our new governor and her new best friend Juliette disappeared off together.

“Our leader is John Smith—I’m his second-in-command. Most of us follow the main questline and help King Azumuth expand the kingdom. Some members don’t participate in combat, though. They contribute in other ways. A few even settled into comfy NPC jobs and just... take it easy.”

Think, think—I wanted to find something important to ask! But Hyun-woo beat me to it.

I reminded her of my… limited physical condition, but Lori, as always, had the perfect solution.

Make something legendary.
