When Maus used Echo, the pulses he sent out would bring back outlines of living beings the pulses resonated off. Maus called these forms ‘imprints’. An imprints wasn’t a physical form; it was that inside part of them.

Some of them were warm, some cold, but one thing held true: Every living being had one. Maus stationed himself nearby while he collected his information.

A stall owner shouting at customers about their wares looked angry, but their echo was bright, warm, safe.

Another stall owner seemed gentle and kind, wore a smile. But in their echo he could see the cold sharpness of them. There was a subtle wrongness to their echo, a slight repellent force that Maus couldn’t quite explain.

Children were often not nearly as bright, but also not nearly so dark, at least the well-dressed ones. Those ones were often, bright enough, their edges softer, their dark patches less consuming.

Then there were those ragged ones, the ones who hid much like him in corners, said scant few words, played quietly out of the way and amongst themselves only. Maus found those echoes curious. Many of them were light, and many of them were warm, but they were not nearly so bright. Not nearly so… whole.

The energy around them gave him the same feeling as the frayed edge of a beautiful shirt, or static on a TV screen. Not bad, just worn in a subtle way of something that should seem better kept.

He'd have to ask the Boss about that too.

Eventually, Maus had a full layout of the city. He’d marked the strongest and the weakest, the patrol routes of the guards, and the location of the prison, which was directly where he headed next.

He certainly could have fought his way through, but the Boss hated inefficiency, and fighting would definitely have slowed him down. So instead, he simply Blinked.

He Blinked and hid, then Blinked again, waited for his fatigue to go down and his mana to recover before continuing.

Maus knew that getting into the prison would be the easy part. It was like a game of hide and seek where his advanced sensory perception let him know when to do which. The trickiest bit had been the wards around the building, something he might have struggled with perhaps even a day earlier.

Instead, he used it as an opportunity to refine the spiritual cloaking technique he and the Boss had worked on at the library.

Truly, Libraries were a place for learning. He nodded at Mother’s wisdom.

Having made his way past the invisible barrier that radiated malevolence, Maus was quick to navigate the prison with much the same caution as the city. It appeared the people in charge here really trusted that barrier because the prison itself was light on security. This changed once he made it to the actual hallway where the prisoners were kept.

Here, Maus paused to access the Inventory function of the Interface. He knew his was connected to the Boss’s, and that was connected to the storage bag.

The key was there.

Now he just needed to grab it and fit it to every door with a prisoner in it while avoiding the gaze of the two guards stationed at either end of the long hallway of the stone dungeon.

As the Boss would say, simple didn’t mean easy.

Getting in wasn’t his first major obstacle. It was figuring out who he needed to get out.

Cells lined the walls on either side of the hall. Enchanted metal bars faced outwards, while each prisoner’s enclosure was separated by a smooth stone wall. Likely some form of elemental mana manipulation, though with none of the refinement the Boss had shown when she used earth.

The light in this place was dismal, and Maus was not impressed by the level of sanitation. The smell was an assault on his sensitive nose, and he wished he had the fine control the Boss had over the elements so he could create a wind barrier without alerting everybody that there was an intruder lurking just past the door.

There were about ten cells, each roughly four square meters in size, and only three were occupied.

How Maus was going to test the key, he wasn’t quite sure. Blink onto the key latch and hide himself there?

What about when he actually tested it? These guards weren’t Tier 1 yet, but they were certainly high Tier 0. If they were guarding prisoners, sensory Skills were likely to be part of their repertoire, as well as high Perception.

It was still morning, so they were all awake as the pathetic amount of light they had access to trickled through the thin stone slits that made up the sole window in each cell. The prisoners were not speaking, nor were they particularly active.

Maus observed for a while longer, thinking, when suddenly the door behind him opened.

Change of guard and breakfast rotation.

Maus was glad he’d filled up earlier because this stuff did not look humane to feed to anyone. Food should not be grey, Maus decided. Brown and beige, perhaps, but grey?

That just felt like cruel and unusual punishment.

Either way, this was his opportunity.

The person bringing in the food looked like an attendant. They were accompanied by a guard with a key ring.

Maus looked at the key in his own paws. It was roughly two-thirds the length of his body.

He remembered going on jobs with the Boss in that hotel room. She never barged up to the potential target and just asked what their name was. She would wait patiently, observe the situation, and look for identifying details to match the person to the profile.

That was what Maus needed to do.

He already knew what the key looked like. He just needed to match it to the one on the ring.

Sneaking through the shadows, Maus Blinked into the base of the cart. He honestly didn’t even need to hide. There was already a rat here, fat and plump and apparently significantly more tolerant of the grey food than Maus would have been.

He watched as the guard unlocked the first cell.

Nope.

The key looked different.

The prisoner was handed their food before the cell was relocked and they moved on to the next.

By the time they’d made it to the final cell, Maus figured this one had to be it. Only when the guard went to open the door, it was once again a different key.

None of them were the same shape as the key they’d received from the Historian. None of them were even the right size.

Maus reflexively groomed the fur on the top of his head as he thought.

Should he leave? No. He was sure he had the right building. There was no other prison.

Was the quest referring to some other kind of ‘prisoner’? What other kind was there?

Maus reached out to touch the bond. He knew Boss would have an idea.

Only the bond was quiet.

Not scary quiet, like his time in that pool when he’d been beyond the beyond. He didn’t remember much of that. Instead, the bond was a soft kind of quiet. A warm and sleepy kind of quiet. The Boss was asleep.

Maus would have felt wrong waking her up. Not for this.

Maus was a good boy, and he was super smart. He could figure this one out.

To stay or to leave.

From inside the cart, he could see the attendant working. He counted the bowls the lumpy mixture had been ladled into.

Three prisoners. Four bowls. One left.

Maus wondered if perhaps there was a cell he’d missed.

But how?

He’d mapped the entire prison. He was positive.

His ability to sense mana wasn’t nearly as good as the Boss’s, but he had his own methods. He used Echo to bounce a pulse off everything he could see and–

Wait.

He’d bounced it off everything he could see.

Were there things he couldn’t see? Who said the prison ended where his eyes did?

Maybe it was like the bunker and there was a secret place beneath the secret place.

He wanted to spread out his mana, see if he couldn’t get any proper feedback, but leaking the volume of mana required would risk his presence being detected. If he went slowly enough to avoid notice, the time it took him to search would be astronomical.

No.

He should try Echo with pure spiritual energy.

Boss had used her psionics in Skills before. Maybe he could do the same thing?

Maus pushed.

Energy flooded out of him, slowly expanding farther and farther.

The guard just continued walking. He didn’t turn around, instead moving towards the guard at the far end of the hall, where he was propped against a wall.

Only, Echo was showing him something else. Something behind the wall?

He couldn’t really ‘see’ anything in the conventional sense. Instead, he could somewhat feel the shape of a space. But mostly, he could feel the lives.

The dull luminescence of the rat currently sharing his ride. The much brighter shapes of the three guards and the attendant.

And somewhere beyond that wall was another echo.

Luminous, but frayed at the edges, like the hungry children on the streets, only much stronger.

Then the guard at the far end of the hall took out a key that looked identical to the one in Maus’s paws. Then he handed it to the food guard handling the food.

After handing over the key, the stationary guard stepped aside, revealing smooth stone.

He pulled hard on a mounted torchlight, and Maus watched what had been a dead-end stone wall slip sideways, and open into another, narrower passageway.

At the end of this new hall was a door. The thing was made up of a pale type of metal with a narrow opening set into the centre of it, just big enough for a key. A couple of wall sconces glowed to life.

That was it.

He watched as the guard placed their hand on the door, and Maus could feel a faint energy forming a pattern that Maus figured was interacting somehow with the door. Then they slipped the key into the lock with a practiced motion and turned it.
