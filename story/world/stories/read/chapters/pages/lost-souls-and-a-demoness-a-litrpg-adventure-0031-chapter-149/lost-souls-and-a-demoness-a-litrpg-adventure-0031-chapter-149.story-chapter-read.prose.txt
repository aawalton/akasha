No matter how many times Jade asked her friend if she was sure about her decision, Siora's answer remained the same. She was determined to walk the path of a Wayfarer, wherever it led. Jade respected the resolve it took to make that choice, even as she worried for her friend. Siora was a gentle person at heart, and most of her contributions in combat came in the form of healing spells and pre-supplied potions. Those made her a vital addition to the team, but becoming a Wayfarer was a big commitment. Once you signed on there was no getting out of it until you either completed one hundred quests or died trying.

Glancing Siora's way, Jade saw no trace of worry in her face. It wasn't a mask, either. Those didn't work on Jade. She was genuinely serene as she followed Jade to the sacred grove. That was nearly as alarming. Siora was naturally curious and generally filled with infectious energy, bouncing from one place to another. Here in her woodland home, she should be scanning the area for interesting plants or telling Jade an amusing story from her childhood. But the weight of her decision lay heavy on her shoulders, smothering such quirks.

In the end, Jade had to admire her for the decision. This path had been thrust upon her, completely upending her life. If Jade had been given the choice when this all began, she was pretty sure she would have walked away. Even now, the idea of taking all these problems, the looming threats, and letting someone else take responsibility for them carried seductive appeal. But it was too late for that, now. They were in the middle of this mess, and the only way out was to get stronger and win.

"There it is." Jade said, pointing ahead. Through the thinning trees she could just make out the forest clearing, brightly lit by shafts of golden sunlight. "Almost there."

"Is everything alright? You sound a little nervous."

Jade thought back to the strange visions she'd witnessed during her first visit. They clung to her still, stirring up distant, painful memories.

I've already conquered you. She thought, banishing them to the dark corners of her mind. Stay away.

That wasn't really how recovery from mental trauma worked, and she knew it. You couldn't just will it away or wait for it to heal like a physical wound, it took time and concerted effort. She'd started on that path by opening up to her friends, but it would take more than that.

"I just had a strange experience, last time I was here."

"Strange?"

"Memories from the past." She muttered, scuffing her boot against the dirt as they descended along a winding path leading to the grove. "I saw them again. Like I was there."

"You had a vision?" The elf sounded surprised. "You never told us about it."

"It was… personal."

"Sorry, I didn't mean to pry into anything uncomfortable." Siora said, contritely. "But the echo of Lady Elaria doesn't manifest for many people."

"Echo?"

"What we call remnants of the lost gods' power. They may be absent, but their influence lingers. Anyway, maybe that's why the priest didn't have an issue with us collecting a few blossoms. They can sense such things. I was expecting more resistance."

Upon arriving in Lysara, Siora had helped Jade seek out the priest responsible for maintaining Elaria's place of worship. The priest, a slight elf named Kymil, greeted them warmly and agreed to their request without much pushing. Jade had thought it had something to do with how they'd helped the town with the undead stronghold, the priest had been on that excursion as well, but Siora's comment made her wonder. Could he somehow recognize she'd had that experience? Did he know it's nature?

And, more pressingly, would she have another?

There was only one way to find out. Jade entered the clearing with Siora in tow, admiring its tranquil beauty. Sunlight glimmered on the still water of the forest pool from which the slender trunk of a delicate sapling rose. The sacred tree was in full bloom, bright pink blossoms dotting its leafy boughs like blots of paint on green canvas. Beside her, Siora lowered herself to her knees and bowed, arms extended and brushing the low-cut grass before her. Jade did her best to mimic the motion. She may not know the nature of the lost gods nor the presence that inhabited this grove, but it was real. Her magical senses tingled, feeling as if a great power lay just beyond her perception. In the face of such a force, a gesture of respect was more than prudent.

The two of them rose after several moments, and Siora gestured for Jade to proceed.

"How many do we need?" Jade asked. "I don't want to gather more than we have to.""Three, to be safe." Siora said quietly. The air was still in the grove, the wind silent. Neither of them wanted to disturb this place of peace.

Jade nodded, then stepped up to the edge of the pool. Its surface was mirror smooth, unblemished by fallen leaf or blossom. It was large enough that she'd have to wade into it to reach the sapling's boughs. Jade briefly contemplated shifting into her demon form and using her wings but discarded the idea almost immediately. She wasn't the most religious person around, but even she knew that adopting a demonic form in a holy place was asking to be smote from the heavens, absent gods or not. As a succubus, being here at all was pushing her luck.

Not to mention that I just killed an angel.

But everything had worked out well during her first visit, so there was probably nothing to worry about. Jade took a deep breath, then stepped into the pool. The water came up to her knees and was surprisingly warm, plastering her loose pants to her skin. She took a few, cautious steps, noting that the tree didn't look so small from within the pool. Reaching up, she gently plucked three blossoms from the closes branch, one after another. Immediately, a light breeze swirled through the clearing, breaking the peaceful silence with the rustling of branches and grass. The water pulsed with transcendent warmth, feeling like it went straight through Jade's skin and into somewhere far deeper. Then, for the first time since she'd first been granted it, her interface flickered.

What's going on?

Jade slowly backed away, looking around, the blossoms clutched tightly in her fist. Her interface fuzzed again, then snapped back into place. A notification popped up.

System Notice

Please stand by.

Stand by? For what?

The sensation of warmth grew, and Jade finally recognized it. It was the same feeling she'd had in that strange dream. Where that distant voice-

"Jade…"

There it was again. A distant call. Faint, but distinctively. Feminine, weary, but with a hint of hope.

"Find me."

She could barely hear it, but the intent was clear. People in need of help had a distinctive manner of speaking. Their tone, the inflection, even in those few words, it was apparent to Jade.

"Who are you?" she asked, backing to the edge of the pool. Faint, golden light shone from it. It flickered, like a candle wavering in the wind. "Where are you?"

"Down." The light pulsed in time with her words. It was already beginning to fade. "Where the seed remembers… the dawn of worlds... Time grows short. The demons are working their magic. The walls of their prison are stretched to the limit. Soon…"

"We know they're trying to escape." Jade said, her heartbeat quickening. Who was this? What was this? Could it really be…? "How do we stop them?!"

"Vastrozun… No seal can hold him forever… We must… We…" Jade could barely hear her, now. She knelt in the water, letting the fading warmth flow through her as she strained to listen.

"Come to me… You… One of the few… Stop this…"

And then it was gone.

"It really was the goddess…" Siora said, awed. "I never thought I'd feel her presence. Hear Lady Elaria's voice…"

They were back in the guest house with the rest of the party, relaying their experience in the sacred grove. Siora and Jade had spoken with a priest of Elaria who, after visiting the grove with them, confirmed their suspicions. The Lady of Blossoms' presence lingered in the pool, an echo of her power. The look the priest had given her after hearing what had happened bordered on a reverence that Jade found disconcerting. The news was already spreading like wildfire, whipping up a fervor amidst the normally peaceful town.

"Has something like this happened before?" Luis asked, looking around. "What does it mean?"

"Not that I've ever heard of…" Fenrin shook his head. "The gods have been gone for thousands of years. Their faiths persist, clustered around remnants of ancient power, but it's never…"

"I studied records of the ancient cataclysm." Tara said. The tek'kalla was pacing back and forth across the room, projecting an air of nervous energy. "Divine intervention all but vanished right as the demons were destroyed… Or I guess now we know they were locked away."

"Our people barely have any records of that time." Fenrin said. "That was before our ancestors migrated into the Labyrinth. I was told they did it to escape the devastation of the demons wars, but I don't think anyone knows for sure."

"The Labyrinth hasn't always existed." Jade said, glancing at Siora. "You told us that, when we first met. Now we know it's a prison for the demons. Somehow sealing them away.""Are you saying the gods made it?" Naomi asked. "I still can't believe we're having a serious discussion about this…"

Jade shared that sentiment, but at this point, she'd encountered so much absurdity that a conversation with a long-lost goddess only shook her a little. As opposed to leaving her hyperventilating in the corner of the room.

"It's my best guess." Jade said with a shrug. "Maybe making it did something to them, and it's why they aren't around anymore.""That's one of many theories I've studied." Tara agreed, finally stopping. "I never delved into religious history much, though. It wasn't my area."

"Too focused on learning how to blow things up better?" Luis asked with a grin.

"Of course." Tara crossed her arms and held her head high. "It's a far more practical branch of research."

"Back on topic." Fenrin said, making a gesture that got everyone's attention. "The… goddess…" He said the word hesitantly. "Asked you to find her, Jade.""That's what it sounded like." Jade said, Siora nodding beside her. "She said something about where the seed remembers, and the dawn of worlds."

"Oh, wonderful." Tara threw up her hands. "This is why I never liked religious studies. Everything is always a riddle, or some strange metaphor. Why can't divine beings ever just say what they mean?"

"The dawn of worlds…" Naomi mused, ignoring Tara's ramblings. "What could that mean?"

"You could think of the Labyrinth as many different worlds." Luis suggested, gently patting Tara on the shoulder in a consoling manner. "Maybe she's at the center of the Labyrinth, and wants Jade to find her there?"

"I thought the demons were at the center of the Labyrinth." Jade said. "They're trapped by it."

"They could be there together." Luis shrugged.

"I hope not… That wouldn't be good."

They continued the discussion, throwing out various ideas on what the goddess meant. Clearly, she was as worried about the demons escaping their prison as they were. If she had a way to help contain them, then finding her had to be their highest priority. Unfortunately, all they had to work with was vague guesses and assumptions. They couldn't search for her if they didn't know where to look. The Labyrinth's quest system, normally quite helpful when it came to things like this, was unresponsive. No markers appeared on their maps, nor did they receive any related quests. Jade found that odd, although she couldn't quite put her finger on why. And then there was the way her interface had behaved…

"We should talk to Aylin." Luis suggested once they'd run out of ideas. "She's studied the Labyrinth her whole life, right? Maybe she'll know what she meant."

"Siora, does your town have a library?" Tara asked. "I could do some digging, if we're going to be here a few days while you work on brewing the potion."

"That's a good idea." Naomi perked up. "I've been meaning to learn more about the Labyrinth's history. If we're going to be some of the first representatives of Earth, getting more background will help us make a better first impression."

"I plan on seeing what more Serrethis can teach me." Jade commented. "The more enemies we face, the more I realize just how right he was about technique and tactics being more important than power."

"That all sounds great." Luis said, nodding his head. "But you're all forgetting something more important.""What's that?"

"Taking a break. No, really." He added, holding up a hand before Jade could interrupt. "We just went through some crazy shit. We're bouncing from one crisis to the next, getting more and more wound up. Sooner or later, that tension is gonna make one of us snap. So, we're going to take a day to unwind."

As loath as she was to admit it, Jade knew he had a point. Hyper focusing on training had cost her dearly in the past, and she had promised to heed her friends' advice. Everyone else agreed, and Siora pitched an idea.

"Let's go to the hot springs together!"

They'd visited the small natural springs during their previous stay in Lysara, bathing after days of intensive training. But that was different than a day of real relaxation where they had the time to truly enjoy them. The elf's suggestion was met with enthusiastic agreement, and it was decided. Tomorrow would be just for them. Riddles, lost goddesses, and saving the world from a demon apocalypse could wait for a day. Hopefully.
