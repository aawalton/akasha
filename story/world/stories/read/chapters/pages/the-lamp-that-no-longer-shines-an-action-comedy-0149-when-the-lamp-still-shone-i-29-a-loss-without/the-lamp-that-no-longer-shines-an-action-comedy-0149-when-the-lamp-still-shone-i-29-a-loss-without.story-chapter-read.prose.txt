[Time]: Year 1921[Location]: Milan'thir · The Palace of Rex Mundi

Two years had not produced a cipher.

Tasia sat by the bay window, watching. Three floors below, Ovelia was walking the second-floor balcony railing: balanced on its narrow iron edge with the thoughtless ease of a stray cat who found railings more philosophically interesting than floors. She was humming something tuneless.

She never concealed her nature. That complete transparency somehow made her infinitely harder to grasp.

Tasia could not name what pooled in her chest whenever Ovelia was in the room. She only knew that looking elsewhere had stopped being an option.

The sudden, crystalline clarity of Ovelia's laugh when something genuinely amused her. The drifting, weightless cadence of her footsteps through the manor halls. The cold, absent pleasure of fingers tangling in Tasia's hair on a total whim, and then the equally immediate, total detachment. An offensive against the senses with no defensive architecture to counter.

So when the ambient mana in the manor began to hum with the distinct, pressurized frequency of a masterwork nearing completion, and the adults' low, careful voices confirmed that Ovelia's long preparations were finally culminating, Something quiet bloomed beneath Tasia's ribs.

Alucard did not share it.

In the parlor, she sat rigid, one-year-old Liandra clutched to her chest with the protective tension of a soldier guarding the last supply cache. Her jaw was set. She had been staring at the empty doorway for the better part of an hour.

Tasia understood the friction perfectly. Alucard's entire psychological architecture relied on control: variables mapped, outcomes predicted, risks managed. Ovelia was a walking null-value who had the entire household by the nose without asking permission. Alucard despised things she could not categorize.

"You are my absolute last hope, Lia," Alucard muttered, pressing her forehead against the toddler's golden hair. "Do not let her corrupt you. Hold the line."

Liandra blinked. Her large, shining eyes held the pure, untroubled clarity of a creature with no conceptual framework for household politics.

She knew the fundamental truths of her tiny universe: she loved Ash more than anything, and Ash's time was endlessly diverted by the strange woman. By all toddler logic, resentment was the correct response.

But whenever Ovelia leaned down, her eyes curved into those crescent moons, resentment became biologically impossible.

Still, Alucard was holding her very warmly and looking very serious. So Liandra offered a solemn nod.

Alucard exhaled a long, aggrieved sigh and pulled her closer.

Then, the absences began to stretch.

Ovelia had always vanished without explanation. But as the preparations entered their final phase, the disappearances grew longer. Days bled into a week. With every empty chair at the table, the barometric pressure in the dining room dropped.

The adults performed an exhaustive theater of normalcy: tea poured, correspondence attended to, the polished rhythm of the household maintained. But navigating the estate in her absence was precisely like navigating a familiar room missing a floorboard. The absence announced itself with every step.

The tension snapped at dinner.Francesca set her fork down with a deliberate, measured clink.

"What is the matter with the Light Dragon?" she said, addressing the chandelier above the table. "Ash and Lia both carry the apex genes of the Deer-Dragon sub-branch. Physical dominance without equal. Or the Fae-Fox Dragon, if she prefers arcana: the Hortanian royal bloodline. Do you know the political capital I spent securing those genetic factors? A premier, rare, spellcasting lineage with limitless potential."

She gripped the mahogany edge, her knuckles white. "If there were a viable methodology for a Primordial Dragon conversion, we would have found a way to approach the Golden Dragon sequence ourselves. That alone is biological insanity. But the Golden Dragon is not grand enough for her. She wants an Apocalypse Dragon."

Angie set her wine glass down. Her eyes moved briefly across the table to Tasia, catching the girl's silent, watching gaze.

"The last Apocalypse Dragon extinguished itself one thousand nine hundred years ago," Angie said. "The same moment the first witch opened her eyes."

She looked at Francesca. "You cannot reverse-engineer a fulfilled prophecy."Beside Tasia, Alucard drove her fork into her roasted meat with enough force to scrape the porcelain.

From the high chair, Liandra tilted her head, small fingers drumming the tray. "Why not?" she said, with the immaculate confidence of a toddler who has not yet learned about impossible things. "Apoca... Apoca-lyptic sounds much stronger than golden. And I can't picture Ovelia all shiny and gold. It doesn't suit her at all."

Francesca let out a long, shuddering sigh that seemed to drain every remaining particle of optimism from her frame. She turned her gaze to the head of the table.

"Ash," she said softly. "You have to talk to her."

Ash looked at her empty plate. The skin at her collarbone was pale, threaded with the faint, luminescent webbing of her overloaded circuits. A quiet, brittle tension lived in the set of her shoulders.

She set her knife down. "I'll go," she said.

It was a beautiful night. A perfectly circular moon hung in the void, drowning the ancient forest in pale, cold light.

Ash found her on one of the Milan'thirskaya family's private floating islands, along with several others they had simply deeded over to Ovelia, because the household operated on a foundational inability to deny her anything she asked.

The primeval timber here had reorganized itself around her: shadows bleeding outward from the ancient trunks, the ambient temperature stripped away, the air biting at the lungs and tasting of frost.

At the base of a massive, gnarled tree, a pitch-black lake had well up from a subterranean river flowing backward against every rational law, pooling in perfect, terrible stillness. It absorbed the moonlight without offering a single ripple in return.

Floating above the dark water, casting the only warmth in the freezing dark, were the [Wisp Lantern Cats].

They were ecological impossibilities, myths whispered among navigators: spawning only where a wild, untamed floating island exceeded three thousand meters in altitude, where a primeval forest grew, and where a subterranean river ran upward against physics.

Soft, glowing embers in the cold. Born, by all accounts, with a single instinct: to find lost travelers in the suffocating dark and guide them home.

Ovelia sat beneath the ancient tree, bathed in their amber light.

When she saw Ash step into the clearing, her expression opened. The habitual detachment was entirely gone. She looked like a scholar who had just proved a centuries-old theorem.

"You chose the Apocalypse Dragon," Ash said. The words plumed white in the freezing air."Ah." Ovelia smiled: light, airy, carrying the effortless confidence of someone who had already done the math. "Of course."

"How do you intend to do it?"Ovelia released the glowing cat she had been cradling. She stood, her hands already moving through the cold air, mapping invisible architecture.

"The methodology is beautifully clean," she said, stepping forward, the manic brilliance burning in her eyes. "I will use the closest surviving subordinate branch of the lineage. Through continuous purification, I will use the omnipotent adaptivity of raw mana to force-feed the draconian genetics, creating a feedback loop that traces the bloodline backward to its most exalted progenitor."

She turned, her smile sharpening into something radiant and a little unhinged. "By deliberately triggering total genetic collapse, I will shatter the modern sequence and reverse-engineer the fragments into the original, primordial source code—"

She stopped.She looked at Ash.

Her sharp mind processed the silence, parsed the expression. She waved a hand, clean and dismissive, sweeping away the remainder of the technical explanation.

"I need an assistant to monitor the collapse thresholds," Ovelia said simply. "You will come, won't you?"

Ash looked at her."No," Ash said. Her voice was slow, and heavy, and absolute. "I will not help you."

Ovelia froze.

The easy, fluid grace locked into sudden, total stillness. Her eyes went wide. The reaction was the exact physical translation of a cat's tail swinging in comfortable rhythm, and then pinned, mid-arc, by a completely unanticipated sound.

Ash stepped into the light of the Wisp Cats.

"The underlying principle of this methodology," she said, her voice shaking with the effort of holding it level, "is exactly the same as [Mana Overload Syndrome]. Isn't it? A continuous, fatal genetic collapse sustained by a lethal volume of raw mana."

Her hands balled into fists at her sides. Frost plumed from her lips with every breath. "You think you can control it. You cannot. You will die, Ovelia. The very best outcome is that you survive and end up exactly like me—"

The last word cracked.

Ash stopped. The aristocratic composure shattered entirely, stripped away to leave only the raw, bleeding thing underneath.

"Stop this. Please, Ovelia. Nothing in this world is worth this risk. You are so young. You have more talent than anyone I have ever seen. There are other paths to power. Just wait. Give it a few more years—"

She stopped speaking.Because Ovelia was no longer listening.

The manic warmth that had animated her face only moments ago was gone, siphoned away in a single instant. Her gaze drifted past Ash's shoulder, locking onto a fixed coordinate in a dimension entirely her own.

Her eyes reflected nothing. She was looking at a scenery that had no room in its frame for anyone standing in front of her. Ash felt her words hit the empty air and die there, speaking into a script that had no role for her.

The silence lasted a single blink.Between one heartbeat and the next, the space Ovelia occupied was simply empty. The Wisp Lantern Cats vanished with her, their warm amber light extinguishing in a single frame.

Ash stood completely alone in the freezing dark, staring at the black water under the unblinking eye of the moon.

When Ash returned, the hour was dead.

The front door opened with a heavy, dragging click. Tasia stood in the foyer, a blanket around her shoulders, and looked up as Ash walked into the light of the hall chandelier.

A cold spike drove itself into her chest.

Ash looked hollowed out. Her complexion had crossed past pallor into ashen translucence, the structural pillars of her spirit visibly gutted, the faint, luminescent burn of her circuits pulsing weakly at her throat.

Liandra did not notice the atmosphere. She was stubbornly awake at the wrong hour, and the moment she saw Ash's face, she slipped smoothly from Alucard's arms and dropped to the marble floor.

She crossed the foyer in rapid, determined strides, her arms raised before she was halfway there. She hit Ash's legs.

"Ash," she commanded, with the precise, ringing clarity of a child who knew she was the absolute center of the universe. "Up."

Ash knelt. She gathered the toddler up and buried her face in Liandra's golden hair. For a moment, she simply breathed. The color returned to her face by microscopic fractions.

Tasia stood a few feet away, watching."Did Ovelia not come back with you?" she asked softly.

Ash did not nod. She did not shake her head. She pulled Liandra closer against her chest. Then, slowly, she raised her free arm and extended her hand toward Tasia, fingers curling in a wordless invitation.

Tasia stepped forward. Ash pulled her in.

Ash's body was burning: the unnatural, feverish heat of a biological engine fighting against its own magic, terrifyingly warm, pressing through the fabric and into Tasia's skin.

Tasia pressed her cheek against Ash's shoulder and closed her eyes. She didn't know what had been said by the lake.

The tears fell before she understood them. They came hot and silent and without permission, mourning a loss she didn't yet possess the vocabulary to understand.
