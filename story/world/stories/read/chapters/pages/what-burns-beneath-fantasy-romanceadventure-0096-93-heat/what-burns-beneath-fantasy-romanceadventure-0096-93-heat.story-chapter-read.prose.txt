Sedgewick Vale was a labyrinth of moonlit streets, shadowed buildings, and creaking signposts when we reached The Deeproot. Jareth held the door open, letting firelight spill out onto the stones, and we filed in one at a time.

Well, almost. Renner was second-to-last, in front of me. After a nod and a quiet murmur between him and Jareth, the paladin went inside.

Renner shut the door and turned towards me. Hazy silver-gray light illuminated a frown and furrowed brows. “You-”

I threw my arms around him, burying my face against his shoulder.

“-okay?” he grunted, clearly caught off guard.

I nodded. I was probably out of tears, but my throat was thick and I didn’t quite trust myself to speak.

Rough stubble scraped against my temple as warm, leather-clad palms closed over my hips. Very lightly, as if half-expecting to be nudged away, then with a firmness that made my heart flutter. Both hands slowly smoothed their way up my waist. Even through my tunic, his touch left a trail of heat behind.

We stayed like that for some time. Finally, voice husky at my ear, he murmured, “You gonna tell me why you’ve been crying for the past hour?”

A helpless little laugh burst from my lips and was muffled by the thick wool of his cloak. My shoulders shook against him. And here I thought I’d been subtle. Of course he noticed. Saints, he notices everything.

One thumb traced a little circle against my ribs, making me shiver from far more than the cold night air. “Ladyship?”

“Joselia… t-told me. About you.”

He stiffened. The pressure of his hands grew almost painfully tight and his voice dropped to a wary pitch. “About?”

“N-not anything personal. About how she healed you.” I sniffled against his collarbone, fresh heat pricking at my eyes. Apparently I wasn’t out of tears, after all.

He relaxed a bit. “What, her little magic potion?”

I nodded.

“And that made you cry?”

“She told me about the woman who gave it to her, Renner.”

He snorted. “What, some lady on the side of the road?”

The tears spilled out alongside helpless laughter. “Some lady?! Renner! A lady who could see the future! Who’d been waiting there for her!”

He didn’t say anything, but the way his frame slackened was answer enough.

I kept going, every word tumbling out in a deluge. “Who had something magical and dreamt about you and needed you to… saints, needed you to be able to fight! Renner-“

“I… get it.” He sounded hoarse. Dazed. “Salt, I get it.”

I sagged against him, burying my nose into the folds of his cloak and jerkin. Cold air thick with the scents of leather and pine filled my nostrils until the moment’s hysteria settled to one of calm. Of safety.

“Who knew how much I’d…” my breath hitched. I turned my face a bit, forehead brushing the underside of his chin. “I’d… need you.”

I immediately regretted the words.

They were too… big. Too real, too heavy, too much to put on him in the midst of all this mess. He was already shouldering all my burdens alongside the knowledge that he’d… ashes, that he’d apparently be tortured in the next few days, once everything came out and our deception was revealed.

It wasn’t fair to add the burden of me needing him. He deserved the freedom to walk away from all this at a moment’s notice, without any guilt or obligation to hold him back. Saints, I almost wanted him to leave! Pack his things and run, with Teela and Royce at his heels, and then I’d be the only one in danger.

And that was fine. That was good. I could handle danger. Not well, but better if I knew the people I cared about were safe. Everything Horace had warned of, iron chains and bloody snow and every inch of me being torn to shreds… I could bear it. I could, if the others were safe.

Every thought came crashing behind the other as I stood frozen and mortified, wishing beyond words that I’d kept my mouth shut.

Apparently, he disagreed.

“Need me, huh?” The husky pitch sent a shiver down my spine. His lips brushed the shell of my ear and his hands slid back down, settling on my hips.

I drew a ragged breath. “I-”

“Mm.” Stubble and warm breath dragged their way down the slope of my cheekbone. “I could get used to you saying things like that.”

I opened my mouth, though whether it was to apologize or explain or take it back or simply kiss him, I wasn’t quite sure.

His grip tightened, pushing back just enough to elicit a gasp and create a little gap between us. Then strong fingers were on the back of my neck, curling into my hair, tilting my head back and saints his eyes were dark from far more than shadows and they were drawing closer-

The door swung open with a creak and a rush of air. “Innkeeper’s got… uh...” Jareth’s baritone faltered.

The world froze. Firelight spilled out of the doorway, illuminating the now-scowling visage barely a breath from mine. Heat flooded my face as my eyes darted past Renner and to the dimpling figure behind him.

“Am I interrupting something?”

“Go away.” Renner’s snarl could have drawn blood, but it only made the other man’s grin widen.

My cheeks grew even warmer, then ignited fully as I caught sight of unruly blonde hair just inside the inn. Royce was turning away from the counter and towards us, and with a quick breath and a quicker step back I pulled free from Renner’s grip and shattered the possibility of my best friend seeing… something he didn’t need to see right now.

I’ve hurt him enough for one day.

“You sure?” Jareth casually leaned against the doorframe, voice smug.

Renner turned a withering glare on the paladin.

“I’m a natural at the whole moonlight doorstep rendezvous move, I could give you some point-”

“Jareth…” my whisper barely crept over the stones, but it was enough.

“Yes, ma’am.” He saluted me immediately, then spun back inside and shut the door with a chuckle.

I took another step back, wrapping both arms around my middle as frosty wind swept my hair and cloak to one side. “I shouldn’t have… I’m sorry.”

Renner’s scowl softened, but didn’t fade entirely. He regarded me with both brows lowered.

“I meant what I said. Earlier, about you and Teela and Royce, about the three of you leaving. You don’t…” I let my gaze fall, searching the shadowed stones helplessly. “You don’t owe me anything. I’d rather you be safe than-”

“Brin.”

Saints, no one had ever said my name quite like that before. Somehow managing to sound incredibly frustrated and achingly gentle at the same time.

“In the past ten days I’ve lost more sleep than I can keep track of, I’ve been taken prisoner, nearly fed to an insane monster, and had the back of my skull caved in.” He closed in, blocking out the night sky and looking for all the world like he couldn’t decide if he wanted to kiss me or throttle me. “And all of that happened while you were lying to me. You also set me on fire.”

“Renner, I’m so sor-”

His hands found my waist and yanked, forcing me close with a roughness that matched his tone. “I’m not here because I think I owe you something.”

My pulse fluttered as I peered up, bracing both palms against his chest. “So… so w-why are you here, then?”

He searched my eyes for a long moment. I almost thought he’d lean down again to capture my mouth with his own… until his shoulders sagged. When he spoke again, his voice was very soft. “I had a Fellbrook. Once.”

I swallowed. “Bellmere? Joselia told me the name, but not… not much more. She said that’s where she found you.”

“I grew up there. About ten years ago some… people… made pacts. Nothing like yours. They wanted fire and blood and…” he hesitated, then heaved a sigh. Warm breath washed over my face. “And you have enough nightmares without hearing the rest.”

“I’m so sorry, Renner.” My throat grew thick and his features blurred. I blinked the tears away, sucking in a rush of frosty air. “You… you don’t have to talk about it, but if you ever need to…”

“Yeah. Well.” He searched my face again, fingers curling into the folds of cloth at my waist. “My Fellbrook’s gone. Dead and buried, and I’ve made my peace with that. But I’ll be damned if I don’t get you back to yours.”

My heart throbbed. I wanted to laugh and collapse into tears and just bury myself in his arms all at the same time. I found myself frozen, quite unable to find my voice, and just stared into his eyes as the night crept on around us.

After what seemed an eternity he gave me a slow, sly smile. “Careful, ladyship. I could get used to you looking at me like that, too.”

My face burned. No, not just my face; my entire body ignited. I bit my lower lip, blushing madly, and gave him a little grin.

He chuckled at that, and then his expression turned thoughtful. “Speaking of nightmares…” He released my waist, making a little flare of disappointment surge through my belly. It turned into a very different kind of surge a moment later when he knelt with one hand settling against the inside of my thigh.

I felt every fragment of that pressure. My eyes rounded as I stared down with a little squeak, stunned still at the thought of exactly what in the realms he was-

“It won’t work on yours, but who knows what else you’ll run into tomorrow. At least you’ll have something.”

His other hand produced the slender, sheathed witchwood dagger. Its hilt glinted like a pearl beneath the moonlight before disappearing into my boot. The pressure against my thigh lifted as he stood back up.

I shook my head. “But that’s yours. I can’t-”

“Yeah, and now it’s yours.” He arched brow, eyes gleaming. “Probably a good thing Jareth gave me a knife, actually. I wouldn’t trust you with a sword. Not after watching you try to fight earlier.”

“Hey!”

“Don’t worry, we’ll keep practicing.”

“Oh. Great.” I huffed, rolling my eyes towards the cloudy, star-spattered sky. “I think your version of sparring is going to kill me, Renner.”

“Nah, I’ll keep being gentle.” His lips curled into that familiar, infuriating smirk. “We’ll go nice and slow. Get you all warmed up and ready.”

My cheeks burned even as frosty wind nipped at them. “Oh, for… you’re shameless, you know that? I take back what I said earlier, you’re much worse than Jareth.”

His laughter warmed the night air, and when Teela opened the inn door to announce that our dinner was growing cold I went inside with a light heart and a giddy grin.
