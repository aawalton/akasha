With the restaurant opening settled, I was finally allowed the luxury of a quiet routine.

It felt strange at first. After the riot scare, the goddess's endless food boon, and the whole Saint fiasco, I kept bracing for disaster. Yet with every day that passed, life became almost normal.

Well, as normal as anything gets when half the things I do on a given day would be considered clinically insane back on Earth.

Fireday mornings were allocated entirely to muscle training with Yon and the team. We would start with cardio, move on to weights, and finish with combat sparring.

Speaking of weights, I had commissioned a few more sets since the original barbell proved far too cumbersome for group training. Having eight people queue up to use a single bar was not exactly efficient.

We added two sets of non-enchanted weights. Those topped out at 400 kilograms, which worked well enough for me, Calr, Katar, Raik, and Vena. One set of gravity-enchanted weights started at 250 kilograms and increased in 25-kilogram increments, up to 2.5 tons. That one went to Kan and Shingo. Finally, the last one started at 1,000 kilograms and climbed in 100-kilogram jumps to a full 10 tons. That one belonged to Yon alone, and watching him grind through his sets was as impressive as watching him drag that boulder across the riverbed.

The old prototype barbell stuck around too, mostly as a spare for whenever someone couldn't be bothered to wait. We also had a few sets of dumbbells made, though those came in fixed weights rather than adjustable ones. It was cheaper that way, and less of a hassle. Finally, we had a few enchanted benches able to resist the weight.

Back on Earth, I enjoyed cardio with an audiobook in my ears, but I was never much of a fan of weight training. Here, though, the camaraderie made it work. Having Yon yell, “Three more, Alice, you can do it!” always gave me a boost, and Katar tacking five more kilograms onto my bar, giving me that look that meant "come on, you can do better than this,” always pushed me forward.

Watching Vena and Calr keep up regardless of their struggle made me feel ashamed whenever I was tempted to quit early, and Kan's sheer, effortless ease at everything was, in its own way, its own kind of inspiration.

Sparring came last, and it was the part of the morning I looked forward to most, if I was being honest with myself. With Vena on hand for healing, none of us held back. Even my banked teleport, the one that was only supposed to activate when facing imminent danger, ended up going off more often than not. Somehow, it was exhilarating. By the time we finished, I would be drenched in sweat, my arms shaking, and my face grinning despite it all.

After a long bath in my secret mountain spring, I would usually head up to my observatory for a few hours of me-time.

Some days I trained my Star Mana retention, sitting cross-legged on the cold stone floor and trying to hold the sensation a little longer each time, resisting its conversion into regular mana.

Sometimes I teleported randomly to hunt for treasure, sample new food, or check if a festival was happening somewhere so I could watch it from a distance.

Other times, I helped whoever my wish decided needed helping that day. A farmer who'd lost half his livestock to monsters, a couple dozen silver donated to help his recovery. A child who'd wandered too far from her village. Once, a fisherman who'd hit his head, got disoriented, mixed up east for west, and started rowing further out to sea instead of back toward shore. And sometimes I just sat and read newspapers, sprawled across my chaise lounge with a cup of coffee slowly going cold beside me, cataloguing which lies about myself had made it into print that week.

On the short nights between Fireday and Waterday, I would sleep and dream on my own. I spent most of those six hours in the Moon Room, either watching something or playing video games. Yes, it turned out I could play video games there, and yes, a certain Australian game studio really did wait for me to be Isekai’ed before they finally decided to release their bug game. I avoided the Star Room for the most part, no matter how tempting it was. Exploring the city through the margin dream projection last time had decimated my Inspiration reserves, and I needed to let that well refill, especially since I already had an idea or two for the next sanctuary expansion.

Waterdays were for being social. Half the time I was helping out at the restaurant, serving guests, joking with customers, listening to Louis hum to herself while she worked, while Silnar trained yet another round of apprentices. The other half I was hanging out with Nada, Ja'a, Kan, or Vena, usually over something entirely mundane: shopping, gossip, an occasional business transaction with Ja'a, a vermin hunt with Kan, or charity work with Vena. Sometimes I visited Sara, who spent most of her waking hours hunched over the ancient Telepath script alongside Ko'i, and I would force the two of them out into the sun to go listen to Riri sing, who had already talked her way into a regular gig at one of the city's taverns.

One day a week, though, was reserved for Lady Marina, who trained me in Soul Shaping.

I dreaded it and looked forward to it in equal measure. The training consisted mostly of Will manipulation. I needed to resist her suppression and, if possible, resist her ghostly slap, the one that kept throwing me clean out of my own body. It was, without exaggeration, a miserable experience. I spent most of the allotted time either being flung out into the world of stillness, fighting to keep from drifting away, or forcing myself back into my body, which was harder than it sounded, since it required convincing my waking soul to merge back into my sleeping body, a task that demanded a modicum of willpower I did not yet consistently possess. Ma'a had reassured me, in her flat voice, that “it takes some people years to master it.”

Still, week by week, I was making progress.

Finally, I spent most of my long nights with Nina. We dreamed together, improved the microphone bit by bit, worked on the observatory telescope, studied different lectures, and consumed a steady diet of Earth media. I introduced her to video games, and we played through a few co-op titles together. She was terrible at anything requiring fast reflexes and disturbingly good at anything requiring thinking outside the box, though maybe that was only because she had never actually seen what the box looked like in the first place. We played a few party games too, the kind that would have killed lesser friendships than the one we had.

But more often than not, when the night stretched long and neither of us particularly wanted to do anything productive, I would find myself sitting on the loveseat, tucked under one of her wings, watching anime until we would wake up. Nina loved the story of a reincarnated girl reinventing paper from scratch, while I couldn't help but wonder whether Lady Laurel felt anything like the elf slayer on the screen.

More than anything else in either world, those moments felt like home.

A week into our muscle training, we were joined by Takur, Hans, and Ki'a, freshly back from their dragon-slaying hunt and apparently eager for training.

The trio turned out to be roughly on par with Raik and Katar, with Takur edging out the rest by a hair. That, predictably, started a weight race. The five of them kept piling more onto the bar, each one hoping to walk away as the strongest non-Kindred in the room, until Yon finally stepped in and shut it down. They were, after all, already dangerously close to the edge of what their bodies could actually take, so he sent the whole lot back to basics: 250 kilograms, consistent reps, stamina building before anyone was allowed to touch another increment.

Nobody argued with him. Yon had that dad energy that made you want to avoid disappointing him despite being roughly my age.

Since Ki’a was here, I had him upgrade my spear once again, expanding its Star Mana capacity and reducing its summoning cooldown.

They weren't the only ones who showed up, either. Word travels fast around the guild, and rumors of this groundbreaking training equipment started making the rounds. Soon, a good chunk of Hano's elite came by to try the weights at least once.

Commander Kitchi, Raik's brother, showed up alongside General Kiddo, his sister Captain Yoka, and Commander Marina, and all four had a go at it. Despite carrying far stronger souls than the rest of us, their raw physical strength was surprisingly comparable to ours, since none of them were Kindred either. It was a small, quiet reminder of just how much of this world's powers operated on different axes.

Kerissa and Hanakudo, on the other hand, were an entirely different story.

Kerissa looked like she belonged on a swimsuit catalog rather than in military leadership, and yet she somehow edged past Yon's previous max bench of 2.2 tonnes, setting a new record at 2.4. Considering that most of her soul strength should have gone into her phoenix transformation rather than raw physical power, that made the number all the more absurd.

Hanakudo, in his normal human form, was roughly comparable to Shingo, unremarkable by Kindred standards. But the moment he shifted into his hybrid dragon form, scales climbing up his arms and sparks of flame hissing out of his mouth with every deep exhale, he blew straight past Kerissa's fresh record and landed at 2.5.

That was apparently all the invitation Hano's elite Freelancers needed. More of them started showing up after that, some I recognized, some I'd never seen before. Each time someone new showed up, I would hand them the coordinates of the enchanter and an estimate of the price.

Three Kindred Ensigns managed to match Yon's personal best, though none of them could touch Hanakudo's dragon-form record. Not until Khoka Karda, Kan's brother, walked in and set a new bar at 4.2 tonnes, grinning the whole time like he'd been waiting his whole life for this.

He gave Raik a challenging look for some reason before winking at me on his way out.

“He really hates you,” said Ja’a.

“He doesn’t hate me,” said Raik with a sigh. “He just hates the idea of me.”

“Can you blame him?” said Hans. “The man got passed over for promotion despite his strength, while you got promoted to sergeant the moment you showed up.”

“I didn’t ask for it,” Raik grimaced.

“Tell that to him, not me.” Hans raised both hands dramatically.

“Wait, isn’t he a lieutenant?” I asked. “Isn’t that the same rank as Garo? It’s a pretty good rank.”

“Yeah, but Khoka joined the Freelancer Guild the same year as Commander Kitchi Agame,” said Calr. “At the start, they had similar mission-clearing records. But after Kitchi got promoted to Sergeant and Khoka did not, the gap widened because Kitchi got access to more restricted contracts and special assignments.”

Kan let out a sigh. “This is why I haven’t told him we were dating yet.”

“You are dating?” Hans's eyes widened. “How did that happen? Spill the tea, sister!”

Khoka’s record held for a grand total of four days.

Then Captain Makai showed up.

He had been dealing with incursions along the edge of the Colossus Dead Zone for weeks, and once back in Hano, he strolled into our training room, fist-bumped Yon, then took over his bench. Five minutes later, he shattered the record at 9.2 tonnes.

The number was so absurd that even the other Kindred people looked dumbfounded. I doubted anyone in Hano could touch that, at least not anytime soon. Maybe someone in High Rock or the Kindred Realm itself.

As it turned out, Makai hadn't come alone. He had run into Nakera and Kuru somewhere on the road back. He found them defending a small village from a golem incursion, and the three of them had simply decided to travel the rest of the way together.

Nakera and Kuru seemed a little unsettled during those first few days, quieter than I remembered them, but it didn't take long before they slid back into the group like they'd never left. Nakera's strength turned out to be roughly on par with Kan's these days, and Kuru had edged slightly past Vena.

The two of them folded themselves into my Waterdays social time just as easily, and if anything, they were more eager to turn every gathering into a small party. Ja’a matched their energy, dragging Kan and me, and sometimes Vena, into taverns, barn dances, and local social assemblies.

So it surprised absolutely no one when it took less than an hour between the moment Nakera and Riri first met and the moment the two of them ended up in bed together.

Halfway through the month, Je'e and Louis opened another location. This one was just a kitchen, with no dining room or tables, nothing but ovens and a delivery window. Lera started showing up near the guild in full uniform, a communication slate strapped to her arm, taking orders and dispatching them to whoever was free. While she was at it, she recruited a handful of struggling Freelancers, the ones scraping by on odd jobs and low-paying escort contracts, and turned the lot of them into a proper delivery corps.

It was one of the more satisfying things I had helped with in this world, offering steady work for people who badly needed it. Hopefully, this would lower the number of struggling freelancers who end up dead doing something too risky because they felt like they had no other path forward.

Mama Dima, Silnar's mother, converted her own shop into another location around that same time and semi-retired from active kitchen duty into a managerial role. She still poked her head into the kitchen often enough to terrorize the apprentices, but the actual cooking had mostly passed to younger hands. By the end of the month, young men and women in bright red uniforms could be seen running up and down the guild district, pizza boxes balanced in one arm, cooling boxes of tiramisu strapped to their backs, weaving through carts and pedestrians alike.

Around that same time, I may or may not have taken Louis to Petra and helped design her wedding dress. Copyrighted pictures of a certain fairy-tale princess may or may not have been involved in that process. I will have to consult with my lawyer before confirming anything further.

I experienced surprisingly fast growth at first, going from benching 200 kilograms per rep to 220 after one week, 235 by the second, and 245 by the third. But after that, I started to slow down considerably. I finally reached 250 by the fifth week, but those last five kilograms fought me every step of the way. Comparable numbers showed up in my squats and shoulder presses too, and more importantly, by that point, the training had started leaving a visible effect on my body.

I'd begun to look physically stronger than Kan, at least by a superficial assessment: slightly broader shoulders, more defined arm muscles, even a hint of abs. Even Nina noticed the change when we slept together for shared dreaming.

There was a single explanation for why I had only started changing this quickly. I had hit my soul limit, or at least the Kindred side of that particular equation. Ja'a confirmed it during one of her usual soul-sight check-ins. I'd grown by a full 4 SB since we made the weights, which put me at 27, comfortably the strongest soul on the team now, ahead of even Raik's 25.

It was strange, honestly, that my soul strength was already this high, yet I was still able to grow it that fast with nothing more exotic than muscle training. But the more I thought about it, the more it made sense. Ja'a had called me a "perfect mutt" back when she first saw my soul, and if I subscribed to the Axes of Creation theory, that meant my soul was built from all seven of those Axes at once, however unevenly distributed.

Working the numbers backward, I could account for most of the growth. My Dreamer side had likely grown by something like 10 SB, since I'd technically ascended the moment I expanded my sanctuary, adding the Tarot Table and pushing it past the limit of what a single college dorm room could hold. My Bloodline side had grown by maybe 4 SB thanks to my lightning training with Garo, as well as my improved efficiency from using Stormshark Whisper. My Kindred side had grown through Edmund's lessons, months of freelancing, and now this, probably around 6 SB, given that I'd apparently just hit its ceiling. The rest, whatever remained, had to be some combination of Mythic, Soulit, Sealed, and Contested. I had yet to push myself in any of those paths, and I had no idea how I would go about doing it with half of them.

For now, I decided to slow my muscle training down and shift the focus toward endurance and stamina rather than raw strength. The last thing I wanted was to accidentally specialize my Kindred side in one direction or another simply because the only adversity my body ever faced was heavier and heavier weights.

Nina and I ended up having pizza for dinner one night, sitting cross-legged on the observatory floor with the box open between us and two mismatched mugs of watered-down wine. Afterward, we headed to my sanctuary to work on electric guitars, since the microphone project was finally done.

After half the night spent watching tuning videos and studying electrical signal wave theory, we were interrupted by the door to my Moon Room cinema bursting open.

Riri stood there, having somehow made her way into my dream despite me not having access to the Great Dream yet. Her face was stretched into a grin, and I immediately knew that whatever she was about to say had to be big.

“You will never believe what happened,” she said, breathless, one hand still braced against the doorframe.

“You slept with Ion the Phoenix,” I threw out a random guess.

She blinked, then grinned even wider.

“I wish…” She stepped inside, letting the door swing shut behind her. “No, it's the Sky Island. It reached the continent’s shore.”

Nina’s eyes widened. “Is it coming here?” she asked.

“It just shifted its trajectory,” Riri nodded. “Slightly south, toward Hano.”

“Really?” I couldn't help but get excited too.

“If nothing changes,” Riri added, practically vibrating where she stood, “we’re getting the Longest Night Festival. Next month, right here in Hano.”

Nina’s wings snapped fully open behind her, sending a few pillows flying.

I had a feeling we were going to have a chaotic month ahead of us.
