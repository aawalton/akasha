Duration: 60 Hours

Resource Gathering mission: 48 hours

Time left till deadline: 23 hours 52 minutes.

"Over here, Destra!" Aodhán called out excitedly as he raced toward a mound of snow, core sense tingling. "I think we've found another."

"About time!" Destra groaned as she raced to meet him. "It's been more than three hours. Even for me, that's too much snow."

Aodhán chuckled. "We're still ahead of the schedule the dragon gave us. It gave us twenty-four hours, remember?"

Destra snorted, waving a hand to scatter the snow mound as Aodhán reached it, revealing a long metallic spike half-buried within the frozen earth, its surface shimmering with an array of black runes.

Aodhán yanked out the spike with a victorious smile and raised it into the air. "I feel a sudden, unexplainable urge to do a victory dance."

Destra cracked a genuine smile. "By all means. I'm sure the cultists will love it. You might even gain more fans."

Aodhán scowled. "Not funny."

He hadn't checked his likability ratings in a while, and that was completely intentional. He wasn't comfortable with the number of fans he'd gained for going against the cultists. It made his actions seem useless in the grand scheme of things, and he couldn't have that—not when he had no one to rely on for motivation but himself.

Still scowling, he threw the grand abyssal pin into his spatial space and raised the wristband to his face, his expression softening subtly as he opened the leaderboard screen.

The display appeared before him in a flash of crimson, and his scowl dissolved immediately. Destra let out a derisive snort. "I cannot understand the excitement you get every time you open that screen. What's the award? Best in Prisoner?"

Aodhán chuckled and angled the screen so she could see. "If that's the award available, I'll take it."

Destra shook her head in reproach, but the moment her gaze fell on the screen, even she cracked a smile.

Despite their camping expenses and the fact that they hadn't gathered any abyssal pins in the last few hours, Aodhán was still holding the third position, and Destra herself wasn't too far behind.

ABYSSAL PINS LEADERBOARD

Mirith Vaelys—1370 points

Orin Draviksson—1240 points

Aodhán Brystion—1180 points

Serenya Serenity—1090 points

Sebastian Rune—1040 points

Baxterion Valenhall—1010 points

Monica Weber—970 points

Daruk Brystion—950 points

Cyrus Valerion—910 points

Destra Mrazek—880 points

Magdalene Al-Tahrizaar—820 points

Well, maybe she was a bit far behind, but she was still in the top ten—which was impressive, considering the fact that she hadn't actively searched for pins in the last few hours.

One thing that really stuck out to Aodhán was the fact that he knew nearly everyone in the top ten. Even if not personally, he had at least heard of them—except for the person holding the second position.

Wasn't that strange? That out of over ninety people, he knew nearly everyone in the top ten?

"What are you still looking at?" Destra's voice cut into his thoughts, and he blinked, realizing he'd gotten carried away. He had opened the leaderboard to check on the progress of the other teams.

Clearing his throat awkwardly, he glanced down at Baxter's name and frowned when he found no increase. He checked Magda's next. She hadn't found any pins either.

"Well, that's not good," he muttered, giving the screen one last look before closing it. "No one has found any pins yet, meaning we still have one to go."

Destra sighed. "We're already in the middle region. We might as well help Magda and Borevin search their area."

Aodhán glanced at the massive expanse of land they were about to search and exhaled deeply. He couldn't blame the others for not finding any of the pins—they didn't have his advantage. But he'd found two already, and if he found the third, then what use was the team, really?

Pushing the thought out of his head, he summoned a storm construct and stepped onto it. Rather than create her own construct, Destra joined him, whispering an incantation to search the snow for Magda's footprints.

After a while, she spoke. "They went east, so I suggest we go west to avoid searching areas they've already covered."

"What if they missed the spike during their search?" Aodhán asked. "What if it was hidden beneath a pile of snow like this one?"

Destra exhaled and shook her head. "This is the worst day of my life."

Aodhán chuckled and spread his core sense as wide as the interference allowed. When it reached its limit, he closed his eyes and whispered, "Void."

Almost immediately, his senses went completely dim. Not even a flicker of light. There wasn't a shred of void essence in the area. But at least the moment he came across one, he would know.

Sighing, he opened his eyes. "We're in for a long search. Try not to fall."

He shot forward before Destra could respond, searching his senses for a shred of the void affinity. However, they'd barely crossed the distance of a mile when suddenly a loud boom of thunder rocked the earth to its core. His core sense screamed as an explosion of willpower—so large, so powerful—tore through his senses like a wave of searing light.

Aodhán turned toward the explosion immediately, eyes widening as he glimpsed a wide area of obliterated cloud, warping and undulating like a mirage as reality strained against the unleashed power.

"Is that who I think it is?" Destra's voice came out in a strangled whisper, her eyes wide as she took in the obliterated clouds and warping reality. "Is that your brother?"

A sharp nod was Aodhán's only response as he turned and shot toward the explosion immediately, his heart thundering with worry. That explosion would attract scores of people, good and bad alike, and though Daruk had never outright stated it, Aodhán had come to recognize the skill as a summons.

Daruk wanted him to find him, and Aodhán couldn't help but think his brother was in danger. Daruk wouldn't resort to this if it wasn't important.

Gritting his teeth, he funneled more willpower into the platform beneath him, the world blurring past as he streaked across the distance like a lightning bolt, Destra's arms tightening around him like a vise.

Yet, Aodhán felt he was too slow.

And he was right, because in that exact moment, he wasn't the only one racing toward Daruk.

Seojun and Mirith raced toward the explosion from the south, mental constructs blurring past the landscape of snow and ice at insane speeds. From the north, Orin Draviksson, Monica, and Sebastian rushed forward, their forms a blur of lilac, blue, and decay.

A trio of Calodans raced toward the signal from the east, their intents unclear. And from the west, several miles away from Aodhán and Destra, Baxter and Brennon jerked around, eyes wide as they felt the explosion of power.

Baxter reached the mountain first, stumbling out of a shimmering portal alongside Brennon, cosmic weapons aimed in preparation for battle. The entire mountain was covered in a thick layer of ice, and the temperature had dropped severely—so cold that even at the 47th tier, Baxter shivered.

Snow fell from the sky in thick clumps, jagged formations of ice hanging down like rain frozen solid. But the battle Baxter had expected was absent. In its place stood a lone figure, humming with far more willpower than he could ever bring to bear.

Daruk smiled when he saw him. "Good to see you again, Baxter."

Baxter lowered his weapons in confusion. "I thought you were in danger."

Daruk's smile turned sheepish. "Yeah, sorry about that, but I needed to find Aodhán, and this was the only way I knew how." His smile thinned. "We are in danger, though. I need to know what dastardly plans he's come up with so I can add mine to them."

As if his mind was only just catching up, Brennon finally made a sound between a squeak and a cough. "What… what the fuck. You're Aodhán's blood brother?"

Daruk's gaze slid to the illusionist, and he frowned, not recognizing the man at all. "Yes. And you are?"

Brennon shuddered, teeth chattering, but before he could respond, Baxter answered for him.

"This is Brennon. Our resident idiot."

"Hey!" Brennon protested, but Baxter ignored him, his expression growing serious.

"We need to be on alert. No doubt your explosion will attract a lot of people."

As if his words had summoned them, two figures arrived at the top of the mountain in a blur of mental energy and wind, scattering snow into the air. The first was Seojun, and beside him, the telepath.

The woman cast a glance across the frozen landscape and pressed her lips into a line of admiration, though there was no surprise there. No shock.

Daruk took a step back.

The woman smiled. "A step back isn't enough distance, I fear, Daruk Brystion." Her smile widened. "My name is Mirith Vaelys. You're already familiar with my companion."

Daruk's grimace tightened, but he forced himself into a polite bow. "It is an honor to meet you, Mirith Vaelys. I must confess that I am uncomfortable with your presence and would kindly request that you stay out of my mind."

Baxter scowled. Brennon let out another strangled sound. Mirith's smile only widened.

"Your reactions are perfectly normal, and I respect them." She glanced at Seojun. "Seojun can tell you how much I love to respect boundaries."

Seojun grimaced, remembering how she had dug a dream out of his subconscious.

"Perhaps we can put a bit more distance within those boundaries," he muttered with a glare at Mirith before continuing. "It would be a favor to me. Sir Daruk has been kind enough to protect my sisters from harm." He bent his head in reverence. "I met them at the base of the mountain. They couldn't come too close."

Daruk smiled at Seojun. "It's alright. I—"

The rest of his words were cut short as another group appeared at the top of the mountain, a burst of decay, sound, and infinity essence heralding their arrival.

"Daruk, are you alright—" Sebastian's words choked off as his gaze fell on Mirith, and his eyes widened. Whatever concern he'd had for Daruk evaporated in that instant as he turned all his attention to the telepath. "You're the telepath. We've been searching all over for you."

Mirith's smile took on a hint of disdain. "You've found me, Sebastian Rune. What is it you have to say?"

"The monsters beneath us," Sebastian began immediately, but before he could finish, Monica cut him off with a burst of decay and a furious snarl as she jabbed a finger at Baxter.

"You betrayer! How dare you?!"

"It's nice to see you too, Monica." Baxter scowled. "I cannot say I'm disappointed that you haven't changed."

"I'm disappointed you did," she snapped, then glanced at Daruk, who now stood at the center of seven individuals—none of whom was the person he'd wanted to see.

"You!" She spat the word like venom. "Where's that brother of yours?"

"Why?" Baxter interrupted. "So you can gut him? What will be your reason for killing an innocent this time?"

"Can we not do this another time?" Sebastian snapped, his expression scolding. Surprisingly, it worked. Monica quieted, albeit reluctantly, restoring silence to the mountain peak once more.

Sebastian didn't hesitate. He turned back to the telepath and began weaving his web. "I don't know if you're aware, esteemed telepath, but we have a disaster coming our way. We shouldn't be fighting each other. We're supposed to be working together at the twin-peaked mountain."

"Because that way you and your crew get to come out alive while everyone else dies." Mirith's gaze hardened. "Or isn't that right, Sebastian?"

Sebastian's expression froze, but he wasn't exactly surprised. He had glanced at the parallels, and the probability that the telepath would immediately accept his offer had been extremely low.

That didn't, however, mean she would never accept, and Sebastian was determined to have her by his side. Right now, though, he had to do some damage control.

Mirith's words had sown a seed of suspicion he couldn't afford. He had to rip it out immediately.

Morphing his expression into one of confusion, Sebastian frowned. "What do you mean, me and my crew? Can we fight these monsters alone? Can you?" Turning to glance at the others present, he continued, "Fighting together is the only logical way any of us will survive. We're talking about four tier-75 creatures here!"

Orin, the sound awakened who had arrived with Sebastian, nodded. "We need everyone if we're to survive this. We have the highest chance of survival when we stand together."

"Of course." Mirith smiled, but the expression was edged with disdain. "It is undoubtedly a very logical plan, but what about the—"

The rest of her words were cut off as the Calodans arrived at the top of the mountain—two men and a woman, all dressed in sandcloths and bearing weapons.

Everyone reacted to their presence violently, essence flaring, but a quick vanishing of the Calodans' weapons soon revealed that they'd come for the same reason as everyone else. Curiosity and worry.

"We are not here to fight!" The leader, the woman, spoke up quickly, hands spread wide as she eyed the overwhelming number on the mountaintop. "We felt the explosion of willpower and were curious. We thought something was happening—a monster spawning, perhaps."

When no one eased their stance, the trio exchanged a glance, and the woman spoke again. "We will take our leave immediately."

"No," Sebastian said. "You should make your way to the twin-peaked mountain. We're all heading there to fight the creatures as one."

The woman nodded and turned to retreat. However, before she could, another group appeared at the top of the mountain in a burst of willpower and lightning.

Aodhán stepped down from his platform with a grimace, aware of all the people gathered around the mountaintop already. However, he didn't spare any of them a glance—not even the telepath, who smiled widely, having sensed him long before anyone else.

Aodhán's gaze was fixed on one person, and one alone.

"Are you alright?" he asked, his eyes searching Daruk's body for any sign of distress or injury.

Daruk smiled. "I feel like a damsel in distress, but I'm fine. I wanted to broadcast my location to you. I guess it worked a little too well."

Aodhán's grimace deepened, and he finally turned his gaze to the others, his eyes hard. These people weren't his enemies, but the fact that they had arrived here earlier than him left a bitter taste in his mouth.

To make things worse, he was in the company of the telepath—all his secrets laid bare for her to plunder. His grimace deepened into a scowl. However, before he could speak, an alarm blared loudly, and a crimson screen appeared before them all.

Aodhán's jaw clenched as he read the message.

THERE ARE TEN GRAND ABYSSAL PINS ON THIS MOUNTAIN. NO ONE IS PERMITTED TO LEAVE THE MOUNTAIN UNTIL ALL TEN PINS BELONG TO ONE PERSON.

FIGHT FOR YOUR OWN SALVATION.
