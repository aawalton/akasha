“You must have a death wish, kid.”

Ace didn’t reply to the silver-haired vampire. His full focus was on her hand as she reached for something on her back. Her fingers curled around a compact metal cylinder housed in an elegant leather harness that crossed diagonally between her shoulder blades. With a subtle turn of her wrist, she released the locking mechanism, and the cylinder slid free with a soft metallic whisper.

She flicked her thumb over an almost invisible rune etched into the metal’s surface. The cylinder responded with a low hum of awakening magic. Before his eyes, the weapon transformed—both ends extending outward in synchronized motion as internal mechanisms locked into place with soft metallic clicks.

In less than two seconds, what had been a modest cylinder became a six-foot staff with wickedly curved blades at each end. The metal gleamed with an oily iridescence that suggested enchantment or, possibly, poison. She spun the double-bladed staff with casual ease. The blades whistled as they cut through the air, leaving faint trails of light in their wake.

Ace settled into a fighting stance, Moonfall balanced perfectly in his grip. The hivemind buzzed through the back of his head, and through their party bond, Ace sensed his teammates shifting into position.

What their opponents couldn’t see was the invisible current flowing between the four of them. The thread that bound them wasn’t just emotional awareness, but rather an entire language of combat forged through shared blood and battle. Through the hivemind ability he’d gained from the alpha slug, Ace transmitted wordless instructions through wordless pulses of intuition.

It just sort of… happened.

It was easy. As simple as a nod of the head or a quick glance sideways, but no one had to even move for the point to come across.

It was every squad leader’s dream.

Marcus, target the two on the left.

A quick pulse of agreement shot back toward Ace.

Good.

Olivia, take the right flank. Clean up anyone Marcus disables.

Another surge of agreement.

Tara, stay central and be ready to heal whoever needs it most.

Everyone slid into place, silent and ready to take out these vampires—or at least go down with one hell of a fight.

The silver-haired leader mistook their silence for fear, her lips curling in cruel amusement as she signaled her companions. They spread out in what would have been an impressive formation against ordinary prey—two flanking left, two right, with two more hanging back to guard their leader.

Seven targets.

No, seven victims.

Ace couldn’t ignore a surge of confidence that bordered on recklessness. These vampires might be stronger individually, but they had no idea what his team had become.

The elegant vampires tensed, their weapons glinting in the moonlight. For a heartbeat, the forest grew completely still—the calm before the slaughter.

Then the silver-haired woman flicked her wrist, and her hunters lunged forward with inhuman speed.

Ace didn’t flinch. As the hunters closed in, his lips curved into a cold smile.

Time to put this new hivemind ability to the test.

“Kill them,” the silver-haired woman said with a sneer.

Instantly, five of her crew attacked. The sixth remained behind to defend her as the clearing erupted with explosive violence.

She couldn’t even be bothered to fight her own battles.

Pathetic.

Two of the five charging vampires shot toward Ace directly, their movements unnaturally fluid as they closed the distance with a predatory focus. They moved like shadows across the forest floor, barely disturbing the dead leaves beneath their feet.

In an instant, Ace tracked his two opponents.

A female vampire came at him from his right side. Tendrils of crimson smoke trailed from her serrated sword. The weapon’s edge caught moonlight along its jagged teeth, and the blade seemed designed to carve devastating gashes and open wounds rather than clean cuts. Her pale skin bore a network of scars across her exposed shoulders and neck that shifted as she moved, almost seeming to writhe with anticipation of the violence to come.

At the same moment, a male vampire approached from Ace’s left, his angular face a mask of cold concentration. Twin curved daggers gleamed in his hands, their edges etched with runes that pulsed with a sickly red glow. The leather of his fingerless gloves creaked as he adjusted his grip. His eyes tracked Ace’s torso rather than his blade, marking him as someone trained to watch for body tells rather than weapon feints.

The two attackers converged from opposite angles in perfect sync. Their eyes held no hesitation, no doubt—only the cold certainty of predators who had never failed a hunt. Their effortless movements accelerated to a speed that would have rendered them invisible to human eyes.

To Ace’s enhanced senses, however, they appeared in perfect clarity—dangerous, yes, but readable. Predictable in their arrogance.

They had no idea who they were attacking.

Moonfall sang through the air as Ace parried the first strike, the blade’s otherworldly glow leaving trails of luminescent silver in its wake. The first vampire’s eyes widened at the unexpected strength behind Ace’s counter. Without pausing, Ace stepped inside the second vampire’s guard, his movements augmented by his military training and now supercharged with vampiric speed.

His Phantom Step Resonance Boots hummed against his calves, transmitting subtle vibrations through his body that mapped the positions of every combatant in the clearing. He felt a presence rushing toward his blind spot—the third attacker trying to flank him.

Bring it.

Ace spun and dropped low, ducking beneath a slashing attack while simultaneously sweeping out the legs of the first vampire. She went down hard, and the crack of breaking bones echoed through the clearing as the vampire’s ankle snapped.

He didn’t give her a chance to stand. Before she could even blink, he drove Moonfall clear into her chest, piercing her heart. Her eyes went wide as he drove the sword deeper, and she went instantly still.

One down.

His second and third opponents slashed at him in unison, and he rolled out of harm’s way. He skidded to a stop, his boots sliding across the dirt for a second before he fully caught his balance. He had managed to put a decent bit of distance between them, so he took the moment to check on the others.

On the opposite side of the small clearing, Marcus stood with his feet firmly planted on the ground. His eyes were closed in concentration as pulses of psychic energy swirled around his head. The two vampires advancing on him suddenly faltered, their movements becoming erratic as his magic took hold.

Terror Cascade.

Well done, Ace said through their connection.

A pulse of gratitude and pride shot from Marcus, but the man didn’t take his attention off his attack.

“Get out of my head!” one of the attackers screamed.

“What the hell?” gasped the other, stumbling backward.

Marcus’s eyes snapped open, and the irises glowed with unnatural intensity. “Each fear you’ve ever had,” he whispered, his voice carrying despite its softness. “Each nightmare you’ve buried. I found them all.”

The affected vampires clawed at their own faces, their coordinated attack dissolving into chaos as they battled hallucinations.

Olivia seized the opening, her expression transforming from cold calculation to terrible joy as she gripped her makeshift staff. While others might’ve thought it to be a laughable weapon, she wielded it as an extension of her dancer’s form, each movement flowing into the next with hypnotic grace. Each pivot, each twirl, gathered momentum that would lead to a devastating blow to whoever was unlucky enough to be on the other end.

The staff blurred as she spun it in a flourish that seemed more ceremonial than combative—until it connected with her victim’s temple. The crack of fracturing bone echoed through the clearing like a gunshot. The vampire hadn’t even registered the attack before Olivia pivoted, using the momentum to power her next strike.

Her target collapsed onto the ground in a heap of blood, brains, and skull fragments. Their daggers fell beside them, landing in the grass with a soft thud.

The second vampire, the only surviving opponent still battling Marcus’s psychic phantoms, swung blindly at threats only he could see. Olivia ducked beneath his wild swings, her body arcing in a perfect semicircle before rising with devastating force. The staff’s end drove upward through the vampire’s jaw, shattering it into fragments that glittered, wet and red, in the moonlight.

The entire exchange had taken seconds.

Only four enemies were left—the two trailing Ace, their leader, and the lone guard, who was growing increasingly nervous as the battle went on.

Ace leaned backward right as the male vampire with curved daggers swung at his face. The man swung again, and again, and again. Each time, Ace ducked out of the way with perfect timing, his intuitive reflexes on high alert as he avoided each blow in the moment before it could hit.

Moonfall hummed in Ace’s grip as he pivoted at the last possible second. The man’s dagger missed Ace by millimeters.

The attacker’s momentum carried him forward, and his eyes widened in surprise as Ace countered. Moonfall sliced horizontally, its silver-white edge catching the forest’s low light as it swept through the air. The vampire tried to shift his weight backward, but Ace had already prepared for it.

“Predictable,” Ace muttered.

He dropped his shoulder and drove forward, changing the blade’s arc mid-swing. Moonfall’s edge sliced open the vampire’s throat, cutting through flesh and bone alike with a wet splat. The vampire’s head remained attached by a thin strip of tissue as his body crumpled. Blood spurted from the nearly decapitated head, and Ace took a step backward to avoid getting hit.

Ace didn’t pause to watch him fall. His Phantom Step boots had already transmitted the vibration of the last vampire approaching from behind. He spun, catching her blade with Moonfall in a shower of sparks. The impact sent a jolt up his arm, but his lips curled into a cold smile as recognition flashed in her eyes.

She was good.

He was better.

Ace’s free hand shot out, grabbing her by the throat. His enhanced strength, augmented by all the bloodshed and buffed with his gear and title bonuses, allowed him to easily lift her bodily from the ground. In her panic, she dropped her sword, and it landed on a rock in the ground with a clatter. She grabbed his wrist, and her legs kicked uselessly at him as her face contorted with terror.

With a single explosive motion, Ace hurled her across the clearing. Her body became a missile, traveling twenty feet before slamming into a massive oak with such force that the trunk cracked. Bark exploded outward from the impact point as her body folded around the tree, her bones shattering with a concussive thwack.

Limp and lifeless, she slid down the trunk, leaving a smear of dark blood on the pale bark. Her body crumpled against the tree’s knotted roots.

A low moan escaped her lips as she tried to push herself up. Her right arm hung at an unnatural angle. Her legs twitched uselessly as she attempted to stand. All she managed to do was pull herself up enough to rest on her elbows.

Blood bubbled from her lips as she collapsed again. Her fingers clawed weakly at the ground, gouging furrows in the earth as she struggled to regain control of her body.

Ace turned his back on her. He could finish her later. The pathetic moans behind him served as proof that she wouldn’t be rejoining the fight.

“Please,” the vampire gasped, words gurgling through blood-filled lungs. “I’m sorry.”

Ace briefly glanced over his shoulder to find Olivia standing over the broken woman. Moonlight sculpted the Blood Dancer’s features into something both beautiful and terrible as she gazed down at her prey.

“Please,” the vampire whispered again. “Just let me go.”

Olivia tilted her head, studying the vampire’s mangled form with detached fascination.

“No,” she said simply.

With that, Olivia knelt beside the broken vampire and plunged her fingers into the vampire’s chest. Her victim screamed. The crack of breaking ribs blended with the meaty slap of tearing flesh. The heady tang of blood coiled through the air, thick and sweet, carrying notes of rust, wine, and something ancient that defied description.

The dying vampire’s scream shifted into a bubbling whimper as Olivia withdrew her hand. She clasped the woman’s cold, dead heart in her elegant fingers, the organ coated in a dark sheen of blood that dripped between her knuckles in thick rivulets.

“Beautiful,” Olivia whispered, turning the heart to catch the moonlight.

For a horrifying moment, Ace thought she might bite into it.

Instead, she squeezed, her delicate fingers compressing the organ until it ruptured in a spray of blackish fluid that spattered her face and throat. She didn’t flinch or turn away—her eyes remained open, drinking in the moment as the vampire beneath her convulsed once, twice, and then went still.

Olivia rose with the same grace with which she’d knelt, wiping her bloodied hand casually against her thigh. She caught Ace watching and smiled—the same pleasant smile she might have offered over coffee in their previous life. Nothing in her expression acknowledged the savagery of what she’d just done.

With that, she stepped over the corpse as if it were nothing more than a fallen log in her path.

Ace felt something cold settle in his stomach. The Blood Dancer was becoming something beyond vampire—something that viewed death as performance art. He filed away the observation for later, turning back to their last two opponents.

After all, they had a battle to finish.

His focus landed squarely on the silver-haired leader and her lone guardian. Ace raised Moonfall, and blood from his victims streamed down its gleaming blade.

“Fuck this,” the leader’s guard said.

He dropped his sword onto the ground and bolted into the forest. He charged into the darkness so quickly that he was little more than a blur.

Olivia’s body tensed, and through their connection, Ace sensed her eagerness to run after him.

Don’t, Ace ordered.

A pulse of annoyance shot from her, and a burst of disappointment followed.

As much as he didn’t want anyone to know about Tara, they needed to stay together. He couldn’t risk the man leading Olivia into a trap, or worse.

“Coward!” the silver-haired leader screamed at the man.

If he cared what she thought, he didn’t show it—because he just kept running until he was little more than a flicker of movement between the distant trees.

The silver-haired leader’s otherwise perfect features twisted with rage as she watched her hunters fall. Her crimson eyes darkened to the color of drying blood, her pupils dilating until they nearly swallowed the iris. A vein pushed against her temple, pulsing with black blood.

The lone leader’s knuckles whitened as she gripped her weapon, and the creak of leather pierced the air as the hilt of her dual-bladed staff nearly buckled under the pressure of her fingers.

Her gaze darted between Ace’s team with rapid, jerking movements that resembled a bird of prey—calculating, assessing, dismissing—until they locked onto Tara. The healer stood protected in their formation’s center.

“Fine,” she hissed under her breath. “If I die, I’m taking the healer with me.”

With lightning speed, her free hand darted to her thigh, and her fingers closed around the hilt of an ornate silver dagger concealed in a sheath on her waist. The weapon gleamed with unnatural brightness as she drew it, its blade etched with runes that pulsed with hungry magic.

In one fluid motion, she hurled the dagger at Tara’s heart. The weapon cut through the air with unerring accuracy, trailing wisps of sickly green energy. Tara’s eyes widened as she sensed the attack, and though she tried to dodge it, she wouldn’t be quick enough. Ace felt her spike of fear pierce through their connection like a physical blow.

She was too far away for him to reach her in time to block the dagger.

Fine.

He had other methods.

Without conscious thought, Ace activated Shadow Realm Immersion, and his body dissolved instantly into the darkness.

The world around him shifted, the colors inverting and draining away as he phased between worlds. The Shadow Realm enveloped him in a now-familiar blanket of shadow. The whistle of the blade through the air became a muffled echo, and time itself slowed. Ace ignored the faceless entities lurking at the edges of his perception.

He was getting used to them watching his every move. To his surprise, he just didn’t care anymore if they were there or not.

Ace spotted the silver dagger. It glowed like a tear in the fabric of this reality—a burning bright spot amid the darkness. Instinctively, he calculated where he needed to be to block it and ran forward just as it closed the distance between Tara and their opponent.

In a rush of shadow and smoke, his form solidified once more as he phased back into the material world.

He now stood in front of Tara, and his fingers closed around the dagger’s hilt mid-flight. The tip of the blade scraped lightly against Tara’s chest, scratching her leather harness as he redirected the blade’s momentum.

Without hesitation, Ace pivoted and transferred all of his momentum into a single, fluid motion. He spun and, with a grunt of effort, hurled the dagger back at its owner.

The silver-haired vampire’s eyes widened in disbelief as her own weapon streaked back toward her, far faster than she’d thrown it.

She tried to dodge, but she wasn’t fast enough.

The dagger buried itself between her eyes with a sharp crack. The blade sank to its hilt, embedded in her skull. Her eyes shook as she stared dead ahead, and a thin trail of blood leaked from the wound in her forehead.

For a breath, nothing happened. She remained upright, frozen in place from the shock of what had just happened.

A moment later, her eyes rolled back in her head. Her body crumpled like a marionette with severed strings, collapsing to the forest floor in an ungraceful heap. The dual-bladed staff slipped from her fingers and rolled across the ground toward Ace as she went completely still.

The clearing fell silent.

The fight was over and, despite having murdered half of the attackers himself, Ace felt nothing.

They had threatened his team, after all.

And that was a death sentence.
