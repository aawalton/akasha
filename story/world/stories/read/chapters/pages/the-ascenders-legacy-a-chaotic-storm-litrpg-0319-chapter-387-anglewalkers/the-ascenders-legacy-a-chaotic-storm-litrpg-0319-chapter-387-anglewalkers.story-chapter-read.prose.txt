“Rotate the plating ninety degrees, Aodhán,” Elder Gumi said quietly as she etched the final rune onto the shin plating. “And keep the thermal flow steady.”

Aodhán obeyed immediately, rotating the heel plate with careful precision.

It had been three hours since he’d poured the molten electric steel into the prepared lattice casts, locking the boot’s silhouette and angular geometry into place.

Elder Gumi had taken over the creation process seamlessly after that, leaving Aodhán to assist wherever he could—rotating sections, maintaining thermal flow, stabilizing the arrays beneath the forge, and occasionally handing her tools before she asked for them.

Aodhán would have said it was a drastic waste of his talent had he had anything of real value to actually contribute, but if the last three hours had taught him anything, it was how lacking his rune smithing skills truly were.

Not only was Elder Gumi inscribing every section of the boot individually with elder runes and layered circuits, she had also forged the entire construct across multiple interconnected segments—sole, heel frame, ankle support, toe shell, shin plating—all within the span of a few hours.

She wasn’t merely fast.

She was terrifyingly precise, carving entire channels of elder runes in minutes while he merely rotated plating and stabilized thermal flow.

Needless to say, Aodhán wasn’t proud of himself.

Over a year ago, he’d drawn his first rune—FEHU—in Professor Caldwell’s classroom. Now, admittedly, a lot had happened since then.

But still…

Wasn’t it absurd that he hadn’t even completed the twenty-four initiate runes yet?

How could he ever draw elder runes and intricately layered circuits if he still hadn’t mastered the basics?

“Rotate again.”

Elder Gumi’s voice snapped him from his thoughts and Aodhán obeyed immediately.

Once again, the suspended shin plate rotated slowly in midair as he adjusted the flow of energy feeding into the stabilizing arrays beneath it. Silver-blue lightning pulsed through the nearly finished construct in branching streams while molten seams along the edges glowed bright orange under the forge’s heat.

Despite being only a prototype, the boot was already shaping into one of the most beautiful items Aodhán had seen in a very long time.

Gold-lined elder circuits flowed within the silver-blue metal like luminous veins, intersecting with embedded chaos strips in ways that somehow looked both impossibly intricate and perfectly natural.

And judging by the density of elder runes gleaming within each section, there was no doubt the boot would earn a very high rank once fused together properly.

“Alright,” Elder Gumi muttered, dragging his attention back toward her as she carved the finishing whorl of the final circuit.

The moment the line connected, the entire circuit burst to life, molten gold light spreading across the shin plate in branching streams before stabilizing into a steady glow.

Elder Gumi smiled faintly. “Now we just need to couple the sections together and synchronize the segmented circuitry to ensure seamless energy flow.”

Aodhán nodded eagerly as though he understood a word of what she had just said and her smile grew self-satisfied.

She glanced over her shoulder and called out, “Kobi, I hope you’re seeing this?”

Aodhán looked toward Elder Kobi whose crucible now glowed white-hot as streams of emerald smoke billowed around him like tame serpents. Sweat poured freely down his face as he maintained the terrifying thermal output required to keep the voltrite stable.

He responded through gritted teeth.

“I can’t say that I am, Elder Gumi. But whatever you are doing, I sincerely hope you’re almost finished because the voltrite should reach full liquefaction within the next thirty minutes.”

“Oh, I’ll be done,” Elder Gumi chuckled before shaking her head. “Voltrite… so stubborn. What has it been now? Seven hours?”

“Nine,” Elder Kobi corrected. “Just about approaching eleven thousand Vehrs.”

Aodhán whistled softly at that, but before he could say anything, Elder Gumi moved, her arms blurring across the table as she began coupling the boot together.

The sole, the heel frame, the ankle support, the toe shells—everything came together with a speed he could barely follow.

His eyes widened, utterly mesmerized as she clicked segments into the internal lattice frame and fastened the embedded chaos strips together with hair-thin metallic binders.

Every movement flowed seamlessly into the next as sections locked together with impossible precision, runic channels aligning so perfectly that lightning immediately began flowing between them the moment contact was established.

This wasn’t smithing anymore.

It was artistry.

And every second he spent watching her only drove home how lax he’d been these past few months with his runic practice.

He wanted to be like this one day. Competent. Ingenious. Inspiring.

He wanted other runic prodigies to look at him with the same awe with which he was looking at Elder Gumi right now.

He didn't even care that the road before him was long. He wanted to walk it.

To understand it and create his own items someday.

He wanted to stand at the top.

Exhaling softly, his gaze drifted from Elder Gumi to the boot itself just as she clicked the final chaos strip into place and straightened, a wide smile spreading across her face.

“You can cut off your energy flow now, Aodhán,” she instructed with obvious satisfaction, and Aodhán obeyed.

But though he had heard her, he wasn’t truly listening anymore, his gaze fixed on the beauty of gold, silver, and electric blue standing before him. Even unfused, it was breathtaking—not in a soft or delicate manner, but in a fierce, overwhelming way that somehow balanced elegance and brutality perfectly.

Elder Gumi’s smile widened slightly at the expression on his face and she nodded once.

“Good,” she said. “That is exactly the look I expect my client to have the moment he sees the completed boots.”

Aodhán glanced toward her and smiled shakily, still struggling to believe an item like this belonged to a child only just awakened.

He exhaled slowly. “Should I bring the fusion chamber?”

Elder Gumi nodded. “Yes. It’s time to make this boot whole.”

Aodhán crossed the workshop and hefted the fusion chamber with a subtle grunt, remembering when it had once taken both him and Zaxus Viremont just to carry the thing across Professor Caldwell's workshop.

The memory brought a smile to his face as he carefully placed the chamber on the forge table. He opened the lid without prompting, and Elder Gumi gave an appreciative nod before carefully placing the boot within it.

She glanced at Elder Kobi. “Where are we at now, Kobi?”

“Eleven hundred Vehrs to go.”

That was approximately twenty-five minutes. Aodhán thought, and a small smile touched his lips. Only a few hours at the forge and he was already getting better at reading the timer.

Quietly pleased with his tiny bit of growth, he returned his attention to Elder Gumi as she nodded. “We are right on schedule then.”

She shut the fusion chamber, and after gesturing for him to take a step backward, she activated it with a zap of pure lightning.

The fusion chamber whirred to life immediately, a flash of searing light and swirling chaos erupting outward in a circle nearly a meter wide. Despite himself, Aodhán leaned slightly forward, unable to suppress his curiosity as a deep metallic hum began emanating from the chamber.

“Please don’t explode,” he muttered quietly under his breath, remembering the last time they had used the chamber.

The hum deepened, vibrating through the forge table until it drowned out the roar of Elder Kobi’s forge and the frantic rhythm of his own heartbeat.

Within the chamber, the boot glowed brighter—silver, gold, and electric-blue light weaving between its plates like streams searching for somewhere to settle. They pulsed in smooth, rhythmic waves, their dance growing increasingly hypnotic with each passing second.

The hum grew louder, and this time, even Elder Gumi grimaced.

The metal and chaos strips had already begun to fuse, their runic channels softening at the edges before blending seamlessly together.

The entire chamber trembled then, and for one terrifying instant, Aodhán felt fear flash through him at the thought that Elder Gumi had somehow made a mistake and the entire thing was about to detonate.

But then the trembling ceased.

Tiny seams along the joints and ridges vanished one after another as though they had never existed. The light within the chamber swelled until it was blinding.

The air grew steadily heavier.

And then—

The construct stabilized.

A moment later, the fusion chamber fell completely still, the light fading to reveal the boot within, perfectly intact.

A slow grin spread across Aodhán’s face. “It worked.”

Elder Gumi chuckled softly. “It seems it did.”

She carefully opened the chamber and lifted the boot into the air—a flawless fusion of silver-blue metal, glowing gold circuitry, and flowing lightning.

The segmented seams had all but vanished, leaving behind only faint angular lines where the sections had once connected. The result gave the boot an intentional, layered elegance rather than the appearance of something pieced together.

Blue-white arcs pulsed softly beneath the surface like trapped lightning coursing through hidden veins, while the embedded chaos strips gleamed faintly beneath the metal, their power now fully integrated into the construct itself.

It was beautiful.

And as the full boot revealed itself, a notification suddenly pinged within Aodhán’s mind.

Congratulations! You have assisted in the creation of a Mythic item. Name yet unknown.

Note: Effects locked until the second leg is made.

Congratulations! You have witnessed a new method of runesmithing. Novice Runesmith >>> Amateur Runesmith.

A slow smile spread across Aodhán’s face as he read the notification.

But before he could ask Elder Gumi what she wanted to name the boot, Elder Kobi shouted from across the workshop.

“Alright, Elder Gumi! Prepare the mold. The voltrite is almost done!”

Elder Gumi grinned.

Then, with complete nonchalance, she tossed the Mythic boot onto the worktable as though it were a mere common item.

Aodhán winced instinctively, hands reaching out to grab it before it struck the table.

She merely glanced toward him.

“Put that to the side and pass me the lattice cast, Aodhán. Let’s see just how much better the voltrite version turns out.”

Five hours later, the workshop had fallen into an uneasy silence.

Elder Kobi had already crafted the second leg of the electric steel boots and was now leisurely engraving its outer runes, but Aodhán barely spared him a glance.

His attention was fixed entirely on the fusion chamber as Elder Gumi carefully placed the completed voltrite boots within it, a faint crease of concentration visible on her features.

The last few hours had been a repeat of the electric steel prototype.

Elder Gumi had followed the process to a tee, and while she had made some improvements to the overall aesthetics of the boots, she had made sure not to deviate too much from the prototype lest she jeopardize the final product.

Carefully, she shut the lid of the chamber before glancing toward him. “Fingers crossed. It’ll be a real shame if this were to explode when the prototype is intact.”

Aodhán nodded immediately. “It’ll hold. I believe in you.”

For a moment, Elder Gumi simply stared at him. Then she snorted softly and activated the chamber with a pulse of lightning.

The fusion chamber whirred to life once more, a deafening shriek emanating from it as emerald-gold light erupted wildly across the surface.

Aodhán took a step back immediately, and even Elder Kobi looked up from the boot he had been inscribing, his expression tense as the light from the chamber intensified almost immediately to blinding levels.

This time, it was Elder Gumi who prayed.

“Please hold,” she whispered quietly, her gaze intent on the shuddering chamber.

A piercing screech split the air then, and instinctively, all three of them flinched.

That didn’t happen the last time.

Aodhán glanced from one elder to the other, his eyes wide. “What’s going on?”

Neither elder responded.

Fortunately, the boots didn’t explode.

Not yet.

Another screech split the air before they could recover from the first, and though no one flinched this time, Aodhán joined Elder Gumi in her whispered prayers, his eyes intent on the chamber as chaos, metal and lightning were forced into submission.

A minute passed. Then two. Then four.

Yet the chamber never stilled.

With each passing minute, Aodhán's heart hammered harder, and by the sixth minute, his palms were already sweating. He wiped them distractedly against his robes, unable to tear his gaze from the chamber despite fearing what sound it might bring next.

But then, after nearly nine agonizing minutes, a blinding flash erupted from the chamber like an emerald sun before slowly dimming to reveal the voltrite boots resting whole and intact within.

Relief surged through Aodhán immediately, so intense he nearly collapsed from it. Then he laughed, unable to help himself.

“Oh, it worked!” he gasped, excitement rising beneath the relief. “It fucking worked.”

“Bless the storm,” Elder Kobi chuckled, relief plain on his face. “For a moment there, I feared I would have to write a formal apology to High Elder Velora for ruining her great grandson’s present.”

“Please,” Elder Gumi chuckled, as though she hadn't spent the last several minutes muttering prayers alongside him. “I told you it would work. You simply had to believe in me like Aodhán here.”

Elder Kobi snorted, and Elder Gumi laughed softly as she lifted the chamber lid and carefully drew the voltrite boots free.

Almost immediately, notifications pinged in his mind, texts crawling across his vision, but Aodhán barely spared them a glance, his attention completely captured by the shimmering masterpiece of voltrite, silverfrost, and gold resting in Elder Gumi’s hands.

The boots looked almost crystalline beneath the workshop lights, their angular plating gleaming like carved emerald glass threaded with flowing gold.

They were phenomenal, infinitely more beautiful than the electric-steel prototype, and when Elder Gumi laid them to rest on the table, Aodhán had to physically restrain himself from touching them.

“What will you call them?” Elder Kobi asked and Elder Gumi smiled.

“I shall call them Voltrite Vector Boots.”

Aodhán grimaced immediately while Elder Kobi sighed into his palm.

“Please no,” the older man groaned. “The first artifact created using your inner-outer carving method and that is the name you choose?”

Elder Gumi sputtered indignantly, and Aodhán realized with amusement that he had never seen her look so humbled before.

“Uh…” She frowned thoughtfully at the boots. “Then perhaps… Voltrite Vector Striders?”

Elder Kobi winced and Aodhán chuckled in sympathy.

“What about Anglewalkers?” he suggested, pointing toward the voltrite boots. “These could be the Voltrite Anglewalkers, and that—” he gestured toward the prototype nearby, “—could be the Electric-Steel Anglewalkers.”

Elder Gumi glanced toward Elder Kobi for approval and he shrugged.

“It’s not exactly groundbreaking, but it’s not quite as terrible as Voltrite Vector Boots.”

Elder Gumi scowled at him. “Very funny, Kobi.”

She glanced down at the boots then and smiled. “Fine. They shall be called Anglewalkers.”

Another notification pinged in his mind and this time Aodhán glanced at it.

Congratulations! You have assisted in the creation of a Blessed Item. [Architect’s Assistant] title gained.

Congratulations! You have been instrumental in the naming of a Blessed Item: Amateur Runic Scholar → Runic Scholar.

Congratulations! Your runesmithing titles have reached sufficient resonance: Amateur Runesmith + Runic Scholar + Architect’s Assistant

Merge initiated…

Your understanding of runesmithing has deepened.New title gained: Amateur Runic Architect.

Aodhán read the messages with a smile before dismissing them. When his gaze returned to the boots, a new menu appeared before him.

[View Anglewalker Stats]

He selected it immediately and another line of text appeared.

Name: Voltrite Anglewalkers.

Classification: Blessed.

Type: Soul bound runic combat boots.

Core attributes: Adaptive Sizing, Momentum accumulation, self-purification, storm power enhancement.

Special trait: Aerial Mobility—Grants the user the ability to walk, run, and maneuver through air as though traversing solid ground.

This item scales with bond.

Aodhán stared at the boots’ attributes in shock.

The name sounded even better now that he was reading it, and the traits were more than impressive, especially for someone who was just starting out—but what really caught his attention was the final line.

Scale with its bond.

That meant the boots’ current attributes—impressive as they already were—were merely the starting point. That single line transformed the artifact from something merely extraordinary into something else entirely.

He glanced up at Elder Gumi, his eyes wide. “Is this… right?”

“Oh, absolutely,” Elder Gumi grinned. “Whoever inherits these boots is beginning their journey very, very fortunately. With an item like this, the child could probably stumble his way into the Core Sect even without talent.”

Aodhán shook his head slowly, wondering what his own life would have looked like if he had started his cultivation journey with something like this.

“I’m done with the prototype.”

Elder Kobi’s voice snapped him from his thoughts, and Aodhán turned toward the older man rising from the forge table, the second electric-steel boot suspended delicately in his hands.

He passed it to Elder Gumi, who inspected it briefly before nodding once. “It’s good. Put it in the chamber.”

Elder Kobi obeyed, and several minutes later, he pulled the completed boot free from the chamber.

It wasn’t quite as beautiful as the voltrite boots, but it was nothing to be looked down on either. And when Elder Kobi finally placed it beside the other leg, the view stat menu appeared.

Aodhán clicked on it without hesitation and another screen appeared.

Name: Electric-Steel Anglewalkers.

Classification: Mythic Item.

Type: Runic combat boots.

Core attributes: Adaptive Sizing, Momentum accumulation, self-purification, lightning power enhancement.

Special trait: Aerial Mobility: Grants the user the ability to walk, run, and move on air as though on land.

Almost immediately, the differences became obvious.

First came the name, then the class, and finally the lack of a soul bound attribute. The greatest distinction, however, lay in the absence of a scaling factor.

These boots would never scale with their owners.

Even so, they were incredible.

The aerial mobility alone made them worthwhile, and without hesitation, Aodhán blurted, “Can I buy it?”

Elder Gumi glanced at him, her expression thoughtful, before shrugging.

“If you can pay for the electric steel and chaos treasures used to make them, then sure. You can buy them.”

Aodhán’s eyes widened. “I, uh… yeah. I can pay for it.”

Elder Kobi shook his head. “Why though? The boots are impressive and all, but you don’t really need them. Not truly.”

“I know.” Aodhán smiled. “But they won’t be for me. They are for my underling.”

Elder Kobi’s eyes widened, and even Elder Gumi smiled faintly.

“That’s very generous, Aodhán. Our underlings are our responsibility.”

Aodhán nodded, and a few minutes later, he was holding the electric-steel Anglewalkers in his hands with a grin.

Now that Crixis was back, it was time to resume their training, and this was the perfect incentive.

The next few minutes passed in a blur of quiet conversation and movement as he and Elder Kobi cleared the workshop together, chatting softly as they worked.

By the time they finished, it was already past midnight, and after saying goodbye to Elder Gumi, Aodhán teleported home.

Varéc had returned and was curled up somewhere upstairs. Crixis, however, had fallen asleep in the living room, most likely while waiting for him to return.

Aodhán smiled slightly.

Leaning down, he placed the Anglewalkers carefully beside him and pulled a cloth over his bare feet.

A growl echoed from upstairs as Varéc sensed his presence. With one last glance at the Anglewalkers, Aodhán teleported upstairs before Varéc could arrive and wake Crixis.

He found the Fury curled up on his bed, wings spread out in a manner that took up the entire mattress.

Aodhán grinned. “I see you had a very good day soaring through the skies.”

Varéc growled happily, sharing an image of himself, Gizelle, Levi, and Caelion flying through the clouds together all day.

Aodhán snorted and shook his head. “Well, I’m glad you had fun. I, on the other hand, have been on my feet for the past seventeen hours.”

Varéc unfurled his wings invitingly, offering him a place to rest, but despite the stress of the day, Aodhán didn’t feel even remotely tired.

He declined the offer with a small shake of his head and moved to his worktable instead. He pulled out his runic practice notes from his spatial storage a moment later and sat down with a quiet sigh.

Play time was over.

It was time he finished learning the initiate runes.
