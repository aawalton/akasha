I almost burst into tears.

It was a ridiculous reaction. I wasn’t happy to see him. I was still angry at him. Perhaps even a little bit afraid of him.

But… now someone else in this awful place knew exactly what was going on. Would believe me, probably without question, if I shared Agnes’ cruel words.

Renner stood frozen in the doorway. He was staring at me.

“Hey, are you alright?” Terrence clasped my shoulder gingerly. “You’re shaking.”

I jerked my head up and down, turning stoically away from my former companion. Perhaps it was foolish, but I didn’t want him to see me so obviously distraught. Not after earlier, after my bold claims of saving Teela and not being afraid and this entire day of helplessly wandering through the woods without a shred of success… My throat felt thick and my voice cracked as I rasped, “Y-yes, I’m sorry, I just… it’s been a l-long day.”

Wood creaked as Ward marched across the room. He was looking at Renner, mouth curled into a grimace, fingers once again inching towards his belt. “A traveler at this hour? And…” the thin man trailed off. Flinty eyes flicked to me, taking in my expression, and a ghastly smile crawled across his face. “Is this him?”

“Him?” Renner echoed, voice gruff.

I sidestepped over, positioning myself very deliberately between the two men as all immediate discomfort was swallowed by the need to mollify the volatile guard. “Yes, this is the man I told you about. And he had nothing to do with Teela being taken.”

Josiah rose from his seat near the bar, soundless and ominous. For a moment I worried that he had been swayed by Ward’s constant arguments. Aveline and Thom had also fallen silent, and were watching us.

Terrence alone seemed at a loss. He looked around, one hand tousling his hair, then gave Renner a little wave. “Er, hello. Brin’s mentioned you. Would you, uh, like to join us for supper?”

Renner prowled forwards, steps slow and deliberate, narrowed eyes flicking from me to our mostly-silent audience. Something about the way he moved made me uneasy. Muscles coiled, fingers twitching… as if he felt on-edge. Wary.

I can hardly blame him. Salt, Ward looks like he’s about ready to draw his sword. And Josiah… I need to fix this.

I cleared my throat, spreading both hands in what I hoped was an appeasing gesture, and offered Ward a wan smile. “Look, why don’t we all sit and… and, if you want, I’m sure Renner will, um, answer any questions you have?”

The man in question stopped in front of me. The scents of pine and earth hit my nose. “Questions, huh? I’ve got a few of my own. What are you doing here?”

“Looking for Teela, of course. What are you… doing… h-here…” A chill ran down my spine as the pieces fell into place. The clear anger on his face, the coiled readiness, what I’d told him about Agnes…

Saints above. Is he here to kill her?

I let out a breath, suddenly very happy that there were five other people present. He couldn’t very well murder an old woman with so many witnesses around.

My gaze fell from his face to his cloak and worn leathers. They glistened in places, dappled with sticky pine sap and scattered fir needles.

My pulse quickened. He left hours ago. He was going to head north. But then, why turn around at night just to… surely he couldn’t have been…

“Were you looking for her, too?” I whispered. I bit my lower lip, eyes rounding, hardly daring to hope.

He took in my expression with a grimace. “No.” Silence crawled between us. After a long moment he sighed, aimed a glare very pointedly at the ceiling, and muttered, “Doesn’t matter. Didn’t find anything.”

Ward came stomping over. “Well, scoundrel, you've got some behavior to account for. And I’ll wager this ends with you in chains.”

Renner looked the guard up and down, his glower unabating. “Scoundrel, huh? That’s a new one. What do I call you?”

The man’s narrow face twisted into a scowl as he drew himself up to his full height. Which was not very much bigger than me, or Renner, for that matter. “Ward Genwell, second-in-command to captain Rell of the Snowmelt Watch. There’s a young woman missing, and you are the obvious suspect. By my authority, I hereby place you under arrest.”

“You’re still not listening to me.” My voice rose to a near-shout as I rounded on the man. “And you’re wasting time. You’ve been wasting time all day, trying to get us to go north instead of…” My nostrils flared as I sucked in a steadying breath and continued with forced calm, “Look, I… I’m sorry. I know this has all been a lot, but please just listen. And… and if you have questions then I’m sure Renner can… er, verify my story.” I cut a pleading gaze to the man beside me.

But he wasn’t paying attention to me. He was looking behind him, towards the door.

Josiah stood near it, burly forearms braced against a windowsill. He was peering out through the glass, as if searching for something in the dwindling light.

“Who’re your friends?” Renner’s voice held that too-casual tinge. The tone he used to ask me what I was thinking about when he knew it was something awful.

Something cold knotted in my stomach. I swallowed. “That’s… that’s Josiah. And this is Terrence, and Thom and… er, and you’ve already met… um…” I gestured vaguely towards the lovely, dark-haired woman nearby. My face grew warm.

Aveline watched us with hooded eyes. Rather than offer any kind of greeting, she rose and drifted towards the kitchen.

Ashes, is she suspicious of him, too? They can’t all be!

“Uh-huh. You got a room, ladyship?”

“Not yet.” My fingers twisted around the borrowed cloak. “We… we only just arrived.”

“Miss Agnes is fixing everyone some supper,” Terrence supplied helpfully. He patted a nearby tabletop, giving a shaky laugh. “Maybe, uh, we can all sit down and hear Miss Brin’s story again? And, er, ask questions? Get this… sorted?” His brown eyes flicked towards Ward uncertainly.

The thin man made no answer, other than to shift slightly towards the kitchen.

In front of the hallway. And now there were people blocking both exits.

My heart pounded madly. They’re overreacting.This is just a misunderstanding. They’re suspicious of Renner but they… they don’t have a good reason, and…

Saints. Oh, saints, what if this is something else?

“Okay.” Renner rubbed his jaw. Leather scraped against his stubble. That, and the occasional crackle from the fireplace, sounded painfully loud in the still room. “Yeah. Sure. Let’s all sit and talk.” He looked pointedly at the two standing figures, then gestured towards the nearest table. “Well?”

Josiah didn’t move. Neither did Ward.

Renner spread his hands and slid into a chair. His pack slipped off one shoulder, falling to the floor. “I’m ready when you are.”

Terrence gave an awkward, too-loud laugh as he claimed a seat nearby. He looked up at me, forehead furrowed.

I returned the uncertain glance, taking a step towards the other side of the table. Gloved fingers caught mine. Tugged. I looked down.

Renner inclined his head towards the chair beside him. Between him and the wall.

I settled into it, then offered Ward a faint smile. “Let’s get this sorted, Ward. What questions do you have?”

The narrow-faced man glanced towards the kitchen. He seemed to be thinking. Then he stalked forwards and braced his hands against the table, hovering over us. “Right. How about an explanation, to start with. Explain your relationship to this woman, and to her missing friend.”

Renner’s eyebrows rose. “‘Relationship?’ We all met on the road, a few days south. I don’t turn down good company.”

Ward’s flinty eyes narrowed. “And why did you decide to come to Snowmelt? We were told you had ‘pressing business’ in the north.”

He shrugged. “Figured I’d get a horse. And if a pretty face like this wants to see the sights, how can a man say no?”

Heat flooded up my neck. I didn’t have time to be embarrassed for long, though; his knee nudged against mine, too hard to be accidental. I flicked my eyes downwards. He was prodding his pack towards me underneath the table.

A chill rippled across my skin. He’s worried, and I don’t think it’s just about being arrested. This is something more. I peered through my lashes, gaze flicking towards the door.

Josiah still hadn’t moved.

Ward stroked his jaw. “Fine. So this morning, was this Teela just an afterthought? Why didn’t you stay and help find her?”

Another shrug. “I need to get to Pinnacle. Spent some time looking for our friend today. Figured I’d hole up here and set out at dawn.”

“And what is it you’re so desperate to get to there for? What is your ‘pressing business’?”

Renner kept his voice even. “That’s personal.”

Unease crawled across the table. Ward drummed his fingers rapidly against the wood, eyes glinting with something that almost resembled satisfaction. “So you’re not even going to say? It must have been important. So important that you’d leave a woman to die- unless you’re the one who did away with her?”

His eyebrows rose again. “Right. So… you think I, what, exactly? Found two women on the road, lured them into a town full of witnesses, and… dragged one off into the wilderness to do gods’ know what? Then waltzed back into town, spent the morning with Brin, made sure she knew her friend was missing, and took off? Did I hit my head before I came up with this plan?”

Ward’s hollow cheeks flushed scarlet.

I peered up at him, at the fresh scowl brewing on his features. “You know that doesn’t make sense. You know it. It’s what I’ve been telling you all day. And it’s exactly what your captain said. Are you truly so convinced there can’t be a monster?”

He didn’t answer.

Renner’s boot bumped into mine.

I snuck a glance downwards. His pack was just beside my foot. I could reach down and snatch it at a moment’s notice.

Why, though? Why is he giving me his things? Does he want me to take the shard? Pull the crossbow out? Grab it all and run for the door?

He was watching Thom, now. The stout man drifted towards the bar and poured himself a drink, seemingly disinterested in our conversation.

I twisted in my chair, facing Josiah. “Think. That version of events is just… it’s ludicrous. And the captain came with me and saw the marks outside our window. He said it was a Fae. This… Renner doing all of this just doesn’t make any sense. You have to see that.”

The enormous man gave no response.

Ward continued to loom over the two of us. “Miss, what I see is a hostile stranger who made some very suspicious decisions. Negligent, even.”

“Hostile?” Renner turned towards me. His expression was all innocence. “Am I coming across as hostile, ladyship?”

My mouth felt very dry. “No.”

“I didn’t think so.”

Ward curled his lip. “You’re refusing to answer questions. I’m choosing to consider that hostile.”

Renner’s chair creaked and groaned as he leaned back, voice laced with derision. “I gotta say; as a concerned citizen with a missing friend, I’m not convinced the Snowmelt Watch is doing its finest work, here.”

Ward’s hands clenched, knuckles pressing into the table. “Perhaps we’re simply ill-equipped to deal with roving murderers.”

“Is the food ready?” Terrence interrupted, voice slightly shrill. He lurched to his feet, facing the kitchen.

Aveline had reappeared. She held the stone mortar in one hand and a large mug in the other.

I caught a faint whiff of apples. Apples and sharp ginger and something pleasantly sweet.

Agnes stepped out behind the young woman. She stood quietly for a moment, smoothing out a few loose strands of wispy gray hair, then gave Terrence a tight smile. “Not just yet, young Master. In fact, I was hoping to ask you to accompany Miss Aveline outside. I need a few more herbs from my garden. She can show you which ones.”

The young man blinked. “More… herbs? Well, ma’am, it’s nearly dark out-”

She tutted. “Oh, you’ve got a bit of sunlight left. It won’t take long. Trust me, the flavor will be worth it.” She winked at him. The skin around her eyes and mouth crinkled.

Aveline drifted forwards, taking Terrence’s hand with a sweet smile. “You won’t let me go all alone, will you?”

He balked, cheeks tinged pink. “Oh, er, no, of course not! Let’s just… we should try to be very quick, I think.”

“It won’t take long,” she echoed, tugging him towards the entrance.

Josiah eased himself to the side as the pair drifted out the door, then closed it gently behind them.

“Ladyship.” Renner’s murmur was too casual again.

“Yes?”

“You know your little trick from earlier? When you almost ruined my boots?”

My palms grew clammy. “Y-yes?”

He leaned towards me. His smile was easy, his tone conversational, but the light in his eyes was dangerous. “Think you could do it again?”

Cold sweat beaded along my temples. Can I fight. That’s what he means. Can I defend myself. Oh, salt, what does he think is going to happen? “I… I can try,” I whispered.

Hushed voices turned my attention back to Agnes. She had sidled over to the bar and was speaking quietly with Thom. The stout man’s eyes flicked towards me. He nodded.

Ward straightened. He was watching the pair, too. When they fell silent he growled, “Well? This solves it, right?”

“Ward.” The rumble made me shiver.

Agnes patted at her apron and pursed her thin, wrinkled lips. “It’s alright, Josiah. I think he’s got the right idea.”

“And what idea is that?” my companion asked, rising to his feet.

She sniffed. “All this business is just dreadful, isn’t it? A missing woman, and the whole town in an uproar. Just terrible.”

“It is,” I rasped. “Please, help us find her.”

The words died in the air. Agnes’ face grew cold again. Ward glowered at me. There was a quiet thud from behind as Josiah stepped towards us. Thom moved to stand beside Ward.

Renner’s hands moved slowly, metal hissing as he drew his glinting broadsword and a wicked-looking dagger.

Ward drew his own.

Agnes curled her lip, drawing back from Renner. “It does make things simpler. You showing up.”

“Happy to help,” he snarled.

She shook her head. “It’s a shame, really. This sweet young lady came to my establishment, and who should return but the villain who killed her friend? Came to finish what he started. Thank goodness these fine men were here to finish you off. But not before you’d killed her. What a tragedy.”

Ward snickered.

Josiah murmured from a little ways behind us, “Baltha, or just… dead?” I could practically hear him wince at the question.

Agnes sighed. “Baltha. Two should keep her happy for a good while.” She gave a dry little chuckle. “I think the wildlife has been quite hard for her, lately.”

“No one will believe this!” I cried, lurching to my feet. My whole body was shaking.

Renner shifted, angling a shoulder in front of me. Nudging me back, towards the wall.

Agnes huffed. “You think? Hm. She may have a point. Perhaps it all happened out in the woods. They buried your bodies, and just can’t seem to recall exactly where.” She tittered. “There. That should be a bit easier to swallow.”

I felt like I couldn’t breathe. My voice rose several octaves, turning shrill with panic. “They know there’s a Fae, the captain knows, he’ll have questions, you won’t-”

“Ladyship.”

“What?!”

“Now.”
