“Enough,” I hear barked out by the taller figure of the two in front of me. Immediately, he disengages, pulling his sword away, though holding it warily. The slighter figure takes a moment to react, but he’s moving sluggishly enough that he’s only just lifted his shorter blades before the word and actions register.

Sarran gives a firm nod, then steps away, sheathing his blade even as Loran does the same, fumbling the movement. Sarran steps towards me. He’s thoughtful, and I think I might even see the sheen of sweat in the light Aingeal is helpfully shedding for us. Good signs. I pull a chair out of my Inventory and he takes it with a nod of thanks.

Of course, Loran is in a much worse state – the moment Sarran steps away, he practically collapses to the stone beneath his feet, looking utterly worn out.

It’s quiet in the training ground – at this time of night, most people are asleep, and I specifically asked Maxen to ensure that no one comes to spy on us. A Seal around us, courtesy of Sarran whose Skill is a lot higher than mine, makes sure that no noise escapes the ring – and that we’re blurry even if spies have managed to get through without Maxen’s awareness.

It might be overkill, but right now, we’re so much at the beginning of this that I don’t want to take any chances.

I pull out another chair and offer it wordlessly to Loran. He shakes his head weakly, so I just toss him a water canteen. He fumbles the catch but nods at me gratefully, drinks deeply, and then lies back on the stone, clearly too exhausted to care about decorum.

“So?” I ask Sarran expectantly. “How would you assess him?”

Unlike a mage who might have used magic, Sarran is primarily a swordsman and has used that as his tool of analysis.

On the note of mages, I remember back to Tiria’s excitement when I told her about Loran’s development. Unsurprisingly, she’s absolutely thrilled. I actually had to be firmer with her than I wanted to be when her desire to examine the changes started interfering with Loran’s duties. I think mournfully of how, even though things have calmed down a bit on the crises front, I don't seem likely to get any more time with her due to her dedication to her research.

“He’s much faster,” Sarran admits, pulling my focus back to him and out of my thoughts. “Stronger too, though there’s less of a change to that than his speed. But the biggest change is to his processing speed. He reacts far faster than ever before, and his movements – at least in our first few spars – were much better thought-out than in the previous spars we’ve had. His dual-wielding has leapt forward and he seems to be thinking several moves ahead. He’s also able to manage to keep at least two trains of thought running at the same time, without sacrificing anything in doing so.”

I smile a little – the memory of hearing Sarran quizzing Loran on principles of being a steward and encouraging him to make measured judgements in case studies while attacking him fiercely with the blade is still amusing.

“However, his stamina is still limited, and as he tires, his advantages reduce in effect. Still, I would say that even at the end of the spar, his performance hovered around the level of his top performance prior to his Evolution.”

I nod in satisfaction, sending a warm sense of appreciation down the link to Loran. He sends me a tired emotional equivalent to a thumbs up.

“Then, if you compare him to a Classer, where would you put him?”

Sarran doesn’t answer immediately, his gaze shifting to focus on something far away and his thumb rubbing the pommel of his sword thoughtfully.

While he thinks, Loran finishes the canteen of water I tossed him and pushes himself to his feet with a groan.

“I bet you’ll be feeling that tomorrow,” I tease him. He sends me a mock-glare.

“Try for a week,” he replies with another groan as he stretches out tired muscles.

I hum quietly, not agreeing with him.

“You might be surprised at how quickly your body recovers.”

“I hope so,” Loran replies fervently, stepping forward to sit in the third chair.

“Genuinely, how do you feel?” I ask him quietly, seeing how Sarran is still buried in his thoughts.

“Sore,” he replies, a touch sourly. Then he sighs. “Better than I would have if Sarran put me through that a few days ago.” He looks thoughtful for a moment. “I think you have a good point about how quickly I’ll recover. Already, I feel my strength returning.”

“It would be useful to know when you no longer feel any of the after-effects of the training,” Sarran interjects, apparently finished with his ruminations. “It will enable me to better guess your equivalent Constitution.”

“I’ll tell you,” Loran promises. “Does that mean you know what my other stats are?”

“Not with needlepoint accuracy,” Sarran warns him, “but I can make a reasonable estimate based on what you have revealed in our spars as I pushed you.” He clears his throat and we lean forward expectantly. The exasperation that drifts down the Bond from Loran almost makes me chuckle – perhaps Sarran has a hidden dramatic side that his apprentice knows all too well.

“While pending information about Constitution, I would say that Strength is your lowest stat, and that your Evolution increased Power more than Endurance. I’d approximate this to be around eighteen points, combined. Next, I would say is Willpower, based on your reaction to my Taunt and Fear Skills. I would say that this is between twenty and twenty-five points.”

I whistle slowly – that’s an impressive increase from his baseline.

“I would estimate both Intelligence and Dexterity to be above twenty-five but under thirty points each. Apart from Constitution, the only one I do not feel comfortable giving even an estimate in is Wisdom.”

Even without those two stats, I find my eyebrows rising as I tally up the points. Loran couldn’t have been above twenty in any of those stats, and was most likely lower than that in at least some of them. Which means that at the minimum, his Evolution has earned the equivalent of twenty-five points, and probably more. With his much more complex internal matrix, Loran’s Wisdom has undoubtedly increased, and his quicker recovery time – he’s already showing fewer signs of exhaustion – indicates that his Constitution has too.

“Would you say thirty points is a reasonable baseline estimate?”

“At least,” Sarran confirms. “Possibly as much as forty.”

Loran looks even more shocked than I feel.

“That’s…at least ten levels even in a good Uncommon Class!”

“It is a significant boost,” Sarran agrees. “Though I wonder how much you are likely to progress without Evolving again – it’s my understanding that most advancement for beasts happens during the Evolution itself.”

“That’s certainly what I’ve observed,” I agree, eyeing Loran speculatively. “I guess we’ll just have to experiment and see. Still, more than doubling the stats of a person is an impressive step forward – and that without a Class stone.”

“I did need a good number of Cores, though,” Loran points out, finally regaining control over his mouth, though his eyes still look a little dazed as his gains are quantified.

I gesture out in the direction of the gates.

“We’re hardly lacking those,” I point out. “Though it did take you some time to work out what was best for you.”

“And guidance from you in how to grow my internal matrix,” Loran points out.

“Only to a limited extent,” I disagree, “and, honestly, a conversation at the end there might have done as much as my examination.”

“Then you intend to spread this more widely?” Sarran asks, his eyes narrowing.

I pause for a moment.

“I feel that it’s worth more exploration,” I say finally. “I don’t intend to announce this from the rooftops, but if we can attract a small group of people who are willing to give it a go so we can see that Loran isn’t an exception in having the capacity to Evolve….”

“We should make sure they’re willing to have a Dominate Bond,” Loran points out. “It might not be necessary, but it was certainly reassuring to know that I had someone keeping an eye on what was happening inside me.”

“Fair point,” I agree. “Alright, I’ll organise it. Do you mind if I direct them to you for any questions about the process?”

“Of course,” Loran agrees. “In fact, why don’t I take responsibility for the group entirely? I know how busy you are.”

“You’re hardly less busy yourself,” I point out.

Loran gives me a look firm enough that I marvel at how far our relationship has come since our first meeting.

“I can delegate some of my responsibilities to make space for this new one. Which is what you should be doing too, Markus.”

“Loran,” Sarran chides. Loran shakes his head sharply, then pushes up from his chair.

“I’m going to run some drills. My accuracy was lacking earlier when I was throwing knives at a target.”

With that, he strides away, with only the barest nod in my direction.

An uncomfortable silence falls between us as we watch him grab a set of throwing knives from a rack and then position himself several metres away from the target. Once he’s thrown all ten of them, he strides towards the target and retrieves them. I feel a pulse of frustration through the Bond – clearly he isn’t happy with the overall score, though all the knives hit the target, at least.

I watch as Loran returns to his position and then repeats his actions. And then again. Next to me, Sarran is almost a tangible presence. Though I have no Bond with him, I can almost feel the words he’s holding back.

“Alright,” I sigh when it grows too much for me, tired as I am. “Out with it.”

“While I wouldn’t have put it the way Loran did, I can’t deny that he has a point,” Sarran replies as soon as I’ve made the invitation.

“About?”

“Delegation. Markus, you are a very…hands-on leader,” he tells me delicately. “And that is good for many reasons. Indeed, I do not know whether we would have come through the crises that have assailed us since arriving without your leadership. However, now that things have calmed down a little,” he makes a gesture with his hand to ward off evil – or, as I would name it, Murphy, “perhaps you could extend a little more trust to your managers. To us, and the citizen leaders.”

“I do trust you,” I object, turning to face him full on.

“I am not saying that you don’t…. But this is the time to show it with your actions.”

I gaze at Sarran, abruptly tongue-tied. I have shown it with my actions…haven’t I?

Sarran continues gently. “Few lords are so intimately involved in every major decision in a settlement unless there is a problem. That is what trusted stewards are for – to manage the everyday life of the people. The lord provides the direction – and the strength. When was the last time you focused on increasing your own personal strength?”

I blink, feeling almost blind-sided for a moment, I hadn’t thought about it like that. I’d thought I was doing what was right for the settlement – managing it. But…he has a point.

If we’d had to fight Tilathis, we would have lost many who wouldn’t have even been able to scratch him. The roc died because Kalanthia and I managed to strike it with a blow that it struggled to overcome and drove it into the water where its natural advantages were nullified. What if we one day have to fight someone even more powerful? Throwing numbers at it isn’t something we can afford to do – we need someone who can match it, defeat it. I need to be strong enough.

And while technically, I work on my own strength all the time – repairing the damage done to me by the ritual even while I’m just sitting quietly listening – I know that’s not enough.

Honestly, thinking about it, I’ve missed having time for things. There are so many magical experiments I’d like to make, so many new things I’ve learned that I want to try applying. And I’ve been neglecting my companions – River wouldn’t have almost killed herself in pursuit of a solution to the food issue if I had been paying enough attention to her.

I can’t regret my efforts in the past few months – as Sarran said, they might have been essential to our success. But now?

Maybe he has a point.

“I still want to be kept up to date,” I tell Sarran firmly.

“Of course,” he agrees. “We can organise a weekly briefing – daily, if there is something of particular importance or sensitivity.”

“And I want to write down a list of my priorities for the settlement. Things like education and developing a new monetary system.”

“Certainly – just pass it to me or Loran and we will ensure it’s carried out.”

I hesitate for a moment longer, then nod.

“Alright. I trust you. And Loran,” I say, raising my voice a little so I know he can hear. I see his shoulders stiffen. “You’re in charge of the Evolution group.”

He turns his head, sending me a grin even as a mix of emotions drift down the bond between us: the hint of nerves and determination to get this right.

It feels like both a lessening of weight on my shoulders, and a constricting band of iron around my heart. Yet part of me still looks forward to finally being able to think about more than just the settlement. And I know just where I want to start.
