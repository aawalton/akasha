The forest was a far better place to be than Volstarn; at least out here, Oswald didn’t have to sidestep coughing bodies or hear the ragged breaths of the sick. The air smelled of damp leaves and rich soil rather than the staleness of illness and decay.

He'd been pushing through the underbrush for quite some time. The weight of his backpack pressed heavier than it should against his shoulders, though not because it was heavy, after all, he hadn’t found a single Duskbloom yet. Hours of walkin’, and nothin’ to show for it.

Oswald had searched every shaded grove, checked the bases of gnarled old trees, even scouted along the narrow streams winding through the forest. Yet the elusive herbs refused to show themselves.

The sun had already started its slow descent, streaking the sky in deep orange. Oswald came to a stop beneath a towering oak.

He leaned against its bark and let his eyes scan the dense foliage ahead. The only movement came from the occasional flutter of a bird disappearing into the trees. No snapping twigs, no shuffling of unseen feet. Guess it’s safe enough.

The boy reached up, loosening the knot at the back of his head. The fabric of his eyepatch slipped away.

He blinked, his vision adjusting, the familiar weight of his Animus pressing at the edges of his perception. The world sharpened.

Shadows no longer swallowed details, and everything once lost in the dim light now stood out in sharp relief. Twisting vines, the jagged edges of broken branches, even the faint shimmer of moisture clinging to blades of grass unfolded before him with startling clarity. That's better, let’s try this again.

Oswald pushed deeper into the forest. For the longest time, his trek was relatively uneventful. But then, a prickle ran down his spine.

His uncovered eye swept over the thick undergrowth, picking apart every shadow. The wind barely stirred the trees, yet a weight settled over him, draping across his shoulders like an unseen presence.

Something was watching him. He could feel it.

A shrill, cackling cry split the air, high-pitched like laughter from something that had never known humor. It echoed from deeper in the forest, distant yet distinct. A second call followed, slightly lower in tone, as if answering the first. Then, silence.

Oswald’s fingers twitched toward his sword. But he hesitated. No use swingin’ at shadows. His grip eased, and he forced his shoulders to relax, exhaling slow and steady. If there was something out there, he wasn’t going to find it by flinching at every sound.

He closed his eyes and focused. Selene had told him once that some monsters carried mana cores. If the Duskwings had a leader, then maybe, just maybe, it would stand out.

For a long moment, there was nothing. Then, he sensed a flicker.

It wasn’t overwhelming or refined like a mage’s mana. This was different. It pulsed wild and uneven, like a flame fighting to burn through damp wood: untamed, erratic, but undeniably alive.

And whatever it was, it wasn’t human. Oswald’s eyes snapped open. Gotcha.

He turned toward the presence, weaving between the trees with newfound purpose. The closer he got, the more unnatural the silence became.

Through the thinning trees, Oswald spotted a clearing. At its center, the land dipped into a shallow, muddy hollow where dark figures lurked, half-hidden by the dimness.

Oswald crouched low, pressing himself against the rough bark of a gnarled tree. He steadied his breath, gaze locked on the creatures before him. Those have to be Duskwings.

They stood hunched with sleek black feathers . Their bird-like heads bore sharp, curved beaks. Though their bodies remained cloaked in their glossy plumage, their monstrous nature revealed itself in their large, sinewy legs ending in curved talons that gripped the mud.

And then Oswald saw it. At the center of the flock, standing just a little taller than the rest, was the one he had been searching for.

Its feathers were darker than the others. Its beak was longer, its frame lean but powerful, every movement slow and deliberate. Unlike the others, which remained poised in stillness, this one looked over the others like a commander inspecting its soldiers.

Oswald’s jaw tightened as he studied it. Probably the alpha.

Luckily for him, when he looked down, he saw that sprouting in dense patches between the tangled roots of trees, were patches of Duskbloom. Their petals swayed gently, almost blending into the murk of the forest floor. There was more than enough to fill his bag.

Ain't gettin’ those herbs without dealin’ with those Duskwing's first. Time to fight.

After a breath, Oswald kicked off the ground and surged forward, closing the distance between him and the flock in seconds. The moment his boot crunched against the hollow’s damp soil, the Duskwings reacted.

The leader’s head snapped toward him, its beady black eyes locking onto his with a sharp, predatory awareness. For a heartbeat, there was nothing—just that piercing gaze, drilling into him like it could see straight through flesh and bone. Then, a guttural screech tore from its beak, splitting the silence like a blade.

The flock moved.

Oswald barely had time to brace before two Duskwings lunged, talons flashing. He twisted hard, ducking low as the first set of claws slashed just inches above his head, slicing through the air where his face had been a second ago. The rush of wind against his skin sent a shiver down his spine.

The second struck from the side, beak snapping for his shoulder. He threw his weight back, stumbling just out of reach, but the creature’s hooked talons tore into his cloak, carving a thin gash through the fabric. They’re faster than I thought.

His boots barely found solid ground before the first one lunged again, relentless. The second circled around, its movements precise, attacks timed in sync with its partner.

They ain't fightin' wild. They’re workin’ together.

Oswald raised his sword just in time, catching the first Duskwing’s strike. The impact rattled through his arm, a sharp jolt that nearly knocked the weapon from his grip. Before he could fully recover, the second creature lunged at his side. He twisted, barely slipping past a strike that would’ve gutted him.

Another swipe. Another dodge. He couldn’t afford to stay on the defensive forever.

Gritting his teeth, he tightened his grip on his sword. When the first Duskwing lunged again, he surged forward. His blade cut across its chest, slicing through feathers and flesh, but the creature barely hesitated.

It let out a shrill, agonized screech, recoiling, but before Oswald could press the advantage, the second Duskwing launched itself at his back. He spun, throwing up his arm just in time. Claws raked against his forearm instead of his ribs. The pain was sharp, but he knew better than to falter now. Time to change things up.

When the first Duskwing lunged again, he stepped into its attack. The sudden shift caught it off guard, its talons barely grazing past him before his blade rammed into the space between its ribs.

A strangled cry ripped from its beak. He twisted the sword free, tearing it from flesh and bone. The moment the creature staggered back, the second Duskwing lunged.

Oswald ducked low, its attack whistling past his head, and in the same breath, he drove his blade deep into its leg. The monster shrieked, raw with pain, its black feathers slick with fresh blood. It stumbled, its balance broken. Oswald surged forward as he drove his sword up beneath the creature’s beak.

The creature convulsed. Its eyes went glassy as the body collapsed, lifeless, onto the forest floor.

The clearing went still for only a moment. Then the leader shrieked, high and frantic. The remaining Duskwings hesitated, their beady eyes darting between Oswald and their fallen kin, before instinct took hold.

With a powerful push of their legs, they launched skyward, kicking up leaves and loose debris in their wake. Their panicked cries faded into the distance, swallowed by the darkness.

Oswald exhaled, watching them go. Yeah, that’s what I thought.

He had no reason to chase them down. That was, until a notification flickered at the edge of his vision.

[Congratulations, you have earned 2 Souls!]

So Duskwings can count to my soul count? That changed things.

Oswald still had a tithe to pay, and he wasn’t about to let easy souls just fly off into the night. His grip tightened around his sword as he launched himself forward in a burst of motion.

The Duskwings weren’t soaring too high. The creatures seemed to have relied on their legs as much as their wings, making their flight clumsy and awkward when forced to maneuver through the dense treetops. They ain’t built for this.

He closed the distance fast. The leader screeched, tilting mid-flight in a desperate attempt to dodge. Meanwhile, Oswald leapt, his blade catching the moonlight before driving deep into the base of its neck.

Wings spasmed wildly, feathers scattering as the Duskwing Alpha tumbled from the air, crashing through the underbrush in a violent heap. Oswald landed in a crouch beside it, twisting the blade before yanking it free. A final, weak shudder ran through the creature’s body before it stilled, its lifeless eyes reflecting the night.

Above, the rest of the flock faltered. They hesitated, their formation breaking for a split second. That was all he needed.

Oswald surged toward the next one. His sword arced through the air, slicing clean across a Duskwing’s throat before it could swerve away. A wet gurgle, then its body crumpled, blood seeping into the dirt.

The others screeched in alarm, panic twisting into chaos as they tried to flee in different directions. He didn’t let them.

One after another, he hunted them down. Duskwings dropped from the sky before they could escape, their shrieks cutting short as steel met flesh. Talons lashed out in desperation, but fear had dulled their coordination.

The last one let out a strangled cry before Oswald’s sword plunged into its chest, pinning it to the dirt. The creature twitched once before falling limp, wings splayed uselessly against the ground.

[Congratulations, you have earned 8 Souls!]

Oswald exhaled, his breath steadying as he wrenched his sword free from the final Duskwing’s chest. The creature’s body slumped fully to the dirt, wings twitching once before falling still. He wiped the blade clean against his cloak, watching the last traces of life fade from its glassy eyes.

Then, a flicker in his vision.

[Congratulations, you have earned 8 Souls!]

A grin pulled at the corner of his mouth. That’s more like it.

With a thought, he pulled up his status. The text soon unfolded in his mind.

Souls: 42Resonance: 1Invocations: ???Days until Tribute: 7

Relief settled in his chest. He had souls, for now, at least until the next payment.

With that settled, Oswald dismissed the system and turned back toward the clearing, looking for Duskbloom. The fight had left the area disturbed, feathers and splatters of dark blood littering the ground, but enough of the herbs were still intact.

He crouched low, brushing over the Duskbloom’s velvety petals. Oswald plucked them carefully, tucking them away into his backpack, one after another, making sure to avoid crushing them.

But as he worked, a prickling unease crept up his spine. At first, he thought it was more Duskwings watching him from the shadows, waiting for the right moment to strike.

His hand instinctively hovered near his sword, eyes flicking through the tree line. The forest was quiet, save for the distant rustle of leaves and the occasional chirp of some nocturnal creature.

Still, the feeling didn’t fade. Somehow, Oswald could feel that this wasn’t the Duskwings. This was something else, something much more ominous.

A deeper instinct stirred within him, urging him to listen. Oswald stood up and felt how the sensation tugged at him, like an unseen thread pulling him deeper into the trees.

The pull in his mind was subtle at first, like an itch just beneath the surface of his thoughts, nudging him forward. At first, he suspected Zvarah, his Animus Spirit, might’ve had something to do with it. But no, he still had control. His limbs weren’t moving against his will, and if he wanted, he could turn back right now and straight to Doctor Cecilia to finish the quest.

Then, he saw movement at the edge of his vision. A shape slipping between the trees, there one second, gone the next. That ain't normal.

After that, Oswald's decision was made. He followed the movements. Whatever was out there, it was fast, darting from tree to tree, just barely visible before vanishing again.

A whisper of air brushed Oswald’s cheek, sharp enough to sting. Something sliced through the space where his neck had been a heartbeat earlier, the edge so close he caught a faint hiss as it passed. His body jerked sideways instinctively. Too damn close.

His boots scraped the earth as he rolled, then came up in a crouch, At the same time, Oswald took out his sword in a single motion.

From the far edge of the clearing, something crawled into view. The fuck is that?

The creature had long, spindly fingers that dug into the soil. Each ended in a hooked black claw, flexing with a sick rhythm. Its arms were bent low to the ground as the rest of its body slithered out behind them. The creature’s skin was sickly pale, stretched tight over bones that jutted out like knives beneath the surface. It crawled on all fours, its limbs too long for its frame.

Its ears were long, pointed, and thin as blades. Then came the eyes, glowing red like embers buried in ash, and they locked onto him.
