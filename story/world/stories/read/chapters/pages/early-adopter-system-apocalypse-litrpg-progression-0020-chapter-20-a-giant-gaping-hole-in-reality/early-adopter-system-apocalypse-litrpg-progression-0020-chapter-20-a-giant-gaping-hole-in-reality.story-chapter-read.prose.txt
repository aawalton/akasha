Scarlet was close to the tree line now. She’d finished up with the harvesting, and decided it would be prudent to take a gander around the perimeter of the yard considering just how useless the CCTV had been at spotting the murderous bat.

The tech simply hadn’t been built for a post-Integration world.

That didn’t mean the tech was completely useless. It had clearly spotted the roidcoons. Only, because certain wildlife hadn’t counted as ‘threats’, her security system hadn’t bothered flagging them. An oversight she’d corrected.

Figuring she’d take a hybrid approach to security, after she’d cleaned up the mess in the yard, she’d gone to the garage and pulled out one of the aerial drones she’d purchased. With one eye on the drone footage, and the rest of her mind paying attention to her surroundings, the drone acted as an extra set of eyes that roamed past the range her senses could reach.

In fact, in the time she’d been outside she’d taken to spreading her senses out as far and wide as she could in a bid to ‘feel’ the world around her.

The mental exercise was the only part of this perimeter sweep she was enjoying. The truth was, she was exhausted. She could feel the gnawing ache in her stomach reminding her she hadn’t eaten since before she had established a digital knowledge hub, murdered a magical bat, and gained a Quest. Maus had more than once wandered off to munch on an edible root, or Blink into the bunker to find a snack. Scarlet, on the other hand, had been working for hours.

She was truly fascinated by the different energy signatures and mana types she was observing, even if she didn’t yet know what those signatures represented. The challenge was distracting her from the fact that her hands were painfully cramped, her legs were wooden, and she was pretty sure her body was starting to eat itself for fuel.

At first, everything had felt like a very loud, indistinct mass of clashing, harmonising, and coexisting energy. She was unable to make out anything specific due to the sheer volume she was processing. As she focused, that began to change. The noise did not dim, or settle, but she was beginning to recognise variations in the fluctuations. There were large consistent undercurrents. Energies that flowed like molasses where they didn’t just pool. Other signatures were smaller, hinting at movement and intent.

As she walked, she tested the limits of what she could observe. The more she focused, the clearer the distinctions became. She was sure recognition would come with time and familiarity.

She was halfway around the tree line when she brushed against something completely foreign. This energy wasn’t new to her senses the way a popular song might be if played on a different instrument. This was entirely alien, and it was loud. It was the sensory equivalent of gliding along a frozen lake only for the ice to crack and drop her into freezing water.

The assault on her senses threw her into a bout of vertigo, causing her to stumble and gasp. She clutched her cane like a lifeline as the overwhelm doubled her over.

Instinctively, she tried to pull her senses back, to shrink away from this wrongness in the middle of the forest. However, she was only barely able to dampen her passive Skills, not entirely withdraw them. She remembered musing earlier about people being able to turn off their senses, and promised herself that in future, she’d be one of those people.

Thankfully, the sensation passed quickly as her mind acclimatised to what it was feeling. Yay super brain, she supposed. It took her a moment to come back to herself and realise that Maus had climbed back onto her shoulder and was in full puffball alert mode, ready to target whatever was threatening her, which, considering his abilities, was a genuinely scary prospect.

Once she’d gathered enough wherewithal to make sure the drone hadn’t crashed in those few seconds of overwhelm, she realised that there was a System notification waiting for her.

[Spatial Anomaly Detected: Portal – Tier 1]

She had to reread the notification because the first time around, her mind refused to register what it said. She looked again, hoping beyond hope that she had not seen what she thought she had.

[Spatial Anomaly Detected: Portal – Tier 1]

Nope, same words.

“For the love of–” Would this day never end? She checked the time and let out an incredulous laugh.

[00:13]

The fatigue of the past several hours was catching up to her, and she took a moment to centre herself before she asked the obvious question. Just what did the System mean by ‘portal’?

[A portal is a stabilized spatial aperture leading to a contained dimensional pocket. The aperture functions as the primary access point to the pocket world.]

She just stared at the words for a while.

A giant hole in space… Just, there?

All right, that was going to be a nope from her. Not today. She was hungry, and exhausted. She had no clue how her website was doing, and she still needed to get in touch with some of her contacts because there was just no way she could manage that time-sensitive Quest alone.

Now there was a portal in her yard? Some sort of spatial distortion she’d no doubt have questions about, answers to which she'd inevitably have to wait for, and just. No.

Only, there was a threat at her door. Literally right there, only a few metres away. It would be irresponsible not to at least make sure this ‘portal’ wasn’t some gaping hole in reality, actively spewing monsters and causing mayhem.

The Guide had said it was an ‘aperture’, not a reinforced containment mechanism. She had no guarantees that this portal didn’t work both ways. She needed to at least check.

She moved in the direction of the now slightly less alien energy signature with measured steps. She was constantly scanning her surroundings, her movements making little noise as she went. She’d long ago learned to be stealthy even when using her cane.

She’d sent the drone off to scout ahead, but while it did pick up that there was a clearing around the area she expected this portal to be, the camera wasn’t picking up anything unusual at all. No glowing gates, or light-sucking singularities, or otherworldly sinkholes into an endless abyss. Just an empty clearing.

Despite this, she could absolutely feel the portal. The closer she got, the more defined the fluctuations became, and instead of the wave of information she’d been overwhelmed with the first time, now she felt somewhat able to pick out patterns in the fluctuations.

She was moving slowly enough that it was almost a surprise when the portal finally came into view. ‘Seeing’ it through her new and enhanced senses had not prepared her for what it would look like.

The distortion in the air shimmered faintly and bent the world around it as though the space nearby was more malleable. The area around the portal felt strangely dense. Tendrils of colour spread from its edges in a way that she was sure formed a pattern along its border, just one she couldn’t quite work out. Perhaps if she just focused on the colours, the way they shifted and flowed...

Focusing too hard on them brought back the nausea, and she could feel a terrible headache developing.

Scarlet slowed as she approached, her steps measured as her overtaxed senses extended ahead of her, testing the space before she committed to closing the distance.

On the bright side, it appeared that all forms of life that could move away from the portal had done so quite thoroughly. Scarlet didn’t blame them. The whole area felt off. It wasn’t that the portal felt unnatural, it was that the distortion itself was so obviously outside of normal that she’d wonder about the sanity of any pre-Integration creature willingly remaining in its presence. Any creature but her, she supposed.

“More the fool me,” she muttered as she shuffled closer. It didn’t take long until she was stopping just outside of that range.

A now very alert Maus was observing the portal alongside her with a focus that mirrored her own. She felt it through the bond.

Inspect.

[Portal – Tier 1Primary Signatures: Water, Nature, IceStatus: UnclaimedStability: 50%]

Scarlet pressed her lips together and stared through the words at the iridescent distortion in the fabric of reality.

The descending hum of the drone brought Scarlet back to herself. Righting it, she thought over all the things she still had to do and gave herself exactly one minute to lament. Then she needed to figure out her next steps.

She had a sinking feeling this would complicate her Quest.
