Aldric inhaled deeply as he waited his turn to enter the Ziggurat. Tyrus stood beside him, trepidation wafting off him to join the cloud of suspicion, anticipation, and curiosity that had formed above them.

He shuffled awkwardly, fingers tapping impatiently against his armor as they watched the Sunstonian entourage walk into the maw of the ziggurat, shoulders tense even in home territory.

“I don’t like this,” Tyrus whispered.

“Me neither,” Aldric exhaled. “But my brothers are somewhere in there. Their safety supersedes my fear.” He glanced at Artemis and forced a smile. “We’re surrounded by champions on all sides, Tyrus. We’ll be fine.”

Tyrus nodded, swallowing down his nervousness. “You’re right. We’ll be fine.”

Aldric wished he believed his own words so wholeheartedly.

Geneva joined them as the Unorian entourage began making their way into the ziggurat.

“Are you ready?” She asked quietly, her gaze uneasy as she subtly traced protective runic patterns around them. Tyrus didn’t notice.

“I’m as ready as I can be.” Aldric replied, and Geneva sighed.

“Be careful, both of you. We have no idea what may be lurking within this structure. Also, if you sense or see anything that you think might be useful, tell me immediately.”

“We will, ma’am.” Tyrus saluted.

“We’ll be careful,” Aldric assured her, and with a sharp inhale, he opened himself up to the emotions flowing around him. With so many people in such close proximity, the dense concoction of emotions burst into his spirit like a river tide of living flames.

Aldric blinked against the onslaught, his Tyrant of Boundless Empathy skill helping him to manage the surge of foreign emotions better. His empathic senses sharpened in response, and his heart grew heavy from the burden of it all, but Aldric refused to dampen his senses.

He needed to be at his very best for whatever came next.

He doubted he would be in any danger with all these champions and generals around him, but his own safety wasn’t why he needed to be in top shape. It was so that he didn’t miss anything by mistake. He needed his senses on high alert. After all, aside from his core mission here, General Lucas had tasked him with monitoring the emotions of the Sunstonians for signs of betrayal.

So far, so good; none of them seemed to abhor any sort of plan or ulterior motive. Everything was good on that front.

The last of the Calodans stepped into the ziggurat, and finally, it was their turn.

Bracing himself for a mental attack, Aldric stepped into the ziggurat with Tyrus by his side and was immediately bombarded with a wave of new emotions, or perhaps, it was more appropriate to say old emotions.

They slammed into his mind like a war hammer—guilt, betrayal, greed, ambition, and fear. But those were the old emotions—so old they felt more like echoes of echoes rather than any tangible impressions.

They were weak compared to what his senses picked up next, and this time, Aldric staggered from the force of it.

These emotions were more recent and stronger—so potent they were like a punch to the gut. And surprisingly, for a place supposedly crawling with cultists, they weren’t negative emotions.

Upon entry into the ziggurat, Aldric had expected to pick up the suffocating feeling of terror or the pungent scent of violence and resentment. He’d expected the blistering sense of agony and the drowning feel of hopelessness.

He had braced himself for those.

But the emotion that slammed into him wasn’t any of those. It was zeal and an overwhelming amount of fanatic devotion, so tangible it almost felt solid.

Tyrus caught him before he could slip. “Are you alright?”

“I’m… I’m fine,” Aldric replied as he regained his footing. Geneva, Zero, Thalia, and Artemis had just come in behind him, and they raised questioning eyebrows when they found him panting.

“What is it?” Geneva asked immediately, eyes scanning the walls for danger.

“Nothing physical. I’m fine,” Aldric assured her. “I was just overwhelmed by the density of emotions here.”

“What emotions?” General Lucas asked from his position in front with the other ambassadors. “Tell us what you sense.”

Aldric gestured haphazardly with a hand. “I don’t know how to explain it. It’s almost as if every single emotion that has ever been felt within these walls has remained preserved for centuries. I can feel emotions so old they feel like echoes to my senses and others that are very new. Recent event.”

People exchanged glances, tensing further at the implication of his words.

After a moment of silence, General Fang asked. “How recent?”

“A week, maybe two.” Aldric replied uncertainly. “I can’t place them exactly, but they should be within that time frame.”

“Can you tell who exactly had these emotions? Are they cultists?” A Calodan woman asked, and when Aldric met her gaze, he shivered at the intensity it held.

It was Seretta Sorrows.

Aldric responded carefully. “I sense a lot of zeal and fanatic devotion. Whether that confirms they're cultists or just... people who believe in what they're doing here, I can't say for certain. But yes, the devotion is undeniable.”

A pause hung in the air as the weight of his words settled over them.

"Who else could it be?" Tyrus whispered in confusion, but in the silence of the ziggurat, his voice echoed for everyone to hear, and the generals exchanged tense glances.

“Keep a lookout,” General Lucas spoke after a moment of terse silence. “And if you sense anything else, report it.”

Aldric nodded, and after a bit of awkward shuffling, they proceeded deeper into the ziggurat.

Despite the grandness of the exterior, the interior of the ziggurat was surprisingly mundane—cracked stone walls, cobwebs, dust, ancient torch brackets filled with mismatched iron, and long corridors that smelled faintly of smoke and magic.

People called out their observations as they walked deeper, keeping their voices quiet just in case there was anything dangerous within the structure. But aside from the obvious sense of abandonment and age, the faint gathering of dusty footprints, or scars on the walls where weapons had obviously been sharpened, there wasn’t much to point out on the first level.

Nothing at all that might tell them more about the cultists.

That changed as soon as they arrived at the second level.

Here, the walls were far from mundane, and though they exuded the same sense of age, the scent of magic was much more potent and obvious in the intricate lattice of shimmering runes that surrounded them.

Just like on the exterior, the runes pulsed to a rhythm only they could hear—shimmering with a bright blue glow that banished every hint of shadow from the level.

It took Geneva a whole minute to decipher them, and when she did, she realized something even weirder.

They are spatial runes, but they are drawn in such a way that they do not seem to have an end at all." Her frown deepened, and she turned around to glance at the entire chamber. "It's like the rest of the circuit exists somewhere else."

"Only the bottom part of the runes is here," Pythia finished, eyes wide as she came to the same realization. "But that shouldn't be possible. How were they activated without a closed loop?"

“They were not?” Geneva shook her head in amazement, turning to glance at the rest of them. “They weren’t activated. They simply exist as part of the structure itself, not drawn on top of it."

“What?!” Everyone frowned in confusion, and Geneva hurried to explain. Only, it wasn’t an explanation at all.

“I don’t know how, but these spatial runes don’t seem drawn. They are too intricate. Who could draw them? But more than that, they are just like those pins Pythia found, with runes on the inside and out. These runes could be a continuation of those on the exterior of the ziggurat. How could anyone possibly keep track?”

A heavy silence descended on the group after she spoke, confusion plain on the faces of everyone. And after a moment, Ambassador Hakim cursed. “By Tihamun. Where did the cultists get this technology? What do they need so many spatial runes for, anyway?”

“It doesn’t matter,” General Lucas responded before anyone could, a grimace etched deep in his features. “We need to keep moving.” He glanced at Geneva. “If you find anything that might lead us to the cultists within the runes, then I’d be happy to listen. But until then, we are on a time crunch.”

Geneva nodded, and they began moving once again. Silence stretched between them like a wet blanket—heavy and uncomfortable—until Artemis broke it with an observation.

"The fabric of space is weird in here." He stated with a frown. "I mean, it wasn't smooth before, but with every step we take, it only gets more convoluted—as if the fabric of space is shrinking."

"Or inverting," Warp frowned. "I cannot make any sense of it, but it feels deliberate. Intentional, even."

General Fang's steps faltered, and he turned to glare at her. General Lucas noticed the look and stepped forward, his gaze intense as he probed. "Inverting how?"

“It’s…I—” Warp hesitated, her gaze darting between both generals. And then her shoulders firmed in determination. “It’s as if there’s an overlap in reality—an opposite parallel, if you will. We're descending, yes, but it almost feels like we're ascending instead.”

The implications of her words settled over the group like a suffocating weight, and Aldric felt his chest tighten. He couldn’t sense what she had just described. In fact, it seemed that only she and Artemis could sense it, and that uncertainty only served to heighten the tension within the ziggurat.

Aldric felt the river of emotions flowing around him surge with uncertainty and dread, and he grimaced.

Two things were clear. Whatever this ziggurat was, it wasn’t an ordinary structure—and the cultists were no ordinary people. How could riffraff and weaklings possess technology such as this? First the pins that could deceive the senses of calamities, and now this structure none of them could make sense of?

The cultists were dangerous, and underestimating them was suicide.

The generals and ambassadors seemed to come to the same realization, and with a low grunt, General Lucas spoke. “Let us proceed.”

A terse silence stretched between them as they walked deeper into the ziggurat, the shuffle of feet resounding like thunder in their ears. Uncertainty slowly morphed into dread as minutes crawled by, and before long, they stood in front of the entrance to the third level.

General Lucas let out a heavy breath and glanced at Nayra. “Before we proceed, can you still sense the anchor?”

Nayra frowned, silence stretching for a few seconds before she responded. “Yes and no, sir. I can feel a resonance in the air. The presence of the anchor is stronger, but it still feels like we are far away from their actual location.”

“Then we need to keep moving,” General Lucas ordered as he marched down the stairs to the third level, portraying a sense of confidence Aldric seriously doubted he felt.

The other ambassadors went in after him, and everyone soon followed.

Fortunately, the moment they arrived within the third level, a surge of curiosity rose to dilute the rising dread.

The runes here were even denser than the last, shimmering with such intensity that it was nearly blinding. Voices rang out all around Aldric as several people began pointing out their observations at once. Even Tyrus said something, but the words barely reached Aldric, whose senses were tingling and straining under the abundance of emotion within the level.

Unconsciously, almost as if he were in a trance, he crouched down, placed a palm against the rough stone floor, and activated Unseen Bonds.

He regretted it almost immediately.

The world fell away, and a cascade of images flooded his mind—fragmented, disjointed, flashing across his mind almost too fast for him to grasp.

He only managed to grasp a few.

A gold-cloaked figure speaking to a huge soldier dressed in azure armor—radiating waves of guilt and determination so potent Aldric could almost taste it.

Boots thundering against stone as hundreds of armored soldiers marched through the corridor, their azure armor gleaming under the light of runes. The air thrummed with purpose, with resolve, with something that felt dangerously close to righteous indignation.

And then the unmistakable black and red robes of cultists, each one radiating zeal and devotion so absolute it bordered on insanity.

The images slammed into his mind like a war hammer, and Aldric staggered, groaning in pain as the skill cut off violently. His knees buckled, and his vision blurred at the edges, but the three images he’d managed to grasp seemed to have been burned into his mind—the emotions attached to them so potent that it was all he could do to prevent them from influencing his own emotions.

Tyrus caught him before he hit the floor, eyes wide. "This is the second time you're staggering. Are you sure you're alright?"

Aldric shook his head. "No, no. I'm not. I… I just saw some things."

"What did you see?" General Lucas asked immediately, pushing through the Calodan and Unorian entourage to stand before him. General Fang and the other ambassadors weren't far behind, each person staring at him urgently.

"Tell us, Empath." Ambassador Hakim urged.

But Aldric hesitated.

He opened his mouth to speak, glanced at the Sunstonian entourage, and closed it again. He wasn't certain of the meaning of the images he had seen, but he suspected—and if his suspicions were true, then he would be damning the Sunstonians and reopening old wounds.

General Lucas noticed his subtle look at the Sunstonian entourage, and his voice hardened. “Aldric Brystion, tell us what you saw. Every single thing. No harm can come to you for as long as I am here.”

General Fang’s gaze narrowed at that, but he said nothing. The Sunstonians tensed behind him. Everyone was watching him, expectant, hungry for answers.

Aldric knew what they wanted to hear, and he knew what those images meant. But despite the severity of his discoveries, he knew this discovery would only derail them further, and every minute wasted was a minute his brothers remained in danger.

They'd been descending into this ziggurat for what felt like hours, uncovering mysteries at every turn. Each discovery had led to more questions and more delays, and this one would be no different.

It may even be worse, and Aldric wanted to avoid any delay as much as he could.

“It’s nothing,” Aldric replied dismissively. “It’s not important to the mission. It’ll only cause a ruckus.”

“Let us be the judge of that.” Ambassador Morozov almost snapped. “Now speak.”

Aldric closed his eyes and sighed, the images flashing across his mind once again. “I saw a cascade of images when I touched the floor. I couldn’t grasp them all, but I grasped three of them.”

“The first image was that of a gold-robed man…” Aldric stated the three images he had seen and the emotions attached to them. The more he spoke, the more insight he got into the age of the images and what they were.

When he finished speaking, the entire corridor was dead silent. But the emotions were anything but.

Suspicion stood at the peak, surging around him like a raging river, and beneath it was fear. Anger came next as people pieced the images together. Vindication. And then grim satisfaction that quickly twisted into a storm of rage and hatred.

“Well, there we have it,” Ambassador Hakim snarled. “The Sunstonian betrayal carved right into stone.” He turned to glare at General Fang. “A man dressed in a gold robe. Tell me that isn’t the fucking Sunstonian king skulking through the shadows to make back-end deals with our enemies.”

“You have no proof of that,” General Fang replied with a forced calm, frustration etched deep in his features.

“There is no need for proof.” Ambassador Morozov replied coldly. “We’ve always known it was Sunstone who betrayed us to the Attilans. And now we have proof.”

“In the images a Ragnarian saw?” General Fang practically spat. "How do we know he didn’t just make this up?”

“Let’s not jump to conclusions just yet,” General Lucas spoke, his voice cutting through the chaos easily. He glanced at Aldric and asked. “The soldier you saw. You said he was dressed in azure armor.”

Aldric nodded.

“Can you describe him?”

Aldric paused, the image flashing across his mind once more. “He was huge, seemingly more giant than man. His skin seemed to be made of rocks, but they didn’t look like bloodline manifestations.”

General Fang cursed, tensing further, but General Lucas continued pressing. “What else did you see, Aldric?”

Aldric swallowed, not out of fear, but from the density of emotions pressing against him from all sides. Fear had risen to the peak, and despite having resorted to violence, Aldric could sense that everyone was ready for it. Some, like Tyrus, are even itching for it.

He pushed against the surging emotions and forced the words out. “The giant’s armor had a symbol at its center.” He stalled for a moment. Two.

“What symbol, Aldric?” General Lucas growled.

Aldric locked gazes with him, knowing the gravity of the words he was about to utter. He took in a deep breath and then whispered. “A crimson spire.”

Chaos broke out immediately, and cries of outrage filled the air.

“Deny that, you Sunstonian pigs,” Tyrus shouted beside him, his voice nearly lost in the roar of chaos as others hurled insults at the Sunstonians. The Sunstonians hurled their right back, except for General Fang, whose form was so tense, it seemed as if he was about to burst open on the spot.

Aldric sighed. The few weeks he had spent with the champions had taught him many things. More importantly, he had learned of their real enemy: Attilan.

He hadn’t seen a map of Attilan yet, nor had he seen their symbol, but the moment he’d grasped the image, he’d known instinctively who it belonged to.

The chaos continued for the next few minutes, until finally, a voice rose above the rest. It was Luminus.

The Sunstonian champion glared daggers at him and snarled. “How do we know you haven’t just coined this dream up? The logic of it is lacking. You said you saw Attilan soldiers marching through this corridor. How would they have gotten in? The whole of Lutia was locked down.”

Luminus’s question rang through the corridor like an explosion, demanding answers Aldric didn’t have. But the ziggurat apparently did, because in that instant, a groan of ancient hinges being turned resounded through the corridor.

It was the entrance to the fourth level, opening into the third.

Energy surged in an instant as offensive skills were activated, all aimed at the entrance. Nearly three dozen different affinities, creating a maelstrom of different energies in the air above them.

The door opened slowly, as if it were far heavier than it looked. Tension spiked. Boots thumped against metal in a chorus, hinting that whoever or whatever was coming wasn’t alone.

And then the door opened fully. A woman appeared, lilac irises gleaming with ethereal light—her Unorian features so delicate and perfect they seemed to have been carved rather than born.

An ouroboros stood at the center of her forehead, and the moment her gaze landed on the alliance, her eyes widened in shock.

For a split second, her entire body went rigid as she took in the assembled alliance—the weapons, the tension, and the sheer number of powerful awakened standing before her. She took an instinctive step backward, blocking the entrance with her body.

“What’s the hold up, Cersi?” A powerful voice came from behind her, and another man appeared, the ouroboros on his head gleaming as he looked questioningly at the woman that was no doubt his sister.

Then he saw them and froze, shock apparent in his features.

Aldric wasn’t sure whether to be alarmed or confused because what were members of the Rune family doing here?

He wasn’t the only one confused, though. Everyone else was confused, all except Ambassador Morozov, who seemed to have been struck dumb by shock.

“What… how is this possible?” He stuttered, eyes wide as his gaze darted between the alliance and the rune family. “Didn’t… Aren’t you supposed to be in Attilan?”

“We are in Attilan.” The man spoke, confusion clear in his voice. “We just entered a few hours ago. Isn’t the alliance supposed to be in Sunstone?”

“We are in Sunstone,” Ambassador Morozov replied, his expression growing even more confused. “How is this possible?”

General Lucas glanced between Ambassador Morozov and the Rune family. “What in Raol’s name is going on here? Can someone just explain to me, please?”
