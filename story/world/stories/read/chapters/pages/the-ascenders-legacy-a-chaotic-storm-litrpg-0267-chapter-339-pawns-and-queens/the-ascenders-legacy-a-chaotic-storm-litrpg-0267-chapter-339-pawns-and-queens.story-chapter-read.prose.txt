Aodhán groaned softly, helplessly, as the sounds of war pervaded his mind—chaos and screams ringing in his ears.

Blood painted the stormy sky in crimson arcs. Thunder mammoths roared incessantly from within rolling clouds. And from the earth below, geysers of superheated energy erupted, spilling lava and ash into the air. The coppery scent of blood hung heavy over the battlefield, nauseating as it mixed with the pungent scent of ozone and viscera.

And all around him, a brutal war raged, the screams of hundreds of dying disciples rising in a cacophony of fear and desperation.

Aodhán's heart thudded loudly in his chest. His ears rang, and from nowhere, a bloody hand grabbed him. He attacked on instinct, a blistering beam of black lightning blasting out to rip the arm off.

A scream erupted. He glanced down at the face of his victim and froze.

Mirelle.

Before he could freak out, another arm grabbed him, and despite what had just happened, Aodhán found himself attacking again.

The arm was sliced off. Blood spurted. Another scream erupted.

Heart thundering, he glanced at his victim and immediately stumbled backward, horrified.

Crixis.

"No, no." He shook his head, staggering back even further.

Another arm grabbed him.

"No!"

But his body moved anyway, several blades of black lightning manifesting around him to slice the person to pieces. Another scream rang out, but Aodhán didn't bother glancing at the face of his victim this time.

Instead, he ran, stepping over dead bodies and severed limbs in his haste.

Another arm reached out to grab him, but he teleported before it could. Dying screams and battle cries filled his ears as he ran, dodging grasping arms like they carried the plague.

His feet slapped against dead bodies, then against wood, and then abruptly, without any warning, he was suddenly running across the deck of a massive ship.

A massive burning ship.

The scent of blood never left the air, only this time, it wasn't tinged with the putrid stench of viscera but the acrid scent of burning flesh and ozone.

A scent he recognized all too well.

He looked up immediately, fear lancing through him as his gaze landed on the Origin breach above him.

A storm Origin breach.

"Brace yourself!" A distant voice cried out. "Another bolt is coming."

But Aodhán didn't need to be told that. He knew just how powerful the coming bolt would be. He knew what it would do to the ship if it struck. He knew the lives it would claim. But beyond that, he knew the damage it would wreak even if he acted.

Because once upon a time, he had acted. And he had regretted it.

But if he didn't act…

"Aodhán!" another voice called out, familiar and warm, and Aodhán just about melted at the sound of it. He turned immediately, a sad smile on his face as he glanced at Daruk.

"I'm in a dream," he whispered softly even as the screams of the injured and dying rose around him. "I know it's a dream."

Daruk smiled. "Then you know not to panic. You've lived variations of this nightmare before."

"I have," Aodhán nodded. "And every time I relive it, no matter what I do differently, the outcomes remain the same. People die."

Daruk scoffed. "People will die whether you act or not. Fewer people will die if you act. I think the better option is obvious." He smiled then and looked up at the breach. "It's just a storm, Aodhán. You can control it. It'll listen to you. You just have to speak to it."

Aodhán shook his head. "It's never listened before."

"No, it never has," Daruk smiled. "But then you had no authority. Now you do."

Aodhán felt the words resonate deeply within him, and with a deep exhale, he glanced up at the breach. Lightning had pooled densely on its surface. It would strike any moment now.

This was the moment he usually ran to the hull of the ship, trying to absorb as much of the bolt as he could. But this time, Aodhán decided to do something different.

Face hardening in determination, Aodhán raised a hand to the storm above and spoke. "RETREAT."

Thunder boomed so loudly it seemed to split the sky in two. Reality flickered, the fabric of space congealed, and then like a bad movie, the roiling clouds began retreating into the Origin Plane.

"Aodhán!"

The howling winds subsided, lightning dispersed, and the ship stilled. But just as the breach began closing, the scene changed, and Aodhán found himself back in the war, with hundreds of enemy Calamities staring down at him in contempt.

"Aodhán!"

Their lips pulled into snarls as they attacked him as one, a beam of utter destruction descending to obliterate him. The beam burned through everything in its path, even the fabric of reality itself, growing larger and larger as it descended, until it engulfed him and—

"Aargh!" Aodhán jerked awake just as the beam engulfed him, his heart thundering in his chest. His entire body was soaked with sweat.

"By the storm!" he cursed as his body shuddered, still feeling a lump of fear in his throat. It had been a while since he'd had the Raventhorn dream, or any other dream for that matter, and it wasn't too surprising that the looming war had triggered one.

But despite the terror and confusion that had pervaded this dream, Aodhán was certain it was no ordinary nightmare. The war part of it had been a little disjointed, but the Origin breach part had to mean something.

He wasn't sure what, but he had a few ideas about what it might—

"Aodhán!" a voice called out from outside; their tone frustrated. "Are you in there, or am I just wasting my time?"

Aodhán jumped to his feet immediately, realizing now that the voice had been calling him for a while. He rushed to the window, pushed it open, and breathed a sigh of relief when he found Mirelle glaring up at him, alive and whole.

"Uh, hi Mirelle." He smiled at her. "It's good to see you."

Mirelle scowled. "I've been calling you for the past three minutes. I even pinged your ring. What kind of sleep were you sleeping?"

"An uneasy one. I'm sorry." Aodhán sighed. "What's going on? How are the others doing?"

"They are doing fine." Mirelle ran a hand through her hair and smiled. "Su Tianan and Kairn have even overcome the twisted effect and are currently resting. But that's not why I'm here. I was asked to summon you."

"Summon?" Aodhán frowned. "Who needs me?"

"Elder Ghaltris. Come on, we're running late already."

"Late for what?" Aodhán asked even as he pulled out his robe from his spatial storage and began putting it on. "It's barely eight in the morning. Where are we going?"

"We are going to the command tent to talk strategy with the war elders and the leaders of the allied forces. Can you be ready in two minutes?"

Aodhán slicked his hair back, cast Ruinous Baptism on himself, and teleported to stand beside her. "I'm ready now. Let's go."

Mirelle wrinkled her nose at him. "You stink."

"Yeah, well, I've not had the chance to wash myself off."

"I gave you two minutes."

"I'll bathe later," Aodhán argued, creating a platform construct for the both of them. "I'm not keeping the elders of the Storm Sect waiting for a bath."

Mirelle chuckled, and a few minutes later, the world became a blur of colors as the platform streaked toward the command center.

It didn't take them long to reach the heavily protected building, and the instant they arrived, Aodhán expanded core sense, instinctively taking note of the number and power of cores within.

The command center was the strategic base of the Storm Sect, protected heavily by an army of Mythic soldiers, just in case the enemies somehow made it into the sect. But while their numbers and power were impressive, Aodhán was more concerned with those inside, and as his senses scanned their cores, he felt his heart stutter in his chest.

There were eleven people within the building, nine of whom were Mythics of significant strength. But the Mythics weren't the ones who seized his attention.

It was the two advanced cores blazing within. Or should he say one of the two?

Dravion Ashscar's core blazed with an intensity that was more than enviable, a burning star among suns. But it was the second advanced core that truly caused Aodhán's heart to stutter.

The core blazed with an intensity that rivaled that of Mythics, so pure and bright it almost seemed to even overshadow a few. And it was only at the 85th tier of advancement.

Aodhán had no doubt whom this was.

He'd heard so much about her in the past few days, but when she hadn't shown up with Ashscar, he'd wondered if she would return for the war at all.

But she was here now, and it seemed the stories he'd heard about her hadn't been overrated in the least.

Nymera Vaelthorn. The strongest Special Disciple the sect had seen in centuries was in that room. And he was about to meet her.

Nervous didn't even begin to cover it.

Subconsciously, he straightened, shifted his robes, and raised his head high, a subtle aura of authority radiating from him.

Mirelle glanced up at him with a frown, sensing a difference yet unable to place it. Her gaze narrowed in confusion as they passed by the guards, raising their rings to identify themselves.

"Are you suddenly taller?" she asked as the guard cleared them for entrance.

"No." Aodhán shook his head. "I'm just nervous."

She smiled. "It's just a strategy meeting to decide what front of the war we'll probably be in. It's nothing to be nervous about."

"Right." Aodhán nodded absently. "There's nothing to be nervous about."

Mirelle pushed the door open, and Aodhán stepped in after her into the massive hall, his gaze homing in on Nymera like a magnet…

Only to find her already staring at him, abyssal black irises piercing straight through him like arrows.

Their gazes locked, and Aodhán felt his heart stutter once more.

Nymera's power was famed. Everyone in the sect knew about it, but somehow, her beauty was something Aodhán had never heard anyone mention.

And by the Maker, did it bear mentioning.

But beyond even that was the mind-bending fact that they had practically the same bloodline manifestation.

Her skin was just as dark as his, and her hair was a white so pure it made his own seem dirty in comparison. Lightning arced around it in a myriad of colors, and on her arms and face, the Lichtenberg scars were visible, only much more severe.

She was beautiful—not in a gentle or refined way, but in an otherworldly way that seemed to suck the air out of his lungs until Mirelle jabbed him in the stomach.

That was when he realized he was staring. Hard. And standing in a room full of elders.

Eyes widening, he bowed immediately. "Good morning, elders. Apologies."

The elders chuckled, and with a smile Elder Ghaltris said, "Good morning to you too, Aodhán. I take it you know whom you spent the last several seconds staring at?"

Aodhán glanced back at Nymera and nodded. "Nymera Vaelthorn."

Nymera smiled. "Nice to meet you too, Aodhán Brystion. It's nice to have another special in the sect."

Aodhán smiled, but before he could respond, Elder Ghaltris spoke. "Try not to let her distract you just yet, Aodhán. Nymera will still be formidable after the meeting—but the war won't wait for introductions. Now that everyone is present, I say let's begin."

Everyone gathered, and Elder Ghaltris continued. "I need to explain a few things about the war and the things we are to expect. I just rounded up a meeting with the leaders of our allies, and I must say they are very dedicated to helping us. That being said, nearly all of them are Mythics, with only a few advanced cultivators scattered among them."

He glanced at Aodhán. "This war will be brutal for advanced cultivators. Special or not, you will be the grunts of this war, and considering the actual reason for the war, I am obliged to present you all with a few options."

"What options?" Ashscar asked, and Elder Ghaltris nodded.

"The reason for this war is to cut down the number of transmigrants in the Storm Sect. To restore balance for our enemies, which means the moment you step foot on that battlefield, you become targets, not of peers, but of Mythics and Calamities." He glanced back at Aodhán, his tone urging. "The first option is that you all sit the war out."

"Not happening." Aodhán responded immediately, shaking his head. "I'm sorry, Elder Ghaltris, but that is not an option I can take."

"Me neither," Nymera spoke, glancing at Aodhán from the corner of her eye. "I didn't cut my adventure short to sit the war out. I want to fight."

Ashscar nodded. "I'm with them. This war is about transmigrants. We would be cowards if we sat back and did nothing."

"No." An elder shook his head. "You will be fools if you walk onto that battlefield. You will die in seconds. Minutes if fate is being generous."

Aodhán opened his mouth to speak, then closed it, logic warring viciously with resolve. The elder was right. A battle against Mythics and Calamities wasn't one he could endure, let alone win. But he couldn't afford to sit back either.

His presence in the sect had sparked this war. His ideas, his pendents, all of it had sparked the Origin Plane's expansion. How could he stand aside and watch other people die without being able to do something?

No, there had to be another way.

He glanced up at Elder Ghaltris, eyes hard. "What's the other option?"

The elders glanced at each other, then Elder Ghaltris spoke, his tone cautious. "The second option is that you do not fight this war."

"Elder Ghaltris." Ashscar began to protest, but Elder Ghaltris simply continued.

"You will not stand on the front lines. You will not be assigned to battalions. You will not be deployed alongside armies. Instead, you will act as… baits."

"Baits?" Aodhán whispered, exchanging glances with Nymera and Ashscar.

Elder Ghaltris nodded. "Halfway into the battle, you three will be deployed onto the battlefield. Your aim is to draw attention. Do something massive. And then get the hell out of there before you're blown to pieces."

"In and out," Nymera muttered, and Elder Ghaltris nodded.

"Our enemies want you dead. If any one of you dies, the expansion of the Origin Plane will most likely come to a halt." He glanced at Aodhán, his tone somber. "If you die, the Origin Plane will definitely come to a halt. Very possibly, it might even begin shrinking."

Nymera, Ashscar, and Mirelle glanced at Aodhán immediately, realizing the gravity of Elder Ghaltris's words. Aodhán himself felt the weight of their gazes like a tangible thing, and when he glanced back at Nymera, her gaze narrowed imperceptibly.

The moment lasted only a second before Elder Ghaltris continued. "The edge we have is that our enemies do not know who the fourth transmigrant is. This is why you," he glanced at Mirelle, "will be joining them in Sophran's place until he regains himself."

Mirelle swallowed nervously, feeling utterly out of place. She might be special, but she was no transmigrant.

Elder Ghaltris glanced at them all. "This option puts you all in danger. The moment you step onto that battlefield, our enemies will attack. They will not hesitate. They will not think. And if you do not get out in time, you will die."

The silence in the room grew even more oppressive, suffocating in its weight as the four specials processed Elder Ghaltris's words.

Then Ashscar spoke. "How will the bait work? What benefit is it to us?"

Elder Ghaltris glanced at one of the war elders, and the man spoke. "Your appearance on the battlefield will be strategic, all the way to the location you appear in. Several war artillery and Calamities will be notified of that location. Everyone who attacks that location will be as good as dead. If our Calamities don't kill them, our machines will."

Aodhán raised a hand to his temples and sighed. "So if we don't get out of the way, we might be killed by our own people?"

Elder Ghaltris exhaled. "Using you guys as bait wasn't our first option, but we knew none of you would agree to sit out the war. This is the only other option we could come up with that doesn't lead to your instant death. It is risky, and we could potentially lose all of you, but—"

"But the sect doesn't coddle or insulate its disciples, regardless of how much our actions might affect them," Nymera interjected, her gaze holding a meaning Aodhán couldn't decipher. "We could be riding our deaths here, and the sect will do nothing aside from laying out the risks."

"Yes." Elder Ghaltris nodded. "We may be one body, but in a time like this, the decision of every person must be theirs. All we can tell you is that it's a bad idea."

Aodhán shook his head. "We are not standing aside."

"Then this is your only other option." Elder Ghaltris glanced at them all. "What do you say?"

Aodhán glanced at Nymera, then Mirelle, then Ashscar, his mind racing with the weight of this decision. He wasn't a fool, nor was he a battle junkie. He understood the risks of joining this battle. Yet he knew with everything within him that standing aside wasn't an option.

He couldn't do it.

The Storm Sect had taken him when he'd had nowhere else to run. They had saved him from certain death. And he couldn't stand aside now and watch them die for him.

He glanced at the other specials, his gaze darting from one person to the other, communicating, arguing, stating silent facts, and then, as one, they all came to a decision.

"We'll do it," Aodhán spoke for them all. "We'll act as baits."

Elder Ghaltris nodded somberly. "Then let's get you all suited up for battle. May stormlight keep you all."

Aodhán glanced at the other specials and shuddered. There were only three hours left before the war began.

And they had just agreed to serve as pawns in a battle of queens and rooks.

May stormlight truly keep them.
