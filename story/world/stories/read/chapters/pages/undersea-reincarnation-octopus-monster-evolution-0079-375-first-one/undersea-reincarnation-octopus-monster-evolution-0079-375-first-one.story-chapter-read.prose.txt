As soon as the floating Octomind had exited the elevator shaft, Henry knew something had gone terribly wrong, and it wasn’t long until the massive prison door came into view but by then, the picture was clear.

Things were much worse that he believed they could be.

But between the minimal amount of attention he could provide this part of his thought process while managing the literal mayhem of bombardment above water, really parsing what his Octomind was seeing required some effort he could barely spare.

That might have been a mistake on his part.

Mana was leaking out of the broken circuitry that lined the walls of the facility, with the cracked, carved symbols flickering off and on. The final metallic door was caved in outwardly, as if an explosion had gone off within, and most noticeably of course, was the silence that pressed on his ears, and the inky fog that blanketed the tunnel.

Naturally, neither of those stopped the dancing shapes and blabbering whispers that telepathically rang in his mind.

As soon as those registered, Henry couldn’t help but spare some more of his attention to what he was seeing down below. Because in all the time he’d spent with the corpse, the intensity of its aura of madness had never been this heavy. This was orders of magnitude beyond that, and he could feel his Octomind losing some of its physical and structural balance as it floated past the door, even with Psychic Resistance fully active, forcing him to expend some more essence to it so it would survive and see what was happening.

Then the darkness parted, and if Henry had knees, this would be where they would buckle.

“[Maurice, we’re pulling back a little,]” he managed once his blood and thoughts finally thawed, and Henry, without waiting for Maurice’s response, teleported them both a short distance away, taking the spot of a furthest clone from the puppeteer before continuing the bombardment.

He needed to pay attention below. Because what he was seeing did not make sense, but he knew it was much, much more serious than the A-rank he was dealing with, and so he tried to explain that to the sputtering Maurice, who immediately got deathly quiet when he heard Henry.

“[It’s… like the air itself has been torn, and the tear is somehow both small enough to fit in the room and much larger at the same time. Velistraine and Attal look much smaller… and that thing—]”

Henry considered casting an image of it. An illusion to show to Maurice. But he didn’t dare to. What if it somehow helped it establish a foothold?

It was like some sort of giant, hunched humanoid insect was trying to squeeze through. Large, star-filled eyes contrasted with a pale, smooth purple carapace. The thing waved a feeler, and a wave of force sent Velistraine and Attal skidding back. Henry watched, mouth dry, and he realized that both A-ranks were bleeding from both their eyes and ears. Even Velistraine was grim looking, eyeing the monster looming over them. Unlike the creature that had been encased in ice, this one was all sleek and much larger. Two pink and dainty—but still terribly sharp-looking—scythe-hands kept the tear in the world open, while the face peered down at the magic and sword wielding A-ranks, flinching mildly from the projectile Attal shot at it as he struggled to get back to his feet.

Velistraine was faster to get on her feet, and she was already running, her sword glowing with red light. At the same time, a familiar golden threads tightened the edge of the rift, working on closing it up, but every few seconds, the creature would reach up with it mandible and bite the thread off.

They were at a stalemate, but Henry didn’t like how… pristine the invader seemed, while the two powerful A-ranks looked to be on their last legs.

This was dire.

Hey there, Henry. Not a good time, I’m afraid.

The prompt was surprising, but Henry had already recognized the golden stitches. He’d felt the System’s magic before. Though before he could say much, the creature’s attention fell on his Octomind, and his blood froze again at the amused, almost gleeful barrage of chittering that reached him, each inflection of its voice like a shiv through his brain. Henry’s connection to the Octomind shook, but he did everything he could to keep an eye on things as he pulled further back from the parasite of the surface which, unfortunately, didn’t seem willing to let their continued offense go unpunished.

The familiarity of the creature’s attention was not lost on him, and a terrible truth dawned on him, one he couldn’t refute with how many Octominds were helping him process things.

The experiments on the previously ice-encased creature had not gone unnoticed. And just how they had observed it, they—or maybe just him—in turn had been observed. He didn’t know how this thing had managed to open the tear into the world, but him and the two A-ranks mucking about with its kinds had brought it to them.

Henry watched as Velistraine leaped and slashed, her figure much smaller in the weirdly distorted proximity of the tear, but the glowing red slash her sword drew on the insectile behemoth’s joint, just above the scythe, still got a pained chitter out of the monster before it retracted its arm a little, earning them a few extra golden sutures.

She’s barely the size of its eye, Henry realized as he watched the woman fall back. Nimbly, she landed on the shattered stone floor, giving Attal a precious moment to prepare for another attack. She threw Henry a quick glance, and her grimness cracked for a moment as she gave him a daring smile.

“Henry! Any chance you have Zer in one of your pockets? We could use the help!”

Horror clawed up his throat. Henry was at a loss for words. He wanted to tell her that yes, he could get the ape. But he’d just cut off his link to hold off the parasite. Scrambling, Henry’s mind scrambled for how he could revert his decision and extend the network back, but it would take hours. Maybe days.

He couldn’t lie to her.

“There’s an issue with the network,” he managed. “But I’ll do what I can,” he promised, as he put all of his Octominds on the task. Maybe he could brute-force a connection to one of the couple of clones still there?

Establish the link, find Zerathstra, collect the mana, bring him, he recited while Maurice helped him in fending off a massive, starfish limb from catching them from below, fully aware of how impossible that was. It was hundreds of miles away.

He’d just pulled all of his clones back.

To his frustration, Velistraine seemed to be aware of that.

“Ah don’t bother. I don’t think we’ve got enough juice left to fend this thing off until he brings his hairy ass here. It’s not tiring as fast as we are and even with Zer…. This thing might still manage to latch on. And we can’t have that. Plus, I really do want to be the first one out there.”

Henry didn’t like where this conversation was going, and looking up at the creature as it worked on gnawing more of the System’s suture off, his thoughts whirled. Should they try evacuating?

No, there’s whole island above… and we don’t know what this creature could do with the rift open. This could very well end up killing the entire world, he thought, considering the whole swarm aspect of whatever this was. They had to shove it back and let the System close the door. But how?

I’m still too weak to help, he lamented, fury and bile rising in his chest. He could barely fend off a mid A-rank parasite, and the two powerful A-rank he’d been looking to surpass were struggling against this creature just as much as he had been against the parasite.

“Ah dammit,” muttered Velistraine, shaking her hand. Then letting out a big sigh, she grinned to Henry. “Henry, can you ferry my crew back to Zer if things turn bad? Tell him I tried, okay? Here,” she added, tearing her satchel away from her belt before throwing it at his Octomind’s arms. “My orb’s in there. If I’m not back in a minute or two, just make sure to work hard, alright?”

What?

“Attal, support!” she shouted, then she exploded forward, her feet cracking the stone below her feet while Attal’s purple skill joined the red aura that surrounded Velistraine as she shot up at the creature’s face like a cannon-ball, whooping as she did.

Velistraine drove her glowing bone rapier into the creature’s compound eye, and burst it like an overripe watermelon.

The screech that came out of the monster made Henry’s vision go dark for a moment, but he didn’t let that distract him. Henry threw the Octomind after her, making it create as many clones as it could. There was a flash of gold as the System smacked the remaining scythe back, and further away, Henry watched as Velistraine dodged the second scythe before she severed the feeler reaching for her.

Then she turned her attention to the second eye while behind her, the wound of the world quickly narrowed.

“Try to survive,” he ordered both his Octomind and his clones. “Help her as much as you can. Store her if possible. Obey every order from her. Try. Please,” he begged. Henry wasn’t sure his Octomind would survive the separation, but Trickster’s Shadows were supposed to never lose their connection to him, no matter the distance. They would be out of range, and he wouldn’t be able to communicate with them, but they should survive.

Without the monster actively working on keeping the tear open, the System was quick to sew the rift closed, and another golden ripple shoved the monster one more time as it screeched in frustration, only half a second before Henry’s manifested Octomind and twelve Shadows managed to slip through after Velistraine, and as the final suture was tied, his consciousness of his thirteen clone was severed, while the Octomind’s consciousness was shunted back into his own mind, leaving only the two clones that hadn’t made in the dark, claustrophobic basement as the ceiling cracked and as dust and stone began raining down.
