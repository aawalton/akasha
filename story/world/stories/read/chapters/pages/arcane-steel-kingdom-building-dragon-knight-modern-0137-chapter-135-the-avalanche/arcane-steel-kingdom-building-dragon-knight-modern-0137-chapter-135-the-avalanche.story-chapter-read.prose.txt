Things were not looking up for Cinderfall.

The situation on the ground deteriorated with the slow, grinding inevitability of a glacier ripping down a mountainside. What Fedric did had huge ramifications for the Hegemony. His solitary walk across no-man’s land was an anomaly that broke the conditioning of the drafted masses.

The Cinderfall front lines had watched him in utter bafflement. The officers assumed he was a dead man, a suicide by automated turret. They waited for the red tracer fire to cut him in half. It never came. He just kept walking, arms raised, stepping over hidden explosives that had vaporized whole squads hours earlier. It looked to the starving conscripts as if the man knew a secret, safe route through the carnage.

The next morning, the fog over the mountains was thick. Three more people attempted what Fedric did.

They were desperate men, soldiers who had lost all hope of ever surviving this grinding meat grinder of a war. They crawled through the ash on their bellies, dragging themselves through the freezing mud, expecting the ground to detonate beneath them at any second.

They made it to the other side. The Aegis Legionaries pulled them into the clean, steel-lined trenches, wrapped them in thermal blankets, and handed them hot food.

The Cinderfall captains considered it a fluke. A malfunction in the enemy's magical traps.

Then, three became twenty. Twenty became thirty.

By the third day, the trickle became a flood. The numbers swelled into the hundreds, then thousands at a time. Cinderfall lords were outright panicking. It wasn't just limited to the cover of darkness anymore. Entire groups of conscripts would drop their rusted iron shields in broad daylight, raise their empty hands, and sprint toward the enemy lines completely unharmed.

This mass defection forced the Cinderfall officer corps to realize a terrifying truth about the battlefield. The traps under the ground weren't proximity-based magical wards. They were being detonated selectively. The rapid-fire turrets were also employing selective targeting. The enemy was actively evaluating every single person on the field and choosing who lived and who died.

The Hegemony was literally bleeding troops to my side of the valley, bolstering my labor numbers while their own shield walls grew dangerously thin.

Cinderfall took drastic measures. The King issued a standing order. Any man stepping out of formation toward the enemy lines was to be shot in the back immediately.

Captains and loyalist mages drew their crossbows and readied their staves.

This brought a new, horrifying reality crashing down on the Hegemony command.

A Cinderfall lieutenant leveled his heavy crossbow at a fleeing farm boy. He put his finger on the trigger.

His head vanished.

A split second later, the sharp crack of a hyper-velocity rifle shot echoed across the valley. The headless lieutenant crumpled into the mud.

Before a Cinderfall officer could loose an arrow or cast a lethal spell to end the life of a fleeing conscript, they would drop dead. Their skulls shattered by long-range, incredibly fast-moving projectiles.

It meant the enemy possessed superior long-range sniping capabilities. It meant the enemy had the capacity to pick off their officers from miles away, at any time. They had always been in the crosshairs. The enemy just hadn't bothered to pull the trigger until now.

...

Nowhere was the tension higher than inside the command redoubt of Cinderfall.

The war council was a room full of heavily armed men scratching their heads, utterly devoid of a working strategy.

First of all, their army should have been twice the size by now. The mobilization decrees should have brought almost a hundred million soldiers to this southern beachhead to push me back into the ocean. But I was choking their supply lines and transit routes. The carpet bombing of the mountain passes and the sabotage of the bridges meant only a tiny, starving trickle of soldiers was actually reaching the main force in the valley.

Secondly, they were constantly losing troops to my side of the line through defection.

At this point, their initial strategy—triggering all of my explosives with penal troops before a larger force arrived to crush what they considered a puny army—was falling apart like a pipe dream.

Everyone was silent. The air in the stone building was thick with tension. No one had a solution. Their magical stone reserves couldn't protect them forever from the constant ship bombardment from the shore. It was a brutal stalemate, and they were the ones starving.

Lord Gravex stepped forward. The older man looked haggard, the dark circles under his eyes resembling bruises. He cleared his throat, the sound loud in the tense silence.

"My King," Gravex said, his voice hesitant. "I may have an idea."

King Theron moved his armored hand from his chin and beckoned him to speak.

Gravex let out a deep breath. "Although it pains me to say this... the enemy representative, Valen, gave me a warning during the parley. He told me we should dig similar structures to the enemy if we are to survive this ordeal."

He swallowed hard, bracing himself for the reaction. "Deep trenches. Like theirs. It would be ideal to protect the infantry from the long-range weapons and the naval artillery fire. We can have the earth mages fortify the mud walls with bedrock, my King. We need to dig our boots into the ground and hold position. We must wait for reinforcements to arrive."

Outrage flared instantly in the Cinderfall war room. Insults and sheer, spit-flying rage were directed at Gravex.

"Cowardice!" roared a heavily armored Duke from the eastern provinces, slamming his fist onto the weirwood table. "You suggest we scurry into holes like rats? We are the vanguard of the Hegemony! We ride, we conquer!"

"You would have us cede the open field to metal toys and rogue elves?" another general sneered, his hand resting on the hilt of his sword. "Perhaps you would like to hand them the keys to the capital while you're down there playing in the dirt, Gravex?"

"My lords, look at the reality!" Gravex pleaded, his voice rising over the clamor. "Our charges are broken before they cross ten yards! Our officers are picked off by invisible archers! If we do not secure our footing, there will be no vanguard left for the reinforcements to join!"

"Then we charge all at once!" the Duke bellowed back. "We overwhelm their traps with sheer numbers! The Mother Phoenix flies with us!"

I watched the live feed from my command chair in the CIC. I was actually surprised. In truth, Gravex had offered the only optimal solution left to them apart from a full retreat. Digging in would save their soldiers from more needless slaughter by my artillery.

Also, if Cinderfall gave up on the aggressive push, it gave me more time to save lives and process the defectors. It gave me more room to progress the Crawler builds on the shoreline. They weren't going fast enough for my liking. We had made quite a bit of progress, each chassis sitting at around thirty percent complete, but they were massive undertakings. I needed the time Gravex was offering.

King Theron raised a gauntleted hand, silencing the arguing lords. He looked at Gravex for a long moment, then nodded slowly.

The simple gesture shocked everyone assembled in the redoubt. It even shocked me.

Theron addressed the assembled lords, his deep voice cutting through the remaining whispers. "In truth, I have something to discuss with you all. I have been in contact with the Queen of the Elves."

He paused, letting the silence stretch tightly across the room.

"She has assured me they are on our side. The Verdant Conclave will be joining the war formally. They are mobilizing their troops as we speak."

Theron leaned back in his heavy chair, a dark, vindictive gleam in his amber eyes. "Let us see how this boy survives the combined might of two great nations."

I stood up, pushing my chair back so hard it screeched against the deck plates.

"Tes, pull up the tactical feed," I ordered, my pulse hammering against my ribs. "This can't be happening."

I brought up the world map on the central holotable.

My forces were currently located at the southernmost point of Aerthos. We had secured this beachhead, carved a glass highway through the coastal mountains, and pushed inland to this valley.

I stared at the borders.

To the east of me lay the rugged, impassable territories of the Dwarves of Khaz'Modan. To the far east of the mega continent, the sprawling, ancient forests of Sylvanheim.

An attack from the eastern border would force me to fight a war on two entirely different fronts. It would stretch my mechanized lines to the breaking point. It would force me to retreat toward the sea.

But for now, I saw nothing on the live feed from The Oracle. The eastern borders looked quiet. I had no idea what route they were taking.

The Shadow Order hadn't made it deep into Sylvanheim yet. We had concentrated our surveillance on the Primeval Forest to neutralise their World-Scythe array, but the Conclave's actual heartland was too heavily warded by ancient nature spirits for my Specters to establish a quick foothold. I didn't have active intelligence streaming directly from their capital. We had only managed to secure a few listening posts in the neutral duchies sitting between the elves and the dwarven mountains.

If the full might of the elven army mobilized and struck my flanks, I couldn't maintain this position. My forces were still too immature to face those kinds of numbers. I didn't have the heavy armor or the localized power grids finished yet.

It would force me to rely entirely on nuclear options.

I gripped the edge of the holotable, taking a slow breath. I needed to remain calm. I needed a counter plan. We had to stop them dead in their tracks before they reached the Aerthos border.

The most effective way seemed to be a preemptive nuclear strike in the lawless territories between the dwarven mountains and the elven lands. It was a rugged choke point. If I collapsed those passes with ICARUS warheads, I could bottle the elven army up inside their own forests for months.

I focused on the feed again. The Cinderfall officers were practically cheering. They all agreed that the elves had a responsibility in this war as an ally who helped start the initial conflict four years ago.

Just as I was playing out the detonation details in my head, calculating fallout drift and civilian casualties in the neutral zones, the heavy doors to the CIC hissed open.

Nyxia barged into the room.

She was out of breath, her midnight-blue gown swishing aggressively around her ankles. She held a crumpled piece of vellum in her fist.

"I have just received news from the formal representative I left at Dragon Valley," she said, marching straight to the holotable.

She slammed the vellum down on the glass.

"Sylvanheim has delivered a formal declaration of war. They are citing unlawful attacks on Sylvanheim lands and the slaughter of their sky armada. Furthermore..."

She looked up at me, her crimson eyes wide.

"They have formally stated their allegiance. They declared that the lands of Aerthos now rightfully belong to Cinderfall, and they are marching to assist the Hegemony in reclaiming them."

Well, I guess King Theron wasn't bluffing to boost morale.

I stared at the map, a bitter, sour taste flooding the back of my throat. I'm starting to think staying in the Obsidian Dominion, bullying the local demon lords and building the Crawlers in peace, was a way better option.

Right now, all we could do was wait. Wait and see what route the Sylvanheim army takes to reach us, and pray we have enough firepower left to hold the line.
