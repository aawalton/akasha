Chapter V.LXI (5.61) - Delving Preparations

Kizu’s hand shook as he stared at his next listed opponent. He’d only received the notice after his most recent victory against an unknown student. This was the bracket information for their first fight after the four-day intermission.

He had been equal parts excited for this upcoming fight and dreading it ever since first hearing about the tournament.

Harvey Hugo.

Part of Kizu had expected himself to face off against the Tainted boy in their final match. A grand duel between the two of them. But with monsters like Sene and Gob Lucas also at the academy, it made sense he’d face Harvey now. There was history between the two of them and the academy likely wanted to ensure the battle happened before either of them was knocked into the losers bracket.

Setting aside the notice, Kizu decided it would be best to focus on the immediate challenge in front of him. His fight with Harvey would take place after his delve into the World Dungeon. With Basil already in the losers bracket, it meant they’d need to leave tomorrow morning after the shapeshifter’s next fight.

That fight Basil had lost had been particularly brutal. Apparently, there was no shortage of personal grudges in regard to Basil and the girl in question held onto hers quite thoroughly. Basil could escape notice easily with a change of his face across campus, so he rarely ever faced any backlash or confrontation for his questionable romantic dalliances. The tournament, however, was another case altogether. With his name publicly available to confirm his identity, it was impossible for him to slip away.

Kizu still winced when recalling the mage’s boiling water technique. On their wet marshy terrain, that spell had been extraordinarily effective. Not a single centimeter stretch of Basil’s skin had been left unblemished and blistered. Even after the match’s conclusion, Basil had remained pink skinned and flushed for hours.

Everything was in motion for his departure. Unlike last time, Kizu properly planned out his delve and even set up Aoi and Stina to watch over Anata and the Kitsune children. To make absolutely certain he wasn’t followed, Kizu arranged a special meeting with a connection through Vincent. Two days into his delve, a specialized griffin trainer was scheduled to arrive to give Anata and Mae lessons on how to properly ride Comet. While Kizu normally would have insisted on being present at a lesson like this, it served the perfect bit of incentive to keep Anata at home while he went ‘exploring with Ione for a few days.’

Even Anata knew enough about the world to understand blatant romantic implications like that. If she didn’t sniff around and question his absence, then Kizu felt confident in his plan.

When he arrived back on Owl’s Respite, he found his plan functioning even better than expected. Anata had five different books out in front of her, all about different aspects of griffins. She rested on her stomach with her feet kicking slowly in the air behind her as she read along, oblivious to his entry.

Kizu recognized the books as gifts to him from Vincent, given to him back when he’d first dumped Comet into Kizu’s lap. They covered an entire range of griffin related topics from hoof measurements to bareback riding. Of course, it wasn’t exactly the same as a hippogriff, but the species were close enough that Vincent deemed them a good study. Kizu, in a desperate attempt to not end up on Comet’s back a kilometer into the sky anytime soon, had left the pile of books on his bookshelf, untouched as he focused on his academics. Besides, the young hippogriff was too small to support his weight any time soon.

He left Anata to her studies.

Down in his work room in the ship’s cargo hold, Kizu took stock of his preparations. Rope, potions, food, and a spare set of clothes.

He wished he’d had more time to prepare more enchanted equipment for himself, but without Anata supplying blood he lacked the trial and error method of creating objects he’d once utilized. Stripping a failed enchantment from an item took a significant amount of blood, which made enchantments one of the most costly of all the branches of magic. He longingly thought back to his first semester’s midterm project. A pair of gloves that allowed him to stick to walls on demand. They’d been ruined when he slew the building-sized polar bear that guarded the Kitsune’s seal in Tross. He wished he’d thought to make another pair for himself back while he still had access to Anata's blood.

Rather than linger on objects lost to him, Kizu turned his attention to the magic items that remained on hand for his use.

The broomstick taken from the crone’s entryway leaned up against his desk. That was by far the most cumbersome of his available equipment and he sadly thought it best to leave it behind. It was too bad, since he’d yet to find a good occasion to put it to use.

His clothes, sewn and enchanted by Basil, were designed to keep him safe and comfortable. Heat resistance, cold resistance, even a bit of stabbing resistance. The clothes wouldn’t keep him safe from a fireball to the face or a scorpion stinger to the heart, but they’d at least offer basic protection from more minor threats they’d encounter.

His necklace, either an extremely powerful enchanted item crafted by his master diviner sister or an artifact from the World Dungeon itself. He had yet to solve that particular mystery. Regardless of its origin, while he wore it, it kept him untraceable through all scrying means. It even protected his magic use from detection. No bloodspawn would be able to track him or those in his party so long as he wore the necklace.

He likewise had his atlas, to direct him in the World Dungeon and hopefully direct him away from any harm. The traps he’d triggered in his first delve had been a result of Basil running off with the atlas to meet Otochi, his uncle, to learn about his family. If his friend hadn’t done that, Kizu likely wouldn’t have an ancient soul fragment embedded in him or an evil version of himself at Otochi’s beck and call. So, keeping the atlas safe this time was a massive priority for Kizu.

And then there was the bell he’d stolen from Otochi’s minions. It woke up the World Dungeon, forcing its attention on him. In the past, that had mostly served to help him. Though, he knew better than to count on it to get him out of sticky situations. The World Dungeon could choose to not assist him, just as it had when he and Ione confronted the witch apprentices. Its activation also sent an electrical current through his body that overloaded any lesser magical items. An inconvenience he needed to remain mindful of.

Sojan would sadly not be a part of his equipment in this delve. The dagger that had proved instrumental in getting him out of his first confrontation with Otochi had to remain embedded in the mouth of the Dragon on the other side of the island. Even if Kizu found a way to fit a Dragon into the labyrinth below, he wouldn’t want to bring his friend. There was no telling how the World Dungeon would react if it realized Sojan possessed one of its children’s soulless bodies. That would certainly only serve to render the bell even more obsolete.

The ring given to him by Roku granted him a bit more offensive capabilities. With a flick of his wrist, he could have a blade of ice on hand. While Kizu had never studied how to properly wield a sword, it didn’t take a genius to know how to stick a bad thing with the pointy end. However, in hindsight, he supposed asking Arclight for basic swordsmanship advice might have been advantageous. Maybe he’d pursue that next semester.

As Kizu reviewed his equipment, he silently lamented that he hadn’t focused more on enchanting in these last few months. Most of his progress in the field had instead been focused on stripping enchantments, since he deemed it a faster means in which to move up in the academy ranking. But, in doing so, he had very few objects at his disposal. Now that he no longer had access to Anata’s blood, it meant an end to reckless experimentation. When developing new enchantments moving forward, he needed to have a careful plan in mind or risk using up all of his blood on a project that failed.

However, his final enchanted piece of equipment helped make up for his enchantment deficit. His spatial ring held dozens of different concoctions. It was heavy on his hand, the weight of the potions reduced, but not entirely gone. His stock consisted of a variety of offensive, defensive, and utility potions. Normally, he kept only a few of the most important potions there, but he needed to carry things like their sustenance potion supply as well. Everyone would have a few on their person, just in case the worst occurred and they were separated, but the bulk would be carried by Kizu.

Not for the first time, he wondered if he should create some sort of necklace to hold his ring. But placing that much weight on his neck risked causing neck and back injuries. Since Kizu had no idea how to heal something like a bad back, he decided keeping it on his hand would be prudent. Especially since it was extraordinarily convenient there.

Kizu elected to leave his scrying orb behind. Even though his necklace offered him protection from tracking, he feared that the academy would still have a means in which of realizing what he’d done if he brought it down into the World Dungeon with him.

As he stored the orb away in one of his potion equipment cupboards, something caught his eye. He’d ignored it ever since picking it up back in the Hon Basin. The crone’s old wooden bowl she used for scrying. His spellsense told him the bowl was nothing but mundane wood, though he secretly wondered if perhaps there was more to it. If it had any other purpose, he’d not found it. The only notable aspect of the bowl were the animals carved into its edges.

On a whim, he slid it into his pack.

With everything now physically prepared, Kizu closed his eyes and spent the next hour mentally scouring his soul companion’s memories for any record of the Blood Lord. To his surprise, he found those memories sealed beyond his reach. He’d thought he’d fully absorbed everything the ancient soul had to offer.

He felt Zayne’s words echo through his soul. “Soon, but not yet.”
