Yelena bit back a terrified scream as the airship tilted once more, spinning violently backward until it faced an entirely different direction. Her soldiers shouted, but the howling winds swallowed their voices, and the crash of thunder above buried what little managed to escape.

Miles away from the fighting Calamities, and they couldn’t even get close.

This damned ship couldn’t get close.

Before any of them could brace for the impact, the ship spun again, shuddering violently in the wind until, finally, it faced north once more.

Or maybe it was west.

Yelena couldn’t tell. The compass had been destroyed, and the raging winds made it impossible to see anything.

What she could tell, however, was that this battle couldn’t be allowed to continue. Not with the thousands of helpless people below. Not with so many lives at stake.

She had to save them.

Unfortunately, none of her soldiers shared her defiance.

“Mistress Yelena!” Vorik shouted as the ship dodged another bolt of lightning. Or perhaps dodged was the wrong word.

Raw, primal electricity shot through the ship’s metallic frame an instant later, and in less than a second—before Yelena could so much as blink—three of her soldiers disintegrated, vaporized into dust and energy.

This time, even she couldn’t dismiss it.

Her eyes widened in horror, and she staggered away from the hull as though it had suddenly become molten. She barely managed two unsteady steps before her legs gave out beneath her.

She collapsed onto the wooden deck, her soldiers too stunned to catch her as every gaze snapped to the place where the three soldiers had stood only a heartbeat earlier.

One of them, Yelena remembered, had been named Miriam.

Yelena didn’t usually care to learn the names of her soldiers. But she had known Miriam because she had known Miriam’s daughter.

And now, in less than a second, she was gone. Vanquished by an attack that hadn’t even been aimed at her. By an opponent who had absolutely no idea she existed.

Slowly, almost fearfully, Yelena turned her gaze toward the battling Calamities, and the hatred in her heart hardened into something cold.

Something merciless.

“Vorik,” she muttered, her voice icy.

The soldier turned, his eyes wide with terror. “Mi... Mistress Yelena, we must turn back. We cannot afford to go any closer. Already we have lost four of our soldiers.”

“We will lose thousands of our citizens if we leave now,” Yelena replied quietly. “If we do not stop them now, they will wreck the entire city and leave us desolate, just as they did fifteen years ago. What would be left of our defiance then?”

The soldiers exchanged wary glances before the captain finally spoke, his voice trembling.

“Mistress, the winds are against us. Even if we were capable of stopping them, we cannot do it if we can't even get close.”

“We do not need to get close.”

Yelena scowled, hatred flashing in her eyes as she rose to her feet. “Aim your cannons at them and fire. We do not need to get close to stop them. We simply need to get their attention.”

The soldiers froze, eyes wide with fear. The last thing any of them wanted was to draw the attention of a Calamity, let alone five.

“Mistress...” another soldier stammered. “Can you please consider our lives?”

“I’m considering the lives of those you swore to protect.” She glared at the man. “Now do as I say.”

Yet no one moved. None dared.

Yelena was ordering them to fire—not at other humans, nor even at weak cultivators—but at Calamities. Beings so powerful they were practically gods. They had to make her see reason. They had to—

“I gave an order, Captain.” Yelena snapped, fury blazing in her eyes. “Aim your cannons before I aim them at you.”

The captain hesitated a moment longer, glancing fearfully at his comrades before slowly nodding.

“Yes, Mistress.”

Yelena barely looked at him. Instead, she raised a hand and pointed as the ship hummed beneath her and the cannons engaged. The captain aligned the weapons with her gesture and swallowed hard.

“Ready, Mistress.”

With all the fury burning within her, Yelena gave the command. “Fire.”

Almost immediately, the deck jolted beneath her feet, every plank trembling as the cannons erupted with a thunderclap so violent it felt as though someone had struck her square in the chest with a giant’s hammer.

But Yelena barely flinched.

Driven by hatred, she watched several bursts of orange erupt from the ship as half a dozen shells streaked toward the figures in the sky like meteors crossing the heavens.

But even they never came close.

A shimmering dome of black lightning spread across the district before the shells could strike, shielding everything beneath the clash of the Calamities above.

The raging winds died in an instant. The ship, which moments ago had been fighting for its life, settled immediately. And all around her, her remaining soldiers sighed in relief, their fingers moving in a prayer she knew all too well.

But Yelena wasn’t relieved.

Because part of her—a very large part of her—had wanted to see those cannon shells connect and explode, if only to see what would happen. Instead, she watched orange bloom across the shield, fire unfurling like molten paint splashed against obsidian before vanishing without a trace.

Not even a crack marred the dome.

Silence reigned over the ship for a long moment. Then Vorik spoke. “We should turn back now, Mistress Yelena. We may be shielded from the Calamities, but we are still too close.”

Yelena didn’t feel too close.

She didn’t feel close enough.

But Vorik was right. With the district shielded, there was nothing left to do but watch. But fold her arms and be useless.

Inhaling slowly, she closed her eyes and let her shoulders sag. “Do as you see fit, Vorik.”

Vorik nodded, and immediately shouted to the captain. “Take us as far back as you can.”

The captain must have nodded because she didn’t hear him answer. However, just as the ship began to turn, Yelena opened her eyes...

…and caught sight of something far in the distance.

Figures standing on a platform. A magical, floating platform.

Her eyes widened.

“Stop!” she commanded at once, her lips curling into a sneer as she pointed toward the distant figures. “Follow them!”

“Who?” the captain cried, eyes wide with dismay. “Let us go back, Mistress. I thought you ordered us to retreat.”

“Not anymore.” Yelena scowled, her hatred surging back with renewed force. “There are cultivators within the dome, and just like their masters, they must be stopped before they start another battle inside it.”

She turned back toward the distant platform and hissed, “Follow them, Captain.”

The soldiers muttered curses and exchanged weary glances. Some even looked on the verge of tears. But Yelena refused to cower.

And inevitably, they were forced to give in.

The ship turned, and a moment later, it dove down to follow the fleeing cultivators.

With her hands tightly gripping the hull of the ship, Yelena glared after them One way or another, she would put an end to their mission in Alcatraz and discover exactly what they were searching for before they killed any more of her people.

This was her father's city.

And she would be damned if she let anyone destroy it while she still had the power to act.

Magic be damned.

It was the greatest strength of his path.

Just like Elder Ghaltris, she was a war general—one with illusion and reality manipulation at her fingertips. Not to mention a buttload of anger and hatred.

MY DOMAIN, MY RULES: REALITY RIPS YOU APART.

EDICT OF STILLNESS.
