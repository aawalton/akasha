A few minutes earlier.

Aodhán floated high above the dueling ring, a proud smile on his face as he watched Crixis put everything he had taught him to use.

They had been training for almost six months now, and Crixis had improved tremendously. The mere fact that he was holding his own against an advanced-class inheritor was proof of that.

“You know,” Orpheus began, “I must say he’s really impressive. A path related to inevitability must have been pretty difficult to come by.”

“Uh-huh.”

Aodhán muttered, barely sparing the man a glance. He had been providing commentary throughout the entire duel and seemed perfectly content to keep talking even when Aodhán didn’t respond.

“Is it the result of a two-path merge?” Orpheus asked, then promptly answered his own question. “Of course, it must be. I wonder what paths he merged to get something like this.”

He glanced at Aodhán, a small smile on his lips. “You must be so proud of your underling right now. I hear this is the reward of all your training.”

Aodhán gave him a flat look.

“It is the reward of Crixis's hard work.”

“Of course,” Orpheus agreed in that not-so-subtle way people did when they didn't truly agree. He glanced back at the duel and frowned. “I do fear, though, that this is as far as Crixis will go. If he thinks he can defeat Lysera Axilas-Vaust with a few parlor tricks, he's sorely mistaken.”

Aodhán glanced at him then, annoyance flickering across his features.

However, just as he did, a loud roar of amazement erupted from the crowd, followed by cheers and whistles. Aodhán immediately turned back, but whatever had caused the excitement had already passed.

And judging by the expression on Crixis's face, it had been important.

“See? I told you,” Orpheus chuckled. “Your underling needs to give up now.”

Aodhán ignored him completely, trying to figure out what he had missed.

Fortunately, he didn't have to wait long.

Lysera attacked first this time, dozens of lightning spears slicing toward Crixis with brutal intent. Crixis blocked, activating Inevitable Shield—Maxim in defense.

But Lysera didn't remain behind her attack and wait for it to land. She moved with it, a blur of white and silver, her hands reaching toward the shield.

Her fingers brushed against it an instant later, and Aodhán watched in shock as the shield was bleached of its inevitability, the amethyst fading to pink, then to pure white.

The spears struck a heartbeat later and pierced straight through, nearly slicing Crixis to pieces before his anglewalkers whisked him out of harm's way.

Those boots truly were amazing.

However, as concerned as Aodhán was for Crixis's safety, his attention was drawn to the floating white essence evaporating from the unraveling shield.

Pure white and easily impressionable.

There was no doubt about it. Lysera had purified the shield to its basest state.

Pure energy.

His eyes widened as his mind began racing, but a violent explosion of energy ripped his attention back to the duel.

Crixis had been struck.

Aodhán winced as he watched him hurtle through the air, blood spraying from the wound. Fortunately, the anglewalkers arrested his momentum before he could be flung from the ring.

With a flurry of amethyst chains, Crixis attacked again, only for every strike to be purified and turned back against him.

He was being pushed back hard.

But Aodhán wasn’t too worried. Not truly.

Because the truth was that Crixis hadn’t even begun fighting yet. If his attacks could be purified, then were they truly inevitable?

What Crixis had been using so far was the target-locking skill he'd developed from Aodhán's explanation of how missiles worked. Now that Lysera had countered it, he would have to become serious.

And that, no one could ever counter.

Not even him.

Mentally dismissing Crixis, Aodhán turned his attention back to Lysera, watching curiously as she purified another beam of amethyst into harmless vapor.

His gaze narrowed, tracking her movements and the fluctuations of her core, studying the stripping process as closely as he could.

It was barely perceptible.

But Aodhán wasn't discouraged. If he could somehow learn Lysera's essence-stripping technique simply by watching her, then he would have gotten what he came here for without paying a single coin.

He wouldn't need her to teach him anything anymore.

***

Crixis dashed sharply to the side, burning through a large amount of accumulated momentum to grant himself an extra burst of speed.

Even then, he barely avoided the barrage of white lightning she sent his way.

After identifying the nature of his path, Lysera had shifted into full offense, making him realize that as powerful as her purification ability was, it was only the first of many combat techniques she possessed.

He had barely reoriented himself before she attacked again, several spears of purified lightning lancing toward him at terrifying speeds.

Crixis didn't bother blocking.

Instead, he launched his own spears to intercept them, still reluctant to use any of his major skills.

Or rather, his major skill, as he only had one.

Word of Inevitability.

It was the first skill he had gained upon walking the Path of Ruinous Inevitability, and by far his most dangerous. But it was also tremendously energy-intensive, hence his hesitation.

However, as the seconds ticked by and he was steadily driven backward, Crixis realized he might not have a choice.

Not if he wanted to win this duel.

Twisting sharply aside to evade another barrage of purified lightning, he narrowed his gaze at Lysera, weighing just how much injury he was willing to inflict to secure victory.

Word of Inevitability was too powerful to use carelessly, and Lysera wasn't Aodhán, who could brush aside his attacks even at full strength.

Twisting aside once more, he unleashed a swarm of amethyst chains at her, each twisting and writhing as they sought to bind her. But once again, Lysera purified them, stripping away their power and inevitability the instant they touched her body.

It was the same move she had relied on throughout the duel. But just as he was about to dismiss her as a one-trick pony, Lysera did something else.

Something terrifying.

She gathered the unraveling essence and repurposed it into jagged lances of purified lightning.

Then she turned them amethyst.

Crixis's eyes nearly bulged from their sockets as the spears shot toward him, waves of inevitability rolling from their tips.

He tried to dodge.

And failed.

The spears slammed into him with the force of a siege engine, punching through flesh and carving jagged furrows across his side before detonating.

White-hot pain consumed him as the earth vanished beneath his feet, the explosion hurling him violently into the air. He crashed back into the ground a heartbeat later, bones creaking and blood spurting.

But he wasn’t down for the count yet.

Hissing through clenched teeth, he opened his eyes to see another barrage of amethyst spears hurtling toward him, their tips gleaming with murderous intent.

He barely had time to hesitate before the words left his lips, no louder than a whisper carried on the wind.

“Be struck.”

And from the heavens above, the storm answered—a single beam of pure, divine amethyst striking Lysera down before she could so much as blink.

The world turned white instantly.

There was no lag. No delay.

Only blinding light.

Then eerie silence as the radiance faded to reveal Lysera, electrocuted and bleeding from a fist-sized hole in her stomach while arcs of lightning danced uncontrollably across her body.

For a heartbeat, stunned silence hung over the arena.

And then chaos—as the crowd exploded into cheers, gasps, and horrified cries, the deafening roar crashing over the arena like thunder in Crixis's ears.

But even as Elder Vaust announced his victory, Crixis's gaze remained fixed on Lysera, watching as her sisters and a healer rushed toward her, lightning crackling in their wake.

It wasn't until he confirmed she was still alive that he finally allowed himself to bask in the glory of victory, panting heavily from the immense amount of energy he had just expended.

Just that one activation had drained nearly his energy pool. Fortunately, he still had enough to stand.

And so he did, a small grin tugging at his lips as he raised a hand toward the cheering audience, elation surging through him.

Aodhán appeared before him an instant later, a proud smile on his face. “You did great, Crixis. It seems all those extra training hours haven’t been in vain after all.”

Crixis chuckled. “No, they haven’t. I’m glad I made you proud.”

Aodhán gave him a firm nod before turning his attention toward Lysera, who still looked thoroughly dazed.

Her injuries were already closing, courtesy of her advanced regeneration and the healers' ministrations. A few moments later, she gasped and flailed violently in panic.

Beside him, Crixis grimaced. “I was trying to avoid something like this.”

Aodhán sighed. “Some things just cannot be avoided, Crixis. Besides, this was the only way you could win, and I would have been very disappointed if you hadn't.”

Crixis glanced at him, then nodded. “Well, I guess you can't eat your cake and have it too.”

Aodhán nodded before turning to Lilian as she came to stand beside them.

“Have you made your decision?” Lilian asked and he sighed.

“Yes, I have.”

There was a beat of silence before she spoke again. “Will she like it?”

Aodhán looked at Lysera for a moment before shaking his head.

“No, she won't.”

He waited until Lysera had fully stabilized before making his way over, giving the healer a polite nod as she stepped aside.

Crouching until they were at eye level, he offered Lysera a gentle smile. “I’m glad you’re alright, Lysera. But I fear you’ve lost this duel.”

Lysera frowned.

“I don’t know how it happened. Somebody must have interfered. Someone must have—”

“No one interfered, Lysera,” one of her sisters said, her gaze fixed on Crixis. “It was the underling's doing.”

“What?!”

Lysera stared at her sister in shock before looking back at Crixis as though seeing him for the first time. She held his gaze for a long moment before slowly turning back to Aodhán.

“I'm not weak,” she began, swallowing as she pushed herself upright. “I'm not weak. You've seen what I can do. What I'm capable of. I proved myself despite the loss.”

“Indeed,” Aodhán said with a nod. “Your path is very unique, and your strength is admirable for your tier. But...” His expression sobered. “Ruin and purification are opposite ends of the same spectrum. There is very little I can teach you.”

Lysera recoiled from his words, betrayal flashing across her face.

“But I proved myself,” she muttered. “And purification cuts across all paths.” Her voice rose. “You are literally here because your path needs me. We are not two ends of a spectrum.”

Aodhán regarded her quietly for a long moment before letting out a slow breath. “I’m sorry, Lysera, but there are certain criteria to becoming my underling. You haven’t met them.”

He raised himself to his feet. “I urge you to ask for something else. Something I can give.”

“I don’t want something else.”

“And yet, its either that or I walk away.”

Aodhán's voice was calm, betraying none of the hope or desperation. Despite watching Lysera intently throughout the duel, he still hadn't understood how she stripped attacks of their affinity and reduced them to pure essence.

He needed her to teach him.

But he wouldn't grovel for it.

He needed her to realize that letting him walk away without sharing that knowledge would be doing herself a disservice.

Something in his gaze must have convinced her of that, and after a long moment, she exhaled, her shoulders sagging in resignation.

“If you won't give me the chance to become your underling,” she began, “then will you at least be my mentor?”

She lifted her gaze to meet his, her silver irises unwavering. “Will you teach me, train me, and invite me to witness ascensions? Because if you cannot do that, then I cannot teach you. This is my compromise.”

She took a slow breath. “I cannot go any lower.”

Aodhán kept his expression neutral as he considered her proposal.

As little as it was, Lysera had indeed compromised, and though it was still a steep price to pay for knowledge, Aodhán found himself not entirely opposed to it.

Nevertheless, if he was going to accept it, he needed to set some ground rules.

“I will train you,” he began. “Once a month. I will invite you to ascensions and any other special events I believe you could benefit from. That is as far as I'm willing to go.”

He held her gaze. “If you accept those terms, then it is done. I shall be your mentor, and you shall be my disciple.”

Lysera glanced toward her sisters, a silent exchange passing between them before she looked back at Aodhán.

A smile bloomed on her face. “I accept, special disciple Aodhán.”

Aodhán smiled back.

“Good,” he extended a hand toward her. “Let’s get to work then.”

Only a few meters away, Orpheus Al'ruhn stood atop a floating platform, a slight scowl etched across his face as he watched the exchange.

Beside him stood his best friend, Cyrenna Blackstone, a faint smile playing on her lips as she watched him watching Aodhán.

Eventually, when she couldn’t take it anymore, she asked. “Do you want him to take you as his underling too?”

Orpheus immediately shot her a glare.

“Surely, you jest,” he scowled. “You cannot possibly mean the words you're sprouting right now.”

“Why?” Cyrenna teased. “Is it because he's younger than you? Or because he's less advanced than you? Or—”

Her eyes suddenly lit up.

“I've got it. Do you fear his underling would take you out too?”

“No, I do not fear that his underling would take me out,” Orpheus snapped, his glare deepening. “But the other two are very valid reasons. Not to mention the simple fact that I do not need mentoring. I am the great-great-great—”

“Grandchild, yes,” Cyrenna interrupted, rolling her eyes. “See? You already have the connection. Perhaps High Elder Zarxan could put in a good word for you.”

He glared at her, irritation written plainly across his face. “I don’t need a good word, Cyrenna. Because I do not need a mentor.”

His gaze drifted back to Aodhán, narrowing slightly. “I will fight him one day.”

A dangerous smile tugged at his lips. “And I will win.”

Where she stood, Lilian smiled at the audacious declaration, not at all surprised by it.

Many core disciples—especially the powerful ones—believed themselves to be on the same level as Specials. And for the greatest geniuses among them...

That belief was almost true.

Almost.

But not quite.
