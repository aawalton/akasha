Cy on his shoulder, Eric walked toward the roar. Every instinct told him to run away from the roar, but that wasn’t how he got EXP, so he pressed on toward it. He had his casting sword at the ready and the Magical Wand on his belt. As much as he’d wanted to practice Ice Spear, his mana was running a little low, and he didn’t want to find out Ice Spear took a half or a quarter of his remaining mana right before the boss fight. After the battle was the time for testing. If he had to, he could always give it a cast, but he really doubted the ice area’s boss would be weak to ice.

Can’t wait to find out what kind of weapon trivializes the Trinotaur, he thought as he trudged through the snow, or armor, or gear. Whatever it was, it had to be OP if it was strong enough to make a joke of that monster. Either that, or the Trinotaur had some kind of exploitable weakness he didn’t know about. If that was the case, it’d be a bit unfortunate, but ultimately not a huge loss. He’d still have learned a weakness of a boss monster, and if he ever ran into anything like it again, he’d know how to take it down, and have the exact gear he needed to turn it into mincemeat with minimum effort.

Yeah… no matter how much the System tried to mock me for what I couldn’t possibly know ahead of time, I think I did okay. Not perfectly, but fine. A passing grade. He was trying to survive, not get an A+. There were things he simply couldn’t know before he went through a dungeon. He could make educated guesses and do his best, but there was no guarantee he got it right. That was simply the way it was. This wouldn’t be the last time he made a mistake or chose the less-than-ideal route, nor was it the first.

But that was what the guide was for. He could help others avoid the mistakes he made. That was what it was all about.

I definitely need to do a bit on these two and on linked dungeons in general. If it’s pre-noon when I finish up here, I can do it after this; otherwise, I can come back out and do it first thing tomorrow, before I hit up a dungeon. There were more dungeons in this direction, after all. He could head out this direction, do the guide thing, then keep heading deeper this way down the misty path.

The monster, whatever it was, roared again, breaking Eric out of his thoughts. It was closer now, close enough that he couldn’t afford to drift off again. He had a feeling he knew where it was, too. The ground rose ahead of him, gently swelling underfoot, then abruptly fell away. The roaring came from that direction, and if he was a betting man, he’d put money on the big boss monster being just over the rise. That being the case, Eric knelt and crept forward, sneaking up to the edge rather than casually walking up to it. He peered over the cliff and found what he was looking for: a big, nasty monster.

A giant, white bear roamed in the valley, occasionally sniffing the air. It wasn’t just large, but enormous, easily the size of a small sailboat. It left deep prints in the snow as it walked, its legs dragging through the accumulated powder and leaving long trails behind it. A small contingent of elves clung to its back or crouched on platforms bound to its sides, holding bows. The bear circled the valley below Eric, stalking in an endless loop. Occasionally, it lifted its head and roared, shaking the valley walls with its ferocity. Bits of snow and icicles rained down, piling at the edge of the valley. Little divots in the powder along the valley’s border marked where clumps of snow and icicles had fallen before.

Sheesh. A boat-sized bear? Eric took a slow breath. On sheer mass alone, that thing was bigger than the Trinotaur. He wasn’t sure he could take it even with a Fireball or two. It’d help if it was weak to fire, but he didn’t know that. All he knew was that it was huge, and it brought ranged backup.

Then again, I’m up on a cliff. He had the advantage here: a nice high vantage point from which to attack the bear that it couldn’t really do much about. It had archers, sure, but Eric knew how to shut them down, and anyways, they weren’t nearly as threatening as a giant bear.

He thought for a moment, then went through his inventory. He had a mana potion; just one, but he could use it if it came down to it. He had Fireball and his Fireball-casting sword, and plenty of Firebolts and Explosions too. Plus, he had his Magical Wand, which he might as well use before the mana potion. Both cost resources, and AA batteries could be hard to come by, but ultimately mana was more versatile than the batteries in the wand. He could use mana to attack, but he could also use it to throw smoke or block arrows with Explosion, freeze things, and heal himself. Plus, he currently had an almost-full pack of AA batteries. Might as well burn a few here.

He took the pack of batteries out of his inventory and shoved it in his coat pocket just in case. As little as he wanted to be fumbling batteries in and out of a toy wand mid-battle, if they were accessible, he’d at least have the option.

Though… if I’m up here, and he’s down there, maybe it won’t be that bad. Who knew? And maybe they’d force him to go fight it face-to-face. Maybe the System would spawn an invisible shield between him and the bear. Maybe this dungeon had invisible walls, in true video-game fashion. He didn’t know. But he was about to scout it out.

Magical Wand in one hand, the spell sword close to hand at his hip, Eric waited until the bear was as far from the entrance to the valley as it could get, then narrowed his eyes. Pointing his hand into a finger gun, he leveled the tip of his finger at one of the riding elves’ heads. It’s just clicking heads. One breath in, one breath out. Pause. He went completely still, just for a moment, holding his breath, his heart between beats. He fired.

The bolt of fire hurtled through the air, hissing faintly as it flew. The elf looked up a split second before the Firebolt slammed into their face. The impact knocked them bodily back, lifting them off the platform by their head. They flipped head-over-heels, then landed face-down in the snow.

No invisible walls here. Just the advantage of height. Despite himself, Eric grinned. This was going to be way easier than he’d feared.

All the elves jolted, leaping into action, shouting at one another and reaching for bows and arrows. One slapped the bear, and it roared again, eyes roaming as it searched for prey.

Eric didn’t wait for them to get their bearings. He immediately pointed the Magical Wand at the bear. The shitty speakers tinkled to life, and he pushed one pulse of mana through the wand after another. Star-shaped mana bolts blasted out of the wand, closing in on the bear to the dulcet tones of Twinkle Twinkle Little Star. The bear roared and reared to meet them, knocking a few of the more-distracted elves off its back. The stars caught it in the chest and shoved it back, each one forcing the bear back another step. Blood burst from its chest, bright against its white fur, and it roared yet again in pain and fury. Dropping to all fours, it took off running, loping toward the entrance to the valley.

The music wound down. The lights faded, and the mana bolts landed soft. Eric cursed under his breath and tore at the wand’s side, ripping out the old AAs and replacing them with fresh batteries. He let the used ones drop to the floor. He could worry about the Earth after he saved his own skin.

The bear was close to the mouth of the valley, rapidly closing in on the point where the stone cliff walls eclipsed Eric’s view of it. Eric chased it with mana bolts, tracking just a little ahead of it so bolt after bolt rained down on its shoulders. The elves on its back hunkered, clinging on for dear life with no time to bother with their weapons.

Down below, the elves who’d been jostled free clambered to their feet. One notched an arrow, firing a shrieking shot toward Eric. Eric dodged to the side, still focused on hammering the bear with mana bolts. Another two arrows screeched toward him, and he had to pause and throw an Explosion between him and the archers to knock them down. But only for a moment. The bear was still rushing his way. He went back to slamming it with mana bolts, relocating slightly along the edge of the cliff to put a tree between him and the archers.

Mid-rush, the bear stumbled, then dropped. Its body skidded forward through the snow, leaving a crimson smear behind it. Eric took a deep breath, relieved, then immediately leaped back into the safety of the tree as the elves launched a volley of arrows his way. The music of the Magical Wand slowed down and grew steadily quieter, so he switched it off. One at a time, he picked off the remaining archers with Firebolts. The archers who’d stuck to the bear and survived its fall sprinted off down the valley toward him. He fired a random bolt or two their way, but they were moving targets, and the standing archers directly below him did their best to stop him from taking out the runners. On the third near-miss from an arrow, Eric devoted all his attention to the standing archers instead. He needed to take out the covering fire before he could focus on the runners.

The last of the covering archers went down. Eric turned, firing a Firebolt after the tail of the runners, but they followed the rest around the edge of the valley and out of sight, hidden by the way the stone rose and the forest climbed out to meet the valley’s mouth. Eric ran for it. They knew where he was; he needed to get out of here before they arrived, or he’d be a sitting duck.

But… that’s weird. I took out the bear. Why isn’t the dungeon closing? I didn’t need to wipe out every snakeman in the snake dungeon. Or are the archer-riders considered part of the bear boss? He didn’t know, and neither Joe nor the System were forthcoming.

As he ran, he fumbled new batteries into the Magical Wand. The pack was already thinning; only half remained of the original 12 batteries. He either needed a much steadier supply of batteries or to fully divulge himself of needing to rely on the Magical Wand. Or both.

His mind ran at the same time, moving just as fast as his feet. Should I have Fireballed the fully loaded bear instead of using the wand? Would that have killed all the elves and done some damage to the bear? I didn’t, because I was worried about the smoke clouding my shots and the AOE not doing much damage to the bear. I barely killed the bear as is, pun not intended, hammering it with my highest single-target spell. If I’d wasted time with a Fireball—especially if it had thrown up a smoke cloud or a steam cloud from hitting the snow—I might not have killed the bear.

No, I think I was right to prioritize the bear. The archers aren’t much of a threat. I can pick them off in the forest, no problem. The bear is… was the problem. And now it’s solved. So yeah… I think I picked right. Or at least right enough. He’d survived, and his life wasn’t under immediate overwhelming threat. His biggest problem was no longer a problem. Sure, there were other ways to get the job done, even with the tools he had, but he’d done an adequate enough job.

Or… I will have done an adequate enough job if I survive this last batch of archers. He couldn’t let his guard down just because they weren’t particularly scary. A well-placed arrow could kill him just as fast as the bear. Just like in any video game, it was the second he let his guard down and allowed himself to make mistakes that he died.

A cold wind blew, whipping the powder snow and solidified ice on the branches into a whirling, freezing, cutting spiral of death. A bit of ice flew past his face and slashed his cheek just under his eye. He flinched, but too late; if it’d been a few centimeters higher, he would’ve lost an eye with no ability to react. Eric hunkered down on a tree, burrowing his face between his limbs for warmth and to protect his face from getting slashed by the ice. The wind grew fiercer, and the cold more intense. Fresh ice and snow pounded down on him from the sky, building up on the exterior of his invisible coat and forming a kind of cocoon around him. Somewhere in the distance, Ice Elves screamed, then were cut off.

To Eric’s surprise, a System message popped up:

Congratulations! For killing the boss too quickly using the obvious cliff exploit, you’ve triggered the Secret Boss!

Ah, fuck, Eric thought, and then the world went white.
