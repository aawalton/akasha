Within a minute, it became clear that she had to remember not only the sequence of turns but also when to change partners without stepping on anyone's feet. Sakura went the wrong way once, nearly collided with an old woman wearing a goat mask, and burst out laughing when the woman steered her back in the right direction.

Lir joined the dancing too, but not before handing his stuffed bird to Gilbert. Sakura caught the mage looking at the gift, then at Lir, before shaking his head in resigned annoyance.

Despite her mistakes, dancing turned out to be enjoyable. Sakura felt a strange sense of déjà vu that surely had something to do with her past life, although she was certain she hadn't had many opportunities to dance even back then. She changed partners several times and had already memorized at least half the movements, though the locals were still much better at it. However, after another couple of minutes, her injured side reminded her of its existence, and she had to leave the circle.

Gilbert met her near a bench. Sakura was out of breath, and judging by how her cheeks burned, her face was probably bright red.

"Well?"

"Wonderful. I've practically mastered the local art of dancing."

"You went the wrong way three times."

"Wait, were you watching me?"

"Not exactly, but you were hard to miss. You kept throwing off the rhythm of the dance."

Sakura sat down on the bench, carefully holding her side. His remark embarrassed her a little, although Gilbert was mostly telling the truth.

"It's a shame you didn't want to join..."

"I warned you I wasn't going to dance."

"That afraid of embarrassing yourself?"

"No. I just prefer to stay away from people who can't tell their right foot from their left."

"How rude."

Gilbert chuckled and sat beside her. Sakura watched the dancers for a while, then looked at him. His face looked unfamiliar beneath the plain black mask. His light hair was hidden, and like this, he really did look more like an ordinary traveler attending a masquerade than a dangerous mage who controlled lightning.

"Can you even dance?" she asked.

"A little."

"Seriously?"

"Do I have to prove it?"

"It would be nice."

Gilbert looked at her, then at her hand resting near her injured side.

"You do realize you've had enough for today, right?"

"I can manage a little. Really."

He said nothing. However, a few minutes later, the musicians changed the tune. This time, they played something much slower, and the dancers separated into pairs.

Gilbert watched them for a moment, then unexpectedly stood up.

"One dance," he said, offering his hand. "And no sudden movements."

Sakura looked at him in surprise.

"Are you serious?"

"I can change my mind."

She hurried to place her hand in his before he could actually take back his offer.

The dance was completely different from the previous one. There was no need to run around in a circle or constantly change partners. Gilbert carefully placed a hand on her waist, above the injured area, and guided her through the slow steps. At first, Sakura watched her own feet, afraid of getting confused again, but she quickly realized that the mage really knew what he was doing.

"You're actually pretty good at dancing," she remarked.

"I told you."

"You said 'a little.'"

"This is a little."

Sakura looked up and met his eyes. Gilbert was standing so close, and she suddenly felt self-conscious about the way his attention was focused entirely on her.

"What?" she asked.

Gilbert raised an eyebrow.

"You're looking at me funny," Sakura added.

"I'm checking whether you're about to step on my foot."

The girl laughed softly.

"I'm managing so far."

"Wonderful."

They continued dancing. Gilbert moved carefully, adjusting to her steps, and soon Sakura stopped watching her feet altogether. She even allowed herself to relax, resting her free hand on his shoulder. The music played softly, and other couples moved around them.

Sakura caught herself studying his face. Under normal circumstances, Gilbert would probably have made some sarcastic remark by now, but at the moment, he was simply dancing with her and did not seem to mind it at all.

When the music ended, Sakura did not step away immediately. Gilbert, however, was in no hurry to release her hand either.

"Well? Satisfied?"

She smiled.

"I am."

"Then let's go before you come up with something else."

Sakura wanted to argue but changed her mind. They returned to the others, still holding hands for several more steps, until Gilbert finally let go near the market stalls.

They found Lir nearby, enthusiastically telling Faerd about his success on the dance floor. Apparently, his success consisted of not knocking anyone over.

Faerd listened without interrupting. Sakura noticed that the man even smiled at one of Lir's stories. Just a little, but it was enough to surprise her. Over the past few days, he had barely smiled at all, and before he was freed from the control imposed on him, she had never even imagined that Faerd's face was capable of expressing anything other than a threat.

A little later, they managed to watch a short performance by a group of traveling actors and try a few more treats. Lir kept insisting that they stay at the festival longer, but Gilbert reminded him of their agreed return time. Despite all the precautions they had taken, they were still taking a serious risk by being among so many people while they were wanted.

Before leaving, the group found an empty table. Lir was explaining that, according to the merchant, his enormous bird was supposed to represent a rare sacred animal native to the region. Gilbert did not believe the story and kept trying to convince the young man that the merchant had simply told him a pretty lie to make the prize seem better. Faerd calmly finished his pie, while Sakura sat with a mug of warm cider, watching them.

The festival continued around them. People danced, had fun, and argued, while several children ran past their table, nearly colliding with a man carrying a bundle of colorful masks. The musicians started another tune, and someone at a nearby table began loudly singing along and clapping their hands.

Sakura finished her cider and leaned back against the bench. For the entire evening, she had not needed to think about chases, guards, obsidian creatures, or any of the other unpleasant things in her life. Gilbert, of course, continued casting wary glances around, but even he had managed to have a drink, laugh, and dance, which had taken the edge off the mage's usual paranoia.

It's actually pretty nice here, Sakura thought. When nobody's trying to kill you, of course...

The thought made her smile involuntarily.

"What's so funny?" Gilbert asked.

"Oh, nothing. I was just thinking that I really enjoyed this evening. For the first time in a long while."

The mage gave a quiet chuckle.

"And you even managed to relax a little, by the way."

"Don't exaggerate."

"You danced!"

"That's hardly an achievement."

"I disagree. In your case, it absolutely is!"

Gilbert shook his head, but Sakura noticed that he was smiling too. Seeing Gilbert so calm and smiling more often was just as unusual as catching a faint smile on the face of the perpetually gloomy Faerd.

They headed home as the first lanterns in the square were being put out. Gilbert had warned them in advance that they would return by the same route, avoiding the city gates. A few merchants and late visitors were still traveling along the road, so their group did not particularly stand out among the others.

Lir carried his enormous bird, occasionally adjusting it or shifting it into a more comfortable position. Faerd still had not taken off his white mask with the butterflies, although Sakura suspected he had simply forgotten he was wearing it and was no longer paying any attention to it. Gilbert walked beside her, occasionally slowing down when he noticed that she was falling behind because of the dull ache in her side.

At one point, they turned onto a narrower road that ran between low trees and an old stone fence. The music was now behind them. The main road was only a short distance away, and beyond it lay the familiar forest path leading back to the guardhouse.

Sakura was just about to say that she would like to visit a festival like this again when a man stepped out from behind the fence. He wore a shabby cloak and held a short knife in one hand. The stranger stopped right in the middle of the road, planting his feet wide apart.

"Stop right there!"

The group stopped. Gilbert shifted slightly closer to Sakura, while Faerd remained to Lir's right, where he had a clear view of both the stranger and the road behind him. Sakura studied the man. He was short and thin, and judging by the smell, he had spent quite a bit of time near the drinking stalls.

"Hand over your coin purses!" he demanded, holding out his knife threateningly. "Quick!"

Lir slowly turned his head toward Gilbert. The enormous stuffed bird tucked under his arm swayed. Faerd looked at the stranger, then at his knife.

"All your money!" the robber repeated. "And no funny business!"

Sakura carefully covered her mouth with her hand. This couldn't be happening. Some drunk guy with a cheap little knife had actually decided to rob them? After everything they had been through?

"Are you alone?" Gilbert asked.

"What's it to you?!"

"Just asking."

The man took a step forward, gripping the knife even tighter. Faerd kept his eyes on him, while Gilbert briefly checked the empty road behind the stranger.

"I said put the money on the ground! Now!"

Sakura glanced at Gilbert and struggled to hold back a laugh.

"Was this part of our plan for getting home too?" she joked quietly.

"No," the mage replied just as quietly.

The robber shifted impatiently from one foot to the other.

"You've got a big problem!" the stranger protested. "I'm armed!"

Faerd looked at the knife once more.

"We can see that."

"Then why the hell are you all so calm?!"

And that was when Sakura finally couldn't hold it in and burst out laughing.
