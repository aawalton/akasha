“Hm… if this continues like this, I won’t really get to test my limits…”

Rusty mumbled to himself as he pulled his halberd from the red-skinned adventurer. The people of this summoning world looked the same as they had last time, similar to the other humanoids he knew, but with various skin colors, horns, and sharper teeth that made them resemble demonic humans.

Around him lay the remnants of an entire vanguard. Five bodies were scattered across the damp stone floor of the subterranean corridor, their blood pooling in the shallow grooves between the tiles. None of them had managed to land a solid blow. Between his enhanced D+ rank agility and increased strength, the battle had lasted less than three minutes.

He was not alone in his endeavor, either. The dungeon continued to spawn weak E-rank monsters that, while useless in a direct fight, served well as distractions. There were more than just skeletons here, too. The dungeon had begun spawning iron living armors, ones that looked remarkably similar to his old body.

“I see that you didn’t need my help to make things better for yourself, Dungeon Heart.”

He spoke toward the dungeon while pulling his weapon free from the defeated foe. The dungeon seemed to have evolved further, even gaining an entirely new level. The attackers appeared to be roughly the same as before, with a few stronger foes mixed in here and there, but nothing too excessive.

“Were those all of them?”

Rusty asked himself as he looked around the chamber. Behind him was the passage leading toward the Dungeon Heart, which was now protected by a much larger door. For some reason, the people of this world were dead set on destroying the heart, while the humanoids from his own world usually preserved them to retain access to the monster resources they produced.

“Strange… Maybe the Dungeon Heart itself yields some kind of material that is worth more than the combined monster resources it produces over several years? But what could possibly be worth that much…”

He had never heard of any way to manufacture Dungeon Hearts into something worthwhile. Perhaps they could be used for something he simply did not know about. The best way to find out would probably be to ask Aburdon or Alexander. Unfortunately, he had not used his last upgrade to obtain the library, so he would need to rely on them for the information.

*CLANK*

“Oh, more visitors?”

As he contemplated the matter, one of the living armors started moving down one of the corridors leading into the chamber. Just as when he had been connected to the dungeon, these ones knew whenever one of their companions was attacked. If they were within range, the Heart compelled them to move toward the source of the attack. This was already the third time it had happened here.

Just as before, Rusty mixed in among the skeletons and living armors to surprise the attackers. It was a simple tactic. He let them grow accustomed to the sluggish iron living armors, then pretended to be one of them by covering his body in dirt to conceal his enhanced frame.

The people attacking the dungeon were not particularly bright, and just as before, his ruse worked.

“ACK!”

“N-no, what is this? No one said anything like this would be here!”

“AHHHH!”

Once again, the last attacker fell to the ground after being sliced apart by his halberd, and the mission finally came to an end.

Congratulations! You have completed your mission.

“Thank…”

“Oh? Are you being grateful? No problem. It was my mission!”

This dungeon heart seemed nicer to him than the old one he had been stuck with, but he did not know whether that was simply because it could not directly control him. With his mission complete, he returned to the summoning chamber, and, just as he expected, the rewards were not all that great.

Received Small Experience Talisman x4.

Received Medium Experience Talisman x5.

Received Lesser Slashing Resistance Talisman x1

The experience talismans he received were instantly spent while he was still within the summoning chamber. They were not enough for him to reach the next level so he ramained level two. The Slashing Resistance Talisman was somewhat pointless, as his body was already resistant to this type of damage.

“Why not a Blunt Damage Resistance Talisman or something better?”

As for the summoning coins, he only gained seven of them. That was two more than what he had gotten from this dungeon last time, but almost nothing compared to all the other missions where he had been summoned by humanoids like Gwen.

“I only have fourteen points now... Not much, but better than nothing, I guess...”

He grumbled before returning to his true body, where, just as before, he saw Gleam sitting on his head.

“(˶ᵔᗜᵔ˶)ﾉ”

She quickly hopped off his helmet after noticing that he was finally moving.

“So, how long was I gone this time around?”

“(ᵕ—ᴗ—)”

“What? That long?”

To his surprise, he had been gone for close to three minutes this time.

“I was in that dungeon for around a day... What will happen if I’m gone for a week?”

He quickly did the calculations in his mind. If one day amounted to three minutes, then spending seven days on a mission would mean he would be gone for a whole twenty-one minutes. While that was not a massive amount of time, it was still long enough for an intruder to attack him while he was away.

To make matters worse, he had stayed for more than a week during some of his previous missions, meaning it was possible for him to be incapacitated for up to an hour.

“This isn’t good at all... Why is it happening now?”

Rusty looked towards Alexander, who had been the previous owner of the Soul Forge.

“Don’t look at me, Rusty. The Soul Forge you inherited is much different from the one I was using.”

“What’s your best guess, though? There must be some logic behind this...”

While Alexander thought, he looked towards Aburdon, who seemed to have finished memorizing all the maps during the short time Rusty had been gone.

“Isn’t it obvious, my dear Rusty?”

“It is?”

“Of course! Let this demon lord explain!”

Aburdon began to speak while still scribing on parchment.

“Your soul is connected to the Soul Forge, is that correct?”

“Yes, I think so, but what of it?”

Rusty nodded as he switched his loadouts to accommodate Edmund’s head within his body once again.

“It’s all obvious! As your soul grows stronger through evolution, the Soul Forge adapts to accommodate your rising authority. To handle greater amounts of mana without collapsing your physical form, the temporal bridge between the two dimensions is forced to accommodate and stretch.”

“Stretch?”

He tilted his helmet to the side, as he wasn’t quite understanding what Aburdon was getting at.

“Precisely. The higher you rank and the denser your existence becomes, the harder it is to accommodate. The Soul Forge has to slow down its time ratio relative to the real world so your soul does not tear itself apart when transmitting across space during a summon. It is simply arcane physics, my dear Rusty.”

“I see…”

Aburdon was an experienced caster and a demon lord. He seemed to understand how dimensions worked, and from what he was explaining, it did make some sense. The energy required to keep him in the Soul Forge was now greater, and some of that energy was being diverted from the magic required to maintain the time dilation.

“I’m not sure about all of that… What if it’s just your connection to the Soul Forge? The closer it is to your being, the lesser the time disruption is?”

Alexander said this while looking through some papers.

“Well… it doesn’t really matter what the reason is. It’s going to be a problem…”

Rusty scratched his helmet as he thought about how much this complicated things. In the past, he had even activated some Soul Forge features while inside the dungeon, as it had only taken a few seconds to return. Now, on the other hand, it was better to avoid venturing inside the summoning chamber unless he had at least an hour to spare, as he couldn’t predict how long one of those missions might take.

“Regardless of whether it is an issue of soul density or dimensional whatever, the result is the same. So let’s just get back to work and prepare for those Orcs. We don’t have much time!”

Alexander and Aburdon nodded and returned to their work. In the meantime, Rusty decided to make a slight change to his regular forge here. There wasn’t much time to dive into the dungeon, and after going through the summoning mission, he was more than aware of the capabilities of his current frame.

He pulled out a few upgraded forging tools along with some new and improved wares. While he had been given trouble by the merchant guild in the past and knew they were forcing him to limit himself so everyone would purchase their wares, things were different now.

“Now this is much better than before.”

Classification:

Enchanted Superior High-Steel Longsword of Sharpness and Piercing.

Type:

Sword

Rating:

Upper-Intermediate

Grade:

Refined

Description:

An enchanted longsword forged from Superior High-Steel, containing Sharpness and Piercing enchantments.

He looked at the enchanted steel blade in his hand. Even though his body was composed of Twilight Steel, it was possible to downgrade the metal into its non-elemental form, which made it possible to place enchantments on it. The superior variant of this metal also allowed it to hold multiple enchantments.

“Two enchantments, and the rating is above Intermediate now… There shouldn’t be that many weapons like this in such a small settlement…”

The sword now had two enchantments and was made from a D+ rank material. The monsters in the dungeon would not be able to resist it, giving him an edge over his competition. He knew that with weapons like this, he could attract more customers, and the merchants would no longer be able to complain that his prices were too low.

To make things even better, he could use his system to copy these items and materials, allowing him to produce them without needing the originals. Normally, manufacturing Superior High Steel ingots from raw materials would require considerable effort, but he could simply press a few buttons on his system window and create them.

‘I don’t even need to worry about gathering the materials. I can just buy them cheaply from the merchants.’

He thought through the plan carefully. It was clear that once the merchants realized he was taking their customers, they might try to withhold their raw materials or sell them at much higher prices. By that point, however, it would probably be too late. With his current position as the second in command of the mercenary group, he had gained several new options that he could not wait to try when the right opportunity came.

“Heheh, I sure hope they try something…”

“Rusty, why are you laughing?”

“No reason, Alexander. Now let’s get back to work!”

With all of that in mind, he and his guides returned to work. The plan was already in motion, and he eventually brought it to the mercenary leader. Even though he technically had two days, he needed to finish the paperwork within one day and use the remaining day to go through the guild hires.

The next morning arrived faster than Rusty had anticipated. Since he had no physical need for sleep, he spent the entire night in the basement upgrading his smithy and making it look like a proper workshop capable of producing the high-quality gear he intended to sell. Once everything was ready, he headed to the Adventurer Guild to meet the personnel they would be working with.

To no surprise, it was a bunch of unruly individuals, some of whom did not seem to like each other. He did not stay long. He simply visited one of the clerks to inform them of the rendezvous point and the time they would be leaving the following day.

After that, he returned to the mercenary building, where he found Galiena training.

‘She is really into it…’

Several E-rank humanoids lay sprawled across the ground, completely out of breath. Some looked as though they were about to pass out. Galiena stood over them, rolling her shoulders as she took a long drink from a leather bottle.

At first, Rusty assumed it was water, but thanks to his humanoid head, he could actually smell the high alcohol content coming from the bottle.

“Ah, Second! Are you done already?!”

She shouted, wiping her mouth with the back of her forearm.

“These new recruits are soft as a baby’s bottom! I was just showing them how we do things in the Steel Wolves!”

Rusty adjusted the paperwork he was holding and nodded.

“Yes.”

“Good. Just leave it in my office. I’ll look at it later.”

She said, but instantly, Aburdon’s voice resounded from his armor.

“She isn’t a good liar.”

“That she isn’t.”

Alexander agreed. His guides had assumed that she would either not look at the papers or fall asleep instantly when she tried to read them. This meant it was best for Rusty to arrive at least an hour before departure to make sure everything went through smoothly.

“Hey, do you want to go for a round, Second? You seem stronger than before…”

She called out to him as he was about to turn around and head out. In reality, he wished he could beat some sense into her, but for the time being, he needed to pretend that he was the weaker of the two.

“No, I still have some work to do. How about you spar with Jakub over there?”

Rusty pointed toward the man who had recently disrespected him. The moment Galiena saw him, she started chuckling.

“Ah, yeah. He might be a good warm-up! Hey, Jakub! Get your scrawny ass over here!”

Jakub’s face instantly paled. He glared at Rusty with pure, unadulterated hatred, but under Galiena’s heavy, expectant gaze, he had no choice but to drag his feet forward and draw his practice sword.

Rusty did not spare him a second glance as he walked away, leaving the fool to deal with the boss’s unbridled violence. This would keep both of them occupied and might also force his new foes to make more mistakes as their rage continued to build. It was a simple psychological tactic he had learned from Aburdon, and strangely enough, it was remarkably effective.

After wrapping things up at the mercenary encampment, he returned to his shop along with the two E-rank guards to show them around. The two were not particularly bright, but they were competent enough to safeguard the two children while he was away.

With that out of the way, the next day soon arrived. He gathered at the camp more than two hours early to make sure no one would mess up the final preparations. As he had suspected, the documents remained untouched.

“She really is the perfect scapegoat…”

He mumbled to himself as he became more and more aware of just how incompetent this woman was. Whatever he did would probably go unnoticed, and if something disastrous happened, he could simply blame everything on her while remaining safely out of the picture.

“Now then… Let’s hunt some orcs.”

Soon, he moved over to the wake-up bell to get everyone up and ready. The forest was waiting, and he could barely contain his excitement.
