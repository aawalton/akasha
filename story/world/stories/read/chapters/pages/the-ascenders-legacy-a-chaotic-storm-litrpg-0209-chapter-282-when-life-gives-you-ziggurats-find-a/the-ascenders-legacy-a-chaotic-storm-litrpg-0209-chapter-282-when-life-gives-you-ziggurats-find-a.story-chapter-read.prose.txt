Ziggurat Basin,

Sector 7, Sunstone.

Aftermath of the collision between the Alliance, and the Runes.

The air was thick with disbelief. Moments ago, the Alliance had come face-to-face with the Rune family—people who, according to Ambassador Erik Morozov, should have been hundreds of leagues away, deep within Attilan.

Yet here they stood, in the very corridor where the Alliance had expected to find nothing but cultists. Suspicion and unease clung to the air, each glance heavy with silent accusation. The tension stretched, unbroken as the seconds dragged on without any suitable explanation—until Ambassador Hakim’s voice finally shattered it.

“Can someone just explain to me what the hell is going on?” he shouted, his voice resounding against the walls of the third level. “Why do we have bloody Runes in the fucking Ziggurat?”

“Mind the way you address my family, Ambassador,” Damien Rune snapped, lilac essence churning threateningly around him. “We are the Runes, for winter’s sake—not some riffraff from your sand-ridden kingdom.”

But Ambassador Hakim wasn’t cowed. If anything, his snarl deepened. “I do not fear you or your family, Damien. Your damned influence ends in Unoros!”

“I doubt all this swearing is necessary!” General Fang cut in sharply, but his attempt at mediation only stoked the fire.

“Shut up, Fang!” Hakim barked. “It’s because of you and your ancestors that we’re in this mess anyway!”

General Fang snarled. “How dare you—”

“And what are you going to do about it?” Hakim stepped forward. “Oh, I wish you would do something. I wish you’d fucking push me—”

“Can we all just calm down?” Ambassador Morozov shouted, moving quickly to stand between them. “We are men of status, for Ascendant’s sake!”

Hakim hissed in fury, while Fang glared darkly at him. Before either could speak again, General Lucas intervened, his voice cutting through the chaos like a bucket of ice-cold water over a burning pyre.

“Pull yourselves together, Ambassadors. There’s too much at stake for us to be bickering like this—especially given the situation.” He turned to Morozov, his tone cooling. “What we need now are answers.”

Ambassador Morozov swallowed hard.

“Like how the Runes are here in the Ziggurat,” Lucas continued. “This mission was meant to be confidential—a fact known only to the Alliance. So how, exactly, are they informed?”

“I…” Morozov stuttered, wiping sweat from his brow.

“Perhaps I should rephrase that,” Lucas said, stepping forward. His gaze hardened. “Who told the Runes about the anchors? How do they even know the location?” He turned his attention to Damien Rune. “Are you working with the cultists?”

“We do not work with cultists,” Damien replied at once, a glare on his face. His eyes flicked toward Morozov—whose own widened slightly in panic. Leaking Alliance secrets could cost him his position as an ambassador, and to confirm it now was to set his career on fire—literally.

Yet, after a brief hesitation, Damien decided to throw him under the bus, choosing brutal honesty over reputational slander. “Morozov informed us of the anchors in Attilan. We decided to pursue it while the Alliance dealt with this one.”

“And why would you do that?” Hakim demanded. “What vested interest do you have in this matter?”

Damien grimaced, readying a carefully crafted speech about unity and shared goals, but Morozov cut him off, his voice laced with bitter resentment.

“The eighth son of Xylixis Rune was kidnapped by cultists.”

It was payback—and the shocked gasps that rippled through the hallway were all the satisfaction Morozov needed.

Damien glared daggers at him, teeth bared in fury, but the ambassador pressed on—his revenge already in motion.

“The Runes wanted to keep the matter quiet. They contacted me and paid me—”

“You mean bribed,” General Fang interrupted dryly, and with a grimace, Ambassador Morozov amended his statement.

“Fine. They bribed me to provide information. I told them about the anchor in Attilan, and they informed me they’d be going there.” His glare found Damien again. “I have no idea what they’re doing here.”

“We are not in Sunstone, you dimwit!” Damien shot back, turning his fury on Lucas. “The Alliance should strip this man of his post immediately.”

“We don’t have time for that,” General Lucas snapped. “There’s a far greater problem before us. You were supposedly in Attilan, and we entered from Sunstone—yet here we stand, in the same hallway. The real question is, “Where is here?”

A thick silence followed. Eyes darted, uncertainty tightening every breath—until a voice, quiet yet trembling with realization, broke it. It was Pythia Fibonacci, the lead researcher who had led them into the ziggurat, and like one struck with an epiphany, the woman gasped sharply, her body going rigid. Her eyes rolled to the back of her head, showing only whites for a moment before suddenly snapping back to normal.

“What is it, Pythia?” Ambassador Morozov asked, walking toward the shaken woman with a concerned frown. “What mathematical deductions were you able to make?”

“We were wrong,” Pythia breathed, eyes wide as she scanned the ceiling, lips moving in quick, silent calculations. “This… the ziggurat isn’t just a religious monument—or some cultist artifact. It’s a passageway… a bridge between Sunstone and Attilan.”

She glanced around and pointed at Aldric. “The Empath hinted at it earlier—Attilan soldiers walking through this same corridor, a gold-cloaked figure seen here—it all aligns with the Lyssarian Theory of quantum space and reality melding.”

“Normal words, please.” Ambassador Hakim grimaced. “So, we can understand you.”

Pythia blinked, as if realizing how fast she’d been speaking, then straightened. A flush rose to her cheeks, but her excitement burned through her embarrassment.

“Of course,” she said, her tone steadier now, eyes still shining. “The Lyssarian Theory suggests that space isn’t fixed—it can be folded or braided together by harmonic runic patterns. If the runes in the Ziggurat have been perfectly tuned, they could create a stable overlap between two distant points in reality. A bridge where both exist simultaneously.”

“So, you’re saying,” General Lucas said slowly, “that this corridor isn’t in Sunstone or Attilan… but both?”

“Precisely,” Pythia replied. “We are standing inside an intersection of realities—a spatial braid. If the theory holds true, then the Ziggurat isn’t a structure built to reach anything at all. It is the thing—a Way.”

Gasps and murmurs erupted immediately. Eyes widened, and people exchanged awed glances. Ways were no new discovery in Lutia. They were used in stories all the time and believed to have existed thousands of years ago. Many people have tried to recreate them, but just like dual cores, they were the holy grail of magical theory.

Many had tried to recrate them, but no one had succeeded.

Yet here they stood—inside one, ancient and functional, born of Sunstonian hands.

Uneasy glances shifted toward the Sunstonians, many of whom shuffled awkwardly, confronted undeniably by the evidence of their ancestor’s treachery. Anxious to redirect the attention away from his people, General Fang interjected. “How does this tie to the cultists? They’re the ones we came for.”

“I’m not certain,” Pythia admitted, frowning. “But I suspect the cultists created a reality junction, grafting themselves into the Way—anchoring their existence to ours while remaining partially outside it.”

Warp’s eyes widened as realization dawned, and she began looking around the ziggurat with new eyes. Even Artemis was doing the same. As a spatial awakened, the way was a concept he was greatly interested in, and to see it with his naked eye… just amazing.

But first, the cultists.

Artemis spread his senses through the Way, tracing its lattice, searching for spatial anomalies—yet found nothing. The Way was truly above mere manipulation of space.

He turned to speak, but Warp’s voice cut through his thoughts.

“If your theory about the junction holds, then somewhere in the Ziggurat there must be a seam—a graft point.” Her gaze slid to Aldric, but she didn’t seem to be seeing him. “Somewhere below us. The empath saw images of cultists marching here.”

“That makes sense,” Nayra joined in, her hatred for Sunstonians forgotten for the moment. “During our resonance readings, we thought the Attilan anchor was stronger—but if it’s the same one, that just means it’s closer to Attilan’s side.”

She turned respectfully to Damien Rune. “How many floors have you climbed, sir?”

“Three,” Damien replied. “This is our fourth.”

“Then this is the middle hall,” General Lucas concluded grimly. “Their stronghold must be below us. What we need now is to locate the graft points.”

“Not one point,” Pythia corrected softly. “Multiple. The cultists’ base should be closer to Attilan—but if there’s an anchor in Sunstone, there’s another above us. Likely more.”

Pythia’s words settled on them all like a heavy blanket, and Ambassador Hakim cursed. “Damn! No wonder they managed to survive so long. They look so weak on the outside that we’ve all been tricked into underestimating them. We looked at them as fools and weaklings, but apparently, they’ve been playing us all along.”

“That’s what happens,” General Lucas said quietly, “when we act as divided kingdoms instead of one. Lutia’s strength has always been unity. That is something we all know.”

At that, a subtle ripple of awkwardness churned through the ambassadors and generals. Even Damien Rune wasn’t left out, but before Aldric could do more than sense that General Lucas’s words carried more weight than what it sounded like on the surface, Ambassador Morozov spoke.

“Now that we know the way forward. There’s no need to dally.” He glanced at General Lucas. “What’s the plan?”

General Lucas sighed. “We need to completely reassess our approach to dealing with the cultists when we eventually find them, but for now, the first order of business to find the spatial scars where their grafts are located. We’ll need to split up, half of us to go up, and the other half down. Everyone with spatial, reality, resonance, or runic expertise is needed in both teams to make sure we do not miss the graft.”

His gaze hardened. “Please, if you find a graft, please do not attempt to open it or even touch it. We do not want to alert the cultists that we are onto them.” His gaze landed on the Runes, his piercing stare driving his words home. “Is that clear?”

“Yes,” the Alliance chorused in agreement, and though Damien grimaced, he nodded as well.

“My people and I will be patient. We also have underestimated the cultists greatly. It will not do to enter their den so casually after all the effort it has taken us to come this far.”

“Good,” General Lucas nodded. “Now Ambassadors, if you will please split your entourage into two well equipped groups so we can proceed.”

The next few minutes passed in a blur for Aldric as half the alliance—led by Damien Rune, Ambassador Morozov, and General Fang—headed back up to the second level of the ziggurat, while the rest descended to the fourth level—or, as Pythia Fibonacci called it, the third from Attilan.

While Artemis, Warp, and even Geneva had joined the upper team, Aldric’s group still boasted formidable strength. Two powerful Runes, Nayra, Seretta Sorrows, Luminus, Zero, Pythia Fibonacci, and a host of other capable figures stood ready.

They gathered before the entrance to the fourth level, General Lucas and Ambassador Hakim taking point. Aldric stood directly behind Lucas, Tyrus at his right and Pythia at his left, his pulse pounding in his chest.

Finally, after so much political dilly-dallying, they were making progress.

“Are you all ready?” General Lucas asked. When the group answered in the affirmative, he pushed open the heavy metal doors, their hinges groaning to reveal an expansive hallway etched densely with null and sound-trapping runes.

The symbols shimmered silver and blue, casting an eerie light across the dark corridor.

“The runes make it difficult to sense or hear anything from the upper levels,” one of the Runes explained, his tone measured. “I’m not sure what benefit that offers the cultists—or whoever built this place—but I’d wager they had ambushes in mind.”

“Or containment,” Pythia added with a frown as the group spread out, descending carefully into the hallway to improve their field of vision.

General Lucas nodded, and turned his gaze to Aldric. “Anything?”

Aldric placed a hand on a rune-covered wall, bracing himself for a barrage of images; instead, he saw nothing. Not one image, even though emotions hung thickly in the air.

“Nothing, sir.” He replied as he pulled his hands from the wall. “I can still sense the blind fanaticism of the cultists still lingering in the air, but it isn’t quite as strong as that of the third level.”

“Perhaps the runes interfere,” Ambassador Hakim murmured.

“Perhaps,” Nayra agreed. “Or it’s simply because this level hasn’t seen activity in a long time.”

She pointed to the dust-covered floor and the dense number of cobwebs, which were nearly twice the amount in the third level. “This level hasn’t been used in a very long time. Even the hinges of the entrance creaks.”

“Which means the cultists barely use this level,” Seretta Sorrows surmised, her voice clipped as she wiped dust from her gloves. “If that’s the case, their spatial graft won’t be here.”

General Lucas frowned. “That’s a possible theory, but I suggest we check thoroughly. Perhaps we might get an explanation for why the cultists avoid this level. Every bit of intel we can get on them is important.”

They all nodded and began searching. Aldric took the assignment personally; it was his brothers who were missing after all.

So much had come up during this whole investigation—first the ziggurat, then realizing it was a way instead, confirming sunstone’s age-old betrayal, and then Ambassador Morozov’s betrayal of alliance secrets.

But amidst all that drama, Aldric hadn’t forgotten the main reason he was here. He was here to find his brothers, and he wouldn’t rest until he did.

He walked down the hallway and back, eyes scanning the air for anything suspicious. He wished he could read runes like Aodhán could so he could at least have an idea what they did.

He didn’t let that discourage him, though.

However, after nearly an hour of futile searching, they finally concluded that there was nothing to see of importance in the 4th level and decided to move down to the next.

The 5th level, unlike the 4th, was covered in golden and purple runes, and the moment Aldric stepped into it, he was immediately hit with a river of emotions so potent and strong they couldn’t have been imprinted more than a few hours in age.

Fear. Excitement. Relief. Overwhelming relief.

The emotions hung thickly in the air like a cloud of doom, so heavy that Aldric felt his body grow ice cold in response.

He staggered, drawing everyone’s attention at once. His hands braced against the rune covered wall. Unseen bonds activated and a cascade of images slammed into his mind at once—far more than what he’s experienced in the 3rd level.

The images flashed across his mind, too fast for him to grasp, all showing variations of one thing.

Portals. So many portals, and even more people emerging from within them—all from different parts of Lutia, all powerful.

Leveraging his willpower, Aldric’s mind latched onto one of the flashing images, and it stilled, playing out in his mind like a slideshow of black and white images.

Like all the other images, it began with a portal that opened directly where he was standing. Two people emerged from the portal, their fear so thick it was choking. A tide of relief poured out of them when the portal snapped shut, and without hesitation, they raced forward towards the end of the hallway, where they disappeared into another portal.

The vision ended, and another began.

The same sequence of events, just different people.

Aldric deactivated unseen bonds after the third vision, panting as a significant amount of willpower was drained from his core. A wave of dizziness slammed into him, and he staggered once again, the weight of his power nearly too much for his feeble mundane frame.

Tyrus caught him before he could fall, asking urgently. “What did you see, Aldric?”

But Aldric didn’t answer. He couldn’t answer. Instead, he stumbled through the crowd of expectant faces, following the exact path those in the vision had taken.

Footsteps echoed behind him as his group followed, but Aldric barely noticed, his gaze fixed on the exact point on the wall where the second portal had appeared. When he reached the end of the hallway, he turned to the right wall and looked up, but there was nothing there—just an empty stretch of rune-covered stone, shimmering a beautiful burnished gold color.

But Aldric refused to be fooled.

Unleashing a burst of willpower into the air, he placed his right hand against the wall and activated unseen bonds once again. This time the images only showed one scene.

A portal bursting to life.

Hundreds—thousands of them—flashed through his mind, some dating back hundreds of years, others recent. Very recent.

The images cut off with a burst of essence, and where his hands had once been, now stood a shimmering knot of golden threads, tightly woven, pulsing faintly like a wound in reality.

Aldric raised a hand to the knot, but before he could touch it, General Lucas dragged him back. “Don’t touch it. We cannot afford to alert the cultists to our presence just yet.”

He glanced at Nayra. “Is that the anchor?”

Nayra nodded silently, shocked at how easily Aldric had found it. “Yes, it is. That’s the spatial graft Miss Fibonacci was talking about earlier. Opening the graft should lead us directly to the cultists.”

Pythia nodded in agreement and glanced at Aldric. “That’s a marvelous ability you’ve got, sir. I doubt we would have found the graft this quickly without you.”

Aldric nodded in thanks, but his mind was focused on something more important. He had found the cultist’s hideout. Aodhán and Daruk were finally within reach. He could feel it. finally, he had some good news to give his parents.

A faint smile broke through his exhaustion as he asked. “When do we attack, Sir?”

General Lucas glared at the spatial graft. “As soon as we get the army of the four kingdoms here. An hour or two at least.” He smiled at Aldric. “You rest; you’ll need your strength for what’s coming next. The ambassadors and I need to get an army together.”

Ambassador Hakim turned to Seretta. “Stay here. Make sure no one touches that thing. We can’t risk alerting them. We’ve underestimated these cultists long enough.”

Seretta nodded sharply. The ambassadors dispersed, already barking orders through their comms as they began mobilizing an army.

Aldric watched them leave, his deviant skill humming within him.

It was time he became the Tyrant of Boundless Empathy.
