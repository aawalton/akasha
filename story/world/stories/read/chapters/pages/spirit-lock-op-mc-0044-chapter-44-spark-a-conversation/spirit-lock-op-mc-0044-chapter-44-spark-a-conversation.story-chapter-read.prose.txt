Sairan was at a terrifying impasse. He was running at half mast, at least by Iron Bones' standards, but that “half” was eating all his power. If he wanted to go all out, he’d run out of steam in five minutes. But could he win in five minutes with this half-cocked performance?

Sairan watched the drone fly around him nervously. He could lie to himself—but not others.

No. He wouldn’t.

Sairan was getting old, and Hayden was at his prime. Sairan would lose, but if he went all out, he would at least say that he won the “race.” Or at least people would scream, “What a beast performance,” or whatever the kids were saying these days. He could then stop and laugh, and say, “Good race,” ending it on a good note.

Hayden appeared ambivalent, but Sairan could feel his mutual respect. There was no way Hayden would keep running. Not after the way he started their first conversation. No, Hayden had values. He was also smart. He was on video—that pressure would make him consider his words when Sairan asked Hayden to join them. With any luck, it would goad him into saying yes on the spot! Or at least agree to meet the Blind Monks. This was good, very good.

It was time to make a powerful ending.

Sairan used the full extent of his spirit.

His legs bulged.

His bones burned.

His arms webbed with veins—

And then he rocketed from his position.

According to Mayle, Hayden had a pure spirit beast and the Clear Line spirit. There was no way he could compete.

Or so Sairan thought.

Hayden’s core lit up, and he launched into a terrifying sprint, catching up in the blink of an eye.

I thought he had the Clear Line spirit! Sairan thought. He used the Divine Eyes spirit to study Hayden behind him, as the spirit provided omnidirectional sight. Once again, he confirmed that Hayden’s main gate wasn’t open, and he wasn’t using either spirit. That said, Hayden had lowered his suppression technique, and his body was shining with gold. Is his infusion seriously that good?

As he considered it, he passed a group of screaming spectators at the halfway mark. Sairan was nervous, but when he saw a woman flash him her sky-blue bra, he laughed. He felt young again, in his prime, competing against the universe’s best. Suddenly, Sairan stopped thinking about business and started running to win. He gave it everything, breathing hot lead, listening to the sharp wind burning his face.

“They’ve hit 86!”

“86 what?”

“Kilometers an hour!”

“In miles, Pikey!”

“Uh… give me a second… 53! Fifty frickin’ three miles an hour. They’re pounding!”

“There’s no way they’re not using spirits!”

“Yeah, but who cares?”

-

No one would’ve believed that Hayden wasn’t using a spirit, and that he could’ve gone far faster if he used his Clear Line as an amplifier. That said, he felt great satisfaction in taking his body to the limit—and pushed it to the edge on the last stretch.

-

Sairan watched in disbelief as Hayden passed by him. He was sweating heavily, and his breath was ragged, but he was winning and could’ve perhaps pulled further on, but he was letting Sairan share in the victory. Hayden was considerate, but he wasn’t gracious enough to let him win.

Hayden was running to win.

And so would he.

Sairan gave one last push and passed Hayden, but Hayden retook the lead at the last second.

The hotel’s crowd size had reached its zenith, and they boomed with thundering applause as the two reached the end. Both took extra strides, circling the area before stopping, gripping their knees, and dry-heaving. Both were exhausted—but Sairan thought his heart might go out.

He coughed and looked to the sky, hair glistening in the radiant sunshine. He gave it everything. That’s all that mattered.

The crowd surrounded him like they used to in the old days. The compliments came in a flurry, but there was one he’d never forget.

“Damn, I thought that guy was unbeatable, but you almost crushed him.”

Sairan looked at Hayden and saw women swarming around him in fun shirts.

“Hotter Every Day.”

“Hugs for Millions.”

“Hugs for Me.“

Hayden was a nobody six months ago, but now he was a celebrity at a school for rich elites. What a phenom.

Suddenly, Hayden offered Sairan his hand. “What a hell of a race.”

A celebration bloomed when Sairan accepted the handshake. The drone circled them, giving Sairan the thrill he experienced when he was young.

Now, it was time to convince Hayden to join the team.

“Hayden, right?” Sairan asked.

“That’s correct.” Hayden slicked his fingers through his hair. “And you are?”

“My name’s Sairan Ikol. I’m a universal blindfold boxing semifinalist, and the current head of the Blind Monks, a universal blindfold boxing team. I came here to personally invite you to join the Monks.”

The crowd fell silent, charged with anticipation for what was doubtless a historical moment.

It was. Hayden gave the most iconic answer in history, so far as Sairan was concerned. It was so monumental that Sairan found himself binge-drinking at dinner, losing his damn mind as his past student, Tiasha, gave him a dozen soft-sell variations of, “Like I said, he’s stubborn.”

Sairan didn’t need the pep talk. He would never forget the way that half of the audience grimaced when Sairan made the offer. It was as if they believed that Hayden would do what he always did—

Reject monumental offers.

“We just don't know what he wants,” Tiasha said, downing the rest of her drink. She lifted her fingers to order another, then motioned for two drinks with her fingers.

“It doesn't matter what he wants,” Sairan said. “It doesn't even matter what he thinks he wants. It's what he's doing. He's moving in one direction, and nothing's going to break that concentration but drugs or women. How's his love life?”

She laughed bitterly.

He scoffed. “Just speak.”

“I'm just laughing because he turned down the richest, strongest, most influential girl in the school.”

He frowned. “Turned her down? As in… she asked him out?”

“In public. On recording. First day. There’s even whispers that he’s sleeping with Mayle. Surprisingly enough, the rumors have convincing evidence.”

Sairan lifted his eyebrows and dropped them with a sardonic expression. “That’s… impressive.” He rubbed his forehead. “That might be why he’s insistent on staying here.”

“I doubt it. He’s a smart kid, so if he were just worried about his life, he’d cozy up with the strongest backer he could. But he’s not. If I had to guess, he’s actually here for the rift.”

“Right…” Sairan swished the wine in his glass, remembering the way Hayden’s entire body glowed with cora. That was unnatural. Severely unnatural. Freakish, even.

“Okay… let’s just… play make-believe…” he said after a pause. “We’ll say you’re right—Hayden wants something from that rift. He’s from the future and knows its opening, so he’s waiting. Then what? He still can’t leave this planet without universal contacts or military protection.”

“Are you asking if he’ll join us then?”

Tiasha lowered her wine glass to the table. “Maybe… He said that his future’s in rifting—what if we use our traveling to give him rift access? We’re traveling to twenty galaxies.”

Sairan laughed. “And threaten his life before the fold?”

“It’s better than not having him,” she argued. “Plus… he just needs to teach us, right? It’d be worth doing it just for that.”

He pondered it. “That’s true… Do you think he’ll be receptive to an offer?”

“If we can approach him, yeah. That said, he shoots down people before they even talk to him. We’ll need a conversation starter.”

Sairan stared her in the eyes. “Do you have a conversation starter?”

Tiasha grimaced. “Maybe. I have a girl that’s pretty, talented, and respectable—and completely socially inept. She’s a bit abrasive at times, and she walks around in a cloak and blindfold like an actual monk. If we can get her in normal clothing, it’ll spark a conversation—if only for him to chastise her.”

“But?” he asked dryly.

“But… she wouldn’t do it for a million credits. And that’s literal. If you offered her a million credits to wear something sexy in public tomorrow, she wouldn't do it.”

Sairan scoffed. “Just say what she wants.”

Tiasha swallowed. “An internship with the Blind Monks. If she gets Hayden in, you’ll give her the ability to train with the Monks. If she can keep up, you’ll add her in a future fold. Do that, and she’ll do anything to get him onboard.”

He thought through it and nodded. “If she succeeds. I won’t give her anything for trying.”

Tiasha smiled. “She doesn’t accept participation awards.”
