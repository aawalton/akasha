After the question, the person behind the door went silent. Sakura exchanged a glance with Gilbert. He shook his head almost imperceptibly, warning her not to answer right away. Lir stood a little behind them, still pale after the long road and the conversation about his father.

"I asked a question," the stranger inside reminded them. "Who brought the Obsidian Hierophant here?"

"And why did you decide she was here?" Gilbert asked, though he understood the question was stupid.

Something tapped softly behind the door, as if someone had set down a cup.

"Because I have not completely lost my mind yet."

Sakura frowned. Pampli was far enough away, the mark on her hand was tightly hidden under her clothes, and even the water here was silent.

"Have you seen us before?" she asked.

"No."

"Then how do you know?"

This time, the pause stretched longer.

"Come in, and perhaps I will answer at least one of your questions. Or you can continue standing on the threshold and talking to my door. As you wish."

A bolt clicked, and the door opened inward. Behind it stood a thin elderly man in a long gray shirt with a dark wool vest worn over it. His hair had almost completely turned gray and fell to his shoulders. His face was dry, with deep creases near his mouth, and a wide pale bandage covered his eyes.

Blind.

Sakura did not immediately understand why that surprised her more than the question about the Hierophant itself.

The man turned his face exactly in her direction.

"There she is."

Gilbert tensed, and Lir asked in surprise:

"You’re blind, and you know exactly where she’s standing?"

"I am blind, not deaf. My ears are my eyes now."

He stepped aside slightly, freeing the passage.

"Ater. If you absolutely need a name before deciding whether I am dangerous."

"Are you dangerous?" Sakura asked.

"Are you seriously going to consider an old man dangerous?"

No one smiled at those words. Sakura chose not to answer. Instead, she automatically looked at him more closely, the same way she had already done with creatures, objects, and people she did not trust.

The world obediently formed the information before her:

Ater

Race: Human

The name and race matched, but Sakura did not feel any calmer because of it. The System did not show whether a person was lying, whether he intended to stab her in the back, or whether the bandage over his eyes really meant blindness. For that, she still had to use her own head, which seemed especially unfair after the long road.

Inside, the station turned out to be much cozier than the previous bathhouse. The first room served as a kitchen, dining room, and workshop all at once, and firewood crackled in the stone stove. Bundles of herbs dried on ropes under the ceiling, and wooden shelves with jars, clay vessels, and scrolls hung on the wall. A whole pile of scrolls. Interesting. Why would a blind man need scrolls? Deeper inside the house, there was the constant sound of water.

Ater closed the door behind them and uncertainly ran a hand along the wall. Sakura had just thought he would start moving through the house while holding on to the wall, but the old man walked around a stool without hesitating, even though Lir had accidentally shifted it out of place only a second earlier.

"You can sense magic. That is not exactly a modest ability for a frail old man," Gilbert said.

Ater stopped.

"I do sense it sometimes, yes. But in my case, it is an almost useless gift," the old man said and walked to the table. "Sit down. You are all tired."

"We did not say we were staying," Gilbert noted.

"Then stand or leave. I do not care."

Sakura gave the mage a look, and Gilbert returned it. Neither of them trusted the old man. They simply expressed it differently: Sakura wanted to ask questions, while Gilbert seemed to prefer searching the whole house first.

Lir sat down first. After a short pause, Sakura followed his example, and Gilbert remained standing.

"Do you think better on your feet?" Ater asked.

"I am more comfortable this way, if you must know."

The old man moved his palm over the empty edge of the table, as if checking where there was free space. Then the air beside his fingers trembled, and he pulled out a wooden bowl with bread. After that, Ater took several small round cheeses and a clay cup of salt from [Inventory]. He laid out the food slowly, but not once did he miss the table or a plate.

Sakura watched his hands more carefully. For a blind old man, he handled things too confidently. Or he simply knew his own house well. Or both at once.

"Do you live here alone?" Sakura asked.

"Right now, yes."

"And before?"

"Before, I was younger. I had friends, family, students. But I do not want to talk about that. My past remains in the past. And now there is only the present. Until death itself."

Sakura tensed at Ater’s words, but did not show it. She watched him closely, thinking about what to ask next.

"All right. Then a question you might want to answer. How do you know about Obsidian Hierophants?"

Ater froze for a moment.

"I have lived long enough."

"Gilbert has studied magic long enough and still knows less about them than he would like."

"He is a green boy who studied in the wrong places and studied the wrong things."

Gilbert slowly crossed his arms over his chest.

"And what exactly should I have studied?"

Ater turned toward him.

"Young mages always believe knowledge is supposed to sit on a shelf and wait until they read the right page."

"I am not that young."

"To me, everyone who argues with strange old men is young."

Sakura almost smiled, but remembered in time that she wanted answers.

"I hear the elements," she said.

This time, Ater changed more noticeably. His fingers, which had been resting on the edge of the table, tightened slightly.

"Which ones?"

"Wind. Now water."

"Already two..."

"Do you know why?"

"Possibly."

"And? Tell me. I need answers."

"You are in too much of a hurry."

"There is a man following me who destroys everything in his path. I think I have reasons to hurry."

Ater was silent.

"I heard about Crowlis," Sakura continued. "Gilbert said he heard the elements too. He was an Obsidian Hierophant. The first one."

The old man turned away toward the stove, and now Sakura no longer doubted that the name meant something to him.

"You know about him?"

"Everyone knows legends."

"I am not talking about legends right now."

"And I am not obligated to tell you everything I know."

"Then why did you let us in at all?"

Ater slowly turned back.

"Because the answers you are chasing across half the world are literally right in front of your nose."

Sakura involuntarily touched her face.

"Right in front of my nose, there is only bread."

"Then start with the bread. People understand the obvious especially badly on an empty stomach."

Lir said quietly:

"I think he won’t say it directly."

"Thank you, Lir. I was starting to suspect that."

"And you are suspecting correctly," Ater said.

Gilbert finally approached the table, but did not sit down.

"Did you renounce magic? There are no active sources in the house, but you sense magic too well for someone who has never used it. And I cannot read your aura."

The old man turned his head toward him and stayed silent for several seconds.

"I was indeed a mage."

"What kind?"

"That is the second question I do not want to answer."

"Why did you leave that path?"

The old man lowered his head.

"Because one day, I realized strong magic convinces a person far too easily that power gives him the right to decide what should exist and what should not."

Sakura looked at him more closely.

"Are you afraid of magic?"

"Destructive magic. Yes."

"And that is why you invited an Obsidian Hierophant into your home?"

"I did not say I considered it a reasonable decision. But fear and necessity sometimes live at the same table."

He reached for one of the clay pitchers.

"You need rest."

"We already rested," Gilbert answered.

"No. You walked for a long time. I can feel not only magic, but also your exhaustion."

Ater took four small cups from the shelf and placed three on the table. He left the fourth near himself. Then he poured a dark brew from the pitcher. It smelled herbal, a little sweet, but with a bitterness that reminded Sakura of wet bark.

Gilbert immediately pushed the nearest cup away.

"No."

Ater shrugged.

"You think I want to poison you? There would be reason for that... and profit. I could turn you over to the enforcers."

Sakura frowned, looking at the pitcher.

"What is in it?"

"Blue verbena, holly root, sleepberry, silverwood bark, and water from the lower spring."

Gilbert looked at Sakura.

"I like the sleepberry and the last ingredient least of all."

"I do not wish you harm," Ater said. "You will either have to trust me to get answers, or not. The choice is yours."

The old man calmly took a sip from his own cup.

"I drank first."

"That proves nothing," Gilbert said. "You may have immunity. Or an antidote. Or you put something only in our cups."

"You are a very exhausting person."

"That is why I am still alive."

"If you are that distrustful, we can trade cups," the old man sighed, tired and irritated.

Gilbert did not refuse. He gave Ater his cup and took the old man’s. Ater took another sip, now from the new vessel.

Lir picked up a cup and cautiously sniffed it.

"If he wanted to kill us, he could have simply not opened the door."

"People are capable of killing in far more complicated and refined ways than simply not opening the door," Gilbert answered.

"He definitely knows something," Sakura said. "And if he can give us answers, we can’t just leave."

Gilbert looked at her as if he already knew how the argument would end and was displeased with it in advance.

"Do you hear how you sound right now?"

"Yes."

"Good. Then the problem runs deeper."

Sakura took the cup. Ater sat across from her, completely calm.

"What will happen if we drink?"

"Perhaps you will see what you have been trying to see for a long time. Some doors open only to those who can notice them."

"Another riddle."

"Would you prefer poison?"

"No!"

"Then be glad it is a riddle."

Lir looked at Sakura.

"I’ll try first."

Gilbert sharply turned his gaze to him.

"No."

"Why?"

"Because you just got out of trouble and could barely walk today. An unknown brew from an unknown mage who claims he is no longer a mage is not on the list of things that are good for you right now."

"But... I’m the least important member of your team, and..." Lir began.

"Don’t say that," Sakura cut him off sharply.

She did not like what Lir had called himself. Lir chewed his lip and then said it differently:

"What if this is the only way to understand what is happening to Sakura?"

"Then Sakura drinks."

"Gilbert is right," she said. "Besides, we have insurance."

Sakura looked at the old blind man.

"An obsidian beast is waiting outside the house. If something happens to us... he understands things very well. And he knows how to take revenge."

"I do believe no one has threatened me with an obsidian monster before," the old man said. If he had not been wearing a bandage, he probably would have rolled his eyes.

Sakura narrowed her eyes, looking at him. The old man really did not look like a treacherous enemy. He was difficult, but honest.

"All right. We’ll drink."

Lir did not put his cup back either.

"If you both drink, I drink too," he said stubbornly.

"Wonderful idea," Gilbert said with sarcasm.

"But if something happens, it happens to all of us."

Gilbert closed his eyes for a couple of seconds.

"Incredible logic."

"I like it," Sakura said.

"Who would doubt that?"

In the end, Gilbert took the last cup.

"If we die," he told Ater, "I will find a way to come back and ruin the rest of your old age."

"Then I will finally have entertainment."

Sakura took the first sip, and the brew was bitter. Then came sweetness, much too strong. Last came cold, which quickly passed from her tongue to her throat and then farther down her chest. She grimaced.

"This is disgusting."

"Medicine rarely tries to be pleasant," Ater answered.

"You did not say it was medicine."

"And I did not say it was not medicine."

Lir drank more slowly. Gilbert only took one sip at first, waited as if expecting to identify poison by the sensation alone, and only then finished the rest.

For the first few seconds, nothing happened. Sakura was already about to ask how long they had to wait when the sounds around them began to change. The noise of water under the floor grew louder, and the crackle of firewood stretched into a low hum.

The room before Sakura’s eyes blurred for a moment, then became clear again.

"Gilbert..."

"Yes, I feel it too."

He grabbed the edge of the table, and Lir turned pale.

"What’s happening to me?"

Sakura tried to stand, but her legs seemed to stop belonging to her. The world slowly tilted. She managed to see Ater, who was still sitting calmly across from them.

"What did you give us?"

"Answers. But they are not always pleasant."

The table vanished from under her hands. The last thing Sakura heard before she fell into darkness was Ater’s voice.

"Look carefully. The past rarely opens the door twice."

Sakura opened her eyes and realized she was standing. That was the first strange feeling. The second strange thing was that Gilbert and Lir were standing beside her, and both looked just as confused.

"Where are we?" Lir asked.

Sakura slowly turned around. Walls of black obsidian rose before them, nothing like ruins.

The castle was whole.

Tall spires reached into the dark blue sky, and scarlet veins ran along the smooth black walls. Lights burned between the towers, and dozens of people in long robes moved across the wide bridge ahead. A huge family crest hung above the gates.

Gilbert turned pale.

"It can’t be."

"What?" Sakura asked.

He was looking at the castle as if he had seen a ghost.

"This is Crowlis’s fortress."

Sakura raised her eyes again to the completely whole obsidian towers, and then the gates swung open.

A tall man in black walked out of the castle, and the people around him bowed their heads one after another.

Crowlis had not fallen yet.
