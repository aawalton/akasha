The Queen barely kept her expression disciplined, but Adon could see the telltale signs of annoyance in her face.

A little twitch in the corner of her lip, a subtle tightening around the jaw, an eyebrow that moved a millimeter out of its usual position.

When did I learn her facial expressions so well? the butterfly asked himself. It was nice, seeing that he understood the Queen better than he had realized, but it was also a little strange. He had never prided himself on reading social cues. Now that he’d gained the ability to read minds with Telepathy, it was not the sort of skill he would ever need to cultivate in this lifetime.

Yet he seemed to have learned Rosslyn’s countenance and all its many variations by heart. He felt a funny sensation inside himself as he thought of that, then shoved it down as the Queen verbalized her reaction, clearly expecting him to read her mind.

He is thinking about that, huh? Rosslyn thought. Who I will marry. Who will get closer to the center of power in my country. The center of power that does not even truly exist right now… Truthfully, I grow so tired of those games. They seem especially ridiculous now, but I have been tiring of them for longer than I can say. Is that truly all that we as a country have to offer to an ally? A chance to get your heirs closer to our throne? Perhaps this is why the Holy Kingdoms have lost so much ground to the Empire over the centuries… She shook her head lightly and thought a little bit louder. Adon, would it bother you if I acted a bit selfishly?

If you did what, exactly? Adon asked.

She told him, silently, what she intended.

As far as I’m concerned, go for it, he sent with a distinctly pleased note in his tone. The only hesitation I would have is if this bothers you at all.

Rosslyn gave him a little smile. It does not. Not in the slightest. After the last few weeks, I know. It seems very early, and it is. In a better situation, we would take our time. The only question in my mind was whether this will harm my country, but from what you have told me, it will not.

As they had this exchange, the Parmonian ruler answered Rosslyn’s question.

“Of course, Your Majesty, I am here to fulfill my end of the bargain with your father,” King Janus said a little coldly. “The privilege of calling on your allies falls to you, along with the rest of what you have inherited. Just because the fight may be more difficult than was anticipated, it does not obviate our sacred obligations under the mutual defense agreement. That pact was always going to end up benefiting one side asymmetrically, based on the low probability that we would somehow both end up being attacked by our neighbors at once. We simply never knew which one of us would be the beneficiary for certain.”

“Good,” Rosslyn said.

“Defense is not the same as reconquest, however,” he mused. “Not to say that we would not help you recover the capital. It was simply a much less daunting task before it fell. It happened so quickly, too. We rode as soon as we could gather our men.”

He’s telling the truth, Adon sent to Rosslyn only. He doesn’t understand what could have happened. He’s very self-interested, but his reputation as a good ally and military leader matters a lot to him. He was hoping he would save the day.

So were we, Rosslyn thought bitterly.

King Janus’s eyes locked onto Rosslyn’s and widened slightly as if he’d just realized something. “Actually, dear Queen, could you kindly fill me in as to what exactly happened? Now that we have a quiet moment alone, it seems to me an ideal time for us to consider what went wrong in the defense of the siege and how we might mount our own efforts.” There was no trace of blame in his voice, but Rosslyn felt it all the same.

The implication, What did you and your family do wrong? seemed an unmistakable aspect of what he was saying.

He doesn’t mean it that way, Adon sent quickly to Rosslyn only.

The Queen gave him the smallest micro-nod of acknowledgement before she replied to King Janus.

“To begin with, the Empire has some new form of weaponry that renders standard defensive siege tactics irrelevant,” Rosslyn said.

“What?!”

“The normal method of siege defense, for centuries, is to get behind the walls and reinforce them with mana supplied constantly by rotating shifts of your local mage corps,” she said. “It makes them very difficult to breach, far more so than in the pre-magical-defense days.”

King Janus nodded.

“The Empire discovered a stone that saps away mana from any surface it touches, including both the human body and objects imbued with it.”

“Ah. That would be a serious problem for static defense.”

“Yes. It seems to be harmful to the person wielding the stone object, as they also experience mana drain, but the Empire has never had a problem with throwing away soldiers to accomplish their objectives.”

“Does this have anything to do with what happened to your father?” King Janus asked. He frowned at the words he had just spoken. “I apologize for the insensitive question, but I have only had rumors to go by on what has happened recently, and—well, that is, he was known for his impressive mana reserves and physical strength…”

Rosslyn nodded. “Yes, it is my understanding that it has something to do with the outcome of the duel. The Empire used one of these weapons to breach my father’s personal magical defenses at close range. Later, once he was dead, they used a larger scale set of objects to drain the city walls of defensive power. That created the opening the Empire needed to shatter our defenses and then overwhelm us with numbers.”

“Would the mana drain not reduce the fight to, um, solid walls versus non-mana-imbued weapons?” the King asked slowly. “If the Empire is using a weapon that drains mana from whatever is in its proximity…?”

“No, because the effect occurs at a relatively short range, and it takes time to drain the energy. Seconds, but still. That is time. The longer the stone of the weapon is in contact with the object, the more mana is drained. If you apply mana to a battering ram, it would still hit with great force even when their other weapon is touching the city walls, draining mana from them. The mana-draining stone does not have time to have its effect on the siege weapon before it makes impact and does its damage.”

He swallowed. “That is more than a serious problem, it is disastrous…”

“It was disastrous,” Rosslyn said.

“So, that is why the city fell so suddenly?”

She nodded.

“Novel weapons are a good reason to lose a battle,” he said in a tone that seemed intended to comfort.

“Not good enough to take the sting out of actually losing,” Rosslyn replied, trying unsuccessfully to keep the slight bitterness out of her voice.

“On the bright side, you know a lot about this weapon,” King Janus observed. “You have taken that much away from the siege. We will be prepared for the next encounter.”

“We received intelligence about it before the invasion,” the Queen admitted. “My father only had a few people he could trust, because the city had been infiltrated by Imperial spies, but he worked with his generals and advisors. They could not come up with any adequate counter to the weapon before we were attacked.”

“Do you think your innermost circles were breached? If the Empire knew that you had become aware of this weapon, that would have given them an additional impetus to attack.”

Rosslyn shook her head. “We carefully investigated at all levels and removed several traitors, but none had breached the layer of secrecy where they would know about this. My father was very careful about this information and about preserving the secrecy of the information supplier. It was part of why he was unable to get much advice on this subject. And the same intelligence source that informed us about the weapon mentioned that the Emperor was already thinking about launching an attack sometime soon.”

The King nodded. “Well, I suppose there was not much more that could have been done. On the bright side, I doubt this weapon will be useful to the Empire in defending the city now that they have taken it. Draining mana away is counterproductive to maintaining magically defended walls, as we have established. So, from our side, a recapture of Wayn is just an ordinary siege. Unless they have some other use for this new weapon, or some other additional surprises, that I do not yet know about.”

“Not as far as I am aware,” Rosslyn said. “And I doubt they would have held such a thing in reserve, given that they did not expect people to escape from the city alive to spread the word about their strategies.”

“Hmm.” King Janus sounded doubtful.

“At any rate, we must plan for the weapons and opponents that we know actually do exist, not invent new bogeymen to fear,” the Queen added.

“Right. Well, it sounds as if what we actually need to do is take a leaf out of the Empire’s book. Prepare to conduct a siege of our own as soon as we are able. Increase our numbers. With Your Majesty present, we will be able to raise additional troops everywhere we go. Give open battle in the field when and if we can, as frequently as we are able to do so on conditions favorable to our forces. Bear in mind that the Empire will use their new weapon, but it seems more useful for offense against a fixed objective rather than a fight between two armies.”

Rosslyn nodded along eagerly now.

There’s a ‘but’ coming, Adon sent to her only.

Of course there is, Rosslyn thought.

“But there is only so much that can be done,” the King said. “We may take back Wayn. Possibly, with Your Majesty’s people rallying to our side, we may even take back the rest of Claustria. Still, the Empire will loom large as a challenge into the future…”

“I am aware that it is an ever present threat,” Rosslyn said. “That is why, for the long term security of all the remaining Holy Kingdoms, the Empire must ultimately be destroyed, one way or another.”

“That would be ideal,” the King said. “But it may not be realistic within our lifetimes. Better men and women than us have tried and failed to bring down the Empire.”

“I know it,” Rosslyn said, frowning. “What is your point, King Janus?”

“Perhaps you also need or desire a more permanent arrangement to secure your lands into the future. Something more lasting than this present alliance?”

With his choice of words and what Adon had told her earlier, the Queen knew instantly exactly what King Janus was getting at.

He was trying to leverage the Kingdom’s current weakness into his own family’s or his own Kingdom’s future advantage. He wanted a marriage alliance. Maybe, failing that, he would accept a more concrete alliance agreement of another sort between their two nations. The latter would be more acceptable to Rosslyn, but perhaps the King would not propose it unless he knew he was not getting the former.

In any case, it was time for her to be “selfish,” as she had characterized her plan to Adon.

“For the short term, we are relatively weaker than we had been, with my father dead,” Rosslyn replied.

The King nodded.

“However, for the long term, the strength of the royal bloodline—and therefore the stability of our defenses—has been secured. Our outlook twenty years from now is stronger than our outlook today.”

Janus frowned. “How—?”

“As the King’s last official act before he engaged the Empire’s champion in single combat, you see, my father approved my engagement to our friend Adon.”
