Ethan gasped as the impossibly sharp blade slipped from his middle. Prime’s living armor form rushed to fill the gap, plugging the wound and suturing it shut. Ethan still fell to one knee, holding his side and struggling to keep his eyes focused.

He’d nearly lost his arm recently, but it had been some time since he’d experienced a proper stabbing. He couldn’t recommend it.

Pushing the pain away, Ethan watched Prince Calevaro stumbling backward, and prayed that his friend had been truly awakened from the horrors of whatever the Followers had done to him. If not, there was little else Ethan would be able to do in his current state.

“Where–?” Cal’s face, still covered in inky black seemed to be searching for answers. Finally, he looked down at himself, dropping his weapons and staring at his own hands. “What did… What did they do to me? No. No. No!”

Calevaro began tearing wildly at the oily Familiar coating him, pulling it away, only for it to snap back into place.

“You can do it, Cal,” Ethan whispered.

The normally taciturn prince roared in fury as he kept backing away, his defiance echoing all around them. Yet he seemed to be making little progress. Even when he did manage to pull a small chunk of the liquid monster from him, it would reform a moment later.

“Enough!” Calevaro shouted, and his hands dropped to his side, squeezing into fists. “Kent, I need you! Now more than ever, I need you!”

Ethan raised an eyebrow. Did his friend mean him? In his confusion and pain, did he think his brother was still here somewhere? Ethan hoped not, he doubted he had the mana even to form the illusion of Kentevaro, let alone borrow any of the man’s power.

As Calevaro began to glow, another answer presented itself. He was heating up, from the inside.

As heat began radiating from the man in alarming quantities, Ethan realized what was happening, and felt a grin spread across his face.

Calevaro had named one of his Familiars “Kent”, after his lost brother. His magma Familiar.

The prince glowed hotter and hotter as he channeled with Twilight power, calling upon the magma serpent within him. Soon, the Emperor’s inky black monster was bubbling and writing in pain, expanding outward and popping as it failed to contain the tremendous temperatures.

From the exposed gaps, magma spilled out, until Calevaro was entirely covered in a thick layer of molten rock. Then all at once, it exploded outward.

Revan was there in a burst of his own flame, the explosion and heat washing over him harmlessly, and sheltering Ethan. He disappeared just as quickly, leaving a clear view of Prince Calevaro, hail and whole, likewise kneeling a dozen paces away.

“Cal?” Ethan asked, while struggling to his feet.

“Bishop?” the prince replied, looking as though he were shocked to see him. “What am I…?”

He stood up–far more easily than Ethan had–and turned slowly around, taking in the chaos of the battlefield. “Of course,” he whispered. He turned back to Ethan. “I remember. It’s hazy, but I remember. The Followers captured me in those tunnels, back on Earth. They have some sort of scientist…he combines Follower technology with magics of different worlds. He…he did something to me.”

“It’s over now,” Ethan said, walking slowly forward. He could feel his wound healing, but movement risked tearing it further.

Cal nodded. “You helped me find myself,” he said. “I remember seeing your face, and realizing for a brief instant what I was doing. I seem to recall seeing Kentevaro, and the confusion of it…”

Ethan nodded. “Yeah, very flattering that you couldn’t kill me. I just wish I’d tried facing you as myself first.” He grimaced as pain lanced through his body.

Cal was under Ethan’s arm instantly, on the opposite side from the injury. “My apologies, Bishop. For the wound, and for…that.”

He gestured at the two Champions battling not far off. Frankly, too close. The demons mostly used physical attacks, but their rolling, flailing grapple seemed to be taking them closer to the plateau where the Emperor still faced off against Dean, Valanor and Savilar.

“Not your fault,” Ethan said. “I wish the Emperor and I hadn’t had the same idea, but you were under his control.”

“True, but…the Emperor wished to use something that unnerved you. Unfortunately, I recalled your tails of this place.”

Ethan looked up at the man with a glare. “You told him about the scorpiursi? You prick.”

Calevaro shrugged. “He might have gotten there on his own. He wanted something intimidating, and he claimed that creature was the worst thing he’d ever seen.”

Ethan shook his head and chuckled wryly. “Well, he can’t be wrong about everything, I suppose. But please tell me he’s going to lose control, now that he no longer has you. I saw that he had Snowbear, and we assumed he was using your animus.”

Cal sighed. “Bek, the scientist, made sure the Bond was stronger than that. He’s the one that found a way to merge the remaining Followers directly with the captured demons. Bek is inside the Champion personally, and it won’t be leaving the Emperor’s control.”

“Then our plan hasn’t changed,” Ethan said.

“We need to kill the Emperor,” Calevaro said, sounding eager.

Ethan groaned in pain. “More you, than me, I’m guessing. But yes. Thankfully, with five of us, we should have a real chance.”

Cal made a face that Ethan didn’t like. “What’s wrong?” he asked.

“The Emperor, he often complained that controlling me was worse than controlling any of the demons, likely because I was never truly converted. His will was directly imposed upon me. Without that distraction…”

Ethan’s eyes widened, and he looked back to the four-way battle. He swore as he saw the truth. The Emperor wasn’t striking any harder, and Valanor seemed to be managing to withstand the blows, but the Follower moved with a smooth and deadly grace he’d lacked before.

His counterattacks came more quickly, he moved between Dean’s blasts of energy, and Savilar’s every blow seemed to be turned aside.

“Damn it. We need to help them. Go. I’ll do what I can, but they need your blades.”

Calevaro looked down at Ethan. No doubt he saw a mana-drained, wounded man who’d been pushed to the limit. He might have even spotted the ongoing struggle of holding back the corruption, which Ethan wasn’t convinced he was managing to hide.

Still, Cal knew what needed to be done.

“Don’t risk yourself needlessly,” he said. Then he released Ethan, and quickly recovered his swords before racing to join the others.

“Do not ignore his wisdom, Ethan Bishop,” Tomo warned.

Ethan forced himself to stand up straight. The muscles in his side protested, but he knew they wouldn’t heal well if he stayed hunched over.

He and Tomo watched as the prince engaged the Emperor. He was an excellent addition to the fight. His wind affinity allowed him to appear as if from nowhere, pure speed allowing for hit and run attacks that kept the Follower continually reacting.

Those extra moments bought Savilar and Dean time to strike, and bone-mace and energy spears crashed into the Emperor, punishing every opening. But Cal was ever the master of cycling, and between those rapid strikes, lightning crashed from the heavens, and pits of magma opened up beneath the Follower.

The four warriors fell into a rhythm quickly, and it wasn’t long before the Emperor was once again on the defensive.

Ethan longed to join them, but knew he’d be a liability without a little healing, and a fair bit more mana. He used the time to check on the other two battles raging around them.

The Champions seemed locked in an eternal struggle, their power so immense, but so evenly matched that neither found advantage for long. The serpent still twisted and writhed, rapping the gargantuan insect-thing in coils of its scaly body, while tentacles wrestled with snapping pincers and a stabbing stinger.

In the other direction, Michael, the Bishops, and the Nator finally seemed to be making real progress against the remaining demons. Richard was making a good show of himself, driving around in the enormous military vehicle, gatling guns and missile launchers firing liberally. It was a relief not to have to worry quite so much about his family, and Ethan turned away gratefully.

Only a few minutes had passed, and the battle was largely unchanged between the Emperor and the four Twilights.

“We have to win this, Tomo,” Ethan said.

“Much rides on our victory, it is true,” the demon agreed.

“I can feel it, you know. The Throne. I think… I think I can almost see it.” He reached out his hand, as if to grasp the thing. To take hold of Dusk’s lost power. Though he knew that wasn’t meant to be his fate. He could almost feel Strategus shaking his head in disapproval.

“It is close, Ethan Bishop. I feel it as well,” Tomo said. “But so long as there are two contenders so close, no one will ascend. It has always been thus.”

Ethan took a deep breath, feeling only a slight pain in his side. Then he drew Last Resort.

“The blade’s name has extra meaning this day,” Tomo said, looking down at his former master’s weapon.

“And it’ll live up to it,” Ethan promised. “Let’s go kill an Emperor.”
