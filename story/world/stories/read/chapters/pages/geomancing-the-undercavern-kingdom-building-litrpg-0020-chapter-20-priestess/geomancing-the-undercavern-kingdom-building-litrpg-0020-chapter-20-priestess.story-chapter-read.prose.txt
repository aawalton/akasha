Carmack had found a letter resting on his table within his strategy room when he returned home. He had long instructed his assistant to leave correspondents waiting for him on the table.

Taking a thin, metal letter opener, he cut away the wax and unfolded it. Glancing over the words, his brow rose.

Looks like it’s time to pay the smithy a visit.

“Hero, it’s good to see you,” Master Smith Rognar waved from the forge as Carmack entered the workshop. “I have your order ready.”

Water hissed and steam filled the air as the smith dunked glowing metal into a bucket and stepped toward Carmack.

“Thank you, Rognar. May I ask how you went with the minithrel?”

“It was fine. The good quality metal you provided. I was a little rusty, to be honest. But I got it done in the end. Luckily, I purchased the skill “Minithrel Bonding” back in the day. My master thought I was a fool for doing so. It takes a while to gain levels as a smith, after all, and he didn’t think we’d see the magic ore often. And he was right. Most of the magical items in Lydrus were sold into the city, and we rarely made our own.”

“Oh? That sounds like a strategic weakness.”

“Haha, you’d be right about that. People love to talk about how great our city was, but the truth is, it was pretty weak, all things considered.”

“Well, it’s good to hear we have at least one smith capable of working with magic ore. I intend to increase the magic ore output. Are you going to be able to continue working on it for me?”

“Me? Alone? Hmm, I’ll be limited in what I can achieve. That said, we have a decent number of smiths who should be able to purchase the skill either now or soon. As long as metal continues to flow into our forges, we should be able to produce the necessary skills for a few smiths. I mean, it’s not like people don’t want to work with the ore. They just haven’t needed to.”

“Understood,” Carmack nodded. “Make sure the skill is a priority for anybody who can unlock it. The importance of minithrel and magical item creation is only going to increase. Keep this between us for now, but I intend for this to be both a strategic resource and a powerful economic tool going forward.”

“Will do hero. You’re going to make a lot of people happy. Most of the kids around here could only dream of working minithrel before coming here.”

“Good to hear it.”

“Well, I better show you why I called you here, hadn’t I?”

“Please do.”

A moment later, an apprentice smith came out carrying a sword on a velvety cloth. Carmack’s eyes widened at the beautiful blade. Myriad colors reflected from its patterned surface, creating a rainbow effect as crystal light cast down upon it.

“Your work is impressive, master smith,” Camrack said as he took hold of the blade.

“It’s alright, I suppose. And I’m no master compared to those in the major cities. Still, it’d hold up against the best blades to have been forged in Lydrus.”

“I bet it would.”

Walking over to a bench, Carmack placed down the blade and retrieved the ruby and monster core from a pouch.

“What are you doing?” Rognar said, craning around Carmack’s shoulder.

“Making a magical item.”

“You? You’re going to enchant that thing? I thought you were some kind of rock mage?”

“Geomancer,” Carmack corrected. “Oh, and I dabble a little in enchanting.”

“Dabble? That’s a greater core, isn’t it? Please, I know you’re a competent man, but this is foolish. Wait for a proper enchanter. That thing could blow up in your face. Heck, even if it doesn’t, that’s not the kind of resource we can afford to lose right now!”

“Unfortunately, Lydrus doesn’t have any enchanters, at least none that survived. In fact, enchanters seem entirely rare on this world.”

“Please, there’s no rush. We can always pay someone from the surface!” Rognar pleaded.

It’s good to know he takes this seriously.

Ignoring the man’s pleas, Carmack raised his hand and it brightened with a warm glow of magical power as he activated “Greater Core Enchanting.” Ribbons of magical energy swirled around the ingredients, merging them together.

Despite being the first time Carmack had used the skill, it seemed similar enough to other magic. This was the benefit of having already having reached the peak of a magical school. It gave him insights that only a skilled lifelong enchanter would be able to top.

Carmack placed the gemstone into a divot on the sword’s crossguard. He then directed the magical energy from the monster core into the gem. Gradually, the energy and color of the core faded, and the gemstone brightened. Even before he had finished, Carmack could feel the magical hum of the blade and the minithrel within reacting to the mana of the gemstone. Purple minithrel veins snaking along the blade brightened with pulses of power as the weapon came to life with magical power.

Having a skill gave you the ability to do something, but there was still a degree of talent involved in using it. Just like a warrior still needed to know how to fight, a crafter skill needed to have a feel for the materials they wielded. Not only that, but crafting skills usually had supporting skills that assisted or strengthened their use.

Having skipped the entire skill tree, Carmack was far from an enchanting expert. If not for innate magical powers and control from reaching archgeomancy, he likely would have fumbled his attempt at enchanting altogether.

However, the magic took root. The ruby was bound to the sword through powerful magic, and minithrel formed the connection to it that made the metal so valuable. But it wasn’t perfect. He could see the imperfections. He could feel how the mana didn’t quite naturally form, and that the sword wasn’t what it could have been. That said, he was satisfied. Perfect, it wasn’t, but it was still a remarkable weapon.

“Amazing,” Rognar murmured. “I can feel the magic from here. That’s an impressive blade. It’d sell for hundreds of gold coins in a place like Golden Jade City.”

Interesting. Despite my less-than-perfect attempt at enchanting, Rognar seems to believe that this weapon is still quite powerful. This seemed like a clear admission that the enchanters on Avalock were more powerful than those on this world.

Not bad for my first attempt. Carmack smiled to himself at the implications.

“Leaving already?” Rognar said as Carmack turned to leave.

“Yes. Thank you for your time, master smith. Please, prepare yourselves and your people for more minithrel. This is just the beginning.”

“Y-yes, sir!”

Taking a step, Carmack staggered and felt a headache creep in. He opened his status page and confirmed he was out of mana.

Okay, perhaps that is why…

If Carmack had run out of mana during the enchanting process and it was forcibly cut short, that would no doubt affect the finished product. It made sense. He was using an advanced skill, and while he had A LOT of mana for a Bronze rank, this skill was beyond what was usually available for people of his rank. In fact, it likely wasn’t used by anybody below Gold rank, if that.

I should have seen this coming. Oh well, it cannot be helped now. Besides, at least I’ve held up my end of the bargain.

Carmack’s understanding of skill use was that it was bad practice to use skills that you lacked the mana for. However, he had already promised an enchanted blade, and intended to deliver on that promise. Besides, if Rognar was impressed, he figured Aphexia would be too.

Looking equal parts bored and unimpressed, Aphexia watched the elite guardsmen train.

“Good enough,” she golf clapped and sighed. “Switch roles and restart the exercise. Whoever was attacking will now be defending, you know the drill.”

He’s back. That dude better have my weapon. Honor or not, I’m not sticking around training these gumbos unless I get paid.

“Been a while,” she rolled her eyes.

“Charming as ever,” Carmack smiled.

Aphexia scrunched her nose. For some reason, she found the hero’s nonchalant confidence equal parts annoying and charming—she hated that more than anything.

If he was just regular old cocky, she could go about hating him the way she did most overachieving male adventurers, but there was something annoyingly persistent about Carmack that just wouldn’t let her do that.

Damn that man and his stupidly infectious smile!

“Only when you’re around,” Aphexia huffed and crossed her arms. She reminded herself that this dumb mage was the reason she was below ground, and not above it. “I’m normally a delight.”

“Haha!” A couple of nearby guardsmen chuckled as they overheard the conversation.

“Oh, come on now, Aphexia. As much as I love our conversations, I haven’t just come here for no reason.”

It had been a long day, and babysitting guardsmen was a boring job. She had just let her mind drift, yes, that was it. That was the reason her usually hawk-like eyes had missed what should have been an obvious detail. Definitely not the way his smile drilled little dimples into his cheeks.

“Just a little something for my favourite guild master.”

“You better not be talking about me. Because I clearly don’t have a guild to master.”

Carmack slowly revealed the weapon, and his smile widened to reveal his teeth.

“My sword,” Aphexia said, her usually dry tone spiking with excitement. Immediately, she wrapped her hands over her mouth in an attempt to dampen her excitable squeal, but that only made it worse.

Carmack’s smug grin widened comically. He seemed far too impressed with himself for dragging such a reaction out of her.

Damn that man!

“Shut up, you,” Aphexia snapped, snatching the weapon and drawing it from the intricately crafted sheath it had come with. “It’s–it's beautiful.”

“Sure is. I'm quite impressed with the smith’s work, if I’m to be honest. Not quite Avalock quality, but better than anything else I've seen in this world up until now.”

The sword let out a metallic whistle as Aphexia swirled it through the air. The metal caught the crystal light immediately, reflecting a spectrum of colors.

“This is… It’s amazing,” Aphexia murmured.

“First time hearing you speak like that. You must really like it.”

“I do,” Aphexia said, forgetting about her irritation.

“Good. I’m glad I impressed you, Guildmaster.”

That one, Aphexia, let slide. If this was what she could expect from being Lydrus’ Avendurers’ Guild leader, then maybe it wasn’t so bad.

Her thoughts drifted to those smug Titanium and Ruby rank adventurers from Golden Jade City who used to look down on her. They had been part of the reason she moved to Lydrus. She played it tough and indifferent, but the truth was, she enjoyed being a big fish in a small pond. However, maybe that wasn’t all that she was destined for.

Maybe I can reach higher peaks working with the hero? Aphexia considered the thought. It wasn’t just the sword that gave her hope. There were plenty of powerful monsters down here, and with the right equipment and help, she could get back to gaining levels.

It had been two years since Aphexia had gained a level. She was only twenty-six. She had leveled fast when she was younger; some even considered her a prodigy. But that wasn’t to last. As she progressed, the difficulty of the quests and monsters got scary. At just nineteen, she watched her entire party get slaughtered when they were ambushed whilst delving into a monster nest. After that, she was never the same. She didn’t want to push herself. Problem was, without challenging yourself against appropriately strong monsters, you couldn’t gain experience.

“Okay, fine. I’m impressed. But this is still just a sword,” Aphexia said, waving the blade through the air a few times before fastening the sheath to her belt and returning the sword to it. “And we still don’t have any adventurers for this so-called guild.”

“One job at a time, Aphexia. I have a few things that need seeing to. Would you mind collecting Commander Gorn for me and having him call up his vanguard?”

“I suppose I can. What’s the occasion?”

“We’ll be heading back into that tunnel,” Carmack grinned. “It’s time our new vanguard were field tested.”

The people had been desperate and hungry when they arrived, and while rations were still enforced, the change was evident.

When they had escaped Lydrus, the prayers people made were to ease their suffering and help them pass to the next world peacefully. Now they prayed for their children's futures and to find true love. They were looking forward to the future, rather than planning their ends.

At this rate, he might just convince me to remain in the underground.

Octavia's eyes flickered up to the stone ceiling. She missed the surface, but most importantly, the moon and the stars. But she couldn't deny the promise that this place brought with it.

“Head Maiden.”

“Yes?” Octavia turned.

One of her juniors stood waiting.

“What is it?”

“The reagents you requested have been collected.”

“Thank you, sister. Have them brought here along with all the senior sisters.”

“Yes, Head Maiden,” the girl curtseyed and left.

Purifying the water that the Great Hero had requested would be easy now that they had running fountains and basins in the temple, yet another reminder of how far they had come so quickly.

That was a thing of the past. The sisters who grew up here would know running water like the back of their hands. They would grow up with a luxury she could have only dreamed of as a child.

How lucky they ar–she cut that thought short. She couldn't be sure of that just yet. As promising as their new home was, there were still many obstacles to overcome. This was a home to others, others who wished to do them harm.

But what choice do we have?

The city she had known her entire life. A city she was proud of. The other kingdoms and states wouldn’t just allow their people to build their own city and claim their own land within their borders.
