The Adventurer’s Guild was empty today, its usual endless chorus of men boasting, men haggling, men arguing replaced by a silence so complete Viktor could hear his own boots echo across the worn floorboards.

He let his gaze roam the length of the main hall and counted fewer than ten people, including a receptionist who sat slumped behind her desk, yawning so widely she didn’t even bother to cover her mouth. Not Rhea, of course. The girl was now at the camp outside his dungeon, along with almost every other adventurer and employee of the Guild.

After all, today was the day.

The door at the back of the hall creaked open, and a bespectacled woman stepped through it. She raised an eyebrow on spotting him, then she walked over to where he stood.

“You’re here, huh? I thought you’d be at the camp with everyone else.”

“So are you,” Viktor said with a chuckle. “You’re not going either, it seems.”

Calyssa shrugged. “Someone has to stay behind to keep the Guild running.” She swept her eyes over the empty tables and message board. “I know, there’s not much running to do today. Still, something might happen while everyone’s attention is elsewhere, so I need to hold down the fort. Because Isadora is not going to do it.”

Of course she wasn’t. Mommy was probably planting herself in front of the entrance to his dungeon, and she would stay there until her precious boy walked back out into the daylight.

“It’s supposed to start in an hour, right?” Viktor asked, glancing out the window.

“Yes, an hour.” Calyssa nodded. “Anyway, how is the progress on your little ‘spread the information until it reaches the dark spirit’s ears’ plan?”

“Honestly? I don’t know. It’s not like I have any reliable way to get that information to him. All I’ve done is talk about it to as many people as I could and hope for the best. Beyond that, it’s completely out of my hands.”

“Unfortunately, we haven’t been able to find anything about the Bronze-ranked adventurer who supposedly joined the Iskora team,” Calyssa said. “The rumor itself turned out to be true, at least. He’s real and he’s with them. But his background remains a mystery to everyone we’ve asked. It seems that even among the Iskora adventurers themselves, not a single person knows who he is.”

Well, that so-called Bronze was actually an Apostle, the ultimate weapon of the Children of the Abyss. Small wonder Isadora couldn’t find out anything about him.

“He’s their trump card,” Viktor said. “It’s hardly a surprise they would want to guard their secrets.”

Calyssa let out a long, tired sigh. “I wonder how this is going to play out in the end.”

“We’ve done everything we could. Now all that’s left is to watch it unfold.”

“You’re right,” the bespectacled woman said. “Still, I can’t help wishing we could have done more. Wishing I could have done more. And...”

“And what?”

She gave him a sad little smile. “Sometimes I find myself wondering how differently things might have played out if Gideon were still alive today. What he would do if he were still the Guildmaster?”

Gideon? That guy was useless. The most significant thing he had ever accomplished was to drop dead at a convenient moment. Oh, he had the charisma to spare, no doubt about it, and people loved him for that reason. But being loved was not the same as delivering results. Viktor couldn’t think of a single thing Gideon could have done better than Isadora, while she had achieved so much more than he ever had.

But of course, he was not going to say that to her face—the man’s most devoted admirer.

“You really believe in him, don’t you?” he asked instead. “The man who fixes everything. The man who sets things right. No matter how dark the road ahead, he finds a way through.”

Calyssa laughed. “That’s putting it a bit too dramatically, I think. But... yes, maybe I really do believe something like that, deep down. Silly of me, isn’t it?”

Yes, it was silly. Or, to be more accurate, it was fucking stupid.

Viktor knew because he had once believed in someone exactly like that. In a man who had a bright smile that never seemed to waver, a man who seemed to glow as if the sun itself were standing right behind him. Twice, he had staked his faith on that man. And twice, he had been left gravely disappointed.

“Anyway,” Calyssa said. “I need to get back to my work. Are you heading to the dungeon now?”

“No,” Viktor said. “You did tell me not to show my face anywhere near Guildmaster Isadora for the time being, remember?”

The woman chuckled. “Ah, right. So you’re going back to the Sanctuary?”

“I don’t know. I find myself getting a bit... restless. I don’t want to sit still doing nothing for the whole day. So perhaps I’m going to wander around the town instead.”

“Oh, I understand that feeling completely,” Calyssa said, the corners of her mouth curving into a knowing smile.

She was probably thinking, “Ah hah! So it turns out you’re not nearly as calm and collected as you always seemed. You’re still a kid who gets anxious on a big day, just like everyone else.”

And she couldn’t be more wrong. What he was doing was, in fact, trying to keep his whereabouts vague, so that no awkward questions would arise later when nobody happened to see him for the rest of the day.

Bidding him farewell, Calyssa walked to the reception desk and murmured a few words to the yawning girl behind it. Then, she disappeared once more through the door she had emerged from earlier.

Viktor was planning to go to his dungeon at once, to prepare for his enemies the kind of welcome they deserved. However, after being left alone in the middle of the near-empty hall, he found the words of the previous conversation settling over him with unexpected weight, and for a long moment he simply stood there.

[Are you thinking about Orion, Master?]

What makes you say that?

[The exchange you have just had with Calyssa. Clearly, the late Guildmaster reminded you of your brother-in-law. And I do find some similarities between them.]

Of course they were similar. They were both fools.

[You say that. Yet in the end, you were the one who made Orion’s dream come true. A world where no adventurer would ever again be shackled to the whims of any monarch. You built it.]

Viktor chuckled. Orion would’ve been horrified if he had lived long enough to see how I achieved that. I did it by crushing anyone who stood in my way—the exact opposite of how he ever tried to do things. Which only proved my point, didn’t it? The dream itself does not matter. Only the doing of it. Only whether you had the power to force the world to agree with you.

[That much is true. But...]

Let's not get distracted. We have an important battle ahead of us. Let’s keep our focus there.

[Yes, Master.]

Viktor turned around and started walking toward the exit. He would head home and have Celeste teleport him into the dungeon.

But then, just as he was passing by the doors of the mess hall, he slowed his steps and gave it a glance. Might as well take a look, he thought. After all, he had come here to check for the last time if there was anything he missed. And he ought to do it properly. There was still a full hour before the competition began, while this little detour would likely cost him less than a minute.

He crossed the threshold and found the chamber just as deserted and echoing as the main hall. Then, he spotted a familiar figure hunched over a drink in a corner. Chuckling to himself, he made his way to the table.

“What are you doing?” he asked the bearded bald man.

“What does it look like?” Azran grunted, not bothering to look up from his cup. “Drinking, obviously.”

“But why here? Why now?”

“Because right now, for once, I have this entire hall completely to myself. Quiet, finally. A rare thing, considering how noisy this place usually gets. Today I can actually enjoy a drink.”

He said it like a man describing paradise, all the while looking like someone who was attending his own funeral. Though, to be fair, the guy’s face had always been dour, so it was difficult to read much into it either way.

Azran stared down into his cup for a long moment before speaking again. “Cedric is going into the dungeon today, huh?”

“He is,” Viktor said. “Don’t tell me you’re only just now finding out about it.”

“Of course I already knew.” Azran snorted. “He told me himself, right after he made up his mind about that whole reckless plan. Yes, reckless. It’s not just the dungeon itself that is dangerous, but he’s also up against those bastards in there.” The bald man glanced at Viktor. “Why didn’t you talk him out of it?”

“I tried, but he was stubborn. It’s impossible to change his mind.”

Azran said nothing in response to that. Instead, he simply raised his cup and drained the rest of its contents in one long swallow.

“Hey, if you’re really that worried about him, why don’t you go in with him yourself? Their team is still short one person, last I heard. You could fill the spot.”

“Me?” The bald man’s brow furrowed deeply. “But...”

“You’re a Gold. You’re stronger than anyone else currently in that party. Having you join would drastically improve their odds. And if things go wrong down there, you get them out alive.”

“But... Cedric has asked me to join his party more times than I can count, and I’ve always turned him down. If I suddenly show up now asking to join, won’t that look strange?”

Viktor chuckled. “It’s all in how you frame it. Tell Cedric that you want to see for yourself how much he’s grown. That you want to know whether he’s finally proven himself worthy. That you’re not there to help him, but to evaluate him.”

Azran mulled that over for a moment. “I could work with that. But the competition is about to start soon, isn’t it? Wouldn’t it be too late to join the team now?”

“There’s still one hour left before the two teams enter the dungeon. And nothing in the rules says a team can’t change its lineup before that point.”

The bald man nodded. “Best hurry, then.”

With that, he rose from his seat and strode out of the hall without another word, leaving his empty cup behind on the table.

Viktor watched the doors swing shut behind the man. Well, this was all he could do for Cedric. He had provided him with an alternative route to the third floor through Ekon, and now he had sent Azran to join him as well.

From this point forward, the Daelin team was on their own. Whatever happened next was entirely up to them.
