Veronica didn’t waste a second. She bolted into the woods, weaving between the trees. Behind her, the camp erupted into chaos.

“She’s running!” one of the mercenaries shouted.

“Get her!” Jorren bellowed, clutching his mangled arm.

Behind her, the shouts of horses echoed through the trees, mixed with the loud commands of their riders. They would be delayed for some time until the horses calmed down from the sudden scare.

Veronica didn’t look back and continued to run, already aware of the general direction of Greystone after traveling with the mercenaries for half a day.

Sage could actually assist and compel her body and muscles to keep running because her own brain refused to do it. Every step felt wrong. Even knowing the curse was gone, part of her felt like she was running into oblivion.

Mana continued to stream in as she moved. The ambient energy was faint, but she pulled in as much as possible. The intake slowed under the strain of a full sprint, but there was no other choice.

If she had known earlier that the nearest town was over an entire day’s travel away, she would have reforged her mana cores and ascended to the 1st tier after first opening her eyes back then. But it was too late to regret that decision. For the time being, unattuned magic would have to do, despite the inefficiency.

Minutes passed before she finally heard the sound of galloping horses echoing behind her, accompanied by the anger-filled shouts of the mercenaries. The torches they carried flickered through the leaves and trees.

If they had to use torches instead of light wisps, then it was clear they didn’t have any magical artifacts on them.

Veronica skidded to a stop, twisting to face the approaching group.

A third of her gathered mana was spent breaking the cuffs. Now, the rest would be used to stop their pursuit. Her hands slammed against the ground as she channeled her remaining mana.

From the Path of Ruin, she unleashed a low Tier-2 spell: Fissure. Like before, however, it was a spell above her tier—and she wasn’t even a first-tier mage yet. The inefficient use of her mana was almost enough to make her cry.

A thin, sharp crack shot forward from below her palms, splitting the earth in a jagged line that quickly expanded. It danced forth like lightning, traveling over a hundred meters, starting small but growing in ferocity as it traveled deeper into the forest.

Earth moved, plants fell, and trees shifted over ever so slightly. To the outside world, it appeared as if Mother Nature had intervened, all to save one girl.

The ground beneath the mercenaries buckled and fractured, creating shallow gaps several inches deep. The spell wasn’t powerful; it wasn’t enough to trap them. But it was enough to misplace the gallops of their horses.

“W-what the—?!” Jorren screamed, clutching his arm as he fell off his horse.

Panicked neighs and shouts echoed back to Veronica through the forest as several mounts stumbled, toppling their riders.

[Mana: 189/200 MU]

She turned and resumed her running.

Considering they didn’t have any mages or aura users, those horses were their lifeline—their best form of travel and a means of carrying their food, weapons, and other items of importance. They would be foolish to continue chasing her instead of tending to their horses.

Hours passed as the night fully set in; the forest stretched endlessly before her. The sounds of pursuit had long since faded, replaced by the rhythmic chirping of crickets and the occasional hoot of an owl. Veronica’s pace slowed as fatigue set in, and she stopped every so often to rest for half an hour at a time or less. Sometimes, she even slept, relying on Sage to forcibly awaken her after a short rest.

The night wore on as Veronica ran; every few hours, she spent any vestiges of mana she had procured in the meantime reinvigorating herself with a Tier-1 Path of Blooming spell: Energize.

It was a spell that rejuvenated her muscles and dampened fatigue, granting a small boost of energy. But it wasn’t all-powerful. The more she used it, the larger the crash that would come later. It was one of the very few spells she could cast from that path.

Luckily, with her increased mana regeneration from her mana rings, she could keep the spell up constantly throughout the night. The problem was that now she was locked into it. The moment she stopped the spell or ran out of mana, she would collapse.

By the time the first rays of dawn broke through the trees, Veronica’s body was on the verge of fainting. Her legs were tired and her feet felt sore. Her clothes were also slightly torn and caked with dirt.

Finally, after an entire night, the forest thinned ahead, giving way to rolling hills and, in the distance, the faint outline of a town.

Greystone. That must be it.

Veronica slowed to a walk, her body trembling with exhaustion. She leaned against a tree, taking a moment to catch her breath and assess her situation. She was in no condition to fight, and her mana reserves were nearly depleted. But she had made it this far. Rest was close, but not quite within her grasp just yet.

Once she came closer to the town gate, she noticed something odd. There were no guards. No officials checking at the entrance.

That was… weird. Right?

Although she spent most of her time in Annesheim, the wealthy capital city, surrounded by nobles, she had visited poorer towns before during her expeditions. Even a remote place like this had to have guards. Was she mistaken?

[Veronica. It appears there is a commotion happening inside this town.]

“Commotion?” she asked.

[A battle seems to be taking place.]

Veronica stepped through the open archway. The distant shouting sharpened as she advanced—people running, guards barking orders, the clatter of metal. A woman sprinted past, holding a crying toddler. A pair of teenagers dashed by with baskets of goods; in their panic, they dropped them, spilling fruits all over the road. Down the main street, a squad of guards rushed in formation toward the commotion.

That explained the empty gate.

She followed the sound.

Up ahead, several soldiers were already engaged with a pack of goblins—eight in total, snarling and swiping with crude blades. The guards formed a semicircle, shields up, spears striking in practiced rhythm. It wasn’t a fair fight; within seconds, the goblins were cut down.

But Veronica’s brow furrowed.

Eight goblins alone wouldn’t be foolish enough to raid a town. Monsters weren’t geniuses—but they weren’t suicidal.

[I am detecting the sounds of a large monster approaching.]

Veronica squinted.

After the goblins were vanquished, the sound of loud stomping came from the forest.

There—at the treeline, past another entrance of town, a large green monster carrying a large wooden club stepped into view. It ran past the gate, attacking the formation of guards that dared approach it.

“An ogre?” she murmured.

Before Veronica could process the situation further, a commanding shout came from behind her. “Everyone! Step away!”

Veronica turned, seeing a man in armor run past her.

“You! Get away from the battle zone!” he shouted, his gaze directed at her. He was a large, bulky man in full plate armor. He looked to be in his forties or fifties, with gray streaks running through his dark hair and sharply cut features set in a hardened scowl.

“Captain Luthen…!” a nearby guard shouted.

The other soldiers, still finishing off the final goblins, flinched at the sight of their captain barreling toward the much larger foe.

“Finish those pests!” Luthen ordered. “Form a perimeter! Push it away from the town—shields up, don’t get hit!”

The soldiers obeyed instantly; the guards’ movements were disciplined despite the chaos. Half broke off to surround the ogre from the sides, jabbing with spears to draw its attention away from the townspeople. The rest dragged the injured to safety.

“Get the bolt cannon ready!” Luthen shouted.

Near the side of the square, two guards ripped a tarp from a squat iron weapon mounted on heavy wheels. It looked like a cross between a cannon and a ballista, patched together with black metal plates, gears, and a short coal chamber beneath the barrel. One guard shoveled burning coals into the open hatch while another locked a mana crystal into the side slot.

The sound of gears grinding became louder. Steam from within the machine began to pour out, rising as white vapor. The crystal flashed blue and continued to grow brighter.

A third guard loaded a giant black metal bolt into the barrel.

“Fire!” someone else shouted.

The cannon fired with a thunderous crack. Steam and black smoke burst from the coal chamber as the bolt shot forward, ripping through the air and punching deep into the ogre’s shoulder.

The ogre roared, staggering sideways as dark blood sprayed across the street.

“We missed its head!”

“Reload!” Luthen barked.

“The crystal’s flickering!” one guard shouted back. “It needs a moment!”

“Squad two! Provide a distraction!” Luthen shouted, reaching the ogre first, his sword slicing into its arm with a clean swing.

The ogre roared in fury, swinging its massive club with enough force to shake the ground. Luthen deflected the blow but was sent skidding back in his armored boots.

“Hold formation!” Luthen shouted, forcing stability back into his stance. “Find an opening!”

The ogre roared and swung again, much faster than before. Guards on the opposite side of it continued to stab and prod at it with spears, but each hit only continued to make the beast angrier.

Glaring down at him, the ogre swiped at the human shouting orders.

Luthen raised his blade just in time. The impact rattled through his arms, pain flaring up his shoulders as he was driven backward several steps.

“Damn thing’s stronger than expected—!”

He ducked as the club carved through the air above him and smashed into a wagon near the edge of the square. Wood detonated outward in a shower of splinters. The wagon collapsed, its contents spilling across the street.

A scream followed.

Luthen’s eyes snapped toward the sound.

A young girl—a child—stumbled out from behind the wreckage. She froze in place, staring up at the towering monster with wide, uncomprehending eyes.

“GET BACK!” Luthen shouted.

The ogre’s head turned slowly. Its beady, enraged eyes locked onto the girl.

It took a step forward.

Luthen lunged to intercept, stepping in front of her—but the ogre was fast.

Its club swung low and wide, catching Luthen across the side. The blow hurled him off his feet and sent him skidding across the dirt. His armor rang as he rolled to a stop, breath torn from his lungs.

He tried to rise, but struggled, faltering back to his knees.

The ogre loomed tall, sizing Luthen up. Its face contorted, as though it were grinning at him.

“Hey! Get away from there!” a woman’s voice shouted from behind them.

Luthen’s head whipped around.

Veronica stood there, in the middle of the street, alone. Her arm was raised, palm flat, fingers stretched taut. Her stance was unmistakable to anyone who had witnessed magic before.

Luthen’s eyes widened. “You—” he said, but paused. His next words were surely going to be a shout. A curse. A command for her to run. But the words died in his throat. His eyes widened with recognition.

He knew that pose.

“Break formation!” Luthen shouted immediately, slowly getting back up. “All units, clear the front! Move, MOVE!”

His soldiers all jolted with uncertainty—but they obeyed. Their spears retracted, and they fell back, widening the circle. The child who had been frozen in fear scrambled to her feet and ran.

The ogre turned toward Veronica. It roared as it spotted her standing still with her arm raised. Her pose was almost like a taunt. The ogre raised its club and took a single step to charge at her.

But a single step was all it could take.

“The backlash from this is going to feel so bad…” Veronica muttered.

That weapon they had brought out still wasn’t ready yet, evident from the guards’ worried faces as they watched their captain fight.

Although she was tired and near her limit, she couldn't simply let an ogre destroy the town she had been so desperately trying to find. Surely guards who were trying their best to save villagers were trustworthy enough.

Luthen turned just in time to see her unleash a spell.

Veronica didn’t disappoint his expectations.

Mana surged toward her, drawn from the air and from the dregs of her own depleted core. Two white wings appeared on her hand. A translucent spell circle burst into existence in front of her palm—thin, faint, flickering with weakness, but still geometrically perfect. Runic glyphs spiraled along its rim, struggling to hold shape.

She normally didn’t need spell circles for this.

But right now, she needed every scrap of control available. Both mana rings within her body rotated quickly, generating more and more mana.

It was rare for mages to be able to cast above their tier. Veronica was a special exception since she remembered every single spell equation from her past life. That, however, didn’t make it any less strenuous.

The ogre’s foot slammed down. Veronica spun her rings to the maximum limit and fired her spell.

A fireball the size of her head erupted from her palm, launching forward with a pitched scream. The spell circle shattered like glass behind it. The projectile slammed into the ogre’s chest and detonated. Unlike the spell against the Fellabear, this one came with much more explosive power.

An explosion ripped through the morning’s sunrise, echoing through the town. A shockwave blasted outward, sending dirt and stone spraying in a wide cone behind the creature. The earth tore open, leaving a small crater where the ogre had stood. Smoke billowed in violent plumes; the scent of scorched flesh and burning wood filled the street.

For a moment, nothing could be seen through the billowing dust.

Then the silhouette reappeared.

The ogre stood. Its chest was a blackened ruin, ribs half-exposed and melted together. Charred skin sloughed off in melted strips, heat-warped and sizzling. The creature staggered, swaying like a felled tree on its final tilt.

Luthen and the rest of the guards all stared, completely speechless. What had they just witnessed?

With a final groan, the ogre toppled backward, shaking the ground as its massive corpse crashed onto the earth, completely dead before it hit the dirt. The street fell silent except for the settling dust… and the soft, exhausted exhale of the girl who had just annihilated it.

Veronica fell to her knees, breathing heavily.

Luthen, captain of the armed soldiers, rushed over to her. He didn’t say a word.

She looked up at him. “Hey… is that child… alright?” she asked with a huff.

He looked her over for a moment before speaking. “The kid’s fine. Probably scared, but they’re safe.”

“That’s good…” she drawled. “Hey—mind… taking me somewhere I can sleep?” Slowly, her body failed her, and she fell to the ground, unconscious and completely exhausted.
