[Time]: Summer Break, Day 58 — 7:55 AM[Location]: White City · Townhouse 107 · Hathaway's Bedroom

Hathaway's central nervous system was vibrating at a sustained, agonizing peak.

In her previous life, university course registration had been the purest distillation of distributed academic despair: ten thousand undergraduates simultaneously declaring war on a single chronically underfunded server, the whole affair less a "system" and more a controlled stampede. In the Inner Sea, the academy registration portal was built on crystalline magi-tech arrays with the processing bandwidth to model planetary weather systems in real-time. The infrastructure was exponentially superior. The panic was identical.

She sat cross-legged at the center of her bed, surrounded by a fortification of pillows assembled at some point during the previous evening's anxiety spiral. Two terminals glowed in her lap. One was her own; the other was a premium corporate model she had explicitly requisitioned from Margaret the night before under the official pretense of academic preparation and the unofficial pretense of not dying.

With a finger that was not entirely steady, she selected [A2: Alchemy (Full Track)] and pressed confirm.

In the same instant, the mandatory curriculum detonated across both terminals: a dense, unrelenting wall of course assignments, without preamble or mercy.

Nine modules, mandatory, no substitutions permitted. Monday opened with [Doll-Making Basics & Soul Architecture], followed in the afternoon by [Elementary Practical Runes & Applications], the word elementary appearing in the official course title with a confidence bearing no relationship to its neighbors. Tuesday: [Transmutation: Variables & Matter] in the morning, flagged by the system as a near-universal A3 examination requirement, and [Advanced Botany] in the afternoon. Wednesday gave her [Specialized Botany: Root & Stem Systems] in the morning and [Intermediate Potions & Potion Quality Attributes] in the afternoon. Thursday ran [Doll-Making Basics & Soul Architecture] again in the first slot, then [Principles of Wondrous Item Crafting]. Friday was [Transmutation: Variables & Matter] a second time, then [Evocation: Advanced Physics & Magical Mechanics], listed with the calm, administrative brutality of a form requesting emergency contact information: the system had annotated that the standard pass rate for dedicated Evocation majors was exactly three percent, implying the success rate for out-of-department students was a floating-point error the server had simply truncated to zero. Saturday tapered to a single morning block, [Magical Tool Creation (Practical)], after which the timetable, for the first time all week, ran out of lecture halls to march her into.

Saturday afternoon and all of Sunday: Assignment Work / Laboratory Self-Study / Rest.

She stared at the final entry until the words blurred.

Rest. It sat there with the audacity of a warranty disclaimer printed in six-point font at the bottom of a financial contract: technically present, structurally irrelevant. Five days of double-block academic trauma, a Saturday that clocked out at noon, and then a day and a half printed, with a straight face, as assignment work and laboratory self-study and rest: three official names stamped across one unbroken slab of time, not one of which was ever going to involve resting. The academy's registrar had issued her a seven-day course load and formatted it to look like it contained a weekend. Do alchemists not require REM sleep? Do they simply photosynthesize anxiety in the laboratory overnight? Is the pass rate of this track three percent because the remaining ninety-seven percent quit and became Hunter-class Witches, where at minimum you get Saturdays off?

But the schedule was the secondary crisis. The primary crisis was in the faculty directory.

[Transmutation: Variables & Matter]: Professor Heidi Lucent.

Heidi Lucent. The Tenth Seat of the High Council, the undisputed, globally acknowledged GOAT of the Transmutation school, personally stepping down from the geopolitical stratosphere to teach a second-year variable mechanics class. The enrollment queue for her seminar was going to get vaporized the microsecond the server unlocked.

Missing Sharon Curie was an academic problem, but missing Heidi Lucent was a crime against her own future, and she had to secure both simultaneously.

The clock rolled over to 07:59:50. She raised both hands, hovering her index fingers over the confirmation touch-points on the dual screens, routed a dedicated processing thread to her motor cortex past its standard reflex ceiling, and locked both screens on target.

07:59:59.

Her fingers struck the glass.

The confirmation cycle completed instantly. Her nervous system, currently running at approximately four hundred percent of its recommended operating load, registered this as a geological epoch.

The left screen resolved first: Enrollment Confirmed. Seat 14 of 40. Doll-Making Basics & Soul Architecture — S. Curie.

The right screen resolved milliseconds later: Class Capacity Reached. Waitlist Position: 12. Transmutation: Variables & Matter — H. Lucent.

Hathaway's soul departed her body via the path of least resistance.

Across the room, Victoria set her single terminal down on the dresser with a soft, aristocratic click. She had been seated in the window chair for the past hour, preparing with the same focused calm she applied to everything. Her enrollment result was delivered in exactly the same register she used to discuss the weather.

"I secured Professor Lucent's seminar," Victoria said, smoothing the cuff of her sleeve. "I did not, however, secure Professor Curie's."

Of course. There had never been any question which course Victoria was going to prioritize. But missing Sharon Curie meant the foundational module of her Alchemy A2 track was broken on day one.

Hathaway watched, still faintly catatonic, as Victoria picked up her terminal. The call lasted approximately fifteen seconds. Victoria spoke four sentences, her voice carrying the effortless, unhurried authority of a building's structural foundation: Yes, it's Victoria. Sharon Curie's seminar. Add an auxiliary seat to the back row. Thank you.

She ended the call and slipped the device back into her pocket. "It is resolved."

"How," Hathaway said.

"The Wellington family has been a primary financial sponsor of Sharon Curie's independent research laboratory since before she achieved global recognition," Victoria said. "The funding contract includes a standing allocation of seminar seats."

Pay-to-win. She had circumvented the enrollment server entirely by calling the venture capitalist who originally funded the developer.

Waitlist Position 12 was still burning on her own screen when she picked up her terminal; the line clicked open on the second ring.

"Professor," Hathaway said immediately, deploying her most professional, earnest, technically-honest presentation voice. "I failed to secure a seat in your sister's Transmutation seminar during registration. Please assist."

An absolute, pressurized silence fell over the line.

Through the receiver, she could hear the ambient sound of a laboratory in session: the low hum of equipment, a faint rustle of parchment, a gas line hissing somewhere in the background. These sounds continued for a full three seconds. Then came a slow, controlled exhale: the specific sound produced by a project manager who has discovered, empirically, that summer vacation is not an accessible resource for someone in her contractual position.

"...Hathaway," Nino's voice finally transmitted, pitched low and carrying with it the professional exhaustion of someone who had audited this exact scenario in her future and elected to prepare rather than avoid it. "You failed to register for my sister's class. And your immediate response was to call me."

The dead air stretched.

"Because," Hathaway offered, with the unfeigned innocence of someone stating a factual constraint, "I only possess your contact information?"

A second exhale. Sharper.

"Your laboratory duties resume at the start of the new semester," Nino said. "Victoria's as well."

The blood evacuated Hathaway's face in a calm, orderly descent. She looked at the seven-day siege still burning on her terminal. "Professor, with respect: reviewing the A2 curriculum in full, I have only Sunday afternoons available—"

"Then you will come in on Sunday afternoons," Nino replied, with the serene finality of a geological formation.

Her one scheduled block of rest, eliminated in a single phone call over approximately eleven seconds.

Before she could process the loss, Nino added a single, precisely calibrated addendum. "If your performance as an assistant remains satisfactory, I possess the administrative authority to grant attendance exemptions for certain foundational theoretical courses."

Hathaway's eyes went wide.

Course exemptions. Mandatory lecture attendance, waived. She had traded Sunday afternoons for the right to skip the lectures most likely to be redundant given her actual development path, and the ROI on that exchange was, upon rapid preliminary calculation, extremely favorable.

"Sunday is ideal, Professor. I will see you then." She ended the call before the ledger could be revised.

Victoria was watching from the doorway, a razor-thin, precisely calibrated amusement lifting the corner of her mouth.

"Professor Nino has confirmed that our assistant duties continue next semester," Hathaway reported.

"It appears," Victoria said, "that the new semester will not lack for entertainment."

Hathaway leaned over and glanced at Victoria's schedule on the dresser terminal. The foundational courses occupying most of her own grid were simply absent. In their place sat a curated collection of advanced theoretical nightmares whose titles made Hathaway's eyes water slightly at the edges.

I am academically several years behind the woman I am dating, her developer brain noted, with the dispassionate precision of a performance audit. The gap is real. The gap is documentable. The gap will be closed.

She turned the terminal face-down on the bed and went to make breakfast.

[Time]: Summer Break, Day 58 — 2:15 PM[Location]: White City · Townhouse 107 · Sub-Basement Alchemy Laboratory

The theoretical study for [Move Earth] had concluded yesterday, and today was the practical bridge.

She stood at the primary workbench, the formula schematic projected above the crucible array, and reviewed the ingredient architecture for what was, in practice, only the fourth time she had processed it with full comprehension.

The dependency tree was elegant. The baseline catalyst was ten-thousand-year sealed mud: earth compressed beneath the crust for an eon, carrying Foundation Memory at such density that it functioned as a geological seed crystal, a resonance anchor capable of calling other ancient rock into sympathetic alignment. The severing agent was a mandible-spike from the Earth-Piercing Beetle, a creature that moved through solid bedrock by conceptually severing the rock's relationship with its surroundings: the organic implementation of an anchor-dissolution pass. The re-anchoring module was scales from the Magnetic-Pole Worm, which imposed aggressive spatial stabilization on any displaced mass, solving the post-transition structural integrity problem. Root-Severing Grass extract dissolved the botanical anchor points; Amnesia Moss Powder induced targeted geological amnesia, forcing the mass to forget its original position; Sunken Asbestos absorbed displacement shock, preventing the moved block from collapsing into rubble on arrival.

The problem was the last line of the recipe.

Neutralizer: must emit extreme, sustained high temperatures in a fully non-combustible state. Formulation at the alchemist's discretion.

She had stared at the phrase at the alchemist's discretion for approximately ninety seconds the first time she encountered it. It was the most optimistically phrased way she had ever seen a technical manual say you're on your own. Custom-formulating a specialized non-combusting thermal neutralizer on her first practical session was the kind of confidence she had not yet earned. There was only one mathematically viable alternative: she had purchased [Spellfire Flasks].

They ranked among the rarer and more costly entries in the premium arcane supply catalog, and their item documentation explained exactly why. Spellfire was solidified flame in liquid suspension, sealed with beeswax and witch's proprietary binding compound, emitting catastrophic thermal output with no oxygen requirement. Its containment substrate was processed from spatial debris recovered after a defective flask had burned an entire folded dimension for eighty-four days: the only material that had survived the incident was now the only material trusted to contain its cause.

She had purchased five, because four felt optimistic; twenty-five thousand Solars in thermal neutralizers, transferred from their reinforced shock-proof case in the corner to the vacuum-sealed materials safe.

She stepped into the decontamination airlock.

The lever engaged. High-pressure sterilization jets blasted from all angles, evacuating the standard atmosphere and replacing it with the laboratory's proprietary blend: mana-to-oxygen at a precisely maintained ratio of 1:2.6, chemically incapable of combustion, providing perfect oxygenation while maintaining a non-flammable environment. The air smelled faintly of ozone and cold stone. The moment it hit her lungs, the background noise of her own anxiety dropped several decibels and her processing threads sharpened to a clean, crystalline focus.

She stepped out of the airlock and extracted the Spellfire Flasks from the materials safe with both hands. The jelly inside the crystalline vials pulsed with a slow, malevolent crimson glow, perfectly contained. She transferred them into the primary vacuum-sealed manipulation box, lowered the reinforced glass lid, pulled the evacuation lever until the internal atmosphere read zero, and ignited the star-silver crucible.

The first attempt folded without ceremony. She had forgotten to calculate the lunar tide compensation for the amnesia moss powder, and the crucible produced a violent, foul-smelling eruption of grey-green sludge before the secondary containment ward sealed the damage.

The second attempt died at the mandible. She had timed the decomposition sequence four seconds ahead of the manual's prescribed curve, four seconds shorter than a single deep breath, and the mixture refused to accept the re-anchoring scales, hardening instantly into a useless acidic block that smelled of scorched iron and reproach.

The third attempt was the worst, because she could not diagnose it. The thermal gradient simply refused to stabilize: the Spellfire jelly pulsed erratically, the required threshold held for eleven seconds and then collapsed, and the batch died in a slow, futile fizzle. She stared at the residue, reviewing every logged variable, and could not determine with certainty whether the problem was her stirring cadence or a micro-variance in the flask itself. She marked the event as unresolved in the margin of the manual, which felt like admitting defeat to the universe.

The fourth attempt, she introduced the Magnetic-Pole Scales before the mud base had fully sintered, and the sudden repelling force shattered the inner glass lining of the crucible, sending a broad stripe of molten sludge down the front of her laboratory coat. She looked at the bench, then at the coat.

That one was my fault.

She changed into the spare coat hanging by the airlock and returned to the workbench.

The final set of materials was arranged before her.

Hathaway ran a brief diagnostic. Hair: singed at the temples. Laboratory coat: fresh but spiritually compromised. Bank account: in a state of quiet bereavement. Remaining Spellfire Flasks: one. Remaining margin for error: identical.

She pulled up the lunar tide reading on the secondary display and spent forty seconds on the compensation calculation, verifying the output twice. The decomposition timing went on the counter with a four-second correction. The stirring interval from attempt three: adjusted by a quarter-second.

She introduced the mud and waited, watching the surface for the precise visual cue that indicated full sinter. She timed the mandible to the exact microsecond, eyes on the counter with the absolute unblinking focus of a developer watching a build pipeline on its final retry before a hard deadline. She deployed the Spellfire jelly in a controlled, even pour, and held the temperature at the required threshold, compensating for the ambient pressure as the amnesia moss dissolved.

The mixture shifted, hissed, flared unstable orange, and then settled.

The turbulence collapsed into stillness. The amber deepened, warming from pale gold to a rich, dark terracotta, and the thermal radiation from the Spellfire jelly vanished, absorbed cleanly into the stabilized matrix. A faint scent rose from the fume hood: cold stone and ancient soil and an eon of compressed time made briefly, temporarily liquid.

She retrieved the crystalline vial with the specialized tongs, sealed it, and set it carefully on the bench.

She collapsed backward into the massage chair in the corner of the room.

The lumbar support engaged with the smooth, mechanical competence of equipment built specifically to absorb the consequences of the Curie school's pedagogical philosophy. Hathaway stared at the ceiling, her chest working to process the hyper-oxygenated air with the minimum possible effort.

She looked at the amber vial, sitting quiet and steady in the fume hood, its glow unhurried and indifferent.

The most lucrative discipline in the academic catalog, her developer brain assembled, running the final summary with the measured composure of an audit that had sustained significant fire damage but remained technically operational, filters its practitioners by requiring them to survive the tutorial. This is, on reflection, a defensible methodology.

She reached for the recline lever.

Alchemy makes you poor for three generations. The chair tilted back. Fortunately, the Ludwig treasury is well-capitalized. The accounting remains favorable.
