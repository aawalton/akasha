“You just saved our lives.” I stated the fact as I ran, drily, unemotionally.

“Why?” She asked, clearly confused. “You could’ve run at any time, right?”

I looked her over. Properly, this time. Mid-battle, I hadn’t had the chance to look at who we were dealing with.

The Hiscariat, at first glance, looked like any of her kind. Her face was thin to the point of starvation, emaciated body hidden by her long, green robes. Tentacles moved like arms, but smoother, pumping to aid her run.

Yet, enough details from her kin differed to pique the interest. She looked slightly tougher than the average Hiscariat. Her eyes were alert, constantly scouting her surroundings. The daggers on her belt looked well-used, practical rather than the pretty ornamental creations I’d seen in the Outpost. Also, her robes, while the same shade of green as the ones we’d seen inside, had a long slit carved into them, allowing for better run speed.

“Let me start over.” I said. “I’m Liam. This is Victoria and Steve. We are very grateful that you came along at that exact time. What’s your name?”

Her voice inside my head sounded nervous, and a tiny bit shy. “Two-Steps-Left.”

“Two-Steps-Left. I like that name. What were you doing out here among all the Ceaseless?”

“It’s what I do. I’m a Scout.”

I wasn’t exactly sure how emotions worked in mental communication. So far, I was leaning on the side of it working more or less like normal speech. That you could put in what you wanted to show, and the more you trained, the better you were at acting.

This lass was either not good at acting or really good. Her voice showed clear defensiveness and more than just a bit aggression, possibly with a side order of petulance. Over her…wait.

“Correct me if I’m wrong.” Victoria said. “Didn’t Fortune-Unfolds tell us that choosing Scout for a class was frowned upon?”

I nodded. “That, of course, doesn’t mean that you can’t pick Mage for a class and work as a Scout. But I somehow doubt that’s what’s going on here.”

She didn’t react, just ran, face set in a stoic expression.

“Cool. So, you were out scouting, you spotted us and wanted to see what the hell was happening?”

She blinked and turned her head. After a moment, she responded. “Essentially, yes. Your group marked a change to the regular pattern of the Ceaseless. Changes are often bad news. So, I investigated.”

“For which we are grateful.” Steve butted in. “Now, have you guessed what was going on?”

“I… have my suspicions. But I believe I would like to hear you say it.”

“Fortune-Unfolds did something. Used a mental trick, a talent, something, to reach into our minds and push us to do his bidding.” I snorted. “I should’ve seen it. Just a few days back, we were talking about how we weren’t planning anything heroic. Today, we were planning to relax, recuperate, visit your market to see if there was anything worth trading for. It made no sense for us to race straight out and get into a fight to the death – especially without learning more about what we were throwing ourselves into first.”

“Yeah, well, he did a damn fine job at making certain we didn’t speak to anybody first. Convinced us we had to keep it secret until we’d succeeded.” Victoria sniffed. “Carajo, that doesn’t even make sense. If he had to get the builders on board, wouldn’t everybody know what was happening anyway?”

Two-Steps-Left blinked. When she spoke again, it was hesitant, soft. “Information lives. That’s a fact in our society. Anything you want to keep secret, you keep inside your head. Anything else is out there, for all to share. With that being said, I don’t think you appreciate what you’re saying. You claim that Fortune-Unfolds, one of the Holders of the Telvan Outpost, has a talent that grants him mental control over others?”

I snorted. “Claim? I’m outright telling you. We had to use a buff and do drugs to raise our Mental attributes and our Mental Resistance enough to break his hold. Gods. I’m never going to fucking live this down. I owe the Lord O’ Freedom an excuse and the biggest gift basket ever.”

She made a soft keening sound under her breath. “I don’t think you understand.”

“What’s not to understand? The bastard sent us out to die. Now, we’re going back to kick his ass and get him tossed in wizard jail, or… whatever you guys use for punishment.” A horrible thought entered my mind. “Unless mental magic is just another type of magic for all of you? Maybe you approve?”

“NO!” The voice in my mind was a near-shout. “No.” She repeated. “That is very much not the case. The Hiscariat are a defensive race, but we thrive on trade. Domination, brainwashing and mental suggestions are banned, for good reasons. If other species thought we were using that-“

Victoria chuckled darkly. “If.”

“If others suspected us of that, they would refuse to trade with us, render all agreements defunct.”

“Okay, I get that. It’s a big deal. So, we’re going to get the prick punished, you guys shower us with gifts as an excuse, and then we all move on. What’s the problem?”

“Oh.” Steve’s voice sank. “Outside of getting proof that he did it, the problem, obviously, is… what are the odds that we were the first he’d target?”

Two-Steps-Left hid her face behind her tentacles. “Exactly! Fortune-Unfolds has been spending a lot of time with every other Holder. Obviously. It’s part of his duties. But… can we know if he’s used his talents on any of them? Are you the first targets or just the first where it’s failed?”

I blinked. “Yeah. That is a pisser of a situation. So, you’re telling me we shouldn’t go straight in shouting accusations?”

She nodded, before shaking her head. “I’m saying, maybe you shouldn’t go in at all.”

We all spoke up at once.

Two-Steps-Left raised her tentacles in supplication. “Please. Just, pay attention. Say I believe you. Say that Fortune-Unfolds actually has managed to surreptitiously earn a talent that lets him affect other people’s minds. How would you prove it? If you go in there and shout your accusations without proof, odds are good you will simply find yourself exiled, and any hope of an alliance with the Hiscariat ruined.” She looked like she’d eaten a dodgy curry, but kept going. “What if, instead, you went home and got a lot of backups? Then, you’d be able to return later on, from a stronger position. Also, I’m guessing you leveled from this?”

We shared looks, nodding in agreement.

“Good. Then, next time, you will be stronger. More resilient. Victoria there can practice her Mental Communication skill, which will strengthen her argument. If, when you return with a group of equally strong fighters at your back, it will be a lot harder to ignore you. I… could even guide you. Go with you and help. I know a lot about the Torn Divide?” Her voice was hopeful.

“Well, that sure as hell sounds like the sensible choice, dunnit?”

“It would be. Pushing for a confrontation right now is, is, precipitous.”

“And who’s to say that it isn’t Fortune-Unfolds who’s nudged you to think like that?”

“No it isn’t. I-“ She stopped running, face frozen in horror. After a while, she asked. “Do you have more of those drugs you mentioned?”

“I don’t actually think he’s influenced you. You know which level he is?”

“Thirty-two.” The answer came instantly. She expanded. “At least twenty of his talent points are accounted for, through known talents.”

“You guys seriously aren’t lying about sharing.” I shook my head in amazement. “Good. At Level 32, it sounds unlikely that – even if you picked something evil like mental control, you’d get a talent that would be able to affect everybody, all the time. Especially seeing as how we actually earned an increase to Mental Resistance when we broke the effect, and I’m betting most Hiscariat have pretty good Mental Resistance too.” Personally, I’d gotten a +2 the moment his effect on me crashed. It wasn’t much, but I’d take anything that’d make it harder for pricks like him to break my mind again. “We’ll just have to assume that he can’t have affected all of the city, all at once.”

“But why not just walk away?”

I showed her a grin that was closer to a snarl. “Seriously? Because the creep raped our minds. I want him to fucking pay! The only question right now is whether you’re going to close your eyes to it, or you are willing to help bring him to justice?”

Even though Two-Steps-Left was taller than either of us by a whole head, she looked small. Lost. “You don’t understand. Fortune-Unfolds is known. He is popular. Loved. I am… not. Even if, somehow, you manage to reveal him, it would have consequences. Harsh ones.”

Victoria walked up next to her and patted her arm. The Hiscariat shied away, but then relaxed again, morose. Victoria spoke up. “Listen, cariño. There are always consequences, whatever you choose. It is merely a question of whether you want to deal with the consequences of doing what you know what is right, or what is wrong.”

Her mental voice was tired. Even so, I could hear the steel in it. “I’ll do what is needed.”
