Tall marble pillars lined either side of the great hall, stretching upward to support a high ceiling crossed by dozens of arches from one end to the other. Each pillar formed its own arch with the next, and filigree decorated their large, rounded bases. In front of each stood a small statue of a woman, angelic in design, each holding a sword planted into the ground.

Across the long path to the throne was a decorative red carpet that continued up the steps of the dais, stopping just before the royal throne. It was a large seat with cascading patterns and winding curves, leaving its backrest both sturdy and hollow in places. Three tall glass windows stood behind it, framed by flowery steel lines that spilled light in elongated patterns across the hall.

“How long until they arrive?” Evelynn asked. She was seated on the throne, one cheek resting against her fist.

Kael stood a short distance off to her side. “They should be here in a few moments, Princess.” His eyes flicked over to her for a moment. “It would be wise to correct your posture. Slouching is unbefitting of a princess.”

Evelynn remained calm, though no less uninterested. She ignored his words entirely, choosing to sit as unbefittingly as she pleased.

Not long after, sounds came from beyond the throne room.

At the front, the throne room doors opened.

Evelynn lifted her eyes to look, but she made no change to her posture.

Viya entered first, wearing her usual composed expression. Behind her were two others. One was thin, cuffed, and blindfolded. The bindings around his hands were heavy iron restraints. They were unenchanted. According to reports, the man wielded neither mana nor aura.

His clothes were wrinkled and stained with dark patches. His face was pale, with thin lines crossing his cheekbones. Although he had only been captured recently, he looked as if he had been imprisoned for years. It appeared he had been thoroughly tortured and interrogated, frailty mages included.

The other man walked a single pace in front of him. He was taller, broad-shouldered, and toned, though not bulky. His clothes were unique, made for a title and profession that only he held within the city. A brown suit covered his body while a long dark coat draped over his shoulders. A belt hung around his waist, holding several keys and small tools, while black gloves and polished dark boots covered his hands and feet. He even wore a neat tie, though it was barely visible beneath his inner tunic.

Once the three of them reached the center of the room, Viya was the first to speak. “I’ve brought Warden Arenzht and the person in question.”

Warden Arenzht bowed his head. “I greet Your Highness.” Without looking, he placed a hand on the prisoner’s shoulder and forced him to his knees.

The blindfolded man jerked. Not because he was kneeling, but because of what he had just heard. His head flew up toward the Princess, even with his sight impaired.

“Your Highness?” His voice was hoarse, presumably after a night of screams. More importantly, there was a hint of panic within it. He turned toward the Warden. “What’s going on? I was promised a meeting with His Majesty.”

Arenzht’s brow furrowed as he looked down at the man. Before he could speak, the air around the prisoner warped.

A heavy pressure slammed down onto his body. The man who had been kneeling became a man who was begging. The ground didn’t crack; the royal throne room was stronger than that. Strong enough to keep its shape even as a man was pressed into it hard enough to nearly break his bones.

A sharp gasp escaped his throat, his chest crushed down against his knees in a deathly embrace. Luckily, his arms had been raised, the iron bindings on his hands resting above his head. Otherwise, he might have decapitated himself instantly.

Evelynn didn’t move from the throne. No, her cheek still rested lazily against her palm. However, her other arm did not share that same boredom. A slight spark of fire existed there instead. Around her wrist was a thin band of silver, marked with faint golden glyphs that pulsed. The symbols rotated slowly across its surface, releasing a muted hum of mana.

Her eyes narrowed as she stared at the begging man. “Impudent,” she said. Her gaze flicked over to the Warden. “I see you’ve tortured him. Yet you haven’t even taught him the basic manners of speech?” Her eyes tightened even more. “Cattle don’t talk.”

The prisoner trembled against the ground, unable to lift his head.

Warden Arenzht immediately bowed, deeper than before. “Forgive him, Your Highness. His behavior is unacceptable, and I take full responsibility for this error.” His actions were polite and standard, but his voice carried no sign of actual apology. No, it sounded more like a rehearsal. “I heard you require this man to answer your questions, correct? It would be wise not to kill him before you can extract those answers.”

Evelynn stared at him a second longer before releasing the pressure.

The prisoner gasped, finally able to breathe again as the crushing force disappeared.

“You’re right,” Evelynn said. “I’ve heard he’s been very uncooperative with you. Only agreeing to this meeting under the guise of speaking with my father. I would normally execute him, but I suppose answers are more important than the training of an insect.”

Her view of the person continued to dwindle. From cattle to that of a mere bug. To distribute drugs and ruin the lives of others, then still have the gall and arrogance to request the kingdom’s ruler, what else could he be but a parasite?

He was just a pest who did not know his place.

Evelynn lifted her chin and glanced toward the Warden. “Remove the blindfold.”

Arenzht nodded. He gripped the cloth tied around the man’s eyes and pulled it free.

The man blinked rapidly as his eyes adjusted to the light. Not only had he been blindfolded for some time, but the light spilling in from the large glass panes struck his pupils directly. He winced and looked down, gaze fixed on the decorative carpet beneath him.

“There we go. He knows how to show respect,” Evelynn said, amused.

Slowly, he looked up as best he could, before his eyes settled upon the throne and the Princess looking down at him.

“You…” he whispered. “You are not who I asked for. You are not the king.”

“My father is away from the capital for a short trip,” Evelynn said. “For the time being, I am as much the King as my father is.”

The man’s eyes darted around the throne room, searching for something. An exit? A guard? Some unseen figure waiting in the shadows for him, perhaps. Evelynn watched him, noticing every movement. Her irritation shifted into mild understanding. The man wasn’t simply impudent. No, he was, but he was also terrified. Panicked, as if something could kill him at any moment.

Viya, standing beside the man, noticed as well. It was hard not to.

Arenzht clasped his hands behind his back, standing straighter. “As I mentioned before, Your Highness, he has been particularly difficult. He has refused to give any names, locations, or anything of value regarding snowfell. The only words he has spoken since his capture were demands to meet the King.”

The prisoner swallowed, jerking his head back up toward the throne. “Because he’s the only one who can protect me. None of you, not a single one of you can do anything. I need the King. Please. I need him right now!”

His voice shifted from a calm plea into distress. Arenzht stared down at him. It was the most emotion the man had shown since his capture.

Evelynn tilted her head slightly, studying him. “Protect you? Why would my father be the only one who can protect you? You’re before me, inside the King’s castle, guarded by hundreds of Vitian’s strongest, and only seconds away from the royal court mages. Yet you still speak with fear.”

The man clenched his teeth, resisting the urge to speak immediately out of turn. “That is because the King is the only one who can act. Regardless of whether you are the princess, you cannot give what that man wants.”

“Man?” Evelynn repeated.

Viya took a step forward. She adjusted her glasses before speaking. “On the way here, he mentioned that he met a mysterious man who provided him the snowfell, and that he was forced to distribute it. He would only feel comfortable sharing information if granted an audience with His Majesty.”

“Well, unfortunately, my father isn’t here right now. So all you have is me,” Evelynn said. She shifted one leg over the other. “So? What is your name?”

There was a slight pause before he answered, as if judging whether it was wise to identify himself. “It’s… Gilbert.”

“So, Gilbert,” Evelynn said without missing a beat, “why exactly are you in my kingdom, in my very own capital, distributing an illegal drug and giving me a constant headache with paperwork?”

Gilbert pressed his lips together tightly before speaking. “I’m not doing this on purpose. I was threatened. I can’t tell you. I can only tell His Majesty.”

“Why? You’ve already made it this far. You’ve refused interrogation, endured torture, and even tried to kill yourself by biting off your own tongue. Now, you are inside the throne room, speaking to the princess. What more do you need? Either speak, or I’ll send you back with Warden Arenzht and let him continue where he left off.”

The prisoner flinched at the Warden’s name.

Arenzht’s expression didn’t change.

Several moments passed in silence.

Finally, Gilbert spoke. “You don’t understand. The people selling snowfell in the streets don’t know anything. Most of them don’t even care what’s happening, as long as they receive a percentage of the profits from the drugs they distribute.”

“We’ve figured that much,” Evelynn said. “The problem is why the drugs are being spread. What is the goal behind it? Drug dealers are nothing new to any kingdom. However, these things are usually done in the shadows. Done in secret, away from the eyes of the law. But that’s not the case here, now is it?”

Gilbert clenched his teeth. “The… flowers move west.”

Viya glanced down at the man, the sunlight glinting off her glasses and making her expression unreadable.

“Flowers? Are you referring to snowfell?” Evelynn asked.

“The flowers… all move west,” he repeated, the words almost painful to speak.

Evelynn narrowed her eyes. She turned to Viya. “Is snowfell a drug made from a flower?”

Viya turned toward her and pushed up her glasses. “No, Princess. Snowfell is not a direct product of a flower, but a combination of crushed leaves from the winterspine tree and various common minerals, all ground into a powder. There are a few more ingredients, but strictly speaking, snowfell is not flower-based.”

“I have a seed, and when it blooms into a flower… it all goes west.”

Evelynn stared at him, and a wave of understanding washed over her. It had taken a moment, but now she realized what he was doing.

“Who are the owners of these flowers?” she asked.

He opened his mouth, about to speak, but stopped himself. “I… the owners… west.”

“Perhaps their base of operations is in the west?” Arenzht suggested. “Or the ringleaders?”

Viya shook her head. “Why would the ringleaders reveal their location? No, maybe it’s the drop location. The place where the drug is distributed.”

Arenzht hummed. “But you said it yourself. Snowfell isn’t a flower-based drug. Perhaps it has another meaning.”

“I apologize, but I am too poor,” Gilbert said suddenly. “Give and take. I’ll give. Flowers to the garden in the west—”

His voice stopped. Not because he chose to stop speaking, but because he couldn’t. His eyes bulged. He toppled over, a strangled gasp leaving his throat as he tried to breathe.

Everyone in the room froze, staring at him. Viya took a step back. Kael placed his hand on the handle of his sheathed blade. Arenzht stood his ground, staring at the man with furrowed brows.

Gilbert choked. Black veins began appearing across his body, each one trailing upward, crawling along his neck and face. His fingers curled, nails scratching into the carpet beneath him as his body trembled.

Kael immediately stepped forward, placing himself between the man and the princess.

Gilbert’s body began to swell, muscles expanding and pressing tight against his clothes until they started to tear. He was bloating, as if being filled with gallons upon gallons of liquid. When he leaned up, everyone saw him more clearly. His chest rose, and a bubbling sound came from his throat; his body just kept expanding.

Arenzht moved before anything else could happen. He held out his palm, facing the man, and enveloped him in his aura. A dome-shaped bubble formed around Gilbert, not large, but tight and small, imprisoning him. Inside, Gilbert kept growing, as if he were about to explode.

But Arenzht wouldn’t let that happen.

He closed his hand into a fist. The bubble surrounding Gilbert flashed dark red and collapsed. There was no scream; there was only the sound of over a hundred bones breaking in an instant.

Gilbert’s body folded inward all at once, crushed from every direction. Once the sphere disappeared, his remains collapsed onto the carpet as a single wet, mangled lump.

For several seconds, no one spoke. Instead, worry settled across every face in the room.

Arenzht was the first to speak. “A triggered curse.”

“…A very lethal one,” Viya said next.

Kael relaxed, letting go of the handle of his sword. “If the Warden had not acted, who knows how powerful that explosion might have been.”

Evelynn’s gaze shifted toward Arenzht.

The Warden bowed his head. “Forgive me, Your Highness. I did not expect a common criminal to have such a curse placed upon him.”

“…He was cursed to die when he spoke. That is why he spoke in such a roundabout fashion. It seems mentioning flowers wasn’t enough,” Evelynn said.

Viya stepped forward, though she avoided the bloodied mass that had been Gilbert. “That does, however, give us a lot of information. Something is happening out west. And considering that snowfell is circulating among all of Vitian’s cities and towns, it must be very far west, somewhere along the border.”

“Or perhaps past it,” Arenzht chimed in.

Evelynn frowned. She looked over at Arenzht. “Check each prisoner involved with snowfell and see whether they are also cursed. We can’t have bombs walking among the people, nor ones waiting inside our own prison.”

Arenzht bowed. “Yes, Your Highness.”

Evelynn turned toward Viya next. “Investigate whether any settlements have recently been set up along the western border. Do the same for any new groups, no matter how small. Mercenaries, bandits, anyone who has arrived in that area. Send a message to Amelia and have her send us a report if anything suspicious has been happening with the western countries. Mosslavar. Oprus. All of them.”

Arenzht and Viya both bowed. “Yes, Princess.”

Evelynn turned to Kael. “Send my father a message and tell him what happened today.” She pursed her lips. “This might be a bit too much for me to handle. Even more than my father thought.”

Kael bowed. “Of course.”

She finally turned toward the mass of blood lying on the throne room’s carpet. “And… call someone to clean that up.”
