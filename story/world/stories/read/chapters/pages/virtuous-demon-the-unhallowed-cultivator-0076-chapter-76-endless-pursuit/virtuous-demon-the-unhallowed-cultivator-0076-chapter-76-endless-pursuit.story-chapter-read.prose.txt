“Don’t worry, Little Shu. I’ll wrap up the story. It is getting quite late.”

In the depths of his slumber, his vision eclipsed between the boundary of reality and illusions. Vacuous.

“In the end, with the help of the righteous immortals, the very Will of the Heavens struck down to punish the Unhallowed Demon, saving the world once more.”

There was no light, nor dark. No colour, no shape. Only scattered memories of words said by his grandfather.

“Mortals plan, demons scheme, immortals laugh while the heavens reign supreme.”

For now.

“What do you think you’re doing in here?! You need to go back and rest!” Lady Seon, one of the healers of the Sect, said to Ming.

“Take it easy. I’m just here to check up on him. How’s he looking?” Ming ignored her complaints, having walked out from the neighbouring room.

“He’s fine. Just a loss of blood, really. His Spiritual Meridians were acting up to the point I thought he became crippled, but they’ve seemed to have stabilised on their own. He should rest for a week.” Lady Seon packed away some of her surgical tools.

The door suddenly opened wide as Junyu made his way in. “Sorry, I just had to check up on Shuren. The door was already ajar.”

Junyu yawned as he walked in, rubbing his eyes.

“Haven’t seen you in a while. You just come back from a missive as well?” Ming asked.

“You could say that.” Junyu looked at Shuren’s body. “So what exactly happened? It must’ve been pretty bad.”

Ming shook his head. “It’s a long story. Let’s just say we managed to fend off a Rank 3 demonic cultivator.”

Junyu stared at Shuren with a conflicted gaze.

Though they had a small argument months ago, they were still cordial. The incident that happened with the Dezuan Forest, with the risk of a powerful enemy looming in the vicinity.

“Didn’t you say you almost ran into one way back with Shuren?”

“Oh, that. In the Dezuan Forest. There were hints, but we never faced anyone head on, unlike you. I’m still surprised you’re even alive.” Junyu rubbed the back of his head. “I’ll visit again later with some gifts once Shuren’s better. You want any snacks in particular? I’ll be heading to the city.”

“I’ll take you up on that offer.” Ming held a finger to his chin, thinking of one of the new trends he’d been hearing of recently. “Right, have you heard of a ‘tiramisu’? I heard it’s been quite the craze in the Suzaku Empire up north. Apparently, one of the royals there invented it.”

The Suzaku Empire, the main ruling forces of the aptly named Suzaku Continent. A Rank 8 Empire, with enough power to rival clans from The Great Nine. The continent itself was just the base for most of their mortals to reside in, and didn’t include the Immortal Domains that existed high above the continent.

Recently, one of their princes had been causing a fuss in the empire. To the point his reputation has begun to spread all the way to even Cloud City.

“I find it hard to believe a royal of the Suzaki Empire is out there inventing new dishes. I’ve seen them once. They’re even more pretentious than Immortals. At least Immortals have the power to back up their arrogance. But I’ll take a look, just for you, my friend.”

As Heaven’s Eye roused, its grace of light entered through the windowsill. An ephemeral cycle that persisted, yet always comforted all life that the dawn of day had begun. But on this morning, Shuren lay on his soft bed for a moment longer.

Using his arm to support his upper body, and raising himself up felt excruciatingly slow. His blue eyes were half-lidded, not out of drowsiness, but rather, refusal to acknowledge the day.

The shirt he wore clung to his skin from the sweat, and his lustrous blond hair was now a mess, tangled and dishevelled. But he ignored the discomfort of his body, instead focusing on the discomfort within his soul.

Thoughts turmoiled in his mind as he stared at his right hand, the one used to kill people yesterday. Something’s off. This unease I’ve been feeling, I’m missing something, something that should be obvious.

Shuren thought back to the incident.

When he dared to fight against a Rank 3.

His gamble had paid off.

“I managed to use the sword. But I almost died in return for those two basic slashes. And even then…”

Shuren observed his inner body.

His cultivation level, which was at the peak of Stage 10, almost about to enter Stage 11, had dropped.

To the beginning of Stage 10.

“If I go on this route, I’ll never reach Rank 2. Pursuing the sword will lead me to a dead end. And maybe even death.” Shuren’s head slumped down.

His mind was filled with disappointment and despair.

“What do I do next? I’m taking one step forward, and yet I stumble two steps backwards. The more I waste time like this, the further I get away from my goal. And even after all that…” Shuren groaned with a dry throat, the saliva sticking to the roof of his mouth.

The death of the one man he begrudgingly saved, humiliated pathetically to the last second. Shuren wasn’t sure to believe the story of his origins, but a gut feeling made him reconsider.

And the words of that woman he had once killed, whose words he couldn’t forget.

It was all for naught.

The lives of the weak are mere dross for the demons. Not even their own kin, nor allies, were spared.

His right hand, bruised at the wrist, trembled with indignation. When he thought back to that white-haired snake bastard, a scourge from a demonic sect with silver words of grandeur, the trembling turned to shivers of a violent emotion.

The trembling enclosed fist slammed against the wall of his room, crumbling it bit by bit. Shuren couldn’t even hear the thud of his fist smashing into the wall, as his thoughts welled into a storm of swords, slicing his rationality to serve his searing anger.

“That cursed snake, dares to humiliate me?! I, the scion of the Liu Clan, the brandished sword of all immortals that serves to slay all demons? If I don’t skin that snake alive and boil him over flames, then my name isn’t Liu Shuren! I swear on the name of the Sword Saint!” Shuren’s eyes stared at his hands as if he were-, no, he was looking at a pathetic weakling.

And with the realisation that his fury and declaration only came from the comfort of his bed, Shuren laughed.

A guttural, demeaning laugh.

“As expected, I still haven’t learnt my lesson. Here I am, pretending to be some righteous hero, when I haven’t even accomplished the first goal of inheriting the mantle of my grandfather,” he said in a hoarse, dry tone.

“I still have a trump card. The deal I have regarding the Throne Of Blades. In the worst-case scenario, I can…” Shuren shook his head.

“No. I can’t. I need to try my best for a few more years at least. I can’t give up this early.” Shuren thought back to his oath on the Granxian Continent, and the deal he made with the new Patriarch of the Liu Clan.

He thought back to his mother and father, and his younger sister and brother.

If he was already on the verge of giving up now, then what about them?

If he managed to succeed the Throne Of Blades, all of their lives would be much better.

He’d finally be able to force the Liu Clan to spend their resources on healing his father.

His mother wouldn’t have to work as an External Elder just to get some more scraps for the family.

His siblings wouldn’t have to be sent off to minor irrelevant and weak people to be mentored and taught.

And most of all, his family would once more have the dignity and status they once had.

Where all of them would become Immortals, and enter the world of Immortals once again.

He stood up from his bed, but Shuren didn’t go to grab a jug of water. Even though each step was excruciating, with all of his wounds flaring up, Shuren still walked. Rather, he stared at his Ruyi Staff at his side. The slicing sound of metal screeching outside the decrepit sheath resounded in his room, and Shuren held the sword up with trembling hands.

With felt the deep grooves of the engravings in the Ruyi Staff. And with that, he swore an oath.

"Either I live, or that demonic sect does. One of us must be completely destroyed." And with that declaration, silent to all but himself, he placed the staff back.

He looked out the window, gazing at the outside world.

“I have to make the choice soon.”

“Whether to waste even more time trying to pursue the sword.”

“Or give it up.”
