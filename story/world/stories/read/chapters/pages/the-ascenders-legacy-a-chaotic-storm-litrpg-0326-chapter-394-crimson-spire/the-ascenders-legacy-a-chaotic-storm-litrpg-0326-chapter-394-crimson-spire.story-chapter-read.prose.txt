The shrill sound of the bartender’s scream snapped everyone out of their shock. And in an instant, more screams rose, shock and horror spiking into full-blown panic.

Aldric reacted on instinct, several strands of Boundless Empathy bursting out of him to get everyone under control. The screams died in their throats almost instantly, panic giving way to stunned confusion, but it was already too late.

Those outside the bar had already heard the screams. Even worse, they could see the grotesque display of blood, viscera, and brain matter scattered across the bar.

They could see…

Aldric’s gaze strayed to the gaping hole in the bar wall and his eyes narrowed in understanding.

Up until that moment, he had feared Boundless Empathy had somehow broken an oath Alan had made, thus killing him. But it was obvious now that this wasn’t a death by oath.

It was an attack. One none of them had seen coming.

Fortunately, while he had reacted by managing the emotions of those within the bar, Geneva and Daruk had chased after the attacker. Whatever suspicions they’d had that something was terribly wrong with this city had just gained proof.

What kind of person murdered someone in broad daylight while a champion sat only a few feet away?

Grimacing, he glanced around the bar at the perplexed faces of all those present and let his senses brush against the emotions in the room.

Fear. Shock. Confusion.

Those were the most prevalent ones.

Aside from himself, there were only three other people in the bar, and their emotions churned together like a storm threatening to break. Aldric peered deeper, searching for a hint of satisfaction, triumph, or murderous intent.

Anything at all that could hint at a spy or collaborator.

But he found none. Whoever had killed Alan wasn’t standing among them.

Sighing, he raised a hand to reassure them. “Everyone, stay calm. I know that was horrifying to witness, but my friends are already pursuing whoever did this. You’re safe for now.”

The bartender nodded mutely, and with another sigh, Aldric turned toward the exit, wanting to see how he could assist. However, the moment he turned, an idea came to mind, and he paused.

He glanced back at the bartender a moment later, brows furrowed in curiosity. “What is your name?”

The bartender stuttered, wringing her apron. “I… Agnes. My name is Agnes, champion.”

Aldric smiled. “Nice to meet you, Agnes. I’m Aldric. Please, get me a cloth to cover this mess.”

Agnes glanced at Alan’s corpse and promptly produced a wide cloth, gagging.

The cloth settled over Alan’s body a moment later, hiding the worst of the gore. Dusting his hands, Aldric glanced back at the two other women in the bar, both of whom seemed to have been enjoying an afternoon meal before all the chaos.

Spreading his arms wide to make himself seem less threatening, Aldric took a stool and sat, facing the two women.

“Hi,” he smiled lightly, reassuringly. “What are your names?”

“Roselyn.” “Sylvia.”

Both women responded at the same time.

Aldric nodded. “Nice to meet you both. I apologize for all this… commotion.” He glanced at Agnes. “Tell me, is this a common occurrence in Ironfel?”

The women exchanged uneasy glances, and a moment later, Sylvia shook her head. “It wasn’t always, champion Aldric.” She glanced at her friend. “But this is the second time it has happened this week alone.”

While Aldric subtly extracted information from the women, Geneva floated high in the sky above the bar, gigantic runes blooming outward from her in search of the attacker.

The runes filled the sky in seconds, spreading across several miles in every direction before hardening into a rigid formation. Golden light descended from them, then, like falling walls, sealing the area in an impenetrable barrier a mile around the bar and cutting off any means of movement or teleportation.

If people had been panicked before, they were downright terrified now.

But Geneva didn’t care.

She clenched her fist, and the runes above burst with blinding brightness as she seized upon the energy signature the attacker had left behind. It was almost imperceptible—a testament to the attacker’s control—but it was enough to track, and Geneva followed it mercilessly, anger simmering in her chest.

What did it say of this city that an assassin had murdered someone right before her in broad daylight? What did it say of her that she hadn’t noticed them until the last possible moment?

Fuming, she pushed the search runes to their limit, pouring more and more quintessence into the array until finally—

She found it.

A spot of crimson in the sea of gold.

Geneva didn’t hesitate. The moment she locked onto the signature, the search formation shifted, countless beams of golden light converging upon a single point several streets away.

The crimson spot began moving immediately, streaking across the city with startling speed.

But Geneva wasn’t so easily evaded.

With a single flick of her wrist, entire sections of the city lit up beneath the assassin’s feet—momentum locks, spatial anchors, energy drains—all snapping into place at once.

The crimson streak stilled. And then, finally, Geneva moved.

She teleported forward in an instant, reappearing directly above the trapped figure. The assassin’s head snapped up, arms and legs locked in place by the massive array of runes shimmering beneath her feet.

Their gazes met.

Crimson irises widened, and the assassin shuddered with undisguised fear.

Daruk arrived a heartbeat later, a blistering chill roiling off him as he closed in from the opposite side. The assassin’s gaze snapped toward him, then back to Geneva, searching desperately for an exit that wasn’t there.

“Who are you?” Geneva growled, her lips twisted into a sneer as she descended to the ground and stopped before the struggling figure, the runes tightening around the assassin at her words.

The assassin inhaled sharply but said nothing. Made no sound of pain.

Geneva didn’t bother wasting time with more words. She grabbed the assassin by the face and ripped off their mask—revealing the face of a girl barely older than Daruk himself. She had a full head of crimson hair braided in a fashion Geneva didn’t recognize, and a sharp jaw that hinted at defiance even through her terror.

She looked almost normal at first glance. Only she wasn’t quite normal.

Or human, even.

“What in Raol’s name…” Geneva whispered, her frown deepening as she took in the girl’s absurd features. Beside her, Daruk’s eyes narrowed in confusion.

The assassin may have started out as human. But she wasn’t anymore.

Her skin was the color of chalk, and beneath it, faint crimson wires glimmered like runes—pulsing with magical energy yet obviously technological. Tiny crystalline implants protruded from behind her ears, and though she possessed no rings around her irises, they still glowed with faint crimson light.

A modified human.

Geneva’s hands slackened immediately.

She would be lying if she said she hadn’t come across modified awakened before, especially among those who had no soul seeds. A reinforced skeletal frame. A grafted limb. Simple modifications made to supplement what their cultivation couldn’t provide.

But this was nothing so simple.

This wasn’t a mere appendage or a borrowed enhancement. It was a modification that extended far into full biological reengineering.

Because the woman before her was no awakened. She was a sleeper—yet she moved and fought as though she had powers of her own.

“By Raol,” Daruk whispered. “Is this what I think it is?”

Geneva swallowed.

The altering of sleepers was a serious crime, hated and judged just as fiercely as necromancy. Whoever had done this had broken at least a dozen laws. And whoever had commissioned it had more resources than any common criminal.

Geneva’s gaze met the assassin’s once more. The fear hadn’t vanished from those crimson eyes—but something else flickered in them now.

Hatred.

Geneva felt her stomach tighten. She wanted to ask who had done this to her. Whether there were others. Whether there was still time to save them. But as her gaze shifted lower—to the insignia stitched into the assassin’s robes—those questions died in her throat and a chill overtook her.

A Crimson Spire.

Beside her, Daruk inhaled sharply, his eyes widening as his gaze fell on the emblem of those who had held Lutia in bondage of the Limit for the last few centuries. He glanced at Geneva, but the champion looked even more concerned than he felt. His gaze met the assassin’s crimson ones then, and a biting chill crawled down his spine.

What was an Attilan assassin doing in Ironfel?

And more importantly—why had she murdered Alan?

Just what was going on in this city?

Back in the Storm Sect, Aodhán, Lilian, and Crixis were finally returning to the special sect after their trip to the core sect. With Lysera still recovering, Aodhán had decided to postpone his training until the following day and give her time to fully heal.

“Thank you, Elder Vaust,” he said to Lilian, bowing slightly as they reached the special sect’s entrance. “I don’t think today would have gone as it did without you.”

Lilian smiled gently. “It is my pleasure, special disciple Aodhán. I hope you learn what you want to and become even more… monstrous—because apparently, you’re not monstrous enough yet.”

Aodhán smirked. “I hope so too.”

Lilian left then, returning to the elder sect, and Aodhán turned to Crixis with a proud smile.

“So,” he said. “How does it feel to beat an inheritor?”

Crixis grinned. “It feels pretty great. I…” He shook his head. “I don’t know. Perhaps because I’m around you and the other specials so often, I’ve underestimated my own strength.”

“That makes sense.” Aodhán nodded, then reached out and gripped his shoulders firmly. “I’m proud of you, and I’m glad you won. But today proves that there are many who would go to considerable lengths to have what you have — to occupy the position you hold as my underling.”

He smiled lightly. “You have enemies now too, Crixis. You must be careful.”

“I will be,” Crixis responded, and the sobriety in his voice made Aodhán smile.

“Good. Now I should probably have a meal and a nap.” He grimaced. “I’m not certain I’ve had either in the last three days.”

“I’ll fix something up,” Crixis said.

They arrived at the mansion a few minutes later to find Sophran and Halo seated on the porch, the former focused intently on a notepad. Scraps of shriveled paper lay scattered around him, and Aodhán grinned.

It seemed Sophran was having some runic troubles.

Sophran looked up and hastily rose to his feet. “Hey, where have you been? I’ve been waiting out here for nearly an hour now.”

“I’m sorry,” Aodhán replied with a chuckle. “I was meeting someone in the core sect.”

He glanced down at the rune Sophran had been drawing and pressed his lips together to keep from laughing. A teacher shouldn’t mock their students—it was a fine way to chase them off.

But Aodhán couldn’t help himself.

He burst out laughing a moment later, and even Crixis chuckled.

“It’s not funny,” Sophran scowled. “You were the one who got me interested in this in the first place. And now it’s twisting my mind in circles.”

“I know, I’m sorry,” Aodhán managed between chuckles. “But you’re enjoying it, aren’t you?”

Halo yipped, while Sophran’s scowl deepened.

“I would enjoy it more if it wasn’t so convoluted.”

Aodhán pressed his lips together again, privately wondering how Sophran would react when he reached the twenty-fourth rune, if the fourth was already giving him this much trouble.

“Don’t worry,” he said instead, still smiling. “Come inside. I’ll walk you through it.”

Halo bounded in first, yipping excitedly in a way that showed she wanted to meet Varéc.

Aodhán summoned the Fury from his spirit, and a moment later, both familiars were yipping and growling at each other in the entryway. Crixis and Sophran followed them in. But just as Aodhán moved to shut the door, reality twisted—and suddenly, a man stood on his front porch.

He was draped in a robe of the deepest black, his skin dark as night, his runic irises blazing like twin suns. Aodhán couldn’t sense his core, but he didn’t need to. The heaviness the man’s presence exerted on the world was answer enough.

He bowed immediately. “Good evening, Calamity. How can I help you?”

His words drew Sophran and Crixis back to the doorway, and an instant later they stood at his side, bowing deeply.

The Calamity stared at them for a moment before nodding. “Good evening, specials. You may rise.”

They did.

The man smiled lightly, though for some reason, Aodhán didn’t think it was amusement.

His gaze settled on Aodhán. “The High Elders request your presence, Aodhán Brystion. Come with me.”

Aodhán froze, his brows creasing in concern. It was never a good thing to be summoned by the High Elders. More likely than not, it meant the summoned was in trouble.

He frowned curiously. “I… if you don’t mind me asking, Calamity. What for?”

“You’ll find out when we get to the elder hall.”

Aodhán nodded slowly, but just as he stepped forward, Sophran’s voice echoed from behind him.

“I’ll come with you,” he said, the statement phrased more as a question.

The Calamity turned toward him, but said nothing. When it was obvious he had nothing against the idea, Crixis spoke up too.

“I’ll come too.”

In response, the Calamity snapped a finger, and a platform materialized—large enough to fit the three of them. And without hesitation, they all climbed onto it.

The last time he had seen the High Elders had been weeks ago, following the ascension of Calamity Ghaltris, when they had gathered to discuss his immunity to the twisted effect. Had they finally found answers?

With another snap from the Calamity, they shot forward—not through space, but through distance itself—arriving at the elder hall in an instant as though the miles between had simply ceased to exist.

They walked into the hall a moment later, and Aodhán instinctively released his aura to shield Crixis from the crushing presence of the High Elders.

His gaze found Zarxan’s immediately, and worry curled in his gut.

Frowning, he bowed deeply. “High Elders, you summoned me.”

“Yes,” the First Seat said. He glanced briefly at Zarxan before continuing, his expression carrying a weight Aodhán didn’t like. “An urgent matter has arisen, and we need your help.”

He paused.

“We need you to leave the Sect.”
