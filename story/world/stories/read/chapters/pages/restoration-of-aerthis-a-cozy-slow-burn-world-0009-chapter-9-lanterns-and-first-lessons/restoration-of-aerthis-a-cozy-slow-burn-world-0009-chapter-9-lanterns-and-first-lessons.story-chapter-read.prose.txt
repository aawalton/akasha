The sun had barely crested the rooftops when Elynara slipped out from her room, her notebook tucked neatly under one arm. The warmth of the covers still clung to her sleeves, but the quiet resolve in her steps kept her from turning back. Another day had begun, and today, she intended to start it with answers.

There was still a half-silence throughout the rest of the church, shifting between morning chores and full activity, and the early light cast a soft gold glow down the hallway. Rather than wait to be swept up in tasks, she made her way to the library.

The small wooden door yielded with a familiar creak. Inside, the same faint scent of dust, ink, and wood polish drifted around her like a greeting. The nun behind the desk offered a mild nod over her reading glasses but said nothing as Elynara moved past her usual spot.

She didn’t reach for anything specific. Just took down a handful of the older-looking books, some cloth-bound and frayed at the edges, others with cracked leather. Their spines creaked in protest as she set them gently onto the desk. One by one, she opened them, squinting at the faded ink and narrow script.

A small travelogue yielded her first find: a scattered list of creatures seen near the outer trails of the forest.

“Slimes – common vermin, cling to shaded hollows and anywhere wet. Nuisance to boots. Unpleasant to blades.”“Bristlebacks – boar-like, quick to anger. Avoid the bristles.”“Crooktail lynx – solitary, territorial, found on mountain sides. Avoid when dusk draws.”

Elynara copied down what little she understood, keeping her writing tight to conserve space. She found mentions of alchemical materials such as powdered horn, slick slime gel, and bloodgrass roots in another book, which was some kind of herb-and-creature field journal.

And then, tucked between dense text on forest routes and old trails, a vaguer entry caught her attention.

“...beyond the edge of the green, westward where the dust takes hold… lands once verdant now devour mana like rot. Few return. Some say even mana dies there.”

A chill prickled her neck. She paused, reading that line twice more before copying it word for word into her notebook.

For the next hour, she worked diligently, sifting through loose observations and fragmentary passages. Notes scattered between poorly sorted sections. A complaint about indexing bubbled up in her thoughts more than once. But even so, she filled half a page with what felt like useful, if piecemeal, information.

Eventually, with a quiet sigh, she stacked the books neatly and slid them back into their places.

On her way out, she offered the nun a slight nod. No words needed.

Today was far from over. And she still had something she wanted to try.

As she stepped outside, Elynara thought about how it had been quite some time since she started practicing with her staff. It became a familiar part of her arsenal, and a helpful one at that. Her casting time and even stability of her mana shapes improved.

And it was finally time for her to try casting something she had been meaning to since her unlucky encounter near the forest. Her fire spell was very crude then, but what about now?

Elynara rolled her shoulders once, feeling the familiar tension settle in her arms.

The little garden behind the church had become something of a personal haven by now. Its solitude, broken only by the hum of bees and the rustle of distant footsteps, offered the perfect place to focus.

She planted her feet lightly on the grass, took her staff in both hands, and centered her breathing.

“In,” she whispered to herself, pulling in a slow breath. “And out.”

Mana followed in a steady stream, a far cry from her first attempts. Guiding it out of her core, she let it settle into the staff until it seemed to prickle her slightly, and then began shaping.

The first orb sparked to life between her palms, flickering at the edges, but holding. She held it a moment, adjusting the friction between the strands. Then let it fly.

It splattered against the boulder she picked as her target with a solid whoomp, exploding and flaring softly on impact, leaving a charred mark behind.

The second orb took better shape. It was brighter, tighter, more refined. She cast it low, letting it arc toward the stone. It struck with a sharp thump, embers blooming and quickly dying in the damp grass.

That one had more weight to it, and impacted with visible force.

“Again.”

This time she took her time. Drew the mana slower, allowing her grip on the staff to steady her focus. The wooden length hummed gently beneath her fingers, helping her smooth the flow before it could fray.

The orb formed bright and clear. Small, but dense. She held it still for a moment, adjusted the internal friction, and… let it fly.

It struck her target near the garden’s edge with a sharp crack, the following small explosion cracking the stone at impact point, a puff of smoke curling up from it.

She blinked. It wasn’t just stable, but stronger than anything she had managed before.

“…That felt good.”

And as excitement and tension of her testing started leaving her, she took note of something she had not seen in quite a while.

The blinking dot hovered patiently at the corner of her vision.

With a small breath, Elynara reached for it.

【You have leveled up!】

Now it was her time to blink. She didn’t know what to expect but that somehow wasn’t it.The dot hasn’t disappeared yet though, so she did the next best thing and checked her Status:

Name: ElynaraLevel: 2(+1)Mana: 16 / 34(+1.3/h)(+0.2/h)

Stats:Fitness: 8Agility: 9Willpower: 13(+1)Intuition: 11

Skills:Mana Manipulation – Lvl 2(+1)Mana Sense – Lvl 1

Traits:Mana Attuned – A body attuned to mana generates and absorbs it more efficiently. Gains an intuitive sense for mana flow and structure.Spirit Sight – Ability to perceive spirits and lingering magical traces. Its strength grows with use and focus.

Elynara let the screen fade, a quiet sense of progress blooming in her chest. Though followed by some confusion. Her Mana Manipulation leveled up, her level increased, and so did her Willpower and Mana Regen.

The improvements were small, but noticeable. And she was glad to take whatever advantages came her way. But if the Level up was supposed to do anything else, she didn’t see it.

More questions for later.

She sat down in the grass, brushing the hem of her tunic aside so it wouldn’t bunch beneath her legs. Starpuff, who had been floating nearby watching her training, zipped down and booped the staff before settling across her lap like a smug cat.

With her core now partly drained again, she formed a slow-floating orb of mana and flicked it toward Starpuff, who pounced and gobbled it up in a glowing ripple of satisfaction.

She giggled, warm and winded.

Her breathing slowed, steady again. The training had paid off.

And though she still had the rest of the day ahead, for now, this small victory felt more than enough. She barely held another thought before…

“Wooooah!”

A sudden cheer from behind nearly made her jump out of her skin.

She turned just in time to see a blur of motion; half a dozen children spilling into the garden path like a miniature stampede. They surged around her, wide-eyed and breathless.

“That was amazing!”“Did you throw that fireball?!”“Are you a real mage now?”“Can you do lightning too?!”“Will you teach me?”

Elynara blinked, flustered and very much not prepared for this.

“I–what? No! I mean, yes, that was me, but–”

“Was it fake magic? Looked real!”

“Are you a hero now?” one boy asked, his eyes comically round.

She raised her hands in half-surrender.

“No, no, nothing like that. It’s just… simple magic. I’ve been practicing.”

But the kids weren’t listening. A few had already broken off and made a beeline for the boulder, swarming it like it was some holy relic.

“Look at the crack!”“She really blew it up!”“I’m gonna be a fire mage too!”

One of the boys scrambled on top of the rock, striking a dramatic pose with a stick held aloft.

“Fear not, citizens! I have defeated the evil tree demon with my flaming fists!”

Elynara stifled a groan, then quickly stepped forward, waving them back.

“Okay, okay, off the boulder, please! It’s still warm, and if anyone falls, I’m going to get blamed!”

There were a few groans and pouts, but they clambered down without much fuss. One girl tugged at her sleeve.

“Will you show us again tomorrow?”

Elynara hesitated, then offered a sheepish smile.

“Maybe. But not all at once, alright?”

They cheered anyhow, shouting fictitious incantations and throwing imaginary fireballs at each other, because apparently a "maybe" was as good as a promise. They dispersed as quickly as they had arrived.

She sighed, brushing ash from her sleeve.

“I didn’t think I would cause a parade…”

“Should’ve seen their faces.”

Mina’s voice came from the side, light with amusement. Elynara turned to find the girl leaning just inside the gate, Pufflet in her arms, a playful glint in her eye.

“They looked ready to name you Fire Queen of Oltenar.”

Elynara groaned softly.

“Please don’t let them run with that.”

Mina giggled and walked over, and together they watched the children vanish around the corner in a blur of laughter and smoke-tailed illusions. Elynara was still a little pink-faced, but her smile was genuine.

Somehow, it was hard to be too embarrassed around Mina.

They lingered in the garden even after the kids had vanished, the soft rustle of wind through the trees replacing their laughter. With the warmth of that spell still tingling in her fingertips and the faintest trace of a grin still visible on her cheeks, Elynara wasn't ready to go back inside just yet.

Mina had wandered over to a patch of grass shaded by the garden wall, Pufflet curled beside her like a living pillow. The creature’s little paws flexed as it stretched, one ear flicking lazily.

When Elynara drifted closer, Mina looked up and, without a word, scooted over to make space.

She sat with a quiet sigh, letting her legs fold beneath her. Starpuff floated down to hover just over her shoulder, seemingly content to join her moment of rest. If its faint pulsing and softer glow was anything to go by, it was still satisfied from her previous training.

Mina leaned over and picked up a small twig. “Want to help tire him out before the next batch of kids shows up?”

Pufflet’s ears perked instantly, tail flicking. His eyes followed the twig like a hawk.

Elynara smirked. “Sure.”

Mina tossed the twig in a low arc across the grass. Pufflet was off like a bolt, tiny claws scrabbling for traction, his puffball body bouncing awkwardly but determinedly as he chased it down.

Elynara giggled under her breath. “He’s faster than he looks.”

“Too fast sometimes,” Mina said, brushing her skirt. “He gets into trouble when he’s bored.”

Elynara reached for another twig and mimicked Mina’s throw. Her arc was a little sharper, but Pufflet still darted after it with a delighted yip.

Starpuff, watching, pulsed with quiet interest.

With a sidelong glance, Elynara picked up a twig and tossed it toward Starpuff without looking.

The sprite didn’t even pretend to entertain the idea. It hovered slightly to the side, letting the twig pass through its green glow without so much as a twitch.

Elynara tilted her head. “Jealous thing…”

Starpuff responded with a slow orbit around her head, like it knew exactly what it was being accused of.

Mina snickered.

“Your spirit companion wants some attention too?”

“Yeah… He’s got moods, I think. Doesn’t talk or anything. Just… vibes.”

“But he listens to you though, no?”

“More than he lets on, maybe.”

They tossed a few more sticks. Pufflet kept bringing them back without tiring, though he eventually flopped dramatically at Mina’s feet, panting with theatrical effort.

The girls sat back, letting the afternoon warmth settle over them. Starpuff bobbed gently in the shade, and for a time, neither of them spoke.

Elynara ran her thumb along the edge of her staff, thoughts swirling, finally asking a question swirling in her head since earlier.

“Hey… do you know much about the System?”

Mina blinked, turned to look at her.

“Not really. I mean… it just works, I guess?”

Elynara nodded slowly.

“Father Alaric says levels are tied to the soul.”

Mina added after a moment, her gaze drifting upward.

“He said something about it measuring your strength, guiding in little ways, and helping you remember what you learned. So you never forget any Skill.”

Elynara’s brows lifted slightly.

“Huh.”

Mina stretched her arms over her head, before continuing.

“That’s what I remember, anyway. And that the System used to do more, or at least that’s what the really old books claim. Like his grandfather’s grandfather times old! Or something like that.”

Elynara absorbed that in silence. And after a pause to digest the revelations, she smiled to herself and leaned back on her hands.

“Thanks.”

Mina just hummed, pulling a daisy out of the grass and starting to pluck the petals one by one.

The talk faded, and they sat with the quiet again, not needing to fill it. Their companions, one a puff of white fluff and the other of green light, either napped or floated peacefully nearby.

After a while, Elynara rose with a stretch, brushing loose grass from her skirt.

“I should practice a bit more before heading back.”

She murmured, mostly to herself. Though Starpuff gave a lazy spin as if approving the idea. Looking to the side, she offered Mina a brief smile.

“Thanks for the company.”

Mina nodded, stroking Pufflet’s fur as he blinked up sleepily.

“See you around.”

Elynara turned and walked back toward her usual training spot near the garden’s edge. The boulder she had struck earlier still bore the blackened crack from her spell, a faint curl of ash clinging to the edges. The wall beyond it showed its age, stones chipped and patched with uneven mortar.

She took her place again, staff in hand.

A steady breath in. Then out.

The flow of mana came even easier now, her familiarity with the fire spell growing. She shaped it with care, focused it cleanly through her staff, and let it twist outward with intent.

The orb flickered to life, bright and controlled, and she cast it toward the far boulder. It struck true, a small explosion and a puff of smoke rising where it hit.

A second cast followed. Even smoother, and quicker. Hitting with the same precision and result.

She smiled, glad at how reliable it has become.

Satisfied, she let her shoulders relax, and sank down onto the grass, staff laid gently across her knees. With a steadying breath, she closed her eyes and let her mind quiet.

Thinking about her advancing prowess, and how quick and reliable it has become, she felt ready. To prove to herself she can handle the dangers of the world.

Well, at least some of them!

And what better time to confirm that but when her motivation is the highest?

Taking a deep breath, she calmed her racing thoughts. The direction had been decided. But first, she had to prepare. The training just now used quite a bit of her mana, so recovering it was a must.

It would be one silly way to go, to be caught up in trouble while capable but on an empty tank!

Meditation came quickly to her; it was almost like second nature already. And with the help of her staff she no longer had to spend hours recovering. It still took time, but no longer demanded her full focus.

She could feel her core filling gradually, each breath pulling in just a little more.

A few birds chirped somewhere overhead. Starpuff hovered quietly nearby, for once not demanding attention. She appreciated the peace.

By the time her reserves felt full, her course was already decided.

Raising up, she brushed the dirt off her skirt, before meticulously checking on her gear. She slid her notebook back into its spot in her satchel. Secured the pouch, and tightened the strap of her staff.

With everything in place, she let out a final quiet breath and turned toward the town, her destination obvious.

The remaining hours of day were not something she wanted to waste.

She was stronger now, a little more capable. And eager to put that to use.

The trip to the guild felt entirely natural to her now. It was almost scary how fast one could get used to routines. Hardly a week of this and she could almost make the trip with her eyes closed already!

Such silly thoughts kept her occupied the entire way there, and she had to blink them away to refocus.

When Elynara entered the guild hall, it was quiet, the early afternoon sun illuminating the worn floorboards with long shafts of gold. The quiet murmur of conversation lingered in the background, but nothing felt rushed.

It was the sort of peace that settled in after the day’s first wave had passed.

She moved toward the request board, her steps slower than before. Her eyes scanned the available listings, but only a few remained. Another herb gathering request sat pinned near the center.

Her hand hovered over it for a moment longer than necessary. She exhaled, steadying herself, and took the sheet.

It was the same type of job. But this time she wasn’t walking out with nothing but optimism and inexperience.

Turning around, she found Alicia already watching her from behind the counter.

Quickly heading over, she handed her the form with a small word of greeting.

Alicia scanned it with her usual speed. Her pen tapped once against the page before she spoke.

“You’re sure?”

“I am. I’ll keep to the safer paths this time.”

Alicia nodded, marking something on the form.

“You know the signs to watch for now, so just remember to pull back in case of any issues. Better to try another day, as they say.”

Once she saw her nod in assent, Alicia handed the processed sheet back, fingers brushing the edge of the page.

“Just remember to return before dusk.”

After another enthusiastic nod and some quick goodbyes, Elynara turned and tucked the paper into her satchel before leaving the guild.

It was time to get going!

The sun hung high in the sky as Elynara walked down the road leading toward the gate. The passing clouds made its glare a pleasant one.

She stopped, taking a moment to just breathe. The air smelled faintly of dust, woodsmoke, and late summer grass.

A long inhale and exhale later, she was on her way again.

Adjusting the strap of her satchel, she tapped her staff once against the packed earth, more out of rhythm than necessity. The gate was just ahead, open and with only one person guiding it at this hour.

Looking beyond the gate, the path beyond stretched into the distance. The forest was just out of reach, not visible yet.

Last time's incident flashed before her eyes as she stopped for a brief moment, but quickly squashed the nervousness down.

It was another herb request, sure.

Only this time, she was ready.

Or at least, more ready than before.

And that was when Starpuff picked the absolute perfect moment to land directly on the bridge of her nose.

Her eyes crossed. Her mouth opened in a half-formed protest.

“Wh–hff! Not now–!”

Too late.

She sneezed.

Stumbling half a step, she swatted gently at the air, catching nothing but her own indignation.

Starpuff twirled upward in a lazy spin, utterly pleased with itself.

Elynara narrowed her eyes, sniffed once, and sighed.

“Why you little…”

Her words faded into a quiet laugh. She straightened her shoulders, nudged her pouch up again, and stepped through the gate without fanfare.

The sprite drifted after her, a faint green flicker in the light.

And just like that, she was off once more. The tap of her staff followed her down the road.

One step at a time.
