CHAPTER 4

"The first kill teaches nothing about combat. It teaches everything about the person holding the weapon." - Codex of the Long Watch, Field Observation, 21st Century

Arthur followed Donna’s gaze into the alley beside the pub. The passage was about three metres wide, flanked by the Grapes on one side and the windowless brick wall of an office building on the other. Overflowing bins lined the wall and a broken pallet leaned against a downpipe. The sky cast just enough purple light to turn the wet concrete into a dull mirror, and in that mirror, something was moving.

It made a low and rhythmic scraping sound, like a heavy object being dragged across stone. And then Arthur heard a sound that he had no reference for. A wet, uneven rasp that sounded like breathing forced through a throat that had not been designed for air.

"Maybe, somebody is hurt," Steve said from behind them, reaching for the most rational explanation available. "The crash we heard earlier, someone could be trapped."

Donna didn't respond. But her free hand had moved to the multitool clipped inside her jacket. She hadn't drawn it yet but her thumb was on the catch.

A man stumbled out of the alley mouth before any of them could move. He was clutching his forearm against his chest, blood running freely between his fingers and dripping onto the pavement. The man's face was white, the colour people turn when their blood decides it has more urgent places to be than the surface of the skin.

"It—" the man started but couldn't finish. Instead he pointed back into the alley with his undamaged hand, the gesture shaking so badly it was almost useless. "In there. It's still in there. Don't—"

Arthur was moving before the man had finished. He heard Donna swear behind him and then her boots on the wet concrete after him. She'd made the same calculation that he had, someone else might still be in the alley, and whatever was in there had drawn blood. Though he was sure there would be a ‘talk’ on discussing his actions with the group before he took them.

He ran towards whatever had hurt the man without stopping to reason. Something was failing in the alley and his body had committed to intervene, the same way it did when he saw a cracked beam or shifting foundation. When a building is failing, you moved to shore it up. Or he was just an idiot.

Donna caught up with him at the alley mouth and went wide, peeling right along the office wall and putting the bins between herself and whatever was making the godawful noise. Arthur understood that she was opening an angle, moving like someone who had been taught that two people crowding the same doorway was how both of them got killed.

***

He saw the bins first. Two of them had been knocked over, their contents spilled across the wet concrete in a fan of sodden cardboard and split bags. But the spill pattern was wrong, pushed outward from the wall like something had hit them from behind.

Then he saw the creature. At first his brain tried to make it a bin bag. A pile of black plastic caught in the wind, shifting and folding in the light. That interpretation lasted approximately one second as the pile unfolded eight jointed legs and dragged itself into the centre of the alley with a sound like knuckles cracking.

Arthur stopped and stared.

“Well, that’s not good,” Steve murmured from behind.

It was roughly the size of a large suitcase, the body was segmented and held low to the ground. The shell looked like dull metal, overlapping plates that reflected the violet sky in flashes. Eight legs clicked against the pavement, and where a head should have been, a cluster of glossy black eyes rotated towards him like a camera adjusting its lens.

Then the damned thing opened its mouth sideways, a seam splitting along the skull.

It was a spider, a sodding suitcase-sized spider. Arthur wasn't afraid of spiders, he had in fact never really given them much thought. But when you are standing in front of one which is the size of a dog, it was a different matter altogether. His body went cold and fear hit him as a physical event, blood retreated from his extremities, his vision sharpened, and he could hear his own pulse in his throat.

His legs told him to run while his brain told him that the injured man was behind him, and if this thing got past him it would reach the man and then it would reach the street.

The load has to be carried.

The creature lunged.

Arthur threw himself sideways and the creature's front limbs struck the wall where his chest had been a moment before, gouging pale scratches into the brick. He felt a rush of air against his face and a hooked appendage tore through his jacket and the skin beneath in a single raking motion that sent a pulse of white-hot pain across his ribs.

Arthur hit the ground and the impact knocked the breath from his lungs. For a terrible, stretched second, he was on his back looking up at the segmented underside while the spider was repositioning its legs to pin him.

That's when Donna came in from the right and saved him from a skewered death.

She drove the short blade of the multitool into the seam between two chitin plates with no hesitation and no wasted motion. Arthur had seen her move like this once before, during a close-protection drill she’d run. It was the kind of drill that private security consultants don’t usually know how to run unless they’d learned it somewhere that wasn’t exactly private.

The creature shrieked in a thin and metallic sound, like a sheet of steel being torn, and it made Arthur's teeth ache. It spun towards Donna only to find her two steps back, crouched low, her blade out and her stance reset.

Steve appeared at the alley entrance holding a length of broken pipe he'd pulled from the collapsed pallet. His hands were shaking and his face was of a colour that suggested his body had strong opinions about what he was doing, but he swung the pipe with both hands and connected with the creature's flank hard enough to stagger it sideways. Swung clean through the line, a superb cover drive.

"Arthur, get a bloody move on!"

Arthur rolled over the wet concrete while his lacerated ribs screamed in protest against the movement. He scrambled up, grabbing the dented lid of a fallen corrugated bin, and slammed the metal shield between himself and the creature as it lunged again. The impact rattled through his forearm and into his shoulder joint like a hammer blow, but the makeshift shield held. The creature's hooked appendages scraped uselessly across the metal with a sickening screech, searching for an edge to grip and pull.

Donna took that opportunity to strike again and this time the blade found softer tissue beneath the head. There was a narrow gap between the chitin plates and she drove the multitool in through the gap up to the handle.

Two inches of steel into the brain proved enough and the creature collapsed. It folded onto the concrete, its legs buckling in sequence, one after another, until it was flat against the ground and still.

Donna circled the body carefully watching the joints, and then drove the point of the multitool into the gap behind its head a second time before she was satisfied that it was not going to move again. Only then did she step back.

***

For several seconds the only sound in the alley was of three people breathing.

Arthur's was ragged and sharp, catching on something that might have been a cracked rib or might have been the aftershock of adrenaline burning through a body that had for the first time understood what the brink of death felt like.

Donna's was controlled and deliberate, the slow exhale of someone forcing their heart rate down by sheer force of will. She wiped the blade of the multitool on her jacket and folded it closed. Her hands were steady but Arthur could see the tremor running through her shoulders.

Steve's was fast and uneven. He was still holding the pipe and staring at the thing on the ground as though he was convinced it would start moving again.

The man from the alley mouth was standing still against the far wall with his injured arm pressed to his chest. His eyes moved from the dead creature to the bin lid and then to the folded multitool in Donna's hands. His eyes were wide and he looked at Arthur the way site labourers looked at the structural surveyor after a near-collapse, the idiot who walked towards the thing everyone else was running away from.

He didn't say thank you, he didn’t even nod. He just turned and walked towards the main road, cradling his arm and putting distance between himself and the alley and whatever had just happened in it.

Donna watched him go. "Wanker. He didn't even ask if we were hurt."

Arthur sat up slowly. The gash across his ribs was bleeding freely and soaking through his torn shirt in a warm, spreading patch that he pressed his hand against out of instinct. The pain was real and immediate, and he was thankful for it. A clean, honest hurt at least made sense, unlike everything else that had happened in the last thirty minutes.

He forced himself to look at the dead creature, and up close, it was worse.

His brain had no analogue or structural comparison for the dead thing in front of them, that had no right to exist on a London street. He had the irrational conviction that if he looked away and back, it would turn into something he could explain. So he did and that did bugger all.

The chitin plates didn’t quite fit together. They overlapped at angles that suggested they had grown recently and in a hurry, like a body improvising armour from materials it didn't fully understand. The legs were jointed in too many places. The eyes were still open, still glossy, still reflecting the strange sky above.

Steve took a step and crouched beside the body. With the immediate danger gone, pattern recognition took over whether he wanted it to or not.

"This is hardly even a species; this is a mutation. Look at the chitin growth. All of this is recent, the underlying body structure is arachnid, maybe something from the Opiliones family, but the scale is—" He trailed off. "Nothing native to the UK grows— hell, nothing native to *Earth* grows chitin like that.”

Steve looked at the pipe in his hands, then at the blood spreading through Arthur's shirt. "Three of us. A blade, a pipe and a bin lid. For one of these," he said, motioning at the thing that shouldn’t be a thing in a rational world.

Donna looked up at the sky and sighed. "Well, that settles the gas leak theory," she said.

Then Arthur felt a pulse behind his eyes and the blue lattice flickered beneath his skin. Text assembled itself in his vision with the same cold clarity as before.

THREAT NEUTRALISED

ETHER ABSORBED: +12

INTEGRATION: 12 / 42

The text faded and Arthur stared at the empty space where it had been. The words had been precise and clinical, also completely insane. Twelve of forty-two. It was a fraction, a progress bar. As though what had just happened in this alley, the terror and the pain and the creature that shouldn't exist, had been weighed, measured, and converted into a number.

"Please tell me— please tell me you both just saw that. The floating text." Steve said, looking between Arthur and Donna.

Donna stared back and then gave Steve a tight nod.

"Ether. It said Ether absorbed. Not energy or mana, which would be more appropriate here. But Ether." He was still staring at the patch of air the words had occupied a second ago. "That's classical Greek, Aither. It's in Hesiod. The bright air the gods breathe, above the murk the humans stand in. Why is this thing using classical Greek?”

"Save the seminar for later. Arthur's bleeding."

Arthur pressed his hand harder against the gash in his side. The bleeding seemed to be slowing, which was good, except that it was slowing faster than it should have been possible and that was something he didn't have the capacity to process right now. He understood that the world had just changed in ways that no amount of engineering could reverse.

He stared at the alley wall to his left. It was old brick, poorly mortared, and suddenly it looked different. For the briefest instant, Arthur could see it like a blueprint. Every weak point, every stress fracture, every place where a precise strike could bring the whole thing down. The vision was sharp, involuntary, and gone before he could examine it.

Twelve of forty-two. Integration.
