Caleb watched with detachment as the legions and the auxiliaries formed up and advanced on the village. Bubbles of darkness formed all around the legionnaires but each were quickly dispelled by a wave of a Decurion’s sword. The arrows that followed were blocked by raised shields, even the ones that would have fallen between the gaps being deflected by the skills of the men. The auxiliaries advanced behind the legionnaires and returned arrow fire. When the legion got close the archers fell back to a secondary defensive line and blew a horn.

Howls filled the falling night and three great horse sized wolves came out from within the village. Aided by his enhanced perception, Caleb could see the odd deformities that covered these creatures. One had patches of scales where fur ought to have been while another had a twisted horn coming from its fore head.

Caleb was about to move into action, when Mettius held him back.

“Wait,” he said.

Caleb turned back to the battle and saw as Flavius ran out from behind the enemy lines with his amazing speed. He was on the first wolf creature before Caleb would have even made it past the lines. He rammed his spear through its chest, the point exiting from the back of the twisted creature. Flavius didn’t slow his charge at the impact and ran through the strike, grabbing the tip of the spear where it exited and ripping it out the back.

Weapon back in hand, he spun it around, sweeping the legs of a second wolf, breaking some of them in the process. He jumped away from the crippled wolf, narrowly dodging the attack of the third. Even as he retreated he threw his spear at the downed wolf, the weapon piercing its side in a wound that would quickly prove fatal even for this corrupted beast.

Weaponless, Flavius held his hands up in front of him as the last wolf advanced, snarling. This one looked the most wolflike of the three, save for the second mouth and jaw it had coming from the side of its neck.

It lunged at Flavius and the Bestiarius stepped to the side of the bite, grabbing the wolf by the scruff of the neck and swinging under it. His swing brought him around the wolf and he landed on its back, two daggers drawn. Faster than Caleb could perceive the slave warrior pummeled his blades into the back of the wolf. It tried to bite at him with its extra mouth, but the wounds mounted too quickly and the creature collapsed on itself.

While this was happening, both sides of the battle held their lines, waiting for the out come of the heroes. When Flavius arose the victor, the legion let out of rhythmic whoop of celebration and advanced once more upon the village.

Enemy hero dealt with, Flavius retrieved his spear and advanced upon the remaining corrupted villagers.

He slew them all before the legion could reach them.

“This is what a Bestiarius does,” Mettius said simply from the side at the end of the brief encounter.

***

The legion found more captive villagers within the town and freed them as they had before. They were to be sent back to Frost Valley in the morning with an escort as the legion prepared to move on to the next village. There were two more left, and none had enough survivors to sustain themselves so far.

In this village there were more than just Dark Archers. The legion counted more than 100 initialized with classes like Swordsmen, Archer, Warrior, and Spy. Each had an error in place of their rarity on the kill notification for any with elevated system access, and using Caleb’s suggested they were able to just that each of these felt like commons, while the Dark Archers had felt uncommon.

Once the town had been occupied Caleb once more found a forge to work in though very little work got done as he continued to study the armor.

“I hear you can fix anything,” Flavius said, walking into the forge without knocking.

Caleb looked up quickly from his work as if he’d been doing something illicit, and relaxed when he saw who it was.

“Not anything, but I’ll always give it a shot.”

Flavius handed his spear to Caleb.

“This is hide steel,” Flavius said. “Don’t ask me how they make it, but its aspected for beast flesh.”

Caleb touched the metal tip of the blade and sent his senses out into the strange metal. The aspect was unlike any he’d ever sensed and he had no trouble believing this was meant for cutting through flesh. A hairline crack went through the spearhead from the tip to the base.”

“How did this happen?” Caleb asked, gesturing at the crack.

“Those wolves had touch bones. Normally Glenda here would have no problem but one of them had a mutated skeleton beneath.”

Caleb examined the blade, the aspect was a greater one, which meant he should be able to repair it without reducing the strength of it. Thankfully there was no enchantment on the blade.

Caleb began heating the blade, keeping contact through the tongs so he can tell when the unfamiliar metal would be ready for forging. This blade, while of a high aspect, had to have been poorly forged to have failed in such a manner.

“Where did you get this spear?” Caleb asked. “And why doesn’t it have an enchantment on it?”

“The Bardi don’t exactly get the best gear,” Flavius said with some bitterness in his voice. “Well, the ones below Secundus rank at least—that’s the rare one. We get the leavings of the equites and champions of the arena. But, it gets the job done.”

Caleb couldn’t disagree, remembering the speed at which the gladiator dispatched the wolves.

“So that’s your role?” Caleb asked, still not exactly sure what qualified as a hero on the battlefield.

“Killing beasts?” Flavius asked. “Part of it at least. Its something my class is uniquely suited for, but not all Bardi are Bestiari. The Bardi are the expendable initialized. We are sent out when the commanders suspect the enemy is holding back greater force. Other armies use beasts for such jobs, but the empire prefers to have more control over their fodder. Beasts can be unpredictable—” he touched his collar “—slaves less so.”

“Why didn’t you pull back after killing the wolves?” Caleb couldn’t help but ask a dangerous question. “Those rebels… I can’t imagine you lacked sympathy for their cause.”

Flavius grew silent, contemplating his answer.

Eventually he said, “I read the reports. These people… they were something less then men. Something more akin to me. No one who would lock their family in a cave for this ‘Benefactor’ deserves mercy. Though I suspect they didn’t do so of their free will. They might not be slaves of the empire, but the empire doesn’t have a monopoly on slavery and in that case they have nothing but my sympathy.”

Caleb worked on the spear in silence after that, making full use of all his magics to do the best job he could. Aside from the flaw that led to the crack, Glenda was a finely forge weapon.

He experimented using sunder to allow him to forge weld the blade back together. Normally he would have had to reforge the spearhead to fix such a flaw, but with Sunder he could hammer the crack and force the energy into welding the flaws together. Once he’d completed the repair, Caleb quenched the weapon in his quenching steel cooled water and used his magic to maintain its integrity.

Flavius watched patiently, and when Caleb was done gave his thanks and departed to return to his own duties.

***

The days that followed fell into a pattern. Caleb would train with Kelia and Mettius in the morning, other initialized in the camp occasionally joining in. Gavin, Doren and Revin who had all joined up as scouts challenged Caleb to a training duel. Without the need to hide his abilities, Caleb went all out, ripping the weapons from the hands of each Frost Dragon Hunter before knocking them over with sudden blasts of wind. Once they were down it was easy to keep them there by applying downward force on all the metal they wore.

When the scouts returned from the next villages, they reported much the same and the legion set out. Each village put up the same token resistance. While the legion’s core was only 100 common initialized Legionnaires with 10 uncommon Decurions, the one to two hundred initialized villagers in each of these settlements stood no chance. The skills of the Legionnaires were all designed to work together to give the power to counter attacks from enemies at much higher ranks and levels than any individual Legionnaire could handle while the Decurions further enhances the strength of the front lines.

While these newly initialized and brainwashed rebels could have wiped the floor with any mundane army, they stood not chance against even this small fraction of the Thousand Blade Legion. Each village had a reserve of mutated beasts to serve as stand ins for heroes and each were dealt with cleanly. Grisim joined Flavius in one such encounter after asking Canus for leave to assist him. Normally the scouts didn’t engage in battles as anything more than skirmishers or ranges support, but the rules of the legion were flexible when it came to leveling up the higher rarity members.

With a class like Imperial Monster Hunter, Grisim was well suited to fighting the giant bear that lumbered out of the battlefield at the last village. Together they ended the bear easily, Glenda and Grisim’s enchanted blade making quick work of it. The battle helped Grisim finally capitalize on his weeks of training in his new class, leveling him up to level 5 and granting him the new skill Celeritas—high tongue for speed. It was a well known skill amongst the imperial classes and allowed the initialized to spend stamina to increase their agility temporarily.

The took the last of the three villages without incident or need for the Equites to join the fray. Each village had a contingent of captives, and all were brought back with the legion as their homes no longer had the people to be self-sufficient.

Once the valley had been cleared of the rebel threat, the legion left Frost Valley behind, leaving most of the reclaimed villagers there. Word had been sent to the legion command of the situation and a retired uninitialized legionnaires were already on their way to repopulate the town.

And then, it was time for Caleb to return home.
