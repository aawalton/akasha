Ethan pushed himself to his feet, eyes wide. Valanor was here. How could Valanor be here? But there could be no doubt. The man had reached Twilight rank–not a surprise, given the intervening years, and how close he’d already been.

Ethan tried to force the surprise out of his mind, they needed to focus on the Emp–

A hulking red and white figure crashed into the Emperor from the side, and the two went tumbling and sliding away in a cloud of dust. They were up and grappling a moment later, the large newcomer revealed to be some kind of humanoid demon, using two massive bones as clubs.

Allowing the Astral to sink into his vision, Ethan confirmed that the demon was only Twilight rank, yet he wasn’t instantly crushed by the Master rank Emperor. Pushed back, yes, and visibly struggling, yet he should have been annihilated.

Then, as the Emperor struck with clawed hands, the cloak he wore billowed up, and Ethan saw the gaping wound in the Follower’s chest. It was a hole the size of a fist, and Ethan could see right through it. He’d spotted something when he’d seen the Emperor last, but hadn’t realized how bad it was–nor how debilitating.

They might just have a chance, now that there were this many Twilights.

He turned back to Valanor, who was still staring at him in disbelief. Ethan paused only to reset the broken bones in his left arm–courtesy of the Emperor’s single attack–before returning the shield-knight’s surprised stare.

“I’m happy to see you, but what the hell are you doing here?” he asked.

Valanor laughed, it was a booming thing, and his deep voice felt like safety.

“We thought you were dead, boy, but that doesn’t mean we were just going to sit on our hands as Nexum tore itself apart. I came here to find Dusk’s power. I didn’t expect…them.” He gestured at the Emperor.

“Followers. Long story, but they’re invaders, and they’re after The Throne. If they get it, they rebuild the new world in their image. It won’t be pretty.”

Valanor raised an eyebrow. “I believe it.”

“How did you know about Dusk’s power?” Ethan asked.

Valanor shrugged. “The Church knew. Now, I am the Church. How do we stop them?”

“Kill the bad guy,” Ethan said with a shrug. “Oh, Cara is alive by the way.”

Valanor’s eyes widened. “That…is glad tidings. Speaking of…” His eyes went to the demon.

Ethan looked at the red and white creature, and he raised an eyebrow. “Who is he?” he asked, knowing the answer.

“Savilar. He’s lived and died many times since we saw him last. For what it’s worth, he’s died enough times not to begrudge you the first one.”

“Lucky me,” Ethan said. “We can trust him?”

“Enough. For now,” Valanor said. “But I can’t leave him to fight alone any longer. Here, take this.” He removed a statue of the goddess from his inventory, roughly two feet tall and made of marble, and pressed it into Ethan’s right hand.

“I’m not feeling particularly religious, right now,” Ethan said.

“You might after you use that. Hurry.”

He turned to go, and Ethan reached out to catch his arm.

“One second, I can feel the corruption in you, it must be eating you up from the inside.”

He concentrated, and it wasn’t difficult to extend the reach of his Spirit from the nearby battle of Champions, and the corruption inside of Valanor was torn away in heartbeats. The knight’s eyes widened, clearing, and his mouth opened in shock.

“I hadn’t realized how much it…” He shook his head. “Thank you. Now hurry!”

With that, he raced off to battle. Once again, his shield found its way between the Emperor’s fury and someone the man wanted to protect. Ethan hoped Valanor was making the right choice, but he had to admit the two fought beautifully together. In seconds they had found a rhythm, still being pushed back, but somehow holding the furious Emperor at bay.

Ethan’s arm was still healing, so he knew he couldn’t rush back in. Instead he moved rapidly away, out of sight behind a large boulder. There, he examined the statue of the goddess, trying to make sense of it. Valanor had seemed convinced that he could–

Ethan blinked in surprise. He’d barely allowed a trickle of mana into the stone figure, but its purpose was already obvious. It appeared there’d be more than one surprise this day.

Pushing just a little more mana in, the statue began to glow, and Ethan carefully placed it on the ground before moving away. A rift burst to life only seconds later.

Ethan stared into an empty courtyard of stone, lit by the afternoon sun under a sky of the most perfect blue. It was easy to forget how beautiful Nexum could be, especially contrasted to the hellish Potentia.

After only a few moments, a young woman walked by, dressed in the white robes of the Church. She dropped a book she was carrying, and immediately sprinted away.

“Um, hello?” Ethan asked, but the woman was already gone. He was tempted to move through the rift, but he understood better than most that there were limits to how much mana could move through a portal before it collapsed. Surely this had been planned, and they had a–

Ethan’s breath caught in his chest as two figures came running toward the rift..

“I’m not ready!” a voice complained.

“How are you possibly not ready?” another voice demanded. “We’ve been waiting for this moment for months.”

“Oh, and you spent every moment of those damned months just waiting for a stupid portal to that shithole to open?”

“Yes.”

“Well, I had things to do!”

The two men leapt through without waiting, and the rift snapped closed behind them, energy expended. It had opened on a bit of an angle from Ethan, and deposited the two facing slightly away from him, looking out at the ongoing battle between Ethan’s forces, and the Followers. The raging Champions were less than a mile off, and the sounds of their battle was like continuous thunder.

“This was stupid,” Richard said. “Let’s go back.”

“This is where we belong,” Dean said with a shake of his head. “If any world ever needed a Bishop or two, it was this one.”

Ethan found himself grinning as he walked up behind his two brothers. “Actually,” he began, “this world is lousy with Bishops.”

The two spun on their heals, Richard’s jaw dropping, while Dean almost collapsed to one knee. He was still missing an eye…Lathander must have done something truly nasty to manage that.

Rich had his arms wrapped around Ethan without even the good grace to offer banter. Ethan returned the hug with a grin, while Dean placed a hand on his shoulder, just shaking his head in disbelief.

“I know,” Ethan said, “I’m supposed to be dead.”

“We never believed it,” Richard insisted. “Well, I didn’t,” he added, making a meaningful head nod toward Dean.

Dean slapped him on the back good-naturedly. “Neither of us wanted to believe the worst, little brother. But what the hell are you doing here?”

“Better question, what’s Aunt Claire doing here?” Rich added, looking over at the closest Bishop fire team. The battle was no longer a losing one, but the fighting remained intense, and Aunt Claire was shouting orders, her face exposed by an aura of frost that seemed to prevent Orion from completely covering her.

Ethan glanced at the battle, then just shook his head. “Too much to explain in full. This is it, this is the last battle. Winner controls the Union. We’re fighting demons and the last remnants of alien invaders.”

“Aliens?!” Richard said, looking far more excited than he should. He slapped Dean’s arm. “I told you that I knew what I saw in eighth grade!”

Dean rolled his eyes. “That was a plane, and you’re an idiot.” He turned back to Ethan. “What’s the plan?”

Ethan stepped around the large boulder he’d sheltered behind, and his brothers–his brothers, he still couldn’t believe this–followed, getting a good view of the Emperor battling Valanor and…and Savilar.

This day was a lot.

“That’s their leader. Master rank. We take him out, and we just might win this thing.”

“Master rank?” Richard asked, eyes wide. “I’m still a Dusk, I think I might have to sit this one out.”

Ethan nodded. “Go find Michael, he’s leading our army of Bishops.”

The brothers looked over to where Michael hovered above the battlefield, draped in his black Familiar.

“Michael is a darkborne?” Dean asked, shaking his head. “Well, of course he is. Go help him, Rich.”

Richard’s face had gone completely serious, and a massive sniper rifle appeared in his hands. “I’ll take care of it.” He turned from Dean to Ethan. “Be safe. Both of you.” Then he was rushing away.

Dean turned back to Ethan. “Okay. That leaves the…alien to us? At least Valanor is still alive. Is he fighting with a demon?”

“He’s a friend–sort of.” Ethan paused, thinking on those words. “Well, he’s a dick, but don’t kill him.”

“Understood,” Dean said, then began walking toward the battle, tossing aside brown robes to reveal deep green armor. “You coming?” he called back.

“Just need a few minutes,” Ethan said. “Burned through my mana to save the day, you know how it is.” Plus his arm was still healing, though he ached to race back in.

“Don’t wait too long,” Dean said. “Besides, I’d like to see what you’re capable of…Twilight.” He smiled with pride, and Ethan actually felt himself blush slightly.

There were times he might have killed to see his older brother look at him with that pride.

Then the man was gone, racing into the chaotic battle, spears of magical light firing ahead of him. The Emperor dodged without much difficulty, but he was suddenly facing three Twilights, and in moments, he was on the defensive.

Ethan flexed his left arm. You couldn’t rush broken bones. It was nearly healed, but the force of his own strikes could shatter a nearly-healed break. But he longed to rush forward anyway. With four-on-one odds, even against a Master, there was a real chance they could–

A black shadow streaked across the sky, moving as if fired from the warring Champions. The shadow landed a short distance from the four-way melee, then shot toward Dean like a bullet.

Dean’s arms came up in time, glowing with power, and he deflected a double-sword-strike, only for a wave of wind to unbalance him, and a pool of magma to open up behind him. He seemed about to fall in, only for Savilar’s clawed hand to snap forward, seizing Dean’s collar and tossing him roughly to the other side of the battle.

The demon’s bone-maces clanged against Prince Calevaro’s blade, and the other two Twilights adjusted to face the Emperor alone. Ethan was just close enough to see the indecision on Valanor’s face. Inky black covering or not, he recognized his prince.

Ethan let out an angry breath. When Cal hadn’t showed up to the battle, he’d let himself hope that the man had been needed to control the Champion rank scorpiursi, or anything else that might avoid this confrontation.

But that didn’t mean he wasn’t prepared for it.

Ethan’s mana was low, but he had just enough left for what needed to be done. Reaching deep inside himself, he searched for the remnants of an ability that he’d sacrificed.

It was time to prove he was a Dimensional Champion.
