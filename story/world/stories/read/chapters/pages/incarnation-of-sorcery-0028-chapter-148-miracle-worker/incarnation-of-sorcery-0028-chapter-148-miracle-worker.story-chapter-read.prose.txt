Ryland deliberated on the best course of action before he got going. He couldn’t deny there was a strong part of him that wished to just appear before the afflicted with a quick [Translocate] and immediately begin taking care of the issue. But things deserved more care. He couldn’t simply throw his weight around however he wished.

In truth, he had already done so when he had confronted the Demiurge in the middle of his palace. But a reckoning there had been necessary, if only to ensure Mavar acted with greater care, regardless of what he intended.

Which suggested this was unlikely to be Mavar’s doing.

For now, instead of heading straight for the poor students suffering the illness, Ryland appeared in High Academ Zevo’s office.

“I suspect you were expecting me,” he said. “Which is explained by your utter lack of surprise.”

High Academ Zevo was sitting behind his desk with an expression somewhere between thoughtful and worried. He was clearly troubled by the situation. After their little altercation at the Crypts, the man had gone on to apologize for his overreaction. Ryland had forgiven him, of course. Water under the bridge.

“Your pupils foretold your arrival,” Zevo said. “Though I hope you haven’t taken in a Rhellian pupil as well.”

Ryland remembered that timid Rhellian boy. “I think he’s just a friend of Viren and Acrypha. But let’s discuss the matter at hand. That of this illness.”

Zevo winced. “Your tone suggests it’s worse.”

“From certain perspectives, it is. I would call it more of a curse, in all honesty.”

“A curse?”

“Indeed.”

Ryland took a deep breath then began explaining what exactly he had surmised from the descriptions he had heard from Viren and Acrypha. Of course, things might be different, and he might come to a different conclusion upon actually seeing the students. Nevertheless, the symptoms were telling.

“These boils,” Ryland said. “They’re grey-green, yes? And the poison is causing the blood to turn… sludgy, for lack of a better term, yes?”

Zevo paled a little, but nodded. “What is this, Ryland? How has this come here of all places? Why is it attacking our students?”

“I can hardly answer all of that without even seeing the afflicted. However, the curse itself acts at a particulate nature, with the power of Conceptual Glyphs. By changing the consistency of the Conceptual Glyphs for whatever organs targeted by the Inscription, one can enact a wide variety of horrors upon the target’s body.”

Zevo sat up straighter in alarm. “Did you say Inscriptions? We’ve looked everywhere and we saw nothing of the sort. Our Healer Mages have been very thorough.”

“Have you checked their manasouls?”

Zevo went silent. He almost appeared to freeze. There was no sound inside the office except for the ticking of a clock.

Ryland always felt a little unmoored when he remembered that whenever he was speaking with a Rhellian, he was potentially speaking with their leader too. That Glyph of Unity from Mavar was multifaceted in its capabilities, one of which was acting as a listening device. It ensured he would never be truly surprised by anything occurring on Rhel.

Except, apparently, by the realization that he wasn’t the only one who could perform Inscriptions on one’s manasoul directly.

“That is…” Zevo shook his head. “Difficult to believe.”

“And yet,” Ryland said. “It is an avenue you haven’t explored just yet. Therefore, it’s an avenue that could explain the current situation. Now will you allow me to see what’s going on?”

Zevo closed his eyes for a long moment. He looked like he wanted to rub his temples but was furiously resisting the urge to do so. Before he replied to Ryland, he talked to a few others through his Glyph of Communication, ensuring that they weren’t about to cause any sort of chaos by bringing an “outsider” in. It was only after everything was ready that they set off.

“Please keep your revelations between just the two of us,” Zevo said as they headed out. Ryland had agreed to not just [Translocating] them all over to the quarantined area. “I will decide who it would be best to disseminate to afterwards.”

“Hmm, alright,” Ryland said.

He didn’t want to keep his findings hidden from anyone, certainly not any of the Healer Mages, but he would accept Zevo’s request. After all, this wasn’t an illness. Not really. This was more of a spell inflicted on others. On students specifically.

Which meant it might very well have been someone within the institute, or someone else who had access to the students recently.

Which further meant they needed to be careful who they revealed things to.

“Is this him?” an old Healer Mage asked when they had gotten deep into the quarantined section of the institute.

“It is,” Zevo said. “This is sub-Academ Marionys Ryland. He was very helpful at the Crypts.”

“Ah, so that’s him.” The old man looked over Ryland head-to-toe, his watery eyes glimmering a little behind his round glasses. “Well, wait a moment. Let me cast a quarantining spell to ensure you remain safe.”

“Thank you,” Ryland said. “But that won’t be necessary. [Eternal Bastion].”

High Academ Zevo stiffened, while the old Healer Mage looked on in awe. They were both clad in the same protective barrier that was encasing Ryland.

“I would have modified the spell circle with a few different Subglyphs,” Ryland said as the two men marvelled at the translucent blanket over their forms. “But since this isn’t a real illness and is more of a spell at work, the normal barrier should work just fine.”

“What even is the Tier of this spell?” the old man asked.

“Tier [26].”

Both Zevo and the old Healer Mage snapped their heads around to stare at Ryland like he had just grown a mushroom out of his head.

“We… should get going,” Zevo eventually managed to say.

“Yes.” The old man coughed into his hand. “Let’s go.”

Ryland and Zevo followed the old man, who moved quite fast despite his advanced years. Several other Healer Mages were in the area, though they all seemed to be staying away from their destination—a large room with a small door and no windows.

Inside, the students were lying unconscious. Almost unmoving, save for the rise and fall of their chests.

A few Healer Mages were in attendance, though they appeared not to be doing anything urgent at the moment. All of them rose and greeted the old Healer Mage and High Academ Zevo, and none seemed quite sure what to make of Ryland. Nor of the fact that he had cast a powerful spell around the trio who had just arrived, which was acting as a superior substitute for their own precautions.

Hopefully, he wasn’t making them feel inadequate or something of the sort. Arrogant as it was to think so, it was worth deliberating as that had never been Ryland’s intention.

But he forgot all about them as he approached the beds allocated for the ill students.

“What exactly will you be doing?” the old Healer Mage asked.

Ryland concentrated as he peered at the students. This wasn’t going to be terribly difficult. He had a multitude of ways of dealing with something of the sort. That said, the fallout might not necessarily be pretty, nor would the method actually be appreciated by anyone present.

Not when all their manasouls had technically been tampered with in the same way already. Just not by an entity who wanted them dead.

“I have a few ways of dealing with the issue,” Ryland said. “It’s a matter of deciding which approach would be best.”

“What are these approaches?” the old man asked. “How do you even know what’s afflicting them?”

Right. Because of Zevo, Ryland hadn’t yet mentioned to anyone besides the High Academ what he had discovered. Zevo himself had only said that he was bringing in a specialist in the matter. Even now, the High Academ was shooting a look at Ryland that practically begged him to be discreet about his discovery.

Ryland decided to consider his options and be careful about how he explained things.

Temporally reverting the manasoul would be a simple procedure to carry out. However, he wasn’t at all certain if that would be ideal. For the students might have their own complications he was unaware of. Especially because reverting the manasoul’s state would create a conflict with the memories the manasoul already possessed, causing mental problems.

Ryland would like to avoid fixing one issue only to create another instead.

Gouging out the Inscription itself would leave the least mark on the students’ manasouls, but the “scarring” it would leave would disrupt a great deal of the internal processes. Similar to the previous idea, Ryland would really prefer not to leave any permanent damage to the students’ ability to use magic.

He was also partial to simply shuttering the connection to mana driving a manasoul. With no mana, nothing about the manasoul would work as intended, not even the Inscriptions placed on it.

The problem was that it would worsen the present complications before the students eventually recovered.

“So the issue is magical in nature?” the old man asked.

Ryland pursed his lips as Zevo winced. These people weren’t that dumb. They’d figure out what was going on even if they did their best to hide things. But Ryland directed all further questions to Zevo. It was the High Academ’s headache to handle this whole mess.

Admittedly, Ryland was a little tickled by the situation. The High Academ was clearly suspicious about what Ryland would attempt to do, yet he couldn’t ask about it in fear of Ryland’s answer leading to unforeseen complications. Basically, Ryland was free to proceed.

For now, Ryland focused on the actual application of his magic. He tested it out at first.

“[Soul Drain Array],” he incanted.

Ryland’s mana emerged with sizzling but focused power, forming a thin needle that pierced into the targeted student’s manasoul. Once inserted, it latched onto the manasoul and formed an expansive array, not dissimilar to the ones created by his [World Node] spell. Only, this immediately drew out all the target’s mana.

Unfortunately, the student himself reacted by unconsciously twitching his body. His skin started looking dryer and his body appeared more spent.

“Sir!” one of the Healer Mages monitoring the situation called out in warning. “The student’s mana vitals are fluctuating.”

“This is normal,” Ryland said, enforcing calm without resorting to his Glyph of Tranquillity. “While the results may appear alarming at first, this will remove the… illness, and the poor student will make a full recovery. Trust me.”

At this point, if Ryland had been present on his own, he probably would have been tackled by the dozen or so Healer Mages keeping an eye on things. It was only the presence of someone like High Academ Zevo that allowed Ryland to work in peace. Somewhat saddening that his magic didn’t yet speak for itself, but then again, if it did, the Rhellians probably wouldn’t let him get anywhere close.

In a minute, Ryland was done. A quick look with [Omniscience] confirmed the fact. Their manasouls were inert but also devoid of any irregularities.

Several of the Healer Mages fussed around the student after Ryland was done. Eventually, they were forced to proclaim that their initial misgivings weren’t entirely accurate and none of the student’s actual, biological vitals were showing any signs of danger.

Which was all the tacit approval Ryland needed to proceed to the rest of the students.

About twenty minutes later, he was done. The students were all still unconscious, but the Healer Mages reported that they were no longer suffering the symptoms from before. No more burning blood. No more poison running through their veins. No more clamped hearts, dried skin, or ugly boils. Well, not any new ones, at least.

While the erasure of the symptoms wasn’t proof that everything had worked fine, Ryland had a certain bit of evidence that was good enough for him.

[ Achievement Recognized: Manasoul Malady

You have healed an illness stemming from an Inscription on the manasoul itself.

Rank up from Titan [1] to Titan [6].

Reward: Recouped Inscription as an Encapsulated Glyph: Glyph of Pure Manifestation [Tier 24]. ]

Ryland would be lying if he said the awarded Glyph didn’t alarm him. Pure Manifestation. That was what was causing this illness in the students? He could hardly fathom how they could have come by such an Inscription.

Because Ryland seriously doubted there was a single person within the institute who could have caused such a thing, even aside from the fact that it was Tier [24].

“Are you alright?” High Academ Zevo asked, watching Ryland’s face quite intently.

Ryland nodded tightly. “Just pondering the implications.”

“Which are?”

They were a little distant from the rest of the group, watching the Healer Mages perform various tests and ensure that the students were safe and protected. Several of them, including the old man, had begged Ryland to reveal what exactly had happened, but he had been rescued from the need to do so by Zevo putting a firm stop to it.

“Whoever caused this is terribly strong,” Ryland said.

“How strong?” Zevo asked. He was restraining himself from reacting to the situation but the tension was palpable.

“The magic was Tier [24].”

“Curse the Calamities.”

The day of the award ceremony finally rolled around. Ryland felt a mite awkward that he was being awarded too, but the Rhellians had doubled down on it. Their reasoning made sense, however.

It was at least twice now that Ryland had more or less saved the Rhellian students. One was at the Crypts. Then again, more recently, when they had been afflicted with a sickness of their very manasouls. Twice now that the Rhellians owed Ryland a great deal. And this was all despite how they had acted when he had first returned to Rhel.

He supposed there was quite a lot to make up for.

There was a lot of fanfare throughout the entire ceremony. It wasn’t just a celebration of achievement.

Thanks to recent events, it was now a celebration of unity as much as anything else.

Ryland wasn’t quite aligned with such a theme when considered from the perspective of Rhel. That said, he could understand their relief at surviving events like the Crypts and the sudden manasoul-illness, not to mention how the institute itself had faced pressure from different sources on how they handled these issues.

It also explained why they came to him again for another problem. Right in the middle of the awarding ceremony as well.

“I trust that you are well, Marionys Ryland,” High Academ Zevo said.

“Well, since I’m supposed to be honoured today as well,” Ryland said. “How can I not be well?”

“Well, that is good to hear. For I have a small request, if you’re able.”

Ryland raised an eyebrow. Things really had changed if the Rhellian Institute was coming to him for assistance directly. “How can I help?” He paused for a moment, then quickly continued before the High Academ could say anything further. “Though I do require reassurance that you won’t be setting down yet more awards upon me. Please.”

Zevo blinked a few times, then shook his head. He had almost smiled for a moment there. Ryland considered that even more proof that things really had changed. “As you wish. As for the matter at hand, however…” He grimaced. “We’re missing some students, and I think you might know what happened to them.”
