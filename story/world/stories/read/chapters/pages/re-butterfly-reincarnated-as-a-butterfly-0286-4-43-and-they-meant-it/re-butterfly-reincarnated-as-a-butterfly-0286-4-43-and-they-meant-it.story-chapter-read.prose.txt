Rosslyn’s words had a bizarre effect on those gathered.

It seemed bizarre to Adon, at least.

Several of the humans had to hide their fear—fear that the demons would react badly and start some physical violence in response to her words. This was mainly the generals less experienced in dealing with the Empire specifically. The more experienced generals, though a little concerned, understood that the demons were not beasts. They could contain their tempers.

King Janus recognized the truth, though.

The demons’ reaction was exactly what he thought it would be, surprising though it was to Adon. The Empire’s emissaries felt a sudden surge of fear.

Within their culture, no one would speak so aggressively unless they were actually confident in being able to kill the person they were addressing. They had superior senses to most humans, so they could also sense the genuine rage and lack of fear in the Queen.

She meant what she said.

So, for a few seconds, the international laws of the continent, and the way that messengers and ambassadors were treated, escaped them. They forgot everything that protected them from Rosslyn’s wrath.

Only those words, “I may choke on my own desire to kill you people,” resonated.

It was while they were paralyzed for those few seconds, before they remembered that they had this world’s equivalent of diplomatic immunity—that they were safe.

During that period, without a word, with just a glance, Rosslyn signaled for Adon to follow her.

The butterfly rose to his feet and walked behind the Queen. A light shake of her head told the guards not to join them.

Adon and Rosslyn walked along a different path than the one they had taken from the fortification. Adon, having spent time in this forest every day, knew this one did not lead anywhere useful in particular. There had once been fruit trees at the end of it, but they had been picked dry and then transplanted elsewhere long ago in the midst of the process of reshaping this area to better fit the war effort.

Now it was simply a spot for Rosslyn to gather her thoughts alone with the one she trusted most.

Despite the fact that they were by themselves, the conversation that followed took place entirely within their minds.

Adon, they meant all of that, right? Rosslyn thought, fighting down her warring feelings of hatred and concern.

They have a plan to massacre the entire population of Wayn if the city is overrun, yes, the butterfly confirmed. The demons will assume that they must be prepared to implement it if the diplomatic party does not return with a deal.

Despicable bastards! the Queen silently raged. They outdo themselves to descend to new levels of depravity! A population that has surrendered to them and begun to serve them, that they already butchered when they sacked the city, to be used as human shields, slaughtered like animals. All because the Demon Army is afraid to face our warriors even behind the defense of our high city walls… I knew they were bestial, I knew they were cruel and twisted, but I never knew they could be so cowardly.

Adon let her fume to herself for another minute before he gently interrupted.

Rosslyn, I agree they’re terrible, he sent quietly. Does this mean we kill the ambassadors now and go attack the Empire?

He knew the answer already, but he wanted her to wind herself down a bit, so they could figure out more constructive solutions.

You never kill ambassadors, Adon, the Queen thought instantly but with a trace of reluctance. Even when the message they convey is a threat and nothing more. Those threats have to be able to arrive safely wherever they are aimed. If we did kill them, we might never have dialogue with the Empire again for the rest of my life. She sounded like she was reciting a lesson she had received from a tutor years ago, but one that was so important she had memorized it. The next words seemed to break the script. Not that it is not tempting to take that gamble. If the Empire could just cease to exist… She turned to look at Adon, who kept his face as neutral as he could. Our children could grow up not looking over their shoulders. It would be an unambiguous good.

Unless the Monster Kingdom is worse, the butterfly gently reminded her.

Yes, that is the other consideration, Rosslyn silently agreed.

Adon walked over to her and took her hand in his. She gave him a little smile. It looked strained, but he could tell he had actually made her feel a little better, at least. He was just slightly surprised when the Queen pulled him into her arms and leaned her head on his shoulder.

The conversation had reached a standstill, and a part of him wondered when they would be missed by the others.

But he and Rosslyn both enjoyed the embrace, so he quieted that part of his mind.

They held tightly to each other for a few peaceful seconds before they were interrupted.

He sensed the new presence telepathically before the person was close enough to see them through the trees, and Adon self-consciously separated from Rosslyn. The Queen was only surprised that he had stepped back for a moment.

Then the High Priestess appeared in her peripheral vision.

“King Janus asked me to come and find you two,” she said quietly. “He would like a sidebar before the talks resume.”

“I hope he is not too bothered about the direction the negotiations have moved in,” Rosslyn said. “Despite our plans and agreed upon strategy, I think this declaration from the demons was always bound to force us into a deviation…”

“Say whatever you like about King Janus, and I will agree with much of it,” said the High Priestess. “But he is a forward-looking man. He has already adapted. That is why he wants a discussion with you. He wants your assent to his new plan of action.”

Adon sensed that the High Priestess was not entirely content with what King Janus had in mind, but she considered it a political matter and therefore somewhat outside the realm where she could credibly give her views.

But the butterfly was able to quickly gather what King Janus had told the High Priestess from a rapid sweep of her thoughts—he was wary of spending too much time inside the mind of someone who channeled the power of the Goddess—and convey it to Rosslyn.

The Queen was only a little surprised.

I suppose I knew we might consider this, she thought.

“Adon, will you walk with me?” Rosslyn asked. She extended her hand, and they looped their arms together.

They walked, like a couple—well, they were a couple, but they did not often have time to spend relatively alone like this—along the forest path, arm in arm, and it felt special to Adon. Romantic, despite the circumstances surrounding them.

King Janus and a couple of his generals met them on the path back. They were still distant enough from the demons to rely on not being heard, and this time, the conversation was held aloud.

“I think we must speak to them directly about their threat from the Monster Kingdom,” Janus said, getting to the point immediately.

“Knowing about that is a major strategic advantage,” Rosslyn replied.

“Respectfully, Your Highness, knowing that they have hollowed out their defenses within this country is our major strategic advantage,” General Saxon said. “But the advantage is not primarily that they do not know that we know. The material difference in strength is a fact, not a product of superior information. It will not go away if we tell the  demons what we know. You have implied that they are weak. But in order for them to acknowledge weakness, given the impediments of their culture, perhaps we must show that we know they are weak now.”

“We could strategically reveal that we know about the Monster Kingdom and offer them some support,” King Janus said. “In exchange for their willing withdrawal from your country. We do not have to say we know they have hollowed out their defenses. Alternatively, we could only discuss the fact that they have abandoned their defensive reinforcement of every part of the country. That information was uncovered by our scouts and spies, and it does not implicate our other source of knowledge about their situation in the slightest, if that was your concern.” He nodded in Adon’s direction.

“My concern is that if we give the demons anything, it improves their bargaining position in some way,” Rosslyn said uneasily. “Or it increases the odds that they can threaten us in the future. We did not plan to reveal our secrets at all when we discussed contingencies for this meeting. We might hope to predict how they will respond to increased information, but we cannot be certain. What if this inspires them to conceive some new outrage? Like their plan to send back the—the children of Wayn, their hostages—by catapult if we besiege the capital? We cannot reason with them as if they were just like humans.”

Actually, I have an idea for how we might deal with that threat, the Queen thought suddenly. High risk, but if it worked…

“I know that as well as you do,” the King snapped, cutting off her train of thought. He shook his head slightly and then spoke in a calmer tone. “Look, we are at an impasse with them now. They will never agree to lose face by surrendering as things stand. We have superior strength of arms now, but we do not want to push them to make good on their threat. More exchange of information is necessary in order to reach a conclusion. And we must have some conclusion. If we remain in this position of ambiguity, neither advancing nor agreeing, it is possible they will resolve their conflict with the Monster Kingdom and reinforce their positions again.”

Rosslyn sighed. “Janus, I know all of that. We can agree to tell them what we know, I suppose. It may yield us new information in their reactions, at least.” She gave Adon a quick but meaningful look. “But we should only do so under one condition. It will at least allow us to avoid giving the Empire’s military higher ups the advantage of knowing what we know. The secret of the Monster Kingdom has been unknown for centuries, perhaps millennia. We cannot let it get out that we are aware of their exact situation…”

A few minutes later, they stood at the negotiating table again.

Everyone remained silent until the King and Queen had seated themselves once more.

Once she was in her chair, Queen Rosslyn glared at the lead demons across the table from her, but it was performative this time. She wasn’t caught in the throes of emotion, though it was not hard for her to fake feeling anger and disgust toward them.

It was the demons who spoke first, which Adon observed was another concession of weakness of sorts on their part.

“So,” said Duke Kastinov. “We have something you value. You have something we value. Let us reason together, to determine how we can each get what we want.”

“If you mean the innocent lives of the people in your occupied territories, yes, you do have something we value,” Rosslyn said coldly. “But before you decide what leverage you have, and what terms you can secure, there are things we must discuss plainly about your circumstances.”

Adon felt the balance of power in the conversation shifting again.

“Go on,” said Duke Sebas. “Please, tell us what you want to speak about, Your Highness.”

“Before we reveal anything to you, you must agree that either we will reach some peaceful resolution to this conflict, or you will remain our guests for the duration of the war, at least until Claustrian soil is emptied of Demon Army soldiers,” Rosslyn said. She let the implication, that the war might not be over even if Claustria was free of demon occupation, simply hang in the air.

“This is outrageous,” said Count Fomoral indignantly. “Unfair. Absurd. Shortly after I reveal information of great strategic importance from your perspective, you demand this. As Your Highness said earlier, please be serious.”

A cold silence settled over the table. Rosslyn pushed her seat back. She was prepared to rise and end the discussions there. The demons would try to return to the capital and alert the military command there regarding what had transpired. The humans would try to outpace them there and catch the Demon Army by surprise.

Then the demon leadership blinked.

“We are willing to agree to your terms,” Duke Sebas said after a moment.

“Wha—” Count Fomoral began

“Shut up, Count,” said Duke Kastinov brusquely. “You are done speaking for the day.”

Fomoral turned a deep shade of red, but he said nothing more. Such was the rigidity of demon command structure and hierarchy.

“I am in command of this delegation,” said Duke Sebas, “and I say again, I have the authority, and I am willing to commit, that all of us will remain in your care until some resolution is reached to this conflict. You may treat us as prisoners of war if the Empire is somehow defeated. Now share what you know.”
