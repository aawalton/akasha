The apex spider struck the hall time and again, dislodging cascading sheets of dust from the ceiling.

After several adrenaline-drenched minutes, it was clear that Kenji’s theory that the structure was impregnable was correct. After a final, frustrated roar that vibrated Nick’s bones, the creature aborted its fruitless assault and returned to whence it came. The lesser webslingers had vacated the premises after enduring the party’s barrage, which meant they didn’t have to conduct a room-by-room extermination after all.

When the silk-bound platform stopped shaking, silence reigned, which felt fathomless contrasted against the clamor it followed. Since it seemed they were safe for the time being, Nick turned in a circle to take in his surroundings.

Given the name, he had expected to find a long hallway leading to various Heritages but that wasn’t what met his gaze. His team was standing in a spacious lobby covered in elegant carvings. Passageways ran to the left and right, leading to offices and an occasional maintenance closet. The party surveyed them first, confirming the chambers were free from monsters and there wasn’t anything valuable inside.

After inspecting the wings, they returned to the central area and proceeded through a fortified security checkpoint. On the far side was a massive enclosure that took up most of the hall. The room’s only contents were a pedestal with a crystal orb mounted on top, and an elegant marble staircase leading to the largest portal he’d ever seen. There was a barrier barring entrance to the stairs, which meant the next step was inspecting the crystal device.

Nick was reaching out to touch the orb when a message from the System wavered into existence.

System Message: Claimable Node

You have discovered a minor node containing a lost wonder.

Hall of Heritages

The Hall of Heritages allows claimants to petition the System’s interdimensional Heritage network. Ten claimants may submit applications each year, and unused petitions will accrue over time. Once an official application has been submitted, the caretakers of the Heritages will invite worthy claimants to receive their legacies. If more than one offer is made, a claimant may choose which Heritage they wish to receive.

Note: An individual may only receive one Heritage over the course of their lives. It is permissible to decline all offers and apply again later, but each submission will consume one of the hall’s annual allotments regardless.

Current Unused Petitions: 1,123.

Status: Unclaimed.

Cost to claim: 5000 City Points. 10 Mana wells of any aspect. Additionally, 95% of the monsters infesting the node must be eliminated, including the Tier-3 predator currently claiming ownership.

City bonuses once claimed: The hall will be added to the faction’s portal network, allowing easy access to the facility. In addition to gaining control of the Hall of Heritages, the city’s floating platforms and the buildings they bear will be restored to pristine condition. Claiming the node will grant a significant amount of planetary and species experience.

Only a faction leader may claim this resource (if it is adjacent to their homeworld’s node). You may use this facility freely while it remains unclaimed.

“Well now.” Kenji rubbed his chin while dismissing the update. “That wasn’t what I was expecting. If we can kill the monsters and claim the node, the hall will offer humanity a tremendous and timely boon. I don’t know how Heritages work, but given everything we’ve learned thus far, I have a hunch that the benefits they bestow are priceless.”

“Over a century’s worth of petitions have piled up,” Nick replied. “The up-front cost is steep, but I can’t imagine this being a bad investment. In addition to the hall itself, it would be great to have a portal to this location. Our people could use it to reach that gigantic satellite dish and pick up Astra’s care packages, and restoring the buildings would make it easy to turn the node into a forward base for defending against the stampede. As a cherry on top, I’m sure there is more treasure scattered across the city, like the gear we found in that hidden room and all those Build manuals.”

“We must defeat the monsters if we want to make that happen,” Bandit added, “including the Tier 3. I know Neek has only revealed a fraction of what he can do after eating all those dreadbeasts and being blessed by the blight, but do you really think we have what it takes to slay the titanic silkslinger?”

“I have no idea,” Kenji said. “But there’s nothing stopping us from using the hall before making that decision. Why don’t we learn more about the Heritages it offers, acquire one for ourselves, then revisit the matter after? If all four of us can pick up a potent Heritage, perhaps our strength will be sufficient to lay the big bad low.”

“First things first.” Nick reached out for a second time. “We need to learn how submitting an application works.” He placed open palm against the cerulean gemstone, focusing his intent like he was using one of the displays the System mounted to its obelisks.

He was expecting some kind of menu to appear, which caught him off-guard when an alien materialized by the base of the marble stairs. Judging by the proportions of their body, it was the same species as the portrait they’d found in the knight’s manor, though Nick couldn’t be certain since the warrior’s head had been covered by their helmet.

The newcomer was eight feet tall, with thin limbs and delicate orange scales covering most of their body. Instead of hair, they had bright yellow feathers sticking out of their head, and a crimson ruff like a mane. Their eyes were clouded, like they had been fast asleep and were still in the process of waking up.

Nick gripped Blightbane’s hilt as his team fell into formation, although he was pretty sure this was some kind of projection—a technology he’d come across several times during his adventures. Sure enough, the figure let out a sleepy yawn, sat down in a chair that hadn’t existed until that moment, and turned to face the party.

“Welcome, worthy supplicants. Out of Belonia’s four billion residents, the ten of you have been chosen to receive the greatest gift our civilization can offer…” At that point, the figure realized there were only four individuals standing before them, none of whom looked remotely similar to the city’s original residents.

“Intruders! I can’t remember the last time someone dared to defile this sacred space. Your suffering shall be the stuff of legends when the Green Guard finishes making an example out of you.” An expression crossed their face that gave Nick flashbacks of someone refreshing a webpage when their connection was bad.

“That’s odd, I can’t reach the garrison’s captain on the emergency channel. I can’t hail the peacekeepers or city hall either. The entire communication grid has gone dark.”

At that point, the figure realized the situation had gone even more off-script than they’d thought. They took in the webbing and the debris filling the hall—the accumulated detritus from serving as the lair of monstrous spiders for decades on end. Not finding anyone else in the building, the native turned to face Nick once more. Their initial nonchalance had become outrage early on, but now it settled into a wary confusion as they struggled to put the pieces together.

“Who are you, and what in the seventieth abyss happened here?”

“I’m sorry to be the bearer of bad news,” Bandit replied, “but we weren’t responsible for any of this.”

“I’m afraid this isn’t a city anymore.” Kenji cut to the heart of the matter. “We came across the hall while exploring a node in the Labyrinth of the Mad God. My planet was added to the fringe after Taltos stole it from the System. I suspect he had a hand in whatever tragedy has befallen your people as well. The city is here, but I don’t think your homeworld survived. You have my condolences.”

“They’re gone?” The figure reeled beneath the shock of Kenji’s words. “I was so certain that we would overcome Taltos’s trial. I never imagined our champions could fail. Everyone is dead and Belonia was destroyed. This… is a lot to take in.”

“Take as much time as you need,” Nick said. “I know what it’s like to have your home and the lives of your loved ones stolen by cosmic forces. May they all rot in hell.”

The native sat for a good long while, mourning a people and planet Nick would never know. Eventually, they rose from their chair and approached the party. “My name is Figuro. I apologize for my outburst as it seems you aren’t intruders after all. Would you mind sharing your story before I decide what to do next?”

With that, everyone sat in a circle. Nick passed around some provisions from his pack as each member of the team told their tale in turn. Given the circumstances, he shared everything, including his connection to the Crimson Blight, and what the Ascendant had revealed regarding the Labyrinth’s true nature and the threat it posed to the universe itself.

By the time they arrived at the present, Figuro had made his decision.

“I see. You are the enemy of my enemy—fellow victims of the System’s indifference and the Mad God’s schemes. If there is any chance that you can deliver vengeance for my people, I’ll help any way that I can. I’m authoring all four of you use the Hall of Heritages, and I’ll guide you through the application process. You should claim this node if you can. The power this wonder can offer might save your world from meeting a similar fate. You would be doing me a great kindness too. Now that I’ve awakened, I don’t want to be trapped here for eons, staring at the bones of my civilization.”

Figuro rose from their chair and began to pace, explaining how the hall worked with an air of renewed purpose.

“If you touch the orb and give it permission to read your profile, the information will be turned into an petition and submitted to the heritage network. The true hall exists in a parallel dimension. This place is merely my planet’s access point, or it was, at any rate. There are millions of caretakers who have entrusted their legacies to the hall, along with their criteria for choosing a successor.

“If a compatible Heritage is found, its caretaker will test you. If you are deemed to be a worthy heir to their legacy, it will mark the beginning a lifelong bond.”

“That sounds lovely,” Bandit replied. “But what exactly is a Heritage, and how long does receiving one take?”

“Apologies. I’ve never provided an orientation to someone so new to the System. A Heritage is effectively a second class that can never be changed. Instead of following a preset path of development, your mentor will tailor the specifics to your chosen path of power, which must be compatible for them to select you in the first place. The first set of traits, abilities, and skills will be available after your initial training is complete, and the class will continue to grow over time as you master the techniques and mindsets involved.

“Of course, that’s only half of a Heritage’s true benefit. Being able to work directly with your mentor whenever you visit them through this access point is a powerful asset in and of itself. You’ve probably received some kind of training or tutorial by the System during integration or subsequent trials.”

The party nodded their agreement as Nick smiled at the memory of learning the Ever-Surging Blade on Rita’s strangely colored world.

“In those circumstances, teachers, traders, and guides are severely restricted regarding the information they provide. As the successor to a Heritage, the System poses no such restrictions on the knowledge your mentor bestows. You can learn details that would take centuries to work out on your own, perhaps even millennia. For a newly integrated species like yours, those insights could well be the difference between life and death.

“As for the time involved, it usually takes years for someone to return from their first visit to the hall, but if you explain your circumstances to your mentor, I’m sure they can offer something substantial within the time you have available and you can learn the rest at a later date.”

“That settles it.” Kenji rose to his feet. “I’m eager to make it back to our people before the stampede arrives, but this opportunity is too valuable to pass up. We should each apply for a Heritage, let our mentors know we’re working under tight constraints, then use the power they bestow to clear the node and claim it for our faction. The Heritages might give us the strength we need to survive this ordeal, and the knowledge we gain will be invaluable over the long-term.”

“I agree.” Nick said. “Figuro, show us how to submit our petitions. All four of us would like to use the hall and receive Heritages of our own.”

“Very well,” the guide replied. “Approach the orb one at a time and let it record your profiles. The caretakers will review them and decide if they wish to extend an offer, which usually takes a few hours. If you are talented enough to receive more than one, I will do my best to help you decide. After that, just step through the portal at the top of the stairs and your mentor will take it from there.”
