"How did you know I was back?” Jane asked, looking from Allen to Bella and back again. “I just got here!"

"I saw you walking in from the market." Bella pushed past her into the shop and headed for the counter. "I grabbed Allen, and he sent Brit to round up the others. They should be here soon."

Jane tried to process this as Allen moved past her and set his own burden down. Deciding not to sweat it, she closed the door. She was happy they were there.

Bella had already cleared space on her small table and was unpacking the basket. There was bread, butter, and cheese inside. Allen set his pot on the table and lifted the lid. It smelled good, a mishmash of meat, vegetables, and a long time over heat.

"It smells familiar." Jane said. “Your mother’s stew, right? From her restaurant?”

"It was what I could get quickly. I hope it’s enough?"

“Of course it is,” Jane said. “Though I didn’t expect this. Thanks, you two. I think I do need it.”

"That's why we came. Now sit down. I’ll feed you.”

Jane sat. Allen ladled stew into a bowl and set it in front of her. She finished half her bowl before Brit and Emily burst through the door, laughing and smiling.

"I brought ale!” Brit called. “And Emily brought wine, so we've got options."

Emily held up two bottles of her own. "I came as fast as I could, when I heard. How was your trip? Did you find her?"

"I did. I’ll tell you about it once everyone’s here. And once I’ve eaten a bit more stew."

Jane accepted the cup of ale that Brit passed to her, sipping on it as Sadie finally arrived, completing the party. They ate and drank and talked, and Jane found herself so cheered she knew she could talk about recent events without breaking down much. It was good, since Emily found it hard to wait.

"So," she said. "What happened? In the other town, I mean. Did you get to meet her?"

"I did. We talked for hours. We baked together." Jane smiled. "She taught me, Emily. I learned months worth in a single night. It was fantastic."

The others were nodding and smiling, clearly happy for her. But Jane noticed that Allen's hand had tightened slightly around hers. He had felt something there that not even Bella had picked up, it seemed.

Jane looked down at her cup. "Felicity died," she said. "The morning after I met her. I found her in her bed, asleep. She never fully woke up, after that."

The room went quiet.

"How?" Bella's voice was gentle. "What happened?"

"She was just old. Her doctor said it was always going to happen eventually. It just happened to happen then." Her eyes grew slightly damp. “It was like she was waiting to meet me, and let go as soon as she had. Of course, her doctor said it could have happened at any time.”

Jane felt tears start to flow down her cheeks. She didn't try to stop them. Nobody here would judge her for them. She ducked her head and closed her eyes, then felt arms wrap around her. Allen was first, pulling her close against his chest. Then Bella was there, then the other girls. Even Brit got a hand on her shoulder. A sob fled out of her, completely beyond her control.

And then, just as suddenly, she was laughing.

"All right, all right." She pushed at them gently. "Get off me. I can't breathe."

Jane laughed again as they slowly disentangled themselves. The sadness felt more manageable now, like her friends were each holding a share of it.

"Come on," she said, setting down her cup. "There must be something fun to do here besides eat."

Brit's face lit up. "Actually, I brought something. I was going to save it for later, but this seems like a good time."

He disappeared out the back door, scooping up a canvas sack that clanked with metal sounds as he beckoned for them to follow. They exited to the backyard and found him pounding two long metal stakes into the soil near the shore of the lake, just a little under a foot from each other.

"What are those?" Emily asked.

"Part of a game.” He reached into the bag again and pulled out a large metal hoop, slightly bigger than a dinner plate and surprisingly thin and light. "The objective is to get this around at least one of the stakes."

Jane hefted the hoop, getting a feel for its weight. "And if I miss?"

"Then you don't get any points. But if you get it around one stake, that's one point. If you get it around one stake and it's touching the other stake, that's three points. And if you somehow manage to get it around both stakes at once, that's five points. Which is basically impossible, but I've seen it done."

Bella was already reaching for the hoop, her competitive instincts engaged. Brit pulled it away, teasingly.

"Jane goes first. It's her yard."

Jane took her position at a throwing line Brit scratched in the dirt. She drew her arm back and threw.

The hoop sailed through the air and landed about two feet to the left of both stakes.

“Oof,” Jane said. “That’s hard.”

"My turn." Bella stepped forward. “I’ll show you how to do it.”

They played until the light was gone. Bella turned out to be surprisingly good at the game, racking up points until nobody else had any hope of catching her. Everyone had fun, though, and by the end of the game, even Jane had made a couple of good shots she was proud of.

As the night drew to a close, Jane felt as taken care of as she had ever felt. They were good friends. Suddenly, the act of moving on down the road the old woman had said fit her so well felt like a much more achievable task.

—

The next day got off to an easy start. By mid-morning, not a single bit of it had become complex. Two of her regular customers were milling around the shop, and Jane had greeted them when they came in. Now she left them alone to continue their browsing. They’d tell her if they needed her, and she was content to take it easy

The burnt-top loaves were moving quickly, as they usually did. The meat buns had found their audience at last, mostly through Dave's efforts with the Underbridge Market. The keln was almost gone. For the first time in a few weeks, she felt she had her inventory well in hand. Her new product adjustments were over for the time being, rapidly being replaced by routine.

"Two of the seed loaves, please. And a dozen of those buns, if you have any left."

"Coming right up." Jane wrapped the loaves and buns in paper, then slid them across the counter. "Anything else?"

"That's all for today. Thank you, Jane."

The door opened and another customer walked in, keeping things even more stable. This was what she wanted. She hoped she was finally back into her normal schedule. Her reliability had been abysmal lately, but there was nothing on the horizon that seemed ready to disrupt her now. Soon enough the king and his family would be back in the capital, and she would once again be a normal, respectable, and reliable source of baked goods.

That's all I ask. Let me have six months of peace before anything gets crazy again.

The morning continued in much the same way. By midday, the initial rush had slowed to a trickle. Jane took advantage of the lull to eat a quick lunch behind the counter, then went back to an elbows-down stance of leaning on the counter, enjoying herself without working particularly hard.

It was not to last. The door burst open just as she was considering closing shop for the day, revealing one of the king’s guards. He was obviously in the greatest of hurries. His manner, even in his obvious urgency, was still polite.

"Miss Jane," he said. "You need to come with me. Right away."

Jane looked around her shop. There were three customers browsing the shelves.

"What's happened?"

"I can't explain here." He glanced at the customers, then back at Jane. "Please, miss. It's urgent. I must compel you to come, at the command of the king."

That, at least, wasn't something she could refuse, at least not without making far more trouble for herself than anyone sane would want to make. She turned to the nearest customer and caught her attention.

"Good news," she said, forcing her voice to stay calm. "Goods are free today. Could you spread the word? And if anyone is upset about having already paid, tell them to come see me tomorrow. I'll refund them."

The woman's eyes went wide, but she nodded. "Of course. Is everything all right?"

"I'm sure it's fine." Jane was already untying her apron and hanging it on its hook. "Just some official business. Nothing to worry about. It’s usually very boring."

Jane grabbed her jacket from its peg and followed the guard out the door, leaving her shop open and unattended behind her.

The guard moved through the streets at a pace just short of running. The route they were taking made the destination obvious before long. Whatever this was about, it involved people important enough to warrant gathering in the most official space the town had to offer.

The meeting room was full of people, but Jane's eyes went immediately to the ones who mattered. The king stood near the head of the long table, and the queen sat beside him. Several royal guards were positioned around the room, but a few were clearly in charge, and suddenly stopped talking when Jane walked in the room.

The entire town council was there, arranged along one side of the table. Xand caught Jane's eye as she entered and gave her a small nod. His face was pale.

"Jane." The king waved at a chair. "Thank you for coming so quickly."

"Your Majesty." Jane sat. "What's happened?"

"The prince is missing."

"Missing," she repeated. "What do you mean, missing? He’s somewhere in the town?"

"He and your friend Dave left days ago," the queen said. "They were supposed to reach the merchant's destination, complete a delivery, and return. We expected them back yesterday."

"They haven't reported to either location," the king added. "Not to the town they were traveling to, and not back here. We've contacted that town and even sent riders. There's no sign of them."

Jane felt the blood drain from her face.

"We've checked everything we can check from here,” the king said. "It's as if they vanished into thin air."

"That's not possible." Jane shook her head. "People don't just vanish. There has to be an explanation."

"That's why we called you here." The queen leaned forward. “You have abilities that our guards do not. We need your help to find them."

Jane looked around the room. The people here wouldn’t have called her if they hadn’t tried everything else. It was true she had some abilities that might help here, even if they weren’t custom fit to the situation.

"I'll find them," she said. There was nothing more to say.

“Obviously, you’ll have whatever resources you need. Our carriage has been outfitted and is ready to go at your command. Captain Harris will assist you.. He has full authority to grant you whatever is needed."

The guard stepped forward and saluted. He was an older man. Jane assumed the captain of the king’s guard was a very advanced rank indeed, and nodded back with respect.

"We should start immediately." she said.

"Agreed." The captain nodded. "As the king said. We can move at your command."

Jane took a deep breath.She had, not too long ago, been hoping for six months of peace. It looked like if she wanted that, she’d have to earn it.

So much for calm.
