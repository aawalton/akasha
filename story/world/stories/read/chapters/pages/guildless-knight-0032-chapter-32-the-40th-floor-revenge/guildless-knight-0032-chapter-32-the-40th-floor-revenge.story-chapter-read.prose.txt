As Alan rose to his feet, he felt a sudden jolt of pain in his legs. His bones probably had some minor fracture left—or so he thought. He raised his right foot and gently placed it on the cold white marble floor, putting slight pressure on it to gauge the amount of damage. I guess they aren't in that bad condition, he thought, relaxing slightly since this meant he didn't really have to visit the Healer Association and get practically scammed for a so-called minor treatment.

"This would've never happened had she actually studied healing," Alan muttered, thinking about Ais.

The healer at the Healer's Association and the adventurers who knew healing spells had a fundamental difference. The spells adventurers learned were made to work in such a manner that they focused more on healing major injuries fast. While they also worked on minor injuries, doing so consumed a lot of mana.

Opening the black door, Alan entered into the main hall. His eyes directly fell on Alisa. She was leaning on the sofa with her left leg on the table in front of her and the other one crossed over it, her long white hairs were opened and cascading down from the sofa's headrest.

Placing your leg on the table was something that would've been considered as a sign of disrespect anywhere else, but here? It didn't really matter.

The sound of the door opening caught Alisa's attention. She turned her blue eyes towards Alan, "Oh, hey, morning Alan."

"Morning," Alan replied. Walking towards her, he placed his left hand at the top of the sofa’s backrest and then plopped onto the soft, cushy thing. "How long have I been sleeping for?" he questioned, looking curiously at Alisa.

Alisa blinked, slightly confused by his question. "Isn't that the first thing you were supposed to ask her?" she questioned.

Alan immediately nodded to her response. I mean, it's not like I got any chance to say something, she silenced me with coffee, he thought internally.

Alisa let out a tired sigh. "What would I do of you," she muttered, looking at the table. Then she looked back at him and continued, "You were out for 2 days and few hours."

"Two days huh," Alan muttered, not giving any reaction to it. "What were you thinking about though?"

Alisa's eyes widened. "Wait! How can you tell I was thinking about something?" she said in an overexaggerated voice.

"You were staring at the ceiling, not really doing anything. You didn't really look sleepy, so dozing off at work is out. The only thing left is, you were thinking about something!" Alan replied, matching Alisa's exaggerated energy.

"Weirdo," Alisa mumbled, with a disapproving look on her face.

"Oh please, you were the one who started!" Alan retorted. He was about to throw in another playful jab, but paused. The teasing glint in Alisa’s eyes had vanished, replaced by a sharp, determined expression.

"Well, how do I start," she began in a calm voice, almost too calm for Alan. Taking a moment, she continued, "I was just thinking about conquering floor 40th."

Conquering floor 40th? She's being absurd, Alan thought, his brows twitching with disbelief. "You aren't—" Alan began, but looking at Alisa's face, he changed his words, "Are you serious?" he questioned, his voice filled with disbelief.

"Pretty serious," Alisa replied with a nod. "This request also comes from the church, since the further exploration of our dungeon couldn't really happen without clearing the 40th floor."

It comes from church? Alan thought. "Does Ais know about this?" he questioned, raising his eyebrows.

"She was the fir— no, second person I told this to. And Alan," Alisa continued, looking in his eyes, "I am taking this mission seriously, trust me. We aren't going down there to repeat the tragedy from 12 years ago," she said.

The tragedy from 12 years ago? Ais’s parents' death, huh, he thought. Then, looking at Alisa's expression, Alan knew what her main motive was for this mission, it was closure. She wanted revenge from the monster who took the life of the people closest to her. Not like I can stop her, he mentally added, letting out a tired sigh. "Are you getting Oswald from Iron Fang guild?" he questioned.

"I intend to, yes. Not only him, I believe we'll need 20+ A-ranking adventurers, all of the S-ranking adventurers of the city, and the top healer no matter what their rank is."

"Was Kraikos anything low to mid A-ranking adventurer hope to hurt?" Alan questioned, his eyebrows furrowed, indicating his confusion.

"Not really, I could see a few A-rankers like you, for example, damaging it," Alisa said.

Right— I am still an A-ranker, Alan reminded himself, lowering his gaze. "I don't really think I'll ever rank up," he muttered, his voice filled with self-doubt.

"You’ll get a skill soon enough," Alisa said, gently patting at his back.

"Yeah, maybe," Alan muttered quietly. "I don't know why they put this weird rule though!" he yelled, frustrated at the adventurer’s association rule of only allowing A-rankers with special skill to rank up to S-rank.

Alisa nodded at Alan’s outburst. "True, true," she said. "But it's not like you won't ever get a skill. Ais and most of the adventurers— like 95% of A-rankers just happen to get their skill earlier than you," she added, a subtle smile tugging on her lips.

"You are mocking me, aren't you?"

"Well, I mean, you are probably slacking off now that you live alone," Alisa said. "I gotta whip you in shape, don't I?" she added.

Alan's eyes narrowed. "Yeah, yeah, whatever—" he said in a sarcastic tone. Then, looking at Alisa, he continued, his expression changing to a neutral one. "Won't several A-rankers be just there to hold everyone back though?" he questioned.

"The thing is, Kraikos has undergone an evolution. It's not just one monster we'll be fighting anymore. A group of adventurers from the Iron Fang guild strike team mistakenly fell into floor 40, due to the floor sinking inwards.

"The female adventurer, you might know her, I believe her name was Maya, had a communication crystal with her. She was in live contact with the main team of the Iron Fang guild when she stumbled upon it. The sound of the sinking floor had awakened it, and after that the only thing the main team heard from her side was, 'There are multiple small Kraikos."

"Maya?" Alan mumbled. Wait, it couldn't be her, he thought. "You aren't talking about the one with dark affinit— are you?" Alan questioned, hesitation clear in his voice.

"The one carrying twin daggers and gifted in close combat, that's the Maya I am talking about," Alisa added, her voice low but certain.

She died? Alan repeated, clearly shocked. He remembered the girl from a joint mission with Iron Fang guild. She had a rather sharp tongue, but overall was a nice person, or at least, that's what he believed.
