Clark Odiadeus Haleford, Third Scion of House Haleford, Champions of Medicine Overflowing, First House of the First Empire of Humanity, looked amazing.

The clothes that their Commander, Henrietta Inq, had given them were perfectly fine. They might not have been super warm, but the eternal day and lack of distinct weather in The Jungle was always a decently comfortable temperature, if sometimes too humid when it did its whole not-rain thing. They also could feel a little weird, because they were magical ink-constructs, but they were still clothes. But covering your body was if anything the less important part of clothing! Clothes were meant to communicate who you were to people who had never seen you before, and communicate your daily mood, or at least your daily plans, to people who did know you!

While some people might think that being stranded on a world consisting of nothing but wilderness, where a five-person Forerunner Expedition were the only humans anywhere, meant it wasn't the most important to have clothing perfectly tailored to you and your exact self, those people were wrong. Yes, it might not have been the most important thing, but given they had a steady supply of food and water, bunks to sleep in, defenses from the dragon apocalypse the world was experiencing, a place that they could level-up, and a mostly-automatic source of endless iron, the human desire to relax and express one's self was well past due! The delights of this new world were more than enough to offset the inconveniences of a crude outhouse, limited spices for their food, a rather odd spell-bed to sleep upon, and a distinct lack of new media to enjoy... but bland clothing was quite past the line of acceptability.

And so, Clark had spent as much time as he could spare, when not cooking or cleaning or changing out the clockwork heart motors of the iron-transport carts or maintaining the Bronzing Crucible or healing his teammates as Alyssa Ride had some spell blow up in her face, or Oliver Smith had an enchantment overheat and burn his hands, or Jacob Veeran got a shallow cut while fighting off a dragon ten times his size, working on making his clothes presentable.

[Unblemish], he thought, might have finally overtaken [Dazzling Smile] as his favorite skill. A year or so of having nothing but the single piece of magic to work with had given him so much appreciation for just how cool it was. He hadn't thought that much about it being his only fully on-Class skill when he had last-minute decided to use [Prince of Shining Streams] as his Expedition Class, but the healing/cleaning skill had so many depths.

⟨Refine⟩ and ⟨Restore⟩ had already proven themselves super useful. ⟨Refine⟩ helped make shiny things shinier, metal purer, rope softer, rock harder, and that kind of thing. ⟨Restore⟩ made wood un-burn, recharged expended batteries and Network Caches, and probably did a bunch more that would be even cooler once The Factory grew more and more.

But his latest subskill, gotten after many, many hours of practice was ⟨Retouch⟩. Because it was new, so new he had yet to even get Henrietta or Oliver to check on its description for him, he didn't know everything it could do. But at a minimum, it had let him turn his featureless black tunic-dress thing into something more befitting a Healer of House Haleford.

He was still limited to a bit of a basic robe template, but he had been able to give himself half-sleeves, recolor it to blue with white trimmings - white with blue trimmings was too much of a change from pure black - and give himself a collar.

Clark loved collars.

He swept side to side, letting the swooshier parts of his outfit do their thing, and he gave himself a few minutes to just appreciate what he'd done. Then, he went back to actually cooking dinner.

"Ooh, fancy colors," Alyssa said. "D'you think you could do something like that for me?"

Clark swept around, seeing that the Ranger was suddenly lounging around on one of their new chairs. Alyssa had actually been the one to make that particular chair, he recalled, but she was now deliberately sitting in it wrong. She hadn't been there just a few seconds ago, either, so she must have fallen out of the sky and specifically landed on it in an incorrect sitting position in just the time Clark had been fiddling with his bowl of tallow.

"I can try!" Clark cheerfully greeted her as he measured out a dollop of fat to cook some vegetables in.

"I'm kind of surprised you even can change the color. I thought Henrietta's magic was more color-limited than that?"

"You could ask her," Clark shrugged. "But it works for me."

"Yeah, but how," Alyssa insisted.

"Magic?" Clark guessed.

"I... hate that answer," she grouched.

"Is that not the answer you gave to Oliver when he was asking the means by which your skill-combinations were working according to your design?"

"Why do you think I hate it?" Alyssa good-naturedly shot back.

Clark laughed, then returned his attention to the food.

"It's not illusory," Oliver noted with some disappointment. "It's an actual transmutation, no discrepancy between sight and physical reality."

"Oh," Clark replied, then thought for a moment. "Is that not better?"

"I mean, yes. It is objectively better for the purposes of you working on our clothing, as well as the potential for your ⟨Retouch⟩ing to be used in other situations." Oliver ceded. "But it is specifically worse for my current focus and active project, so it's unfortunate in that narrow sense. Oh, and also it looks to be relatively... fragile, I guess is how I'd describe it?"

"How can you have a fragile piece of magic?" Alyssa asked.

"I'm getting there," Oliver shot back. "The easiest way I could describe it is that its form-Attunement is incomplete. So it will probably repair back to this shape, but exposure to other strong magics will probably break the effect, and simple physical breakage or disassembly would cause it to revert. I'd be curious to see how it interacts with thicker objects too. It's a coating of magic that reaches inwards, which makes me wonder if it can only do the cloth because of how thin it is."

"So... it's a superficial change?" She asked. "Yeah that checks out, for [Unblemish]."

"It's still an intensely valuable skill," Henrietta stepped in, expertly dispersing the brewing argument before it could properly erupt. "What were you hoping for, Smith? I thought you were working on your new staff."

"I am," he confirmed. "But illusions are an incredibly powerful tool for all kinds of development and more advanced technology, and because ⟨Integrate⟩ says that it will cause items affected by it to grow in sophistication, I was kind of hoping to get something in there that might eventually help me with making full holograms."

"Ah. So you were hoping that ⟨Retouch⟩ might contain some associations with elemental Illusion which you might be capable of copying."

"But I thought Illusion covered how everything looked?" Clark asked. It was an element he'd become quite familiar with over time, and many of his classes involved it in some form. Even [Dazzling Smile] was a Light and Illusion skill!

"It is," Oliver absently confirmed. "But when appearance and reality are perfectly entwined, elemental Illusion is also entwined with the other elements. For what I want, I need to isolate it. And it's not like how I could just force myself to get mad to get a sample of elemental Hatred for when I was making the deflection wards. I didn't need that to be isolated, because I also wanted everything Associated with Hatred to also be deflected. For this, I specifically want just the Illusion. They're pretty easy to manipulate and get detailed with, and therefore useful for lots of things."

"Oh."

"But I don't truly need it," Oliver shrugged. "I'll be making the actual staff out of steel, bronze, copper, and glass."

"So everything we currently can make," Alyssa pointed out.

"No. I'm not using reeds, clay, or lumber for this one."

"Spoil the joke, why don't you."

"Why do you want a new staff?" Clark asked. "Is it on account of your old one merely breaking down as you use it, in a means which I cannot repair?"

"There is a bit of that," Oliver agreed, "I did the best I could with that staff, but I just have far better tools now and a better staff makes everything easier. But I want to make a Pillar of Industry-type staff, because it's effectively perfect for our situation, it ties into our current goals really well. Especially when it comes to integrating more variance in what our machines can make, as I'm scaling into proto-automata. Things like different thickness for our iron plates and rods, and getting more intelligent conditions active. The wood kiln really showed the limits of my Law enchanting abilities. Also, our baseline enchantments have gotten a lot more sophisticated, so I want to get some kind of Broadcast Network relay in it..."

"Sounds ambitious," Henrietta remarked.

"It sounds more complicated than it is," Oliver shrugged. "If I wanted to stretch, I would incorporate hand-holds into the construction, but I'm trying to keep it to strictly a Pillar of Industry, even if it's probably going to be a bit less refined than I'd like."

"See, you say the word 'handhold' and I feel like what you and I think about in response to that word are very different," Alyssa commented.

"A handhold in the context of spellcasting is something designed to hold or brace the fingers against, to help with getting specific positions more reliably," Henrietta answered, before Oliver had the chance to do so himself. "But how do holograms fit in with advancement or control?"

"Oh," Oliver blinked. "I thought that was obvious. Prototyping and instructions. As we get into more complicated machinery, having blueprints will be more important... why do they call them blueprints?"

"That is due to the original copying process involving a light-curing ink that turned blue when cured," Jacob didn't look up from where he was running his mana-wreathed hands down his [Frostblade], causing runes to briefly light up along its length. "The design would be copied to tracing paper with heavy ink, and the blue-print would then be exposed to light, such that when it finished curing, any portions that were not covered by ink would be dyed blue."

"Huh." Oliver paused in his System-interaction. "That's neat. Anyway, yeah! I used holograms all the time in school. They're fantastic for modeling and planning. I didn't expect to get full functionality at the start, but if there's something, I'd expect something that approximates skill growth to develop it over time?"

"And for that, you just needed to isolate the element?"

Oliver nodded.

"Well," the Commander mused, "It's a little different from what I've done in the past, but have you considered..."

Magic flowed from Clark's hands, his skill's golden mana folding around the metal he was holding onto and turning it absolutely pristine.

[Unblemish], he mentally chanted, pushing the Skill as much as he could. ⟨Refine⟩. ⟨Retouch⟩.

Magic poured out from him, his skill working as hard as it could to render the scaffolding he was touching absolutely pristine. Little bends straightened out, the surface became shinier, and the rust that kept trying to form needed to fight Clark to do their thing. Not that there was much rust, but it was enough to make the perfectly flattened iron plate look like metal, rather than a mirror.

And they needed it to look like a mirror.

The heart of an illusion was that senses lied. That seeing was not believing, that reality did not bend to the thoughts and senses of those who beheld it!

And what better tool for that, than a mirror?

Not that one mirror would be enough, oh no. Instead, the iron contraption he was on contained two mirrors. One, hanging out over the edge of the Spire, and the other just past its edge. Both were set at an angle, so that they each reflected what the other saw. What that meant was that if someone was directly below Clark, on the 'ground floor' of First Tower and looked up, they would just see the sky as it was directly above the other mirror.

But that meant that they wouldn't see Clark, and in so doing, he would be cloaked by an illusion of invisibility.

It required absolutely perfect mirrors, though. Ones entirely without blemish, and as fine as they came.

And it was a perfect job for Clark, as a result.

He clung to the metal, using the support rods as a handhold, and poured as much of his power as he could into ensuring that all of it was absolutely pristine, that his touch would perfect it.

Alyssa suddenly bounded up next to him, talking as she began to slowly drift back down. "Ollie wants to know if you can try to pulse your spell a bit more? The constant fixing is causing interference."

"But if I do not continuously maintain it-" Clark began.

"I know, I know," Alyssa's head had sunk to below his feet, and she was calling up to him in her response, "But the idea is that if you..."

Her voice became inaudible, and Clark shook his head to communicate that he couldn't hear her. A moment later, she stopped floating, dropped to the ground below, then simply ran up the side of the Spire and perched on its edge to talk to him. "The theory is that the increased magic is screwing up the elemental formation. But if you pulse it, a few seconds of it being inactive, when we're dealing with a hundred-foot distance... it shouldn't break the illusion."

"I shall attempt it!"

Alyssa tossed him a thumbs-up, then dove off the cliffside.

Clark did his best to pulse his magic, with a few more variations as Alyssa relayed extra instructions, until Oliver decided to try engraving a few more runes on the back of the plate. After he was done with that, then it worked!

"Why do you sound surprised?" Alyssa huffed when Clark provided his congratulations.

"I am not! I am merely celebratory that we have accomplished such a momentous task!" he replied.

"I mean, I wasn't sure if it would work," Oliver shrugged, even as he twisted his fingers in all kinds of twisty ways over the spell-tablet he had been working with. "The end positioning wound up being so fiddly, I'm kind of still amazed that it worked at all."

"But it shall suffice for your staff, shall it not?" Clark inclined his head to get a better look at Oliver's new artifact. Truthfully, it didn't look like much, simply a clay tablet with a hole in the middle, yet Clark knew that the hole was merely an illusion - its solidity was readily visible to his mana sight, a definitive sign of an actual illusion.

"Oh," Oliver grinned widely, "It very much shall."
