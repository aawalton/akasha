Chapter 198 / B3-43: Welcome Home, Margen

After leaving High Park, Joe had less than an hour to kill before the ceremony that would officially announce the return of Margen to Fort Coral. Even though he was dreading being up on stage in front of all those eyes, Joe was ninety percent sure that everyone would be looking at the mighty living legend and not him. At most, he hoped he would only have a few minutes of spotlight thrown his way as Septimus Silver extolled the virtue of the Adventurer’s Guild and Joe’s place in Margen’s rescue.

He was tempted to find something to do to pass the time before he had to join the assembly, but he knew all too well that if he allowed himself to get too sidetracked and end up being late, Kenda would not be happy with him. Considering she was his date for the ball at House Jarlscarp later that evening, he decided he might as well play it safe and stick around Founders Plaza. He and Mojo found a bench in a comfortable bit of shade and watched as the finishing touches were being added to the stage erected in the middle of the plaza.

Although there were touches of the other founders’ colors: red, white, and gold, predominantly, the banners and stage bunting were in Margen’s signature blue. It took Joe a minute of confusion as he studied the octagonal podium to realize what his brain was trying to tell him was wrong. The large chest-high stage was in the dead middle of the plaza, right where the towering central fountain typically stood. The fountain depicting the four founders, which was at least 12 feet tall, was nowhere to be seen. Joe wondered if they had moved it, shrunk it, shunted it into extra-dimensional space, or what.

As he pondered the mystery, someone stepped up to the bench he was at. Joe looked up to find one of the city council members standing before him, one he had met before, Hogam Goldboar. The spirit-shaman had helped Joe on the beach during the Cauldrakon event, and he was one of the few people, outside the residents of the Abbaca House, that Mahq spoke about. While the young spiritualist seemed to walk his own road, he did receive some mentoring from the dwarven manitou.

“It is good to see you again, Joe,” the leader of the Lorekeepers stated, extending his hand in greeting. Joe took it, and after a firm shake, Hogam settled onto the bench beside them. Mojo seemed slightly wary of the powerful spiritualist, but a friendly headscratch dispelled the broodling’s caution. The shaggy shug-monkey hopped into the man's lap for full petting. The manitou’s host of spirits seemed to welcome Mojo into their midst, swirling about the pup.

After a few minutes, Hogam turned his gaze to the center of the plaza, squinting at the bright sun-lit stage, and sighed. “In half a bell that will be us up there. Are you excited? What you did, recovering Margen, was nothing short of miraculous. You seem to have a knack for pulling off miracles, Joe,” the man added, wagging a pair of immensely bushy dwarven eyebrows. Hogam was referring to when Joe channeled the divine might of Mercy Suku and her goddess to heal Rattler Squad during Cauldrakon’s attack on the city.

“Thanks. To be honest, it was dumb luck in finding Margen.”

“Maybe so, but getting him out alive was not luck. That was you, Joe. Even just bringing Doomherald out of the Erlking’s lair would have been celebration-worthy."

“I guess so. I’m just a bit weirded out thinking about the whole city staring at me,” he said, watching the workers set up a lectern on the stage. “You don’t sound too overjoyed about being up on stage either,” Joe noted.

“Oh, my reluctance has nothing to do with the number of eyes on me. You get used to that in my role and vocation. Trust me. For every person watching us, there will be twice as many spirits checking us out.”

“You know that just made it worse,” Joe sighed.

The manitou chuckled and continued with his thoughts. “For me its going to be the temperature. I’m not sure why we are doing this in the late afternoon during Seedsow. It’s going to be blindingly bright and unbearably hot on that podium. My clan are deep dwarves, not mountain smiths. This was the lightest formal wear I own, and I’m already starting to sweat here with you in the shade. Down there in the sun, we’ll be a pair of flanks on the grill.”

“Oh, I can help you there.” Joe reached into his shirt and pulled out some of the ice drake scales he had removed from his gambeson and stashed in his new outfit. “I learned this trick with my armor. My suit has all these little pockets that are perfect for holding cooling items, like these." Joe extended the discs to Hogam. "I came from someplace much less tropical. If this is spring in Fort Coral, then I expect to be melting come summertime. Put those around your neck, under your arms, and on your wrists. Those seem to be some of the best places to bring down body temp. Well, groin too, but if you go that route, then you can consider those a gift,” Joe amended with a smile.

“Very clever. I have made friends with a [Spectral Snow Wisp] who I can call on to cool me down, but these are a much better idea. I will get them back to you right afterwards.”

“Don’t worry about it, sir. Now that I think about it, those are the scales I first purchased, and they are just about out of mana. I doubt they will last through the summer. Keep them. I am learning how to ward, and one of the wards on my “To Learn” list is a cooling ward.”

“Ah, the FrostBring ward. That’s a good one to know around here. If you learn it, you’ll have an easy way to make yourself a good bit of coin, inscribing it for folks around town. Especially with the summer heat just around the corner. How long have you been warding? It’s a very useful skill to know, especially since it’s one of the few crafter skills that are class-based. Not everyone gets a chance to learn warding, unlike leatherwork or smithing.”

“Just a week or so. It’s a pretty neat skill. Like alchemy and enchanting, there are tons of options. I just wish the number of wards you could know wasn’t so limited.”

“Each of those has its own limitation: time, money, or skill. Alchemy is slow to prepare and takes a great deal of equipment. Enchanting is by far the most expensive of the empowerment crafts. Warders are forced to specialize to a limited number of wards they know. I think The One Above is trying to keep it all fair and balanced.”

“Sounds about right,” Joe agreed. He signed again loudly before admitting what he already knew was true, “I guess it's about time we got down there.”

“Buck up, Joe,” Hogam said, clapping his shoulder. “It won’t be that bad. If you can charge under a legendary storm beast and channel the might of a goddess and saint, you can handle an appreciative crowd and a damned-bright sunny day.”

Knowing the city’s lead spiritualist was right, Joe squared his shoulders and decided to stop resisting. The more of a deal he made about being up on stage in his head, the more uncomfortable he would be. Mind over matter wasn’t a sure-fire solution, but he knew psyching himself out was surely going to make it worse.

Together, he and Hogam crossed the plaza to find out where they would be placed. Joe kind of hoped his spot would be beside the good-natured dwarf.

Hogam had called it. Even though the sun was hours past its zenith, the radiant heat off the podium was sweltering. Thankfully, the head of the Storm Wardens, Archdruid Dawnsong, summoned a cooling breeze that swooped through those on the elevated platform every minute or so, reminding Joe a bit of an oscillating fan, cool, then hot, then cool again.

From the high stage, Joe looked out at the massive crowd filling Founder’s Plaza to the brink. Sailors in loose shirts and sun-faded scarves leaned shoulder to shoulder with merchants, priests, performers, and well-dressed sea-traders. Flags snapped overhead: deep red, sun-gold, and white, lightly interspersed between the majority that bore Margen’s cobalt blue shade. Drums pulsed somewhere in the crowd, uneven but enthusiastic, and laughter rose and fell like the tide.

On the raised platform at the heart of the plaza stood Joe, alongside Myllo, Tez, and a stunning Kendell. Joe had no idea how her ball-outfit was going to compete with the garb she had chosen for her stage appearance. The primarily white and gold attire was elegant and formal, yet was able to give the suggestion of armor, stating to the gathered throng of Forters that she was an adventurer first and foremost.

Joe tugged at the collar of his new tunic, which was suddenly far too tight, only to have her hand find his. She drew his fingers away and thankfully didn’t let go. She squeezed his hand and tipped her chin as the man of the hour stepped up on stage, which drove the crowd wild. The resounding cheer was deafening and infectious. Joe found himself free of Kendell's supporting grip and clapping along with everyone else.

The ancient general was taller than anyone else on the platform, broad-shouldered and composed, bearing the legendary blade, DoomHerald, across his back.

The riotous shouts from the crowd coalesced into a thundering chant. “Mar-gen! Mar-gen! Mar-gen!”

The ancient general inclined his head slightly, acknowledging the people with a quiet dignity that only seemed to make them shout louder. This was Fort Coral after all. Any city that chose the god of merriment as its patron deity was not one to hold back.

Joe watched with a bit of envy as the mighty swordman acknowledged the vast sea of chanters, wishing he knew how to do that. Instead, he wiped his palms discreetly against his trousers.

“Steady, Joe,” Kenda spoke beside him, taking his hand again. She was almost shouting, but Joe was pretty sure nobody other than the guilders around them would be able to hear her over the crowd. “They are going to cheer for you as well, in a minute.”

Joe blinked. “They … no. Not like that.”

“You’ll see,” she shot back. “You brought him home. You’re going to be standing up there with him in a minute. Trust me, you don’t want to look spooked. Remember, this is a city of sea dogs and net draggers. They will bust your chops forever if they smell a good target.”

“Oh, for crying out loud,” Joe groaned, getting another hand-squeeze from the russet-haired guilder. Before Joe could add more, he was interrupted by a trumpet, ringing out with a clear and bright clarion. The bellowing crowd slowly settled; the chanting faded into scattered cheers, then into a hum of anticipation.

A tall man stepped forward, made his way to the lectern, and raised one hand for silence. It was Septimus Silver, the leader of the Adventurers Guild. Even though Myllo was the hands-on captain of the guild, Joe understood how Septimus held the highest seat in the organization. The man had a presence that demanded attention. Just looking at him told everyone that here was someone who wielded great power and influence. His silver-threaded coat shimmered in the sunlight, matching his argent hair perfectly.

“People of Fort Coral!” Septimus called, his voice carrying effortlessly across the plaza.

The crowd answered with a roar. Septimus waited, letting the energy crest and break before continuing.

“We gather tonight not merely for celebration, though we have much to celebrate, but for remembrance, and for gratitude.”

He gestured outward, toward the sea. “This city was not built in a day, nor defended in a single battle. It stands because of those who came before us, those who fought, who sacrificed, who refused to yield when the tides turned against us.”

“And among those names, few are more honored than this city’s founding four: Mira Dreamseer.” Septimus paused long enough to let the crowd cheer for the legendary diviner. “Jequa the Red.” More cheers. “Tintze, the Master Trader.” The level of cries rose even higher. Either the clever schemer was a crowd favorite, or Septimus was succeeding in working up his audience. “And last, but of course by no means least, the great general who is lost no more. Thanks to the valiant efforts of the Adventurer’s Guild, the Lord of the Sword once more walks among us. Welcome back, Margen, the Mighty!”

The crowd erupted again, even louder than before. Septimus raised his voice to meet the noise. “Long have we told his story as legend! Long have we honored him as one lost to time. But tonight … tonight, Fort Coral stands witness to a miracle.”

He stepped aside, fully presenting Margen to the people.

The reaction was immediate and overwhelming. Cheers, whistles, shouts, some people even wept openly. Sailors banged mugs against the sides of barrels. Bosun whistles shrieked in shrill exuberance. Casters invoked bursts of brilliantly colored illusions that shot through the air like fireworks.

Margen stepped forward and simply raised one hand. It was not a grand gesture. It didn’t need to be. The crowd gradually quieted, drawn in by his powerful, steady presence.

“I thank you,” Margen said. That was all at first, and yet, it held the vast throng on bated breath.

“You cannot imagine what it is like to find your own dream has become real. The crazy idea Tinz, My, Jequa, and I strove for of a safe place for our people on some far shore. A dream born of desperation, yet sprinkled with hope. The longest of long shots, only possible through Mira’s amazing gift. She saw one tiny grain of gold in a barrel of ashes. And so we fled Glandryth, following that minuscule, glinting gamble across the Hornlands. Yet here it is. Here you are!” he bellowed, driving the horde of forters crazy again.

He let the elated shouts continue for a few beats before holding up his hand again. He had the crowd totally enthralled. In seconds, the cheering fell silent as they waited for his next words.

“I am prouder of you all than I can possibly express. You have built our dream. A thriving city on Coral Bay. Not the Old Fort crouching on the cliffs. Not the makeshift huts and muddy roads. Not walls unfinished or a harbor unguarded.

“My wildest dream was a sturdy haven, far from the Necromancer’s blight; a bastion to keep us safe. I see now what it has become; what you have made of it. I have been brought home not to a simple soldier’s shield but to a beautiful jewel, full of life, love, and cheer. Merry be, my friends. Merry Be!”

The crowd completely lost its composure. It shouted itself hoarse. Joe found himself swept up in the riotous glee. By the time it came for the limelight to fall on him, he didn’t even care. Having his name chanted was a bit embarrassing, but Joe locked up his nerves and let the joy around him become his joy as well.
