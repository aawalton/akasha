Since Afallon Caer was the main destination of their entire trip to Avalon, they didn’t enter unprepared.

Between Veronica, Gwen, Nasha, and Orban, they had brought nearly everything they thought might be useful for their expedition. Gwen and Nasha carried large backpacks containing most of their supplies: food and water, dungeoneering kits, all their potions, and leftover pillipilli fruits. Aside from the warning that there might be traps within, they didn’t know much else about what to expect.

Phillus walked ahead of them, guiding the way to the castle’s entrance. A pair of white doors sat out front, large enough for even him to pass through.

“Have you actually been inside before, Phillus?” Gwen asked.

“Many times,” he replied. “Before Avalon was sealed, this castle was regularly used by Morgan and several of the guardians. I myself prefer the Lightless Spring, but I did occasionally come here for various things. Past these doors should be the receiving hall. From there, we’ll be able to reach most sections of the castle.”

“He could be anywhere inside, then, is that right?” Veronica asked.

“Indeed. He may very well be waiting behind the doors for us.”

“Phillus, I’m going to literally cry if he’s actually standing there. Don’t jinx it,” Gwen said flatly.

“What is a jinx?” he replied, turning his head.

“Ignore her,” Veronica quickly interjected. “Let’s just get this over with.”

They reached the front doors and looked at one another before nodding.

Veronica took the first step forward. The doors themselves didn’t have handles, so she simply pushed them open.

As Phillus had said, the inside was a receiving hall. A massive lobby stood before them, lined with multiple pillars and statues of knights holding swords. Several halls and exits were spread around the room, leading deeper within. A staircase at the far end led up to a second floor that branched into walkways along the walls, almost like a manor.

Veronica was the first to step through.

“Well, I’ll be,” Orban said, walking behind her and looking around.

“Wow…” Gwen said softly, trailing behind them. “This place is beautifu—”

Her words were cut short.

Veronica immediately felt something was wrong. She turned around in an instant.

But no one was there.

Gwen. Nasha. Orban. Even Phillus. They were all gone. Not just them, but the doorway she had come through had disappeared. She stared at a wall made of some type of white stone, like marble.

“Gwen?” Veronica said out loud. Immediately, she turned back ahead, and this time, the lobby had changed.

The giant foyer of the receiving hall had vanished. In front of her wasn’t a giant staircase leading to the second floor, but another wall. To be precise, there were three walls in front of her. Each had a set of white doors similar to the one she had entered through.

Veronica narrowed her eyes and froze, taking a moment to think. She wasn’t in a grand receiving hall anymore. No, she was in a small room. Four walls, with three doors.

She quickly turned around once more, and her expression grew dimmer.

Now, there was a set of double white doors behind her, where the solid wall had been.

She turned and faced forward again. The walls and doors were still there. She repeated this motion a few more times until it seemed that nothing else would change.

It was a small room that would take her no longer than a few seconds to walk from one corner to the other. Four walls, with four doors. If she hadn’t been standing close to a wall already, she might have gotten completely lost as to where she had entered from.

“Gwen? Nasha? Orban? Anybody?” Veronica called out.

There was no response.

Sage. You still here with me?

[Yes, I am always with you.]

How reassuring. Now tell me. What’s going on?

[I am unsure. Whatever phenomenon that caused everyone to disappear is purely physical in nature. I do not sense any signs of mental tampering.]

So this isn’t an illusion?

[This may still be an illusion, but one that affects the world itself instead of your own perception. Further testing may be needed.]

Veronica looked around at each wall. Everything was identical. The only thing that stood out so far was that she was standing a few feet from the door behind her. Going backward might be her safest bet.

Be on the lookout for anything strange, Sage. I’m counting on you to be my second pair of eyes.

[Got it.]

Turning around, Veronica placed both hands on the doors behind her and pushed them open.

Her eyes narrowed once more.

“Damn it…” she muttered.

Beyond them was another room. Three walls stood in front of her, with three sets of doors. A nearly identical copy of the room she was currently in.

Veronica glanced behind her one last time to make sure nothing was out of the ordinary. When she saw nothing, she took a step forward and crossed into the next room.

The door behind her remained open, and she could see back into the starting room.

“Any suggestions for what I should do?” Veronica asked out loud.

[Try opening each door in the current room but do not exit through them.]

Veronica did as Sage suggested and began opening each door. She went to the first wall on the left and pushed its doors open.

As expected, it was another room. Three more walls, three more sets of doors. When she turned to the side, aiming to open the middle set of doors, she noticed that the door she had entered through had closed.

She frowned.

Sage. How did those doors close?

[The doors shut themselves the moment you opened the left-side set. They didn’t turn or creak; they simply closed in an instant.]

Veronica stood there for a few moments, trying to think it through. The door she had just opened was still open, leading to the next room. If already-open doors closed whenever she tried to open a new set, then there was no choice but to push forward, opening them one by one until she reached the end.

If there was an end.

The next fifteen minutes were spent simply studying the illusion.

Veronica tried everything she could think of, including some of Sage’s suggestions.

She conjured some ice on the ground, placing it as a marker before traveling to a new room. She opened one door, held onto its hinges, and used a gust of wind magic to open the opposite set. She also opened one set of doors but stayed in the same room, using wind to open the opposite doors in the next room.

Sage recorded every test she performed. There were some consistencies she had found.

Firstly, no matter what, she could only ever have a single set of doors open. Even if she used wind magic to open another set, the first would close. There was no physical motion to it. It just… happened. Like the doors simply vanished and were replaced by a closed set.

Secondly, she never came to a repeated room. Even after setting down chunks of ice as markers, she never reappeared in a room and found ice or a melted puddle, even if she went through to another room and took the same set of doors back.

Thirdly, even if she destroyed the walls or doors, nothing changed. She used one spell to blow up an entire section of a wall. It did lead her into another room without using the door. But when she remained still and tried to blow up the next wall, the entire thing reappeared in front of her, door and all. She couldn’t even see the flash of her explosion before the wall was instantly replaced.

The same thing happened if she attacked the corner of the room, hoping to break into three other rooms at once. The moment her spell struck, it disappeared. Not because her magic was being canceled, but because the wall instantly repaired itself before she could even catch a glimpse of the other rooms. Of course, the same thing happened with the floor and ceiling. This created the illusion that the walls were indestructible.

Whatever illusion she was in, it only allowed her to move into one room at a time.

She thought about going all out and using her most destructive spell to brute force her way through. However, if the others were also stuck in a maze like this, she might accidentally kill one of them with her magic. She couldn’t afford to be reckless.

“I can’t seem to escape these rooms by trying to outsmart the illusion. For some reason, it just wants me to keep going through new rooms,” Veronica said out loud.

Maybe if she just ran forward in a single direction, she would find something new eventually.

Illusions and tricks were never her forte. Hopefully, the others were also trapped in the same space as her so she could find them.

“I’ll go ahead and run through a few dozen rooms. Sage, record if anything different happens in any of them.”

[Very well.]

Veronica took a deep breath, walked over to one of the doors, and placed her palm on it.

Hopefully, I can find one of the others. Maybe someone has figured something out. Nasha’s probably good at this type of thing.

As she pushed the door open and prepared herself to run, she froze. Her eyes went wide.

[This is the first significant change in environment since you entered this illusionary space.]

In front of her was a spacious room with gray walls, lined with wooden racks holding weapons like spears, swords, and shields. Two chandeliers hung above, illuminating the room with flickering candlelight. At the far end were full sets of black armor that seemed to stare at her eerily. Between them was another door, like any other.

An armoury? A barracks?

Veronica looked around, but then she paused. Her ears twitched.

There was a low, scraping sound. The sound of metal.

One of the suits of armor near the far end began to clink and clatter. The pieces shook as if worn by an invisible person. It took a step forward, its movements uncontrolled as if it didn’t know how to walk. One by one, the other sets of armor walked off their stands, all with an awkward gait. But gradually, within seconds, they moved much more smoothly. All six suits of armor began pulling weapons from the racks.

[They appear to be hostile.]

Yeah, thanks, Veronica replied mentally, already gathering mana for a spell.

The first knight rushed her.

Veronica raised her hand, fingers clawed as she swiped upward at it.

Seven traces of light flashed before the armored knight. A moment later, seven explosions echoed through the room, engulfing the entire armoury in flashing orange and red and immense heat.

Veronica stared at the devastation; the entire room was now flooded with raging flames.

Then her eyes widened; she instinctively dashed backward as a sword swung through the flames, slashing at where she had been standing.

Still covered in smoldering flames, the black knight walked out completely unscathed. It almost looked like a demon knight covered in infernal flames.

“Not even a scratch?” Veronica murmured, looking at it.

It didn’t take long for the other five knights to walk out through the door, each one covered in brimming flames. Just like the first, none of them were injured. At least, it was hard to tell, considering they were just pieces of armor with no host.

Veronica channeled mana into both palms.

“Looks like this might be a bit difficult,” she said quietly.
