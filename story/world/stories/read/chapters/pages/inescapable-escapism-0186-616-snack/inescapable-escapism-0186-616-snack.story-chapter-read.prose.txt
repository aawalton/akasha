Slowly, my sight returned to normal, but still, I couldn’t look away from the mirror. I was transfixed by my eyes. The darkness had drained out of them. My irises were no longer a strange dark hue that I wasn’t used to seeing on my face, but there were still a few dots of gold scattered across the lighter shade of blue. They weren’t glowing so brightly that they seemed to be emitting light anymore, but they refused to leave completely.

Had they always been there, I couldn’t help but ask myself as I continued to stare at my own face. Or had my eyes truly changed? That shouldn’t have been possible. As far as I was aware, the colour of a person’s eyes was permanent. It didn’t change over time, and the other world shouldn’t have been able to impact that. Nothing like that had ever happened to me before. I’d developed bruises because of something that happened to me in another reality, but nothing more. Nothing permanent.

Maybe I was just being paranoid, I tried to tell myself. Perhaps I’d always had light coloured dots in my eyes, and I’d just never noticed before. I didn’t spend much time staring at my reflection, so that felt possible, but anxiety still pulled at me. I wanted to know for sure.

Did I have a picture anywhere? That would be good. Then, I could compare how my eyes used to look to what I was seeing in the mirror, and I could be certain. If there were no golden speckles in the picture, then I’d know that something about the other world had changed me.

I couldn’t remember ever taking a photo of my eyes, though. I wasn’t sure when or why I would have done that, but I needed to check. I had to grab my phone and flick through it. Maybe there was one of Phoebe and me that I could zoom in on or something.

My eyes left the mirror, and I started to turn towards my bed where I’d last seen my phone, but I didn’t make it that far. I had barely rotated before my gaze landed on my door, and I froze again.

It was open. Wide open.

Panic sparked in my chest, and I felt my breath catch as I stared at it. How long had I been standing there? How much time has passed whilst I was entranced by the sight of my own eyes? My mom could have walked past to surreptitiously check on Rex, and she might have noticed me there. If she came by again and noticed that I was still there, it could be an issue. She’d want to know what it was that I was finding so interesting about my reflection, and I couldn’t think of a single excuse.

My gaze flicked back towards the mirror one last time, lingering on my eyes for a few more seconds, before I hurried towards the door, making sure my footsteps made no sound. I paused, holding my breath and straining my ears to pick up any noises, but I heard nothing other than the faint hum of the television coming from downstairs.

Relief lapped at me as I slowly shut my bedroom door and let out a sigh. My body longed to stay there, to keep my gaze fixed on the bathrobe hanging on the back of my door, but I forced myself to turn. My movements were careful. Reluctance coursed through me as I did, and it was impossible to ignore the fear thrashing in my heart.

The urge was still there. That was what worried me. I could still feel the desire to walk towards my window and stare out. It wasn’t as strong as it had been before. It no longer felt so strong that I may cease to be if I ignored it, but part of me still longed to look upon the sky.

Before I could stop myself, my eyes flicked towards the window, and I caught sight of the night sky. An electric jolt shot through me, but that was tempered by surprise as I realised it was dark outside.

When had that happened, I asked myself stupidly. I couldn’t remember what time it had been when I’d left my reality, but I was certain it was not dark. The sun hadn’t even begun to set, so at least a few hours must have passed, if not more.

I had to check my phone, I realised before taking a step towards my bed, where Rex was sleeping. He didn’t even stir as I reached over him to grab my mobile, wincing as the screen lit up and guilt speared my heart.

It was so late. Rex should have had dinner more than two hours ago, and I felt awful. I had snuck him a handful of food and a chew when we’d gotten home, but that wasn’t enough. He must have been starving, and he probably needed the toilet too. I hadn’t let him out at all since we got back from Duncan’s.

“Rex,” I said softly, smiling as he lifted his head to look at me for just a second before lowering it again, his eyes fluttering shut. “Awww, sleepy boy. I’m so sorry for waking you and forgetting to feed you, but it’s dinner time. Do you want some food?”

At that, Rex’s eyes opened again. He sat up, immediately alert, and I smiled again as he stood. Rex stretched, yawning widely, before leaping off my bed.

“Good boy,” I told him as I slipped my phone into my pocket and followed him towards my bedroom door.

As we made our way towards the stairs and began to descend, the sound of the television grew louder. I glanced into the lounge as we reached the bottom, trying to work out what my parents were watching, but I couldn’t tell. Some kind of car chase was happening on the screen, but that was all I got. I quickly looked away, my eyes finding Rex again. He’d started to turn into the kitchen, but I spoke quickly to stop him.

“Rex,” I called softly, hoping the loud gunshots blasting through the speakers drowned out my voice. “Toilet first, then food, okay?”

I wasn’t sure whether Rex would understand that, but he seemed to. He turned away from the kitchen, following after me as I went into my lounge. He didn’t seem too bothered about the fact that he’d have to wait a little longer to eat, but I felt terrible. I felt like an awful pet owner, and I needed to do better. Rex deserved better.

Automatically, I pulled back the curtains that covered the French windows before freezing as a realisation slammed into me, breaking through my guilt. The moment I opened it, I would not be able to resist the urge to look at the sky. It hadn’t been too difficult before. I’d managed to glance at it from my room, and I hadn’t been sucked in, but I was pretty sure it would be different if I did it again. There wouldn’t be a window separating me from the stars. I would be out there, exposed, and I wouldn’t have anything else to distract me.

Apart from Rex. He’d help, I told myself. Somehow, I was certain that he would keep me from spending an eternity waiting for the stars to change, and I wasn’t sure how, but he would.

Taking a deep breath, I twisted the key before sliding the window open. Rex rushed out past me, but I hesitated. Instinctively, my gaze flicked around the garden and field beyond, scanning it as I checked for movement or anything that seemed suspicious before catching myself. I let out a silent snort as I shook my head, aware that I was being silly. I wasn’t at the Academy. There was no need for me to be checking my surroundings like that.

My eyes sought out Rex, finding his dark form easily. The light Dad had installed last week had come on the moment Rex stepped onto the grass, and it shone brightly, illuminating Rex and the rest of the garden.

I smiled as I watched him trot back and forth, following the scent of something that must have run through the garden. It was probably just a squirrel or a bird or something, but Rex seemed intent on finding it, and I refused to let myself look away, even as I felt the night air caress my face, trying to tempt me into tipping my chin back.

It was pleasant enough outside. It had been way too hot earlier on. I’d almost melted when Duncan and I had taken Rex for a walk, but it had cooled down a little, so it was a much more comfortable temperature. And there was a bit of a breeze. It brought the scent of hot, baked dirt and something else, something that was ever so slightly sweet and not quite of this world. I couldn’t place it, but I’d smelt it before.

My hands curled into fists, and I fought to keep my gaze on Rex. It should have felt nice. The gentle breeze should have been lovely, and I knew that I should have been enjoying it, but I wasn’t. I couldn’t. It reminded me too much of the world I had just been in, and that set me on edge.

A shiver raced through me, and I blinked, my eyes watering from the effort of staring at Rex. The urge to look up had grown stronger and stronger, and I wasn’t sure that I could ignore it for much longer. Even if I did, if I managed to go the entire night without looking at the sky, I knew it was pointless.

I couldn’t avoid it forever. There was no way I could go through my entire life without ever looking at the sky, and I wasn’t sure that I even wanted to. I’d never see another sunset again, never see the stars, and the thought of that forced me to take a deep breath. My nails dug into my palms as I squeezed my hands even tighter, preparing myself for what I was about to do. My muscles were tensed. I could feel a tightness in my jaw, and my heart was racing as I slowly let my gaze drift upwards.

The moment my eyes found their target, an unconscious sigh slipped from my lips. I could feel my body sag as it relaxed, and I had to lean against the door frame to stop myself from falling as I stared at the night sky.

I longed to look at it for longer, but fear forced me to rip my gaze away. My eyes darted towards Rex, checking that he was okay and still sniffing the grass, before finding the sky once more.

It was a nice night. There were barely any clouds hiding the stars from view, and I wasn’t sure whether that was a good thing or not. Perhaps the urge to get lost in the wonders above me would have been lessened if I could not see them? I wasn’t sure, but part of me was glad I could see them.

They seemed a little brighter than they usually did, I noticed. The stars felt clearer, the dots of light a little more distinct than normal. I couldn’t see them as clearly as I had in the other world, obviously, but it felt as though if I just squinted at the stars, I could bring them back into focus.

Something cold and wet bumped into my leg, making me jump. I blinked, looking down quickly, before letting out a laugh.

“Oh, Rex. I’m sorry. Am I blocking the door?” I asked as I moved aside so he could enter. “Good boy. Shall we go get your dinner?”

Rex let out a soft, excited noise, and shame tightened its grip on me as I followed him back towards the kitchen. Rex waited impatiently as I grabbed a clean bowl out of the cupboard, pouring in a scoop of his dry food before eyeing the cans of wet food.

The turkey and chicken one seemed to be his favourite, I decided as I selected it. That was what I’d given him yesterday, and he’d devoured it. He always ate pretty quickly, but he hadn’t even paused when eating it. I pulled the tab, peeling the top off the can and starting to reach for the fork we kept in his cupboard to section out his food before pausing.

“You know what?” I asked softly, glancing over my shoulder at the door. “You get the whole can tonight, boy.”

Rex was silent. It seemed like he wasn’t willing to risk making a noise in case it alerted my mom, and she stopped him from getting it, but he crowded closer to me. I couldn’t help the giggle that escaped my lips as he licked my cheek, but it was quiet, too soft to be heard over the noise of the movie.

“Alright, alright, I love you too, but sit,” I ordered, a smile on my lips. “Good boy.”

He did as I asked, his gaze fixed on the food I was preparing for him. I dumped the chunks of meat and whatever else was in the can onto his biscuits, mashing it with the fork a little bit to separate it, before placing the bowl on the floor for him.

“Go get it,” I told him.

Rex did not hesitate. He pounced on his food and immediately began to gobble it down. A distracted smile pulled at my lips as I watched him for a few seconds longer until a small piece of something fell from the fork onto my hand.

“Ew,” I muttered, eyeing the lump of jelly on my skin.

I hurried towards the sink and washed it off, shuddering slightly at how gross the texture of it felt. I rinsed the fork, putting it in the dishwasher before grabbing the now-empty can of Rex’s food from the side where I’d left it and washing it out too.

Dropping the can into the recycling, I turned and surveyed the kitchen, letting out a sigh as I realised I should probably have something to eat too. It was late, almost eleven, and I still hadn’t had dinner. My parents had, though. There were pots still soaking in the sink, so Mom must have made something. Dad always put the dishes in the dishwasher straight away when he cooked. Mom got annoyed if he didn’t.

I wandered towards the fridge, half-tempted to check whether there was anything left over for me, but I knew there was no point in doing that. Dad always made extra for me, but Mom didn’t.

That was fine, though. Mom normally cooked dishes with meat in them. She always said she refused to make two separate meals, so if I wasn’t going to eat what she made, I could cook for myself, and I understood that. It made sense. After all, I was the one who decided to become a vegetarian, not Mom. Both of my parents still ate meat, and that was their choice, so I tended to just make my own food.

I looked around the kitchen, trying to work out what to do, but I knew it was too late for me to cook anything, and I couldn’t really be bothered. Plus, Mom and Dad were watching a movie. Mom would get annoyed if I made too much noise and interrupted that.

Part of me was tempted to just not eat. It seemed like a lot of effort to make anything, but my stomach was already starting to rumble, and I didn’t want to risk having to sneak down in the middle of the night if I got so hungry I couldn’t sleep again. Mom had caught me doing that once, and she still made snide comments about me eating in secret.

I pulled the fridge doors open and looked into it blankly, hoping that inspiration would hit me, but it didn’t. Everything I could see needed to be cooked. The only exception were the carrots that I could see through the drawer at the bottom.

Staring at them, I tried to work out whether I had the energy to peel one. It wasn’t hard, and it didn’t take long, but it still felt like more work than I could give. I probably didn’t have to peel it, though. Some people ate them with the skin on, and I knew that. Whenever they had carrots in the canteen at school, they always had skin, and that didn’t bother most people, but it made me uncomfortable for some reason.

I didn’t have it in me, I decided as I shut the fridge again and pulled open one of the cupboards. A faint bubble of distant disappointment rose within me as my gaze roamed the boxes of plain cereal before I shut the cupboard and turned around. My eyes landed on the bread bin, and I stepped towards it, preparing to be let down again.

“Huh,” I muttered to myself as a bag came into view.

There were only a few slices left, I realised as I pulled it out, and my disappointment grew stronger. I could have one piece and save the other two in case Mom and Dad wanted them for lunch in the morning. A slice each wasn’t really much of a breakfast, but it was better than nothing.

The war waged on in my head for a few more seconds before guilt got the better of me. I lowered the bread back into the container, planning on returning to the cereal cupboard and having a bowl of something tasteless, when something caught my eye. A slight smile pulled at my lips as I turned the bag, getting a better look at the date on the label before pulling my phone out to double-check.

I was right. The bread would be gone off by the morning, so Mom wouldn’t eat it. Dad probably wouldn’t mind too much. He wasn’t too picky about use-by dates, but if Mom noticed it first, she would just bin it. That meant I could eat the rest of it without feeling too bad.

Ripping the bag open, I pulled the slices out and slotted all three into the toaster. I glanced down at Rex as I binned the bag the bread came in and got the butter from the fridge, putting it on the counter before grabbing a plate and a knife.

My fingers drummed on the work surface as I waited for my toast to be done, the sound inaudible over the loud crashes coming from the lounge. I glanced over my shoulder, curious about what they were watching, but I couldn’t see the television from where I was standing. All I could see was my dad.

He was seated in his usual chair, his eyes fixed on his phone rather than the movie. However, he must have felt my eyes on his face because he looked up, and a tired smile appeared on his lips. I saw him say something to my mom before standing stiffly and walking towards me.

“Hey, sweetheart,” he said as he stopped in the doorway. “How you doing?”

I smiled as I glanced at the toaster before turning towards him.

“I’m alright, Dad. How are you? How was work?”

Rex looked up from his food and spotted Dad. He spun around, racing towards him to greet him quickly before returning to his dinner.

“Ah, it was fine,” Dad replied with a wave of his hand. “How was Rex today? Has he been a good boy?”

“The best,” I told him, leaning down to stroke Rex’s back.

“Aww, I’m not surprised at all,” Dad said. “And how was your day? Get up to anything good?”

My smile widened, and I felt my cheeks begin to warm up, but thankfully, the toast popped up behind me. I turned quickly, seizing the opportunity to hide my blush from my dad.

“It was good. Didn’t really do too much,” I lied as I plucked the toast out and began buttering it quickly.

“Good,” Dad said, pausing before adding, “You having a snack?”

“Yeah, just making toast. Want some?” I asked, holding a piece out to him.

Dad waved a hand.

“It’s all yours, but thank you, darling,” he told me. “Did you not have dinner? Your mom said you did, but if I knew you hadn’t, I would have—”

“No, no, it’s fine,” I said, interrupting him quickly as the guilt in his voice made my heart ache. “I did, but I only had something small, and I’m still hungry.”

I tried to send him a reassuring smile as I placed the lid back on the butter and put it in the fridge again, but Dad seemed unconvinced. I could feel his eyes remaining on me as I put my knife in the dishwasher and returned to my plate on the side.

“Are you sure?” he asked. “I don’t mind whipping you up something if you want?”

Love and guilt fought in the pit of my stomach, and I shook my head, hoping he did not hear the loud gurgle his offer caused my stomach to make.

“Yeah, I’m sure. This will be enough.”

Dad’s frown deepened.

“Okay,” he said, unconvinced.

He was obviously still worried about me, and that made me feel even worse. I racked my brain, searching for something to say to distract him, and another loud noise came from the lounge.

“What are you watching?” I asked, glancing towards the room.

“Ah, some spy thing your mom wanted to watch,” he said, looking back over his shoulder.

The mention of spies made my eyes widen, and I was glad Dad hadn’t turned back towards me because he definitely would have noticed. I needed to put more work into my acting classes at the Academy, I told myself. I cannot allow something, like the unexpected mention of spies, to give me away like that.

“Oh yeah?” I replied, my tone carefully controlled. “Is it any good?”

Dad shrugged, his attention returning to me.

“It’s not bad,” he said in a voice that made me assume he was lying. “Not exactly my cup of tea, though. These kinds of movies are all a bit the same, aren’t they?”

I was more prepared that time. I didn’t let my expression give anything away, and I was sure the smile I sent my dad was perfect.

“Yeah, they are a bit.”

Dad shook his head.

“I just don’t see the appeal, you know?”

I wasn’t sure whether he meant that he didn’t see the appeal in watching spy movies or in being a spy, but either way, I just grinned and said, “Me either. You should probably get back in there, though. It seems as though you’re missing a fight or something.”

“Great,” Dad said unenthusiastically. “Make sure you bring your plate down when you’re done.”

“I will,” I told him.

Dad pushed away from the door, hesitating before adding, “But if you forget, I can always bring it down when I come up to say goodnight.”

“Thanks, Dad. Enjoy the rest of the movie.”
