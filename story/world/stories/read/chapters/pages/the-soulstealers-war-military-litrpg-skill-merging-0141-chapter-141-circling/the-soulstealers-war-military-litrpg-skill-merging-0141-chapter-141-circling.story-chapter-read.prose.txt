Milsige spent all morning circling around the tournament camp, asking the other squires what they knew about Sir Ricdine. He dropped hints that Sir Ricdine had seemed to miraculously grow in strengths.

Just before noon, he found Lord Ceonwin’s squires, Willaf and Foal, outside the main tournament stable. Of course, the part-time Dupe squiring for him wasn’t there. Sergeant Levi, huh? What a pathetic excuse for a soldier.

When he asked them what they knew about Ricdine, they both swallowed and shared a glance.

Finally, Willaf said, “Don’t know anything about it.”

Suspicious. Horribly suspicious.

“I’ve got sceats,” Milsige said. “How much would it take to tell me what you know?”

“More than you have,” Foal said.

So Lord Ceonwin had something to do with it. You could never get a squire to betray his master, though. Especially not a master like Lord Ceonwin. But of course Lord Ceonwin would have something to do with this.

He learned nothing, so he moved on.

When it was halfway through the afternoon and the sun beamed down on the camp, blazing brighter and hotter than he ever thought possible, he’d only discovered one clue. One of the squires thought he’d seen Sir Ricdine’s back chainmail steaming one morning, for no explainable reason. The thegn didn’t war enchanted armour.

Milsige couldn’t just flail around. If he didn’t report anything useful to Lord Hinlaw, he was going to be in trouble.

Next, he tried talking to the workers at the tournament registration stand. Maybe there was something about Ricdine’s land claim that he could use. But the workers only said, “His papers of thegnage were perfect. If you wish to send an envoy to his lands to check, that would take months.”

Yet another dead end. Kicking the dirt, Milsige stormed away.

Finally he camped outside Sir Ricdine’s tent for as long as he could, watching, waiting, hoping the thegn would show himself. Maybe Ricdine had already left. Was he using Lord Ceonwin’s training course again?

As the day dragged on, Milsige crept closer to the tent, his hands trembling. Hinlaw’s threats were vague, but typically, failure would result in a beating. The last thing he wanted was a beating. In his twenty-five years, he’d never known a thegn who was as rough with his squires as Lord Hinlaw.

And it’d be even worse if Hinlaw’s son lost the tournament. Milsige would be the scapegoat.

Finally, Milsige broke cover. He took a risk, but he had to. What other choice was there? If Ricdine was here, then he’d be in trouble, but if the man wasn’t…

Milsige ran to the tent, then pushed the flap open and leaned inside.

No one was here.

He released a sigh, then ducked inside the tent and searched around. There were a few barrels, which worked as nightstands, a bed of furs that it didn’t look like anyone had slept on. The tent was old and tattered, and wind blew in a hole in the back, but there were only a few tall bushes back there.

Still, he leaned out and looked around. Maybe it was a secret exit? There were no footprints.

That alone wasn’t evidence. It wasn’t good enough, nor did it prove anything about Ricdine. Not on its own. It only explained how the man was slippery enough to escape now and again without anyone noticing.

Milsige looked around the room. Of course, the man’s armour was heaped in the corner, buried beneath a tarp, but you wouldn’t always wear your armour, especially if you were just training. Not evidence, either. Unless—

As Milsige shook out the chainmail hauberk, he found a scorch on the interior back of the chainmail. An eye-shaped scorch mark.

That wasn’t natural. And that might explain the steam…

He set the chainmail down, making sure to fold it up the way he left it, then placed it back into the corner.

Not that was something he could tell Hinlaw.

After breakfast, I donned my regular Dupe armour and met with Habit and Yesman in the cellar waiting room, preparing to enter the Labyrinth. Yesman asked, “How close do you think we are to being able to tackle the level guardian?”

“We must be getting there,” Habit said. “None of my squad-mates are gaining Skills as fast as we used to. The System doesn’t see it as much of a challenge for us. Besides, after taking out the quarter guardians, we’re supposed to challenge it as Steels and Titaniums.”

I shook my head. “Let’s make this the last week of training. Then, next week, your squads take out the remaining two quarter golems, and my squad will clear a meeting place around the very central plaza and prepare the level guardian. Once the quarter guardians are down, we’ll wait one more week, then go after the level guardian. We’ll destroy it in record time for anyone in our generation.”

“You two really think we can do it?” Yesman said. “Only three squads? Previous generations needed at least seven squads working together to pull it off.”

“I think by next week, we’ll be ready,” I said. “Gain as many Skills as you can this week, then we’ll go for it.”

Yesman nodded. “It’s a plan.”

Once the Drillmaster allowed us to begin, we rushed to our usual meeting place, and debriefed the rest of our squads on the plan.

“Why wait an extra week after slaying the quarter golems, brother?” Brawl asked. “It would be easier to pull off all in one week, and it would leave us less exposed. Another squad could try to steal the kill.”

“They could,” I agreed. “But I’d rather have more time to reassess if something goes wrong, or if anything changes. I don’t think any other squads are expecting us to try taking the level guardian down this soon.”

“There are three other squads in the top positions, now,” Anchor warned. “They could get uppity.”

“They’re only there because they have been prioritizing monster kills over Skills,” I said. “But they’re not ready yet.” I hadn’t exactly seen them fight, but no other groups had been practicing as much as we had, that was for certain.

This week’s training session against the water-aspect quarter golem went about the same as usual. I used my Skills to manipulate it, and I used my Focus to draw on the memories of the Gold-tier level guardian. At the same time, I continued practicing my defensive spearwork. For today, I tried to help Brawl with the vanguard tasks, distracting the golem’s attacks and deflecting debris before it could hit the rest of my squad and hurt someone.

But with such a massive boost to my Focus, I could work on more than just one Skill. I needed one more spear-related Skill if I wanted to give my first resonance the best upgrade I could, so I concentrated on my grip.

You could get a Skill for grip adjustments if you practiced it enough. It was technically part of general spearmanship already, but if you put enough effort into it, it would turn into a Skill on its own, which you could then use for yourself and your following merges.

All throughout the training exercise, I continued shifting my grip on the spear, ensuring that I always used the perfect grip for what I needed to do. If the golem attacked with a swell of searing steam, I loosened my grip as I darted out of the way. If the golem slammed its hands down and created a surge of rocks, I changed to a tighter grip but spread my hands out to deflect with the shaft of my spear.

Near the end, when I used [Mass Hammer] to help the golem speed up its movement, I registered it preparing to take a swipe at Habit’s squadmates. They would have been ready, but their druid was lagging behind today—the Path-walker hadn’t reforged his body yet, but the rest of his squad had been gaining Skills far faster than he had.

I rushed in. My instinct was to jab with my spear, but I wouldn’t accomplish anything. I could reverse the Art, but that would only slow the golem.

Besides, I needed to buy time, and that wouldn’t work against the level guardian.

When I got closer, I dove, then wedged myself under one of the golem’s falling column arms. I reached up, hands outstretched, and crossed them above my head, as if I was blocking a heavy overhead strike.

Well, not as if. I was. But it wasn’t coming from another human in sparring practice. It was coming from the golem.

I clenched my teeth, preparing to be pounded into paste, but my rational mind told me I could resist it. The irrational part, that still hadn’t gotten used to my new body, didn’t believe I’d live.

Instead of killing me, the beast’s arm collided with my forearms. A boom rang out around the plaza. The ancient paving stones cracked below my feet. My muscles screamed out in pain, and something popped in my back.

I should’ve been a pile of red goo. Instead, I held its arm up. That wouldn’t have worked before reforging my body.

It only lasted a few seconds. As soon as Habit’s squad was out of the way, I leapt aside too. The arm slammed into the ground, creating a shockwave. I rolled away, but debris pelted my back. It didn’t pierce my chainmail, but before, it would’ve given me a nasty bruise. Now, it felt like someone had flicked my back, and that was it.

“Are you alright?” Sage asked, running over with the rest of my squad.

“I’m good,” I said. “Do you—”

“I sense some internal damage. A few strained muscles. Nothing major, and we can heal it later—”

A horn rang out through the underground hall, signalling the end of the weekly delve. We backed out, and Sage healed what she could on the way out, before I could even register the soreness. I probably wouldn’t have, even if she hadn’t healed me.

I’d blocked a golem’s arm. And I’d lived.

We were supposed to spend the afternoon relaxing, but I wasn’t sure exactly how to do that. It wasn’t like I didn’t know how to relax, but I just couldn’t. Not with the most important fight of the tournament lingering overhead, like an axe waiting to fall.

Instead of heading to Winworth with the others, I decided I’d spend my afternoon running a jousting course, using Sir Ricdine’s armour. When I arrived at my tent, I took note of my armour. As usual, I took care not to leave any footprints on the ground as I approached from the back way, and I made sure to mess up Burr’s as well.

When I arrived, something felt different. Out of place.

I narrowed my eyes, using my newly enhanced Focus to draw up the old memories I had, then to run them through my mind and assess them. What was different? Barrels, bed, furs…

The armour. It was slightly out of place, shifted to the side slightly. Someone had been here, snooping around. It was almost the same as the way I’d left it, but not perfectly. They were suspicious of me, that much was certain.

But could I do anything about it?

I’d have to be even more careful, and I’d have to show myself around the camp more. Someone was suspicious about Sir Ricdine. Beyond that, I had to make the best use of the time I did have left. The joust would take place halfway through the week, and I had to win.

That meant consuming as much Presence from my weekly Labyrinth run as possible. That meant pushing my attributes as much as I could, and forcing myself to finish the rock-flipping course.

And of course, I’d need one last Skill merge.
