As Arisia escorted Ash and Fabian toward the closest platform, Zerathstra hovered over the site of the duel for a few extra moments. Reaching down the trees and their roots, he spread his senses out, confirming that no stray A-rank had been attracted by commotion. There had been one, sure, but while it encroached on his territory for a bit, it slunk away fast enough and went back to circling the wide area around Thalis.

It had been the right call to call the end of the duel. Otherwise, it would have likely joined the fray.

The fact that they’ve drawn its attention enough that it risked trespassing in my territory says a lot, Zerathstra thought as he sensed the blue lobster’s aura pulse in the distance. It was itching for a fight, but he guessed the recent beating he’d given it was still fresh on the crustacean’s mind. Not to mention, it must have gotten into a scrap or two since the wave started. Though considering its nature, Zerathstra could understand that it would come sniffing after sensing the fight.

It must have sensed a rival. But Velistraine hadn’t used any of her active Skills. She had not even used much when it came to her Aspects. No. The lobster had been attracted by Henry.

This is absurd, Zerathstra thought with a chuckle, shaking his head. The fact that they managed to hurt and immobilize her through her defensive passives while still at the very start of B-rank…

Zerathstra eyed the duo as they floated their way back up to Thalis to get some rest, then eyed the approaching Velistraine and Arisia. “My humbled student,” he called out, a wide grin on his face.

Velistraine rolled her eyes. “Yeah, yeah. Let’s get it over with. You were humbled, Velistraine. You underestimated them, Velistraine. We all knew they were little monsters. Yeah, they hit a bit harder than I expected for fresh B-ranks. And yeah, they’re a pain in the ass. Like, I couldn’t make a move without 16 pairs of eyes catching me. They couldn’t react fast enough, but they better not be able to with nearly 130 levels between us. The fact that they managed to draw blood says enough.”

Zerathstra just rolled his eyes and glanced toward Arisia to find her chuckling, which took him a bit by surprise, her eyes tracking the duo as they disappeared back in the city. Nodding sagely to Velistraine, the captain stared at him, then her brows furrowed in annoyance.

“Screw you, Zer. I’m getting a drink,” she shot back before she kicked the air under her feet and flew away.

Zerathstra didn’t miss the small smile she was barely holding on to. She had fun, too.

Stretching his arms above his head, he turned to Arisia, and she nodded back. He was hopeful. Cautiously hopeful. And whatever happened, he was looking forward to seeing how far those two would get.

***

Two days after the duel with Velistraine, the energies that permeated the air of the Pillar showed their first sign of weakness, announcing that the Wave was on its way out. Which meant that soon, Henry and Maurice would be leaving Thalis.

Well. Technically, they were leaving. Realistically, Henry fully planned on leaving a couple of his Shadows around. Tuck them away so he’d be able to access them whenever he needed. The only worry was how far he’d be able to stretch this new network of his and right now, it was growing fast, especially thanks to his duel with Velistraine and the last couple of days of having his many clones scout and fight. And his network wasn’t the only thing that was growing stronger.

Predation of the Trickster (B): Level 1 → Level 2

Lifeweaving of the Trickster (B): Level 1 → Level 2

Thaumaturgy of the Many-Faced Trickster (B): Level 1 → Level 2

Dancing Shadows of the Trickster (B): Level 1 → Level 3

Aspects, as always, leveled up when they were used and pushed, and Henry was glad to see that the removal of System’s assistance had no effect on that. Still, the two new levels in Dancing Shadows allowed him to establish some rough calculations and using some of the graded ropes he’d gotten from the city, Henry had a decent idea of how wide his network was about to become and how fast it would grow.

At B-rank, level 1 in the Dancing Shadows Aspect, I had a total of around 150 clones, with each maintaining perfect connection for up to half a mile. Then the connection weakens until it becomes inert at around 1.5 miles, at which point it goes dormant until it comes back within range, Henry listed, eyeing his mental whiteboard as he floated alone in his private pool, the starts shining far above him while Thalis slept. Taking the data for level 2 and 3 into account, he saw a growth of 20% per level for the number of Trickster’s Shadows, and around 10% increase of range, both for the perfect, inner range, and the workable, outer range. Suffice it to say, while the range would not grow as fast, the number of clones would balloon pretty quickly. By his estimation, Henry should have something close to 700 clones by the time the Aspect got to level 10, and each of those would a perfect connection for up to 1.1 miles, and a workable, fuzzy connection for up 3.5 miles.

3.5 miles multiplied by 700… that’s a network of 2450 miles, Henry thought, blinking. Damn.

Sure, for those estimates, his assumptions needed to be further confirmed and for that wide of a network, he had to keep his relays mostly straight. But still, that was a staggering figure. It meant Henry would be able to cross thousands of miles in barely a few seconds, so long as he could channel enough mana for it. And that’s before the Aspect gets to A-rank, he noted. The thought drew a smile on his lips. Henry couldn’t wait. But for now, this should should allow him a decently wide network, and he’d bet he could play with the placement of the clones to improve things here and there.

Letting a sigh out, Henry stared a little at the sky and enjoyed the feeling of the night breeze on his skin while a few paces away, Maurice grumbled, glaring down at three metallic orbs as if they’d stolen his secret cache of kraken roe. In the last couple of days, Maurice had tried a few times to crack through the orbs’ anti-divination enchantments but unfortunately, he got no success there. It seemed that merely upping his Seer Aspect to B-rank, even if it was thematically appropriate, was not enough to pierce through the orb’s protection.

Leaving him to it, Henry turned his attention back to the swaying canopy. Henry had to admit, he felt a little sad at having to leave soon. He’d come to really enjoy his stay in Thalis. The toughest part had been swallowing the many—and fair—criticisms and harsh training of Arisia, but even she had mellowed down significantly.

Henry even caught her smiling like… twice in a single day. And when Henry had asked her about how he could go about increasing his stats now that he didn’t have a menu, her explanation came through immediately. There was no demonstrations. No tests.

“Not having access to the stat purchasing menu doesn’t mean you can’t improve your stats,” she said when he and Maurice asked about the subject. “In fact, now you won’t be limited as much in how many you can acquire at every stage, though the cost will be increased significantly as you will not be as efficient as the System itself. Here’s what you need to do.”

Arisia went on to explain the method one raised their stats without the System’s assistance. Henry would need to dip into the energies represented by the tokens and infuse and hold them into his body. He’d have to saturate and refresh those energies right on the edge of discomfort, and then he’d have to wait until that part of him was soaked enough to earn him a point. Some stats would be easier to figure than others. Like Constitution, where he had to focus the energies on the connective tissues of his skin and the resilience of his flesh. For Strength, he’d have to do the same, but focus more on his muscles. Spirit was the simplest, as he needed to infuse his soul, while Perception and Dexterity would be a little more challenging. After all, Perception wasn’t just about sight and hearing, in Henry’s case. It was in his skin. In his soul when he sensed mana. So on and so forth. As for Dexterity, it’d be a balance between his brains, nerves, and muscles.

Suffice it to say, he had his work cut out for him. But he had help. Already, Henry had put a few of his Octominds on the task. Bringing up the stats in question, Henry whistled.

They’re really getting up there, aren’t they, he mused. But he still had 8 points he hadn’t assigned yet. So Henry divided them somewhat evenly between his three main stats. At this point, the two weaker stats would never catch-up, and Henry was content with hitting hard and tanking well. Interestingly, the potent energies that surged and filled his body and spirit with Strength came out the same spot where the tokens went.

I should study that bit further, Henry thought as he finally looked at the stats.

Name: Henry Dubois

Species: Veilwatch Kraken (B+) - Level 37

Strength: 117

Constitution: 141

Spirit: 118

Perception: 83

Dexterity: 83

Now, he just needed to stockpile tokens, his excess conceptual essence, and cores.

Gotta make the most out of what’s left of the wave, Henry thought as below, his Shadows spread out of the trees, hunting, collecting, and pushing at the limits of their range.

Henry was looking forward to seeing what the next few levels would bring.

***

Three days after that, the wave was officially over, and the relief was palpable throughout Thalis. The mood was celebratory, and wherever Henry walked, he saw big smiles and festive decorations hung about. Already, he was seeing younger people hustling and bustling, bringing up wide platters of vegetables, meats, and fruits up to the arena where, most likely, yet another feast was being prepared.

Henry found it funny how the young ones managed to escape school only to end up working twice as hard helping in the preparation of the festivities, but they were clearly having a lot of fun, so all the better.

Another notification flitted through his interface, but Henry didn’t need to see what it was for. He could sense the swell of power in his soul. Specifically, in the area responsible for Trickster’s Shadows. The Aspect had gained another level, reaching level 4, and suddenly, the furthest images he was receiving became much clearer, which allowed Henry to send his clones even further. In the last couple of days, Henry had used a somewhat oval network, which he sent toward a specific direction. But now that the need for his clones was lessening, he was slowly converting the oval into a straight line, sending it all the way to where he had first opened his eyes in this world.

I wonder if I’m close, he thought, looking east. Would my clones even reach it?

Henry wasn’t sure about the distance they had crossed to get here. It might be the work of hours, and it might be the work of days. But he had time. For now, he had to keep moving his clones, which was pretty easy with his Octominds supporting him and thankfully, he hadn’t needed to replace many of them. Between their excellent camouflaging abilities and with Faint Presence, rare were the beings that could detect or catch them. And whenever one of them was destroyed—which only so far happened nineteen times—the nearest Shadow would just summon a copy and they’d resume operations.

Out of a bit over two hundreds clones, nineteen casualties was trivial. Especially with how easy they were to reverse.

Henry turned his attention back to his surroundings and let the Octominds continue the process of spreading out the network while he picked up a roasted fish-skewer from one of the many improvised little shops and happily chomped on it. Taste buds are working well, he noted as he chewed. The shapeshifted human form was getting better with every cast. He even made so the food he ate with his human mouth would reach his beak.

Henry continued his climb, taking his time to chat and talk and eventually, another pulse spread out of his soul as another Aspect took a step forward. Above his head, the only visible Octomind not busy with the network twirled in the air, trailing a haze of mana behind it as it pumped one of its tiny arms in celebration.

Grinning, Henry patted on the head while the two continued their climb. “Congrats, little buddy!”

That’s one more level to Thaumaturgy, Henry thought with a smile and through one of the handful of clones he kept around Thalis, Henry eyed the entrance of a particular cave. One that he had recently explored and where he had met a particular blue A-rank. He had posted this clone since a couple of days ago. But he had been too busy, and frankly, he’d rather rest a little more before he attempted this one. Still, with the recent level ups and all that, maybe he could do one last duel before he left with Velistraine. To see how he’d measure against a A-rank in a serious fight.

But not today, he told himself as he accepted a drink from Zerathstra. Tonight, he was taking it easy.
