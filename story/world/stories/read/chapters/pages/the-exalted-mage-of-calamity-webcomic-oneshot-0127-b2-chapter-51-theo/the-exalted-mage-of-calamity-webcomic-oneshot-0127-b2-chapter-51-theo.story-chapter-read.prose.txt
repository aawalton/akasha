High above the trees, the Mirabelle sailed through the sky, heading north. Onboard, Veronica, Gwen, and Nasha kept their eyes peeled, searching below for any sign of the missing blacksmith. Alongside them was one other person, desperate—more so than any of them—to find Orban.

“Just… how much money did you all have…?” Theo murmured, looking around the sturdy deck of the Mirabelle.

During the auction, he and Orban had left immediately after the starmetal was sold. Not many people stayed for the final fifty or so lots, considering their astronomical prices. Only a few of Maftem’s most powerful and wealthy had seen the Mirabelle, along with the other airship sold to another noble.

“You sure this Orban guy went north? We’ve been sailing for nearly an hour and haven’t seen a trail yet.” Gwen shielded her eyes with one hand, peering down at the road stretching into the distance.

Theo nodded. “Yes. According to Mrs. Bellory, he already left the city through the north gate. I have no reason to believe she’d lie.”

Nasha leaned against the ship’s railing, staring at the teenage boy. Theo looked over but couldn’t hold her gaze for more than a moment; her sharp stare felt as though she were studying the blood beneath his skin. Not to mention, he had seen her cut a broom apart in under a second.

“Hey,” she said.

Being addressed so directly, Theo forced himself to face her. “Y-yes?”

“Why did this master of yours decide to ditch the city anyway? And aren’t you his apprentice? It sounds more like he abandoned you out of nowhere and ran.”

Theo opened his mouth, nearly blurting out a response, but caught himself before the words escaped. “No… he didn’t abandon me.” His eyes shifted forward, staring down at the road leading away from Maftem. “He probably left to save me.”

Gwen stepped closer, tilting her head toward him with a raised brow. “Save you?” she asked. “Sounds like there’s a deeper story here.”

Theo gripped the ship’s railing, his eyes dropping to his hands.

Gwen straightened. “We’ll lend you an ear if you want. But if it’s too much, you don’t have to tell us. We won’t pry.”

Theo’s lips twitched before his shoulders relaxed. “No. It’s… nothing super personal or anything.” He looked back down at the road. “I met Master Orban roughly four to five years ago. Before that, I was a slave to a man named Berakalli. I mainly worked as a servant. Anything he needed labor for, I did. Hauling packs like a mule, fetching things at his beck and call, tending the garden. He worked with merchants often, so I was sometimes forced to come along and scout ahead for monsters.”

Gwen’s eyes lowered slightly, but she kept watching Theo’s face, listening patiently.

“I became a slave when I was nine, after my home was attacked by bandits in Arasarne, a small town far east of here. I lost my parents in the attack, along with many others. Anyone who survived was taken by the bandits.”

“Bandits raided an entire town? Was there no one who could fight back?” Gwen asked.

“No,” Theo said softly. “The town wasn’t wealthy. We weren’t poor either, but we didn’t have the resources to deal with an organized group that large. There were probably… fifty or more of them.”

“How did you end up a slave here if bandits took you?” Nasha asked from the side. “Was this Berakalli guy one of them? Hiding in plain sight?”

Theo shook his head. “No. Berakalli was just a wealthy man in Maftem. I was assigned to him as an official slave after the bandits were killed by several parties of adventurers. I assume word eventually got out that Arasarne had been attacked, so the Adventurer’s Guild issued a commission. I was only with the bandits for a couple of months before the adventurers found and killed them.”

“And since your town was destroyed and everyone was already dead, you were turned into slaves.”

“What?” Gwen said, louder than she intended. Her lips pursed. “If you were kidnapped by bandits, how is it fair that you became slaves after being rescued?”

Theo remained quiet.

Nasha continued watching him. “You were forced to help them, weren’t you?”

“Help them?” Gwen repeated. Her eyes flicked from Nasha back to Theo. “What is she talking about?”

“Yes… we were,” Theo said slowly. “After they took us, we were forced to help with other kidnappings and murders. We were slaves. If we didn’t do what they said, we’d die on the spot. Or worse, they’d torture us and make an example out of us.”

“And because you technically committed crimes, the kingdom wouldn’t simply let you go,” Nasha said.

Theo nodded slowly.

“What?” Gwen chirped. “That doesn’t sound fair at all! If… if you committed crimes because someone forced you, then that shouldn’t count against you! If you were forced, then… then… you shouldn’t be punished! None of that was your fault! None of it!” Her voice carried something deeper than simple sympathy.

Empathy.

Theo looked over at her, slightly stunned. Then his eyes fell back to the wooden railing of the Mirabelle. “Thanks… that means a lot to hear.”

“Where do Orban and Berakalli come into this?” Nasha asked, shifting the conversation.

Theo looked at her. The nervousness in his body had faded, replaced by something heavier and more solid. He stared ahead at the road. “Berakalli was the man I was sold to. It was much better than being held captive by the bandits, but it still isn’t something I remember fondly.”

His lips pressed together before he continued. “After three years of working for him, when I was around twelve, he suddenly had a heart attack in his study. His maids and butlers found him later that night and panicked. During the chaos, one of the servants I was friends with unshackled our bindings and let us escape.”

“You… escaped? Just like that?” Gwen asked.

“It usually wouldn’t be that easy,” Theo said. “Even if Berakalli died, my slave status would remain, and I’d pass to his next of kin. Luckily, he didn’t have any children, and most of his relatives hated him. His estate was repossessed, and many of the servants moved to other houses. I ended up living in Maftem’s slums for a few weeks. That’s when I met Master Orban. He… took pity on me.”

“So he took you in?” Gwen asked.

“Not willingly at first,” Theo said. “There was more to our meeting, but it basically came down to me begging him to let me work for him. I was still an unregistered escaped slave, so there wasn’t much official work I could do in the city. Working privately for one person who agreed not to expose me was the best option I had.”

“And he agreed,” Nasha chimed in.

Theo wore a faint smile. “After weeks of begging, doing chores around the smithy, and working as hard as I could to get his attention, he finally did. But more than that, he went to the city council, bought out my slave contract, and dissolved it. All under the condition that I’d work as his apprentice. Said I had… an annoying ‘heart of fire’ like a smith’s forge. That I’d make a good smith as long as I didn’t waver. I’ve been with him for the last four years. I’ve never been happier.”

A warm smile appeared on Gwen’s face. “He… sounds like a good master.”

Theo’s smile widened at the compliment. “He is.”

Nasha walked over and hugged the boy from behind. Theo immediately blushed and jolted. “W-wh-what?”

Nasha smiled behind him, loosening her arms slightly. “Sounds like you’ve been through a lot. Thought you might appreciate a hug.”

Theo’s face remained red, but he didn’t pull away. Instead, he balled his fists. His eyes watered, though the tears refused to fall. He wouldn't let them.

“Oh, fine,” Gwen said after watching for a moment. She stepped forward and hugged him from the side as well.

A few moments later, footsteps sounded from behind them. “Did I… miss something?”

Nasha and Gwen pulled away and turned toward Veronica, who had just stepped onto the deck.

Theo turned as well, trying to keep his expression neutral. He didn’t say a word.

Gwen glanced at him, then back at Veronica. “Nothing major. He just told us a little about his relationship with Orban.”

“Sounds like a kind man,” Nasha said.

“That’s great,” Veronica said, walking over to join them. “I’m assuming he’ll be happy to forge the starmetal for us, then.”

Theo’s expression changed. More specifically, it fell. “Um… actually, I don’t think he’ll be happy at all.”

The three of them looked at him.

“What do you mean?” Gwen asked.

Theo shook his head lightly. “Master Orban has been dealing with a lot of financial trouble at Steelhand Smith after the commerce groups started targeting us. He racked up a lot of debt. He was hoping to acquire the starmetal and find a wealthy buyer who wanted a weapon made from it—someone who’d pay him a life-changing amount.”

Veronica raised a brow. “How much does he want? A hundred thousand vix? I already have the starmetal, so…” Her voice trailed off as the realization hit her. “He was relying on the scarcity of the starmetal to heavily mark up the price, wasn’t he?”

Theo nodded. “Yes. Since you already own it, he can’t justify charging an enormous commission. If he did, you could just find another smith capable of forging it. Even if you had to travel, it would probably still cost less than what he’d need to ask.”

“But if he’s the one with the starmetal, he could charge whatever he wants, since he’d be the only smith who could forge it and have the material on hand,” Nasha said, resting a finger against her cheek.

“That’s right. Even though starmetal is difficult to forge, there are a few skilled blacksmiths in Vitian who can work with it. The material itself is even rarer.”

Gwen stood quietly as the three discussed the starmetal until something caught her attention in the distance. She turned toward the front of the ship, gripping the wooden railing.

“Hey, guys,” she said, cutting their conversation short.

The other three turned toward her, then followed her gaze down to the road ahead.

Trails of craters and unearthed ground scarred the path below. Rocks and dirt had been thrown everywhere, leaving the area looking like a battlefield trampled by rampaging beasts. There were even several black specks below that looked oddly human-shaped, slumped against larger overturned rocks.

More importantly, some of the rocks looked as though they had been ripped straight from the ground and shaped into… giant stone arms. Several pillars stood curved along the battlefield, as though the earth itself had been commanded to throw punches.

Near the center sat one particular black speck.

Veronica stared at it. With her enhanced vision, she could see him clearly, sitting against a gray stone and breathing heavily as he stared straight back up at them.

“Looks like we found him,” Veronica said.
