Jane kept her hands on Felicity's arm, letting her magic flow as quickly as she could manage. She had no idea if it was helping. The magic she was pushing wasn't a real healing spell, as always. She had never been able to master those, despite years of training at the academy.

Her alignment ran in different directions, toward the destructive end of things, and toward the intuitive use of magic to do complex things. Lately, working with and around so much water in Glenfall had helped her develop her affinity for water-related spells as well. But healing was a rare knack, and she simply didn't have it.

In this case, a true healing spell might not have helped anyway. There were no clear injuries on the old woman's body. She wasn’t cut or infected. It was more like her life force was a fire in an oven, something that had once burned bright but had now dwindled down to embers. The fuel was running out, and Jane was left desperately blowing on the embers trying to keep a spark alive.

She was pushing unfocused magic into the woman's body with the intent of encouraging life, the same way she sometimes encouraged her yeast to grow at the bakery. It was more like providing a general boost, a bit of extra energy for the body to use however it needed in the ways it would work anyway. It was an enhancement, at most.

She could tell it was helping, at least a little. Felicity's breathing had steadied slightly since Jane had started, and her hands had warmed just a bit. It certainly wasn’t fixing the entirety of what was wrong here, but it was something.

The sound of footsteps on the stairs broke her from her trance. Edna appeared in the doorway, out of breath and carrying a large leather bag.

"Step aside, please," Edna said, moving toward the bed. "Let me take a look at her."

"I'm pumping in some amount of healing magic." Jane didn't move her hands. "It's not much, but I think it's helping. Her breathing was worse before I started."

Edna's eyebrows rose, but she didn't argue. Instead, she moved around to the other side of the bed, positioning herself where she could examine the old woman around Jane’s efforts.

"Keep doing what you're doing, then." Edna put her hand on Felicity’s wrist, taking her pulse. "Let me know if you start to run low on whatever it is you're using."

"I will."

After taking the old baker’s pulse, the doctor lifted one eyelid and peered at the eye beneath, then checked the other. She pressed her hand against Felicity's forehead, then moved it to her chest, then checked her heart rate one last time.

"Her pulse is regular," Edna said. "Weak, but regular. Whatever you're doing, keep doing it."

"What's wrong with her?"

"I don't know yet." Edna pulled a small bottle from her bag and uncorked it, waving it under Felicity's nose. There was no reaction. "What happened? When did you find her like this?"

"Just now. Maybe ten minutes ago." Jane's voice was steady, but her hands were shaking where they rested on Felicity's arm. "I came to visit her this morning, and she didn't answer the door. I could tell something was wrong, so I came in."

"She was like this when you found her?"

"Yes. She hasn't moved or opened her eyes. I tried to wake her, but she wouldn't respond."

Edna nodded, continuing her examination. "And yesterday? How was she yesterday?"

"She was fine." Jane felt her throat tighten. "She was excited. We stayed up late talking and baking together. She was showing me things. Teaching me. She seemed more than fine."

"That sounds like her."

"What if I caused this? What if staying up late was too much for her? Is this my fault?"

Edna stopped what she was doing and looked at Jane directly.

"No," she said. "Absolutely not."

"But if I hadn't come…”

"Jane." Edna's voice was firm. "Listen to me. Felicity is old. She's been active her whole life, and she's been declining for years. I've been checking on her regularly since I moved to this town, and I've watched her slow down step by step. This was always going to happen eventually."

"But it happened now. Right after I visited."

"And it could have happened tomorrow, or a week ago. I assure you nothing would have stopped her from doing what she wanted to do, regardless of how old she got.” Edna returned to her examination, her hands gentle as they moved across the old woman's body. "If anything, you probably gave her one of the best days she's had in years. She loves to bake, and she loves to teach. You gave her both of those things, with someone who actually understood what she was talking about. An entire army couldn’t have kept her from that."

Jane wanted to believe that yesterday had been a good thing. But it was hard to feel that way when the woman was lying motionless in front of her, barely breathing.

"Is she going to be all right?"

Edna was quiet for a long moment before answering.

"I don't know," she said finally. "I'm not going to lie to you when I've seen this before. Sometimes people rally. Sometimes they don't. Even if she comes back to us for a while, this is the beginning of the end, Jane."

"What can I do?"

"What you've already done. Is giving her more of your magic helping her at this point?”

It wasn’t. It wasn’t hurting her, but there was only so much reinforcement a body could hold onto. Jane had passed that point minutes ago.

“Not really. There’s nothing more I can do for her today.”

“Then stop doing it. Immediately,” the doctor said. “That doesn’t mean you have to leave, but I refuse to have you cause magical problems for yourself that I have no idea how to fix. Can you do that?”

"Of course."

"Good." Edna gathered her bag and moved toward the door. "I’m going to get some tools and supplies. I'll be back as quickly as I can. If anything changes, anything at all, come find me immediately."

"I will."

"She's lucky you came when you did," Edna said quietly. "It wouldn’t have been good for her to be alone."

Then she was gone, leaving Jane alone with Felicity.

Please, Jane thought again. Please don't leave yet.

She settled into a more comfortable position, preparing for a long wait. She wouldn’t leave, she knew. It was the least she could do. Hours passed. Jane lost track of time, eventually.

Edna came and went several times, bringing supplies and checking on the patient. She administered various treatments that Jane didn't understand, but mostly just monitored her condition. If there were large changes to it, she didn’t mention them to Jane.

Cindy appeared at some point, hovering in the doorway with a tray of food and water. Jane ate and drank mechanically, hardly tasting what she was eating. The girl didn't say anything to Jane, directly. She gave a hug to the old, sleeping woman and whispered some words in her ear, though, before she left.

Sometime in the middle of the day, Edna brought her a sealed envelope. She opened it to find it was from her driver, who she had completely forgotten was coming back that day.

Jane, take your time. As much as you need. I’ll be here. If you are of a mind to refuse, remember that I’m an older man, now. I’d hope someone would do for me as you are doing for her.

The afternoon was fading into evening when Felicity finally stirred.

It was a small movement at first, just a slight twitch of her fingers. Jane called for Edna, almost sure the doctor wasn’t there to hear her.

"Felicity?" Jane leaned forward, her heart suddenly pounding. "Can you hear me?"

The old woman's eyelids fluttered open.

Her eyes focused on Jane's face. Recognition dawned in them, followed by something that Jane read as surprise.

"Jane." Her voice was so quiet that Jane had to lean in to hear it. "You're here."

“Yes. I am. How are you feeling?”

“Fine enough. Though I must admit I’m tired.” She glanced around the room. “Edna’s been, I suppose?”

“Yes.”

“Then you already know what this is.” Felicity moved her hand to Jane’s arm. Jane found herself mystified by how quickly she had come to love that hand. It was warm again, somehow. “I’m glad you asked me if my life was worth it last night, Jane. I don’t have to explain it now.”

Before Jane even knew she was crying, a tear fell on the old woman’s hand.

“Now, none of that. It’s all right. But I do want you to promise me something, Jane.”

“Anything.”

“Keep on the road you are on. I promise you it’s the right one for you.” The hand on Jane’s arm slacked, just a bit. “And remember me every now and again while you walk it. Can you do that?”

“Of course,” Jane said. “Of course I will.”

“Good.” Felicity gave a small smile. “That’s very good.”

She was asleep again, then, and gone soon after. When Edna found Jane a bit later, she was weeping like a child.

—

Jane woke up the next morning hardly remembering how she got to bed.

She lay there for a while, not moving, just staring upward. It was like gravity had increased overnight, pressing her down into the mattress with a force she couldn't yet find the will to overcome.

The events of the previous day came back to her in fragments. She remembered Edna guiding her down the stairs and she remembered walking, though she couldn't remember where. She supposed it was here, really. She remembered climbing into bed at some point, still wearing her clothes from the day before. That was getting to be a habit.

I should be sad, she thought. I should be devastated.

Lying there staring at the ceiling, she found that she wasn't, exactly. She was certainly sad, but the raw grief she had felt the night before seemed to have faded somewhat. She thought about why that might be. Felicity Cast had been old and had lived a full life, just as she said. Her death wasn’t a tragedy in the way a young father or mother’s life would be. That was part of it, if not the full picture. The rest of it came to her quickly, when she went looking for it.

She wouldn't have wanted me to be sad. She basically told me not to be.

The practical woman Jane had met would have approached her own mortality with a grounded attitude. She would have long ago made peace with it, lived whatever life she had left to live, and would have thought there was nothing to be very sad about.

It was a reasonable position. It was probably even correct. Jane still found herself disagreeing with Felicity, in part. The old woman still had so much she could have taught Jane, but that was only a minor concern. Jane hadn’t ever put it into a full thought, but she had been planning on coming up here often. She had expected more nights like the last one, more time spent getting to know her new friend.

She had only just found her, and now Felicity Cast was gone. Jane couldn’t obey her completely, whether she wanted to or not. But she thought she could probably compromise.

I'm going to be sad for a while, Jane thought. Whether you would have wanted it or not. That's just how it is. But I’ll keep it in check. I’ll be happy I met you, I'll live my life, and I’ll remember you. Just like you asked.

That was the deal, she decided. It seemed like a good balance.
