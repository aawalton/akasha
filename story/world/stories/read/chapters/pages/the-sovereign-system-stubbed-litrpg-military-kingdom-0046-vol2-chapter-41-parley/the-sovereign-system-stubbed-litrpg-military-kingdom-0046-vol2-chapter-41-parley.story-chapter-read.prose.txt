Word of the decision to surrender to Selis spread swiftly throughout the fortress, breathing new life into the weary ranks of soldiers. Though some harbored lingering doubts, most agreed it was better than waiting around to die.

Luke’s bold choice to expend the majority of their rations had paid off—it significantly boosted morale across the camp. Surprisingly, Tristan hadn’t opposed the idea. Luke had expected the old quartermaster to balk at the thought of parting with their remaining provisions.

But the reality was clear: even if the Selis General accepted their terms of surrender, there was no chance he would let them march away with their supplies intact.

Luke’s gaze drifted toward Tristan, silently observing the older man.

He was focused solely on his food, already working through his fourth helping. Apparently, yesterday’s grueling climb had left him utterly drained—unsurprising, given that it had taken over eight hours to scale the mountain.

Looks like superhuman strength has its drawbacks, Luke mused.

His dream of leading a battalion filled with enhanced soldiers flickered out. Their strength might be formidable, but keeping them fed would require an unsustainable amount of resources. It would mean hauling extra provisions for every campaign—a logistical nightmare.

Not that he expected to gather such a force anytime soon. The four under his command had taken months of interaction and effort before the system allowed him to recruit them formally.

As the designated time for departure approached, the four soldiers Luke had chosen began to grow visibly tense. He didn’t blame them—their lives, along with those of their comrades, hinged on the outcome of this negotiation.

One wrong move could mean death for all.

Luke placed a hand on Tristan’s shoulder and offered him a small, reassuring smile.“It’s time,” he said quietly.

Tristan nodded with calm resolve, as though the matter were routine. His confidence wasn’t forced—Luke could tell. It came from years of hard-earned experience on the battlefield.

That was precisely why Luke had selected him to serve as their envoy. Tristan wasn’t just enhanced by the system—his age and military background made him the ideal candidate to navigate the delicate intricacies of negotiation.

“I’ll go request the meeting now,” Tristan said as he stood and stretched. Despite his age, he stood tall, his back straight as a spear, his muscles lean and powerful.

“Oh,” Luke murmured, as if suddenly remembering something. He reached into his coat and pulled out the royal seal—the emblem that marked him as a General in the King’s army—and handed it to Tristan.“You’ll probably need this.”

If the enemy was to take the negotiation seriously, the royal seal was essential. In a world governed by Confucian principles, protocol held great weight. Small gestures carried immense significance.

Without that seal, how could the Selis General believe their request was even legitimate?

Luke glanced at the four soldiers he’d chosen to accompany Tristan and gave a subtle nod. They responded with steady, determined expressions—an unspoken promise of loyalty and courage.

He hadn’t chosen wrong.

As the group moved toward the northern gates, a quiet confidence settled over Luke. With a thought, he summoned his system interface and navigated to his current quest, scanning the details with a furrowed brow.

Auxiliary Quest: Preserve your forces

Details: Even when forced to retreat, the Sovereign does so with dignity.

Task: Successfully retreat from Xiandong Fortress with more than 70% of the initial forces remaining.

Progress: 74% of friendly forces remaining.

Reward: Skill Selection

Failure: Unknown

No one had died since his arrival the previous night—at least, not enough to shift the percentage. That was a relief, but uncertainty gnawed at him.

The quest stated that he had to “retreat successfully with over 70% of his forces.” But what did that truly mean?

Did they all have to survive the journey back to Ralis City?Or would simply leaving the fortress with that number fulfill the condition?

If it were the latter, they might be in the clear. But if the system meant the former... then the quest was nearly impossible.

With no weapons, no supplies, and no horses, protecting ten thousand soldiers on a month-long trek back to the capital was a logistical nightmare. Even without enemy interference, exhaustion, hunger, and terrain alone would take their toll.

If that’s the case, I might have to abandon the quest entirely... Luke thought grimly.

Fortunately, he had contingencies. If all went according to plan, he would link up with the Yunxi forces—assuming they weren’t also caught in a siege. That was the entire reason he’d sent Richard and fifty of the King’s Guard ahead in the first place.

“General, are you okay?” a voice interrupted his thoughts.

Luke turned to see Christian at his side, concern etched across his weathered face.

“Mmm. Just thinking ahead,” Luke replied with a nod. “But there’s only so much you can plan for. The rest depends on your ability to adapt and overcome whatever obstacle stands in your path.”

Christian stared at him for a long moment, then bowed his head and cupped his fist with reverence.“Thank you for your teachings, General.”

Luke blinked, then chuckled softly. To him, it was just a line he’d picked up from countless textbooks and lectures at the military academy—phrases passed down by esteemed generals like Master Boyd and the late Master Gale.

But to Christian, a twenty-two-year-old soldier who had never received formal officer training, those words probably sounded like pearls of wisdom.

He couldn’t fault the man for taking them to heart.

Before Luke knew it, Tristan had already climbed the ramparts, his tall frame now clearly visible to the Selis forces below. In any other situation, standing so exposed would have invited a hail of arrows.

But the long spear in his hand, draped with a strip of white fabric fluttering in the wind, made his intention unmistakable.

A call for peace.

“I’d like to negotiate the terms of our surrender!”Tristan’s voice boomed across the plains, startling everyone within earshot.

No one had expected such power in the lungs of a man his age. But more than that, it was what he said that caught everyone off guard.

Down on the plains, outside the reach of archers, enemy soldiers stirred. Though Luke couldn’t see their faces clearly, he imagined their expressions were quite amusing in this moment—confused, perhaps even incredulous.

There was no response at first. But when a lone soldier broke away from the ranks and headed back toward the enemy camp, Luke knew it was only a matter of time. The Selis General would soon be informed of the Marxx Kingdom’s request for parley.

This was, perhaps, the most nerve-wracking part of the process—at least for the Marxx soldiers watching from the walls. Yet despite the tension, both Luke and Tristan remained composed. If they were anxious, neither of them showed it.

Sure enough, ten minutes later, the messenger returned—this time accompanied by a small entourage. From their armor and bearing, it was clear these were officers of the Selis army, though none appeared to be the General himself.

They moved through the sea of soldiers, who parted wordlessly to let them through. The officers advanced toward the fortress walls, their purpose unmistakable.

“Throw down your bows,” Tristan ordered sharply, scowling at the archers stationed along the ramparts.

Several soldiers, who had forgotten themselves in the heat of the moment, quickly obeyed. Holding weapons in such a situation was tantamount to sabotaging the entire negotiation before it began.

They didn’t know Tristan’s identity as a vice-general, but his tone left no room for argument. Whether it was his commanding presence or the knowledge that he’d been selected as the envoy, the soldiers complied without question.

“I am Commander Greene of the Selis army,” called a man with a dark goatee, stepping forward. He stopped well within archer range, his voice steady. “To whom am I speaking?”

“Vice General Tristan Gale of the Marxx Kingdom,” Tristan replied without hesitation. “I request a parley on behalf of General Drakon, to discuss the terms of our surrender.”

Commander Greene considered his words for a long moment before nodding. “We accept your request for parley. Where shall the meeting take place?”

Tristan didn’t answer right away. Instead, he grinned.

Then, without warning, he hefted the spear in his hand and hurled it into the air.

Gasps erupted from the ramparts, and several soldiers flinched—Luke included. For a split second, it looked as if the man had lost his mind.

But the spear arced over the commander and his retinue, landing solidly in the earth halfway between the fortress walls and the Selis formation.

“Neutral ground,” Tristan declared coolly, as though he hadn’t just thrown a weapon toward the opposing army.“Open the gates!” he bellowed, turning smartly on his heel.

He shot Luke a sly grin as he descended from the ramparts.

Luke blinked, stunned by the man’s audacity.

They were at the mercy of the Selis army. Why would Tristan risk offending them with such a move? Wasn’t he afraid of provoking the enemy General?

But when Luke saw the calm confidence on Tristan’s face, he could only sigh inwardly.

I’m sure he knows what he’s doing... right? he thought, doing his best to suppress the creeping nerves in his chest.

A quick glance at the four soldiers accompanying the envoy revealed that he wasn’t the only one unsettled. Their expressions mirrored the tension in his own gut—tight jaws, darting eyes, clenched fists.

“Remember,” Tristan said in a low voice, just loud enough for them to hear, “be respectful—but don’t show weakness. If they sense fear, we might not leave this place alive.”

He didn’t wait for a response. Instead, he gave Luke a firm pat on the shoulder and continued down the ramparts without another word.

It was enough.

Luke understood. This single moment conveyed all he needed to know about Tristan’s earlier actions.

A sense of calm settled over him. He’d studied countless texts and war chronicles in the military library at Clayton City—and even more during his brief time at the Royal Academy. While parleys like this were rare, there was precedent. And in those accounts, one lesson stood above all:

Neutral ground mattered, both geographically, and psychologically.

Without this, a parley could turn into a slaughter. The smallest misstep, the faintest trace of perceived weakness, could unravel everything.

Nuance determined survival.

The fortress shuddered with the clatter of chains and the groan of weathered wood as the soldiers began raising the northern gate. It was the only gate in the entire stronghold—a strategic liability. While easier to defend, it also meant they had no alternative escape route if things went awry.

As the heavy wooden doors creaked open, a wave of uncertainty rippled through the ranks of the Marxx soldiers.

Outside stood the enemy.

Twenty thousand strong, fresh and unwounded—having held off from launching a full-scale siege. In stark contrast, the defenders within the fortress were haggard, bloodstained, and drained by days of tension and dwindling supplies.

The disparity was undeniable.

And yet, no movement came from the Selis lines. They watched. Tense, alert—but seemingly willing to honor the call for parley.

At the forefront of it all walked Tristan, as composed as ever. The four handpicked soldiers flanked him closely, not with weapons but with folded planks of wood and rolls of canvas slung across their backs.

They were there to build the tent. A sign of peace.

As they approached the spear Tristan had thrust into the earth—marking the neutral zone—Luke could feel the weight of countless eyes settling on him. Dozens, maybe hundreds of his own soldiers stared in silent anticipation.

In their faces, he saw the unspoken questions. The fear. The doubt.

And yet, he didn’t waver.

“Leave the gates open,” Luke said, his voice level and clear. “Anyone who disobeys this order will be deemed insubordinate and dealt with according to military law.”

He didn’t look away from the five figures on the field below. His tone brooked no argument.

This is our only shot, he told himself, swallowing the knot in his throat.
