Everyone was forced to close their eyes as tendrils of Mirith’s power brushed against their mind—not seeking entry, just piercing through. No doubt, the telepath could have been more subtle with her approach, but it was obvious this was a power play on her part.

Unfortunately for the rest of them, it was brutally effective.

It pierced through their minds too fast for any of them to react, and in a staggering show of power, Mirith dumped the details of Aodhán’s insane plan into all their minds at once.

Seojun, Brennon, and Destra staggered from the force of it, and with gritted teeth, Destra cursed. “By winter’s tits, I already know the plan! I was there when he came up—”

But her words were cut off as the entirety of Aodhán’s plan bloomed in her mind—in all their minds at once—unfolding so intricately it took several seconds of silence to process it all.

With each second that passed, the shock on all their faces deepened. Yet, no one was quite as shocked as Daruk was.

Daruk stared blank-faced as the plan unfolded in his mind—a chaotic mesh of assumptions, luck, skill, sheer absurdity, and overwhelming risk. The latter was, of course, to be expected, but when Mirith had said she preferred Aodhán’s plans to Sebastian’s very logical plan, Daruk had expected... he didn’t know what he had expected, but it certainly wasn’t this.

He turned to glance sharply at Aodhán, trying to wrap his mind around the sheer absurdity of the plan his brother had come up with.

Will imprints? Viewing Origin Planes as streams of living elemental and conceptual consciousnesses flowing just beneath the fabric of reality rather than the corrupted fragments of a previous reality fused into their current framework, because, well, that was how he had viewed them.

That was how everyone viewed them. That was what they were taught.

Admittedly, the topic of Origin Planes had been heavily contested over the years. Everyone who had ever written or spoken about origin planes had confessed to not knowing much about them, and even his patron, Nzinga Al ’Arish, had been very vague about them the one and only time they had spoken.

Daruk admitted that all that they knew of the Origin Planes was conjecture and assumptions, but at least those conjectures made sense.

Aodhán’s understanding of the Origin Plane, however, had taken all they believed to be true and simply flipped it upside down. If this had been brought to him by someone else, Daruk would have discarded it immediately. He wouldn’t even have given it any thought.

But coming from Aodhán? Daruk hesitated to do that despite his disbelief.

He had watched Aodhán grow in power over the years. He watched him come up with weird ideas and implement them successfully. He had watched him break nearly every rule of advancement, the strength of his spirit breaking limits of what they would have considered impossible.

His familiar was a prime example of that. And then there was also his Aspect—a feat that should have been until the Mythic class at least.

And to top it all off, the telepath believed it. With all her power and abilities, Daruk doubted she was one to run off with an idea she didn’t fully trust in.

“I’m not.” Mirith responded to his thoughts, her words cutting through the uneasy silence that had descended over the mountain like a knife. Her words drew everyone’s attention, and with a serious expression, she gestured toward Aodhán.

“I drew this information from Aodhán’s mind a few hours after his evolution, and at first I was skeptical of it, just as you are now, until I tested it myself.” She glanced at Aodhán, who was glaring daggers at her, and then cleared her throat. “Ahem! Perhaps, it’ll be better if Aodhán explains it himself.”

Aodhán scowled, realizing now that Mirith had been spying on his mind long before he even knew to be cautious of her. That information didn’t give him more confidence in her. But he had shackled himself to her already. There was no going back.

Exhaling deeply, Aodhán turned to face everyone gathered on the mountain. He couldn’t explain to them how he had come about the information of what Origin Planes truly were, so instead, he said.

“I stumbled on a very powerful inspiration during my evolution to the advanced class, and my life has not remained the same since I had this shift in perspective.” He smiled and continued. “Many of you do not know, but I gained a seal during my evolution, and till now I haven’t suffered the twisted effect from it.” He glanced at Daruk and smiled. “I don’t think you realized that.”

“I… I didn’t!” Daruk stuttered, his eyes wide in shock as he realized the truth in Aodhán’s words. With all the chaos of battle and consequent bounty hunting, he’d completely forgotten about the seal Aodhán had gained during his evolution.

But while he dealt with the shock of that little knowledge, others battled an intense disbelief and confusion as to how such a thing could have happened.

“That’s not possible.” Sebastian stared at Aodhán in disbelief, his expression daring Aodhán to prove his words. “The twisted effect is a natural consequence of seals. It is impossible to avoid it.”

“And yet I did,” Aodhán replied calmly. “Daruk—and apparently Mirith—are my witnesses.”

Mirith nodded. “I am a witness. He is telling the truth.”

Chaos broke out immediately as everyone began speaking all at once, the disbelief in their tone mounting until Monica spoke, shattering that unbelief with a shocking statement.

“I don’t think he’s lying.” Her words came out in a shocked whisper, as if even she couldn’t believe the words she was about to utter. “Baxter and I witnessed his advancement. We watched from afar, and from what we saw, it makes so much sense that he gained a seal. But he didn’t go on a rampage. We would have known. And five hours later, Baxter and I attacked a cave where he and Daruk must have been resting.” She looked up at them then, the confusion clear in her tone. “What sort of twisted effect lasts less than five hours?”

The disbelief drained from their expressions in that moment, and Sebastain asked. “How… how is that even possible? Are you telling us that you overcame the twisted effect with a simple shift in perspective?”

Aodhán frowned. “I believe so, but I cannot be sure unless someone else does it and experiences the same thing.” He glanced at Mirith, but before he could even ask, she shook her head.

“I haven’t gained any seals recently, so I couldn’t test it out, but I do believe it to be true.” She glanced at the others and added. “Aodhán’s inspiration isn’t just a perspective shift. It’s a shift in understanding and relationship with our Origin Planes. And it’s very possible that such a drastic change in understanding affects our relationship with the origin planes.”

Aodhán nodded sagely, impressed with how vague Mirith had kept her words. The last thing he wanted was to give his understanding away to the cultists and empower them even further than they already were.

A beat of silence followed as the implications of Mirith’s words settled over the group like a weight until Baxter broke it, his tone somber.

“Is that why you wanted to give us your miracle rain? Why you told us to prepare? You want us to test it out?

Aodhán nodded. “Yes. It was the only way to share my knowledge with you at the time while also empowering you against the looming calamity.”

“What if it hadn’t worked?” Brennon asked cautiously. “What if we’d all gained seals and gone on a rampage?”

Aodhán grimaced. “I was aware of the risks, Brennon, and that was why I suggested we do it so close to the monster’s awakening. If you’d gone on a rampage, you’d have been faster and stronger. If you hadn’t, then you would still be stronger, only without the chaos of the twisted effect affecting you.”

Another beat of silence passed, and then finally, Daruk spoke. “Before I say anything, I must say I’m impressed by all this. I’d completely forgotten about the twisted effect and how it didn’t affect you. If this is what you think caused it, then I wouldn’t hesitate to give it a try, and I doubt anyone else here would say no. However, there’s one thing I don’t understand, and that is how this whole Will Imprint thing leads to our freedom. Could you please explain it?”

Aodhán smiled. “That’s the tricky part. Doing this doesn’t exactly get us out of here. But the planes are flowing like a river, carrying stuff from place to place. From mind to mind—"

“It’s only a matter of time before someone stumbles upon it.” Daruk finished, eyes wide, and Aodhán’s smile widened.

“Exactly. I wish I could explain to you all without announcing it to the world, but I can’t. The good thing about it, though, is that you have nothing to lose from trying it.”

Monica grimaced, always the first to be against any good thing. However, this time, she was the first to agree.

“Then, let’s do it.” She said. “We have nothing to lose and a whole lot to gain.”

Baxter nodded in agreement. “Monica’s right. I’m in.”

It was a weird idea, unleashing explosions of willpower into the origin planes as will imprints, but they had nothing to lose and a whole lot to gain if it worked.

One by one, everyone agreed to give it a try. But then, that left the matter of entering the origin planes themselves.

The origin planes weren’t realms people could just enter willingly. They had to be invited by the plane itself, and that only happened during times of evolution, intense meditation, and powerful inspirations.

These sorts of events triggered that heightened mental state where reality fell away to reveal one’s origin plane as if it had been waiting all along. However, with no monsters to kill or cores to absorb, the prospect of evolution and advancement was out of the picture for everyone here except, of course, Aodhán—though he wasn’t sure Sebastian wouldn’t try to kill him if he tried to absorb him again.

However, with cores out of the picture, they had only two options left: intense meditation or powerful inspirations.

The former would take several hours of consistent, deep meditation, but that was time they didn’t have, which left them with the final option.

Powerful inspirations.

Thankfully, Aodhán knew just how to trigger those with his miracle rain. However, just as he began to raise his hands to the sky, he remembered what he had said earlier and promptly stopped.

“We can’t do it now,” he said, shaking his head. “We still have about twenty hours left till the forty-eight-hour deadline.” He glanced at the clock at the edge of his vision and said. “I suggest we do it when there’s only an hour left.”

“But we are already gathered here.” Monica protested. “What if something bad happens before the one-hour mark?”

“That’s a risk we’ll have to take.” Mirith replied firmly. “It would be foolish to risk us all falling under the influence of the twisted effect now with over twenty hours to go.”

“And with nowhere to channel all that chaos.” Aodhán added with a grim smile. “It’s better to wait. That way you have an opponent, and we have your strength. Everybody wins.”

But that was a lie.

Not everyone would win because he would be sacrificing a great portion of his own power and seals to create a truly powerful miracle rain, now Tempest of Transformation, leaving himself weaker for the final battle.

But there was no way to avoid that. HE GAINED STRENGTH TO GIVE IT, After all.

For now, though, his main objective remained Serythion. The faster they could free the dragon, the better their chances against the looming calamity.

Behind him, Mirith’s gaze narrowed in subtle respect. She understood now why the others admired him so much. It would most certainly kill her to be so selfless.

He had grown stronger. Drastically. And there was nothing the cultists respected more than strength.

They had severe doubts that he could manage it, but Jethro had learned not to underestimate the transmigrant and had proposed the brilliant plan to make them fight for the pins—wasting willpower and cutting down their numbers.

But apparently, he hadn’t learned enough, because now that it was all over, it turned out the battle had only served to bring them all together, even putting the transmigrant at the helm.

Bane stuttered. “I… I have no answer for that, Fated.”

And to make things worse, he would now have to fill the shoes of a man whose path he’d only just learned. The elders turned to look at him urgently, expecting him to issue out a command, a line of action. Anything at all.

He couldn’t have been more unprepared for the fate he’d been so prideful of.
