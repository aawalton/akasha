In the pitch-dark room sealed shut with only a small ventilation shaft, Jinyo and Rina sat knee-to-knee in conversation, exchanging the information they had received that day — information that concerned the daughter they had been searching for.

"Is it really true that that person knows where Ryse is?" Rina fixed her gaze on Jinyo, pressing him for an answer.

"I don't know either. Theo wasn't in a sound state of mind, but he was trying to tell us something. If I had to guess, he must have encountered something terrifying deep in the heart of the forest — and it's not good news for us at all." Jinyo was so tense that his brows were nearly furrowed together. They could hear the sounds of singing and dancing drifting in from outside the house, cutting sharply against the tension that both of them felt. They were the pillars among the survivors, but the most troubling problem was not getting everyone back home — it was that they could not find the daughter.

The beautiful face that usually maintained its composed, cool demeanor bore small dark shadows beneath the eyes, concealed with makeup. Jinyo knew that Rina worked harder than anyone, spending nearly the entire day out in the sun fighting dangerous monsters, yet it should not have left Rina in this poor a condition. Only Jinyo, Naho, and Gretel — the children who knew Rina well — could see just how worn down she truly was.

"That child matters greatly to me. She is the last Palmer, and the one Norn left for me. No matter what, we have to find her."

"Same for me. It is my own responsibility. If I cannot save Ryse, then the power I have gained has no meaning." Jinyo had not forgotten what had caused everyone to become trapped in this cursed forest. He could barely close his eyes to sleep when he thought that he was the reason so many people had lost their lives. If he was the cause of the daughter's death, Jinyo would absolutely have no face to show Norn.

"Right now, I am confident that Ryse is still alive and is likely somewhere in the heart of the forest."

"That doesn't sound like good news at all."

"Just the fact that Theo is in this condition is already terrible news in itself. I cannot even imagine how terrifying the enemy he encountered must have been." Jinyo raised a hand and pressed his fingers between his brows. He could still vividly picture that battered, blood-soaked body — it felt as though it had happened only moments ago. Theo was the strongest among the Hunter exam candidates. He had not even been going all-out, yet his level of skill far surpassed both Jinyo and Nephrin. If even Theo had barely escaped with his life, how was Jinyo supposed to deal with such a threat?

From their long time spent in the cursed forest world, they had come to understand that the forest was divided into the inner forest and the outer forest. The inner forest was thick with fog and swarming with Eltharoc, while the outer forest had no Eltharoc but was populated with a large number of pumpkin-headed humans. The area where Jinyo currently was sat precisely at the midpoint between the inner forest and the outer forest.

The deeper one ventured into the inner forest, the higher-leveled the monsters that appeared. No one knew what lay at the very heart of the forest, because not a single person had ever survived from the deepest interior — or rather, there was now one exception: Theo. Nephrin had told him that someone had spotted Theo running for his life out of the inner forest all the way to the torchlight area. If anyone had found him any later than that, there would not have been a single breath of life left in him.

Jinyo had been assigned a mission ever since entering the cursed forest. Beyond the Lord of the Mountains, there were two other types of entities that were likely mini-bosses he needed to kill. The system called them [Witch of the Dark Forest] and [Soul Lantern Demon]. Jinyo had already killed one — the Lord of the Mountains — but as Rina had explained, the Lord of the Mountains kept being reborn and returning over and over, even if it was never the same entity each time.

"Rina, I think there are two others that are likely stronger than the Lord of the Mountains. I don't know whether you'll be able to handle them."

"Don't say ridiculous things. What they call the Lord of the Mountains isn't even an appetizer."

"That's true, I misjudged. But it's better not to be careless — especially since they're likely connected to the Unknown." The moment Jinyo finished that sentence, the confident smile on Rina's face disappeared. The word "Unknown" seemed to cast a spell over her that brought her back in line.

"You're right. Anything connected to those things is never good. Even though I'm confident I won't lose to anyone, I still need to increase my combat power as fast as possible — because apart from me, no one else has any real capability. Especially you."

"Ha ha, it still hurts even when it's directed at me." Jinyo laughed it off, but there was not a single thing he could argue against. The best he could do was to support everyone — whether it was Rina, Esther, or Theo. Jinyo currently had planar at the Knight level, but it still could not compare to Rina's even by a fingernail's margin. Even though their levels were only 10 apart right now, the gap in their planar was in the hundreds of thousands, if not the millions.

In the end, level was merely a tool to help draw out the potential of its user. If there was not enough power to draw upon, one would ultimately end up the loser. Jinyo conducted a thorough examination of Rina's body. No matter how busy things became, the routine he had always carried out had to continue. The treatment for planar poisoning had already been completed. All that remained was the chronically damaged meridian that was still slightly broken.

"Let's continue the treatment. But first, I have something new I'd like Rina to try."

"Go ahead then." Rina waited in anticipation. Jinyo burned resin wood to create a small warmth, then lifted a tiered lunchbox brimming with rice cooked until mushy, mixed together with other finely chopped grains. They did not look appetizing at all — everything had been dyed blue, making it entirely unenticing. Rina lifted a spoon and brought it to her mouth. It was sweet. Sweet and delicious, but not something one should eat alongside rice. It was like pouring soup onto a hamburger — no matter how sweet and tasty the soup was, one would not want it paired with a burger dripping with melted cheese and juicy meat. It was a contradictory flavor: good-tasting on its own, yet unpleasant in combination.

Rina began to sense that some kind of energy was circulating intensely through her body. Her breathing grew heavier, and she had to sit in meditation to adjust her planar flow.

"Rina, channel your planar along the points I'm about to tell you." Jinyo activated his Sage's Eye. The energy-transfer points along the meridians appeared in his vision and in the system window. He guided her step by step. Rina had no choice but to follow every step Jinyo described exactly — otherwise, the meridians that had been healed would be damaged again, and they would have to start all over from the beginning.

Jinyo began to massage and channel energy through her. He had her lying face down on the bed. The temperature inside her body rose until sweat poured without stopping. The sound of labored breathing filled the sealed room. The young woman's face flushed deep red from the embarrassment of having that pair of palms knead and press along her vital points. For some reason she could not explain, she felt addicted to the sensation of his hands.

It felt strange — her body and mind had gone entirely limp, as if completely surrendering to subjugation. She was disgusted with herself. The sweat was dark and viscous, uncomfortable and unpleasant. Above all else, the surging heat was affecting her thought processes.

"This porridge really does speed up the healing — at this rate, the meridians should fully recover within a few days. No — they'll even be stronger than before." Jinyo opened the system window to check. The treatment of Rina was proceeding successfully! Where the progress gauge used to increase by only +1 or +2 at a time, it was now jumping by +5. The effect of the magical food was undeniably real. He ran his hands gently over the surface of her body, the warmth of his palms soothing her trembling frame.

"Hey — you're going a bit too far."

"Ah, sorry." Jinyo quickly pulled his hands back. He had not even realized that the position of his hands on Rina had been… inappropriate. He had nearly touched her hips. Jinyo glanced sideways, trying to gauge how angry Rina actually was. Her hand reached out and grabbed his arm, pulling him closer. It was so sudden that he lost his balance. Jinyo used his other arm to brace himself from falling, but ended up so close to Rina that their bodies were nearly pressed together.

"Is something wrong, Rina?" Jinyo asked.

"You touch me here and there and you feel absolutely nothing at all?"

"No — I genuinely don't feel anything."

"Is that so… How boring. I shouldn't have expected anything." Rina clicked her tongue. She released the wrist she had been gripping and let it drop as though discarding an oversized, irritating piece of trash. Inside Jinyo's chest, something felt empty and hollow. He stared at the young woman's flushed face and her thin, sweat-soaked clothes clinging to her figure and revealing everything. The ruby-red eyes she turned on him held a mixture of anger and pain.

Rina averted her gaze from the intense stare Jinyo had fixed on her — as if sending the message that she did not want to see his face anymore, not right now. It was like a needle stabbing straight into the center of Jinyo's heart. It felt awful, and unbearably suffocating.

"Sorry. It's my fault. I'd like to be alone for a little while — and don't eavesdrop, either."

"Understood. If you need anything, just say so." Jinyo slowly rose to his feet. He stepped away from this suffocating situation without looking back. Rina rolled over and buried her face in the bedding. All manner of unsuitable thoughts swirled in her head, haunting her over and over again in a relentless cycle. She envied Naho for being able to openly ask for what she wanted so easily. Rina slowly removed her clothes piece by piece, then used a damp cloth to wipe the liquid from her body. Her eyes were red and her breathing was still erratic.

This is bad. I can't manage the planar that Jinyo put in — it's more voluminous and concentrated than every other time. Ah… that sinful man has absolutely no sense of responsibility. No — that idiot doesn't even think about such things in the first place. Rina closed her eyes and exhaled a long, hot breath, focusing on adjusting her energy to expel the excess planar Jinyo had left behind. But it was far too difficult to expel through normal means. It took nearly an hour in the dark room before her body finally returned to its normal state — leaving her so drained that it felt as though her heart had fallen out of her body, floating away together with her very consciousness.

Jinyo left the house and sat down beside a tree where Esther was meditating with her eyes closed, circulating her energy. The sound of his sigh made Esther's eyelid twitch. She opened her eyes to find Jinyo sitting there wilted like a dried-out branch, and immediately felt so irritated that her eyelid twitched again.

"Get lost. I'm busy."

"I haven't even said anything yet."

"Just the sound of your breathing is enough to break my concentration. Get lost!"

"Don't be so heartless to me. I go out of my way to provide you with good food and water, you know. I consider myself a benefactor of yours as well."

"..." Esther was effectively silenced by the weight of that debt, left unable to breathe. True — if not for Jinyo, she would be back to gnawing on tree roots and eating food of livestock-grade quality.

"Without you I wouldn't starve to death either. But since I am a man of great compassion, I'll go ahead and listen to your problems."

"It's just that Rina keeps scolding me all the time. Why is that?"

"A marital problem!? Don't come consulting a single person like me about that, you father of two!" Esther jabbed her finger at Jinyo so hard it nearly planted itself in the middle of his forehead. Esther's emotions erupted like a volcano on the verge of explosion. So many exhausting, heavy problems bearing down on her, and he comes to consult her about woman trouble!

"I have absolutely no experience with women. I genuinely mean that."

"With a face like that, you've really never dated anyone?"

"The only people I am close to are my little sister, Rina, and Norn. I don't really talk with anyone else. Between Rina and me, it has always been more like just friends. I know that Rina likes me to some extent, but I don't understand how much she likes me or what she actually wants from me — and I don't understand why I seem to irritate her in everything I do, even when I'm genuinely trying to be good to her. I don't understand it at all."

"…." Esther's face went blank. As blank as the intentions with which she had resolved to listen to Jinyo's problems. Yes — all of it was worthless. Every effort to help ease Jinyo's personal burden was utterly and completely worthless.

"What does Esther think?"

"How would I know? I'm not your wife, and I'm not that bloodthirsty woman either. This is your problem. I've barely known her for a month, but you've known her your entire life. The fact that you don't understand a single thing about her means you are an irredeemably hopeless man."

"Oof!" Jinyo clutched his chest. It hurt — it hurt so much he could not breathe, and he could not argue back against a single word of it. The feeling of wanting to die welled up and flooded his chest until he wanted to throw himself into a river. Perhaps becoming fish food at the bottom of the ocean would contribute more to the world than he ever could.

"You're right. I am a hopeless man." Jinyo's body gradually drained of all color, turning so white he nearly dissolved into thin air.

"Hey, don't get all depressed like that. That was my fault."

"I feel terrible. If only I had paid more attention to Rina…"

"Sigh." Esther let out a heavy breath. When this man fought or did just about anything, he was genuinely reliable and dependable — so why was he so infuriating at times like this that she wanted to punch him? He was bad at communication and spoke without any finesse or tact. Choosing to come to Esther for advice had been the worst possible choice Jinyo could have made. But then again, Jinyo probably had no one else he could turn to — or rather, even if he did, he would still choose to rely on Esther over the others.

He was a father of two, yet he could not resolve issues within his own family without turning to an outsider. Truly useless.

"Consult a woman — oh! Right, that's true. Better to ask Naho."

Naho Southerncross was the woman whose actions were the most impossible to predict with any kind of logic out of everyone Esther had ever known. Neither of the Southerncross siblings was remotely normal. Consulting each other would get absolutely nowhere.

Nephrin was both beautiful and intelligent — she must have had enough experience in love to offer proper guidance, and she was a woman just like Rina! It was sure to work.

[Your son, Gretel Erindel, is displeased. Affection value -30]

He even agrees, does he. Jinyo jolted in surprise — he would have to quietly shelve this for now and clear up the misunderstanding with Gretel later.

The conflict between himself — who was called the Celestial Demon — and the enemies who claimed to have descended from the heavens.
