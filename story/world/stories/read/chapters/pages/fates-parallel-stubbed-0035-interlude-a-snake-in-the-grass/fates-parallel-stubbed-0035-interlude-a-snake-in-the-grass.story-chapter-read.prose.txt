Hyeong Daesung sighed with frustration as he finished yet another useless meditation session. No matter how he tried, he was unable to detect any sign of the linking meridians Lee Jia had described to him. He didn’t doubt the truth of her words—it was actually a fairly popular theory among academics that the body, soul, and mind must be metaphysically linked—he simply seemed to be lacking something fundamental.

He had his suspicions as to what that might be. Both his master and his current teacher were experts—or as close to experts as existed—in the field of interdisciplinary cultivation. Progressing too far in a single discipline made it much harder to train in others, until eventually the rotes and habits became so ingrained into one’s cultivation that it became impossible to even awaken in a second discipline. This was why none of the teachers—nor his master—had successfully trained in other disciplines. It wasn’t a lack of will, but ability.

As such, Dae was rather proud of the fact that he’d managed to awaken in all three. He was part of a relatively small group of students who had managed to accomplish that—a group that shared only one common element. Dae’s thoughts drifted—as they often did these days—to the mysterious young girl that had brought them all together.

By most of the metrics people used to measure such things, Lee Jia was not special. Average looks, a spiritual ancestor as common as his own, and a background that wasn’t difficult to guess at—as much as she tended to avoid the subject. Indeed, the most remarkable thing about Lee Jia in a place where remarkable people had gathered in the hundreds, was how unremarkable she was.

And yet she had attracted some of the most remarkable people in the academy, and many of them owed their success in interdisciplinary cultivation to her. Since coming to this academy Lee Jia had revealed her single most remarkable trait—her talent.

Dae knew he was a prodigy—it wasn’t something he liked to brag about, but it was a simple fact. He was the youngest mage to ever reach the peak of the mana comprehension stage. He had even been putting off his breakthrough into the third stage to focus on other disciplines. Lee Jia’s talent made his cultivation look pathetically slow by comparison.

In less than half a year, she had ascended to the second stage of cultivation—in all three disciplines. Additionally, she had somehow unified them into a single cultivation method which was, by all appearances, greater than the sum of its parts. Dae had been trying to replicate it for weeks, to no avail. His first guess was that he would need to raise his other disciplines to match his magic. A seemingly impossible task, given his rather abysmal affinity for them.

He’d barely managed to replicate Lee Jia’s unusual calligraphy technique after weeks of practice. Something she’d come up with in an afternoon, on a whim. Dae grumbled to himself as he wondered if Lee Jia was even aware of how ridiculous her abilities were. She had a domain! That was supposed to be impossible without being able to manipulate divine energy.

In passing, it occurred to Dae that all of these things also applied to Lee Jia’s friend and roommate, An Eui. If Lee Jia was a riddle, then An Eui was an ancient puzzle mechanism locked in a vault and buried at the bottom of the ocean. The girl was very secretive, and Dae had not learned anything about her that he couldn’t have figured out from the brand on her forehead.

Most of his knowledge about An Eui had come from their field expedition. There he had learned that her cultivation was a match for Lee Jia’s—an exact match—with the exception of their elemental affinities. He had also learned just how close the two of them were. The explanation of both had come from Xin Wei’s observation of Eui’s near-death experience.

Dae blushed and felt a pang of jealousy before shaking his head. He’d done some...research on the subject of dual cultivation since their adventure and Xin’s accusation. He blushed again—most of the materials on the subject were very explicit. The conclusion that Dae had come to was that they were not dual cultivators—at least, not in the typical fashion—but then, nothing about them ever seemed to be typical.

With a heavy sigh, Dae wished he could consult with his master on the subject. They had corresponded by letters, but some things could only be discussed in private. As if summoned by his thoughts, he heard a sound at his window. A paper crane was there, pecking at the glass until he opened the window, at which point it flew into his hand and unfolded itself into a perfectly uncreased letter.

His master was fond of the spell as a way of sending letters. Dae wasn’t capable of writing it himself, but he had a few talismans made by his master that could replicate it. As Dae read through the letter, his expression darkened, then he broke into a cold sweat. By the time he finished, his hands were trembling and he swore as he dropped the letter.

“Oh no! Oh, ancestors, this is terrible. I have to warn them!”

Dae charged out of the house, leaving the letter on his desk. He wasn’t worried about someone reading it—he hadn’t yet been assigned a roommate.

How are you, my boy? I received your last report—what an adventure! News of Yan’s death travelled fast; I actually discovered it before even receiving your letter. Qin’s already making moves, but I was well prepared for it. They’ve been forced to make some concessions to get their thinly veiled agents in, and I’m taking full advantage.

I’ve officially stepped down as prime minister! Unfortunately, the Seong clan got another one of their own to replace me, but that’s a small price to pay. I’ve still got influence to spare, but now I am free to act. I’m on my way to the Grand Academy as we speak, and I’ve hand picked some new students that should make things very interesting.

See you soon, Dae.

—Former Prime Minister of Goryeo, Grand Magus Do Hye

As Dae ran for the western wing of the campus without a clue how he was going to contact Lee Jia, he cursed himself. He loved his master like a father, but to that man the world was a laboratory, people were test subjects, and words were nothing more than the tools of social engineering. His friends weren’t ready—he wasn’t ready! He should have been careful what he wished for.

The man driving the carriage sneezed and then chuckled. Tae In-Su eyed the strange man warily. An old man with serpentine eyes and scales running up along his neck and over his bald head. Despite the power radiating off of the man, Tae In-Su couldn’t sense his aura at all. Of all the people to respond to his plight, he had never imagined the prime minister himself would show up—former prime minister, he reminded himself.

“Is it really necessary to travel together like this?”

Tae complained, once again, about the travel arrangements. He was certain he could make better time on his own, but Do Hye had insisted that they should travel together and seemed determined to travel by carriage.

“Can’t let you go on your own, my boy. Never know when you might meet a thief on the road, heh! And if you’re travelling with me, you travel my way. Enjoy the spring blooms!”

Tae In-Su grimaced, but dropped the matter. It was embarrassing beyond belief that he had been outwitted by some random petty criminal and lost his token. For what must have been the millionth time, he cursed that damn Lee Jia. No doubt she had either pawned the token off for a handful of coins or lost it in the belly of whatever beast devoured her in the wilderness. He hoped it was the latter.

To take his mind off of his embarrassment, he regarded the other passenger of the carriage. The girl was well endowed despite being rather short—with a thickness around the hips, thighs, and bust that managed to enhance her figure without crossing the line into pudgy. She was pretty, he supposed, but her hair was a light blonde that came close to white—a color which put him off entirely.

Still, while she wasn’t his type, he found himself considering whether he should try his luck anyway. The light furred rabbit ears atop her head marked her as a member of the Sun family, and while their status wasn’t all that remarkable among nobles, their ancestry was. The Sun family was one of the few noble houses that could trace their human ancestry all the way back to pre-imperial Qin, and their spiritual ancestor was said to be the legendary moon rabbit.

“Forgive me, my lady, for the late introduction. I am Tae In-Su, the former administrator of Nayeong. Might I ask who I am sharing the company of this carriage with?”

The girl swept Tae In-Su with a bored expression and sighed.

“Sun Jaehwa—and before you get any ideas, I should warn you that my last suitor was murdered in cold blood.”

Tae’s friendly smile froze on his face. What ill fortune! Well, if he was hesitant before, he’d completely lost interest now.

“My condolences, Miss Sun. I assure you my only intention was to make conversation to pass the time on our trip.”

Sun Jaehwa rolled her eyes, evidently not convinced by the smooth lie.

“Sure.”

She turned her eyes back to the road and went back to ignoring him. Tae sighed as Do Hye started chuckling at his expense. This was going to be a long trip.
