The goddess's tale continued. Jade listened with her companions as Lady Elaria described the demons' campaign of conquest across the stars. Civilizations clashed, peoples were conquered, and worlds razed before their divinely granted might. Some gods gifted great power to other worlds, hoping they could use it to stop the demonic hordes. Rather than describing the subsequent devastation wrought by those clashes, the goddess opted to use her illusions to show them. Jade squeezed her fists tight when she saw the shattered worlds, scoured ruins, and rusted wastelands that followed. Other gods took more direct action, turning their wrath upon the people they themselves had lifted to greatness. That too met with little success, as the demons had learned more than the gods had intended from their holy gifts.

"One man in particular stood apart from the rest." Lady Elaria explained. She conjured an image of a tall, broad shouldered creature with indistinct features. Jade found it hard to focus on him, as his form was shrouded in shifting shadow. Only his eyes stood out, burning pits of molten crimson that pierced the veil of his dark mantle. "Many demons suffer from minds damaged by the divine essence that flows through their blood. But Lord Vastrozun found a way to counteract this decay. I don't know how long it took, but he eventually discovered the most dangerous secret of all. How to harvest divine power."

"Harvest?" Tara asked. "How does that even work? Divine relics contain only hints of pure cosmic essence, and you can't even touch most of them safely."

"I found that out that hard way." Jade said, thinking back to her brush with the death essence they'd found in the Lysara stronghold. She'd touched it during her fight with the vampire, and now she recalled the brief visions it had shown her. Destruction. Devastation. And a tall figure wreathed in shadow…

"Lord Vastrozun had two major advantages over other mortals in this regard." Lady Elaria explained. "One was the powerful body he had forged for himself, and the second was his existing link to the cosmos.""Link?" asked Naomi.

"All demons carry a link to their home world and the seeds of power gifted to them, even now. They hold a spark of divine power within them, twisted though it may now be. This makes them rather resistant to its volatile nature, and Vastrozun improved his form until that resistance became something greater. Now, he carries within him a core of both dark and renewal essence. Renewal to fortify his body, and dark for its might. With this new power, he imbued the essence of dark into his many followers, further enhancing them. Not all could withstand it, and fewer still could do so without damaging their reason and compassion, but the result was a race of creatures more powerful than anything the Universe has seen before or since. It is Vastrozun's power that maintains the breach his followers have created in their prison, and it is his might they now draw on to make good their escape."

"Do I have that same link?" Jade asked. "I'm a demon, now, too."

"All wayfarers do, in a way." Lady Elaria gestured towards the glowing sigil on the back of her hand. "The Labyrinth's gift is also one of divine power, although we've learned from our mistakes. The spark of essence you each hold is smaller and more stable than that of a true demon. It greatly accelerates your growth, but not so much that it begins to eat at your spirit."

"A gift, is it?" Jade said, trying her best to keep the bite from her tone. This wasn't a being they could afford to antagonize, but that struck a nerve. "If the Labyrinth hadn't come to Earth, our people wouldn't be in danger. None of this would have happened.""Perhaps not to you." The goddess replied evenly, meeting her glare with a level gaze. "But the demons have been on the verge of escaping for a very long time, as it turns out.""As it turns out?" Luis asked. "That makes it sound like this is all news to you.""In a way, it is. When we, the awakened gods of the cosmos, realized the scale of the threat the demons had become, we acted. There was no way to destroy them, not without an utterly unacceptable level of collateral damage, but we did have one advantage. That link to their home world, the one that served to channel their sacred power, was also a tether that bound them to it. Using it as such, we drew demons from across the cosmos back to the world their ancestors came from. It was far from easy, and there were some who escaped, but in the end, we caught almost all of them. Then, we sealed them away using a newly forged world. The Labyrinth, as you have discovered. It surrounds their planet in its entirety, cutting it off from the world tree and utterly isolating it from other dimensions. Our children still had their sacred home, but their careless exploitation of its gifts had soured its fruit. It was no longer the paradise they were born into, but a purgatory of their own creation."

"I'm surprised they didn't destroy each other." Jade said, still processing that deluge of information. "The ones I've met haven't been the most… stable."And neither were the instincts she constantly suppressed. If they weren't telling her to kill something, they were inflaming her more carnal desires to heights that seemed to shroud her vision in a haze of lustful need. And this was supposed to be a more stable form of their divine power? That thought sent a shudder down her spine. Maybe she could understand Malice a little more, now, in a twisted way.

"They very well might have, if not for Vastrozun. Since his rise to power, he has ever been their leader. Demonkind reveres him with such fervor, I sometimes wonder if he somehow imbued that as a compulsion into the gifts of power he wove into his people."

"But why?" Jade asked, squeezing her fists again. "Why did you make the Labyrinth behave like it does? Why does it come to world after world, stripping them bare?"

"That… isn't quite what we intended." This time, the goddess let out a deep sigh. Her shoulders slumped slightly, and a heavy melancholy stilled the air around them. "This world's creation was on a far greater scale than any of us had attempted before, and it took its toll on us. One by one, drained of power, we slipped into a deep slumber. The others faded until only I remained, guiding the Labyrinth's growth. Already the demons chipped away at the bars of their prison, and it wouldn't hold forever. So, I imprinted upon it a mechanism that allowed it to grow on its own. When the recent disruption finally woke me from my slumber, I found that aspect of its function had warped over the millennia. It was supposed to link worlds together, drawing materials only from stellar bodies that would have no impact on sentient life. These links allowed the people of those worlds to reach each other and gave them the chance to train their best and brightest warriors to heights that would otherwise be near impossible to reach. But, as our captives drew closer to freedom, the Labyrinth's impulse grew more aggressive. In an attempt to prevent their escape, it has ravaged worlds in ways it was never intended to."

They sat in silence for a time, processing that. They'd debated several of these questions again and again over the past few months. Finally receiving definitive answers was a relief, but also a burden. Jade had a strong urge to lash out at Lady Elariel. She freely admitted that she was at least partially responsible for the disaster that threatened Earth, and for how she and her friends had been pulled from their homes and thrust into a world of conflict. Thanks to this place, she wasn't even human anymore. Worse, she was now kin to the creatures who it sounded like were practically born from sin. And yet…

Jade rolled her ankle, remembering the sharp pain that used to accompany the motion. She thought of her brother, bravely fighting on one day after another. A world of magic and mystery gave him hope that he didn't have on Earth. Siora had always tried to show Jade the better parts of the Labyrinth, and she had to admit that she herself had benefited from it in more ways than one. So, no. Erupting at the goddess would only be the action of a petulant child. Maybe that's what a real demon would do, but Jade wasn't one. Not entirely. She had the comforting warmth of Seraphine's lingering celestial aura to prove that.

"What do we do?"

This time, Siora broke the silence. She sat on the grass, her arms wrapped around her knees.

"There has to be something, right?"

"The goddess wouldn't have told us this story if she didn't have a plan." Luis said. He was sitting beside Tara, who was rapidly keying notes into one of her suit displays.

"That’s right." Jade nodded. "You want us to do something, don't you."

"As I said before, it will be your choice."

"Why us? There have to be many powerful Wayfarers in the Labyrinth. Why are you paying special attention to us? Surely anything we can do, someone else out there can do better.""For one simple reason." The goddess said. "Because you're a demon, now."

There was another beat of silence.

"What?" Naomi asked, confused. "Why does that matter?"

"You want me to go to their world."

It came to Jade in a flash of insight. Something had been nagging at her for a long time, ever since she'd heard that demon races had only recently started becoming options for new Wayfarers. Why had it changed? And why, if the Labyrinth was a prison for the demon race, would it allow outsiders to take their forms and powers? It didn't make any sense, but the entire system seemed far too intelligently designed for it to have been an accident.

"What?" Luis asked, giving voice to the confusion emanating from her friends. "What are you talking about?"

"If this Vastrozun is the only one capable of sustaining the link between the demon world and the Labyrinth, then someone has to stop him. But if he's in their home, surrounded by who knows how many of his followers, who could possibly reach him?" Jade met the goddess's eyes with an even gaze. "Am I right?"

"For the most part." She admitted. Her lips twitched in a frown, but she didn't look away. "No one, not even an army could make it through the forces currently being assembled on the far side of the breach. Not in enough time to achieve anything meaningful. If I had awoken earlier, perhaps I could have found a way to stop them before they reached this point. But I did not, and the Labyrinth's consciousness, an echo of my will, appears to have taken matters into its own hands. It has allowed a small number of people like yourself to assume their forms in the hopes that one of you will achieve what no one else can."

"Hold on." Naomi said, raising one finger. "You want Jade to, what, assassinate the lord of the demons? The being you just told us has god-like power?" She looked between the two of them, her eyebrows raised. "I mean, she's good, but… that's beyond her. It's beyond any of us.""It is." Jade crossed her arms. The goddess couldn't be serious. Six months ago, she would have laughed at the concept of magic being real. She was a long way off from being able to confront someone with that kind of strength. But surely the goddess knew that better than any of them, so… maybe… "Unless you have a way around that?"

"Jade, you aren't actually considering this, are you?" Naomi asked, aghast. She grabbed her arm, as if afraid she would leave right then and there.

"Let's at least hear her out."

Naomi exchanged a concerned look with Luis but fell silent.

"You're correct." Elaria continued, reaching into the folds of her emerald dress with a slender hand. The movement was slow, deliberate, and Jade was again struck by just how weary the goddess looked. How drained. "I would not ask you to embark on a fool's errand with no chance of success. Which is why I have prepared this."

A swirling breeze ruffled the folds of Elaria's dress as she withdrew her hand, revealing the midnight gem clasped in her fingers. The crystal was long and slender, tapering to a point at both ends. Thick fog swirled within its dark facets, lit with sudden flashes of violet lightning. It was like someone had captured a thunderstorm in a gemstone, condensing its power and violence into a single, minute point. Its very presence darkened the tranquil glade, as if the sky beyond the dome was now filled with ominous clouds.

They all stared at the relic, transfixed by the boundless power they sensed waiting within. Jade suppressed a shudder, and a chill ran down her spine. Again, she was reminded of Aylin's splinter of divine death essence. Merely holding that had almost been enough to kill her, and the power trapped within the goddess's crystal somehow felt even more powerful.

"This is a splinter of the concentrated essence of Destruction. It was gifted to me by Thane, one of the many gods who sacrificed his power to me so that we could imprison our wayward children here. He is gone, now, but his power remains. I have shaped this vessel so that it can be safely wielded by mortal hands, granting whoever holds it the ultimate authority to dismantle all that lays before them."

She tossed the crystal in the air. It remained there, floating a few feet above the ground, sparkling with violet light.

"As the wielder of Growth, it saddens me to burden another with Destruction. It is anathema to my nature. But the threat the demons pose to the worlds of the cosmos is too great for me to abide." Elaria shifted her gaze back to her, her emerald eyes filled with mournful regret. "I know it is much to ask, Jade, but there are few who could succeed at this task. And of those who might be able to, you are the only one who has answered my call."

She gestured towards the divine relic.

"There is still a chance to stop a new cosmic war before it begins. If you can make your way through the host of demons standing between us and him, this artifact would give you the power to defeat Vastrozun."
