His hands stilled on the torn fabric he was using to bind her shoulder. Blood—so much blood—was soaking through the cloth.

[Certainty: Are you sure, Clive?]

The notification caught Clive by surprise. It had been so long since he chatted with Certainty. Months of silence. And now… now, when he was elbow-deep in Jill's blood, trying to keep her from bleeding out, this was what she had to say?

"I don't have time for this." He dismissed the notification with a sharp gesture and pressed the bandage harder against Jill's shoulder. The fabric was already saturated. "We're not talking about this."

He tore another strip from his shirt. Seal the shoulder. Stop the bleeding. Keep his hands moving. Any other thought was unnecessary.

[Certainty: I'm helping you out. She's not the Jill you think she is. Just because she looks like her and talks like her, doesn't mean she is her.]

"She is her."  He tied off the bandage, moving to the gash on her arm. His own wounds throbbed with each movement, but he ignored them. "This is Jill. I know her."

[Certainty: You knew her. Past tense. The woman you knew couldn’t shoot moonbeams from her eyes. Don’t get confused Clive. This isn’t Jill. It’s a monster.]

[Certainty: It erases who she is. Clive, think. The Jill you loved, would she really try to murder you?]

Jill stirred under his hands. A small sound of pain escaped her lips. Clive's chest tightened.

"She stopped," he said. He checked her pulse. Weak but steady. "When the berserk state faded, she stopped. That's still Jill in there."

[Certainty: For how long? Until she wakes up again?]

Clive couldn’t answer. He still didn’t understand why Jill was hostile to him. But there was one thing he knew with absolute certainty.

"I'm not killing her," he declared. "Don't ask me again."

[Certainty: Very well Clive. You’ll regret this. Don’t say I didn’t told you so.]

"I won’t.”

He swiped the notification away.

Silence settled over them. Above, the blood moon had faded completely. Nearby, Azura lay curled on her side, licking the wounds in her scales closed.

Clive. Her mental voice was cautious. I think we should at least tie her up.

His hand tightened on Jill's wrist. The thought of binding Jill like a common criminal made him uncomfortable.

They found a cave carved into the hillside. Azura limped inside first, sniffing for threats. Clive carried Jill, her head against his shoulder, and set her down as gently as he could against the back wall. He used what remained of his shirt to secure her wrists to an outcropping of rock.

He stood, stepped back and gazed upon Jill’s fallen figure. Even now, she was beautiful.

Aren’t you cold?

Azura’s voice startled him.

He glanced down. Right. Shirtless. The cave air bit at his skin now that he was still, but the adrenaline kept him warm. Blood, both his and Jill's, had dried in dark streaks down his ribs.

"I'm fine," he replied. But when he looked around, he noticed Azura near the cave entrance. The dragon had curled into a tight ball, wings folded close, scales trembling. And Jill—slumped against the rock, unconscious, her torn and bloodied clothes useless against the mountain cold. Her lips had gone pale, tinged faintly blue.The cold would kill her before morning if he didn't do something.

He gathered some fallen logs from the cave mouth and built it into a proper pyre. He took out his brush and painted a layer of red over it. The wood caught fire instantly.

"Here." He positioned the fire between them. "This should help."

Thank you. Azura stretched one wing toward the heat, letting out a long sigh as warmth seeped into her torn scales.

Clive sat.

The adrenaline was dying now. He could feel it draining away, leaving him hollowed out.

The fire crackled. Heat washed over his bare chest while the cave's chill bit at his back. He was shaking, he realized. Had been for a while.

He pulled his knees up, wrapped his arms around them, and stared into the flames.

Hours passed. The fire burned low. Clive fed it when the flames started to gutter. Another log, another brush of red paint to keep it going.

Staring at the fire was easier than thinking. The flames twisted and curled, eating the wood grain by grain. He could lose himself in that. The crackle and pop. It was better than thinking about Jill tied to the rock behind him. Better than thinking about the scythe coming down. Better than thinking about anything at all. He fed the fire again.

Flames climbed higher. He didn’t know how long he sat there. Long enough for the blood on his hands to dry and crack. Long enough that the sound of movement behind him made him flinch.

Clive's head snapped around.

Jill stirred.

She lifted her head slowly. Then her arms jerked—once, twice—testing the bonds. Her eyes snapped open in shock.

She pulled harder, but the bindings held firm. Her breathing quickened as she looked around the cave.

She stared at him but said nothing.

“You must be hungry.” He reached for his sketchbook. A few quick strokes, and a sushi box materialized in his hands. He opened it and held it out toward her.

"You loved the Otoro, remember?"

Jill's eyes dropped to the sushi. The fatty tuna glistened in the firelight, pale pink marbled with white. Two perfect pieces.

They'd been poor back then. Sushi was a luxury they couldn't afford except on special occasions—her birthday, maybe. An anniversary. Those rare nights when they'd splurge and damn the consequences. She'd always saved the Otoro for last, closing her eyes when she ate it, that little smile playing at the corners of her mouth.

"There's wasabi too," he said when she didn't respond. "And the ginger. Just how you like it."

She stared at the cave wall.

"Jill... what happened?"

Silence.

"Why did you attack me?" He leaned forward, trying to catch her eye. "Please, just… just talk to me. Tell me what's going on."

Nothing. She might as well have been carved from the same stone she was tied to.

"Jill, please." His voice cracked. "You're hurt. You need to eat something. You need—"

She turned her face further away. Shutting him out completely.

Clive sat there, still holding the container. The Otoro was cooling in his hands now, losing its warmth.

They were right next to each other, yet the space between them might as well have been an ocean.

"Okay," he said quietly. "Okay."

He set the container down within her reach. Close enough that she could get to it if she stretched. If she wanted to.

The fire crackled. Jill didn't move.

Clive returned to his spot by the flames and tried not to watch her from the corner of his eye.

“Whenever you’re hungry.”

The fire crackled. Neither of them spoke.

As the night went on, Clive slowly dozed off. His eyes grew heavy. He tried to fight it—had to stay alert, had to watch—but his body had other ideas. His head dropped. Once. Twice.

The third time, he didn't lift it.

It was the sound of stomach growling.

Clive's eyes opened. The fire had burned down to coals, bathing the cave in dim orange light.

It tipped and spilled over.

The Otoro tumbled out onto the dirt.

Clive stood. His body ached but he crossed to her and picked up the container. Brushed the dirt off the fish as best he could and set it down directly in front of her.

Jill stared at the food.

"My hands," she said finally.

Clive, don't.

She tried to kill us. Don't be stupid.

Clive—

Jill's hands came free. She didn't look at him. Didn't say thank you. Just grabbed the sushi and started eating.

She didn't savor it this time. No closing her eyes, no little smile. She ate like someone starving, barely chewing, swallowing almost whole. The Otoro disappeared first. Then the salmon. The yellowtail. She scraped wasabi directly onto the rice and stuffed it in her mouth.

When the container was empty, she set it down. Her hands stayed in her lap, wrists red and raw where the binding had been.

She still wouldn't look at him.
