The vibration finally died under Barry's palms.

"It's done," he said, peeling off his gloves. He sounded tired, but relieved too.

Bela came over and pressed a hand to the huge crystal. Barry was right. The hum was gone, and in its place was a clean, almost musical stillness that felt close to magic.

"So what now? We build houses? Something comfortable?"

Barry nearly laughed, but didn't. "You've got the wrong idea. Healing was only the first layer. The crystals I handed out, the ones we handed out, have been scanning the entire population this whole time. Now we move to phase two."

Bela's boot scraped the stone as she stepped back. "What does that mean?"

"Every prayer. Every private fear. Every half-formed want the giants ever fed into the network. It's all sorted and sitting in this processor." He shut down the interface and turned to the three still standing in the hall.

He didn't wait for agreement. One look at their faces was enough. The extractor was already in his hand, and the needle was in Thor's arm before the god had finished processing what he'd said.

"Ow! What was that for? I'm generating plenty of energy as it is." Thor held still, but the flinch had already happened, and he hated that it showed.

"Phase two, like I said. Bela, you're next."

Her hands shook once. She couldn't answer, because she was too busy doing the math: all that data, every need of every person. What would Barry do with it? The answer came to her, and she didn't like it. Not one bit.

Loki, still wearing a frost giant's borrowed face, felt the needle too, but the pain meant nothing. What bothered her was the needle itself. Almost nothing could pierce a god's skin, so what was it made of?

[Lab construction complete.]

A clean chime cut through the air. It didn't come from his goggles or his watch, but from the system that had brought him here in the first place. These days it only gave him notifications about what he actually needed. No missions, no rewards, none of that nonsense.

"Right on time. The lab's ready." Barry headed for the old temple wall without checking whether anyone followed. Of course they did.

Inside the temple, all three of them felt it: a different hum, low in the floor, or maybe somewhere inside them. Barry pressed his palm flat against the stone, and a seam slid open to reveal a staircase leading down. Bela edged closer. The tunnel was beautifully built.

"When did you...?" She gave up mid-sentence. Whatever the answer was, it would be smothered in his smugness.

They went down. Above them the door sealed with a low hydraulic sigh.

Thud.

Another door opened at the bottom of the stairs, and the underground base came into view. Barry strode in like he was late for something. The other three hung back for a few seconds, staring.

The workshop shouldn't have existed, yet there it was, finished, lit, and already running. Dwarves moved between stations, passing in and out of a steady blue portal that hung in the air like a clean cut through the room.

Thor stopped dead in front of it. He had watched Barry work for days and told himself he understood the scale of it. He didn't. He stepped through. For a few seconds he stood in the real workshop on the other side, breathing air that smelled like home in a way nothing here did. Then he made himself turn around and come back.

He knew then that Barry had never needed his help getting home. He could have gone any time he liked.

Loki felt awe, which was rare enough that it took her more than a second to recognize it. This mortal had built a working second workshop under their feet without asking a single god's permission. But the awe didn't stay pure. By the time Thor stepped back through, she was already sizing up the portal and wondering whether Barry had any idea what he was sitting on. The possibilities...

Barry ignored them both. He fed the three blood samples into the machines, which puffed steam and water and slowly pushed five raw masses of meat and skin onto the trays. Some had blinking eyes. Some had fingernails. It was so grotesque that all three of them backed away. Barry didn't. He slid the trays onto a moving panel that carried them into an oven-like device.

"This is phase two?" Bela's voice had gone thin. "What are you doing?"

"Clones. Typical sci-fi stuff. You'll understand once you go through the memories I implanted."

Bela's mind raced. When had he implanted them? When he raised her intelligence? Later? While she slept?

Ting!

The ovens chimed. On the trays lay five frost giant infants, small and still, glistening with red slime. Bela's fingers hovered, then touched one. "Why... why would you make babies?"

"Strange thing to ask. To succeed their parents, obviously. Take on their problems and live on."

Loki stopped being surprised and started moving. "Let me help."

"Finally, a volunteer. Thank you, nameless giantess. My actual assistant is busy judging me."

"I'm not... I didn't..."

Barry and Loki settled the infants into green liquid cylinders, five in all. Loki's hand stayed on the last one half a second longer than the job required. When they were done, she wiped a smear of condensation off the glass with her sleeve, caught herself, and stopped.

The bodies grew fast. Limbs lengthened, faces resolved, and within minutes five adults floated in the tanks, eyes shut.

Barry ran the analyses right away. IQ scores and preloaded data scrolled across the system screen.

"I never wanted this much work," he muttered, to no one in particular. "After this, I'm free."

He split the entire prayer archive across five new crystals, coded like the others but more advanced, and seated one in each brain. They sank in like mush and wired themselves permanently into the public network, linked to every crystal he'd given the people.

One of the clones opened her eyes a fraction of a second early, still mostly under, and found Bela's face before the other four had stirred. Then all five were awake together.

"Barry, please tell me you're not making a whole new set of gods."

Silence.

Barry turned to her, his eyes lowered and clear. He was disappointed.

"Not literally, Bela. They won't have powers like our Thor here. But they'll have the mental capacity to rule, to lead, with everything at their fingertips. All the information on the people. Your people. How they feel, what they think, what they'll do. Everything."

"But that would be..." She took half a step back.

"What? Unfair? Stop being naive, Bela. Grow up."

Bela's breath caught. Thor's hands closed into fists with nowhere useful to go, and one drifted to his hip, where the hammer should have hung, and found nothing. He had no idea when he'd lost it.

Loki felt a cold thrill, as if she were standing at the edge of something.

Bela kept her eyes on the floor and gripped her own hand tight. She knew down to her bones that this was wrong. So she stepped up.

"They'll know a problem before it's even finished happening. They'll solve it before anyone's done praying," she said, her voice low and unguarded. "Does that leave anyone a choice in anything?"

Barry didn't turn around. His hands kept moving on the keyboard. "Naive, again. There wasn't much choice before, either. I just made the machinery visible. That's all."

"So we're supposed to hand over our fate, just like that?"

He didn't answer. Thor's jaw tightened. "Barry, you've built something great. Something marvelous. But what you're giving the people of this realm isn't a gift." The words came out slower than his usual volume, and angrier for it. "You have to stop."

"Agreed," Bela said. Her eyes were wet, and she didn't blink it back.

Barry raised a specialized gun and fired at the nameless giantess.

The glamour tore. The pressure in the room shifted, a pop felt more than heard, and in the half second of dead silence that followed, Loki's true shape stood where the frost giant had been.

"Loki?" Thor's hand went to his hip again, but now it was glowing. The tattoo glowed too.

"She's been here the whole time," Barry said, as if he were remarking on the weather. "And she wants Asgard. So go on, get busy."

"You piece of shit!" Loki was already moving. Her boots slipped once on the stone, a short screech that cut off as she caught the wall and used it to take the corner faster.

Somewhere down the corridor a support pillar cracked with one sharp report. Then Thor's voice carried back up the passage, low and furious. He had shoved Bela aside on his way past, and she hit the wall hard.

Barry walked over and stood staring down at her. He exhaled long and slow in the settling dust, a man already tallying how much of his evening this was going to eat. The five clones stayed in their cylinders, eyes open, while the loading screen crept toward one hundred percent.

"I should've just raised your IQ, but the stimulus boosted your EQ too. Your empathy. My bad." He leveled another gun at her, this one loaded with a different syringe. "But I'll fix it. Don't worry."

"Wh-why?" She couldn't feel her legs.

"I want to retire. I'm not babysitting gods and their messes for another week."

"They won't stop, Barry." She watched the readouts. "I'm not smart like you, but I know one thing. The prayers. The people. You've given them something that always answers now. And when they find out what's actually answering..."

"I know," Barry said, pressing the gun to her head as she kept going.

"They'll worship it or try to kill it. That's the pattern you've missed."

"I know. Thank you for everything. You'll thank me later, don't worry." He pulled the trigger.

Once she lay unconscious, he sealed the lab doors and started a soft lockdown on the lower levels. The clones stepped out of the draining tanks, green liquid sheeting off them, and stood waiting for orders. His orders.

Phase Two was live.
