Velidan

I was covered in blood, smelled vile, and… seemed to be naked.

The rest of the world came in fragments. Firm, cold stone beneath my feet. Still, rotten air around me. The scents of creatures in the distance. None were moving. Perhaps they were all dead.

I couldn’t see much beyond my own body. Everything was covered in endless, cloying dark; the thick black fog that swallowed the world. It set every nerve on end, churned blood into heat and warped all thoughts to mindless frenzy.

It was thinning, though. That felt… wrong. It never thinned, it simply was.

I lifted one hand. It was larger than I… remembered? Did I remember? I had hands. Of course I had hands. But they were… different. Less bloody.

I examined my flesh in the perfect, unutterable silence of a realm gone still, with all the confusion and care of a man waking from several centuries of sleep.

Longer, perhaps?

The hand was coated in bits of countless other creatures. My gaze trailed down. No inch of my body had been spared the gore. Some was clearly fresh, some long-dried, some so black and crusted that it felt akin to a hardened carapace.

Had I always been covered in blood? I certainly smelled like it, but…

No. Not forever. There was a before.

Things flickered in the back of my mind. Things that had names, things that I knew, but couldn’t place. They came as words and concepts without images, because all images were warped into the realm-consuming black.

Family. Home.

I stilled. Breathed. Searched for fragments from before.

Family. Home. Mine. Then… buzzing. A chill on the wind and a great shadow, something that lurched and wailed and buzzed into teeth and bone, rattled through the skull as it fell upon-

Oh. No. Gone. Gone. Don’t think of those. That way lies madness.

I shook my head. The motion was slow, as if done underwater. Unfamiliar, though I’d surely done it before.

The faintest, palest gleam of light flickered above.

I braced for the rush of air, the roar of an oncoming assailant, the hot tearing of my flesh. It would hurt, it always did, but it would bring them in reach and there was no escaping me then. Next would come the snap of bones, the iron scent of blood and the rip as I ended it.

Nothing came.

Silver winked high above, just at the edge of my vision. Above, before, had been… something. Sky. Stars. A moon. I couldn’t picture them. They’d been swallowed by the fog.

I didn’t look up. I wouldn’t fall for it.

Celestial bodies were gone. They were before. There was nothing good or beautiful left, not above, not anywhere, and I wouldn’t be so foolish as to raise my eyes, longing for light and met instead with the bite of fangs.

Still, nothing came. I waited in the quiet, frozen stillness of a realm holding its breath.

And still, that little silver gleam. Taunting me. Daring me.

It took a very long time, but I finally looked up. Teeth bared, seeing red, ready to kill.

And I remembered what a star looked like.
