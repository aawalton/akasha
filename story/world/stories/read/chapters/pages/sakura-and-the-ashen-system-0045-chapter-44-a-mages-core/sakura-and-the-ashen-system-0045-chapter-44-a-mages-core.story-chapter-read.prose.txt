The scavengers moved fast, bunching together into uneven dark patches. From a distance, they looked like huge sick cats with all their fur stripped off. They had large bald gray bodies, thin legs, sharp shoulders, ragged ears, and eyes that were far too big and black. They ran along the canyon wall almost silently, with a catlike ease.

Sakura understood right away that there were too many of them.

“Gilbert,” she called quietly but sharply, already backing toward the camp.

The alarm went up faster than she could repeat it out loud. The mages were already prepared for a fight. They didn’t look like professional soldiers, but they were ready to defend what was theirs, and their own people, to the last.

The scavengers came pouring down. They spilled over the canyon ledges in small, fast groups, knocking loose pebbles and grit, then cleared the last few feet in one jerky leap. They moved exactly like scavengers, in that same twitchy way, like creatures used not to hunting fairly, but to waiting for the weak and the dead.

“Don’t let them get behind us!” one of the scouts barked.

Sakura didn’t even have time to tell which one it was. Everything happened too fast. The first creature had already slammed into the outermost tent, hooked its claws into the fabric, and yanked it sideways along with the support pole. A mage with a staff struck with a short flash of yellow light, and the thing was thrown back against the rocks. It immediately scrambled back up as if it hadn’t even understood it was hurt.

Gilbert was beside Sakura almost at once.

“Stay closer to the fire pits,” he said shortly. “And watch above. They’re coming from there.”

Sakura nodded and immediately pulled out a scroll. This time, she didn’t even have to think long about which one to use. She wasn’t dealing with obsidian creatures she could devour or tame. Not with slow laboratory slimes. This time it was fast, numerous vermin. The most important thing here was to contain them and keep them from getting into the very center of the camp.

She opened the scroll almost automatically.

[Dagger Swarm]

The daggers shot forward in a dense flock and pierced two scavengers at once, the ones that had already jumped off a ledge and were racing toward a stack of crates. One of them shrieked so sharply that the sound sliced through her ears for a second, then dropped at once. The second one still twitched, trying to crawl somewhere, and then went still.

Sakura let out a sharp breath and turned around.

The camp was fighting off the attack without any complicated tactics. For the most part, the mages were improvising, trying not to let the scavengers get too close.

One of the mages thrust both hands forward, and a streak of bluish cold spread across the ground. Two scavengers slipped, hit the rocks with their bony sides, and in that moment one of the scouts finished them off with a short axe. A little farther away, a woman with a hard face and singed brows splashed liquid fire from a flask onto a leaping creature, and it immediately started spinning, screeching and clawing at itself. Sakura saw mages who, like her, worked with scrolls. There were others using runes. And some were fighting back with spears, because they seemed to have less mana than anger.

Sakura caught herself realizing that she no longer lost herself in this kind of chaos the way she used to. Unexpectedly, she understood that she had already gotten used to things like this happening in her life.

A scavenger darted between two tents and lunged at the girl Sakura had seen by the fire earlier that day. The girl didn’t even manage to raise her hand properly. Sakura moved before she thought and released her new skill.

[Suffocating Petals]

A dull scarlet glow flared at her palms, and a swarm of petals wrapped around the creature mid-leap. On a living, jerking body, the skill looked even more disturbing than it had on obsidian creatures. The scavenger shrieked, arched, and thrashed, trying to tear free from the tightening cocoon, but the petals only clung tighter to its dry hide, slipping between its ribs and winding around its neck and chest. The swarm compressed harder and harder, as if invisible fingers were tightening a noose. The creature clawed at the air for a few more seconds, then went limp with a soft wet crunch.

The girl by the fire stared at Sakura with wide eyes.

Sakura didn’t even have time to say anything to her, because a new shout rose from the opposite side of the camp.

That was where things got worse.

The scavengers themselves suddenly scattered aside, as if even they had been forced to give way. A long black body slid out of a dark crack between the rocks.

At first Sakura didn’t understand what she was seeing. Then something inside her tightened unpleasantly.

A snake.

No, not just a snake. An obsidian creature shaped like one. Long, flexible, as if carved out of polished black glass, with a segmented body and a narrow head. The same crimson glow ran down the ridge of its back, the glow that always marked these things with that heavy, alien magic. It moved quietly and confidently, and in its presence the scavengers looked even more like petty mongrels that bite, but don’t pose any real threat.

“Oh, damn it,” one of the scouts breathed.

Sakura understood right away. Most likely, this was the same thing she had already seen before everything started, when something black had crawled across the canyon wall. And most likely, this creature had driven the scavengers down here into the camp.

The snake struck without warning.

One of the mages barely managed to raise a shield, but the obsidian body slammed into it from the side with enough force to throw the man aside. The crimson crack along the snake’s body flashed brighter, and Sakura clearly felt that if this thing broke into the center of the camp, everything would go bad very quickly.

Gilbert had already raised a hand, but Sakura beat him to it, because she was closer and had already seen the creature’s path.

[Devourer]

The skill hit the target with sudden fury. The snake jerked in the middle of its lunge, as if something invisible had yanked it back. The crimson light inside its body flared bright enough to outline every curve of its black frame for a moment. Sakura felt that familiar horrible instant when the alien obsidian force broke apart and came at her, and she still didn’t let go.

The pain came immediately. It was already familiar, but still deep and hot. Sakura barely suppressed the urge to cry out. The obsidian snake cracked all along its length at once. The black body split into a web of crimson fractures, the light burst outward, and then the whole creature began to crumble into heavy dark dust and ashy scraps that tore off the rocks and streamed toward Sakura.

The flow hit her arm.

Sakura clenched her teeth, feeling the foreign power sink under her skin together with pain, heat, and that brief, dangerous sensation of almost sweet strength. She had devoured the snake.

The camp froze. The scavengers, who had just been throwing themselves at tents and people, seemed not to understand what had happened. The mages understood very well.

Far too well.

Sakura felt that too. Even without looking, just from the way the silence around her changed. Where a second ago there had only been battle, now there was wariness. Fear.

“So it really is her...” one of the mages said quietly.

“The Obsidian Hierophant,” another said just as quietly.

Sakura was breathing hard, staring at the place where the snake had just been. Her arm throbbed with fresh pain, and inside her, as always after [Devourer], that dark, dangerous feeling of strength rose for a brief moment. But now it drowned immediately in something else.

The fact that she had just saved these people, and they were afraid of her.

The fight, meanwhile, was dying down. Without the snake and the boldest scavengers, the rest of the creatures lost their nerve and started to fall back. The mages drove them away from the camp. Soon it was over. Only the scavengers’ blood on the rocks and the signs of destruction remained to show that a battle had just happened here.

The camp was still breathing hard, checking the wounded, but it was already clear that no new attack was coming. The scavengers were gone.

And only then did it become clear what this victory had cost them.

First Sakura noticed one. A young mage by the collapsed tent, almost just a boy, the one she had already seen in passing. His throat had been torn open, and his face still held a stunned look, as if he had never fully understood what had happened to him.

Then she saw the second. Another thin, weak-looking boy, a little older. He lay by the rocks with one hand pressed to his stomach. A dark stain was spreading fast across the cloth, and his eyes were already glazing over.

“No...” the same girl by the fire whispered.

It was as if all the mages sagged at once. The fighting spirit was gone from them. They were just people again, people who had lost two of their own. And the worst part was how routine it looked, as if they lived through other people’s deaths every day.

Marven came back into Sakura’s view. During the fight, he had disappeared into the chaos. Now she saw him again. He took in the wreckage, the dead scavengers, the ruined tents, and then stood looking at the two bodies of his people for a long moment.

His face had grown even harder than before.

“Take them,” he said at last, his voice low. “We’ll hold the farewell before dawn.”

No one argued. Sakura stepped back and stayed out of the way. Right now, anything she said would sound wrong. Even sympathy. Especially sympathy. She could feel the stares on her too clearly, and there was nothing good in them. As if these people were trying to tell her in silence, You brought this here. And maybe they were right. Maybe that snake had come down into the canyon for her, just as other obsidian creatures had done before.

One of the mages, passing by her, paused involuntarily and looked at her hand. Sakura clenched her fingers tighter.

She had helped.

But that changed very little.

After some time, the farewell ritual began. The bodies were wrapped in cloth, and two bowls were set beside them, filled with oil, dried herbs, and something bitter-smelling. The mages spoke quietly. One of the older mages passed a hand over the bodies, and pale glowing signs appeared on the cloth, as if memory itself were trying to hold on to the person for just a moment longer.

Sakura did not go closer. Gilbert didn’t either.

They sat by the farther rocks, where no one drove them away, but no one invited them to be part of the ceremony either. The hours dragged by slowly. The camp was repaired. Torn fabric was sewn up, blood was washed away, the fires were rebuilt, and the tents were put back up.

The night deepened and settled, as if none of that sudden attack had happened at all.

A few hours later, when the fires were burning lower and most of the camp had fallen quiet, Gilbert finally came over to Sakura. At first he simply stood there, looking somewhere in the direction of the farewell fire, and then at last he said,

“Marven agreed.”

Sakura looked up at him sharply.

“To what exactly?”

Gilbert met her eyes directly.

“He’ll give us the core of one of the dead.”

For a few seconds Sakura just froze, trying to fit that into her head. Marven had really agreed to desecrate the body of one of his own, to take the core from one of the dead. Right after they had just been sent off with a magical rite.

The thought made something inside her clench unpleasantly, and at the same time she understood too well what it meant.

They had a chance. A heavy one. A revolting one. One bought at a terrible price paid by someone else. But still a chance.

Sakura slowly exhaled.

And then all she could do was ask quietly,

“And what will it cost us in return?”
