I wait, muscles tense, staring through the forest with my companions for the signal that the beasts are almost upon us.

Is everyone in position? I ask through the Alliance Bond. The other lords confirm quickly. They're well hidden enough that I can't detect any trace of their presence, but that's the benefit of the Bond.

Is everyone prepared? I ask Maxen next.

Yes, my lord, he answers. All forces are in place.

I admire how calm and even his mental tone is – how he doesn’t let slip even a hint of annoyance that I’ve asked him the same question three times already. I know I'm fretting, and I do my best to settle myself before my nerves start bleeding into those around me. But it’s just surprisingly difficult to wait here, knowing that a beast wave is coming towards us with every moment. I can even get an exact idea of where they are thanks to Sirocco. It doesn’t help much – I’m just too restless, wanting to do something.

But this is the best approach – we all agree on that. Given how well the strategy Elarion and Josiah used against the last wave worked, we’ve decided to repeat it. Hopefully the beasts won’t be expecting it. Then again, even if they do, that doesn’t mean they can necessarily do much about it – the crowd-control Elarion and Josiah are able to offer combined with the amount of people we have for the offence should overrun the beasts in short order.

Still, no plan survives contact with the enemy and I don’t know what kind of trump cards the Tier threes in particular might be able to pull out. I know there will almost certainly be casualties, either in this battle or the ones to come, but the more we can minimise them, the better.

That’s why we have healers with us, though the settlement can hardly spare them, I remind myself. And why we’ve brought the last of our healing potions from Moriax.

Feeling guilty for stealing away both, I worked with the healers in the settlement until the last minute, joining the army only moments before we left – the fewer parasite-controlled people left behind, the better.

At least the forest seems to have calmed down a bit since the manastorm ended. We’ve only been attacked three times. And with the numbers we have, dealing with the small packs of mutated beasts was easy enough. We haven’t had another encounter with carnivorous plantlife, thank goodness.

They’re in range. Sirocco’s voice breaks through my thoughts. At the same time, Valence reaches out through the Alliance Bond.

They’re here.

I feel a sudden sense of relief as the wait comes to an end, even as my muscles tense in preparation to act. It’s a little premature – the beasts might almost be here, but I won’t be immediately involved.

A ripple of tension moves through everyone around me as the first beasts come into view.

We hold, the tension only heightening as the beasts crest the edge of the hill and start walking down into the natural bowl of loam and trees. More move through the forest. More still, until practically the full army is revealed and the frontrunners are beginning to head up and out of the bowl again.

“Now!”

Josiah's call comes from his side of the battlefield. A torrent of rain begins to fall from the clouds he's been quietly gathering above, even as roots spring from the forest floor and begin entangling the beasts' legs.

Ice spreads wherever the water touches, and the water itself is directed to cover as many beasts as possible. The Earth Mages are in fine form, subtly shifting the ground beneath the beasts' feet and pulling those on the edges back into the dip where the others are already struggling. I activate my own trap of stored Earth mana and spears erupt in and around the dip, killing several beasts outright and injuring many more.

I notice a boar-like creature with rage in its eyes as the earth gives way under it and it slides down the slope to impale itself onto one of my spikes, taking several of its companions with it. It's chaos among the beasts, and yet not one of our people is caught in the crossfire.

The confusion doesn't last long. The Tier twos and Tier threes in particular begin fighting back quickly. Several of the beasts appear to have some plant magic of their own – the roots around the edge of the dell start writhing, darting towards our forces as sharp spears. A battle for control breaks out between them and our Plant Mages – the mages’ attention is now split as they still need to maintain control over the plants in the dell.

Still, none of the beasts seems capable of dealing with ice even if some have control over water, so Elarion's work goes unchallenged.

I'm tense, wanting to step in and do more, but it's not my turn yet.

“Front-line, advance! Archers, fire at will!” Maxen’s voice rings loud and clear and receives a response from all around the dell. Fighters bearing spears and halbards and pikes step forward, attacking the beasts around the edges who are the least hampered by the roots and ice. Meanwhile, archers fire arrows at the centre, causing great confusion there and disrupting many of the beasts who were previously fighting the bindings or who had been beginning to use wind or force attacks to strike at us.

At the same time, my own companions leap forwards. The Tier twos mostly attack the front lines with the other melee fighters, though Ninja sends Storm’s shadow in to cause havoc among the beasts in the middle, swapping with it every time the beasts think it’s harmless to any above a Tier one. Her path is marked by blood and dying beasts, though she steers clear of the Tier threes.

Bastet, on the other hand, goes head to head with the scorpion-like Tier three, using her air-magic to redirect its poison shots from its tail into its own allies – the way the venom corrodes the beasts it hits, I’m very grateful for her efforts. Aware of the ice creeping around the Tier three, she chooses to avoid using her flame abilities except to help her fly.

Meanwhile, Artio takes on the other Tier three, using his incredibly upgraded plant magic to trap the boar-like beast and prevent it from charging and using its tusks to rip our allies to pieces. Its stomps still manage to shake the ground, but that creates more problems for its own allies than the rest of us, given that the strength of the quakes is stronger closer to their epicentre.

Though Sirocco remains sailing above, not getting involved much, Ivor and Noir do their best to aid from above, dropping large rocks on any beast that looks like it is about to charge. Ivor also uses his tattoo to shoot ice shards, and distracts beasts at ideal moments.

Even the okapi-like and lion-like Tier threes I Bonded in the last battle get involved, though I notice them linger at the edges of the conflict, only attacking when they’re sure they can take a beast down without putting themselves at risk.

Finally, the Tier threes are pinned down – Bastet has successfully distracted her opponent enough for Elarion to immobilise it with vines of ice, and Artio has wrapped the boar up so tightly that it can barely snort.

Now, it’s my turn.

I take to the air on my wings, rising high enough above the battle to avoid being an easy target, and call for Noir to keep watch over me while I enter the Battle of Wills.

The first goes relatively smoothly – the pig-like Tier three puts up solid resistance but makes little headway against me while so thoroughly tied up and defenceless, thanks to my allies' work. The second is harder. When I force the Bond into place, I feel significant strain – I haven’t been able to convince them that they should accept my Bond, and I am too conscious of time being short to elongate the Battle.

Before leaving the settlement, I released as many Bonds as I could, even releasing those like Alyna and Rory who have proven themselves trustworthy – Rory seemed relieved while Alyna seemed strangely disappointed.

The only human Bonds I currently hold are with Maxen, Loran, and the two saboteurs – the last two because I don’t trust them; the first two, in order to keep the lines of communication open. Yet even with so few pure Dominate Bonds in place, I still feel the strain of these two attempting to break free.

Should I release him? Artio asks when I emerge, probably feeling the additions to the Bond network.

Not yet, I reply quickly, also sending a message to similar effect to Elarion. Let me remove the parasites first.

Removing the parasites doesn’t mean that the beasts will necessarily stop fighting the chains holding them, but at least I won’t have to worry about a parasite-controlled beast getting away, only to attack again later.

I briefly check the wider battle – the others have it well in hand – and then turn my focus to removing the parasites from both Tier threes, hoping that fulfilling my promise will make them more willing. I don’t take the time to heal the network of scarring all over their bodies – if they decide to stay with us I will, but otherwise it’s too much time and mana for beasts who might be our enemies later.

As it turns out, the pig-like Tier three is somewhat grateful. The scorpion-like Tier three, however, refuses to even acknowledge that the parasite existed, stubbornly insisting I must be mistaken. No amount of convincing makes any difference. In the end, I give up.

The two Tier threes I Bonded before the battle have proved at least somewhat useful, but I have no desire to force beings to fight alongside us against their will. The other two traded healing for service so I don’t feel guilty about them.

After a few fruitless minutes trying to reason with the scorpion, I warn it plainly not to attack us unless it wishes to die, and then release it. It disappears into the undergrowth almost immediately. The boar, evidently more inclined to believe me than the scorpion was, has no apparent desire for revenge but asks to be released as well, choosing not to accept my offer of later healing. I give it the same warning and release it. It follows the scorpion's example and vanishes into the trees.

With the battle all but over, we quickly shift into clean up.

The butchers we brought move through the battlefield, processing carcasses. Fortunately, it turns out that Butchers tend to have a Skill that eliminates parasites in the meat they process and we proved on the carcasses from the beasts attacking the settlement that the Skill works on them. Unfortunately, it requires its subject to be dead so it’s not something that can be repurposed to help people infected with the parasites, but at least it guarantees safe meat for the settlement.

Meanwhile, the healers set up a field station, with each unit managing their own wounded and bringing the most serious cases forward, using healing potions to deal with the lesser injuries. The Plant and Earth Mages work to restore the dell to its original state, blood and gore disappearing as though it never was.

I catch a whiff of something truly awful and glance over to see a Classer levelling up, the black, viscous substance oozing from her every pore. Their commanding officer is already berating her for not removing her gear first. Her stammered apologies and explanation that she didn't know what to expect suggests this is probably one of those given a Class stone just before we set out. Still, it's a good sign. The stronger each individual becomes, the stronger we all are.

This Classer isn't the only one to have gained enough experience, and soon a section of the battlefield has been set aside for people to undress, level up, and be doused by Water Mages before dressing again.

I check my own status screen and am pleased to find I'm on the cusp of level thirty-five. Before today is over, I suspect I'll have hit a hundred percent – my second level since arriving on the Lost Continent and my fifth since leaving Moriax.

Elarion steps up beside me as I debate how I can best help, Layton and Josiah following.

"Did that seem a little too easy to you?" Layton asks bluntly, slight unease in his voice.

"Easy?" Josiah gives him an incredulous look. "Speak for yourself. Gathering that volume of rain and directing it precisely where we needed it was not easy."

Layton’s lips quirk and I just know he’s about to make a mocking comment that, if they were not cousins, might easily be grounds for a duel, but even as cousins will set off some bickering.

"I know what you mean," I tell Layton, to head that off. I glance at Josiah. "The individual actions may have been demanding, but overall it went very smoothly and almost exactly to plan."

"Exactly," Layton agrees. "It seems odd."

Elarion shrugs elegantly.

"I don't see why it should. We planned carefully, and we were clearly stronger than the beast army in both numbers and ability. If things had gone wrong, that would have been the anomaly."

"Elarion's right," I agree after a moment of thought. "Just because we were prepared doesn’t mean this was a trap. But we mustn't let it lower our guard. This was a wave of beasts separated from the main force, and they were clearly not expecting to face our full strength all at once."

"Future engagements are likely to be considerably harder," Valence adds, joining the group last. "Especially since they know we're coming."

I look at him.

"What makes you say that?"

"I’ve just been speaking to my scouts. They reported seeing several beasts at the back of the army slip quietly free of the bindings and choose escape over engagement."

"And your scouts didn't try to shoot them down?" Elarion says sharply.

Valence gives him an indignant look.

"Of course they did. But if they'd been successful, I wouldn't be reporting this."

Elarion draws himself up slightly, clearly preparing to make some sort of cutting remark. I interrupt.

"There's no point assigning blame. It was always likely the Tier four would get word – either through a beast slipping through our net or through some form of communication via the parasites." I reach out to Sirocco, who is still soaring above. Can you go and observe what the Tier four is doing? How it's reacting?

Of course, she agrees.

I refocus on the group.

"Sirocco is going to observe the rest of the beasts. I’ll let you know when she reports back. For now, the best we can do is finish up here as quickly as possible and move on." I look around. "I'm going to go and help the healers – perhaps consider what you can do to help speed our recovery and preparation. The less time the Tier four has to prepare for our arrival, the better."
