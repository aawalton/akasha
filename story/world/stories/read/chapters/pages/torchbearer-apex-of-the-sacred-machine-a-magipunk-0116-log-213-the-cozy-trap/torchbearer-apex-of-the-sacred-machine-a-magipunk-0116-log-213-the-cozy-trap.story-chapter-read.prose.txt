It definitely was past midnight when I finally made my way back home. I wasn’t in a hurry, even though I had to pull Arx a little tighter against the nighttime cold. It felt oddly like coming home after a night out with your friends, when the world is quiet, your heart content, and your muscles thrumming with restful desire.

My feet brushed through the ankle-high grass and I thought back of all the times I’d walked the streets of Berlin like this, heels resting in my purse and comfortable sneakers protecting my feet. As I entered the circle of light emanating from the house, flashes of my last life joined the first. Laughing with Lorelye as we ran from a wedding we’d crashed, dressing up Zurne so he could attend a ball with us, that one time Stax had managed to get Underbrook into a suit, plastering on a smile because Jirrie had chosen to appear at the Gala in her goddamn overalls and I was pretty sure she did it to get a rise out of me…

Good times.

I stepped up onto the porch and opened the front door to enter the empty house.

Well, almost empty.

A cyan scarf wrapped itself around my ankle the second I stepped over the threshold, pulling me toward the kitchen, where Chris was already waiting in front of their bowl. The cat stared at me with accusations of murder by starvation in their eyes, their scarf pulled taut as it tried yanking me into the room.

“Chill out, Chris!” I wanted it to sound angry, but I was too tired and too happy to see them.

“Beep!” Betrayal most foul!

“Hi fridge, what’s for dinner?”

Feast. (-_- )ノ

Its door cracked open languidly. There was a flash of bright blue broken time, and I held a bowl of sliced fruit and nuts in one hand, and a bag of nachos in the other. Chris got canned cat food instead of their usual assorted meats. Then the fridge’s compressor started buzzing in a slow, pulsing rhythm that sounded disturbingly like a snore.

“Snacks it is,” I yawned—not even mad about it—and walked over to the couch.

I stopped by the coat hanger to awkwardly take off Arx (surprisingly hard to do while bone tired and holding a bunch of midnight treats) and put my dirty boots next to the door. Then, finally, I fell into the soft cushions with a content sigh.

Even after subtracting the 76 LB I spent for surprisingly delicious snacks, today had definitely been worth it.

It had never shown up, and so I couldn’t close Nexus, but it didn’t matter much in the end. I’d checked the program’s readout, and the Feral didn’t sound too threatening, just a bunch of letters and numbers that I’d come to associate with the weaker variants. Perhaps it was just a lone grazer that somehow managed to escape the other Ferals. If so, good for it!

I still wished I could have killed it, closed off the Domain, and allowed the murder meadow to recuperate. I popped a nacho in my mouth and tried to sort my tired thoughts as it crunched between my teeth. Maybe I could upgrade Nexus next, gamble on getting an upgrade that allowed me to reject connections. It wouldn’t help with my overarching goal of escaping the laptop, though. Less connections meant less Logic, which was the opposite of what I needed.

Chris hopped onto my stomach with lazy grace, and I nearly choked on a slice of apple I’d been chewing on. While I was struggling for breath, the cat pulled the bowl closer with a gentle paw, sniffed the fruit, then deemed it a food unworthy of their refined palate and curled up where it lay.

With my coughing fit finally subsiding, I started petting the little would-be-assassin. “Miss me?”

“…beep,” they admitted after a brief moment of indignant hesitation.

“I mean, I shouldn’t ask. After all, how could you not?”

Their response was to pointedly poke their claws through my shirt, and I laughed, which sent me into another series of coughs that made the cat bounce on my stomach.

I could tell they didn’t appreciate the jostling, partially because of the stink eye, but mostly because of the claws.

“Sorry, sorry,” I said, evening out my breathing. “I’ve been meaning to ask, though: could you start working on a project that would allow us to alter the real world with Logic…?”

I trailed off because Chris was just staring at me with one feline eyebrow raised. Then they snorted an actual giggle.

“So I take that as a no?”

Finally, Chris nodded, their feline grin telling me plain and clear that they thought the mere idea was hilarious.

“Well then how the fuck are we supposed to get out of here?”

They shrugged as if it wasn’t their problem, beeped as if retelling themselves a good joke, and chuckled again.

Then, their giggles faded into a deep sigh and they went back to napping.

“Very charismatic and helpful, Chris,” I deadpanned.

“Beep!” They snuggled closer and purred.
