An indeterminate amount of time later I woke in the darkness, confused. It was deathly quiet. Something was very wrong. Where was the ever present hum of motor vehicles zipping past outside my window? The sound of my neighbours arguing through the wall? The snoring lump of problem gambler in bed next to me? I felt around with my hand and it slapped against something cold and solid.

I opened my eyes and then tried to open them again. Stone walls. I frowned at them. Where was my familiar bedroom? This was some nightmare. I would figure it out in the morning. With a groan, I turned, arms reaching for a duvet that was not there. My elbow slammed into rock. What the fuck was I lying on?

I sat up with a jolt, and everything came flooding back.

“Oh yeah…”

No wonder I was cold, I had passed out on dirty stone floor. My stomach gurgled, and I clutched it. It felt like it was trying to digest itself. “Alright, alright.”

I had five points of mana again, which meant I must have been asleep for… at least three or four hours. If mana usage was a muscle, did it require food to sustain it? I clutched my stomach again as it growled once more. “Okay, okay, not the time to wonder, I get it.” Perhaps some food will give me the energy to start a fire? Yes, good plan Rachel. Food, heat, more sleep.

Looking around, I found the pitiful pile of leaves I had [Foraged] earlier. Wild garlic, chickweed, yarrow and some hickory. All technically edible, even the hickory flowers.

The leaves did not become any more appetising the longer I looked at them. It was one thing to go foraging in the woods on a sunny Sunday afternoon, returning home to make wild garlic pesto with oodles of parmesan and olive oil … to eat it with toast or pasta… and then there was this.

My stomach rumbled again. Fine. Food was food. Salad - it was just salad.

I stuffed soggy leaves in my mouth and chewed, stopping to spit out a slug. Just a very fresh salad. Excellent. Good. Wonderful. I choked it down. Of course, it probably had very little nutritional value and wouldn’t keep me going for long. I knew it was traditional for new adventurers to exist off berries and mushrooms, but realistically, that's a starvation diet.

I chewed doggedly, spending the next few minutes fantasising about buttery, hot, delicious potatoes, but I think it made the process worse. My stomach rumbled some more, and I eyed the remaining pheasant eggs. Here was protein but I had no way to cook them. I was sooooo hungry. Fine. FINE.

Raw eggs are back on the menu, boys.

I had a clear recollection of Ian Stole-All-My-Money Fuck Face cracking raw eggs into some kind of after gym concoction, in between his normal activities of lying and breaking my heart. He was always fine afterward, more’s the pity. I slurped down the contents of two eggs.

They went down my throat in a cold, slithery gulp. If I died, I died. Salmonella seemed a ridiculous thing to worry about when I was more likely to be gored to death by an insane magical beast.

One need met, the cold started eating into my bones, so I went hunting for firewood, creeping back out into the forest through the sticky mess of brambles to gather as much wood as I could. Wood was plentiful, though damp. I gathered a great armful, slowly, carefully hauled it back and barricaded the stout door once more, laying them out under the roof to dry further.

The light in the forest outside was dark grey now, instead of pitch black. Birds were singing in the trees. Dawn must be coming. It had been a long night.

There was no fireplace, so I made a temporary hearth out of loose bits of masonry, positioned under the roof, but near the broken part so the smoke will be able to escape, and piled it high with the driest bits of wood. I might not have had any matches, or fire magic, but I had watched a few survival videos on YouTube. Sticks, friction… hopes and prayers to whatever godforsaken blighted deities that watched over this cursed place.

“Sorry,” I murmured as I got to work. Just in case any gods were listening. If they were they didn’t comment.

Hunched over a piece of wood, I twizzled a stick back and forth with manic determination, sweat dripping from my brow. By the time I had coaxed a thin wisp of smoke upwards my excitement nearly killed the baby flame, but I controlled myself, spinning the stick faster, faster, until there was the barest hint of smoulder. With agonising care, I laid the piece down on a bed of tinder, cupping my hands around it, blowing gently. “Come on, come on-”

A tiny flame leapt, and just like that I had fire.

Feeling particularly primal, I fed it with twigs and leaves, and then larger pieces until I was sure it wouldn’t go out. I sat back on my heels, admiring my handywork. The ruined room was flooded with firelight and warmth, hidden from the outside forest by the walls. The smoke found its way up through the ruin of the roof easily enough, escaping out into the steely light of the coming dawn.

[Would you like to claim Ôstarmânoth as your base?]

“Yes,” I said, immediately, rubbing my eyes. “What? Actually, wait, what does that mean?”

[Please allocate your base bonus: the act of claiming a base gives you a choice of temporarily boosting either your strength, dexterity, endurance or mana by three points, applied while you are at home or within 50 feet of your base. This bonus can be reallocated at dawn, for the same amount or to different stats. The bonus levels with your base.Also, once a day you may summon a basic fire.]

I was too sleepy for this, but it sounded all right. Great even! Making a fire had been harder work than I expected. I thought about the base bonus stats. Sluggishly. So…if I changed my mind I could reallocate the points the next day?

[Correct.]

“Endurance,” I said, and promptly fell asleep again. It would have been nice to watch the first sunrise but [Witches] needed their sleep.

I awoke sometime later, when the fire had died down. Looking up, I could make out the outline of a gargoyle perched on the roof above, staring stoically out at the trees, and outlined by the rising sun. After adding some more wood to the fire, I fell asleep once more, comforted in the knowledge that I was not alone.

Next time I awoke it was proper daytime.

Sunlight filtered through bright green leaves, flooding through the hole in the roof. I sat up with a groan and poked at the ashes of the fire. Then I remembered I could start a fire.

“Fire please,” I said and snapped my fingers.

The embers of the fire flared back to life, and I rushed to feed it wood. I had never felt more magical! Before I could enjoy the moment too much, my stomach announced that I was hungry again. Chomping leaves, I distracted myself from the taste by checking my stats. The mana regeneration confirmed I had been asleep for a good amount of time:

Rachel Jones

Class: Witch lvl 3

Skills:

Skills:

Traits:

Status:

Strength: 5

Enough mana to work with, excellent. Time to get to it!

“How do I level my base?” I asked the system, remembering the achievement I had received in the night. The system was not forthcoming.

“Okay, okay,” I said. “I’ll figure it out myself. Morning!” I shouted up to the gargoyle on the roof. He did not move but that was okay. I figured that was his thing. He was doing a grand job of being a statue, and he had found me this nice place.

My muscles were so stiff I felt like I, too, was carved out of stone. Standing, I stretched, feeling like I had slept on a bed of lego. Glancing down at my limbs - the state of me was much more distressing in the light. I needed a wash. Again.

And not just cause of the smell - I needed to clean out my wounds properly. Things were looking a bit crusty, a bit weepy. Despite washing in the stream, I had somehow managed to pick up fresh dirt, and some of my bruises had blossomed into fairly spectacular shades of blue and purple.

Various wounds twinged and started to ooze a bit as I moved. Where to start? I couldn’t make soap out of thin air.

I paused. “Can I?”

[No]

Ug. Boo. My previous good mood dampened a little as I looked around at the dust and debris of the ruin I had chosen to be my base. Suddenly, all the things I had taken for granted in my old life felt like intense luxuries. The dingy little flat I had shared with Ian Fuck-Face may have been badly insulated, with a tiny kitchen and worn, threadbare carpets, but it had had electricity. A roof! And a bathtub. And wonderful hot, running hot water. What would I give for a hot bath right now?

I squeezed my hand into a fist so hard my nails threatened to draw blood. Oh that was weird, my hands looked normal again. I examined them in surprise.

“What’s that about?” The system did not respond. Ah well, it was probably for the best. They had been kinda cool but they would have stood out, assuming everyone here had normal hands and skin. I sighed, rocking back on my heels. Making soap, even magical soap, in a ruin in a random forest, without a scale or a functioning kitchen, or a fucking stick blender, without safety gear – this was going to be challenging. I squished down the worries and forced myself into action, my body protesting.

The other Archetypes and goodness knew who else were out there right now, probably punching squirrels and starting wars and inventing coffee and getting stronger. I would be strong too, but I would do it my way. With soap. And hopefully a minimum of near-death experiences.

To make soap I needed a solid base. A clean base. Hot baths would come later.

I thoroughly [inspected] the ruin. While it was merrier in sunlight, what remained inside was junk - rotting rags and piles of old leaves and rubble. Part of me was hoping I would find some nice treasures hidden away, but there was very little that was usable - a few bits of broken furniture, nothing whole enough to be used except as firewood, brambles, sticks – and a bucket! A plain old, solitary bucket. A bit rusty.

I studied it with great suspicion.

“Hello?” I whacked it hard with a stick.

It went boing.

[Cast iron pail, quality: poor]

I watched it carefully for a few minutes more. It did nothing suspicious, which to be fair was exactly what a Sentient Bucket Archetype would do if it knew it was being watched. On the other hand… if it was a [Sentient Bucket] presumably [Inspect] would come up with something else? Also, it could have murdered me in my sleep if it had wanted to? Maybe? I wasn’t sure what a [Sentient Bucket] was capable of.

I decided this way lay madness, and went to fill it with water. The stream was close by, but I had to be extremely careful burrowing backwards and forwards through the [Sleepy Briars]. One wrong move and suddenly I was drowsy. Two wrong moves and I was taking a nap. The middle of the thicket was not a safe place for napping.

Finally I made it back, rested but peeved, and placed the full bucket it over the fire to boil.

“I will, however, be keeping a close eye on you,” I told it, as the water started to heat up.

While I was waiting, I cleaned. This ruin was going to be my home for the foreseeable future. It may not have been a castle but I kinda liked it. I liked the cute gothic architecture, (more my style than any of the functional boxes I had lived in up till now). I liked how the only sounds I could hear were the swishing of the boughs, and the comings and goings of various forest creatures. It was defensible and hidden away… yes it would do nicely.

For now.
