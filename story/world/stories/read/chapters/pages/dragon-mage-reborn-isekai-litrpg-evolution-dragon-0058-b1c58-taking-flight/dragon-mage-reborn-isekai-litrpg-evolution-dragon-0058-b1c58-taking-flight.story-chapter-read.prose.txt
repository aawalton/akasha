I could hear the fear in Nolan’s voice as he called my name. It drifted farther and farther as I fell into an endless blue abyss. My heart pounded in my chest as I tumbled through the air, my tail whipping wildly behind me. Something stirred within me—a primal instinct buried deep in my draconic blood. The membranes along my back tingled, and I felt muscles I‘d never consciously used before flexing beneath my scales.

Wings. I had wings.

I knew I’d figure out how to fly sometime soon, but this situation forced an early lesson. They unfurled from my sides with a snap, catching the air and sending me into a nauseating spiral. Pain shot through the delicate membranes as they strained against the wind for the first time.

“Freya, spread those wings and fly!” Nolan shouted at me.

I forced myself to focus through the pain and disorientation. The wings were there, they were real, but they weren’t cooperating. Every attempt to stabilize sent me careening in a new direction. I flailed my limbs, trying to find some semblance of control.

‘Think, Freya!’ I scolded myself. ‘You’re a dragon! Flying should be instinctual!’

But instinct wasn’t helping. Each desperate wing flap sent me tumbling in a different direction. My body wasn’t used to wings, yet!

‘Calm down, relax,’ Nolan encouraged me, forcing my racing thoughts to slow. ‘Don’t think about how far you’re falling. Take a moment to clear your head and feel the air. Feel its flow— don’t over exert yourself and wrestle with it.’

I stopped fighting against the wind and instead surrendered to it, letting my body go limp as I continued to fall. The sensation was terrifying but necessary. I needed to understand how the air moved around me before I could move through it.

The wind caressed my wings, teaching me its language. I felt how it curved around my body, how it slipped against the thin membranes of my wings. With a deep breath, I extended my wings fully, feeling them catch the current.

Instead of flapping wildly, I tilted them slightly, angling my body. The response was immediate—my descent slowed, and I began to glide.

‘I’m doing it!’ I thought, though “flying” would be a generous term for my unsteady gliding. Still, it was better than plummeting to my death.

I got better at it. Each adjustment became more intuitive, my body learning what my mind didn’t yet understand. The pain in my wing muscles faded to a dull ache as I circled back toward the floating platforms, making actual flapping movements!

Above me, I could see three tiny figures peering over the edge of the cloud. Nolan was leaning dangerously far out, with Lun gripping the back of his cloak to keep him from tipping over.

When I landed on the platform, Nolan quickly cupped my face. I could hear his heart racing as he gave me a look over and said, “Are you all right?”

‘I’m fine, Nolan!’ I tweeted. ‘Did you see me down there? I was killing it!’

‘Shit, don’t you ever scare me like that again!’

I understood he was terrified for my well being, but I just felt like a kid who just got off her Barbie bike training wheels.

Nolan turned over to Typha pissed as hell, my eyes catching a particular puffy vein along his temple when he barked at her and said, “What the hell is wrong with you? You nearly killed her!”

Typha’s face dropped. “It was an accident! The wind—“

“Save it,” Nolan snarled. “You’ve been nothing but a liability this entire trial.”

‘Nolan, she seriously accidentally bumped into m—’ before I could finish, Nolan snatched her collar in his fist and tilted her along the edge as if he were about to drop her! ‘Nolan, don’t!’

“Imagine if she had died? A life for a life? Except yours is worthless.”

‘Nolan, you’re scaring her!’

Typha’s eyes widened with genuine fear as she teetered on the edge, her body half-suspended over the endless drop. “I swear I didn’t mean it!” she shrieked, clawing at Nolan’s wrist.

‘Stop it! This isn’t helping us get through the trial! It was an accident!’ I shouted at him.

‘It was careless!’ he hissed. ‘She made it clear from the very beginning that she doesn’t give two shits about you!’

“Get your hands off me!” she cried. “Please!” The platform underneath her right foot chipped, a large chunk of it falling into the cloudless bottom. It triggered the water works, Typha bawling as she begged, “I don’t want to die! I’m scared!”

“Puzzle is solved!” Lun cried out. “Let’s move, hurry!”

Nolan’s grip loosened, and he shoved Typha back onto stable ground where she collapsed, sobbing. His face was a storm of emotions, but he stepped back.

‘Look, I get it. We’re all on edge,’ I said, trying to be the voice of reason. ‘But we have to work together or none of us will make it.’

The storm in Nolan needed to taper off before we could proceed. Fortunately, it didn’t take long for him to defuse. When the leader of a party falls, the rest of the party would soon follow, and he knew that. So he collected himself quickly, and focused on the path ahead.

Thanks to Lun, we made it across to the castle in the sky. It sat on a gigantic pillowy cloud, its texture airy and soft. The castle towered over us, its ivory spires piercing the sky like needles. Ornate windows glittered in the sunlight, but both the golden gates and its golden doors were closed.

Lun informed us that his codex revealed the removal of those prior restrictions, which was great, because I was sure the castle had a few floors of baddies for us to clear before we could move forward to our next trial. Everyone was ready to fight, except Typha.

Her usual brash demeanor had completely crumbled. She made it clear that she didn’t have a bone of dungeon experience in her. So basically she was like her brother, but worse. She was in fact a liability, but we couldn’t leave her behind—I’d seen what Zev was capable of….

I felt bad for the roughness Nolan had shown her, though it was clear from the way he kept glancing at me that the fear of losing me had overridden his usual composure. He'd been ready to throw her off that platform, and the realization shook me. I knew I was a big deal, and that Typha was bratty and snobbish, but at the end of the day, she was still a victim in all of this, much like her brother. Much like all of us.

I had to ask him, just to be sure. Through our private link, I needed to know if Typha was safe. Nolan mentioned he wanted to scare her shitless, and that he didn’t have the heart to really toss her off. Whether an accident or not—although, he did hesitate when I asked him even if it wasn’t an accident. I looked at him funny, that face on the gunslinger not giving me the answers I was looking for!

It was okay—as long as I knew he wasn’t heartless. And his strategy did work; the girl did in fact mellow out.

But still….

‘Hey, Nolan, I don’t think Typha is going to make it,’ I said inwardly. ‘She seems pretty shaken up.’

‘Not my problem,’ he fired back casually.

‘Well, you did have her thinking you were going to toss her off into an abyss.’

‘She needed to learn a tough lesson. I’m not the type to sugarcoat shit. Besides, I gave her plenty of time to straighten her ass out.’

‘It was a pretty harsh lesson, though.’

‘Freya, you’re too damn soft.’

‘One of us has to be! It’s how we balance each other out.’

‘What was the point of this conversation again?’

‘Look, I know she isn’t exactly the friendly type, but the least we can do is try to comfort her.’

‘Great. Have at it.’

‘It’d mean more if we both did it.’

‘You seem to be forgetting a few important things here, Freya. She hates your guts, nags and complains literally every breath she takes, and she is an inconsiderate brat with nothing to bring to the table. Should I remind you that it’s her fault we were even dragged down here in the first place?’

Well check and mate. What the heck did I say to that?

Nolan was spitting facts. He shut me up, for the meantime….

“Okay, this is the part where we get our shit together,” Nolan said as he stood meters across the front doors. We all paused, ready to take orders from our fearless party leader.

“We need a strategy,” Lun said as we stood a few meters before the castle’s gates. “Nolan, what’s our play here?”

“Standard formation. I’ll take point, Lun covers ranged attacks, and Typha...” he glanced at her, “...stay in the middle and get out of our way.”

‘What about me?’ I asked.

“Freya will stay in the middle with Typha. Keep formation unless something goes wrong, then you improvise with those new wings of yours.”

I felt a flutter of excitement at the mention of my newfound flying ability, though the memory of that terrifying fall still made my stomach clench.

Once we reached a yard or two before the gates, it opened automatically. The short walk to the castle doors gave my nerves enough time to build up. Staring at the structure long enough made it feel even more imposing. The castle doors swung open with a low, resonant groan as we approached. No guards, no immediate threats—just a vast marble foyer stretching before us. Sunlight filtered through stained glass windows, filtering rainbow patterns across the polished floor.

Inside was much warmer than outside. I suggested that Nolan gave Typha his Stone Fist items, so she wouldn’t rely on me to defend her all the time. The gunslinger was skeptical, seeing as the pair had some weight to it. Typha was skeptical too, but when she slipped it on like a glove, she was able to block her face with them and throw punches.

“They are slowing down her reaction time,” Nolan noticed. “Use it only defensively.”

Well, he didn’t have to worry about that. The poor girl didn’t even look like she wanted to wear them let alone fight. I guess that beef she had with Nolan truly put things in perspective for her—her life was in our hands. Without us, she was easy core food for all of these monsters. At the same time that meant that we could leave her behind if we really wanted to, so now, she realized that she had to be on her best behavior.

“Nothing appears particularly alarming, yet,” Lun observed, until a tall woman came down one side of the duo staircase in front of us….

She was dressed in a sparkling white sleeveless dress, her long puffy blond hair as soft as clouds. Her eyes were blue, even the white parts, matching her shimmering diamonds, blue earrings and necklace. She was giving off princess vibes, the youth on her face making me believe that she was around her mid 20’s.

For a moment, she stood at a step and observed us, Nolan readying his shotgun, and Lun, his scepter.

“Uninvited guests,” she said in a soft-spoken tone. “How pleasant.”

“If it’s any consolation, we don’t want to be here, either,” Nolan remarked.

“I sense you’ve already defeated my twin brothers?”

“Twin, brothers?” Lun asked.

“In the Chamber of Earth? My evil eldest thought it entertaining to transform them into stone, fitting to him, seeing as hard-headed they were. But I don’t take kindly to jokes.”

“Really? You could have fooled me, with that cloud bridge out there,” Nolan quipped.

“A means to get rid of unwanted pests like you,” she hissed. “But since you made it here alive, I’ll make sure you never leave this place.”
