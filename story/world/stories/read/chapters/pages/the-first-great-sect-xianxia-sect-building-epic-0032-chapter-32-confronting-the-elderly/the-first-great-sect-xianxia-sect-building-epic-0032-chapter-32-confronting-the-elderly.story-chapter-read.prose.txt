Thunderous steps sent everyone scurrying away, servants, vassals, and even adults with lightning in their veins. No one would dare be in the way of the oncoming storm that was Liao Hua.

The Clan Hall had been sealed. Hua hadn’t truly understood the dearth of powerful Qi signatures she was sensing. Oh, there were those in Body Tempering and even a few in Qi Gathering, but there was no one of any great strength.

There was a room on the second floor of the Main Hall. If one kept their valuable formations and most potent Alchemical aide in one building, it made sense to also have a secure meeting room. She made her way there, glaring down the one guard who looked up at her.

The door’s security systems fell away at Hua’s approach, recognising its Lord, and she pushed open the doors to the meeting room.

The doors swung so far and fast that they struck the walls. More than one elder sitting at the great table flinched at the bang. They appeared like a constellation to her senses. Hua looked them over, fury warring with her because this was a full Council meeting. And they had thought to have it without her.

Qisan is here! He barely counts as an Elder!

The Council of Elders was made up of twelve elders. Those who had joined her in the binding ceremony and those who attended the purification ritual in the city. Twelve had become ten with the deaths of Qiqi and Shenhe. But most of the remaining ten were functionally unimportant. They may have the resources and wealth to be problems, but they did as the elder Shens of the main branch demanded, which fundamentally arrayed them against Hua. A total of four from the Shen Generation and two of the Qi Generation. She knew their names, had honoured them as was their due as her elders, but they had never been of great importance to clan politics.

Hua nodded once at the table. She savoured the abject despair some of them wore and took comfort in the deep hate of others. Most of the less useful elders were cautiously blank.

“Greetings, Elders. It seems my invitation to this meeting was lost whilst I was busy in the city which is the foundation of our power. Thankfully, I know the elders would never think to forget this lord.”

As she spoke, she had been making her way around the table at a steady pace. Her goal glittered the same colour as her eyes looming atop the gathering at the far end of the room.

She ascended the dais that separated the Elders from the Patriarch’s seat. Hua sat gracefully where her father had presided over meetings just like this. His was a bench gilded in gold that towered over the others. Terribly uncomfortably, it allowed her to look down at her elders, taking them in. The contempt, the distaste, and the wariness.

“Now that I am here, we can begin.”

“Liao Hua, you go beyond yourself.” She ignored that name. “Liao Hua, do not pretend you cannot hear me. Why do you dare sit in the Patriarch’s seat?”

“I wonder who you refer to by that name. This Lord is Liao Weilong. To my left sit Elders Shenyi and Shenyu. Shall I go around the table until I reach you as well, or have you remembered the names in this room, Liao Shenhou?”

She would make no friends by doing this, but she also knew they would never be her friends. This generation of Elders were juniors to her father, one generation below him. The Shen Generation had grown up protected by her father’s strength and learned their techniques at his knee. She was his wild child with too few manners they’d put up with knowing she’d be married off soon enough and would learn to behave by then. Now they were forced to pretend that she was their lord in name and truth, a girl who had claimed a generation name and usurped an elder brother. All because of the old woman pretending to have no part in this.

Her grandmother, sat apart from the rest, pretending to be a doddering old lady who simply enjoyed the sunlight coming from the narrow shutters running along the top of the east wall. That was the deepest betrayal. The woman who raised her and taught her had allowed this attempted usurpation to occur. It took everything she had to keep her Qi calm, within the boundary of her body.

“Girl, your disrespect will not serve you well,” Elder Shenhou warned.

“Respect is given to those who earn it through their actions. It can be lost faster than the oriole is shot through the neck by an arrow.”

“What act has this venerable elder committed to lose your precious respect?”

“Know me by my name if you desire a modicum of face.”

A bang broke the staring match. Elder Shenyu, the one who left bastards everywhere. He was beyond angry, thick veins pulsing in his neck and forehead.

“Your role is a lie we perpetuate,” Shenyu growled, Qi thickening in the air. “There is no value in it. Your disrespect has gone on long enough. Leave that seat or be removed forcibly.”

“If my role is merely a lie, then why do you not anchor the formations? I can feel the thinness of your Qi from here.”

“You dare.”

“I do.”

Elder Shenyu rose to his feet, furious embarrassment reddening his face. Hua very carefully relaxed further into her seat. She would not give him face by treating him like the threat he was.

“The only lie we perpetuate is the number of children you have, and I think the Wei Generation has enough scions without silver-haired bastards running around,” she threw out contemptuously, flaring the full radiance of her qi.

The light of six stars burned within her. Six Meridians unlocked and saturated with qi. And one which carried so much Qi that it felt impossible. A quantity that let her bridge the gap in their Cultivation more than she should be able to. Part talent, part luck, mostly fury at the heavens.

“Now sit down, Shenyu, or challenge me if you despise the idea of your new Lord so much.”

His Qi rose in answer. If she was the feeling of bones shaking after a thunderclap, he was the storm tearing a home apart. His power was greater, that much couldn’t be denied, but the difference was not so abyssal as she expected.

When had he last held a sword, taken a life? Hua trained every day with live steel. She had honed herself knowing how close death was in every swing. He likely knew more clan techniques, but Hua had grown up in an era where what he considered transcendent was standard. Would that be enough to kill him?

Maybe she would die here, but if so, who the fuck cared?

Hua was not going to back down. If she could not survive her elders, then Heaven would strike her down before she achieved anything.

“This old lady recognises her grandson, Liao Weilong,” Grandmother spoke for the first time. “If any have an issue remembering him, this one is willing to remind them.”

Her Qi flickered and the weight of it slammed down on them all. Even Hua felt the cloying suppression. This was the Lady Liao, the Hummingbird Blade, she who first laid down the foundation to heaven, and built a great clan despite merely being the second wife of a fourth son. Power was a form of forgiveness and Grandmother was the most powerful person in this chamber. Her word was law.

Hua breathed carefully through the suppression, pushing her Qi against the cloying grip surrounding her spiritual. Grandmother was smiling at her in that amused way of a dragon seeing their young first use their claws. This entire meeting was a test on Hua’s ability to coral information and maintain an air of control. Only now, when Hua had barred her teeth and threatened a duel, did Grandmother interfere.

She hated the old woman sometimes.

Elder Shenyu’s brother grabbed his sleeve and tugged. The Elder sat, furious, blotchy resentment on his face. His jaw was clenched, his forehead veins popping. If not for the ever-present sound of hummingbird wings flapping, he would have launched himself and ripped Hua’s head off. But that was life. Sometimes you had the more powerful backer. And if he was foolish enough to challenge someone a full realm above him, his brother wouldn’t let him die a worthless death.

“Now, what were we discussing?” Hua asked calmly, turning to Elder Qiang, as though they all weren’t being weighed down by heavy Qi.

Her aunt had the strained smile of someone reminded just how far they stood from the summit of Mt. Tai. Hua had always trusted her enough to let the twins stay at her household when Hua needed to leave. That trust only went as far as political power could throw her and Hua’s power lay mostly in her grandmother’s Qi.

But she was more of an ally than most of the elders.

“The state of the clan finances and the challenges we will face in distributing them appropriately.”

To better decide how to siphon funds for pills and Cultivation resources, went unsaid. And I suppose to pay off however many bastards you’ve all collectively sired.

“This Lord has yet to inspect the treasury, but he will do so after this meeting. He hopes the treasury accurately reflects the reports given to the honourable Elders,” she said. If anyone lied, she would know it within the day.

“Uncle Shenhou is more versed in the matter of the treasury. This ignorant Qiang would not dare step into his domain.”

Shehou’s frown deepened. Being thrown in front of the archery range was never a fun place to be.

With heavy resignation, he said, “We have five hundred and twenty thousand silver taels in the treasury with double that in paper currency, as well as twenty thousand gold taels. A convoy with payment for our last grain and mineral contracts was meant to be received two days ago, but it has either fallen to banditry or been drowned by flooding. How would you see us through this, Weilong?”

Those numbers had no meaning to Weilong. She had never once needed to pay attention to what she spent. Hua had been a daughter of high standing in a Cultivator Clan. The chances of her marrying both a Cultivator of greater realm and outside the clan were low. She would have dealt with Cultivation matters whilst her husband dealt with more mortal concerns. And were she to enter the Foundation Establishment like her grandmother, able to live over a century, the same would hold true with her second and third husbands if she so chose.

Still, she could make some educated guesses.

A gold tael was roughly twenty silver taels. All told, they had somewhere in the range of two million silver taels taking every source into account.

If she were forced to answer, she would say they had enough in the treasury for a year or two of full expenditure. The problem with trade lines was how the moment they became irregular, guaranteed income had a habit of vanishing. Their trade wagons could have been swallowed by the earth and the barges carrying coal might be at the bottom of the sea.

And whilst she may have been ignorant of the precise details, she did have something the Elders lacked: the very real experience of watching the mortals behave in the aftermath of heaven’s betrayal.

“So we only have a million silver in real money,” she said as the realisation cut through her.

“Excuse me? Did you mishear me?”

“No, I merely heard what you are deaf to. Elders, beyond the purification rituals, have any of you walked the city that bears our name since the world broke beneath our feet? Have you seen the dead mortals pilling the back alleys? The looting, the corruption? I spent the first few days organising the city we hold a duty to. I pulled more corpses from rubble in those few days than I have ever made. Do you know what madness I saw?” Hua did not wait for an answer. She already knew the answer. “I saw people walk past paper bills because they did not care. I saw them kill for a single silver tael. People do not care for paper, but silver is something you can bite. It can’t be used to light a fire. Paper money means nothing unless people believe in it.”

“Surely not. Even mortals can’t be so foolish,” said a younger Shen after the beat of silence had gone on too long. “You speak of madness. Money is money. It matters.”

“Do you think these eyes would be deceived?”

Everyone in this room had black or green eyes. Everyone. She was the only one without them, and not a person in this room could claim not to remember the vibrancy of her green eyes.

Now they were gold. One gave gold at weddings, at celebrations, as great gifts to show prosperity and wealth. Perhaps even the colour of divine lightning.

Hua hated them. They were the eyes that saw Qing disappear into this world. Every day that she saw them, she would see a reminder of her failures.

“My honoured grandson brings up unpleasant memories of the Yellow Cap War. When the chaos was at its worst, the price of bread could double in hours, and it would do so every day for a week.” Grandmother paused then. The force of her suppression became tinged with the feeling of starvation. “The value of goods became absurdly inflated as currency lost any coherency. We will need to reassure the people that their bills have meaning if we don’t wish a loaf of bread to cost more than we spend on pills in a year.”

“These are not those dark times.”

“And yet, history always finds a means to rhyme. We must curtail this at the source. The Dragon Throne is distant but the treasuries of Laio are close. We have resources to back the currency for a short while.”

“It will not be enough,” Shenhou countered. “We’ll burn through the treasury in a month. We can’t survive by becoming a counting house.”

Grandmother smiled her dragon smile. “Perhaps not as we are and not alone. But I suspect our new Lord has a solution ready. Tell us, what were you doing this afternoon that needed so many soldiers?”

“This lord went to speak with the Magistrate of Liaojiangkou to discuss a plan to ensure stability for the future. Such was the importance of seeing to the future of our home instead of ignoring it,” she said pointedly, though she did not look specifically at anyone, “that this lord went to personally secure the Magistrate’s support. As distant as the Dragon Throne may be, people still fear the dragon’s roar. I have received the Magistrate’s conditional agreement to lay claim over the Imperial assets in the region. The clerks, the soldiers, the food supplies, the wagons and horses.”

“Potentially the tax treasury,” Elder Shenhou said, eyes widening. “That… that could be enough to get us through the next few months without the peasants burning our gates down. Perhaps even a year or more.”

Hua shook her head. “You underestimate my ambition, Honoured Elder.”

“Oh?”

She was going to have to amend her agreement with the Magistrate. Hopefully, he wouldn’t be too upset with her changing the terms of their agreement.

“I mean to seize every tael of gold and silver that I can and make the Liao Clan the only counting house in our territory.”

That pronouncement was met with complete silence.
