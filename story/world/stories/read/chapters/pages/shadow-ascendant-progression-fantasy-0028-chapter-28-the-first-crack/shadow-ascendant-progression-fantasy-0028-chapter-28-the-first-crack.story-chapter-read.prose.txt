Instructor Serena pushed herself from the massive rock and started retracing her path… the path I wasn’t even able to see her move last time.

“What are you all waiting for?” she asked in a low voice, not even looking in our direction.

Silence met her words before Alfie, the elf, spoke.

“Do we even need to learn this kind of application of Enhancement magic?” he asked. “It’s not like mages are supposed to fight on the front line, and if breaking the boulder is what you want to teach… isn’t using magic the way to go?”

“And what’d you do when you’re about to run out of mana, your arm is broken, your channels ruptured, leaving you unable to cast a spell as your neck is being crushed by an ogre?” Instructor Serena asked.

Being crushed by an ogre after getting your arms broken? That sounds far-fetched. I thought internally.

“I was once in a similar situation, only the monster was a massive ogre chief. You know how I survived? Wanna guess?”

“By enhancement magic?” Noelle asked.

To which Instructor Serena nodded. “Exactly. My broken arms made me useless as a mage... However, I could still cast a few weak spells, although they weren’t strong enough to defeat an ogre.”

“So, to survive, I literally became a tough nut to crack, pouring all the mana I could into my neck to protect it, while using my legs to break that ogre’s ribs. I also broke my leg in the process, so yeah… it wasn’t a cool fight at all. But had I not learnt enhancement magic, I wouldn’t be standing here in front of you all.”

“And that’s precisely why I want all of you to learn it. It’s also something you’ll need a basic understanding of to move to the second year, so it’s not like you can skip it anyway.” She shrugged. “Does anyone have another question?” Instructor Serena asked.

To which a few responded with a no, while others, like myself, shook their heads.

“Well then, get to it. I’ve already told you all that you need to know. First, circulate concentrated mana through the limb you wish to empower, and then strike. The more concentrated your mana, the more diffusion would occur, and the stronger your attack gets.” She glanced to her side, looking toward Instructor Chloe. “And whenever any of you gets injured—a fracture or minor bleeding, Miss Valmont would heal you. So… start the training!” She added with a loud clap of her hands.

The training session started with her words, though everyone seemed a little uncertain, including me. While I had moved closer to the boulder, standing within punching range, I was still hesitant.

The first one to hit the stone would definitely get the attention of everyone, and I wasn’t keen on it.

I looked around—everyone else looked hesitant as well, except for Seraphina. She was knocking on the boulder, and then suddenly, she punched it with a loud thud. The rock didn’t crack, nor did her hand… then again, why would her hand crack?

Seraphina pulled back her hand and looked at her knuckles with a thoughtful expression.

Is she testing her limits? I pondered for a moment before turning my attention to the boulder at hand. I brought both my arms up near my chest, taking a punching stance before I punched the rock at the speed of a snail.

It didn’t hurt! Well, of course, it didn’t; it was more like touching than punching, but still. With time, I continued punching the rock, gradually increasing the strength I exerted, only stopping when the punches began hurting—just not too bad.

I glanced at my fist; my knuckles were red now… and that’s when a loud crumbling sound caught my attention.

My head snapped to the left. Marcus Levile had broken through. Then, with a swift [whoosh,] the ground beneath grew and enveloped the rock.

It was Instructor Serena’s spell.

“Good work, Marcus!” Instructor Serena exclaimed, her eyes widening. “Didn’t think anyone could clear the test this fast. Chloe…” she added, glancing towards her.

Instructor Chloe nodded as she hurried towards Marcus with her hands enveloped in a bright yellowish aura—the aura of light affinity. “Low Heal,” she murmured, grabbing his hand, and in the same instant, she stopped… a slightly confused expression forming on her face.

“There’s no injury in your fist,” she murmured as she released his hand and looked to her side, where now Instructor Serena stood with a mildly amused look.

“Have you learnt enhancement magic before today?” she asked.

“I have not, ma’am. I just repeated what you taught, and that worked somehow. It’s all thanks to you, ma’am,” he replied with a smile.

Talk about buttering your instructor… Marcus certainly is good at it, I added in my mind. Everyone in the class looked astounded as well. Till now, only a few like Mabel and Seraphina had chipped a few pieces from the rock… but Marcus? He destroyed it in one clean strike.

“You were able to pour enough mana to protect yourself? Hmm…” Instructor Serena turned her eyes towards us. “You all must remember this too—while Enhancement magic increases strength to the greatest degree, it also affects durability, speed, reaction, reflex… everything.”

Her eyes turned to the other side as she glanced at where Mabel and the others stood. “That’s why Marcus didn’t get hurt, and that’s exactly what you all should aim for as well. Do you all understand my words?”

“Yes, ma’am!” everyone shouted in unison. I shouted as well, though perhaps not as loud.

Turning my eyes back to the boulder, I relaxed my body. I knew the force I could roughly exert without hurting myself seriously, without enhancement magic, and that was the same speed with which I’d strike the boulder.

“It shouldn’t hurt that much,” I whispered to myself, squeezing my eyes shut. I pressed my eyelids together, blocking out all the light, and focused on visualizing my mana core.

I glanced down. My mana core was the same as always, a ball of glowing mana, half red, half violet, right at the center of my chest. Focusing harder, I began picturing my mana vessels, though just the ones connecting my right hand and brain to the core.

Mostly because I wanted to pour as much mana as possible, and I thought not picturing my other limbs might lower the flow in them. I wasn’t too sure; it was, well, a guess.

The mana arteries appeared in front of my eyes, bright and white—they glowed because of the mana flowing through them. I pressed my eyes harder, my forehead straining, after which the mana flow increased erratically before steadying.

As I had suspected, I could flow more mana than before. It only made sense since I was focusing more.

I looked at my fist with my eyes closed. As Instructor Serena had told us before, the mana certainly seeped in from the arteries to the surrounding area.

Was I stronger now? That’s something I didn’t really know; I could only hope.

I continued pouring mana into my hands until they couldn’t hold anymore, and then I opened my eyes.

My eyes didn’t get any sharper, nor did my hearing improve; everything felt normal. Well, everything except my forearm at least, felt heavier, my body like an unbalanced weighing machine.

I tightened my fist as I withdrew it a little. Will it work? I wondered as the rock beside me crumbled.

Seraphina Victoria had completed the training exercise as well. Not letting myself get distracted, I exhaled once, and then punched, putting all the strength I could behind the blow.

With a loud thump, my punch landed on the boulder.

The impact rippled through my bones, a deep vibration that started at my knuckles and moved to my shoulder.

Did it work?! I wondered excitedly. It wasn’t hurting; it must’ve worked, I thought, when suddenly, a sharp pain gripped my knuckle.

I bit my lip, attempting to muffle any sound I might make; getting embarrassed in front of everyone was definitely not something I desired.

I gulped, turning my eyes towards the boulder. I wasn’t able to replicate what Marcus had done… no, the only thing I managed was to make a small indent on the surface.

I had failed—miserably at that.

“Hmm…” Instructor Serena murmured.

I glanced at her; she was looking at me. She’s disappointed, isn’t she? All the time I wasted condensing mana, and all I did was—

“Nicely done! Arnos.”

Why is she complimenting me as if I did anything… wait, no, she knows my name! I mentally yelled, genuinely surprised. I mean, why would she even know my name? I’m just her student.

Before I could spiral further into my thoughts, a faint [crik] sound entered my ears. My eyes snapped towards the boulder, at the dent I had made.

[Crik–Crik–KRIK!] Loud sounds entered my ears as thin lines emerged from the dent I’d made on the stone. Like a spiderweb, they spread across its surface as I blinked with astonishment.

Did I…?

I couldn’t even complete my word. The rock began shattering right in front of me, it didn’t blow into small pieces like when Marcus or Seraphina broke through, but into large chunks.

As the first piece touched the ground, the grassy earth rose, swallowing it whole, Instructor Serena’s magic didn’t let even dust escape. The broken pieces of the boulder pressed into the ground, forming a slight elevation that soon vanished as she stopped using her magic.

Instructor Serena walked towards me, her eyes on the ground for a moment, and then at me.
