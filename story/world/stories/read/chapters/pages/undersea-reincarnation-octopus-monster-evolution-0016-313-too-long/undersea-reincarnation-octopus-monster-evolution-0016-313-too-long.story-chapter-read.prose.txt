Two days after Invisibility was merged within Mimicry, Henry was ready to check one more item out of his list. He’d been mulling it over for a few hours by now, weighing the pros and cons as he batted away at a school of purple crabs that wanted to clamber up the trees of Thalis and by now, he felt good about his call.

Henry was going to leave both Bite and Evershifting Strike mostly untouched. He wasn’t going to fuse them to anything. The two skills were different enough, and had different enough uses that he genuinely felt he’d lose out if he’d altered any of them. Which meant he just had to let one of them take the center of the Aspect, and he’d be done with that item.

Once I’m done here, that is, Henry grumbled as quickly swam toward another of his clones. It was his turn to be on watch, and with all the heightened energies, the crabs that lived below the tree city had suddenly exploded in numbers and swarmed, and so at all times, sometimes had to be on watch lest they got too high.

Henry didn’t mind it. It let him both practice with his new Aspects, and prepare himself for more skill fusions.

Soon enough, his shift was over, and after dropping off Maurice to hangout with his new friends, Henry tuned out the sound and voices he could pick up from his different clones before he let his consciousness dip within as he sank below the rippling waters of his pool. He took a moment to enjoy the quiet, but he could only do that for so long before the unease he could still feel within his soul shook him out of it.

It was time to get some work done.

Slowly, Henry narrowed his focus on one of the areas he could feel some tension lingering in his spirit. By now, he was pretty familiar with his targets, and so he quickly found his quarry, where two skills were still jostling for supremacy, each trying to set roots and become the center of Predation of the Trickster.

Within the Aspect, the two skill-roots were still in conflict, with Henry constantly and actively suppressing them with the help of his Octominds. Considering that Bite was higher level and it just was… bulkier than Evershifting Strike, it could have won and taken control already, if Henry had allowed it too, but he had needed time to think and until only recently, he’d been considering fusing them before changing his mind.

Now, he could resolve the issue and take one item off of his to-do list.

Taking a moment to study the large skill, Henry quickly noted how Bite was connected to Maw of the Trickster and Voracious Grip, the two skills which he had gotten just by upgrading Bite and its previous Aspect. It was the same type of connection that Partial Shapeshift shared with Mimicry. Or the one Inkjet shared with the clones. That connection added a little complexity to the two remaining fusions, but it was nothing he wasn’t ready for.

Henry put a pin in his observation and instead focused on the current task. Now that he could sense the process and now that he was convinced of his choice, Henry relaxed his grip on Bite, while keeping Evershifting Strike restrained, and the process began.

They really are like roots, Henry thought as he the skill-root of Bite spread out through Predation of the Trickster, resonating with it. While Predation gained a little of Bite and its hunger, the skill itself gained more weight. It wasn’t about just devouring and incorporating the prey’s strengths, anymore. There was something else in there. It was as if… with every prey that fell to his sharp, crushing beak, his existence at the top of the food chain was proclaimed. Every bone he shattered, every scale he tore into, was a hymn to a great predator.

Henry backed away from the powerful impressions emanating out of the merge and focused on the process itself. Like a fish finally put in water, the skill was inhaling its new environment, growing stronger and faster. The skill and the Aspect pulsed rhythmically, resonating out of sync at first, but slowly got in tune until, after what felt like only seconds, the process slowed, and it came to an end. At least, the initial part of the process. Now, he had to wait a little for everything to stabilize. The Aspect and skill needed a resting period, after which it should be stable enough for Henry to start exercising and reinforcing it.

That’s one down, Henry thought with a smile. With that done, he also let Evershifting Strike take root as a secondary skill of the Aspect, where it would serve just as well as it has done before. The whole process had lasted around twenty minutes, so Henry still felt fresh. It wasn’t as if had much to do, of course. He just had to let the skills do what they wanted to do. They just had to do it in the order that would work best for him. So without further ado, Henry got started on the second task.

Henry would be lying if he didn’t say he was getting stir-crazy with all of this skill fusing stuff, but he was progressing, fixing and improving his foundation, and the sooner he was done, the faster he’d be able to get there and get back to hunting and leveling. Plus, once the fusions were done, he’d finally be able to push the new Aspects to B-rank, upgrading their cornerstones and opening up the way to B-rank.

It would be worth it. He was sure of it.

After a quick heads-up to Maurice that he was going to be busy for a bit, Henry dove back within his own mind this time, it was the shifting nature of life that filled his senses.

This fusion, on the other hand, might take him a minute.

***

A few hours later, Henry groggily opened his eyes, the new combined skill pulsing deep in his soul as he completely and utterly relaxed all of his limbs. He stared up at the sky, no thoughts in his mind aside from the swaying canopy and the orange and blue-violet colors of dusk. He stared up, eyes vacant, too tired to listen or think of anything, and just floated within his own pool. It had taken… a lot to conciliate those two skills. It had involved countless casts of both skills through the same nexus, getting something functional and stable, then nudging it int the direction he wanted. It went without saying, it took all of his brainpower and practically everything he had in storage when it came to Lifeweaving essence.

So he needed a rest, and that was why for a while, Henry just stared at the sky, thoughts empty. It could have been seconds or minutes later, but at some point, a voice reached his ears.

“Good job.”

That made him twitch. He had quickly recognized who it was, but the tone was unfamiliar. Flicking his eyes to the right, he saw Arisia sitting at the edge of the pool, her feet gently paddling in the water.

Henry had rarely seen such a genuine smile on Arisia’s face. He had almost asked her who she was and what she’d done with his instructor, but he refrained.

“Thanks,” he said, waving an arm. He held off on loading the new definition of the cornerstone skill and instead, he focused on the guest who had apparently come to see him. Plus, if he were honest, he was too tired to deal with his interface at this moment.

He could manage talking for a few minutes. But not much else.

Arisia stared into the water, and Henry didn’t know what to say at first, so he stayed quiet. It was easier anyway. But eventually, a question trickled down to his barely functioning mind, and he realized he had to ask it. Now that he thought of it, he had to admit it had been bugging him for a while. If they were sitting around, he might as well get to know her a bit more.

“How come you’re still B-rank? If you don’t mind me asking.”

Arisia had been the one who’d taught Velistraine, from what Henry had learned. Even though the instructor looked like she was only in her sixties, she’d been around for at least a century, forming and teaching the talents of Thalis. So how come she was still B-rank and hadn’t gotten to the next rank?

If there was one who could make the jump, it should be her.

Arisia looked up at the sky, leaning back as she stopped paddling her feet in the water. “A-rank… requires a certain temerity. A certain willingness to take risks. Unlike the ranks before it, you have to get out there and push yourself. Find the strongest of B-ranks and measure yourself against weaker A-ranks—if you could even call those weak. Unlike attaining B-rank, A-rank cannot be reached passively. Even when there’s a Wave, the ambient energies are never enough to push a B-rank to the next level. But that’s not really why I never took that last step.”

She looked at the stars, and her smile seemed to slip for a bit. Arisia sighed, and looked down at the vein in her hand. “I don’t want the longevity that comes with A-rank. I am… content. With my life. But I don’t want to extend further than this.”

Henry just listened. He could understand a little where she was coming from, but he had no words for her, so he just let the soft sound of rustling leaves fill the air for a moment before she spoke again.

“I’ve cremated my family. My boys and girls. Some of my grandchildren. If there’s an afterlife out there, I’d like to see them there, someday. I’d like to see Zerathstra freed of his duties, if possible. I strongly believe that we’ll soon find answers. That thing with the turtles and the orb is more than we have figured out in decades and I really hope we find answers, but if I’m being true to myself and what I feel… I do not want the longevity that comes with A-rank. It is… too much. Too long. And I’m too tired.”

She fell silent once more, and Henry, having recovered from the mental exhaustion he’d been feeling, lifted himself out of the water.

“I’m sorry for your loss.”

There wasn’t much else to say, really. He now knew why she didn’t want to take the final step and having seen Zerathstra and the responsibilities the ape had on his shoulders, he could empathize.

For him though? Henry didn’t feel that passing on A-rank was really an option. At all. His goal of figuring out how he’d arrive in this world hinged on him attaining that rank. And so did his safety and Maurice’s. If he wasn’t A-rank himself, he could be crushed by one at any time. Plus, he wanted to know what happened after A-rank. Arisia and Zerathstra didn’t really put that much pressure on him. At this point, he wanted the answer just as they did. It wasn’t exactly a life goal for him as it was for them, but it was up there.

Arisia slowly got up, the smile back on her lips. “Anyway, congratulations. I came here to check on your progress and make sure you’ll be ready for tomorrow.”

Henry blinked. “Ready for what?”

“A banquet. Tomorrow might be the last peaceful day for a couple of weeks. A time to celebrate with friends and family. While we have started to see fighting, the worst of it is yet to come, and one never knows what can happen in such times. So make sure to get some rest and to get back to work. You need to have your fusions done tomorrow,” she added, the familiar stern look in her eyes.

Henry instinctively straightened up, but then the woman’s facade cracked and she chuckled, shaking her head. “Really, though. Good work. I have high hopes for you. But don’t burn yourself out, okay? You don’t have the responsibilities we do. So make sure you come to the banquet tomorrow. Live a little. Or I’ll have Zer drag you there.”

Henry chuckled and nodded, then waived her off as she left. Now that he was alone once more, he could take a look at his interface. Pulling up the updated cornerstone of Lifeweaving of the Trickster, Henry pulled a thread of healing and began channeling it into his brains and body. He had to recover before tackling the most challenging fusion yet. But this was a good step forward. No one could deny him that.

Lifeshaper’s Forms

Passives:

Faint Presence: When camouflaging, your mana and mental presence will be muffled.

Ingested Memory: Mimic some of the physical properties of recently ingested prey and recently sampled matter. Exotic anatomies can be understood better through consumption. Up to three mimicked properties can be remembered permanently. [Scales of the Serpent][Draining Touch of the Mesmer Eel]

Lifeblood Reserve: Your body continuously accumulates excess vitality, forming internal reserves of health and mana. While at rest or meditating, your natural regeneration accelerates, enriching the stored reserves further. When injured or depleted, you may draw upon this hidden well to restore yourself.

Actives:

Trickster’s Camouflage: Turn invisible or change your appearance through the mimicking property of your skin, taking the form, shape, color, and even mana signature of familiar lifeforms and objects.

Mimicking Shell: You can weave familiar forms around your true body. The mana signature of the forms can be mimicked as well.

Lesser Shapeshift: Alter and reshape your physical form, molding one or multiple limbs into complex, adaptive structures. Well-practiced forms can be accessed swiftly. You may gradually adjust your overall size, mass, and proportions. The drawbacks of smaller forms are significantly reduced. Maximum Mass: 550,000 kg

Life Surge: Expend mana and Lifeblood to release a regenerative surge that restores health and purges ailments. It can be channeled to others with touch. For a greater mana cost, the regenerative effect is greatly empowered.

Lifeblood Orb: Create a consumable and storable orb containing up to 50% of your own health and mana.

Henry dismissed the interface, feeling pretty happy with the results. He was sure he could do more cleaning up within the Aspect, but that could wait a little. The most difficult part was done, and he just had one big fusion left. The biggest, arguably. So why not take a short break?

Closing his eyes, Henry allowed himself to drift to sleep, and it wasn’t at all difficult. At least this way, he’d be in his best shape for the next bit and as sleep took him, little bit of hopes lit up his spirit.

All this work would be worth it. He would make sure of it.
