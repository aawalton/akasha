Ferris closed his eyes as he focused on the bite he’d just taken of the Core Tree’s fruit. He immediately grimaced, not having expected the fruit to be salty. The other two had tasted much better.

Letting the flow of information wash over him, Ferris ignored the taste and kept eating. This seed could grow into an ability called Assimilate Object. It would let Jade incorporate an object inside their body as an ‘artificial organ.’ The slime would then be able to move the item around, like moving a limb.

Thinking for a moment, Ferris got excited. The ability was amazing.

Immediately, he thought about using it to protect Jade’s core. A metal cage of armor could protect against the kind of attack the chain demon had used to kill the slime. He didn’t know if a solid ball of metal cutting off all connections was a bad idea, but worst case he could just make some holes in it. The slime journal had mentioned Janice finding abilities that covered the slime’s weakness of a core, and this fit the bill perfectly.

Ferris couldn’t help but laugh. An hour ago, this ability would have terrified him. Before he’d learned that Jade could remember important memories, he could’ve never used it. The only reason he felt safe to cultivate next to the slime was the chain binding the slime’s core. With this ability, Jade could simply assimilate the chain, open the lock, and then be free to reach his head.

Ferris’ breath caught as he looked at Jade in shock.

Jade had a problem, namely the chain’s cage constantly restraining them. This ability directly solved that problem. Somehow, Adaptability had reacted to the real-world problem with a spiritual solution.

Thinking back, the Core Tree had given the abilities Grow and Jump, which had already ripened before he first saw the tree. Before bonding Ferris, the slime had lived at the slime ranch… inside a giant pit. Both Grow and Jump could theoretically help the slime escape a pit, or even just get more of the precious magical berries. The ranchers only let larger slimes—full from eating—up the ramp to get a berry, after all.

Ferris looked around. He hadn’t seen anything directly touch the Core Tree, but that didn’t mean nothing had. The ants or birds could’ve done something when he wasn’t around, but he still felt he was missing something. Abilities should have a spiritual representation, like a pool of acid for the Consume Pool.

Closing his eyes, he focused on feeling for the subtle vibration he felt around his Heartfire and Qi Pool. First Steps of the Soul claimed it would be present around all abilities.

After a moment, he definitely felt it. The sensation was like a quiet buzzing sound he had long grown accustomed to, though felt rather than heard.

Trying to follow the feeling, it only led him to the Core Tree. I guess that makes sense, he thought. It was an ability, although he didn’t usually think of it like that. Would he even be able to tell two vibrations apart if they were right next to each other?

Ferris looked down at Jade. He’d just admonished himself for trying to solve puzzles without talking to his familiar, maybe the slime had some intuition on how their own ability worked?

“Jade,” he said, “do you know how the abilities here work? You should have Adaptability, which affects this tree.”

“No,” the slime responded, “I just know fruit are special.”

Well… back to solving puzzles. But first, he needed to finish the fruit. Trying not to think about the strange taste, Ferris returned to eating.

The Assimilate Object ability was much more complicated than either Grow or Jump. Ferris had expected the increase in complexity, as typically a Core Tree would only give abilities that are possible for the soul to support.

Before bonding, Jade could only support abilities that would grow naturally. Now, Jade’s Core Tree had access to Ferris’ soulspace, and the presence of his mental body. Some extra resources may need to be acquired, but often abilities made use of what already existed in the soul.

Ferris didn’t know the specifics, but he’d heard that this was a basic concept when attempting to acquire a targeted ability. For example, Hollowfield’s information on how to learn Fire Resistance probably listed a plethora of resources to bring into the soulspace. Uncle Winton had mentioned there was more to it, but not the details.

Thankfully, Assimilate Object didn’t require expensive magical resources like the spirit gems Ferris had refined—just a lot of time, effort, and qi. It needed an entire frame built, as large as a building. Ferris would then have to connect the frame to a construct item in the middle, representing the object to assimilate. The frame would represent Jade, while the connection to the object was the slime’s ability to manipulate it. When it was all ready, the ability would create a connection between the spiritual object and one inside Jade’s real body, assimilating it as an artificial organ.

After finishing the fruit, Ferris had more questions than answers. The ability referred to the item as an ‘artificial organ,’ but would it be inherently immune to the slime’s acid? How stable would it be inside the slime's gelatinous body? He expected it couldn’t fully leave the slime’s body, but could it extend partway? If it could extend out, could Jade still manipulate it outside? Would the new organ prevent Jade from moving or climbing? How many organs could the ability support? The information only described manipulation, but the word ‘organ’ implied that the item could have other functions…

The only way to answer the questions would be to experiment. The problem was: the ability was expensive. He would have to spend a lot of time and qi constructing the necessary structures. Was it worth it?

Ferris tried to evaluate the ability for his situation. Elder Harper had said it only took one solid attack ability, properly developed, to reach the second tier. Assimilate Object sounded powerful, with both offensive and defensive potential, but would it be better than spending that time and resources elsewhere?

In the end… he wasn’t sure. He had three ability seeds, but Ferris had already excluded Jump from serious resource commitment. It would let Jade close distances faster, but wouldn’t help the slime beyond that.

Both Grow and Assimilate Object were good enough offensive abilities to let Ferris begin delving—once he’d healed his leg. Assimilate Object gave potentially better defense, but Grow was simpler to develop. He wanted to pick one to start focusing on, but Assimilate Object was too complex to evaluate as quickly as he had the others. Grow and Jump were straightforward: make Jade bigger or more mobile. Assimilate Object could be used in a multitude of ways, but had many potential weaknesses—or strengths—hidden by unanswered questions.

The strongest item Ferris owned was the magical chain. Using the ability to make the chain an ‘organ’ could solve two large problems for Jade.

First, the cage meant to hold a slime’s core could work as armor. Ferris was already using it as makeshift protection, but this would improve the defense. The core was a deadly weakness. The cage would help… but would it be enough to keep Jade alive?

Second, the chain could be used to hold onto enemies. Currently, it was easy to throw the slime off before the acid could work, but that wouldn’t be possible with a length of magical metal wrapped around the target.

Ferris shuddered a little at the memory of the chain demons suffocating him. It would be like that, but with magical acid. The similarity between the ability and the demons’ self-moving chains was stark. Ferris wasn’t sure if fighting the chain demons had influenced Adaptation or not; Assimilate Object could control more than chains… but felt oddly suited to them.

If Jade incorporated the magical chain as an organ, was it permanent? ‘Assimilate Object’ implied less flexibility than something like ‘Control Object.’ The construction instructions implied adding or removing organs required changes in the slimespace. If removed, would that destroy it in the real world?

Even worse, in the wrong circumstance, would the chain just give an enemy easy leverage to pull the core out of Jade’s body?

Beyond the strengths and weaknesses of the ability, did it fit Ferris’ path? He was still discovering what that path would be, but something about the ability felt right to him. Ferris had been drawn to the slime familiar bond for the flexibility of Adaptation. Assimilate Object may have unknown weaknesses, but one thing was obvious: the ability was versatile.

That resonated with Ferris in a way Grow hadn’t, despite the other ability’s strengths. Ferris wasn’t sure if he would immediately focus time and resources on Assimilate Object, or if he would use the magic chain, but he did think he would eventually cultivate the ability.

Pocketing the seed, Ferris turned back to the task at hand: gathering qi.

Gleefully, he saw that some of the clams had already produced a new chunk of qi, proudly displayed in their open shells. A bit more than half of them had reopened, while the others were still closed.

Ferris and Jade started harvesting, although only from the middle of the riverbed. He didn’t want to waste the double-length chain on qi chunks that a single-length chain could reach.

The work was long, although more relaxing than the spirit gem refinement had been. The longer chain broke and was replaced twice before he had fully cleared the center. Afterwards, he used one single-length chain to gather the rest along the edges.

The Qi Pool was much restored. Still not quite as large as the bathtub full Ferris had when he left Bluereef, but maybe three-quarters that size. The cost of creating the chains hurt, but now he had the ability to rearm and clear the fort.

As happy as he was to see the pool swell, he needed to use it. First, he created a new cooking pot, as he had no way to remove the hardened demon in his old one. At least he didn’t have to rebuild the frame that held it over the fire.

This let him make a new demon-reinforced spear. Ferris wanted to use what he had learned to make a better spear, and he decided to try using just the bones from the chain demon corpse.

The corpse, despite being much larger than the portions he needed, was starting to look picked over. It had been a gruesome, unpleasant task to separate chunks small enough to fit into the pot, but now that he needed just the bones… it was so much worse.

Ferris had gutted and prepared fish since he’d been old enough to hold a knife, so he at least knew what he was doing. The work was awkward without one of his parents’ extra sharp knives, not to mention the demon wasn’t put together like a fish. Still, the most important skill was how to mentally handle the horrible butchery. Focus on the work, and don’t think about where it came from.

When Ferris was done, the corpse was little more than a mound of fleshy bits. All of its chains had been used for the qi fishing, so there wasn’t much left. He would need a fresh demon corpse soon, but that shouldn’t be difficult.

With liquid qi and bones in roughly equal measure, Ferris cooked and cooled the mixture. Setting his hand in the grey liquid, he pictured a bone spear. Following the nature of bone, he wanted this spear to have both strength and flexibility—along with a vicious point sharp enough to pierce armor. While thinking of the intent of the spear, a strange intuition led his mind to memories of using a spear. Ferris had spent years practicing and training with the weapon, and he fed those thoughts into the heart of the intent.

Pulling his hand—now holding a pale bone spear—out of the pot, Ferris immediately felt a difference. The bone spear he held didn’t radiate light like the magical tools from the refinement device, but it felt more solid and real in his hands than any spear he had ever held, either spiritually or in the real world.

Tentatively, Ferris went through a practice routine with the bone spear. It moved in his hand like an extension of his body. As if the picture in his head of what he wanted effortlessly translated into action, giving him a level of grace he had only seen from his teachers.

Wielding the bone spear was surreal, and for a while, he lost himself to the forms. It was like a meditative trance had taken over him, drifting peacefully from stance to stance.

He lost track of time, adrift in the flow. Slowly and inevitably, he reached the end of the routine, moving back into a ready stance.

In a daze, he sat down to meditate on the experience. He felt like a lifetime of experience had just shifted in his head, settling into a new level of understanding.

When he opened his eyes, he looked down at the bone spear. The soulspace was his soul, and it was said that cultivation could leverage the soul to reflect and train his mind. Somehow, years of practice had been brought into focus and… intensified. He hadn’t learned anything new, but this spear had drawn out a mastery of motion that had previously only existed in his head.

It was like for years he had imagined singing a song in his head, but could never articulate the correct pitch… until suddenly he could. Crafting and training with this bone spear had translated his intentions into actions, showing his body how to do what his mind pictured.

At least, that was what it had felt like.

This raised so many new questions. Could he acquire a spear with understanding beyond his own, and train with it? Was this the method cultivators used to gain unnatural levels of skill and mastery? Sometimes it felt like Adepts were always naturally talented at anything they tried. In fact, his uncle was able to play at least five musical instruments…

Ferris had never really thought much of it; of course the man could do anything and everything better than everyone else. He was an Adept.

Unfortunately, none of the questions had answers, and he still had work to do. Jade had settled down nearby, somehow lost in the feelings of the bond. Now that Ferris was more aware again, the slime rolled over and bounced happily.

Ferris never would have been able to guess from watching, but he felt through the bond that Jade was going through the spear forms… or at least trying. He chuckled at the barely relatable movements, and then looked around for his next task.

To clear the chain fort, he wanted a new crossbow.

Ferris considered whether he could make a better crossbow this time. He didn’t have the skill or knowledge to make the second crossbow blueprint from the jade slip, but he did want to make as good a weapon as possible. Perhaps there were a few small improvements he could make.

He thought he had enough qi left, but the only part of the demon corpse remaining was the flesh. He’d rather use different components.

Ferris smiled at Jade; they just needed to hunt a few demons.
