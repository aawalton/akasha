Ward lunged around the table with a shout, sword raised. Steel flashed as it bore down.

Renner was faster. His dagger hand twitched up, tossing the weapon, and gloved fingers closed over the wicked-looked edge before snapping out. Silver spun through the air, and metal clanged as his larger blade met Ward’s.

Josiah bellowed in pain, staggering back from the knife’s impact.

My eyes followed the motion in disbelieving horror, but I didn’t even have time to scream; Thom was climbing over the table. Coming towards me. I traced.

Agnes shrieked, “Don’t let her-!”

“Veth!” Heat roared through my insides.

For one awful moment, I wondered if it hadn’t been long enough. If I was still drained from earlier. I would ignite the table, and perhaps the men around me, then collapse in the middle of it all. Leaving Renner to fend everyone off while my body slowly roasted and I railed helplessly an entire world away at a Fae who probably thought the whole situation was very amusing.

It was a horrifying thought.

It wasn’t what happened.

What happened was almost worse.

The fire caught on Thom’s tunic, illuminating his heavy jowls in a flash of fierce light. Then his hands as he tried to smother the flames, and then his teeth and tongue as he screamed. Pungent sulfur and burning hair stung my nostrils.

No oh no he’s burning he needs help-

I quashed the urge to lunge forwards and douse the blaze. I could help him after. Assuming I was alive.

Thom fell back, crashing to the floor, curdling my blood with every shriek.

Ward cried out and fell backwards, too, clearly caught off guard as Renner bore down on him. The thin man swung up, shoulders jerking with every parry, and Renner’s sword crashed down. Fast, relentless, nearly breaking past the other man’s blade again and again and again. Metal keened in an ear-splitting, unbroken ring.

I whirled towards Josiah.

The big man was on one knee, face twisted into a rictus of pain, black knifehilt sticking out of one shoulder. He clutched at it, staring with vicious intent towards Renner.

I raised my hands. “Ve-ngh!”

The world flashed white. My palms slammed into wooden floorboards. The back of my head throbbed and an iron lantern clattered to the ground beside me. From behind, Agnes gave a triumphant little hiss.

I heard the rapid thumps and rustles of boots and cloth being pressed against what was likely scorched flesh. Thom was howling. Agnes snapped something at him.

“Help, help, help me now-!” Ward backed up rapidly, sword clenched in both hands. Frantically blocking every blow of Renner’s violent onslaught, but clearly unable to return a strike. Lips drawn back, teeth bared, gray eyes wild. Frenzied.

It was the look of a man who was facing off with death, and knew he was not winning.

Renner’s free hand dropped, spinning another knife from its sheath. His sword flashed high, forcing Ward to lift his own blade up to parry, and the dagger flashed low. The thin man hissed, twisting back, unbloodied but ashen-faced.

My vision sharpened. I pressed my palms against the wood. Got to my knees, gaze swinging towards Josiah as the huge man rose, blood dripping from one dangling arm, and moved towards-

Thick fingers wrenched into my hair. My scalp lit on fire as my head was yanked back. The grip on my hair grew horribly taut. Then stars burst behind my eyes as the hand snapped down, and my face hit the floor with a hollow thud.

I tasted metal. My nose flooded with something sticky and warm. I heard Renner shouting, but it sounded like he was somewhere very far away.

Someone else was screaming, too. Calling me all kinds of vile, vicious things in between wails and oaths. A hand closed over my arm, and another my shoulder, and the world spun as I was dragged onto my back. Crushing weight slammed onto my ribs, forcing the air from my lungs.

Hazy gray smoke framed Thom’s face. The fire was out, but its damage had been done. Blistered red flesh shone beneath ruined clothes, spreading up onto his shoulders and the underside of his jaw. The smell of burnt hair and acrid, melted fat made my gut rise.

I took him in, the anguish and pain and fury on his livid face. For a moment, I felt sorry for him. I wanted to apologize.

And then his fist came down.

Everything went white. My ears rang. Heat spread out from my lips, smeared and spattered its way across my nose and chin and cheeks. Shaking hands lifted without a thought to cover my face. He dragged one away. The next blow left a sharp impact on one cheek, snapping my head to the side.

My vision spun. Too bright. Hazy. But I saw the silver. It was blinding. For a moment, I thought it would hit me.

It didn’t.

There was an awful, wet gasp. Like someone trying to breathe through a throat-full of water. The weight pinning me down shifted as Thom fell to the side, one hand clutching at his ribs and the dark hilt buried within them. It pulled away crimson. He gaped down, head swaying back and forth, mouth moving soundlessly.

He’s got the trembles.

I sucked in air through numb, split lips. Choked on a mouthful of blood.

Gotta live. Gotta thank him. Can’t die. Don’t die.

I couldn’t seem to make my limbs move, much less push myself upright, but I managed to roll my eyes towards the sound of ringing metal. The upside-down angle made me feel nauseous, but my heart rose along with the bile.

Josiah had joined the fight, one hand choked up on the haft of his axe. He lumbered after Renner, grunting with every slow, forceful swing. The smaller man danced back, ducking blows and batting away Ward’s lunges, keeping table and chairs between them. Free hand falling, fingers wrapping around another wicked blade. Working it free.

His face shone with sweat. He was panting. It almost looked like a fight he couldn’t win. Almost.

But, even upside-down and half-conscious, I recognized that look.

Calm. Lethal. About to make a brutal choice.

He slipped behind a table. Spun the dagger out, gloved fingers closing over the edge. Then he actually winked at Ward. The other man bit out a curse, lunging toward him, sword crashing sidelong. Steel whined as the thrust was swatted aside.

Josiah’s boot slammed furiously into the table, sending it crashing to the floor with an explosion of wood and clattering dinnerware. The enormous man staggered as he did so, drifting to one side. He was slowing down. Breathing hard. He couldn’t catch up.

He’s going to win. Saints, he’s actually going to win.

Hope swelled like a rising dawn. And was torn away.

Something tugged at my belt, and a gnarled hand clapped over my mouth. Two bony knees dug into each arm, pinning my wrists to the floor.

And something very cold and sharp settled against my throat.

“Stop!”

The three men froze.

Agnes’ voice was level. She glowered at my companion. “I’ll slit her throat. Drop your weapons.”

The chilly steel pressed down. My pulse thrummed against it like a trapped bird.

Renner didn’t move. He was looking at me.

“I’ll do it,” she hissed. “I’ve done it before. It’s not difficult.”

She can’t mean that. No one can mean that.

My thoughts spun. Vaguely, I was aware that I should have been very frightened. Weeping, perhaps. Begging her.

Instead I just felt… a hazy, distant indignance.

He tried so hard. He fought so hard. He almost saved us. He shouldn’t die because of me.

My eyes lolled towards Renner. I couldn’t speak, but I met his eyes and managed to shake my head a bit. Even managed a muffled, “Mmph, mm-mm. Mm-mm!”

It wasn’t quite as good as shouting ‘run, save yourself!’ But it was a good try.

“I’ll do it,” Agnes hissed. Bony fingers tightened, digging painfully into my cheek, and the knife pressed down-

Steel clattered to the floor.

I moaned denials against her wrinkled skin. Felt her shiver a bit, watched her eyes drift shut for a breath, and realized with grim satisfaction that she had been very afraid.

Good.

“Good man,” she said in a short, clipped tone. “Ward, come help me. Get her gagged and bind her wrists.” She snapped her eyes to Renner. “The knife stays right here. Anything from you, any movement, and I’ll-”

“I get the idea,” he snarled.

No. No, don’t give up. Throw a knife. Pick up your sword. Run out the door. Don’t just stop!

I squirmed as hard as I could, trying to buck off the woman’s slight frame. She grunted but held fast.

He can still win. If I can get her off, even for a moment, maybe he can-

“Him, too?” Josiah panted, broad chest swelling with every breath. Scarlet spattered the wood around his feet.

“Her first. Then, yes. Strip off your armor,” she snapped at Renner. “The cloak, boots, everything. You seem like the type to have weapons tucked away.”

Renner hesitated. He looked torn.

I met his gaze. Flicked mine towards the door with another muffled moan. Begged him to run.

He didn’t.

I closed my eyes, tears sliding out as I listened to the rustling of cloth, leather, and concealed metal being stripped away.

“You’re making a mistake.” His voice was even and low. Promising violence, even now.

Agnes scoffed. Her hand lifted, freeing my mouth just long enough for Ward to shove a wad of cloth into it. What followed was an awful few moments of struggling and weeping as he tied a gag, then flipped me onto my stomach. He bound my wrists so tightly that my hands started to tingle painfully with the need for blood.

“Check her for more weapons. You never know,” Agnes ordered. She kept the knife pressed to my throat and kept her spiteful, narrowed eyes on Renner. I heard more stripping leather alongside the soft swish of cloth.

Ward slid his narrow, pinching fingers down my sides, to my belt and trousers, poking and prodding as he went. Then he yanked off my boots.

I forced myself to remain still, sucking in wet, thick gasps of air through my burning nose.

“That was it.” He sniffed, nodding towards the dagger she’d taken from my belt. Then he gave my friend a vicious leer. “Your turn.”

Everything of Renner’s was on the ground save his tunic and dark, singed trousers. He spread both hands, stone-faced, as Ward stalked over with a fresh length of rope.

A few moments later, he sat bound and gagged beside me. Josiah turned to Agnes, who was tending to Thom. The man lay on his side nearby, still clutching at the knife in his ribs. His face was ashen. Every breath was a shallow gasp.

“He gonna live?” Josiah rumbled.

She sniffed. “Depends on Baltha. I’ll ask her for more of that foul herb. For you, too,” she gestured with a wince to the big man’s shoulder.

“And the plan?” Ward rubbed his jaw. “Still take ‘em to the cage?”

Agnes thought for a moment. “Yes. Yes. I’ll have Aveline stay here and tend to Thom. We’ll walk them down and wait for her. I think with two, so soon after the other, she might feel generous.”

Josiah grunted, then collapsed into a chair and closed his eyes. His face shone with a heavy sheen of sweat.

Agnes continued, “We’ll bury their belongings tomorrow. With Thom, if we need to. Ugh. Poor dear. You vicious little witch.” She spat on me.

I turned away, fighting for breath through my clogged nostrils. Then the inn door creaked open, and my blood ran cold.

Terrence took one step inside and froze. The color drained from his face and he gave a garbled, choked cry. “What happened? What’s, what is this? Why-”

“Terrence, dear,” Anne’s voice rose to a sharp pitch. “It was terrible! They’ve been in it together. Admitted as much, and look what they did to poor Thom!”

He spun all around, tawny hair swaying and jaw dropped wide. “What? But that… that doesn’t make any-”

Ward rolled his eyes, slashing one hand towards the fragrant mug on the bar. “Oh, salt all this, just have him drink the damn wine. I’ll lock him in a room and he won’t know any better by morning.”

Terrence balked. Then he dragged his feet backwards very slowly, staring at Ward. “W-what? Know any better? What?” Frightened eyes shifted towards my face. He choked out a cry, growing even more pale. “Saints, what did you do to her?!”

“Don’t fret, love.” Agnes poured spices into the mug. She tilted it this way and that in her hands, giving the young man a tight smile. “We don’t want you mixed up in all this.”

He was breathing hard. Hands clenching. Looking around like a trapped animal.

“It didn’t work,” came a murmur from near the door. Aveline had shut it behind her and was frowning at me. “That one- she had three cups. At least. It didn’t work.”

I blinked. My left cheek was swelling, and my eye along with it. Everything looked watery and too bright.

Agnes paused. “You’re sure? What about him?” She jerked a thumb towards the silent figure beside me.

She sniffed. “Well, he didn’t have any. She interrupted us. It wasn’t my fault.”

The older woman snorted. “Didn’t say it was. Hm. You said Lewen was none the wiser?”

Aveline flicked her dark hair back. “Yes. But I don’t know about the others. Didn’t think to… I wanted to come see you when I heard. She…” the beautiful young woman bit down on one plump lip, peering at Agnes. She looked almost frightened. “She shouldn’t have come into town. She’s not supposed to. That’s not the deal.”

“What are you all talking about?” Terrence moaned. He looked like he was going to faint.

So they drug the other townsfolk. They give them some kind of herb to make them forget when people go missing. Ashes. And it didn’t work on me, for some reason. Thank you, thank you, any gods or saints who can hear, thank you.

Thank you that I didn’t forget Teela.

Agnes tsked, snapping her bony fingers. “Right. Well. This will be a bit more difficult, I suppose.” She fixed her sharp gaze on the shivering young man. “Master Terrence, you’re going to come for a walk with us. And you’re going to see something very frightening, but you’ll be safe. I promise.”

I railed against my bonds. The ropes dug into my wrists and I choked on blood as I tried to shout out from behind the gag, jerking my head back and forth in pleading denial.

They’re going to have him make a deal. And then he’ll be part of all this. He won’t be able to help it.

What if he refuses?

Could the creature erase his memories of this grisly scene? Would he wake tomorrow, pleasantly baffled but ready to believe the story of Renner attacking us in the woods?

If not… if he refused… would they kill him? Feed him to the hag?

Terrence’s eyes were shining. “I… I don’t want to. I’m not going to.”

Agnes drifted forwards. “Sweetheart, I’m afraid it’s not a choice.”
