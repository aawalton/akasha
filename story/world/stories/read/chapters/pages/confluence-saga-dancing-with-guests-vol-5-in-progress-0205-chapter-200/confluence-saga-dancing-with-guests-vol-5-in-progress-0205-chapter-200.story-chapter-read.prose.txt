I've lived almost everywhere you can imagine, and many places you can't.

A week here, a decade there, a year hiding under a rock while creatures you think are made up myths searched for me.

So believe me when I tell you that everywhere is the same, despite how different it might seem. The rulers rule, the guards guard, the people live their lives with some degree of awareness of the structure of their society. Take away their differing appearances, their cultural biases, their local customs and you are left with a society that could fit anywhere.

Of course you can't take those things away, I'm well aware of that. But learn how to live within those details and no one will see you as anything but a newcomer or visitor, a distant friend they forgot they knew.

Learn to live in a Husk that isn't you, to survive the symptoms that come from the disassociation or dysmorphia? Then you can live anywhere, be anyone you need to. Then you'll be ready for your next assignment.

Fay set her fork down and smiled at Bri. They were out for a surprise dinner for the third time in as many weeks. Each time Bri had set something up without letting her know, despite the fact that Fay was certain this could not have been a last minute decision. The place was too fancy, and too busy, for it not to have been planned at least a few weeks ago.

"I figured it out," she said.

"I know."

"You've set up reservations on the days you know I'm going to be testing the changes to the integrations. You're making sure I remember to stop and spend time with you."

"I'm making sure you don't get so absorbed in your work you lose track," Bri answered. "And I know that each time you've gone back in the morning with answers you came up with while we were out."

"Why pretend it's a surprise?" Fay asked.

That was the part she couldn't figure out. Bri was right about the rest. She would have gotten caught up in the intricacies and forgotten what time it was. She would have worked through the night if not the next day on getting the tiniest issue resolved and found herself caught in a loop.

Talking about it with Bri, telling her where she was stuck had given her ideas that seemed so obvious in retrospect. Not to mention it meant spending time with her, which was always a good thing.

"To see when you'd figure it out." Bri said. "To give you a puzzle to solve that has nothing to do with Constructs or Techniques or Seer Secrets or whatever Yshe knows that you would never have thought an Engineer would."

"How many more reservations are there?"

"Not telling," Bri said with a laugh. "That will ruin the surprises to come. You do remember that you have to wrap up early tomorrow, we have a resort to explore."

Fay smiled before nodding. She wouldn't have time to test everything she wanted to, at least not if she wanted to see the results the same day. Then again leaving her presence detection to run for several days before it actually could react to her might turn up some issues she hadn't considered.

"And then we're visiting them next week."

"I know," Bri said. "I'm not the one who forgets what day it is when there's a problem to be solved. I'm not the one who is trying to set up a system that will remind her of her commitments so that she doesn't get scolded by her fiance."

"I didn't use to have these issues," Fay said with a frown. "I knew what work I had to do, when everyone expected their updates. When I still worked in the Council building I knew it was time to get going because-"

"You knew because the routine ran around you. Just like you knew when they would return from their latest Dive. The work you were doing could be finished. This new work is something where no one else expects the answer."

Was that what it was? Not having a deadline, not having any expectations except her own? Not feeling like half of what she did was pointless and simply a matter of satisfying a request that likely wouldn't be looked at?

She knew Valene cared about her reports. She knew he used the information she sent him. But even then, couldn't he have done it himself? Probably, but he also likely had other priorities. As for the work the Council sent her? The work she had done overall? The fact that it was never brought up again after delivery, even when she knew it wasn't as complete as it could be, had left her feeling as though no one actually cared.

There had even been times she'd felt that way with Anya and Seb. She'd done research, found answers, and they hadn't needed it. They'd figured things out on their own. But they still listened, they still talked about it with her. They still-

"Fay."

"Sorry, I was just-"

"Thinking about why you keep getting caught up?"

"And how much of the time it felt like the work was pointless or wasted."

"It never was," Bri said.

"It felt like it. They didn't actually care, they just wanted the report."

"You cared, you learned. You wouldn't have known the details about Lord Percivus if you hadn't gone digging about something else. You wouldn't have stumbled on to this newest project if you hadn't been nosy."

"I wouldn't have met you if I hadn't gone looking for better coffee."

"Exactly."

"I love you."

"I know," Bri said. "I love you too."

"Thank you for understanding, for being there."

"I knew from the first time you walked in that you would get distracted. Now are we getting dessert tonight?"

"To go?" Fay suggested.

The look in Bri's eyes was all the answer she needed.

Seer Deirdre had been the most interesting. She had met with each team individually, forcing the rest to wait in the lecture hall.
