Chapter V.XXXV (5.35) - Squeak

The Dragon burst from the tree and took flight. He shot straight up in the air, relying solely on his powerful initial push off and not even bothering to unfurl his wings until he slowed, hundreds of meters over the forest. As gravity tore him back down to the land of mortals, the Dragon finally stretched out his wings and allowed them to unfurl and snatch the air beneath him. That extension offered him control of his descent.

Kizu watched, heart beating fast, as Sojan dove out of the sky in his direction. Beside him, Anata gripped his arm. A brief glance at her revealed not fear on her face, but rather jubilation. Her eyes were wide, her uncovered scarlet eye sparkling in the sunlight, and an open smile revealed her elongated canine.

Sojan crashed into the trees beside them, knocking down a dozen of the smaller ones on his impact. The wood crunched under his weight and velocity. Kizu noted that there were now hundreds of trees nearby toppled over and uprooted from the dagger’s antics.

“Kizu!” Sojan greeted him. “You didn’t even flinch this time!” The Dragon bore its teeth in what might be either a toothy smile or a dangerous leer. Knowing Sojan, Kizu decided to take the gesture as the former of the two options.

“As if I have anything to worry about,” Kizu replied. “You’d bounce right off one of my barriers. The only reason I didn’t put one up was because I figured it might knock you loose.”

“Trying to act tough in front of your niece? You fleshbags care too much about what others think of you.” Sojan huffed and a plume of black soot puffed out of his snout.

Before Kizu could come up with an appropriately snarky reply with, Anata scrambled over the fallen tree trunks and threw herself at Sojan. Her small form smacked into his scaled leg and she clung to him.

“Hello, little Anata!” Sojan greeted the girl. “It’s been several months now. Careful. Don’t cut yourself on my scales. The last thing I want is your disgusting blood smeared all over me.”

Anata let go of the Dragon leg and crossed her arms in a pout.

Sojan snickered and sat back, snapping more branches.

“Do you still drink her blood every day?” Sojan asked Kizu.

Kizu hesitated. Sojan was the only one other than himself, Mort, and Anata who knew about the properties of Anata’s blood. Kizu had purposefully resisted the urge to study the hyper blood restoration capabilities of the Harbinger’s blood.

“I… don’t ever ask her any,” Kizu replied, a bit more defensively than he’d intended. It was completely Anata’s decision to donate the blood to his growth.

“But you do? How often?”

“Maybe once every day or two. She has to be careful to not alert anyone to her blood’s properties.”

The Dragon’s face contorted and for a moment Kizu thought it was anger.

“I can’t understand why anyone in their right mind would willingly consume that filth. Blech. Just the memory of that taste sticks to my mind like grime. No amount of good blood since has managed to flush off that residue from my memory.”

Then Kizu placed the Dragon’s expression. Not anger, but disgust. His heartbeat slowed at the realization and he offered his friend a shaky smile.

Hands on her hips, Anata kicked the Dragon’s leg. Sojan roared in response. He thrashed about, his tail whipping into downed trees and sending them flying. All carefully orchestrated so as to not hurt the little Harbinger.

Anata giggled and dashed back over to Kizu to avoid Sojan’s dramatic tantrum.

Sojan ceased his fake fit and slumped down on the fallen deforested patch of wood. From there, Kizu and Sojan chatted about recent events while Anata scurried among the random logs, peeking up occasionally.

It was extremely apparent that Sojan was bored with his life out in the forest. The cursed knife enjoyed yapping and the few sentient magical creatures on this side of the island avoided him on sight.

“If the blood weren’t so delectable, I might even consider letting you stab someone new with me,” Sojan said. He mournfully puffed a small cloud of smoke through his flared nostrils. “Alas, I’m far too weak willed to allow this bountiful supply of euphoria slip through my serrated teeth.”

“Has the headmaster spoken to you about the long term?” Kizu asked. “You can’t stay out here forever.”

“There’s been talk,” Sojan replied evasively.

“Talk?”

“Yes. Precisely the word I said! Well done, Kizu! Your short term memory is impeccable. No sign of recent brain damage. I can make that diagnosis without even needing to be inserted into your spine.”

Kizu opened his mouth to retort and try to pry the information out of the dagger when he realized something felt odd. It took him a moment to place it. The distinct lack of a giggle at Sojan’s funny comment.

Scanning the landscape covered in piles of dead wood, Kizu saw no familiar black bangs. No movement at all.

“Anata?” he called out. “Where’d you go?”

No response.

Still, Kizu did his best to control his rapidly increasing heartbeat and stomp down on his nerves. He’d only been talking to Sojan for a few minutes. She couldn’t have gone far. No need to panic.

Kizu jumped into the air overhead and created a spatial barrier under his feet. From his vantage point Kizu saw… piles of overturned trees.

Sojan turned his draconic head skyward and locked eyes with Kizu.

“No sign of her?” the dagger asked.

“No.” Kizu jumped forward a bit and tried from a different direction as he fruitlessly searched. “I shouldn’t have brought her here. Taroe said as much when I mentioned our outing to him. It was only supposed to be a quick visit. What could go wrong? Any wild monsters are easily controlled by Anata. There’s nothing to be feared by anything out here. Especially with you around to scare off most predators.”

“She can’t control me,” Sojan pointed out, breaking through Kizu’s frantic rambling. “Who else can’t she control?”

“Humans. And Tainted. And Kemon. And gnomes.” Kizu considered. Then he felt the color drain from his face. Once, during his conversations about soul magic, someone had mentioned to him that Tainted and Kemon were related similarly to gnomes and another species.

Goblins.

—

Last week Anata had spotted Mae and Rui running off hand in hand. They’d later been hiding in a corner together, whispering. The experience had caused something to spark in her. Not jealously, exactly. But a strange sense of longing. It reminded her of when she’d been a prisoner, watching the world pass by without the ability to interact with it. She wanted the same thing Mae and Rui had.

So, when a green boy her size poked his head out from under a tree and offered his hand to her, Anata had decided to experiment and see if she could understand Mae’s reasoning a bit better.

Part of her knew it to be a very silly idea. But there was a thrill when he took her hand and tugged her along. She figured she could always just open her mouth and demand to be released if she found herself in any real danger. She silently promised to herself that she would be back before Kizu and Sojan even noticed she was gone.

Anata followed the little green boy through the foliage beyond Sojan’s collapsed stretch of the woods. He grunted unintelligible noises that Anata vaguely detected to be happy. She noted that Basil would probably have a fit if he ever saw the state of the rag the green man wore as a loincloth. Resisting a laugh at the thought, she examined her new friend more thoroughly.

A smooshed face and scraggily hair, the little man kept glancing back at her and squealing, as if surprised she was still there every time. His palm was clammy and his skin slick. Likely a result of the heat and humidity. Anata could empathize with the discomfort. Since he didn’t speak any language she recognized, Anata decided to bestow upon him the name Squeak. That’s how she’d think of him.

They entered a clearing and Squeak’s grip on her hand tightened as a dozen others of his same species all turned their gazes on him and her. A bonfire burned at the center of the clearing with several skewers of meat sticking out around its edge.

Squeak gesticulated with his free hand while squealing. The language was rather odd. It relied on high pitched tones that fluctuated. Anata listened closely but it was completely different from all verbal languages used by anyone at the academy or their side of the island.

Another little green person emerged from a bush. This one had his large, bat-like, ears pierced by several small bones. He held a staff. Also made of bones. Based on the quantity of bone accessories, Anata surmised he must be some sort of necromancer like Aoi. He stood no higher than the rest of his people, but they clearly showed deference to his authority as they bowed their heads and quieted their high chitters.

The necromancer pointed his staff at her and Squeak. Her companion yelped then dropped her hand and scurried around her, hands on her shoulders as he hid ineffectually.

“Lower the bone stick,” Anata demanded, not liking how it was pointed in her direction.

The necromancer waggled the staff in her direction but did not comply. He seemed to be making a demand of her as well. In the mess of noise, Anata caught a single familiar name. Otochi.

That set off her nerves even more than the lack of reaction from her verbal demand. She tried again.

“Go away!”

Rather than listen, the necromancer stepped in her direction. The exact opposite of her demand. He was growling something in a low pitch, unlike the higher tones Anata had heard used for normal speech by the others of his species.

Squeak shoved her and Anata fell the dirt in front of the necromancer.

The world around her dimmed as the necromancer raised his staff high. Despite the lack of light, the bonfire to her side grew in size and consumed the sticks of meat lined up around it. The heat amplified. The green people all scurried away.

The deep voice of the necromancer thrummed and consumed all noise. Anata tried one last time to order this creature away from her but her words were lost even to her.

Anata tried to call on her power. Her words were useless against this necromancer so she opted for a different strategy. She pulled back the invisible veil that kept her from wild monster detection and silently willed anything in the vicinity to rescue her.

The green necromancer brought down his staff, aimed at Anata’s head. She raised her arms to protect her skull from the impact of the glowing collection of bound up bones. A familiar pose. She’d taken it several times when her father’s servants had been upset. Not enough blood available from her to cover the entire population of servants.

However, the familiar expected pain never spiked through her.

Anata opened her eyes and found color had returned to the world.

A man with green hair and a blue uniform stood between herself and the necromancer. He held the staff in one hand. A monstrous leg tore through his boot and dug into the soil.

Kizu addressed the necromancer, his voice cold and deadly. Fury radiated from her uncle as he spoke two words.

“Get. Lost.”

—

Kizu’s grip on the goblin’s bone staff tightened with enhanced strength. It snapped and the magic released from the energized artifact exploded out. Thankfully, while Kizu had jumped in immediately upon spotting Anata, he’d already adapted to prepare a safety net for them.

An antimagic barrier dispersed the staff’s magic as the weapon chaotically discharged its energy.

Still, Kizu only set up the antimagic wall between himself and the goblin. Not behind Anata’s assaulter.

He watched as the energy fried his goblin attacker in an instant, turning the green man into little more than a pile of goblin bones and bone jewelry. The goblins beyond him fared a bit better, as he’d taken the brunt of the damage. Many of them only lost limbs or smaller bits of their bodies. Bones devoid of flesh clattered to the forest floor as the goblins screamed in pain.

Good thing for them they didn’t need to suffer for long.

A roar overhead eclipsed the goblin yowls. The clearing went dark and a blast of wind sent leaves and sticks flying into the air. The bonfire blazed to new heights as it fed on the draft. For a moment, that blaze served as the primary light in the clearing as a shadow blocked out the sun above them.

A light, brighter than the earlier sunshine, blossomed from within the shadow.

Kizu huddled over Anata, shielding her body.

Rather than protect them with barriers, Kizu opted for the safer option. He jumped both himself and Anata back to the campus, tapping into the academy’s beacon. Neither of them witnessed the flaming carnage that befell that portion of the forest.

Only days later, during his next visit with Sojan, did Kizu take in the blackened wreckage. Nothing remained of the goblins. Only a crater of soot remained where the clearing had once been.

Unlike Kizu’s last encounter with goblins, he did not fall to his knees and puke. Instead, he bent down and picked up a handful of ash. He let it slip through his fingers. The breeze snatched the ash midair and scattered it.

His earring had struggled to translate the words of the goblins. It likely had to do with them speaking a unique dialect of a language or the muddled understanding they had of one another. However, it had caught a single word before Kizu jumped in to protect Anata.

The necromancer had called her, “Harbinger.”

Kizu stared at his blackened hand while he wondered how, exactly, an uncivilized and barely sentient species knew what Anata was.
