An Eui sat on her bed, hugging her knees to her chest and staring at the spartan walls of the bedroom she shared with Jia. Each side of the room had a bed, a chest, and a wardrobe, but the walls were completely barren. Eui had never felt comfortable decorating the room, and Jia simply never saw the need.

Despite her dour mood, she couldn’t help but smile at the idea that Jia found this hovel they lived in to be the height of luxury. She never seemed to take anything for granted—it was adorable. Of course, thinking about Jia just brought Eui back to why she was in a bad mood in the first place.

For the past half a year, Eui had rarely ever been alone. She and Jia did almost everything together now—cultivation, training, socializing with friends, even bathing sometimes. Eui’s face grew hot and she hugged her knees more tightly. The one thing they didn’t do together is study.

Eui had joined Jia and Hyeong for their study sessions a few times, but it had been awkward and uncomfortable. If it was just that, then maybe Eui could have tolerated it, but ever since their breakthrough it had become impossible for Jia and Eui to hide their emotions from each other. There was no way Eui could hide her feelings while watching Hyeong’s awkward flirting fly straight over Jia’s oblivious head.

“You should have told her already. Now it’s too late, you’ve ruined everything.”

The voice in Eui’s head was familiar—it was her own, after all. For as long as she could remember, her thoughts would turn against her like that. Reminding her of all her failings, the mistakes she made, how stupid she was. Until recently, that voice had gone unchallenged.

“It’s not too late! Jia will understand! Even if it is your fault, she’ll forgive you!”

The second voice wasn’t hers, but it was welcome. A little piece of Jia, the optimist, the one who took nothing for granted, the one who knew Eui better than anyone else and accepted her anyway. The girl who—despite Eui’s best efforts—she’d fallen in love with.

Eui buried her face in her knees and bit her tongue. She’d promised herself she wouldn’t let that happen—that Jia wouldn’t become a second Sun Jaehwa. Even admitting her feelings to herself in her thoughts felt like a betrayal. It was a wonder Jia hadn’t already discovered her secret.

“It is a betrayal! You betrayed Jia, you betrayed Jaehwa, and you betrayed yourself!”

“You don’t owe Sun Jaehwa anything, and you can’t choose your feelings. Jia will understand!”

That thought was a small comfort, but even with reassurances, Eui couldn’t bring herself to believe it entirely. Would Jia really accept her? If she did, could Eui bear with mere acceptance? She was—reasonably sure that Jia wouldn’t accept her feelings. Tolerate them, forgive them, yes, but not accept them. Not—not reciprocate them.

Eui wasn’t sure her heart could handle being broken a second time.

“You’d deserve it! What have you ever done to earn love? You’re worthless!”

“...”

Eui felt like crying. Jia’s voice was always conspicuously silent when it came to matters of love, just like the real thing. Eui had lived with Jia, felt her emotions, shared her thoughts—and it was like there was a huge romance shaped gap in her mind.

It wasn’t that Jia was unaware of romance, or that she didn’t notice Hyeong’s clumsy flirtation, or even—even that she was unaware of Eui’s feelings. It was more like she pretended not to notice. Convinced herself that she didn’t so thoroughly that she even believed it. How else could Eui possibly keep her feelings secret?

Even Rika had noticed, much to Eui’s embarrassment. It had been a pleasant surprise to find someone else like her in this place. She wasn’t sure how she had figured out that they were the same—they had both just known, somehow. Since then, Takeda Rika had been the only other friend and confidant Eui had, other than Jia.

Eui sighed, why couldn’t she have fallen for Rika instead? It would have been so much easier, that way. Rika really wasn’t her type, though—too tall—and Eui was fairly certain she had her eyes on someone else, anyway.

She shook her head and tried to get her thoughts back on track. Her head had been such a jumbled mess of fears and anxieties lately that it was hard to keep them all organized. Her complicated relationship with Jia, the unprecedented nature of her new form of cultivation, the demonic core that even now was slowly feeding on her own essence—so many problems without any solutions.

Ever since their breakthrough, Jia and Eui’s cultivation was inexorably linked. They could meditate on their own, but the effect was miniscule. Ienaga had warned them about it, and even told them to make their commitment one way or another before breaking through to the second stage of cultivation. They had made that commitment the very same day, but—Eui had intended to give Jia a second chance, after revealing her feelings.

“How convenient for you then, that you broke through before you got the chance! I’m sure the butterfly will be delighted to learn that she’s caught in the spider’s web.”

“It’s not like that! We couldn’t have anticipated this!”

Eui clutched her head as her own mind went to war with itself. Why was she like this? Why couldn’t she just do the things she knew she should, even when she wanted to?

“Because when you do what you think is right, people die.”

“You saved Sun Jaehwa! You did what you had to do to survive!”

“You didn’t have to kill those travellers!”

Eui shook her head. This wasn’t helping at all—she needed to focus on something else. The demonic core! She closed her eyes and focused within herself, to that place next to her heart where the core slowly absorbed a fraction of the essence that circulated within her body. It kept her cultivation stable, no matter how much she used the destruction element, but at a cost.

Eui wasn’t entirely sure what the cost was, really. It just felt like the more it absorbed, the more of herself it took with it. As if the more she fed it, the less human she would become. It terrified her, but without it she would never have mastered the first form of her Six Arms of Asura technique.

Before, the slightest failure in controlling her ki would send it rampaging through her body. In small amounts it would cause injuries, but in larger amounts—she had nearly met the same fate that the creators of the technique had, to be consumed by her own essence. Now, the core would absorb the uncontrolled ki without any harm—it made it much safer to practice.

Since the expedition, she’d been able to master the first form—or arm, as the manual referred to them—and had made significant strides on the second. The demonic core would cover for her mistakes, but it got stronger every time it did. Thinking back to that sword demon Jianmo, his core had become so large that it visibly protruded from his chest.

Would that happen to her, if she continued to rely on it? At what point would she lose her humanity entirely, becoming a demon in body, mind, and soul?

“You never had any humanity to begin with. You were always a demon. This is just proof.”

“That isn’t true!”

It wasn’t true, Eui knew that even if she didn’t feel it. She had done research on demons with Jia, and they were supposed to be ruthless, soul-stealing killers without a shred of humanity. All demons had a core, but at the very least it seemed that having one didn’t necessarily turn you into a demon right away.

“You’re stalling. Trying to distract yourself from what you have to do.”

“You can’t keep it a secret forever. You have to tell her or it will just lead to both of you getting hurt.”

Damn it. The only thing worse than the voices in her head arguing was when they agreed. They were right, of course—the longer she put it off, the worse it would be for both of them. She was just scared. Eui took a deep breath to strengthen her resolve. She would do it, she would confess her feelings to Jia—

“I’m back! Eui, you should have seen Dae today. He was freaking out about his master coming to the academy, it was hilarious!”

—tomorrow. She’d tell her tomorrow, for sure.

“Coward.”
