Under a torrent of blurred pink light, the Jawgore’s bruised skin split, tenderized muscles tore, and bones fractured.

A playing card of pure white light cracked through the air like a bullet, slicing through the monster’s head, exploding within its skull. Obliterated brains burst out across the courtyard.

Strips of bleeding flesh littered the ground as a hail of glowing bullets fell from the dark rain-cloud above, scouring the resistant third Jawgore. With half of its bloody skeleton showing, it slumped to the ground. Ren shook her hand off and canceled her spell.

Max dusted himself down and gave the demonic hound a pat on the head. “I was having such a good time, I didn’t check to see which of us killed first.”

The elf rested her rifle across her shoulder and shot the magician a glare. “I think you know, but don’t want to say.”

He shrugged and sent the hound back to hell with a wave. “Perhaps, as the gracious host, I’ll buy the first round at the tavern, anyway.”

Ren rolled her eyes.

After wiping the blood from his face, Theo stepped over to the pair. “I can’t really eat or drink here anyway, but I appreciate the consideration.”

He looked at the three dead Jawgores. While definitely a lot tougher than expected, it hadn’t taken too long to whittle them down with the overpowered skills they had. The important thing for him was that the two magicians could keep up. So far, they’d exceeded his expectations at every step.

The vampire smiled and looked out at the carnage spread across the courtyard. “Now we have some time to loot, at least.”

“Three steps ahead of you.” Max adjusted his hat. “Although there probably isn’t anything worth your time, unless you’re short on consumables?”

“Wouldn’t say no to any buffs. We’re going to need them.” Theo went into his Inventory and withdrew some glass bottles he had found earlier.

Before either magician could question him, a vibration ran through the ground. The three turned their eyes to the temple, which began to shake and crack. Stone tore apart as debris tumbled down to the edge of the courtyard.

“This is efficient, at least.” Ren took a few steps back to ready her rifle. “Not often a whole dungeon comes for you.”

Theo wiped his mouth. “I’m not one for counting chickens…”

As thick chunks of the temple crashed down into the courtyard, several large arms burst out from the darkness within. The first was humanoid, even though it was the size of a car. Then a crab claw, followed by a spiked tentacle. Vibrations continued to shake the air as the creature within emerged from its rocky coccoon.

“Ah.” Max pulled a face. “If it isn’t the amalgamation of the remaining Guardians, I assume?”

Theo adjusted the grip on his hammer and grinned.

Crimson fire burned from Sally’s eyes as she stood in place, tense. She adjusted her footing slightly as she grinned.

Half of the roaches were packing up their equipment, or readying themselves to be picked up by the new vessel hovering over the forest. The rest had their weapons trained on her, for if she tried anything silly - or their leader gave them a command to turn her into mulch.

It was exhausting drawing so much energy into her tired and broken body. The Radochs were clearly blind to what was going on, and all she had to do was wait.

One of the smaller bugs jittered back and forth in place before glancing up at the enthroned leader. “Sire! There’s a problem communicating with the ship.”

Graken tapped his thin fingers together in annoyance. “What kind of problem?”

“T-they’re not responding. I can’t tell if they’re even receiving my request for retrieval.”

“Perhaps it is that asshole, Dezz’ol. He’s never forgiven me since that planet with the strange wolves.” His mandibles clicked as he glared up at the vessel. “Still, it is protocol to respond to a hail.”

Sally chuckled. “You don’t get it, do you? Must arrogance be the demise of every last one of your kind?”

Graken turned his ire toward the zombie and flexed a fist in her direction. “Quiet, corpse. If she moves, shoot her.”

“S-sire?”

Most eyes went upward to where the small Radoch pointed in shock. Some still had the wherewithal to keep themselves trained on Sally, but they were missing out.

A faint blue sheen ran over the new Radoch vessel. Then it burst. Rather than an explosion, what was once solid metal fell downward in thick plumes of smoke. It was as if a mile-wide dark avalanche dropped straight from the sky.

“It is a trap, capture-” Graken began, before the thick blanket of fog hit the forest floor, darkening a large swathe of the area surrounding them.

“Dispersing the mist!” Another Radoch called out.

The whir of machinery amid the sudden, blind commotion stopped at the crack and buzz of something electronic. With a pulse of air, the atmosphere changed, and a small area surrounding the clearing quickly cleared of smoke. Fading from dark gray to a pale white, each of the bugs came back into view.

As did Sally, although she was no longer in the same place.

She stood close to the grouping of elite bugs, a broken tablet in her mouth. With a hiss, the ability-limited area fizzled away. She spat the electronics onto the ground as the technician she stole it from staggered back.

The skin on her legs split as roots grew from within. Snaking tendrils twisted from her ruined arms. As her eyes darkened, she said nothing at all.

Hunter pushed his hat up and narrowed his eyes. “Can’t see nothin’ now. Should we go find Sally?”

“No rush. She has currently transformed into a giant plant and is eating the remaining Radochs.” Archie rubbed his palm with a thumb. “We’d only get in the way at this point.”

“Hmm. I’ll take your word for it. That must be why the dwarves vanished.”

The wizard shot him a dry smile before glancing over at the smoking mountainside. Bully had managed to make it to the last three Radochs before taking a crossbow bolt through the head. He was now safely back on the Omen, and probably glad to be out of the war-zone.

Aside from debris and corpses, nothing remained.

“Thanks for holding the line. Greater Illusion+ and Fogfall took all I had, pretty much. Hopefully you’re not as drained.” Archie sighed.

Hunter spun his revolver in his hands a couple of times. “Hard to say. Part of me feels fresh as a daisy. Other part is congested. Whether that’s a limitation of my form, or I just haven’t grown enough… who knows?”

The wizard brushed dirt and dust from his robes. “All answers we hope to find in due time. For now, we must continue in ignorance until time is less pressing. Let’s take a stroll, as Sally should be finished by the time we reach her.”

After a moment to check the surrounding area, the pair started their way down the mountain. Smoldering trees lay ruined as the fighting had destroyed much of this early part of the forest. Explosions, conjured spells, beams of energy, and more mundane methods of war had carved a deep scar through the dwarven hold.

The rest of the deep fog had dissipated now, and the two were able to meander their way down toward the clearing with little issue. A thick cloud of varying smells filled their senses. Burning wood, internal organs, and rotting vegetation.

Hunter pushed through the clearing first, revolver at the ready.

It was the scene of an over the top body horror movie. What remained of the grass was slick with gore. Puddles of dark blood turned the earth into mud. Broken limbs, alien organs, and destroyed tech littered the area. Every breath of air had a damp, coppery tang to it.

In the middle of this destruction was a mound of rotting vegetation, several thick tentacle-like roots and wide leaves lying wilted on the sodden ground. At the center of this pile lay Sally. Fully formed and uninjured, but covered from mouth to stomach in foul roach brain matter.

With her eyes half-closed, she glanced over at the two making their way over.

“You had little strength left, and you risked using an Ultimate?” Archie admonished.

“Eh.” The zombie closed her eyes and smiled. “I know my limits.”

Hunter raised his hat with the barrel of his gun and pulled a face at the surrounding destruction. “Looks like the rest of ‘em weren’t prepared for that.”

“The usual arrogance being their undoing.” Sally groaned as she struggled to push herself up to a sitting position. “Plus, I was really hungry.”

The wizard lent his hand to help pick her up. “Stretching yourself this thin when we have a return journey to make is ill-advised… but I’m glad you’re okay.”

She gave him a tired grin. “Touch and go for a moment there, all ego aside. They’re still a pushover individually, but grouped together with all sorts of odd skills—especially ability limiters—it gets a little dicey.”

“We mustn’t forget this is a smaller scouting force.” Archie gave the zombie a pat on the back. “Unfortunately, we’re taking the difficult path back home.”

Sally deflated. “Of course. Good thing I have such a strong will, so I’ll get there, even being this tired.”

The two men didn’t look too convinced, but had little option but to believe otherwise. Hunter holstered his gun and gave the wizard a nod.

“Best we part ways, as I don’t know how this will go.”

Archie nodded. “I have enough power reserved to teleport us to the other side of the world. From there, it’s all up to you.”

“Sure thing. Was mighty nice meeting the two of you. We’ll meet again, I’m sure of it.”

Sally pouted. “You have to find Sanctuary. We’re allies now.”

“I’ll find my way. I don’t think the person I seek is on your world… but it feels like your partnership is the key.” He rolled his neck from side to side. “It’s inevitable, really.”

“Sounds like you spent too much time with Edward. Let’s go, Arch.”

The pair vanished with a flash of blue light, leaving the shadowy man alone in the clearing.

He took a deep breath in and sighed. With a dull expression on his face, he held his open palm out toward the ground.

The crimson puddles and muted grass started to lose their saturation. Trees on the edge of the clearing cracked and fell, their bark stripping away with a slight hiss. The ground cracked as dark energy bloomed and swirled around the man.

It was time to see if he could erase an entire planet.
