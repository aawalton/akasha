The world began to take on a new, higher definition. A definition he found difficult to explain.

The detail in which he could already see had been… amazing, to the point where it felt like it should be impossible to see with such clarity, such depth. And yet, as he continued to look, with looking being the only thing he could do now, he continued to see even more than he knew he could. He could see farther and deeper. Closer and wider.

He could see the very subatomic particles that formed the atoms that formed the elements that formed the stone of the walls.

He could see deeper into the artificial darkness of the Dark Tunnel than he could when he’d first arrived—indeed, he could almost make out the individual particles that made up that artificial darkness.

But he couldn’t see everything.

Time passed as he continued in this state of simply seeing. Without being about to feel his body or hear the world around him, without being able to taste or smell, the image his eyes translated to his brain became all he knew of reality outside his own mind. Cross-legged, sitting on the stone as he was, he began to tap a finger on his knee, curious about something. Curious about how senses worked, how they were wired, and whether or not…

There.

Xavier smiled, though he couldn’t feel the smile form on his face. After a full day of sitting in this state, tapping a finger on his knee, he finally saw something new. Every time his finger tapped, there was a ripple. A wave, emanating from the point of impact.

A soundwave.

He watched as the sound rippled in the air. From just seeing, he couldn’t decipher exactly how it “sounded.” Not yet, anyway. But he thought with enough practice that he would be able to.

Seeing sound wasn’t why he was there, of course, but he felt as though it was a step in the right direction, that his senses were beginning to mingle—or, at least, one of his senses was.

He pushed a small string of energy into the air, one that would pass right in front of his eyes and saw… not a thing. But that didn’t deter him. He’d known he wouldn’t see anything, just as he hadn’t seen that ripple in the air a day ago when he’d started tapping his finger against his knee. It would take time to gain a new perception like that, even in such an open state as he was.

As he sat there, he tried not to let his mind wander, tried not to let it go down a hundred different tangents. There were always so many things on his mind, and just about all of those things were about the future.

Right now, he needed to root himself squarely in the present.

He sent more strings of energy into the air, larger ones that to his spiritual sense would be incredibly bright. No longer was he worried about the Void Dungeon-created beasts who would be able to sense that energy. The Void Dungeon knew he was there. There was something watching him, even all the way back here at the start. Something down that tunnel that he couldn’t sense anymore—not since he’d switched off all his other senses—but yet he knew it was there.

If they were going to come for him here, they would have done it by now.

He couldn’t feel the energy leaving him, and that was a damned strange sensation. Without his spiritual sense active, he couldn’t discern how much energy was in each of his cores, nor could he feel his energy cycling around his body. Such a process had become as automatic to him as breathing, and though he couldn’t feel himself breathing he was clearly still doing that, so he imagined that the chances were high he was still cycling energy even when he couldn’t feel it.

Though he couldn’t feel the energy leave him, he still knew he was successful at making it happen, because at one point, he’d grown a little restless and switched his spiritual sense back on just to make sure he was actually pushing energy into the air in front of him. Because if he wasn’t, then this whole damned thing would be a waste of time.

He had been pushing energy, of course, and he felt like a fool for checking, for breaking his trance of sight.

It reminded him of the times he’d tried to meditate back before he’d been integrated into the System. He’d set a timer and tell himself he wouldn’t open his eyes until the timer went off. Then he doubted that he’d even set the timer at all. This would cause a debate inside his mind about whether to check until finally he caved in and, of course, the timer was always running.

The flood of having his spiritual sense return almost overwhelmed him, and he instantly shut it back off, returning to only being able to see, and somehow it had become strangely comforting only having the one sense—that was something he wouldn’t have believed when he’d started doing this, not with how dulled everything had felt.

It made him wonder just how overstimulating it would be to have all his senses come back at once…

Xavier forced in a long breath deep into his stomach, watching it rise, then forced it back out. He shut his eyes—briefly, for that was an incredibly vulnerable way to feel—centred himself, then opened them again, and continued to push energy out of him, having it pass in front of his eyes over and over.

He pushed the energy out in a pattern. A kind of criss-cross pattern. The energy would cross in front of his eyes and then dissipate, floating away in all different directions. After a while, he felt as though he could see where that energy was going, but he was sure that was just a figment of his imagination, that his mind was filling in the blanks because it logically knew where the energy would be, as he was the one pushing it there.

More time passed. Days, maybe a week.

Hell, he could have been sitting there for an entire year and he wasn’t sure he would have known, not in the state he was currently in. There was simply no way for him to tell—though he supposed there was a chance the Personal System might know…

Eventually, when he was just starting to wonder if the Personal System had sent him down a path he wasn’t ready for, he began to see… something. At first, it felt like floaters in front of his vision. Those little shapes people sometimes see, especially after they’ve just looked at a bright light. But he realised he hadn’t seen floaters for a very long time, and he didn’t know why he would be seeing them now. Besides, these didn’t appear when he turned his head to look a different way—if they were merely floaters, they should have.

It also didn’t seem like it was a figment of his imagination, because the wisps he saw didn’t follow the same trajectory that he’d been imagining.

Xavier blinked. The wisps were still there. He turned all of his focus toward those wisps, deepening his sight, then widening it to look at the wisps from each side of his peripheral vision. It looked different in his peripheral—somehow more solid. That seemed like a strange notion until he thought about all the things people tended to see in the corners of their eyes, things that turned out not to be there at all when you looked… like thinking you saw a man standing in the corner of the room, but really it was just a dressing gown on a hook.

But what if those things people thought they saw in their peripheral actually were there? What if the mind simply tried to explain it away with, it was just your dressing gown? What if the peripheral vision was somehow better at seeing strange things—like, say, a spirit who hadn’t yet moved on? For even back on Earth before the System had come there were spirits.

They’d always existed and they always would, System or no System.

Maybe the peripheral vision has held onto some of those things from the deep past…

He examined the wisps in as many ways as he could think of until finally he just stared at them. And as he stared the wisps became more and more solid. Though he supposed solid wasn’t the right word, not for energy. But was it energy? For to his eyes it didn’t look as energy looked when he saw it with his Immortal Lens-empowered spiritual sense.

Of course it’s going to look different, just as seeing soundwaves is different to hearing sound.

It looked like… gas, drifting through the air, if gas gathered in dense clumps. He tilted his head to the side, staring at those clumps. The longer he did, the more they made sense, the more real they felt. And it wasn’t just these clumps that he saw. He saw smaller wisps of energy, like dust motes scattered through the air, floating this way and that until they came to the stone walls. Once they hit those solid stone walls they didn’t pass through them as energy should—they simply disappeared.

He frowned at that. Was he seeing that right? He almost moved to stand but realised how foolish that would be in his current condition. Instead, he turned his head to the side and pushed a stream of energy out of him and toward the wall. He saw the clump of thick energy shoot through the air in a straight line.

It hit the wall and… disappeared, just as those small energy-motes had disappeared.

But was it disappearing, just because he couldn’t see it anymore? Or was it going somewhere he simply couldn’t see? It would make sense. Even with his Immortal Soul lens-empowered spiritual sense he hadn’t been able to pierce those walls. They were solid in far more ways than one.

Yet there was something strange about what he was seeing. Something he hadn’t identified when he’d merely sensed it with his spiritual sense. To his spiritual sense, when he’d done something similar, he’d thought he could simply no longer sense the energy.

He wasn’t so sure about that anymore.

Xavier looked deeper, straight at the wall, at the smallest particles that were there. Technically, he could use his Immortal Soul lens to see just as deeply—but that only saw energy. As he looked deeper, he realised he could see both the wall and the energy that came to the wall, though he couldn’t see the energy as clearly and strongly defined as he would be able to see it were his Immortal Soul lens activated.

Still, it was obvious to him where the energy was now that he’d acquired the ability to see it with his natural sight, and when the energy drifted to the air it didn’t pass through the stone. It didn’t disappear, either, though it had appeared that way—no, a third thing was happening that he hadn’t noticed before.

The energy was being absorbed.

It wasn’t passing through the wall, it was entering the wall, and that was a distinction he hadn’t noticed before, a distinction that his spiritual sense hadn’t picked up on. Perhaps it was merely because he hadn’t focused on it enough, or maybe there was something about seeing it with his actual eyes that made it more evident.

He wasn’t quite sure, but now that he saw it, he couldn’t unsee it. It made his focus shift to the stone of the wall. The deeper he looked at the molecules that made up the wall’s elements, pushing more energy toward the point where he was deeply staring, he realised those molecules were taking on the energy, and that energy was dispersing through the walls. It was incredibly subtle. So subtle he wouldn’t have been able to see it if he wasn’t searching so hard for something to see.

So, what does this mean?

He supposed he’d known that the walls weren’t natural. They were far too strong to be natural, after all. Then again, in a way, they were natural—as the Void Dungeon was a naturally occurring being, even if the void wasn’t its original home.

But still, they weren’t merely stone.

Xavier began tapping his finger on his knee once more. He pulled his gaze away from the minute section of wall, having seen enough. He watched the soundwaves ripple in front of him.

The stone wall isn’t just a stone wall; it’s the Void Dungeon itself. I’m surrounded by it, all around me, and it absorbs any energy it finds.

He supposed that wasn’t such an unusual thing to learn, but it was off-putting, nonetheless. Perhaps that was how it sensed when he used energy so acutely. It wasn’t the beasts out there—it was the very wall surrounding him.

Maybe that was what he’d sensed watching him all along.

The walls…

Xavier released another long, unfelt breath.

Now that he could see energy with his natural sight, it was time to switch to a different sense.

He wondered what else he might be able to discern about this place when he did.
