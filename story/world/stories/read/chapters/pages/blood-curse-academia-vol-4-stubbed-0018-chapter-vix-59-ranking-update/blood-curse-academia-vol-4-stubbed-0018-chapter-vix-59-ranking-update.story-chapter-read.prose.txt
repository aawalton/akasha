Chapter V.IX (5.9) - Ranking Update

Kizu watched as a school of fish fled the presence of a shark. The predator's gills flexed and its left eye met Kizu’s. While it lacked any magical traits to indicate unnatural intelligence, the sea beast’s natural presence alone was a terror enough. It reminded Kizu of those polar bears he’d met up in the northern reaches of Tross. Some beasts didn’t need magical assistance to inspire terror.

“They won’t hurt you,” Roba said from the other side of her desk. “That wall might appear to only be glass, but I assure you there is far more keeping the sea at bay.”

As the shark ducked around a coral reef in pursuit of its prey, Kizu’s attention returned to the elderly woman. They sat in her office. Their first meeting since the Dragon attack on Shinzou Academy.

The walls of her room were windows into the bottom of the sea and every time he had visited they always had some new bit of action on display. Rather than having an aquarium in her office like a mundane person, it was as if Roba’s office was the aquarium for the fish to spectate. A reversal of roles.

“I’m not worried about being hurt by a shark,” Kizu replied. “I am more worried that a dagger might fall out of the mouth of a Dragon and relinquish its power on a Calamity.”

Roba leaned forward, elbows on the table, and steepled her hands. “The headmaster has your companion, Sojan, under strict surveillance while he trains him how to best utilize his abilities.”

“Will you finally give me the location?”

“No,” Roba replied bluntly. “Until the Dragon’s soul has been utterly purged from the body, it is too dangerous to allow students near it.”

“I’m the one who bested it up in the first place.”

“With the help of a niche potion and a mythical artifact from the depths of the World Dungeon.”

“After I had already killed a necromancer controlling a horde of undead,” Kizu insisted. “I fought the Dragon after expending my limited resources.”

“Be that as it may, you need to learn patience.” Roba remained as immovable as stone on the subject. “But on the subject of your capabilities, let us examine your current rankings. Recent events have caused us to miss a few of our meetings. I am interested to see how your ‘limited resources’ have progressed.”

A folder appeared in Roba’s open hand and she set it on the desk between them. It flipped open, revealing the first document.

The top of the paper was labeled, Combat, Rank 244. Below that, there were detailed reports of his fights on the weekends with the summoned monsters. And a section dedicated to his own essay on his journey to Tross for his mission alongside Evie.

First, they discussed the fights. Kizu tried to give explanations of why he’d chosen the methods he had for each fight listed. Roba listened and, in turn, responded with advice on how to capitalize on his objectives.

“I am grateful you agreed to pair with young Ms. Alito,” Roba said, moving the conversation to the Tross mission. “While part of her gradual increase in confidence is certainly a result of her coming to be more comfortable in the academy over time, I do credit your influence for a part of it as well.”

“Evie is a friend.” There wasn’t much more to say on that subject in Kizu’s opinion. He would have happily teamed up with the Kemon girl regardless of Roba’s wishes.

Roba nodded slowly. “And your target for the first of the tournament challenges? Have you made any progress there?”

Kizu’s eyes narrowed at the old, sunspotted woman. “Can I trust you not to sell me out as a means to make my challenge harder?”

She waved a hand dismissively. “Yes, yes. As an academy administrator, I already have records should I choose to peruse them, I am simply asking to speed up the process. Rest assured I have no intention of interfering in the competition.”

“I have someone named Yugo. I’ve been learning everything I can about his daily schedule. I think I know enough to make my move this weekend. But one slip up could give up my identity so I want to be careful.”

“Good.” Roba nodded slowly. “Do you want me to share all the information I have about the boy?”

Kizu paused. “No.”

Roba’s mouth cracked into a toothy smirk, akin to that of a laughing alligator. “Good. I wouldn’t have given you any.”

While doing his best to keep his expression neutral, Kizu let out a breath of relief. He’d correctly judged that to be a trap.

The folder flipped to the next page. Astronomy, Rank 197.

“You’ve improved very slightly in this subject,” Roba noted. “Still a disappointment considering your unprecedented rise at the start of last semester.”

“I have a plan to improve it more,” Kizu said. “It might take me a couple weeks of work first, but I’ll break the top 100.”

“You say that with confidence. Very well. Surprise me. Though I believe your divination rank will be harder to improve. 63 as of right now.”

“I…don’t have any ideas on how to improve that one. I’ve stagnated,” Kizu admitted. “Do you have any thoughts?”

“You’ve improved by one rank since our last meeting. At the point you’re currently at, that is exactly the trajectory I’d hope to see from you. Every rank higher will be an exponentially more difficult task. Simply keep at your current tasks and exercise patience.”

“Fine.” Kizu sighed. Improving one rank every few weeks was not where he wanted to be at.

The shark had returned on the other side of the glass. It slowly swam across the length of the office, a bit of fish flesh stuck between its teeth. Its skin glittered in the light that left Roba’s office.

“History continues to improve. As does your politics ranking.” Roba sounded approving as she manually flipped between the two pages. On the first, History, Rank 502. On the second, Politics, Rank 577. “You must be dedicating a good amount of your studying to those topics. That explains the stagnation with astronomy.”

She nailed the truth with that guess. There was only so much time Kizu could dedicate to reading textbooks every day. He couldn’t even remember the last time he read a storybook for pleasure.

“I have tutors for those two subjects,” Kizu explained. “Aoi is helping me learn about politics and Faible is teaching me about history.”

“The princess and the gnome. Interesting company you keep.” Roba waved a hand and the folder’s pages flipped. “The professor for Rejuvenation and Restoration F must be teaching the magical aspects of the subject now. You’ve finally moved up a few rankings.”

Taroe had folded to his begging and allowed him access to one of the patches of flesh from class and, for the last three days, Kizu had dedicated the majority of his free time to slicing open and reknitting it back together. The nice part about the task was it let him use his mind for other things, so he also read history books or helped Anata with her homework simultaneously.

The page in front of him read, Rejuvenation and Restoration, 752.

“Can’t say the same thing for conjuring though,” Kizu muttered. He remained dead last, sitting at 800.

His last class in the subject was particularly unproductive as he spent the entirety of the period getting grilled by Sene, who was convinced he must have stolen her token during the Student Council meeting. The girl wasn’t even angry as much as frustrated that she didn’t know how he’d supposedly done it.

“Brewing, first in the class, same as ever,” Roba commented dismissively. “Numerology has a slight improvement. 601 now. Good steady improvement there. And music is now at 198. A substantial improvement there. Over 100 rankings up. I suppose this implies that your meeting with Professor Ignis went well?”

Kizu nodded. Every encounter he had with the professor improved his opinion of the fiery haired tired man.

“The last three have very minimal movement as well. Enchanting is at 144. Illusions at 47. And your elemental rank is 136.”

Roba flipped to the final page, which displayed all the rankings together.

Combat 244, Astronomy 197, Divination 63, History 502, Politics 577, Rejuvenation and Restoration 752, Conjuring 800, Brewing 1, Numerology 601, Music 198 (Piano), Enchanting 144, Illusion 47, Elemental 136.

Kizu, displaying his numerology improvements, quickly added up the numbers in his head. It came out to 4,262. He was steadily approaching the 1,000 mark required by the headmaster. But, once he cut down on the rejuvenation and restoration rank, the rest would be painfully slow to push forward.

He glared at the 800. After several months of knowing about his defect in summoning, he was still no closer to clearing that particular hurdle.

For the next twenty minutes they discussed different strategies for improving his study habits and, for once, Roba seemed happy about their progress.

“Excellent.” The old woman leaned back in her seat. “Now, normally we would explore a new avenue of spatial spellcraft.”

“Are we not today?” Kizu asked. His heart fell.

“No. Instead we’re going to discuss an errand I need you to attend to in Port Kallis. I’d rather you handle it today than put it off for a later time. In return, I’ll teach you something special in one of your next tutoring sessions. A very useful spell.”

“Alright.…” Kizu frowned. He had agreed to do Roba favors, but entering Port Kallis right now seemed a bit uncomfortable with the current political climate.

Roba reached into her desk and pulled out a burlap sack with a knot at its end. She tossed it to Kizu.

For a moment, he thought it was the same exact sack she had him use to dispose of blood samples back in the first semester. But no, as it plopped into his lap he immediately recognized that it was enchanted. Whatever was inside the sack was extremely lumpy. Like dozens of bags of porridge.

“Don’t wear your academy uniform as you deliver it.” Roba paused. “In fact, throw on a wig to cover your hair as well.

Kizu ran a hand through his hair. “A wig?”

“Colored hair is more common on those from the eastern regions of Tross and I don’t want you to draw needless attention. And I believe I read in a report once that your hair doesn’t allow dye to stick to it. Some sort of brewing accident. So a wig is the natural solution.”

“If I’m hiding my identity, do you want me to wear makeup and a corset while I’m at it?” Kizu asked dryly.

To his horror, Roba drummed her fingers on her desk, actually considering the question, before shaking her head. “I don’t believe you’d pass well enough. You’ve grown too tall, your neck and shoulders too wide. No. You would need too much work for that to be a viable solution. And if magical alterations were detected it would bring even more scrutiny on you. A simple black wig should suffice.”
