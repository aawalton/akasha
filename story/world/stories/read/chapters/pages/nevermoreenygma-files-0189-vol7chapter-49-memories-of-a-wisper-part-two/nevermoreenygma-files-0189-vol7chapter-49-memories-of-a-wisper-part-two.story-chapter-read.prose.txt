Chapter 49Memories of a Wisper – Part Two

The trafficking of feys continued to be a problem, though to a lesser extent than before. In every country where Mai and her team operated, they would discover clandestine networks exploiting feys in every way imaginable. From forced labor to the black market of slavery, prostitution, espionage, and even training them as private armies for the wealthiest.

In some parts of East Asia, network issues stemmed from superstition and old tales. In Japan and many other parts of Korea and China, people still believed in the myth of mermaid meat that could cure and give immortality, which had appeared in so many legends in previous centuries. In the 20th and 21st centuries, these myths persisted, but now the legend had evolved and spread to various types of feys. That had been one of the keys to the discovery of the core in North Korea that led to the operation years ago. Now the rumors and legends had quieted down a bit, but there were whispers that other networks around the world had shifted production lines—and with them, the legends as well. Though it wasn’t the only problem, nor the only mission she and her teammates had to undertake.

One of the most horrific cases she had encountered during that time was a well-known rumor about pregnancy farms. These were places where hybrids were created, and some wealthy families were interested in them.

On one of those farms in the United States Kingdom, they even discovered that someone had beaten them to it. The feys they had been holding had been freed by someone else. Witnesses to the carnage spoke of a small fey girl with a kind of tentacular tails or appendage and wings, that had ravaged the place looking for its teenage daughter and had disappeared after rescuing her and eliminating all those responsible. That fey was never found, but witnesses said they had never seen a fey like it before.

Humanoids feys were not the only ones in danger. Other species of feys were also hunted, sold, and exploited.

Other times… missions ended in a more unexpected finding.

On one occasion, Mai received a mission that took her to the southern United States. She had not expected to encounter a trafficking network specialized in jackalopes, those strange horned rabbits supposedly only a myth of American folklore.

“Are you telling me there’s a jackalope farm?” Mai asked when the mission was explained to her.

“Not just a farm,” Jinat replied, “an entire damn ranch.”

The covert operation took weeks. They managed to infiltrate the facility and discovered that not only were they breeding jackalopes, but they were experimenting on them. It was said that the blood of these creatures had special properties, but the results of those experiments were horrifying.

When the night of the assault arrived, Mai led one of the infiltration teams. They crossed the vast ranch, avoiding armed patrols and motion sensors.

Upon reaching the stables, they found cages filled with jackalopes in deplorable conditions. Some no longer responded to stimuli, others were covered in scars from the experiments.

The mission ended in a firefight between their team and the traffickers, but they managed to free the animals and destroy the ranch.

It was a victory, but only a small one among many battles.

Afterwards, one of the largest organizations in the territory, the Armitage Foundation, took responsibility for safeguarding the creatures.

***

The first ten years in Wishper passed like the blink of an eye. Between rescue operations, infiltrations, and combats against illegal groups, Mai became a key pillar of the team.

Her name was known in the underground world in many places, not only in Japan anymore. Some considered her a threat, others a legend, but no one denied that wherever she appeared, traffickers and mercenaries fell.

Yet with every mission, Mai felt the battle was endless. For every trafficking network they dismantled, two more would emerge elsewhere in the world.

The world was a battlefield that never slept, and she had accepted walking that thinnest line between justice and vengeance. There was no law for the feys, so they had to make their own, aided by certain magical and human groups and communities that, for one reason or another, had become involved in the occult world and had stayed there. Various types of secret wars were being waged around the globe, and even Wishper did not know all of them. Despite the reach of organizations worldwide, no one was certain how much remained hidden.

Advances in technology and surveillance networks had changed the landscape, but they had also pushed organizations to operate in far more covert ways in a constantly shifting globalized world.

Wishper, with its bases, the Armitage Foundation in the Tripartite Empire, the Sleeping Dragon in the Asia-Pacific zone, the European Ministry of Magic, the Special Commission in Africa, Veria in Russia, the Australian Special Union, and many other organizations, some known and others unknown, moved in a layer that tried to leverage technology to act.

But at the same time, they had to to blend or hide from human society, with the secret support of governments that had no interest in revealing the hidden world. There were already enough new environmental problems to deal with, let alone adding a new layer to the world. Around the globe, conflicts over basic resources like water had begun, and governments had to try to maintain order.

Compared to the global picture, the Inoue Section in Japan seemed barely a small boat in a vast ocean, Mai thought. Certainly, the years had given her perspective on that at a new level.

But how much longer could it continue like this?

And while she tried to balance her life around the world, she would sometimes return home. Unfortunately, on one of those trips, she had to say goodbye to the house where she had met her friend and mentor. Upon receiving the news from the Inoue Section, she had to take a few days to come to terms with it.

April 2024

Mai looked at the house in silence, hands in her pockets, with a neutral expression that hid the slight unease in her chest. No matter how much time passed, every time she returned to Japan, she felt her life split between two completely different worlds. But this time, she had returned with a sadness.

The house in Yoyogicho was no longer there. The urban redevelopment had been swift and thorough. In its place, where the small shelter Miho had left behind for her once stood, there were now construction machines, safety barriers, and signs announcing the start of demolition work for the construction of an office and apartment complex.

She had to let that part of her past go and move everything remaining to the Saitama house. It was near the Naguri Lake. It was larger, quieter, with a backyard where cherry trees bloomed every spring. There was a nearby lake, and the area was beautiful and natural, but it wasn’t the same.

“At least I still have this,” Mai thought as she crossed the entrance.

The interior was almost the same as she had left it last. She hadn’t spent much time in Japan, and in that house, only a few solitary days over the years, but every time she returned, she made sure to clean, make small repairs, and ensure the house remained in good condition. Neighboring houses were slightly distant, but the neighbors had known Mai since Miho was alive, so they were pleased that someone would now spend more time there.

Now it would be the place to store the things she had brought from Tokyo, paying a fairly high shipping cost that required four trips to move everything.

Money was not an issue—not after years of working with Wishper. The salary was high, and although she didn’t have large personal expenses, almost everything went into maintaining her identity within the hidden fey government system. Owning a house registered in her name for the next fifty years hadn’t been cheap, but at least it guaranteed she wouldn’t lose this last memory of Miho. And in case anything happened to her, she had already taken measures to ensure that all of Miho’s magical objects, along with her books, would end up in the right hands.

She pulled one of the boxes she had brought from Tokyo and opened it. Inside were photographs, old books, and some items that had belonged to Miho.

She lingered over one photograph in particular. It was of the two of them, taken at a summer festival years ago. Mai wore a look of annoyance, arms crossed, while Miho smiled carefree with a cotton candy in her hand.

“Why was I so angry that day?” Mai murmured with a faint smile before placing the photo on a shelf.

She spent a while in the house, going through things, organizing papers, and making sure everything was in order. Her time in Japan was always brief, but she couldn’t afford to forget what she had left behind.

A couple of friends and some neighbors came to help with the unpacking, though she decided to keep other things sealed until she had more time to return. She called Aika who had installed the special barrier in Miho’s house and had the same done here, reinforcing the one that already existed. When she finally prepared to leave, she took one last look at the house before locking the door.

“See you in a few months or years.”

With that silent farewell, Mai left the Saitama house, heading toward the next destination waiting for her.

***

The years 2025–2027 were a turning point for the world.

From being in China for most of 2025 Mai moved further west, alternating missions between Russia and Europe while the world sank deeper and deeper into chaos. The Chaotic Era was no longer just a term used in reports or political speeches—it was a reality everyone experienced firsthand. In Russia, rearmament had begun years ago, fearing the entry of other countries seeking water resources, as was happening in South America.

Cities that once had mild winters were battered by snowstorms, while temperate regions turned into scorching ovens. Under these conditions, Wishper missions had became more extreme, and more dangerous.

The outside world had transformed into a far darker and more dangerous place over the past years. At first, Mai thought it was all just rumor, something corporations wanted to use to justify new control measures. But as the years progressed, the news came more frequently: wars over resources, water shortages, unstable climate. Everything seemed on the brink of going to hell, as if humanity had passed the point of no return and was now struggling to hold onto what remained.

Since joining Wishper, the global landscape had changed gradually. Multinational corporations, had allied with shadow governments and criminal groups to exploit the growing crisis. The struggle for resources, especially water and rare materials, became the backdrop for some of her missions.

In her first year with Wishper, the world seemed caught in a tense calm. The great powers still maintained control, but the climate in the second decade was already shifting. Temperatures rose or fell without warning, natural disasters struck places never considered vulnerable, and forced migration due to environmental disasters became a crisis that borders could not contain. And amid all this, OOPArts had become a valuable and dangerous commodity on the black market.

Mai remembered one of her missions in northeastern Russia, where she had to infiltrate a military base guarding an artifact capable of altering the weather. Reports indicated that the artifact was being sold to radical groups seeking to influence world politics. For days, she fought to prevent the artifact from falling into the wrong hands, unaware that beyond that mission, the same type of corruption was seeping into governments worldwide.

Governments were now embroiled in disputes over resources and land control, water for growing data centers. Countries in the Southern Hemisphere began to crumble under the weight of internal crises. Water reserves were depleted or privatized, agricultural fields withered under the relentless northern sun, or plagues and populations struggled to survive. In the north, the climate became unpredictable, and extreme cold began to dominate. Mai had been on missions where the cold seemed to freeze her bones, but nothing compared to what was happening in the world.

Tensions were rising, and Wishper’s role became increasingly unstable in many governments. Criminal groups, those trading OOPArts, knew how to exploit the chaos to hide and expand their empires. Mai, along with the team, became a cog piece in that invisible war for stability. The organization was willing to do everything possible to curb the trade of dangerous artifacts, but even they could not control everything happening outside their missions.

Sometimes, Mai felt a growing unease. She was fighting for a world that was falling apart and couldn’t help but wonder if it would ever be enough. The desperation of people in the hardest-hit countries was palpable, yet the corruption of those benefiting from the crisis was even more tangible. She had seen how corporate greed had driven entire governments to hand over what remained of their resources just to stay in power, leaving millions condemned to an uncertain future.

“The world keeps spinning, heaven and earth don't give a damn about our fights, but” Jinat once told her, “ we’re here to try to keep it from falling off its axis. Otherwise we are fucked.”

Missions became increasingly dangerous over time. During an operation in the Mongolian desert, Mai had to face a group of traffickers who had seized an OOPArt capable of producing water in desert areas. Artifacts like these were coveted by everyone: governments, criminal organizations, and even radical groups that saw them as key to survival. The battle was fierce, but in the end, the team managed to destroy the artifact and prevent it from falling into the wrong hands. The price of using that artifact was a requirement for blood sacrifices to activate it. Very young blood sacrifices.

And every so often, they still encountered trafficking of feys and magical creatures—like on that mission in northern Russia were the icy wind cut her skin like invisible blades.

Temperatures had dropped to -40°C, and every breath was a battle against the cold. Mai’s team lay sprawled on a snowy cliff, observing through thermal scopes the massive armored train moving across the Siberian tundra.

“Have you ever hijacked a moving train at this temperature?” asked one of the operatives, a feline fey named Ylva with the tail moving under the tactic gear and clothes.

“No,” Mai replied, her lips barely moving. “But I’ve never rescued feys in a sandstorm either till few weeks ago. There’s always a first time.”

Ylva smiled. “Then let’s do it.”

The objective was to recover a shipment of captured magical creatures before it crossed the border and was sold to the highest bidder. Among them were unicorns, sylphs, and a race of small winged humanoids previously unknown to exist in that region.

The team descended the ice-covered slope using anchor ropes, approaching the train undetected. Once in range, Mai and another agent leapt from the incline and landed on the roof of the rear carriage, clinging to the metal structures with magnetic hooks.

The guards aboard quickly realized something was wrong. Gunfire rang out, metal creaked, and muffled screams were carried by the wind. When they enter Mai disarmed an enemy in a single motion, breaking his wrist before hurling him off the train. Another lunged at her with a knife, but Mai dodged and delivered a kick to his throat, leaving him choking in the ground.

While the rest of the team secured the train cabin, Mai descended into the cargo wagon. What she saw inside turned her stomach:

Cages. Dozens of cages holding magical beings, chained, drugged, or mutilated to prevent escape.

One unicorn had its horn partially sawed off. A wingless pixie stared at her with hollow eyes from a cage far too small for her body.

Mai clenched her teeth.

“Let’s get them out of here,” she muttered, feeling her rage burn even in that deadly cold.

The train was stopped, and within just five minutes, the helicopters from Whisper arrived to transport the rescued individuals, the intelligence, and anything else that might be useful. The train was blown up along with all the guards. It was better not to leave any clues, since another team would already be deploying for the next phase of the mission: an assault on those behind that trafficking operation.

That operation would now be behind Mai; it was better to move the teams to completely different areas once the mission was carried out. To avoid any setbacks or early identification: changing passports, new identities. She had gotten used to it.

Weeks later, the new team she was in was sent to the other side of the world, in scorching heat. At least Ylva was there.

Rumors of an prehistoric artifact had been located in the ruins of an ancient settlement in the Sahara Desert.

That information came entirely from the precognition department, and they had been wrong in the past. But, even so, it was better to verify it. It was better to find out it was nothing than to later regret it if someone got their hands on it. After all, that city was just a legend… till now.

They would have to drive there and walk a little way so as not to arouse suspicion. Primarily because the place where those ruins had been found did not exist from the air. It was what was called an evanescent land in the middle of the Er-Iguidi desert, where a civilization had once existed that had built a kind of labyrinthine city, of which only certain Carthaginian legends warned that one should never set foot in that cursed place.

The legend of the City of Mamurth and its strange spider god.

The mission seemed simple: recover the OOPArt before well-armed mercenaries could sell it to the highest bidder.

But the problem was not just the enemy.

The afternoon heat was still unbearable. Temperatures exceeded 55°C, and merely moving under the scorching sun was a challenge. The team of five, led by Mai and Freya—a fey sorceress who had joined about five years prior—wore thermal suits designed to dissipate the heat, but even so, every step in the sand felt like walking on fire till they reached the shadows of a mountain range.

“If I pass out, just leave me here and go on,” joked one of the operatives, a man named Eron.

“Don’t do it—I don’t want to have to carry your ass back,” said Sebas, a fey with a face covered in tribal tattoos.

Mai, sweating despite her protection, tossed him a water bottle. “If you pass out, I’ll use you as a human sandboard.”

They reached the mountains and entered a gorge where sunlight barely filtered through, and emerged again into a new desert stretching for miles, where the view faded into the sand.

But there was a gigantic depression of several kilometers where some ruins could be seen. As they left the gorge, Freya tapped Mai on the shoulder and pointed to the right side of the exit. Someone had written something carved into the rock in English.

Shin was here.

Mai frowned in confusion, and the others just shrugged.

“We are not the first ones, apparently,”Eron said

“And now we’re not alone either,” Mai pointed out. There were several footprints in the sand, and they looked as though they had been left only a short while ago.

“Maybe…” Ylva began.

“Maybe there’s a snitch,” said Eron.

“Let’s go. Whoever they are, we have to get whatever’s here back,” Freya ordered.

They reached the ruins as the sun began to set. The enemy team was already there, digging into what appeared to be a sealed chamber beneath the sand. Mai and her team moved silently, taking advantage of the twilight to flank them and observe. According to the vision and the maps they had been given by the precognition department, that was the place they were supposed to go. It was too much of a coincidence that someone else was already there.

Without realizing it, Sebas walked past a sensor that went off. “Damn it!” he muttered angrily.

“Take cover!” Mai ordered.

Hiding among those ruins, that had undoubtedly been silent for millennia, the skirmish began.

When the gunfire began Mai dodged a shot and took cover behind a rock. Her ears caught the whistling of something moving far too fast: a projectile designed to pierce magical barriers was directed towards Freya.

“Be careful! They know you are the magic user.”

“They’re targeting us with specialized ammo!” Ylva shouted.

Mai rolled across the ground and fired back, hitting a mercenary holding a rocket launcher in the shoulder.

The fight was chaotic, but quite short. Sand flew, explosions lit up the night, and shouts and orders were punctuated by the wind. Results: four mercenaries captured, three dead. They didn't say who had ordered him to do that, but they could ask the interrogation team. At least they hadn't managed to send any messages out, since communications in that area had been cut off ever since they entered the gorge.

When they finally secured the underground chamber, they found the artifact and extracted it to the surface: a metallic sphere with inscriptions that seemed to move under the moonlight.

Mai lifted it carefully.

“I hope this damn thing is worth it,” she muttered.

The sphere pulsed once in her hands—and the desert answered.

Something moved around them, fast and soundless, leaving only some kind of small circles, collapsing sand, and the sudden absence of heat around the fire, as if the night itself recoiled even more. One of the mercenaries screamed before being flung sideways, his body lifted by nothing visible and hurled against the ruins with bone-breaking force. Another vanished mid-stride, dragged beneath the sand as if the ground had opened a mouth. No magic signature, no thermal distortion—just violence without a shape.

The other two mercenaries muttered something about the children of the city god and ran away. They tried to stop them, but before they could catch up, the fugitives were lifted up and torn apart in midair, while around them those circles in the sand multiplied. Mai's hair stood on end, and a terror she had rarely felt ran from her head to her toes.

It was like a sixth sense, but she didn't need many clues to know what it was.

She was a moth and sometimes reacted instinctively to certain insects and animals.

She knew it. Those circles in the sand. They were insects. And that city had been consecrated to the spider god Mamurth.

She took out her hidden antennae and made them vibrate.

Spiders. Fucking big and invisible spiders.

“Run and follow me! Now!” Mai ordered.

They didn’t argue. The ruins came alive behind them: sand erupting in violent spirals, invisible impacts striking stone hard enough to crack it, something vast circling just beyond perception.

Freya tried to raise a barrier and was knocked flat by an unseen blow that sent her skidding across the sand. Mai grabbed her arm, the artifact clutched tight against her chest, and pulled her up. They ran—blind, half-stumbling—guided only by the echolocation, memory, and the thin hope that the gorge still existed.

The desert pursued them.

Pressure slammed into their backs like hurricane-force fists, throwing them forward, shredding equipment, tearing packs loose. Eron fell and did not get up, something heavy passed over him, leaving only a crushed outline in the sand. It was too late for him. Only red pulp remained, being dragged along by the sand. By the time they reached the narrow defile between the mountains, lungs burning and suits screaming warnings, the ground was already trembling beneath their feet. They ran trough it and crossed the threshold just as the air behind them collapsed inward with a sound like distant thunder.

The mountains sealed the city forever. Again.

Stone sheared and folded as if guided by an unseen hand, the gorge convulsing shut in a violent quake that sent the team sprawling. When the dust settled, there was no pass—only an unbroken wall of rock where an entrance had been moments before.

Later surveys, satellites, and ground-penetrating scans revealed nothing: no caverns, no voids, no trace that the City of Mamurth—or whatever dwelled within it—had ever been accessible at all.

Officially, the site did not exist. Unofficially, Wishper marked it as sealed beyond recovery.

Whatever lived behind those mountains had not followed them.

And that, Mai knew, was the only reason they were still alive.

Subsequent reports indicated that the OOPArt had the ability to purify contaminated water. In a world on the brink of collapse, that was worth more than all the gold on the planet. As Mai later learned, Jinat had sent the artifact to a community in Africa where a volcano had contaminated the water supply.

The leak was caught, and there were no further breaches. However, nothing more was ever heard of the informant's fate, just as had been the case with Eron, whose body was never recovered.

The years kept moving, and Mai grew accustomed to living in a world that changed too quickly. Missions became more complicated, enemies more ruthless, and the thin line between good and evil increasingly difficult to discern.

Sometimes she wondered if she would ever find a reason to stop.

But the world still needed people like her. And almost without realizing it, she had become an important part of Wishper.

Mai had learned to accept her white hair as part of her existence, but in recent years, the transformation had apparently become irreversible. From 2030 to 2045, her hair had never darkened even once.

Between the emergence of the Aeon, organizational restructuring, the conquest of space, internal conflicts in the hidden world, and the wars over resources and against disease, she continued.

Everyone, in one way or another, continued—even if many carried a certain hopelessness about the world they were heading toward.

When the Dark Events finally hit the news, Mai was not surprised.

For decades, she had seen them slowly filter through as rumors in the halls of the common world, covered up by governments and academies who preferred to call them “localized anomalies” or “instrumental errors.” But by 2037, it became impossible to hide.

For the first time, the veil was lifting. Lies no longer sufficed.

While many spoke of conspiracies and setups, Mai felt something like relief.

It was terrifying, yes, for humanity to see what she had witnessed for years—that fractured face of reality crumbling under its own weight—but it was also fair. Finally, ordinary people knew that not all monsters were in myths, nor all tremors came from the earth. And though fear spread like a disease, Mai thought that perhaps, just perhaps, they now had a chance.

To understand. To prepare.

Or at least, to face directly what had always been there.

At first, she had tried to maintain a positive attitude. No matter how dark the forecasts of the future, no matter how desperate the circumstances, she had always believed that what they did made a difference, however small. And that belief had kept her standing.

But over the years, the wear began to show.

She had seen too many colleagues die, too many friends. People with whom she shared laughter at a base of operations or with whom she drank after an exhausting mission. People who, in the blink of an eye, ceased to exist.

She couldn’t help but wonder if she would be next.

Freya often told her that her problem wasn’t coldness, but the opposite.

The Weight of the Years.

***

It was after a mission in Korea that Mai confronted part of her past.

Over the years, she had wanted to gather information, but it took several years before she could see her from a distance.

That girl. The leader’s daughter.

She was now a grown woman. She had built a life and had joined the historical committee to preserve what had occurred in North Korea during the regime’s time. She knew of the hidden world due to her memories, but had lived her life trying to correct some of her father’s mistakes.

Mai thought of speaking to her, but her friends took the idea out of her head. That woman had had to live with the terror her father had inspired while presenting himself as a loving father to his daughter. She had made peace with the past and accepted it, trying to make amends. But that didn’t mean that if she saw her father’s killer, she would react the same. Jinat made sure Mai let the thought go.

Seeing the girl grown into a woman really made her reflect on all the years that had passed.

During the early years of her career as a Wisper, she had stayed strong enough not to let the missions, the pain, the deaths of her comrades, and the tragedies of the situations she faced completely drag her under.

She convinced herself that, at the very least, they were making a difference. It was small, fragile, but still meaningful. She had felt the thrill of saving lives, the satisfaction of dismantling organizations that trafficked in creatures, of restoring hope to a world that constantly seemed bent on devouring itself.

But over time, that thrill, that inner fire, gradually faded, fed by fatigue and the growing number of losses she endured. It wasn’t a sudden change—no day when she woke up and everything was different. It was more like silent wear, a slow erosion that crept into her soul.

At first, Mai believed that the physical and mental wear of missions was just a passing phase, that her energy would recharge at some point, that what she was doing would be worth it, and that eventually, everything would stabilize. But the years didn’t pass as she had hoped. Each mission only brought more horror, more lost lives, more sacrifices.

She had seen too many comrades die. Some fell heroically, others simply vanished into the shadows of that endless war against the darkest corners of the hidden world, the Dark Events, or mysterious artifacts. Their bodies swallowed by distortions of reality. Each one had a story, all had names, dreams, and Mai remembered them all. No matter how brief their interactions had been, how fleeting their moments together.

Over the years, she had formed a small family of lost souls bound together through work. But in the end, anyone’s life could be extinguished in a sigh.

By 2045, the hope that had once shone in her heart had become but a shadow of itself.

She no longer felt the urgency to change the world, in fact, what she most wanted was for things to stop being so heavy, so difficult to bear. Yet at the same time, she could not let go of the weight of what she had learned, what she had done, what she still needed to do. Astonishingly, she had spent forty-one years in the world, thirty-three of them in an unending war.

Wishper had been founded in the 1990s, but before that, another group had existed from the late 1940s to the early 1990s called Tempus Fugit, which had, to some degree, done the same. Today, it was said that few of its members survived, and Mai had only ever met one of them during an operation in South America. She wondered, truly, how they had managed to endure for so many decades after surviving the Second World War. Perhaps they had been like her? Bearing the same emotional burden after so many battles?

Freya, Nerea and Miria had been among the few who could pull Mai out of her emotional bubble. They knew exactly how to understand what was happening in her mind, yet never let it affect their relationship. Freya had a peculiar ability to look at things from a colder, more distant perspective, and it was that distance that somehow kept her safe from the darkness that loomed over others. Nerea never let emotion get in her way on missions, which was why she had survived so long on the recent battlefields.

Yet she also saw what was happening to Mai, and she did not ignore it. It wasn’t a problem for her—it was a sincere observation.

One night, after a particularly bloody mission in India, while at the Calcutta base, Mai sat on one of the crates, staring into space with a glass of liquor in her hands. Freya approached and silently sat beside her. They sat in silence for a few moments.

Finally, Freya broke the ice. “You care too much, Mai,” she said, her voice low, but with that characteristic firmness. “It’s not that you’re not strong. It’s that everything you do affects you, consumes you. Despite you try to hide it.”

Mai didn’t answer immediately. She stared into her glass. She could feel the pressure on her chest, as if every word Freya spoke pierced her soul. It was true, but she didn’t want to admit it. She didn’t want to give in. She had tried to do everything possible to prevent her emotions from dominating her life, to let her thoughts flow in a dark wave. But in the end, it was impossible not to feel affected.

“If I didn’t feel anything, I wouldn’t be any different from the bastards we fight,” Mai said, her gaze fixed on the glass.

Freya let out a bitter laugh. “It’s not that you don’t feel anything. It’s that you feel too much. And that’s why… that’s why it hurts more.”

“And you? How do you do it?”

“Sex, sex helps a lot, and that too,” Freya said looking at the glass, her voice calm but somehow carrying deep weariness. “It is not that I don’t let anything affect me, of course it does. But you… you keep feeling every death, every failure, every scar. I don’t know how you bear it.”

Mai set her glass aside and took a deep breath. She had felt like this for so long. She no longer knew whether it was the fatigue of war or simply the accumulation of years of struggle, but something inside her had broken. “I don’t know how I do it,” she whispered, her voice almost inaudible. “Maybe I shouldn’t anymore.”

Freya said nothing. She simply looked at her, understanding everything Mai hadn’t said. Mai had come so far, endured so much, but she was at the edge of her endurance.

“But, you do,” Freya said with a faint smile. “And that’s what makes you unique, Mai. What makes you different. Don’t be so hard on yourself.”

Mai looked at her, her eyes reflecting all the sadness and resignation she had carried inside for years. “Maybe… maybe I’m not as strong as I thought.”

Freya nodded gently. “You are, more than you realize. But you also have feelings. There’s nothing wrong with showing that you care, Mai.”

Mai closed her eyes, letting those words seep into her heart. And maybe, somewhere, all that pain, all those losses, would finally mean something.
