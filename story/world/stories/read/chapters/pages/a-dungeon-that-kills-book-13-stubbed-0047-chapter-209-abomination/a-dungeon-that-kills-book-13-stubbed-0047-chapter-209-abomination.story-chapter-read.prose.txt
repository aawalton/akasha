In an instant, the boulder disappeared—shattering into a cloud of thousands upon thousands of furious, shrieking fragments that tore through the air in every direction.

Viktor had taken the precaution of retreating his vision high into the sky beforehand to spare himself the deafening report of the blast, but even at that height, removed from the epicenter by a considerable distance, the concussive roar still hit him like a slap, and the way the shockwave rippled through the atmosphere made him rather glad that he was not here in person.

Then, when the dust finally settled, he came back down to the ruined earth below. And he saw a man who stood next to the very center of the fresh crater that had just been gouged into the ground.

The Apostle, of course.

The robe he had been wearing hung from his frame in tatters, its hood gone entirely, revealing a young-looking man with long, gray hair. Multiple wounds marred his bare skin, but they were already in the process of knitting themselves back together, and soon they would disappear as though they had never existed. The damage he had sustained in the first devastating instant must have been much more grievous, but his regeneration worked with such terrifying speed that it had reduced the injuries to nothing more than superficial scratches already in the process of fading away.

So not even an explosion to the face, up close and personal, does the trick, huh?

The three Iskora adventurers, on the other hand, were far less lucky.

Valeska lay sprawled on her back, her body contorted at an unnatural angle. Where her right leg should have been, there was simply nothing left below the hip, and through a ragged, weeping tear in her flesh, the pale curve of her pelvic bone could be seen. Her left hand had lost three fingers, while the right side of her face had been flayed raw, muscle showing pink where an ear used to be. Her eyes stared into the artificial sky above, wide and empty.

Rowan fared a bit better in terms of limbs, all four of which remained more or less attached to his body. His chest, however, was a mess of open wounds. A hole roughly the size of a clenched fist had been torn into his side, and his organs were exposed to the open air, glistening and pulsing with each desperate breath. Not to mention, two of his ribs, maybe three, had decided to break free of that wreckage of a torso, stark white against the surrounding gore.

Cynthia had lost both of her legs outright, while her right hand had been flattened into something that didn’t even bother distinguishing between bone and flesh. Her chest rose and fell in shallow, rattling gasps that grew weaker with every passing second, each one of them accompanied by a wet gurgle and a spray of blood that bubbled at the corner of her mouth.

Which was, Viktor supposed, the natural outcome when a normal human happened to be standing anywhere near an exploding boulder. Either you died quick or you didn’t, and if you didn’t, dying quick started to look like the better deal.

Meanwhile, the only Iskora adventurer who was not affected at all by the blast that had shredded his “friends” apart drifted over to the Apostle’s side.

“You’re... quite something,” Xander said. “I’ve heard about you from Khalid, but hearing it and seeing it with my own eyes are totally different matters.”

The Apostle chuckled. “This is nothing special, really. If you want to be impressed by something, then look at the lengths he’s willing to go just to kill me.”

“Who?”

“Who else? Our mutual friend, of course.”

“The dark spirit?” Xander frowned. “This was his doing?”

“Yes. The man does seem to have a particular fondness for explosives.”

“Anyway,” Xander said, casting a glance down at his broken companions, who lay scattered across the bloodied sand. “Looks like this is as far as this lot goes. Useless, exactly as I expected. Now it seems it’ll be just the two of us going into that stone building. What should we do with them, then? Just leave them here, or finish them off?”

To that, the Apostle gave his answer, in the same smooth, unbothered voice of his.

“They can still fight.”

“What!?” Viktor and Xander said at the same time. In perfect unison.

Ugh... If it had been someone else, he might have found it funny.

Xander stared at the Apostle for a long moment. “You’re joking, right? Even coming from you, I still find that hard to believe.”

Instead of offering any further explanation, the gray-haired man simply turned away and walked toward Valeska at a leisurely pace. He reached down, closed his fingers around a fistful of her hair, and dragged her ruined body across the ground toward where Rowan lay groaning. With his other hand, he seized the former soldier by his leg and hauled both of them to Cynthia, before unceremoniously throwing them down on top of her, stacking the three ravaged bodies into a single heap of torn flesh and exposed bone.

As they moaned under him, the man knelt down beside the heap and pressed both of his palms firmly against it.

And then—

The three bodies began to merge.

They screamed—Valeska and Rowan and Cynthia—a raw, tearing sound that rose up from all three throats at once. Their screams blended into one another just as thoroughly and inseparably as their bodies did, until it became impossible to tell where one voice ended and the next began, impossible to say which among them was suffering more than the others. All the while, the three bodies—no, the one body—writhed in the sand, their limbs twisting free from their original places and settling in new locations.

Despite the sheer volume of three people crying out in agony at the same time, the wet, grinding sounds of the transformation remained clearly audible. Viktor could hear it all—flesh rippling and shifting, skin stretching and fusing, bones snapping and realigning, muscles threading and weaving themselves together into something else entirely.

When the sounds finally stopped, the Apostle withdrew his hands from the mass before him and rose back to his feet, unhurried as ever. He smiled as he gazed down upon the result, admiring it the way a craftsman might admire a particularly ambitious commission.

And then, the thing began to move.

It stood on two legs, one of them Valeska’s remaining limb, the other formed from Rowan’s pair fused together into a single thick appendage. Its torso was a grotesque patchwork created from three people’s ribs, and from it grew three heads and five arms, protruding at angles that made no anatomical sense whatsoever.

The funny part was that while the Apostle didn’t bother to fix Valeska and Rowan’s heads, keeping them as scarred and ragged as the blast had left them, he had gone out of his way to restore Cynthia’s face to its original, unblemished state. And now, the young woman’s beautiful features stuck out like a sore thumb when perched on top of that heap of ruined flesh.

Looking at such an abomination, Viktor suddenly found his Ettins almost handsome by comparison. And even a heartless bastard like Xander couldn’t stop the twitch that ran across his face at the sight of it. Probably the most human reaction one could get out of him all day.

“What... what did you do to us?” Valeska asked.

“I gave you a second chance,” the Apostle said, his voice infuriatingly smooth as ever. “Without me, you would have been lying there in the sand, writhing in agony as you prayed for death to come and take you. Now, you’re standing here instead. You live, and you continue the fight.”

“Why the fuck would we want to fight for you?” Rowan barked.

“Because I am the only one who can fix you. If you don’t want to do as I tell you, that’s totally fine. You’re welcome to stay here and spend the rest of your life in that appearance. Which, to be honest, probably won’t be very long. The other adventurers who cross your path will see nothing but an abomination, and they will hunt you down the way they would any other monster. Though if you are lucky, perhaps the lord of this dungeon might find your existence amusing enough to keep you around as a pet instead.”

“I... I don’t want to...” Cynthia sobbed.

“Then there exists only one path left open to you—forward. Pick up your weapons. And walk with me into the great tomb.”

And so, with no other choices available to it, the creature reached down and gathered up the very weapons its former, separate selves had once wielded as three distinct people. Two of its five hands gripped the haft of Valeska’s double-edged axe, while another two took Rowan’s sword and shield in turn, settling into stances his muscles still seemed to remember. And the last remaining hand, the one that had once belonged to Cynthia, trembled as it closed around her ashwood staff.

“You seem to be having some difficulty there,” the Apostle said as he saw the staff nearly slip from her unsteady grasp. “Allow me to help.”

He reached out, taking hold of that hand.

And it began to melt.

Cynthia screamed again, a shattering sound torn straight from those pretty lips. And it was not only her. Rowan’s voice rose alongside the young aeromancer’s in that same instant, and Valeska’s as well, all three cries merging into one single chorus of anguish. After all, they were one now. Their binding was so complete they were incapable of suffering separately.

By the time the Apostle finally released his grip and stepped back, the limb in question had merged with the staff, fused together so thoroughly that flesh and wood had become indistinguishable from one another.

“What... what did you... why did you...?” Cynthia stammered, staring down in horror at what had once been her hand.

“I simply wanted to make sure you wouldn’t lose it,” the gray-haired man said with a smile. “No need to thank me.”

He turned away from the abomination he had created, gazing at the great tomb that loomed over the walls of the mortuary complex.

“Let’s go, then. We shouldn’t keep our host waiting.”
