Golden-scaled hands reached out of the cell. Behind them, I could see a face I wished I hadn’t.

“Hypnos granted me quite the delicious treat,” she said, licking her lips for effect. I’ll have to thank him. Sometimes they really go above and beyond.”

I cast a mega spell. The giant spell circle emerged beneath my feet, quickly growing until it reached a diameter of twenty yards or more.

Red statues were rushing at me on both sides. One thought and marble pillars emerged from the floor, knocking them into the water. There was no point in relying on stealth anymore.

“Look at you,” the siren clapped. “Haven’t you grown? I fully approve,” she laughed. “It makes you all the more delicious.”

Her attitude hadn’t changed since last time, though she too had grown quite a bit. Looking at her status box, I could see that she had acquired quite a number of additional specials, although all the cards had been taken away. Whoever had placed her here clearly didn’t want her using them. Even more curious, her name was now shown as: Princess of the Dead Sea.

“I thought you were dead,” I said.

“Just because you killed me?” she giggled. “I told you. You can’t kill me. Don’t despair, though. What we had was special,” she let out a slight sigh as she said it. “I’ve never been killed that way. Not even close.”

Several more guard statues came rushing from around the building. They, too, suffered the same fate. I pushed them into the water without a moment’s thought.

“Better get your reward before it spoils.” The siren looked at the red sphere. “Would be a shame if you stopped growing.”

I looked at her, then at the sphere. The two were dangerously close to one another. I had already seen her kill a creature while in the cell, using my divine blades, no less. Both weapons lay on the ground now, but were a reminder that I had been careless.

I summoned Serp and slowly made my way to the cell. Once the tip of the saber touched the sphere, it was surrounded by a crimson glow. The holes on his width shrank until they disappeared completely. That was his treat for the day, though the lack of reaction made it clear that he wasn’t happy with the situation.

Unsummoning the saber, I then retrieved my divine blades. All the time, my eyes didn’t leave the siren.

“Always so cautious,” she tilted her head as if she were looking at a puppy—one she was planning to painfully dismember given the chance. “You’ve seen that I don’t have any weapons. Or cards.”

“Yet you still killed your guard.”

“Oh, him? He was just a pawn eager for a promotion,” she waved her hand. “I knew the moment I saw him that he wouldn’t last long. I even think I told him that I’d kill him, but he didn’t listen.”

Apparently, he was something that could be killed.

Quietly, I waited.

“Alright.” The siren stepped back. “I’ll play along with your shy guy routine.”

Distance wasn’t the issue. I knew that if she wanted to harm me, she’d try. At the same time, I refused to be afraid. Taking another step forward, I reached down and retrieved the Paladin card.

Acquiring the rares of all four initial houses has permanently granted you the special Versatile.

All skill restrictions are now diminished.

The reward was rather nice, but it was the last thing on my mind right now.

“Why are you here?” I stood up, remaining a step away from the bars.

“I thought that little thing would have told you.” The siren seemed surprised. “That’s interesting. Then again, she never met me in the flesh. I’ll have to thank her as well.”

She wasn’t using her foresight.

“She’s quite the morsel, isn’t she?” the siren laughed. “So provocative and flirty, you’d never guess she was a virgin.”

“A virgin?” I couldn’t help myself.

“You have to be a virgin to become an oracle. At least at the start. She’s fine now. Do you want to have her?”

I narrowed my eyes.

“Or maybe you prefer the femme fatale? There’s definitely a thing going on there.” The siren’s smile widened. “And she’s definitely better than most. Although, I’d be careful. Speed is a harsh mistress. Those that follow Mercury constantly move forward, never looking back.” She walked up to the bars. “Or maybe—”

“Stand back while I bust the bars,” I interrupted.

“You really want to free me?”

Once again, I could hear the surprise in the siren’s voice.

“Wasn’t that the deal?”

“Silly boy, you can’t free me here,” she giggled. “You’re a few cards short of breaking this place. Besides, it’s not the bars that make the prison. The pawn that wasted his time watching me was just a decoration, a safeguard to order the statues into action if they messed up.” She looked at the nearby canal. “If he were alive right now, she’d be dragging their asses out of the water, cursing every second of it. No, it takes a Grand Arcana holder to guard a Grand Arcana holder. Coincidentally, that’s the same person you’re after.”

The same person? This was something I didn’t expect. All this time, I had assumed both entities to be separate. As far as I was aware, there was no relation between trial monsters. They, like us, were just puppets placed in an artificially created world whose goal it was to kill as many of us as possible. Even in cases in which we revisited the same world and faced the same entities, they didn’t seem to remember us.

“Surprised?” The siren slid her fingers along the adamantium bars. “And here I thought that you knew almost everything. The whole reason you came to chase your rule breaker was because of me.”

“Elaborate.”

“The trial doesn’t matter. It’s only a prison to keep me out of sight. I’d like to say that you were the reason for that…” she let out a sad sigh. “But there are other reasons. Still, if it makes you feel better, you can say that you killing me was the final straw.”

Thanks to the Observer card, I knew that she was lying; at least as far as I was concerned.

“They wanted to sweep me under the rug, so they locked me up in a trial,” she added.

“Bullshit,” I said. “You’d be released the moment the trial is over.”

“Now you’re getting it.” She clapped. “That’s my real jailer. Kill the Grand Arcana holder, and you will complete the trial. That’s where our interests align. You want him dead, I want him dead, Hypnos and his group want him dead…” she waved her hand, drawing a circle in the air with her fingers. “Even the rule-masters want him dead. You need the identity of my jailer. I have it and I’ll gladly give it to you.”

“Then why all this?” I didn’t like where things were going one bit. “You could have just told Eleonor and be done with it.”

“And miss my opportunity to see you?” The siren giggled. “Why should I? When they brought me to my new prison, they made sure to leave all my statues behind. Supposedly, I was to be taught a lesson. You can’t blame me for wanting some eye candy. I couldn’t be sure who Hypnos would get. At first, I thought it was that sweet goth oracle, but seeing you again…" she pressed her face against the bars, “… invigorating. Just looking at you makes me imagine hundreds of ways I could inflict pain.”

It won’t work. I stood defiantly.

After ten years I had come across my share of temptresses and psychopaths. A surprisingly large part of them happened to be players. The siren was just a twisted monster that reveled in the idea of creating as much discomfort as possible. Given a chance, she’d be slowly gauging my eyes right now while describing our surroundings in great detail.

“Who is it?” I asked.

“Aww,” she said in mock disappointment. “Are we done playing already?”

I wanted to cross my arms, but I knew that I couldn’t afford to. Nothing in her behavior suggested that she wouldn’t try to injure me for fun.

“Always so strict when it comes to business. I like it.” The siren stepped back. “Names don’t mean a thing, but I can tell you he’s a member of the aelf court.”

“So we’ll have to attack them.”

“Doesn’t matter. Whichever side you choose, he’ll show up and make sure that you fail. You can take the fight to him, or wait for him to take it to you. Either way, he’ll show up when you pick your side.”

Despite everything, I had to admit that was a clever approach. If we chose to attack the aelves, he’d be the most difficult to reach. If we went after the jungle elves, he and his army would betray us, attacking from behind.

“He’s the alternative win condition,” I uttered. “And he has the Stoneweaver card.”

“I knew you’d get it. That’s what makes you special, but also paints a target on your back.”

For a split second, a glint of sadness flickered in her eyes.

“Being a prodigy’s never easy.” Her tone changed. “Everyone glares at you from the shadows, secretly hoping you’d fail. When you don’t, things turn into a living hell. Getting three Grand Arcanas this early on will cause quite a stir.”

“I know.”

“No, you just think you do. Up till now they’ve mostly been playing by the rules. If you complete this trial, your life will change in ways even you can’t possibly imagine.”

“If?” I couldn’t help myself.

“Nothing is guaranteed in the Grand Arcana.” She looked me in the eye.

She was correct. Nothing was ever guaranteed, though I would have preferred if she had shown greater confidence.

I looked at the canal again. On its bottom lay eight statues, each still capable of killing me if I gave it a chance.

“Aelf court,” I said. “Any details?”

“You’ll recognize him when you see him.” She slid her index finger along her lips.

“Right.” I still hated her. Only a monster would deliberately risk our lives when she could have communicated all that through dreams.

Still looking at her, I took a step back.

“Where are you going?” The siren tilted her head again.

“You told me what you wanted. Now I must do the rest.”

“And your payment?” She laughed again. “You really forgot that, didn’t you?”

“Payment?” I asked reflectively.

“I thought that the morsel would have told you. You get here to prove you stand a chance and I’ll tell you a few other things in return. For example…” she arched back, “You can tell her more yes than maybe.”

More yes than maybe? “What does that mean?”

“Tut tut.” She wagged a finger. “That’s for her to know. As for you…” she paused. “So many questions and yet so many restrictions. Your betrayal wasn’t a selfish act. There was a deeper plan behind it. People pulling the strings, maybe even deities.” She straightened up. “You’re constantly worried about your sister, and you should be. She might not be part of the Arcana yet, but there’s a good chance she will be.”

“What?” I rushed forward and grabbed the bars.

An instant before I did, the siren moved back.

“I can’t tell you how or why or when, but you’ll need to pick up the pace. You have a chance of changing things, but only if you don’t slack.” She retreated into the darkness of her cell. “You can say that this trial is your one big test. If you manage to complete it, you’ll stand a chance. If you don’t… well, then I suppose nothing will matter.”

“Who’s after my sister?” I shouted, yet there was no response in return.

The siren had said all that she had wanted, while also getting the satisfaction of seeing me in this state. I had no idea how she was still alive, but I absolutely despised her right now. If it weren’t for the bars, I'd have considered killing her myself.

“Oh, please, do,” her voice came from the darkness. “Kill me with your magnificent saber. Then we’ll share even more than before.”

I let go of the bars and stepped back. After everything, she remained just as insane as ever. I didn’t even have a certain way of determining whether she had told me the truth or not.

After standing there for several more seconds, I turned around. It was unsafe entering the canal from this spot, so I leaped over to the other bank and continued in the direction I had come from. Steam came off my clothes as I used spell circles to dry them out.

I’ll have fangs, Serp said, his voice quieter than usual. I just need to grow a bit more.

“It’s fine. You’ll get there.”

Yeah, I know… Just… Thanks, old man.

Was he actually thanking me? This was the first time I had seen him react in such fashion. One could almost say that he was a living being, not just some Arcana card.

“No worries, Serp. Just stay focused. You’ll get your fangs soon enough.”
