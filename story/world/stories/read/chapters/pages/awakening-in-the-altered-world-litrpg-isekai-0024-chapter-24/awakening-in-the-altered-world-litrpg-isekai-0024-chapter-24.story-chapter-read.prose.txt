Finally.

His suspicion was upgrading the rarity of the Mana Manipulation Skill also provided a slight across-the-board boost to its abilities, regardless of mana type.

New supports and support levels clearly mattered for a Skill, but its overall rarity grade also seemed important.

He inspected the Skill structure in his soulspace and saw it’d gained a plethora of new lines—the majority of them were related to his Force mana, which he’d been explicitly training, but plenty were in areas he didn’t even recognize; he could visually see the difference.

Which also raised a question, actually—if Skill rarity upgrades provided this across-the-board boost, why wasn’t it better to add a bunch of slight supports, and rack up those +1’s? Was there an incentive to go deep in a Skill, rather than wide?

But from what he could tell, plenty of build guides did recommend going deep on a few supports.

He had a few hypotheses, but he moved onto the next notification.

It was a feeling he’d never had against the dueling bots—even if he’d tried very hard in his duels against them. He couldn’t even articulate precisely what the difference was.

Well, the talent difference was far bigger. As was the pressure, like I couldn’t stop the fight mid-match. And I actually got slashed, a lot.

He’d barely been touched by the dueling bots, now that he thought about it; his worst damage might’ve been the strike to the face.

And his improvement in this fight wasn’t comparable to anything he’d felt before. It certainly beat casting Skills at thin air, by a long shot.

He’d had to pull himself out of v-space to restrict himself from doing more 1v1 duels.

Never really thought I’d be the fighting type, but I guess if the conditions are right—it’s kinda fun, you know.

God, those Templars fucked me up.

They felt so far ahead of him it was insane. Even when he’d equalized in Level it was like he was fighting a different species of human. I really freaking tried at the end there, too.

It’s almost demoralizing.

But he didn’t really feel demoralized, was the thing.

It felt like he’d been shown exactly how much he could grow, even at Level 3. And something told him those Templars only showed him a fraction of the possibilities there was; he didn’t believe they were the ceiling at this Class Level for a second.

He did regret going that hard, and he also didn’t regret it.

He was wrecked, mentally; that was undeniable, but—if I can get to that rift at like 90% of my usual self, I’ll count it as a win.

It was close to closing time—somehow he always seemed to find himself staying until the facility closed.

He was famished.

But he thought about his bank account, and also the fact that he’d probably need to buy gear tomorrow for the rift—he’d get no virtual sword in real life. And something told him bringing a kitchen knife or something wasn’t going to cut it.

It is what it is.

It seemed very clear to him that he’d live or die by his mana.
