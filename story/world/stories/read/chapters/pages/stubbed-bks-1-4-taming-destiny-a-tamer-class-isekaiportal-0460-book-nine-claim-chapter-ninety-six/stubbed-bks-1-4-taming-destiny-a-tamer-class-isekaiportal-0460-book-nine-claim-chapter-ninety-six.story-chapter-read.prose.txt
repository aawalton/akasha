The cave system is…weird. And revolting.

I look down at the floor of the tunnel through which we’re walking, and my mouth twists again in disgust as it glints wetly back up at me. Even the texture beneath our feet is unnerving – stone should not have any give to it, let alone cling for a moment like slime when we lift our feet from it.

A shout rings out from just behind me and I turn to see that something has exploded from a hidden hole, red strings of slime clinging to it glinting in Aingeal’s light, and then slide back down with the prey it’s grabbed. Reacting quickly, I stab with my spear, pulling back so as not to stab through the beast and into the person trapped beneath it.

My spear point is joined by a glowing blade that presses enough force into the beast that it half-explodes.

I glare up at Valence even while I wipe my hand over my face, pulling away the blood and gore now dripping from it.

“A little warning, perhaps?” I suggest caustically.

“My apologies, Lord Markus,” he quickly answers, pulling his sword back with embarrassment twisting his features.

I sigh. It’s not the first time we’ve been showered by gore post explosion – unfortunately Valence was apparently unable to switch out the Skills he’d imbued in his sword and Force Transfer was one of those that works so much better out in the open than it does in this confined area.

“It’s fine,” I tell him, reaching out a hand to Maxen who turns out to be the one attacked. “Are you injured?” I check with him.

“Only minor cuts,” he reports. Nonetheless, I send flesh-magic into his body – we’ve discovered that this place is a nightmare for anyone with an open wound.

The red slime that coats the walls, turning it into what looks like the insides of a living body, is infested with baby parasites. Wounds need to be dealt with immediately before the parasites have time to work their way into the flesh of their unwilling hosts. As long as they are dealt with immediately, any healer or person with a healing Skill that deals with burgeoning infections – which is, interestingly, most of the basic first aid style Skills – can get rid of them.

“Did anyone else get clipped?” I ask, receiving a chorus of responses in the negative. “Alright, let’s keep going.”

I’m not at the front – that’s a trio of dedicated scouts. Instead, Valence and I are in the front of the main body of our group. We’ve chosen to bring quality over quantity. Valence and Layton both brought their four best physical fighters – I asked Maxen to bring the same – and we have a quintet of Mages, each representing a different discipline – Water, Fire, Air, Force, and a generalist mage.

“Which way should we go?” one of the scouts asks, the trio pulling back to the main group. I can see why – ahead, there’s a junction of three passages and we’ve already established that the scouts mustn’t go so far ahead that we lose sight of them.

I glance at Ninja, padding near me.

“Any ideas?” I ask her. She’s been working with the scouts, sending Storm’s Shadow forward and backward to check for threats. It’s not like she can see through the shadow projection’s eyes, exactly, but when the shadow returns to her, she gets an idea of what it’s seen and sensed. Very useful in this kind of environment.

The passage in the centre splits into two more passageways, she reports, but has nothing to add about the other two passageways – I guess she hasn’t explored them.

“Alright, thanks,” I tell her with a sigh, then close my eyes and try to send magic into the earth.

I’m the only one here with a powerful earth-aligned Skill – though others have Skills linked to the earth, particularly the Water and generalist mages, none of them are above Journeyman. That makes me responsible for figuring out our way through the maze when the other scouts can’t see where to go. Which, in this twisting labyrinthine mess of natural and terraformed passageways, is often.

The main issue is that I’m not actually touching the earth, meaning that I have to press my magic through the membrane of domain-protected slime to reach the stone below – possible, but tiring and limiting.

Unfortunately, the slime isn’t flammable, and seems to suck the magic out of any sort of fire-related Skill that lands on it. After I tested it, I decided that there was no way I should let Aingeal anywhere near – the fire elemental has been relegated once more to a hands-free torch.

Water fares little better. A powerful-enough jet of water can carve a way through the slime, but as soon as it cuts off, the slime around quickly flows back into place and within moments it’s like nothing happened. Similar with other magic.

My Flesh-Shaping seemed the most likely to be useful, but, again, the power of the Tier four’s domain prevented me from getting very far. I might have been able to defeat it if I put my whole strength against the Tier four’s, but that’s not how it worked out. The parasite’s ability to hide is reflected in the domain – instead of offering me something to press against, my attempts to grasp control are like I’m trying to grab a greasy and wiggling piglet. Strength means nothing if I can’t get a grip.

We do what we can with what we have. And in this case, that is an ability to sense what is in the environment around us – as long as it’s not too far. On the upside, I’ve noticed that the domain bolstering the slime has been strengthening little by little – I hope that that is a good indication that we're heading in the right direction and not a misdirection. Certainly, it seems to correlate with the compass-like instrument held by one of the scouts that points towards the closest Tier four aura.

“I think we should take the left hand passageway,” I say finally, opening my eyes. “It’s the only one that heads downwards within the next fifty metres – and that’s where you said the instrument is pointing, correct?” I look pointedly at the scout bearing the compass.

“Yes, my lord,” she confirms. “No change with that.”

We continue forwards, moving carefully and doing our best to check for ambushes. Unfortunately, just as the slime limits how far I can press my earth-magic, it also muffles Skills others use to warn them of threats. Traps trigger in unexpected places, but most of the time we’re able to react quickly enough to avoid serious injury.

Most of the time. There are exceptions. A pit suddenly opens after half of us have already passed through. A fighter from Valence’s retinue falls in and a second only avoids it when Bastet bites the back of his armour to pull him back, the male larnatis helping to brace her so she isn’t pulled in by his weight. I was uncertain about bringing him even with a Bond in place, but I’m glad to see his automatic reaction is to help her.

Quickly making my way backwards, I wince as I see what’s happened. The pit is full of sharp bone spears and has a layer of thick red slime at the bottom, making it look far too much like a mouth. The Classer who fell in is in a bad state – he has two bone spears sticking straight through him, one through his head and the other piercing his chest. He isn’t moving.

Using my wings to prevent myself from meeting the same end, I jump in after the Warrior, placing my hand on his head and sending flesh-magic into him.

My stomach sinks as my magic easily enters his body – nothing there to resist it.

Sighing, I look back up.

“Is he…?” Valence asks, hesitantly. His expression clouds over as I shake my head.

“I’m sorry,” I reply sadly.

“Oh Mul,” Valence murmurs, his face twisting in grief.

We’re silent for a moment. This isn’t the place to pause for long, but we can take a moment to grieve for someone who shouldn’t have died here. And to renew our determination to deal with the mastermind that’s already cost us far too much.

And then the moment passes and I reach down to pull the Warrior – Mul – off the spears that killed him. He doesn’t deserve to be left here – and the Tier four doesn’t deserve to benefit from his blood.

I pass his body over to Valence who thanks me disconsolately. The body vanishes a moment later, no doubt put into the other heir’s Inventory.

As we continue walking, following both what I’ve sensed and the direction of the compass, my fingers clench into a fist. We’ve been remarkably lucky in our battles so far – the reports I received about the casualties reveal many injuries, but few deaths. But this one was so sudden and so unexpected that it’s hit me hard.

We’re coming for you, I think savagely towards the Tier four. You messed with the wrong people.

Lathani, the closest one of my companions to me, obviously senses my mood and moves forward to rub herself against me soothingly.

We will kill the threat, she promises me. Then you will not need to worry.

I force a smile, reaching up to scratch her behind her closest ear.

Thanks, I tell her gratefully, then let my hand drop as we continue walking in cautious silence.

While outwardly appearing calm, my rage continues to boil under the surface.

If not for this damn slime! I think angrily to myself. Without it, we would be able to see the ambushes and traps from a mile away. But it’s only getting thicker. If it keeps doing that, we may find ourselves wading through it before we reach the Tier four – and that’s hardly going to be good for us.

There must be a way of getting rid of it.

Even as we continue walking with even more care than before, I keep thinking through and testing out possibilities, dismissing one after another. We fend off several more attacks and just about avoid another pit, this one designed to drown rather than to stab.

But it’s only when we’re doused in the slime from a hole that, unbeknownst to us, exists in the tunnel ceiling, that I manage to make any sort of progress.

Filled with sheer disgust at the thought of any of those parasites managing to get in my mouth, my nose, my ears, my eyes, I press outwards with my domain. And, incredibly, the slime itself is kept at bay.

I blink, watching the slime slide down what looks like an invisible wall a couple of inches from my body.

Bastet, can you extend your domain to protect us? I ask hopefully.

I can try, she agrees. I sense her concentrating. A moment later, the slime goes flying in both directions, as if it’s suddenly been repelled by an expanding bubble. The bubble doesn’t quite cover our whole party, but everyone who it does cover is abruptly clean of slime. Even more importantly, so are the walls, floor, and ceiling of the tunnel.

Revealed now the slime has been banished, is the macabre carpet of bones that lay beneath – the sheer deadliness of this creature made obvious.

Well done, I tell her. How long can you hold this for?

Not long, she replies, and I can already feel she’s straining. The domain is heavy.

“Then we mustn’t waste time,” I say aloud. “Come next to me – that way your domain can highlight any ambush holes or potential traps around us all.”

As soon as she does, the male larnatis close on her heels, I put my hand on her shoulder. I helped her once in a battle with another Tier three – I can use the Skill I earned there to help her here. If it will help her keep the rest of us safe, it’s absolutely worth the effort.

Following our lead, the other larnatis extends his own domain. It’s weaker than Bastet’s, as well as being wild and unfamiliar, and more than a little uncomfortable, but it helps keep the slime off us.

And so, sending a flicker of gratitude to both of them, we keep going.
