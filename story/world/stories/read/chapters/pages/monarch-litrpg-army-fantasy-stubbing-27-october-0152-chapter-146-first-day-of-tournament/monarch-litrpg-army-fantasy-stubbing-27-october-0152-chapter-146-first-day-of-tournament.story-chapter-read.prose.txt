The arena was packed.

The last time Rayne had visited, he had thought there were already a ridiculous number of people present. Looking back now, that day felt almost empty compared to what he was seeing.

Every road leading toward the arena was overflowing with people.

Humans, elves, and several races Rayne didn't even recognize moved through the streets in a constant stream. Voices blended together into one massive wall of noise. Hawkers pushed strange trinkets into passing hands and food carts shouted about food and drinks while guards constantly barked orders for everyone to keep moving.

It was chaos. Organized chaos, perhaps, but chaos nonetheless.

The closer they got to the arena, the worse it became.

City guards stood at nearly every intersection trying to prevent bottlenecks from forming. Unfortunately, it was obvious they weren't prepared for this many people.

Every few minutes somebody stopped walking to argue, buy something, or simply stare at the massive arena ahead. That was usually enough to create a pileup behind them.

Rayne found himself pushed forward by the crowd more than once.

The tournament was clearly a much bigger event than he had expected.

People weren't just coming to watch fights. For many of them, this was probably one of the biggest sources of entertainment they would see in several months.

Not everyone had to struggle against monsters, train with weapons, or march across battlefields. Most people simply worked, slept, and repeated the process.

Watching warriors beat each other unconscious in an arena was probably exciting.

Of course, some people didn't have to deal with the crowds at all.

Nobles moved through the streets almost effortlessly. Specifically, their guards moved people for them.

Heavy-armored men walked ahead of carriages and noble parties, clearing paths wherever they went. Most commoners immediately stepped aside without complaint.

The few who didn't were usually encouraged. Sometimes politely. Sometimes not.

At one point, Rayne watched a young noble walking toward the arena with three armored guards around him.

One unfortunate man failed to move quickly enough and immediately found himself shoved into the dirt. Another received a slap across the back of his head.

"Move! Don't you see who's coming?!” one of the guards shouted.

The crowd parted immediately.

Several people frowned as the noble passed, but nobody actually said anything. The young man himself didn't even glance at them. He simply continued walking as though nothing had happened.

For a moment, Rayne found himself wondering how nobles managed to behave like complete assholes in public without consequences.

Then the answer came to him.

Blood.

Whether people admitted it or not, blood still mattered. Most commoners had spent their entire lives being told nobles were above them. That some families were simply born to rule while others were born to follow.

Maybe that would change eventually. Perhaps in a century, maybe longer. But right now, that was simply how the kingdom worked.

With those thoughts in mind, Rayne continued moving forward alongside the rest of his party.

Eventually, they reached the arena entrance.

Unlike ordinary spectators, Rayne, Bran, and Jason weren't required to pay. Participants entered through a separate line while everyone else handed over coins before being allowed inside.

The rest of the party split away shortly afterward and headed toward the spectator sections assigned to them.

Meanwhile, Rayne followed Bran and Jason deeper into another tunnel that led to the stands built for the participants on the first floor.

The fighting wouldn't begin for another hour.

First came the opening. Then the tournament officials would reveal the match pairings. Only after that would the actual battles begin.

By the time they stepped through the tunnel and looked out across the arena floor, the stands were already filling rapidly.

Thousands upon thousands of spectators occupied the seats. The noise rolling down from above sounded almost like distant thunder.

And standing in the middle of the sand below were several figures already waiting. One of the figures standing below was actually the halfgar they had registered with several days ago.

The crooked nose made him easy to recognize even from a distance.

Beside him stood two knights encased in full plate armour. Neither moved much, their hands resting on the pommels of their swords as they silently watched the arena around them.

Standing between them was an elderly man wearing flowing red robes.

His hair had long begun fading from age, leaving more grey than black, but there was still a straightness to his posture that immediately drew attention. Even amongst the others, he naturally stood at the centre.

Rayne found himself guessing that the man was probably responsible for managing the arena. Five minutes later, he learned that he was right.

The old man stepped forward and raised one hand into the air.

Almost immediately, a spell matrix appeared above his palm. Several glowing circles spun around each other before drifting upward and settling near his mouth.

Then the man spoke.

“Everyone, I am pleased to see that once again another Valers City Tournament has managed to grip our city and bring all of you here today.”

The effect was immediate. His voice spread across the entire arena with unnatural clarity.

Thousands of conversations died almost instantly as spectators turned their attention toward the arena floor. Even those seated in the highest rows could hear every word perfectly due to the spell he used.

The old man smiled as the noise gradually faded.

“I am Albert Whiskers, manager of this arena, and I hope all of you will have a wonderful experience over the coming days.”

A few cheers rose from the crowd, and Albert waited patiently before continuing.

“And I say that with complete confidence because this year we have several participants who are certain to excite you.”

The crowd immediately grew more attentive as Albert spread his hands dramatically.

“First among them is the Crimson Guard.”

The reaction was instant. Cheers erupted from several sections of the arena while curious murmurs spread through others.

“You all know that they serve His Highness, First Prince Melakar. Today, a young party from the Crimson Guard will enter this tournament to demonstrate their skill, their discipline, and exactly why they serve the first prince.”

The cheering only grew louder at the mention of the prince. Albert allowed it to continue for several moments before raising a hand again.

“And to watch over them, His Highness himself has chosen to grace this event with his presence.”

As he spoke, Albert pointed toward the noble stands.

Rayne followed the gesture along with thousands of others, and for the first time since arriving in Valers, he finally laid eyes upon the first prince of the kingdom.

In a single word, the man was exactly how Rayne imagined a prince should look.

He possessed the same blonde hair as the third prince, but that was where the similarities ended. His face was sharper, his posture straighter, and there was a natural charisma around him that immediately drew attention.

The moment the crowd's eyes settled on him, the prince raised a hand and waved.

The response was immediate. Cheers rolled across the arena like an ocean wave. Thousands of voices shouted his name while others simply applauded. Even people seated far away stood to get a better look.

It was obvious that Prince Melakar was popular, or at least the commoners didn't see his face often.

Rayne, however, didn't spend long looking at him. His gaze drifted towards the man standing beside the prince.

Atticus.

The same man who had beaten him little more than a week ago. His cousin stood slightly behind Prince Melakar, speaking quietly into the prince's ear while maintaining the same smug look Rayne remembered from before.

As though sensing his attention, Atticus suddenly turned his head. For a brief moment, their eyes met across the arena.

Then a faint sneer appeared on Atticus's lips before disappearing just as quickly.

Rayne felt his jaw tighten. The man already knew he was participating in the tournament for a while. That much was obvious.

Beside him, Bran followed his gaze before muttering quietly. “Is that him?”

Rayne nodded. “Yes.”

Jason looked towards the noble stand and frowned. “Doesn't look that strong.”

“Don't judge by appearances,” Bran replied immediately. “He's a spellsword in the end.”

Jason only grunted.

The large soldier clearly wasn't impressed, though Rayne suspected that attitude would change quickly if Atticus started throwing mana around.

Fortunately, Albert continued speaking before the conversation could go further.

The arena manager spent the next several minutes introducing other notable participants.

One party came from Astra Academy. Three third-year students stood proudly in one of the lower noble sections, earning a respectable amount of applause from the audience.

Another introduction drew even louder cheers.

An adventurer party known as the Black Hawks rose from their seats and acknowledged the crowd. Judging by the reaction they received, they had built a fairly impressive reputation amongst the commoners of Valers.

Rayne quietly memorized every group Albert mentioned. Any of them could become future opponents, and there was no telling who they might fight first.

But then suddenly, he felt it.

Albert's gaze briefly settled on him, and Rayne's stomach immediately sank. A second later, realization struck.

Honestly, he should have expected it the moment the halfgar was so shocked seeing his surname during registration.

A Frayser was almost as exciting to the crowd as the Crimson Guard. Perhaps even more.

The rebellion was still fresh in people's minds. Many probably knew the name better than they knew the names of most noble houses.

So, Rayne simply resisted the urge to frown and waited. Soon enough, Albert smiled broadly before speaking again.

“And finally, at the very end, we have someone particularly special participating in this year's tournament.”

The arena gradually quieted.

“A person who, by all accounts, shouldn't even be alive today.”

Murmurs immediately spread through the crowd, and Albert’s smile only widened.

“So keep your betting coins close because I suspect many of you will want to wager for him... or against him.”

The murmuring grew louder. Then Albert pointed directly towards him.

“He is Rayne Frayser of the Valerian Army.” For a brief moment, silence filled the arena, then Albert delivered the final blow. “Yes, everyone. The last line of the traitors.”

This time the reaction was completely different than the names before him.

The cheers vanished, the conversations died, and suddenly, thousands upon thousands of eyes turned towards Rayne.

Some carried curiosity, others carried disbelief. A few carried outright hostility. Yet all of them were looking at him.

For the first time in his new life, Rayne felt as though an entire city was staring directly at him.

The participants gathered throughout their stand were also looking at him. Some openly stared while others whispered amongst themselves, and he heard each word.

Fortunately, Albert wasn't finished yet. The old arena manager raised his hands once more and smiled toward the crowd.

“With that,” he announced, “I am certain this tournament will become something the city speaks about for years to come.”

The crowd responded with loud cheers, their attention back to him.

Albert waited patiently before continuing. “The battle rosters have already been posted outside the participant waiting rooms. For those of you looking to put your coins to good use, they have also been delivered to the betting halls.”

That immediately earned another reaction from the audience.

“I truly hope all of you have a wonderful time today and throughout the coming days.”

With those final words, he offered a slight bow before stepping away from the center of the arena with the halfgar and the knights.

The moment he did, Rayne had no intention of remaining where he was. Neither did Bran.

The old man immediately turned and began leading them back through the tunnel toward the waiting rooms. Jason and Rayne followed without hesitation.

After being singled out in front of half the city, Rayne had little desire to remain standing in full view of everyone.

As they walked, Jason shook his head. “That was quite an opening.” Rayne glanced at him. “The old bastard practically threw you into the mouth of a dragon.”

Rayne grunted. “It gets him more bets.”

Jason raised an eyebrow. “Meaning?”

“Meaning everyone will want to see me fail,” Rayne replied. “The more people care about the outcome, the more money gets wagered. He's a good businessman.”

Jason let out a bark of laughter. “Nate's going to make a killing.”

Rayne couldn't really argue with that. The man was probably already running towards the betting room with a big pouch of coins.

As he imagined that, Bran turned a corner and the waiting area finally came into view.

Several groups had already gathered there.

Just outside the entrance stood a large wooden board covered with sheets of parchment. Parties crowded around it, studying the tournament pairings while discussing their chances.

His party stopped near the back, and it didn't take long for Rayne to find their names. Almost immediately, a wave of relief washed through him.

Their first opponents were another adventurer party. Not the Crimson Guard, not the Astra Academy students, none of the more famous groups Albert had introduced.

Just an ordinary adventurer team.

For a brief moment, Rayne relaxed. Then he noticed Bran frowning beside him. The scout's eyes remained fixed on the board.

Rayne opened his mouth to ask what was wrong before his own gaze drifted lower.

A second later, his stomach dropped. Just beneath their bracket sat another name.

The Crimson Guard.

Rayne stared at it for several seconds before the realization fully settled in.

If his party won their first match and the Crimson Guard won theirs, there would be no avoiding it.

The second round would be against Atticus.
