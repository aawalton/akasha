My mouth sets in a grim line as I take in the chain before me. I glance back at Elarion who’s standing close behind, his arms crossed, a forbidding expression on his face, though I’m pretty sure I can see fear gleaming in his eyes.

But for what reason – fear that I will discover his hand in the sabotage, or fear that I will assume he’s involved when he isn’t?

The first thing he said to me when I landed on his ship was that he was relieved I had survived, and swore that he had not been involved in the failure of the chain. Of course, I don’t have Kalanthia’s ability to read minds, but he seemed to be truthful.

The corroded links in front of me, however, make it very clear that someone has engaged in sabotage. Though the question is how – as River, Iandee, Artemis, and Orion confirm, they stood guard over the anchor and the ropes binding it to Elarion’s deck at all times. Then again, with magic around, perhaps I shouldn’t be surprised that physical guards weren’t enough to keep it secure.

Kalanthia, Lathani, I need your help to determine if people are telling the truth or not, I send into the Bond network. Both are currently in my spatial tattoo, as I suspected I would need their aid.

Though wary of potential betrayal, I still flew to Elarion’s ship since it was the easiest option. And it’s not like I came alone: apart from those accompanying me in my tattoo, Noir, Sirocco, Ivor, and the newly-Evolved Bastet soared by my side. The last might need to rest even more than I do, but she can keep going a little longer – this can’t wait.

Lathani eagerly emerges, Kalanthia not quite as enthusiastic as her cub, but willing to help nonetheless. Elarion, his captain, and his attendant take a step backwards when a second Tier three comes to join Bastet, stepping out from behind me. She perches on a patch of deck that really looks too small for an elephant-sized nunda.

“Lord Elarion,” I start formally, drawing his narrowed eyes back to me. “Do you know anything about why the chain snapped despite the magical reinforcements woven into its forging?”

It’s a relatively vague question, but his response will tell me where to take the interrogation.

And it is an interrogation – we both know it. If Elarion is guilty of sabotage, then I have to deal with one of my allies conspiring to murder me – and everyone else on my ship with me. If he’s not, then I’m still going to have to deal with an investigation to find the saboteur.

That’s another reason why I flew in rather than taking a boat or bringing my ship close enough to walk across a gangway. I don’t want to risk the saboteur – or saboteurs – escaping onto another ship. To that end, Garous has been ordered to keep his vessel at a distance from our two, and the four other ships that are swiftly approaching us will be signaled to do the same when they’re close enough.

“I…” Elarion hesitates in answering my question and my eyes narrow at him. “I did not damage the chain.”

Truth? I ask Kalanthia and Lathani while not taking my gaze off the other lord.

Truth, Kalanthia agrees and Lathani wordlessly seconds her conclusion.

I nod almost imperceptibly. His wording of his statement, truthful as it might be, is very telling.

“So you didn’t damage the chain, but do you know who did?”

“Are you certain that the chain did not just snap under the strain? It is designed to hold a ship against the movement of the ocean currents, not against a Tier five’s power,” Elarion hedges.

I fix him with a stern look.

“Lord Elarion, do not do either of us the disservice of playing the fool. Enhanced steel should have been able to hold against ocean currents, even those moved by a Tier five’s domain. Besides, snapping under pressure would not explain the corrosive damage that is obvious. This is deliberate sabotage. Now, again, do you know who damaged the chain?”

Elarion looks at me levelly, his expression blank and unreadable.

“I do not know who damaged the chain,” he tells me, his voice ringing with a truth that I don’t need the nundas to confirm. However, Kalanthia adds a warning that his thoughts are racing fast and multiple faces are flashing through them.

“But you have a good idea who did,” I guess shrewdly.

He hesitates a moment longer, eyeing me calculatingly, then lets out a long, slow breath that deflates him.

“Lord Markus, there are many on my ship who do not enjoy Titanbend’s hegemony over the noble House of Forestheart. Many who might see it as poetic justice for a chain to be broken that causes the Titanbend scion to be consumed by a powerful beast.”

Again, though the nunda’s confirmation of his words as truth is useful, I don’t need it. This might be the most honest thing he’s ever said to me, our conversation before leaving Moriax included. And I strongly suspect that this isn’t only occurring to him now; if others thought it while we were struggling for our lives, I suspect he did too. However, to his credit, he personally didn’t act on it.

“Then, is there anyone who is likely to have not only thought such a thing, but taken action on it?”

“Several,” Elarion admits reluctantly after a moment.

“Their names and positions, please,” I press, not allowing him off the hook.

Elarion hesitates again, his eyes displaying an agony of indecision. By this point, I could easily order the whole ship to be interrogated – could have done that even before this admission. However, this is a very important moment. Someone acted to kill everyone on my ship. If their target was indeed me, then they treated everyone else as collateral damage. If he stands with that person, even by refusing to name them, he cannot be my ally.

“Lord Elarion,” I continue firmly as the silence elongates. “Whoever this person is, they’re guilty of attempted mass murder. Their attempt to kill me would have killed everyone on my ship with me. Sailors, fighters, crafters, slaves, innocent children alike. And though I know the courts in Moriax would hold the attempted murder of a Great House heir to be the worst crime of the lot, I personally would consider the indiscriminate method to be most worthy of punishment.”

I lean forward to emphasise my words. “Children, Elarion, children would have been murdered for no good reason. Your House might resent the ruling of the king two generations ago, but at the very least you must admit that it was directed at those who had done the wrong.”

Around us, I sense the eyes of the crew, hear the unusual quiet on the desk as those around try to listen even as they pretend to be busy. I could pull up a Seal, but I don’t. Let them hear. Let the pressure of their watching eyes act on their lord.

Elarion almost visibly winces when I bring up the ruling of the king, but his eyes hold more respect than they did a few minutes ago. He gazes at me speculatively for a moment more.

Then, as he nods fractionally, something in his eyes hardens.

“I do not know who did it,” he tells me firmly. “I was not involved – neither in the planning, nor the execution. I am only guessing that it was committed on my family’s behalf as I did not encourage the person’s actions by word or deed. Nor do I know if they were acting alone or with others.” His words ring with truth that Kalanthia and Lathani then confirm. “However, those who have served my family for the longest would be my first suspects – you understand why I would not wish to reveal them, surely?” he finishes, almost pleading.

“I understand,” I agree, “but if they had kept their resentment to muttered words and dark thoughts, we would not be here having this conversation. The fact is that they acted on their resentment in a way that led to innocents being caught in the crossfire.”

“I understand,” he admits, his unhappiness still evident in his eyes. “Which is why I will tell you my suspicions – as long as you assure me that you will investigate thoroughly and only punish those truly guilty.”

I cross my arms and give him an unimpressed look.

“What, exactly, in my whole approach to this situation and everything else suggests that I’m going to take your suggestions as guarantees of guilt without investigating further?”

Elarion eyes me for a moment and then nods again fractionally.

“Point,” he admits. Then, taking a deep breath, he reluctantly lists some names. I notice the captain looking at him in surprise a couple of times, and Elarion’s attendant looks almost as unhappy as Elarion himself.

Once I have the list of names, I question Elarion’s attendant to check that he is not personally involved in the situation. When I’ve confirmed, with the help of the nundas, that he wasn’t, for all the resentment towards my family that infests his thoughts, I send him off with River, Iandee, and Lathani to find the named people and to bring them to the deck.

With everyone assembled, some emanating resentment or fear strongly enough that even I can sense it, I prepare the next round of interrogation. Having mind-reading nundas on my side makes finding the truth so much easier than it might otherwise be. There are the equivalent of truth serums here, of course, but there are antidotes that people can take ahead of time and one of the suspects is Elarion’s herbalist. On Moriaxar, a Judicator’s Skills would be used for this purpose, but we don’t have any of those with us since they’re all sworn to the crown.

Since I don’t want all the suspects hearing each interrogation, I conduct them one by one under Seal. Though, this time, I make sure to include the nundas so they can continue sensing the suspects’ thoughts.

It doesn’t take too long to find the culprits. Elarion was right to have named these people – every one of them apparently holds significant hatred towards me. Not personal hatred, of course, but just because I’m a Titanbend. All of them considered doing something, but only two of them actually acted on it.

In the end, it all becomes clear. One of the culprits was the herbalist – she acted in the background, whipping up a strong corroding potion as soon as it was clear that our survival depended on that single braided strand of metal. This potion was powerful enough that it was capable of corroding the protective enchantments as well as eating through the magically-reinforced steel of the chain.

Her partner, the one who coated the chain in the potion, is a Warrior who was part of Elarion’s personal retinue. His part in the conspiracy was to lean dangerously out of a port hole below the stern of the ship, directly under where the chain stretched between our two ships. By using a pole onto which he tied the potion vial and then dexterously manipulating it so the potion tipped out and onto the metal, he avoided the watchful gazes of my companions. They hadn’t considered that something might attack it from that direction since Elarion doesn’t have any Beast Tamers with him.

Both herbalist and Warrior deny their actions at first, but, when confronted with their lies, the herbalist goes quiet while the Warrior crosses his arms and admits his guilt without any sign of remorse, proclaiming that he would willingly fight any day against the oppressor who has so injured his liege lord’s honour.

I ask Kalanthia and Lathani to keep a careful watch on his thoughts in case his bluster and emotionally-laden words are a shrewd strategy to hide the involvement of other people, but they don’t detect any lie when he proudly declares that only he and Kera Wallis had the courage to take action against the oppressor.

I release the Seal on the Warrior, mentally asking my companions to keep a sharp eye on him – I’m not at all certain that he won’t decide to just turn around and attack me. In a way I almost wish he would – that would give me the excuse to tear him apart as my anger would dearly like me to do. Not only for my own protection, but to prevent him from ever putting all those on my ship in danger once again.

But, even if my instincts are often useful, they don’t rule me. I have enough awareness to understand that Ser Falkonir and Kera Wallis might have been the ones to act on their hatred, but resentment of my family runs deep on this ship. While I might be satisfied with borrowing Bastet’s claws and slitting the throats of the two who tried to feed my whole ship to a Tier five, all they would see is a scion of an oppressing House killing those they’ve known and worked with for years.

Nor would such a summary execution be well-received across the fleet. Hearing both crime and punishment in the same breath is unlikely to have the best impact, especially among the other four ships that might not have noticed the difficulty we had in the last moments. Tyrant is not a good look and is a hard label to shake off once gained.

So, though part of me regrets the Warrior’s self-preservation when he eyes the pony-sized raptorcat crouching over me and snarling and then steps away, my logical mind knows that it’s for the best.

“Join Kera Wallis,” I direct him, fighting to keep my voice level. Perhaps he realises how close to the edge I am as he follows instructions without any further boasts. Or perhaps he just doesn’t want to speak now it’s not only me and the two nundas listening, but most of a curious crew.

Silently, he stands beside the herbalist who’s currently looking plaintively towards Elarion. The lord in turn is pointedly avoiding even looking in her direction. Her own words and Kalanthia’s commentary on her thoughts have told me a lot about why she acted the way she did.

Kera Wallis is an aging lady and used to be Elarion’s nursemaid. After he left the nursery, she transitioned into helping the Alchemist of the time and gained enough knowledge to take over most of the role when he retired. She knows about the relationship between House Titanbend and House Forestheart and believed that by breaking the chain she was protecting Elarion. He is so much more valuable to her than a ship of faceless others that, even though the attempt failed, she has no remorse about having attempted it.

I’ve been thinking about how to deal with the culprits even while the interrogation took place, but although I’ve ruled out a number of punishments, I still haven’t decided exactly what to do. But I know where to start.

“Elarion,” I start levelly. “I wish these two to have a fair trial before representatives of all of the ships. Will you swear on your honour that they will be confined to a room in which they cannot do any further damage until such time as everyone is assembled?”

He hesitates. I understand why – by making it a question of his honour, I make him their jailor. But that is exactly why I’ve asked. Kera Wallis and Ser Falkonir don’t care two hoots for anyone on my ship – but they do care for Elarion.

If I take them to my ship, they will try to escape or perhaps even sink us, not caring who they injure or kill in the process. On Elarion’s, however, they will probably be far more careful. And if they’re not, then that works to my advantage anyway – people are less understanding of others when their own blood has been spilt.

In the silence, the mutters of those not even pretending not to watch any more increase. I don’t know which side the crew would fall on – those who are not Elarion’s direct retinue but crew recruited by the captain I engaged. I doubt Elarion knows either.

Then Elarion’s expression firms. Perhaps he has remembered that I wasn’t the only target; perhaps he realises that this choice affects his whole future as my ally.

“Very well,” he says finally, his tone clear of any trace of the reluctance that he must surely be feeling. “I swear on my honour that Kera Wallis and Ser Falkonir will be kept confined until their fair trial. I further swear that though I refuse to maltreat them, they will not be given any opportunity to repeat their crimes.”

He leans a little more than necessary on the word ‘fair’, but that’s understandable.

He’s made his choice – the one I hoped he would make. It’s enough for now. It remains to be seen whether he will accept and uphold their punishment later.
