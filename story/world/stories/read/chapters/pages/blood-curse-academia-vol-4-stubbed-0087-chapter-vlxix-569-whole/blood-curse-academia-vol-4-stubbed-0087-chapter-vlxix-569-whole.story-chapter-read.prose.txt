Chapter V.LXIX (5.69) - Whole

As he slept, Kizu battled with himself for control. When Zayne had abandoned Kizu, he simultaneously forced Kizu’s missing soul sliver back into his original host body during his departure. That missing piece of his soul now had a vacated corner of Kizu’s soul to slot back into, but it had altered drastically in their time apart and the fit was not nearly as clean as it might have been. Lucky for Kizu, the soul sliver had spilled a soul annihilating potion on himself directly before, or the growth would have been too much entirely. It also left his evil persona in a weakened state, easier to dominate.

Still, the ordeal was not a simple affair. The doppelgänger had clearly been plotting an usurpation of his original flesh for some time. That planning left him with far superior soul defenses. An area of study Kizu hadn’t ever bothered with, instead relying on Zayne to protect him.

Thankfully, even now trapped in an unconscious state and battling with his soul for control over his body, friends remained on hand to lend their aid. Zayne hadn’t been something his soul likely expected to encounter or account for and his brief contact with the ancient spirit had left the splinter weakened. Also, Mort’s bond with Kizu helped bat away several of the doppelgänger’s most vicious attacks. The monkey lacked the ability to go on the offensive with his soul, but Mort shielded Kizu and kept him intact through it all. Still, if he didn’t know better, he might have thought the soul fragment had a far weaker version of Mort assisting his continued existence as well. Regardless, Kizu felt himself winning in their competition through sheer resilience.

As he gained the upper hand, Kizu banished pieces of his doppelgänger’s memories into his blocked off storage, just as Roba had taught him. Bit by bit, Kizu dominated the soul sliver and regained control over himself. As he did so, he realized just how rash this endeavor had been. With Otochi out of his lair, his doppelgänger had greedily seized the opportunity when Kizu and Ione entered the World Dungeon. Only after he captured Kizu did he slam into a barrier as he realized he couldn’t actually put his plans into action due to unknown restrictions Otochi set on his body, to keep him under control. After that, Kizu's fragment had panicked and been doing his best to throw together the semblance of a new plan.

Ione and Basil were meant to be a backup method of coercion, to force Kizu to sacrifice his soul to the doppelgänger. Thankfully, it had never come to that and his friends were simply tossed to the side.

As he dismantled the soul’s memories, Kizu learned a great deal about his doppelgänger’s life post departure from his main body. It was not a pleasant existence, down in Otochi’s lair. When the Blood Lord had awakened him, he’d not only uncovered sealed memories, he’d put the worst experiences from Kizu’s childhood at the front of the soul sliver’s mind, effectively shattering his sanity. He then left him for over a month to survive getting hunted by bloodspawn. He lacked true human blood for them to devour, but they gleefully tracked and tortured him through the World Dungeon.

As Kizu reached the meat of the memories, those that would expose Otochi’s plots, he encountered a new obstacle. His soul sliver’s own vindictive and spiteful nature refused to allow him access. That corrupt piece of him now knew without a doubt it would be reabsorbed by Kizu and, instead of self-preservation, it reversed course and dived head-first into a self-destructive action. To Kizu’s horror and frustration, it began to unravel the memories, fracturing them into tiny, unreadable splinters in a final act of defiance.

When Kizu’s eyes flickered open, he still heard the echoes of a cackle. His doppelgänger was vanquished. And yet, the soul sliver still exited their final exchange with the last laugh.

“Kizu?” Ione set a warm hand on his arm.

Taking a steadying breath, Kizu forced himself into the land of the conscious. He sat up and took stock of their predicament.

They remained deep in the World Dungeon, somewhere in Otochi’s lair, judging by the chill in the air. The blue torches on the wall flickered, offering light with no warmth. He spotted a frail Basil sipping one of their nutrients potions. The shapeshifted had lost a lot of mass since he’d last seen him and required excessive amounts of food to restore himself back to full strength. More than they’d have access to down here.

Next he spotted what remained of Kumiho. The Kitsune Harbinger remained alive, but that was all that could be said about her. It seemed that Ione and Basil hadn’t even noticed her yet, as she appeared simply a lump of red and brown fur remained in the cell he’d left her in. She hadn’t spoken up or brought any attention to herself.

Kizu suddenly recalled with extenuating detail the methods in which his doppelgänger had tortured her over the last few months. That memory, the soul sliver had decided to leave intact and prominently in his mind.

His irritation with the memory slipped into a profound sadness that threatened to seize him as he realized he no longer felt the familiar presence of Zayne. He’d never truly understood the soul sliver’s presence until its absence. He still retained all of Zayne’s memories, stored back in his mind alongside his doppelgänger’s, but the soul itself was gone.

“We need to get out of the World Dungeon,” Kizu said, forcing himself out of his slump. He would have time to grieve for Zayne after they reached safety.

“Obviously,” Ione responded. She crossed her arms. “First, mind telling me what this all is?”

Kizu glanced down at where she was staring. There were identical body parts to himself scattered on the stone floor beside him. His doppelgänger’s body, now fallen into disuse. Kizu might have thought it a flesh golem, if not for it being an exact replica of himself. Obviously, it was scrawnier and with longer hair, but it was a version of him that only diverged at the moment the mirror ensnared that piece of his soul. More than a little uncanny.

“It’s the evil version of me. I reabsorbed his soul.”

Ione’s eyes widened. “Is it like with Chiame? Will it try to take control of you when you sleep? Will you need to purge it from you?”

“No.” Kizu shook his head and slowly got to his feet. He dusted himself off as Mort made himself at home on his shoulder. “I’ve fully seized control. There’s nothing there left to attempt a coup. Obviously, I’m not a soul mage and don’t have the exact answers, but I suspect it has to do with originally being part of my soul to begin with.”

“So… if I rejoined with Sene, would I lose myself?”

Kizu hesitated.

“Honestly, I have no idea. It’s a question for Aoi. But I somehow doubt that you’ll ever rejoin with Sene’s soul. Knoff mentioned how difficult the process is. Without Zayne sacrificing himself, I doubt the transition would have been so smooth.”

Ione made a sour face and looked like she wanted to say something on the subject, but Basil strode up, ending their conversation.

“Did you find my cousin?”

Kizu grimaced and gestured at the legless fox in the nearby cell. He quickly explained what had been done to her and both of his friends listened in solemn silence.

Once his story was complete, Ione simply sketched a summoning circle on the floor with her chalk and a lizard mount crawled out. Basil helped her secure Kumiho under its skin flap. Nobody spoke during the process. Not Ione. Not Basil. And not Kumiho.

With the Kitsune Harbinger safely tucked away, they turned back to Kizu.

While they’d been working on that, Kizu had worked on looting. Obviously, he had no intention of taking the horrific torture tools lining the walls, but he reclaimed both his rings, atlas, and bell from his doppelgänger’s corpse. Thankfully all of his belongings had survived their scuffle without any damage.

Seeing no further reason to stay, they departed into the heart of Otochi’s lair.

Kizu and Basil both acted as guides through the underground palace. Kizu, using his atlas, and Basil, using his memory from his time staying as Otochi’s ‘guest.’ While also a prisoner of the Blood Lord, it had been a far pleasanter ordeal than the one Kumiho had experienced and he’d had basically free reign of the lair. They navigated through the labyrinth of corridors, Kizu occasionally casting divination magic to check around corners for nearby threats. He’d reclaimed the crone’s bowl and filled it with water stored within his spatial ring. While there was nothing overtly magical about the wooden bowl, it served as a perfect conduit for divination.

“I… smell spawn,” the Kitsune woman said, her wheezing voice muffled by the skin flap.

They all stopped and double checked the passages. This was the direction of the exit. They could loop around, but there was no guarantee the extra time spent wandering the corridors wouldn’t result in more encounters with bloodspawn. It was fortuitous they’d gone so long without bumping into any. Instead of doubling back, they prepared for a fight.

“Wait.” Kizu held up a hand while he examined the incoming bloodspawn in his bowl of water. “I have an idea. Stand back and be ready for combat when I give the signal.”

A quick illusion and he assumed the gaunt form of his doppelgänger. Longer, greasier, hair fell across his shoulders.

“Pulling out your Drama Club main character credentials?” Basil asked, sounding more amused than the situation called for in Kizu’s opinion.

“We’ll see if I can get information from them. Stay in the shadows near me. My necklace’s radius only reaches so far.”

They did as he commanded, stepping to the side where Kizu weaved an illusion of darkness around them. Then, for an additional security measure, he flooded the area in a scent illusion. In a mere moment, the entire hallway stank of campfire smoke.

He heard Basil gag from the shadows, followed by the sound of Ione whacking him on the head. Ironic, as Basil usually was the one making people gag with his overwhelming perfumes.

The bloodspawn rounded the corner and paused, eyeing Kizu with caution.

“Not playing with the prisoner?” one of them asked. “Bored of her for the day? It reeks over here. Did set the Harbinger aflame?”

“He is still mortal and forced to consume subsistence to survive,” the other one commented. “Perhaps he roasted weenies over her burning fur.”

Kizu tilted his head back and forth. The illusionary hair likewise naturally flopped across his shoulders. Despite everything happening, he felt a measure of pride at his absolute command of the illusion. The branch of spellcraft hadn’t been a primary study this last year, but he hadn’t allowed his skills to atrophy either.

“I suppose so.” He smiled. “There is so much to do. What’s next? Someone to kill? Torture? Devour?”

“Not much for you to do,” one of the bloodspawn scoffed. “Lord Otochi demanded you remain behind.”

“And you!” Kizu snapped back, his face twisted in a snarl. “You’re stuck here too!”

They looked at one another and shrugged, a smirk on both of their faces. “We volunteered. Unlike you, we could have gone if we wanted to.”

“Lies!”

“Ha. You’re delusional. Go back to your hole and gnaw on your bone, dog.”

The other bloodspawn elbowed his companion, clearly believing the other to have crossed a line.

Kizu decided to play into their fear. He couldn’t cast his usual spells, but a short Primordial chant popped into his mind from his studies under the crone. He muttered it darkly, knowing his doppelgänger used hexes.

They both scurried back, fear in their red eyes.

Kizu finished the chant and raised a pointer finger at them. Then, to his shock, he felt the magic instinctively jolt out of him. Through his spellsense, Kizu witnessed a hex swirl around the pair, then it bound them together, sealing their flesh at the hip.

They stumbled and scurried away from him, tripping over their apologies and feet in equal measure.

Kizu stood there for several long seconds, staring down at his finger.

“I have access to hexes again,” he said as Ione and Basil stepped up to his side.

Basil clapped him on the shoulder. “Great! I’ve heard of all sorts of fun curses. Mind if I call in a favor? I know a few classmates who are in need of some humility.”

“You deserved what you got in your tournament bouts,” Kizu replied numbly, barely paying Basil any mind.

Ione stepped right up in his face, making her giant grin far more difficult to ignore.

“You realize, if you have hexes… then perhaps another field of study just opened up to you.”
