The pieces fall into place.

“Are you talking about Kalanthia?” I blurt out.

The nunda snarls again, but this time I sense a slight lessening of the frustration that was in it the last time.

Yes, now, for the last time, bring her out.

I hesitate for a moment. I can’t – she’s not here. But I doubt that the Tier four will accept that answer.

But not answering isn’t an option either. Every second that ticks by sees the snarl ratchet up in menace and the Tier four’s posture tense even more. Hopefully the single ward remaining between us will stop him if he actually pounces.

“She’s not here,” I say finally.

You’re lying! the nunda replies immediately.

“You can read my thoughts,” I point out. “Does it feel like I’m lying?”

That makes the nunda pause for a moment. Then the snarl returns, his sword-like teeth on full display.

I can smell her all over you!

I blink.

“Your sense of smell must be really good, then. The last time we saw each other was quite a while ago.” Not long after we made landfall, actually. Just in case his telepathy is good enough, I focus on my last view of Kalanthia, loping up the beach away from me.

The nunda’s snarl hitches for a moment, then resumes at a lower intensity as he eyes me with confusion written on his body language. He even lifts his head into the air and wrinkles his nose, taking in deep breaths through his mouth.

But her scent is fresh. It was at the site of the challenge; it is here. You must be lying. In contrast with before, his tone is far more hesitant.

At the site of the challenge – the battlefield with the Tier four? My eyes narrow as a possibility occurs to me. Lathani, can you come? I ask in the Bond network. I receive a hesitant agreement – even fun-loving Lathani is clearly frightened by this powerful member of her species.

Markus, are you sure? Bastet asks, her posture tense, her eyes fixed warily on the Tier four. She is only Evolved.

He’s still behind a ward and she can use the shadows, I reassure my companion, though admit that my words are also for myself.

One moment Bastet’s shadow on her other side is empty; the next, a black pony-sized leopard has appeared in it.

The male nunda inhales, then swings his massive head to stare at Lathani and freezes.

You’re not my mate, he says slowly after a long moment, the touch of his telepathy far more delicate than ever before. Yet you smell very like her.

I’m her cub, Lathani replies bravely, though I can see her back legs trembling, and sense her desire to fade back into the shadow. I send her a mental caress, but sense that, though it helps, it’s not enough.

I glance at the Tier four nunda who’s still frozen in place, then decide that I’m not going to leave Lathani to face him alone.

Backing up a bit, I go around Bastet’s hindquarters, and move quietly so I’m level with Lathani’s right shoulder. Placing my hand between her shoulderblades, I lend her my silent support. In exchange, Lathani leans some of her weight into me, replying with worthless appreciation.

The nunda’s sharp gaze, still glowing with white light, doesn’t miss a single gesture.

Kalanthia’s cub. That’s why the scent was not quite right, he concludes thoughtfully. Inhaling again deeply, his snarl dies down. Kalanthia’s cub…and mine.

I inhale a sharp breath. Of course. Talk about an absent father coming back to town, I think to myself.

Memories of the conversations I had with Kalanthia early on in my stay in the other world come back to mind. I remember her telling me that she was there to offer her cub a better chance of surviving to Evolution – I hadn’t realised at that time that she was speaking of a different world, though now it makes sense that she had known of humans – and Tamers. Nor had I realised that she’d had the cub already – or been pregnant – when she went to that world. Though I’d never seen any signs of her mate, I’d kind of assumed he was from the samuran world.

Yet my realisations create only more questions of just how all that is possible given that even she can’t just hop across worlds like they’re stones in a river. And what are the odds I’d meet her in the other world on the way to this one? Is the samuran world like some universal train station?

I’m pulled out of my thoughts by Lathani’s tentative voice.

You are…you are my father?

The nunda eyes her thoughtfully for a moment.

Unless she took another mate in our time apart, yes.

“I don’t believe she did,” I remark, finally remembering why ‘Prime Nunda’ is a familiar term. The very first day I met Kalanthia, she gave me part of her Will – part of her soul, I suspect – in reward for saving Lathani. The information box that came up had named her as the mate of the Prime Nunda – or something similar. The memory is not crystal clear. And why would it have displayed that if she had chosen a different mate?

The Tier four gazes at me speculatively, then returns his attention to Lathani.

My cub, he repeats again, his tone shifting into protective and possessive as he rakes his gaze over her. You have grown. This time he sounds a little sad.

Then, as if a switch has been flicked, he tenses and his snarl rips out again.

We all tense in response. What now? I wonder with some exasperation. I thought we were finally getting somewhere.

The nunda glares at me, his domain pulsing out with incredible strength. The primal fear it invokes pushes me back three paces before my logical mind is able to catch up and force my feet to still. Taking a deep, shaking breath, I press my own domain out. The Tier four’s domain is far too strong for me to repel properly, but I can at least prevent it from invading my skin. My heart finally slows from its frantic beat and I look up to glare back at the nunda.

“You agreed to pull back your domain,” I remind him as calmly as I can while both fear and anger roil inside me. Absently, I notice that neither Lathani nor Bastet seem affected – the Tier four must be concentrating its domain on me.

You have chained my cub! The Tier four accuses me.

Before I can respond, he calls down a bolt of lightning far enough away that it won’t accidentally hit us – though I suspect that Lathani is his main concern – and overloads the last ward. Wonderful.

The next thing I know, I’ve got a nunda stalking towards me, murder in his eyes. This feels familiar, I think with a touch of hysteria even as my mind flicks through the various options.

I’m on the verge of taking to the skies and flying back to the barrier as fast as my wings can take me when Lathani steps decisively in front of me.

The Tier four nunda pauses, looking indecisive, clearly not wanting to go through the daughter he’s just found out he has.

Mother already got angry about that – and forgave him for it, Lathani tells the nunda sternly. If I wasn’t once more reliant on her to keep my insides where they should be, I might find the repeated situation exasperating. Not that it was his fault – I’m the one who Bound him. She still sounds terribly proud about that. Since all’s well that ends well, I’ve forgiven her for it – and wouldn’t want her to leave the Bond network anyway.

The nunda seems to consider that for a moment. When he relaxes a little, I can finally breathe more easily again.

Where is your mother? the Tier four asks.

Gone somewhere, Lathani replies with a tone that’s just a little too nonchalant to be real. It’s been so long without contact from Kalanthia that we’re all worried about her. I know she’s alive, but that’s the limit. Even Packleader can’t reach her.

Packleader…

I eye the nunda warily, hearing his threatening rumble pick up again. What, is he angry that Kalanthia isn’t the leader or something?

You chained my mate? the nunda demands, prowling towards me once more with his teeth bared.

Oh hell, I think to myself, resisting the urge to slap my forehead. Lathani…. I glare at the nunda and she has the grace to look a little ashamed.

The Tier four sees the interaction and doesn’t approve if the way his snarl intensifies is anything to go by. Once more, I prepare to evacuate – quickly. If he doesn’t like my answers, I’ll probably have split seconds to get away.

“Kalanthia is able to break our Bond whenever she chooses,” I tell the nunda as firmly as I can, pressing my sincerity at him in hopes he’ll pick it up. “We had to have one so she could come with us to this world, and so far she hasn’t chosen to break it.”

Why not? the nunda demands, though at least he’s halted again. He also lessens the pressure of his domain a touch – a relief since I’m starting to feel a good deal of fatigue at resisting it. Bastet steps back and presses against me, offering me her silent support – all she can do right now without risking herself.

“You’ll have to ask her,” I tell him levelly.

Then call her and I will, he replies threateningly.

“It’s not that simple,” I sigh.

The snarl kicks up a notch.

Then make it simple.

I stare at the nunda flatly. Tier four or not, he’s acting like a spoilt child right now and I want him to know it.

Perhaps he picks that up from my thoughts as he shifts his weight backwards a little and his snarl quietens a little.

Why is it not simple? he asks after a moment.

“Kalanthia didn’t tell me where she was going,” I start, deciding that emphasising her independence is probably the best place to begin. “In theory, I can teleport her back. However, in practice, that would take a huge amount of mana – more than I have available for sure – and she might be very angry about it if I interrupted something important.”

The nunda considers my words for a moment.

Where did she go? I gesture towards the north-west. She went to find me, he decides with certainty. Our territory used to be that way. You have no reason not to bring her back. Then he bares his teeth again, his domain pressing threateningly on me once more. Unless you have something to hide.

I control my breathing. If the nunda is correct about Kalanthia’s intentions – if – then perhaps she won’t be absolutely furious at being forcibly teleported back. However, that doesn’t solve the issue to do with just how much mana I’ll need to complete the teleport. Given Kalanthia’s power and how far she must be, the cost to bring her here will be astronomical.

Perhaps the nunda reads my reluctance in my thoughts because he once more leans closer, his teeth bared, his eyes glowing even more brightly. I refuse to run or cower like scared prey, instead standing tall without flinching as he gives his ultimatum.

Perhaps I should be more clear, Binder. If you do not bring my mate back to me before the next dawn, I shall take my cub and wipe the stain of you and your filthy human subjects off my territory.
