Seb had just finished his coffee when he noticed the stranger entering the dining hall. He went to pick up Anya's mug to get them refills only to pause when he noticed Gwen's entire table stand up and come to attention while staring towards the door.

Anya? he sent, pointing their shared attention at the newcomer.

Tall and dangerous, she answered.

Seb barely held back his confusion as he turned in his chair. Anya's description was strangely accurate. The man was tall with light brown hair, but the main impression he got was one of danger. He wasn't doing anything unusual, simply standing there, yet Seb had no doubt that nothing could happen that would surprise the man.

The stranger took one step towards them then another, his eyes focused on the fae further in the room, only to hesitate as his gaze dipped and caught sight of them. Or rather, Seb assumed, of Anya. Gwen and the others recognized him, he seemed to recognize them as well. They'd identified Anya at first sight, it would make sense for someone who knew them to do so as well.

An instant later Gwen and Cara were standing next to their table while the remainder approached at a more normal seeming pace.

"Lord Percivus," Gwen said. "We didn't expect to see you here. I thought you were heading into seclusion to continue your studies."

"Gwen," he answered. "His Eminence offered up my services as a teacher as part of the arrangement for your attendance."

"But then why are you only here days later? You could have arrived with us if…"

"I was informed two days ago that my specific skills would be of benefit."

"My Lord," Bern said stiffly as he joined them. "I am pleased you are here. My sister insists that this one is family without authority. If you would set all our minds at ease by confirming that she is not it would simplify matters greatly."

Seb felt Anya's irritation threaten to boil over. Every time Bern spoke of her, around her, or even to her he seemed determined to cause offense. He seemed determined to dislike her simply because of how she looked, dislike them both for that reason and others.

"She is," Cara said sharply. "Mistress Gwen gave me orders to obey anyone the geas saw as family and…"

"And I hurt her. I didn't expect the question Anya asked and Cara was caught between our words," Gwen clarified as Cara trailed off awkwardly.

"That is not sufficient evidence that she is-" Percivus began.

"Enough!" Anya said as she stood up quickly enough to send her chair tumbling. "Must you all speak as though I am not present? Must you discuss me as though I am an object to be judged and weighed? I didn't ask to be family, not to anyone who cannot politely include me in a conversation about me that I can hear."

Seb stood more slowly and circled the table to stand beside her. There was nothing for him to say, he could repeat her words but that would do very little when the entire group seemed struck by her words.

"I apologize," Gwen said. "Again."

"Not sufficient to be certain you are family," Percivus said. "However sufficient to require proper consideration. I will not gainsay Gwen's prerogative without evidence."

Seb saw Gwen spin and grin triumphantly at Bern before flushing slightly and turning to face Anya.

"I still consider you family."

He could feel Anya relax slightly, only to tense as Percivus spoke once more.

"I will be resuming my role as your teacher while I am here, assuming you have not already been assigned a mentor?"

"We have not," Morgan said, finally joining the discussion.

"Have any of you come across a student capable of Bladesong?"

Seb tensed as they all turned to face him, Percivus only a moment after the rest as their intent became clear.

"Seb, Anya's Partner and husband," he said as calmly as he could under the pressure of Percivus's gaze. "That would be me."

"That," Percivus said. "That makes you both most interesting."

Anya glanced across the arena floor to where Percivus stood talking with Sarna. He'd joined the other fae for breakfast before following them all to their dueling lesson and then spend the last however long it had been in discussion with their mentor.

Finally he stepped to the side as Sarna turned and gestured to the two of them. Anya could feel Seb at her side as they went to join her, both of them uncertain yet secure in their knowledge that they could, and would, handle it together.

"I told you a few days ago every group is assigned a mentor." Sarna said as they came to a halt in front of her. "What I did not mention, and only mention now that it matters, is that individual students also end up mentored in certain cases when a teacher feels they would benefit from focused attention beyond the dedicated lessons."

"I believe Seb will benefit greatly from having me as his individual mentor," Percivus said from a few feet away. "Bladesong is complex and has impacts on every aspect of an Awakened's combat style. I can teach you how to use it, however from what Mistress Sarna has said you will benefit more from others focusing on your techniques and style while I help you incorporate it all into a whole."

"What does that mean?" Seb asked.

"More work and less free time."

"You're going to mentor him but not me?" Anya asked.

She saw Percivus hesitate. Their encounter earlier hadn't been her happiest moment of the day.

"I cannot," he said. "I will not prevent you from being present for his lessons if you are free, nor expect him to keep anything from you. As I said earlier I will be their mentor, if I also mentor you when your Path is so different from my own it will imply I am acknowledging your heritage."

She frowned. What did his mentoring her have to do with him saying whether or not she was part of the ruling line, that she was part fae? It didn't change anything about who she was. It changed the questions she had about her father and put a label on why she was different, but that was it.

"Gwen can name you as she wishes. It holds some weight but does not change anything. Were I to name or acknowledge the same it would be truth, and truth is something that does not become untrue simply because I was mistaken."

"Your opinion holds more weight," Seb said.

"My words and actions hold more weight."

Seb?

Think Partner vs Consort. If a Consort says it the Sovereign can disagree-

-but the Partner has full authority.

It made sense. They had all called him Lord, treated him as an authority. If he made a decision it would be respected, even if it wasn't right.

"Why is my Path too different?" she asked.

"You fight bare-handed, your Path is one purely of your self. Mine focuses on my blade and on my presence on the field. We might both face our challenges head on, but I cannot truly teach you how to fight weaponless."

That was… fair. The use of a weapon would change things for them. She doubted she'd be able to hold her own against him even if he was unarmed, but that didn't mean he could teach her. She could be handed a weapon she'd never seen and win against someone lower level.

"I don't focus on using my sword," Seb said.

"Which is part of why I would not be your only teacher. As is your being a skirmisher."

"There will be time later," Sarna said before raising her voice. "Pairs and trios. Sasha, I'm aware your skills do not translate into dueling so the Hammerswells will be a trio today. This will give us six groups and this field can handle four matches at a time with proper barriers to prevent spill-over between them."

Anya watched as the Blehmans and the fae split themselves into groups. Morgan joined Bern and Keldren to form a trio while the Nora paired herself off with her archer, Robin and Luke with the last of their group. She hadn't noticed him do anything of note in the two duels from last class, but apparently he had some use or she suspected he would have been sent to the sideline the way Sasha was.

"The point today is to practice, to work on your coordination," Sarna shouted. "You are to treat this as training, as sparring. Avoid true injuries and reset when outcomes are clear. I will let you know when to change matchups."

Anya glanced at Seb, feeling his thoughts reaching towards hers, for the meld that kept them in sync. She realized they were grinning, both looking forward to the moment, to doing what they had spent so many hours doing, testing their abilities, their limits.

"No Bladesong today," Percivus said. "Even if you're desperate."

"Against anyone?" Seb asked. "Even those who know what it does?"

She saw what she thought was the flicker of a smile on Percivus' face as he looked at the groups of students, his eyes seeming to linger longest on Bern and Keldren.

"You already have against one of the two groups that would definitely recognize it. I suppose it would only be fair for you to use it once against her brother, but only on the second matchup."
