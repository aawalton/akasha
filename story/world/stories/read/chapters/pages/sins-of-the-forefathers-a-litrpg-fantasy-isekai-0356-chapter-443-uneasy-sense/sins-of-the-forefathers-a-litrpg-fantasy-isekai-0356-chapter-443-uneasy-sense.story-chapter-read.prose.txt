“This isn’t natural,” I murmured to myself, barely aware of my companions at my sides. “It can’t be.”

And I was sure it wasn’t. The cavern that apparently dwelt beneath Addersfield wasn’t. It was so huge, so monolithic, that I didn’t feel comfortable calling this a mere cavern.

This was more like a caldera. At least, in size. But the shape of it…

It had to have been created through artificial means. While I wasn’t exactly an expert on geologic processes, I didn’t think it was possible for a cavern of this size to be so precisely spherical.

Half-spherical, to be precise. This place was a perfectly inverted half-sphere that stretched on into the distance, far beyond the limit that even my eyes could see. I could just barely make out the roof, far, far above us, but that was only because there was only so far that the Long Road descended into the firmament of Vereden. And even then!

Even then, the entrance into the caldera sloped downward into a ramp that led to the actual floor of the cavern.

But beyond even the sheer titanic size of this underground vault, what really caught my notice was the stonework.

Smooth. Perfectly, evenly smooth. So much so that it was nearly glasslike. That’s how I would describe how the walls abruptly transitioned from the admirable stonework and stately carvings of the Long Road created by the Dwarves of old. The difference was so stark and so noticeable all at once that I was immediately suspicious.

The Dwarves hadn’t done this. It would have taken either actual machining on a scale that just wasn’t capable with Mystical effects, or heat at an incredibly consistent, incredibly intense strength to create. And I had been inside a volcano before. I had seen what naturally melted rock looked like, deep in the heart of Shurenga’s home.

This wasn’t something caused by a volcano.

I brushed my hand against the glass-like stone wall of the tunnel that led down into the caldera…

And wondered.

I had suspicions about just what might have caused this.

I was startled out of my musings when I heard Bleddyn speak up from behind me. “Odd, ain’t it?” When I turned in place, I found the werewolf wasn’t even looking at me. Instead, he was gazing out into the distant floor of the cavern below, a complicated expression on his face.

I turned and followed his gaze, contemplating what must have been the main base of the Unchained.

It, also, wasn’t what I had been expecting.

I had thought it might have been something similar to the encampment I had awoken from my coma in, in truth. Perhaps it was unfair of me, but I had expected ramshackle collections of tents populated by grim, determined former slaves. I had expected a lack of real facilities, poor or entirely absent discipline, and food lines doling out simple meals to the downtrodden.

The opposite was true.

Sprawling out onto the floor of the cavern below was a true town. Something far larger than I suspected, the Unchained had either the resources or the skilled manpower to create.

Plus, you know…

“You didn’t build that,” I said with certainty.

It was built in the style of a Dwarven town.

“Aye,” Bleddyn admitted easily, nodding. “We didn’t. We found it down here.”

I was extremely thankful that the town below us wasn’t a one-to-one copy of Addersfield. If it had been, I think I might just have had an aneurysm if it had been. Still, it was easy to recognize the architectural style that Addersfield had been constructed from, looming large in my memory. It reminded me deeply of the many, many towns, hamlets, and even small cities that the expedition had either spotted in the distance or even stopped at on our journey across Velancia. It wasn’t built tall like, say, Valle Spinnetta had been, but it was still built wide enough that I thought it was overall bigger than Addersfield had been.

Which was damned odd, now that I’d noticed that. What the Hell was a town larger than the one that had been on the surface doing down here?

Had Anguis had this built? There was no way in Hell Magnus had a damned thing to do with it all.

Especially because of the central feature, smack dab in the middle of the town.

At first, I had wanted to think of it as a kind of enormous pillar, stretching up all the way to the distant roof I could only barely make out far above. It was huge, massively so, easily as wide as the very stretch of the Long Road that led down into the caldera. Only…that wasn’t right, was it?

As I watched, a gated section of the pillar opened up at the base, exposing a massive platform, upon which an unspeakably tiny squad of humanoid-shaped figures stepped out and into the town proper. With a rumble, the gate closed behind them, and through the iron bars, I could see the platform they had just stepped off of begin to ascend.

That entire pillar was nothing more than a giant cargo elevator, wasn’t it?

The whole thing was hollow.

“Do ye remember, when I told ye about the stockpile that was underneath Addersfield?” Bleddyn continued, audibly enjoying the amazement on my face. “Back before ye originally left, we hadn’t set eyes on it yet. We only had your word that it existed, and it lit a fire under our asses. Well, ye were right. It exists, and it was down here. Nearly every single damn building ye see down there was filled to the godsdamned brim with everything that the slaves of Adderfield harvested. And let me tell ye, they harvested a lot.”

I barely managed to drag my eyes away from the spectacle below, as the innumerable carts and wagons of the refugee train began to settle in around the town. “Was filled to the brim? How much of it have you run through?

Bleddyn shrugged, held up a hand, and waggled it back and forth. “Maybe about a third of it, all told. Most of that, we ate. But a good amount of what’s down there is cash crops. Stuff that we could sell, and sell it we did. Both to fund our efforts in freein’ the slaves of Velancia, and…,” He sighed. “To repay Rhoscara and Prince Elysael for all they were doin’ for us. Guess we…don’t gotta do that anymore.”

I hid a grimace at that and changed the subject. I pointed out into the distance at the gigantic cargo elevator. “I’m guessing that’s how Magnus’s underlings were transporting everything they harvested down here. But…why is it still in use? Unless something has massively changed, the surface should still be outright poison to everybody. What’s the point?”

My response was a baffled stare from Bleddyn. “How the hells should I know? Nate, I haven’t been back here in months. Last time was when I was settin’ out with a different batch of refugees, about to head down to see you.”

I flushed slightly in embarrassment. Ah. That’s…right.

“Seems like all I do these days is ferry around refugees,” I heard Bleddyn mutter under his breath tiredly. He shook his head to clear it and raised his voice. “But if I had to guess? Last I checked, we had a small outpost up there, hidden in the burned-out ruin of the buildin’ that hid the entrance to down here. We usually had a few people in it to keep watch over the surrounding forest.  Had a Ward Stone and everythin’, if only to keep the monsters away from them. Before the Skyfall, the entire area was a hotbed of monster activity. We thought it was a holdover from the Break we pulled off. Hate to think what a pain in the ass it has to be now.”

I hummed as I felt a familiar presence approach us from behind. Sylvia and Renauld had remained with the wagon that held my true body, while Bleddyn and I had stepped aside to have a chat. There wasn’t much better to do right now, considering just how clogged the line was to get down into the caldera. Either one of the two who had remained behind must have let him out of the back.

Sure enough, a few moments later, I turned my head to watch as Fade approached Bleddyn and me. I was only barely able to restrain my sense of amusement at just how awkward the Spirit Wolf looked.

Bleddyn didn’t even bother. He just started outright laughing at my Familiar, and I could understand why. During the trip, something Fade had predicted had come true.

His antlers had fallen out. Or rather, the remaining antler and the stub of the old one.

Normally, that wouldn’t have been much worth remarking on. It was expected that any animal that had antlers for them to fall out on occasion. But from what I had learned, this was Fade’s first shedding. He was finally old enough to lose his first set of antlers, and on the ride here, he had been a bit anxious about it. When he had finally shed them, the Spirit Wolf had whined to me about how the shed site stung from being exposed to air, and wanted me to do something about it. A Healing potion would have been a bad idea, considering that it might just close up the point where his new antlers would grown in.

So…

I had wrapped most of his head in bandages instead to cover the growth sites, layering more over his already existing ones on his eye. The result was a bit comical, with the wolf having most of his head completely wrapped, with only his ears and a single eye poking through the white of the linen.

“What the hells happened to you?” Bleddyn chuckled at the bandaged wolf.

In response, Fade gave the man a foul look with his single visible eye. That only caused Bleddyn to laugh harder. “Piss off, half-breed.”

“He said, ‘piss off, half-breed,” I said helpfully.

That just made Bleddyn laugh harder, the insult rolling off his back easily.

Eventually, Bleddyn calmed down and just rolled his eyes at the glowering wolf. “Anyway, enough of this. We should get goin’. Looks like the guards are processin’ your lot. I should go check in with my people down there. We’ll meet up again later, yeah?”

I exchanged goodbyes with the man, and then he jogged away, immediately disappearing into the press seeking to descend into the caldera. Turning away, I found that Bleddyn had been right, and the wagon that had carried us this far was near the front of the line. I could see Renauld waving to try to catch my attention from up on the bench. I started walking back that way, only to pause when I realized that Fade wasn’t following behind me.

I turned back and saw that the Spirit Wolf was standing near the edge of the cliff that bearded the caldera, not far from where Bleddyn and I were speaking. He was staring out over the caldera with an unfocused stare in his visible eye, as if he were trying to see something just out of view. My Familiar only snapped back to reality when I kneeled next to him.

“What’s up?” I asked him quietly. “Is something wrong?”

Fade tilted his head back and forth for a moment, visibly unsure. “I…don’t know?” His uncertainty was thick in his mental voice. “This place feels weird to me, Nate. Like…there’s a scent and a feeling to it that set my fur on end.”

I was instantly put on guard. Fade’s senses were far sharper than my own, owing to his half Spirit nature. If he said something was wrong, I believed him.

“Is it a trap?” I urged in a quiet voice. “Are we walking into danger?”

To my relief, Fade shook his head. “Not that kind of weird. It’s more like…there’s an impression in the Aether down there. Something really, really, really old. It’s as if…” He took a deep, deep breath through his nose, then, as if he were physically scenting the Aether. For all I knew, he was. “There was a lot of death here, a very long time ago. Either a bunch of people died all at once down there, or something really strong did. But it’s weird. There’s no impression of rot, or resentment, or anything else to the Aether. It’s more like a sensation of pure death.”

Death…

As in Death Aether?

Something I knew was that Aether could be naturally influenced by its environment, taking on either aspects or elemental affiliations from its surroundings. Death Aether was something that was totally possible, and typically something that only popped up in minuscule proportions in grave or lichyards. It was only in places like ancient mausoleums, like the one I had delved into outside of Elderwyck, that enough of it had accumulated to be sensed in the way Fade was talking about.

You know. The place that had been infested with wild undead.

That this caldera was apparently rife with Death Aether…

Not a great sign.

“We’ll…keep an eye on it,” I eventually sighed, standing back up. “Nothing else we do right now.”

Fade nodded quietly and followed after me as I returned to the wagon holding Sylvia and Renauld. Bitterly, I couldn’t help but come to a realization as I did.

I was getting damn tired of feeling helpless in the face of all these unknown dangers.

Damn tired.
