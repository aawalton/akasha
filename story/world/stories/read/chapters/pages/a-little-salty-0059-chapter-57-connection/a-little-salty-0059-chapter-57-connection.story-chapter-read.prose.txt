In the end, I judge Piria good for introducing me to cider, and very little else.

What does a champion do?

Whatever their god asks of them.

What does Shirasil ask for?

Not much.

Why are Widengra’s champions after me?

Because I killed Maru.

Are they going to try to kill me, too?

At this, Piria grimaces. “They’re not supposed to. Killing champions are taboo. Lorata would be upset if she discovered any god intentionally had a champion killed.”

She hesitates, and I can guess why.

“But I already have killed a champion,” I say. “So the pantheon might be willing to look the other way.”

Piria reluctantly nods. “Also, new champions aren’t seen as, erm, valuable as older champions. Or I guess, I should say low level compared to high level.”

Figures. Only the strong survive in this world. Though how Piria is level 89 with such low stats and a frail form is lost on me.

“But you should be especially careful around Widengra,” Piria says, lowering her voice and eyes darting around our table, as if someone might be listening in. “He’s, um. He’s not doing well these days.”

Cyros raises an eyebrow. “What does that mean?”

“Oh.” Piria fidgets with her fingers. “I’m. I’m not sure if I should say.”

“You brought it up,” I point out. “And whatever you tell me, I’m going to tell Cyros, anyway.”

Cyros gives me a curious look I can’t decipher. But I can make a guess: “I appreciate that, but how come you didn’t tell me any of this stuff before?”

I avoid his gaze.

Piria takes another sip of her drink, then nods to herself. “He’s been more prone to acting out, recently.”

I snort. “Only recently?”

“More likely to disobey the rules of the pantheon,” Piria clarifies. “I suspect the only reason he left you alive before was because your ascension to champion meant another god was probably watching. But if he thinks Lorata won’t find out, it could be dangerous for you.” She fidgets some more. “Which is why it would be a good idea to make your position official within the Heavens.”

I snort. “What, you want me to go do some sort of ascension ceremony and meet-and-greet with the gods?”

She stares at me.

I lean back. “You don’t actually mean that?”

“It would provide you more security,” she says. “It’s been enough time now that Lorata would probably accept you.”

“Probably?”

The dracid shrugs helplessly. “Well, um, I mean, you are a Traveler, so there always remains a chance, but… No, Lorata would listen to Shirasil if he insisted. He is the oldest god, after all.”

“He is?” Cyros interrupts. “I thought Lorata was the oldest.”

Piria starts wiggling uncomfortably again. “It’s… complicated.” She looks back up at me. “Regardless, I’m sure Shirasil would stop Lorata from doing anything to you!”

How reassuring. “Forgive me if I doubt Shirsail would stick his neck out for anyone.”

Piria tips her head. “But he already has. Intervening to save you from Widengra was a big risk.”

I wrinkle my nose as I lift my second glass of cider to take a drink. “Yeah. Sure.”

Piria frowns slightly. “I can’t do anything that will make you believe me. But Lord Shirasil is not the villain you seem to think he is. You don’t know him like I do. Behind everything he does, he only wants to help people.”

I just roll my eyes, taking a long gulp.

Piria lets out a small sigh. “I know he sent me here to help you, and perhaps even to help you see reason, but I don’t think I’m the best suited for this. You should reach out to him directly. You may be surprised.”

“No thanks.”

“Wait, you can do that?” Cyros asks, looking between us, bewildered. “How?”

“I can’t,” Piria clarifies. “But my lord told me that Sal’s nature as a Traveler has provided her certain special abilities. Like a way to instantaneously contact Shirasil.”

She’s talking about a new function that appeared in my System interface after Shirasil made me his champion. It’s called Contacts, and right now, it only has his name in it. It appears like a standard chat interface, but I’ve never attempted to use it, beyond trying—and failing—to add other people to my contact list.

I frown at this thought. “Do you mean you don’t have this ability?” I ask Piria.

She shakes her head. “No. Only the gods have access to the magical network you can use. Well, the gods, and now Travelers, too.”

Huh. That’s news to me. I had just assumed that the champions all had System access like their gods. But I suppose it’s a good thing they don’t; if they had, Maru would have been able to Check me and see from the start that I was the “magical aberration” she had been looking for. I probably wouldn’t have survived that first encounter.

Lucky me.

“Sal, what is it you want?” Piria abruptly asks.

I startle out of my thoughts, the question catching me off guard. “What do you mean?”

“I mean, what motivates you?” she asks. “What do you want from life?”

“I want…” Weirdly, I find myself hesitating. I’d been about to say revenge, of course, but for some reason, the first thing that popped into my head was the Starlight Inn. The smell of fresh bread as I work with Iski in the heat of the kitchen. Creeping quietly through the woods as I help Gugora hunt rabbit for dinner.

I shake my head. “I want to get stronger,” I say. “Strong enough to fight back if anyone like Maru comes for me again. Strong enough to stop Widengra.”

Cyros’s eyes widen at my blatant blasphemy, but Piria doesn’t seem surprised.

“Then you need to use your god’s gift,” she says. “You can’t claim to want strength more than anything, but then snub power given to you because it came from the wrong source. And that Gauntlet isn’t the only strength you can draw on. You’re connected to Shirasil, so you’re connected to his magic. You can use his mana as your own, if you open yourself to the connection you now share.”

My stomach churns sickly in my gut. I know what she’s talking about, even if I’ve been trying to ignore it. A niggling sensation in the back of my mind, the knowledge that there’s something else—someone more—just on the periphery of my awareness. A source of magic I could tap into, if only I was willing to embrace it.

But embracing it would mean embracing Shirasil. And I’m not ready to do that just yet.

“Well,” Piria finally ventures when I say nothing, “I suppose that’s on you to figure out. But… try to keep an open mind? I understand why you feel betrayed by Lisari’s actions. Why you feel tricked and resentful.” She glances toward Cyros briefly. “But perhaps there is some room for nuance. There were reasons she initially kept things from you. Maybe ask her about it next time you talk.”

I press my mouth into a thin line, and I can feel Cyros’s gaze burrowing into the side of my head. “Sure. Whatever.”

Piria sighs, offering a sad smile. “Well, Sal, it’s been good to finally meet you. And I hope we can get to know each other better, moving forward.” She reaches over to set something down on the table in front of me. It’s a dracid scale the size of my thumb nail, the same color as Piria’s. “If you carry this on you, I’ll be able to find you, no matter where you are. Shirasil won’t; this just works for me. And if you break it in half, I’ll come to you. I’d love to talk again, when you feel like it.”

I stare at the scale for a long moment, debating whether or not I should take it. I don’t like the idea of being tracked. But it’s Shirasil I’m upset with, not Piria. And against all odds, I think she really means what she says.

I grab the scale and pocket it. I wonder if she’ll be able to track it while it’s in my Inventory.

“Thanks,” I say shortly.

Cyros gives me a disappointed look.

“And thanks for the drinks,” I add.

Cyros nods approvingly.

Piria brightens. “My pleasure! Well, I suppose I should let you all get back to work. That market appeared absolutely chaotic. I can’t imagine how you buy or sell anything there. But I suppose Lord Shirasil would approve of that.” She chuckles.

She does know Cyros and I work for an assassin guild, right?

Piria stands up, and Cyros follows suit while I quickly down the rest of my drink. Hey, I’m not about to let it go to waste.

“It was a pleasure to meet you,” Cyros says. “It isn’t every day you meet a legend.”

Piria’s cheeks darken. Is she actually blushing? “Oh, you’re too kind. It was nice to meet one of Sal’s friends as well!”

I can only open and shut my mouth at this exchange.

Piria flags down the bartender, pays for all of our drinks, then leaves the shop with a congenial wave. Cyros and I stare after her.

“Were you flirting?” I ask him, incredulous.

He grins mischievously. “Why, are you jealous?”

“Absolutely not.” I head toward the front of the shop as well.

“Wait, really?” Cyros catches up. “Not even a little?”

Ugh. Boys. “Not even a little,” I confirm.

Cyros doesn’t attempt to hide his pout.

“Now, come on,” I say, turning toward the Guild. “Let’s get these jobs turned in and make some money.”

Cyros brightens. “At least I got what I needed before the Market crashed this time.”

I raise an eyebrow. “This time?”

“It always ends with some sort of blow up,” Cyros says. “City Guard, gangs, whatever. Crashed by the Champions of War is probably a new one.”

“Great,” I say flatly. Unfortunately, I still had one more job I’d wanted to get done. I’ll probably have to add that one back to the job wall, without knowing when the next Night Market will be.

But his words remind me of the last job I’d completed, retrieving that strange relic hidden in the gourd bottle. I frown at the memory. I felt something weird when I touched it. Probably I should just hand it over to the Guild and finish my part of the job, but I want to find out what’s in there. I feel… drawn to it, somehow. A quick look before I complete the job won’t hurt anything, right?

Cyros is quiet while we walk. I can tell he wants to ask me something, but I think he’s waiting for me to talk first. I don’t like talking.

But I probably owe him this, at least.

I mumble under my breath.

Cyros tips his head. “What was that?”

“I said I’m sorry,” I grumble, glaring at the cracks in the road as we walk. “I should have told you.”

“Yeah,” he agrees. “You should have.”

I shrug. “Thought it wouldn’t matter if I ignored it.”

“Why would you want to?” Cyros asks. “I mean, do you even know what an honor this is?”

I scoff. “Easy for you to say.”

“Piria was right, you know,” Cyros says, frowning at me. “You can’t claim to want to get stronger but then shun power when it’s given to you.”

“Because it’s not mine,” I say. “I didn’t choose it. I didn’t earn it.”

Cyros snorts. “Power isn’t something you earn, no more than luck is. Sometimes you’re born with it; sometimes you fall into it. Sometimes you’re given it, or you cultivate it for yourself. But power isn’t something people have because they deserve it, it’s something they just have. And now that you have some, you have to decide what to do with it.”

“That doesn’t sound fair,” I say.

He raises an eyebrow. “Is luck fair?”

The lingering taste of cider sours on my tongue. “No.” I can’t believe Cyros is lecturing me on the unfairness of life.

“Fine, fine,” I say, waving him off. “I get your point. I’ll think about it.”

“Good,” he says. “And you know, you don’t have to figure this power out on your own, right? I can help. Like I did with the Tournament.”

I narrow my eyes at him. “Why? What’s your angle on all this?”

Cyros looks wounded. “I want to help because I’m your friend.”

I stare at him.

He cracks a smile. “And because if you got yourself killed, it would look really bad on my record.”

I snicker. “Wouldn’t want to make you look bad.”

“You better not!” He shakes a finger at me. “I’ve got a reputation to uphold.”

“A reputation for impulsively inviting new members into the guild?” I tease.

“Hey, that one’s your fault,” he replies. “You basically cornered me.”

I grin. “Yeah I really had to twist your arm. Man, you must have been really lonely.”

“Well my life has certainly been more exciting since you’ve been around,” he says, refusing to take the bait. “And by exciting, I mean exhausting."

I shove his shoulder, and he laughs, trying to turn the move into an arm-bar. I slip out of his hold and try to trip him, but he dodges around my leg without even trying.

Laughing, we fake spar our way down the street, and for a little sliver of time, champions, gods, and blessings are the furthest thing from my mind.
