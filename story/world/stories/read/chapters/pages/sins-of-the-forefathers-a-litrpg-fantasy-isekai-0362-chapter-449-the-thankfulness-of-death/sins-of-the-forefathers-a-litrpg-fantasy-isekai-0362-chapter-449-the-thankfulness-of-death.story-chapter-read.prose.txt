I’m not quite sure what I was expecting…

But it hadn’t been this.

Under the light of both my light Skill and that of my staff, the form that Tlazo was currently inhabiting was revealed to me as he rose up from the altar.

In the form of what looked like a zombie.

When I had known Tlazo, he had been a true Lich. A floating skeleton in an impeccably styled, clean, black and red robe. Four arms instead of two, with a bear's skull stripped of flesh and an impressive rack of stag antlers atop his head, the Lich had cut a figure so frightening on our first meeting, I and everyone else had nearly attacked him at first sight.

Well, that and because he’d had his skeletal arms elbow deep in the chest cavity of my old commander Hook. Not for any nefarious purposes, of course.

No, because he had been a Surgeon in life, and had been saving the Dwarf’s life out of the goodness of his non-existent heart.

Tlazo had been an odd specimen, that’s for sure. But impressive in his own right.

This form was decidedly…not.

As I had said, it was a zombie, and it was completely naked, bereft of all clothing. I think the only reason I wasn’t seeing anything that would be genuinely upsetting, considering the state of the corpse, was that Tlazo had only sat up on the altar, leaving the shift that had been covering him draped across his hip. As it was, the light of my Skill allowed me to see that these remains were truly dessicated…and yet, oddly intact, unlike those found on the first floor. The remaining skin on the animated corpse was completely leatherized, with every ounce of vital essence, both fluid and energetic, having been leeched from its skin. It was so thin that I had absolutely no problem counting every last bone in its body. Not an ounce of muscle or organ tissue still remained in its body, nor was there any soft tissue. No eyes, no tongue, and definitely no hair.

A curious thing, though, about whatever preservation technique Tlazo had employed. I could still tell that, once upon a time, whoever this once?

Their skin tone had been decidedly darker than my own. Very much so.

I…you know what? Now that I had a better look at this body, I had a better name for it than just zombie.

I’d say it was more of an unwrapped mummy, really. The preservation was very deliberate and expertly done at that, with a level of care not common with your garden variety of undead.

Instead of eyes, I was regarded with familiar twin azure flames, but that wasn’t what had caught my attention so thoroughly.

No, it was instead the enormous amber crystal embedded in the chest of the corpse. The ribcage had been carefully splayed open, and I could quite easily see that all of the internal organs had been removed.

In their place was the crystal, and it took up all of the space that the heart and lungs would have. Perfectly smooth and polished, it slotted in nicely and snugly, nestled upon the bottom ribs and held in place by the sternum.

I had a suspicion about just what that was, and it was fascinating.

“Is that…” I breathed, pointing at the crystal with my free hand, not holding the staff. “Your…?”

Tlazo looked down with a nonchalant air. “Oh, this?” He shifted the leathery lips of the corpse into a smirk. “Yes, it is indeed one of my phylacteries. Well done, you. If I was still a teacher, I’d give you full marks for observational skills.”

That snapped me out of my wonder, and I rolled my eyes. “Yes, Professor, thank you, Professor,” I shook my head, suppressing a laugh at the sheer absurdity of the situation. Here I was, trading banter with a Lich.

How times change.

“You know, they still haven’t filled your position at the Academy,” I said off-handedly, as Tlazo fully rose from the altar with remarkable smoothness. I think I had expected far more creakiness out of the living dead. Maybe some stiff joints. But no, if anything, the preserved corpse moved with startling smoothness and control. Thankfully, he was using that control to deftly tie the shift into sort of an improvised almost toga, with one strap over a nut-brown shoulder, cinched at the stick-thin waist.

Finished with his impromptu clothing, the Lich looked back up at me with a decided air of irritation. “Is that so? How sloppy,” He tsked, shaking his head. “I’ll bet the entire department has collapsed in my absence. How like Greycton to let a good thing fall into disrepair.”

Well. There was some old bitterness.

I hummed non-committedly, unwilling to butt into that matter. Whatever had happened between him and Grey that might have led to him faking his death, it wasn’t my business.

Silence fell between Tlazo and me for a moment, as we examined each other. I, the strangely well-preserved corpse, and he…

I noticed Tlazo’s gaze lingering on my staff, and my grip tightened almost involuntarily.

He noticed. “Well, haven’t you been a busy little blade since last we met, Nathaniel Hart?” Tlazo said, chuckling lightly. Even that sounded remarkably natural, considering his lack of lungs. “You must have triumphed over the Calamity since the last we met, considering you still live. Well done, you. Not only that, but you must have spent some time at the old alma mater. Tell me, how long has it been since I was scattered by that brute?”

My spirits…sank. There was a lot to parse from that sentence. “You’ve been asleep all this time?” I asked quietly.

Somehow, the twin flames of Tlazo’s eyes sharpened in sudden attention, catching the resignation in my voice. “Answer my question.”

I sighed, unbowed by the force of his demand. Once, I would have immediately started to bow and scrape before the unnaturally powerful existence, trying to escape his notice. I was past that, now.

I’d experienced and suffered too much to care.

“It’s been a little over a year since then,” I said with a shake of my head. “There have been some ups…and some downs. We’re currently the down part of that.”

Tlazo…sighed heavily, dragging one leathery hand down his face in exasperation. “Of course,” He groaned. “It’s always something with the living. Don’t tell me yet, I want to air out this drafty cavern. As welcome as the Death Aether is, a little variety wouldn’t hurt, wouldn’t you say? One moment, it won’t take me a moment to open the skylight.”

…skylight?

I snapped my head up to the ceiling above us, and for the first time, I noticed something.

From the doorway, there hadn’t been enough light to see it. But, since I was in the center of the room now, there absolutely was a large, circular stone impression up there.

Like it could move out of place.

In a sudden, wild panic, I lunged forward at Tlazo as he raised one hand. I don’t know exactly what he was about to do. Presumably, the old Lich had some manner of control over this compound. I wouldn’t put it past him.

If he was about to open it up to the open air…

I couldn’t let that happen. If that skylight was a direct access tunnel to the outside, the both of us were doomed. I didn’t have an APD on me. I had no method of surviving the deadly corrosion of the Skyfall without both an APD and an active ward scheme to hook onto. More importantly, though?

It would kill Sylvia and Fade, too.

The husk of a zombie that Tlazo was inhabiting didn’t flinch, or even tense up, as I grabbed its hand in mid-motion, before it could point directly at the apparent skylight. But the Lich did stop in place before he could doom us all, even as his flaming blue eyes seemed to narrow at me.

“Stop,” I breathed, a wild panic beating in my chest. I didn’t really have a heart in this clone body, but my internal Aether and Mana could still be influenced by my emotions.

And right now, they were surging from the strong feelings coursing through me.

“Or you’ll kill us both.”

The desiccated, leather skin of the mummified corpse’s hairless brow drew together, as Tlazo eyed me with suspicion. “I was merely going to let some fresh air in…how could that have possibly…” He studied me for a moment. I don’t know what the Necromancer saw in my face, but it was apparently enough for him to frown deeply. “What has happened, Nathaniel Hart?”

Seeing that the Lich seemed to have taken my warning to heart, I backed off, releasing the dry, papery wrist of the preserved corpse as I did. A shuddering sigh escaped my lips. “A…lot, Tlazo. Let’s just say that a lot has happened. Is there someplace I can fill you in? Or do you want to do it in…” I looked around at what I could really only call a ritual chamber, and then shook my head. “Here?”

“No…” Tlazo said quietly, flickering his eyes about. “Come with me. This hallowed place is not…suitable.”

With remarkable smoothness, considering his chosen mode of transportation, Tlazo pivoted the mummified corpse on one heel and strode out of the chamber, his makeshift toga snapping from the movement.

I followed behind him quietly, reaching the door in moments as I followed the Lich. But before I stepped through, I spared the empty room one last glance.

Hallowed, eh. What was so hallowed about it?

I had a feeling I wouldn’t like the answer.

I typically didn’t, when it came to the Netherim.

……………………………………………..

“…I see,” Tlazo said in a soft, almost resigned tone of voice, closing his eyes as he did. “That’s…unfortunate.”

“Unfortunate?” I almost snapped, sharper than I intended. “That’s all you have to say? Unfortunate?”

Tlazo had led the two of us back down the hall from the apparently ‘hallowed’ chamber and into the room that I had pegged as a communal dining room of sorts. There, we had sat at one of the long abandoned, dusty stone tables, with its long benches. They uncomfortably reminded me of the picnic tables I had sat with my parents in my youth. Only, instead of being situated in the middle of a bright and verdant public park, these were in the middle of some long-forgotten Netherim tomb.

There, sitting on those benches, I had filled Tlazo in on the things that I considered important. I glossed over the defeat of Rhazal and said little to nothing about my exploits on Gorenzan, the Netherim bunker therein, or the death of Tatsugan.

That was my business, and for as much as Tlazo had aided me in the past…

We weren’t friends.

But maybe we could become allies.

Instead, I spoke of how, not more than a few months ago, the entire planet of Vereden had come under siege from the phenomenon of the Skyfall. How Blutstein had rallied in the face of danger and launched an exploratory expedition. How we had discovered that its origin was that of the Mad God. How we had journeyed across the length and breadth of the continent to reach Rhoscara, in search of allies there.

And of how Anguis Savoy was puppeting the Mad God, all the while he drained the ancient Elf of his Divinity. All so he could ascend himself.

Only for Tlazo to call the situation ‘unfortunate’.

I bared my teeth at the almost contemplative-looking Lich. “Surely you have more to say than that. Unfortunate is something you say about accidentally spilling your tea. Not to mention how the entire planet is being poisoned. Do you not care? Not even the fact that your wife is somewhere out there, right now, in the midst of it…Rafael?”

That certainly caused Tlazo’s eyes to open, if only in irritation. The look he shot me then was distinctly caustic. “I see you’ve been speaking to people about things you shouldn’t,” He said crossly.

I snorted, still irritated myself. “Everyone I possibly could,” I admitted shamelessly. “Grey called you an asshole, by the way. And Honoka, your wife, had an idea about just where you had stashed your phylactery. I’m guessing she knows about this place, and might just pop in one day.” I lost some of my irritation then, growing more somber. “If…she’s still alive, that is.”

A small frown appeared on Tlazo’s lips, then, as he gazed over my head and shook his own. “The woman still yet lives. I…would know if she had died.” At the startled, hopeful, and confused look that appeared on my face, Tlazo sighed irritably and waved me off. “It’s none of your business, Hart. Just…insurance I instituted in her regard, many, many years ago. But, yes. She…does know of this place. Greycton does not, but she does. We discovered this ancient site together, as a family…once upon a time…”

I blinked at the wistful, almost nostalgic tone of voice from Tlazo, before nodding slowly. “Right…you, Honoka, and your daugh-”

I didn’t get a chance to finish my sentence. Tlazo’s eyes snapped over to me, and the burning blue flame within them flared bright enough to light up the entire hall briefly. “Silence,” He snarled at me. “Speak not another word, else I pop this contemptible little construct you parade about in.”

I reared back, not…quite as intimidated as Tlazo likely expected me to be. But still, I held my almost instinctual rebuttal, guessing now wasn’t the time to antagonize the Lich.

Interesting that he had noticed I was a clone, though. Guess I shouldn’t be surprised.

Just as quickly as it had appeared, Tlazo’s fit of pique passed. His undead shoulders sagged in place, and he looked away from me for a moment. Eventually, he mastered himself and met my eyes as if nothing had ever happened. “Why are you here, Hart? Were you seeking this place specifically? I wouldn’t be surprised if you were, considering its history. Especially considering the connections to your current plight. I’d only question where you found knowledge of so venerable a place. I thought I’d scrubbed it completely from the Academy Archives.”

I blinked, confused. “Ah…I just kind of…stumbled across it following my Familiar’s nose. Wait.” I paused. “What do you mean, connections? Do you mean…”

I mean, I know they were Netherim, but that didn’t mean everything that had to do with the Netherim had to do with them.

“To the gods, yes,” Tlazo said matter-of-factly, raising one bare brow my way. “You mean to tell me you found this place by sheer chance? That’s…deeply surprising.”

“Considering this is where the gods were born.”
