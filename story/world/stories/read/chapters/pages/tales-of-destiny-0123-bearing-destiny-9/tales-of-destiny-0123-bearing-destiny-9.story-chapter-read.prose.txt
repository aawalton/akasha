The silence that followed was resounding. Meng De’s face, already pale, went white, and then red. Even the sounds of tussling and games had stopped, leaving the air hanging dead and still. Somewhere in the crowd of the now silent party someone muttered something, too far, too low to be clear, but it sounded a little like ‘traitors’, and a little ripple of laughter rang out. Meng De’s face became almost purple, embarrassment and rage and something else simmering in his eyes.

“Sir Meng, I do not…” Rixiong began, hoping to ‘de-escalate’ as Ran Fen would say. He did not have a good impression, but…

“I will go, I need education in the ways of my own ancestors, it seems,” Meng De hissed, turning away.

“Yeah, you do! Stiff ass tree sitter,” Yongqi called after his retreating back.

“Yongqi, please,” Rixiong said.

“Pfah, fine, I’ll leave it there,” Yongqi grumbled.

“That was unnecessary, but this Alingge of the Daigiya appreciates your words.” Alingge clasped her hands, bowing her head. The hart at her side did the same, lowering her head until her muzzle nearly touched the grass.

“My words ain’t much,” Yongqi grumbled. “May the Daigiya be able to wander the hills and sing under clear skies free of worry again one day.”

Alingge closed her eyes for a moment and then straightened up. “Yes. But Junior Brother, I will take up no more of your time. I can see that you are eager for the games.”

Yongqi scoffed. “He’s an energetic kid! I don’t disapprove of that, or these games. What kind of party doesn't have a wrestling ring at the center!”

Rixiong laughed, scratching the back of his neck. He glanced the way Meng De had gone, but could no longer see or sense the other man. The shock of Yongqi’s words was wearing off and though there were many whispers and looks his way, the games were starting up again.

“Well, if Lady Alingge doesn’t mind, I will enjoy them. Will you be coming down to the ring as well?”

“Not just yet, though perhaps later. I may enjoy pinning my Junior Brother later,” Alingge said in amusement.

Yongqi hooted with laughter, and Rixiong joined him obligingly. That would be a fun match. Alingge was very thin, but her superior cultivation would surely make it an excellent challenge to learn from!

But the days of games and training would not last forever.

***

The sky rumbled and shook.

It was exhilarating, Rixiong thought. This was not merely games upon a training field, it was not illusory training or spars where there was always a small amount of holding back, no matter how good the Sect’s medicine pavilion was. This was war, and the Sun's power in his blood thrummed with resonance for it.

His feet pounded across the rough gravel of the road as he ran along it, wind singing in his ears. Zhengyi’s feet clanged like temple bells as she ran beside him. Ran Fen and another Inner disciple flanked him, following in his wake, as did the second realm soldiers he had been seconded.

He vibrated with energy; his every instinct demanded that he bound ahead, toward the sounds of battle rippling out over the hills.

“Keep it steady boy. A proper warrior doesn’t leave his band behind.”

Yongqi didn’t speak aloud now, the spear's gravelly voice echoing in his head. Rixiong took a deep breath and calmed himself.

“Contacts over the next hill,” Ran Fen said, her arts carrying her voice through the whistling wind. “We are ordered to support the medical pavilion at the site of the village center. Main force occupied pursuing the original attack force.”

Though it was said that the war was ‘on pause’ in the wider province, in truth there were small skirmishes almost weekly. Events where the Sect’s forces had to rally to drive out probing attempts and sabotage… or so he had heard. This was the first time Rixiong had been deployed against real foes by the Sect.

“We move swiftly then, our comrades need support!” Rixiong replied. “Increase pace!”

The soldiers echoed his order, and their small unit raced up to crest the hilltop, and below they got the first glimpse of the enemy.

Rixiong had heard older disciples describe the ith, but they did not give the creatures justice.

Below were fields of barley, stretched across the valley nestled in the hills, much was trampled, burning, or ruined, and the source of the toxic black ichor which steamed on the hard-packed soil was obvious. They… undulated across the ground, seething masses of half liquid flesh, studded with mismatched eyes and hideous mouths, their slick, rotten black surfaces glistening unpleasantly with ripples of dull color. Below Rixiong saw a dripping sucker covered tendril curl around the body of a fleeing cow, and the animal screamed horribly at the touch, hair burning, hide melting at the touch, before it was stuffed whole into a gaping maw full of human-like teeth with a sickening crunch.

Rixiong whirled his spear above his head, setting the weapon for his charge. With the passing of months, it had gotten easier, both from training… and growth. Rixiong had found himself shooting up like a sprout since he had begun cultivating in earnest at the Sect. His bellowing warcry drew the squirming things eyes to him, and they shrieked as the men following him peppered them with bolts trailing boiling contrails of scalding steam.

Eyes burst and flesh ran like wax under the heat… but for all of that the slimy masses seemed no less vigorous. But it did distract them. Rixiong launched himself from the earth with a crack, sunlight raw and hot gathering in Yongqi’s blade. Force would not do such beasts as these in. But Yongqi had taught him the arts of purification.

The first of the artificial monsters shrieked from a dozen mouths as his spear plunged home, the shockwave of the impact blasting a crater in jellylike flesh and scattering hissing gobbets for meters around. The radiating sunlight boiled away toxic qi and meat wherever it even came close to touching. Writhing tendrils reached for him, slapped and grasped at his limbs, but he batted them away, sensing the revolting impurity of their qi.

Even with his arts hardening his body and spirit wherever it touched him, he felt burning welts rising on his skin. The wind howled with Ran Fen’s arts and the steady mechanical fire of crossbows and steam, and swiftly the vile things were torn apart, leaving only toxic pools where they had been. Glorious! Foes he could truly test himself against!

He nodded to Zhengyi, who had reached his side as the foes were finished, and once more called for the advance. His eyes caught on a shape in the trampled barley fields as he went though. One shape and then another. A fallen Sect disciple, a first realm, half eaten by acid, the body of a mortal man clutching his sickle, sprawled in the dirt.

…There were more still if he looked further on, though the things seemed to devour bodies. He felt a stirring of wrath in his heart, sparks and embers glowing.

“Focus it. The zenith focuses their rage to a white hot point, to pierce and crush the wicked.”

Rixiong pressed his lips into a thin line and grunted agreement, the others forming up behind him as they resumed their advance.

Their formation blurred through the fields, toward the smoke and ash that curled lazily into the sky. They cut down more constructs, more twisted and mutated beasts, breaking through the desolation of the fields and finding their way around the jutting spur of mountain rock that cut off sight of the village center from the fields nestled into the highland valley.

The air stank.

It burned in his nose, a mixture of rot and sickness, of burning wood and flesh. It felt oily, coating his nose, his throat, his lungs in something unpleasant and dirty. The center of the village had sunk into the earth. Rixiong had seen a sinkhole once, a place where the earth gave in, dragging boulders and trees down and leaving them jutting at crazed angles. Mortal homes were much less sturdy than ancient trees and the bones of the earth. Shattered structures littered the steep muddy slope.

There was so much blood, staining the shattered timbers and tiles. Rixiong felt a stirring of memory, his oldest memory. Fire, cruel laughter, screams, the sound of tearing flesh. He thought Ran Fen had said something, but it was like something was whistling in his ears. He spotted the flag of the Sect, a silver pennant over a small formation on the far side of the sinkhole, with the markers of the medical pavilion. There were many mortals behind their line, cowering, praying, trying to retreat as the cultivators stood between them and the chitin armored beasts and pouncing ith who leapt upon the line.

They teemed in the ruins of the village, dragging bodies from the wreckage, hurriedly throwing everything they could reach, man animal or ith into a cauldron at the bottom of the sinkhole, from which fresh horrors of rotting flesh boiled and poured.

Their coming had not been silent, a line of loping ith soldiers and their crawling warbeasts was coming around to face them, blocking off what remained of the road.

But Rixiong’s eyes locked onto the village behind them, to the cauldron and the corpse draggers. So much had collapsed and broken, and there were so many bodies, men and women and... he saw, sticking up from the crumpled, muddy earth and debris, a very small hand jutting out, stiff as if reaching for the sky that its buried owner could not see.

He saw one of the underearth monsters grasp that hand and yank the rest of the ____ out of the earth to haul to the flesh cauldron.

The whistling was very loud. Yongqi shouted something, but he could not hear it.

***

“Rixiong, Rixiong! Slow down a moment, we are leaving…”

Ran Fen felt a bit sickly. She had in her life seen the aftermath of a murderous spirit beast attack in her family's lands. No road was ever perfectly safe even in the Peaks. But the scale of this… taking part in an actual battle rather than a hunt was a thing that shook her. The pace of pushing through enemy after enemy, her dantian was beginning to ache a little, but her friend had continued to push on without any pause. They were beginning to get badly out of formation and it was like he was not listening at all…!

She startled, when he roared.

She had heard him bellow and roar before of course, but those were far different. There was always something joyful, playful down in the depths of that fearful and beastly sound. She threw up her hands, wind pushing back against the waves of boneshaking sound assaulting her. Shu Ri, the other disciple in their unit skidded to a stop beside her.

The roar was too low, too ragged, like a dying wounded beast as it lashed out against the soldiers whose spears were taking its life. Dust and grit exploded outward as her friend disappeared, cratering the earth. She heard Yongqi cursing, berating aloud now, but the sound vanished under the shockwave of impact as Rixiong plowed into the enemy formation without care or hesitation. Weapons and techniques bit into him, scored his skin, tore his cloak, cracked his armor… and Yongqi and his fists pulped ith raiders in turn.

Zhengyi landed beside her looking grim.

“Lady Ran, I think you will need to take command.”

***

“I must attend to my Master, who cannot fulfill his duties at the moment. Please excuse me.”

Zhengyi was formed of the Sun's fire and the blood of the earth. Metal was the product of this union. She was a pureborn daughter of the Zenith, contained in a shell of sturdy reason, kept from raging out of control.

The Zenith was the lord of battles, and thus, the lord of violence and rage. For He had known wrath since the moment of His birth. To cultivate His fire was not a task which the weak minded could accomplish without losing themselves.

Nor even the strong minded, at all times.

She liked Lady Ran, and Lord Rixiong was a good champion.

She only hoped he would emerge from this first brush with the core of the Sun better forged.
