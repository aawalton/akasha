Chapter 132: Just You, Me, and Us

There was a sigh on the other side of the communicator, and the woman said, “Unfortunately, I did know him. I don’t think I can help you. I don’t know where he is.”

Jack nodded and replied, “No surprise. If you’re alright with it, I’d still like to chat with you about him. I haven’t been able to reach him in ages.”

“Neither can anyone else.” There was a pause, perhaps as she was deliberating about his request, and the silhouette flickered. Finally came the reluctant reply, “Alright. I’m curious enough. Come up.”

The shadow silhouette disappeared to be replaced by the prior hologram, now looking at him rather coolly. “You will now be escorted to Ms. Limer's door.” He nodded to one of the toughs who’d stepped forward and momentarily made significant eye contact with Jack.

“Thanks.” Jack smiled apologetically to the deceived hologram AI even as he was headed toward his ‘escort.’

Knowing better than to make small talk with a walking anvil, Jack quietly let the large fellow lead him to an elevator and then to the condo door of Frances — or her boyfriend's, rather. The door opened automatically, and he went through, the ‘escort’ somehow making it clear from his shifted stance that he was going to stand right there while Jack was inside.

I wonder how much of a problem he’d actually be? There’s the problem of a likely close combat skill gap, even though I have such a speed advantage. I still think I could take him without metal, though. I should train for shit like this…

He quickly banished such thoughts, though, as he proceeded inside, the AI-controlled door shutting automatically behind him.

It was a beautiful place, deliberately made for a pleasant aesthetic as soon as one walked in. White marble panels were separated by loose stones as gaps, which expanded to the side to showcase colorful plants in frames and stylish platforms, with even a little bubbling fountain making a relaxing sound. Down a wide hallway, on one side was a wall made of polished wooden bricks that contrasted with the white. Some sort of intricate folding door was open beyond this, painted in a scene he couldn’t make out while it was folded.

The slim denizen of the place, waiting and watching while leaning on a wall, matched the white contours, in a ‘towel cloth’ bathrobe that ended at the knees, fluffy bunny slippers, and headwear consisting of another tower — the iconic, almost whimsical image of a woman fresh out of the shower. This one had also wasted no time with her chosen relaxation beverage, as she had a massive glass in her hand with red wine inside.

Frances watched him approach while sipping the wine, her blue eyes scanning him judiciously. That she read the potential danger of him was clear — Jack hadn’t exactly been unimposing before the change, but now? He couldn’t really hide that he walked apart from the greater populace. It was likely that the bouncer-type had sensed it, too.

Somehow, the bags Jack was carrying, now easy to see by their authentic logos — including one with a doofy-looking smiling teddy bear — made some of the tension in her shoulders leave. “Let’s have a seat,” she said, otherwise calm and collected. “Wine?” She walked past the folding door into the immediately open living room area, large, spacious, and sparkling clean. It was… architectural, with a visible mezzanine, a kind of simplistic elegance, broken up by a few potted plants and quaint abstract art.

“No, thank you, Miss,” Jack replied.

“I’d prefer if you drank with me.” She took a seat in the middle of a big, fluffy couch and crossed her legs. “At least a sip, hmm? Do you prefer hard liquor? A mixed drink?”

“Ah, alright. Wine is fine.” Jack took a seat on the couch at a right angle to her, setting the bags to the side. Looking around, Jack asked, “What does a place like this run, anyway?”

Frances took another sip of wine and stared back at him blankly. “I have no idea. A lot.”

Right. A floating platform delivered his wine, to which Jack narrowly avoided saying ‘thank you.' He took the smallest but deliberate sip to satisfy Frances, set it down on the marble coffee table, and asked, “How did you meet my dad?”

“He just asked me out. Wasn't creepy. He knew I was a stripper. I’ve often been into older guys, so.” She sipped her wine.

“He hasn’t had much luck with women.”

“He said the same thing. His wife royally screwed him. Godly screwed, more like it. Your mom?”

Jack nodded. “The one and only former Ms. Laker. Got embroiled in a cult. Tempted away by a devil.”

“And made another in the process. It happens so often. I was an orphan out of a situation I didn’t even get to know about. I think independence should just be illegalized for good. To hell with free will. We suck at it.”

“The case has indeed been made. But at least my mom is in a stable situation. Supposedly, that is. My dad is out there doing who-knows-what.”

Frances took another long sip of wine. “Was he a good father when you were younger?”

“Definitely. Kind, supportive, always took time. I know he loved me. Still does, even. I imagine the lack of contact is some manner of shame.”

Frances nodded. She peered over at him, smiled faintly, and took a breath, her eyes finally shifting away. “He talked about you, said you were doing well as a ferry pilot. He would mutter about keeping money on the side, just in case his son needed it. Not that it ever worked out that way.”

Jack shifted in his seat and fought off a frown. He reached for the wine and took another sip. He huffed. “I’d never go to him for that. He’s got a rep for borrowing money from others.”

Sighing as she spoke, Francis said, “Yes, he owes me, too. In any case, I could be wrong, but you seem to be doing a little better than most cabbies.” Her eyes shifted over to the bags, and she formed a lopsided grin. “Are those overpriced Runner’s Strip gifts actually for me, by the way?”

It was not a serious query, and Jack laughed as he shook his head. “These are for my… girlfriend.” His hesitation made her raise her eyebrows, which made Jack scoff at himself. “Sorry, yes — girlfriend. I should probably say that twice as confidently.”

Amused, Francis sipped her wine and nodded rather sarcastically, her eyes widening. “I’d say. Be thankful you hesitated in front of me instead of her.”

“I am. It’s just that it was kinda sudden, and there’s only been one date, but there’s been an agreed second date, and the beginning energy of boyfriend-girlfriend, and why am I telling you this?” He smiled and cringed at the same time, shaking his head.

Frances laughed. “I did pry, after all. Anyway, it sounds adorable. Did you meet her as your client?”

“Technically, yeah.” Sensing what she was getting at, Jack said, “I’m not piloting anymore. I got into another opportunity in plat-to-plat trade.” He left a significant hesitation after the statement. “Suffice to say it’s significantly more lucrative.”

Her slow nod spoke volumes about the wheels turning in her head about the things that could mean. Finally, she shrugged. “Like father, like son, I suppose.” She took a sip of wine.

“I know my dad got into trading all manner of things.” Jack held up his hands and grinned with a bit of devilishness thrown in. “I’m the last one to judge, trust me. I just want to know he’s safe. Ish. Ideally, I find his ass, or talk to him, but yeah… I’m just worried about him, I kinda think he’s in trouble, and maybe I can help him, you know?”

Frances took a slow sip of wine and squinted her eyes in the middle distance. “No judging, you said?”

“Not at all.”

“Complete confidentiality?”

“Absolutely.” The easy lies of a fed. What the hell else could he say? Just you, me, Memoria, and the rest of us. “All I care about is keeping my dad’s stupid head on his shoulders. The extraneous to that doesn’t matter. I didn’t hear, I wasn’t here, and so on.”

Another slow nod from her as she studied Jack, and finally a sigh as she set the wine down, and pulled her legs up to curl herself up more on the couch. “Well, first of all, I actually met him as a dealer. A smuggler. I was into some… bad stuff. We connected. He was into some less bad stuff, but I dragged him into mine on top of it, and mixed it like an inferno with his own alcoholism. I’m… sorry.”

Jack shook his head and gestured with a hand, bidding her to continue. Damn it, Dad. You’ve really spiraled.

She looked away and shrugged. “We started to fight over things and split up. Can’t say we weren’t both fragged in the head. I guess losing my especially generous dealer was a good trigger for me. I met someone who helped me get clean, and now I’m living in his house and… yeah. Here I am.”

Jack took a deep breath and nodded his gratitude. “Well, at least I know.” He smiled suddenly. “But how about you, Ms. Limer? You’ve gone and hypocritically made a case for free will with your turnaround.”

She snorted and rolled her eyes, though her lips twitched. “The jury is still out, Mister, trust me. And call me Fran.”

“Jack, if you forgot.”

Frances obviously struggled internally. She rubbed her cheek, not looking at him for a spell. She touched the wine glass, then pulled away, rubbing her fingers together subconsciously. Finally, she sagged slightly as she shook her head, having come to a decision. Her eyes squinted slightly. “You seem like a nice guy. Tell me you aren’t smuggling drugs, please? Even if you are.”

“I’m not — that I guarantee, nor would I. Cross my heart and hope for a meteor.” He made the cross over his chest, to boot.

Biting her lip, she nodded again, as her eyes squinted further. “I think it was… a month ago. He called me up out of the blue in the middle of the night, chattering excitedly and oddly about some new wonder drug I should try. I not-so-gently refused. He got ugly in turn, asking why I was suddenly ‘too good’ for him and shit like that.” She shrugged. “I told him to frag himself and never call me again, then hung up. He tried calling back, so I blocked the number. Knowing him, it was probably a burner.”

Jack winced and felt far worse inside. His dad being like this was an ongoing nightmare for him. “I see. What did he say about the drug?”

Her brows were deeply furrowed as she tried to recollect. “I was half-asleep at the time, so I don’t remember everything. ‘Spectacular trip’ was something he said. Vision of heaven? Something like that. No resistance after use, supposedly, but that’s always a line. Wanted me to try it. I chalked it up to him wanting to score and maybe get a repeat customer, but who knows.”

Not good. “Do you still have the number in your phone?”

“Don’t bother. I know how he operates. He’s paranoid. I’m sure that thing was hurled off a plat a month ago. Anyway, I… can give you something better. Let me get my phone. Need to take this towel off anyway.”

She rose and hurried around a corner.

“So far, so good, yeah?” Jack asked Mini internally.

It was Memoria who answered, though. “A spectacular trip, you might say. You cracked the shell. If she’s got some number — which I rather suspect — have her call it.”

“We’re not going to drag her entirely into this, are we? You want her to feign interest in the drug?”

“It’s tempting, but risky. He may detect the bullshit, and we’ve already found him to be exceedingly shrewd and wily. Dangling you is the other risk. If he isn’t a fanatic, he’ll keep you away, right? But what if he is?”

Jack took another sip of the wine and grimaced. “He’d want to… reveal the truth to me. Pull me into this in some way. Right?”

“Exactly. So the question is, did Freddy reach out to Frances due to her perspective on why, that is ‘to take advantage of a mark,’ or did he do it to pull someone he cared about into it? You know him best, Jack — I’ll let you decide. Choose as an agent, hmm? Not as a son, nor as a man grateful for the cooperation of a beautiful woman reacting out of guilt and her growing motherly instincts.”

A test, clearly. “Roger that, Mother.”

“Why that face?” came the question as Frances walked back in, towel-head discarded and her somewhat damp red hair now down as she smiled at him. “Is the wine that bad?”

“Oh, not at all,” Jack replied as he returned the smile. “It’s just the situation. If only my dad hadn’t gotten himself into this shit… you know.”

“And hadn’t met me to make things worse,” Frances said as she sat back on the couch, but this time right next to him, close enough to catch the scent of her flowery shower products. She brandished her phone out in front of her.

“I didn’t mean-”

She waved a hand quickly. “It’s fine, Jack. I wouldn’t want to have met me then, either. Something to remember. This rehab book I use teaches you to do that, you know. Mindfulness, checks against the urges. Who wants to turn back into a monster? Or go back into the womb? That sort of thing. I need a lot of those reminders.”

She spoke as she manipulated her phone with significant effort, not virtually, but to pop the phone from its case. She finally did so. “Ugh, gross. Haven’t done this in a while, sorry…” She dumped some detritus out on the couch to the side, along with several small slips of various paper. She sifted through the slips and picked a small pink one out that was scrawled with text. “Here we are.” In two manicured, purple-painted fingernails, she held out the slip to him.

He took it and held it out, frowning at a bizarre-looking code.

“Bingo!” Memoria exclaimed in his head. “That’s an email, of sorts. We just cracked it. Empty, deleted messages, but still open. We recovered the messages — four, all from her, all vague or ‘I’m here,’ that sort of thing, all related to the initial drug deal meetings. He’s paranoid, alright. Keeps contacts separated, and it's hard to trace. This gives me no link to track other deals, not without other emails. Names aren’t used, but inferred from who was given the email. Plenty of plausible deniability.”

Jack made his face appear puzzled. “What even is this?”

She chuckled. “I needed help with it, too. It’s an email, but you have to get one like it. It's on the deep web or some shit like that, I dunno.”

“Do you still have access to it?”

She very reluctantly nodded. “Yeah…”

It was at that point that Jack had to frame what he wanted to request of her. The choice was easy, even if he checked his bias about ‘bothering the pretty lady trying to get her life right.’ His dad was who he was, even as it scared him to admit the consequences. “Can you use it and tell my dad I need to contact him?” He quickly held up a forestalling hand at her immediate facial reaction. “You don’t have to admit spilling any beans, you see? It’s actually better than me using it. You can just tell him I sought you out, looking for traces of him.”

Her eyes flickered around as she put the pieces together. “Shit. You’re right… I could’ve just leaked it to him without telling you anything, and avoided being a rat. Damn it.”

Jack suppressed a laugh and turned it into an apologetic smile. “I think anyone would forgive you being a rat to a man’s worried son, eh? Again, I only want what’s best for him.”

She nodded, bit her lip thoughtfully, and then sighed as she picked up her phone and opened an app. “Your words, or mine?”

“Yours, but let me see them first. Don’t hit send before I leave, though.”

“In case your movements are being watched, in which case, the timing might be considered?” At his nod, she grinned. “We could’ve been a fragged-up family. Three paranoids in sync. You’d probably be the brains of the operation.”

Jack exhaled through his nose and smiled politely at that. She’s barely older than I am — then again, maybe she just looks that way.

She typed away, then showed him the results.

Hey. Regardless of what's going on with us, I thought you should know your son, Jack, showed up on my doorstep. He’s worried about you. Nice kid. Hope you can touch base, lies cocked and ready or otherwise. Here’s his number…

Jack raised his eyebrows. “That’s absolutely perfect, Fran. I wouldn’t change a thing. Well done.” He momentarily pulled out his phone to show his newly assigned number for the ‘role.’

Her smile was wry as she typed it in. “A perfect traitor, me.”

Jack leaned forward on his knees and met her eyes insistently. “Hey, that’s not true. He needs anchors, too, if he’s going to have any chance out there. As an addict, I’m sure you get that. He’s burned a lot of bridges with family, but some are made of steel. I won’t ever abandon him.”

“Made of steel? You’re a poet, Jack.” She blinked and looked away, trailing her fingers through her hair. “It might be best if you go soon. No offense, but my boyfriend wouldn’t likely care too much for the visit.” Her eyes rolled upward to indicate ‘the room’ as a whole. “Sorry.”

“Of course.” He rose. “It’s no trouble. Thanks for the help. And the wine.”

“I’m happy to help. Really. I’ll send the message in five. And do call the building to leave a message if you hear from him. I… don’t want to talk to him, but it doesn’t mean I wouldn’t want to know he’s alright.”

“Obviously, I can’t promise anything, but I will if I can.”

She smiled, then nodded over at his bags still on the couch. “Don’t forget the gifts.”

“Oh, right…” Jack took them, flashing a final appreciative smile, and then turned to depart, feeling her eyes following him until he passed the folding door.

“Now, we wait, eh?” he asked Memoria internally.

“And pray, yes.”
