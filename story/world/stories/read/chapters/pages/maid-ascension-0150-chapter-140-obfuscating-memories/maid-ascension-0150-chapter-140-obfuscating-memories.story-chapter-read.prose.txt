As the memories flooded back into her mind, Lira Velora slouched in her seat, feeling smug about herself. She had done it. She had defeated the Truth Stone.

A few weeks ago, after the interview over Taren’s death, Lira had set out to find a way to beat a truth spell. Her first thought was to overwhelm the stone, perhaps by creating a shroud of disturbance that formed a small magical null space over it and then hiding that with mana obfuscation. She abandoned the idea almost immediately, since the stone was in the care of a mage, and a competent mage could spot a disturbance by feel alone, without needing Mage Sight.

The second idea was far simpler: what if she actually told the truth?

Casting Mind magic on herself was risky, since it meant manipulating her own knowledge and memory, and a single mistake could cause permanent damage. Luckily for her, she had a few leads she could explore.

The first hint came from something she had read back when she was researching medicine. Among the many ailments she had studied was something called amnesia, a condition that made people forget their memories, either partially or completely, usually after a blow to the head or a period of severe mental anguish. She was not willing to inflict either of those on herself, but what interested her was the fact that, in some cases, patients recovered those lost memories. That meant they had never been completely deleted, only obscured. She did not need to erase her history or rewrite it from scratch; all she needed was to add a layer of obfuscation over her mind that she could remove later.

Imprinting the spell was one of the hardest things she had ever done. There was confusion over whether memory belonged to the domain of the mind or the soul, and she also needed the spell to be undetectable from the outside, since a spell that stayed active until dismissed would be spotted by any competent mage casting Mage Sight. What she needed was two spells, one to construct the layer of obfuscation and another to remove it, and both had to be one-and-done spells rather than something she needed to maintain, actively or passively.

Pika. Cognar. Veshar.Mind. Knowledge. Concealment.

Pika. Cognar. Luneth.Mind. Knowledge. Purification.

For the first spell, she needed to visualize the false memories that would cover the true ones, so she had to spend a few hours writing an extensive backstory for Lira Tenlan while also going over every detail that needed to be repressed: anything linked to Lira Velora’s childhood, anything related to magic, and anything involving her planning and committing murders. Those last two sets of memories were replaced with images of her cleaning the library, since without the Dust Gathering spell, she would have needed to work a lot of overtime to keep the library clean. She gave Tenlan a few habits to match her own: a girl who grew up reading in the Musgrove library, who had endless curiosity, who kept a to-do list in her journal and followed it faithfully, and who had a deep love for calligraphy.

She did not cast the spells immediately. Instead, over the past week, she had slowly tested both on superficial things. First, she changed Cook Brenneth’s name to Gwyneth and had to navigate the awkward interaction of calling a woman she had known for six months by the wrong name. Then she escalated slightly by changing the memory of her having orange hair to black, which gave her a mild case of dysphoria each time she noticed her orange hair at the edge of her vision. The purification worked without a problem every time, restoring her mind to its original configuration without any loss of memories.

The real problem was that the spell could not be tested on the thing that mattered most. Once she enacted her plan in earnest, Lira Velora would be temporarily sealed away, and Lira Tenlan would have to find her own way back to the original. That meant the way back had to be written down somewhere she could not easily miss it. She worked through the possibilities for most of an evening, since a note left in plain sight would be visible to anyone, while a note hidden too well would never be found by a girl who did not know she was looking for something in the first place. In the end, she settled on a combination of both: two pieces that would only make sense together, one to nudge Tenlan toward the other.

The morning after she killed Kavard, once the cleanup was done and her hands had stopped shaking, she opened her journal to the to-do list and wrote a line at the very bottom of the page in her ordinary hand.

After a stressful day, remember to relax with the help of Calligraphic Foundations by Master Jorel Velora.

It was the sort of line that looked as though it had been there for days, and a girl about to face a day of questioning would have every reason to follow those instructions. Then she carried her favourite calligraphy book, the one written by her grand-uncle, to the secret alcove and set it on the small table beside the chair. She tore a narrow slip from a clean sheet of paper and wrote the second piece on it:

Magic Incantation:

Pika. Cognar. Luneth.Mind. Knowledge. Purification.

At her core, Lira Tenlan was still Lira, and upon finding a note in her own handwriting, or at least the handwriting she had adopted as Tenlan, claiming to be a magic incantation, the knowledge-hungry girl would have no choice but to try casting it. Since the spell was already imprinted, the intent behind trying to cast a magic spell would suffice.

She folded the slip once and tucked it between the pages, where it would serve as a bookmark she would find as soon as she opened the book. There were a few points of failure in the whole plan: Tenlan could ignore the line, lose her journal, get too spooked by Estophon to even consider going somewhere as suspicious as her alcove, or decide the slip was a prank and throw it into the fire. Velora would stay buried until Tenlan learned magic anew and recreated a spell to clear her mind.

Lira accepted the risk, since you couldn’t really kill the crown prince without taking any.

Only then did she sit down at her desk, with the sun rising over the palace, and cast the Memory Concealment spell.

Lira stretched in her alcove, the paper still in hand.

“Pika. Fila. Curad.”

She waited for the writing to turn completely to ashes before dropping it into the washbasin she kept there.

Looking back, there seemed to be a difference between herself and Lira Tenlan, since Tenlan had been happier than she was. Tenlan was freer when chatting with Tessa, hugged Lyth without any reserve, and showed more feeling when facing Estophon than Lira Velora ever could. She did not scheme as much while carefully considering every word she said. Was that what her life would have been like if she had not chosen this path, if she had kept her head down the way Pallan wanted her to?

She shook her head. That was not something she should dwell on, especially when the image of Lyth crying kept getting stuck in her mind.

She could not believe she had killed his brother and then tried to console him, so she forced her mind to move on from the thought.

What she should worry about instead was whether she could use these spells in response to Aric’s ultimatum. She wasn’t sure, since Lira Tenlan had drifted too far from Lira Velora’s personality, and Aric’s Grand Perception, which linked perception to memory, could technically notice the difference.

The spell was not perfect either. She had obfuscated both Pallan, as part of her past as Velora, and her witnessing Taren’s death, as part of her magic use, but she had not obfuscated the fact that she knew Estophon had killed him. That had left her with a confusing half-recollection. Other than remapping her entire life, mishaps like that were bound to happen, and there was no avoiding them.

The question remained: what would she do about Aric? She looked around the couple of shelves inside the alcove and sighed. Maybe this persona had outlived its usefulness, since she had absorbed at least seventy percent of the library’s contents and learned more than sixty spells.

All she had left to do was kill Kelroth.

Maybe it was time for Lira Tenlan to disappear. Even Lira Velora could do with a vacation somewhere fresh, where no one knew her, and preferably somewhere she could cast spells in the open without the fear of being executed.

Lira stretched once more and decided to call it a day. The sun had set while she was busy sorting through her own memories, and her eyes were heavy enough that the thought of her bed in the maids’ quarters was too tempting to pass up. Besides, she should avoid sleeping in the library, especially today. She slid the false wall back into place, pulled the tapestry straight behind her, and stepped out into the library, where the shelves had gone dark and only the faint orange glow of the candle she was holding remained.

That was when she noticed another light, flickering like an open flame, coming from the direction of her front desk. She did not remember leaving a candle there.

“Paldina. Ladar. Pristana. Turat. Oshkan.”

She stepped back at the edge of the tapestry before casting her danger sense, but it stayed quiet, which only made her more curious, since it either meant the light was harmless or that whoever was holding it posed no threat to her.

She walked slowly between the shelves until the desk came into view, and she saw who was standing beside it.

Court Mage Edward Emberborn stood in the middle of the floor in a coat of red and gold, looking like a living flame. He was also the source of the glow, with light emanating from his body. Lira froze, her mind racing through everything the man could have seen, heard, or felt. Her grand-uncle’s book should still have had an active mana-obfuscation bookmark, so he would not have sensed her burning the slip or even casting danger sense.

Lira did not dare move an inch. She just stood there, staring at him. To her utter shock, the flamboyant mage turned toward her and started clapping, slowly at first, then with real enthusiasm, sparks of flame bursting from his hands whenever they met.

“Congratulations, Archmage,” he said. “That was an outstanding performance!”
