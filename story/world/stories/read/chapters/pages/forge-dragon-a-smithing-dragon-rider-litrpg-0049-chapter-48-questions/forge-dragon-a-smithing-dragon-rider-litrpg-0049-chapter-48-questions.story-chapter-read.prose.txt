“Caleb Turbius Draconis,” Drusus said when Caleb had finished his work—Drusus’ work that is. Caleb had done the whole week’s worth of forging in an afternoon. “I still don’t believe it.”

He’d been captivated by it all and when Caleb had turned in for the day he’d bombarded him with questions.

“And what was with all the wind—and why didn’t you use—Oxygen!”

“Whaty-gen?” Caleb asked, homing in on the new word.

“”Oxygen,” Drusus repeated. “The stuff in air that makes iron rust. We also need it to live, some say. You must have done something to remove it right?”

Caleb nodded, thinking about the new word. It seemed that his master’s knowledge, if not his skill, was a bit provincial. He’d have to ask after basic smithing things to see if there were more gaps in his knowledge.

“What other things are in air?” Caleb asked. “I’ve noticed that most of the air is made up of something that doesn’t react with steel. It’s only this oxygen stuff that causes scale.”

“That’d be the nitrogen. Supposedly it’s good for plants if you can get it into the soil, but that’s all I know about it—also, I think its in your pee.

Caleb pressed for more details but only learned that the air was called oxygen and that when it reacted with the iron it “oxidized.” The terminology was useful in helping order Caleb’s thoughts. He’d already been wrong about naming mage steel and likely quench steel had some known name—though Drusus didn’t know it.

He feared that he’d be laughed out of any forge in the capital if Kelia and his dreams ever take them there.

The next morning, Caleb asked Mettius some questions he’d been wondering about.

“What do you know about the errors?” Caleb asked Mettius between swings of his hammer against the prince’s shield.

“Nothing,” Mettius said, firing a Force Bolt back at Caleb.

Caleb chose to take the blow and reinforce him armor instead of dodging. He’d hammered metal stakes into the ground around the training yard to practice his Imperium propelled dodges, but Immutable Metal often too less mana and he knew he could handle Mettius’ Force Bolts if the wizard didn’t empower them greatly.

Caleb shattered the next shield, drawing on Arcane Power Attack, driving home his disbelief.

“Really, I don’t,” Mettius said, raising his hands to signal a pause to the training. “My father may know more, but I only found out about it a few months back. Strange beasts have been appearing with these corrupted classes. Those I spoke to fear it may be signs of further system degradation but I doubt that. The monsters are new, and the system doesn’t interact with monsters.”

“Could that be part of the degradation?”

“I don’t know, I’m not a systemari,” Mettius said. “I fear it’s some sort of new class that was discovered. One that and create these beings. It wouldn't be the first time.”

“When has this happened before,” Caleb asked.

Mettius looked around him, considering before answering.

“It’s not exactly a state secret, but there was a class called the Necromancer. It was a mage with a high affinity for death. A high level one could raise a legion back from the dead. There was a necromancer a few decades past the Thousand Blades had to put down. They lost a lot of legionnaires in the process and they were only sent in after the necromancer destroyed two mundane legions.”

“So there's some sort of ‘corrupter’ class out there?”

“I don’t know, but that's my guess,” Mettius said, ending the topic.

They sparred more until Caleb decided he’d waited enough to ask another question.

“Why is this legion out here?” Caleb asked.

He knew that they were here for a possible rebellion, but that was the task for a mundane legion, not one full of initialized. The other 9 centuries of the legion were all busy on the front lines of the empire’s ever expanding border, and to spare one for such a task was odd. Caleb had heard grumblings in the camp about as much. The legionnaires to a man all wanted to be on the front lines where they could level.

The empire had means to train a new initialized legionaire to level 5, but beyond that they needed battle to level.

“I don’t know—really this time. I asked. No one told me. My guess is that it has to do with these errors. I didn’t know about them until I came here, but that doesn’t mean it wasn’t known. All I know is that there’s another group unhappy with my father’s—may-he-reign-forever—rule.”

Caleb wasn’t sure how he felt about the mission of stamping down a rebellion against the emperor. He’d never had strong feelings about the empire one way or another before his mother had been taken. They were a nuisance at worst, taking the young brash initialized whenever they came by recruiting, but they’d never taken anyone who didn’t want to go—until his mother.

He’d harboured dreams of finding her and taking her back, but never dreams of overthrowing the empire. The idea never occurred to him. And now, here he was sworn to serve that empire. He couldn’t destroy it even if he wanted to. But, that oath didn’t compel him to commit what he was as a potential atrocity.

What would these rebels even look like? Woodsmen with axes? Miners with picks? The empire had a monopoly on the initialized of any great partial talent. What hope would a mountain village have against a hundred blades of the Thousand Blade Legion? What hope would they have against Caleb alone?

Caleb was sad to realize that the answer was none. He consoled himself with the knowledge that he’d not have to partake in the fighting if it came to that.

Caleb spent his second afternoon in the forge creating arrowheads for Kelia. She’d improved her Frost Bolt drastically in just a few days, but her archery skills had been honed over the past decade of her life, and to throw that away now seemed like a waste. Instead he decided to use his new skills to supplement hers. First he made a set of broad heads in case they faced similar corrupted beasts. There was nothing wrong with the ones the legion had, but he felt better knowing she’d be using ones he’d made himself.

Then he moved on to making bodkin arrow heads. This he put a lot more thought into. He wanted to make arrows specifically designed to penetrate armor. He requisitioned some meta steel and some cold aspected steel from the legion, and forged 40 such arrows, 20 of each. When it came to the quench, Caleb once more used his quenching steel to help rapidly cool the metal while maintaining its integrity through the process. The resulting arrows were—while not as hard as his hammer—extremely durable.

Drusus watched Caleb work—he now spent all of his time off watching Caleb work. When he saw how he quenched the arrowheads in water he almost shouted in protest but he held his tongue, inspecting the finished result eagerly.

When he saw that the arrowhead was unwarped and intact, he insisted on testing it out. Caleb allowed Drusus to bring the arrowhead over to the fletcher and came back a short while later with a completed arrow and bow.

“Would you do the honors?” Drusus said, setting up an armor rack with a nearly destroyed lorica that was beyond saving.

Caleb took the bow. He nocked an arrow and drew it back, pulling his hand to his cheek and looking down the shaft at the center of the armor a dozen meters away. He closed his eyes, viewing the armor and arrow through his affinity senses but made no adjustments. His increased perception made it far easier to line up the shot than it had been the last time he’d fired a bow and he marvelled at how confident he was in his shot—the lateral accuracy at least. He had no idea how powerful this bow would fire and expected he’d be off a fair margin vertically.

He loosed the arrow and followed its flight with his magical senses. Time seemed to slow as his acuity and perception tracked the projectile, and he could already tell he was going to miss. The bow had been more powerful than he’d expected, and he’d aimed too high expecting more drop to occur. He’d not accounted for his increased strength when he’d drawn the bow and assumed it had a lower draw weight than it really did.

Caleb called a gust of wind down from above over the distance of the arrows flight, altering the flight jus enough that instead of flying through where the head ought to have been, it struck dead center of the left portion of the armor, right where the heart would have been. The arrowhead struck the plate and continued right through, the arrow sinking up to its fletchings which then were ripped off as the arrow flew through the hole.
