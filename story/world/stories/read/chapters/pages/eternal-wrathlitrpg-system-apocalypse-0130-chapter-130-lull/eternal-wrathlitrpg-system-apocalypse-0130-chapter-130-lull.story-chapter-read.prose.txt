“That was one dangerous battle. I wasn’t sure we would come out of there alive.” Andrew plopped to the ground, breathless. Now that the moon was gone, he was in no hurry to fix the roof. The notification also gave them ample time to prepare — thirty hours. They could rebuild the base from scratch with that amount of time.

The worrying thing was the ambush notification. He was thankful the system told him that they would be attacked when they gathered resources from now on. Most ambushes were not so kind as to announce themselves. But the system didn’t tell him how strong the ambush party would be.

To rebuild the roof, he needed logs. With the rebars provided by the system, he could create strong columns to support the roof, but he still needed logs cut into planks to make the roofing — and his supplies ran thin because of the previous assault. So, he needed to gather resources in the forest again, whether he liked it or not.

“Well, we have James exactly for this reason.” Andrew dismissed the notification. If it were anyone else, he would be worried. After all, even Andrew could be killed with a single weapon skill. James, on the other hand, had survived such strikes multiple times. He could handle skill clashes, and his chimera would allow him to recover missing limbs. Andrew glanced at James, who was inspecting the beetle curiously.

The beetle was tough — literally undefeatable until they clashed with their own kin. James wondered whether that effect was due to the armor, which had now been reduced to a piece of ordinary steel, or whether it came from their body parts.

How glad James was when he found out it turned out to be the latter.

[Would you like to modify your body? Y/N]

[Available body parts:Mobila Tank Beetle Armor (Physical and Magical Resistance +90%, Mana -150) (Extra Slot)Mobila Tank Beetle Horn (Extra Slot)]

“I’ll take the armor,” James said.

[Understood. Where will you place the armor? Chimera will form a circular shield of around 30-centimeter radius wherever you deem fit.]

“...I knew it was too good to be true. Well, let’s go with the hand.” James sighed, expecting a full-body impervious armor like the one the beetle had. Chimera seemed to have downgraded its ability before attaching them to him as body parts. As the gamers said, the one you have will always be nerfed compared to the one the NPCs had.

Motes of black light came out from James, consuming the beetles and transferring their power to him. A round black shield glistened in James’s hand. He had expected a rough shield, similar to the rock crab he had met inside the dungeon, but this was a pleasant surprise. James knocked his shield, testing it, and it returned a solid thunk.

After taking the beetle’s body, James lay down, fatigue from the prolonged battle starting to creep in. The warmth of the sun caressed him, lulling him to sleep.

No one woke James. His body ached all over as scabs formed around the bite marks left by the jackals and dogheads. Seeing the timer, six hours had passed just like that. James looked around and found he wasn’t the only one slacking; even Andrew was still asleep when James strolled around the camp. Andrew had slept later than James, as there were wounded soldiers needing his attention.

James then readied his mount, intending to go toward the forest. Civilians idled around, sometimes being ordered by Xiaoming or Bernard to help grab the fish and coconuts they had harvested. They lounged about, unwinding the tension and fatigue that had built up over time. Their wounds might be healed, but the fatigue and tension weren’t. They had just survived a life-threatening battle — it was important to rest.

James let them be and drove his raptor toward the forest. The notification about an ambush had slipped from his mind as he yawned while riding deeper into the woods.

Like an idiot, James’s raptor trotted through the forest in a calm, serene manner, like a tourist going flower-viewing in spring. Such idiocy was punished with haste. Two golden arrows shot toward his head and heart. James threw his body to the side as fast as he could. Thanks to his ears, James heard the arrows coming for his life. However, it was a tad too late.

James dodged the arrow that went for his head, but the one that went for his chest pierced his shoulder, hitting his golem arm.

“Fuck! An ambush? That never happened before!” James cursed, confused. Only then did he remember the notification about an ambush in the forested area just before he went to sleep. Cursing his idiocy, James pulled his sword. His right golem arm refused to move, as an arrow was lodged in it.

James scanned his surroundings, and three Anubis warriors approached — two wielding bows while the other carried a spear. Another golden arrow aimed to reap his life. This time, James was fully alert, and the golden meteor bounced harmlessly against his new shield. James chased after the Anubis warrior with rage and fury he had never felt before.

Two dozen ice spears and a sword skill later, James had cleaned up the ambush party. After paying the system 400 coins to fix his arm, he started logging again. This time, his eyes and ears were open wide — not even mice or birds escaped his notice.

“That was so stupid. The system clearly warned me,” James cursed while grateful for being alive. Thankfully, there was nobody around, or he could have died of shame.

Outside the forest, Andrew and Sanders waited for him.

“James, are you okay? I was wondering whether you were attacked by monsters. The notice said there might be an ambush.”

“I was attacked, all right. Motherfuckers nearly killed me. They had archers with bow skills suddenly shooting from the back,” James said. He then recalled his experience. The reason he was almost killed was not simply because he was lax — the enemy had also hidden themselves so well that James couldn’t see them, even though the forest was basically a pine forest with little shrubbery. They had concealed themselves among the trees, hidden from James’s line of sight.

“...” Andrew twitched. His head already went through the list of survivors who had life-detection skills. “You should bring Helen later. She has some sort of detection skill,” Andrew suggested.

James tilted his head, wondering who Helen was. Andrew explained that she was on Bernard’s team and had a unique skill — ambush detection.

Again, James was amazed at Andrew’s resourcefulness. He had already committed their names to memory even though this dungeon was the first time they had met and worked together. James hadn’t even gotten the names and skills of his volunteers yet.

“Alright, let’s bring Helen next time,” James replied.

Now that they had all rested, the logs James carried proved to be an essential supply. In ten hours, they had rebuilt the base, abatis, and barracks. Andrew had also moved the barracks to the corner so that if those beetles ever came again, the damage would be minimal.

“Everyone, we brought logs! Let’s rebuild our walls. Ah, make a pitfall here. What about the stakes? That’s secondary — we can use spears to stab from the high ground if they fall into the pitfalls. Ah, Helen, please go with James. The resource sites have enemies in them now. We’re going to need more logs. After that, go toward the quarry; we need more cement and stones.” Andrew went with Bernard and Xiaoming, directing them toward the base’s defenses.

Helen proved to be a better addition to the team than expected, detecting ambushes well before James saw them.

“Sorry, those monsters are weird. My detection range is supposed to be over one kilometer, but they just suddenly appear there.” Helen said.

“They probably got teleported in by the system. As long as we don’t get killed, I don’t mind,” James said as he cleaned up the monster with his arrows.

James snuck in four more trips with his raptor, bringing tons of logs and cement, then spent the last four hours resting at the improved base camp. With rebars, Andrew could make reinforced concrete, but since he couldn’t get more, he used them sparingly.

The base camp now looked like a small historical fort James had seen on YouTube.

As the timer reached zero, three dozen Anubis warriors came from the three gates. Each group bore six shielded warriors with khopeshes, two spear infantry, two archers, and two mages. They marched with calm poise, confident and unhurried. James assessed the marching infantry with a grim look.

This won’t be easy.

“Alright, move out, people. The enemy is upon us!” Andrew rallied the soldiers.

James drew his bow and tested the waters. As expected, the soldiers in front reacted with their shield skill, drawing the homing arrow to themselves.

“Andrew, I’m going to meet the enemy head-on. Be careful—these are no ordinary foes. They’re powerful.” James turned to Andrew, then charged toward the approaching enemies. He opened the fight with Ice Spears, which were deflected by their shields.

The archers followed up with their bow skills, but their shots were also deflected by James’ new shield. James then switched to his mind magic. This magic had no substance, so when it struck the shield bearers, they collapsed to the ground, writhing in the immense pain that Mind Flay caused.

James dropped the shield bearers one by one, intending to finish the archers and mages next—but suddenly, the world turned dark. His mind reeled with creeping horror.

“System, what happened? Was I teleported?”

[No. Survivor James has been hit with mind magic: Sense Alteration. Your eyes have been blinded.]

“How did I not see the spell? Dispel it now!” James shouted as he leaped backward. It was just a fraction of a second, but when his senses returned, two arrows nearly took his life—one hit his flank, and the other struck his stomach. They had purposely avoided the shield.

“Fuck!” James cursed. Adrenaline flooded his system. He lunged forward with his sword, activating Vorpal Lunge as the last of the shield bearers went down. His blade reaped one of the Anubis archers in a clean sweep.

James exhaled a stream of fire at the rest of the group. His horn fired lightning bolts recklessly. Pain surged through him as the arrows lodged in his body shifted, but he couldn’t care less—he had to finish the archers and mages before the shield bearers regained their senses.

The fire helped immensely. It blinded them, scarring their faces. James soon beheaded them with his sword as blood poured from his wounds.

[Would you like to spend 300 biomass to cure your wounds?]

A notification popped up before James. He had won his side of the battle—but the fight was far from over.
