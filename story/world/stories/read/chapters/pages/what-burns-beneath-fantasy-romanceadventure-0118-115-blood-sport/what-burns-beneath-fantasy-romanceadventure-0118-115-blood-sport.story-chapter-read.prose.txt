‘One chance to save a soul.’

Our trek up the Pit was paved by cloying cold and the foul smells of misery. The arena loomed on one side, and on the other… labyrinthine tunnels dappled with maws of dark, rancid rock. Some barred, with roaring monsters and flashing fangs behind. Some unbarred, but with moans, growls and rattling chains within. Some held pleading voices. One held what sounded like a crying child. Some were silent.

I couldn’t bring myself to look inside any of them. Just walked with my eyes down and both hands clapped over my ears, quickly turning my world to muffled quiet and unyielding stone.

‘One chance to save a soul.’

A few more gray-skinned creatures scuttled about. They carried strips of dripping meat and bowls of water. Each had a jingling key-ring on their belt.

Eventually the tunnels gave way to steep steps splitting away in different directions. The High Fae spoke amongst themselves. One gestured towards me and said something, then looked disappointed at Velidan’s response. Some of them laughed. More talk. Then, as if a hundred creatures weren’t suffering behind them, they calmly climbed the steps. Soon after, Velidan pushed open a heavy door, dismissed the gray-skinned Fae waiting within, and we found ourselves alone on his dais.

It was a balconied, enclosed semi-circle of stone near the Pit’s top. The space held a table of dark wine, slick-looking fruits, and cherry-red wedges of meat. A wrought-iron chair sat near the balcony. The back area was lined with thick furs and pillows. Black sconces of flickering, purplish flames scented like heady perfume and thick incense clouded the air.

He rounded on me as soon as the door closed. “What was that?”

I felt numb from the walk and dizzy from the smells. Both were probably evident in my weary, “Horace.”

Black brows shot up, clearly expecting more.

I pressed cold palms to my face. They came away wet. “H-he keeps showing up. He says he wants to help me. One of my f-first nights here, he told me that names- I told you about this, remember? Then he scared Slip the other night. Didn’t she-”

“She said a small creature conversed with you out of earshot while she hid in a bush.”

“Oh.” My breath caught. Out of earshot. I suppose that’s a good thing. I winced at the clear anger on his face. “She tried to stop me. I promised her I wasn’t in danger.”

“Your sense of self-preservation can hardly be trusted,” he snapped, glowering down. “What did this Fae say?”

I swallowed. “That I reminded him of someone. He… he can see the future, you know. He keeps giving me advice, although it’s not been very helpful.” My chest swelled in a weary sigh. “He was in the mortal realm, too. In the sunlight, no less! He told me he’d be here, and that I should say hello.”

“So you did?!” He towered closer, crowding me against one curved wall. Reminding me that he was bigger and stronger. “You’ve been blindly trusting this creature? Obeying its instructions, while ignoring mine at every turn?”

“Well… yes.” I drew myself up, shaken but refusing to be intimidated. “Saints, he hasn’t threatened me or my friends! He’s never kidnapped me! Of course I’d rather listen to him than you!”

Dark eyes narrowed. “He nearly got you killed down there. You were obviously speaking to something, then obviously lying about it. You’re very lucky only Garruck noticed.” When all I offered was silence, he gave a frustrated snarl. “What did he say this time?”

“Just…” I searched the ground, hands spread helplessly. “Just things about hubris being a downfall. About how this place was full of madness and that unsharpened claws could still cut and… and…” ‘One chance to save a soul here.’ Ashes, just one? I want to save all of them! Heat pricked behind my eyes and my throat thickened. “Nothing else.”

“Brin.”

Anger rose with the tears. Liana was, perhaps first and foremost, whom I hoped Horace had meant. I glared at him, rising to the balls of my feet and pouring every ounce of bitterness I had into my voice. “It’s not your concern.”

The sharp hiss of breath made my hair stand on end, but any retort was drowned by Embriel’s booming voice.

“Hope everyone’s comfortable!” The cry resounded through the arena, impossibly clear and vibrating along the stones. “Grab your drinks and settle in, friends; our first round is a ravenous manticore against a hag!”

Horror and macabre curiosity drew me to the balcony. A glance upwards showed that, around the arches, eyes and teeth shone down. There were countless creatures watching from above, many of them cackling with obvious glee. Some were drooling.

I gasped at the sight. “There’s more Fae above us.”

“I told you,” Velidan said coldly from beside me, “this is a prison, a feeding ground, and a spectacle arena all at once. Many creatures are allowed down to pick the bodies clean after the entertainment.”

“Entertainment.” I could have vomited on the word. “This place is vile.”

“It serves its purpose, Brin.” He stared downwards, lips pressed into a thin line, and added darkly, “You don’t belong here.”

I gave him a questioning look at that, but before I could voice anything Garruck dragged an enormous, hump-backed figure out from one of the tunnels. He threw her to the stones- now lit by flickering braziers, with the carved rune seemingly completed- then lumbered into another cavern.

Jeering echoed from across the Pit. Several High Fae were gathered around their own balconies, most with drinks in hand. Heavy smoke curled through each dais, obscuring much of whatever lay inside. Embriel, Saffine, and Kallian were above and to the side of us; I could just make them out against the railings, pointing down as a lion-maned manticore burst into the arena and the hag’s terrified scream rang out.

I turned away. “Why do you want to stop him?”

“Because he’s an idiot.” Velidan braced both hands on the stone, sneering between the High Fae above us and whatever was happening below. “Twenty years of the Day Court’s silence means little. We’re immortal, Brin. A few decades is nothing. Perhaps they are in mourning, or have temporarily stopped caring about your realm, or perhaps Anduin is throwing a tantrum. Whatever the reason for their inaction, it won’t last. Embriel slaughtering and stealing a few hundred humans over the past decade is one thing; the Night Court invading your realm won’t go unnoticed. They’ll retaliate, and we,” he gestured to the scene below, “are clearly not in a position of strength. He’ll get everyone here killed.”

I shook my head. “But why don’t you just stay out of it, then? If whatever goes to attack our realm will eventually be punished by Day Fae, can’t you stay here and-”

“That was the idea,” he spat, turning obvious frustration towards me. “Until Embriel spoke of creating permanent portals a moon ago. Such travel within this realm is easy, but tearing a door into your realm? It’s incredibly difficult. So difficult that it’s hardly worth the effort, not to mention that most creatures aren’t powerful enough to come back without help. And he wants to rip an opening for anything to walk through. Including,” he’d begun pacing during this, boots slamming on stone and voice thick with fury, “any justice-crazed Fae from the Day Court. Imagine something like your foolish hero storming into this realm. It’d be a massacre.”

“They… but Day Fae wouldn’t hurt the good creatures, surely! The nymphs, the goblins, Alora-”

“I don’t know. I’ve no desire to find out.” Velidan stopped pacing, then turned a vicious look my way as his voice fell to match it. “And that’s only half of it. If a doorway between realms appears, some idiot humans might throw witchwood seeds in. Perhaps living trees will smell prey here and creep through. They flourish in soil where Fae have died. Did you know that?”

The blood drained from my face. I pictured my beautiful, beloved grove… sprouted from ground once covered in fallen Fae.

“This realm’s soaked in our own blood,” he continued bitterly. “All it takes is for a few roots to wind their way in, and we’d have a Night Court drowning in death. Again.”

As if to punctuate the statement, a blood-curdling screech rose from below. It died abruptly.

“Well, she didn’t last long!” Embriel’s mocking laugh rang off the stones. Howls and hoots echoed from above. “At least someone’s eating well today. Next up is… oh, this should be fun. Three shadewolves and a griffon. I wonder if mad wolves remember how to work together!”

I staggered away, fighting the tide of bile, and choked out, “And this isn’t ‘drowning in death?’”
