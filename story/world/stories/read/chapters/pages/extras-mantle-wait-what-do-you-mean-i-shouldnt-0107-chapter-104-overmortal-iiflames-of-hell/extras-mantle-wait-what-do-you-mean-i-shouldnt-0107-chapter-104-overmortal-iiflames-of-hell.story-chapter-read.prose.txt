"Oh, you suicidal bastard!” Orodain snarled. “I’m going to tear you limb from limb."

Blood covered him head to toe—mostly his own, but a good part Jin's as well—and his right arm hung at an unnatural angle where Jin's explosive barrage had caught him.

“This… pain… is nothing compared to the crucible of our Lord,” the cultist spat, a heavy clot of blood splattering at his feet.

His grin trembled but did not break. “Slowly,” he whispered. “Make it last.”

Jin, however, watched through the grey-washed lens of his focus state as Orodain's seemingly miraculous healing struggled to keep pace with his injuries. The wounds that had once healed so quickly now showed a much slower, less obvious recovery.

The cultist wobbled—barely perceptible, but there—and a cold, calculative part of Jin's mind logged that as valuable data.

Yet Jin held no illusions about what would happen if he rushed in now. Orodain would be ready, would turn that apparent weakness into a trap, and take Jin's head off the moment he committed.

You may be an elite Order III, but… Jin thought coldly, feeling the skill's emotional suppression wrap around him like a comfortable blanket. I have everything I need to win now.

The previous exchanges—those brutal, bloody sequences where they'd clashed and separated and clashed again—hadn't been wasted. Every strike, every parry, every near-miss had taught Jin something crucial to how Orodain fought.

And Orodain—despite being one of the strongest opponents Jin had ever faced alone—was not without flaws. Strength had bred assumption: that he was always right, always the strongest, and always the winner. His patterns rarely changed, only minor variations layered over the same foundation.

At his core, he was a bull. One who preferred to bulldoze their opponents with unrelenting power.

He always opened with the dark sickle. He favored closing from the right. His observation-type skill lagged by a fractional delay whenever Jin feinted or changed his tactics.

And the dark-gold flames… those were the tell. They flared brighter exactly two heartbeats before every major attack.

Patterns. Exploitable patterns.

Narrator. What can I leverage that I haven't used yet?

The response came as structured data flooding his awareness.

« Current active skills: Thermokinetic Engine at baseline regulation, Inevitable Doom was unsuccessful, your new star is maintaining emotional suppression at approximately 50% effectiveness, likely due to recent awakening.»

New star? Jin’s mind lingered on the words for a fraction before moving on, filing that to figure out later.

«Available skills: [Essence constructs] for defensive layering and probable kinetic transfer. [Spatial Seal] for movement restriction. [Entropy Zone] for—»

Wait. Jin's gaze—still locked on Orodain's recovering form—flickered briefly inward as information clicked into place.

Essence Armament. I can create construct shields and barriers. And if attacks hit those barriers...

«Thermokinetic Engine would register the kinetic impacts,» the Narrator finished. «Correct. Defensive constructs would function as secondary absorption surfaces.»

A plan crystallized in Jin's mind, and he smiled for the first time since… the changes.

He wants close combat. Wants to overwhelm me with speed and skill. Fine.

Let him hit me. Let him hit my shields. Let him feed my engine until I have enough to end this.

Across the ruined corridor, Orodain finished the barest amount of healing and rolled his right arm as he settled into a combat stance.

Those black eyes tracked Jin with predatory focus.

"Ready to end this?" the cultist asked. "Or do you need a minute to bleed some more? It’s annoying I can’t see your terrified expression behind that damn mask."

“But your trembling legs gave all your feeling away, newbie.” Orodain laughed loudly, and Jin was thankful once again for his focus state, or else he would have most definitely chuckled.

Orodain was mistaking his legs, which were indeed trembling, but that was not out of fear; no, it was due to the crazy amounts of kinetic energy surging through his legs and body at the moment.

Jin's grip tightened on Iron Howl.

He'd been using it wrong, he realized. Treating his gun like a precision instrument—one shot, one kill, carefully aimed and loaded with spells—but Iron Howl was more than that. Could be more than that if he stopped being precious about ammunition conservation… which at this point was not an issue. The habits from the dungeons were still lingering, and Jin promised himself to sit down and look at himself properly after all this is over.

« Use the gun like it's actually a gun.»

Thanks for the words of wisdom.

"I was giving you a chance to heal… didn’t want you to topple over and make a spectacle," Jin said, and his digitized voice made Orodain's grin falter slightly.

“Is that so—” Orodain snarled.

Jin backstepped, snapping distance open as Iron Howl and he squeezed the trigger—once, twice, three times—then again.

BANG BANG BANG BANG!

Four shots, fired faster than Jin was used to. [Thermokinetic Engine] fed a precise kinetic surge into his trigger finger, accelerating the motion beyond human limits.

Essence-charged rounds screamed downrange, each bullet trailing a lance of blue light.

Orodain was caught off guard. One round punched clean through his shoulder, drawing a sharp grunt of pain. He hauled his sickles up just in time to deflect two more, twisting his body at the last instant as the final shot tore past where his heart had been.

“You son of—” Orodain’s snarl shattered as another burst of bullets.

BANG BANG BANG BANG!

Orodain stopped speaking and focused on dodging and trying to cover the distance between them, but Jin made sure he never let Orodain succeed. He used his shadows, his boots, to always stay a good distance away from the man.

“Stay put, you damn bastard!” Orodain said. “Did your ascension give you nothing but speed!”

“Just you wait, this will stop your sickles,” Jin said, trying to rile the cultist.

He wasn't aiming for clean kills anymore. He was just suppressing, forcing Orodain to react, to move, to burn stamina defending instead of attacking.

And while the cultist was distracted from what he had just said. Jin’s left hand—Essence Edge clenched tight—moved through a rapid sequence of gestures. He leaned fully into its two-hundred percent essence manipulation boost to activate a skill for the first time.

He felt his reserves emptying as the skill took hold and essence surged outward in response.

It split and slid into place as multiple thin barriers—translucent sheets of compressed silver-blue essence—appeared in front of Jin.

Even though there were 6 barriers stacked in front of him, all of them were uneven, rough, and looked like a cheap copy.

That was evident from the chortle of laughter that reached Jin’s ears.

There was a surge of flames as the man blurred forward—predictable. So predictable now that Jin had understood his patterns—and laughed even harder as the dark and gold sickles scythed through the first barrier like tissue paper.

The construct shattered, and Jin felt the kinetic force from that strike travel through the breaking barrier and into the Thermokinetic Engine's hungry grasp.

Yes.

The Engine drank it down, converted thermal to kinetic, and Jin felt his muscles respond with power as he pushed more essence into the essence constructs.

"This won’t hold my blades for long!" Orodain called out as he tore through the second barrier. "What will you do once they are gone—"

Jin pushed himself as he moved rapidly, essence flooding his legs via Engine boost, and came up firing from a new angle.

BANG BANG BANG!

Three more shots, each one deliberately aimed to miss by inches. The rounds screamed past Orodain's head and shoulders to impact the walls and floor around him, embedding themselves in stone and wood.

"Hehehe… Get some aiming practice in your next life!" Orodain said with mockery, “What will you do when you run out of—”

Jin saw the man’s eyes widen fractionally.

Orodain stopped and frantically looked all around him, looking at the bullets embedded all over the walls and the floor.

Each of them was faintly glowing, pulsing with unstable essence that Jin had deliberately pumped into them during firing. Not enough to detonate immediately. Just enough to sit there, waiting.

Figured it out… Good.

"You're trying to box me in,” Orodain breathed, expressions shifting to something darker. “Heh, you think that orb was the only defense-based artifact I had—”

Jin fired again, cutting him off—two more rounds, both "missing" to embed in walls flanking Orodain's position, and with a wave of Essence Edge as the embedded bullets started glowing wide, resonating with each other and surging in power.

“NO. YOU DON’T THIS TIME!” Orodain yelled as his form burst into dark-gold flames, dashing towards him like a firestorm.

The cultist's blade crashed into Jin's third barrier. The construct held for a fraction of a second—just long enough for Jin to feel the massive kinetic impact—before exploding.

More. Give me more.

Jin smiled behind his mask and summoned more layers of barriers. This time, he made them thicker, more durable, and positioned them to funnel the cultist's approach.

Orodain's second strike took out the fourth barrier. Then the fifth. Each impact sent waves of force through Jin's constructs, and each wave fed directly into the Thermokinetic Engine's conversion cycle.

Energy flooded Jin's system—not the gradual buildup from before—this was rapid, almost overwhelming, his body struggling to contain and redistribute power faster than Regulator could safely manage.

«Warning,» the Echo assigned to Engine regulation pulsed. «Absorption rate approaching 40% capacity. Muscle strain is increasing. Recommend discharge or reduction—»

No. Keep going.

Jin backpedaled, firing as he moved. Not at Orodain—the cultist was too fast and too skilled to be hit by straightforward attacks—but at the space around him. More bullets are embedded in the walls.

And more barriers, summoned as fast as Jin could form them, each one positioned to slow Orodain's advance while feeding precious kinetic energy into the Engine.

The cultist tore through them like tissue paper. But each destroyed barrier made Jin both stronger and pushed him to dangerous levels of kinetic energy coursing through him.

Orodain lunged, closing the final gap, and Jin barely managed to throw up another barrier before those sickles found his throat.

CRASH!

The construct exploded. Force hammered into Jin's chest, but the Engine grabbed that impact and converted.

50% capacity. 55%.

Jin's body burned with accumulated energy, and Jin was thankful for the muted pain, or else he would have collapsed from the sheer pain.

Jin focused on shooting and parrying with Essence Edge as Orodain attacked relentlessly.

"For the eternal dark!" Orodain yelled suddenly, and his aura surged.

That dirty, dark-gold presence exploded outward, assaulting Jin’s mind as his own aura rose to protect him.

Jin staggered backward and saw flames erupting along Orodain's body. Not just coating his weapons anymore, his entire frame became wreathed in dark-gold fire.

The temperature in the corridor spiked so rapidly that Jin felt sweat begin to pour down his body.

Orodain stomped, and the floor responded as dark-gold flames spread outward from Orodain in a rapidly expanding circle of fire that consumed everything in its path. His embedded bullets detonated early, the secondary detonations adding to the inferno.

“What now, rat!” Orodain spoke from the epicenter of flames, and within seconds, the entire corridor was ablaze.

The heat was incredible. Even from ten feet away, Jin felt it trying to cook him alive inside Reduvia's armor. His exposed skin reddened. His lungs burned with each breath of the hot air.

Jin yanked hard on his essence reserves—down to 28% now and dropping—and pulled on his original affinity.

Cold cryo essence flooded outward from his core, fighting against the flames pressing in from all sides. The temperature around him dropped sharply as frost began to form on his armor.

And he poured that cryo along with his astral essence into essence constructs.

The barrier that formed was different from before. Not translucent blue but white, shot through with crystalline patterns that looked like frost on glass. It surrounded Jin in a roughly spherical shell, and where the dark-gold flames licked against its surface, steam hissed into existence.

"A variant of Ice affinity!" Orodain's voice cut through the roar of flames, and Jin saw the cultist standing at the heart of the inferno looking completely unbothered by the heat. "But fire burns everything away!”

The flames intensified. Jin felt his cryo-infused barrier straining.

« You can't hold this for long, Jin. Your essence is bleeding too fast. »

An idea struck him.

What if I just... absorb it all?

« Do what? You chains won’t be able to—»

Not the chains… Not just the chains.

Jin dropped half his cryo barrier—the half facing away from Orodain—and let the flames rush in.

Heat crashed over him, and his skill [Thermokinetic Engine] roared to life.

The skill grabbed the heat from the flames. His internal temperature spiked—dangerously high, the narrator yelled in warnings, not understanding just what in hell Jin was doing—but all that he cared for was the Engine, which was roaring to life.

Thermal became kinetic. Heat became force.

60% capacity. 70%. 85%.

"What are you—" Orodain's voice carried genuine confusion now. "A desperate last gamble, perhaps?"

Jin didn't answer as he focused his mind to keep summoning more barriers—not to block the flames but to channel them—to create surfaces that would catch the heat.

Jin felt a sharp sting bite into his right wrist—then another, and another—he was fortunate he’d pumped the Field alchemy injector with high-tier healing potions, even if he knew he’d be paying for it later. The aftereffects would be brutal.

« I’m sure that’s for future Jin to handle. »

Regardless, layer after layer of flimsy, barely formed constructs materialized around him. The flames lashed at them, melted them, destroyed them as his essence emptied.

90% capacity. 95%.

Jin saw “Critical Warnings” flare across his vision from the regulator, but he ignored them. He couldn't stop—not yet—not when he was so close to having enough power to end this.

He formed more barriers. Summoned more cryo essence to fight the heat. Let the flames keep coming, keep burning, keep feeding the engine that was rapidly approaching its breaking point.

“That’s it?” Orodain said, sickles hissing as they burned through the barriers. “All that anger, just to die standing still?”

Orodain paid Jin’s actions no mind, convinced Jin was making his final stand. He walked forward without haste, his sickles burning through Jin’s barriers as if they were never meant to stop him.

"You're feeding the flames yourself," Orodain chuckled, each step melting another layer of essence. "Did you think I wouldn't notice?"

He was five feet away now. Close enough that Jin could see the individual droplets of sweat evaporating off the cultist's skin, close enough to see the manic gleam in those black eyes.

"Soon this will be over," Orodain said.

Yes, Jin thought through the grey haze. It will.

Silver chains erupted from Jin's chest—the Mantle of Harvest answering his call—and instead of lashing out at Orodain, they latched and coiled around Jin's own body, traveling to the arm holding Iron Howl.

Dark-gold flames poured into Jin like water down a drain. Essence and thermal energy, both being sucked away from Orodain's inferno, filtered through the Eternal Sovereign Soul, converted and refined.

His knees almost buckled. The pressure and pain he felt at that moment were so immense that they made themselves known even through his grey world.

130% capacity. 145%. 160%.

« Too much Jin. Way too much. You are going to tear yourself apart if—»

Then I’ll release it.

His hands moved. Iron Howl came up, barrel pointed at Orodain's approaching form. The cultist saw the motion, and his expression shifted to something almost pitying.

"Really? One last shot? How—" Orodain smirked.

Jin pushed.

All of it. Every accumulated drop of energy. He channeled the overwhelming force through Iron Howl's Resonance link, as his first cultivation star grabbed the flames and changed them.

Transformed them into something purer, brighter, more fundamental than mere fire.

The bullet in Iron Howl's chamber stopped glowing dark gold.

Started burning Golden-white.

Orodain's expression shifted from confidence to alarm in the space between heartbeats.

"Oh," the cultist breathed. "Oh, what are—"

"Hell," Jin said quietly, his digitized voice carrying the weight of absolute certainty, "be upon you."

And pulled the trigger.

The world became light.

The blast was silent—beyond sound, beyond comprehension—Jin watched through grey-washed vision as a sphere of golden-white beam expanded outward from his gun like a laser, consuming everything it touched with the casual indifference of a star incinerating a planet.

Orodain's aura—that powerful, elite-level presence built over decades—popped like a soap bubble. His dark-gold flames extinguished as if they'd never existed.

And his body...

The cultist's torso simply ceased to exist. The attack carved through flesh, bone, and essence all at once, leaving nothing but fine ash where his torso had been a moment before.

His legs stood for a moment—just a moment—still upright through some combination of momentum and muscle memory.

Then they toppled backward, hitting the scorched stone floor with a wet thud that sounded impossibly loud in the sudden silence.

Jin staggered. His legs felt like water, muscles trembling uncontrollably.

His focus state was slipping, and with it, the colors started bleeding back into his vision in painful bursts, emotional responses starting to filter through the cracks.

The grey world became red, and gold and black, and with sensation came pain.

Everything hurt. His side, where Orodain had carved him open. His hand where the sickle had kissed bone. His entire body from being pushed well past safe limits by more kinetic energy than any Order II should have been able to contain.

But he was standing. And Orodain wasn't.

Small victories.

Jin's mask stayed on for three more seconds before his shaking hands pulled it free. He sucked in a breath of scorched air and let himself feel—just for a moment—the weight of what he'd done.

The injector on his wrist hissed again, pumping more healing compounds into his bloodstream. Jin watched his health slowly climb from critical to merely terrible and allowed himself the ghost of a smile.

Small victories. Indeed.

The corridor was a ruin… Walls melted… Floor scorched black. The ceiling had partially collapsed, leaving twisted metal beams hanging at angles that defied physics. And in the center of it all, Orodain's legs lay in a spreading pool of blood that steamed in the residual heat.

Jin's chains retracted with a whisper of fading essence, and he finally—finally—let himself sink to his knees.

He'd done it. He'd actually done it.

One hell of a graduation, Jin thought distantly as consciousness started to slip. One hell of—

SYSTEM INITIATING...

TRIALS OF THE ETERNAL ONE: 1ST TRIAL CLEARED.

What—

Jin barely managed to get the word out as the world tilted sideways, and darkness took him.
