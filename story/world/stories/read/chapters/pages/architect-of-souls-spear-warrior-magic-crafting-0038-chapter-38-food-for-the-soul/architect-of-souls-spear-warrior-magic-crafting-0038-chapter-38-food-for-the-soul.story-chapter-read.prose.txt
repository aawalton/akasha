The next morning was a struggle. Ferris had just had two nights of limited sleep, combined with a full day of strenuous work, and another ahead of him. Refining spirit gems didn’t exercise his physical body, but still wore on his mind. If he kept neglecting sleep, he would suffer for it.

Looking at Jade, Ferris was hit with a jumble of feelings. Happy to have made progress understanding the slime’s memory. Relieved that Jade remembered him. Frightened by how much he cared. Concerned with how trusting he had immediately become.

Logically, he felt confident Jade wouldn’t kill him in his sleep. That was a big step.

The slime might forget other instructions, then eat something they shouldn’t. Ferris was pretty sure he could mitigate this risk with regularly repeated safety lessons. Even with the confusing results of timing Jade’s memories, the slime had never forgotten anything in less than seven hours. He would just refresh the lessons every four.

Ferris used the last of the soothing cream on his hand, which was feeling a lot better. As a cultivator, he should heal from minor injuries faster than before. His natural healing would only improve as he advanced, although Ferris was looking forward to the convenience of a healing ability.

Mother Lily served oatmeal with a medley of fruits for breakfast. Ferris mixed his together, the sweet fruit complementing the porridge. Jade still insisted on each type of food being fed to them separately, so they could properly rate them.

“Ferris dear, hold up,” Mother Lily called to him after breakfast, as he was about to get up.

“Yes, Mother Lily?” He was getting used to her title, but it still felt wrong… and made him miss home.

“I heard back from my husband’s old delver friend. She will be joining us in three days for dinner.”

Ferris immediately perked up. The chance to speak to someone with actual experience with a familiar bond!

“Thank you, Mother Lily!”

“Of course, dear. Now run along, I won’t keep you.”

Ferris had a new pep to his step as he carried Jade towards the Ox building in the third district for work.

He wondered what kind of monster Mother Lily’s friend had bonded. Ferris needed to prepare a list of questions he was comfortable asking beforehand, to get the most out of the opportunity. Although, answers always led to more questions!

The Ox building was the same as the previous day, and Ferris was given an identical—although different—room to work in. Adept Theodore didn’t meet Ferris that morning, and the attendant simply provided the refinement device with his first gem to refine, a cloudy red.

Ferris laid down on the cot with one hand on the refinement device, the other holding Jade. The slime’s chain was no longer tied up to the table, instead wrapped as tightly as he could make it around the core’s cage.

Ferris meditated, working his way through his thoughts. He felt he had taken a step forward in understanding Adaptation. But, if it worked by solving problems, could the Core Tree ever produce fruit not specific to a problem? Would that prevent other ways of getting targeted abilities from the tree?

The ability the tree had given, Assimilate Object, made Ferris wonder what kind of path he wanted. Clan Bluereef used the spear—and teamwork—to focus on hit-and-run attacks. Ferris had been able to use some of that to fight the two chain demons with the bone spear… but it worked so much better to use the crossbow. He liked using different weapons to take advantage of weaknesses. The spear countered the imps, the crossbow against the chain demons, and… well, he didn’t have a way to counter the frog demons, but he could find one.

The delver groups of Clan Bluereef all had to have the same speed and maneuverability to work together. If Ferris took a different path, how would he integrate back home? Could he keep up with them if he were versatile enough? If he focused on having enough tools to counter weaknesses, was that a focus at all?

Clan Bluereef didn’t focus only on the spear; they also had some kind of secretive spellcasting water ability. Ferris had been told it helped give them utility to fight underwater…

Ferris didn’t have any answers, and so he let the questions appear and disappear naturally. Soon, the meditative technique quieted his mind and brought him to his soul. Instead of immediately going to the refinement device, Ferris and Jade appeared in his soulspace.

Ferris walked up to the large pile of black rocks he had collected yesterday. According to Adept Theodore, these were the spiritual corpses of monster souls. If so, Ferris couldn’t believe they wouldn’t have some use. He grabbed two of the rocks and took Jade to the slimespace.

Ferris wanted to test what would happen if Jade ate one of the rocks. Walking up to the edge of the Digest River, he dropped one of the black rocks into the acid. It quickly dissolved away within a couple of seconds.

Tossing the other rock to Jade, he asked the slime to eat it. Jade immediately caught and swallowed it as Ferris watched above the Consume Pool. The grey birds once again began circling the acid waterfall, until a rock appeared in the middle of their circle.

As rocks tend to do, it fell down into the pool with a splash. The copy appeared exactly as it had in Ferris’ hand, not changing size at all. Several nearby ants jumped into the pool, swimming towards the black rock. Unfortunately for them, the black rock melted away before they even reached it. The ants didn’t have long to regret their choice, as they also soon died.

Ferris sat down to consider the results. He’d had a small hope the rock would be immune to the acid, but that was squashed. If Jade helped Ferris by eating the pollution while he worked, the black rocks would be lost, dissolved away.

The problem was, he wanted to collect and store the black rocks. He believed there was some way to use them, although he didn’t have one yet.

On the other hand, if he let Jade eat—and effectively destroy—the rocks, he could work faster. Jade could roll around consuming everything on the ground, leaving Ferris to knock down anything fused to the crystals.

Faster spirit gem refinement would mean either more merit, or more time to work on other projects. Weighing that compared to a rock of unknown use, the answer was obvious: have Jade help. Ferris had a pile of the black rocks to experiment with; he could reevaluate the value if he discovered something.

The only real risk came from dissolving the black rock. Would it hurt the clams? Ferris had been unwilling to take that chance when a full set of qi chunks were sitting on the riverbed, but now he’d collected it all.

It was still a risk, but one he was willing to take. Given slimes’ reputation for eating everything, it wasn’t likely to cause permanent harm. Ferris did remember Foreman Carter saying that eating metal would change the slime fertilizer, but he didn’t know if that would matter. The more likely side effect would be losing some qi production, in which case Jade could stop eating any more.

In any case, he needed to try things if he wanted to learn more. Ferris would just have to watch and adapt.

With a plan, Ferris and Jade left the slimespace and entered the refinement device’s gate. Along the way, Ferris grabbed his new bone spear, happy to have a weapon beyond the pickaxe. The red gem’s crystals presented the same as yesterday’s, emitting a sweltering heat.

Getting to work, Ferris was immediately grateful for Jade’s help. Not having to collect anything in the sack sped him up considerably, and they were able to clear out the gem in about three-quarters of the time as yesterday.

Happily, Ferris turned the clear red gem in. Unhappily, the second gem was another cloudy blue. This gem contained the same layer of water along the ground. Ferris was still unwilling to risk Jade being ambushed from the water, and he had to slog through it alone.

After turning in the clear blue, the third gem of the day was cloudy yellow. Ferris and Jade took a break for lunch before working on it.

Thinking about his experiment feeding Jade the black rock in the slimespace, there was another long-overdue test. The first night after bonding Jade, Ferris had wanted to see what the Consume Pool ability looked like from inside the slimespace while Jade was eating real food.

Jade’s memory problem had made Ferris afraid to meditate, unconscious, touching the slime while they actively ate. After last night, he was now willing to try it, although the test required some planning.

Ferris had to touch Jade to enter the slimespace. If the slime rolled around to eat food, Ferris would lose contact and be kicked out of Jade’s soul.

What he needed was to maintain contact with Jade while the slime ate. Without risking his hand.

The slime could selectively let food in on one side, while keeping the other side firm. Ferris had often fed Jade while holding them in his lap. With a chuckle, he remembered Brix holding up Jade as a shield while Cyrus tossed food at her. It was only things that were too close together or connected that gave the slime trouble.

The problem was, once Jade started eating something, they couldn’t spit it out even if they wanted to. The process of pulling the food in wasn’t under the slime’s conscious control. This made it risky to have Jade eat while he was meditating.

Lunch was rice balls wrapped in fragrant leaves, with pickled vegetables packed into the center of each one. There were a dozen rice balls, as Mother Lily had taken to sending more than two standard portions. Ferris would have been happy with two or three of them.

Taking one of the rice balls, Ferris pulled it apart into two smaller pieces. He compressed each a bit, sticking the rice together. Then he turned to Jade and lifted the food up.

“New game, Jade. Please wait to eat these,” Ferris said as he had Jade stay still. He placed both halves leaning against the slime.

“I will practice until all the food is gone,” Jade said.

Ferris chuckled. He hadn’t even explained the game yet. The slime just remembered that practice meant eating from the night before.

“Okay, now I want you to only eat one,” Ferris said, while sending the idea of Jade eating the left ball of food.

Jade bubbled a bit at the idea, but dutifully swallowed the correct rice ball. Thankfully, the ball on the right was left untouched.

“Ten out of ten!” Ferris complemented, to which Jade rippled. The slime was happy enough to bounce around a little, at which point they rolled on top of—and consumed—the other rice ball.

“Zero out of ten.” Ferris had to adjust the rating, as there was now nothing left of the other ball.

“Unfair!” Jade was devastated. “I want to try again!”

It soon became apparent that this was an easy game for Jade. Ferris escalated to more than four balls of food, which the slime ate one at a time. As expected, there was some difficulty if the food was touching or too close, but other than that Jade had good control of what part of their body allowed food to pass through.

It was interesting to see the relative ease Jade had with this game, compared to the struggle last night eating the demon’s corpse while leaving the chains behind. Those chains had been tightly wrapped, even entering the demon’s body in some places.

Ferris tested that Jade could eat just the food when his hand was laying on top of the slime, but Jade was more concerned about the test than he was. Knowing what to look for, Jade’s feelings shined clearly over the bond. The slime had an excellent sense of what they were touching, and the difference between Ferris’ hand and the rice was stark.

Finally, he placed two clumps of food against Jade’s body. He lifted his newly healed right hand, but then hesitated.

It was silly. He’d rested his other hand on the slime while meditating dozens of times by now. But still, it took him a few seconds to work up the nerve before placing his hand on Jade.

Ferris took a deep breath, then closed his eyes.

It took longer than usual, but eventually he found himself in his soulspace. Ferris quickly traveled to the slimespace and sat down at the edge of the pool.

Ferris reached out over his connection to Jade. The bond was between Ferris and Jade’s souls. He wasn’t sure if distance could stop them from talking, but meditation couldn’t. They had tested it before, when Jade’s mental body was still recovering.

“Jade,” Ferris closed his eyes and thought, thinking about the slime. Trying to feel that connection in his soul.

“Ferris! You told me you were sleeping,” Jade responded.

“Meditating, not sleeping,” Ferris sighed, “that isn’t the point. Please, eat the first piece of rice ball.”

Jade responded with a feeling of excitement, then the birds circled the waterfall as a giant version of the rice ball appeared above the pool of acid.

He watched as the massive thing appeared, then started to fall. Ferris suddenly realized what a bad idea this was. He let out a frantic cry, running away as quickly as he could.

It landed with a giant splash, spraying acid up and out to cover the area.

Some landed on him, eating quickly through his clothes. Ferris threw himself to the ground, rolling around in the dirt.

“Ferris! Are you okay?!” Apparently, Jade could feel his distress over the bond.

“Yeah,” Ferris said, painfully sitting up and knocking some of the green dirt off him, “I’m fine, I just didn’t expect such a big splash.” He tentatively checked the burns. Although painful, they wouldn’t kill or incapacitate him.

In the pool, there was pure chaos. More grey ants than Ferris could count were swimming around the huge chunk of squished rice ball. They would approach the food, tear off a chunk, and then try to swim away with it, only to dissolve in the acid… usually along with the food they were carrying.

Ferris had expected some of this. He’d often seen the giant chain and cage in the Consume Pool; it was still there, wrapped up at the bottom. The acid level had risen after a normal-sized meal, which implied anything from the real world would be larger than life.

Even still, seeing the process at this scale was alarming.

It was hard to see through the chaos, but there was some kind of burst of bubbles every time one of the ants would latch on with their mandibles.

While he had guessed the food would be large, seeing it was still humbling. Although hard to compare, it matched the scale increase of the chain: at least a thousand times bigger. No wonder a meal could make the river’s levels rise.

Ferris had observed Jade eating a lot in the real world. The food here disappeared at the same rate that the slime’s real body digested food. Usually, Jade’s meals magically disappear from the outside in. Ferris remembered the horrific view he had of the lanky chain demon’s face melting away right in front of him. Layers had been stripped away, one by one, until there was nothing left.

The library book on the nature of acid claimed that ‘magical acid’ usually had components that the mundane world would not consider ‘acid.’ Until reading that book, Ferris had assumed acid worked by pulling matter apart, much like a cube of sugar dissolving in water. Perhaps… Jade’s magical ‘acid’ had a component that did pull things apart, through the grey ants. The acid aspect in the Consume Pool caused the chemical reaction, but the ants magically enhanced and sped up the effect.

This supported his belief that the ‘copy’ that appeared above the Consume Pool shared some kind of two-way link with its original in the real world. Pulling apart the copy affected the original. When it was destroyed, something was left behind in the Digest River as food for the clams.

The book had described the physical signs of the chemical reaction of mundane acid, like releasing gas and heat. Ferris wondered if the burst of bubbles around the ant’s mouth was related, or if just submerging the item in the green liquid was sufficient.

Ferris looked around the slimespace. Outside the dome was an ocean of green acid, likely the spiritual representation of Jade’s acid aspect. The grey ants were also everywhere, not just in the Consume Pool. Maybe that was due to the ants being part of the magical nature of Jade’s acid, more than just the ability. Although, Ferris had seen the acid of the Digest River melt things like his fishing chains without the ants jumping in to help…

He just didn’t know yet.

Eventually, Ferris wanted to develop attack abilities like Acid Bolt. Perhaps, if he included the ants in some way, those abilities would be stronger. Was that why the book on familiars had thought so highly of spirit animals?

While he was musing, the ball of rice was completely dissolved in the pool, along with the army of grey ants that had jumped in.

“Next!” Jade said helpfully over the bond, followed by the grey birds yet again circling the waterfall. Ferris now suspected the birds had something to do with how Consume Pool worked. If the ants and the clams were both part of an ability, then the birds likely were as well.

Before, he’d assumed they were simply reacting, gathering in response to the Consume Pool making a copy. But what if the ability wouldn’t work at all without them? He wondered.

The problem was, the only thing he had seen them actually do was fly in circles or randomly around inside the dome.

Maybe there would be clues up in that tall tower they seem to nest in? He hadn’t been able to climb up to investigate it closer, as it didn’t have the easier slope of the two mid-sized towers.

The second ball of rice appeared inside their circle, high above Ferris. A new swarm of grey ants responded quickly after it hit the water… but not instantly. It was surprising how many of the ants appeared, swarming out of the sandy yellow pillars. How big was it down there? Did they ever run out? How quickly did they reproduce?

Ferris considered his hand. After Jade swallowed it, Foreman Carter had had enough time to notice the problem and pull Ferris’ hand out before he lost it. Yet not before it started to dissolve.

A well-known tactic against slimes was to knock out the core with a stick, similar to how the chain demon had killed Jade with the swipe of a chain.

If Ferris could make the acid faster, the stick would melt away instead of knocking out the core. Currently, the food had to fall down into the pool, and then wait for the ants to swim over. If there was no distance to fall, and the ants were already ready and waiting, would it dissolve faster?

Of course, Ferris currently had no way to shorten the fall. Even if he had a way to control the ants, they couldn’t wait inside the deadly pool of acid. Still, it was something to work on.

Did the food appear inside the circle of birds, or did the birds circle around where the food appeared? He originally thought the waterfall made the food appear, as the Consume Pool needed a flow of energy through a meridian like it to access the real world.

The ants finished tearing apart the second rice ball, and Ferris stood up to walk down the Digest River. Yesterday, he’d finally fully collected all the qi from the riverbed. Today, the clams were still closed, although he knew from before that some of them had been able to make a new qi chunk after a single day. Ideally, Ferris would feed Jade enough food over the course of the day for every clam to produce a qi chunk by the time he cultivated each night.

Foreman Carter had said that slimes would get larger as they ate, and then eventually throw up acid if they got too big. Jade had been eating three large meals a day, but hadn’t grown at all… at least not that Ferris had been able to notice. Perhaps it was just a matter of quantity, as the slimes in the pit at the slime ranch had been eating a lot more.

Ferris looked at the area surrounding the pool and river. Two giant rice balls had a small, but perceptible effect on the acid level.

What would ten rice balls—all massively enlarged—do? What about a hundred? A thousand?

The Consume Pool and Digest River had a rising bank around them to accommodate growth, but eventually they would overflow. Ferris wondered if that was the limit Foreman Carter had mentioned: when a slime would expel liquid after overeating. If so, the current level was still far from that point.

Yesterday, less than a fourth of the clams produced a new chunk of qi. The ones that had were mostly near the inlet from the Consume Pool. More food should feed more clams, he thought.

That made his next step clear. After work, he needed to find additional sources of food for Jade.

Both the slime, and Ferris’ Qi Pool, would feast.
