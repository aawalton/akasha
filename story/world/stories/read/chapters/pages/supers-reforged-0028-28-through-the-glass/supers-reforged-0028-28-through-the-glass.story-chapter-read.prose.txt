Lunch was a busy and crowded affair in the city center, especially so close to the Civic Response Center and the many offices of bureaucracy and government that had found a home near it.

“That’s our target,” Vera said as she nibbled at her toast distractedly. Nadia, on the other hand, looked completely at ease, working through her steak with meticulous care.

“Indeed he is,” Nadia said. “Eat. You need to look normal.”

We ate and kept an eye on our target. A middle-aged man, nothing out of the ordinary that I could spot at a quick glance. He grabbed a salad and sat down to eat it in a less-busy corner of the bistro, with only a thick glass pane separating us. There were flowers and lush trees painted on the glass, which gave the place a nice and cozy air. It also allowed us to keep an eye on him.

“I have to admit, I have never eaten here but this toast is not bad,” Vera said, a bit flat. She still looked tense, but Nadia’s glares were at least forcing her to try to relax. Strangely enough, she didn’t seem to stare at our target much.

I did my best not to either, following his movements in the corner of my vision and nothing more. Supposedly, the police were also keeping an eye on him.

“Really?” Nadia asked. “It’s the most popular place near the CRC, though, isn't it? Cheap but good quality, and the portions are rather generous. Or so the reviews say. Never been much of a restaurant girl until Adrian.”

“I normally eat at my desk,” our manager replied.

My girlfriend grinned. “Then that makes this our very first date all together?”

“Date?” I almost choked.

Vera raised an eyebrow.

“That’s right,” Nadia continued, nonplussed. “The police raid is just a little bonus. The main dish is this.”

I shook my head, but Vera looked amused at least, so no worries there. We kept chatting and pretending to be just another group eating like the rest of the people in the little restaurant, until slowly but surely the crowds began to thin.

When it happened, it was so quick it would have caught me unaware had I not known beforehand. Police entered through the front door, through the service door and somehow even through the bathroom door, storming the place. They moved quickly, but unhurriedly, in a way that almost made them look unassuming.

Well, if one discounted their bulletproof vests, cuffs hanging from their sides, deep-blue uniforms and the guns. Yeah. But it took a few seconds for the man to register that several men and women were converging on his position, which he spent with his mouth hanging half-open, salad almost spilling out, eyes blinking.

“Freeze!”

The room was dead quiet, and the single yell almost reverberated. A few of the police officers were keeping everyone calm, ordering the patrons to stay seated.

Then the target realized the show was for him specifically, and he sat up quickly enough to launch his chair backwards.

“Hands where I can see them!” ordered the officer at the head.

His hands shot up, but they were glowing. A burst of power exploded out from them, red-white lightning arcing everywhere and grounding itself on the nearby tables, the walls, the ceiling. It hit nobody, though, and after the crack that followed, the silence was deafening. My ears rang, but I was still not moving beyond just taking position on my feet, table and chair cleared to allow me a quick path to the man.

He powered up again, but before he could fire, one of the officers tasered him. He convulsed and dropped to the ground, foaming at the mouth, noises escaping his throat. The power died before it could blast out again, and I relaxed a fraction.

“Well, that was under—”

Then the wrenching noise from the ceiling, where one of his strange lightning arcs had hit a metal ventilation panel. It swung, then came loose right on top of a woman staring up with frozen horror. The rest of the world froze at the same time, and I ran.

Around the divider was the long way, through the whole frozen lunch crowd, too far to make in time. Through the glass was the only path. And seeing that woman, with her two kids eating near her? One was a girl, must have been nine or ten, staring at her mom with her little kid’s burger in front of her. The other was a boy, older, face scrunched up in that pretend-I-do-not-care teenager behavior when presented with a strange situation and wanting to look cool. The mask was cracking in real time, I could see it frozen in his face, as he realized this was not cool, this was dangerous, and his mother was about to get speared by the sharp edge of the ventilation panel and he could never save her in time.

I saw all this, and perhaps I should blame my lack of experience too, yeah. But this was experience, this was supposed to be an easy job that would make me gain the field experience I needed. And it was almost going to be that. Standing by, ready to act, slowly build the nerve.

Except this. So I put my arms up over my face and ran straight into the glass, which was spiderwebbed with cracks from the lightning striking it, and perhaps that saved me from a world of pain. Not all the pain, though. I thought, what harm can already broken glass do, even if it’s still standing?

It was more solid than expected, for once. Sharp, once the pieces started to drag against me, their arrested, frozen momentum thawing at my passage and gravity reasserting itself on them. They scraped along my winter coat and clawed at my bare hands, but I was through, leaving a cone of glass shards behind me that stopped right where they were, a human-shaped hole in the middle of them.

I looked up, feeling the seconds trickle by. I moved the woman, out from under the panel and a couple of tables over. Her kids were already clear of its path, frozen witnesses at their table.

I was barely done by the time the freeze ended. A boom, the panel spearing down right where she had been sitting, the rest of the cracked sheet finally letting go, broken glass everywhere, and nobody got hurt.

The woman mumbled something, looking much worse for wear and rather disheveled. Moving her had not unfrozen her, which I knew already from all my frozen-time… fun, with Nadia. The sudden displacement made her dizzy, and her son was staring at me, wide-eyed. I gave him a thumbs up, awkward, but not as awkward as he was.

“Are you alright?” I asked her, still being stared at by her children.

“What?” she mumbled, but her eyes were on the metal grate spearing through the chair she was sitting on a moment before. “I, yes? I think? Are you a superhero?”

I nodded, feeling the whole room stare at me even as the police arrested the man they had come to grab. They were not looking at them, they were looking at me, and I felt suddenly very self-conscious. I tried to think about that thing they told me to say, about the CRC and rosters and something? But it didn't come to mind.

“Did you see that?” It was her son that came to the rescue. “Mom, he just… moved you out of the way like it was nothing! Thank you, mister. I’m going to awaken too, and then I’ll also be a hero!”

I blinked. “You better,” I told him, feeling control return to me. In my mind, was a loop: nobody can do anything to me in frozen time. I was never in danger. This was an easy operation. Why is my heart beating like this?

And I hated it, so I played it cool. “I’ll be watching you, boy. You better not turn villainous or I’ll be on you before you know it.” I snapped my fingers. “Just like that.”

And then the noise washed over us, I said the last few things to the spooked family, played it cool with the boy, and was back with my girls. As the adrenaline rush slowly faded, it was replaced by warmth and… pride.

“My Adrian! So cool and brave!” Nadia said. I had expected some banter or comment about me playing it cool with the boy, but none came. Instead, she saw my bloody hands, which had endured the worst of the cracked glass, and gasped. “You hurt yourself!”

Vera was already producing some disinfectant and gauze from a first aid kit she had hidden in her purse.

“I guess I’m not as invulnerable as I thought,” I said, chuckling.

“Adrian,” Nadia said. “I am serious. You hurt yourself. Come here.”

She kissed my hands, covertly, while Vera was busy with the kit. Just a kiss. No tingle, no light. I thought she was going to heal me for a moment, but the cuts kept stinging. She held my fingers close to her and suckled on them with a giggle.

“A kiss makes it all better, doesn’t it? You reckless handsome infuriating beautiful hero?”

I smiled. I was still in pain, pulsing upward into my awareness as the adrenaline receded. Nadia could not heal me here, could she? Not with Vera right there.

“Of course,” I said.

Vera rubbed me with disinfectant. I winced, and then we were back in the car on the way home. That’s when Nadia couldn’t resist anymore and pulled me to the back of the garden, out of Vera’s sight, and closed the cuts on my hands. All of them were shallow, nicks from the glass, but the throb stopped all at once, and it felt like a full heal.

“Oof,” I said, sagging a bit. “It was starting to be annoying.”

Nadia shook her head, dragging me back towards the house. My hands were still hidden by the gauze, so Vera would not suspect anything. She tossed her Mind Privacy gear on the small table by the door, then removed mine. (I almost forgot she had to do it because my hands were hurt and blew my cover right there and then).

“So,” she said, glaring at me from the sofa. “What did we learn today?”

Vera was trying to light the fire, but the store-bought logs did not seem to agree with her.

“That glass is sharp even in time-stop?” I attempted.

“Smart-ass. If glass can still cut you, then other things can hurt too. You need to be more careful.”

“And I will be,” I said, cupping her face. “You are so cute when you fuss over me so much.”

“I…” she whined, grabbing my hands. “That’s unfair, Adrian.” Then she dropped them, making me see that Vera was looking at us. “Oh. Nero’s here,” she said, nodding toward the fireplace. Nice save!

The cat had materialized on the hearth rug at some point during the lecture, as cats do, and was supervising Vera’s losing battle with the store logs like someone who had never paid a bill in his life.

“I… apologize,” Vera said, flustered. “I thought lighting some store logs would be easier.”

“There’s a trick to them,” Nadia said, crouching by the hearth. “I’d let Adrian show you, but his hands are wrapped. Watch and learn.”

Then Vera saw the cat. “Ohmygosh, he’s so cute.” She straightened a second too late and cleared her throat. “He seems… very agreeable,” she amended, the professional mask sliding back on. The tips of her ears had gone pink.

Her slip earned a “mrow” of disapproval. Then Nero cracked an eye open, looked at her, stretched, and curled back to sleep, except this time he was facing us.

“Can I… can I get closer?”

“Yeah, go ahead,” I said.

She approached, gingerly, carefully, and sat on the rug a good meter away from the cat, as if scared her presence would make him run away. I chuckled and sat next to her, much closer to the furball, and began to pet him.

“See? He cares more about sleeping than running away. Honestly, though, it means he trusts you.”

I thought back to when Nadia first came to my house, and how Nero had hissed at her and avoided her. She still had the drain, back then, and he had wanted nothing to do with her. Even after I took it away, he stayed a wary stranger for a good while, until he chose her. He still kept a sliver of that wariness, sometimes, because he was a cat and he remembered when she was scary. It had taken him a while to warm up to her, and yet here he was, immediately feeling safe enough with Vera to just sleep in front of her without a care in the world. Or pretend to, because I was fairly certain he was awake and only pretending to sleep so we’d pet him. Silly, I’d pet him even MORE if he actually went looking for pets, but that’s cats for you.

“It’s your turn now,” I told Vera. She straightened up, looked at me, then at the cat.

“To do what?”

“To pet him?” I said with a chuckle.

“I don't know how?”

“Because you never had a cat, right,” I deadpanned. “Come on, it’s not rocket science. Do as I do.”

It was quite funny. I’d pet the cat, then stop and wait for Vera to mirror me. Then she’d stop, unsure what to do. So I’d pet Nero again and she mirrored me again, but only once. We played this little game a few times, but we weren’t perfectly in synch and Vera’s fingers brushed against the back of my hand.

“I’m so sorry,” she said, hand snapping away.

“For what?” I asked.

“Your bandages,” she said.

“Oh, it’s nothing. You can touch me all you want.”

She went still for a moment, and I realized what I’d said. “I can, can’t I? What would Nadia think if she heard you, I wonder?”

“She heard,” Nadia said from the hearth, where the fire had finally caught. “And she thinks you walked into that one all on your own.”

“Uh,” I stammered. “I just meant…”

Vera’s giggle was like music. “I like teasing you, I think! You are fun! And your cat is super soft. You really think he trusts me?”

“I do, yeah,” I said, happy to change topic. I saw her eyes linger on my hands, though, and wondered if she could tell that I was not feeling pain or if she was thinking about my unintentional flirt and subsequent tease.

To think she’d tease me!

I stopped that thought. That stuff is for when you are in a relationship, not stuff to do with your boss over an accidental touch. But man, did I want to show her I was not someone she could fluster without retribution. But alas. Well. A man can dream.
