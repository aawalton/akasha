Blurb:

The system arrived without warning. It did not bring heroes; it brought survivors. And the Tapestry does not care what you were before it swallowed Earth. It only cares how hard you can swing.

Nathan MacGill was just an ordinary man before the power grids died and what remained of humanity was dragged into a brutal, system-governed nightmare. Now, stranded in the crumbling, monster-infested sanctuary of Tarnstead, he is forced to adapt or be eaten. He doesn't want glory. He doesn't care about conquering dungeons, playing politics, or hoarding System Points. He has one singular, burning goal: to level up and carve a bloody path through the wilderness to find his missing sister.

Armed with a heavy iron hand-axe, a scythe-edged short sword, and the lethal Skirmisher class, Nate dives headfirst into the meat grinder of the Weeping Woods. Refusing to cower behind a heavy shield, he fights with high-speed, surgical violence, turning the momentum of terrifying apex predators into their own brutal executions.

But surviving the mutated horrors of the red zones is only half the battle. Inside the sanctuary walls, terrified survivors are splintering into vicious, rival Guilds. As resources dwindle and cutthroat factions threaten to tear the fragile town apart from the inside, Nate realizes that a lone wolf will not survive the escalating threats of the Tapestry.

To beat the system and reunite his family, he must navigate the treacherous waters of human greed, forge unbreakable alliances in the mud, and push his body past the absolute limits of human endurance.

The Tapestry demands blood to fuel its economy. Nate is ready to pay the toll.

And here is chapter one!

Nathan MacGill was a giant of a man trying to do a very delicate job.

He knelt on the hardwood floor of his living room. His broad shoulders hunched forward. His thick, dark beard brushed his collarbone as he focused on the glass box in front of him. In his massive, calloused hands, he held a pair of thin steel aquascaping tweezers. With agonising care, he planted a tiny stem of Rotala into the dark substrate of his 100-litre aquarium.

He had been staring at the tank for three hours. This was his routine to decompress from a hard days work. His mind naturally fixated on problems until they consumed him. The breakup six months ago had left a void in the flat, and his brain had tried to fill it with endless spirals of what-ifs. The aquarium was his anchor. It was a closed ecosystem. He could control the water chemistry, the light cycle, and the nitrogen levels. If something went wrong in the tank, he could physically fix it.

He gently released the tweezers. The plant stayed rooted. Nate let out a long breath and wiped his forehead with the back of his wrist.

The filter hummed a steady, reassuring rhythm. The LED light cast a crisp white glow over the vibrant green plants and the schooling tetras darting through the water. It was peaceful.

Then, the world as it had been known ended in an instant.

One moment, the planet buzzed with the familiar hum of electricity. Lights glowed warmly in homes and streets. Engines roared steadily on motorways. Screens flickered with endless streams of information. The heartbeat of modern life pulsed with certainty. It was a rhythm so constant it was easy to forget it was fragile.

Then, without warning, it all stopped.

The filter in Nate's tank died. The LED light snapped off. The flat was plunged into darkness.

Nate froze. His first thought was a blown fuse. He stood up. His massive frame blocked the faint moonlight spilling through the living room window. He reached into his pocket and pulled out his phone to use the torch. The screen was black. He pressed the power button. Nothing happened.

A cold spike of unease hit his stomach. His sister, Chloe, lived two hundred miles away. His parents were even further north. He tapped the phone screen harder. It was a lifeless brick of glass and metal.

Outside the window, the steady drone of London traffic ceased. It was replaced by an unnatural silence that felt heavier than any sound.

Every wire, every circuit, every battery that once channelled the lifeblood of civilisation turned to lifeless metal and plastic. The glowing screens across the globe blinked out simultaneously, plunging billions into sudden darkness. Streetlights flickered and died.

Nate stepped to the window and looked down. The street below was pitch black. The glow of the city skyline, a constant fixture in his periphery, was simply gone.

But something else surged in to take its place.

An unseen force rippled through reality. It was silent yet suffocating. It felt like the weight of a thousand storms pressing down on the world’s spine. The very air grew thick and electric. This was not the hum of technology. It was a strange, new energy that defied explanation. Nate took a breath and coughed. It filled his lungs with a taste like smoke and ash, yet sharper and more alive. It crawled beneath his skin and bone. It awakened something primal within him.

Everywhere in the world, time seemed to freeze. The chaos of existence paused in a heartbeat’s breath. Humanity felt a warmth unlike any before. It was deep and raw, like sunlight filtering through dark clouds. It spread beneath the skin and settled in the marrow.

And then, the destruction began.

The skies burned first.

Nate looked up. High above the dark city, a commercial airliner dropped out of the clouds. It had no lights. There was no sound of engines. It was just a massive silhouette of metal plummeting earthward like a wounded bird. Thousands of aircraft, stripped of their power, were falling. Pilots screamed into radios that no longer transmitted. Some lucky enough to crash in empty fields met death with a strange mercy. Others slammed into dense cities. Their fiery impacts transformed steel and concrete into fountains of flame and ruin.

A blinding orange flash erupted on the horizon. The ground trembled beneath Nate's boots. A shockwave rattled the glass of his window. The sky turned a hellish orange as fires exploded across airports and city centres, illuminating the chaos below.

Nate did not stand there analysing the situation. Instead he felt a singular, hyper-focused directive. He needed to move. He needed to get to his family somehow.

He turned away from the window. The darkness of the flat was absolute, but he knew the layout by heart. He moved with surprising speed for a man of his size. He grabbed his heavy leather boots by the door and shoved his feet into them. He pulled his thick winter coat from the hook. He found his rucksack in the hallway cupboard. He did not know what was happening, but he knew he was not staying in a dark tower block.

He shoved his hands into the kitchen drawers. He found a large torch. He flicked the switch. Nothing. The batteries were dead. Or the concept of batteries was dead. He tossed it aside. He grabbed a heavy kitchen knife and shoved it into the side pocket of his bag. He took three large bottles of water from the counter and stuffed them inside.

He swung the rucksack onto his broad shoulders. He opened his front door and stepped out into the corridor.

The emergency lights were off. The fire alarms were silent. He heard the sounds of doors opening. Panicked voices echoed in the stairwell.

"Hello?" a voice called out. It was Mrs Higgins from down the hall. "Is the power out for everyone?"

"Stay in your flat," Nate called back. His voice was a deep, booming rumble in the confined space. "Keep your doors locked."

He did not wait for a reply. He reached the stairwell and began to descend. He lived on the eighth floor. As he took the steps two at a time, he thought of the people trapped. Elevators lurched violently across the city, freezing mid-descent and trapping terrified passengers in claustrophobic steel cages. Their breaths would be shallow and panicked in the stale air. Underground tunnels, usually bustling arteries of the city, now echoed with the screeching of derailed trains and the screams of those crushed beneath twisted metal, swallowed by blackness thicker than the night.

He burst through the ground floor fire exit and out onto the street.

The cold night air hit him, thick with the smell of burning rubber and petrol. Society had collapsed in an instant.

Traffic lights had failed. The intersection at the end of his road was an anarchic battleground. Cars had collided without warning. Their precise computer-guided systems were rendered completely useless. Horns blared in a deafening chorus, but there was no order. There was only a cacophony of chaos and despair.

Nate ran towards the intersection. A massive lorry had jackknifed across four lanes. Several smaller cars were crushed against its side. At high speeds, metal and glass had turned into weapons. Pileups snaked for miles down the arterial roads. Twisted wreckage mingled with broken bodies. Drivers trapped by jammed seatbelts screamed for help that would never come.

"Help!" a voice shrieked from a crushed saloon car nearby.

Nate did not hesitate. He sprinted to the vehicle. The front end was crumpled against a concrete lamppost. Steam hissed from the ruptured radiator. A man was trapped in the driver's seat. The door was buckled and jammed shut.

"Hold on," Nate grunted.

He braced his heavy boots against the side of the car. He gripped the twisted metal of the window frame with both hands. He closed his eyes and pulled. His massive shoulders bunched. The muscles in his back strained against his coat. He let out a roar of effort. The metal shrieked in protest. The hinges snapped. Nate ripped the door entirely off its frame and threw it onto the pavement.

He reached in and unclipped the man's seatbelt. He hauled the driver out by his collar, dragging him clear just as a secondary fire ignited under the bonnet.

"My wife," the man coughed, pointing a trembling hand back at the wreckage.

Nate turned back. He took one step towards the burning car.

Then, the world stopped again.

It was not just the power this time. It was reality itself. The flames licking at the car engine froze in mid-air. They became rigid sculptures of bright orange and yellow. The plumes of black smoke hung motionless in the sky. The screaming driver beside him went completely silent, frozen with his mouth open and his hand outstretched.

Nate tried to move. His heavy boots were glued to the pavement. His arms would not respond. His heart pounded in his chest, but the rest of the universe had been paused.

He felt that strange, electric energy surge again. It pulsed through his veins. It was the same energy that had crippled the nuclear power plants, turning them into ticking time bombs. It was the energy that had silenced the financial districts, erasing trillions of data entries and leaving servers as useless tombs. It was the force that had transformed hospitals into mausoleums, stopping life-saving machines and leaving surgeons frozen in pitch-black rooms.

Nate could only move his eyes. He looked up.

The unnatural darkness of the sky shattered. A brilliant, blinding blue light fractured the heavens. It looked like glass cracking. The fractures spread across the sky in a massive, glowing grid.

A sound chimed in his head. It was not a noise heard through his ears. It vibrated directly against his skull. It was a crisp, clear chime, like crystal striking crystal.

A translucent blue rectangle appeared in his direct line of sight. It hovered in the air, casting a faint glow over the frozen street. Text began to scroll across the surface in sharp, white letters.

[INITIALISING SYSTEM INTEGRATION] [SCANNING PLANETARY ANOMALY: EARTH] [MANA DENSITY: CRITICAL] [POPULATION CULLING ONGOING. SURVIVAL RATE ESTIMATED: 50%]

Nate stared at the floating words. His breathing hitched. Half the population. His mind instantly flashed to his sister. He pictured her smiling face. He pictured his parents in their garden. He tried to roar, to break free of the invisible chains holding him, but his body remained utterly paralysed. He could only watch as the text continued to update.

[CALCULATING INDIVIDUAL POTENTIAL] [SUBJECT RECOGNISED: NATHAN MACGILL] [COMMENCING TUTORIAL PROTOCOL]

The world around him dissolved. The frozen street, the burning cars, the dark city skyline. It all turned to white dust and blew away into a void. Nate felt a sickening sensation of falling. He plummeted through endless blue light. The air was ripped from his lungs.

When he slammed into the ground, he was no longer standing on a London street.

He gasped for air and pushed himself up onto his hands and knees. The surface beneath him was smooth, polished white stone. He was in a massive, circular room. The walls were made of the same glowing blue light he had seen in the sky. There were no doors. There were no windows. It was utterly sterile. It was a blank canvas of a room.

He stood up slowly. His massive frame felt heavier here. His knees ached. He patted his coat. He still had his rucksack. He still had his boots. But he was entirely alone.

Another chime echoed in the empty space.

A new blue screen materialized directly in front of his face. It was larger this time.

[WELCOME TO THE TAPESTRY] [YOUR PREVIOUS WORLD HAS REACHED THE END OF ITS CYCLE. IT HAS BEEN INTEGRATED INTO THE TAPESTRY.] [TO SURVIVE, YOU MUST GROW. TO GROW, YOU MUST CONSUME.] [PLEASE SELECT YOUR STARTING CLASS TO CONTINUE.]

Nate clenched his massive fists. He wanted to hit something. He wanted to break the screen and demand answers. But the pragmatic, problem-solving part of his brain, the part that carefully maintained complex ecosystems to keep fragile things alive, told him to stop. Getting angry would not fix this. Panic would not bring the power back.

He was in a new world. The rules had changed. If he wanted to see his family again, he had to learn these new rules faster than anyone else.

He took a deep breath. He forced his muscles to relax. He reached out a thick, calloused finger and tapped the blue screen.

"Right," Nate muttered. The sound of his own voice was small in the vast, empty room. "Let's see what we have to work with."
