In the darkness, entombed within a living tree guzzling more mana in a single second than I could house in the entirety of my core, I gritted my teeth and flexed a fist. This was going to hurt.

The first punch did indeed hurt, but it wasn’t until the eighth that I broke a knuckle. Each strike was empowered by Shatter Point, and the results were both impressive and underwhelming, contradictory as it sounded. Bark splintered and cracked beneath my fists, and given its unnatural solidity, I suspected I’d be able to cave in armour with my punches now. But there was no dramatic change, no stylistic upgrade. Just further force projection into the target.

Despite the power of my upgraded skill, I couldn’t carve my way through who knew how much magically empowered living wood without a weapon. I changed tack, calling out to Jacyntha, who I could still hear calling to me from outside.

Clearly, the obvious bulge protruding from the trunk wasn’t visible from the outside any longer. I winced as I gave my fist a critical look. It was battered and bruised, blood marring the knuckles, though it was currently just pain—I hadn’t lost my grip strength or any functionality. I knew I would if I kept it up though, and there wouldn’t be much point in escaping this tree just to be unable to wield my weapon at the end of it.

Then I smacked myself in the head. I wasn’t thinking clearly about any of this. Shatter Point didn’t need an actual punch to impart force, just physical contact. I could do it with a flick if I wanted! And more than that, as I caught a glimpse of the back of my hand, I noticed the burn scar from my artifact bond with Resolution.

Cursing myself for a fool, I shunted mana down that link and watched the burn flare with cool blue light. A few moments later, I heard a thud as my spear slammed into the tree roughly level with my hand. It was stifled by at least several feet of bark, but it would hopefully direct Jacyntha far better than my muffled voice would.

I continued slamming my palm into the wall of wood, making slow and steady progress, before I was interrupted by a loud thud. Then followed a crack, sharp and reminding me of nothing so much as ice falling away from the peaks of the Dragonspines. A third impact soon followed with a crunch, and then glorious light was flooding into the chamber through a narrow gap.

Jacyntha’s axe receded, replaced by her grinning face as she caught sight of me. “Thank the gods!” she exclaimed. “Stand back.”

I’d barely had a chance to shuffle backwards before her axe came thudding into the wall, obliterating wood and tearing a rent from the tree with ease. I caught an emerald glow sheathing the weapon and lighting up the ritual scars wending their way down from Jacyntha’s shoulders and across her chiselled arms. The axe disappeared and she reached forwards to grip the edges of the great rent she had created in the tree.

I had never seen such a beautiful sight, as the barbarian woman ripped open an entrance—or more accurately an exit. Great shoulders bunching and arms straining, her long hair matted to her forehead with the exertion of the act, she was a sight for sore eyes indeed.

I grinned in joy at seeing my rescue and took a step towards her as she spoke.

“Hurry!” she shouted, beckoning me over. “The castle is safe, but Vera is still out there!”

It was all the encouragement I needed, and after a quick check on my core, I dashed over. She had a single moment to look confused, and then I barrelled into her, arms wrapping around her thick waist, and then we were airborne.

My mouth was already stretched into a grin, but the wind stretched my cheeks even further as we fell. I laughed at the ridiculousness of what I was doing; falling, my arms wrapped around the waist of a furiously bellowing barbarian woman I had just tackled, who continued to scream in panic as we fell to the ground at least fifty feet below while an army advanced on the castle that even now was slowly being ripped from the earth by the titanic tree we fell from.

The sheer insanity of it somehow made it easier to accept, and I waited almost patiently as we tumbled through the air, until, at the last moment, I shunted mana from my almost empty core into Break-Step.

I hit the cobbles with a groan, my momentum mostly stolen by the skill but the heavy form of Jacyntha collapsing over my back and driving the air from my lungs. We coughed and rolled to our feet, and I turned to watch with awe as Jorge’s skill, supported by whatever energy remained in the World Tree’s seed I had given up, continued to climb towards the sky.

The trunk was now almost as wide as the castle itself at its base, and it must have topped out at over three hundred feet tall, spreading its great canopy far and wide to dominate the field. The castle itself had been uprooted entirely, and the inner courtyard we stood upon was currently suspended off the ground, adding an additional layer of protection to the castle. The keep was wrapped entirely within the ligneous embrace of the tree which wore the outer walls like a bizarre dress around its trunk.

I panted, feeling wrung out on a spiritual level, and my hands and fists abused and raw. Nathlan and Sadrianna were stirring, looking around in confusion as the golden light withered beneath their skin marking whatever transformation the World Tree had bestowed upon them now complete. Both felt different to my mana senses in ways that I couldn’t quite comprehend, though their power was undeniable.

Jorge was unsteady on his feet, his face sallow and waxy and the healthy stockiness he had always boasted now missing. His armour hung limply, and his frame looked feeble in a way more than physical, like all the vibrancy and power he had once cultivated was now long gone.

Jacyntha stood beside me though, and she looked the opposite. She’d always been tall and powerful, but each nervous fidget now held a new potential. I was the same; other than my injuries, I felt better than ever physically. I was stronger, more enduring, more agile… a whole host of benefits, subtle and extreme, and I had spurned most of the seed’s power. I couldn’t begin to imagine what the full treatment would have done.

Jacyntha had presumably taken it all in, root and branch, and she buzzed with the euphoria of it, pacing and bouncing on the balls of her feet, great axe held tightly in hands that clearly craved action.

“What’s happening out there?” she asked eagerly. “I wake up to find a huge tree, the sky on fire and Jorge wailing in angst…” She trailed off, leaping over to the wall and climbing it in a few easy movements before staring out at the fields below.

I ran over, stopping just long enough to grab my shield from where I had dropped it during my trance-like state enthralled by the World Tree. I strapped it on and followed her with only a few winces and explosive breaths to alleviate the pain it caused my injured fist.

“They’re leaving!” Jacyntha pronounced with joy.

I followed her pointing finger and saw two of the companies of soldiers wheeling away, turning towards the forest and hot-stepping it back to where they had come from. The remaining cavalry were still milling around the base of the titanic tree but considering that the castle was now a score of feet off the ground, they didn’t seem quite as keen to attack it as before.

The commander was down, identifiable in the unearthly golden glow of the false dawn above by his larger horse, fancier armour, and the clear deference with which several of his men were trying to pull him from beneath the body of his horse where it kicked and whinnied frantically on the ground, a massive chunk of masonry piecing its chest. He had the haft of a great arrow through his own breast, so I suspected he had died before the fatal charge into a conjured mountain range or impossibly vast tree.

Half the horse-born mercenaries seemed determined to cut and run, and the other half were trying to convince the cowards to join them in dismounting and climbing the tree itself to get into the castle. That argument was made significantly less attractive by the arrows, skills and general death raining down from above by the rebels still manning what remained of the walls of the former Castle Ryonic.

All except one, that is. I watched a black streak leap from the wall and land on the back of one mercenary who had evidently decided to call it quits and was busy cantering away. Fandar—the gaunt figure unmistakable and his pale face flashing in the gloom as his hood was thrown back by the wind of his passage—landed on the back of a horse, his hand shooting out to punch the soldier in front of him in the ribs three times, before the mercenary toppled off the horse. Based on the way the mercenary gasped and writhed on the ground afterwards, I suspected Fandar had used more than his fist in that attack.

He snapped the reigns and rode hard towards the somewhat confused ranks of men and women still remaining, their previously neat companies now in disarray as commanders shouted and argued. Vera had tried to tell me the plan earlier but I’d been too enraptured by the rising golden glow of the World Tree’s stirring, so had missed the explanation.

It was pretty clear from context now though that the plan had essentially been for the whole fucking world to change and for that to spook the invaders enough for them to call off the attack. Had this been a singular army with a singular goal, the commander may have been able to push on with an assault regardless of the chaos, but with their competing priorities and interests, the Sunset Court split apart immediately under the pressure.

Each leader must have been panicking at the likely instability this event would cause within their own polities, and most seemed to be of the mind that their presence, and that of their soldiers, was needed more acutely at home.

Only a single company chose to fight on, staying grimly in formation as their comrades fled past them. Dressed in white surcoats over simple leather armour, they wielded an array of weapons as varied as the lands of Tsanderos itself, but all held position resolutely.

Fandar was currently riding hard towards them, jumping to his feet to balance on the saddle, bow drawn and loosing arrow after conjured arrow even as the horse below galloped in a panic towards the ranks of soldiers.

I soon saw why he rode so hard, as behind the ranks of soldiers arrayed in a loose horseshoe, a battle between two fighters continued unabated. Vera fought against a mage of some sort in a bright white robe—unnaturally clean in the muddy field—with long, white-gloved hands waving hypnotically in the air like a pair of snakes above her head while she evaded my friend’s attacks with ease. Vera was still on her feet with blade in hand, but there were no dancing flames surrounding her.

I grew cold as I realised what was happening. I saw a few bodies strewn about in clumps, looking burned and ravaged by steel and fire both; evidence of Vera’s efforts to slow the approaching army. Now though, the remaining company—some hundred or so soldiers, at a glance—had her surrounded, edging forwards with spears and polearms bared as they tightened on her in a noose of bristling steel.

Even as I watched, she pulled her head back and screamed at the sky, blood-red fire spurting to life in front the soldiers closest to her. They reared back in response, but then the woman in white wriggled her hands and the fire winked out abruptly.

That they were willing to close in on Vera—the Butcher of Sternsbridge and a powerful 3rd tier berserker—could mean only one thing; the white-robed mage had her locked down tightly. Her strangely writhing arms seemed to pluck at invisible strings, snaring and binding Vera as she tried in vain to cut through the magic holding her back and advance on the mage.

And Fandar was riding in to help. The walls didn’t need much defending anymore, and sitting on the sidelines while someone else fought his battles clearly wasn’t enough for him. I cursed his stupidity even as I cheered his courage. The rebels would need a leader to protect the country from the coming storm if Vera fell, so him riding off to his likely death was a terrible move from a strategic viewpoint.

But I couldn’t fault him for it, and Jacyntha seemed to agree, as she turned to me and grabbed me tightly in her strong arms, grinning manically down at me.

“Come on, little Lamb. I need your cushioning.”
