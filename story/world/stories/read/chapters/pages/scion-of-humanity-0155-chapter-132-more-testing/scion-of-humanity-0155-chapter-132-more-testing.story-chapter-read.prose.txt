Blake rounded on his intelligence advisor.

“So, what’s up with Indigo?”

Corwin nodded. “Good, you noticed.”

“You mean, noticed that he’s an idiot, brown-noser? Hard not to. Why’d you recommend him? I thought you were a better judge of character than that.”

He nodded slowly. “I used to think so as well. At the time, he had a lot of support behind him, and I believed his steadfast backing of you was a front to build his platform. If so, it was a shrewd move, something that would serve you well.”

“So, you liked that he was a power hungry, brown-noser?”

Corwin sighed. “No, I believed it was a temporary strategy. I thought you could use someone else on your council who knew how to play the game. Nothing against your mother or Jessica, they’re smart women, but they don’t have experience in politics.”

“Chancellor Tessay does,” Blake reminded him.

“Yes, but he is insular. I’m actually worried that he may have plans to break off and form his own faction with his tribe.”

Blake snorted. “Good luck with that. They just got bunkhouses there. He and anyone else that followed him to the new Apache faction would have to leave the whole town behind and start over from scratch.”

“You wouldn’t prevent him from leaving?” Corwin raised an eyebrow.

“I’m not going to press-gang people into joining or staying in my faction. It’s not only morally wrong, but it’d bite me in the ass later as well.”

“Even if he claims the Eagar faction town for himself?” Corwin pressed.

“Now that’s a completely different story. Those are Terran Alliance buildings. If he tries to take them, I’ll make a special trip to perform a very public execution.”

“Good to hear,” Corwin nodded. “Now, back to Indigo. I have a suspicion that he is a spy for President Warner.”

Blake’s brows rose. “Really? I thought he was just incompetent. You think it’s an act?”

“I suspect so.”

He sighed. “How much of a suspicion are we talking about?”

“Not enough to be actionable, but more than enough to investigate.”

“How do you investigate something like that? All his communications would be private through holo-chat.”

“That’s why I’d like to request one hundred mega-nano in funds. I believe a trip to Prescott is of immediate importance.”

Blake’s forehead wrinkled in confusion before he finally put two and two together. “Ah, so you talked with Jennifer about her first ability. I assume she refused to work for you?” he smirked.

Corwin grunted.

“If you’re going personally, that means you want to learn ‘Read Thoughts’ yourself.”

“That’s correct.”

“And, why can’t that come out of your budget?”

Corwin lifted a finger. “One, using department funds for personal power would be embezzlement.” He then raised a second. “Two, those funds are reserved for information gathering initiatives.”

“You mean bribes,” Blake snorted. “Well, if I approve of you siphoning them for yourself, it isn’t embezzlement. So, go ahead, just make your future bribes smaller. There’s plenty of greedy people out there.”

“That’s too much of a delay. It could take over two weeks to build up one hundred million nano in that fashion. We need to know as soon as possible if we have a spy on the council.”

Blake scratched his nose. “Well, I agree with you there.”

He was suddenly glad he was vague with the Raid Boss details in the meeting.

I’d be pissed if Warner showed up with an army, ready to steal my First Kill feat.

“Fine, I’ll tell my mom to transfer you the nano. While you're in Prescott, can you check up on our military spies? Your daughter already read them, but I figure you’d be better at interrogation.”

“Indeed,” Corwin agreed. “However, for that, I will require additional nano. I’m not certain if my Magic Power is high enough to overcome their resistance.”

Blake sighed. “Fine, I guess we’ll slip you another one-twenty.”

“You won’t regret your decision. Ferreting out all of Warner’s spies is of the utmost importance.”

“I hope not, but I don’t think Jeremy and Jimmy work for Warner. Jennifer said they reported to some guy named Scott. Although, Scott could be a middle-man. Just, whatever you do, make sure you don’t let them know you can read their thoughts when you interrogate them.”

“Do you take me for an amateur?” Corwin asked, offended.

Blake shook his head. “Sorry, just had to be sure.”

“So now that there’s not a potential spy listening in, do you mind filling me in about the Flagstaff situation?”

Blake briefly considered keeping information about the Raid Boss attack to himself, but quickly thought better of it. There was no point to an intelligence advisor without intelligence. Over the next few minutes, he told Corwin everything he knew about the upcoming situation.

“You don’t have a more exact date?” he pressed.

Blake shook his head. “No. When it appeared, I was still hiding with my family. I didn’t even know about it until much, much later when I eventually moved to Phoenix. I heard about it over post-scenario beers, years later.”

“So the information is suspect.”

“Yep,” Blake agreed.

“What if the threat level was exaggerated? This could be a lot of effort and expense for nothing.”

“It won’t be a waste. While they’re stationed in Sedona, they’ll be scouring the city for survivors. For the last couple of weeks, people have been wary. They’ve stayed put in their houses and hid. Now, they’re out of food and water, so they’ll be a lot more likely to take a risk and join us. Matter of fact, our scavenger teams are still finding people in Pinetop, Show Low, Eager, and Payson. That, and I don’t think anyone’s even bothered to save people in Sedona yet.”

“I suppose you’re right about that,” Corwin agreed. He pushed himself out of his seat with a grunt and rubbed his knees. “Well, I best get to it.”

“Bad knees?”

Corwin nodded. “For almost forty years now, ever since basic. That’s what happens when you run maneuvers with over a hundred pound rucksack.”

“Well, here, let me heal you.”

“I’m fine,” the older man waved away his offer.

“It’s no big deal,” Blake ignored his intelligence advisor. “Besides, my energy regeneration is doubled for an hour after I heal someone. That someone might as well be you.”

“If you insist.” Corwin shrugged. A moment later, he sighed in relief, thanked Blake, and left the room.

Great! Now it’s time for more testing.

Now that the room was empty, he could test his spells without being interrupted or questioned. Blake had no intention of informing others he could modify abilities without the Collective’s assistance. Especially since his latest attempts left him in a weakened position. The last thing he wanted to do was over-extend himself and pass out. He would be defenseless, at his enemy’s mercy.

First, I need to learn to control my aether. I can’t keep blowing through all of my energy every time I change anything.

Before Blake began, he sat down on a conference chair and leaned backwards. Then, he closed his eyes and visualized his reservoir of aether. After supper and the long council meeting, it was currently full to the brim.

Next, he focused on his second aether ability, Adjustable Dampen Sound. He could already increase or decrease the area affected, but only to a certain extent. Currently, a sphere ten feet in diameter was his limit.

Blake hoped to make it much larger.

While he focused on his intent, a twenty-foot diameter sphere, he simultaneously concentrated on his aetheric energy reservoir. In order to magnify the effect, he needed to siphon more than just the six percent the default ability required.

How about we try and double that.

Before his concentration could slip, he quickly activated the ability.

Immediately, he felt a sharp spike to his head, lost his vision, and his entire body felt like it exploded.

That can’t be good.

Once he recovered, a few seconds later, he checked his status and confirmed his fear.

Well crap, now I’m out of aether again.

Blake wasn’t sure how far his custom ability had reached, but it had been cancelled almost immediately when he ran out of energy.

Okay, I probably need to find some other place to test this out.

He had waited until the three-story faction hall was entirely empty before he made his attempt. Unfortunately, the massive amount of aether he fed into the ability may have drastically increased its size and far eclipsed the large building.

Or maybe it was just ten percent bigger. No way to know until I stop passing out.

Luckily, due to his First Responder feat, his aether was already regenerating at twice the normal speed. While he waited for his reservoir to refill, he decided to reposition.

Blake left the building, strode through the semi-lit streets of his town, and admired their new additions. Debra and the other enchanters had created aether-based lanterns which lined the roads. Their light, however, only extended so far. This was intentional, as they could have easily doubled or even tripled the lumens. However, that would have required far more aether upkeep.

A high energy requirement was the main reason they decided to enchant the lanterns with the aether spell, Illumination, rather than the mana-based, Guiding Flame. The light from Illumination was entirely in the visible spectrum. Guiding Flame, however, wasted most of its mana on infrared and heat.

For the same amount of energy, these aether-based lanterns were brighter and lasted longer than the mana-based alternative. Every day, four aether classers were paid to walk through the entire town to refill the lamps.

At first, they were given a handsome sum. However, once word spread of a way to make easy, safe money, interest peaked. Everyone wanted a side-gig to make some extra cash. Subsequently, Donna lowered the fee until interest waned. Now, they paid a quarter of the initial amount, yet still had people willing to do the job.

Supply and demand at work.

Blake waved at the few people who recognized him on the dimly lit street, and was surprised when he saw the light still on in Oliver’s Alchemy Workshop.

Looks like I’m not the only one up late working.

He was impressed with his brother’s new work-ethic. With his handicap, Oliver had to work three times harder to achieve the same results as the other alchemists. Even with the extra hours, he barely stayed ahead. As Blake passed by, he heard a sharp pop followed by loud cursing from within.

Blake winced in sympathy, but continued on his way. His plate was too full to stop by for a chat. Not only did he have to run an entire faction, but he intended to complete two scenarios a day and learn to cast abilities without the Collective’s assistance.

Before he passed through the open gates into the starlit night, he healed a guard to reset his First Responder timer.

Once outside the protection of the Dome, everything was quiet. No one wanted to scavenge in the dark. Any light source either drew monsters like a moth to the flame, or potential raiders, eager to murder their fellow humans for nano.

Despite the limited visibility, Blake had no problem navigating the wide street on his way to the main highway. He quickly found a nearby vacant building, scanned for sentient life with Oblate Mind Sense, and then settled in to wait for his aether reservoir to partially refill.

Twenty percent should be enough for a second test.

This time, rather than visualizing himself overloading the ability with aether, he imagined ‘scooping’ out about fifteen percent.

Hopefully it won’t take more than that.

Once he felt the energy was sufficiently separated from his main reservoir, he fed it to Adjustable Dampen Sound and activated his ability.

This time, he was not met with a blinding headache. The spell remained active, with a diameter of around fifteen feet rather than ten.

Now let’s see how much aether I used.

He opened up his interface and grinned.

Four percent left, close enough.

While Blake had aimed for fifteen percent, sixteen was pretty close to that. It left him with an extra four percent, which gradually shrank before his eyes as his Dampen Sound remained active.

Okay, so even with First Responder active, increasing the size drains a lot more aether. It also didn’t double like I thought it would.

It had been a long time since Blake had to perform geometry, and he could not remember the formula to calculate the area of a sphere. However, if he were forced to guess, he believed the ability encompassed an area at least three times larger than before. Although, well over half the coverage was wasted below ground or affected the second floor above him.

Next upgrade, I should make it Oblate, like I did with Mind Sense. That way I don’t waste so much of it.

Blake cancelled the ability to let his aether regenerate. While he waited, he contemplated his next test.

Should I try to manually make it Oblate without the Collective’s help?

He could already adjust the radius of Dampen Sound on the fly. If he could do the same with its shape, it would boost the spell’s usefulness to a new level.

Blake would be able to silently assassinate monsters from above while he extended his ability directly toward the ground below. Once his targets died, he could readjust the shape and the size of the spell to save aether, without the need to cancel it first. He would no longer need to wait between casts for the ability to become available again.

Although, that sounds pretty advanced. First, I should probably see if I can limit how much aether Spatial Step uses. Make sure I got this part down before I bang my head against the next problem.

Once his aether reservoir was a third full, he walked to the edge of the large room and aimed for the opposite wall.

Hmm, that's gotta be about forty feet away. Let’s see if I can teleport the whole way.

A normal Charged Guided Spatial Step let him reach a distance of twenty feet. If he wanted to double the amount traversed, he would need to feed additional aether to the ability.

Maybe give it an extra five percent?

Blake ‘scooped’ the extra aether out of his reservoir and focused on feeding it to his Spatial Step. Once he was ready, he activated the ability.

Yes! It worked! Kinda…

Unfortunately, he misjudged how much farther the extra five percent would send him. He appeared a good twenty-five feet outside the building rather than just inside the outer wall as he intended.

Well, at least I didn’t pass out. Progress!

Blake turned around and headed back inside, eager to fine tune the ability. The next time he attempted to reach the far wall, he only fed it an additional two percent of his energy. After he activated it, he successfully remained within the building, but landed about eight feet short of his goal.

He was about to celebrate his incremental progress when something disturbing suddenly occurred to him.

What happens if I exit inside the wall?

According to the Architect, its system aided people with spell casting by simplifying it. However, if Blake’s suspicions were correct, it also provided built in protections to prevent that exact situation from occurring.

Okay, looks like I found my next test.

Despite the late hour, Blake found himself full of energy. He could not wait to test out hundreds of things. This was something new and exciting that no one else had ever experienced. If mastered, he could become far more powerful than anyone else at his level. Especially if it worked on other energy types.

Blake suddenly remembered that he would need to wake up early enough the next day that he could complete two scenarios.

Shit, there’s no way I can sleep right now. I guess that’s what coffee’s for.
