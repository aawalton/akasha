“So, is this the brat you want me to train, Ling Xin?” Li Houran asked curiously, assessing the boy before him.

“That’s right. He is my first disciple, Ji Ning,” Ling Xin said with a nod, then added, “Also, don’t call him a brat.”

Ning cupped his hands politely and bowed. “Greetings, Elder Li. Thank you for agreeing to guide me. I’ve heard that you are one of the greatest spear masters in the sect.”

“That I am,” Li Houran said without modesty. Then he leaned slightly closer to Ning and added, “Still, it’s strange. Someone as eccentric as Ling Xin actually has a polite disciple like you.”

“Hey, I’m right here, you know,” Ling Xin interrupted.

“That’s exactly why I’m saying it,” Li Houran shot back, glaring at her. “And you, stop adding random, unlabelled pills when you refine medicine for me. I don’t want to go bald again.”

Ning paused, then quietly leaned toward Ling Yinran, who was standing nearby and trying very hard to remain a background character.

“Senior Sister,” Ning asked in a low voice, “does Master have many enemies in the sect?”

After all, from what he had seen, cultivators took great pride in their hair. Being bald usually meant one was a Buddhist cultivator. If Ling Xin casually turned people bald, that was practically asking for trouble.

Ling Yinran patted his shoulder lightly. “Um… how do I put this? They might be annoyed, but her skills make up for her… quirks. So you probably don’t have to worry, Junior Brother.”

“That didn’t sound reassuring at all.” Ning sighed.

“Then how am I supposed to test the effects of my pills on Foundation Establishment cultivators?” Ling Xin muttered, clearly still thinking about it. “Moreover, in my defense, I always compensate everyone with extra pills.”

“Then test them on yourself,” Li Houran said bluntly. “Anyway, you said your brat has some problems with spear cultivation?”

“You’re still calling him brat…” Ling Xin muttered, then continued, “Yes. He’s stuck at the Penetration stage and can’t seem to reach Unity. After he made a ‘unique’ pill, I wanted to reward him, so he asked for guidance to break through this bottleneck.”

“Unity, huh?” Li Houran nodded. “That’s the first step toward forming intent. It’s normal to get stuck there.”

He paused, then narrowed his eyes slightly.

“Wait. You said ‘unique’ pill?” he asked. “Don’t tell me your disciple is also making those strange pills of yours.”

“I don’t like calling them strange,” Ling Xin replied calmly. “But yes. He somehow turned a Fasting Pill into an aphrodisiac.”

Ning, who had been quietly fading into the background of the conversation, felt his lips twitch.

He really did not want that information spreading.

“Tsk.” Li Houran clicked his tongue. “I take it back. This brat is exactly like you.”

He looked at Ning again, this time with a more interested gaze.

“That means this might actually be fun.”

Ning felt a faint sense of danger when he saw the menacing smile on Li Houran’s face.

His master could afford to be carefree because of her strength. That did not mean others would treat him the same way.

“Alright,” Li Houran said, rolling his shoulders slightly as he picked up his spear.

“Let’s begin.”

...

“As far as I know, Unity is the first hurdle in any form of weapon cultivation. I hope Junior Brother can successfully cross this barrier,” Ling Yinran said as she sat on the sidelines, watching.

“Mm. While I am not too well-versed in weapon cultivation,” Ling Xin added, “Unity is the first step toward comprehending intent. It is the process of making the weapon an extension of one’s will. Using the weapon becomes as natural as moving your own limbs. So yes, it is a hurdle. You are turning something external into something internal.”

She spoke calmly, instructing her niece as they watched the training.

Neither of them truly walked the path of weapon cultivation. While Ling Yinran’s puppet techniques could be considered a branch of it, they did not follow the same structure from Scattered Hands to Unity. Instead, her path focused more on the number of puppets she could control and the level of refinement each puppet reached.

“That sounds quite difficult,” Ling Yinran admitted. “For us puppet masters, we use strings as a shortcut to make external puppets feel internal, as if they are part of us. Junior Brother might have some trouble with this.”

“Don’t worry about it,” Ling Xin said. “Li Houran may be a jerk, but he is one of the most accomplished spear masters in the sect. Given how rare true spear masters are here, he is one of the best instructors you could find.”

In truth, spear users were not as rare as they appeared within the wider cultivation world. Their scarcity in the Pure Qi Sect was more a matter of trend.

Whenever a cultivator reached the Golden Core stage, a wave of disciples would inevitably try to follow the same path. Whether out of admiration or a desire to share in that path’s fortune, the result was the same.

And since the Golden Core cultivator of the Pure Qi Sect was the sword master Ge Xuan, the sect was overwhelmingly filled with sword cultivators.

...

In the middle of the training ground, Li Houran stood. He was tall and broad-shouldered, with a spear resting loosely across his back in a casual manner that suggested complete familiarity with the weapon.

“It’s rare for Ling Xin to ask me for a favor, especially for someone else,” Li Houran said. “So I’m quite interested in your skills. She told me you reached the Penetration stage on your own. Show me what you can do first. Let’s see where you stand.”

“Yes.”

Ning lifted the spear and settled into his stance, feet shoulder-width apart, rear heel angled, weight evenly distributed, and shoulders level. The Eight Stabilities had been ingrained into his body by now, keeping his shoulders, hips, arms, and legs properly aligned.

He began with the first form of the Falling Leaf Spear Technique: the grounding thrust.

Power traveled from his rear foot, up through his spine, into his shoulder, and finally through his arm as the spearhead drove forward with unified force. From there, Ning continued through the sequence, transitioning into a sweeping arc that controlled the space around him before a retreating step flowed naturally into a counter-thrust.

The Double Dragons movement followed, his waist rotating and his spine acting as the axis while the spear moved as an extension of that line.

Then came Overturning the Dragon, the signature move of the technique, gathering force from the entire body before redirecting and amplifying it into a single attack.

Ning completed the sequence and returned to a resting stance.

Li Houran had not moved the entire time. He stood with his arms loosely at his sides, watching with an expression that had gradually shifted from mild curiosity to something more thoughtful.

“Again,” Li Houran said.

Ning repeated the sequence.

“Stop. Grounding thrust.”

Ning held the position as Li Houran began circling him, examining the angle of his rear foot, the line of his shoulder, his grip, and the slight forward lean of the spear.

“Your footwork is clean,” he said. “Your grip is correct. Your weight distribution is textbook.”

He completed the circle and stopped in front of Ning.

“Did you learn this on your own?”

“I had some guidance from another disciple, Zhang Feng, for a time,” Ning replied.

“Never heard of him,” Li Houran said, glancing at the spear and then back at Ning. “Still, your skills are not bad.”

At first, Li Houran had assumed Ning’s foundation would be flimsy, mostly because the boy had learned much of it from books during the foundational stage. Weapon mastery required a properly built base, and that usually came from receiving hands-on guidance from an experienced teacher.

Seeing Ning’s textbook-perfect form, however, forced him to reconsider that assumption.

It seems this brat has some talent, Li Houran thought.

Without knowing about Ning’s unique ability, he naturally attributed such precision to talent. In a way, he was not wrong, since Know Thyself was an integral part of Ning.

But perfect form did not guarantee success.

“Your foundation is solid,” Li Houran said, crossing his arms. “Your stance is good, your grip is good, and you know how to move your weight properly. Honestly, there’s not much for me to correct there.”

He looked Ning over once more before asking, “So, do you understand what that means?”

“That the problem isn’t the foundation,” Ning said, glancing briefly at the panel.

[Falling Leaf Spear Technique: Penetration (Completion)]

It had remained that way for three months, despite Ning drilling the technique consistently through the competition preparation period, his transition into the inner sect, and even during his alchemy sessions with Ling Xin.

Zhang Feng had taught him the fundamentals clearly: Scattered Hands, then Penetration. The body and spear working in concert, knowing not simply how to swing, but where to swing.

Ning had reached Penetration and understood it thoroughly. By any reasonable measure, he was good at it.

The problem was Unity.

In the manual, Unity was described as the stage where the separation between cultivator and weapon began to disappear. Technique stopped being a collection of individual movements and became something continuous and instinctive.

Ning had approached that problem methodically, the same way he approached almost everything else. He read the theory, isolated variables, drilled each component, and adjusted whatever flaws he could find, yet the panel had not moved.

That was precisely why he had come to seek guidance from an expert.

Li Houran reached back and took the spear from his own back.

“Well, your stance isn’t going to tell me anything else. Come at me.”

The first exchange lasted three seconds.

Ning closed the distance with a grounding thrust to test Li Houran’s reach, only for the elder to step offline and redirect the attack with a lazy half-rotation of his spear. He did not even properly block it, merely nudging Ning’s line of attack aside.

The second exchange lasted slightly longer. Ning used a sweeping arc to force Li Houran’s lateral movement, then drove through with Overturning the Dragon, but Li Houran allowed the arc to pass, stepped inside his reach, and placed the butt of his spear lightly against Ning’s sternum.

Defeated.

“Your technique was fine,” Li Houran said as he stepped away. “The sweep forced me where you wanted, and following it with Overturning the Dragon made sense. So why didn’t it land?”

Ning thought about it. “Because you knew it was coming.”

“Of course I knew it was coming. You were trying to stab me.” Li Houran gave him an unimpressed look. “Think again.”

Ning considered the exchange more carefully. The movements themselves had been correct, and Li Houran had not reacted particularly quickly. If anything, the elder had begun moving before Ning fully committed to the attack.

“The technique was… telegraphed,” Ning said slowly. “Not in the movement itself. You knew what I intended to do before I actually did it.”

“There you go.”

Li Houran rested his spear against his shoulder. “That’s what’s wrong with your spear. Everything you do is clean, probably cleaner than most disciples at your level, but it’s too clean. You decide what you’re going to do, then your spear obediently does exactly that.”

He shook his head.

“You’ve practiced the forms so much that you’ve turned yourself into a damn manual.”

Ning looked down at his spear.

That was certainly one way to describe it.

“You’re not bad,” Li Houran continued. “I’ve seen disciples spend five years getting to where you are. But against anyone good enough to read you, all that perfect form starts working against you. They know where the spear is going because, half the time, you’ve already decided where it’s going before the fight gets there.”

They exchanged blows again, but this time Li Houran began actively testing that weakness.

“You know,” he said as he sidestepped a thrust, “your spear style is boring.”

Ning rolled beneath a sweep and drove upward with a rising counter, only for Li Houran to catch it on his shaft and redirect it skyward.

“Very good form, though,” Li Houran added. “Painfully good form.”

Ning drove forward with Overturning the Dragon.

Li Houran turned it aside again.

“And there it is.”

Ning retreated several steps as Li Houran followed.

“You already know these movements, brat. Your body knows them too. You’ve probably practiced that thrust thousands of times, but every time you use it, you’re still up here deciding how the whole thing should happen.”

Li Houran tapped his temple with one finger before suddenly thrusting.

Ning twisted aside.

“Unity isn’t about forgetting everything you learned. That would be stupid. You spent all that time practicing for a reason.” Li Houran pressed forward again, forcing Ning to parry twice before giving him room to retreat. “The problem is that you don’t trust any of it unless you’re consciously controlling it.”

Ning remained silent as he considered those words.

His usual approach was simple: observe, understand, choose the appropriate response, and execute it as accurately as possible. It had worked extremely well so far, but Li Houran was pointing out something Ning had never considered.

After thousands of repetitions, did he really need to consciously choose every movement anymore?

Li Houran spun his spear loosely in one hand. “Your thinking got you this far, so I’m not going to tell you to stop thinking. Just stop trying to order your spear around every damn second. Give it a direction and trust what you’ve already practiced.”

He raised the spear again.

“Otherwise, you’re going to stay exactly where you are.”

They exchanged several more blows. Ning tried changing his timing and varying the order of his techniques, but Li Houran continued reading him with irritating ease, sometimes moving before Ning had even fully committed to the attack.

After another failed exchange, Li Houran clicked his tongue.

“I feel at this point you’re just wasting my time. Your attacks are too predictable. Even with my eyes closed, I could see through them. I thought you had some talent at first, but it seems you’re a dime a dozen.”

Ning took a breath and looked at him for a moment.

“Elder, are you trying to get a rise out of me?”

“Yes,” Li Houran admitted without hesitation. “But it doesn’t seem to be working with you.”

Li Houran had deliberately been trying to bait Ning into abandoning his standard form. It usually worked with his disciples; smack-talking while beating them down often provoked enough anger or frustration to force something different out of them.

The brat in front of him, however, was calm.

Too calm.

From the beginning of his journey, Ning had known that face-slapping and provocation led to a surprising number of deaths in this cliché world. Because of that, he had made a habit of staying chill whenever possible. Insults were ultimately just words.

Being so calm had its advantages and disadvantages. The advantage was that he was remarkably difficult to bait.

The disadvantage was this situation right now.

Li Houran remained quiet for a moment before suddenly laughing.

“I’ll be honest, I’ve been doing this for eighty years, and that doesn’t happen often. Usually, by this point, they’re trying to take my head off.”

“So emotions help in breaking through Unity?” Ning asked.

“They can,” Li Houran replied. “Anger is just the easiest one to drag out of someone in a fight. Get them angry enough and eventually they stop worrying about whether every little movement is correct. Their will takes over.”

He paused, then added, “Doesn’t have to be anger, though.”

Ning looked at him. To be honest, using anger to force a change was not a bad idea in this world. They might not be Saiyans who could evolve into a new form through rage alone, but this was still a world where thought and will could affect reality to a certain extent.

Li Houran rested his spear against his shoulder.

“So if making you angry doesn’t work, what does? What do you actually care about, brat?”

The question caught Ning slightly off guard.

What did he care about? What actually mattered enough to him that it could become part of his spear?

The numbers going up, obviously.

Fields yielding high-grade grain after months of work. A cauldron producing a pill with clean purity and no waste. His panel ticking forward after finally understanding something that had troubled him for weeks. The satisfaction of identifying a problem, finding a method, and watching that method work.

More than anything, Ning wanted to keep progressing and understanding the mysteries of this world.

Transmigrating here had been strange at first, but he had gradually grown used to his new life. His curiosity, however, had never disappeared.

Ning looked down at the spear in his hands.

All this time, he had treated it as a weapon he needed to control. Every movement had a correct angle, every stance had a proper distribution of weight, and every technique had an optimal moment to use it.

That thinking had brought him all the way to Penetration.

Perhaps the problem was that he was still doing it even after practicing those movements thousands of times.

His body already knew the stances. His hands already knew the grip. His spine knew how to rotate, his feet knew where to step, and the spear itself had become so familiar that Ning could perform the entire Falling Leaf Spear Technique without consciously recalling a single instruction.

So why was he still trying to control every part of it?

Zhang Feng had told him long ago that the spine was the great dragon and the spear the first dragon, with man and weapon overturning the sea together.

Ning had understood the mechanics behind those words.

Perhaps now it was time to actually trust them.

“One more try,” Ning said. He had understood his problem, and now he wanted to test whether he could correct it.

“If you say so. I’m just getting paid anyway.” Li Houran raised a brow, interest flickering in his eyes.

Ning stepped forward with the grounding thrust.

Li Houran moved offline to redirect it, only to find the line shifting before his spear made contact. The difference was tiny, only a few degrees, but Ning had changed the thrust midway instead of stubbornly completing the movement exactly as he had practiced it.

Li Houran redirected anyway. He was a Foundation Establishment cultivator, after all, and something that minor was nowhere near enough to trouble him.

Ning gave ground, then suddenly planted his rear foot and rotated his spine into the Dragon Turn. His spear swept outward in an arc Li Houran had already seen several times, but instead of following rigidly into the expected continuation, Ning allowed the movement to change according to Li Houran’s response.

Something had changed.

“Again,” Ning said.

They exchanged several more bouts, and each time Ning tried to interfere less with movements his body already knew. At first, the difference was almost nonexistent, but gradually his transitions began to loosen. A thrust no longer had to finish as a thrust simply because that was what he had intended when he started it, and a sweep could change halfway through if Li Houran moved differently than expected.

During the third exchange, Overturning the Dragon came at a moment Li Houran had not anticipated. The technique itself was unchanged, but the obvious intention that had preceded it before was beginning to disappear.

Li Houran blocked it cleanly and grinned.

“Better. Let me go on the offense now.”

He reached Ning in an instant.

His spear moved differently this time. There was none of the casual testing from before, and Ning’s eyes immediately glowed blue as he struggled to follow the incoming attack.

He saw the trajectory and understood where the spear was going, but instead of consciously searching for the correct response, Ning trusted the thousands of repetitions he had already put into his technique and moved.

His spear rose and deflected the attack.

Another strike followed, and Ning responded again, his feet adjusting through Shadow Steps as the spear moved naturally with his body. Li Houran increased the pressure, forcing Ning backward, but the more Ning stopped trying to dictate every individual movement, the easier it became to respond.

Then Ning went on the offensive.

Overturning the Dragon.

His spear surged forward toward a vulnerable point, but his opponent was a true master of the spear.

“Good! This is better. Come, show me your limits!” Li Houran laughed, clearly enjoying himself now.

Ning did not respond. He had already entered a flow.

For the first time, the spear no longer felt like something he needed to consciously command. The stances, sequences, and calculations he had repeated thousands of times had not disappeared; they were still there beneath every movement, but Ning no longer needed to think about them individually.

Shadow Steps anchored his footing while his perception sharpened, allowing him to catch every subtle shift in Li Houran’s stance and every change in pressure behind his attacks. Usually, Ning would have tried to process each observation consciously before choosing a response, but now he allowed his body to respond to what he already understood.

Gradually, Ning began to recognize what his own spear was becoming.

Swiftness.

His style had always relied on momentum building upon momentum, with each movement preparing the next until everything converged into a decisive thrust. His precision had never been the problem. The rigidity came from his insistence on deciding exactly how he would reach that final attack before the fight had even developed.

He did not need to abandon precision. He simply needed to let the path toward that final thrust change.

Ning stepped forward, and his spear shot out, shifting its angle in the middle of the attack according to Li Houran’s response. When Li Houran moved to intercept, Ning adjusted again, allowing one attack to become another without pausing to consciously select the next technique.

Li Houran’s expression changed slightly.

“Good,” he said quietly.

Ning barely heard him.

Each strike flowed naturally into the next, and for the first time, there was no obvious sequence for Li Houran to read. The techniques Ning had practiced were all still present, but they no longer appeared in the same rigid order.

Unity.

He had touched it.

Ning drew in a breath as his rear foot pressed into the ground and his spine rotated, gathering force through his entire body. His qi moved with it, and the spear responded without hesitation as everything converged toward a single point.

THRUST

Li Houran moved to intercept, but Ning’s eyes glowed blue as he caught the response and adjusted his footing through Shadow Steps, accelerating once more as the spear changed its line at the final moment.

The clash rang out across the training ground.

The difference in cultivation was far too great to overcome, and the sheer impact sent Ning backward. Yet when he regained his footing, he saw that Li Houran had been forced half a step back.

For a brief moment, the training ground fell silent.

Then Li Houran smiled.

“Good,” he said. “Now that is a spear.”

Ning exhaled slowly as the strange flow began to recede. The spear was still the same weapon he had been holding a few minutes earlier, but his understanding of how to use it had fundamentally changed.

Li Houran considered him for a moment before raising his spear again.

“Watch carefully.”

His spear moved.

There was no visible preparation or transition between stillness and motion. One moment, Li Houran stood calmly before him; the next, Ning felt a sharp pressure directly between his brows.

His pupils contracted.

Before his mind could even begin to process what had happened, the tip of Li Houran’s spear was already resting mere millimeters from his forehead, perfectly still as though it had always been there.

Ning froze as a bead of sweat slid slowly down his temple.

“…I didn’t see it.”

All he had perceived was a flash of sharpness, and in the next instant, he had already lost. If this had been a real fight, he would have died before he even understood what had happened.

It was frightening, but what caught Ning even more strongly was the sheer difference between that strike and everything he had just accomplished. His own breakthrough had felt significant only moments ago, yet Li Houran had casually shown him another mountain standing beyond it.

Ning’s eyes brightened.

The cleaner the defeat, the clearer the direction.

“How interesting,” Ning murmured, recalling the brilliance of that strike. “I didn’t even see it.”

Li Houran looked at Ning’s expression for a moment before laughing aloud.

“Hahaha! I wanted to see how you’d react after getting a taste of actual spear intent. Most youngsters look like someone poured cold water over them.”

He lowered his spear.

“You look like you want me to do it again.”

“I do.”

That only made Li Houran laugh harder.

“Good. Maybe your spear isn’t so boring after all.”

Ning looked at the spearhead again. “So that was spear intent?”

“Just a little of it. Any more and Ling Xin would come looking for my head.” Li Houran tapped the shaft of his spear lightly against Ning’s chest. “What you reached just now was Unity. What I showed you is what comes after.”

Ning nodded slowly.

There was quite a distance between the two.

Li Houran rested the spear across his shoulder.

“Still, forcing me half a step back the moment you touched Unity isn’t bad. I suppose I can stop calling you brat now.”

Ning looked at him.

“Really?”

“No.”

Ning should have expected that.

Li Houran grinned and began walking away.

“Congratulations anyway. You’ve crossed the hurdle. Get used to Unity first, and then we can talk about ten steps, one kill.”

Ning had heard the phrase before in old martial stories, but judging from Li Houran’s tone, it had a more specific meaning among spear cultivators.

“What is that?”

“The next thing you’ll be chasing,” Li Houran replied without turning around. “The first real step toward spear intent.”

Ning looked down at the spear in his hands.

He had finally broken through the bottleneck that had held him back for three months.

And apparently, he had already found the next one.
