Lord Draxas craned over the chessboard, mulling over their next action. They had expected the mammoth to be powerful, and Elmira had been a useful pawn, drawing out some of their opponent’s techniques. But after seeing the match, they found themselves staring down at the board filled with uncertainty.

Seeing Mala’s abilities firsthand had truly woken the kobold up, and they were no longer boasting endlessly. This was no normal elite. Elmira’s strategy for the challenge had been near perfect. Her technique was almost flawless. Lord Draxas knew that it would have taken one of the fastest and most nimble of warriors to defeat her, and Mala seemed to have it all.

Worst still, that wasn’t their main reason for concern. It was how Mala had bested Elmira that truly made the kobold pay attention.

Careful now. Even though we have all the pieces, this is no ordinary enemy.

Lord Draxas took a long moment to act, but eventually moved a piece and took Frostborn’s last rook. It was a safe move while they considered their next turn.

Only a couple of tiles away from Mala was a curious challenge piece in the middle of the board. It had caught Lord Draxas’ attention some time back. Unlike the other from previous matches, this challenge had a rarity, and it was Mythical at that.

The problem was that if they took the Mythical objective now, their opponent would have a chance to react. And Draxas wasn’t sure they wanted to be on the receiving end of Mala.

But what is “Second Chances, Live with Goliath” supposed to mean?

Of course, the System threw up new things with little explanation.

Lord Draxas looked up as the young girl opposite him made her move. As predicted, she moved Mala one tile forward and placed her right up beside the Mythical challenge objective.

Lord Draxas slammed their fists against the sides of the table. They hated this. They felt like they had been dragged into a situation where they were forced to act. As they couldn’t exactly just ignore the Mythical challenge, they let the other side have it.

I could take another piece this turn, limiting our opponent’s side. Or, we could move on to the Mythical Objective, forcing Frostborn to challenge us for the tile.

Since it was a challenge objective, both sides would be at risk of losing pieces if they lost the challenge. But there could be other benefits to landing on the tile first, like getting to choose certain aspects of the challenge.

We could end it all here. Take out the king and finish the floor. It would mean fewer auction points, but it’s better than risking a nasty surprise.

Lord Draxas stroked its scaly chin, considering its options. They couldn’t exactly ignore the Mythical Challenge. Especially not with “second chance” in the name.

We still have three tokens. No need to take chances if their king is involved. If we play them all now, they can’t possibly win.

A smile creased the kobold’s scaled lips.

They moved their closest pawn forward, taking the contested tile with Talia. But before relinquishing their turn, they silently activated their three tokens and looked up to the trembling girl to see what would happen next.

She glanced down nervously at her king, then moved Mala onto the same tile, challenging Talia.

“You fell for my trap!” Lord Draxas roared with laughter. “I activate my tokens and move Mo’han, Aaron, and Mandor to join this tile. AHAHA! Game over!”

The players could inspect every piece on the board, and the girl could no doubt see that Mo’han, Mandor, Aaron, and Talia were a serious challenge, even for someone like Mala.

“I-I-ah…” she shook her head, lost in indecision.

But they are done for now.”
