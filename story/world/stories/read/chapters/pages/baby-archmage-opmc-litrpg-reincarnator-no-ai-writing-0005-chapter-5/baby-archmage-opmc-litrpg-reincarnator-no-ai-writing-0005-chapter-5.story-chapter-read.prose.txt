Description: Your Perception enhances the precision and efficiency of Mana-related Skills while Mana Sight is active. Every 10 Perception increases Mana Precision and Mana Efficiency by 2%

[Current Bonus: +10%]

Holy crap that was so good. That was way better than what I had expected. This was a scaling Perk!

Math had never been my strong suit, but I applied myself and started making calculations. At 100 Perception, I would have 20% increased efficiency and precision. 40% at 200 Perception, so…200% at 1000 Perception.

That sounded insane. Granted, I was still quite far away from that, but given how much Perception I was getting from my skills, I would eventually get there. I didn’t know how much mana a real spell would spend, so this might prove useful.

First, I needed to test how much 10% more efficient and precise control of mana really meant. I wasn’t sure efficiency applied here, but precision of control should, right?

There was a noticeable change in the amount of mana particles I perceived. But most importantly, I could now see what the mana particles were orbiting. Miniature suns of bright white light. I tried reaching them with Mana Manipulation, but they absolutely would not budge. My skill level was clearly too low.

If I leveled my Mana Manipulation higher, maybe I could interact with them. I still didn’t know much about magic or how to cast even the simplest of spells, but I had theories. These mana particles were moving in chaotic orbits. If I arranged them into a coherent shape, maybe they would create a spell. If the mana was just arranged in the right order… Like a recipe? Would that create a spell?

If so, being able to perceive these mini suns with orbiting particles was essential to casting larger spells quickly. Moving each particle individually would be a pain in the ass.

That was a worry for future Luka. I just wanted to play with my new Perk now.

So I focused my Mana Sight and directed my will at the nearest mana particle.

The difference was noticeable. I could now move the mana particles with much higher accuracy, and when I willed them to be still, there was less vibration. I also felt like the act of controlling the mana particle was just a smidge easier. The difference was subtle, but it was there. And if I were to do this for thousands of hours, any lessened strain and ease of practice would add up, especially as my Perception got higher.

After I completely tired myself with practice I relaxed in my crib and let out a tiny, contented baby sigh.

I listened to the sound of night. It was never entirely quiet. Somewhere far the waves of the sea undulated in a soothing rhythm. The wood of the cabin creaked idly and some bird cawed in the jungle. The steady breathing of my sleeping parents gave me peace. My father snored lightly and hogged some of their blanket. My mother yanked it back with a relaxed, practiced move. I suppressed a giggle. This was happiness.

I gradually improved my ability to speak. I also practiced crawling, which was really annoying. I wanted to walk. Practicing these life skills too a surprising amount of time and brain power and I had less time to work on my magic during this time. I considered it a little holiday. I had been working hard at my magic since birth for a year. It was time to brush up on my baby-skills and make my parents proud. After I figured crawling out, I started using the dining table’s chairs and legs to walk around it. My parents watched me with delighted pride and father always scooped me up when I did a particularly good job, which made me happy.

Sometimes we played outside in the mud by our house with my mother. She had absolutely no problem with getting muddy with me, and we would get into splashy wars. Afterwards mother would wash me and sing to me. I tried to sing along to learn to speak better.

I had a much better handle on speaking by now. When mother left me alone for short moments to get something outside, or greet a visitor at the door, I would talk to myself in longer and more complicated sentences than a baby had business speaking in. I figured it was important to be able to express myself well. And if the System made kids develop faster, it wouldn’t be too implausible that I could speak well. Apparently there were Attributes and Skills like Eloquence and Articulation, but I didn’t have those. Maybe a Merchant or Politician would benefit from those. I shuddered at the thought of a Politician class.

One evening, I was ready to show my parents the full prowess of my walking abilities, and I let go of the chairs and took a few tentative steps towards my mother who was sitting on a large cushion on the floor. Once I got the handle of it, I didn’t wobble and ran and lunged in her lap and giggled.

She scooped me up and started peppering me with kisses. “That was amazing, little gem!”

“This is great!” my father exclaimed. “Look at that balance. My boy! You will be the strongest fisher of your generation.”

Oh, father. I am so sorry.

“Luka, you are such a talented boy,” mother said and patted me on the head.

“Mama,” I said and hugged her leg.

“I can’t wait to boast to the boys!” father said. “They’ll be jealous. Jako’s kid can barely crawl.”

“What of Marek’s kid?”

Father scoffed. “Hmph. Vorok. That kid is older than our Luka. He is almost four years old. I heard Marek gave him a spear to lean on when he was just a baby to expedite his learning. Walking that early surely built strong muscles. That boy is big.”

My father looked at me with a thoughtful expression.

Mother glared at him. “You are not giving him a spear. What would be the point anyway? He doesn’t even have the System yet.”

“I fear Marek’s kid already has the System,” father grumbled.

“That’s early,” mother said.

“I’m sure Luka will unlock it early too.”

“Don’t compare Luka to older kids,” mother said.

“I’m just saying we should have—”

Mother gave father a stern glare. “What? We should have made him earlier so you and Marek can continue your stupid rivalry through your kids?”

“I don’t think that,” father said and raised his hands. “But do you think Marek will let it be? He will absolutely raise that kid to hate our boy. And with our boy being younger and smaller…”

“Oh,” mother said and instinctively reached to touch me. “I didn’t think of that. “Do you really think Marek would turn the boys against each other?”

“The man can be petty… But don’t worry,” father said and smiled. “I’ll make him the best Fisher in generations. You’ll see. Just you wait until you unlock the System, Luka. Papa will teach you everything you need to know.”

I decided this was as good a time as any. Of course I wanted to make my parents proud and blow their minds. I also didn’t want them to worry. Seemed like father had some less than friendly rivalry with another fisher. I wasn’t thrilled about picking up their generational grudge, but I was sure I could handle a three year old bully if it came to that.

Still, I decided I should tell them now, because most importantly, I wanted them to tell me about the damn System!

“Mama, papa,” I said. “I have.”

“You have?” mother asked. “Have what?”

“I have… word box. System?”

Both of my parents looked at each other with eyes wide as saucers.

Mother grabbed me by my shoulders and shook me to the point I almost fell. “Are you sure? What do you see?”

“Mama,” I said. “Scary.”

“Oh… I’m so sorry baby. Here, let me pick you up.”

Mother put me in my raised seat at the dining table, where all of their important conversations were had.

“When did you unlock it? With walking? Do you have the walking skill?” Father asked.

“Walking is not a skill, dummy,” mother said and ruffled my father’s hair.

“Could be,” father said. “The system rewards intent. Nobody unlocks it at the point where you’re intentional about learning to walk.”

“Hmmm,” mother said. “Good point. I hope he didn’t. It will be a bitch to unlearn.”

Two important pieces of knowledge. First, you could unlearn skills. That was good. I filed that notion. Second was that my suspicions had been correct. The System rewarded intent. But I had to specify something.

“Papa,” I said. “What is intent?”

Father grinned at me. “It means to do something on purpose. To want to get good at something.”

“If I learn spear like papa,” I said and got the widest grin on my father’s face. “Do I have to only train spear, or train and want to have skill?”

Mother watched me intently. It was a little unnerving. She nodded to herself. “That was a complicated question. The System must have made him more lucid.”

I was really glad that was a thing, because it made my questions a lot less suspicious.

“What difference does that make?” Father said. “Of course you are learning the spear!”

“Now, now love,” mother chided. “That was a good question. He should learn how this works.”

“I can’t believe we are teaching this to a baby.”

“I’m not baby!” I protested and pouted which made my parents laugh.

“Little gem,” mother said. “This was a good question. The answer is that you have to want the Skill and Levels. Not necessarily knowingly. On some level of your mind.”

“He can’t understand what that means, no matter how genius,” father said.

“I can!” I protested.

My mother smiled kindly and continued. “I cook our meals, but I don’t have the cooking skill. I wouldn’t get it even if my Skill Slots weren’t full. Because I don’t want that Skill and I don’t need that Skill. So on no level of my mind am I calling for the System to grant it to me.”

“A Skill Slot is what you need to learn a new Skill,” father said. “You get your first free slot at Total Level of 5. Total levels are…”

I listened patiently for my father to tell me what I knew already.

“So if I train with spear, but don’t want skill, then I don’t get it?” I asked.

“Why wouldn’t you want a spear skill?!” father asked, absolutely crestfallen.

“That is correct, little gem,” mother said. “But don’t you want it?”

“Yeah! Don’t you want to be a fisher like your old man?” father said.

Absolutely not. Any Skill Slot not used for magic was a Skill Slot wasted.

“I don’t know,” I lied. “I have to try first.”

“That’s more like it!” father said. “Then it’s decided! I am getting you a baby spear tomorrow!”

Mother gave him an exasperated look, but she was smiling.

“You are free to become what you want, little gem. But Fishers are loved by the village.”

“What about Elder?” I asked. “What Skills he has?”

“Elder Piraka?” mother asked. Then she shared a look with father.

“Well…” Father said rubbing his neck. “If not a Fisher, then being his apprentice would be… acceptable.”

Mother laughed. “It is even more honorable than being a Fisher. You just want him to follow your footsteps.”

“Hmph. Can you blame a father for that?”

“Elder Piraka is our Shaman,” mother said. “Shaman is a person who helps everyone in the village. Very important.I don’t know all of the skills he has, but at least some skill with potion making, like Alchemy or Apothecary. Medicine for a certainty…”

“What about magic,” I asked. “Does elder know magic?”

“Our little island has very little tradition in magic. Only the Shamans know. Great sorcerers used to live here thousands of years ago, but those times are past.”

“Don’t fill his head with all this,” father said. “Elder Piraka will not take a baby for an apprentice.”

Mother glared at him and continued.

“Only their ruins remain, and the stories. They say the Shamans visited the ruins and learned magic. Some of it was passed down from the larger islands.

“There’s more islands?” I asked.

“The world is big, son,” father said. “We live in a small corner of it. Our home is called the Tapoka Islands.”

“Yes,” mother said. “And this island is called Asaid. But further out in the world, there are powerful people who know magic. Beyond that are the Immortals. We sometimes see them battle in the sky.”

Now that was more like it! Immortals fighting in the sky? I absolutely needed to see that.

“That’s enough of that,” father said. “Filling his head with such nonsense. If you want to talk about magic, tell him about Piraka and his potions. To the rapids with these fables.”

My mother was quiet for a while, as if contemplating something. Then she nodded.

“Don’t you understand, Jonaka,” mother said quietly. “He is special. Walking? Already talking like this? He has the System unlocked, for crying out loud.”

“What are you saying?” father asked.

Mother cupped his cheek. “Remember when you were a kid? I remember. I’ve remembered you since I was three years old, love. I fell in love at first sight. And I didn’t even know it.”

Father took her hand. “I was a fool to notice it so late. I’m lucky you didn’t go for someone else.”

“I didn’t want someone else. I knew it from the start. Just like you knew you wanted to become a fisher from the start. When I fell in love with you, you were in love with the spear.”

“First time we have this conversation, and what does he ask about? Not spears. Magic.”

Mother leaned to kiss father’s cheek. “You don’t have to. We can ask Elder Piraka. Maybe Luka can become a Shaman.

Huh. So the competitive spirit in the village wasn’t all fun and games? This rivalry my father had with Marek had actual stakes. Father could lose face and status because of me?

I did not want that. Should I learn a spear skill? I could at least keep up with the other kids. Maybe if I learned to use mana with the spear, I could even compete…

I absolutely didn’t want to disappoint my parents or make them ashamed. Much less weaken their status. Status in a small tribe like this was everything. But… I didn’t want to learn the spear. I didn’t want to become a Fisher. I wanted magic.

If you do not become a Fisher, I will be disappointed, because I would love to teach you. But if you choose a different path, like becoming an apprentice to Elder Piraka, then I will just get stronger to deal with Marek.”

“About him being special. What if becoming a Shaman is too small for him? What if this island is too small for him?”

My father swallowed. “Then… I will watch proudly to see how far our son can go.”

Mother pulled him into a kiss. “Good father. Good man. My man.”

Mother and father taught me many things about the system. Like the Skill Slot intervals. They were indeed tied to total levels. 5, 25, 50, 100, 200, 400, 800, 1600 and so on. Similar logic applied to Skill Evolutions and Perks, like getting Magical Insight at level 10 Meditation. The intervals could change depending on the person and specific Skill, though. Usually people gained a Perk at levels ten and twenty. At twenty you also gained a Skill Upgrade, which provided more Attributes. But in rare cases you would get a Skill Evolution.

Skill Evolutions were rare and valuable. Mother had been a Jungler who was a person who collected plants and stuff in the jungle, and scouted and trapped beasts. She had gained a Skill Evolution called Sixth Sense, which was a powerful awareness ability. I wanted to learn it later!

Skills could be unlearned by simply neglecting them. Again, intent was the key word. If I were to learn Swimming, and then stopped Swimming, I would start dropping levels in it. It would happen even faster if I actively wanted to discard the Skill. Depending on how high the Skill was, it would take anywhere from a week to several years to unlearn a Skill. All the Attributes would be gone too, so one couldn’t just pick up easy to level Skills and discard them.

I liked that. It meant that making a mistake with a Skill wasn’t permanent. Surely, it would waste a lot of time to unlearn a Skill. I still had a Skill Slot and I wasn’t sure what I wanted to use it on. I could learn an awareness ability and maybe start working towards Sixth Sense. Apparently all awareness Skills increased Perception, so that was a strong option. But I wanted to prioritize magic, so I wanted to think about it a bit more. Or maybe I would need to sacrifice a Slot to deal with Marek’s boy. I’d have to see. Either way I wanted to learn more about magic. Unfortunately mother knew very little. Elder Piraka would know more.

I needed to learn from him. But I was just a baby, and my parents had agreed that I should act normal. It was starting to become clear to other adults that I was not like the other kids, but we didn’t want anyone to know I had unlocked the System yet. My seeming intellect and ability to walk and talk was still plausible, but unlocking a System this early? Even my parents had been a little worried.

If only they knew I had already unlocked it a year ago…

A few months passed and I trained my skills diligently. Spring had turned to summer and I finally met Vorok. I immediately understood what my father had been so concerned about.

I was playing around with the other young kids, making sand castles by the beach. We were of varied ages. Most of the kids were three or four. There had apparently been a bountiful year when a lot of the villagers had had kids. My father had been doing some breakthrough training and gotten a Swimming-related Skill Evolution, which is why I had been delayed. So I was the youngest of our generation. Mother hesitated putting me in with the other kids, but father insisted I get socialized. I think he was right. I had never been the most social person in my previous life. In this life I resolved to change that and value the connections that life offered me. So I joined the other kids in play. They were all older than me, but they accepted me for the most part, since I was a pretty happy guy who didn’t mind sharing.

The mothers watched us while the fathers fished on the rafts. It was so relaxing, and to my surprise spending time with the other kids wasn’t so bad. They were really friendly and open, but funnily enough they were already very competitive. It seemed to just be the culture of the island. But it wasn’t hostile. Everyone was of the same tribe, but everyone just wanted to be the best. The kids who had unlocked the System were mostly wrestling with each other, or throwing sticks, imitating their fathers.

But at some point one of the largest ones decided to take a break after suplexing a few younger kids into the sand so hard that the mothers had to interject. I knew who that kid was. It was Vorok, son of Marek. And the way he was manhandling the other boys told me he wasn’t a nice guy. The mothers agreed. He was told to take a timeout and come back to play later.

And for some reason that kid made it my problem.

Vorok was bigger than the rest of the kids in every way, and he suddenly loomed over my sand castle. He had blonde wavy hair and dark eyebrows with a protruding brow. The look on his face was way more imperious than a toddler should have. He crossed his arms.

“Give,” he said.

Other kids around us stopped what they were doing and turned to watch us.

“Give what?” I asked.

“My sand.”

I pointed at an empty spot next to me. “Sand there.”

“I want this sand.”

The implications were pretty clear. I wasn’t about to take this from someone who was still shitting their pants.

“No,” I said.

Half of the kids around us flinched. I immediately understood why. The big kid’s face took on a really ugly scowl.

“No?” he asked and took a menacing step forward.

“My sand,” I said.

He looked down and kicked down my sand castle. Then he grinned at me and started stomping until there was nothing left. Then he looked at me and sneered.

This was a challenge.

So naturally I got up.

“I said no.”
