“If you have the chance, will you fetch me another bolt of the good silk?” Hadra asked.

Hadra didn’t see it, but she could hear Amadra licking dry lips. “I don’t know how to tell–!”

“The bolts in the third bay on the left. The silk will be a shiny green color,” she said.

Amadra gave a deep curtsy.

“Yes M’lady!” she said before she practically bolted out of the room.

Hadra rolled her eyes.

“My lady,” she sneered to herself. “What a joke.”

Thus far Denarla had been a lonely place. She hadn’t truly met anyone she cared about. The servants all treated her with deference, or mostly ignored her. They were perfectly willing to fetch whatever she needed — water, food, clothes, and anything she could ask for — yet, none of them provided… company.

Amadra certainly hadn’t dared treat her as anything less than a full lady. She hadn’t thought she’d been all that harsh, but the girl was absolutely terrified of her.

Most days she spent in her room, designing new dresses in her mind, and idly playing with needles. They could do so many things. With the Weaver’s Orb, she had turned her needles into a passive army that could dance to her whim and do whatever she needed them to.

Amadra might have seen her doing so once or twice. It hadn’t even been on purpose the first time the girl had walked in on her orchestrating her dance of needles…

She regretted that. While at first she’d been happy to show the little twit that she wouldn’t be walked over, she hadn’t anticipated her reputation spreading to the rest of the wait staff. The one group of people in this castle she might actually have some kinship with, she’d alienated on the first day.

“Might as well get this over with,” she said.

Her needles filled the room. Opening the armoire, she directed them to bring out a chorus of dresses. Not for the Duchess. Her newest creation had been finished days ago, but even the Duchess hadn’t believed her when she’d told her. She’d been asked to take more time to make sure it was perfect, and even better than her last.

She had gracefully accepted the delayed deadline and proceeded to make designs for new ones instead.

She stared at the three dresses before her. All of them were the sort of thing she might’ve only dreamed of wearing back home.

The duchess insisted she wear the like every day now. She was favored it seemed.

She sighed and picked the middle one. It was gorgeous. Everything was.

“My family might be dying right now, and I’m picking out dresses,” she thought irritably.

She couldn’t be too angry. She was making an absolutely incredible amount of coin. Even Mera would probably be impressed by four gold coins, but would’ve traded all of it for a hint of news from home.

Of course, that amounted to jack all. No amount of cajoling, begging, or even coin, was convincing any courier to take bandages south, let alone weapons.

“If only messages could be delivered as easily as thread,” she thought, staring at the marching lines of needles as they let the chosen dress drape down over her. Once the garment was on, more needles set about brushing her hair. She’d gotten quite good at it, and if she let them graze her scalp just right, it felt divine.

She would be early if she left now, but she felt it was better to wait around for her audience with the Duchess than to sit alone in her room any longer.

She left and began the long walk from her quarters to the Sitting Hall.

She’d presented her first dress in the grand hall, but since then she’d presented three more, each with widened eyes of delight, followed by the henpecking and the putdowns. All fake. All false.

The Duchess loved her dresses, and Hadra was almost tempted to tell her she wouldn’t make any more of them unless she stopped the almost habitual backhanded compliments and sly criticism.

That might backfire horribly though. She didn’t want to be here. She wanted to go back home, where she could help… but… well.

There was a war down there, and she couldn’t help but be thankful not to be anywhere near it.

“Gods Mera… what would you do if it were you up here?” she thought as she walked through the halls.

Probably be dead. Mera was a terrible liar and worse at hiding secrets. Was she at the rift now, working to contain it?

Two ladies appeared around the next corner. Rather than be offended, they stepped aside and bowed their heads as she passed. She had no idea who they were, but her clothing apparently marked her as higher ranked than some actual nobles here in the palace.

She had no idea how it all worked, and hoped to the gods that she didn’t ever run across anyone she needed to bow to.

Visiting nobles, family members, dignitaries, and rich merchants could be found in the palace at all times. Many were here to present new products and delicacies to the Duchess. Some to talk with her council, or advise her on matters of state. Some were here to beg for aid as their lands were coming under attack.

There weren’t any nobles around the Great Ophiscian. Hadra’s mother had taught her that there used to be, but the Summer of Bone had been devastating to the high and the low. Many strongholds and castles had been torn to rubble by the rift monsters back then.

She had learned that Pemolar’s Hill had technically been one of the provinces under the purview of Lord Eredon. He’d been gifted the land, and promptly never visited it or cared about it. Tornet’s Reach, Claremont, Dolundont, Mitoras, and all the other towns around the Ophiscian were a melting pot of lands belonging to nobles who’d already had ancestral homes and expressed little interest in relocating toward the Rift.

This attitude had been so prevalent, that eventually the King had simply gifted the lands to the Generals who controlled the three war camps, and as long as they continued producing their goods, was content to ignore them.

Hadra felt lucky. She felt like every interaction with nobles and the Duchess was like walking on a thin thread. One wrong word could put her in the stocks for a whipping.

She had an explosive temper, but was otherwise well in control of herself. Mera wouldn’t have lasted a week.

Heavens above she missed Mera…

She arrived at the Sitting Hall, proud that she hadn’t gotten lost. She held the dress, folded as neatly as she could manage. She’d been told this would be a more informal setting and that she should bring the dress, as if to a gathering among friends. No need for showmanship or excessive finery.

Wearing a dress that probably cost more than her entire home, she didn’t think she could’ve managed to feel more out of place.

Two guards stood at the door, and they stopped her.

“Lady Weaver. Perhaps it would be best if you left and attended to the Duchess in the morning?” the guard insisted, stepping in front of the door.

She frowned. That was odd. She’d never come to the Sitting Hall before, but no guards had ever even spoken to her when she’d been summoned. They’d just known who she was.

“The… duchess summoned me. I’m fifteen minutes early, but I–!”

“Lady Weaver. At the very least, it would be wise if you attend the Duchess at the appointed ti–!”

The door suddenly opened.

“Ah, my beautiful Ilhadira!” came the Duchess’s saccharine voice. “Come in, come in! I was just about to come fetch you, but of course, you anticipate my needs!”

Hadra smiled. Even if she could be overbearing and childish, the woman did certainly have a soft spot for her.

“Guard? What are you doing?” the blonde woman asked.

The man stepped aside and bowed. “Apologies my lady. I believed you were not ready to accept–!”

“Nonsense! She has my newest dress! I feel I’ve been waiting an eternity to see it.”

“I’ve… done my best!” Hadra said.

“As you ever have! I’m sure it will be even better than the last!” she exclaimed, as she placed her arm around the uncomfortable weaver and guided her in, her eyes fixated on the folded bundle Hadra carried.

Hadra stepped inside, but saw the guard grimacing. His eyes weren’t those of a scolded soldier. Instead he looked… defeated?

Moments later, she had no time to think about the odd reaction of a palace guard as she was swept into a room that gleamed like the sun.

Veils hung from the ceiling creating a canopy of curtains that felt more like a maze than a decoration. Magic torches with every color of magelight burned in sconces powered by lines of mana that trailed through the walls.

Etherveins. She’d only ever heard of them from stories, but she gasped at their beauty. Even here, where everything was amazing, the lights held something special.

“Do you like it?” Astra Denarla asked as she gazed around the room.

“It’s amazing,” Hadra commented, gazing at the contained lights glowing red, blue, yellow, and green.

The door shut behind them, startling both her and the Duchess. The woman turned, glaring at the door, but it seemed she decided to let it go.

“I’m so excited to have you here!” the Duchess cooed. “My Chamuet left to visit his mother and isn’t due back for days. I’ve wished very much to speak with you more privately.”

“Oh?” Hadra replied. “I confess, I still feel quite lost here. These clothes you’ve given me… the other ladies treat me like a noble.”

“A talent like yours deserves noble treatment. Even my own talent is Elite,” she replied. “Besides, I’ve had good luck recruiting commoners recently. Chamuet has proven both invaluable, and endlessly entertaining. Have you met him?”

“For a short time, my lady, when I presented you with your first dress,” she replied.

She had only met the man once but he’d left an impression. She wouldn’t mind meeting him again.

“This is the Weaver?” asked a petulant voice from a divan near the back of the hall, with two other women. Both of them had large men behind them, holding out their hands and conjuring balls of flame to keep the ladies warm.

It was called a hall but it was truly more of a lounging room. It was nothing alike, but Hadra suddenly found herself longing for the bar in Pemolar’s Hill.

“Indeed!” Denarla said. “Effira, this is Ilhadira. My weaver.”

The woman in the closest sofa nodded. “A pleasure, I’m sure.”

“Th-the pleasure is all mine my lady, Effira!” Hadra said, dipping into a curtsy.

“Come now, there will be none of that–Ahh!” the girl screamed.

The fireballs both suddenly winked out, as arrows sprouted from the men’s chests.

“Wh-what!?” Hadra exclaimed, horrified as armed men seemed to materialize from nowhere.

They grabbed the ladies lounging in the divans and held blades to their throats.

“What is the meaning of–!?”

The blade ran across lady Effira’s neck, and a line of blood poured down her beautiful dress.

“Gods! What is happen–Mmmph!”

The two other women were murdered just as quickly, and Hadra suddenly found her own hands wrenched painfully behind her back as soldiers seemed to materialize out of the room’s many veils, a sword appearing at her own throat.

“S-Sorry little weaver,” came a stuttering voice. “She… she has to die, and we can’t have any witnesses. Hope the gods treat you well, in your next life.”

“N-no! No wai–!” her voice was cut off as a saber cut across her neck.

The guard stepped away from her, but she lingered, still standing for half moment, clutching her throat. Warmth pooled down her chest.

Her… throat had been slit.

“Effira! Imrisalda! Olette! G-gods what is this!? Eschal!? It can’t be!” the Duchess cried as her arms were yanked behind her just like Hadras, and a blade placed right near her neck.

“You ignored all my advice.”

Hadra didn’t really notice when she’d fallen to the floor. Breathing was already becoming difficult. She was going to die. Just… collateral damage. Like she was nothing.

The voice was low. Cold. Hadra wasn’t sure if she’d ever heard the man before.

“You’ve heeded no warnings. Even now, our people are dying and you sit here, playing dress up in your halls like it means nothing!”

He walked down from the shrouded back of the room, his fingers idly running through the hair of one of the women his men had just murdered.

Hadra still had enough sense to think, though she didn’t know how long she had. The cut had been light, but any cut to the throat was fatal! Everyone knew that. Gods, she’d seen it play out in wounds from the attack at home!

“Mostorn!?” the Duchess cried. “You dare! The king will have your head for this! No! I’ll be taking it now! Guards! Arrest this man!”

No one moved.

“G-guards!? I am your duchess! Ahh!” she screamed as the guard holding her arms wrenched them further back, painfully.

The words had faded to a dull hum in the background, as a growing determination built, even as her lifeblood pooled on the floor. She tried to swallow. Couldn’t. She didn’t know how deep the cut was, but she knew that stopping the bloodflow was the only way she could survive.

Needles rose into the air.

Hadra was furious, but she shoved down the fury, turning it into cold concentration. She’d only get one shot at this. Even at the edge of death, the needles piercing her throat sent agony shooting up her spine.

She probably one of the best weavers in the entire country. She had needles. She had thread. She had a thin narrow slice. Veins were small, but not as small as the ridiculously thin silk she’d been given. More importantly, veins had no nerves. Through the blood, hadra found the slit in her own veins and began to stitch it together. Weaving had always come naturally to her, but even she could barely believe what she was attempting.

It was madness, but she had no other choice. The vein was so small, yet thick enough to gush blood. She should be dead. Would be in minutes if she didn’t get this right.

And she did. Slowly. Ever so slowly, the vein the sword had cut, closed, unnoticed as she lay on the floor.

“Without you, this kingdom might have a god's damned chance,” Mostorn said. His eyes were wild. He didn’t even seem to care about Hadra, eyes focused only on the Duchess. “They slaughtered my hometown, you know? Everyone in it. While you sit here and do nothing!”

“With you gone, and something will finally begin to–!” He cut off with a gasp.

Hadra had enough.

The man gloating had been the one giving the orders, so a needle pierced his throat first. Then she focused on the man who’d slit her throat.

Her needles spread out, practically invisible in the dim glowing veils. She’d blinded a troll before, with needles, just like Mera had done with her paste. This was no different. Just a bit more… visceral.

The man lost his eyes, with a shriek.

Mostorn was still choking so Hadra turned her needles on the others, not daring to move lest they realize what had happened. Her throat burned. Gods, there were so many. At least seven men, some with armor, and some disguised as servants, all glancing around for attackers they couldn’t see.

The next eyes to be pierced belonged to the man holding a blade to the Duchess’ throat, merely because he was in her line of sight, and that felt more natural to her. Hadra didn’t dare let that be her only strike.

“What is happening!?” one of the men screamed as he sliced uselessly at the veils.

The palace had given her such small, perfectly sharp ones to weave with, and thread so fine she’d been able to sew her own vein shut.

Still, it was only a matter of time before someone realized who was attacking them. Slowly, she began to crawl towards one of the rooms luxurious chairs, hoping to go unnoticed beneath.

The leader was quivering, and she felt the needle under his thumb as he tugged it out, holding it up to the dim light, a tiny river of blood sliding down his throat. She could practically feel the realization cross his mind, even as she tried to send more needles for his eyes.

“W-weaver. It’s the Weaver!” He cried.

“Fggg!” she tried to curse but only a gurgle bubbled forth. Blood still seeped lightly from the wound.

Abandoning stealth, Hadra took control of every needle she had. The sheer numbers made them much more clumsy and imprecise but she could barely lift herself.

Two more struck for the man’s eyes but he moved before she could hit him, before putting on a helmet with a thin visor. Needles swam around the room in currents, letting themselves be temporarily visible before dispersing in a hundred different directions. Visible, only long enough to prove they were a threat before seeking out all of the attackers.

Your Multitasking has improved!

The other men turned to face her immediately and began to lunge for her, but they didn’t make it far before they, too, screamed and clutched their eyes.

"Weaver! Please, you won't be harmed! Let us kill he–!" Hadra silenced that lie outright with more needles.

“I can’t see! Oh gods, I can’t see!” someone shouted, interrupting the madman. That gave Hadra the time she needed.

Another man was stabbed, nearly all the way across the room. It was much harder to control the needles she couldn’t see, so she missed his right eye, but managed to hit his left. Simultaneously, she knelt to the ground and grabbed up the sword Astra’s attacker had dropped.

She didn’t hesitate and made a wild downward swing, the blade cutting sharply into the neck of the man who’d grabbed her.

“Lady Weaver! You must know that the Duchess is killing us. Killing Denarla itself! She needs to die!”

“Fucgk you!” Hadra screamed as she crawled laboriously back to her feet. The loss of blood had her feeling lightheaded and dizzy, making her wonder just how the hell her needles felt so precise.

Clumsily, she ducked behind the veils hoping to get out of sight. To help this, she began to have clumps of needles gather around the sconces, blocking their firelight and casting the room in spots. She didn’t have enough needles to do more than dim a small area though.

“Please! Please call off your nee–!”

“Silence!” Astra screamed. “Guards! Guards! Attack! Murder! Murder in the Palace!”

The door didn’t budge. Didn’t the woman realize that her guards were the attackers!? These ones anyway.

“G-Guards!?” the Duchess’s voice was weaker now, shocked at the lack of response to her orders.

Hadra’s eyes narrowed as reason began to override her panic.

Needles covered the room. Glints of silver were everywhere and they spooked the terrified men more than they could actually hurt them. Numerous as they were, they were only needles. If any of these men wanted to keep their eyesight, they’d have no choice but to kill her, and this wasn’t working anymore. She needed a new method of attack.

Needles picked up the torn veils of the once beautiful room.

“Lady Weaver, I—oh heavens!” Mostorn gasped as he finally spotted her. Momentarily, her bloody neck stunned him even more than the assault. She must look like a monster out of a fairy tale.

A veil suddenly wrapped around his face.

A mere glance wrapped the two remaining soldiers in front of her, while more needles kept the rest distracted. She heard a mild thumping of footsteps from outside.

In agony, she turned and threw the dress she’d spent so many painstaking hours making into the face of another man trying to sneak up on her from behind one of the room’s ornate pillars. It wrapped itself around their mouths and twisted behind, but the needles weren’t strong enough to hold up such heavy cloth. The man threw away the garment and stared at her, bloody murder in his eyes.

He raised his sword to strike but without warning, a crown smashed into his forehead, knocking him back a few steps.

That was all the time the duchess needed to come hurtling toward him, dress flying behind her like a kite, before a small ceremonial dagger plunged into the attacker’s chest.

The men around the room let out still more muffled screams, as they grabbed and tore at the seemingly living cloth. Some escaped, but others didn’t.

Hadra crumpled to the ground, her body trembling, but her mind resolved. These men had tried to kill her! Just for being in the same room as their intended target!

The needles weren’t strong enough to keep the men from ripping the veils off their faces eventually, so she began to stab them anywhere she could. She stabbed and stabbed, her skill burning within her as she used her weaving in a way she’d never have dared to before.

The doors suddenly burst upon and Hadra turned, only to see a horde of guards pour into the room. Without hesitation, they began to slaughter the attackers, while forming a protective perimeter around the Duchess.

Finally, when the last attacker had a sword through him, she let her needles fall to the ground. Trembling. A spear was pointed at her, but she barely noticed it. The man looked more afraid of her than she was of him.

She was dizzy. Somehow she was leaning against a chair, gushing blood all over it, panting for breath. Was she going to die anyway?

"Hold!" The duchess cried. "She saved my life! Hold! She saved my damn life! Get that spear away from her you fool!"

Bubbling up within her, as she stared at the blood on her fingers, she had a hysterical thought. 'Maybe... maybe I'm on an adventure after all. Ey, Mera?'
