If you’ve never had to fight a giant millipede made of shadows, you’ve never lived.  My powerful uppercut transitioned into Batman-Descends-On-Van, my arms spread to either side like poor imitations of my real wings as I poured a torrent of fire into Art’s face.

The crimson lances from the bunnyborgs' mana-cannons slashed into his neck and sides, then they charged. Wrist blades slashed to the lyrics of a Wham! song about yoyos.  The borgs had an eclectic taste in Earth music.

Tunguska-Event-Was-Just-A-Light-Fondling sent me crashing heel-first into the top of his skull.  His extremely bony skull.  The shockwave from the impact ran up my leg, making my knee ache.  I backflipped away as my minions went to work.

“You’ve got some fight in you!”  Art sounded worryingly happy about this state of affairs.

“I’m going to enjoy this,” I replied as I hit the ground in a superhero landing pose.  My already fucked up knee buckled, but then my tunic was torn to shreds as I returned to my true, and most majestic form.  My head reared back, and I roared.  The echoes bounced back and forth within the Womb.

Three more portals opened around me, and BB-209s stomped through, the whine of their gattling-cannons spinning up caused Art to freeze for a moment.  Then they let loose and chunks of shadowy armour began to chip away, whizzing about like goth confetti.

I shook out my back leg to dispel the ache from the failed heel-stomp and landing, then slithered forwards.  Hungry-Hungry-Hydra set my tails to tangling his neck and his first few pairs of legs, but it left my flank exposed.

Three exceedingly pointy limbs stabbed into my scales and bounced off.  All that pain from the God of Crafting when he upgraded my outer shell… priceless.

“You are surprisingly tough.  But it’s always the same, the harder the coating, the gooier the insides.”

My jaws slammed closed on what I could only assume was the biological equivalent of battleship armour.

“Jeebus Thist!” My gums hurt.

“I also have a tough hide.”

I used How-Steve-Irwin-Should-Have-Died and coiled myself around his upper section and attempted to death-roll him down to the ground.  He was stronger than I’d given him credit for, and all I did was wrap up a bunch of his forelimbs.

It was enough.  My tails could reach a decent distance down his body, and we all know what happens when a load-bearing pillar is removed.  Art slammed down to the ground, his head crashing into my left side.

As a dragon, it was an unusual experience to be immobilised by a foe.  Sure, the Wyrm could have done it if I’d been stupid enough to get that close.  That prick of a mage who attacked the Mill before I outed my glorious real form had done some shit that made my wings feel like stone, and forced me to be earthbound for a while.  As a rule, though, I was usually free as a bird.

The Womb was big, damp and spacious.  The roof was a good fifty metres above my head, metres I could gladly use to keep this entemophile’s wet dream from trying to chew through my scales.  His somewhat disturbing interest in my gooey insides also felt slightly more sexual than I was comfortable with, but this might just be my being twitchy based on how degenerate most of the inhabitants of Helstat seemed to be.

Whatever his motivations, I wasn’t terribly happy about being eaten out.  Phrasing.  Having something make a hole in me and push its way inside…  There just wasn’t a good way to frame this conundrum.

Art shook violently, bouncing my back against the ground.  I unwound one of my tails and stabbed the chitinous tip into one of his many eyes.  His body reared back as a spray of grey ichor flew from the wound, and I took the opportunity to slip away from the pointy limbs that were trying to find out what my guts felt like.

My wings beat, feathers rustling, flying up to the ceiling where I sank my claws into the soggy rock and tried to think.  Claws, teeth, and dragon fire had all failed.  As had my most potent Dragon Fu moves. The minions were doing good work, but they weren’t doing it fast enough.

Even as the thought passed through my mind, they redoubled their efforts.  Wrist blades flashed at shadowy limbs, and more grey blood leaked out as armour was chipped away.  The BB-209s all focused their fire onto Art’s exposed underside.  He had reared up to try and reach for me, and my minions exploited the tummy scales being on full display.

The focused fire cut through, but Art rolled and squirmed away, crushing half a dozen bunnyborgs in his escape.  He began to clatter up the wall, his own legs biting deep into the rock as he climbed towards me.  I sent a blast of fire at him, but he shrugged it off and kept coming.

Time for the old one-two magical punch.

“Conjurare Barnaculum!” Best-spell, no one can say otherwise, summoned a wodge of crustacea inside Art's open mandibles.  My mage hands had been pulled when I retreated, and now I formed them all together, making a giant, hybrid fist to my side that shot across the gap between us and hammered my beshelled friends right down his gullet.

It looked like a lightning strike.  Two of the mage hands had taken the light orbs with them, and as the composite fist had moved, it had left an afterimage behind.  Unfortunately, it had also taken the only source of light in the cavern as well.

Not too much of a problem for me with the good old Predater-vision, nor for the cyborgs with their more esoteric visual systems, but Granvil and Bighorn both started cursing eloquently from below as they tripped and stumbled their way across the uneven ground in the sudden darkness.

Art did not look happy.  It was hard to tell, his face being composed of armoured plates that made expressions difficult at best. Nonetheless, that sight would forever be etched into my memory under the title of “pissed off shadow millipede monster”.  I needed to get my hands on a magic camera at some point for moments like this.  Maybe I could borrow Kat’s camera crew and have them remote-view and record all the good bits?

He began to twitch and shake as I broke the mega-fist apart, and had each hand use a fistful of barnacle shells as cutting tools on his much softer insides.

“Now I’m inside you instead,” I growled, then winced with regret.  “Not like that!”

A rumble of angry pain rang out, and then Art convulsed and fell to the ground with a thud.

“Those are fucking expensive,” I grumbled as I released my claws and dropped more gracefully, glaring at the smashed BB-209s he’d used to break his fall.

“What…”  The mage hands spread out inside him, like a drop of dye in intestinal water, moving down his length toward where his carapace split into two prong-like tails.   “You’re all up in my guts!”

“At least I bought you dinner first.  Seafood platter.”

Backing away, I turned and headed over to where Granvil and Bighorn were clutching each other in the darkness, huddled against a wall.  A lightorb blinked to life as I pulled it from storage and tossed it gently at them.

“I think we’ve got this covered, guys.”

“Is it dead?  You basically told it to eat my people!” Granvil accused.

“Hey, if King Stony-Beard agrees to join my faction properly, I’d have to deal with any other unspeakable ancient evils that ought not to see the light that you idiots dig up and piss off. Call it leverage.”

“If you can kill that thing… I’ll suggest he agrees.”

“And pays extra for the new variants of bunnyborgs?”

The strobe-like red flashes of mana-cannons firing were still flickering away as the minions continued to try to slice Art into manageable chunks.  They were making slow but steady progress.

The first body part sculptures had appeared.  Spidery things were being jammed together quickly to the refrain of “Don’t Worry, Be Happy!" which was a particularly disturbing soundtrack to accompany the steady dismemberment taking place.  The borgs' artistic expressions were spread across the current location of the battle.  Bits were tossed away to the troopers at range, who fired their mana-cannons, then quickly converted the body parts thrown at them into nightmare-fuel still lifes.

I padded closer, claws clicking on the rock, and continued internally destroying Art.  “I can’t get the kill, but my minions will benefit from your death.”

It hadn’t really occurred to me before.  Were the bunnies levelling up?  They did tend to fall into the category of disposable, and most of the earliest variants were likely scrap metal and smears of pulped flesh these days, but they had levels before implantation, and presumably continued to gain XP or… it would be biomass?

“You can’t kill me…ergh, what the hell?”  A number of my mage hands inside his abdomen had started slicing at the muscles that linked his segmented armour together.  “Look, maybe we can talk about this?  I can probably help you out!”

“Oh, now you want to bargain?  I offered you some primo grub to just be chill, but nah, you had to try and flex on me.”  Wrath was in a bit of a snit in my mind.  I padded closer, neck curled back and ready to strike if a broken section of armour presented itself.

“A bit?”

“Well, tough shit, Bugsy.  You had your chance, and the world has had quite enough of… whatever the hell you are.  Time to clean out the tunnels.”

“You’ll need me…”

Art’s movements were getting weaker.  More and more of his limbs were being turned into stickman figures in various obscene poses as the borgs repurposed his limbs.

My jaws moved closer to his array of eyes, and I gave him a level twelve grin.  “I’m not so sure.”

“You can’t reach the heart without a guide.  Dave will trick you, lead you astray.  He’s smart.  Outsider knowledge,” Art grunted.

“Like Kat?  I can always bring her over to help out.”

“Different… departments.”

“Is this some bureaucratic rivalry bullshit?  Some war between the ass—the lovely gods who run the WOO?”

“Yes!”  Art’s voice was pained and panicked now.  Almost all of his legs were gone, and sections of his carapace were being pulled away.  It was like watching ants chop us a piece of fruit and ferry the chunks back to the nest.  Except the nest was a wax works museum after a fire.

My worker ants stopped cutting and blasting in an instant, with no verbal command needed.  They stood frozen, the ones up on Art’s back simply kept their balance as what was left of him shifted slightly.

“And you can help me out?  But I don’t need you.  I can just ask Kat.”

“Won’t work.  She’s… got to play by their rules.  I’m a native…”

My snout was inches away from his eyes.  The inky darkness in them was dimming, and the reflective coating that made them shine ever so slightly was fading.

“I think it might be a bit late for you, Art.”

“Just… don’t freak out.  I won’t hurt you.”

“Erm, do what now?  You’re the one spread out like Ikea furniture before the build.”

One of the eyes, the one just above the tip of my nose, peeled open like a flower, and something squirmed inside.  It was pale white, and in keeping with Art’s motif, had far too many legs.

The albino millipede uncurled and slithered out on his suddenly lifeless former body.

“You might have to carry me?”  The foot-long creature said in a high piping voice.
