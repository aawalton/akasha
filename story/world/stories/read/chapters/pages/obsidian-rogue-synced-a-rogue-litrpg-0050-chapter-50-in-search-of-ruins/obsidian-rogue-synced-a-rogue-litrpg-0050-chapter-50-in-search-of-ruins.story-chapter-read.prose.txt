But Levi wasn't going to go down without a fight, and maybe there didn't need to be one.

I must applaud your bravery… or stupidity,” the man said as he toyed with one of the numerous rings on his fingers before suddenly fixing his gaze on Levi. “You stand before the Butcher of Talpha. The Black Baron, who has slaughtered his way to the upper echelon of magic. And if I tell you to take down your hood, you take it down because if I have to do it myself, you wouldn't be breathing.”

Levi cursed before slowly dropping his blade to his side and reluctantly pulling his hood down, wincing as direct sunlight from the window hit him in the face for the first time in weeks.

A satisfied smile shone on the butcher's face as he stared at Levi, or more precisely, his brand, which was now very visible due to the absence of his hood and the sunlight that spilled across his face, highlighting his facial features.

“I have wondered for days about the whereabouts of the obsidian stone, and I feared it might have fallen into the wrong hands. But, but thanks be to Cerpital it hasn't.” The blackbaron said. “ I take it that you met the retainers of your predecessor.”

“They were, “Levi said as he thought back to the events of that fateful night.

“Good. It would have been terrible for us to get off on the wrong foot.” The butcher turned and walked into the adjoining hallway at the end of the stairs, clearly waiting for them to do the same.

Levi turned to stare at Xara, who simply shrugged her shoulders, which was kind of a signal for him to know that the mage supported whatever decision he’d make.

In the end, Levi felt there was much to be gained from discussing with the Black Baron, so he cleared the rest of the stairs with the mage following, as they joined the Butcher of Talpha in the adjoining hallway, and quickly followed as he led them into a large room.

“Alright, have a seat,” the Black Baron said as he stretched a hand towards a few empty seats on a round table that looked ready to fall over at any moment. Despite their suspicions on the table, the pair were reluctant to offend the mage, so they sat beside each other at the other end of the table, while the Black Baron took a seat at the very head of the table, a few chairs away from Levi and Xara.

So the moment I sense a lie, we are done. And while I do not care about you,” the butcher said as he looked both Levi and Mage in the eyes, “I do very much need to ensure the safety of the obsidian stone. “So if I find you deceitful or telling half-truths, I will take the stone from you. And I'm sure you know what will happen if I do.”

I'll die, Levi thought. “I do.”

“But as touching as both your stories and journeys are to me, I must say that my utmost responsibility is to ensure the safety of the Obsidian Rook as well as its leader, and so far I am unimpressed seeing it being wielded by an E-ranker such as yourself,” the butcher said with eyes narrowed toward Levi. “However, it would be unfair of me not to give you a chance to prove that you are worthy of the Obsidian Stone, after all that you have gone through to get it here.”

Levi gripped his dagger underneath the table, not liking the direction the conversation was heading.

The Black Baron smiled as his eyes drifted towards Levi's hands around his daggers. “You can put those away because they sure won't help you against me. However, you would soon have need of them because I will be taking you to a C-rank dungeon, and if you are able to survive and impress me while doing so, I will be open to considering leaving you with the stone and taking you to the council to claim your seat as the Obsidian Rogue. Now, only one question remains,” the butcher said as his fixed gaze on the rogue grew more intense. “Are you willing to prove yourself worthy of this stone, or do I have to tear it out of you?”

Levi gritted his teeth.

He had known that his stake for the stone would be contested, and to be honest, he really didn't care about being the Obsidian Rogue or having the damned stone, which he would have happily given back to the Baron. But the only way the stone could change hands was if he lost his life. And Levi very much hated that scenario, so he was back to the same situation he had been on that fateful night, with another mage asking him to choose between taking the stone or losing his life.

The only difference being a harder task was set before him, as a C-ranked dungeon was an almost impossible task for Levi to overcome, just as a fight against Ghost would be. On thinking back, he realized that he had struggled even with Xara by his side in an E-rank dungeon a few days ago. And now he would risk having to fight in a C-rank dungeon at the very least, when all signs pointed to it being almost impossible to survive.

“Of course, I am not as heartless as they make me be,” the Black Baron said. “Some call me the Butcher of Talpha and others the Black Baron, but in truth, I am only a soldier made to defend Rinesta from the greatest of threats. And the Obsidian Rogue is a position that should be filled by one who is able to hold their own against the threats of our world. So do not think for a second that I ask you to prove yourself just because you are not of noble blood, or that I want to snatch the stone from you. No, I ask you to prove yourself because the task that lies ahead, if you do take your seat as the Obsidian Rogue, goes a lot further than any single C-ranked dungeon could ever compare.”

This was the first time he had heard about threats to Rinesta, and as long as he had lived, it had been very peaceful. Even with the recent frequent Monster leaks. But the Black Baron definitely hadn't been talking about Monster leaks in such a manner, so he had to wonder what the Butcher had meant by threats.

The Black Baron shifted his attention to the mage. “You haven't told him?” he asked.

Levi turned his attention to Xara, who had a guilty look on her face. “I didn't think it was my place to,” she said.

“Well, it is now,” the Black Baron said. “Tell him.”

Xara took in a deep breath and let it out dramatically. “Rinesta has been plagued by a particular breed of high-level synced monsters in recent years. We call them Ragnar's creatures beyond what the average synced can handle, even in groups.”

“Very few times do they attack, but once they do, the casualties are devastating because the lowest of them are B-rank, and there's confirmation of an S-Ranked Ragnar, which is where those on the council come in. The power of the founding stones granted to them massively improves the chances of the people of Rinesta.” Xara said. “For the most part, we have had major failures when it comes to winning the battle against the newer crops of Ragnars who have been multiplying at a rate that we cannot possibly match, and now the elite synced of Rinesta are stretched too thin, which is why we are beginning to lose the battle.”

“Tell him about the dotted lines,” the Black Baron said.

Xara sighed, “There are some among the upper echelons of the Rinestas synced elites who believe that the Ragnars are somehow connected to the increasing frequency of monster leaks, but we haven't been able to prove it or eradicate the Ragnars, and it almost seems like we are fighting a losing battle.”

Levi couldn't believe what he was hearing. It seemed like he had been living in a shell all these years, oblivious to what was really happening in Rinesta. And the nobles had been the ones keeping the true information from those at the bottom, like himself.

“So you see, why I must make sure you have the potential as well as current capability to step into that battlefield like the rest of us.” The Black Baron said, “I’d call you a weak riff raff, but I was once like you. Weak, with not a single drop of noble blood in my body. But I have risen across the ranks, struggled, shed blood, poured sweat, lost friends, lost family, just to become the A-rank that I am today. And I have to know that all my sacrifices were not in vain.”

“So if you cannot prove that you can handle a C-rank dungeon, or at the very least impress me, the stone will be taken from you and given to another who will aid the efforts of the war,” the Black Baron said. “Of course, since you have said that your journey has been intertwined with Lady Noir, then I find it acceptable to let the two of you battle together in the C-rank dungeon. That is, if she wishes to participate. But if she refuses, your only options are either to impress me in the C-ranked dungeon or fight me now… and lose.”

Levi paled at the Black Baron's words, as it was now very clear that his life partly rested in the hands of the mage, because if she said no and refused to fight with him in the C-ranked dungeon, then he might as well take his chances against the Black Baron, because there was no way he could survive a fight in a C-ranked dungeon alone and a death by fighting the Black Baron might be more merciful and quicker than being killed by monsters in the dungeon.

Each one of them lost in their own thoughts as they considered the implications of what was going to happen if they so much as failed to impress the Black Baron or clear the C-ranked dungeon.

I can't believe I worked this hard just to come here and hear this rubbish, Levi thought, because not only had he now endangered his life, he had done the same to the mage, and it was all his fault because he hadn't had the foresight to plan ahead in case he didn't get any help from the Black Baron. Of course, the Black Baron hadn't said he wasn't going to help, but this seemed more like a death sentence than anything.

Many negative thoughts crossed his mind, and Levi found it very hard to push them aside because he knew he could have done something else. But the goal of getting to the Black Baron, who he hoped would be able to solve all his problems, had seemed too alluring, especially with an infamous assassin hot on his heels.

Levi supposed he should have known better, as he had been raised to expect that not all things were painted in black and white, and that something like this could have happened. But then again, he had to believe in himself and Xara. If there were any E-rankers that he knew that would be able to clear a C-rank dungeon, it would have to be himself and the mage.
