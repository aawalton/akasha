I’ve caught you! Bastet crows eagerly. I’ve killed you, Packleader. I can eat you now.

I roll my eyes.

“It’s only one of my pieces,” I tell her. “I’ve still got three out.”

I’ve still caught you, she reminds me, bashing my piece with her talon and knocking it off the board, then nudging her own one into place.

“Alright Lathani, your turn,” I sigh, then make a startled noise as Bastet nudges me hard enough to knock me over. I can’t help but break out into a grin when she lays her head on my chest, looking at me smugly.

I caught your piece, she tells me. I’m going to win.

No, I am, Lathani tells her competitively as she spins the hexagonal cuboid held by a metal piece held up by two rock spurs. Since their paws would find it difficult to throw dice, I had to improvise.

You’ve only got two pieces out, River reminds her. The rest of us have at least three out – even Markus.

I’ve got three now, Lathani tells her smugly as the spinning cuboid stops on a six and she’s able to nudge a piece out of ‘home’ and onto the plus sign shaped board. It’s something I created with magic this morning when I woke up far too early and couldn’t get back to sleep.

River, seeing how I was allowing my mind to torture myself about everything I should have done and didn’t do, suggested playing a game. I chose this one – I used to play it with my parents when we were on holiday. It has good memories attached to it. Better ones now – I never imagined my companions would be so fun to play a game like this with.

River takes her turn and I resign myself to being Bastet’s cushion for now. I don’t mind it all that much and lift my hand to stroke it through her fur. But, no longer distracted by the game, my thoughts start to wander again.

The Tier four parasitic controller is dead, so it won’t send any more beasts to attack us. It’s been two days since then and the healers and I have finished clearing out the parasites from those we’d identified as infected. We still need to examine everyone else just to be sure – it’s a pity that the parasites didn’t go inert with the death of the Tier four, but at least they don’t seem to cooperate in the same way now the mastermind is dead.

Their stealth is a lot worse too – I don’t need a Dominate Bond anymore to find them, though it does make things easier. Nor do they self-destruct as easily – they’ve got their self-preservation back. It meant I was able to inspect the hatchlings and remove the parasites infecting half of them without too much difficulty. I’m still considering Binding them, though, for the other advantages a Bond offers – I’m in two minds about that.

All of that means that I finally don’t feel like I need to spend every minute of the day in the healing rooms – the healers are more than capable of removing the parasites in their current state. I only need to be called in when there’s a very deep-seated infection or the parasite’s network is particularly extensive. A Bond really helps in both those cases.

With Elarion’s keen senses, the Water Mages have cleaned out each of the wells, so we don’t have to fear reinfection from that vector, and I made sure to cleanse the underground tunnels with fire – once the Tier four was dead, the protection of its domain vanished. The only risk is that some parasite-controlled beasts escaped into the forest despite the best efforts of those outside the cave system to prevent them from doing so. They were all Tier ones and twos, so chances are they’ll be eaten before they can become dangerous, but it does make me wonder what happens if a more powerful beast eats an infected one. I’d like to think that the parasite dies too, but given that they seem to have infected my people orally, I fear that it’s not as simple as that.

Still, there’s no point in worrying about that right now. Without their mastermind, the individual parasites are a minimal threat – they’d have to infect a Tier four to pose any real threat to us and, based on them not doing so before, I have to guess that they aren’t capable of it. Our hunters and butchers know what to look out for – no one will eat any meat that hasn’t been thoroughly checked first, and we’ll check the beasts regularly.

At least the massive amount of meat that we’ve gained thanks to the battles with the Tier four’s minions means that our food situation is far easier. And Elarion’s been in the fields most of every day ever since he escaped the network node, frequently consulting with Artio. Hopefully, he’ll come up with a solution soon. If nothing else, we have enough Earth Mages here that we could create land of our own – an island if that is the only way to keep it free of this network.

All of that combines to mean that I can actually have a relaxing morning for the first time in I don’t know how long.

Bastet, let him up, would you? Markus, it’s your turn. The heavy weight shifting off my chest with a grumble is an indication that I’m being allowed to continue playing. Rubbing at Bastet’s ears, I lean over and spin the die.

Finally, we finish the game – River wins, unsurprisingly since she only had two pieces get sent home, unlike the rest of us who had at least four restarts. Bastet is second. I’m next and Lathani staunchly refuses to accept that she’s last, insisting that she ‘let us win’.

“Good game," I tell them, doling out scratches to all participants – cheek rubs to River. Trouble soon comes to demand his own scratches, Blaze and Spot quietly insisting on having their turn too.

Then, checking my watch and seeing that it’s about breakfast time, I get myself dressed and we head out to find some food. Since the crisis with food is over, we’ve gone back to eating in the normal places, so I head downstairs and into the dining room.

There, I’m surprised to see Layton, Valence, and Elarion already there – particularly the last. Valence is digging into a pile of meat with quiet focus, while Elarion is nursing a cup of tila, a faintly grumpy frown on his face. Layton is leaning back in his seat with a thoughtful look on his face. I’d guess he’s waiting for food rather than having just finished based on the clean cutlery before him.

“Good morning,” I greet, striding into the room. We exchange nods and they greet me in return as I slide into the seat at the head of the table. A moment later, a server appears at my elbow.

“Can I get you some breakfast, my lord?” she offers professionally.

“Yes, please.” I listen as she relates the options – almost all some form of meat, cheese, or eggs – and then make a selection. She pours me some tila from a silver teapot – tilapot – on the table, and then hurries away.

“You’re later than usual this morning, Markus,” Layton tells me with faint amusement.

I send him a wry grin.

“I’ve been awake for hours – playing a board game with my companions.”

“A board game?” Valence asks. “What is that?”

“I’ll show you sometime,” I promise, not feeling like explaining right now. I look at Elarion. “I’m surprised to see you here. I would have thought you’d be in the fields.”

“I’ve been there already this morning,” he admits. Then, seeing my surprised look, “I woke up and my mind started considering solutions to our farming challenges,” he admits after a few moments of silence. “It was not conducive to elongating my slumber, much as my body aches for it.”

“Are you still suffering from the fight?” I ask, concerned. He’d mentioned that the Forestheart Skill took a great deal out of him, even once he released it and the tree died, but I hadn’t realised that the aftereffects would extend so long after it.

“Minimally,” he replies shortly, which I take to mean he’s suffering more than he wants to admit.

“Have you seen the healers?” I press. He sends me a slightly annoyed look.

“They can do nothing for this.”

“Alright,” I sigh, not enjoying knowing my ally is in pain. “Well, if you’d like me to check you out, let me know,” I offer.

“It is not the first time I have overextended a Skill,” he dismisses. I don’t take offence – I knew it was unlikely he would take me up on it, but had to make the suggestion nonetheless.

I turn to Layton.

“I assume you didn’t wake up early to go out to the fields.”

“No,” Layton agrees, then gives a wry grin. “I think I am just too used to urgency. I woke up and could not return to slumbering.”

“I know what you mean,” I sigh. “That’s why I was playing board games – to distract my mind a bit. Where’s Josiah?”

Layton gives a little chuckle.

“Snoring fit to wake the gods when I walked past his room.”

I smile.

“At least he’s able to lie in.” I look over at Valence. “How about you – you didn’t want to sleep in a bit longer?”

Valence shrugs.

“I never do. Mornings are the best times to train.”

Layton and I exchange looks, both completely agreeing on one thing: morning people are odd.

Conversation pauses briefly as a server comes in with two plates of food – one for me; one for Layton.

“Speaking of the battle, thanks to all the Energy it offered, it only took me a single hunt yesterday to push me to level forty-three,” Layton reveals once the server leaves the room again, his tone one of someone bursting with pride but doing his best to be nonchalant about it. “That makes two whole levels since we left Moriax. It took me a year to go from level forty to forty-one.”

“You’re above level forty already?” Valence asks in surprise. “I’m only at level thirty-eight.”

“And what were you when you left Moriax?” I ask curiously. Generally asking about someone else’s status sheet is impolite, and a grounds for a duel if pressed, but given that he’s already been open about his current level, I figure that it should be fine.

“Level thirty-five,” Valence tells me, a proud smile tugging at the corners of his mouth.

“Well done,” Layton congratulates him. “Three levels is impressive.”

“I, too, have made some progress,” Elarion admits, the grumpy lines in his forehead lessening. “Two levels as well.”

We look at him expectantly. He pretends not to see, taking a bite of his food and chewing slowly.

“You don’t have to tell us if you don’t want to,” I tell him just before feeding myself with a bite of my own breakfast. I hum in pleasure – the cooks have done a good job today.

Layton and Valence look faintly disappointed, but I do my best to seem unaffected, no matter how curious I am.

“I am not so cruel as to deprive you,” Elarion says a moment later, a gleam of amusement in his eyes. I’m glad to see that he seems to be shifting into a better mood. “I have reached level forty-six.”

We all congratulate him and he even allows himself a moment to bask in the praise. At level forty-six, he’s among the highest-levelled of the expedition.

“That is truly impressive,” Layton admits. “Two levels in less than a year when past level forty-five…. The last time I heard of such fast progression, it was during the last civil war.”

“Well,” Elarion remarks with a sardonic expression. His eyes come to rest on me. “Travelling with Markus seems almost as dangerous.”

I mock bristle.

“I resent that,” I humph, crossing my arms theatrically and putting my nose in the air.

Quiet chuckles roll around the table. I’m privately glad that we’ve built up enough camaraderie that we can joke around like this without fearing that someone will be offended. Still, given everything the last nine months has held, it would be more surprising if we were still at the level of stiff manners.

“In all seriousness, the last time I was in situations of danger with such regularity was when I was fighting on the frontlines against the Nethyar,” Layton admits. “However, even then I could not have said that the individual encounters were as perilous. I would never have dreamed to have encountered a Tier five. The two Tier fours were bad enough.”

Valence shakes his head.

“It is just as well that we have no means of contacting home. I fear my father would have a heart attack, else. The most dangerous situation he allowed me to be in was fending off a beast wave on one of our cities – and that was only with a guard to ensure I was not truly harmed.” I decide to stay quiet about the miniature teleportation circle Sarran has that, while very expensive to operate, has allowed us to keep Nicholas at least a little up to date. A pity it’s only one-way.

“My mother was the same,” Layton commiserated. “But my father insisted that I join the army – thought it would be good for me. I hated it at first, but the experience has been incalculably valuable to me since I became lord of my House.”

“My father believes calculated danger is necessary for growth as well,” Elarion agrees. “However, his tasks are nothing like what I have experienced on this journey. Chaos seems to be Markus’ constant companion. We should take advantage of the time to rest while we can – who knows how long this period of quiet will last.”

I glare at the lord.

“Don’t jinx us,” I warn him.

“Jinx?” Elarion mutters, but Valence interrupts before I can decide how to explain it.

“Markus, you are the only one to have not revealed your progress. Would you care to do so?” He sounds eager, though his tone is still polite.

I tap the table with an enigmatic smile on my face.

“I’m level thirty-five,” I admit after a moment. Then, allowing my smile to grow a little. “I was level thirty when I left.”

There’s a moment of silence, then Layton curses, surprisingly colourfully for the usually restrained lord.

“Five levels?” he demands. “How?!”

“You’ve almost caught up with me,” Valence points out, confusion creasing his brow. “Yet we have been through the same situations.”

I shrug.

“Perhaps my Wisdom is higher than yours,” I point out – that is the stat that determines how much Energy someone absorbs, after all. “I also gain a little Energy from my companions – not much, but the higher Tier their prey, the more they will send to me.”

It’s something that doesn’t seem to follow any mathematical calculation – or if it does, it’s a complex one that takes into account factors such as depth of the Bond, how much Energy my companion wishes to give, how much I am willing and able to take, as well as the actual amount of Energy they’re gaining. Still, Nicholas indicated that it exists and I’ve seen enough circumstantial evidence to be confident in his assertion.

My breakfast companions don’t appreciate the ‘cheat’ that it gives me, if their half-serious comments are anything to go by. And I don’t even tell them about the Energy Hearts I’ve also been absorbing – until or unless I manage to establish a new supply of those, I don’t see much point in talking about them.

The conversation drifts onto other topics, but I quietly fade into the background. The discussion of levels has got me curious about what my status would say about my progress.
