Chapter 91: The Manor

Leo had never understood why nobles were so obsessed with living in the most needlessly complicated buildings imaginable. He slammed shut the door of the fourth closet he’d mistaken for a possible room, then continued stalking down the hallway.

Lord Mercox’s manor had been like that too, he thought idly. He turned the corner and hurried down the next corridor. He hadn’t noticed it as much back then, having grown up in those labyrinth-like halls, but in retrospect it had probably been even worse than this.

Leo’s jaw tensed, and he shoved the thought down. “Fucking illusions,” he muttered. All he’d needed was a glimpse and now here he was, thinking back on memories he hadn’t dared touch for half a decade. He’d built a new life since then, he was a goddamn [Fragmentholder] for crying out loud, but all it took was one little nudge for him to feel like the scared servant he’d once been.

Cursing, Leo urged his legs to move faster. He pulled up the party map again and saw that Spade had wandered a little closer. Maybe she’d be able to find the manor herself, Leo hoped. The [Executioner] didn’t strike him as the kind of person to get derailed by the past. She was probably the most mentally strong of them, if those scars were any indication.

Allan’s dot, on the other hand, hadn’t moved at all. It was disconcerting, and Leo couldn’t help the creeping unease that rose the longer and longer it took to find the [Healer].

No signs of Lord Lowthell either. Leo had expected at least another illusion or spell at this point—the man clearly had the magic range for it. But there was nothing at all. The silence was unnerving.

Finally, Leo swung open a door that revealed a spiraling staircase. Exhaling in relief, Leo first used [Judgement] to make sure they weren’t fake, then quickly ran up them. A few windows separated each flight, but outside, he couldn’t see anything beyond the thick mist and the dark silhouette of the forest. Leo moved faster. The air was colder up here, and he unconsciously tugged his cloak closer as protection against the chill. His footsteps echoed in the staircase, and it only added to his nerves.

Finally, Leo made it up to the top. There, a single door swung open into a narrow passage. He quickly discovered the source of the cold. This hall, if it could be called that, was a bridge connecting his current location to the center tower he’d seen earlier. The walls were open and arched, letting in the strong gusts, and when he looked down, he had a clear view of the manor below.

Leo swallowed and quickly crossed the stone walkway. It felt sturdy enough below him, and he didn’t have a problem with heights, but he wasn’t taking any chances.

His steps slowed as he neared the tower proper. The bridge led to a small landing space where windows in every direction gave him a full view of the surroundings. Leo could see chalk lines etched across the stone floor, and when he bent down to rub at them, they flaked away easily. Fresh. He straightened, narrowed eyes turning back to the spiraling stairs running throughout the tower. He was almost positive Lord Lowthell had been here earlier.

Leo stepped closer to the staircase. Now the question was, up or down? On the party map, his dot practically overlapped with Allan’s, which meant the [Healer] was in this tower somewhere on a different level. He grit his teeth. He was half-tempted to call out, but that would risk Lord Lowthell hearing him.

He tried to use [Distant Eyes], but the spell wasn’t working. He couldn’t tell if it was because he needed to touch the target, then activate the spell immediately afterwards for it to work, or if it wasn’t working because Allan was unconscious. The latter was a chilling thought.

His fingers clenched into a fist. Screw it. He couldn’t afford to waste more time here. That pervasive unease in his gut, the creeping chill that had followed him since entering the manor—that was at its strongest when he looked down. With that in mind, he threw caution to the wind and bolted down the stairs, half jumping down the flights with a few uses of [Air Walk] to right himself when he missed a step or fell too far.

Leo reached the bottom of the staircase in record time. It was dark there, no windows around to break up the cold stone walls encircling the space. If he looked up, he could see the staircase winding upwards until it disappeared beyond his range of vision. He couldn’t hear the wind at all here. The air itself was stifling.

A single metal door sat at the bottom of the stairs. There was nowhere else to go, and when Leo pressed his ear against it, he couldn’t hear anything inside. Given how thick the door was, though, that didn’t mean anything. He crouched down and unsheathed his dagger, holding at the ready in one hand while his other fumbled for his lockpick. It took some fiddling, but finally the lock clicked open.

Leo tensed, waiting, but still there was no movement. He stored his lockpick away and took a deep breath. Fingers tightening around the dagger, Leo mentally counted to three, then he kicked the door open and quickly scanned the area.

Inside the confined room, there was no sign of Allan or Lord Lowthell.

Instead, he found bodies.

There had to be at least fifty of them. Propped up against the cold stone walls, heads lolling forward. Fallen limply onto their sides. Crumpled to the floor with sprawled out limbs. Some were entirely skeletons, white bones stark against the dark greys of the space. Others had decomposed to a point where their bodies had become a twisted mixture of bones and rotting flesh. The overpowering stench immediately hit Leo’s nostrils, a mixture of iron, decay, and a sickly sweet undertone lingering beneath it all.

He quickly clapped a hand over his nose, shoving down the instinctive lurch in his stomach. He could feel stinging bile rising up his throat.

Doing his best to breath in as little air as possible, Leo slowly crept forward, his dagger still raised. The room was fairly compact, a simple rectangle entirely enclosed in hard stone walls with no windows or other openings in sight. Chalk lines and other runes were etched into the floor below, and he could see dark stains where old blood had dried and crusted.

There were no signs of life or movement inside the chamber. No weapons or food or water. There was only him and the dead bodies.

Swallowing, Leo carefully moved further inside. The bodies were all gathered around the perimeters of the room, leaving a pathway in the center to walk down that led towards a small raised platform in the back holding up a large box. There were no chains or other shackles that Leo could see, nor any other signs of struggle. Given the emaciated state of most of the bodies, that fact immediately jumped out at him. Some of these people had clearly starved to death, imprisoned inside the room for a long period of time, so how had they all been kept in place? There weren’t even scratches on the floor.

He stepped towards the back first, fingers still clenched around his dagger. Small steps led up to the platform. When he reached the top and looked down, he found another body lying within the box. Compared to the other corpses, the clothes the woman inside wore were significantly more expensive in appearance. Someone had closed her eyes and folded her arms over her chest. She looked like she was merely sleeping, but when Leo activated [Judgement], nothing came up. [Judgement] didn’t work on the dead.

Frowning, Leo spun around to face the bodies placed throughout the room. He stepped away from the box and crouched down to get a better look.

Based on their clothing, these people had probably been villagers, but he found it hard to believe that all of them had come from the town they’d passed. Lord Lowthell must have taken people from other places, too, probably snatched up unknowing travelers lost in the mists.

A few of the bodies even looked fresh, not too far in condition from the pristine corpse lying in the box. Leo stopped by one of them—a young man who didn’t look much older than him, and that just sent his stomach rolling again—and placed a hand on his pulse. His eyes widened when he felt a faint beat.

“Hey!” Leo carefully shook the villager. His head lolled to the side. His brown eyes were open, but they weren’t seeing anything. They stared listlessly forward. “Can you hear me?” Leo called again.

No response. He quickly activated [Judgement].

Name: ThomasAge: 21Level: 10Class: [Field Worker, Tier 0]Personal Skill: [Optimism Lvl 1]Status: [Mind Locked]

Leo’s brows furrowed. He’d never seen a status like that before. A cold sense of dread was quickly rising in his gut.

The listless stare, as if the villager wasn’t fully present but instead lost in a memory. The lack of restraints around the room. The dead woman in the back, dressed in the finery of a noble. Lord Lowthell’s mysterious sick daughter who’d disappeared from the public eye.

“I believe those of us with illusory magic possess a greater obligation to truth than anyone. We may manipulate what others see, but we ourselves must never be drawn into our own illusions.”

Sol’s voice echoed throughout Leo’s mind. His heart thudded.

The [Fragmentholder] sprang to his feet, the room and other bodies forgotten as he bolted for the door. He didn’t know the specifics of what was happening, what exact magic Lord Lowthell was using, but it was clear that he’d drawn these people in with illusory memories of the past, then found a way to lock themselves inside their own minds until their bodies withered away and died.

The villager, Thomas, was still technically alive even though his mind was gone. Which meant their party status sheet health percentages meant nothing.

Leo threw a hand back, casting [Firebolt] to launch himself upwards. He propelled himself up the stairs using a mixture of [Sprint], [Air Walk], and [Firebolt] to climb up the tower at record speed, passing the center landing location in less than a minute. He knew he was wasting mana, but at the moment he didn’t care. All he could think about were the dead stares of those bodies, the fact that Lord Lowthell hadn’t been in the room and was probably with Allan right now.

Leo gripped onto the railing and vaulted over, landing at the very top of the tower. Sharp wind whistled through the open structure, the building overlooking a sea of silvery mist, but Leo didn’t slow down or give his surroundings a second glance.

Raising his hand, he launched a [Firebolt] straight at the door. A jet of flame flew towards the metal hinges, heating them up and softening them. Before the fire had fully dispersed and the hinges had a chance to harden again, he stepped back and kicked down the entire thing.

The door slammed down onto the ground with a heavy thud. A cloud of dust rose from the impact, but Leo didn’t blink. He kept one hand around his dagger, raised and pointed at the center of the room.

There, a tall figure dressed in a long embroidered cloak slowly rose and turned weathered features towards him. [Judgement] immediately confirmed the man’s identity. Leo’s eyes narrowed.

“You.”
