I had a desire.

An old, rotted desire.

One that I couldn’t let go.

No matter what.

It was cold. Cold and heavy. The sensation of drowning in his own subconsciousness, sinking continuously down. Shuren struggled to keep his eyes opened, his eyelids shivering. Within a few breaths, that flickering wick of his clarity soon dissipated.

With his eyes opened, he stared at the white and dull ceiling. Yet, with open eyes, he recalled an old memory.

It was cold. Cold and heavy. Shiny and beautiful. Supreme and divine. When he had first wielded a sword in his youth, these were the thoughts that came to his mind. And with that steel in hand, everything clicked. As if he were whole again.

“Remember that feeling. For one day, you must grasp it within your body. You must feel complete without it in your hand.” His grandfather stood in a small cave, while the rain outside pelted towards the mountain side. In front of the young Shuren was a small cup of milk, peppered with obsidian crystal shards, half empty.

With the sword held over his hand, Shuren looked towards his grandfather. And he imparted to him a shard of wisdom; an aspect of the sword.

“For a true swordsman can merely use their conviction to wield a sword. An extension of their heart. Its hilt held by pride, its waves a formation of thoughts. When the body is forged to be a true sword.”

These old memories burnt away in the depths of his mind like burnt oil paper. Even if the paper had burnt, the flame continued to flicker.

And that fragrance of oil would forever remain.

Shuren ignored the heaviness of his body, raising himself up from his bed.

He sat in silence, within the cold room.

No, it was not the room that was cold, but his heart.

Something had been hollowed out.

Something vital, and intrinsic to his body.

Yet, Shuren held a calm expression even as he failed to recognise what he was missing. That sensation was so prominent that Shuren didn’t even notice the dull and sore pain coursing throughout his entire body, nor the stitches in his abdomen.

Without the thought coming across his mind, Shuren grasped for his abdomen with his hand, through his clean hospital robes. He ignored the old sensation of scars that scattered throughout his torso, that gradually grew larger towards his left side of his chest.

“You recovered quite fast. Here I was thinking you’d be unconscious for a week,” Wuden said as he leaned against the wall, wearing nothing but a white gown. When he personally helped with Shuren’s injuries, he held some thoughts at the back of his head.

This guy, he’s no ordinary scion of the Liu Clan. Especially with those marks I saw…

“The only thing you need to worry about is the soul injury you have. It will gradually heal up, albeit taking multiple months, if not longer. But… If you plan to break through to Path Foundation soon, don’t. Not until your soul is in stable condition.” Wuden warned Shuren, leaving the room. “Sei will look after you in the meanwhile. Let her know if you need anything.”

The door closed without a sound as Wuden left. Shuren was left to his own devices, but he said no words. All he did was bring out his right hand, staring at it.

His left eye twitched.

“If you want to bring fruits to a patient, make sure they’ve been cleaned thoroughly. Do you understand?” Lady Sei, one of the disciples of Elder Harmony, criticised the swordsman in front of her.

“They’ve just ripened, it's fresh and clean.” Ming took out a crimson persimmon and took a bite, the taste of honey trickling down his lips. He made sure to rotate the persimmon to show her the ripeness. “What makes you think this is dirty in any way?”

Irritation surged across her face, though Ming couldn’t tell. “Don’t eat the food if it’s for the patients.” She grabbed the bag of persimmons off Ming Wu, casting a veiled cleansing field on it. A simple technique known to all healers to ensure sanitation. “This is an emergency care room, so follow the rules. And don’t step in again with a sheathed weapon. Do you even clean that thing? Make sure any dirty blood-”

Ming raised both his palms towards her while holding the bitten persimmon, the sword on his waist gone. “Better? Just let me walk in for a visit brother.”

“B-brother?! ” That struck a nerve in the lady in front of him. Sure she was wearing a surgical cap that covered her hair, and was tightly bound with a white overcoat, but this guy in front of her shouldn’t have mistaken her for a man. She huffed, shoving the bag of persimmons back into Ming Wu’s hands. “That’s lady to you!”

“Sorry, slip of the tongue. Apologies, lady.” Ming raised an eyebrow as he watched the healer storm off. He chucked the half bitten crimson persimmon straight into his mouth, devouring it in one bite. *I should stop hanging out with just other boys all the time.

Ming strode in, allowing the sanitising veil to encompass his body. Though it was quite early, he knew Shuren had a set routine. He’d always be up this early, and he lay there on his bed reading a book. Ming didn’t need to squint to see the fine print on the leather bound exterior. ‘Dan Zi’s Scriptures. The number one poet beneath the heavens!’. Strange. He had never heard of the name, for the supposed greatest poet.

Shuren naturally looked towards Ming, setting his book aside. He had a weary gaze as he faced Ming. “What’s with that face? Come sit down.”

“You don’t look so bad yourself. I was expecting you to be in an absolute miserable mood, even if you didn’t technically lose.” Ming joked. “They called the duel a draw. I kind of wish I stayed to watch.”

“It should’ve been an easy victory. But that Tez somehow had a soul damaging technique at Rank 1. Tch, he wouldn’t even have landed it on me if I thought he had already lost. But I believe he’s being investigated now for his strange techniques. He’s no normal disciple.”

Ming sat on the visitor’s chair while placing the bag of persimmons at the bedside. “I reckon he’s got a master at least. Anyway, I got you some thousand year old Elysial Persimmons.”

Shuren nonchalantly stuck his hand inside, grabbing a persimmon. “Thanks. Didn’t know you had a legendary fruit from a forlorn realm.”

“Hah. Don’t eat it yet.” A plate had appeared out of nowhere and Ming chucked it at Shuren. Of course, he caught it with ease and placed the persimmon on top.

Shuren sighed. “You’re a Rank 2. Since when did you break through to Path Foundation?”

“Not long ago, during the week you were stuck here.”

Ming pointed his index finger at the persimmon, as he shouted, “Cut!” The persimmon sliced itself into multiple pieces. Though the shapes weren’t quite even.

Shuren ate a piece as he mumbled out, “You done showing off?”

“Yeah yeah. You should’ve been at this stage by now as well.”

Shuren decided to ask a question. One he had asked many times before. “So, how was it like entering Path Foundation?”

Ming held a serious expression. “Can’t tell you that. I finally understand why my dad and all the elders said that it can’t even be put to words. If someone tries to tell you the sensation, they’re trying to mess you up on purpose. If I give you my experiences, it’ll only serve to screw you up.”

Thinking back to the answers of his grandfather and everyone else in the Liu Clan, Shuren could only sigh. “I guess so. It’s only Rank 2 and Rank 6 that have this situation. Since those are the only times you can supposedly see the Grand Paths that make up this world. Whatever that means.” Then came the question Shuren had to ask. “What’s your Path?”

“Guess.”

“They say those with ambition tend to be of the Fire Path. But you’ve only focused on your sword so… I wouldn’t be surprised if you reached Metal Path at least.”

“Yeah that’s all bullshit. There’s no emotional connection to paths.” Ming suddenly appeared right in front of Shuren.

Then he realised. “You’re on the Space Path? That is quite rare.”

Ming rubbed the back of his neck. “Initially I thought it didn’t make much sense, but over time I kind of get why I got it. It suits me well.” Ming teleported back to his chair, eating another fruit.

Shuren didn’t even have the energy to celebrate Ming’s advancement, but he shook his head. “Once I get sorted, I’ll gift you something at the banquet. Now’s just not a good time.”

Ming wasn’t the type to coddle him with words. “I warned you, didn't I? Don’t get yourself caught up next time.”

Those words made Shuren chuckle wearily. Would there even be a next time at this rate?

“Oh, don’t take more than one a day. I personally grabbed these just now from the Ming Clan’s spirit gardener. The effects will diminish otherwise. The Gogong Simmons will just slightly improve the recovery of your meridians.” Ming leaned back on his chair as he held his arms behind his head. “Still, I wonder how good an Elysial Persimmon would be. Maybe reform one’s body and soul?”

The jam-like consistency of the Gogong persimmons also improved the worn out throat of Shuren. Even the scent of honey was strong, the quality of these fruits were indeed amazing. “Elysial fruits huh. A place only known in old folklore. Perhaps it did exist once upon a time.”

“So, what’s next? You gonna ask the main Liu Clan to give you some help?”

Shuren snorted. “I don’t need to run away for every little bit of help. This is a small matter.”

Ming stared at him with suspicion. He was well aware that naturally letting his soul to heal would take a long time. He knew very well Shuren wouldn’t waste his time stagnating for that long. But how else would he gain the resources to heal himself if he wouldn’t use his own clan backing?

“You sure? Rank 3 pills don’t exactly grow on spiritual trees. Don’t be so prideful to refuse help from your own clan, Shuren.”

“Ming, are you going to run back to your clan whenever you run into a hurdle like this? We aren’t coddled children anymore.”

It was the first he heard such a thing. Every person he’d ever met in the upper echelons of the cultivation world would never hesitate to use every resource at their disposal. But Shuren’s situation with his clan was strained.

He leaned his chin on the sword hilt that appeared out of nothingness, pondering. A habit of Ming, he really couldn’t be attached away from it. “Hmm. The path we walk is paved by the seeds of resources. Why should we act like stragglers?”

Shuren sneered at Ming. “Are you going to run back to the Ming Clan every time you go through a setback?”

Ming raised an eyebrow. *This guy already repeated himself, why’s he so obsessed?

“No, but it’s different for you. Get to the point Shuren, I know you’re not wasting months falling behind. Trying to reach Rank 2 with an unstable soul is utter idiocy.”

Shuren got out of his bed to stretch his body. Just expanding his muscles caused a dull sore pain to travel around it. All of his fractures had at least fully healed, but stinging pain from his abdomen still ached outward. He could somewhat move Qi slightly in his body, and he expected to recover the ability to cycle qi in a few days. The main issue was the qi reservoirs in his meridians completely drying out alongside his injured soul.

Shuren continued to stretch, moving onto his legs. “I’m thinking of going out for a breather. Spirth Gharuwood on the outskirts of Silverwoods specialise in rejuvenating soul injuries. Instead of cycling and meditating just to recover my old strength, getting those would be a better idea.”

Ming raised his chin off his sword. “No guts, no glory aye. Instead of just buying them you’re actually going to grab them yourself? You should reach Stage 12 and be prepared to break through at the same time.” He stood up, patting Shuren’s back with slightly too much force.

“Ease it,” Shuren winced. “I’ll leave after the banquet my father holds. Shouldn’t take longer than a month. Right, have you heard news of Wei’s state right now?”

Ming held a strange expression, aware of the backstory behind Wei. He held back his sigh, opting to give Shuren few details. Better he learns of it himself after he leaves this place. “No idea myself.” I’ll tell him later, once he’s recovered. Now’s not the time.

“I’m not an idiot. Speak up.” Shuren cut straight to the point.

“Heh, fine. He’s still recovering, but he’s pretty much a cripple. Unless he begs Su Kang like a dog to be healed up. Also, the rumours around Liu Wei seem to be true.” Ming answered bluntly, waiting for Shuren’s reaction.

Shuren’s fists tightened, fingernails biting into his palm.

“That kid even hated seeing the sight of blood, so why would he go around abusing disciples? I… I need to talk to him first. At least hear from his side.” Shuren held a hand to his forehead, thinking back to the past.

And he thought back to how he would hang out with Su Kang all the time he met up with Wei.

Su Kang… What’s your purpose in being here? I doubt it’s just to be on a vacation… I know he wants to prove himself to his father to climb up the ranks, so what use would it be to be here? I’ll need to investigate as soon as I can.

A healer disciple walked in with a meal for Shuren. He bowed, ignoring the wince of pain, greeting the lady. “Thank you for your help as always, Lady Sei.”

“No no, this is just my job. Good thing you listened to my advice, physical rehab will improve your health. Many other disciples forget to pay attention to the development of their Talisbody at your stage.” She stared daggers at Ming when she noticed the sheathed sword at his waistband, before deftly placing the plate on Shuren’s bedside. “Wuden has left it up to me to decide your dismissal. I’ll let you know tomorrow if you’re fit to leave.”

Lady Sei was one of the students looked after by Core Harmony. Though she wasn’t a Core Disciple yet and was still a Rank 1 cultivator, her knowledge and skills in the path of a healer was immense. She left the room after she said those words.

“I’ll take my leave then. Good to see you aren’t sulking about it. Hurry up and break through to Path Foundation,” Likewise, Ming waved as he left the room. Lady Sei was waiting outside the room right as he left.

“The next time you don’t listen to my words, I’m not letting you in, understood?” Still glaring, she said. Ming Wu began to feel a bit awkward seeing only a pair of auburn eyes with a masked and capped face staring at him.

As seamlessly as he stored his sword away in his soul space, he brought out another Gogong persimmon, placing it in Sei’s hands. “Forgive me, my lady. I’ll give you this as compensation.”

He abruptly walked away, leaving the emergency hall. “Hey, you think this is enough to bribe me!” She yelled out at his back, before feeling the stable spiral of qi inside. “Oh, it’s actually a Grade 2 spiritual fruit. Hmph, I’ll accept it this time.”

Shuren sat once again on his bed, staring at his meal. A simple fruit porridge with spring water and a cup of tea. The tea here was nothing amazing ever since Shuren embarked on the path of spiritual tea brewing, but it would do.

He picked up the cup of tea, though slightly trembling as he stared inside the cup. His own reflection mired in dark liquid. A stoic face with no expression.

His face rippled as the cup shook.

Shuren drank his cup in a single gulp, washing it down his throat. With the loss of appetite, he put the meal to the side, instead walking up to the window to gaze outside of the sect.

In the distance, he could see Cloud City at the base of the mountains, with the Volkahor Mountains at the far northwest. He gazed at that spot in particular.

“I’ve unknowingly ended up being involved in a scheme, and I should’ve realised it when I went to investigate Evening Clouds weeks ago. The turmoil between the citizens of Cloud and refugees from Yestervaul is only getting worse from what I hear. And from what the elders have mentioned in my presence, the Unhallowed Monolith is almost about to be unsealed…”

Shuren thought back to the earliest days he joined the Seven Clouds Sect. When his first missive ended up with him finding the corpse of a Silvent child. Just why would a demonic cultivator’s pet end up lost and fallen near the vicinity of Cloud City? The news of that incident would’ve spread by the farmers themselves with relative ease as well.

Even the Unhallowed Monolith was changing, and Shuren remembered when he made a report on the strange occurrence with that enigmatic creature, he noticed a flicker of a worrying expression to the elder he reported it to. Let alone just the tension between Seven Clouds Sect taking over the destroyed city of Yestervaul.

If he were to give Lixi’s word an inkling of seriousness, then the fact that this supposed demonic sect didn’t wish to grab the wrath of the Seven Clouds Sect Leader, but still continued to get involved multiple times near the province of Cloud City was far too strange.

Shuren put a hand on his injured abdomen.

Tez was also a major suspect.

He reeled down his emotions from the duel, forcing it in the back of his mind for now. He was clearly backed by some faction, with the amount of strange techniques and weapons he had, that clearly wasn’t in the capabilities of a Rank 1 cultivator.

But if Shuren could figure this much out, then right now, Tez should’ve been under investigation by the elders right now.

“Tez. Liu Wei. Su Kang. They’ve all been involved, which led to this duel with me and Tez. The Elders have been spending all their time on the Unhallowed Monolith, which has garnered the attention of other factions. If Su Kang is coming representing the Unchained Crown Sect… And the demonic sect has been getting active as well… Something big is going to happen soon.”

“And Tez definitely is being backed up by someone powerful. He was sent to Cloud City for a purpose. Is it the Unhallowed Monolith?”

Shuren’s eyes were icy cold. But a vein twitched around his neck.

“I have to figure this out.”

Shuren’s cloudmulet buzzed with a sound, and the wisps of clouds formed into a sigil. One Shuren recognised.

“An Elder is calling for my presence? It’s… Elder Spiritweaver?!”
