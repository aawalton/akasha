Finn tore through the underbrush, his lungs burning. The forest canopy above no longer glowed orange, the last rays of daylight slipping away as shadows stretched long across the dirt path. He knew every bend, every dip between the roots, every shortcut through the brambles. Just a couple more minutes and he’d be back. Speed and stealth were both things he was good at. He’d been sneaking out of Greystone long before anyone thought to stop him.

Tonight, that habit might save lives.

His mind whirled as fast as his legs moved. The memory of that towering demon’s chest burning—of Veronica planting herself between him and fire that could have swallowed the world—refused to fade. The heat of it still clung to his skin.

She had blocked it. She had actually blocked it.

But she had been scorched for it, her arms trembling and her body smoking beneath the ash.

Finn clenched his fists as he ran, his nails digging into his palms.

He couldn’t help but think—

This is my fault.

When Old Thom was being attacked, he should have screamed and called a guard over. He shouldn’t have gotten involved. Now she had been injured because of it.

He never expected a demon to be capable of that much destruction. So much that it didn’t even need to give chase. He had always thought the horror stories and tales of demons were exaggerated, just there to scare children. Now he knew they weren’t merely fairy tales.

Right now, everything depended on whether Veronica could kill it, even with all her injuries.

Can she…?

He shook the thought away. He couldn’t let himself dwell on what would happen if she fell. He had one mission.

Everyone needed to be alerted. Every guard needed to be awake and bearing arms. The lives of Greystone rested in his and Veronica’s hands.

He retraced his steps, sprinting up the small hill, and before long, he spotted it.

The tree line thinned, stone fences and rooftops coming into view as the lights of the town speared through the forest.

Greystone.

He broke into the open, weaving through the first paths leading toward the gates. People were still out—drinking, eating, and laughing as if the town weren’t teetering on the edge of ruin. A few guards leaned on their spears near the edge of town, chatting idly with plates of roasted meat in hand.

Finn sprinted up to two of the guards, gasping out his words before he could even catch his breath.

“There’s a demon in the forest!” he shouted. “A huge one! Veronica’s fighting it right now—you need to warn everyone!”

The male guard turned sharply. His eyes narrowed the instant he saw Finn. “You.”

Finn blinked. “What?”

“You’re the boy,” the guard said, stepping closer. “The one Old Thom said was taken.”

The woman beside him straightened, adjusting her grip on her spear. “Holy shit. Did you escape? Your face is bruised, and your lip is bleeding.”

Finn’s breath hitched. He was exhausted, but still filled with the need to act. “Yes! My name is Finn. And quickly, you need to alert the guards in town! There’s a demon in the forest, and Veronica is fighting it right now, and—”

“Slow down,” the male guard said.

“I can’t slow down!”

“You can, and you will.” The guard’s voice sharpened. “Old Thom was found with a stab wound, half out of his mind and bleeding into the dirt. He said a boy had been kidnapped. Now that boy comes running out of the same forest, screaming about demons.”

The woman guard stepped closer, looking over his features. She placed a hand on his shoulder. “You’re safe with us. Now tell us who took you.”

“Cultists,” Finn said without missing a beat. “Masked ones. They were wearing dark robes. They took me into the forest to sacrifice me to the demon, but I escaped because Veronica protected me.”

The two guards exchanged a look.

The man sighed with pity. “I’m sorry, kid, for making you try to remember. Come with us for now. We’ll take you to the infirmary, and we’ll collect a statement so we can find whoever kidnapped you.”

“I said it was cultists and a demon!” Finn snapped. “You’re not listening! Veronica is fighting it right now! We need to warn the town and tell everyone! There isn’t time!”

“There’s always time to get a statement from a kidnapped boy who stumbled back with a bruised face and blood on his mouth,” he replied.

“I’m telling you to believe me! There’s a demon in the forest, and the cultists are going to attack the town soon! Wasn’t Old Thom stabbed in the stomach? Why won’t you believe me?!”

“Calm down, kid,” the woman guard said, holding out a palm. “It’s not like we don’t believe you. Old Thom is being taken care of in the infirmary. And the only reason we’re out here on guard tonight is because he was stabbed.”

“He’s delirious from blood loss,” the male guard said. “He kept spouting nonsense about cultists, demons, and sacrifices. The same things he’s been saying all month. But we’ve looked every time and found nothing. I think he’s just hallucinating and seeing what he wants to see.”

“Yes, and,” the woman cut in, “even if there is something dangerous in the forest, we can’t send an entire squad out looking for it if you won’t tell us who they are. Stop calling your kidnapper a demon. We need a physical description. Otherwise, we can’t do anything. Baron Welterman gave strict orders that nothing is to disturb the viscount’s visit today unless something is confirmed. We can’t alert the town just because a boy said he saw demons or some shady cultists.”

Finn stared at them, the anger within him boiling over.

“I saw it,” Finn said, his voice shaking. “It had horns and claws and fire in its chest. It tried to burn me alive. Veronica stopped it. She sent me back.”

The male guard’s expression softened, which somehow made it worse. “Finn, you were kidnapped. You were beaten. You’re probably seeing things as worse than they were. Maybe it was fire magic. Maybe a large man in armor. Maybe a monster mask. In the dark, after being dragged through the forest, a lot of things can look like demons.”

“It was a demon!”

“Then Captain Luthen will hear about it after you give your statement.”

“After?” Finn’s voice cracked. “There won’t be an after if you keep wasting time!”

The woman sighed, but there was concern beneath her irritation now. “Enough. You’re hurt, scared, and shouting wild stories in the street. Come with us before you collapse.”

Finn lashed out with a sudden kick, slamming his boot into the man’s shin armor. His foot throbbed instantly against the metal. He winced and hopped back, hissing in pain.

The male guard startled, then barked a laugh. “Careful, lad. Armor’s not soft.” He reached out to ruffle Finn’s hair. “Easy now. I know you mean well, but just come with us. We won’t let anything else hurt you. Eat something. Let the adults do the worrying and protecting.”

Finn slapped his hand away, his eyes burning. “You’re wrong! She’s out there fighting right now, and if you don’t believe me, people are going to die!”

“H-hey!”

Before they could stop him, he spun and bolted into the streets, weaving between drunken townsfolk. His toe still burned, but he didn’t slow. If the guards were blind, he’d find someone else. Someone who would listen.

Finn tore through the streets, his chest heaving and his eyes darting around for another patrol. Someone had to believe him.

“Oi, kid.”

The voice came from his left. Finn skidded to a stop, dust puffing up around his boots. A man leaned lazily against the doorway of a shuttered building, one hand in his pocket and the other holding a cigarette that glowed faintly in the twilight. Smoke curled upward, wreathing his weathered face in a haze.

“You are…” Finn started, struggling to place him.

“Garrick,” the man said easily. “Owner of the Tipsy Barrel, other end of town.” His eyes narrowed as he studied Finn’s heaving chest and wild expression. “What’s got you running like the devil’s at your heels?”

Finn clenched his fists. “Because he is! Veronica’s out there in the forest, fighting a demon. A real demon! And the cultists—they’re after someone named Claire. She told me to warn the town before it’s too late!”

His voice cracked. He hadn’t meant to spill it all at once, but it burst out of him like a dam breaking.

Garrick didn’t laugh. He didn’t sneer. He simply took one last drag from the cigarette and slowly blew out the smoke. “Claire, huh? That’d be the viscount’s daughter.”

Finn froze. The words caught in his throat for a moment. “The… the viscount’s daughter?”

“That’s right.” Garrick tilted his head, studying him with tired eyes. “If cultists are after her, then you’ll want to head straight to the baron’s mansion. Don’t waste your breath on the guards. Find Elise—the baron’s maid. She’s sharper than most and as loyal to the baron as they come. Tell her Veronica sent you. Tell her Claire’s in danger. If you’re sincere, she’ll believe you.”

Finn’s pulse thundered. Veronica hadn’t just sent him to warn the town in general—she had given him a real mission. To save the viscount’s daughter. A noble. Maybe this Claire person was who the cultists were after. The very same target they had spoken of every time he spied on them.

He nodded hard. “Right. Elise. Mansion. Got it.”

Finn turned to run, but Garrick’s voice followed him.

“Good luck, Finn, the Greatest Hero of Greystone.”

The boy froze mid-step. His head whipped back, but the man was already turning into the alley, grinding the cigarette beneath his heel as he went.

Finn’s brow furrowed. For some reason, the man seemed unfamiliar. Finn knew of the Tipsy Barrel, but had he ever seen the owner? He was certain it was someone else, but the image just wouldn’t form. And more importantly, a lingering thought came to mind that he couldn’t shake.

Did I ever tell him my name?

Finn didn’t stop running until the cobblestones gave way to the wide lane leading straight to Baron Welterman’s mansion. The sun had sunk low, painting the walls in darkening orange. His chest heaved, his lungs clawing for air, but he pressed forward until he reached the wrought-iron gates.

“ELISE!” he shouted, gripping the bars so hard that his knuckles whitened. “Elise! The viscount’s daughter—Claire—she’s in danger! The baron has to alert the town!”

His voice rang through the square, startling a few passersby. Some glanced at him and muttered, while others ignored him outright. But Finn didn’t stop. He kept screaming Elise’s name, kept shouting about demons and cultists and fireballs. His throat felt raw, but he didn’t care.

At last, the heavy front doors opened.

Elise stepped outside, flanked by two armored guards. Her silver-trimmed maid’s uniform swayed as she crossed the walkway with measured steps, her expression cool but edged with irritation. She stopped just short of the gate, folded her hands, and arched a brow at him.

“You.” Her voice was firm, faintly clipped. “Who are you, and why are you causing such a ruckus outside the baron’s residence?”

Finn’s words tumbled out. “Veronica—she’s in the forest fighting a demon! There are cultists—Claire is their target—they’re trying to summon something worse! You have to warn the baron—warn the whole town before it’s too late!”

The guards exchanged skeptical glances. Elise’s expression barely shifted. She listened, but it was clear none of it was landing. Her eyes half-closed, her arms crossing as though she’d heard a hundred tall tales before from people trying to meet her master.

Finn’s fists clenched. “I saw it! With my own eyes! The demon—its fire—” He stopped, his breath hitching, then pressed forward desperately. “Veronica blocked it. She stood in front of me and cast this… this massive shield of mana. She saved my life!”

That made Elise pause. Her brow twitched upward ever so slightly. “A mana barrier?”

“Yes!” Finn nodded frantically. “Big enough to block everything. The whole blast!”

The guards gave each other an incredulous look, but Elise didn’t dismiss it. She was a mage herself; she knew what that meant. Slowly, her frown softened, replaced by a glimmer of disbelief that edged toward concern.

A Tier-1 mage pulling off a mana barrier? That was unheard of.

“…You’re certain?” she asked.

Finn swallowed and nodded hard. “I swear it. On my life.”

Elise studied him for a long moment. Finally, she sighed and straightened, her tone shifting to something more measured. “Very well. I’ll grant you entry. But understand this—” She lifted a finger, her gaze sharp. “I will be the one to inform the baron of what you’ve told me. Both the baron and the viscount are in a meeting. If this turns out to be a lie—if Veronica did not send you, or if you’ve fabricated any of this—you will be held accountable. Prison, at the very least.”

Finn’s throat went dry, but he forced himself to stand taller, his fists clenched at his sides. “That’s fine with me,” he said, though his voice cracked slightly. “I’m telling the truth.”

Elise studied him for another heartbeat, then gestured. The guards moved to unlatch the gate.

As the iron creaked open, Finn let out a shaky breath. He had made it through the first step.

The gate shut behind them with a heavy clang. Elise didn’t waste time. She motioned for Finn to follow and swept up the marble steps with long, determined strides. The guards followed them toward the entrance until she paused halfway and turned her head sharply.

“Back to your posts. I’ll handle this.”

They hesitated, then saluted and returned to the gate. Finn hurried to keep pace with Elise’s quick steps, his small boots clicking against the polished stone.

Inside, the air changed. It was cool, perfumed, and relaxing. The baron’s mansion felt like a different world from the noisy streets outside. Finn’s wide eyes drank everything in as they climbed: oil lamps set in gilded sconces, portraits of stern-faced men and women with the same sharp brow as Welterman, carpets so thick his feet sank into them. He had snuck into plenty of places before, but never here. The opulence pressed in around him, alien and untouchable.

They reached the landing, and Elise stopped. She glanced down at him, her voice clipped. “Stay here.”

Finn nodded, fidgeting as his eyes darted between the grand chandelier overhead and the tall oak doors at the end of the hall.

Elise strode forward, her heels whispering across the carpet, until she stood before the double doors. She knocked firmly.

“Master, I have an important message to relay.”

Silence.

She waited a moment, then knocked again, sharper this time. “Master?”

Still nothing.

Her brow creased. She looked back at Finn, her normally composed features faintly unsettled. Then she pressed the latch and pushed the door inward.

The hinges groaned.

She stepped inside.

A heartbeat later, a scream tore from the room.

Finn’s blood froze. Before he could stop himself, his legs were already moving. He sprinted down the hall, shoved past the half-open door, and burst inside—

Then froze.

The meeting chamber stretched before him, bathed in the flickering glow of firelight.

Bodies. Specifically, two bodies.

And lots—and lots—of blood.
