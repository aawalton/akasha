This wasn’t a quest….

Unlike my other inductions, my codex didn’t advise me to invite Zaiden to my domain, which had me thinking that Nolan was right about this being a bad idea. But it was too late to have second thoughts, and honestly, I wasn’t interested in backing out, either. What other options did we have? He was dying. Lun would have to pump out more mana to keep him alive with every trial, and Nolan would have to haul his body around, hoping that the conditions we’d meet along the way wouldn’t further complicate his chances of survival.

I stood by my decision to do this, and it didn’t matter if I didn’t have the support of Nolan and my codex.

Immediately, I called my command to surrender him into my domain, and a bubbling violet blackness engulfed him. There, he would be safe from the elements of the dungeon, where he could heal properly.

After the ordeal was over, Lun and Nolan stared at me. The expressions on their faces were mixed. I understood inducting a native wasn’t moral, but it came from a place of kindness. I was hoping they could see that….

“Well, while I was against it before,” Lun said as he paced around me thoughtfully. “I can’t say I’m really hating the idea, now. Still, I have plenty of questions.”

“Keep those questions to yourself,” Nolan said bitterly as he got up and pushed forward. “Let’s go.”

He was mad. I didn’t like it when Nolan was upset with me, but I couldn’t sit around and do nothing while Zaiden suffered. I followed after him, my wings tucked close to my body as we continued along the glowing path.

“So, where exactly is he now?” Lun asked, hurrying to catch up. “This thrall business is fascinating, really. In all my years of study—“

“Drop it,” Nolan snapped without looking back.

Lun kept glancing between us, clearly sensing the unspoken argument.

I reached out through our private link. ‘Nolan, please. I had to do something.’

‘You didn’t have to do that,’ he replied, his mental voice tense. ‘We don’t know what consequences this will have—for him or for you.’

‘I can feel him,’ I told Nolan. ‘He’s... resting. Safe.’

‘For now,’ Nolan replied harshly, and then he sighed. With that sigh, his temperament was a lot lighter as he followed up with, ‘The whole idea of having a thrall was to induct monsters, not people. There’s a very fine line between a good and bad dragon. You have a compass that guides you in your decision making. Your heart determines which path of dragonhood you take. You have to understand that you’re unlike any other dragons in a sense that your codex has given you the tools to lean in the wrong direction. Fast leveling, multiple elemental affinity, and necromancy…. I’m not saying you’re losing your way, but—’

‘My path is clear, Nolan. I’m not going to turn into your enemy. I can promise you that!’

‘Let this be the one and only time you do this, Freya. Promise me. No inducting natives, and no reanimating natives. Deal?’

I felt my throat dry up. ‘Deal.’

The green path stretched onward, a straight route ahead through the frozen wasteland. Without Zaiden to carry, we moved faster, and after I made that deal with Nolan, the tension between us melted away, making the journey feel faster, too. Once we reached the building I spotted along the horizon, Lun quickly confirmed that it was some type of observatory. The location was fitting, sitting on top of a high mountain, blending with the sky with its off white stone exterior.

A circular structure with a massive domed ceiling loomed against the frigid sky. The green path ended at its entrance, where we noticed a set of towering double doors etched with celestial patterns on it.

“I suppose this is our next challenge,” Lun said, approaching the doors cautiously. “Better than freezing out here.”

As we entered, warmth washed over us—a blessed relief from the biting cold. The interior was a vast circular chamber with a polished stone floor inlaid with metallic constellations. At the center was a complex mechanism—an oversized model of the night sky. Planets, moons, and stars were suspended in an intricate arrangement of brass and silver. The mechanism rotated slowly, emitting a soft blue glow that illuminated the chamber.

“An orrery,” Nolan said, approaching it curiously. “But far more complex than any I’ve seen.”

“It’s enormous!” Lun gasped.

Around the walls, six stone pedestals stood at equal intervals, each bearing a crystal orb that remained unlit. Above the orrery, projected onto the domed ceiling, was a map of constellations I didn’t recognize—patterns of stars unlike anything I’d seen on Earth.

“Look at the inscription,” Nolan pointed to a band of text that encircled the orrery’s base. “It says, ‘Align the heavens to unlock the path. What was broken must be made whole again.’”

Lun approached one of the crystal orbs on the pedestals. “There is a magical current running through these.” He passed his scaled hand over one, and it flickered briefly with light. “Responsive to touch, but nothing’s happening yet.”

I examined the central mechanism more closely. Several of the celestial bodies were misaligned or completely detached from their orbits. Some hung at awkward angles, while others were missing entirely.

‘It’s a puzzle,’ I said, circling the orrery. ‘The alignment is wrong. We need to fix it.’

Nolan studied the ceiling projection. “These constellations must be our guide. See how they form patterns? The orrery should match what’s above.”

I reached out with a claw to touch one of the misaligned planets—a small blue sphere dangling from a thin brass arm. As my claws made contact, the orb glowed faintly and rotated slightly at my touch. The mechanism responded with a soft click, settling into a new position.

“Careful,” Nolan warned. “I doubt we get unlimited attempts at this.”

With careful movements, I nudged another sphere—this one reddish-orange—watching as it rotated on its axis before locking into a new position. The moment it clicked into place, one of the crystal orbs on the pedestals around the room flared to life with golden light.

“Progress!” Lun exclaimed, clasping his hands together. “Each correct alignment activates one of those orbs. And each of these pieces here correspond to a set of constellations in the overhead projection. Here, let me help.”

“These aren’t just random patterns,” Nolan said, his voice hushed with realization. “They’re telling a story….”

I tilted my head, following his gaze upward. The stars formed shapes that seemed vaguely familiar. It was drawing something, but I couldn’t tell what. As we moved more celestial bodies into position, the image became clearer to us.

“It looks like a tree,” Lun said. “The Tree of New Beginnings, the origin of Etheryn.” As he pushed the last piece in place, a bong noise came from the last lit orb. Once all of the orbs were lit, a doorway carved itself on the other end of the observatory. The stone dispersed into a gold glowing light, and Nolan nudged us to take his lead and follow him right into it. He had a feeling that it was an exit, but just in case, he had Lun summon a bubble for each of us.

We were back in the temple, but it looked different….

This time, there were no rocky spikes in the center, with a suspended Seiddayan girl with an attitude problem hovering over it. Typha was on the floor where those spikes were, the ceiling over her reduced back to its regular size. She was just unconscious, at least, I was hoping she was. I rushed over to her, my snout nudging her cheek gently. I checked her chest—still breathing. Nolan and Lun came soon after, and then Lun prodded her with the butt of his scepter.

“Hey! Wake up!” he cawed at her as he proceeded to poke her shoulder.

I took in a sigh of relief. We completed the first elemental trial. She was safe, for now….

“Heavy sleeper,” Lun said to Nolan, and then Nolan crouched down to her and began to stir her in his own, erm, cruel way.

“Hey look, I reaaallly don’t mind you dozing off here,” he whispered tauntingly in her ear. “Makes my life a lot easier without having to hear you nag and bitch and complain about everything. You think orcs are bad? Try an entire dungeon designed to make your life a living hell. No scrolls, no items, and no fucking luck. Just a bratty kid with a klepto ring. Too bad you can’t loot the monsters. They aren’t worth shit until they are dead. Would be a real show to see you try and maneuver around them with your sad excuse of a level. Except we won’t be around to watch. Karma is a bitch, isn’t it?”

Typha was one proud sassy girl. I was not so shocked that all of that teasing stirred her some. Her eyelids fluttered, and a soft groan escaped her lips.

“Look who’s finally joining us,” Lun remarked, stepping back as Typha’s eyes slowly opened.

She blinked several times, disoriented, and then bolted upright with a gasp. “What?!”

“Relax, you’re fine, unfortunately,” Nolan said, muttering that last part under his breath as he got back up on his feet. “We cleared the first trial.”

She scratched the back of her head as she squinted at us. “Where am I?”

“Still at the temple of the King of Elements,” Lun replied. “Boy, you really don’t remember? How hard did you hit your head?”

“I… I didn’t,” she mumbled, and then her eyes snapped wide and the girl suddenly regained all of her senses. “The trials! The earth trial….” She patted herself down frantically. “My ring! Where’s my ring?!”

“Calm down,” Nolan said, raising a hand. “Your precious ring is still on your finger.”

Typha looked down and sighed with relief when she saw the copper band still securely in place. “What... what happened to me?”

“You were suspended in the air,” Lun explained. “The temple used you as collateral. If we failed, you would have been impaled!”

“Impaled?” Her voice rose to a squeak. Then, she looked around the floor she was kneeling on, and then it clicked. Her memory came back, and with it, a demand for the whereabouts of her brother. “Where is Zaiden?”

“With Freya,” Nolan said without skipping a beat.

Typha checked behind me, and when she didn’t see her brother, she checked again, both sides. Anxious, she got on her wobbly feet and asked, “Where? I don’t see him.”

“He is in her domain.”

She froze. “I’m sorry, what?”

“Don’t worry about it. All you need to know is that he is safely recovering.”

She glared at Nolan. “No, that answer is not good enough. Now tell me, what did that necro dragon do to my brother?!”

My chest tanked.

“Hey, you watch your—”

“I’m done being nice!” Typha growled at Nolan. “You can act all buddy buddy with a dragon, but that doesn’t mean I have to! Especially after you just admitted to her inducting my brother into her thrall! That’s what that domain is, right?! A place where that dark dragon hides her slaves?!”

I flinched at her words. The accusation stung more than I wanted to admit, especially after the moral struggle I’d just had with Nolan about this very issue.

‘He was dying,’ I projected, forgetting momentarily that she couldn’t hear me.

Nolan stepped between us. “Your brother was critically injured. His condition was deteriorating rapidly. Freya saved his life by taking him into her domain where he could stabilize.”

“Saved his life?” Typha’s voice cracked with shock. “By enslaving him?! How dare you make that decision for him!”

“We didn’t have many options,” Lun interjected, his tone surprisingly gentle. “The cold was killing him. His heartbeat was fading. I was running out of mana to keep him stable.”

Typha’s hands trembled as she clenched them into fists. “Get him out. Right now!”

I lowered my head, wanting to explain that it wasn’t so simple. That I didn’t fully understand how to summon my thralls once they stepped into my domain.

“Well?! What are you waiting for?!” she continued to bark at me.

“She’ll give him back once he’s feeling better,” Nolan said, fishing for an excuse for me. “Until then, there’s no urgency to bring him back, especially in the condition we left him in. He’d continue to drag us down.”

She stepped up in my face angrily. “You better clear your ears out, because none of that is my conc—”

“I have no problem leaving you here,” Nolan said bitterly as he slipped in between us. This time, he wasn’t joking with Typha. “You touch one scale on her, and I don’t give a rat’s ass what happens to you or your brother. Trust me, I won’t lose any sleep at night, because as far as I know, Freya and Lun are the only reasons you’re breathing right now. So if you want to keep it that way, you better shut your trap and fall in line. I’m sick of your attitude.”

The temple fell silent. Typha’s face contorted with fury and fear, her mouth opening and closing like she wanted to scream but couldn’t find the words. Finally, she took a step back, her eyes darting between Nolan and me. “I mean, you’re not a complete dumb piece of shit, are you?” she said to Nolan, her eyes beginning to swell up with tears. “You know… you know what dragons did to our people?! They burned our cities to the ground! They slaughtered thousands!” Tears streamed down Typha's face. “My parents died when I was four. I watched dragons tear them apart while Zaiden kept me safe. So forgive me if I don’t trust your scaly friend with my brother’s life!”

I recoiled as if she’d struck me physically. Her pain was raw, real—and completely justified. Dragons like me had caused her suffering.

“My brother is all I have. He raised me, through all the bullshit we had to go through, and all the rotten jobs we had to take to keep ourselves alive!” She raised her hand with the klepto ring on it. “This… this was our only salvation. After those evil beasts took everything away from me! And here you are, a human, a full-blooded natural prey to these predators, walking around like they aren’t hunted for a reason!”

“Not all dragons are like that,” Nolan countered, his voice steady but tense. “Just like not all Seiddayans are thieves or all orcs are brutes. Freya has done nothing but help us. She risked her life to save yours and your brother’s.”

“That doesn’t make my trauma any less real,” Typha shot back, wiping her tears with the back of her hand. “You can’t just expect me to—”

“No one’s dismissing your trauma,” Nolan interrupted, his voice gentler than I’d heard it before. “But right now, we’re all trying to survive. Your brother is safer in Freya’s domain than he would be out here. So suck up the tears and shake it off. We still have a dungeon to raid.”

Typha really had no choice but to cooperate. I understood where she was coming from, but I couldn’t relate to her deep distrust for someone who had proven countless times to be on her side. I was hoping she’d get over it the farther along we went through this dungeon, because we were far from finished. The king’s statue revealed our next trial, wind, and just like before, we were threatened with another trap. Party separation was his favorite, but Lun was quick. He didn’t fall for the trap floor tile leading to the door with the yin and yang looking symbol on it.

Quickly, he stretched his arm out and ordered all of us to stop. I broke my pace into Nolan’s leg, nearly tipping him over. The mage’s staff glowed as he hovered a few inches above the suspicious stone, his keen eyes scanning the intricate patterns carved into the floor. “See these pressure plates?” he called back to us. “They’re designed to trigger when weight is applied. Classic dungeon engineering.”

Typha crossed her arms, still wiping residual tears from her cheeks. “So how do we get across?”

“Air magic,” Lun replied with a hint of pride. “One of the few advantages of being the strongest mage of the Empora District!” he gloated again, before he gestured for us to gather closer. “I can levitate all of us across the trap floor—but we’ll need to stick close together. My area of effect isn’t unlimited.”

I watched as Lun’s staff glowed brighter, the air around our feet shimmering with arcane energy. My paws lifted gently from the ground, and I felt weightless—like swimming through air. Nolan seemed unfazed by the sensation, but Typha let out a small gasp, her hands instinctively reaching out to steady herself. I was a little confused, considering I’d seen her easily hopscotch down a row of wind pockets when she had escaped us by the dungeon we raided, you know, where she had looted us? Now she couldn’t keep her footing steady on a bigger area?

I ignored it and focused on reaching our next trial door. For the air trail, we opened the door to an even more bizarre test than the earth element—an empty sky….
