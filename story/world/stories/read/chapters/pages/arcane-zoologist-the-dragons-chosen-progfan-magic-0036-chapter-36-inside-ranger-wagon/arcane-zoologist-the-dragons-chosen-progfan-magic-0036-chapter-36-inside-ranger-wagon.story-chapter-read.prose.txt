Sneaking her way to the ranger's wagon was easy. The crowd was thick, and a small girl like Callie could easily disappear among them. Admittedly, it was a bit overwhelming to be surrounded by so many people, especially when many of them were trying to push Callie out of the way to get to one market stall or another.

But eventually, she managed to get to the edge of the market, where the crowd wasn't so thick. The ranger's wagon was standing nearby, along with one of the rangers who sat at the front bench. Thankfully, it wasn't the scary woman with cold eyes. Callie felt like that woman would have noticed her instantly.

Help… Mom…

I'll get you out real soon. Just wait a little longer.

Sticking low to the ground and hiding behind the many crates and boxes littering the sides of the streets, Callie sneaked her way to the wagon. She deliberately took the long way around, approaching the wagon from the side where the ranger in the front wouldn't see her.

When she had finally reached the wagon, unnoticed, Callie's heart thundered. Should she get caught… Callie didn't want to imagine what the horrible rangers would do to her. But fear coming from the thunderfox kits was enough to spur Callie into action.

She opened the back door of the wagon as quietly and quickly as possible and slipped inside, closing the door behind herself. For a few minutes, Callie remained still and listened to see whether anybody had noticed her climbing inside. Voices from the market were buzzing outside, but none of them were approaching the wagon.

After it was clear that Callie had indeed managed to get inside unnoticed, she took a look around. This wagon was much larger than Silas's wagon. In fact, it was so big it could basically be called a house on wheels. On one side was a couch that looked comfortable enough to sleep on. On the other side, there were shelves with books, vials, and things whose use she couldn't understand. No doubt to capture arcane beasts with.

In the corner of the wagon, covered with a large black cloth and secured with rope, were four cages with the fox kits. They were whimpering miserably, clearly scared for their lives.

"Don't worry, I'll get you out!" Callie whispered, making her way to the cages.

She pulled the black cloth slowly, so as not to startle the little ones. But the sight she saw wasn't what she had been expecting. The bottom of the cages was lined with dry hay. Each kit had a water bowl, and a few had bandages wrapped around their legs. They also looked noticeably cleaner than before, although the black cloth was still wrapped around their tails.

Although the kits were quivering from fear, they looked… well cared for. What was going on here?

Callie shook her head. Perhaps the rangers were merely cleaning the kits up to make them look nicer. Or they intended to let them grow a bit before getting more of the fur.

Determined that this must be the case, Callie reached for the cage door to unlock it.

"And what is it that you intend to do once you let them out of the cages?" A harsh female voice asked.

Callie whirled around and saw that the ranger captain was standing there, staring down at Callie. When did she get in here? How come Callie hadn't heard her open the wagon door?

"Well?" The woman prompted, her arms crossed on her chest while tapping one foot impatiently.

"I… I…" Callie stuttered. She had no clue what to do or what to say. The possibility of being caught obviously crossed her mind. But she hoped she would have some sort of warning. At least a chance to run away in case one of the rangers came back.

But there was no escape. She would have to face this woman head-on. Could she even save the little kits at this rate? Could she even save herself?

"You thought you would release them into the masses and then what? Have them injure dozens of innocent people with their lightning? Watch them get trampled by the frightened masses?"

"NO!" Callie called out. Such a horrible thing… It couldn't happen! Could it?

But thinking about it… The woman was right. The kits were already frightened. Even with Callie's mark, they were likely just too young to understand that she just wanted to help. The only other creature they would trust would be their mother.

Even if Callie had successfully released the kits, it would have only caused more chaos. But… What else could she do to help them? Even with the chaos, perhaps there was a chance that at least one of the kits could somehow make their way back safely to the forest.

"I-It's best that they escape and have a chance to live, instead of dying at your hand," Callie said, although her voice was quivering. Nonetheless, she was slowly moving backwards, closer to the cage. She would have to take her chances.

"Even if by some sort of miracle these kits managed to get out of the town, they would still die," The woman said harshly, her steel gaze locked onto Callie.

"Wh-What?"

"They are far too young to survive without their mother. Without her care, they would either starve to death, get snatched up by some larger predator, or exhaust themselves by using too much of their magic," the woman explained harshly.

Callie looked at the kits. They indeed still looked very small. Granted, it could be that all thunderfoxes were small, but she didn't know how big an adult thunderfox was. Was it the same size as regular foxes? If that really was the case, the ranger leader was right.

There was nothing Callie could do to help them. Her eyes filled with tears, and she felt magic roiling within her; the frustration and the helplessness of it all made Callie want to just release it wildly. Why was it that she couldn't do anything to help these creatures?

"No, please, just don't start crying…" The ranger leader sighed and crouched down. "I went too far. Sorry. We never meant to hurt the kits."

"You're going to sell them to dragonbloods, aren't you?" Callie snarled, wiping the tears out of the corners of her eyes.

"No, we're not selling them. We're trying to help them," the woman said in a somewhat gentler tone. Although she still looked incredibly scary.

"Help them? You captured them and put them in cages!" Callie accused.

"Would it have been better if I let them hurt you and your family? Or let them exhaust themselves to the brink of death? These little ones don't know their limits yet. They would just keep attacking with lightning until their hearts stopped."

"But… the cages! And their tails are covered! You even put a black cloth over them to leave them in complete darkness."

"Yes, the cloth of their tails prevents them from discharging lightning. I believe cages are quite obvious. It wouldn't do anyone any good if they were running around wildly. As for the cloth on the cages, don't you think they would be calmer if they were covered and wouldn't get startled by their surroundings?"

"W-Would they?"

"Of course. They wouldn't see humans that frighten them so much. It was possible that they would sleep and recover some of their energy, but a sneaky little girl just came in and exposed them to a frightening world."

Callie looked at the kits. They were trembling. So much so that their voices had gone silent. They didn't even dare to plead for help. All Callie felt from them was unadulterated terror.

"B-But you're hunters. You hurt arcane beasts," Callie muttered.

"We are not hunters!" the woman shook her head, as if she had just been insulted. "We remove dangerous arcane beasts. That is all people need to see."

Callie blinked. What she said seemed cold, but the woman had called them arcane beasts. Everyone always called them monsters, with a rare few exceptions. "You don't kill them?"

"Not unless there's no other choice."

Callie didn't quite understand it. It seemed like the ranger didn't want people to know she was helping arcane beasts. "Why are you hiding what you do?"

"Because frightened people don't want mercy. They want to be sure that whatever it was that scared them is gone."

"That's stupid!" Callie blurted out.

"I know it is. I wish things were different, but sadly, we have to work around people's fear."

"S-So what are you going to do with the little kits?" Callie asked.

"We're hoping to find her mother, but so far we haven't had any leads. Which is very odd. A thunderfox mother would never leave her kits alone for long. One from my team has remained at the nest to see whether the mother would return, but she hasn't… I'm afraid something must have happened to her," the woman explained.

"No…" Callie looked at the trembling kits. She didn't want to think that their mother would be… dead. Maybe she had just gotten lost.

"Can't you follow her trail?" Callie asked, remembering her lessons with Mister Blackthorn. He had taught them how to recognize arcane beast tracks. It was for the purpose of avoiding them, but it might as well be used for finding one.

"We tried to, but the trail broke off. It was really peculiar. Almost as if she were carried away in the air. Or as if… someone had erased the trail."

Callie couldn't imagine why anyone would do such a thing. But it instantly made her think of the egg thief. They had placed traps in the forest to catch Snowglow and stolen a glauxis egg, which was something nobody would even consider doing. Could the same person be involved here?

"CALLIE! WHERE ARE YOU?" Mom's voice could be heard over the bustling chatter of the market crowd.

"Mom!" Callie perked up. She must be worried out of her mind.

"That's your mom? We'd best get you back then," the woman said, and after covering the kits again, urged Callie out of the wagon.

She threw a glance back at them. If only she could do something to help. To find their mother somehow. Callie's magic did react strongly to arcane beasts, so could she somehow sense the thunderfox mother if she tried hard enough? Like that time with the glauxis egg.

"Callie, thank heavens!" Mom noticed Callie the moment she emerged from the ranger wagon, pulling her into a tight embrace. But the relief didn't last for long. The moment Mom released Callie, her expression changed into that of stormy anger.

"Seems like your daughter got swept along with the market crowd. I let her into the wagon to give her a chance to calm down a bit," the ranger woman said. "It's a common enough occurrence."

"You're that ranger who saved us from the monsters. Thank you very much! We are in your debt," Mom said, bowing politely and urging Callie to do the same.

The ranger leader had been kind enough to lie for Callie's sake so she wouldn't get in trouble. But that wasn't right. She had done something wrong and nearly made a huge mess of things. Had the ranger not intervened, the kits would be running around the marketplace, injuring people and getting hurt themselves. Mom could have been caught up in that.

Callie took a deep breath and shook her head. "No… that's not true. I snuck into the wagon."

"What?!" Mom looked at Callie incredulously.

"I wanted to help with the little kits. But I was wrong. I was wrong about what the right thing to do was and nearly made a huge mess. I was wrong about the rangers. Mom, I want to do things the right way!" Callie said, looking resolutely at Mom.

"Do the right thing? What would that be exactly?" The furrow in Mom's eyebrows was growing deeper by the second. Callie knew that there was no escape from a thorough scolding later. But even so, she would persist.

"I want to help the rangers find the thunderfox mother!" Callie announced.
