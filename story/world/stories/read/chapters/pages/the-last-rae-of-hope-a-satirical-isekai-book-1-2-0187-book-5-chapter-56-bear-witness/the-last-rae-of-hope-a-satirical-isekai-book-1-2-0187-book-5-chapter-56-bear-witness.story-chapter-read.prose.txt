I believe I’ve mentioned previously that I’m a bit afraid of heights. Gravity doesn’t go easy on me, and when there’s too much nothing between me and the ground, I get a little anxious. The bitter winds didn’t help, either, making it harder to hold on to Sparkles’s scales as we gained altitude.

“Aura Cálida!” Nora shouted from the dragon’s right shoulder blade.

“Going back to magic words?” I shouted, noticing a field of tiny blue sparkles forming around us.

“A little hard for me to concentrate up here,” Nora replied. “My imagination’s lagging, but the words help me hold onto my magic thoughts!”

“I’ll take your word that your words are necessary,” I called back, then added mildly, “Oh, that’s better.” A warm pocket of easier-to-breathe air enveloped us, forming a buffer from the chilled upper atmosphere. Nearhaven was now far below us, just as Paradise loomed larger on the horizon.

Olethros, hands behind his back, climbed up Sparkles’s neck one slow step at a time until he stood across from me. His hair and robes were quite calm, though his green eyes burned with what anyone else might have called malicious intent.

I just see it as earned retribution.

With both hands raised, he began tracing simple runes in the air. After forming about half a dozen, he struck them with his index fingers, pushing them forward. They shuddered, then shot farther across the sky, expanding and forming half-folded, mirror-like shields that rotated on their axes.

Reminds me of how Galenus controlled parts of the Bastion.

But will his shields hold—

Peew!

The first laser shot hit one side of the shield, reflected onto the other, then shot harmlessly to our right. Several more beams shot from Paradise’s barrier, widely scattered so that most had no chance of catching us. More than a few, however, found Olethros’s mirror shields and bounced off, leaving scorch marks.

“How does he know about lasers, anyway?” I shouted in exasperation. “He even got the noise right!”

Nora groaned. “Rae, real lasers don’t make that noise! I was telling him about the movie versions. Gaah!”

“You and your Sci-Fi fixations…” I grumbled without much conviction.

If not lasers, it would have been death beams or blasts or some other ranged energy magic, I’m sure.

Olethros summoned a few more runes, but instead of pushing them out to the periphery, he directed them to Nora. “Take it from here. I think it’s about time Rachel and I announce our arrival.” He tilted his head. “Whenever you’re ready.”

“Keep steady,” I said to Sparkles, then let go of her horn. Knowing I probably looked ridiculous, I planted my feet on a flat part of her head and drew Will from his sheath. Olethros summoned another temporary gold staff and leveled it at Paradise.

The laser shots came more desperately now, taking out a few shields at a time in stuttering bursts. I ignored them as best I could, focusing on attuning with Olethros even as I held Will out at arm’s length.

The demon king imagined the beam striking straight through Paradise, exploding the whole thing on contact, and watching the island catch fire before it plummeted into the sea.

“May I remind you there are animals, even people—perhaps, and NAUGHT itself up there?!”

He sighed, though I saw a smirk tugging at his lip. “I suppose you’re right. We’ll settle for the barrier—for now.”

Before I could berate him further for his imagination, I felt his iron resolve surround me. Our auras flared and swirled around our weapons, dark purple and pastel pink threading around a rainbow matrix of magic.

Can’t miss… Three… Two… One—

A quick jolt shook Sparkles as a shield exploded a little too close for comfort.

“Sorry!” I heard Nora call from behind as I tried not to tumble. “Distracted by how awesome you two look!”

“Now’s not the time!” Tetora bellowed, hugging onto a giant scale for dear life.

“Agreed!” Aleph boomed from a similarly uncomfortable position.

Gritting my teeth, I drew the sword back to jab it forward. Olethros mimicked my forward thrust, and our spiraling beam shot toward Paradise with a loud whine.

A few lasers were vaporized as our combined attack careened forward. It struck the barrier, spreading along the cracks it made across its surface. Several small explosions shot out from the epicenter before the entire structure shattered with an ear-splitting screech.

Then a giant shadow shot out from the island. It dipped low, then caught a thermal and climbed.

“It’s the roc we flew to the high temple!” I gasped, watching it swirl up even higher.

Olethros tilted his head back.

“Something is riding it.”

As the words left his lips, the Roc began its descent, claws outstretched, screaming.

“Hold on,” Sparkles warned, her words coming out more like roars than anything.

I swore it was a barrel roll, but Nora disagreed, saying it was merely a sharp bank to the left. Whatever evasive maneuver we executed, we lost our footing and, in most cases, our handholds, staying in place only because of the safety harnesses we had worked out. Even as we tumbled like rag dolls, one hand clinging to a scale and the other holding Will, I saw the Roc pass us by, carrying a human-sized Angel.

“Parley!” it screamed, yanking at the Roc’s reins. “Parley!”

Parley?

“Captain Sonea?!” I cried off the starboard bow. “Sonea!”

The Roc screeched, circling another updraft to renew its attack.

“Parley!” Sonea screamed again, raining blows down on the giant bird’s back. It shook then, screaming as it tried to dislodge her.

Sparkles dodged again, then blasted the Roc’s tailfeathers with fire before veering off to the side and heading after it.

I’m gonna hurl…

“Ahh…” Sparkles rumbled. “A fly-by would be the best way to disembark… I can’t fight properly with you bouncing around back there.”

“A fly-by?!” several of us shouted in panic.

“Get ready to tuck and roll!” Nora crowed. “I’ll make sure we survive the landing!”

“Captain Sonea might not be completely out of her mind!” I shouted shrilly. “She’s still screaming for Parley!”

“That roc isn’t interested in talking,” Tetora bellowed from behind me. “I’ll take my chances on the fly-by!”

“I call for a party vote,” Vernie yelled. “Cats land on their feet, but I don’t get the same guarantee!”

“The bird is a distraction,” Aleph called. “But a dangerous one.”

“My vote counts as majority!” Sparkles bellowed with flames shooting to end our argument. “I’ll skim the south edge. Get ready!”

Sparkles veered again, first blasting directly at the Roc before turning toward the edge of Paradise. The landscape rose hard and fast, grass blurring as a long, flat stretch appeared as the dragon leveled out.

How many snaps does this harness have?!

“Now,” Sparkles commanded, dipping a wing.

Sparkles disengaged us with a swift wiggle, and Nora’s barrier encased us the moment we became airborne. Olethros disappeared for a moment, but I didn’t have time to consider it. I rolled and bounced, but somehow I didn’t quite hit the edge of Nora’s barrier before heading off in another direction. Once we slowed to a reasonable velocity, the barrier popped.

Sparkles screamed as she took off after the Roc, her form stretching and weaving as she went. I only watched for a few moments before turning my attention to our landing zone.

Paradise was completely lost. The pastel sky was gone, empty of whimsy, and the grass had been reduced to a sickly whitish gray, as if drained of life. The animals were also gone, frozen in crystal the same way the citizens of the Inner Sanctum of Chairo had been. A wolf had been stilled mid-step. The deer behind it looked like it had been sleeping when it was hit.

Nora tapped her staff, and I saw its white crystal flash. “Take a good look at what’s up here, Speranza,” she announced. “I’ll let you draw your own conclusions.”

Olethros reappeared, mist streaming out of the serpent’s mark on my sword. “Lead the way.”

The city ruins nearby were even more razed than before, with several buildings reduced to piles of boulders. I was surprised to see that Relias’s tower had fallen completely. Still, a closer look showed several crystal figures surrounding its base, their arms raised in either supplication or anger.

Maybe both.

Olethros’s eyes suddenly widened. “A heartbeat.”

We all stopped. “What?”

He shook his head stubbornly. “I know what I heard. That statue has a very slow yet very real heartbeat.”

“I heard it too,” Tetora grunted.

Nora sucked in a breath. “If it’s the same as the crystal people in Chairo…!”

If those crystals survived the blast, that is…

Squinting, I tried to imagine what shape lay beneath it.

“A priest?”

I started to reach for the statue, but Olethros grabbed my hand. “It would be safer to attend to this later. Remember Landon’s warning about the crystal.”

Knowing that Nora’s staff was still broadcasting, I nodded swiftly. “Yes. Later. For now, let’s head toward… Euphridia’s Temple? Eh?”

I turned around several times, figuring I was disoriented. However, there was no sign of her domicile in any direction.

“It’s gone! It’s—everything’s gone!”

A loud hum resonated from ahead, where the city center opened into a shallow crater. Behind it stood a figure in richly decorated white-and-gold robes, a dark mask beneath the hood obscuring everything below two glittering sapphire eyes. In his right-gloved hand, he held Olethros’s scythe. Its shaft twisted around a gold staff, and its blade stretched and curved to hold the azure orb of NAUGHT atop the strangely fused weapon. Behind his shoulders, two magical circles spun. The inner was as dark as midnight, while the outer spun with gold, the spaces between generating a swirling mix of unstable animus and amity.

“Relias!” we shouted in unison.

“Welcome to Paradise, one and all, and bear witness, if you so choose,” Relias said calmly. “The world you knew is ending, and I am building that which comes after.” He paused, then tapped the end of his dual staff to the flagstones, summoning swirling waves of gold and midnight. “Unfortunately, none of you have a place in my new one.”
