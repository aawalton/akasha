Get stronger, huh? That seemed to be everyone's advice. It was even what he told himself when trying to find some sort of straight path forward. Perhaps it didn't need to be more complicated than that. It didn't, Luke decided as he opened the door to his shelter and stepped inside. There was no use in worrying about problems on a global or even national scale. As things stood, there was nothing he could do about any of it, except grow stronger. If another country invaded them? Stronger. If aliens decided now would be a good time to beam them all into space? Stronger. It was the answer to all questions and all problems. In this new world, more than ever, individual strength counted for a lot. That, and the power of the collective. That second point, they'd start working on later that day.

With a little more time to spare before Milla and the others would arrive, and no one daring to go near the shelter despite their glares, Luke settled down on his bed and readied himself for some training.

Lemira was on her new bed, sitting cross-legged with the monster core in her lap, eyes closed and her lips twitching, as if she were reciting something to herself. Elijah had opted for the chair and sat straight and stiff, with his hands on the armrests. He, too, kept his eyes shut. A light moved either on top of or inside his skin, glowing bright white as it darted this way and that.

Not wanting to slack off, Luke ate, dropped some food near the others, and then settled in to train a skill that always needed improving: Threads of Mana.

Extending a thread more than a couple of feet required a whole lot of mana. That was mana he couldn't spare in most fights, since running out was a constant issue even with all the tools at his disposal.

With practice, he'd improved the skill's reach by quite a lot at this point, but more was always better. Relian, The Fallen Shepherd, had managed more right from the start. Not being privy to the secrets in that man's now-dead skull, Luke saw no other way to improve than through sheer grit and determination. So, to that end, he extended a thread outward from his chest, pushing forward while holding his own body back. Once he found the maximum reach, he forced it forward while refusing the flow of mana from rushing through his mana channels and into the thread. The strain was immense, but it was nothing he hadn't battled against before. It was a constant struggle, pitting his Willpower against the problem at hand, but it didn't take up much of his concentration, despite it all. That was how he'd trained while doing other things throughout the last couple of weeks. Leveling up his enchanting would have made for the perfect complement to this training, but he'd once again forgotten to scavenge for mundane items.

Maybe this once, it would be acceptable to spend some credits in the Integrated Shop to get some goodies Luke could use. Food and water were the cheapest items in the shop, but those weren't valid targets for enchanting, unfortunately. The least expensive items, which worked with enchanting, were training weapons made out of wood at 50 credits a piece. Seeing how close he was to pushing to Level 3 Enchanter, Luke figured it was a worthwhile expense and purchased ten of them.

Keeping a firm grip on his thread of mana, Luke opened the enchanting menu in his interface and selected from the still short list of available options. Splitting his attention between the two tasks was enough to make sweat bead on his forehead, but Luke gritted his teeth and kept going, even as he admitted to himself that working that thread perhaps wasn't as easy as he'd thought, not when trying to extend its reach.

Even so, he got it done.

Wooden Practice Sword of Improved Health: +4 Vitality.

Wooden Practice Sword of Improved Strength: +3 Strength.

Wooden Practice Sword of Improved Intelligence: +3 Intelligence.

Wooden Practice Sword of Crushing Weight: A slight amount of weight added.

Wooden Practice Sword of Speed: Provides a minor speed boost when wearing an item with this enchantment.

Wooden Practice Sword of The Mind: A slight increase in mana regeneration.

Congratulations! You have reached Enchanter Level 3!

Grinning to himself, Luke stopped and caught his breath. Sweat poured down his back and down the front of his T-shirt, and relaxing his clamped jaw took some doing. Massaging his chin, Luke got up, checked the watch, then headed over to the shower. With one close, there was no need for him to stink up the place.

What better place than to check what new enchantments became available than in the rectangle of soothing calm and cleansing peace? Just as Luke had hoped, a few new options became available at the new profession level:

Enchantment of Soothed Nerves: Provides a slight increase in steadfastness.

Enchantment of Flow: Provides a slight increase in the speed of muscle activation.

Enchantment of the Competent Mind: Improved stability in spell casting.

Enchantment of Unerring Aim: Slight improvement of stability when wielding ranged weapons.

Enchantment of Leadership: Your skill-based orders are more likely to be followed.

Well, well, well... Some of those were more useful than others. The unclear descriptions didn't provide much in the way of specifics, but Luke was used to that. Even if all those options wouldn't be useful to Luke himself, there wasn't a single one the guild wouldn't find some use for, he was sure. Yes, he'd hoped for enchantments to give more attributes and speed, but he was sure those would come later.

The next step on his enchanting journey would be to use these new additions and test them out. If his previous testing was anything to go by, these would provide nothing more than small boosts in their respective areas. That was fine, of course. Level three was nothing. With more time and practice, there was no telling how powerful the profession could become.

Exiting the shower, having donned new clothes beforehand, Luke found the other two talking in the main room, which doubled as his own bedroom.

"Core things complete?" Luke asked as he put on his beloved robe, the many runes glowing in gold and purple.

"They are," Lemira confirmed.

Luke waited for more of an explanation for a couple of seconds, but carried on when she didn't share anything else. "Well, okay, then. The others should be here any minute, so we should probably get going."

Elijah got up. "Your sister?"

"That's right."

"She'll take leadership of this guild you've been talking about?"

"Correct!"

"What will she name it?" Lemira asked.

"Uh," Luke began. "You know, I'm not sure. Guess we'll ask her. Let's go!"

Once back outside the shelter but still inside the station, Luke made the structure fold in on itself and put it back into his inventory, again marveling at the convenience. Why had he bothered renting an apartment at all? Because he didn't know something like this existed, that's why. There was still so much to learn about almost every aspect of the system, but with everything happening at breakneck speed, there just hadn't been enough time to go around. At this point, having reached a decent level compared to most other people, you'd think Luke could allow himself to slow down a little, but that was a pipedream. There was no slowing down. If Lemira was right and one of those system events was approaching, he needed every little scrap of experience he could gather before then. No matter what sort of event occurred, being stronger would always be better than the alternative. Weakness would lead to death. Everyone in the guild would have to work hard to catch up and then keep pace.

As luck would have it, no guards were posted on the stairs up to the tracks, which meant the trio arrived without hassle with five minutes to spare.

Hannah stood there, waiting for them. "Hey," she said, raising her hand before holding it out to Elijah in greeting. "Who's your new friend?"

"Elijah," Elijah said.

Luke explained Elijah's situation, and Hannah nodded with a mixed look of interest and empathy. Lemira barreled into her at that point, throwing her arms around her in a bear hug.

"Friend Hannah! We meet again!"

"Uh, hey there," Hannah said, hugging her back. "Nice to see you doing well."

"Ray? Curtis?" Luke asked.

"You think those two would be on time?" Hannah asked.

"Guess not," Luke said with a sigh before remembering to ask. "How was practice?"

Hannah made a face. "Lots of drama already, but the playing was fun. At least it was until the power went out."

"In the garage or wherever you were practicing?"

"Nope," Hannah said, looking tired. "In all of Seattle. I think we're on the brink of the whole grid collapsing."

"At least some companies are building reactors, or whatever you want to call them, powered by monster cores. We'll get power back soon enough!" Ray said, coming up the stairs with a huge grin on his face. "Told you we'd be on time!"

"I'm not seeing Curtis," Luke said.

Ray cast about, then shrugged. "He was right here with me. Guess he decided not to come."

In that moment, someone appeared down on the tracks, then another, and another. One by one, people arrived out of thin air. Some jumped up, a few climbed, and once or twice, Luke and Elijah bent down to help someone climb up to the platform.

A great deal of greetings, hugs, handshakes, and more followed as Luke greeted a whole bunch of people he'd met before, and some he hadn't. When Milla arrived last, together with a coughing Mumu, the woman who'd brought him back to Chicago right after he set off the nuke. Even now, she had a cigarette hanging from the corner of her mouth. She drew in a deep breath, nodded in Luke's direction, coughed, and disappeared again without a trace.

"Had a good trip?" Luke asked Milla, who'd clambered up the side of the platform to join the boisterous group of Integrated.

"Might as well throw these clothes away," Milla complained. "They'll smell like cigarette smoke forever." She surveyed the platform, craning her neck. "Where's Lana?"

"Uh, I'm not sure. It's kind of chaotic here."

"Greetings, Milla, sister of Luke," Lemira said, pushing her way toward them.

"Hey there," Milla said, taking her hand. "You're Lemira, I'm guessing?"

Lemira reached up a little, as if to scratch at her small horns, but caught herself and gave Milla a small but warm smile. "I am."

Milla smiled back, then wrapped her arm over Luke's shoulders, turning him to face the people she'd brought. "So, what do you think?"

"This'll do," Luke said, nodding. "This'll do."

There were quite a few new faces in the crowd, but they, along with those Luke recognized, all looked back at him and Milla, waiting for them to speak. Talking was Milla's job, Luke figured, so he waited along with them, taking his time to share a look with each of those he recognized.

One was vibrating with excited energy and kept throwing up metal horns as he did his best to rile up those around him. Nash had quit ISG and moved to New York, along with Priyah, Sam, Mas, and Savannah.

Other familiar faces filled the crowd, both those he'd met recently and those he almost didn't remember:

Josh the Jumper, Emma the archer from back in the dungeon where they retrieved the gem of communication, Martin from ISG, along with Rolf the barrier mage, who made sure they survived being dumped out of a helicopter, and their healer, Cass. Luna, from Milla's DIA party, was present as well, along with Tiffany and Triss from way back in the Tutorial Dungeon. How Milla tracked those down, he had no clue. As if that wasn't enough, Kenny was present too, the healer in the hospital where Luke reconnected with Tim.

Those were the ones he recognized, but more were present. A lot more. All in all, Milla had gathered around fifty people. Luke supposed she'd chosen a different strategy from Phoenix Phalanx's, and he was glad for it. They'd need a lot of people to get this damn city under control. He wished Mateo and Penny could've joined too, but he understood why they'd decided to stay in Chicago. Mateo had his family and his business, even if that business probably wouldn't last much longer. Landscaping wouldn't be a top priority going forward, Luke guessed. Penny had family and friends to consider, as well as Mateo's safety, since she'd appointed herself his guardian angel.

Luke had messaged them both a bunch. Mateo's talk with his wife worked out, as Luke had known it would, but they weren't about to uproot their children to move to a far more dangerous city. Made sense.

Milla drew in a deep breath before speaking. "All right, people! We're here! Now listen up!"
