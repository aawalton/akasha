I quickly review who is where, then reach out to Noir and Ivor.

Apparently some baby samurans have joined the battle. Can you rescue them?

They and Fenrir are the only ones of my companions who are currently involved in the battle. The others are either out with Bastet, working inside the settlement to deal with the crisis there, or looking after the hatchlings – at least, that’s what they were supposed to be doing.

Noir reaches back a moment later.

I see them. They’re small enough they’re mostly being overlooked by the bigger beasts around.

Can you rescue them? I emphasise.

We’ll do our best, Noir promises, Ivor sending grim agreement. If we can reach them.

Don’t risk yourselves, I urge. And thank you.

I glare at a blank spot on the wall, feeling torn. I can imagine it – the baby samurans in the crush of beasts throwing themselves at the wall. I trust in the alcaorises, but I know what battle is like – even with their best efforts, it may just put them at too much risk to get close enough to the ground to grab the half-sized samurans.

I need to be there, I think to myself. I need to help them.

But my duty is here, with the healers.

Yet how can I focus on removing parasites when the youngest of those under my care are in such danger?

I hesitate for a moment longer, and then make my decision.

I’m coming, I tell Valence firmly, standing up from where I had shifted to kneel next to the one who would have been my next patient. I have a quick word with the healer’s apprentice – letting him know where I’m going and what to do with the ferals.

You are? asks Valence in surprise a moment later.

Don’t the healers need you more? adds Layton.

I’m already striding out of the building.

The ferals are straining and growling at their restraints, but they're as secure as we can make them and pose no danger to anyone else. Those with heavy infections who haven't yet gone feral have been pre-emptively restrained as a precaution. The ones whose infection hasn’t yet dug too deeply into their systems have been quarantined. The situation is under control and can wait until the wall battle is dealt with, I tell them firmly.

Well, Valence replies a moment later, cautiously optimistic. Having your magic to help us would certainly help us to finish faster.

I’m grateful he doesn’t comment on my obvious priorities – I’m feeling guilty enough over them as it is. Not enough to change my mind and turn around, however.

I’ve just grown my wings and launched myself into the sky when I feel Bastet reach out to me.

What is it? I ask her urgently, growing my wings and starting to fly.

We've found where the beasts are coming from.

Well done, I tell her. She pays my praise no heed.

Another wave is on its way, she warns. They're already halfway between the river and the settlement. And they’re going to keep coming unless we deal with the source.

Which is what? I ask even as I curse mentally at the news.

Something powerful just across the river. We couldn’t get close enough to find out what because it’s guarded by a large pack of beasts. Many Evolved. Some Enlightened. Those are the ones it’s sending little by little to attack the den.

Sirocco, listening in, obligingly sends me a picture of a bird’s eye view and I curse again.

What is it? Layton asks sharply and I realise I must have accidentally sent my displeasure over the Alliance Bond.

My companions have got back to me about what’s causing these beast waves and it doesn’t look good – what’s been thrown at us so far is only a fraction of what this creature can bring to bear.

Indeed, there must be hundreds of beasts there – possibly even thousands.

Worse, there’s another wave on its way – it’s likely to hit us in the next quarter-mark.

My scouts confirm your estimation on timing, Valence replies a moment later, his voice tense. If another Tier Three joins before we've dealt with the first, we're going to be overwhelmed.

Just as well I’m coming, then, I tell him with grim humour. I’m almost there. The conversation might have only taken a couple of minutes, but I can fly pretty fast and the cavern isn’t that big.

I take a brief moment to despair at how the situation seems to have so thoroughly fallen apart again – just when we seemed to be getting it under control. Then, pulling myself together, I reach out to Loran.

If the Earth Mages are no longer needed for buildings, please can you send them to the wall. We may need them. I’m almost there now.

Yes, my lord, he agrees quickly, with only a brief moment of surprise at my sudden change in plans.

I choose not to land on the wall. Instead, I tuck my wings in close to my body and use my momentum to send me straight through one of the holes that was made through the anti-manastorm barrier. Out in the air, I flare my wings open, taking in the chaos even as I search for the samuran hatchlings.

And chaos it certainly is. The beasts are far more sparse than the last picture my companions sent to me – the defenders’ efforts to whittle down the attackers have had good effect. However, I see immediately what Valence meant when he described his problems with the Tier three.

It seems to either be a plant that has become mobile, or an animal that has dug so deeply into its plant-related powers that it is now not immediately distinguishable from an oddly stout tree. It’s doubly dangerous for the settlement guards – not only is it sending out roots to try to undermine the wall, but it’s quick to raise a root or shift a branch in the way of the arrows and magical attacks launched from the walls, saving its less cautious brethren time after time.

Fortunately, I know how to deal with irritating trees.

Reaching down my Bond with Aingeal, I request whether he can be spared – the last I know he was working with Happy and the other blacksmiths, forging fetters for the ferals.

His response is tentative – he wants to come and help, but he is needed there too.

I send him a reassuring reply. If he can’t come, it’s fine – I can create enough of an inferno myself. But if I know he’s coming, I will be able to concentrate on finding the hatchlings.

That makes his resolve firm. I know he likes the hatchlings – he’s played enough with them, suppressing his heat so they can treat him like a ball and toss him through the air. He announces he’s coming – and that he will return to the blacksmiths as soon as possible.

I send him gratitude, but decide to act in the meantime.

Concentrating on creating a ball of intensely hot lava in each hand, I pause only to make sure the hatchlings aren’t close to the Tier three. Then, darting above the tree – higher than it can reach – I send the balls dropping one after another.

The mobile tree reacts to the first missile by lifting one of its branches into the way. Bad idea. It lets out a hiss of what sounds unmistakably like pain when the lava splashes all over its branches and onto its trunk beneath, already starting to make the wood smoke and blacken from its incredible heat.

While it’s distracted, I let the other ball fall straight onto its trunk. The hiss grows louder and it writhes, taking out some of its own allies as it flails its branches around. The lower Tiers don’t seem to care about pain, but this Tier three certainly does.

I decide not to keep pressing my attack until the Tier three dies. Aingeal is on his way, and every second counts for the hatchlings. The opening I’ve given them should be enough for the fighters on the wall to overcome the now-vulnerable Tier twos without too much difficulty.

I’m grateful to see that Noir and Ivor each already have a hatchling hanging from their claws. However, my fears have proven true – they’re struggling to get close enough to the ground to grab others. Their style of flying just isn’t quite agile enough to do what they need to without risking themselves.

Fortunately, my own wings are far more agile, the way I can project air-magic out of them at any angle means I can change direction in a fraction of a second, even flying backwards.

Stay up in the air and look for them, I direct the alcaorises. Tell me when you see one and I’ll grab it and toss it up to you.

It’s a pity my tattoo only works on those Bonded to me. I abruptly regret not Binding all of the hatchlings earlier, never mind the risk of damaging their souls while they are still developing. If I had, they would never have been able to put themselves in danger like this.

Following Ivor and Noir’s directions, and also keeping an eye out for myself, I quickly manage to rescue four more hatchlings. Fortunately, all but one are grateful to be rescued – two of them had even been cowering on the ground under as much cover as they could find. The fourth snaps at me when I grab him so I click my teeth firmly together and press my soul outwards, lined with my displeasure.

He flinches and makes himself smaller. Continuing to glare, I throw him upwards, warning Ivor that he might be a problem. As a result, the white alcaoris catches him in his mouth rather than his spare front paw which is weaker than his occupied back paws.

The three of us continue searching for the last two hatchlings, taking advantage of the opportunities that present themselves to kill or disable the beasts around. I’m peripherally aware of the Tier three screaming at a high pitch as Aingeal’s fire continues my efforts, overcoming whatever resistance to flame that it has, and baking it from within.

I do have to be a bit careful, though – since I’m not in direct communication with those fighting from the wall, there are a few close calls with spells that land where I would have been if I hadn’t noticed them just in time.

Yet, I still can’t find the last two samurans.

As the earth starts rippling under the feet of the few remaining survivors, my worry reaches a fever pitch – if the Earth Mages have joined the fray, the babies could be crushed without anyone even realising they’re there.

I’ve tried to use Inspect several times, but the red glow of enemies masked any glow around the samuran hatchlings. Still, I do it again – maybe enough have fallen that there’s a chance I’ll spot them.

Flying over the battlefield, my eyes turning this way and that, I pulse Inspect, focusing on trying to find the samuran hatchlings.

Nothing, and this time I can’t say it’s because of the crush of enemies – their red glows faded with their deaths.

I swallow hard, the thought I’ve been trying to keep at bay speaking more insistently in my mind.

Not sure I want to know, but knowing I need to, I pulse another Inspect. This time, I focus on finding not the samurans themselves, but their bodies.

My heart sinks as I spot two golden glows.

I should join the battle that continues to rage closest to the Tier Three – though crumbling to ash in patches, it is determined to take some of us with it. Its roots ripple through the earth, attacking the wall with a determination that only the condemned can wield.

But instead I rapidly descend down to the earth, landing with a thump next to the closest of the golden glows. Others can finish off the battle – I’m the only one who might be able to pull off a miracle.

Hauling away the carcass of a beast lying on top of him, I search for signs of life in the broken body that’s revealed. His chest isn’t twitching, and the gaping wounds no longer pump blood.

Still, I thrust magic into his body – I had thought Artemis dead until I used magic. I only need a spark to remain to fan back into the flame of life.

Yet my hope falls little by little as I search his unresisting body and feel not a single hint of enduring life.

“No.” I murmur, surprising myself when I hear my own voice. “No!”

The second golden glow wasn’t that far off. I run to it as fast as I can, again clawing away the boulder that lies upon the baby samuran. I press magic into this one’s body, searching again for any hint of life.

Finally, I lean back, my hand falling limply to my side. They never even lived long enough to earn their names….

I’m barely aware that the battlefield has fallen silent, or that two large forms have come to earth, several smaller ones clustering around the body of their fallen sibling, their eyes solemn, but not understanding. Not yet.
