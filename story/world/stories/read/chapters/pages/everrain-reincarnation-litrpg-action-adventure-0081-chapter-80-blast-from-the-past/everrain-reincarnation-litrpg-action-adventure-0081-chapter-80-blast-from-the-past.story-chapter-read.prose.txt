Day 373, 12:30 AM

We reach the one hundred and first floor. My mana reserve exceeds my previous run’s full reserve, and I’m ready for a rematch. Most importantly, Redo is no longer red. I’ll fight Hardiuse and his outer god overlords as many times as it takes until I’m victorious.

Hadriuse is there, waiting for me, this time, his form entirely golden right from the start.

“Did you forget we were here and traveled too far back, restoring the entrance? Or does your power return you exactly one hundred and sixty Everrain’s years into the past?” The golden creature speaks in interweaving twin voices, each echoing the other.

I restored it by returning? And what do they mean one hundred and sixty years?

While one part of my mental engine is stalling, the other is present here and now. The calm chit-chat doesn’t relax it one bit, and rightfully so. A golden bullet hits my heart in the vision, paralyzing me.

I jump to the side, tackling Edna, who stands next to me, just as the projectile is about to pierce my chest. Next is a golden whip, from which I roll away, followed by a cluster of golden lights, seemingly fired from a laser shotgun.

Wait, if I restored it, that means I destroyed it the last time. That means Vengeful is one way of dealing with them, but if Vengeful can destroy the crack, so can magic.

The parallel train of thought drops the questions. I will have to go over them at some point later, but that will probably have to wait until the next redo. Instead, I focus on analyzing the golden crack. My brow furrows, BSD’s information differs from my expectations. One hundred percent orange mana, base components - none.

I was certain the crack would have all types of mana in equal measure, or possibly register as pure magic, but I guess saying it’s a power from the stars, or more accurately beyond them, makes sense, too.

I will a spell which would destroy the crack and stretch my hand towards it, but nothing happens. My mana remains inert. I cast lightning arc, and as expected, mana jumps at the golden crack, dealing no damage.

Maybe the crack is indestructible. How about sealing it? I stretch my hand towards it, trying to work my mana again, but nothing happens. Again.

“Are you trying to use magic to seal the source empowering this world’s magic? You really are some kind of bastard hatchling, to not know even something as basic as the seventh law of magic.” The golden Hadriuse shrieks and snarls, the eerie sound vaguely resembles a laugh, one performed by a barely sentient beast which had never interacted with humans and lacks their vocal capabilities.

But even though one of them is taunting me, these things are feeding me gold. For one thing, I now know magic would be useless against the crack in the future loops, so I will be able to throw spells to my heart’s content until we reach this place. Another oddity is that they consider me a newborn god for some reason. It could have to do with the anarchist class hitting the new level, or possibly having high stats, or, I suddenly recall the biggest reason for their mistake, the Godly trait.

So many questions, but none seem as critical as the implication of the world going on for a century and a half after my death and then rewinding. That means Manny had to face our problems alone after I was gone. Countless people were born and died in a century and a half, and then those entire generations vanished.

Guilt forces those thoughts to the surface, despite how horrible the timing.

What is the exact extent of my ability? Can you rewind time for one world alone? What happens with the stars, people would notice constellations and other planets moving into unexpected places. So how big is the change? A world, a star system, a galaxy? The universe? Something even bigger?

The only solace I can find is that if the same events play out, they will result in identical consequences. So even if I impact the whole universe, only my immediate surroundings would change. I hope.

There’s the butterfly effect theory, but whether I overthrow a government or conquer a continent won’t change what’s happening on another, assuming there’s no direct communication between them. Especially if I don’t do anything as drastic.

Both my thought processes brick up from the sheer magnitude of my conclusion as I try to justify and defend myself. A soft tendril of gold seizes the chance and strikes.

I see it in my vision, and try to move away, but I’m too distracted. Instead of striking my chest, it  latches onto my elbow.

I resist and try to pull away, but Hadriuse pulls with equal force, and there’s no escaping nor breaking the thing. It stretches and a blob of gold oozes from Hadriuse, following the thin line until it reaches my arm.

Instead of wrapping itself around me, the whip melts, crawling up and down my arm, and trying to invade my body. My physique of fifty-three makes me highly resistant to magic, so, other than locking the joint, there’s little it can do.

I push my mana, willing a spell which would dissolve and block the infectious energy, but all I’m achieving is buying more time. A vision hits me, Hadriuse is about to shoot another golden bullet, and I make the lizard’s choice.

Mana flows towards it, and the captured arm detaches itself from the rest of my body. With the resistance suddenly gone, Hadriuse staggers back, his back hitting the crack.

Why is he standing next to the crack? He warned me to kill him and destroy the gate, is that the body’s last act of rebellion, or do the outer gods want him there for some other reason which gives me the opportunity to wipe them both out at the same time?

Detached from the rest of my body, the arm turns golden in a blink, while the one growing from the empty socket does so much slower. Discarding and regrowing a whole limb through nothing but an act of will to shrug off an unstoppable attack is something so manly and badass, and yet Hadriuse won’t let me enjoy my moment.

Two dozen tendrils lash out towards me, and I run, opening as much distance as I can from Edna, trying to keep her out of the attack’s range. I roll, dodging the tendril which I foresaw would wrap itself around my waist. The golden thread whistles through the air above me, and another dozen join in.

The room is now a tangled mess, and I can tell Hadriuse is having trouble manipulating them. As the number increases, his control grows worse, they collide and merge, growing thicker, but he just keeps spawning more.

Finally, I understand his plan. With enough of them, he won’t need control. Ultimately, I have to approach him to destroy him and the gate, so wasting time is working in his favor, not mine.

I charge at Hadriuse. A thread sticks to my hair, and I grow bald, discarding the infected part. Next, a vision warns me that a thread will strike my neck. I raise my hand and sweep, blocking it. The next moment, I disconnect my hand at the wrist, sweeping clear the path towards the golden archmage.

Then I realize how dumb I am. Another tendril is about to strike me, but I transmute the air in its path into a wooden pole, sticking out from the ground. The tread latches on, and converts it into gold.

With a thought, I spawn thirty more such poles, grounding all of Hadriuse’s golden threads. I approach the golden idol, my step light, my arm and hand regrowing themselves.

“I figured out your weakness. Why you struggle the more threads you create.” I walk towards the crack leisurely. “You don’t see things in real time. You are lagging a split second, maybe a whole second. That’s why your doll’s movements are choppy and disturbing.”

I smile, now two steps away from my target, well inside Vengeful’s blast area.

“Edna, you can leave, I can kill them on my own.”

Hadriuse stops his attacks, and speaks with the two seconds of delay I had noticed. “We can bargain. We can offer you a cut, your divinity is still fragile, we can offer enough power to see you through five levels.”

I hear Edna’s footsteps behind me, but she needn’t concern herself. I won’t believe these things no matter what they offer. But it gives me another idea.

“Be polite the next time we meet, think about your offensive offer, open up a path for me, and we will discuss it.” I turn back to look at Edna. “I told you I can kill them by myself.”

She shakes her head, beaming me a smile. “We can kill them together.”

My heart shakes. She looks just like Manny. And just like Manny, she blasts us both to hell.
