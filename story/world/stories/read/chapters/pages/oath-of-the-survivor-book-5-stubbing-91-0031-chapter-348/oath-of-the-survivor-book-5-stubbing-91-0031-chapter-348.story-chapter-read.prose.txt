“Well, don’t just stand there.  I even took the liberty of filling your plate!  Why, the food is sublime, you simply must try some.  After all, it was your guild that provided it!  It would be quite rude to leave it uneaten.  Quite rude indeed!”

Kyle just stood, processing everything as well as he could.  The orc’s sheer power threw him for a loop on its own, not to mention the fact that his demeanor was entirely out of place for such a serious engagement.  The sharply-dressed A Grade appeared more concerned with enjoying the provided confections than actually conducting the investigation.  Then and again, Kyle knew that looks could be deceiving.

“All due respect, Iberious, but I’d rather cut to business.  This meeting has my whole future on the line, I’m sure you could appreciate that the pressure isn’t exactly good for the appetite.”

“Bah!”  Iberious said with a snort, raising his free hand.  “If one’s future hangs in the balance, all the more important to enjoy a fine meal when one can!”

With a snap of the orc’s thick fingers, Kyle found himself sitting on the chair opposite Iberious, and felt a warm, crunchy morsel of fried food in his mouth.  He bit down on it reflexively, soft melted cheese contrasting against the fried breading.  It was light, spicy, and savory, all at the same time.  Delicious.  Of course, even if he could admit the food was great, that didn’t mean he was going to put his guard down around somebody who could crush him with a thought.

“Flexing your power isn’t a great way to get me to loosen up,” Kyle shot at the orc, who seemed wholly unbothered.

“Oh?  I thought it might help reinforce the point that, if I wanted you harmed, it is well within my power to do so.  Or, perhaps, that you may even feel comforted by the sincerity of my words and intentions with this delectable spread of food.  Mayhap even that you’d be at ease knowing that you’re in the presence of somebody who will not be bullied by the Gray Conglomerate, or any of the other forces that have taken an interest in you.  That the only thing that matters to me is ferreting out the truth, in whatever strange form it may deign to take.”

“You already know a lot about me, and yet I haven’t the faintest idea who you are, or who you represent,” Kyle replied, an edge in his voice.  “And you’ll forgive me if I don’t take your words about ferreting out the truth at face value.  A lot of misrepresentations were made about me throughout this Tribunal in the name of ‘truth,’ and I’m not exactly confident that finding the truth is anybody’s primary objective.”

“Bahahaha!  Mr. Mayhew, you are far too young to have such a jaded view of the Collective!  And I will choose to interpret your words as coming from a place of hurt rather than an accusation against my honor.”  With that, the orc’s plate filled up with food once more, and he dug in with gusto, though never took his eyes off Kyle.

After he’d cleared the plate a second time, he continued.  “As I mentioned before, my name is Iberious.  You’ve already deduced that I’m a much higher level than yourself, and likely understand that I’m not here to harm you.  My title, however, is something that you are not yet aware of.  I am the Guildmaster of the Collector’s Guild, a longtime acquaintance of your own Guildmaster, and a potential future employer of yours.  I also happen to be one of the longest-serving officials within the entire Guild system, and know it better than all but a scant few.  The integrity of our institutions is of paramount importance to me, and to those to whom I will be presenting this report.  Now, have I earned enough of your trust to where you feel comfortable eating some of this delightful food?  I find civil conversation tends to be best underpinned with a good meal.”

Several pieces clicked together in Kyle’s head, and he slowly nodded as he grabbed a meat-filled pastry and began to eat, much to Iberious’ approval.  A Grades weren’t existences to be taken lightly, and he was aware of fewer than fifty across the entirety of the Collective.  Simply knowing that Iberious was affiliated not just with Collective governance, but also with the Guild system was a huge comfort.  As a Guildmaster, Iberious had access to a tremendous network of both resources and information.  Being part of the Collector’s Guild made that even more true, with virtually all of the most valuable natural treasures that hit the market being sourced by his guild members.

All that to say, the orc wasn’t somebody who could simply be bought, at least not by anybody with normal means.  And, while the people responsible for his recent troubles certainly had resources, they weren’t at that level.  If they were, they simply would have taken Kyle and been done with it.  He could only assume that Ger’Sinh and his patron were at least loosely affiliated with his adversaries, and the space magic-wielding dragon was likely only in the middle of B Grade at best.  Still beyond his current abilities by quite a bit, but nothing that would inspire an ancient A Grade to move.

Feeling Kyle’s tension easing, C.H.A.D.D. took the opportunity to pose a question of its own.  [GUILDMASTER IBERIOUS, GIVEN THAT DR. MAYHEW AND I TRAVEL TOGETHER, AM I INVITED TO COMMENT ON THIS CONVERSATION?]

“I don’t think that would be an issue,” Iberious responded, absently scratching at the curly white tufts of hair on the side of his head.  “From what I’ve been able to gather, you two have been working together for a long time.  Factoring in the bounties still active for your successful capture, and I would even find it prudent for you to contribute as you see a need.”

“You know about the bounties?”  Kyle asked, not having thought about those since after his job on Pokke.

“Oh yes,” Iberious said with a chuckle.  “Your file noted those quite extensively.  While Ma’Rin and Ma Enterprises have done quite a nice job cleaning many of them up, it didn’t take me long to find several that are still active.  None of them are priced terribly competitively, I might add, but they still exist if you know where to look.”

“And where would that be?” Kyle asked, raising an eyebrow.

“Nowhere that you will want to spend time,” the orc deflected, a twinkle in his eye.  “Now, before we begin the discussion in earnest, a few things to note.  This is a space where you can speak openly, and specifics revealed to me will not be included in the report to the Collective, other than that I have factors influencing my recommendation.  That said, in the unlikely event that you confess to severe criminal activity while under our employ, I do reserve the right to have formal charges brought forth and investigations opened.  Any questions?”

Kyle shook his head, having expected that much from the notes Suierrillax and Jarberry provided.  “Nothing at this point.”

“Well, stop me at your leisure should that change.  Now, as a formality, I will warn you that I’m going to activate an identification skill of mine.  I’ll have no issue keeping my aura contained, but you may find it to be a tad uncomfortable for a moment.  Are you ready?”

Of course he’d be using a high-tier identification skill, Kyle admonished himself.  Even a skill like Identify could note the fluctuations in somebody’s aura.  How much more powerful would an identification ability be after it was upgraded at least twice over?  He nodded his consent, selfishly activating Identify himself to see if he could gain any inspiration for his own upgrade paths.

The eye covered by the monocle gave off a faint glow, and Kyle could barely make out concentric rings of energy appearing within the eye, as blinking nodes of light twinkled like stars along the path illuminated by the rings.  At the very same moment, Kyle felt as though his very soul was laid bare.  As Iberious’ expression grew more serious, the feeling only intensified for a moment, before it finally dwindled down to a less oppressive sensation.

“Apologies, my good fellow.  I spotted something unexpected, and it took me a bit by surprise.  Now, shall we begin?”

“You saw the injuries from Zierlan?” Kyle guessed, measuring Iberious’ reaction.

“I was quite familiar with that development from your file, given that the full version was unredacted for Guildmasters.  No, I was surprised by the stitching of your injury.  Still grievous, undoubtedly, but it looks as though…”

Iberious trailed off for a moment, a faraway look in his eye, until he visibly shook, blinking a few times.  “Ahem.  Enough about that.  On to the business at hand.”

“Got in some trouble, huh?” Kyle asked, not able to help himself.

“Quite,” Iberious admitted.  “Now, to my questions.  Where did you acquire the World Tree Seed now bound to your body?”

“I acquired it back on Earth, my home planet,” Kyle answered honestly.

“And you located it during your employ with Corthian Mining, acting in your official capacity as a contractor?”

“It was located during an operation, yes.  However, it was an important relic of my planet.  And it’s not as if Corthian Mining compensated me for its discovery.  We also found quite a few pseudo World Tree Seeds, that were sold to Corthian Mining afterward.”

“Let us rewind the clock further, shall we?  Prior to the events that led to your world’s – Earth’s – demise, were you aware of the existence of either Corthian Mining or the Collective?”

“I was not.”

“What was your understanding, if any, of the nature of riftwyrms?”

“I’d never heard about them until I met with Corthian Mining,” Kyle replied cooly.

“Given that one had allegedly destroyed my planet, yes.”

“I mean that I intend to keep investigating riftwyrm attacks, once I finally get the chance to settle in and focus on anything more than what’s right in front of me.”

“And what other explanation do you have, if not a random riftwyrm attack?”

Now, is it true that while you were on Earth, your last two assignments for Corthian Mining, after the discovery of the World Tree Seed, went quite wrong?”

If Corthian Mining took the threats more seriously, like they did with the first two beasts we encountered, I don’t think that we’d have seen casualties near the levels we did.”

“An interesting perspective.  Now, let’s fast forward to your Practicum.  Your showing in Phase One wasn’t particularly noteworthy, but you began to make a splash in Phase Two.  I must admit, I’m curious to hear more about your clash with the bandit named Ger’Sinh.”

Kyle sighed.  It was going to be a long conversation.
