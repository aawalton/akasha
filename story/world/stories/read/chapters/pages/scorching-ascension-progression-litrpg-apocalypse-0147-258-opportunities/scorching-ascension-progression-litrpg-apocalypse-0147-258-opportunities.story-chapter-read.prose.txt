With the unfortunate taste of blood and sand in his mouth, Ethan suppressed his cough and refrained from spitting or even breathing as he tried to get his feet under him. With his eyes closed, it was a little tougher than he would have liked, and he kept expecting something to hit him, but he couldn’t afford to open his eyes, not when he could literally feel the grains of sand sprayed against his eyelids.

It wasn’t ideal. Especially when he could smell, hear, and feel the presence of the monsters only meters away from him. To top it off, in that instant right after he got the drop on it, the worm had activated its odd ability once more and while the claws weren’t too difficult to keep on, the wings were a much different story.

Ethan’s attention was torn between keeping the wings from slipping out of his mental grip and dissolving away, and the desperate need to channel mana to them so he could lift off and create some distance away from the creature. Maybe it was that desperate need to use them, or maybe it was because he had cut down on spellforms, but it only took him a few more second to reaffirm his grip on the spellform and shove his mana through the dampening effect of the worm, and a moment later, relief flushed through him as he burst out of the sand cloud, away from the ear-splitting screeching and from the coppery scent of blood and finally, he could open his eyes.

Below, the massive body of the worm bucked and twisted on itself, sending blue, viscous sprays of blood up in the air and on the sand all around it. Its head hung limply, but the rest of its body was squirming in distress and panic, and Ethan was a little surprised it still had that much energy. Then again, it was in its death throes, and unless it had a powerful healing ability, this should be a killing blow. So before he took stock of his inner situation, Ethan glanced around, noted the man who’d stopped running and was peering back at him, then noted a few flashes over the horizon, barely visible through the bright lights of the three suns above.

Keeping the man in his sights, Ethan turned his attention inward. If he were to describe the connection he felt to his two spellforms, he’d say it was like holding on to a pull-up bar. For the claws, it was secure enough. As if his palms were just a little sweaty, but all of his fingers were secure enough around the metal bar, and he could hang in there for a while still. For the wings…. Well, it was like the grip he’d have at the end of a set. His joints were sore. His muscles tired. And he needed to grit his teeth and flex as hard as he could to keep holding on.

All in all, his gambit worked. If he had kept the scales as well, he would have lost the wings. He wouldn’t have been able to maintain them, and he would have been still down there, trying to dodge tons of squirming flesh as the creature continued to roll and twist. Also, now he knew more about the worm’s magic. Having tanked it from up close, he could tell it had been a magic of some sort. A pulse. He had no clue as to the details of the why and how, but it was no Zenith state, because he’d been channeling that one the way down, and that part of him had gotten zero opposition. If the dispelling magic had been a Prime manifestation, he would have felt it. He was sure of that. He’d clashed enough against other Zenith users to be sure of that. Which meant that—if he were to use the same imagery—the worm’s magic was like heavy weights around one’s ankles, and if his grip on a spellform was weak enough, it’d be dispelled and in that same manner, it made channeling mana harder, so casters would be at a disadvantage near such a monster, but if one were to discard the ranged advantage and get in there?

“They’d do a lot better. It’s really not that resilient, considering its levels. Much stronger against casters,” he summarized, and catching a movement from the corner of his eyes, he saw the man who’d been running slowly approaching, a handaxe in hand, so Ethan opted to finish up the kill before the stranger considered “contributing.”

Flying a little higher, Ethan grit his teeth and pushed against the remaining lethargy he could still feel in his Arcanums. First, he worked on bringing back [Draconic Skeleton], immediately increasing his weight manifold as the spellform slowly empowered his body. He knew very well that this might attract the attention of more worms, but he needed to practice against this sort of … hindrance, for a lack of a better word. This magical interference. And maybe it was because he was getting used to the weight against his spiritual ankles, but as the spellform finished activating and as he started on the next one, specifically, harpy’s talon, he felt that his control was slowly but surely speeding up. It was either that or the debuffing magic was fading, but whatever the cause was, the result was the same, and once the talons finished forming, he activated his familiar, metallic scales.

Those came the easiest. Almost like putting on a familiar, comfortable vest. And with his final defense ready, Ethan lined himself above the slowing worm, and let himself drop.

From his angle, it looked like it wanted to dive back into the sand, but the broken vertebra of its neck was making that a little difficult and as wind rushed in his ears, a thought drifted into his mind as he tucked his elbow and braced.

Now that I think of it, wouldn’t the vertebra make this creature more of a snake than worm?

The thought was then shunted away as he impacted straight on the creature’s head, right as it stopped moving for a moment. To rest, he supposed. There was a feeling of things snapping under his feet, and a mighty thud that reverberated across the sand as blue blood and viscera splattered, and a beat later, a notification graced the corner of his sight.

You have defeated [Pale Maw (II) - Level 97]

You have reached Level 90. 1 stat point awarded.

“And there we go. Finally,” he smiled as he read the text, and spreading his wings wide, Ethan went to extricate himself from the carcass, only to find his feet somehow stuck, and as he grumbled and tried to hoist himself out, he realized something was off. His surroundings were too quiet.

Immediately, he stopped moving and listened, eyes scanning his surrounding as he looked for the man who’d been nearby moments ago, only to realize he couldn’t see him, and that told him all he needed to know.

Ethan bared his teeth and ignited his scales while simultaneously, he channeled everything else to his wings. Seriously? The man was gonna take a shot at him, after he’d saved his life? To be fair, that last part had been a side-effect of him wanting to down one of the worms, but still, the point stands.

With a last kick, he dislodged his leg, and his wings finally exerted enough force to pull him out of the mess of flesh, sinew and cartilage, but before he could gain enough altitude, he caught the glint of metal and instantly cut his wings off and flared his flames. He wouldn’t be able to fly fast enough to dodge, so the recently empowered flames would do.

The metallic blue glow appeared first, and as if a cloth of invisibility was ripped away, the handle and hand holding the weapon came out next before the rest of the man appeared out of thin air. While Ethan was interested in the ability, he had no time to spare for it and instead, he wrenched his own hand away as the blade whistled toward his wrist, parting away the flames that barely managed to slow it down.

Right. This was all about the bracelet. But did the man want to take his hand off too? It was a little alarming how easily the blade got through the flames, but his opportunistic opponent had really overextended in his ambush, opening his flank for retaliation.

Ethan twisted his hip and brought his foot up, cracking the short-haired man right in the guts. His dark eyes widened and his mouth opened wide as the air rushed out of his lungs, and Ethan had the pleasure of seeing him get some airtime before he crashed back down in the sand.

Floating down after him, Ethan landed next to the still wheezing man, kicked the handaxe from his hand, then crouched down next to him.

“Nice try,” Ethan said as he grabbed hold of the man’s forearm. The man tried to pull his hand out, but he was a little too winded still, and so Ethan slid a clawed finger under the bracelet and tugged. There was a click, and the band came loose. Immediately, a shimmer began shining around the man’s form, and as he fell back down once Ethan released him, the man gave him a little shrug, still breathing hard.

“At least… I can get back to dealing with the mess outside,” the man said, groaning as he tried to get up to his feet while the light around him swelled, and a beat later, the stranger was gone, leaving Ethan alone next to the massive body of the worm. Even the bracelet that’d been between his claws disappeared.

He looked left, then right, then shrugged and took to the air, his scales shimmering under the suns’ rays as he scanned the structures over the horizon, a smile tugged on his lips, which only widened when he took a glance at his status page and noted something new in available upgrades.

Ethan could now purchase a new Arcanum title. One that would join Arcanum of Forms and Arcanum of the Fire Elemental, but he’d look into that later. For now, he just wanted to practice and use what he had a little better.

“Let’s continue exploring, then.”
