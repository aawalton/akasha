Samuel’s body ached.

Though he was no slouch, Samuel did not consider himself a specimen of physical fitness, something he was most certainly regretting at the moment. If only he had trained his body as much as Rose often had.

To think he was a fool who had considered it ‘too extreme’. He knew Rose certainly would have been in better shape if she was in his position.

His feet were on fire.

Even through the pain, he did not stop running. Regardless of how much his body begged him for some much needed rest.

To do so would be death.

He did not dare to look back, but he knew what he would see if he did.

More than a dozen snakes, each at least twice as thick as his arm, chased him with furious hissing, their dripping fangs promising death if they caught up to him.

Every so often, one of them would get dangerously close or try to leap at him, only to get swatted out of the air or pushed back by his telekinesis. It was only a combination of the skill and an intense fear of death that drove him forward.

He could just barely take note of the path he followed, though he was sure he had missed his way at some point. Not like it mattered. He would sort it out once he was no longer in danger of being pumped full of snake poison. At the moment, getting free of this hell was his sole priority.

After what seemed like hours of running, the whole process almost started to seem routine. He moved past the monotonous forest with consistent, if shaky strides, shooting out bursts of mental energy every time he felt a snake get close. He had to conserve his shots even more now, which had led to some very close calls. He was not particularly comfortable with that, but he simply lacked enough psi to do anything about it.

The sun was still high in the sky, but he could feel it would likely soon start to set, which was slightly concerning. He already had to deal with some clever snakes trying to use the advantage of climbing trees to ambush him, which were often his closest brushes with death.

Having to worry about darkness would be nearly impossible. It was, of course, unlikely that would actually become a problem though. There was little chance he would be able to keep running till the sun actually set.

He was actually beginning to run out of hope when he finally found salvation.

Or at least what he hoped it was.

Up ahead, without warning, he could see an abrupt end to the forest. Where his vision had been filled with the monotony of trees stretching on seemingly without end, he finally found something new. Just a few hundred metres away, the trees gave way into hard rock that stretched on for as far as he could see.

Instinctively, Samuel knew that was salvation, even though he had not come across the area before. Perhaps he would have thought to be more cautious were he not tired, in pain, and without much choice, As it was, the only thing his mind registered at the sight of a different environment that was somewhere outside the venomous forest. With any luck, it would mean the end of the snakes’ territory.

Most importantly, it would mean safety.

With the end in sight, he took one last risk to sneak a peep at his pursuers. The snakes, at the site of the different terrain, had different reactions.

Some of them seemed to give up on the chase. Most of them even, and he noticed the number of snakes pursuing him over the last stretch dwindle to just four.

That was, of course, still four too many, and he pointedly did not think about what would happen if the snakes could actually follow him into the flatlands.

He deflected one last desperate leap from a particularly determined snake just before he jumped over the border dividing both terrains.

For the first time in hours, damp soil was replaced by hard rock under his feet. There was also a change in the environment that he couldn’t quite pinpoint. It felt like he had stepped into a land with a different purpose.

Before he could relish the change in scenery, he quickly turned back to see large snakes hissing at him in a clear display of anger, yet they remained in the forest, unable to cross the border. He almost hissed back at them, but refrained at the last moment. He suspected he was safe from them in this new environment, but it was better not to push his lock.

He simply watched them slowly get tired instead and leave the edge of the forest, going back to the more central parts of their territory. A few moments after he started looking at them, a notification chimed in his head, and bold lines of text appeared in front of his vision.

You have entered the Hollow Fortress, domain of the Lich Prince. You have been marked as an intruder.

As Samuel looked at the message, he knew he should be worried. He had lost his way somehow and found himself not in the lush grassland he came from, but a place called The Hollow Fortress. The fact that this place supposedly belonged to a Lich was even more bad news, considering what he knew about fantasy creatures.

None of those worries truly registered in his mind, however. He was simply far too tired to do anything about them.

Instead, he forced himself to walk away from the forest till there was at least a hundred metres of distance between them. It was not much, but he was not in good enough condition to do better.

When he reached an area that seemed reasonably far, he simply picked a random spot and lay down on the hard rock, sparing little thought for all the potential ways he could get murdered in his sleep by any kind of dungeon monster.

He was asleep in seconds.

*	*	*

Samuel opened his eyes to a sea of stars.

The ground beneath him was not the most comfortable of beds, hard as it was, yet he continued to lie on it. The view of an unobstructed night sky was not something he often got to witness, used to the city life as he was. Its beauty was undeniable, yet he found himself unable to enjoy it for long before the reality of his situation caught up with him.

Hollow Fortress.

Somehow, he had missed his way as he ran from the forest, and instead of the rich grasslands that led to his house, he found himself in a sea of hard rock, likely a different part of the dungeon.

From the mention of a Lich King, Samuel already had suspicions of what he would meet in this gloomy place. As anyone versed in fantasy media would know, the presence of a lich usually implied other lesser undead to be under its command. Especially in the context of a dungeon.

He already had a good guess at what he would have to face in the dungeon.

Samuel groaned.

Looking at the darkness around him, he couldn’t help but curse his bad luck. What were the odds that he would land in an undead’s domain in the night. A part of him hoped that trope stayed fantasy and the time of day made no difference to any potential undead enemies.

Unfortunately, he couldn’t quite bring himself to believe it.

‘Hope for the best but expect the worst,’ he thought to himself.

Samuel opened his bag, thankful he at least had the foresight to pack some supplies. Unfortunately, he had not expected to be outside for more than a few days at most, so he did not take much food with him.

So even though he would have preferred to fill his stomach, he refrained from eating, hoping that the small lunch he had earlier in the forest would sustain him for a while. There was no telling when he would be back, after all. He was at least glad he’d managed to bring a flashlight, which reduced one disadvantage.

Pointing the light at the forest, he stared at it with longing, suspecting he would find it easier to trace a path back home if he took that route. He was even tempted to go for a moment, but closer inspection of the trees showed him multiple snakes.

Nope.

There was no way he would go back to that hellish forest until he was considerably more powerful. Not with how close to death he had just come. The fact that all the snakes were poisonous made it too dangerous.

Instead he turned back wide expanse of rock and made sure his was ready,

Psi, check.

He frowned at that. Full mental reserves was not something he had expected at all, considering he had only slept for a few hours. A full night’s sleep had not been enough to fill them up just the day before, and he had increased capacity now. He would not pretend to be an expert on his strange new reality, but he would have expected something like mental energy regeneration to be fairly straightforward.

After a moment of thought, he shrugged. If anything, it was a reminder to not assume anything he knew still applied.

Rested, check.

Knife…

He searched around before remembering that both his knives were somehow abandoned during the chase. Which meant his only weapon was a walking stick.

His Grandma’s Walking Stick.

A sigh of resignation left his lips. He almost cursed his situation and thought about quitting, but the image of Rose hitting level ten and killing monsters without abandon filled his mind, and he strengthened his resolve once more.

He knew his sister would thrive in this kind of situation, and there was no chance he would let himself lag behind her.

Besides, it didn’t seem like he could actually quit. Not if he wanted to leave this dungeon alive. And though Samuel was willing to take more risks than most, he had no intention of actually throwing his life away. Especially not to some video game monsters.

So he stopped giving excuses or trying to feel sorry for himself and started walking in a random direction, hoping it would at least take him closer to home.
