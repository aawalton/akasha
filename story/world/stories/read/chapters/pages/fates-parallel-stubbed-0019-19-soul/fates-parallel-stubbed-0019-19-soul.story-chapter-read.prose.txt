Jia had found that moving items into and out of the ring was extremely simple. With a thought, she could pick an item out of the ring and it would appear in her hand. Similarly, as long as she was touching something, it only took a thought to vanish it away safely inside the ring.

After they had finished ogling Jia’s newfound riches—and spent an entirely appropriate amount of time mocking Yan Zhihao’s style of dress—Jia and Eui focused on the most intriguing items within the ring—the jade slips. There were eight of them in total and they all seemed to be manuals for different spiritual techniques. Without any other way of identifying them, Jia decided to simply read them one by one.

The first was Awakening of the Dragon’s Heart—obviously Yan Zhihao’s personal technique. It was a very dense technique that seemed to carry a cultivator all the way from the qi gathering stage through to spiritual core forming with a variety of fire-based techniques. It seemed to be deeply mired in the history of the Yan family and contained a great deal of flowery, self-aggrandizing language.

“Well that’s useless.”

Jia complained as she set the slip aside. She didn’t understand any of the actually important parts of the technique, and she really didn’t care about how great the Yan family thought they were. Realizing that she wouldn’t be able to make sense of most of the manuals without awakening her qi, Jia went through the remaining slips more quickly.

There were two more in-depth techniques—Tranquility of the Verdant Marsh, and Valley of Illusory Mists. Jia didn’t try to figure out what they did as both were just as dense as the first. Four more manuals had simple, single-purpose techniques, and Jia was at least able to determine what they were meant to do, even if the how of it was lost to her.

Domain of the Earthen Realm was a technique that created a zone around the user allowing them to control...something. That part wasn’t very clear to Jia, and the language in these manuals was always vague and poetic.

Earth Rending Sword Art was actually quite familiar to Jia. She was almost certain it was the technique that Guan Yi had been using in their spars to project qi attacks from his weapon. As Guan had alluded to, the technique was much more powerful when used with a metallic edge.

Body Lightening was a very simple technique that would alter the weight of the user. Mastery would allow the user to stand on a leaf without disturbing it, or to run across water without so much as a ripple. This was apparently considered a basic technique that all cultivators were expected to learn.

Lastly, Path of Heavenly Ascension was a simple but highly advanced technique that could only be mastered at higher stages of cultivation. Despite the difficult requirements, Jia’s eyes lit up with excitement as she realized what the technique was for—a cultivator who mastered the technique would gain the ability to fly.

“Oh, hell yes! We have got to learn that one!”

Jia had to agree with Eui’s assessment. Flight was absolutely a priority. Or, it would be except—

“It can only be learned by a cultivator in the—uh, core forming stage? I think that’s the third one, but I’m not sure.”

“Laaame! I wanna fly!”

Eui flopped over on the couch as she lamented the cruelty of fate. Jia giggled and tried to reassure her.

“I’m sure we’ll get there, eventually.”

There was one more jade slip and Jia was a bit nervous. Elder Qin had suggested that the secret to unlocking her qi would be here, but Jia was starting to think Eui might have been right about her getting scammed. With trepidation, she read the final jade slip.

Enlightenment of the Qi-soul

By the God-Emperor’s decree, the great sects of Qin must heed the words contained herein.

The first tenet: The body and mind are chains which restrict the soul. Enlightenment can only be achieved when the mortal needs of the flesh, and the mortal desires of the mind are cast aside and burned away in divine tribulation.

The second tenet: To prepare the soul to survive the heavenly fires of ascension, the natural energies of the world must be refined within the cauldron of the soul. Only a spiritual body of purest divine energy may survive the annihilation of the mortal chains.

The third tenet: To begin the path of enlightenment, spiritual impurities must be purged to open a path through the meridians to the dantian of the soul. Sense the natural laws of existence, know these words as truth, and take the first steps on the True Path.

Awaken, my children, and follow me on the path to divinity!

The contents were brief, but each phrase seemed to slam itself into Jia’s mind with greater force than the last. The final line left a burning feeling in her chest that reminded her of what Yan Zhihao had done to her when they first met. Except instead of her ki pushing the feeling away, the burning sensation just continued to build in her chest unabated until it became difficult to breathe.

Breath!

“Jia are you ok!? What’s wrong?”

Jia had no time to respond to Eui as she quickly took a meditative pose. Her intuition was screaming at her that she needed to control her breathing right now. Without a second thought, Jia used the breathing techniques from Qin’s foundation building technique—now the only thing she remembered from the original jade slip. Despite the burning pain in her chest, she concentrated on her breathing and tried to sense that labyrinth of qi within herself.

As the sensations in her body fell away, the pain in her chest did not subside, only becoming more intense as the meditation seemed to force her to focus directly on it. Recalling the words of the jade slip, Jia tried to allow her thoughts to drift away as her bodily sensations had.

Within the labyrinth of her meridians, a brightly burning energy surged through her and carved away at the impurities of her soul. Without her thoughts, Jia only had pain and fear to guide her actions. She tried to calm herself as the energy burned through her meridians and as she did, it slowed, then stopped, then seemed to recollect itself in a reflection of her own emotional state.

Not thinking was much harder than Jia had expected. When she calmed down the pain would abate, but without the pain to use as a focus, her mind would wander and she would lose control again, starting the entire process over.

As Jia kept trying, her grim determination itself became another emotion to focus on. With her determination as a guide, the energy began to carve a path through her meridians in a straight line instead of scattering randomly.

The pain returned as it burned through what Jia understood to be the impurities of her soul, but she was ready for it this time, and didn’t allow her focus to slip. The energy burned its way in a straight line from her lungs down through her core and past her stomach. The process was agonizingly slow—with each breath she took the energy inched its way along and pure qi filled in the meridians behind it.

Eventually, the path through the meridians gave way into an empty space below her stomach. As the burning energy broke through, the pain immediately subsided, and the qi being drawn in by her breathing gathered in that space—her dantian—and began to circulate within it. Caught by the tide of Jia’s qi, the strange burning energy that had opened her meridian was pushed back up through the path it had created and purged harmlessly by her next exhalation.

Jia spent a few more breaths drawing in qi and circulating it within her dantian. It was a very intuitive process, fueled more by feeling than thought. Unlike the energy that had opened the path to her dantian, or the stale qi that clogged her meridians, the qi that flowed within her dantian felt like a part of her—like her aura or the ki within her body.

Satisfied that whatever danger she was in had passed, Jia allowed herself to wake from her meditation. The first thing that she noticed was that she was slick with sweat. For a panicked moment, she worried that the purged impurities had covered her as they had when she awakened her ki, but that didn’t seem to be the case. The next thing she noticed was that Eui had fallen asleep next to her.

Eui’s sleep seemed fitful, and she wore a pained expression, holding Jia’s hand in a vice grip. Jia noticed that her face seemed to be streaked by tears and experienced a sudden surge of empathetic emotion. Jia took a steadying breath as her own eyes began to water—she felt her emotions much more vividly than she had before.

Awakening her mana sense had been like seeing new colors that hadn’t previously existed. By contrast, unlocking her qi felt like she had been looking at colors through a hazy film of glass which had now been lifted, except with emotion instead of color.

It was an imperfect analogy, but Jia had no other way to describe it. It was so vivid that seeing evidence of someone else’s emotion was enough to cause a sympathetic reaction. Jia knew that this was going to take a lot of getting used to, perhaps more than any of her other awakenings.

With Eui’s head in her lap, and her hand thoroughly captured, Jia couldn’t really move anywhere without waking her. Instead, she considered what had just happened while she watched Eui’s fitful rest.

The jade slip had come with some kind of...test? A trial to forcefully awaken the user? Why? It’s not like an unawakened disciple would have been able to use it anyway. Or was there some kind of special training that would allow them to do so? There was too much that Jia still didn’t know about the world of immortal practitioners.

What she did know for certain was that there was no way she was putting Eui through that—that method of awakening seemed unnecessarily cruel and dangerous. What if she hadn’t been able to let go of her thoughts or reign in her emotions? What would have happened if that energy had just rampaged through her soul unchecked? Nothing good, she was sure.

As much as Eui was her best friend, Jia knew that she existed in a constant state of emotional turmoil. The risk was simply too great. With her mind made up, Jia reached over and stored the jade slip safely away within her ring. She would find another way to help Eui awaken.

After such a heavy week of cramming, Jia wanted to take the next day off to recover. She was surprised by how quickly her more minor scrapes and burns had healed, but her right arm still stung painfully from where she’d taken a direct hit from Yan’s flames. An injury that should have put her in a hospital, and here she was complaining that it wasn’t entirely healed after a day.

She found an unexpected problem with taking the day off after getting used to a week of non-stop training.

“I’m boooored!”

Jia flopped over dramatically on the couch, with her legs dangling over the side. Eui cracked an eye open from her meditation and scowled down at Jia’s head in her lap.

“You do realize there’s an entire other couch for you to sit on, right?”

Jia pouted up at Eui, puffing out her cheeks.

“You owe me from yesterday!”

Eui blushed slightly. She had been extremely embarrassed when she’d woken up in Jia’s lap, and it was fun to tease her about it.

“B-be that as it may, I’m trying to meditate and you’re extremely distracting.”

Eui glanced at Jia’s legs as she spoke, then sighed with exasperation.

“Look, if you’re so bored why not just go do something? You’d normally have a tutoring session with Hyeong, right?”

“I don’t wanna!”

Jia kicked her feet petulantly. This was the precious day off that she had earned after training so hard all week—there was no way she was about to waste it on reading lessons. After she’d learned to read and write, Dae had just seamlessly moved on to more complex magical language.

“You were the one who told me that unlocking my qi with that jade slip was too dangerous—and I trust your judgement—but I’m never going to do it the old fashioned way if you keep pestering me!”

“But nobody told me that heightened emotions included boredom! How come I can meditate for twelve hours straight without a problem, but five minutes of relaxing makes me want to pull my hair out?”

Jia stretched her arms and sprawled out even further across Eui’s lap, much to her chagrin. As Eui clenched a fist, Jia saw a vein bulging on her forehead and considered that perhaps she’d been teasing her friend a bit too much. Nah.

“Don’t you dare! I worked too hard on it. Speaking of which, would it kill you to brush your own hair?”

“Yes.”

Eui narrowed her eyes, but refrained from further comment.

“Fine! It’s almost midday, right? Why not take up Elder Qin on his offer now that you’ve awakened? Start learning to fly or something while I sit here digging through my meridians for the next ten years.”

Jia winced a bit at Eui’s tone. While she’d agreed not to risk the jade slip, she was clearly not happy at falling behind in cultivation.

“Maybe...but it’s supposed to be my day off. I’ll go if you go.”

Eui let out another exasperated sigh.

“I don’t think the invitation came with a plus one, Jia.”

“I don’t care. If Elder Qin doesn’t like it then he can fuck off.”

Idly, Jia wondered if Eui’s foul mouth was rubbing off on her. She had cursed like that in her fight with Yan as well, though that had been an intentional ploy to throw him off. Eui arched an eyebrow at Jia.

“Yeah, and he’ll probably do just that. I don’t think he’s going to be particularly heartbroken at not having to teach you, considering how reluctant he’s been to teach anybody at all.”

It was a good point, but Jia wasn’t in the mood for good points. She didn’t know why she felt so clingy today, but her emotions were running wild after her awakening and it was suddenly much more difficult to moderate her mood.

“I’m not going if you’re not going. Besides, maybe I can get him to help you awaken without making your heart explode or whatever the heck that thing was going to do to me.”

“I really doubt you’re going to convince Elder Qin to do anything he doesn’t want to do, but if it gets you off of my fucking lap, then fine! Let’s go.”

The eastern pavilion was actually a large, three-tiered pagoda overlooking the river that ran through the eastern part of the campus. Unlike the bustling urban center of the campus or the spartan training field, this district was lush with parks and gardens. The pavilion itself had a number of hanging flowers and a pleasing vista of the river running through the gardens.

Strangely, despite the cold weather, the gardens here seemed to be as bright and colorful as if it were a fresh spring bloom. Lee Jia could only assume that there was some sort of formation at work.

It occurred to Lee Jia as she approached that this was also the side of the campus where the boys’ dorms would be. While the girls’ wing wasn’t without its own leisure areas, these seemed to be much nicer. Looking around, there didn’t seem to be any sign of Elder Qin.

“Come to think of it, he didn’t actually specify a day. How is he supposed to know we’re coming?”

“Should I choose to focus my attention, there is no place in this academy which is beyond my perception.”

Lee Jia and Eui were both startled by Elder Qin’s voice appearing behind them. Eui even drew her knives as she twisted around, eying the elder warily. Qin arched an eyebrow at her and scoffed.

“Put those away before you hurt yourself, child. Miss Lee, I thought it was implicit that my invitation was for you alone.”

Lee Jia glared at Elder Qin and crossed her arms in defiance.

“Eui is my closest friend and ally. If you want me to ‘walk the path of true cultivation’ then I will do it with her beside me, or not at all.”

Eui seemed to be more surprised by Jia’s words than Elder Qin, who simply raised an eyebrow and stroked his long thin beard.

“I see. That is a powerful bond for one you’ve met so recently. Why, then, has she not undergone the trial of awakening?

“Is that what you call that? I’m not putting my friend through that torture, you psychopath! What would happen if she failed?”

Elder Qin frowned.

“Mind your tongue, child. I’m willing to grant you patience due to your ignorance and your recent awakening, but do not test me. A failed trial would prove her unworthy of my illustrious ancestor’s techniques. Miss An’s meridians would be burned away, the path of cultivation forever lost to her. In rare cases, this results in qi starvation and death.”

Eui blanched and then grimaced at Elder Qin.

“You tricked Jia into taking that trial without warning! She could have died.”

Qin nodded sagely.

“Yes, she could have, and so might you. No disciple who undergoes the trial is warned of what it entails. They are prepared for it in other ways, but that is a matter of culture and upbringing. Miss Lee succeeded because she was worthy—because it was her destiny. If you seek to join your destiny to hers, then prove yourself worthy of it.”

Jia and Eui exchanged a look, and Jia shook her head slightly. Eui turned back to Elder Qin.

“I think I’m with Jia on this one. It sounds like that trial is just gonna kill me. Do you really put all your young disciples through that? What even is that jade slip, anyway?”

Elder Qin shook his head ruefully.

“No, it’s typically reserved only for servants of the imperial family or the great sects. Each awakening stone was created by the hand of the God Emperor himself. There are a limited number in existence and all but one is in the possession of either the imperial family or the great sects.”

Lee Jia paled.

“Does that mean someone’s going to come after me to get it back? A-are you ok with me having this?”

“It’s of no consequence to me, and even if it were, I would not lower myself to rob even a nominal disciple. Yan Zhihao might, but he will do so alone and only after recovering enough face after his recent embarrassment.”

Lee Jia released a sigh of relief, then returned her attention to matter at hand.

“Elder Qin, surely there’s a safer way to help Eui awaken aside from years of arduous meditation?”

Elder Qin Zhao regarded the two girls cooly, stroking his beard as if contemplating something.

“I suppose there is one other way.”
