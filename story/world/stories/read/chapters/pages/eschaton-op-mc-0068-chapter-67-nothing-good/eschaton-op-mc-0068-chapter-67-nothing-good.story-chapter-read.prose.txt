All three of Second Squad’s heavy vehicles are rumbling with low-RPM malice, and Sergeant Callisto’s fireteam grips their weapons nervously. Private Dillion Anders and Specialist Sandra Willis carry the squad’s recon UAV. Corporal Richardson is the squad’s medic.

“It’ll be fine, yeah?” Sergeant Callisto says, doing her best to calm their nerves. “Look, Mach is here. We’ve seen first hand what he can do. Stay locked in and keep a look out. We’ll be fine.”

Thomas, Stryker 1’s gunner shakes his hands out before gripping the remote weapon station’s controls tightly.

Fractures are incredibly hostile, and diving into even something as ‘low’ as a Category-2 is still very possibly one of the most dangerous things a Typical can do. On the entire planet there’s less than 10,000 people who can go into one and be confident they won’t be severely hurt or even killed. A Cat-2 might be a serious step down from the Cat-3 earlier, but there’s not much difference between dead and super dead.

Even packing their new SCAR-Hs, battle rifles chambered in higher calibers than their old M4s doesn’t really mean much. Anything they might face inside will either be too tough for them to hurt, or too fast for them to hit.

At least they have armored vehicles, but even that isn’t a guarantee. The average monster in a Cat-2 might have some difficulty chewing through the up-armored Stykers, but disabling their weapon systems would be child’s play for them. The remote weapon stations aren’t nearly as well armored as the rest of the APCs.

“Let the big guns and Mach take care of the monsters, and just do your jobs. Remember, we’re not here to kill shit, we’re here to do smart stuff.”

“Mach’s been in there for five minutes. Time to go. Vehicles, advance.” Lieutenant Matterson orders over the radio.

The grumbling becomes a growl as the engines rev up, and the Stryker lurches forward, just behind Lt. Matterson as he leads from the front.

“Here we go!” Thomas cries, and the entire squad barely holds onto their breakfast as the feeling of falling is nearly overwhelming. Callisto clenches her teeth.

“Oh, god,” Sandra groans. “I hate this. I hate this so much.”

Thomas scans the area with his gunnery sight, and the other vehicles are doing much the same. After a few seconds of silence, Lt. Matterson sends the dismount order.

“All clear. Second Squad, dismount. Sergeant Callisto, take command of the ground section.”

The door opens and slams to the ground, and Sergeant Callisto is the first one out.

“Come on, move it! Go, go go!”

The Fracture is a wide open field, with grass nearly five feet tall. Callisto’s eyes are just barely over the tips of the vibrant blades of grass. All of Second Squad pour out from their vehicles and make an arc around the entrance of the Fracture.

Machina prowls around the tall grass, looking for monsters. His gunmetal gray cloak wraps around him, and grass slides off it with a hiss.

“Mach!” Callisto calls out, and his faceplate, filled with blue fire, turns to her. She makes a cutting motion with her hand.

“Cut the grass! Give us some sightlines!”

He nods, and begins to cut huge swaths of the tall grass down with his sword. He pauses after a short while, and then goes to start collecting it.

“Mach, don’t worry about that, we got it. Eric, start bundling that stuff up!” Callisto tells him, and Mach nods before resuming clearing out the area.

“Why do you think he wants that stuff?” Sandra asks.

Callisto shrugs. “Hell if I know, magic shit probably. But if he wants it, he gets it. Come on, get the drone set up. Who knows what’s out here.”

“Nothing good.” Dillion mutters while putting together the launcher.

Callisto snorts. “That’s why they pay us the big bucks. Thor, set up the core drill, on the double. Jericho, you’re on the gas collector.”

Both of their respective fireteams start pulling equipment that was strapped to the Strykers’ hulls. Off to the direction Lt. Matterson designated as arbitrary north is a forest, though they seem to be oddly colorful for mere trees.

Looking up, Callisto notices the sky is made of glass, oddly enough. Or rather, there’s a glass roof high above. What she thought was an oddly close horizon is actually white walls, though how far they are is impossible to tell. Space and distance get fucky inside Fractures.

A few more minutes go by, and the cleared area expands as Mach acts like a lawnmower. Eric and his fire team are quickly bundling the long blades of grass with zip ties.

Callisto, scanning the horizon again, points at the oddly colorful section of it.

“Are those flowers?” She asks. Machina nods without looking at her.

“Yes. They’re about eighty feet tall. Best guess is they’re about three miles away. Distance inside a Fracture is always an uncertain thing.”

The squad’s drone launches with a ka-chunk, and the propeller comes to life with a buzz.

“Do you mind if I take control of that?” Mach asks.

“Go for it, Mach.” Matterson replies. Evidently Mach is transmitting over the radio as well as verbally. Although, for someone like him, is there really much of a difference? Callisto isn’t exactly sure, but puts it out of her mind.

The drone slowly spirals around their position, searching for anything that might be a monster trying to sneak up on them. Mach spins around with a snap, and the entire squad tenses, looking in the same direction.

“Monster, about forty meters that way.” He says, pointing and charging towards it. “I got it!” His cloak flutters behind him.

Evidently the monster notices its attempt at stealth has failed, and charges in kind. Grass disappears, flattened under the monster, though both Callisto and whatever it is is too short for her to actually see it. Machina and the monster meet, and the unseen creature hisses in rage.

The snake leaps above the field, and Machina dodges out of the way before grabbing the twenty foot long snake by the tail. He whips it into the ground, and it dies with a sickening crunch of shattered bone. His cloak whirls around him, almost alive in how it snakes and slithers out of his way.

The entire execution—it certainly wasn’t a fight—took just a handful of seconds, barely enough time for Second Squad to ready their weapons.

Not that they would have done much good.

Callisto pushes that thought aside too. They weren’t brought along to fight, but to assist Mach in picking apart any secrets the Fracture might hide.

Machina drags the half-smashed corpse of the snake back to their position, leaving a smear of gore behind. Thin rivulets of blood run off the cloak, leaving it pristine. He expertly takes the snake apart, carving the monster into smaller, more manageable chunks. Eric and Jericho, both experienced hunters, are the only two with the stomach to assist him in packing away the gruesome catch.

Machina finally comments on the various scientific instruments after finishing with the snake. His impassive nature refuses to betray his thoughts.

“Sergeant, what are those?” He says, pointing at them.

“Uh, core sampler, a gas collector, and a seismometer or something. Supposedly it’ll map the ground of the Fracture or something like that, I dunno. They didn’t tell us how it works, only how to set it up. There’s a few others.”

“Right. Well, once you’re done, we’ll move on. I’m not going to leave you here alone.”

It takes almost an hour for the core sampler to finish, and by the time it’s done, all the other scientific experiments are finished. Second Squad rapidly breaks them down before storing them back on the vehicles. The multiple bundles of grass vanish with a flash light as Machina stores them away.

“Where do you want to go next, Mach?” Lieutenant Matterson asks over the radio.

Mach points towards the forest of flowers. “*That seems interesting. I wonder what’s over there?”

“He sounds almost excited to be here,” Sandra mutters.

“I don’t really blame him,” Callisto says. “Haven’t you seen the kind of collateral damage he causes just by existing? Here he doesn’t have to care.”

“Besides, as lethal as it is, this freaky shit is kinda cool,” Thomas cuts in. “Where else are you gonna find a flower as tall as a tree?”

“You don’t have to leave the nice armored vehicle with the big gun,” Richardson points out.

“That part is nice,” Thomas concedes.

The trio of armored vehicles accelerate, and Mach easily keeps pace. He passes the Bradley to take the lead, cloak fluttering behind him. The tree-sized flowers get closer, though not for free. On multiple occasions snakes attempt to ambush the convoy, though ‘attempt’ is the operative word. Through the drone circling overhead, Mach’s easily able to spot them long before they become a danger.

He marches back with a live snake. It writhes and wriggles in his grip, but fails to do anything of substance, despite being more than twice his size. He holds it up in view of Stryker 1’s gun.

“Thomas, would you mind shooting it for me? I want to see how effective the gun you’ve got is.”

“Is he fuckin’ serious?” Thomas says before replying over the radio.

“Uh, are you sure, sir? I can’t promise I won’t hit you.”

“I’m sure. Go ahead and shoot it, please. Give it a good burst.”

Thomas pulses the trigger, and a burst of eight rounds rips from the gun to slam into the head of the monster. The round skips off the heavy scales of the snake, though it’s clearly rattled. It stops writhing for a brief moment and goes limp. Blood trickles from where Thomas shot it, but that’s the limit of its injuries.

“*Keep shooting it until it dies, please.”

Thomas doesn’t have to be told twice, and he holds down the trigger on the remote weapon station. A few rounds miss and bounce off Mach. His only reaction to being slapped in the face by a .50 caliber BMG round is a slight jerk of his head.

After just a few seconds of consistent fire, the monster’s tough scales finally fail, and rounds start plunging into its soft insides. Bursts of blood and viscera splatter over Mach. The snake goes limp. Thomas ceases fire, and Mach drops the ragged and mutilated corpse. He stores the monster away after cutting it into smaller chunks.

“Eugh, that’s gross.” Anders says, looking at Thomas’ gunnery console. “I can’t imagine how he doesn’t mind being covered in blood and guts. That’d have me running for the nearest puddle.”

“Shit just changes when you’re bullet proof, Private. I bet he doesn’t care about a lotta shit you would.” Callisto says.

Lt. Matterson comes over the radio. “Hey, Mach, you done playing with your food? We’re burning fuel here.”

The convoy slowly rolls up to the flower forest as it gently sways in a gentle breeze. A few petals, each one the size of a sail, break loose and dance in the wind. A faint buzzing can be heard, though barely. It must be deafening outside if Callisto can hear it through the Stryker’s armored hull. Mach points up.

“We got flyers. Wasps. Lots of them.”

“All vehicles, look up and fire at will!” Lt Matterson calls out over the radio.

Tracers light up the sky, red bolts arcing up and slamming into a swarm of wasps.

“Oh, I fuckin’ hate bugs!” Thomas groans.

“So quit bitchin’ and shoot em!” Callisto growls.

“I am!”

The heavy 50. caliber machine mounted up top is muted through the Stryker’s armor. The muffled thumpthumpthumpthump of the top gun makes the interior jump and shake. The Bradley joins in with its 25mm autocannon, and catches a wasp with the end of a three round burst. It explodes in a burst of yellow and green gore. A dozen more replace it, and the swarm grows.

“Holy fuckin’ shit there’s a fuckin’ lot of ’em Sarge!”

The swarm of wasps, each one half as large as the Strykers themselves descend from tree tops, weaving around colorful petals. As one, they dive towards the armored convoy.

Machina leaps into the air with a boom, sword in hand. He leaves behind a crater and a cloud of dust. A vicious slash screams through the air, and carves through three wasps. He grabs a fourth and throws it down. It crashes to the ground with a crack, its yellow and black carapace shattering. He lands on it with a crunch, killing it.

The heavy guns of Second Squad vehicles reap a deadly toll on the diving insects, and a dozen more explode, hammered into paste by the Bradley’s wrathful autocannon.

One of the wasps lands on Stryker 1, and everyone inside is slammed into one wall as the vehicle nearly tips over. It’s only kept upright by the wasp’s own beating wings as it tries to lift up the heavy vehicle. A terrifying stinger, at least two and a half feet long, stabs towards the front of the Stryker. It skitters in between the slats of the cage-like slat armor before sliding off the nose of the armored vehicle with an ear-piercing screech.

Thomas, somehow still in his seat, screaming curses, hoses the wasp’s basketball-sized compound eye. It bursts, and the wasp twitches and writhes before dying, the .50 caliber rounds pulping what little brains it had.

The wasp’s body slumps to one side, still gripping onto the slat armor, and pulls the Stryker over. The APC falls onto its side with a boom, and Callisto is slammed into Richardson just across from her. Anders goes head first into Sandra’s face, and she screams as his helmet breaks her nose with a crunch.

Thomas is still somehow in his seat, with one hand still on the RWS’s gunnery station. He screams at the swarm of bugs buzzing around the embattled convoy.

“COME ON! GET SOME, FUCKIN’ GET SOME!”
