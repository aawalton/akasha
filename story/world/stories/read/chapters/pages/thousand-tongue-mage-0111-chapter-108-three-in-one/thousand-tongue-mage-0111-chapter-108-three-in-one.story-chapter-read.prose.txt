The capital may be screaming around them—flames chewing through roofs, civilians wailing in alleyways, giant bugs slamming into barricades—yet the carriage cut through it all like a spearhead. Ifas drove straight south like he was late to a feast, and Enki appreciated the driver not slowing down for his sake.

He knelt on the roof of the carriage, one boot hooked under a ridge of carved metal so Ifas’ violent driving couldn’t throw him off, and reached into his cloak to pull out a handful of pebbles. These weren’t the best ammunition he could ask for, but they’d suffice for Giant-Class bugs. He fed a stone into the chamber and pulled back the bolt, raising his rifle to fire.

A giant ant’s head burst on a roof. He chambered and fired. A giant ant’s neck tore in half and topped into a burning cart. He chambered and fired again. The giant bugs seemed just as surprised, stumbling and falling to the ground in droves as his carriage sped by and he unloaded a storm of precise bullets at their heads.

When Ifas swerved the carriage around the corner and Enki reached under his cloak for a second fistful of pebbles, he smelled something that didn’t belong in this city of death and smoke.

Food.

Kita poked her head out of the carriage window below, shouting. “The first ant meat strips are cooked! Come down and get some!”

Enki reloaded and kept his cheek against the rifle stock. “Bring it up to me.”

After a brief pause, a conveyor belt of tiny army ants assembled itself beside him, rising from the carriage window and curving up the side to reach his feet. He glanced at the ants only briefly before firing at another nearby bug, and he had to admit, he was a little taken aback watching the first steaming strip arrive by being trickled along the belt of tiny little legs.

Her Art is pretty strong if it can not only heal her, but also be used to make constructs like these.

Or is it just her who’s strong?

He snatched the meat strip up with one hand and bit down as he fired another shot into the horde. The taste surprised him as well. His half-inorganic body meant he couldn’t taste much of any ‘normal’ seasoned food, but the meat strip was extraordinarily salty, and it was smoky in a way that made it almost… delicious, in a way.

Ifas is a good cook, too, he thought. If Kita weren’t choking to death down there because of how salty these strips are, I might believe him to be just an extraordinary chef.

His next shot was heavier. He aimed at the lower supports of a leaning warehouse and pulled the trigger. The projectile punched into stone and crushed a dozen bugs with the collapsing roof. He fired again, and again, walking the shots along structurally weak buildings to fold them into the street, blocking off entire routes and forcing the horde to find new paths to their targets.

He chewed, swallowed, and didn’t let the satisfaction show on his face as he kept eating the meat strips being sent his way.

“A good chef doesn’t neglect taste even when the world is falling apart.” Ifas glanced back over his shoulder while still driving, grin audible in his voice. “It’s not the best because I don’t even have a name for this ‘dish’, but how is it?”

Enki took another bite. It was his fortieth, because he was eating as fast as Kita could send them up.

“... It’s okay,” he said at last.

Ifas laughed like that was the highest praise on earth, then snapped the reins again. The carriage surged even faster now, the giant silver ants dragging it through debris and blood.

The streets widened. The buildings grew heavier, more ornate, and more defensive in structure. The roar of battle behind them swelled—two armies descending from the northern wall as cannons now thundered inward—but the closer Enki got to the southern wall, the quieter those roars sounded.

They were close enough to Regalia now that the air itself felt different, so Enki narrowed his eyes at the heavy stone gates carved into the bottom of the southern wall at the far end of the street. Calling them ‘gates’ might not be an understatement, though. They were ten-meter-tall giants reinforced with metal and chitin, and beyond them lay the beach where the Crawling Seas bashed against the empire. Beyond them were where two hundred of the three hundred Spore Knights were locked in constant battle against the Crawling Seas, holding the Swarm back from devouring the entire southern end of the continent. Beyond them was nothing but death and the men who fought to stave it off at all costs… even if it meant ignoring the fact that the capital city behind them was being ravaged from the inside out.

Only the Spore Knights inside the empire were turned into Regalia’s undead soldiers. The remaining two hundred must still be outside those gates right now, fending off the Crawling Seas.

They’d clear out all of the giant bugs in the city in less than an hour if only I could warp them inside, but…

The Spore Knights had to stay outside. They wouldn’t be able to help him with protecting the city, and they certainly wouldn’t be able to help him with Regalia, who was standing at the very top of the wall.

She didn’t look hurried. She didn’t look threatened. Her long cape was a river of black zombie ants, wriggling and flowing around her like royal fabric made of decay, and she barely glanced at them as Ifas continued barreling their carriage down the main street towards her.

Tens of thousands of zombie ants immediately poured from her arms and into the carved stone, and the top of the wall immediately spiderwebbed with cracks and splinters, weakening, sickening… but the decay didn’t stop there. If she could decay and shatter even this one small section of the southern wall, that’d allow her to easily kill the Spore Knights still fending off the Crawling Seas from behind, and then there’d be no wall to stop the Crawling Seas from pouring into the continent.

Golden eyes.
