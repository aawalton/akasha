Tessa arrived with the news before breakfast had even properly started, breathless and panting.

"Lord Taren is dead."

Lira tried to show the right amount of surprise on her face, matching the other maids already in the kitchen, letting Tessa have her moment as the girl spread the gossip with barely contained excitement.

"He fell off the outer wall last night," Tessa explained, sliding onto the bench across from Lira as the other maids gathered to listen. "One of the night guards found him at dawn. They're saying he must have wandered up there drunk and lost his footing."

"Seriously?" Lira said with a frown.

Tessa shrugged. "That's what everyone is saying. He was always stumbling around fully in his cups. It was only a matter of time, honestly."

It was a remarkably convenient conclusion for the entire palace to arrive at within a single morning. Lira wondered whether someone had deliberately seeded it, or whether it had simply grown on its own out of the man's own reputation.

"What happens to the Lord Steward's seat now?" Lira asked.

Tessa shrugged.

"Sir Harwin is probably keeping it," speculated Brenneth, Old Marga's second.

"And Cormenfield?" Lira asked.

"Should stay with Lord Noah," the older woman said. "I don't see any reason for that to change now."

Tessa reached for a piece of bread off Lira's plate. "I heard Sir Harwin's already living in the grand steward's manor with Lady Rosalind. I wonder if they'll get kicked out, or keep it until a new steward is appointed."

"Who knows," Lira said, taking a sip of her milk. Knowing what she knew, she couldn't help but suspect this had been planned from the start, maybe Taren always meant to be removed the moment he had served his purpose.

For the rest of the day, Lira returned to her duties like nothing had happened.

The library ran its usual routine, with only a handful of noble visitors. In the afternoon, when the noble youths trickled in after their sword training with Deren, she finally felt the shift in mood as they clustered into smaller groups, heads bent together, murmuring about Taren's death, about whether Harwin's new wife had cried at the news. Rather than push book suggestions on them, Lira let them talk it through, processing the complexity of the subject of death.

By the time the sun had slid halfway toward dusk, the doors opened again, and this time it wasn't another noble youth. Sir Estophon filled the doorway first, a man she didn't recognize a step behind him, Sir Liam trailing after both. Her stomach dropped the moment she saw them; she understood, with a sinking feeling, that she was the reason they had come.

Estophon's eyes found her immediately, then flicked past her toward Henry, who stood behind Lyth's chair. Henry caught the look and started moving even as Estophon and the stranger crossed toward Lira. Sir Liam stepped in to cover Henry's post, taking his place at Lyth's back.

"Miss Lira Tenlan."

"My lord." Lira dropped into a curtsy, hoping it hid how fast her pulse was beating.

"We are here to investigate you regarding Lord Taren's death."

He must have caught something in her face, because he raised a hand before she could speak, his tone gentle, almost apologetic.

"Don't worry yourself, miss. It is a formality, nothing more. We are quite certain his death was an accident." He gave her a reassuring smile. "To prove our good faith, we've brought Sir Henry along as a witness, since the two of you are already familiar with one another. He can attest that nothing irregular, or magical, took place."

Lira nodded slowly, her thoughts already racing. She was in a precarious position; she might not have killed Taren, but she had planned to. More than that, she had no wish to be investigated at all, not when she had already done enough to be hanged a hundred times over. The only thing working in her favor was that whatever narrative Estophon was building probably didn't call for a maid to be the scapegoat.

Her eyes found Henry. Whether the knight would actually be fair to her was another question. They weren't friends, not really, and she knew better than most how much he still doubted her.

"Let's find somewhere private to speak," said the Captain of the Royal Guard, and Lira nodded and led them upstairs, to one of the cleaned rooms where a table and a handful of chairs already waited. She chose this room because one of her paper ropes lay hidden beneath a shelf nearby, and some quiet part of her mind was already measuring the distance to the window, readying herself to use it if things turned for the worse.

Estophon set a jade stone on the table.

"Do you know what this is?"

Lira shook her head. She could probably guess, but she didn't know for certain.

"I need you to use words, not gestures."

"No," she said aloud. "I don't know what it is."

The third man, the one whose name she still didn't have, looked at the stone and gave a small nod.

"I need you to say one truth and one lie, in that order."

"I have orange hair." A pause. "My name is Elam Croft."

At the second statement, the stone shifted, the faintest tremor against the wood.

Estophon glanced at it, satisfied.

"Do you know why you are being investigated?"

"Yes. I must have come up in a Detect Hate divination." Lira kept her voice level.

"You know of it?" Estophon's brow lifted slightly.

"There's a memoir in the library. Written by a retired investigator. It described what is normally done when a noble's death is suspected of foul play."

"And do you suspect you hated Lord Taren enough to be named in it?"

Lira nodded, then caught herself and remembered the rule. "Yes. He assaulted a friend of mine. A sixteen-year-old girl who works in the library, under my supervision."

"What is this girl's name?"

"Maid Tessa."

Estophon looked toward the man, probably the mage guild's Diviner, and the man drew a folded list from his coat pocket. Lira let her eyes pass over it for only a second, long enough to read every name on it at once.

Celene was first. Harwin second. Two unfamiliar names came next. Then Lira's name sat fifth. And Rosalind sixth. She noticed that Yolanda, Mina, and Eve weren't there, and considered if it was the list of people in the capital that hated him.

"There's no Tessa on this list," the stranger said. "Not in the top twenty. She doesn't meet the threshold."

Estophon's eyebrow rose toward Lira.

"How is it you hated him more than the girl he assaulted?"

Lira shrugged, then made herself answer properly. "She's cheerful. Lively. She is probably already moved past it. I haven't, because it happened while she was under my care, and I couldn't do anything to stop it in the moment."

Estophon glanced at the stone once more, found it still, and nodded.

"Very well. Let's go through the standard questions."

"Did you kill Lord Taren?"

"No."

The stone didn't move.

"Did you cast a spell on him?"

"No." This one was true in the narrowest possible sense; Eagle Eyes was a spell she had cast on herself, not on him.

"Do you know who killed him?"

"If I had to guess, I would say the crown ordered it, so he wouldn't remain in Sir Harwin's way."

It was the closest she could come to avoiding the question outright, an answer that was vague and true in equal measure.

Estophon's brow lifted again at that. "That's a bold accusation to make."

Lira shrugged in response, pointing at the stone that hadn't moved. "I would rather not speak ill of the dead, Sir."

Estophon nodded.

"That's all I need, Miss Tenlan." Estophon rose from his chair. "My apologies for the intrusion on your time."

"Of course, my lord," Lira said, rising with him. "I understand."

"You've been more patient about this than most would be." A faint, tired smile crossed his face. "Good evening to you."

The third man gathered the stone and the list without a word and followed him toward the door.

Henry lingered a moment longer than the others. He hesitated, his eyes landing on her for a beat too long, and something in his expression made Lira's stomach tighten. She could have sworn he was on the verge of asking something, about her real name, probably. But in the end, he said nothing. He only gave her a short nod and turned to follow Estophon out, the door clicking shut behind him.

Lira let out a slow breath once she was finally alone. She could barely believe everything had gone well. One wrong move, and her head would have been on the block.

For a man who had struck Kavard and murdered Taren, Sir Estophon had been surprisingly gentle with her. She couldn't help but wonder if she would have survived a more contentious interview.

Still, now that it was over, she couldn't help but feel relieved. More importantly, she had a rough shape of the questions asked in an interrogation like this. It would probably be needed soon, either for Kavard or for Kelroth.
