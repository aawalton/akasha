After they left the lake behind and resumed their journey toward Vantara, the air grew quieter—cold air rustled against their bodies—as though the forest itself had drawn a long breath and refused to be affected by the hot rays of Aethel, the sun of Ethereum.

"There's someone ahead," Sebastian said softly, his voice low but absolute.

Vector's reaction was immediate. His hand rose in a sharp, controlled gesture, halting the marching teams without the need for spoken command. The formation stilled at once, discipline settling over them like a cloak. No one questioned Sebastian's instincts; no one doubted his perception.

Vector turned his gaze toward him.

Nothing needed to be said.

Sebastian understood instantly. In the next moment, he was gone—not vanished as though swallowed by magic, but already in motion, his body slipping soundlessly between trees, advancing toward the unseen presence with predatory calm.

…

High above, concealed within the thick canopy of a towering tree, Ron crouched with practiced ease, his eyes scanning the forest ahead while the rest of his hunting team remained some distance behind.

A faint disturbance caught his attention.

Seeing the undergrowth foliage shift, he was certain that the disturbance did not belong to the wind.

Beast… and not just one, but many, he thought instinctively.

He opened his mouth to signal his fellow hunters, to warn them of approaching danger—but before he could draw breath, something seized him from behind.

A hand—iron-strong—locked around him.

His vision jolted.

His body was wrenched backward from the branch as though he weighed nothing at all. He hadn't seen anyone approach. Hadn't heard a footstep. Hadn't sensed the presence as they descended from the canopy.

His eyes widened with raw disbelief.

He struggled on reflex, twisting violently in the stranger's grip, but the difference in strength was overwhelming. His limbs felt like those of a child grappling against a stone pillar.

"Obediently follow me," a calm voice whispered near his ear. "Do not attempt any tricks."

The tone was not loud. Not threatening.

Yet the power behind it was undeniable.

Ron stilled.

He felt it clearly—the sheer density of strength contained within this man. Any resistance would be futile, and more importantly, dangerous.

Slowly, he nodded.

The stranger did not radiate hostility. There was no killing intent pressing against his skin. No malice coiling in the air. Only caution.

That alone allowed Ron's racing heart to settle—slightly.

Then he followed the lead of the man.

Moving through the undergrowth, Ron's mind began working rapidly. Who are they? Why did they capture me?

Moments later, they broke through a screen of foliage—and Ron froze.

Tens of figures stood in formation.

Disciplined.

Armed.

Silent.

He also found the reason for his capture shortly after. Once they had interrogated him and discovered that he was a Venyar, a villager of Vantara, the shift in atmosphere was immediate. The cold restraint in their posture eased, though vigilance remained.

Ron observed them carefully.

These were not common hunters. Their equipment was refined, their stances trained, and more importantly—the man who had captured him…

Ron's eyes flicked toward Sebastian.

High-level Body Refining Warrior, he concluded inwardly, swallowing. Stronger than our Village Chief… perhaps even far stronger.

Now that fear had lessened, reason returned.

They must have lost their way, he thought.

It was not unusual for outsiders to wander too deep into the Naga Forest while hunting beasts. Many misjudged the boundaries between the outer and inner regions. And judging by the faint traces on their boots and equipment—the type of soil clinging to them—they had likely strayed farther than intended.

Yes… that must be it.

They captured me to ask for directions.

His confidence in that explanation grew.

But then—

Doubt.

A deeper, more unsettling question surfaced. He had never seen them in Vantara before. Not one face was familiar.

And that was impossible.

Vantara was not a large settlement. Every stranger was noticed. Being a member of Vantara also meant he was a soldier, so he normally knew if any adventurer entered the village. Though not as many, some people still came here in search of beast materials or to gather herbs and medicines from the Naga Forest. And a group of this size—moving in formation, armed, disciplined—could never pass through unnoticed.

Unless…

They had not come from Vantara.

Ron's gaze moved carefully across their numbers again. It was not just the size of the group that unsettled him. It was their unity. They did not carry themselves like independent adventurers temporarily cooperating for convenience. They moved like a single force.

And that realization unsettled him far more than his capture ever had.

Because if they were not lost—then they had come here deliberately. And if they had come deliberately…

…

Vector observed Ron's face closely—the confusion, the guarded suspicion, the way his eyes kept darting between the armed figures of the Astrum Forces as if trying to measure threat against intention.

Without wasting another moment, Vector reached calmly into his inner coat and revealed the Resistance token.

The insignia caught the filtered forest light.

"We are from Carnatic City," Vector said evenly. "We have come to Vantara to meet your Village Chief. What we carry is not a weapon—but intelligence."

Once Vector explained their purpose clearly—without arrogance, without concealment—Ron nodded and gestured for them to follow. The rigid caution in his shoulders eased, though not entirely.

After that, they relied on Ron to guide them to Vantara.

It was the most logical course of action.

No outsider could navigate the winding arteries of the Naga Forest better than a Venyar scout. And from Ron's own admission, their meeting had been fortunate. Had they continued at their current pace, unfamiliar with the most efficient forest routes, they would likely have reached Vantara only after dusk—when visibility declined and the forest grew treacherous.

Now, with Ron guiding them, they would arrive two to three hours before sunset.

Their travel time had been reduced significantly.

And more importantly—

They would not wander blindly into territory where beasts prowled freely.

And also… before their movement began.

Kuoooo—

Ron released a clear, melodic call to inform his team that waited ahead. It was the way of secret communication between the Venyars, as Vector learned after asking Ron the reason for his action.

Kuwoooo—

An answering call echoed from deeper within the woods.

An efficient communication method indeed, Vector thought. Efficient. Subtle. And almost invisible to outsiders.

Without another word, Ron adjusted direction, and the group began moving toward the source of the returning call.

They did not walk long before emerging into a small clearing where another Venyar hunting team waited.

The moment they appeared; tension thickened in the air.

The Venyar warriors stood poised, hands resting near blades and bows, their eyes sharp as they assessed Vector's group. Suspicion was not hidden—it was worn openly.

One man stepped forward—broad-shouldered, composed, clearly the leader of this team.

"Ron," he called out, his tone firm but controlled. "Who are these people? And why are you accompanying them?"

Ron stepped ahead, closing the distance between the two groups so his words would not carry unnecessarily.

"They came from Carnatic City," Ron explained steadily. "They claim to be members of the Resistance." Ron then explained that Vector had shown him the seal. And also, he recounted how he had been captured, subtly indicating toward Sebastian and informing them of his strength—that he was probably even stronger than the Village Chief.

Vector caught the subtle emphasis on "claim." He also noted Sebastian's action of capturing Ron and the mention of his strength… but Vector ignored it.

Wise.

Trust—but verify.

Ron continued, "They have requested an audience with the Village Chief. They say they carry critical intelligence."

The Team Leader's brows furrowed.

"What kind of intelligence?"

Ron shook his head. "I do not know. They insisted it must be spoken directly to the Chief. That is why I am escorting them."

The leader's gaze shifted toward Vector's group—measured, calculating, about to demand clarification himself.

Marcus stepped forward before the question could fully form.

"You'll hear it from your Chief," Marcus said curtly, his tone neither hostile nor deferential—simply firm. "This information is meant to be shared once, and properly. With the right people present. Let's not waste time discussing fragments in the forest."

His eyes did not waver.

"Let's get going."

The Venyar leader studied them for a long breath. Worry flickered across his features—subtle, but present.

Without further delay, he made a quick decision. He dispatched several of his men ahead—likely to inform the village of approaching outsiders—while he and the remaining hunters repositioned themselves strategically.

Vector saw their actions for what they were—half escort and half surveillance—but he let it not bother him as he followed their group.

The forest path that followed bore clear signs of frequent use. Beast tracks were sparse, and disturbed earth revealed deliberate patrol routes. Broken branches and subtle markers hinted at regular clearing.

It became evident.

Vantara's warriors actively purged the beasts along this approach.

Before encountering Ron, Vector's group had fought multiple creatures on the way—common predators of the outer region. But from this point onward, the forest grew noticeably quieter.

The Venyar maintained their borders well.

As they advanced, the dense canopy gradually thinned. The wilderness gave way to signs of sustained human presence—cut tree trunks, reinforced pathways, elevated watch platforms partially concealed among the branches.

Soon, more villagers came into view.

Some returned from hunts, carrying beast carcasses slung across their shoulders. Unlike the Astrum Forces—who had harvested only essential materials to maintain speed—the Venyar transported nearly whole carcasses. Meat, bone, hide—nothing was wasted.

They were a self-sustaining community.

By the time the sun tilted westward—casting long golden beams through the thinning forest, roughly between the third and fourth hour past noon—Vector's group approached the outer perimeter of Vantara.

And then—

The gate came into view. Before they could proceed further, a group approached from within the gate. At its center walked an elder.

The elder stepped forward. His bearing was calm but authoritative, without arrogance.

"Greetings," the elder said, his voice calm but firm, carrying neither hostility nor warmth—only measured clarity. "I am Arthur Venyar, the Village Chief of Vantara."
