The ship lay cracked in half and twisted like a broken fortune cookie, its exposed ribs clawing into the sand.

Amos was the first to appear, though I use the word carefully. He was translucent, his hands clutching several golden cores. He didn’t translocate down the hull so much as slide, stuttering with every movement. Olethros shot forward, calling up waves of animus in his wake. Within moments, a pulsing purple magic circle bloomed around them both, about five feet in diameter, where Amos huddled with his collection of cores.

“You’re among friends!” I shouted at the wreckage, just to make sure they knew that.

A hatch on the other half of the ship creaked open hesitantly, with the royal, golden-haired twins peering over the edge dubiously.

“Fabian! Marcus!” I waved as I ran across the sandy beach, exaggerating the movement.

Captain Morin’s head popped up as the twins started climbing down. “Yer alive! By the Goddess!” he bellowed, shaking the rickety wood holding the ship together.

Olethros flinched at the Captain’s shouts and shadowy vines erupted from him to stabilize the structure. “I need you two here, now,” he hissed to no one in particular, dark smoke roiling off his shoulders as he squeezed his eyes shut.

A chorus of snips and snaps filled the air as Ragnerus and Aziza appeared, only for them both to startle at the sight of the shipwreck. Olethros pointed resolutely, and Ragnerus held out a hand along with the Demon King to solidify his hold on what was left of the ship. As Captain Morin descended next, several more familiar faces emerged from the vault of the ship.

Prince Mito reached into the hatch, pulling up a somewhat seasick Emmy in tow. Father Irijah followed, supported by several ex-priest subordinates. Father Titus emerged with Kiko, young queen of ninja mischief, along with her conscripted friend Erdric. Kiko chittered and danced impatiently while Erdric struggled to carry Beast, the ship’s cat, to safety.

But the last man out made me actually pause.

Roderick the Rooster, leader of Asha’s Refuge, hit the sand and crowed, rolling on his back as he loudly swore never to take to sea again.

Aziza simply watched from a distance, not one to join the rescue. Eventually, she took one hard look at Amos in the summoned magic circle, and amplified its shadowy output.

“How did you manage to hold it together?” she demanded of her ex-subordinate. “Never mind how you replicated the Bastion’s translocation process. I need to know how you managed to keep the current flowing through wood, of all things.”

Amos flinched, bowing his head deeply as he clutched the demon cores protectively to him. “Nails and rivets. Some of them might even still be attached.”

“Give Galenus to me.” Aziza reached past the circle’s threshold with hungry, clawed fingers.

Amos pulled a rather cracked core out of the mix carefully, shaking as he held it out for Aziza to grasp. “He wanted to stay behind, to make sure no one was lost mid-translocation. But Tarek and I wouldn’t let him…”

She sniffed, grabbing the core and pulling it to her chest with a crimson glow. “Stubborn old goat never did have any self-preservation skills. But I’m looking the other way on this one.” Her eyes stared off toward the sea, then she cleared her throat menacingly. “And I expect you to make me an offer soon, Amos. I’ll overlook any past transgressions, and we can talk about your assignments once we reach an accord.”

Amos blinked several times, then shook his head stubbornly. “I serve the Demon King, Dread Mistress.”

“Hmph,” she replied, pouting slightly. Then she caught me staring. “Don’t think for a moment I’m giving up,” she snapped. “In fact, feel free to tell him exactly what you saw. It’ll make my victory even more satisfying.”

I just gave a non-committal shrug, knowing it was best to stay out of it entirely.

Tetora pulled Aleph along to greet Prince Mito and his twin brothers. Meanwhile, Father Irijah, Father Titus, and the other priests descended on Nora, where they held an immediate conference of escalating whispers.

“With the Skreethi?” I heard Nora shout at one point, and the priests all nodded with gusto.

“And Captain Sonea?” she then questioned.

With that, they turned solemn, shaking their heads no. Nora, however, looked suspicious of whatever they were reporting.

Kiko and Erdric decided that it was time to accost me, but I was ready for them.

“There’s no way you two kids were on that ship’s manifest,” I said, giving them the evil eye.

“Two stowaways were added to it later!” Kiko confirmed with a cheeky grin.

Erdrick puffed out his chest, the cat having long since escaped him. “It was her idea, but I… I agreed with it!”

Vernie slid up next to me, and Chester hopped out of her tiny shadow.

“Mrow,” was all he had to say, and the two children stiffened and hung their heads.

I can't believe how many people came here...

With a massive reunion almost in full swing, Councilman Inutilis made his way onto the beach, flanked by the demon Morcelos and Taika, the latest Grandmaster of the Tiger Clan.

“I was to tell you that you were in violation of our terms,” he said, looking around with a smirk. “But it looks like your use of magic here was necessary.”

Taika cocked her head to stare at the Councilman. “Could you at least pretend that you’re a neutral party here? We have serious concerns to discuss!”

Inutilis looked up hard at Paradise, where Sparkles was again, looking for all the world like a tiny, angry gnat hell-bent on boring her way through the barrier protecting it.

“That is the most serious concern we have, my dear lady. Dealing with a few angry residents pales in comparison to having that thing attack us like it did Chairo."

Taika snapped off a growl at being called a lady, then snarled in my direction. “You!”

“Yes?”

She jerked her clawed thumb skywards. “Get rid of that thing! I’m tired of seeing it.”

Morcelos tried to clear his throat, but it just came out like a set of wet gurgles. “What my fellow advisors mean is that now that we’re all here, we would hold council as discussed.”

A few people grunted in weak agreement, but the majority of the crowd was more interested in finding solace after recent events.

I sighed. “Let’s make this quick. I think we all know what needs to be done.”

I’m just not sure how to get up there to do it.

“Two out of three of us representing Nearhaven already said it needs to be taken care of,” Taika grunted, looking at Morcelos. “What, you think someone from the party of ‘let’s just unleash the dragon and the demon lord at once’ is going to hesitate now?” She turned to Prince Mito. “So what say you, Prince of Chairo? Was the attack on the Holy City enough for your castle-bound cronies to wake up?”

Prince Mito had become wise enough not to take the full bait, and merely shrugged. “I say that for too long, Ecclesia has been a state-controlled church.” He frowned then. “It doesn’t escape me that our country’s name doesn’t help with that, but I believe now’s the time to declare a schism.” He then looked at Olethros almost whimsically. “Would Your Majesty like to make a show of support in solidarity?”

Olethros pretended to consider it. “Only if you recognize demons and hybrids as full and equal citizens of your nation.”

Roderick, and to some extent Aleph, visibly stiffened in surprise. His Dark Majesty simply shrugged, running his fingers through his hair. “I’m looking to limit the complaints of the masses, going forward. It would be best to set certain expectations now.”

“If you set the precedent, I’m sure my homeland of Porta will follow—eventually,” Inutilis mused on behalf of his nation. “The King of Turri is truly a mystery even to me, but my best guess is he’s lying low until the problem is solved for him.” Then he looked around. “Anyone want to deliberate one way or another on his behalf?”

No one seemed particularly interested in representing him at first, but then Nora cleared her throat. “Skreethi are sentient; this conclave recognizes their intelligence and will accordingly mark all their territories in Turri as inviolate. Any attempts to harm or remove them will be treated the same as doing so to a demon, human, or hybrid, and the accord we’re signing off on needs to stipulate that we anticipate identifying additional species in the Wastelands with similar intelligence needing similar protections.”

Aziza, who had been poorly pretending that she wasn’t listening in, nodded slowly. “I can name quite a few myself, at that.” Then she turned back to mending the large crack in Galenus’s core.

Without meaning to, I disrupted the entire discussion. Tears started flowing from my eyes, and there was no way I could stop them.

“Sand…” I tried at first. “I got some… in… my eyes...”

Then I started to bawl openly, rendering my attempt at lying utterly futile.

“This is all I think I ever wanted to experience, and now that I have—I’m sorry—it’s not a bad thing—I’m just so overwhelmed by the idea of finally seeing everyone on the same page—”

And then that fucker had to ruin it all.

Several electric-blue beams shot from Paradise, all converging on the tiny speck that was Sparkles. She dodged several of them, but they were like tiny laser pulses being scattershot across the horizon. I saw her flinch visibly as one seemed to hit her head-on. She fell what appeared to be several hundred feet, but course-corrected, swooping and dipping as she broke off from her attacks.

The three demon lords started talking in hisses and snarls.

Nora’s face, however, brightened considerably as she watched them, and she started sprinting inland from the beach a bit, her staff glowing.

I took a moment to wipe away my snot and tears before chasing after her. “What’s happening?”

“We’re gonna get her attention! Prepare for an emergency landing!”

“What?!”

Olethros pointed to the ship’s wreckage, and his siblings each translocated half of it to either side of the beach. Nora, meanwhile, had started drawing familiar runes in the sand with the butt of her staff. With an imperious tap, they swiftly started forming concentric magic circles that glowed a bright blue. He waved both hands, skimming off the top layer of ocean water to form a giant dark cloud at high altitude, blocking out the sun’s overhead rays and ensuring the beach glowed with runic energy.

Then the two still-wet piles of wreckage spontaneously burst into crimson fire at the other demon lords’ commands, and I finally put it together.

“It looks just like where we met Sparkles!” I gasped. “The two giant burning fires and the giant magic circle in between!”

Nora laughed manically. “And who else but us heroes laid eyes on her prison, huh? This will be sure to get her attention.”

With nervous optimism, we turned our attention skyward. Sparkles was still ducking and weaving, and it was hard to tell where she would end up in any one moment. She eventually made it to where the pulses of energy fell and fizzled into the water, where they dissipated after a rather desperate yet explosive flash. Once Sparkles realized she had escaped their blast range, she headed straight towards us with a desperate sonic scream.

It was at that point that we all scattered from the beach’s edge, unsure of how controlled her descent was. Even with the demons trying to dampen her fall with shadowy nets, her impact had to have been painful. She plowed into the sands headfirst as her colossal, half-collapsed left wing fluttered uselessly. After skidding to a stop, she glowed brightly for a moment.

Nora and I nodded to each other and ran forward.

One of Sparkle’s eyes opened. It searched aimlessly for a moment, then settled on me. With a shudder, her entire body pulsed once more. As her iridescence slowly faded, she shrank into the form of an old woman at the center of the gigantic crater she had left.

“Sparkles!” I cried.

She turned her head, never lifting it. “Looks like I’m going to need your help to kill that three-faced bastard after all, kid. But let me take a rest first, will you?”

Then she sighed, closed her eyes, and lost consciousness.
