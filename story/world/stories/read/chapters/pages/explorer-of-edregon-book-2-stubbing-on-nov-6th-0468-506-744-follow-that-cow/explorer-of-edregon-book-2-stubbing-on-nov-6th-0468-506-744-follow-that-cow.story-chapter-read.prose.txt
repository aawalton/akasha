By the time Vin and Alka explored the dungeon enough to satisfy Vin’s class, the sun was already starting to go down. Vin was in the middle of debating if they should risk continuing on versus running through the Underside to spend the night within the safety of Terra’s walls, when they received an unexpected surprise. The two of them were standing next to the stone frame leading into the Underside when something stepped through, startling the two of them and causing them to leap back as they prepared for a fight. Whatever had just entered the fragment from the underside was big, powerfully built, and cast a shadow over them. It took Vin a moment to even realize what he was looking at, and he blinked as his mouth fell open.

“Drintus?! Is that you?”

“Vin the human!” Drintus laughed. The absolute mountain of a minotaur grinned, hot air shooting out of his bull-like nostrils as he rolled his knuckles across one of the many dents in his chest plate. “It has been too long! I am pleased to see that you are still fighting to this day!”

“Someone you know, Vin?” Alka asked, not having lowered her sword that was aimed at Drintus’ muscular back.

“Yes, he’s a friend!” Vin said hurriedly, motioning for her to put her sword down. “Alka, this is the minotaur we told you about. The one who helped us snatch your body from the librarian ages back. Drintus, this is the friend you helped us save.”

“I did not even recognize the golem underneath all that black metal!” Drintus laughed, turning and rapping his knuckles across his chest plate in her direction as well. “It is good to meet you as well, Alka, friend of Vin’s!”

“Likewise,” Alka said, her eyes flaring as she took in the giant war axe strapped to Drintus’ back. “I would love getting the chance to spar with you sometime.”

“The feeling is mutual!” Drintus grinned. “Tell me, friends, what brings you to this fragment? The town of Diveral is one of the alliances my own town of Harmstead has worked hard to establish.”

“Currently, we’re exploring,” Vin admitted, realizing they may have just gotten a lucky break. “Drintus, is your fragment adjacent to this one? We’d love to follow you back to your town if you wouldn’t mind.”

“I do not mind,” Drintus said. “However, I have business I must attend to here first. If you are willing to wait until evening, I will lead you back to my people.”

“I have a feeling he’s more than willing to wait,” Alka snorted. “He’s got plenty of magic he’s working on.”

“That is right, I forgot you were a mage as well as a warrior!” Drintus said, still grinning down at him. The fact that Drintus stood at nearly eight feet tall and absolutely towered over them was something Vin had completely forgotten about. Even for a minotaur, the man was a giant. “Then that works out great! I will return in a few hours, you should wait for me in the Tunnels where it is safe.”

“Tunnels? Oh, we call it the Underside,” Vin said. “Out of curiosity, what are you doing in town?”

“We have a fairly basic alliance with Diveral. My people help with some of the heavy lifting, and in exchange they help us with our farming. It is a good, simple system! I am here to retrieve more seeds and check in on my people.”

“In that case, don’t let us keep you,” Vin decided, hooking a thumb toward the stone frame leading into the Underside. “We’ll be in your Tunnels, so just come grab us when you’re ready.”

“I shall!” With that, Drintus turned and jogged off through the dungeon, retrieving his war axe from his back to cleave through a poor monster that tried jumping at him.

“Man, I missed out on meeting some fun people while I was sort of dead again,” Alka grumbled. Following him through the stone frame, she kept her head turned around one hundred and eighty degrees while they left the dungeon behind, as if trying to keep the warrior in sight. “He seems like a riot.”

“Drintus is definitely a character,” Vin nodded, recalling how the minotaur hadn’t even hesitated when they’d asked him to help them save their friend’s life. Despite having known them for all of a few minutes, he’d gone to war against the librarian and her golem army, even leaping off the floating island with them and attempting to cushion their deadly fall with his own body. He was basically the personification of ‘ride or die’, and Vin was happy they’d gotten to see him again. “There’s a chance his fragment isn’t one of the last two we need to explore to complete the third ring, but I’m willing to make a small detour just to visit the land of the minotaurs. I assume you don’t have any objections?”

“Are you kidding? If the rest of them are all like that guy, I think I may have just found my new vacation spot,” Alka snorted.

Once they’d finished talking, Alka left him to his spell work, heading back into the forest dungeon to keep killing the monsters held within. While she did that, Vin slipped into Introspection, thankfully having gotten used to spotting Ovord in the dark void by now. It helped that the man was actually meditating himself this time around rather than standing right in front of him. He was clearly working through whatever he’d seen with Beyond the Veil while looking at Alka’s soul, so Vin remained quiet as he got to work.

As excited as he was for the ability to cause his body to burst into flames, he decided putting some more time into figuring out the tier-3 spell he was working on for the potential trip to Lumel’s fragment was more important. He managed to make a good chunk of progress, but by the time he felt a sharp sensation of pain, he still had a long way to go.

Coming out of Introspection, Vin spotted Alka beside him, actively pinching his arm while she shook him. A few feet behind her was Drintus, giving him a curious look.

“This a good stopping point? You’re not going to blow up, are you?”

“I’m tempted to do it just for the bit, seeing as my divine boon will refresh soon enough, but I wouldn’t want to hurt Drintus,” Vin admitted. Getting to his feet, he smiled at their new companion who was now carrying a large sack over one shoulder. “Everything good?”

“Everything is great! My people are happy, and the people of Diveral are very happy! I heard word of the feat that friend Alka here accomplished yesterday. Most impressive!”

“Eh, someone had to do it,” Alka shrugged. “It was not a big deal.”

“I have faced off against the mind spiders myself, it is a big deal indeed,” Drintus argued. “Even with Total Resistance, fighting through their attacks is painful and hard. They are right to celebrate you!”

“Drintus, I feel like I should ask. Are all of your people like you, or are you a special case?” Vin asked. He’d tried to come up with a better way to phrase the question, as he wouldn’t have liked having someone ask him that. But Drintus was a very straightforward person, and Vin figured he’d rather he not beat around the bush.

“I am a member of the honorable Vanguard!” Drintus declared, rapping his knuckles across his chest plate again as he laughed. “We are an elite few who are given the deadliest of missions for our people. Before Edregon, that was mainly launching solo assaults against invading forces, or taking on deadly monsters all on our own. Now, I venture into different fragments and do whatever needs to be done!”

“So you’re like some sort of special forces unit,” Vin said, definitely seeing how that worked. He knew he’d be terrified if he saw the eight-foot-tall wall of muscle roaring and charging him down with that war axe held over his head. “Are there a lot of you?”

“There were a few dozen once. But most did not make the journey over to Edregon,” Drintus admitted, a sad smile slipping over his snout. “As we are given distant, dangerous missions, it is not often we are home in Harmstead. With the increase in monster attacks, I was one of only three currently recuperating within Harmstead’s walls when we were taken. One of them, the oldest and deadliest of Vanguard members, did not make the journey despite having been sitting right next to me. And the other died only two weeks later while exploring a new fragment. I am all that is left of the once mighty Vanguard.”

“That tracks with what we’ve discovered so far,” Vin said, following the minotaur as they walked through the massive cavern that was the Underside. “They must have been in at least their third prestige. It seems anyone with a level of 60 or higher wasn’t able to be reset when the Gods brought the fragments over, so they were left behind. Though some people who had cracked into their fourth prestige and passed level 80 were given the option of coming along for the ride, so long as they didn’t mind being locked in a divine sanctum for the rest of their lives.”

“That would make sense,” Drintus said, rubbing his jaw as he thought. “He was not the only one who did not make the journey who should have.”

“Hey, Vin,” Alka said, giving him a pointed look as they approached the corridor that would lead to the next portion of the Underside. “I know you’ve had magic on the brain, but I think you might be forgetting something.”

“Huh?” Vin asked, blinking as he followed Alka’s gaze and realized he was an idiot. “Gah! I completely did! I was so surprised to see Drintus again I didn’t think about the Underside at all.” Vin stopped the minotaur just shy of the corridor, shaking his head when Drintus gave him a confused look. “Sorry, Drintus, we can’t follow you. We haven’t been to your fragment up on Edregon proper, so we can’t access the parallel portion of the Underside. If we had Lumel with us it would be different, but we don’t, so… maybe we can head back up and just take the long way? Though I know it’s already late.”

“Do not fret, friends, I did not forget how the Tunnels worked,” Drintus laughed. “Follow me and I shall take care of it.”

Vin shot Alka a look, but she merely shrugged, doing just that and following after the minotaur. As they approached the connecting corridor, Vin prepared himself to walk head-first into an invisible wall that he knew would stop them. Yet right before that could happen, Drintus raised a powerful fist over his head, his voice booming out across the Underside.

“Into the Breach!” Drintus roared, a veritable wave of energy radiating off him that swept through Vin’s entire body. It was similar to the Second Wind ability he recalled the minotaur using on them during their battle in the floating library, but far more powerful. Rather than feeling like he’d just gotten a full night’s rest and was refreshed and rejuvenated, Vin suddenly felt like he was sprinting downhill, following Drintus as the minotaur led them into the most important battle of their lives. Before he even knew what was happening he was screaming as he chased after the minotaur, only coming to a stop when he ran straight into Drintus’ back what felt like moments later.

“What in the…” Vin blinked. He was startled to find he’d run not only through the corridor connecting the two parts of the Underside, but through the entire empty cavern as well. The stone frame leading up into what was presumably Drintus’ fragment was standing before them, and Vin turned to spot Alka looking at her own hands with glowing eyes wider than ever before.

“I felt something,” she whispered, her fingers shaking slightly as she curled them into fists. “I haven’t felt anything since the last time Vin let me take over his body when I was a ghost!”

“And we somehow ran straight through the dimensional barrier,” Vin said, glancing over his shoulder and still trying to figure out where the last few minutes had gone. “What was that?”

“My level 50 Capstone!” Drintus laughed, laying large hands on each of their shoulders. “I am honored that you both chose to follow my charge. Into the Breach is a powerful ability, turning all who charge under its influence into an almost unstoppable force capable of running through just about anything.”

“Drintus… exactly what level are you?” Vin asked, staring up at the minotaur in awe. He’d been a powerful warrior all those months ago when he fought beside them in the library, and Vin had a feeling the man hadn’t simply been sitting idly all this time since then.

“I am level 64!” Drintus said, leaning back and laughing at the shocked look on Vin’s face. “I believe I am the strongest of my people, as is only expected for a member of the Vanguard! Breaking through to level 60 was difficult, but I shattered that barrier just as I do any other! Nothing can stop the Vanguard!”

“Is there any chance I can convince you to pay our fragment a visit and instruct some students of mine?” Alka asked. “I would very much like to see you fight them.”

“Perhaps. That does sound fun, but I am quite busy,” Drintus said, turning and heading into the corridor leading to his people’s dungeon. “Come, follow me. Allow me to introduce you to Harmstead!”
