A loud bang shook the gambling den.

The wall beside the table burst outward as a man was thrown straight through it. Wood splintered and shattered along the ground. The wall cracked outward as dust and rubble spilled down in pieces. Moonlight spilled in as the man’s body tumbled across the sidewalk, rolling until he hit a street-lamp.

For a moment, the night outside went still from the chaos.

Bystanders walking along the sidewalks stopped where they stood. Some turned with food and drinks in hand. Others froze mid-conversation, eyes widening at the sight of a man lying unconscious, sprawled across the ground.

Several people inside the gambling den rushed toward the new hole in the wall, peering out into the street.

Inside, the table had been shoved aside. Cards and chips lay scattered across the floor. The dealer stood there, frozen, one hand holding a deck. The inspector in the vest, who had been called, had stopped several steps away. The rest of the people at the table simply stood, staring down at the little girl.

And in the middle of everything—stood Veronica.

Her arm was raised, trembling slightly from the spell she had just unleashed. A faint glow lingered around her palm, beneath the black glove she wore.

Her face was pale, fixated on the man who lay groaning on the ground. Her eyes never left his sight. Despite her condition—she wanted to use another spell, to really teach that man a lesson.

Victor staggered slightly behind her, one hand pressed against his bleeding side. “Vee…”

Veronica jolted, looking back at Victor, kneeling and trying to examine his wound.

The people all around them stared at her. Some recognized her—of course they did—she was a regular who always appeared in a dark cloak, always sticking near Victor. Some assumed she was his daughter. Others, however, knew the truth.

“Hey… isn’t this pretty bad? Using magic like that?”

“What happened? Why’d that man go flying through the wall?”

“That girl’s going to be in trouble with the guards. Why is she even here?”

“Hey—do you not see her glove, man? Look at it.”

People began looking at her more closely from where they were. Her hood had fallen backward, and they could see the small, pointed ears poking through black hair. More importantly, however, was the insignia she was wearing on her glove.

It was the king’s insignia. A person of high prestige, recognized by the King himself; someone who carried Vitian’s protection.

“…why is someone like her here?”

“What’s going to happen now?”

Several people began to edge away, not wanting to get involved. The inspector’s eyes narrowed from the side, recognizing the insignia.

It didn’t take long for the guards to appear. Half a dozen arrived, half wearing pristine barseratum armor, and the other half mages carrying orichalcum staves.

They began assessing the situation, confirming the details of what had happened. Blooming mages and holy priests were called over to examine Victor, who had just been stabbed, and to collect the man lying unconscious in the street.

“Veronica, you need to come with me,” said one of the guards.

He was a tall man in his mid-thirties. A knight, and the head captain of the guards in Annesheim. A veteran eighth-tier aura user.

Thirty minutes later, Veronica had been escorted back to her dorm room in Valeborn Academy. A little while after that, her assistant came by.

“Veronica, you’ve caused a lot of trouble today,” she said.

Viya was several years older than Veronica. She was in her late teens, but not yet a full adult. However, she had been entrusted by the kingdom to act as Veronica’s arms and legs. Likewise, she held full responsibility for disciplining her and caring for her well-being.

She had long, sleek black hair and wore a pair of glasses. She always carried herself upright. Although she wasn’t a noble, she was assiduous and demonstrated exceeding diligence in her duties.

“…Is Victor okay?” Veronica asked.

She was sitting on the side of her bed, looking down at the ground, refusing to meet Viya’s gaze.

“That man at the gambling den?” Viya repeated. “He’s fine. A wound like that, blooming mages don’t even need to bat an eye.”

Veronica stayed silent.

“I hope you understand the lesson here,” Viya continued. “Because you snuck out, you got an innocent man hurt. If you weren’t there, none of this would have happened, and this friend of yours wouldn’t have a stab wound in his stomach.”

Veronica clenched her fingers.

“You’re going with me to apologize to the headmaster. Tomorrow, we’ll also have to apologize to the king for your behavior.”

Again, Veronica stayed silent. Her mind whirred through the situation again.

Could she have done anything differently? Was it wrong to call out the man for cheating? But Victor didn’t care if he won or lost…

“Veronica.”

But he certainly wouldn’t be happy if he lost vix to a cheater, right? And using magic without permission in the city was a crime…

“Veronica.”

Especially in a gambling den. Even though she had used it, it was only because he had done it first. Why should she apologize? She didn’t do anything wro—

“Veronica!”

Veronica jolted. She blinked a few times, looking around. She was in a clearing, with trees all around her. People were walking about, carrying crates, documents, and various other items and equipment.

“Hey. You okay? You were spacing out. I called out to you like four times.”

She turned, spotting Parek.

“Ah… yes. Sorry. I was just… lost in thought for a moment.”

The corner of Parek’s lips stiffened. “It’s alright. After… what happened, I can’t blame you.”

The two of them were at an outpost outside the forest. There were several hundred refugees after the latest demon attack. Many were injured and currently being treated by healers, while others helped with menial tasks around the outpost.

“For the time being, everyone here will be safe since we’ve got a shield generator running. After several hours, everyone will begin moving and heading to Fort Mannow.”

“I see,” Veronica replied. “What about the portals? Have we found any of those yet?”

Parek shook his head. “No. We’re searching all over Vitian for signs of where the demons are coming from, but so far, there’s been no luck.” He crossed his arms, frowning with annoyance. “It’s obvious that something is causing this. Even though we can’t find out where they’re coming from, they’re arriving in organized packs.”

“If we can find whoever is leading them, I’ll make sure to completely annihilate them,” Veronica said, brows lowered. “Evil, rotten bastards. I didn’t spend my entire life studying, trying to survive, just to fight a war against some stupid demons trying to invade us.”

Parek sighed, shaking his head. From the corner of his eye, he spotted a woman approaching them.

“Ah, Tana. How are the villagers doing?” he asked.

Tana came up to them, acknowledging them with a nod. She wore a red and white robe with the insignia of a leaf on her right shoulder. “Most of them are doing fine and should recover. There are some with… more permanent injuries. We just don’t have the resources to fully heal them while also fighting demons.”

Parek’s gaze lowered. “How many?”

“Fourteen with injuries that may never heal properly,” Tana answered. “Another twenty two who will need constant care for the next few weeks. The rest should survive if nothing else happens.”

Veronica’s fingers tightened around her staff.

Nothing else. As if the world had ever allowed them that luxury.

“Make sure the worst cases are marked for later transport,” Parek said. “Fort Mannow has more supplies than this outpost. Once we get them there, we can have the stronger blooming mages take another look.”

Tana nodded. “I already have their names written down.”

Veronica remained quiet for a few moments, watching the healers move between the wounded. Some refugees sat in silence with bloodied bandages wrapped around their arms or heads. Others clung to their families, their eyes hollow and unfocused. A few children huddled near the supply crates, too exhausted to cry.

Then her gaze shifted. “What about the girl?” Veronica asked. “The one I brought back.”

Tana paused.

Parek looked over at her as well.

“She isn’t injured,” Tana said carefully. “At least, not physically. A few scrapes and bruises, but nothing serious.”

Veronica waited. There was something more to it.

Tana glanced toward the far side of the outpost. “But… she refuses to talk. She hasn’t said a word since we brought her here. She won’t eat either. One of the apprentices tried to give her water, but she barely reacted.”

Parek exhaled slowly through his nose.

Veronica’s eyes lowered.

Of course she wouldn’t talk. What was there to say?

“I want to see her,” Veronica said.

Tana hesitated. “Veronica…”

“I just want to see her.”

For a moment, neither Tana nor Parek answered.

Then Parek gave Tana a small nod.

Tana sighed softly. “Alright. Follow me.”

They moved through the outpost, passing rows of injured villagers lying on blankets or makeshift cots. The shield generator hummed in the distance, its faint blue dome stretching over the camp like a thick pane of glass. Beyond it, the forest stood dark and unmoving, as if the demons were only waiting for the right moment to return.

Tana led her past the healers, past the wagons, and toward the quieter edge of the outpost.

There, beneath a lone tree, the girl sat on the ground.

Her knees were pulled tightly against her chest, arms wrapped around them. Her face was hidden behind tangled hair and dirt-streaked sleeves.

Tana stopped several steps away. “She’s been like this for a while.”

Veronica stared at her. The image from earlier returned before she could stop it. The torn blue cloth. The blood on the demon’s claws. The name whispered through tears.

Milo.

Veronica’s grip tightened around her staff until the wood creaked faintly beneath her fingers.

Tana lowered her voice. “Do you want me to stay?”

Veronica shook her head. “No,” she said softly. “I’ll talk to her.”

“…Alright. I’ll head back first, then,” she replied, before turning and walking away.

Veronica stepped forward, slowly approaching the tree.

The girl didn’t look up.

For a moment, Veronica simply stood there, staff held loosely in one hand. She had faced demon warlords and archfiends, monsters large enough to crush entire towns, and battlefield decisions that would have broken most people.

And yet… this was just as hard.

She lowered herself beside the girl, sitting on the ground a short distance away. Close enough to speak, but not so close that the girl would feel trapped.

“Are you okay?” Veronica asked softly.

The girl didn’t answer.

Veronica waited. Waited for however long it would take.

She kept both hands around her staff, eyes lowered toward the dirt beneath them. Around the outpost, people moved, cried, whispered, and worked. Healers called for clean water. Refugees spoke in hushed voices. Somewhere nearby, a child coughed violently. Many had suffered through excessive smoke inhalation.

For a while, they sat there in silence.

Then the girl spoke.

“…Why couldn’t you save Milo?”

Veronica’s chest tightened.

The question came so quietly that, for a moment, she almost wished she hadn’t heard it. But she had.

The girl’s fingers clutched the fabric around her knees. “You people can fly. You can make lights and fire and… and do amazing things.” Her voice trembled. “So why did Milo die?”

Veronica opened her mouth.

But no answer came.

“Why couldn’t you save him?” the girl asked again. Her voice broke at the end. She shoved her face deeper into her folded arms, shoulders shaking as she began to cry.

Veronica stared at her.

There were plenty of explanations. She had been too far. Parek hadn’t seen the demon in time. The battlefield had been chaos. The demons had attacked from too many directions, and even a Tier-10 mage couldn’t stand everywhere at once.

But none of that mattered.

Not to the girl.

Not to Milo.

Veronica lowered her gaze. “I’m sorry.”

The girl’s sobs grew sharper. “It should have been me.”

Veronica froze, eyes widening.

The girl’s voice came muffled from behind her arms. “I should have died with Milo. He told me to run, and I ran. I left him there. I should have stayed. I should have died too.”

“Don’t say something like that,” Veronica said, her voice tightening despite herself. “Milo wouldn’t want you to—”

“Don’t talk about him like you know him!” the girl shouted.

Veronica went silent.

The girl lifted her head just enough for Veronica to see her tear-streaked face. Her eyes were red and swollen, filled with a grief too heavy for someone so young.

“You don’t know him,” the girl cried. “You don’t know anything about him.”

Veronica’s fingers tightened around her staff.

The girl’s voice dropped, but the hurt in it didn’t fade. “Go away. I don’t like you.”

The words struck harder than they should have.

Veronica stayed there for a moment, then slowly nodded. “Alright,” she said quietly. “I won’t bother you again.”

She stood, brushing the dirt from her robe. The girl turned her face back into her arms, refusing to look at her.

Veronica took a few steps away, then stopped. “If you’re hungry,” she said, without fully turning around, “ask anyone in white robes. They’ll get you something.”

The girl didn’t respond.

Veronica waited another second, then walked away.

She didn’t know how to comfort someone.

She never had.
