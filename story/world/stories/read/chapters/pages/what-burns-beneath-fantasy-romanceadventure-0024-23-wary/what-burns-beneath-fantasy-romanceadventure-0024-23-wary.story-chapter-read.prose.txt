There’s something here.

Here. In a town. In a tavern.

Just a few days ago, I would have laughed away the possibility. But after the past few nights…

I trembled, cringing at every ominous shadow in the dim hallway. There’s something in the inn. Maybe. Or maybe not. But… maybe. My eyes darted to the doors stretching out on either side. Each bore a small patch of brightly colored paint. My own bedroom was smeared sky-blue, the same shade as my key, so logically…

I didn’t know which room Teela had been given, but I found the red door quickly enough. I took a deep breath and knocked. Muffled sounds echoed from inside the room, but there was no answer. I tried again.

“Go away!”

I could practically envision the scowl he was doubtlessly aiming at the door. “Renner!” I hissed. “It’s Brin!”

There was a short pause. Then, “Go away.”

“No!” I knocked again. “I need to talk to you! It’s important!”

There were several long moments of silence before the door swung inwards. Renner stood before me, wearing only his dark trousers and, oddly enough, his brown leather gloves. He braced his hands against both sides of the doorframe, glowering at me.

Heat flooded my skin as, before I quite realized what was happening, my gaze slid downwards. Taking everything in with a flush. Arms and shoulders sculpted by use. A broad chest taut with lean muscle, long-faded scars, and downy chestnut hair. And, lower, defined hips narrowing beneath- I jerked my gaze back up, cheeks flaming.

Behind him, sitting on a very mussed bedspread, was the dark-haired barmaid. Her frock had been pulled down enough to reveal bare, rounded shoulders. Her cheeks were flushed and rosy.

“What?”

“Oh, salt, I’m… I didn’t…” I tore my eyes from the giggling young woman and met Renner’s glare. “Look, can we talk?”

His expression implied that I’d grown a second head. And that he was considering tearing it off. “Ladyship… no.”

“It’s important!” I hissed, casting a furtive glance towards the woman. “There’s something… I don’t know, I think there might be something downstairs! Something bad!”

He curled his lip but leaned out a bit, looking down the hallway. Considering my words.

I worried at my nightgown sleeves, studiously keeping my gaze on his face. Don’t look down. Don’t gawk. I… I’ve seen men like this before. Not often, but… and this is Renner! He’s horrible. It’s nothing to be so-

“Does your friend want to join us?” The barmaid tittered, waving at me. One hand reached out, grasping an unopened wine bottle on the nightstand. It stood beside a mug brimming with fragrant ciderwine. “I don’t mind. She’s cute!”

Renner made a choking sound as I retreated into the hallway. “Oh, salt, n-no. No, thank you. Just need to, um, borrow your… my… Renner.” Heat bolted up my throat again. “I mean, not my…” I swung my gaze to the irate, half-naked man looming before me. “I think there’s something… um…” I bit my lip, trying to think of how to convey my thoughts without alarming the young woman. “L-like from the fog. Here.”

“Salt and ash, hold on…” he growled, then spun on one heel and strode across the bedroom. I glanced away, face burning, as he cradled the woman’s chin and stooped to murmur something in her ear. She giggled, flopping back down onto the bed. A moment later he rejoined me with his tunic, boots, and belt re-secured.

As soon as the door was shut I burst out, “Down in the common room! You remember the stablemaster from earlier?”

He glowered at me.

“Right, of course you… come look at him! There’s something wrong.”

I whirled away, snatching one gloved hand and tugging him towards the stairs. He grumbled as we went, but allowed me to pull him along. When we reached the bottom landing I pointed towards the fireplace, where the unsavory older man was still hunched over his drink.

“There,” I hissed, “Do you-”

“Salt, can you be any more obvious?” he muttered back, and it was my turn to be tugged forwards.

I forced my gaze ahead, away from the distressing figure, as he led me to a small table set against the far wall. Renner very deliberately pulled a chair out and gestured for me to sit. I did so, facing almost away from the stablemaster. Renner took the seat opposite me, which faced the man in question almost directly.

Copper eyes held mine. He still looked unhappy, but there was something else in his demeanor, too. Wariness. Readiness.

“Okay,” he breathed, leaning forwards. “We’re here. What’s got you all worked up?”

I swallowed, fighting the urge to turn around. He’s being smart. If there is something here, pointing and staring will just alert it. “His shadow,” I whispered, pulse thrumming. “Look at his shadow.”

Renner’s gaze flicked away. He frowned a bit, watching intently.

This close, and with nothing to do but wait, I found myself studying the features of his face. Deep-set eyes flickering warmly in the firelight. A furrow between drawn, dark brows. Rough stubble along a strong jawline, smooth lips twisted downwards. We must look very intimate, sitting like this. Another flush of heat chased the thought.

He looked at me. One eyebrow rose and he gave a low chuckle. “Close enough for you, ladyship?” His eyes darted back towards the hunched figure. “His shadow looks like a shadow.”

“Watch,” I pleaded. What if I was wrong? What if it’s not something here, it’s something wrong with me? I wrung my hands together as Renner turned his attention behind me once more.

He followed me. I told him there might be a monster down here and he followed me. Most people wouldn’t march down the stairs to investigate if you told them there was something dangerous lurking around.

Who is he?

“I don’t see-”

I whipped my head around, staring at the solitary man. Sure enough, his shadow looked entirely normal. I turned back quickly, my heart pounding. “It was wrong,” I hissed. “Just a few minutes ago. He seemed upset, so I went over to talk to him and it… it didn’t move like he did.” My voice took on a low, somewhat frantic pitch. “It happened last night, too! With Agnes.”

Renner’s frown deepened. “What, at the other inn?”

“Yes! She came to my room and…” I chewed my lower lip, hesitating, then forged ahead. “She wanted to… she thought that… she showed me the coins you gave her,” he grimaced and I continued quickly, because that was entirely not the point right now, “And asked if I needed help. But then her shadow…” I buried my face in my hands, aware of how mad I probably sounded. “It was like his,” I finished with a moan. “It was moving by itself.”

He was silent.

“I thought I was just tired. Imagining things, you know? But then it happened again. Stars. Maybe I’m just finally losing my… ugh.” I groaned, fingers dragging through my hair.

“You folks alright?” Master Lewen’s voice, warm but tinged with worry, made me jump. The stout innkeeper had approached us quietly and was looking at me. “Need anything, Miss?”

“We’re fine,” I managed, smiling weakly. “Thank you.”

He gave a little nod and drifted away, casting a concerned glance over one shoulder.

My chest swelled with a heavy sigh as I turned back to my companion. “Look, I’m sorry…” my voice faltered.

His expression had flattened into something dark. He was glaring behind me- towards the stablemaster, presumably. “Something wrong?” The tone matched the look; rough, bordering on hostile.

I turned.

The stablemaster’s weathered face was bone-white. He was staring at the two of us.

He almost looks frightened. Just like before.

“No,” the older man ground out, voice strained, and stood. He was shaking. “No. I thought…” he coughed, bumping against the table. His mug tipped with a clatter, dark liquid pooling across the wood. He barely seemed to notice and instead turned away, visibly trembling, and fled out the front door.

“Gil? Hey, Gil!” the innkeeper called, running towards the door. “Gil! You alright?!”

Chilly night air gusted in from the darkness. The few other patrons had gone quiet, watching uncertainly.

Lewen turned towards us, looking thoroughly baffled. “I miss something, folks?”

I spread my hands helplessly. “I… I don’t know. He seemed upset about something, but I’m not sure…”

“Seemed even more upset when he saw me,” Renner muttered under his breath, drumming his fingers rapidly against the tabletop. His eyes were fixed on the door.

Master Lewen grabbed a rag and moved for the soiled table, shaking his head sadly. “Probably drank before he came in. He’s had a hard run of it.” After a moment he straightened and added kindly, “Don’t take it personally, folks. Gil’s always been… He’s just… He can be a bit off, sometimes.”

“It’s fine,” I offered, for lack of anything better to say.

Master Lewen continued to clean as the other patrons resumed their quiet chatter.

I leaned in towards my companion. “Something’s wrong.”

He frowned, cutting his eyes back towards me. He was quiet for a long moment. Then, darkly, “Yeah.”

I bit down on my lower lip, fighting back a swell of relief- and fear. He believes me. He thinks I’m right. But… salt, what’s going on, and what do we do about it? It’s not as though we can just leave. It’s well past sunset.

Renner’s voice broke into my musings. “Lewen. You have any rooms with more than one bed?”

The hefty innkeeper straightened. “Of course, sir. The beds are a bit smaller- rooms for families, you understand.”

My companion grunted, still frowning at the door. “Right. The lady will pay for one.”

“I will?”

Master Lewen walked behind the bar, opening a wooden cupboard. He fished out a small iron key and approached us. “Is this about Gil? I’m sorry if he made you uncomfortable, Miss.”

Renner didn’t give me a chance to answer. “Our other friend; the one with the big hat. What room did you give her?”

“Yellow, sir. But I’m sure she-”

“Thanks,” he stood, sounding entirely insincere, and frowned down at me. “Come on.”

I rose, turning to the innkeeper. “Thank you,” I said, gently elbowing Renner in the side. “Is it all right if I pay you in the morning?”

He nodded, handing me the key. It bore a small white patch of paint. “Of course, Miss. I’m sorry again. Please, enjoy your night.”

I thanked him once more and we headed up the stairs.

Renner’s lips were pressed into a thin line. “Grab your things and head to that room. Get Teela, too. We’re gonna sleep in shifts and leave as soon as the sun’s up.”

I nodded furtively, swallowing. “O-okay. You’re… you’re coming, too?” I peered up through my lashes, feeling suddenly shy. It wasn’t that I was uncomfortable sharing a bedroom with a man. Royce and I had dozed together beneath witchwood branches and under the summer sun plenty of times. And it wasn’t from the memory of earlier; of bare skin and a warm, muscular body very close to mine.

It was the sudden feeling of wanting him there.

He believed me. He followed me. He listened and he isn’t afraid and… and I think I’ll feel safer if he’s nearby.

We paused at the door to his room. Heat blossomed through my cheeks as I thought of the beautiful young woman waiting inside.

He heaved a forlorn sigh but held my gaze firmly. “Yeah, ladyship. I’m coming, too.”
