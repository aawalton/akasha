The creature that originally came from that large core was no longer anywhere to be seen. Instead, what stood in front of him was something far more grotesque than that already messed-up creature.

Eyes in places where eyes should not be, a shoulder that was kept together by intertwined arms with elbows sticking out everywhere, and hands that peeked out of places you could only have nightmares about.

Ashton found this thing absolutely revolting. Not because it was actually something more disgusting than he could handle, but rather because it reminded him of the Summoner in a few ways. They were both similarly, unimaginably disfigured after all, as if they were cobbled together from the imagination of what numerous people believed to be the 'worst thing to see approach you in the middle of the night'.

But, at the very least, while this creature was just growing stronger and stronger, as long as Ashton managed to kill it, this was the perfect gift to convince the Spawn of Akrazoth to reconsider the current state of their contract.

Ashton rushed forward and stabbed forward the stone spear that he was currently holding. The ice edge, while brittle, was sharp enough to cut into that strengthened flesh at least once or twice before having to be reformed. Luckily, water was much more malleable than stone, so it was fairly easy.

Plus, attacking with mostly the ice edge did protect the stone base, so Ashton had to have the gnome repair it far less often.

While he was jumping around, leaping between those stretched-out limbs that were trying to swing at him while a dozen mouths cried out from all over its body, Ashton also continued to burn its flesh into charcoal.

While it wasn't technically effective at slowing it down the way it slowed the other sentries, the heat still weakened the flesh, making it easier to cut through, and when going beyond that, the carbonized, black flesh was still blocking some of its movement.

Either that, or while it was moving, the blackened meat would crack off and fall to the ground in a way that it didn't seem like it or even the ground could re-absorb, at least not at the same immediate speed as the unburnt flesh.

Like this, though it was taking a lot longer than expected, Ashton was whittling down on its body. He was removing muscle, fat, bones; everything that there was to destroy.

There were parts here and there within its body that had more concentrated mana. These were the weakpoints—those small cores—that were on the bodies of the sentries that the creature absorbed.

Whenever Ashton destroyed one of them, it seemed like parts of its flesh was beginning to tense up, unable to move the same way as before. The sentries the core belonged to were clearly still connected to it. And so, Ashton should be able to essentially reverse all this easily enough as long as he steadily kept at it.

One, then two, then three; the individual weakpoints shattered essentially the moment Ashton came close to them. While they were now hidden within the monster's body, they were far more brittle than before, and even then Ashton had been able to break these in one hit, so this was all very welcome.

Before long, the thing that stood in front of Ashton, almost unable to control its own form anymore, just stared at him. Its screams were slowly turning into cries of pain. Ashton didn't know if this creature was truly able to feel that pain, or if it was simply trying to evoke some sort of empathy from Ashton as some last-ditch effort to survive.

"Sorry... if you have a soul in that mess you call a body, I hope it can peacefully pass on," Ashton struck the large monster one more time, and watched as it fell.

Ashton focused on the message. Intermittently, whenever he broke one of those weakpoint cores, he would get a message popping up, telling him that he killed a sentry, but this thing just on its own was already massively powerful already.

Luckily, Ashton was able to break down its 'connection' well enough.

For the entirety of the fight, it was clear that this thing wasn't acting fully on its own accord. Ashton was pretty sure this entire 'growth' area was one massive hive-mind working together. Most of the time, a hive-mind like this that wasn't directly physically connected communicated through mana.

With every attack, Ashton was working on slowly interrupting that connection more and more. He didn't exactly know how to do this before now, but he took some hints from how he was interrupting the connection of a constellation to this world. While the principles were deeply different, there was still some application from one to the other.

So, with the connection interrupted, the artificial living being in front of Ashton 'died', though it was never really alive in the first place. Likely, it was just impossible for its body to reconnect back to the rest of the growth, and would only end up being absorbed back into this world. However, Ashton was not going to let that happen.

He used the ice-edge of his weapon to cut into the flesh on the ground and used the minuscule gap to burn away the connection, lastly lifting up some stone to at least momentarily create a physical barrier as well.

With this, Ashton had enough time deal with this monster before it could be torn apart by the 'growth' again.

For now, Ashton ignored the fact that the gnome and salamander reached level 10 and were ready to advance, and opened his grimoire. He cut into the back of his arm with the ice-edge, dripping some blood onto one of the pages. The blood disappeared almost immediately, flying away in a thin cloud of dust.

The mark on Ashton's arm started to burn like a hot iron was pressed into it again, as the Spawn of Akrazoth was summoned right into said mark.

The spider-like creature made of two hands climbed out of the mark, and stared at Ashton with the tiny black eyes hidden in its skin.

With a slight grimace, Ashton spoke to it.

"You've been bored, haven't you?" he asked. The monster stared up at him. Its finger-like legs tightened up. It had been.

"Well, there's a reason for that. This contract that we have—I'm unhappy with it. I gave you my eyes and parts of my heart and lung. My finger, the bodies of humans that passed and are now yours to do whatever you want with," Ashton focused his channelling range on the demon. "I'm going to cancel our contract."

The demon flinched. That was clearly the last thing that it expected. While it was taking advantage of the fact that it was given more power in the contract, the only reason why the Spawn of Akrazoth had been able to get its hands on the wonderful parts that it had been was because Ashton supplied it. The others like it, even those more grown than it, had to make do with the corpses of other demons or the rare outsider that fell into hell by accident. This was how this one had been getting by as well, after al. But the parts that this one had received were far greater, unimaginably so. It definitely didn't want to lose out on that opportunity.

But... it wasn't like Ashton could easily cancel the contract anyway. It would require a large sacrifice, something that the Spawn of Akrazoth could not deny under any circumstances. And since he was clearly so unhappy with the form this contract took, it would be hard to come by such materials.

At least, that was what the spawn assumed. But what was laid out in front of it was virtually a feast of parts. Yes, it was injured, and yes it was rather sloppily put together, but as a being that lived for the art of shaping and influencing bodies the way that the spawn did, it was essentially a work of art.

"That's what I'll be offering to cancel the contract," Ashton said, in a clear voice, clearly speaking of the corpse of the 'Child of the Growth'.

The demon knew that it wouldn't be able to deny such an offering. Not under any circumstances would the contract that tied it and Ashton together allow something like this to be turned down.

Did it get too greedy? Was its opportunity to get its hand on all these wonderful things over now?

"Except..." Ashton continued, "if you're willing to redefine our contract. Make it more equal, I will instead give that to you simple as an offering to commemorate that redefinition."

The demon stopped. It seemed relieved in some way, but also annoyed. Demons were prideful, and hated to be played with. Yes, this one was barely even a child, but children could get even more upset when they thought they were wronged.

"Don't worry, I'm not going to be the one in power. It's going to be equal, just like all the other contracts I've formed. You have been the only one that's uneven," Ashton explained. "You will still get the normal offerings that you would otherwise. Some pieces of monster, plenty of my blood, and when we get the chance, I'll let you have your way with the remaining parts of monsters that would otherwise just rot away. You can make requests, and I will do my best to keep you happy, as I do with my other summons, but you will have to make do with whatever I give you, in the end."

The Spawn of Akrazoth just sat there on the back of Ashton's arm. It was contemplating the suggestion. Ashton waited. If this didn't work, he would cancel the contract and try to re-form a new one. It would be some wasted time, and he would have to figure out what else to summon, since forming a contract with the same sort of being you just cancelled one with wasn't possible.

He would rather not, but he would figure something out.

But luckily, this was no issue.

The demon agreed.
