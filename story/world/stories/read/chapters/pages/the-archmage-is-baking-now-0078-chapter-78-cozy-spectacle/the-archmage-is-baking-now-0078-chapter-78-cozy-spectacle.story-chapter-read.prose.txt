Jane stood and offered the king a small curtsy, which felt appropriate.

"Thank you for telling me all of this," she said. "And for the apology. I know that’s not easy."

"Easier than my conversation with your aunt, I promise you." The king smiled, wry and seeming to still carry a bit of regret about provoking the world’s strongest aunt. "Go on, then. Enjoy the rest of your day. You've earned it."

Jane made her way back through the meeting hall and out into the square. The morning's chaos had for the most part cleared, though work was still ongoing to get everything back to its normal, clean state. The cooking stations were being dismantled down to the removal of the tables, and as Jane watched what was left of the morning’s festivities disappeared into alleys and sheds until they’d be needed again..

She looked for Bella but found only the empty space where her station had been. The army griddle was gone, the supply crates were gone, and Bella herself had vanished somewhere into the flow of people heading away from the square. Jane supposed she had gone back to her shop to start preparing for the inevitable rush of new customers. It’s what she’d do, in the same situation. There was something about being alone with food worries that tied them together, and it was nice in a way to know she had that in common with her best friend.

I should find her later. Make sure she's all right.

For now, though, Jane found herself uncommitted. The morning had been intense, and she wasn't quite ready to go back to her bakery and start prepping for the next day’s baking. She needed something else first, and she had a good idea of what would fill the gap in her day.

The walk to the Underbridge Market was one of walk-it-with-your-eyes-closed familiarity. Allen’s workspace was similarly easy to find. She had been there so many times that her feet knew the way without her having to think about it, which let her enjoy every aspect of the walk, sinking into the familiarity of it all with a sort of casual empty-mindedness.

She rounded the last corner expecting to find him alone at his workbench, maybe tinkering with some new project or cleaning up from the showcase preparations. Instead, she found him sitting on a crate with Dave and Brand, the three of them sharing what looked like the remnants of the festival-provided breakfast.

She had introduced Dave and Brand hoping they might become friends, but she hadn't expected it to happen quite this fast. Then again, maybe that was exactly how friendships worked when you were young and had time to spare. It was how it had worked for her, anyway, and she was pleased to find the ease of building relationships was more universal than she had once imagined it would be.

"Jane!" Dave spotted her first and waved her over. "Good timing. We were just talking about you."

"Good things, I hope."

"Mostly."

Brand's expression was better, somehow/ Whatever Dave had shown him in the last few days, it had clearly done some good. He was joking with her, too. It all seemed like good signs.

Allen stood and pulled another crate over for her to sit on, positioning it close to his own. Jane settled onto it gratefully, her legs aching from the morning's work.

"I'm surprised you're not working," she said to Dave. "No runs today?"

"I do, but this one's a bit different." Dave grinned. "Someone needs a ride out of town later. A merchant, heading a few towns worth of travel down the mountain. Brand's going to come along for the trip."

Jane looked at Brand, surprised. "You're leaving? Already?"

"Just for the day. Maybe two." Brand shrugged. "I never get to travel in a normal way. I've never actually seen it from the ground level like that. Every time I've traveled, it's been in a royal carriage with curtains drawn and guards everywhere. This time I’ll be helping with the carriage and unloading goods."

"Won't that be awkward?" Jane asked. "For the merchant, I mean. Having a prince along for the ride?"

"He won't know." Brand's smile turned into something resembling a smirk. "I bought some secondhand clothes this morning. Just regular traveling clothes. As far as the merchant knows, I'll just be Dave's friend who wanted to tag along."

Jane tried to imagine Brand going incognito under the power of a simple outfit change. It wasn’t just clothes that marked him as royalty, after all. There was a lot happening in his bearing and posture that for her were dead giveaways that he was not an average man of average origins. She wondered if the merchant would see the same things.

"Are you sure that will work?"

"It worked well enough yesterday." Dave laughed. "We walked through half the town and nobody recognized him. Turns out people don't expect to see the prince buying meat pies from a street vendor."

That does make sense, I suppose.

Jane thought about the guards she had seen trailing the royal family. They were professionals, trained to protect their charge without being obvious about it. She wondered how they were going to keep up with Dave's fast carriage on a trip to the capital, but decided it wasn't her business. If Brand wanted to spend a day pretending to be normal, she wasn't going to be the one to stop him. She rather hoped nobody did, really.

"Well," she said. "Have fun, then. Try not to get into too much trouble."

"No promises." Brand stood and stretched. "Dave, we should probably go check on the carriage. Grease the axles. That sort of thing. Right?"

Dave nodded and stood as well. "Good idea. It’s always good to do a once-over before a big trip. I want to take a look at it before we go anywhere."

They said their goodbyes and headed off together. Dave was excited to run through the details of carriage maintenance, it seemed, and was already going through all the details he could think of. Jane watched them go, two young men, about the same age, both trying to figure out where they fit in the world. She hoped the friendship would last, though she hardly saw how it could. Brand did have to go back to the capital eventually, after all..

"That was unexpected," Allen said once they were gone.

"Which part?"

"I didn't think Brand would take to Dave so quickly. Or that Dave would invite him along on a job."

"They're good for each other, I think." Jane leaned against Allen's shoulder, letting some of the tension drain out of her. "Brand needs someone who doesn't treat him like royalty all the time. I don’t think Dave even knows how to treat someone that way. I just hope it help brand feel a little less lost."

"Lost?"

"New to everything, and not knowing where they fit in." She tilted her head to look up at him. "Speaking of which, I had a conversation with the king this morning."

Allen's lifted his eyebrows. "One on one?"

"Yes. He pulled me aside after the showcase." Jane sat up straighter and faced him properly. "He apologized for the whole situation with Brand. He said he contacted my aunt about it, and she set him straight."

"Your aunt?" Allen's laughed. “I would have liked to have seen that. She told him off?"

"She threatened to turn him into a toad."

"Of course she did." Allen almost giggled. "I bet it worked, too."

"Apparently it was very effective." Jane reached out and took his hand. "The point is, he's not pushing the whole romance anymore. Whatever plans he might have had about me and Brand, they're done. He said so himself. We don’t have to worry about it."

Allen lifted his free hand and pressed a kiss to her forehead. He was chuckling at her, although she could hardly guess why.

"I was never worried, actually."

"You weren't?"

"Not really." He pulled back enough to meet her eyes. "Imagine you coming to me and saying sorry, Allen, I’m just switching over to this man I just met. It’s just not you. You might stop with a person, but you certainly wouldn’t replace them on the fly.”

Jane snorted.

"Thanks, I guess," she said. "Not least for being so calm about all of this."

“No problem.” Allen leaned back into her on the crate. “Now tell me about your morning already. I missed almost all of the showcase.”

"It was chaotic. Bella won first place, did you hear?"

"I heard the announcement. I’m glad for her.”

“Would you want to win, if you had the choice?” Jane squeezed his hand. “I bet you could, if you went all-out.”

“I might even just with what I’ve thrown together.” Allen said. “But it’s not that important. You are already right here, so my life is already pretty good. Nothing is really going to make it much better.”

She kissed him then, this time getting him on the lips and not pulling away for a good, long time.

"Well, well. Look at you two."

Jane turned to find Emily approaching, a smirk on her face and a bounce in her step.

"Making a spectacle of yourselves in public," Emily continued. "Holding hands where anyone could see. It’s scandalous, really."

"We're just sitting here."

"Sitting very close together. Looking very cozy." Emily’s smirk widened. "Don't mind me. I'll just stand here and observe."

Jane leaned over and kissed Allen on the cheek, making a point of it. "You can’t shame me out of this, Emily. I'll do it as much as I want."

"Good for you. You should.” Emily pulled up a crate and sat down across from the other two. “I was looking for you, Jane. I have news."

"News about what?"

"The cookbook.” Emily pulled the satchel off her shoulder and opened it, withdrawing a small bundle of papers. "I found her. The author."

Jane found her heart suddenly beating faster. "You found something about her?"

"More than something." Emily held out the papers. "Her name is Felicity Cast. She wrote that cookbook almost twenty years ago, and I get the feeling she was already fairly old then. After that, she didn’t ever set pen to paper again that I can tell. She just had that one book in her, and stopped once she got it out. I half expected her to have died already."

"But she didn't?"

"She didn't." Emily’s smile was triumphant. "She's alive, Jane. She's been living in a small town about two days' travel from here for the past thirty years. I wrote her, and she wrote back almost immediately"

Jane took the papers with trembling fingers. The topmost sheet was a letter, addressed to Emily and written in a series of short, almost terse sentences.

I’m pleased to hear my book found a reader. Would welcome a visit. Always happy to discuss the craft.

"She wants to meet me?"

"She wants to meet you.”

"Two days' travel," Jane said slowly. "That's not far."

"It's not." Emily agreed. "You could be there and back in less than a week."

Jane thought about everything she would have to arrange if she wanted to take a trip. It wouldn’t be all that complicated a task, but it would mean leaving Glenfall behind for a little while, just when things were finally settling down. But the woman who had taught her to bake was alive, and she was willing to meet her, and Jane found that she wanted nothing more than to see her face to face.

"I'll have to think about it," she said. "Figure out the logistics."

"Of course." Emily’s smile suggested she already knew what Jane's answer would be. "Take your time. She's not going anywhere."

Jane looked at the letter one more time, then carefully folded it and tucked it into her pocket. The author of the cookbook that had changed her life was out there, waiting to be found.

Soon, she thought. I'll find her soon.
