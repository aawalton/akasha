Samantha proceeded over a crystalline bridge, down into a void, with the shadow of the great Palace that housed the Hall looming over her. She kept her eyes open, not completely trusting purely spiritual vision, even as she allowed pneumamancy to guide her. Samantha was alone for the first time in these particular spaces, and it felt all the more foreboding and spooky for it. Dust still caked the bridge's surface, with no signs anyone had ever walked the path at all.

She went up a corkscrew bridge underneath the palace, leading to a back door that was a circular black mirror. It was nearly perfect, without cloudiness or distortion. Ozra’s face, wearing her huge tophat, stared back at her instead of her own. She found the indentation that would take her through and put her hand into its perfectly fitted place, and pressed in. Light enveloped her.

Engineering. A huge, circular chamber with many bronze catwalks interlacing around it in levels, with enormous plastic-looking transparent cylinders like pipes curling all around, filled with a special concoction of mana and pneuma that glowed brightly with intensity, secondarily providing light. For the first time, Samantha could really appreciate and fathom the composition of the tubes. The energy was an intricate transmutative cocktail, pulling from the palace and fueling Ozra’s shenanigans within it. Engineering largely existed within the resonating aura of that energy, essentially.

Light passing through a special prism, wherein it becomes spiritually solid.

Perhaps fortunately, no one was here like last time, so she didn’t have to deal with Ozra’s gremlin maintenance people.

Samantha approached the fountain in the center, with the empty pedestal in the center, where the hologram of Ozra would appear if called.

Arms crossed, tapping a finger to her lip, Samantha pondered what to ask. Finally, she called, “Ozra Bosk! Tell me about the third door. The third challenge.”

Triumphant, machine-noise-polluted music played, somewhat painful with the trumpets, and Ozra appeared in her cleaner, more presentable steampunk attire, her top hat as magnificent as ever. She was saluting with a smile on her face. “Congratulations, Successor! You won!” Fireworks spewed from behind her briefly. The image leaned forward, waggling her eyebrows. “I bet your second Strange Phantasm is a doozy, eh?”

Samantha raised an eyebrow. Hmm. I didn’t mention Ralli, and mentioning her last time got a much different response. Not everyone got her, though. That much was clear.

Ozra winked, then leaned back up and shrugged. “On to the next thing, hmm? The rewards are promising and transformative, but don’t focus on that; focus on the goal of succeeding in the challenge and win-win-winning! Forget everything else. Good luck, Successor!”

She flickered out, and Samantha next said, “I’m facing the third door after having experienced the life of Rallindin. I’ve had… quite a journey in between, but I remain uncorrupted by Entropy. Is there anything different to tell me?”

A haunted-looking, hatless Ozra was sitting at her desk, rubbing her face with a hand, eyes directed into the middle-distance. When she spoke, it was as if to herself. “Triangulation. The Successor in the middle… a third power to round out order and chaos… but who? I don’t know them.” She shook her head and met Samantha’s eyes. “How can I know who, when the future is fluid?”

The image winked out.

Samantha frowned. That didn’t tell me much. Triangulation. Mmn. She said that before, of the three of us together, me, Ozra, and Ralli. Maybe I just thought that was what she meant, or maybe this is just another deepening. My strength, now enough to center on and make something even stronger out of our alignment.

“Ozra Bosk,” Samantha called, “tell me of the fourth door.”

The image that flickered on was one of a mature Ozra seen from the side, with her full, clean regalia on, her hands clasped behind her back. “Tsk, tsk!” She shook her head in admonishment. “Don’t get ahead of yourself, Successor! Focus on the challenge ahead of you, not the one in the distance. Things can change drastically in your life between them.” But she suddenly turned, smiled, and pointed a finger. “You’ll get it, though. I believe in you!”

The image winked out.

Rather than continue to ask questions, Samantha found herself drawn immediately beyond the fountain, directly forward from the fountain, to the ‘north.’ Like the other paths, it had a heavy bronze door at the end, leading into the outer superstructure. This one had Ozra’s ‘Prime Mover Engineer-Architect’ rune directly in the center, without any warnings. She felt a heavy deja vu in approaching it, yet also a deep, contrasting hesitation.

“Wait, haven’t I been over here before?” she muttered to herself.

Searching her memory, she was at a loss. It felt like she had, but she couldn’t recall. Increasingly, she doubted that she had, and it was coming from past lives.

Finally, she remembered Ozra had mentioned the ‘north door.’ She’d said it was her workstation, notes, and ‘everything needed to continue the work.’ Opening it had special requirements, and going in… forfeited one’s ability to return to the world.

Indeed, that made Samantha unwilling to approach it all the way. She paused a few meters away, staring at it, wondering what was beyond.

Has anyone else ever taken her up on it? I don’t feel like they have. I bet one requirement is passing through the fourth door, come to think of it. I doubt Ozra would want someone who still had hope to give up.

After a moment, Samantha turned away. She glanced at the pedestal, considering what else might be asked, but it didn’t seem like there was anything more of relevance that she could pull from the limited ‘keyword’ interface that it was.

She made her way down the right side path and into the smaller chamber that was the Challenge Room, which looked much like the inner workings of a steampunk clocktower, with bronze gears turning and copper pipes curling around in every direction.

She noted the oval portals around the facility, most reached by steps. The ground-level Room One was black and opaque, while the slightly higher Room Two was white. The other two doors were higher up.

Samantha floated up telekinetically this time, first pausing by the sealed-off fourth door, coated in bronze. It was so completely closed off, it didn’t really feel like a portal at all to her, just a hunk of metal.

Totally closed off. Not my door, by any means. Would it change if I suddenly screwed up, got corrupted, and escaped, or has that already been made impossible?

But she knew the truth. She just wouldn’t, not under any circumstances. She’d fight to the last shred. She would go after the Sage, and either come up totally victorious or not come up at all. Much would be decided down there, in the Sage’s realm — more than she properly understood.

But at least, for the worst-case scenario, the Cycle would end upon her death, and that’s why there was no more Fortuneteller. Things would start over. Again.

Sighing, Samantha floated back down to the third door, and this felt far different. A huge, tangled concoction of bronze strips was covering it, something she soon realized was a physical puzzle waiting to be unraveled.

Deciding she was game, Samantha took hold of it with telekinesis and began manipulating it methodically, pulling and pushing pieces around, at first slowly, and then more and more rapidly, as she worked almost expressly through a process of elimination. Any error at any point basically reset the whole puzzle, needing to be done from scratch.

It’s a lot like cracking a code by guessing. The odds here are… astronomical, though.

Once she got to a certain point, the time needed was just going to be too much, so she started utilizing her intuition to get through it. It wasn’t perfect, but it made a huge difference and more or less compacted the odds into something manageable and feasible in a short period of time.

Perhaps an hour later, she finally cracked it, and the bronze construct of thousands of interlocking pieces folded away, unveiling a mirrored portal different than the other two — this one was pearlescent, making a faint rainbow pattern on the surface, adding color to the pervious black and white of the other two. She could not see her image properly on the surface, as it was warped into too many interlaced shapes.

Something other than order and chaos, is it? I guess there’s only one way to find out what that could be.

She passed through the surface, and many swirls and vortices of color took shape as a new world and experience…
