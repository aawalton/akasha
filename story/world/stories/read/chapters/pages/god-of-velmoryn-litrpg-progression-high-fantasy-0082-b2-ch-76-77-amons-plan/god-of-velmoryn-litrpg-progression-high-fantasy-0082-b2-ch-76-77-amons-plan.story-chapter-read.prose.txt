Endless dunes stretched in every direction, the air above them warping beneath the relentless heat. Countless grains of sand seemed almost white beneath the blazing sun, reflecting its rays.

There was no shade in sight, nor any sign of life.

Well, other than a trail of footprints slowly disappearing as shifting sand and the occasional weak gust of wind erased them one by one. The breeze offered little relief against the merciless heat. If anything, it only seemed to make breathing more difficult.

The trail belonged to Amon and the warriors traveling with him.

It had been five days since he joined the army the humans had sent to exterminate the monsters.

In reality, it was an army in name only. Regardless of what they called it, it was a squad at best.

Eleven people.

And out of those eleven, six were Bronze Rank, four Silver Rank, and one Gold Rank.

That Gold Rank was Amon himself.

Which was precisely why he had nearly turned around and left for another settlement when he learned just how pitiful this so-called army was.

He was not afraid for his life.

No, he could protect himself from most threats with ease, and if things became difficult, he could always escape by entering my realm.

He simply believed these people were too weak and insignificant to be worth his time. Or rather, he was not confident in his ability to turn them into strong followers.

Even though Amon was powerful, he had not properly mastered his magic. If beasts as strong as a tharuun appeared, he would not be able to protect so many people. And based on what he had heard, creatures of that caliber were exactly what they were hunting.

“Verde, six Bronzes… and I tested their mana. They don't measure ranks the same way velmoryns do. A Bronze Rank velmoryn would easily wipe the floor with a Silver Rank human,” he complained, wiping sweat from his face.

“You are already with them. Stop whining and pay attention. See if any of them might be useful for our plans.”

“Easy for you to say. You’re not the one being roasted alive.” He spat to the side and immediately regretted it as his mouth became even drier. “I should’ve learned water magic…”

I chose to ignore him.

The strength of those humans had very little to do with his foul mood. I knew the real reason behind it. Amon hated the heat.

He hated it with his entire being and struggled against it even more. That was why he had asked Gundir for a cloak that would keep him cool.

But even that was proving ineffective in the desert, where one could probably fry an egg on the ground.

“Your Holiness, please accept this.” A young woman turned and handed Amon a strange gelatinous object. It was roughly rectangular, slightly larger than a cherry, black as tar, and faintly translucent.

“Really, Amanda? I gave that varnok blood to you, not him!” a man barked from the side. “And stop calling him ‘Your Holiness’! He’s a priest, not an archbishop! Besides, he doesn’t even serve our god…”

“Pay him no mind,” the woman said, shooting the man a cold stare.

The man muttered something under his breath and increased his pace, eager to put some distance between himself and the two.

“Thank you, Amanda,” Amon said as he accepted the varnok blood.

“Verde, be a lamb and use Guidance on this. I can’t sense any mana from it…”

He was becoming increasingly shameless.

Still, I could not deny that this was exactly the kind of thing Guidance should be used for.

[Varnok Blood (Refined) ]

Humans have discovered a method to refine varnok blood into a medicinal preparation that drastically lowers body temperature.

The substance helps maintain a stable body temperature in extreme heat and provides protection against overheating during severe fevers.

“Why haven’t you used this before?” Amon asked Amanda after I relayed the information Guidance had provided.

The woman pulled back her black headscarf slightly, revealing her face.

She was pleasant to look at, with thin lips, a long straight nose, and large brown eyes. Her skin was spotless, without even a single bead of sweat on it.

“They cost far too much for low-ranking mercenaries like us. Our pay barely covers our equipment,” she said.

She removed the veil entirely, letting her wavy dark hair fall loose. Running her fingers through it, she adjusted it before covering her head and face once more.

“The baron only provides enough for us to ration it, so we can recover and keep the heat from weakening us too much…”

Amon’s eyes remained fixed on her as she spoke, listening attentively. For all his complaints, he was paying close attention to every word and detail.

“Can you tell me more about mercenaries?” Amon asked as he offered the varnok blood back to Amanda. “I cannot accept this. I cannot allow you to suffer in my stead.”

His voice was so gentle and sincere that even I found myself wondering whether he genuinely cared that much.

“No, you don’t need to worry about me.”

As Amanda tried to object, Amon clasped her hands and placed the gelatinous mass back into them. A small smile appeared on his face.

“I can endure the heat through my Lord’s grace. His love is all the comfort I need.”

“You charlatan,” I laughed, no longer able to remain silent.

This time, he ignored me.

Pressing his hands together, he looked up at the sky. He offered no prayer and said nothing to me. Though when a droplet of sweat rolled down from his brow and into his eye, he immediately cursed inwardly.

“Your Holiness…” Amanda tried again.

But Amon simply shook his head.

“Tell me more about mercenaries, please.”

The woman removed a small waterskin from her belt and pushed the varnok blood inside, shaking it vigorously to dissolve it.

“There is not much to tell,” she said after taking a small sip. “Every region has a mercenary guild where anyone can register a company or join an existing one. Once our ranks are verified, we can begin accepting contracts through the guild.”

“Are you assigned to specific regions?” Amon asked.

In truth, neither he nor I had any idea what those regions actually were.

Aria had attempted to acquire a map, but strangely enough, such information was incredibly difficult to obtain. Scrolls describing magical spells were easier to find than maps showing human settlements, kingdoms, and whatever these regions might be.

“No. We are free to travel, but we must notify the guild whenever we change regions and surrender our badges to receive new ones.”

Amanda pulled a small wooden badge from her pouch and showed it to Amon.

He reached out to take it.

Before his fingers could touch the badge, the ground trembled beneath them.

Sand shifted.

The dunes began to move like waves upon the sea until two bulges formed beneath the surface and started racing toward the mercenaries.

“It’s them!” the leader of the group shouted.

She was a Silver Rank mage who had not cast a single spell since Amon joined the expedition.

She quickly ran toward Amon and Amanda, taking position beside them. The rest of the group immediately moved into their predetermined formations.

Those with shields, swords, and spears formed the frontline. Four men and one woman, all calm and focused.

Behind them stood the two mages and Amon, while the archers took position at the rear.

Every one of them faced the two bulges racing through the sand, their speed increasing rapidly.

Rather than focusing on the battlefield, I noticed that Amon had not drawn the wand I had made for him. He looked unimpressed, almost bored.

“These beasts are weaker than the average velmoryn, Verde,” he grumbled.

Despite his disappointment, he did not allow it to show on his face. Instead, he clasped his hands together and closed his eyes as if offering a prayer.

The spearman was the first to engage.

He leaped forward and thrust his spear directly into the moving mound of sand.

A metallic clang rang through the Window as his spear shattered.

The mound collapsed and the creature beneath it came into view. It resembled a worm with scorpion-like pedipalps and a tail ending in a vicious stinger. It was enormous, large enough to swallow a grown man whole.

The creature surged toward the spearman, its circular mouth opening wide. Hundreds of razor-sharp teeth stared back at him.

The leader’s mana flared.

Dark blue steam hissed from her body as the spearman vanished and reappeared a step away from where he had been standing.

The wormlike monster missed its target and continued forward for several more meters before beginning a wide turn. Its massive body did not seem suited for sharp movements, forcing it to circle around before it could charge the spearman again.

Space magic?

Now that was interesting.

Unfortunately, I could not use Guidance on the leader to confirm whether she was truly manipulating space.

The leader dropped to one knee and coughed blood onto the sand. Her mana faded immediately afterward, but she still managed to shout.

“Eric, now!”

The burliest member of the group responded.

Planting his feet firmly in the sand, he raised his shield and slammed his sword against it.

Just before steel struck steel, both weapons shimmered with light.

The instant they collided, the shimmer burst outward, sending a wave of translucent energy toward the two monsters.

As the wave washed over them, both creatures immediately turned their attention toward Eric and charged.

The man who had scolded Amanda moved next, followed by the remaining two frontliners.

At the same time, the archers released their arrows.

Green light shimmered along the shafts as they whistled over the mercenaries and buried themselves deep into the nearest monster’s body with a hiss.

The wormlike creature continued charging toward Eric even as the flesh around the arrows began to rot. It seemed completely numb to pain, the skill the shieldman had used dulling its senses and forcing its attention onto him alone.

The frontliners reached the creature moments later and attacked with everything they had.

Their weapons scraped against the worm’s thick hide, leaving only shallow wounds. Its skin seemed almost as hard as metal, judging by the harsh sound of steel striking its body.

Even though that particular monster was accumulating more and more injuries, the other one behind it remained completely unharmed.

And it was rapidly catching up.

The wounded worm had begun to slow, partly due to the damage inflicted by the frontliners, but mostly because of the rotting effect of the arrows.

As I noticed just how effective those projectiles were, I narrowed the Window onto the archers, wondering why they had stopped firing.

Only then did I notice that each of them had just a single green arrow remaining in their quivers.

The rest were ordinary.

I could not be certain, but based on their limited number, I suspected those arrows were supplied by the baron as well.

“I’ll deal with the one on the left. You must kill the other!” their leader shouted as blue mana erupted from her body once more.

This time, however, blood followed immediately.

It streamed from her nose, eyes, and ears as the steam pouring from her body thickened. Soon, traces of crimson mixed with the blue mana surging around her.

The others barely heard her words over the battle, but none of them missed the flare of her mana.

It roared like a waterfall crashing from a cliff, drowning out every sound in the vicinity.

Then, just as it became overwhelming, it vanished.

Her mana disappeared as abruptly as a candle snuffed out by the wind. And almost immediately, the monster in the rear roared.

Or rather, it produced a disturbing creaking noise as a massive chunk of its head vanished, as though some enormous creature had taken a bite out of it.

It’s definitely space magic.

I was certain now. Their leader possessed the ability to manipulate space.

As I shifted the Window toward her, I saw that she had collapsed onto the sand. She was unconscious and convulsing. Apparently, her mana could barely sustain her own magic, or perhaps spatial magic demanded something beyond mere magical energy.

Either way, the woman was in terrible condition.

Foam mixed with blood seeped from the corner of her mouth, staining her lips red. Her eyes were wide open, showing only the whites as her body trembled uncontrollably.

Amanda crouched beside her while Amon channeled mana into her body, trying to stabilize her condition.

With one monster dead, the mercenaries quickly finished off the remaining one.

The toxic arrows were clearly devastating. By the time the creature died, its body was riddled with cracks, chunks of flesh collapsing inward as the poison rotted it from within.

I made a mental note to find out how those arrows were made.

Even if they proved useless for the velmoryns, I needed a way to counter them. If these low-ranking mercenaries had access to such potent poisoned projectiles, who knew what kind of weapons the Night God’s army might possess?

Acting as the god of an almost timeless race, I had focused solely on Noah’s powers and the kinds of creatures he might have created. In doing so, I had completely forgotten why humans had climbed to the top of the food chain on Earth.

They were not the strongest. Nor the fastest. Nor the most terrifying.

Humans were intelligent.

And that intelligence allowed them to create tools and weapons that bridged the gap between themselves and creatures far superior in raw physical ability.

Who knew how terrifying humanity’s weapons could become in a world where magic was real and people could bend nature to their will without relying on technology?

No, I couldn’t overlook them. I shouldn’t. I would study not only Noah and his forces, but humanity in general.

“She will be fine,” Amon said as mana faded from his hands.

“Can’t you heal her with holy power? I heard you…” Amanda began.

But Amon interrupted her with a shake of his head.

“I was acting according to my Lord’s will before. Using His power without His command... I cannot do that. Not unless one of His believers requires my aid.”

“Then why the hell did you come?” a rough voice called from the side.

A man who had argued against Amon earlier returned. He was drenched in dark green blood, the sticky, slimy substance coating his sword and hands.

“Amanda could’ve calmed her down. You did shit during the fight, and now that it’s over, you’re still doing shit…”

“Stop, Leon!” Amanda shouted, covering his mouth with both hands. “We must not insult holy priests…”

“He isn’t holy, Amanda. By the Lord of Rain, have you forgotten who our God is?” Leon snapped.

Then, as Amanda practically dragged him away, he lowered his voice and continued muttering under his breath.

“Well, he has a point,” Amon whispered into my mind. “Anyway, who cares about them… Verde, did you notice Catalina’s magic? I’m pretty sure it was spatial! I have to study it, Verde. I have to master it!”

For once, I did not mind Amon’s obsession with magic.

If anything, I was pleased he had already decided to pursue it.

Space manipulation was one of the most terrifying forms of magic I had seen so far. The chunk missing from the worm’s head was proof enough of that. If Amon actually managed to master it, he would become far more dangerous than a Silver Rank mage with barely a tenth of his mana reserves.

Amon gently lifted Catalina’s unconscious body. She was calm now, her face peaceful. He carried her over to the rest of the group and laid her down beside Eric.

“Thank you,” the man muttered, his voice weak.

Amon nodded and walked toward the corpse of the wormlike monster.

Amanda had climbed onto its body. In her hands was a strange circular disk, obsidian black in color. She would place it against the corpse, watch it for a few moments, then lift it and move it to a different spot.

“What are you doing?” Amon asked.

“Eh?” Amanda exclaimed, clearly surprised. “This? It’s used to extract an Essence from fallen monsters. It’s a low-quality artifact, but more than enough for beasts below Gold Rank.”

Amon nodded knowingly, his gaze fixed on the artifact.

His strange and exceptional magical perception allowed him to sense mana so vividly that he could almost see it.

That was why he could easily identify different magical patterns and even analyze spells as they were being cast. I still had no idea how he had acquired that ability. There was nothing in his status window that explained it.

He watched the disk as it suddenly shimmered after Amanda placed it on what had to be the tenth location, if not more. The obsidian surface became coated in green light. The energy swirled across it before gathering in the center.

“Your Holiness,” Amanda asked suddenly. “Do you… do you think we can do it?”

“Do what?”

“Destroy their lair?”
