Eola hadn’t dreamed about her parents in almost a year, but that had ended just after she’d learned about Mana purification and the impure Mana inside of dungeons. Since then, she’d had similar nightmares at least two or three nights of the week.

It had been hard.

But not seeing them in three and a half years had been hard, too. She’d been at home, by herself, when she’d gotten the news. One of Lord Card’s spell-swords had delivered the news, along with a letter. He’d been the only one to get out before the gates sealed. Ricarn. Jereth Ricarn. That was his name. He’d gone straight for the Lemiene house in Greenarbor, and he’d told her everything. He’d also brought the sword and wand.

The official notice from Lord Card had arrived the next day, and it said something very different from Ricarn’s account. And Ricarn had disappeared three days after that. Eola had kept her head down—a very sensible thing for a fifteen-year-old girl with no magical talent to do—and moved in with her uncle shortly after that.

So, yes, the nightmares were hard. But living in Greenarbor with her uncle, knowing what had happened to her parents, had been hard, too. Eola was used to doing hard things. She’d been doing them for a long time.

And this time, she’d had someone waiting for her.

They hadn’t talked much; after she told him about her parents, there hadn’t been much to say. But he’d been there, letting her slowly relax and, for the first time in almost four years, let someone hold her. He wasn’t her mom and dad, but he also wasn’t trying to be. He’d never been trying to be. That was for the best. She didn’t need that—not from him.

She pushed the rest of that thought away, face heating up.

As they sat on the couch, Eola’s thoughts flashed from her parents to her former roommates, Evelyn and Bannoque. They’d spent hours in this exact spot, and it had always felt…obscene. But Colin’s arms had been around her for a good half-hour, and if Roth or Patrice saw, they’d definitely assume…

And yet, it didn’t feel like that at all. Even when she’d calmed down, they sat there, time passing. It was…

The seventh bell rang.

“We should…we should wake the others,” Eola said as she let go of Colin. “The exam…”

“Right,” Colin said. “I won’t tell anyone, Eola. You have my word.”

Eola closed her eyes, breathed, and stood up to knock on Patrice’s door.

As the sun rose, Eola and Colin woke the others, and they shared a nearly silent breakfast of apple porridge in the academy’s cafeteria. Eola ate robotically. Truthfully, her stomach wanted nothing to do with the delicious-smelling gruel, but they wouldn’t get many opportunities to eat once they arrived at the library. The others ate with a similar lack of enthusiasm.

Then it was back to their suite to collect their gear, and Eola Lemiene, Colin Tremory, Patrice Clerk, and Roth Gerr filed across the academy’s melting grounds and into the library.

They weren’t alone, either. Three dozen students stood outside the gate to the second floor, talking amongst themselves and studiously not looking at the other groups—or at the librarians and professors circled around Headmaster Tomlish.

Catrine Andrese stood near the front, next to Garreth Skagett, who’d donned a set of armor that put Patrice’s plate to shame. The two girls who followed Catrine around were there, too. They’d geared up in brigantines just like Catrine’s, and they carried smallswords on their hips. The sleek, dangerous look was somewhat spoiled by the double satchels they both carried, criss-crossed across their chests. Catrine, of course, wore nothing but her armor, sword, and bandolier for her spellbook and wand.

Then there was Ferrin’s group. There were only three. In addition to the swordswoman, one carried a spear, while the other was—to Eola’s shock—decked out with a siege mage’s war staff and a half-dozen rings on his fingers. It would have looked gaudy if Eola hadn’t known that all siege mages wore at least that many. A good siege mage fought like Colin, but had the resources to mitigate their Mana consumption with ensorcelled items.

Irisbreeze and Wenderly—the girl who’d trounced her during the Magical Dueling mid-term—had teamed up with a pair of elves. All four wore smallswords, and all four were armored much like Eola.

“Welcome, combat-track hopefuls,” Headmaster Tomlish said, his voice shockingly loud in the all but silent library. “Each of your eleven teams is, hopefully, prepared to enter the third floor in just a few minutes, attempt the Library Exam, and return here with your prize in hand. This test does not determine whether your education ends or not, and before we begin, I would urge any student not confident in their abilities to abandon it. There is no shame in the non-combat track—and I should note that they also get the day off.”

Eola glanced at Colin. He looked just about ready to take the offer, and she touched his arm. He jumped and looked at her, then shook his head slowly. “Sorry.”

“We’ll manage this together, Colin. Breathe,” she whispered back.

“No one? Very well. The Library Exam, then. The objective is simple. You will enter the library’s third floor, acquire a list of books chosen by me, and exit,” Headmaster Tomlish continued.

“That’s it?” a student asked.

“If you haven’t been paying attention in Instructor Clearance’s class, do your team a favor and leave now,” the headmaster replied. “The third floor is not to be taken lightly. As such, each team will be accompanied by a librarian and a professor. Neither will interfere or offer advice, assistance, or aid, with two exceptions. If either believes a student’s life is in grave and immediate danger, they may remove that student from the library. This will, of course, cause them to fail the test.

“The second exception is if a Bright Ball is cast in crimson. In that case, any professors or librarians nearby must immediately come to the signal-caster’s aid, for a red Bright Ball means interference with the Library Exam from a higher floor, and first-year students are not expected to be able to fight said threats.”

As the headmaster listed groups by last name, then assigned a librarian and professor to them and sent them through the gate, Eola turned the Library Exam over in her head. Atta weaved between her feet as she thought, purring softly.

For most teams, speed would be their best chance of success. Wenderly and Irisbreeze’s team, for example, was built to cut through monsters, but lacked staying power. None of them, as far as Eola knew, could cast the prerequisite five First Order spells to be a Journeymage. If they didn’t run into any major obstacles, they’d be the first ones done. But if they did? They’d be lucky to pass at all.

Ferrin’s trio was almost their polar opposite. If the siege mage could get set, and the other two could protect him, they’d be able to fight almost any third-floor monster and win handily. But they’d have to pace themselves so he didn’t run out of Mana too quickly.

And then there were the exceptions to the balance between speed and power. Catrine’s team, for instance. Her scent magic was powerful enough that only feral books would give them any trouble. They’d be able to move fast and hit hard, because she could stop most threats in her tracks. The other three wouldn’t have to ration their Mana too much, as long as she could keep casting.

Eola nodded. Yes, Catrine’s team was good. But she was pretty sure hers was better—or at least, that they could be.

"Tremory, Gerr, Clerk, and Lemiene, your team is with Instructor Tarik and Librarian Tagg,” Headmaster Tomlish said, and Eola blinked, hand on her smallsword, and walked to the gate.

The Library Exam had begun.

The first time Eola had entered the library’s third floor, she’d realized one critical detail that Instructor Clearance’s lectures about it had missed.

It was enormous.

She, Tagg, and Instructor Tarik stopped for a moment as Colin whistled, the sound echoing from the bottom of a dozen or more floating rooms. “Whoa,” he said a moment later.

“Whoa is right.” Patrice stared up at the floating rooms, and at the twelve flights of stairs connecting to them. A purplish mist hung in the air. Eola shivered slightly, but masked it by drawing her smallsword. "You told us, but I didn’t believe you.”

It was impressive. The sheer scale of the third floor had overwhelmed her the first time, and the coordinates she’d followed had proved themselves less than useful in it. The shelves, ladders, and arched entrances to the staircases all looked the same, and unlike the first floor’s bright, welcoming aisles and reading nooks or the Row and Shelf system on the second floor, this was a proper maze.

And it was beautiful, too. If Eola ignored the barrier between the third and second floor—the one she’d crashed through at the end of her journey—the floating shelves drifting through the room like clouds were almost calming.

Almost.

Eventually, Patrice pulled herself away from the view. “Right then. Where are we going?”

Tagg cleared his throat. “Here’s your list. You’re all competent enough to be here. I’ll keep an eye out for upper-floor horrors, but you’re on your own, mostly.” He handed a folded and sealed piece of parchment over, not looking at Instructor Tarik as he did.

The professor studiously avoided eye contact with the librarian as well. “Apprentices, as my colleague here has said, you are on your own. Good luck.”

Patrice broke the seal and unfolded the parchment, and Eola got her first look at the task ahead of them.

Recorded Musings of Apostle Antoinnene, Volume Seven

The Water War, Thirty-Eighth Century Post-Y’aer

Notes and Fragments: Willowsong’s Sonnets, Untranslated

The Love Letters of Dame Brandwell and Duke Inverly

A Briefe Historie of the Alemic Tongue

Next to each of the five titles, the headmaster had helpfully written a pair of coordinates and a brief description of the room it could be found in. It wasn’t much—number of stairwells, its overall shape, and a single unique shelf, desk, or rug in each—but it was enough to give them a chance.

“The coordinates are pretty useless,” Eola said. “We should navigate by sight.”

“Let’s go,” Roth said. “We’re wasting time.”

Eola nodded and picked the farthest stairwell to the right—the farthest one from the route she’d taken during her first journey. Her sword and wand readied, she climbed the stairs. “Don’t rely on the railing. Some of them are fake.”

Atta’s claw pricked her neck, and when she turned her head, the cat looked at her smugly before hopping off her shoulders.

She grinned back, a look that screamed ‘see, I can learn.’

The cat’s only response was to hurry up the stairs.

When she caught up, it was in a room with two rounded sides butting up against a long, straight one, the second rounded side half a flight of stairs above the other, almost B-shaped. The lower section was crammed so full of jumbled shelves that it was all but impossible to see more than a few feet ahead, and the whole room listed to the left by a good five or six degrees. Eola pulled a book off the closest shelf. “Ensorcellment for Warlocks. This isn’t one of our rooms. Let’s keep moving.”

“Agreed,” Patrice said. She had her hammer in a spear-grip, ready to drive the spike into whatever she found around the next corner. It was a wildly impractical weapon for the narrow labyrinth of shelves, though, and Eola took the lead again.

As they worked their way through, a shadow passed overhead. Eola watched a stairwell click into place at the highest point of the room. “That’s our target.” She turned the corner, then stepped back, holding up a hand. “Unweeded.”

“Any feral books?” Colin tried to peek around Patrice and hide behind her at the same time.

“I don’t think so. I’ll do a little weeding. Patrice, Colin, can you watch my back?”

Patrice nodded, then elbowed Colin’s ribs until he did, too. Somewhat reassured that at least one of her friends would be ready, she took her sword in one hand and got to work. Just like the other half-dozen times she’d found and weeded sections of the library, the weapon really wasn’t right for the job, and after a moment, Roth cleared his throat.

“What?” she hissed.

He held up his hatchet. “Move.”

She did, and Roth scowled as he hacked at the thickest vine until it parted. The moment it did, her Spark Shot scorched the vine’s Mana, incinerating it without triggering the library’s anti-fire defenses. “Well done, Roth,” she said.

He nodded curtly. “Keep moving. The sooner we’re through, the sooner—“

“Down!” Colin screamed.

A magical vine thrashed, and pages tore free from a dozen books, whipping themselves into a frenzy around the magical core. Mana surged across the pages. Eola grabbed Roth’s shoulder, pulling him down and into a shelf as the feral book exploded outward. Her elbow ended up in Roth’s stomach; he pushed her off of him, and she recovered, sword in hand.

Options.

A feral book was far from the worst thing she’d prepared for. Colin could easily incinerate it with his ritual, but it’d cost him. He’d need to start ‘purifying’ Mana from the dungeon, and the longer he put that off, the better they’d all be. His rituals would nearly empty him, and while ‘purification’ would allow him to keep casting, he’d quickly end up with a soul full of impure Mana. In the long term, his rituals weren’t a viable solution.

Spark Shot, though. That’d work—especially her modified version.

She started to draw. The Old Alemic text filled her spell-chain’s central ring, and she quickly—or as quickly as she could, at least—drew the Forsooth marks for Hasty, Reaching, and Skilled. Patrice pulled Roth back toward Colin as Eola finished her Spark Shot.

A whip of fire appeared at her wand’s tip, blue flames putting off enough heat to warm her cheeks. She flicked it back, then snapped it forward with all her strength. Her wand bent slightly in her hand.

The cord of burning blue flame bent much more sharply.

Eola slashed her wand across the paper tornado like a sword, and the whip followed it, cracking loudly as it tore pages in half and wrapped around the Mana vine surging through the feral book’s center. More flames—this time orange—rippled down the vine as its Mana burned.

Behind her, Colin breathed a sigh of relief. “Thank Y’aer. I was worried I’d have to do it.” Sure enough, his half-finished ritual sketch collapsed in a mirror of the dying feral book.

Eola looked over her shoulder. Her eyes met Instructor Tarik’s, and he nodded once.

It had cost her. She estimated she’d spent two of her bars of Mana on just that spell. But it had been less expensive than forcing Colin to finish the ritual and start filling his soul with dungeon Mana.

They were halfway up the next stairwell when they encountered their next problem. Eola was thinking about Colin, and her nightmare, and so the giant, magical snake moving through the room above them almost slipped her notice.

It slid past the stairs’ exit, seeming to float. Twenty feet long, or maybe thirty, its scales shimmered with ever-changing color, and Eola froze. Behind her, Patrice gasped, and she resisted the urge to shush her fellow apprentice.

Eola had never seen a Srilla in person. She watched until the thin, almost string-like Mana construct’s tail slid by, then looked over her shoulder.

Tagg, for his part, seemed to be almost on the verge of saying something, and Eola thought back to her conversation with him right after she’d…acquired…Atta. ‘Srillas fixate on their targets, but they’re not too smart until they’ve fed.’

Atta hadn’t moved since the magical snake passed by. She just stared nervously at the top of the stairs.

“Right,” Eola said, “we’re sure our book’s in this room?”

Colin paused, stared at the list, and nodded slowly. “It’s the right shape. We could always…uh…come back later?”

“Not an option,” Patrice said. She stared at Roth.

So did Eola.

“Which one do you want?” he asked after a moment, opening his satchel.

“Noise cancellation,” Eola said.

“How about a distraction to buy us time?” Patrice asked.

Roth rolled his eyes. “I’ll try the noise cancelation countermelody, then. We’ll see how well it does here.” He selected a brass flute, blew into it experimentally, and then started to play.

As the spell formed, Eola pointed at Tagg and Tarik. “Get in close. The countermelody doesn’t have much range.” Then, without waiting for them to start moving, she stepped up the stairs. The spell washed over her, and her feet made no sound as she stepped into the Srilla’s lair—and the most likely hiding spot of the first book on their list, Recorded Musings of Apostle Antoinnene, Volume Seven.
