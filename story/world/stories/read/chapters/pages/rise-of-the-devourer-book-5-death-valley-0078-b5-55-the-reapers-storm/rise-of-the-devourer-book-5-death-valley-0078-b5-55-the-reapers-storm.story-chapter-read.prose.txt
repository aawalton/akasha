When Vor withdrew his sword, he left behind a solid core of moonlight at the back of Noah's chest that weighed on Noah's entire body like an inner gravity, pulling him tight together, making every movement apart from curling into a ball an extreme effort.

Noah refused to submit. He remained stubbornly rigid though it cost him more effort than he'd imagined possible.

Vor ignored him. He'd grabbed Noah by one leg and dragged him behind him as they walked off at an angle directly away from where the Shadow Heart's pull promised the escape back to reality waited. Technically, at least for now, it would move them closer to the Reaper, the angle acute, but the further they went the less helpful that would become as their paths diverged more and more from anywhere Noah wanted to be.

Noah tried to bite at the light in his chest, but couldn't get a good angle. It may well be affixed to his spine, actually, which made for a very awkward position.

He tried grabbing it with his hands, but they just went through it without any interaction.

"Devourer?" Noah whispered. "Any extra powerups you want to unlock before it's too late?"

Bite your leg off?

"That might make it hard to run. I more meant a way to get rid of this."

Bite his leg off?

Noah looked at Vor's legs, then shrugged. "Worth a try."

He stopped resisting the pull that wanted to crumple his legs up into a ball, converted all his strength into reaching out to grab Vor's leg as it came back in reach, and bit into it with all he had.

Vor kept walking.

Noah's mouth and Devourer alike struggled to cut through the tangible body from their ghostly status, but even once he worked his way through the skin, there was very little there for him to devour. Vor's substance was thin and ethereal in the most irritating way possible, like eating steel-coated styrofoam. As soon as Noah swallowed one tiny, unsatisfying bite, the gap flowed back closed nearly instantly.

He continued for several minutes, too stubborn to give up just because he seemed to be making no progress, as Vor ignored the fact his prisoner was gnawing on his shin.

Vor in fact seemed to be ignoring everything around them, walking with a steady cadence and muttering an unfamiliar litany under his breath. His previous arrogance was nowhere to be found, as if he'd lost all interest in Noah after capturing him, instead in this near-trance state.

"This isn't working," Noah finally conceded, releasing Vor's disgustingly crunchy and filmy spirit leg. "What ever happened to souls tasting like chocolate?"

That was Death mana. The Watcher tastes like broken glass. Not my preferred diet, though I'm currently hungry enough to try anything.

"Are you ever not hungry?"

Not that I remember.

Noah made a face. "So biting mister metal-styrofoam isn't making any progress. Any other ideas?"

He kept trying to open his Astral Script, kept reaching for Paths that he'd become so used to having available.

He felt blind without Abyssal Awareness feeding him details about his surroundings, limited to only what he could see. He missed the comfortable deadly crackle of Abyssal Inferno against his skin, ready to lash out at anyone who came near. He kept trying to start a Life Siphon each time he damaged Vor's annoyingly sturdy body.

The ambient pressure of the Underworld didn't help. It had been easy to ignore at first, just one more part of how alien the place was, but the farther Vor dragged him without Noah getting any say in the matter, the more oppressive it became.

It's not just that. We're getting closer to the Watcher's seat of power.

"That explains the creepy feeling of being stared at," Noah muttered. "Here I thought it was just more wisps."

But now it'd been pointed out, he couldn't help but look upward. There was no Watcher's Moon here to form a visible mark in the sky, but he could swear he saw a silvery glow on the horizon coming from the direction they were heading.

We're closer to the Reaper than we've ever been yet, the Devourer commented. See that shadow over there?

Noah turned his head and, indeed, there was a dark cylinder of shadow rising like a supertornado away to the side. If he'd had Astral Step he could have reached it in moments, but to run the whole way on open ground with Vor able to outrun him at the best of times...

Give him a decoy, the Devourer suggested, a smirk in his tone.

"If this is just an excuse for you to get me to gnaw my own leg off..."

He's clearly not paying attention to us. This could be our best chance.

"At what? Hopping? I can barely move."

"I can fix that." Dylan's voice was small and tentative from the back of Noah's neck. "I can get between you and his anchor. Then you can move it to where you can reach."

And then you can eat it.

No, Shadow interrupted. If we're leaving a decoy it needs to have the mark, or he'll notice his power shifting.

Noah almost burst out laughing at how absurd the plan was, but he'd never been one to shy away from bizarre things. "Okay, let's do it."

Dylan slipped down the back of Noah's shirt, cold and slick and making him shiver. He tried to watch, but whatever Dylan did it was over so fast Noah couldn't catch it. Then Dylan circled his torso pushing the glowing moonlight anchor in front of him like a tiny Sisyphus.

Noah's internal gravity shifted weirdly the whole time, straining to follow wherever Dylan pushed the light as it slowly moved down his chest, across his leg, and into his shin just past where Vor's hand gripped his ankle.

Noah waited a moment in case the man noticed the change, but Vor's chanting and walking didn't falter.

He leaned over and tentatively bit at his own knee. The shadowy substance of his translucent body came apart easily, so much so that he'd completely separated his lower leg from the rest of his body before he quite internalized what he was doing.

For a moment he lay here, watching Vor continue walking with the glowing Noah-leg, leaving Noah himself behind.

He wanted to laugh, but that would have been very ill-timed, so he stifled it instead and shifted his attention to figuring out what to do about his new missing limb. If this were the real world, Tony could rebuild it in a heartbeat, but as it was...

"I can handle it," Dylan said, wrapping two of his tentacles around Noah's knee, then stretching himself out all the way until the other end of his other tentacles reached the ground.

Noah carefully stood, impressed by Dylan's adaptability. It took them a few false starts to figure out their rhythm but before long they were jogging, then running.

By the time Vor Ashen realized he'd been tricked, they would be well out of his reach.

As they ducked into the supercell wall of shadow, the distant figure was still walking without any clue that they'd slipped away out from behind him.

The area inside the perpetual hurricane was peaceful and still, the eye of the storm, and only after stepping inside the Reaper's domain did Noah fully appreciate how much pressure the Watcher's presence had been exerting against him. He almost slumped with relief, like stepping into an air conditioned room on a scorching summer day.

Noah’s mother stood waiting, her hair tied up, looking healthier than he could remember ever seeing her. She smiled gently and held out her hands. "Welcome, Nehua. And you, small one.”

Dylan waved one of his tentacles, almost lost his balance from where he was holding Noah’s leg up, overcorrected, and only by grabbing the woman’s shoulder did Noah avoid falling.

She didn’t seem to mind almost being fallen on, only laughed lightly and steadied him until he'd stabilized his footing. “So, you're the one who's been causing all this ruckus, is it? There's not much that happens around here, you're definitely the most exciting thing to happen here in ages."

Noah was still just staring. He'd been told the Reaper took the form of people you loved, but he hadn't seen her in so long and it hit him in ways he'd forgotten were possible. Even knowing it wasn't really her. Even telling himself over and over it was just an illusion.

"My rivers reach to all realms, young primordial. This is not a form stolen from your memory. It was given to me by its bearer."

A hundred unsaid everythings filled Noah's chest, begging to be said, but he wasn't here for sentimental reunions. "I was told you can answer my questions."

"Some of them. Others you must discover the answers yourself. But I can tell you what the young goddess could not bring herself to, and more besides."

"Please." Noah couldn't force anything more out through the thickness in his nonexistent throat.

The Reaper nodded and gestured for them to follow. "Some of what you want to know is better shown than told. And much of it is better told at rest than standing at the door. We have no need to rush here. Be at peace."

Noah nodded stiffly. She led them through the shadowed interior of the stormwall and into the true eye, where endless blackness could be seen above instead of the grey-tinted sky of the Watcher's outer regions.

Below the night sky lay a paved courtyard with a fountain at its center, surrounded by tea tables and elegant chairs, all lit by a solid stripe of warm white lights that ringed the courtyard.

"Do you want information first, or your trust returned?" She looked deep into Noah's eyes, but he got the feeling it wasn't him she was looking at.

What trust is that? the Devourer asked, sounding more hesitant and uncertain than Noah had ever heard from him.

"I see. Then your path will be longer than I expected." She nodded, then turned away and pulled up a table. "Please, sit. Ask your questions and I will answer."

"I don't know what to ask." He had so many questions. Who was Raelinah? Why was Noah here on Erandir? What was the ??? that he was collecting shards of? Had his mother known—
