By the time we returned to the city, it turned out that we weren’t the only participants there. As the guards were checking us, one of them let slip to Sparta that three more mercenaries had arrived. Based on the numbers and the lack of specific description, I assumed it wasn’t the goths.

To some degree, that was a shame; I would have liked to catch up with Pup.

I immediately asked to go see Lord Steen, but apparently both he and his “herald” had been called to the castle for an important discussion. I was still given full authority to come and go as I pleased and, apparently, so had the other three participants.

Sparta was eager to go check on them, so I let her. With so many eyes on us, any semblance of privacy was gone. It didn’t help that all the inns and taverns had been instructed not to let me book a place. Each time I asked, I got the same response: “His Lordship has arranged for you to stay at the inner barracks.”

After the fifth attempt, I finally gave in.

The room I was granted was set up for eight people. The beds were still there, and even if blankets were removed from all but one, there were obvious signs that at least half had been occupied until my arrival. I suspected the only reason its former occupants weren’t upset with me was because I was a “sacrifice.”

By evening, a sixth participant had arrived. Thanks to the Listener card, I heard whispers about him long before he went through the gate. From what I could make out, he was one of the combat-oriented participants. I had hoped that he’d be led to Lord Steen, granting me the excuse for a long conversation. Unfortunately, that turned out to be half true. The guards escorted the participant to the castle, where I was definitely not invited.

“Fuck it, Sparta, where are you?” I looked at the streets.

She had promised to find me after “catching up.” So far, almost a full day had passed, and there was no trace of her. She was using her Thief card, no doubt, for I couldn’t hear a whisper about her or the rest of her group. As the saying went, all promises in the Grand Arcana are temporary.

At nightfall, I went to check a few temples throughout the city. The requests had drastically changed since the first day. Most of the deities had learned that I wouldn’t agree to a pact, so had focused on more practical requests.

Seeing how I killed the moonelk, they were flooding me with requests for desired animal parts, which they were willing to exchange for unspecified items, cards of no description, or even a booster sphere. With ten days remaining, I had no choice but to indulge them.

Following the information given to me by Breath of Moonlight and my past experience, finding the creatures in question was relatively easy. It could be said they were strong, having a Major Arcana card or two at their disposal, though they remained no match for Serp. If anything, it was more difficult to get him not to eat the organs I had to offer.

For the next few days, life was a combination of eating, hunting, shitting, and then sleeping. Back in my previous run, participants used to call it the drag: a long and unimaginative routine that seemed absolutely unnecessary. No one complained too much, though. Having any connection to civilization, even if it was fake, was much better than the standard lifeless trials, which killed off a quarter of the participants.

Now and again, I’d find someone to have beaten me to the prize. Other times, I’d be the one who was first, leaving monster corpses behind.

Several nights, I used the dream necklace in an attempt to have a conversation with the dream goddess of Hypnos, and each time, I’d end up disappointed. Something was going on behind the scenes, causing the deities to keep their distance.

I had to learn more about the rule-breaking and how that connected to the people who had betrayed me. At least now, I knew that Sparta wasn’t among them. What about Pup, though?

“Still haven’t found it?” a guard lieutenant of some sort asked as I went to get my food from the barracks’ kitchen. With supplies getting tighter and tighter, most of the good food was distributed among the nobles and the soldiers.

“Not yet.” I didn’t pay him any mind. He didn’t have the authority to cause me any pain and was too weak to try. “Killed a few worm carriers.” I looked him straight in the eye. “Along with their worms. How many times did you try to take the monster down?”

A sudden silence filled the room. To most, this probably sounded like an accusation, and to a degree, it was. What I really wanted to know was how many infected were out there.

“Enough.” The man didn’t blink.

You were there, weren’t you? I smiled on the inside.

“How many like me were sent?”

“More than I could count.” He frowned. “All of them talked big and ended up worse than dead.”

“Good thing I’m better. At least, I always come back.”

I used the Alchemist card to get a sense of the food, just in case. No one had tried to poison me so far, but I strongly suspected it was a matter of time.

“After two more weeks, it won’t matter.” The lieutenant grabbed his own mug. Even as he drank, his eyes didn’t leave me.

“What happens then?”

“Viceroy Geleen’s armies will cross the mountains. Either he’ll kill the monster and take over this place, or get everyone infected and raze it.”

I was impressed. Someone in a trial-world had a bleak view of things to the point I could almost consider him a participant.

“Why don’t you just leave? Take everything and leave the place to rot. It’s not like much will change.”

“What’s the difference?” He placed his mug on the table. “The beast is blocking the path to the mountain. The Crystal Fields block everything else. The only way is to go through the forest and hope to get somewhere safe.”

He was right about that, though not in the way he thought. There were several monster nests deep in the forest. On one occasion, Rayle had had the great idea of going there in search of treasure caches. Based on a some rumor about ancient ruins, he was convinced that there was something valuable there and had persuaded Avon and the rest. To be honest, I had also gone along with the idea.

The thought of finding a hidden Grand Arcana card was enough to tempt anyone. Ultimately, we hadn’t found any treasure, but the monsters were very real. A hydra must have made a nest somewhere, for the entire area was crawling with its spawn. We had spent several hours fighting the monsters off until Avon had given the retreat order. Even if none of us were seriously hurt, there was no point in continuing the battle we knew we couldn’t win.

The only escape was through the Crystal Fields and for that—

The sound of horns filled the air.

Everyone froze, still listening to the signal. Three horns. From what I remembered from my previous run, that was an overall city emergency.

Organized chaos followed. Everyone left their food half eaten, rushing to the armory to get their gear and weapons.

With no time to waste, I dashed towards the nearest window.

Five of Rings! I strengthened my bones and leaped out.

The moment my feet touched the paved road, I went for the northern gate. The horns had sounded from there, plus it was the direction where most of the noise was coming from: whispers, chatter, the sound of hundreds of boots all running in the same direction.

Maybe we’ll have a fight? Serp asked eagerly. Would be nice for the treats to come to us for once.

I doubted that to be the case, but I couldn’t ignore the possibility. According to what I remembered from my previous run, the monsters of the first wave had tried to break through the walls. Only later did they focus specifically on the gates.

One scary option I could think of was for a worm to have found its way into the city. All it took was one tired guard to miss one of the creatures hidden in a traveler’s bag of pieces of clothing for the dreary life of the local inhabitants to become ten times worse.

“Crossbows!” I recognized Lord Steen’s voice giving the order. “Three rows! Shield bearers in front!”

Was he setting up for an attack? Ignoring any finesse my cards granted me, I used my strength to ram through the crowd towards him.

Normally, it took me fifteen minutes to reach the north gate. Despite the crowd, I managed to get there in just over five.

Hundreds of soldiers had formed tight rows in front of the gate. Additionally, I could see several dozen ready to pull the chains to open them.

“I have urgent news for his lordship,” I shouted.

The lie was obvious, but in the panic, no one cared. Lord Steen turned in my direction, then waved his hand, indicating that I be let through. There was nothing else he could do; he needed me right now.

“What’s going on?” I asked once I reached him. “Reinforcements?”

“Not quite.” He turned toward the gate. “Open the gates!” he yelled.

Without question, soldiers pulled the chains. A creaking sound filled the air, as the massive stone and steel doors cracked open.

All crossbowmen aimed at the open space, ready to shoot at a moment’s notice. Lord Steen must have taught them well. They were far too organized even for a professional army. The secret probably lay in the Trainer card he possessed.

Observer. I concentrated, staring through the opening gates.

It took me less than a second to recognize the people approaching. There were only three of them, or rather, what was left of three. All were covered in wounds, their clothes and remnants of armor torn to shreds. If they were normal people, I’d have said that they were beyond saving.

The goths, I thought.

See? They weren’t that tough, Serp said gleefully.

Shut up! “You sent them to kill it?” I turned to Lord Steen.

“I gave them the same offer I gave you,” he said. “They were faster to follow through.”

You bastard! The man had probably done the same to dozens of groups. Being a participant himself, he knew how important it was to complete the trial first, so he had made use of that, tricking others into charging blindly at the creature even if they were unprepared. The arrogance of the goths had probably made them easy prey. Having the protection of Hypnos and the ability to see future events had probably made Eleonor feel invincible. The one beside her, the new goth leader as it seemed, probably thought even more of himself, for he hadn’t bothered strapping on a single piece of armor.

It was a wonder that they had managed to survive at all. Eleonor was barely in condition to walk. Pup was limping behind her. Only the arrogant piece of shit in front could be said to have gone through the ordeal with relatively few wounds.

“Stop there!” one of the soldiers shouted as they crossed the giant threshold.

A leather satchel was tossed at the group’s feet.

“Check yourselves!”

“They’re fine,” I whispered to the lord.

“The others must see,” he whispered back. “It’s not just about them.”

“You shouldn’t have sent them.” I wanted to kill him right now.

If I let Serp go wild, he probably could kill most of the nearby soldiers. Then what? The trial would continue, and I’d have Harlequin and a few deities on my back for ruining their fun. It wouldn’t be a nice way to continue in the Grand Arcana, but sometimes I felt it would be worth it just to have that single moment of satisfaction.

“They’ll live,” he said with the heartlessness of a participant. “Keep in mind there’s only one monster. You should be worrying who’ll get it first.”
