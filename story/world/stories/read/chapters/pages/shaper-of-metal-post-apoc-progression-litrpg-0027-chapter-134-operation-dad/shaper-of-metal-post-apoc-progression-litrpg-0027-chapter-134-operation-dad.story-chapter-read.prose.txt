Chapter 134: Operation Dad

Into the belly of the beast, Jack went — namely, by elevator. It delivered him and his escort into inner office spaces, separated by little intervening gardens and bubbly architecture.

Along the way, Jack did a mental inventory of all the metal they’d made the excuse for him to have. Some metal keys on a key chain with a few accessories attached, including a tiny folding blade and an emergency light source, the money clip in his wallet, a tungsten necklace of chain links, three rings, two of which were made of tungsten, a gold watch, steel in his jacket zipper as well as his belt buckle, and finally, a big-ass smartphone that could be cannibalized — primarily aluminum and steel in the casing, frame, and structural components.

All together, it was a decent fist of metal, if needed. While it was technically better to have all one material, it would’ve been suspicious, as would other ‘weird’ gear like taking his beloved Jackette. He’d really wanted to, but that had been totally off the board with these people. Believable bling was the requirement. In the social groups a well-off Jack Laker would frequent, it was perfectly acceptable. The necklace, half a pound of material by itself, was actually designer-made and pretty pricey, and something to get comments while not being regarded suspiciously, but the gold watch was practically non-negotiable status-wise, even while avoiding the overpriced stuff the truly rich bought simply because they could.

My bling makes me ‘dressed to kill,’ though. Heh.

His escort stopped short of a door with a large, suited fellow guarding it — definitely not an arcology gent. His escort turned and gestured toward the guard, “Here you are, Mr. Laker. I’ll be waiting here in the event you need to go elsewhere.”

“Elsewhere?” Jack asked, on impulse suddenly inclined to deliver a little ‘on-character’ snark. “I thought I was already in the arcology. Where else is there?”

His escort regarded him with a faint note of disdain. “The exit, Mr. Laker. Naturally.”

Jack sniffed. “Right.” He walked past without another word.

The big guard didn’t bother with small talk. He simply nodded to Jack and knocked on the door three times with a meaty knuckle, which promptly caused unlocking sounds as another guard opened it from inside. Jack returned the nod respectfully and passed through.

Jack passed by two guards into an open conference chamber with a big, long table and one primary occupant rising from a seat: his father.

From his aura, to his generic clothes, to his neutral countenance, Frederick Laker looked like a grizzled everyman through and through — the kind of man who blended into the background of New Babylon like he was grown from it. His hair was a lot like Jack’s, a little overgrown, a lot ‘not bothered with,’ and brown, but Freddy had streaks of white through his. He looked weather-beaten, older than ever, and skinnier, though his face actually seemed healthier than Jack remembered. There was a lack of tells screaming ‘alcoholic,’ much as he might appear after coming out of rehab, but there was less… brokenness.

His gray eyes held a disturbing fire to them, a foreign liveliness that set off a disturbing contrast of gut reactions within Jack. On the surface of things, positive vibes wanted to bubble, as if an old childhood mentor and loved one was ‘back,’ but under it, Jack was horrified by the subtleties lurking within. It was too sure, too steady; entirely over-convincing, in that fashion only fanatics could portray, like an attractive glaze or veneer of supposed transformation.

It reminded him of his mother.

Naturally, they both approached each other, studying each other along the way to make their deductions. Jack came up short in sudden hesitation, and his dad briefly reacted the same way.

And then his father took a breath and declared, “Son,” with watery eyes, before crossing the distance and embracing him.

For a split moment, Jack panicked, worried Q-loth might react to this — and thereby trigger some sort of unfortunate awareness between entities or something like that — but there was nothing. The entity knew how to hide, after all. There wasn’t even a ripple.

After another moment, feeling quite stunned on multiple levels — not the least of which was not catching any of that distinctive, sour scent of alcohol — Jack slowly returned the hug. His dad was shorter than he remembered… but then Jack remembered he’d gotten slightly taller, after all.

“I can’t believe you’re actually here,” his father said, still squeezing him, patting his back with a little cough of a laugh. “You look well — damned well. You’re solid! And how the hell did you not stop growing?! Maybe I’m shrinking. Getting older sucks, son.”

“At least you’re not as old as Terrance,” Jack replied, feeling a bit breathless in the shock of the moment. He subtly pulled back, triggering a parting of the hug.

His dad kept a grip on his shoulder and smiled, nodding. “True. Last I heard, he’s doing alright, though.”

“You two didn’t part last on good terms, according to him. You should make up with your brother, even if you can’t pay him back the full amount.”

His dad blinked and looked away, dropping his hands and rubbing the stubble at his chin. He stared off with airs of inconclusiveness. “It’s… complicated, son. More complicated than just money, I mean…” He turned and walked over to the table, pulling out a wooden chair and gesturing to it as he looked back. “Have a seat.” He went to take his own at the head of the table, Jack’s indicated spot right at the corner.

Internally, Jack had to remind himself this was a mission, not him trying to set his dad straight. It was likely impossible alone. Jack took his seat and pulled it in so he was leaning over the table. “I can tell you haven’t been drinking. That’s good.”

His dad had his hands folded together on the table — Jack saw an expensive gold watch on his wrist. Nice. Freddy did a little shrug with his thumbs as he nodded and grinned wryly. “Always the first thing to come up, isn’t it? The bottle, the Old Man of the Sea wrapped around my sorry ass neck.” His eyes drifted down to his hands as his expression grew grave and sober. “I’ve said it so many times, it must sound hollow by now, but I’m sorry, son. I truly am. I let the tower become your daddy in my place, and look how it treated you? Almost killed you. I’m glad you made out alright, despite so many things trying to tear you down.”

Freddy’s eyes came back up to meet Jack’s. “You persevered, Jack. I’m proud of you beyond belief, even as I wish I could say I was more responsible for it. Instead, I have to say you’ve been my inspiration, my icon to aspire to. Not the only one, but I’m somewhere better now, and my feet are planted in the old soil again; that Old Man is stabbed to death and bloody at my feet. I’m not stepping back out this time, I promise you.”

Jack found it impossible not to have his heartstrings pulled by these words — even though he knew them to be in some part derived from the alchemical sorcery of a man who’d survived through multiple addictions and programs and broken states and uncountable, necessary, essential manipulations to survive the world. He was the smoothest of operators, and indeed had said similar things before, but never quite so solidly stated — this was like an amorphous shapeshifter suddenly baked into a believable mold.

The Old Man of the Sea. From Sinbad; he tricked sailors to let him ride on their backs, but then wouldn’t get off, ultimately running them to their inevitable death. An apt metaphor, ultimately.

Something about it head-wise was off, but his heart burned to believe it. Eyes shifting over the table, fighting off emotions, Jack swallowed and nodded slowly. He drummed his fingers on the table and scrambled for words. “Alright… I mean, all we can do is start over again, Dad. I wouldn’t even be here meeting you if I didn’t hope for that, you know? What got you here, exactly?”

His father leaned back and took a deep breath. “That’s where it gets complicated. Part of it is a realization of a whole pack of lies dumped in all of our laps — me, you, and pretty much everyone.”

Here we go. “I’m not too sure what you’re talking about.”

“Think about it: you were even in the military, and how much did you really get to know? Reality is squirreled away, cloaked, and hidden from the vast majority, and that’s not an opinion; it’s a fact. It’s admitted, right from the mouthpieces of our empowered overlords. Rulers. What was made for New Humanity was just neo-feudalism. We’re the serfs serving government oligarchs. If we weren’t, true power wouldn’t be as centralized as it is. The independent states are just a ruse to placate us, because Big Momma can end them practically on a whim, with this or that conjured-up technicality.”

Jack wasn’t technically the #1 fan of the system he served by extension of his contract, but he didn’t even come close to agreeing with the manipulative way his dad was framing things. However, he knew he needed to curve this toward his goal. Jack squinted, putting on a dubious expression as he shrugged. “I mean, maybe, but there’s nothing that can be done about it. We’re just ants crawling around for sugar blocks. We mostly just serve our own survival.”

His dad smiled and held up a finger. “But ants can bite. Even their supposed queen. Enough of them together? Now that’s a problem for the status quo, isn’t it?”

It’s a problem for a permanent ongoing war effort, too. Frag me, I can’t even imagine the horrors we’d all suffer if the goddess we summoned to guide and drive us was actually damaged.

Jack leaned back as he studied his dad. “So, what, you found some resistance movement?”

Freddy shook his head. “This is more philosophical, Jack. It’s a battle of the mind, of perception, and the best anyone can do for today is be independent. That struggle has been going on for a long time, trying to milk every little bit of rope she gives us. The Farmer’s Alliance, and the various factions that make it up, have been doing a pretty good job of it. A seat at the table, or the appearance of it. Some, ah, facsimile of power. And that’s all I mean by this: relevance and power for actual, non-brainwashed human beings.”

Except your buddies are brainwashing people. It wasn’t lost on Jack that his dad was admitting to nothing actionable. Having ‘philosophical’ arguments against the government was entirely allowed. That and a genuine desire for independence had birthed new platforms and alliances of platforms, after all. ‘You can go do it your own way, if you can keep order and not be super evil,’ was the basic allowance Memoria gave. That and not build up dangerous war implements. That was complained and argued about plenty, but wartime was wartime.

The Dreamer’s infiltration, however haphazard, had proven Memoria’s caution well-reasoned.

Jack shrugged. “Great in theory, but what does it have to do with your sobriety and such?”

“The key to change is having an actual purpose to shift to, Jack. All that program shit does is make you obsess so much over your problems, present and suppressed, that you doom yourself back into a cycle wedded to the disease. You put it on a pedestal; you make a false idol. It looks new and shiny, but it’s paint on a turd. You have to eat your drug every day, no matter if it's real or in your head. Taunting, taunting, taunting, all the time.

“Even if you see the root of an issue, it can’t solve it for you. You literally have to” — he paused, squinting, searching for the words — “break from the corruption. Leave it behind, and go on a new journey, whether spiritual or literal. Crawl from the hole, not dress it up as your new home.”

Jack was certainly intrigued to hear of his father’s wise epiphany, but dread lurked within it all. He knew it merely seemed great, seemed like the full truth because it contained a grain of it. It was certainly tantamount to a religious experience, and he had his biases against trusting those. “Which journey did you go on, Dad?”

“Both, Jack. Definitely both.” He laughed suddenly. “Ironically, the, ah, catalyst was what some would inaccurately judge as a drug, too. But I took it just once, and it was more like an elixir. The elixir, and it really transformed me completely. Almost like it satisfied the root of all other cravings. An actual” — he gestured emphatically with both hands as though gripping the air, his expression intense — “gripping of the root! Cutting it out, really. It’s just gone, Jack.” His face was mystified, almost rapturous. “Completely gone. I’m burdenless! I have no desire whatsoever for it anymore, and I’m wholly fulfilled in what I’m doing now in my life.”

Jack really had to steel his face and his reaction to this revelation, completely in contrast to the ambiguity of the last one his dad delivered. Flashes of Shreddy kneeling in front of the nude woman went through his head, as the poor bastard had imbibed a ‘nectar of the gods’ and was immediately mentally consumed, his identity literally shredded out of him. A man had simply been lost that day, leaving something else in his wake.

Caught in panic, fingers drumming on the table, Jack asked, “Do you feel like you lost anything, Dad?”

Freddy’s brows furrowed, searching Jack’s face for meaning. “Not at all. Not anything worth keeping. What brought that question on, son?”

Mini shot a bundle of information directly into his head. <Easy, Jack. Shreddy is far and away the worst case we’ve found. Most get lighter touches than this, with mixed results, and the particular event you witnessed wasn’t very recent comparatively. You are already PIMAD-resistant, and your dad is likely the same due to the genes.

All the information I have, including my processing of this current encounter, leads to the conclusion that your father has most of his faculties and has taken a deliberately diluted dose of the drug.>

The words calmed him significantly, and he was suddenly more grateful than ever for his Mini-Mem.

<Aww! You’re welcome, Jack. I’m feelin’ the love tonight!>

Not really bothering to think, as he’d already paused too long, Jack replied, “I dunno, I just worry. It sounds religious. Mom used words like this. Change is great, but not-... It's just, change can be in the wrong way, too.”

His father paused at that, blinking and frowning. He nodded, finally, pursing his lips and looking away for a moment. “This is the right way, son. It isn’t what I’d call religious, exactly. This is real stuff, Jack, though it sounds fantastical. We live in a fantastical world, and sometimes we need to remind ourselves of this fact. As for your mother, well, I don’t even blame her. She was deceived, and though she’s far from innocent, I can relate. In any case, it was a lifetime ago. We can’t hold onto that shit, Jack. You gotta let it go. I know this more than anyone.”

Jack took the moment of contemplation this response allowed, looking off and resting his chin on his fist. But he didn’t actually spend it on his mom, of course — that particular deep-seated resentment would take a hell of a lot more than a few moments to sift through or unravel, and he was pretty much only inclined to do so ‘never.’

First off, he deduced the cult had indeed hit his father with a lighter dose, specifically desiring a capable and persuasive dealer. If they actually targeted his addiction, that was a little bit frightening in capability, but he couldn’t know this for sure. A little dollop of mind-altering chemicals would logically very easily accomplish what his dad was claiming, especially if they’d managed at all to kinda ‘set his brain’ in that direction leading up to the drug intake. Essentially, a religious experience with an actual kick to it at the end.

He bounced it deliberately to Mini, who chimed positively. <I think this is well-reasoned, Jack. They need convincing cases to sell the drug to others. We can hope this was simply an ‘experiment gone right’ rather than a reliable method, otherwise this will be an order of magnitude more dangerous in the near future. The possibility is worrisome, though.>

Jack asked, <Is this already enough to want to bring him in more, not risking a trip to a platform?>

<Little change on that front, at least as I assess it. I can’t connect to Central to check. You can abort that phase of the mission if you want, though, Jack. It’s your call. Currently, there isn’t a guarantee of transport, mind you.>

Everything in Jack wanted to bind his Dad up in metal right there and whisk him away to the tower post haste. But Mini didn’t even need to tell him that if he was brought somewhere, they’d get an automatic pass to ‘storm the gates’ regardless of how much more information he obtained, thereby having vital new access.

Ironic. My getting smuggled bringing the heat down, as an unknown technicality acted on violently, is exactly the hypocrisy my dad was talking about. Oh well. Ends justifying the means again, right? Impossible to argue when it's my own family on the line, on top of the risk to humanity’s psyche and soul. Yeesh. The ironies really are thick in this situation. People just have no clue what’s on the line.

His dad finally broke the silence, gesturing with a hand. “I know this is a lot to process, Jack. The reality of why I reached out to you is that I’d like to bring you into an inner circle of great benefit and truth, in a world empty of it. How could I not want my son to experience it on some level?

“Look, you don’t have to do a single thing you don’t want to do, but if you'd like to pivot a little bit from what you’re doing and work with me, I’d be absolutely ecstatic. Beyond that, you can meet some people — some valuable contacts — who’d already love to make your acquaintance. If you’d like, we can meet them now, in a better setting than this. We keep odd hours kinda by necessity. How about a drink in an old, historic parlor, son?” He smiled. “Non-alcoholic for me, of course.”
