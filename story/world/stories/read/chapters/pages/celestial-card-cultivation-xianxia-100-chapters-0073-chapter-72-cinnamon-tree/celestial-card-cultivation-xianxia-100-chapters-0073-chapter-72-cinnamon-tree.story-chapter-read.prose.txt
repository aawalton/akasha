I have to remember to analyze the death rate of cultivators in the Southeast province; something seems strange.

— Diary of Xu Ziqing, Imperial Scribe, Tianqiu Plane – now known as The Origin, Outer Realms

Jianyi felt like the time he had spent watching his mother negotiate with suppliers and customers had paid off.

He had just sold his allotment of cultivation pills to a senior brother in the outer sect, and he thought he had gotten a very good deal.

Whether that was true or not in the end didn’t matter. What mattered was that, together with all his other activities, he now had the contribution points necessary to do what he needed to do. The first stop was the Treasure Hall to buy a simple body cultivation Qi Card.

After his Celestial Core Card had… eaten? Subsumed? His spirit cultivation qi card, Bamboo Shoot Grove, before it could hit his middle dantian, he had not been able to make any progress in his cultivation.

No matter how many times or how long he practiced his Eternal Spear Meditation, no qi accumulated at the bottom of his lower dantian—his Core Card continued to gobble everything up and seemed stuck in the ‘Evolving’ stage.

Celestial Core Card

Unique

Affinity – Core Card

Converts cultivator’s qi into any affinity.Evolving.

Elder Sun’s explanation that the card required a balance of yin and yang, and would therefore not allow him to single-mindedly focus on spirit cultivation, resonated with him. It was also something that deeply interested him anyway, though the reduced cultivation speed might cost him later.

Of course, one didn’t need a qi card to cultivate one’s body, but Jianyi didn’t think that his Core  Card would unlock without it.

That had been the driving force behind his whirlwind contribution point acquisition over the last two days, and what made him walk to the Treasure Hall in the outer sect at that moment.

He had already acquired two cards with contribution points and an essence he had acquired as part of the spoils for one of the raids he and Elder Sun had conducted against the demonic cultists that plagued the Empire and Shiqiu City specifically. The Treasure Hall was a truly impressive place, and Jianyi had lost none of his wonder as he entered for the third time in the six and a half months he had spent in the sect by now.

He was briefly distracted and shook his head at the thought that he was now closer to his seventeenth birthday than to the day of his trial, which had set him on the path he was currently on.

After shaking himself, a smile appeared on his face as he took in the splendor of the Treasure Hall.

On the left side of the almost cave-like room were dozens of glass cases, each holding a dozen cards. They were sorted by type, element, and rarity—Jianyi was sure that if loitering were allowed, most of the outer disciples would be spending the majority of their time in the hall, gawking at the cards and dreaming about how they would help them on their path to ascension.

Alas, it wasn’t.

Not even to look at the wares on the right side of the hall, which were even more varied than the cards opposite. Here, there was a hodgepodge of natural treasures and crafted items—armor, weapons, pills and potions, formations… whatever item had ever appeared in any of the stories that Jianyi had loved so much as a child, was here.

Under each item displayed was a card describing it in beautiful calligraphy.

The Treasure Hall was a treasure itself.

With a deep longing, not just for the items, but for times in which he would have the time and status to be able to browse without purpose, he turned to the left and sought out one of the attendants responsible for the card section of the hall.

A young woman was wiping invisible fingerprints from one of the display cases, each hip-high, made from dark wood and an interior clad in white silk, and he bowed politely to her. “Would you be able to help? I am here to acquire a Qi Card?”

She looked up with a start, either having been lost in thought or deeply concentrated on cleaning the glass. “Of course, disciple, it would be my pleasure,” she said with a warm smile, quickly recovering from her initial surprise. “A group of disciples managed to harvest a bunch of Bamboo Shoot Grove cards recently. I assume you are looking for an uncommon version?”

“Indeed, I am looking for an uncommon card. I would need a body cultivation Qi Card, however.”

“Excuse me?!” she exclaimed, looking at him, eyes wide.

A few other attendants had been interrupted by the young woman’s half-shout and were looking in their direction.

Both Jianyi and the attendant blushed—he really didn’t want any further attention, and certainly not people knowing that he was branching out into body cultivation.

“My apologies, disciple. Those cards are rarely bought, but I do think we have a few—most are directly routed to the battalion on the other side of the mountain, but we always have some for alchemists, etcetera…” she was quietly mumbling in the end, trying to cover for her embarrassment.

She directed him to a display at the very back of the room and, after a moment of hesitation, pointed towards a jade card with the two golden lines that indicated it was uncommon.

Cinnamon Tree Sprout

Qi

Uncommon

Minor increase of passive body cultivation

A very simple card but—hopefully—exactly what Jianyi needed right now.

As much as he prided himself on his discipline, the fact that his own Core Card had taken part of his cultivation progress away from him had hurt and made it a little harder to go through all the different exercises and meditations required of him.

He handed his sect medallion to the attendant. “Very good. I would like that one, please.”

Hesitantly, she took the medallion. “Are you sure? I thought body cultivators get sent to the frontlines right away?” she whispered the question.

“Yes, I am sure, thank you for your concern, though.”

He imagined that rumor among mortal servants related to the test on intake day, but was in no mood to correct her—not that she was completely wrong, anyway.

A few moments later, he left the Treasure Hall, the Cinnamon Tree Sprout card safely stored in his ring.

It was time for his second stop on his spending spree, the Library.

His time helping Miyao research body cultivation arts had paid off—narrowing down the options for him had gone relatively quickly.

The majority of body cultivators these days apparently used affinity-specific cultivation techniques—even the ubiquitous ‘imperial’ technique was available in five variants, one for each element.

That meant a cultivator with the Water affinity would use potions, essences, natural treasures, and any other tool that was based on Water.

From what Jianyi could understand, that was primarily a question of comfort—it was less painful than interacting with other affinities.

That was clearly not a dimension that Jianyi would take into account when making a decision—even if he wasn’t in the fortunate position that, due to his Core Card, the downside of using multiple affinities didn’t apply to him.

Especially, as it seemed quite clear based on his research that techniques that used all five elements were simply stronger.

He had quickly been able to narrow down the choice to three techniques with the help of primarily one book that summarized their characteristics.

Divine Body Strengthening Technique

A technique that truly deserves the ‘divine’ title. To this day, no technique has been developed that provides as smooth a path to ascension for those choosing the path of the body. For each step and each realm—all the way to the Jade Body Realm—a detailed plan of potions and pills is laid out that will provide nearly effortless progression. Divine are also the costs, as only the rarest of ingredients will be used. The perfect path for a Core Disciple of a powerful sect.

Jianyi’s desire to have—just once—a way forward that sounded relatively easy had made this technique very attractive.

Diamond Heart and Spirit Art

This, dear reader, is the pinnacle of cultivation and only for those striving for true perfection. It is not a body cultivation technique. It is not a spirit cultivation technique. It is both. It aims for the almost impossible, refining body and spirit purely with the help of qi. Sophisticated qi circulation patterns, a prodigious use of cultivation pills, and time—lots of time—make this possible. There are convincing stories of cultivators spending decades in closed-door cultivation, stepping through the realms of body and spirit in lockstep.

True, it would probably require him to abandon the Eternal Spear Meditation, but perfection sounded good—and there weren’t that many techniques that aimed for ascension to the highest realm.

Soul-Forged-Body Path

A very interesting technique, as it employs slightly different tools for each cultivation realm. As any decent technique, it uses the appropriate element for each step of each realm. What truly differentiates this ‘path’—as it is slightly pretentiously called—is that its circulation patterns are rather rudimentary; instead, it relies on the cultivator’s will to guide the aspected qi to its destination. Apparently, a rather pragmatic approach for those who have the strength to see it through.

In the end, the choice hadn’t been that hard.

After presenting his thoughts to Elder Sun, his teacher had agreed with his choice: the Soul-Forged-Body Path offered a good trade-off between costs and speed, while still providing a path to the very end.

Sure, if he had all the time in the world, the Diamond Heart and Spirit Art sounded interesting—but time was not something he had in abundance, and if he interpreted the signals correctly, Elder Sun wanted him to accelerate his progression further.

The Divine Body Strengthening Technique still sounded attractive, but he was no core disciple, no son of a noble family. He was the son of a tailor, and even if he had the resources—he suspected that Elder Sun would be open to supporting him in some shape or form—he wouldn’t want to spend wealth on something that could be equally achieved with a bit of effort. He still knew the value of a single silver; spending enough money to buy every mortal’s home in the region just on his sixteen-year-old body would be decadent. Undignified.

To his delight, the young librarian who had helped him with his very first steps on researching techniques was at the counter of the Library.

“Welcome back. Are you the disciple who asked us to prepare the Soul-Forged-Body Path?” she asked with a smile and raised eyebrow.

Jianyi nodded. “It is good to see you again. And, yes, indeed, that was me.”

“You truly are walking your own path, aren’t you? If I recall correctly, you also used the Martial Meridian Art, correct?”

Jianyi smiled, delighted that she remembered. “Yes, I did. It worked very well for me, and I couldn’t have done it if you hadn’t pointed me in the right direction!”

She chuckled. “I am not sure my impact was that large, but I am glad it worked out. Here you are,” she said, handing him the jade slip with the technique.

In turn, Jianyi handed her his medallion, and a few seconds later, the last of his contribution points were gone.

It had been an expensive afternoon, but definitely worth it—at least he hoped so.
