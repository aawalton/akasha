Henry didn’t even want to look behind him as the submerged, glowing moss-covered walls blurred around him. Like a child running down a dark hall, a part of him didn’t want to turn back and see what was coming after him, but he was neither a child, nor could he afford such willful ignorance. Plus, while he had stashed most of his Avatars away, he still had a handful of them around. A couple were in the large chamber that was still not allowing the water to seep away, and a couple he’d already passed on his way up the tunnel. So as he broke out of the water and flew up the path his Skill showed him, he could see the yellow-scaled figure step out of the shimmering gateway and into the cave with them, making his—or her—aura even more palpable.

Maybe it wasn’t exactly at Zerathstra’s level, but every instinct told him this newcomer might still be a little beyond him. Dangerously so.

“Hello… you?” Henry began tentatively through his remaining clone, while his real body flew faster and faster, following the path shown to him by Find the Path. The individual that showed up didn’t look like either Gar’Dak or Pearlescent Mask. He—and Henry had the feeling that this was a he, though that might be the whiskers giving him a false impression—was definitely shorter than the beast master. He seemed a little wispier, but he didn’t feel weaker. Every movement of his was smooth and deliberate, and it almost looked like he hadn’t even registered Henry’s words. Instead, his gaze flicked up, his jade eyes glowing softly in the dark cavern, and right as Henry went to cast Identify through the clone he used to speak, the A-rank disappeared.

Then he reappeared a few feet away from Henry’s real body in a flare of mana, blocking their path toward their divined exit. Instantly, the air congealed around his limbs, bringing Henry to a complete halt as if he’d suddenly been caught in one powerful instance of Telekinesis, but the only thing he could see was a semi-translucent sheen across his skin.

For a beat, Henry was dumbfounded. It felt like a mountain had suddenly trapped his limbs, and in a fraction of a second, multiple deductions flashed into his mind.

Outrunning this Merfolk would be difficult. Short Blink might not be enough. He needed to get a feel for his furthest Avatars, and he’d have to pray this A-rank didn’t also carry a teleport-blocking device on him.

“You’re the kraken that attacked my city.”

The words were calm. Conversational. He didn’t even have that reedy timber to his voice. But still, Henry’s alarm only grew sharper, and so did his annoyance.

“Your people kidnapped my friend, and I retrieved him. Bit of a stretch to call that an attack on your city,” he retorted, and in the same breath, he spoke to Maurice. “[I don’t like this. In case things go south, I need to make sure you’re safe.]”

“[Maw?]” Maurice asked, and Henry confirmed. “[Maw.]”

That way, if something were to happen to Henry’s main body, he could always recover from one of the many clones he had tucked away in Thalis and its surroundings, and Maurice would be safe. So without any further discussion, Henry activated Maw and for the barest moment, he felt the individual in front of him try to swat the skill nexus away, but he’d had enough practice with Arisia for the Merfolk’s attempt to fail. A beat later, Maurice was gone, and Henry found himself face to face with this new individual, and now that they were using skills on each other more openly, he attempted another Identify. The first attempt didn’t work. The second, he fed some Predation as Unfettered Echoes was running low, and that gave it enough power to get a more accurate reading of his opponent.

What Henry got as a result made him sweat a little more.

[Merfolk (A) - Lvl 71]

That’s… not good, he thought, immediately putting more effort in connecting to his clones on the surface, but the rocks of the caves were making that a challenge. It was as if he was trying to hear what a couple people were whispering two rooms away, but he was slowly narrowing his senses on his targets. He just needed a few more seconds, then he could switch places with a surface clone and make a run for it. Henry pulled on his mana, warily eyeing the Merfolk as the latter stepped closer to him, and right as he was about to activate his switch—

His Avatars were gone. Hearts hammering in his chest, Henry tried to make sense of why he’d been cut off from his target, only to realize a beat later that, in fact, he’d lost all contact with his clones. Even the ones he’d left below in the chamber. Even the one he knew was only a few yards ahead of him. He knew for a fact it was there because he could see it, as he could peer through his own Invisibility, but he couldn’t sense it through the skill that had created it. He couldn’t sense any of them. They were completely cut off from him.

As if they were completely out of range.

“I might be busy, but I’ve read the reports and I’m aware of your… mobility. I’m also aware of how certain krakens can create copies of themselves. The Trickster Kraken, I believe. Which means, you somehow fused that ability with a short-range teleport, I’m guessing? Well. Considering my countermeasure is working, I’d say I’m right.”

Henry had been leery of potentially wasting his mana on trying to break through the restraint. But considering his clone-swapping skill was nullified, he might need to rethink that. As for the Merfolk in front of him…

“I’m on a mission for the System,” Henry began, attempting one last argument while simultaneously considering his escape. He might not have many options aside from a series of Blinks and Dashes until he was out of range of whatever the Merfolk was using to restrict him. “But your people keep trying to interrupt and block me. What is it about ‘I’m not interested’ that they are not getting?”

The Merfolk shrugged, his pale yellow scales reflecting the purple and blue moss that surrounded them. “You just had to accept the invitation and we could have discussed it. But instead, you murdered one of my Guardians. Which you will be compensating me for,” he said, matter of fact. Then a smile tugged on his lips. “As for this… claim that the System is making you do its bidding? Well. Even more reason to chat and keep an eye on you, isn’t it?”

Henry eyed the cold, jade eyes meeting his own. Somehow, this Merfolk was rubbing him the wrong way even worse than the one they’d beaten. He reminded Henry too much of slimy salesmen and loan sharks. How easy and friendly they were before you signed the dotted line, and how utterly ruthless and nasty they’d become once they got their hooks in you.

“I’ll pass,” Henry said. Then with a flare of Predation-empowered Dispelling Pulse, he broke the congealed film blocking his movement and Blinked past the Merfolk, which immediately lit up a spark of hope, only for it to get immediately dashed as the A-rank appeared right next to him, his hand blurring in a chopping motion right as he was about to teleport forward once more.

Henry reappeared a couple dozen yards higher, with two arms missing. He still couldn’t sense his clones and off-handedly, he tasked two Octominds with forcing a connection through while he fed Flight some Predation-essence and rushed up the tunnels.

The Merfolk kept up with him.

“This is good,” the yellow-scaled A-rank said. “Tiring yourself will make things easier.”

The Merfolk whipped his fluked tail out, and Henry, even though he was sure he had dodged, was sent careening into the wall for a moment before he course-corrected. But still, as he Blinked and flew as fast as he could, he was glad he’d put Maurice in his Maw.

At least he was the only one in danger. Relatively, that was.

Henry kept following the direction shown to him by Find the Path, healing himself whenever his flesh was burnt with the Merfolk’s bright yellow rays, stabbed, or mushed against the impossibly hard stone walls. Like a spectator within his own body, he didn’t focus on the abuse the Merfolk was inflicting on him. He didn’t focus on the fact that the A-rank was toying with him. He just let the goading and taunts glide right off of him. One day, he’d pay the Merfolk back, but for now, even if it ended up costing his life, he was getting out of here.

Henry kept doggedly following the path and by now, the A-rank wasn’t pulling his punches as much. But aside from the occasional stab and cut, the Merfolk wasn’t having that much success slowing Henry down, especially when Henry could just Blink out of it as soon as he saw the mana around him move. Not to mention, Henry still had plenty of Lifeweaving conceptual essence on hand, so healing whatever was inflicted upon him was easy enough.

Mana was going to be an issue soon, though.

In the end, the Merfolk didn’t seem to have whatever it was Pearlescent Mask had used to block his teleportation, but it wasn’t as if that was slowing the monster down. He could keep up with Henry just fine. In fact—

Henry stilled as his form was splattered against the stone, but only for a beat before he recovered and kept running, tuning out the shouts of the Merfolk as he reviewed what his Octominds had just sensed, hearts hammering in his chest, but not out of fear or exhaustion, but … cautious excitement.

They’d been trying to feel out the nearby Avatars so hard and so intently, even attempting to use Find the Path and a dash of Unfettered Echoes to feel past whatever the Merfolk had been using, that somehow, their senses had picked up something else, and now that they caught the scent, they narrowed their attention on its source, which must have caused the clones in question to react, as vaguely, as if he was hearing a whisper from across the world, Henry heard the familiar voice as if it was seeping out of a dream.

“[What’s wrong, mini-Henry? You’re not dying on me too, are you?]”
