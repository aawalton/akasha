Kelia checked on Avis after they were all changed into the cleanest hides they could fine. Even the most blood free were far from what Caleb would call clean. Whatever fanaticism had overtaken these people had also precluded basic behaviors like proper hygiene. Caleb decided to keep his lorica on, placing heavy cloaks over himself to disguise the armor. Mettius kept his pauldrons on, hiding them under a cloak, and kept his hands under the fur cloak to hide the expensive metal. Kelia had the most thorough disguise, having found the woman Dark Archer she’d slain to have been roughly the same size, and the arrow wounds left far less blood than the hammer blows to the head.

Avis was fine to Caleb’s surprise, though a new section of her feathers had been replaced with the ice blue ones. She was sent up above to scout ahead once more while Bog was instructed to wait in the forest just outside of the village for Caleb’s signal.

The gate stood a few hundred meters apart from the forested mountain valley they travelled through. Caleb felt his height constrict in anxiety as they walked out into the open. Archers lined the wooden palisade scanning the trees while a small number near the gate focused a moat had recently been dug around the wall, though it was only a meter deep. The presence of shovels and pickaxes suggested the work was ongoing.

The wall spanned the whole valley, reaching from the cliff face of one mountain to the face of the other across the valley.

At the gate, the archers above stood expectantly until Kelia gave the pass phrase, after which came the mechanical sound of gears rolling against each other before the gate began to descent. For theme to walk across. Caleb extended his senses to the equipment and easily located the mechanism that controlled the draw bridge.

They passed through with no further questions, the guards having turned their attention full to the tree line once the pass phrase had been given.

“That was way easier than I anticipated,” Mettius said when they were away from others.

“Do you think it’s a trap?” Caleb asked.

“No, I think that some side effect of whatever gave them these classes may have made them less autonomous. Like they are following their instructions to the letter and not to the spirit. They seemed fixated on the tree line and looked away as soon as we gave the pass phrase.”

The town behind the wall was busy with activity though not as much as Caleb would expect. There were workers at work in every other building, but it ought to have been every building. A woodcarver’s bench stood vacant next door to both a baker and blacksmith who were at work. On the pattern continued as they walked through the village with hoods up. The raised hoods were not a suspicious look as everyone wore a hood or some sort of hat against the mountain chill.

They followed Magnus’s directions to the eastern mine entrance. The industry of Frost Valley lay primarily in mining. The mountains they stood between with rich with cold aspected iron ore and they sold it through Caleb’s village. Even if Caleb didn’t know, he would have realized this was a mining town by the sheer amount of metal present. Everywhere he went he sensed steel. Hinges, pots, buckets, nails in the buildings. Iron was common back home, but they did what they could to use cheaper materials when they worked well enough. Not here.

He was tempted to use his magic but restrained himself.

Around the mine entrance stood massive piles of gathered ore, deep beneath Caleb saw signs of bins that he knew to be there from his past visit. Whatever was happening to these villagers and their initiative hadn’t hindered their mining operations it seemed. Even as they watched men and women were hard at work pushing cart loads of ore up to the pile and dumping it before turning back into the mine.

The miners had no specific uniform, the only thing marking them as such was the grime and dim expressions that covered their faces. The trio moved to a place out of sight of the guards and began to dirty their already unclean clothes. Caleb doffed his lorica and threw it into a mine cart while Mettius did the same with his armor-wincing as he took it off.

Caleb put turned to his Magical Perception and saw that threads of essence connected the armor still to Mettius.

Once they’d disguised themselves as best as they could, they threw their weapons in the card, covered it with a tarp and threw some dirt on top and then pushed the cart down the tracks to the gate.

They walked in past the guards and received hardly a second glance and headed into the dark maw of the mine entrance.

They quickly realized they had a problem. They’d brought no light sources and the workers who’d been coming and going hadn’t carried any either.

“Let’s put our equipment back on,” Caleb said. “We should hear anyone coming and I can follow the cart tracks through my affinity. There has to be torches or lanterns in here somewhere. Even if they can all see in the dark now they couldn’t always do that.”

Caleb used his Imperium to don his armor as he had the first time he’d worn it, and lifted Mettius’s gear up out of the cart for him to don as well.

They left the cart, and Caleb took the lead, following the directions out Magnus had given him, only in reverse. In his escape, Magnus had simply followed the incline up but had passed many branching tunnels going down. He’d made a mental note of each path and Caleb and Mettius had both made a point to memorize it as he spoke.

Caleb felt the metal all around him in the iron mine as he walked. There was the rails, discarded tools, and ore all around him. Walking in the darkness deprived of his sight, he focused fully on his Magical Perception, trying to paint as full of a picture as he could. The air was still in the cavern making his senses foggy, but he realized it took less than a thought to fix that.

He used his Imperium Ventorum and pushed the air in front of him as he walked. This had a chain reaction that caused all the air in the passage ahead to move.

“Whoa,” Kelia said, as the tunnel’s contours came into focus for her as well.

Mettius didn’t question it, having understood the implications of the wind. Caleb could now in blurry detail make out the cavern ahead.

They continued on, leaving their cart behind at the first turn and venturing down the path Magnus had described. Caleb felt the going was still slow as his feet caught on the uneven stone floor even as his hands groped at the wall ahead. Eventually they heard sounds up ahead and now instead of pushing the air ahead, Caleb drew it towards him, bringing the sound it carried with it.

“I think I hear crying,” Caleb said, having the highest perception of the group. Mettius touched his pauldrons, and Caleb sensed something change in the deep web of magic within them.

“I hear it too. Definitely crying,” he whispered.

They moved on slower until Caleb sensed more metal on the edge of his perception, though he couldn’t make out what. He suspected it to be the weapons of the guards, but it could very well have been a barrel of pick axes. Each piece of metal appeared to his perception as a sphere representing the weight of the metal, magical items making it hard to know sizes since they appeared much heavier.

He signaled a stop.

I need to see, he thought.

He pulled out his hammer and examined it with his perception. He knew every detail of this weapon and could sense those details if he was touching it, but still it appeared a sphere to him if he lost connected. He held it out in the air in front of him with his Imperium and probed it with his senses, trying to trace the outline he knew so well. He felt it with his hand, rubbing the hammer to try to outline the hammer to his Magical Perception and then tried to do the same with his mind but to no avail. There was no connection in his mind between what he felt and what he sensed. Much like the mana he saw didn’t impair his actual vision despite appearing over his sight, so too was his physical and magical senses detached.

Then he knew what to do.

Still lifting the hammer with his Imperium, he probed it, applying pressure to the metal from all sides as if he was squeezing it. As soon as he applied the lighted amount of pressure, the shape of the hammer crystalized in his mind. He couldn’t make out the intricate details of the grain structure and fine flows in the metal as he could while touching it but he saw the shape.

Magical Perception has increased to level 7.

He held in a shout of celebration, but still let out a satisfied grunt.

Applying the same light pressure to the metal up ahead, he sensed two short swords, two knives, and a dozen buckles and other sundry metal accessories.

“Two guards up ahead,” Caleb whispered. “I’ll take them alone.”
