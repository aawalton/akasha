Elena’s death hovered inches away, and gods above and below she was going to die next to two absolute idiots.

The redheaded thief pressed herself against the moldy stone wall, the rusty tang of old blood hanging in the air as spikes jutted from the floor where she’d been standing heartbeats before. She shot Gav a withering glare that could’ve curdled milk. The telepath had the decency to look sheepish as he helped Evie navigate around the deadly trap he’d triggered.

“‘I have a sixth sense for danger,’” Elena mimicked, her voice dropping to a mocking baritone. “‘Trust me, I’ll know if there are any traps.’ Remind me again how that’s working out for us?”

Gav’s jaw tightened. “One mistake—”

“Three,” Elena corrected, holding up fingers in rapid succession. “The poison darts, the collapsing floor tiles, and now this. We’re not even ten minutes into this godforsaken tomb, and you’ve nearly killed me. Repeatedly.”

“That’s not a fair assess—” the well-dressed lycanthrope started.

“Repeatedly,” Elena enunciated.

She turned her attention back to the room they’d been sequestered to. Moonlight filtered through cracks in the ceiling, casting silver ribbons across the chamber’s elaborate stonework. Ancient carvings of beasts and men locked in eternal combat adorned the walls, their eyes seeming to follow the intruders with hollow malice.

Gods, it was gaudy.

“To be fair,” Evie chimed in, her voice unnervingly cheerful for someone dodging death traps, “the floor tiles weren’t entirely his fault. Those were very cleverly disguised.” She beamed at Gav with a smile bright enough to light the gloom, completely oblivious to Elena’s eye roll.

Elena sighed, uncoiling her whip-dagger from her belt. The weapon’s segmented blade gleamed in the dim light, a reassuring weight in her palm. “Let me take point. I’ve been doing this since before either of you learned to tie your boot laces.”

“What Elena means,” Evie translated, patting Gav’s arm sympathetically, “is that she’s worried about us and expresses it through biting sarcasm and thinly veiled threats.”

Elena snorted. “No, what Elena means is that I’d like to make it back to Cade and the others without being skewered, poisoned, or crushed.” She moved forward cautiously, tapping the floor with the hilt of her dagger before each step. “And I especially don’t want to explain to Nora why I let her precious ward get flattened by a boulder.”

Gav pulled at the collar of his expensive silk shirt, now damp with sweat and grime. The garment had probably cost more than most people made in a month, and now it looked like something fished from a gutter. His normally perfect hair hung in disheveled strands across his forehead. “I just think we should approach this as a team.”

“We are a team,” Elena replied without looking back. “I lead, you follow, and we all stay alive. Perfect teamwork.”

“Ooh, look at this!” Evie darted past Elena, ignoring her hissed warning, to examine something gleaming on the far wall. The siren’s movements were graceful, almost dancing, as she navigated the chamber with seemingly careless ease. Yet somehow, she avoided every trigger plate Elena had carefully identified.

“Evie!” Elena’s voice cracked with panic. “Get back here!”

Too late. Evie had reached a circular panel embedded in the wall, her fingers already tracing the intricate designs etched into its surface. The metallic disc began to glow with an eerie blue light, responding to her touch.

Gav surged forward, but Elena grabbed his arm. “Wait,” she whispered, her eyes tracking the floor. “There are pressure plates everywhere. One wrong step and—”

A low rumble interrupted her, and the room shuddered. Elena cursed as sections of the wall began to slide apart, revealing a hidden passage. But more alarmingly, the doorway they’d entered was sealed shut with a definitive boom that echoed through the chamber.

“Well,” Evie said brightly, turning to face them with an apologetic shrug, “at least we know which way to go now.”

Elena pinched the bridge of her nose. “This is exactly why I work alone.”

“Except you don’t,” Gav pointed out, carefully picking his way toward Evie. “You work with Cade. And Jer. And Orro. And—”

“They’re different,” Elena snapped.

“How?” Gav challenged, an edge creeping into his voice. “Because you’ve known them longer? Or because they don’t make you uncomfortable with abilities you don’t understand?”

Elena’s eyes narrowed to dangerous slits. “Watch yourself, wolf boy.”

“Both of you, stop,” Evie interjected, her normally bubbly demeanor momentarily subdued. “Listen.”

From the newly revealed passage came a sound—a dry, rattling wheeze, like parchment scraping against stone. The hair on Elena’s arms stood on end as a figure emerged from the shadows.

It moved with jerky, unnatural motions, its body a patchwork of desiccated flesh and exposed bone. Tattered robes hung from its frame, adorned with faded symbols that matched those carved into the walls. But most disturbing was its face—or what remained of it. Where eyes should have been were empty sockets filled with a ghostly blue light, matching the glow from the disc Evie had touched.

“Oh shit,” Gav muttered, instinctively stepping in front of Evie.

The creature raised a skeletal hand, pointing at them with grotesque deliberation. Elena wished he’d hurry the fuck along.

“Seekers of the forbidden,” it rasped, its voice a chorus of discordant whispers. “To proceed, you must prove your worth. Answer my riddle, or join me in eternal vigilance.”

Elena glanced at Gav, who looked as though he’d rather face a pit of vipers. “Let me guess,” she said dryly. “You hate riddles too?”

“You have no idea,” he confirmed through gritted teeth.

The undead guardian tilted its head at an impossible angle, the blue light in its empty sockets flaring. “Three lives have I. Gentle enough to soothe the skin, light enough to caress the sky, hard enough to crack rocks. What am I?”

The chamber fell silent save for the distant drip of an unidentified liquid somewhere in the darkness.

Charming.

“Well, that’s just great,” Elena muttered, mind racing through possibilities. “Gav, any telepathic insights from our bony friend?”

Gav closed his eyes, concentrating. After a moment, he shook his head. “Nothing. It’s like trying to read static.”

The guardian made a sound like crumbling stone—possibly laughter. “Your mind-gifts are useless here, shifter. Only wisdom will save you now.”

Evie had gone very still, her head tilted slightly as if listening to something beyond human perception. Her eyes, normally bright with mischief, had taken on a distant, unfocused quality that Elena recognized all too well.

“She’s hearing the stars,” Elena whispered to Gav. “Don’t interrupt her with all of this.” She gestured at all of him.

“You just pointed at all of me,” Gav whispered, offense clearly written across his furrowed brow.

“Exactly. Now, shut it.”

Gav nodded slowly, though his fists clenched and relaxed slowly by his sides. Elena couldn’t blame him; she’d had the same reaction the first time she’d witnessed Evie’s strange communion with forces beyond mortal understanding.

The silence stretched, becoming oppressive. Then, without warning, a grinding noise filled the chamber as sections of the ceiling began to lower, revealing rows of wicked ice spikes.

“I think we’re on a timer,” Gav said unnecessarily, eyeing the descending death trap.

Elena uncoiled her whip-dagger fully, the segmented blade extending with a metallic hiss. “Maybe we can break those,” she suggested, though doubt colored her voice.

“You’d need a battering ram,” Gav replied, his eyes darting around the room, searching for an escape.

The ceiling continued its inexorable descent, the temperature in the chamber plummeting as the ice spikes drew closer. Elena’s breath frosted in the air, her fingers numbing around the hilt of her weapon.

And then Evie began to sing.

Her voice filled the chamber, ethereal and haunting, weaving a melody that seemed to dance with the shadows. The words were ancient, in a language Elena didn’t recognize, yet somehow their meaning resonated in her bones.

“From heavens high to oceans deep,

Three forms the answer takes to keep.

Gentle touch upon the skin,

Vapor rising light and thin.

Solid force to break the stone,

Water’s wisdom stands alone.”

The guardian’s eerie blue gaze fixed on Evie, its skeletal head tilting in consideration as her song echoed through the chamber. The descending ceiling slowed but didn’t stop.

Evie’s song ended, and she blinked, her eyes refocusing on the present. “Water,” she said simply, her voice returning to its normal cadence. “The answer is water.”

For one heart-stopping moment, nothing happened. The ice spikes hovered mere feet above them, close enough that Elena could see her distorted reflection in their crystalline surfaces.

Then the guardian raised its bony hand again, and the ceiling halted.

“Wisdom indeed,” it rasped, the blue light in its eye sockets dimming slightly. “You may proceed.”

A section of the floor slid open, revealing a staircase spiraling down into darkness.

Elena released a breath she hadn’t realized she’d been holding, lowering her weapon. “Nice work, songbird,” she said, genuine respect coloring her tone, which surprised her more than her two companions.

Evie beamed, the tension of the moment already forgotten. “The stars have all the answers, if you know how to listen.” She turned to Gav, batting her eyelashes playfully. “Were you impressed by my hidden depths of wisdom?”

Gav laughed, the sound slightly strained but sincere. “Definitely adding ‘riddlemaster’ to your list of charms.”

Elena watched their interaction with a mixture of amusement and something else—something that felt uncomfortably like envy. The easy camaraderie between them, even in the face of death, reminded her of simpler times with Jer and Cade, before gods and tournaments and world-ending stakes.

“If you two are done making children with those eyes of yours,” she said, moving toward the staircase, “I’d like to get moving before our bony friend changes his mind.”

The guardian had retreated to the shadows, watching them with its eerie gaze. As they approached the staircase, it spoke again.

“Beware what lies below,” the undead warned, his voice like wind through a graveyard. “Not all challenges can be solved with cleverness alone.”

“Noted,” Elena replied dryly. “Any other cryptic advice you’d like to share?”

The guardian’s response was unexpected—a sound like breaking glass that might have been laughter. “The thief with a conscience, the wolf who fears his own teeth, and the siren who hears too much. An interesting trio to brave these depths.”

Elena froze. “How do you—”

“Oh, we knows many things, Elena O’Keefe,” the guardian interrupted. “Even gods whisper secrets to the eternal dark. And we heard your whispers too, daughter of Darkreach.” It gestured toward the staircase. “Go now. Your companions await your success... or your failure.”

With those unsettling words hanging in the air, the guardian dissolved into wisps of blue light, leaving them alone with the descending staircase and a growing sense of unease.

“Well,” Gav said after a moment, “that wasn’t creepy at all. What’s Darkreach?”

Elena shook her head, trying to dislodge the chill that had nothing to do with the ice spikes still suspended above them. “Let’s go. And stay close this time.” She shot a pointed look at Evie, who responded with an innocent smile.

“I promise to be the very picture of caution,” Evie said, already peering eagerly down the dark staircase.

“Somehow, I don’t find that reassuring,” Elena muttered, taking the first step down.

The staircase spiraled deeper than seemed possible, the walls changing from worked stone to natural rock as they descended. The scents of earth and something else—something old and strange that tickled at the back of Elena’s throat—wafted up the passage. Their only light came from a small magical torch Gav had produced from his pack, casting dancing shadows that played tricks on Elena’s eyes.

“Does anyone else feel like we’re being watched?” Gav asked, his voice hushed.

Elena had been ignoring that exact sensation for the past several minutes. “Probably just paranoia,” she said, though her hand remained on her whip-dagger. “This place is probably designed to mess with your head.”

“Or,” Evie suggested cheerfully, “it could be the spirits of all the failed adventurers who came before us, doomed to wander these halls for eternity, envious of our living breath.”

Gav and Elena both stared at her.

“What?” Evie shrugged. “Just considering all possibilities.”

“Has anyone ever told you that you’re terrifying sometimes?” Gav asked.

Evie tilted her head thoughtfully. “Nora says I have an ‘unsettlingly sunny perspective on the macabre,’ if that counts.”

“It counts,” Elena confirmed bluntly, turning back to the stairs. “It definitely—”

She stopped abruptly as the staircase ended, opening onto a vast underground chamber.

Unlike the tomb-like rooms above, this space appeared to be natural—a massive cavern with a ceiling that disappeared into darkness. A subterranean lake stretched before them, its black surface perfectly still, reflecting the glow from luminescent fungi that clung to the walls and ceiling like stars in a midnight sky.

And in the center of the lake, on a small island of stone, stood a pedestal. Atop it rested a crystal orb that pulsed with the same eerie blue light as the guardian’s eyes.

“I’m going to go out on a limb and say we need that,” Gav said quietly.

Elena sighed but nodded, scanning the chamber for threats. “The question is, how do we get to it?”

The lake’s edge was a clean cut of stone, descending sharply into water so dark it might as well have been an abyss. No bridge or path was visible connecting the shore to the island.

“I could swim across,” Evie offered. “Me and the other sirens learned to swim as a part of our training. ”

Elena shook her head firmly. “Absolutely not. We have no idea what’s in that water. Besides, I just thought that was a stereotype.”

Evie giggled. “It is, but a few of our order, before Destiny took us in, totally lured people while they were on vacation one time. It’s actually a funny story—”

“That water is unnatural,” Gav cut in, his nose wrinkling. “I can smell it from here. There’s something... wrong about it.”

Elena crouched at the edge, studying the surface. It was unnervingly still—too still for natural water. When she tentatively extended her whip-dagger to touch it, the liquid didn’t ripple as expected. Instead, it seemed to grab at the blade, pulling with surprising force.

She jerked the weapon back, a chill running down her spine. “Definitely not swimming.”

Evie had wandered off again, examining the cavern walls. “Look at this,” she called, pointing to a series of carvings near the lake’s edge. “It’s the same symbols that were on the guardian’s robes.”

Elena and Gav joined her, studying the ancient markings. They depicted humanoid figures standing at the edge of what appeared to be the very lake before them, their arms raised toward the island.

“This one looks like they’re... singing?” Gav suggested, tracing a figure with outstretched arms and what might have been musical notes surrounding its head.

Evie’s eyes lit up. “Oh! Maybe it’s a resonance test!”

Elena raised an eyebrow. “A what now?”

“A resonance test,” Evie repeated excitedly. “It’s an old siren trick. Certain frequencies can affect physical matter—create pathways, bridge gaps, that sort of thing.” She gestured at the carvings. “These symbols are showing the correct tonal pattern to solidify the water’s surface.”

Gav looked impressed. “So… you can sing us a bridge?”

“In theory,” Evie confirmed, though her confidence faltered slightly. “I’ve never actually done it myself. It’s more of a theoretical knowledge thing. Me and Nor-Nor kind of left before I learned how to do it. But!” She raised a hand toward the cavern’s ceiling. “I watched my sisters do it one time! How hard can it be?”

“Great,” Elena muttered. “She watched it one time.”

But as she looked at the impossible distance to the island, Elena knew they had limited options. “Alright, songbird. Show us what you can do.”

Evie nodded, taking a deep breath.

Then another.

And another.

“Should we—” Gav started.

“Shhh!” Elena hissed.

The siren closed her eyes, centering herself, then began to hum—a low, resonant tone that seemed to vibrate in Elena’s chest. The hum grew, transforming into a melodic sequence that echoed throughout the cavern, bouncing off the walls and returning changed, as if the chamber itself was joining her song.

The still surface of the lake began to respond, ripples forming not from physical disturbance but from the sound itself. The ripples converged, creating patterns that matched the vibrations of Evie’s voice.

Gav grabbed Elena’s arm, pointing. “Look!”

Where Evie’s song was strongest, the water was changing—becoming solid, crystalline, forming a narrow pathway that extended from the shore toward the island.

“It’s working,” Elena breathed, genuine awe in her voice.

Evie continued singing, sweat beading on her forehead from the effort of maintaining the precise tones. The crystal bridge grew, inch by inch, reaching toward the distant pedestal.

But then something shifted. A note faltered, and Evie winced, her rhythm momentarily disrupted. The partially formed bridge trembled, sections beginning to dissolve back into liquid.

“Keep going!” Elena urged, stepping closer.

Evie recovered, picking up the melody again, but the damage was done. The bridge was forming unevenly now, sections solidifying while others remained dangerously fluid.

“I can’t—” Evie gasped between notes, her voice strained. “Can’t maintain it... all the way across.”

Elena made a split-second decision. “You don’t have to. Just hold what you’ve got.”

Before either of them could react, Elena took a running leap onto the crystallized path. It held her weight—barely. She could feel it flexing beneath her feet, threatening to revert to liquid at any moment.

“Elena!” Gav shouted, starting forward.

“Stay back!” Elena commanded, inching forward cautiously. “The bridge won’t hold us all. Keep her singing, wolf boy.”

Gav hesitated, then nodded, turning back to Evie, whose face was contorted with effort as she maintained the song.

Elena moved as quickly as she dared across the treacherous surface. Beneath the crystallized layer, she could see movement in the dark water—shadowy forms circling, waiting for her to fall. The bridge continued to deteriorate behind her, leaving no possibility of retreat.

Halfway to the island, the bridge ahead began to fail as well, melting back into the hungry water. Elena increased her pace, knowing she was running out of options. Twenty feet from the island, only scattered patches of solid surface remained.

“Fuck it,” she muttered, and broke into a full sprint.

She leaped from one solid patch to another, the segments crumbling beneath her feet the instant she moved on. The final gap was too wide—at least ten feet of open water between her and the island’s edge.

Elena uncoiled her whip-dagger in mid-run, lashing it toward a rocky outcropping on the island. The segmented blade wrapped securely around the stone, and she used her momentum to swing across the gap, landing in an ungraceful heap on the island’s shore.

From across the lake, she heard Gav’s whoop of triumph, followed by Evie’s exhausted but delighted laughter as her song finally ended.

Elena pushed herself to her feet, retrieving her weapon with a flick of her wrist. The pedestal stood just yards away, the crystal orb pulsing with hypnotic blue light. She approached cautiously, scanning for traps.

Finding none, she reached for the orb—then hesitated, years of thieving experience screaming warnings in her head. This had been too easy.

“Wait!” Evie called from across the water, her voice hoarse from singing. “Elena, there’s something wrong with the—”

Too late. The moment Elena’s fingers touched the crystal, the cavern shuddered. The island beneath her feet began to sink, water rushing in from all sides.

“Shit!” Elena grabbed the orb, tucking it into her belt pouch as she scrambled for higher ground. But there was nowhere to go—the island was disappearing rapidly beneath the lake’s surface.

Across the water, Gav had stripped off his expensive shirt, preparing to dive in despite the unknown dangers lurking below.

“No!” Elena shouted. “Get out of here! Take Evie and—”

Her words were cut short as the water reached her knees, then her waist, rising with unnatural speed. The shadowy forms she’d glimpsed earlier were converging now, drawn by the disturbance.

Elena drew her backup dagger, preparing to fight whatever emerged, when Evie’s voice cut through her panic—not singing this time, but speaking with eerie authority.

“Daughter of water, child of stone, hear my command: part.”

The lake’s surface rippled, then—impossibly—split down the middle, creating a dry pathway from the island to the shore. The water reared up on either side like walls, the shadowy creatures within thrashing against an invisible barrier.

Elena didn’t question the miracle.

She ran, sprinting down the exposed lakebed as water towered on either side. Behind her, she could hear the strain in Evie’s voice as she maintained whatever power she’d called upon.

“Hurry,” Gav urged as she neared the shore, his eyes fixed on the trembling walls of water. “She can’t hold it much longer.”

Elena lunged the final distance, Gav’s strong hands catching her arms and pulling her to safety just as Evie’s control faltered. The walls of water crashed together with devastating force, sending a wave washing over the shore that soaked them to the skin.

For a moment, they all stood in stunned silence, dripping and breathing hard.

Then Evie collapsed, Gav barely catching her before she hit the ground. Blood streamed down the siren’s nostrils and ears, staining her hair and tunic.

“Evie!” Elena was at her side in an instant, pushing damp hair from the siren’s too-pale face. “What the hell was that?”

Evie’s eyes fluttered open, her smile weak but present. “Just… felt right. No big deal.”

“No big deal?” Gav repeated incredulously. “You parted a lake!”

“A small lake,” Evie corrected, attempting to sit up before thinking better of it. “Very small, really. Hardly counts.”

Elena shook her head, a strange mixture of exasperation and newfound respect swelling in her chest. “You’re something else, water girl.” She patted her belt pouch, confirming the orb was still there. “We got what we came for. Let’s get out of this death trap.”

Gav lifted Evie easily, cradling her against his chest. “Next time,” he said to Elena, his earlier antagonism replaced with wry camaraderie, “you can take point from the beginning.”

Elena snorted, leading the way back toward the stairs. “Oh, now you’re willing to follow. Typical.”

But there was no real bite to her words.

As they climbed, Elena found herself revaluating her judgments. She’d written Gav off as a pampered telepath playing at being a thief, and Evie as a flighty songstress too naive for their dangerous world. Today had proven her wrong on both counts.

“Hey,” she said abruptly, pausing on the stairs. “You two didn’t do half bad down there.”

Gav raised an eyebrow. “Was that almost a compliment? From Elena the Impenetrable?”

“Don’t push it, wolf boy.”

Evie, still nestled in Gav’s arms, beamed up at Elena. “Does this mean we’re friends now? Because I have friendship bracelets I’ve been saving for just the right moment.”

Elena rolled her eyes, but couldn’t quite suppress her smile. “Let’s not get ahead of ourselves.”

They continued their ascent, battered and soaked but successful. Elena led with her whip-dagger ready, but her posture had subtly changed—no longer keeping herself separate from the others, but positioned to protect them.

Perhaps, she thought, there was room in their little family for a few more after all.

The guardian was waiting when they emerged, his eerie blue gaze fixing on the bulge in Elena’s pouch where the orb rested.

“You have claimed the Heart,” it observed. “Few have succeeded where many have failed.”

“Lucky us,” Elena replied dryly. “Now, if you don’t mind, we’ll be on our way.”

The guardian’s skull tilted. “The Heart carries a price, thief. It reveals truths... even those you may wish to remain hidden.”

“Cryptic warnings are Nora’s department,” Elena said, gesturing impatiently for the exit. “Just open the door.”

The guardian made that strange glass-breaking sound again—its laugh. “As you wish.”

The sealed entrance rumbled open, revealing the passage beyond. But as they moved to leave, the guardian spoke once more.

“The goddess-child draws them all—light and shadow, creation and destruction. When the false king rises, remember what you learned in these depths.”

Elena froze. “What do you know about Chubbs?”

But the guardian was already dissolving, its form breaking apart into wisps of blue light. “Prepare,” it whispered as it faded. “The board is set, and ancient players make their moves.”

Then it was gone, leaving only its cryptic warning echoing in the chamber.

Elena, Gav, and Evie exchanged troubled glances.

“Well, that’s not ominous at all,” Gav muttered, adjusting his grip on Evie.

“We need to get back to the others,” Elena said firmly. “Now.”

They moved through the exit, the heavy stone door closing behind them with finality. The orb pulsed in Elena’s pouch, its light visible even through the thick leather, beating like a heart.

None of them noticed the symbol that briefly glowed on Evie’s palm—an ancient rune matching one of those carved on the cavern wall. None of them saw it fade into her skin, leaving no trace of its presence.

And none of them felt the ripple that passed through the fabric of reality as something ancient stirred, awakened by their actions far beneath the earth.

The false king was rising, and they had just handed it the key.
