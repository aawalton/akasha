Getting everyone moved and settled was weeks of work, but a work I enjoyed. It wasn't quite to the level of my personal projects, but it was a gratifying experience. It was around the time that I'd finished that and was slowly ticking off items from my to-do that Chien came to see me.

“They're back!” he declared.

“News?” I asked, putting down what I was working on and going to join him.

“Don't know yet, they're just now in range of us finding them. They'll be here in an hour or two.”“Well let's go meet the returning heroes,” I said with a smile.

“We don't know if they're heroes yet.”“Even if they didn't make it all the way they still are. They did something none of us have tried before, and for that alone they are heroes to me.” That was something we could emphasize, if not just for them than for all of us.“You should write that down for future generations. That's a good one.”“If you knew the number of things I'd written down over the years you'd puke.”“Probably, writing is still a bit overrated in my opinion,” he finally agreed with a shrug.

He did this now and then, and it was never truly clear what was and what wasn't a joke. He'd accepted writing as a useful tool, but I knew for a fact he didn't prefer it for cases where other options were available. Nor was he alone in that belief, many elves thought it was a bit silly and overdone. Well, perhaps one day I would convince them all of the joy and excellence that was the written word.

Also of note was that this lack of literary love lead to a low level of literature languishing about. Fiction was a thing sometimes, but all those stories were simply told, sometimes with a performance to go along with them. Even then they tended not to be the fantasy or sci-fi of my previous world, but simple stories based on real things in the world around us. Perhaps it had something to do with even children being able to remember what this or that was like, and not wanting to be interrupted by them.

I'd done my part to add a few bits of old fiction here and there, stories of chimeras and other things some of which I blatantly stole from my old world. It had been over a hundred years here, and even if copyrights were inter-dimensional, which I was pretty sure I could argue they weren't, and even if we had had an agreement to upkeep them, which we didn't, the IP was surely through its course by now.

But this, the returning heroes, this was all part of our world, not a simply story, but something for everyone to remember.

We met at a large open area outside of the city, one designated for the return. It was little more than a field, but little more than a field was needed for this action. Other's seeing the airships returning also headed that way, curious onlookers and those who'd heard the news gathering around the edges, cheering as the small group of flying machines came near and hovered over the arrival zone.

They didn't land as such, but lowered, mooring cables sent down and tied off, and baskets of those who'd left slowly lowering. People cheered as the leaders came down first, waving to the gathered crowd. From about halfway down the first basket stopped, and magic formed a megaphone for the words to echo to everyone watching.

“Friends, we've traveled long an hard, seen new lands, and new creatures, and new peoples that we've never met before. We return whole and well, but please give us some time, let us see our families and friends, let us rest after our long journey, for we have much to tell.”That leadership quickly came down, along with most of the other airmen who'd gone, leaving only a skeleton crew which would shortly be relieved, and I motioned them to join me.

“I know you miss your families, so let us be quick, what did you find?”They described to me one by one a land I'd never seen, cooler than ours, trees of different kinds, fruits of different kinds, animals they'd never seen, though from the descriptions I recognized horses at the least. It sounded temperate, flatter, and brief explanations said there were almost no magical beasts.

“People?” I asked, confused.

“Yes your majesty,” Ema, a healer sent with the group responded, clearly concerned. “But odd ones.”“Odd how? And you took precautions when meeting them right?”“Of course, as you instructed. I was healing, looking for disease the whole time. Did the same on the return trip, no seeds, no live animals, and we checked everyone.”That was good, I had no desire to have the intercontinental disease spread that had happened on Earth when Europeans began to travel the world. Who knew who would have the worst ones. There'd also been explicit instructions to stop any seeds, plants, or the like from crossing as much as possible, because I knew the scourge invasive species could be.

“Good, good, odd how though?”“Well your Majesty...” she began. “Some of them were sickly in a way I've never seen people be sickly before. Their elders, or ancients, I'm unsure, but their hair was white. They weren't strong, they were respected, but weak, dying, I did what I could but I got the feeling they were... old, like animals old, dying of it. Their ears were also odd, so short, so rounded. Otherwise they looked much like us, perhaps a bit more muscular on average, a bit more hairy.”Humans, she was describing humans almost to a T. I'd have to see if they really were humans, have to see one, and wasn't that interesting. I knew humans didn't live long in comparison to us, but it was something.

“Even normal members looked, wrinkled, sagging, I feared disease but could detect none, and did what I could. There was more though, they had almost no magic users, few healers at all, and I looked, determined to try and question them on what was wrong. Sadly the language was quite different and I couldn't get a good answer, but they didn't seem to think anything was wrong, some were sickly in the same way even.” She was clearly alarmed y that, but having seen no evidence of disease had returned.

“It is possible that is the natural state for them. Many animals age and die in such a way.”“But people your Majesty? People don't.”“These people might. We'll have to send another expedition at some point, really get to know them, to know their language. I may need to go with that one...”
