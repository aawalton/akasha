Callie glanced at Mom and Dad fearfully. What was going to happen? Did anyone in the village see the glowing mark on Callie's arm? Even if they haven't, how will they react to what happened with the glauxis?

No, no matter what happens, I can't regret it. The villagers are safe, and the glauxis got his egg back. It is the best possible outcome.

Or so Callie tried to tell herself, but she didn't dare to release Mom's hand, which she was clutching tightly. To her relief, Mom gave her a reassuring squeeze and said, "Keep your head held high, Callie! You saved the whole village!"

"I'm not sure if Aemon will agree," Dad said.

"He doesn't agree with a lot of things, but even he must see the reason for this situation," Mom said.

"I'm coming too," Callen said, firmly grabbing Callie's other hand.

"Of course. All of us are going," Mom confirmed, and Dad nodded firmly.

Just like that, surrounded by her family, Callie made her way to Chief Aemon's house. Despite the early morning hour, many of the villagers were already awake. They watched Callie pass by and whispered among themselves. But the look in their eyes wasn't the same she was used to seeing. Not pity, not annoyance, not disappointment. There was something else there, although Callie couldn't tell what it was exactly.

When they finally reached the chief's house, the man himself was already waiting in the entryway, while a small crowd had ended up following Callie and her family. All the stares in her direction made her want to dig herself into the ground. Why did they have to stare at her so much?

Much to her relief, somewhere among the crowd, Granny Ethel appeared and came to stand beside Callen. "Aurelwynn family, do you mind if I join you in these talks?"

"We would be happy to have you, Ethel," Mom said with a smile.

"What is this? A festival dance? I only need to talk with Calluna and her parents. Go home and get to work, you gossipmongers!" Chief Aemon called out to the gathered crowd.

Begrudgingly, the people scattered, although they were walking very slowly and often would glance back, perhaps hoping that the chief would change his mind.

"Ethel, Callen, there's no reason for you two to be present either," the chief said firmly.

"I'm staying with my sister!" Callen squeezed Callie's hand tightly.

"Ethel?" the chief turned towards the elderly woman, who merely shook her head. He didn't press her to leave.

"Very well, come inside." Chief Aemon headed inside.

Callie couldn't help but feel trepidation. It wasn't that long since she was summoned to talk with the chief. She would be lying if she said that she wasn't scared. But this time, Callie's whole family was with her. Mom in particular had a very strong expression on her face—the same one she wore before going on a hunt.

"It'll be alright, Callie," Mom said firmly and gently pulled her inside Chief Aemon's house. They followed him into the same room where they had a meeting over the Aurelia burn incident.

The chief sat at his usual spot at the end of the table while everyone else sat on the right side of the table. Callie in the middle, Mom and Dad on her right, while Callen and Granny Ethel sat on her left.

"Okay, we all had our time to rest over the night, so I'm not going to dance around the matter and get straight to the issue at hand. Calluna Aurelwynn, you have endangered the whole village with your actions!" Chief Aemon said, looking Callie straight in the eyes.

His gaze was so fierce that Callie just barely managed to keep his stare. But keep it, she did. Barely. Her hands were trembling furiously in her lap, and she inadvertently sank deeper into her chair.

"What are you saying, Chief? Callie—"

Chief Aemon raised his hand, silencing Callen midsentence. "Silence. You will get your chance to speak. Calluna, answer me truthfully. How long have you known that the glauxis has been near the village?"

"I… Since I passed the obstacle course…" Callie admitted.

"You passed together with Aurelia. I remember the day well. It's been almost two weeks since that happened. Tell me how long you've known that its egg has been missing?"

Callie sank deeper into her chair. "I-I found that out a few days after that…"

"And who did you tell about this knowledge you have learned? Knowledge that was critical for adults to know, don't you think? You yourself witnessed what the glauxis is capable of when your mother nearly lost her arm, no?" Chief Aemon growled, his voice filled with anger.

Callie trembled under his stare, but she was also determined to keep her head high. In this case, Callie was sure that she had done the right thing. There was no reason for her to lie.

"I told my brother and Granny Ethel," Callie managed to say. She worried a little that the blame would be put on the elderly woman, but to her surprise, Granny Ethel smiled approvingly at that.

"So she did," the elderly woman confirmed.

Mom and Dad shared a glance, appearing hurt.

"Why didn't you tell us?" Mom asked.

"Oh, Maeve, you all should know all too well why little Callie only confided in me. You all so much as see a footprint from an arcane beast and your instinct is to HUNT it. Meanwhile, Callie can see that they are more than just mindless monsters you take them all for. So who do you think she would confide in? The people who would undoubtedly hurry to grab their weapons, or someone who would actually try to see her point of view?" Granny Ethel said.

Callie couldn't believe her eyes. Mom, Dad, and even Chief Aemon all looked guilty. Like children who had been chastised by their mother. She was yet again reminded just how respected Granny Ethel was in the village.

"Even so—" Chief Aemon began.

"Enough of this charade. You've known about the egg for just as long as Callie. I've told you about it after all," Granny Ethel said in exasperation.

Callie stared at Granny Ethel, then at the chief in disbelief. She had told him. The chief had known about the egg all this time.

Chief Aemon looked annoyed to have been revealed, so he cleared his throat. "I was about to get to that part, Ethel. Yes, I did know about the egg. I have been searching for it for a while. However, I didn't know that the source of this knowledge was Calluna. Please, do explain how you knew that the egg had been taken. Did you see the thief when he took it?"

Callie didn't know how best to answer. How much was she supposed to hide? Just revealing her magic to the chief… What if he told dragonbloods about her? Nervously, she looked at Mom, hoping that she would know how to best answer the question.

"Tell him the truth," Mom said.

Callie took a deep breath and nodded. Mom was right. Lying to the chief wouldn't do her any good, not when he was already mad at her for hiding things. "The glauxis told me that his egg was stolen."

Chief Aemon blinked in surprise. "Th-the monster told you?"

Callie hesitated. Was it alright to tell him the truth, really? She was so afraid of people finding out the truth about her magic and, worse yet, about the dragon. Surely, telling him about the dragon was too much?

Mom gently lifted Callie's forearm and rolled up her tunic. "It was thanks to this."

Callie's mark was exposed for everyone to see. The chief looked at it from afar. "What is that? Come closer so I can see it."

Callie went up to the Chief nervously and showed him the mark, which now had three prominent petals on it.

"What is this? Certainly an unusual mark, but I don't see how it relates to the matter at hand," Chief Aemon said, reaching out and running his finger over the mark. The mark produced a gentle glow when he touched it, and the man hurriedly withdrew his hand. "What in the heavens? Is that magic?"

Callie nodded. "It's what allows me to talk to arcane beasts…"

"Th-That's unbelievable… Why? How?" The chief gaped for a moment, then cleared his throat. "What is this exactly?"

"We do not know how it appeared, only that it has been growing stronger," Mom said before Callie could say anything.

She stared at her Mom for a moment, then at Granny Ethel, who gave an approving nod. Did this mean that she shouldn't tell the chief about the dragon? Only about the magic mark? That seemed to be the case, so Callie explained about her mark, how it seemed to grow petals when she interacted with arcane beasts, and how her magic grew stronger with each petal.

"Th-This is far more than I had expected to find out. To think that a child in our very village would suddenly develop magic abilities," Chief Aemon's face was as pale as a sheet. In fact, this was the first time Callie had seen the seemingly unshakable chief appear… afraid.

But it wasn't that he was afraid of her. He was touching her magic mark without much hesitation. It was like he was afraid for her. No, not just for her but for the whole village. If the chief was afraid, then Callie felt that she should be even more afraid.

"You haven't taught her anything, Maeve, Rowan?" The chief addressed Callie's parents while his gaze was still firmly locked onto the mark on her forearm.

"The way we were taught would never work for a child as small as Callie. You know that," Dad said firmly.

Chief Aemon nodded in agreement as he rubbed his chin, deep in thought. "This certainly explains why you weren't eager to tell anyone besides Granny Ethel. Tell me, Calluna. Are you able to control your power?"

She pondered the question for a moment. Could she? Magic seemed to appear when she needed it most, but there was no way she could claim to have control over it. In fact, Callie didn't understand much about her magic at all. It was why she worked so hard in her survival lessons. So she could go to the library and learn more about it.

"No…" Callie admitted.

The chief clasped his head for a moment. Clearly, that was not the answer he was hoping to hear. "Okay… What else have you learned through this mark? D-Did the glauxis mention who the thief was? Can you somehow… influence monsters with it?"

"No… I can only speak to them. I can't force an arcane beast to do anything. Also, I don't think the glauxis knew who the thief was. Surely, he would have attacked the thief directly had he known who they were," Callie said.

The chief's face seemed to grow more desperate by the second. "And you don't know who the thief is either?"

"I…" Callie thought for a moment. She wondered whether she should mention Mister Blackthorn's weird behaviour and how she had seen him near the location of the egg when she had found it.

But she had no proof. Mister Blackthorn was well respected in the village. It wasn't like she had actually seen him steal the egg. Callie still would much rather believe that it was an outsider who had done it rather than somebody from the village.

"I don't, sir…" she finally muttered.

Chief Aemon nodded, accepting her answer. "It would be too convenient for you to know that. More importantly, are you sure that the glauxis won't return?"

"Unless somebody steals his egg again, I don't think so."

"Won't it seek revenge?" Chief Aemon asked skeptically.

"Surely if it had wanted to harm the village, it would have done so already. Instead, the creature chose to observe from afar, merely hoping to regain its egg. Besides, now that it has its precious egg back, I believe it will devote its whole attention to raising it," Granny Ethel said.

Chief Aemon nodded, actually looking relieved. Only for a brief moment until eyebrows furrowed again and his lips pursed into a thin line. "But you having magic is an issue in its own right. Should the dragonbloods learn of this…"

Heavy silence descended upon the room until the chief spoke up again. "Does anyone else know you have magic?"

"Um… Tristan… Although I haven't had the chance to explain it to him yet…" Callie admittedly.

Chief Aemon rubbed the bridge of his nose. "That noisy boy… I'll have to talk to him later. Ethel, make sure you remind your apprentice that he is not to speak a word of Calluna's magic to anyone. And I mean anyone. Not even his family. The same goes for everyone here."

His stern gaze turned to Callie. "You cannot tell anyone about your magic. You cannot show anyone your magic. You must learn to control it in complete secrecy. Maeve, Rowan, I trust that you should be able to deal with this task better than anyone else in the village."

"So… I'm forbidden from using it?" Callie gasped.

"Forbidden from letting anyone see it!" The chief clarified. "Should this knowledge leave the village, you could be separated from your family. And let me tell you, when dragonbloods believe something is rightfully theirs, they will stop at nothing to obtain it. The whole village might suffer the consequences."

Callie's heart sank for a moment. She had hoped that perhaps she wouldn't have to keep hiding so much. She wanted to use magic to help arcane beasts. To help the dragon. Surely it hadn't given her magic just so she could just hide it from everyone.

But it wasn't like the chief was wrong either. Should dragonbloods learn about Callie's magic, she would be taken from her family. She didn't want that. Callie couldn't imagine living without her family.

"I'll keep it a secret," Callie said.

"Good. We also have the problem of the egg thief. Who would be mad enough to steal an egg from a monster and bring it into the village? Endangering the lives of everyone like that?"

Granny Ethel cleared her throat. "Aemon, I don't think that is something children should be thinking about, don't you?"

The chief threw a glance at Callie and Callen, then nodded. "You're right. Calluna, as the village chief, I pass my judgment on you. You were right about seeking out the glauxis's egg. You were right to return it and solve this problem peacefully. But you were wrong to hide it from your parents and from me. Your actions endangered the whole village."

Callie shrank back from his stern words. She knew he was right. After all, Mom had believed Callie, and it turned out that the chief and other adults had also been searching for the egg and hoping to solve the matter peacefully.

"I will not stand for any more secrets. Anything relating to your magic or arcane beasts, I want to know about it immediately. This doesn't impact just you or your family. This impacts everyone! You will not be going anywhere alone. Maeve, Rowan, I want you to watch this child like hawks and report everything to me, even if it doesn't seem important."

"Of course," Mom agreed. Callie couldn't help but feel guilty. They were still hiding the biggest secret of them all from the chief. But since neither of her parents, nor Granny Ethel had mentioned it, perhaps the chief wasn't supposed to know it.

Dad did say that this knowledge could shake the world to its core. Whatever that meant.

"Good. Now head on home. I still have a whole village to calm down."

As their family turned to leave, the chief said. "Despite all your secrecy, you still worked hard to save the village. It was thanks to your efforts that the glauxis left and nobody got hurt. Thank you, Calluna."

Callie blinked in surprise. The chief actually thanked her. She didn't know how to feel about it. Her chest swelled with happiness and pride that her efforts had been recognized. But ultimately, all she managed was a weak nod as she latched onto Callen's hand.

"You did well," Callen confirmed, smiling at her.

"I'll do better next time," she said, determined to actually tell her parents the whole truth, even if arcane beasts were involved.

But despite the happy feeling of being praised by the chief, there was also heavy dread in her stomach. The egg thief was still somewhere in the village, and who knew what he was going to do next.

Then a realization hit Callie. The whole village had seen her return the egg to the glauxis. The thief had hidden the egg. The thief had seen everything going on in the village. They knew exactly who took the egg he had stolen and returned it to the glauxis.
