Maiya ended the call, which didn’t leave Vir with the same sense of loss as it always had previously. Now that he could generate Affinity prana, he could initiate calls of his own anytime. Indeed, every orb of every affinity was available to him, and so he carried a few just in case.

Vir turned to the wide-eyed girl beside him. “Sorry, Siya. I’ll have to get someone else to show you your bedchambers while I’m gone.”

She shook her head, tugging his pants. “I will accompany you.”

Vir looked into her eyes for a long moment. On the one hand, the last thing he wanted was to leave her here alone to deal with her emotions. To cry over the loss of her father and world in solitude.

The very thought nearly broke his heart. She needed to grieve, yes, but not alone,, as she had been for far, far too long.

And if there was one thing he had learned, it was that despite her mannerisms, Siya became extremely stubborn about things she set her mind on.

“Alright,” he said with a sigh. “But this is a Vimana we are up against. Not even you are immune. You’ll be left far away from danger, understand?”

Siya nodded obediently. “I’ll not make a nuisance of myself. You might benefit from having me close by, however.”

That Vir couldn’t deny.

He ruffled the girl’s hair before taking her hand, leading her to the back wall of his bedchambers where a hidden door embedded into the wall led to a set of spiral stairs, which in turn led to the roof.

He didn’t have to take the emergency staircase he’d had installed, of course, but Siya’s gleeful giggle proved he had indeed made the correct decision.

As for the four-winged contraption that sat upon the roof, Vir had to admit the Mad Thaumaturge had outdone himself with this one.

Siya ran up to the contraption of wood and seric, brushing her hand against its hull.

Iteration IV, as Saunak referred to it, was a far cry from the original design Vir had been pressured into testing for him way back when.

Gone was the open cockpit design that exposed Vir’s face to the elements. Iteration IV was larger than any of its predecessors and featured an internal cabin with side-by-side seats placed in front of a glass bubble canopy that wrapped around the front, giving him an unobstructed view in every direction.

Moreover, the cabin was insulated and outfitted with comfortable chairs and enough room for four.

The wings were the real change. Where the old airship had used fixed prana-discharge wings, the set of four wings on Iteration IV were highly flexible, articulated at multiple joints, and designed to flap rapidly like that of an insect, each beat pushing prana out through controlled bursts that gave him a precision the old design had never managed.

Hovering, in particular, was now something Vir could do almost without thought. Saunak had studied insects for a full year before coming up with this redesign, or so he claimed.

Whatever the case, it was more stable in flight, easier to control, more comfortable, and more convenient than any design before it, even if Vir did prefer the sleek aesthetics of Iteration III.

He pressed his palm to the hull and injected a burst of prana, which extended a small set of half-stairs that Siya scurried up almost before it was down.

Vir took his seat at the pilot’s chair while Siya sat beside him as he manipulated the controls.

The wings extended and began to flap, creating a high-pitched hum as they lifted gently off the castle roof into the crisp morning air.

As impressive as Samar Patag had become, one could only appreciate so much from the ground. The air provided an entirely new perspective, and Siya leaned forward, face pressed against the glass as she peered at the speeding landscape below.

The city blurred beneath them as Vir accelerated, eliciting an excited squeal from his passenger.

The walls soon rushed past them, giving way to the placid Gargan Sea west of the city.

Their destination was not the Vimana, however. Considering how Vir didn’t know where to begin searching, that would have to wait.

For now, he had a different goal in mind. Vijaya Stronghold sat in the land that had until recently been the Deep Ash, and where the Gargan military was based.

Something Vir might have to reconsider in the new world, given its vast distance from the rest of the country. Until recently, the Ash had been his domain to exploit. Just an Ash Gate away. That was all gone now, and despite the military advantages it took with it, he was glad to be rid of that blight.

The journey took an entire hour even at top speed, and that too only because Vir knew exactly where he had placed the small city after the reunification of realms.

The time vanished, however, as Siya bombarded Vir with question after question, her little mind proving an insatiable monster of curiosity.

Vir happily entertained them all, right up until the fortress city appeared on the horizon.

The place was buzzing a hive of activity with two dozen airships already in the air and two dozen more in the act of launching from their berths.

Vir had given the mobilization order before departing, and as ever, his Asura did not disappoint.

Forty airships, all of various Saunak designs. Some with wings that beat like his own, while others were more conventional, sporting enormous long hulls with sweeping decks.

The fleet numbered five hundred airships now. Deployed across the demon realm and the Ash, their primary purpose was to patrol the Ash for any signs of anomalies. The forty here represented only what Cirayus had been able to pull together on short notice.

Such a far cry from the days of the rebellion, when even forty vessels boasting such impressive capacity and armament would have been an invaluable boon.

“What is that?” she asked, pointing to an especially oversized ship at the center of the formation.

“That would be the command ship,” Vir said with more than a little pride.

A different category of vessel entirely, it was easily five times the size of the standard airships flanking it, its hull plating thicker and its wing arrangement far more complex. “And we are about to land upon it.”

If Siya was excited before, she was practically glued to the canopy now, eyes wide as they approached the craft.

Asura signaled him with bright prana lanterns, indicating where to land, and Vir brought his airship down onto the command vessel’s upper deck, beside another, slightly larger personal airship he knew belonged to Cirayus. The deck was large enough to accommodate five such craft of similar size.

“Welcome aboard the Sarvaak, Akh Nara, Lady Goddess Siya,” an Asura said in greeting, bowing so deep Vir worried for the demon’s back. “Lord Cirayus and Commander Balagra await your arrival in the command room.”

The words elicited a smirk from Siya and an involuntary twitch of the lips from Vir. He’d always detested that name, and despite his best efforts to have it changed, there were some things even the Akh Nara was powerless against.

Vir gave the demon a nod and gestured for Siya to follow. Crossing the vast deck, Vir led her through a door and down a set of stairs and through a hallway below to a large room at the end.

Inside, they found Cirayus and Balagra standing around an incomplete map while a half dozen Asuran intelligence operatives worked around them like a swarm of prana hornets.

“So,” Cirayus said, all four arms crossed. “A rogue Vimana headed for the demon lands, eh? Just when I think I’ve seen it all, the realms—or realm now, I suppose—delivers a most tantalizing surprise.”

“Search teams are mobilizing as we speak,” Balagra added, sharing none of the Ravager’s levity. “Each has been tasked with a particular region and contains a beacon by which we might find them. Vimana are not the easiest constructions to miss. We’ll locate them soon enough.”

But will we find them quickly enough to affect the situation? Vir wondered. That was the true question. After all, they hardly needed to search at all when the Vimana was heading for the Demon Realm. Presumably a demonic city. In the worst case, they might have to order a full evacuation. That would be a nightmare and a half, even in good times.

Now, after such a profound change and with half the realm in mourning, was not a good time.

Balagra pointed to the navigational charts spread across the table, delineated into neat grids, each with its own alphanumeric designator.

“This will also give us decent coverage of the new terrain,” Balagra noted. “First proper mapping run we’ve managed. Shall I assign you a grid, or will you act on your own?”

Vir didn’t immediately answer, looking to Siya instead. “Do you have any way of tracking Vimana?”

“I might be able create one…” Siya said with pursed lips and a frown.

“But it would hurt you, like it did before, yes?” Vir asked. He still had no clue how she managed to create the prana accumulator for him, and her explanation had gone entirely over his head. Suffice it to say it cost her a great deal of energy.

Siya nodded.

“Then we do this the old-fashioned way,” Vir said. “Siya and I will scout on our own. Time is of the essence, so let’s make it count.”
