The tree was blazing with mana, alive to it as it siphoned from the world around, but most of all; from Jorge himself. It drained him as it grew, and each foot it pushed skyward left the old man more and more empty.

Whatever changes the seed had made within me were clearly still ongoing, powered by the overflowing mana it continually produced within my soul, because I saw the parasitic skill more clearly with each passing second. I could see clearly the tree turning mana into matter instantaneously as it grew impossibly fast. As I watched now with my increasingly refined mana senses, mouth hanging open with shock and horror, I noticed Jorge was diminishing with every passing moment.

I didn’t know all the details. I knew there were a million complexities that I was surely missing, and knew also that this had been Jorge’s choice. I didn’t know where Vera was, didn’t know if that army we had seen was marching behind the broken cavalry charge even now, didn’t know what had happened beneath the earth between Jorge and Markas, and didn’t know what Jorge’s ultimate plan had been.

What I didn’t understand about the situation I was in could fill tomes, while what I did could fit inside a thimble. ‘Only a fool rushes to judgement, while the wise learn all they can’—I knew that… But I’d always been a fool.

Here and now, as I saw my mentor—and more importantly my friend—being drained to a husk by his own skill, I decided I couldn’t let it stand.

I ran, sprinting at the tree and leaping onto it.

My body felt lithe and lethal, the seed still altering it in minor ways, even now. The golden light beneath my skin hadn’t faded, and because I hadn’t directed it to alter my own class and skills, it was hard at work altering me in other ways. My body was stronger, my mind sharper, my soul sturdier in some strange metaphysical way. I could feel my skills growing. Not changing, per se, but advancing all the same. The system tried to ram notifications down my throat, but I blinked them away. The ringing was back, but when had I ever struggled to ignore that in the heat of the moment?

Behind me, Jacyntha exclaimed something as she moved for the first time, and with a quick glance back I saw Nathlan and Sadrianna still immobilised, held in the grip of the divine transformation. Two statues limned in gold, and a third turning my way with shock as the light faded from her powerful frame. A small part of my mind noted it, wondering why I had woken earlier than everyone, and Jacyntha next after me, but there was no time for intellectual considerations in the midst of a crises, so the thought was discarded.

I scampered my way up the trunk until I reached the bulge that held Jorge within, and I drew my hatchet with a snarl. I wasn’t gentle or careful, but I knew well the supernatural endurance and fortitude that I enjoyed, and could only imagine that a 4th tier was nigh invincible when it came to my attacks. At least, I hoped that was still true considering the power being drained from him even now.

I used Shatter Point liberally as I hacked my way through the groaning trunk. Like The Mountain’s Gate before, it was more effective now, each blow detonating on impact and sending chucnks of bark showering around me. But this tree wasn’t made of base wood. Just like Resolution, the material had been blessed by magic I didn’t understand, and it was supernaturally tough.

I growled as I struck again and again, and by the time I made it through the several feet-thick bulge of tough bark, my face and arms bore a mess of cuts and splinters. Still, it hadn’t only taken seconds, and I could still feel Jorge alive within.

I reached within the crack, dropping my hatchet without care as I ripped away great chunks of wood and forced myself through the narrow, ragged entrance I’d made. My boots sloshed in a goopy mess of thick sap that poured out of the bulge, exposing Jorge cocooned within.

His eyes were wild and I saw anger flaring within them. “Get out, you fool!” he yelled, though his voice was weak, as diminished as the rest of him from the drain the gigantic tree was exerting.

I ignored him and reached out, gripping his arm in a vice and hauling him bodily out of the trunk to fall to the ground below. The tree had grown immensely and at impossible speed, but the bulge was still near the base, and so were still only about thirty foot off the ground. If Jorge couldn’t survive a fall from that height, he truly was lost. Indeed, the very fact that I could haul him out of the tree against his wishes only confirmed that this would have killed him had I not intervened—the Shepherd should not be unable to resist a 2nd tier, no matter if one was blessed by the World Tree or not.

He hit the ground hard, and I landed behind him only seconds later, Break-Step absorbing my excess momentum with consummate timing. The tree halted its growth and creaked ominously above us as Jorge picked himself up from the ground. The movement was sloppy and slow, but he rounded on me with such hopeless rage that I was momentarily taken aback.

“What the fuck are you thinking, Lamb?” he shouted, gesturing wildly at nothing in particular.

I tackled him to the ground before he could get in another word. “Where’s Vera?” I yelled in his face, matching my own desperation to his. “The army? What the fuck is going on, and why the fuck do you think you can just throw your life like that without any of us fucking noticing?”

He hesitated a moment, before grief clouded his vision. “She’s out there, lad. Slowing the army as best she can while I stopped the charge and saved your sorry arses. We’ve got only moments before they break through now, and your wall is already long gone.”

I pulled him to his feet. “Then let’s meet them like men,” I growled, something in his resigned tone making me angrier than I could truly articulate.

“I’m out, Lamb. Done. I can barely stand, y’see? Ain’t no way you can face down the Crimson Company right now, and if we’re not done with them in a hundred breaths then Vera’s fucked too.”

He sighed, looking older than I’d ever seen him. Rather than simply streaking through his long braid, the grey had colonised it entirely, and his eyebrows looked almost frozen given the white at their tips.

“That was my plan,” he said, pointing at the half-formed tree that loomed above us, cracks and groans echoing from its depths as it listed dangerously to one side. “That was my…” he trailed off.

I looked at him, taking in the age that had been visibly stolen from him and recalling the weakness of his frame as I’d torn him from the tree. It had drained him to power its growth, and without that power source it would crack and fall on all of us. That would not only leave the castle open for the cavalry that even now milled about on the other side of the gargantuan trunk but probably do a decent job of crushing us and the rebels on the way down too.

I could well understand why he was so angry. From his point of view, I’d just doomed the whole castle and made all of this pain and death entirely pointless. But I couldn’t let him die, not after all we’d come through to get here.

As a 4th tier, he had more mana than I could fathom and could power that skill-tree hybrid—or whatever the fuck it was—for far longer than I. But he’d also been nearly drained by its creation, and I suspected he’d already been weakened by his confrontation with Markas before. I might not be able to give the tree energy for long, but I could definitely stabilise the situation. Besides, I had power in abundance right now. It welled from my core and suffused my very being. The seed was not finished in its task, and neither was I.

“Fuck it,” I said, and sprinted for the tree again.

I didn’t see his face, but I could well imagine the emotions Jorge went through as he realised my intention, because the shout that tore its way from his throat was hoarse and broken. “Lamb, no!” he screamed, but it was too late.

Jorge’s shout followed me in my climb up the tree, but I was swift and sure. Stride The Edge and my own experience had me reaching the tree’s strange, sacrificial chamber in seconds, and then I was squeezing my way through the ragged gash I’d torn in it only moments ago. As soon as I made contact with the thick sap still drenching the chamber, I felt the mana drain. How Jorge had held endured this for more than a single second was almost incomprehensible to me.

Within the first heartbeat, my natural mana reserves were gone. Siphoned away instantaneously. The feeling made me gag, and I dove into my soul-space to see what was happening, and why I was still conscious. Mana deprivation like that should have killed me instantly, if not knocked me out cold, just like when I’d activated my pathbound skill for the first time.

The mystery soon revealed itself, though. My silver-blue mana was gone, but my soul wasn’t a dark void. Instead, it bloomed with light. Vibrant and golden and utterly perfect to behold. My core was now a molten volcano, a gilded glow, and it overflowed with the power even now despite the monumental drain.

A single siphoning tendril speared towards the roof of my soul from inside that golden core, virulent power sucked from the centre of my being towards what looked to be an enormous twinkling tree that dominated the void above; just as End of the Hunt dominated my skills. It was blazing with light, ley lines of power reaching up through the trunk to race along the dim branches and out to the very tips that still rested in shadow.

My vision was focused internally on my soul, but my ears still picked up the sounds of crashing from outside. The tree was growing once more, the ominous creaks dying away to be replaced the groaning and cracking of new growth as the titan climbed ever higher.

The World Tree was the most powerful being in Tsanderos, possibly in existence for all I knew, and its seeds were appropriately potent. But they were designed to uplift and empower mortals. Even their prodigious reserves of power would eventually run dry.

This tree seemed inexhaustible. Would it grow forever? Spear skyward until it dominated the entire continent and challenged the World Tree for primacy? Or would it just grow until running out of power, then fall to the ground once more to cause further devastation? I didn’t know.

All I could do was try to hold on until things were stable and then extract myself as cleanly as possible. As the moments slipped by, I watched my core dim. It was subtle, but that glorious golden glow weakened bit by bit, and I realised that I was nearing the end of the seed’s blessed power.

My skills still spun in the void, cavorting and writhing with divine power. They weren’t changing though, weren’t twisting upon themselves or altering their purpose. Just improving. I hadn’t even asked for it, but the seed seemed incapable of maintenance—it sought growth and change above all.

The system’s notifications were far from my mind, but I could see the plainly enough the changes within my skills and knew them to be reaching beyond the soft cap of level ten even now. A Frozen Pyrre, The Mountain’s Gate… all of them growing. A most importantly for me; Vanguard of the Lost. My salvation. The one skill that could grant me what others couldn’t. My memories. My identity. My past.

I heard a voice. Dimly, as if from a great distance.

“Lamb!” it called.

It was familiar, but my mind was bending, struggling against the conflicting desires of the seed and the tree. If I let the tree continue to drain my core, the seed’s power would soon be spent, and my skills would cease their miraculous growth. Vanguard of the Lost already gave me glimpses of the past at only level 6. If I let it climb higher, what could I do with it? Breacing that soft level cap, climbing further to level 20… I couldn’t be sure, but I suspected my memories would be within reach not in a century, or even a decade… but today. Now.

If I took this power and used it for my own ends, I could finally recall my past this instant. I remembered the feelings that lingered whenever the memories of my past life came too close to the surface. Love, an aching feeling of loss and a burning desire to recover something I couldn’t quite recall. There was such a purity in that feeling, such a bittersweet nostalgia for something that it took my breath away simply to dwell on it. The memory of a memory, and still it felt so potent. What did the real thing feel like?

But to do so would be to risk everything I held dear in this new life. The people outside, the goals of my companions. Innocents, too. Even the lives I had claimed so far—the soldiers I’d killed—would mean nothing if I abandoned things now for my own selfish goals.

My new life or my old? I thought once more of the mountains and my class and the epiphany that had woken me from my seed-driven trance. I was of Tsanderos. My answer was clear.

“Lamb!” a voice called again, more insistent this time.

It was urgent, slightly feminine, and husky from battle. There was a depth to it that spoke of an imposing physicality possessed by the owner, and a slight accent that tickled the edges of my mind in recognition. Jacyntha, I realised. I should have recognised it immediately, but my mind was coming apart at the edges simply from channelling the insane powers shared by the seed and the tree.

I grunted, unable to look away from the slowly dimming light of my core, and the slowly brightening light of the tree above my soul. I watched with agonised intensity as the representation of the tree above my soul inched ever closer to completion. The golden light was filling every bough, every branch, but some of the upper leaves were still dark and waiting for the glorious light. Once it was full, it would be stable. I wasn’t sure where that certainty came from, but I held it close regardless. Just hold on. Just a few final leaves.

I knelt there in agonising stillness. The pain was nothing like when the seed had taken control of my body and mind. There was no physical pain at all, honestly. But the sheer power that was coursing through my soul—the shocking well that was draining from my core with every passing instant—was ripping me apart at the seams. It took everything I had just to hang on.

I knew this would kill me now. Even though the mana being drained by the tree wasn’t my own, the sheer quantity of it would kill me as a byproduct. This was a skill created and cast by a 4th tier, and my feeble mortal body, no matter how uniquely enhanced by the seed already, couldn’t hope to endure its demands. I would rather die than let this skill lose control and wreak havoc among my companions, but was that the only choice?

It wasn’t my power that was being used—it was the seed’s. I was just the vessel, and I could leave without it.

It seemed an impossible task when I considered the strength of the siphon connecting my core and the tree hanging above my soul. The power of the seed and the monstrous capacity of the tree had reinforced the channel, just as my skills grew and solidified from use. Except in this case, the sheer quantity of mana had hardened the siphon into spiritual permanence. Could I sever that connection even if I tried?

But I didn’t need to, I realised. I just needed to extract that seed from my core. It had descended from the heavens and drilled its way physically into my heart, crossing the rubicon to burrow into my soul. If I could just extract that spiritual engine from my soul and force it back out into the world, I could leave safe and sound.

I focused, hardening my intent into a weapon and commanding the seed to rise. I pulled it from my core with inexorable slowness. Every inch of movement felt like climbing a mountain, my breath burning in my lungs like a cage, but I persevered. Thinking of my companions was enough, and Jacyntha’s voice outside, calling out with increasing desperation, helped ground me in my sisyphean struggle.

As I dragged that incredible well of power across the void of my soul, I had time to mourn the true sacrifice I was making. It would take years to master Vanguard of the Lost in the traditional sense. I may well have to ascend to the third tier and upgrade the skill once more to even capture all of my old memories, let alone hold them. Right now, as I strained with every single iota of my being to expel the seed from my soul, I was in fact pushing away the very key to my past.

But that paled in significance before the immediacy of my purpose. I was Lamb of the Mountains, and I was born of Tsanderos itself. My first priority would always be here.

I forced the seed from my soul and screamed as the animating golden light left me. I fell to the ground, half submerged in sticky sap and completely wrung of all energy. My mana was beginning to trickle back into my core, but after channelling the god-like quantities of power only moments ago, I felt weak as a babe. My limbs were heavy, and I coughed as I tried to claw my way to my feet.

Darkness greeted me as I dragged my gaze away from my soul and back into reality. There was a faint golden light quickly leaving my body, and with its weak illumination I could just about see the edge of the chamber. The rent I have hacked in it t rescue Jorge from earlier was gone, sealed over by layers of living wood—clearly, the growth of the tree hadn’t just been upwards.

“Lamb!” I heard Jacyntha call once more, and now I understood why it was so muffled. She was outside the tree still and couldn’t find me at all. My weapons were gone, my hatchet and fang dagger discarded below and resolution far from reach. I had nothing in my storage ring that could count as a weapon, and I had no idea how thick the tree now was. But it was between me and my goals, so I strode over to the scarred trunk, and raised my fist.

Time to see how strong Shatter Point had grown.
