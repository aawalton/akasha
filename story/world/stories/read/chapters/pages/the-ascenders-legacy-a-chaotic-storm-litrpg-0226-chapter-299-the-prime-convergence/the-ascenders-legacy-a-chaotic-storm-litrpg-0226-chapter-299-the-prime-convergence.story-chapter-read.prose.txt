Jethro wasn’t prepared for the army of harvests converging upon him with righteous fury in their veins and bloodlust in their eyes. But that didn’t stop him from reacting—the threat of the Calamity temporarily pushed to the back of his mind in the face of a more imminent threat.

And though the harvests were no calamity, they were still a significant threat.

For the past few days, he had watched every one of them overcome limits, break boundaries, and use their abilities in ways he could never have imagined. And together, the risk they posed to his life was no joke.

But Jethro had several advantages none of them had.

At the 50th tier himself, he stood on equal footing with the most powerful among them. He had more seals than all of them combined, and after Jian’s death, his spirit rivaled--perhaps even exceeded--that of a transmigrant.

But beyond all that, he had the advantage of knowledge.

He had watched them fight. He knew their weaknesses, their strengths, and their flaws, and in the past few days, he had learned how to counter them all.

They might be many, but he was Fated. A god to their mortality.

And with a smirk of utter disdain, he too levitated into the air, even as the skies split open, and a river of lightning descended—a thousand bolts of crackling electricity converging upon him as one, molten and white, like liquid fire itself.

It was a judgement of storm and vengeance.

But it was a judgement he’d learned to overrule. And in a swift motion, he raised a hand to the descending judgement, seals skittering under his skin like insects at his command until a single black seal appeared on his palms.

It was a void seal.

The judgement of molten lightning struck him, and an explosion of void essence erupted from his palms in response—a wave of darkness so inky it was like a solid shadow.

Both forces collided with a boom of thunder, but no one bothered to wait for the result. They knew Jethro would survive. No one among them could defeat him alone. Not Aodhán. Not Daruk. Not Mirith. No one.

But together they could bring him down. Without hesitation, they attacked as one, skills and abilities exploding from every direction.

The temperature dropped to abysmal degrees as Destra muttered an incantation. Ice spread across the ground Jethro was standing on, and from it, a jagged array of gigantic ice spikes erupted, their tips gleaming with an unnatural sharpness that was obviously Daruk’s doing. Lilac essence exploded as several beams of searing infinity shot out from a dozen points at once, all converging on Jethro. Space screamed as the point Jethro stood in caved, cracked, and then imploded, only to be ripped open once again as a siren of concussive resonance shot into the imploding space to rip Jethro apart. Fire fell from heaven like a judgement that engulfed the world in searing light. Entropy shot into the maelstrom of chaotic essence to destabilize it further. Darkness and light bloomed in a symphony of destruction. Gravity intensified into a single point, and the earth fractured into a thousand jagged spikes all converging on Jethro to skewer him to pieces.

Each attack was empowered by willpower, chaos, and quintessence. And when it all collided, chaos erupted in a wave of power so massive the earth cratered and reality itself burned.

No one in the advanced class should have been able to survive such a barrage. Even champions would have struggled.

Yet, when the smoke of the explosion cleared, Jethro remained alive, standing, completely unharmed save for a single line of blood running down the side of his head.

“You ungrateful wretches!” he snarled, blood beading at his lip. “You cannot defeat me. I am Fated. I can do this all day.”

But that was a lie. Jethro couldn’t do this all day.

With Core Sense, Aodhán could sense just how much that attack had rocked Jethro. He had burned over two dozen seals and a significant amount of willpower just to block all their attacks.

If they could hit him with such intensity a dozen more times, he would fall. The strain of channeling so much willpower and quintessence all at once would eventually catch up with him, and then he would become no different than the rest of them—weak. Mortal. Killable.

But Jethro was also aware of that.

He didn’t give them the chance.

In an instant, he split into a hundred copies, multiplicity essence erupting out of him in a blue wave that warped the air.

And then he attacked—all hundred copies simultaneously.

Aodhán barely dodged the reality blade aimed for his throat in time—his form flickering in a burst of violet lightning to reappear twenty meters away, a line of blood dripping from his neck.

Eyes wide, he cupped his injured neck. At the 35th tier, he wasn’t nearly strong enough to face Jethro head-on. Not in power. Not in seals. Not in experience.

But he had something Jethro didn’t.

He had an Aspect.

And he was going to abuse it.

The clone lunged again, blade dripping with essence so dense it shredded the air. This time, however, Aodhán didn’t teleport or raise a defense. Instead, his body unraveled into silver lightning, chaos pouring into his spirit at the barest thought.

His aspect hummed within him, unrefined, crude—yet ready. And without hesitation, Aodhán phased right into the clone.

Reality warped as his crackling form passed through solid flesh, like lightning through mist. The clone screamed as its energy instinctively flared, seals burning against Aodhán’s form in rejection.

It was like trying to force two souls into one body. One had to die--and it damn well wasn't going to be Aodhán. With brutal determination, he forced his way deeper into the clone’s chest, silver lightning crackling wildly around him.

The resistance vanished an instant later.

And Aodhán let the water loose.

It exploded out of him—not as a trickle, but as a torrent—watery essence flooding through muscle, bone, and blood as though a floodgate had burst open within. The clone convulsed violently as bubbles formed in its bloodstream, thousands of them, expanding faster than its regenerative seals could adapt.

Veins bulged and burst. Flesh ruptured.

It was like nothing Aodhán had ever witnessed or imagined, notifications screaming in his mind in acknowledgement of this new skill, but Aodhán ignored them.

Instead, he clenched his fist and whispered. “Burst.”

The clone exploded from within—blood, bone, tissue, and essence vaporizing into a mist of crackling electricity.

Aodhán stumbled out of the explosion, body reforming midair, and electricity sparking all around him. His chest heaved from the rush of adrenaline. He had just done something he could never have thought possible, and as he glanced down at the number of Jethros still alive, he felt the inexplicable urge to do it again.

Aodhán didn’t resist.

He teleported—his body unraveling into violet plasma to reappear a split second later behind the clone Yuna and Seojun were battling.

The clone lunged at him, blade cutting through lightning residue.

Aodhán met it with bare hands.

He had learned from the last attack. He didn’t need to phase into the clone. He just needed contact.

The moment his palm brushed the clone’s forearm, he flooded its body with microscopic filaments of water essence—tiny enough to slip through veins, arteries, and capillaries before the clone’s defenses could push him out.

Such finesse and control weren’t things Aodhán could have achieved on his own. This was the Aspect leading him, guiding him—and Aodhán didn’t resist.

Willpower burst out of his core like a tsunami, and with a clenching fist, he compressed.

Bubbles erupted—thousands of them expanding all at once within the clone’s bloodstream. It convulsed mid-swing, eyes bulging as veins ruptured, organs tearing apart in silence. Its blade faltered—then its entire body popped, exploding into mist and residual energy.

Yuna stumbled backward, a bleeding gash on her forehead.

“Thank you—” She began, but Aodhán vanished before she could finish, his gaze set on his next target.

With terrifying speed and precision, Aodhán moved through the clones, getting better and deadlier with the destruction of each one. In only a minute, he took out six clones.

Each one he touched exploded, but Aodhán wasn’t satisfied with killing clones. The real Jethro was among these clones somewhere, and Aodhán was intent on finding him.

For the next few minutes, Aodhán transformed into a killing machine of such power and intensity Zatya would have died from pride. Without hesitation, he ripped the clones apart, each motion efficient. Each death brutal.

And just as he ripped the 19th clone apart, Core Sense finally detected a difference. Despite bearing the same core energy signature as the original, the clone cores lacked a subtle density—a cohesion that the original possessed. With that distinction identified, Aodhán spread his awareness outward scanning the battlefield until he found it; a blazing core in the distance, denser than the rest.

The real Jethro.

He teleported toward the battle immediately, a wicked smile crossing his face as his gaze landed on Jethro, who was locked in an intense battle against five women—Monica, Serenya, Mirith, Borevin, and one woman he couldn’t identify.

Only this time, the Fated was looking worse for the wear, over seventy seals dim, heaving and panting heavily. Half his face was slick with blood, and one arm hung at an unnatural angle, clearly dislocated.

“You will pay for the death of my friend, you piece of shit!” Monica snarled, hatred pouring from her as she unleashed an endless barrage of decay. “Today is the day you die. I’ll send you to your maker with my bare hands.”

“He has no maker!” Mirith gritted out, her smile a blade of cruelty and concentration. “Only theft and arrogance. The guard in your mind is high. But it was only a matter of time before I broke through it.” She leaned in, veins bulging on her forehead. “Now we strip it away.”

Jethro’s eyes widened—but before he could react, his head snapped back as invisible talons plunged into his psyche. A scream tore from his throat, raw and animalistic, as the air itself warped under the strain of a mind being split apart. His aura flared violently, wounds rupturing across his skin like glowing sores.

“Get out of my head!” He roared, eyes bloodshot and wild.

But Mirith’s cold smile only widened. With telepaths so rare, Jethro hadn’t managed to claim the seals and innate skills of one. Despite his mental fortifications, this made him weak. Vulnerable. Prey.

And with hatred burning in her heart, Mirith dug deeper into his mind and began to rip it apart. Her pupils dilated into endless azure pits as mental energy bled from her body in concentric waves. Veins of telepathic essence—jagged, luminous, and violet—snaked from her fingers into Jethro’s skull, spreading like roots through glass. He staggered, clutching his head.

Borevin and the others didn’t stand idle while all that happened.

The earth beneath Jethro’s convulsing form transmuted into null metal, shackling him to the earth. A bubble of turquoise serenity held Jethro’s head completely still despite his spasming body. And despite his shackles, wind spears skewered down from above to nail Jethro to the ground.

The clones reacted instantly, abandoning their battles to save their master before the null binds could drain him dry. Aodhán couldn’t allow that. With a growl, he raised a hand to the churning storm above and pulled—hundreds of violet lightning spears crashing down to skewer them to pieces in an instant.

But the clones weren’t the only ones trying to get to Jethro.

High in the sky, another battle was ongoing, desperate elders held off by an army of telepaths, the champions, Runes, Varéc, and the Skalk itself. Energy ripped the air apart as both parties clashed brutally.

And when Jethro realized no one was to save him in that moment, something broke within him. And with the last of the energy still rushing through his pathways, he attacked—an inferno of searing white flames erupting out of him to rip assailants apart.

But the flames barely traveled a meter from his body before a burst of serenity forced it all into stillness. The flames paused and then receded.

But Serenya didn’t stop there.

Her eyes glowed with blinding calm—a serenity so absolute it became violence in its own right. The air bent around her as her aura pulsed outward, compressing the flames into Jethro’s own chest. They folded inward like dying suns, tearing through flesh and bone before being swallowed by his convulsing body.

“Rest,” she whispered, voice trembling with a cruel gentleness. “You’ve taken enough.”

Jethro screamed—not in rage this time, but in animal terror and agony. His will surged to retaliate, but Mirith crushed the thought before it could even form. The fire burned his flesh, tearing through muscle and tissue until it reached bone.

Jethro screamed again—a wordless, broken sound, but not one person looked upon him with mercy.

Rather, they converged once more and unleashed their wrath on the suffering Fated.

The air collapsed.

Gravity collapsed inward in a violent surge, crushing inward from every direction at once. Ice erupted beneath him in jagged pillars. Shadow spears followed, then light, blood, fire, and space. Each affinity carved its own punishment—flaying flesh, boiling blood, exposing bone.

And then the Skalk itself joined, leaving its position in the sky to enact its own vengeance. Relentlessly, it attacked, spearing Jethro with its skeletal protrusions of death and despair.

They could have ended him in that moment. But no one wanted to grant him that mercy. What could have ended in seconds, stretched into minutes--each blow deliberate, each strike measured.

And by the time their wrath waned, Jethro was nothing but a spasming ruin of burnt flesh and torn tissue, pinned to the earth by dozens of spear constructs. Blood seeped from countless wounds. Nothing was left untouched—save for his heart, still fluttering weakly, and his head, bowed beneath the weight of all he had done.

Aodhán approached him then, his expression hardening as he knelt beside what remained of Jethro. Mercy flickered in his eyes, but he pushed it aside. This man had taken the lives of hundreds and had nearly taken his own life multiple times.

He didn’t deserve mercy. What he deserved was retribution, and Aodhán wasn’t about to let his empathy get in the way.

Jaw tightening, He smiled at the dying Fated. “I told you I would go down your throat like a crown of thorns. How does it feel now, Jethro? How does it feel when you’re the one suffering?”

Jethro gurgled, trying to speak, to plead, but only blood came out.

They had gone overboard, Aodhán knew. But the death of Brennon and Baxter still hurt. And because of that hurt, he wanted to leave Jethro here to suffer forever.

Never dying. Never healing.

But he couldn’t do that. That wasn’t who he was. That wasn’t who they were.

Swallowing hard, Aodhán stood to his feet.

“This is for all the souls you harvested.” He whispered as energy, chaos, and willpower gathered within him like a maelstrom. The last of his quintessence joined the growing energy, creating a viscous ball of black and gold energy that had now become as familiar to his spirit as breathing.

He channeled it all into the tattoo on his wrist, and in a voice no louder than a whisper, Aodhán activated his icon.

Thunder boomed, reality screamed, and from the heavens themselves, a loud voice echoed.

~~ICON MANIFESTATION—THE PRIME CONVERGENCE~~

The churning storm blackened as the icon blazed to life on his wrist—a spiral of converging elements pulling inward to a single, inevitable point. Power flooded through him, not from within, but from everywhere—reality responding to his call. Willpower, energy, and chaos bled out of him so powerfully he was nearly drained dry. But Aodhán didn’t care. He raised a hand to the heavens—and reality obeyed.

The storm was a gathering—a convergence of all that could be gathered. That truth had birthed his icon—and now, it was being proven.

Blood converged first.

Every drop spilled that day—harvest and clone, victim and innocent—rose from the broken earth. Rivers of crimson lifted from the earth, streams of red drawn from corpses, and mist wrenched from the very air itself. It gathered above Jethro like a second sky, an ocean of blood suspended by will alone.

Then came the metal.

Every weapon scattered across the battlefield—swords, spears, arrowheads, shattered armor—ripped free from where it lay and screamed through the air. Thousands of fragments spun together, merging into a howling cyclone of steel that carved the sky as it joined the crimson tide.

And then the pain—not physical, but emotional—every scream Jethro had caused, every tear shed by his victims, every moment of agony from all of them present. It converged into a visible darkness, a roiling shroud of torment that layered the ocean of blood and metal like judgment itself.

But Aodhán wasn't done.

Fear came next—not just Jethro’s, though his terror burned brightest, but from every harvest watching, from the battlefield itself, and even from Aodhán’s own heart. It gathered into another layer of living shadow that coiled through the storm of pain like a venomous current.

Finally, he gathered the clouds themselves—thunder and lightning converging into a pillar of white-hot plasma that connected earth to sky, Jethro suspended at its epicenter.

Aodhán’s spirit groaned as the convergence above him thundered, manifesting a pressure so immense reality itself shuddered.

Perhaps it was overkill, but Aodhán wanted to make sure that by the time he was done, nothing would be left of Jethro. Nothing that could return or reform. The menace of the cult of the watch would end here, by his hand, forever.

His hands closed into a fist. "Converge."

And like a storm reaching its inevitable collapse, everything fell inward at once.

The ocean of blood crashed down like a tidal wave. The cyclone of metal compressed into a sphere of spinning blades. The gravity intensified until space folded in on itself. The lightning pillar contracted into a single point of infinite brightness. The fear, the pain, the essence, the will—all of it converged on Jethro simultaneously.

The explosion was soundless at first—too vast for sound, too immense for comprehension.

And then it hit—a clap of thunder so colossal it tore the clouds apart. Light erupted in a blinding flash that burned afterimages into every eye. The ground cratered so deeply it exposed bedrock. Shockwaves rippled outward in visible distortions of space.

When it cleared, there was nothing left of Jethro.

Not ash. Not bone. Not even a stain.

Only a perfectly smooth crater where he'd stood, and the lingering scent of ozone.

Aodhán lowered his hand, the icon still glowing on his wrist.

Around him, absolute silence.

Then Destra whispered, voice trembling with awe. "What... what was that?"

Aodhán stared into the cratered earth, heart heavy with finality, and whispered, “That was Vengeance.”
