I’m not quite sure why…but those words struck a deeper sense of dread in me than I was expecting. So much so that I could feel the hair on the back of my blood-soaked neck start to rise on end.

I may have been feeling the exertions from the long stretch of combat through the streets of Karag Korvok. But the sound of the patiently waiting mutant’s words on the other end of the hall was like a shot of energy potion in my veins. Suddenly, I was far more alert than I had been previously, and my forces and I had just spent several minutes carefully navigating our way through pews filled with strangely quiescent Skarned.

Something about this guy…it was like I was suddenly under the gaze of some kind of amused, patient predator, watching from the shadows.

Matters weren’t helped by the sheer number Korvokar gathered in prayer lines before the altar at his back. There were as many of the elite, mutant warriors gathered over there as I believe we had encountered through the entire Hold.

If this came down to a fight-

(And it would, I was positive it would)

Then this was going to be a hard battle.

Still, I wasn’t one to turn down an advantage. If this thing, who or whatever it was wanted talk, I was inclined to listen to it.

This would be far from the first bloodthirsty madman I’d patiently listened to, before I tore out their throat.

I exchanged a quick glance with Sylvia, patiently waiting for orders off to my side, and nodded at her. I gestured forward with one hand, and our gathered forces carefully began to march down the aisle towards the altar…

And the person who had beckoned us forward.

As we neared the kneeling lines of Karvokar, my hackles only rose further when I noticed they weren’t quite as enthralled as the Skarned were. Whereas their more mindless, raving brethren sat docilely upon the pews at our back, these guys were instead fully conscious as they knelt in prayer. More than one of them opened their eyes and stared at us in open intrigue as we passed their position.

Well.

Either intrigue, malice, or hunger. Usually, a combination of the three.

But as we reached the foot of the grisly altar at the back of the hall, and the figure who had beckoned us forward came into the light…

I didn’t bother to hide my grimace.

If I had thought the Karvokar, with their half-flayed, mutated bodies, had been a disturbing sight…

Then this thing was barely recognizable as a person.

They, it, stood before the altar with its hands clasped before it, standing so utterly still, motionless, to such an inhuman degree, that I was positive it must have complete, precise control over its body. Its chest wasn’t even moving from unconscious breaths, it was so still.

If I didn’t know any better, this thing had human proportions. It stood at nearly eye level with him, despite still possessing the hallmarks of a Dwarven skeletal system.

Where the Karvokar had been only half-flayed across their entire bodies, exposing that oddly waxy, hardened muscle I knew to be as tough as steel, this thing was completely bare to the world. Not a single inch of skin was left upon its form, and yet that was only the beginning of its twisted existence. It was mutated like the rest of its degenerate brethren, but in a far more disturbing, almost crafted manner. There was a distinct impression that this thing had been carefully created according to a distinct form of aesthetics.

Where many of the Korvoks seemed like grotesqueries meant to frighten, this thing…

It was an art piece created out of living flesh.

Somehow, someway, the individual muscle fibers that comprised its flayed form seem to have been first carefully separated from each other, and then twined with something. More careful inspection of the…person? Revealed that it seemed to be Mythril.

Every single muscle on the body of the Korvok before me had been intertwined with mithril thread. The result was shining braids of maroon and silver, framing a tall, oddly thin form. No secondary sex characteristics were still present upon the carefully sculpted flesh of the ‘Dwarf’, making it impossible for me to tell if they had once been either male or female. Their voice, earlier, hadn’t given me any hints either, sounding perfectly androgynous to my ears.

And yet, for all of their exposed, Mythril entwined self, they weren’t completely naked. Odd, diaphanous, transparent veils draped their form, one around their shoulders, the other draped around their hips. The effect was such that the upper settled around their torso like a short cloak, while the bottom was nearly skirt-like in appearance. They were so thin that they wisped upon an unseen breeze, drifting like suspended silk. They offered no obstruction to sight from how easy it was to see through them, allowing me to see the figure’s raised and exposed sternum. Upon it was carefully, lovingly carved a rendition of the self-same splayed figure, suspended above the altar.

At the sight of the patiently waiting figure, more than one gasp of horror issued from the throats of my battle group. I couldn’t blame them. I only barely held one in myself.

The Skarned and the Karvokar were hideous, horrific all of their own…but this thing was on another level. It felt like it wasn’t just a violation of common morality simply existing…but a violation of the very concept of existence.

There was no other word for it than wrong.

Carefully, I did my best to ignore everything about this creature, despite the alarm bells ringing in my head, and met its eyes. I found them to be a bright, almost scarlet red, far brighter than the blood and muscle they seemed to so revere.

And filled with a patient benevolence I had only ever seen within the gaze of priests.

I did my best to keep my breath even as I met its eyes.

It was harder than I expected.

“Well?” I said stonily, my voice echoing throughout the cathedral. “Here I am.”

“Here you are,” The creature agreed softly. Still, there was an…odd undertone to its voice that I didn’t care for. A flang that seemed to originate from somewhere inside its chest. For all I knew, it could have been because of the wire. “As are we all. For it is written, ‘In the space of a breath, there is a spark, and that spark is life, the gift which is the shore upon which That Which I Am is written’.”

I nearly jumped out of my skin when the masses of Karvokar all around us droned a response. “In The Truth.”

To my side, I felt it as Sylvia shivered and tightened her grip on her blades. She was far from the only one.

When none of us responded, the creature merely smiled at me with disturbingly perfect white teeth. “Be welcome in this hall. By my word as Rendarch, you will come to no harm howsoever long you have my blessing to be here. I…am Rhogann, third of the Rendarchs of the Red Truth, known as the Unveiled within the Hold of Blessed Korvok. Please, who might you be?”

This was…not exactly what I had been expecting. Despite how we had essentially fought our way through tides of Korvoks to reach this spire, we were being greeted like I was a courtier. “My name is Marshal Nathaniel Hart,” I said shortly, eyes fixed on this ‘Rhogann’. “Of the Order of the Polaris Reach, leader of the Herztalian Expedition.”

“Herztal…” Rhogann tasted the word thoughtfully. He hummed. “You are quite far from home, Herztalian. How is it that you’ve come to be here, in the heart of Blessed Korvok? And at so auspicious of a time, at that.” He (She? It?) chuckled. “As always, providence is the driver of all. Truly, we are blessed.”

A long, slow breath escaped me, and I didn’t loosen my grip on the drawn form of Terractus in my right, nor the laser in my left. “Auspicious. Is that what you call the assault on Karag Varla?”

Rhogann’s fleshless brow raised in surprise. “How could it not be? After long years of distance, we are now reunited with our misled brethren. A glorious age of Truth dawns on the horizon for the Holds. It is the most joyous occasion of my life. In the life of all true sons and daughters of Blessed Korvok. But…pardon me, Marshal Hart. I do not mean to be rude, but how is this any concern of yours?”

I think, if anything, it was the honest expression of confusion on the skinned, Mythril braided face of Rhogann that really disturbed me about them. It was like the mutant really, truly couldn’t understand just why I, or anyone else for that matter, might object to the slaughter and torture of an entire city of people.

“This is an internal matter of faith among the children of Yorgun,” Rhogann explained patiently. “The people of Blessed Varla were deceived in ages past and closed their ears to the Truth. For many years, we despaired at the ultimate fates of their eternal souls. It was only with the ascension of the wise and benevolent Ul-Korveth, blessed be the fruits of his labors-”

The surrounding Karvokar echoed the Rendarch’s words. “Blessed be the fruits of his labors,” They droned faithfully.

“-that this has, at long last, been corrected.” Rhogann then perked up, visibly happy. “Are you perhaps here because you are interested in the Truth of All Things? Have you come to be enlightened by the faith? That would be a joyous occasion indeed. We of course have planned to send missionaries south to spread our blessings, but to think that you might come this far all of your own volition-”

I cut the rambling monster off with a sharp shake of my head. “No,” I said firmly. “We’re not here to join your…faith. We’re here to rescue the people of Varla from it. Where have you taken your prisoners you stole from Varla, Korvok? Where is Azarus?”

Slowly, the smile on Rhogann’s face faded away, as the stares from the surrounding Karvokar became heavier on my back. In its place, a very flat expression appeared on the Rendarch’s face. “Azarus…if I recall correctly, this is the name of the Velancian who stood shoulder to shoulder with Jarl Jorvik, correct? However, I must admit to some confusion. These people do not require rescue. They are already in the process of being rescued from their ignorance. As we speak, they are being blessed by my fellow Rendarchs. Some, like my flock that surrounds us-”

Rhogann swept out an arm at the Karvokar who knelt upon the stone of the hall.

“-will have the Truth of All Things carved into their lying flesh, and they shall be saved. Others, those with wicked hearts, shall falter before the Truth, and instead be saved as one of the Skarned. They shall repent through service to us, the Truthseekers, and upon their death be granted opportunity to be enlightened in the next life. You see, our new guests do not require rescue, Herztalian. We are already doing this thing, and far more efficiently, in a more honest manner, than what you would inflict upon them.”

My eyes flickered over to the nearest pew to where the Skarned sat like mindless dolls.

So.

They were just the refuse, was that what he was saying? Skarned were essentially failed Karvokar, who couldn’t withstand the process to ‘save’ them?

God, it was all so sick. This was almost industrialized torture, only with a twist of religion to.

There was no arguing with these people. You couldn’t convince a zealot with anything but force.

My eyes narrowed at Rhogann, and I stood up straighter. “I’m not going to ask again. Where are you keeping your victims, creature?”

This time, the silence in the hall was far more oppressive. It lasted long enough for me to become aware of a faint shifting coming from all around us.

The Karvokar had begun to stand from their kneeling positions. They weren’t doing anything.

Just…standing and watching.

Eventually, Rhogann broke the silence. “Oh, there’s no need to ask anymore, o’ misguided heretic,” They said softly. “You shall meet them soon.”

Slowly, the mutated, skinless existence of Rhogann the Unveiled…

Raised its right arm to point directly at me.

“Take them, my faithful flock. They must be enlightened.”

In the space of an eyeblink, my Netherim pistol snapped up to point at the Renarch’s head, and I pulled the trigger.
