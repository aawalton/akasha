“Fuck! Fuck!” Andrew stomped on the floor and threw his spear in anger. He had lost half of his men for nothing. The dungeon core was shattered, reduced to nothing more than a piece of glass.

“Andrew, calm down. I know how you feel, but there is no way around it. We can simply try again, right?” Sanders said.

Meanwhile, James brought out his new toy—the Icarus Wing. It was not a wing that flapped like a bird, but a jetpack like those in superhero movies. He assumed this wing was similar to his golem arm and legs, and he was right. This wing was not shouldered like a backpack; it had two needles that would bore through his back and fuse with his spine.

James winced at the six needles that jutted out from the wing. It was not the pain that made him hesitate, but the usefulness of the wing.

System, how long is the cooldown?

The Icarus Wing needed 10 seconds of downtime before it could be re-used again.

That changed a lot of things. Ten seconds was a short time. James then understood that this wing was not supposed to be used for flying, but for jumping. Right now, James relied on his sword skill to lunge at his enemy. With this wing, his lunge could cover more distance—possibly over 100 meters instead of ten meters like his Vorpal Lunge.

But before James could equip the wing, someone touched the teleportation sphere, teleporting them to the waiting room.

“Who was it? Who teleported us? I haven’t inspected the goods, for fuck’s sake! Couldn’t you wait?” James spat. Memories of that trial dungeon where Kaina teleported him before he could plunder that angel resurfaced in his mind.

“I did. I thought we should do it on Earth. Who knows what other surprises this dungeon might have for us?” Xiaoming said. “Sorry I didn’t consult you first.”

“...Ah. Sorry. You’re right.” James calmed down. If he thought about it, Xiaoming’s decision was right. They should leave the dungeon first before handling the goods. Who knew whether there were other monsters around?

He then sat down and pressed the wing to his back while tightening the belt on his waist. Then, a sharp pain surged from his spine through his brain. He could feel the sharp spikes coil around his spine. An electric shock ripped through his brain.

“Argh!!” James groaned as the wing etched itself into place.

“James, are you okay?” Sanders asked him, worried.

“Nah, it was just a little bit of pain attaching the golem wing. What did you get?” James took a few deep breaths and asked Sanders.

“I got Crystal Seer. It will embed itself into my arm, and from it, I can see and detect all kinds of mana around me.” Sanders showed him his arm. There, a blue crystal embedded itself deep into his wrist. The function reminded James of his right eye that he got from the trial dungeon, but this one was an upgraded version, as it could detect mana from his blind spot.

“Good for you. You will be a fine mage,” James nodded. He then observed his surroundings. The other survivors also inspected their rewards. Some got golem arms, some got legs, some got horns. Various types of golem parts were given out by the system for defeating that last boss. Andrew got a pair of boot; judging from its appearance, James suspected that the boot had a similar ability to his wing, allowing users to jump or even fly for a short time.

As the loading screen went to full, the world flashed white, and they were thrown onto the desert sand unique to Australia.

“Congratulations! I see that the dungeon has been cleared. Is this all who have returned? It must have been perilous, huh?” Two soldiers greeted James and the team.

“The dungeon is cleared, but we didn’t get the core.” Andrew sighed with a bitter face. “How is the UN? Did they manage to cull the red dungeons?”

“Meh, the world has gone to shit, man. You’ve been away for only a month, but three cities have been nuked by the UN. The red dungeons were like those horror movies—zombies were around, sometimes demons, sometimes fantasy creatures. That was easy, but what about locusts? Then exploding toxic rats, a gazillion of them. Then there was a dungeon that spawned ghosts. Those things were passed through tanks. The UN had to nuke the city to exterminate them.”

“I see. Well, if we kill the boss, the dungeons will be freed. monsters could no longer spawn from i How many red dungeons have we freed?” Sanders was optimistic. Even if they were to nuke all dungeons, as long as humans win, dungeons will decrease and Earth will eventually be freed.

“Those ethereal monsters can only be dealt with using elemental damage. Try a flamethrower or a stun gun. Explosions, shrapnel, and bullets work little against them,” James said, pulling from his experience in the previous dungeon against the god of pestilence.

“Nukes do wonders against them. It seems the UN is preparing more nukes for these red dungeons. There have been 15 red dungeons in your absence, and more than a thousand to go. Will you participate in the defense against red dungeons?” the soldiers said.

“I need to make a call.” James was about to head to his motel, but a man in a suit came over to him in a jeep. Unlike those from the guild, he was less formal.

“Here you go, Sir James. I am a butler sent by Ryan to monitor the dungeon you are conquering.” The butler handed him his phone.

“Ryan, how is it going there?”

“Sir James! May I guess you had another successful dive? You might not know, but the UN is preparing for a war against dungeons. Recruitment posters are everywhere.”

“I see.”

“Ah. By the way, Mr. James, there is a red dungeon I would like you to conquer. The government is thinking of sending another nuke, but that town has hospitals and resources. Do you think you could change the government’s mind? The war on that front is not going very well,” Ryan asked in a roundabout way.

“Huh?” James was bewildered at Ryan’s request. Since when did he care about hospitals and resources?

“I am sorry. Let me be blunt then. My cousin is a refugee from that town. The government said the nuke wouldn’t affect the next town, but you know how trustworthy a government is. As long as we can kill the boss, the town should be freed, right?”

“I assume tanks, guns, and bombs are not effective? What are the monsters?”

“Yes. The monsters are liquid lava.” James tilted his head, but he knew how weird monsters could get, so he didn’t question it.

“And the government thinks nukes can solve everything?”

“They thought they just needed more firepower. Bombs and tanks could kill these lava monsters—they are just resilient. A monster can be killed after bombarding it with 200 artillery shells,” Ryan said. “I have some videos from Silas.”

“You can’t just nuke everything! There are still tens of thousands of these dungeons left! If you nuke everything, the Earth would become uninhabitable by the time the last dungeon popped!” Andrew roared. James had left the phone on loudspeaker; Andrew’s outburst was shared by many. Nukes are not a solution, just a band-aid.

“That’s what’s keeping the UN from launching any in my hometown, Mr. James. But if those monsters were to expand, we would have to do it regardless.”

“Alright. I got it. But you guys owe me one. Now, how do I get there? What about you?” James glanced at Xiaoming and Bernard.

“We won’t. Sorry, but I already have enough monsters for the day,” Bernard and Xiaoming said.

“I will go with you. I am against the way they do things,” Andrew said.

“Of course, Mr. Andrew. I am always in your debt. I shall arrange an airplane for you to Mexico.”

“Oh, you were an expat? Didn’t know that,” James said. Ryan looked like a thoroughbred American to him.

“My grandfather was, Mr. James. Please don’t tell anyone.”

“I don’t care about that. Just send me the ticket,” James said.

“Understood,” Ryan said.

“Alright! The dungeon core was unfortunately destroyed, but we cleared another medium dungeon! We can consider this a victory!” Andrew rallied his men.

“Yeah!” The men cheered with him. James was amazed at how fast he had gathered himself. He had been moping a few moments ago, and now he stood in front with that reliable persona back. Motioning with his hand, James called for a taxi with his butler.

Wading through the airport was less than pleasurable. Now that the guild wasn’t backing him, he had to go through customs. Alas, with the world having gone through some shit, people unexpectedly cared less about him bringing guns and swords as long as they were checked as luggage, not carry-on. But they still had to go through customs.

Through the small window of the airplane, James could see that the city of Tucson was on fire. Red-hot lava flowed through the streets, and monsters, similar to golems, slithered along. Tanks and artillery lined up behind, firing barrages of explosive rounds, pummeling the monsters into mere molten goo.

However, the monsters were not defeated. They merely reformed and marched on, plowing through any barricade the soldiers had erected as if they were mere mounds of dirt.

“I don’t think my magic will work on that guy,” Sanders muttered.

“Ice magic it is. Anyone here with ice magic or an ice weapon?”

“I can change the enchantment. What about you?” a soldier with upgraded beginner weapons said. It reminded James of his old lightning axe.

“I have an ice axe. If ice or water is their weakness, this will be easy. I have ice magic as well.”

“Me too!” another of Andrew’s men said. “This ice golem hand can shoot an ice beam!”

James’ volunteers also had a variety of elemental weapons. One of them was a Bow of Everwinter, which could convert its bow skill into elemental damage.

“Alright. We are landing. Let’s get to work, men,” Andrew said. “The UN said they will support us. We’ll test our weapons against the smaller enemies first. If they prove effective, we’ll go through the city center and kill the boss!”

Silas, being the omniscient bureaucrat he was, knew James and Andrew were coming and let them do whatever they wanted. A jeep carried them from the airport right into the enemy lines.

This time, James finally had the chance to test his ice axe—a weapon that had proved ineffective in the previous dungeon.
