The lanterns swung from their hooks and all of the children were screaming. Each passing second only made the rumbling grow until the sky split. A searing column of gold and black flame tore down from the clouds and struck just north of where we all were.

With it, the ground shook. Many of the villagers simply fell over. My knees almost buckled from beneath me. The large fire in the square winked out, and that column folded in on itself. From its center, a beast rose.

The beast was a bird, at first glance. It was tall, at least twice the size of the nearest huts, and even its shadow was almost enough to cover the square whole. Half of the bird's feathers shone with a brilliant gold, almost too bright to look upon. The other half were a sickly black that looked almost wet. Where they met, the feathers seemed to smoke. Drops fell from those black feathers, and where they fell the air hissed.

Then there were the eyes. It had five of them, three of them on one side, and two on the other. All of the eyes were different sizes, moving in different directions. When the beast's large beak opened to let out a cry, the sound that came out was strange. Less like a monster attacking a village, and more like a thing in great pain.

I had heard cries like that before. I had once heard a Goddess weep, and it had sounded less broken than this. This thing before us was a Divine Beast of some kind, but I had never seen one that looked like this.

I knew this creature. Or at least I knew what it might have once been. It was a phoenix. They had been little more than myth, even in my time.

The beast took a step forward, and its neck moved up, left, down, right. All in jerky motions. The pupils of its eyes contracted and expanded, none of them in sync with each other.

Hamel's two guards moved before I did. The first was a broad man, one whom I had seen patrolling the length of the wall in the morning. He charged forward with his sword outstretched and a small mark flaring on his arm. He struck the creature. His sword bounced off. The phoenix's head did not even turn to regard him.

It moved a single wing lazily, and then the man was flying. He crashed into a nearby wall, and did not rise.

The other guard was smarter, or perhaps more cowardly. She threw out her hands and out came a great rush of air. All it did was ruffle some feathers. The woman cursed and looked at the villagers. "Run, damn you!" She was already running. "Take the southern path! Fuck, move!"

That cry was enough to knock the villagers out of their haze. Then they began to move, all at once. Mothers swept up their children. Old men hobbled. Some fell, and others moved to pick them up. I saw Tom lead Martha around by the arm. Only then did I realize that Sara's hand was no longer in mine. I looked, and saw her running with the rushing throng. Good. That was good. She was smart.

The beast still had not moved from where it stood. It simply watched the people rushing away, as if their running mattered not. Then it raised its head and let out another cry, one that was somehow more pained than the one before it.

Then there was light, and enough of it to drown Hamel's square whole. The black and golden feathers both ignited. The gold ones glowed white, the dark one glowed black. I had seen Hellfire and Balefire- the two strongest flames the system had. I had burned cities with flames hotter than the core of any mortal furnace. The flames I saw now were worse. They should not have existed at all.

The Hero rushed to my side, sword in hand. When she had managed to retrieve it, I could not say. "It's another Divine Beast," she said. Her voice was steady, her hands were not.

"If only it were just that." I murmured. I had seen something in the shape of a Cerberus. I had seen a Divine Beast whose skin ran with black corruption. Both of those had been wrong. There was no word for what stood in front of us now.

"They'll all need time to run. They have a head start but the crowd will slow them all down," she looked around. "We are going to buy them some time. That's all."

"That's all? I intend to kill the thing." I bared my teeth and put all the bravado I did not feel into the words.

Ash looked at me, but I was already running forward before she could argue. Mana filled my legs, the weak mana of this world. I still crossed the distance in three long strides.

The beast had ignored the other guards. It did not ignore me. I reached its leg just as those many eyes focused on me. I pressed my right hand into one leg, and channeled my mana. The Requiem answered, painfully slow. The scales beneath my fingers began to grey and the flesh softened. The beast's weight shifted. It was working.

Then I felt something stir beneath the flesh. I pulled my hand back right as the beast's leg ignited in a burst of flame. Gold flame reached the decayed flesh. It cleansed the rot, and in its place now were fresh scales, brighter than the ones I had just taken with the Requiem.

And the beast attacked. One of its great wings came for me. I darted beneath it, rolled and came up to the creature's other leg. I pressed the Requiem into the phoenix's flesh again. The power was too slow. The creature's other wing came down. I threw myself sideways. The wingtip caught my shoulder and spun me, making me hit the dirt and roll on the ground. I saw Ash during the roll.

She darted in from the other side, her blade pressed to the phoenix's wing. It drew a smooth line across, even as the mark on her left flared. And with it the wing severed and fell in complete silence. One moment it was attached, and the next, it lay on the ground. The creature wailed. Its wing twitched on the ground like a severed limb. For a single moment, I thought we had a chance.

The twitching wing burst into flame and smoke, and that smoke crawled back towards the beast, where the wing had originally been. Smoke curled and twisted, and when it was done, the beast now had two wings where it had before had only one. One was a brilliant gold, and the other was a sickly black. And the wings were wrong. They bent in a direction that simply made no sense.

The beast reached for Ash, but she had already moved to its other side, to its other wing. The wing barely even hit the ground before it started to burn. The smoke rose and returned. The creature that stood before us now had four wings instead of two, all of them wrong.

Ash's voice was shaking. "Lily. We can't do this."

My shoulder ached where the wing had clipped me, but I was on my feet. "We will. You strike it again and I'll hold longer this time."

"You're not listening!" Ash stepped back. Her sword hung at her side. "We are making this worse. It'll keep coming back."

"Then we kill all of it at once."

"With what?" She demanded. "With what?"

I did not have an answer. I knew there was none. I had my pride, but I was not a fool. I wanted to run. I wasn't even in my battle dress, I was in rags, and the closest thing to a weapon I could get my hands on was a stick. There was no shame in running when the odds were impossible.

Truthfully, I did not understand how this thing hadn't already killed us.

The creature had turned away from us now and its four wings were spread wide. The fires across its body flared, gold and black. It was looking south toward the fleeing villagers, who were practically stumbling over themselves now to flee.

"We can still run," Ash said. That was not what I would have expected from a Hero.

"We can't run," I said distantly. The words came from me before I even knew to voice them. I knew what this fool Hero was doing. She was going to ask me to run, and then give herself to some noble sacrifice. Heroes were ever in a rush to get themselves killed.

Ash stared at me, really stared. "Suppose we can't."

I was already moving again.

Ash was right, the crowd had slowed them all down. Most of the villagers crowded around the western edge of the village.

I saw the problem at once: too many people and too few paths. The crowd had crushed together in many places. I could see Tom near the front, shoving people through. Martha was beside him.

For the first time, the creature moved towards the villagers. And its gait was wrong. A third leg had begun to sprout from its belly, and the thing was half-formed. For a second I thought it might simply fall over. It did not.

I threw mana into my legs and ran past it. "Don't you dare look away from me you vermin!" I screamed, loud enough that the surrounding forest must have heard too.

I did not know why the beast was ignoring us, did not understand why it hadn't killed everyone and everything already. I just knew that I had to get its attention. I had to-

I saw her. Sara. The girl was not with the crowd. She stood twenty feet apart from them, and she was sitting on the ground. Her hands were over her eyes, and her eyes were shut.

The bird creature's head swung toward the crowd. Then one of its eyes, the smallest, found the girl sitting on the dirt.

It took a step toward her and let out a cry.

My body moved and I did not decide. I did not consider the odds of victory or defeat nor weigh my regal life on a scale against a single human girl. I did not consider that I was in rags, and not in my battle dress.

Sara sat there, and a creature was walking toward her. I was between them the next instant. The creature's beak opened and the fire gathered in its throat. I could see it building, gold and black flame twisting together.

"You foolish girl! Run damn you!"

I spread my arms as wide as it would go, covering as much ground as I could. I used mana, all of the true mana I still had within me. The blast crossed the gap and hit me in the chest.

My feet left the ground. I spent no moment suspended in the air. I hit the earth on my back and skidded before coming to a stop.

Something was broken inside me. I could feel it. Not my mana core -not that, at least. Ribs, perhaps? The pain was a white, screaming thing that swallowed every thought. Without my ancient mana, without a demon's inherent resistance to fire, I would be dead. No, if I had been any other demon, I would have been dead all the same.

I lay there. Sara ran beside me. She was crying but she was alive. Why did she not run?

The creature stepped closer and I could feel the heat of it, see the shadow of it, even with my vision swimming. It was going to strike again. A figure ran in front of me, just as I had run in front of the girl. It was Ash.

The Hero was between me and the creature, her sword raised. It was the only weapon she had. Though, what good a sword would do against a thing that fed on its own death, even I could not say. "Get up!" she said, voice ragged. "Lysanthia, get up!"

I wanted to. The pain pinned me to the earth as surely as the creature's talon might have. Ash looked down at me. Her eyes were wide.

It fired at her, a smaller ball of flame she cut with her blade. Another, then another. Her blade was glowing crimson. It seemed she still had some of her sword aura left after all. Why the beast did not use its earlier attack, I did not know.

"Why?" she asked. Ash's breathing was labored.

The word made no sense. Nothing made sense. The world was pain and the sound of a child crying beside me. I could not feel my legs. Distantly, I felt the hum of my Cradle. It did little for me now.

"Why did you do that?" Ash's eyes were wide as she looked back and not with the anger I had seen that night in the forest. "You should have run. You could have run. Why did you-"

"I don't know." The words came out broken -barely above a whisper.

"You don't-"

"I don't know." I said, coughing louder this time. It hurt to speak. I turned my head, just enough to see Sara beside me. Her eyes were open now, and wet. She was staring at me. It seemed she had stopped crying, and in its place was a wide eyed, soundless horror.

"She's a child," I whispered. "She has only just started. The world-" I coughed again, and this time there was blood. "-is big and…."

I tried to breathe. "My life is old."

I could not find the rest of the words, could barely understand the few I had forced out of me -had been forced out of me, by this moment. A life barely begun might be worth more than one that had run its long and bloody course. Even if it was mine. No, perhaps… especially if it was mine.

Sara was staring at me, and she was crying. A girl who made vases from mud for a stranger she thought was pretty. A girl whose father had taught her to shape clay, and who had not shaped it again until I had come along. A girl who had put her small hand in mine.

Ash's stance shifted. The creature had come closer now. Its beak opened, and it was getting ready to attack again. Ash faced it alone, as Heroes often did. She would lose. She had to know that, but she stood in front of it anyway.

I wanted to help. I could not even rise. I reached inside my core and found it all but empty. I had a third of my reserves left, and all of those reserves were weak mana. I could not help her. I could not do anything.

And then I paused, because I did have two other things. I looked at my arms, at the black and white Spark, both of which had not done a thing in this battle.

I reached for them, and gave them everything I had left.

And then I did a thing I had never done. I asked, not demanded.

Please. I do not wish for a fragile beginning to end.

It was a prayer, and I did not know who I prayed to. Something answered anyway. And then warmth came from my left. A warmth I had only just come to know. It was the warmth from a healed cut. From a mended fence post. From a small and pathetic fire. Most of all, from a small hand held in mine.

The Cradle's Spark became a single white Line that stretched to my elbow.

The phoenix took another step forward towards Ash. Towards Sara. I wished to destroy it. Not for pride nor to gain something. I wanted to destroy it because something else deserved to live. And so I asked again.

Please.

This was, perhaps, the first time I had wanted to destroy something to protect another.

And then there was cold, and I knew it well. It was the land of Ashia, drowned in forever darkness. It was the Dwarves, who would forge no more masterworks. It was the corpse of the great dragon Ozriel, whom I had swatted from the sky.

The Requiem stirred. The black Spark spread and became a Line that seared down my forearm and stopped.

Both marks pulsed together. Warmth spread through my left and cold spread through my right. Strength returned, some echo of it.

I rose.
