Solomon sat there in silence for a moment. Sen had never experienced having magic stolen from him, but he’d seen the effects it could have on people.

“Asteroid Eye,” Solomon said. His words were empty of mana. He nodded. “Well, I had to try.” Solomon sighed and rose to his feet. His eyes fell on Sen. “You can see it too then? The Fate Lines?”

Sen’s brow furrowed. “Fate Lines? Are you talking about the threads?” he asked.

“Is that what you call them? The connections that weave all reality together? Those lines we magicians tug to change the nature of the world? Threads?” Solomon asked.

From what Sen knew, knowledge of the threads of magic wasn’t common. Jonathan Brightsail had mentioned only speaking with two others about them. One of them being the Shield Lord. He doubted Solomon was the second. It made sense then that others who saw them would come up with their own names for them. “Why do you call them that? Fate Lines?” Sen asked. Perhaps it was just a name. Perhaps there was meaning. Either way, opportunities to talk with others who could see them were rare.

Solomon held his palm toward Sen. “Have you ever had your palm read?” He pointed to a line that started just above his wrist and crawled up toward the base of his middle finger. “That is the fate line. One of many that those who believe in such things read.” Solomon chuckled. “If magic comes from the System, are the lines we see then not lines of the Hand of Fate? Are we not reading the lines of the world itself when we close our eyes and study those paths?” he asked.

“And do you see you patterns in those lines?” Sen asked. Solomon’s approach toward the threads seemed more philosophical than grounded in logic. That didn’t mean there wasn’t wisdom in his words.

Solomon looked down. “A pattern? If one exists, I have not seen it.” He looked up and stared at the stars in the night sky. “The Fate Lines I once looked at for guidance were burned away by another. As was all I had. I lost my way until one day I turned toward the stars and found a new path.”

“Hunting those responsible.” Sen stated. He’d already seen that much in Solomon’s memories. It seemed Solomon’s magic had been altered by the same people who killed his family. He’d started over, layering new threads over the grave of his old ones. “You’re one of the strongest people I’ve ever fought. Why is someone of your caliber working for a man like Thessus? At this stage, wealth and power are things you could take for yourself whenever you wanted. Why stoop to work for a man like that?” A mage who could create a spell as powerful as Ceres could easily seize all the gold and influence they wanted. It just didn’t make sense.

Though he knew part of the answer, didn’t he? Solomon’s memories of the fight were clear. He intended to stop Ceres from destroying the town if it had overwhelmed his opponent. He’d been prepared to kill Gosen, but not to wipe out all of Vitree. That changed things. When powerful mages fought, they accepted they would put their lives on the line. He wouldn’t hold a grudge over something like that. If Solomon had truly threatened the town without remorse, Sen wouldn’t have a choice but to kill him. But as it were, there was a chance they could avoid coming to blows again. Provided he had a good explanation for siding against the dreadbeasts in the first place.

“You may not believe me, but Thessus is the key to conquering a greater evil. Even greater than the things he was involved in. For what it’s worth, I would not have abandoned the dreadbeasts to their fate forever. I just needed to maintain things for now,” Solomon said. “I needed his trust.”

Sen scowled. “He’s not a mage, and his forces are unimpressive. You could’ve taken whatever knowledge he had by force.”

“No. It’s true I could force Thessus’ hand, but those he’s connected with would see through such things. He works with men far more powerful than I,” Solomon said.

That was interesting. Solomon was a lord-class mage at the peak of his rank. Were the people he was talking about royal-level mages? Prince or King class?

“Who exactly are you hunting?” Sen asked. “I heard Meteor Haven was a group of cult hunters. Was it a cult that took your family and magic from you?”

Solomon sighed. “I don’t know. I believe so, but I don’t know. All I remember is his mana signature, and the color of the man’s eyes.” He looked up and stared at Sen. “Red eyes. Like yours.”

“Eyes like mine are rare,” Sen said.

“That they are,” Solomon said. “The one I’m hunting, he is a great threat to the world. Many good men and mages have died at his hands. I witnessed it myself. If he weren’t so dangerous, I never would’ve been okay with…” He trailed off, his eyes moving in the direction of the dreadbeasts.

“A word of advice, Solomon. The destination does not vindicate the journey. It matters how you get there. Whatever you’re fighting for, whatever good you’re trying to do in the world, it matters. You don’t get to save the world on the backs of slaves,” Sen said.

“It was a temporary measure…but you’re right.” Solomon smiled, but it didn’t quite reach his eyes. “Of course, we always tell ourselves that, don’t we? That we’ll undo the bad we did to do good.” Solomon returned Sen’s gaze. “Perhaps you are wearing another disguise, or perhaps you are wise beyond your years…but you’re right.”

There was something about Solomon that made Sen want to spare him. Even from the beginning of the fight, he’d promised to take something from the man instead of killing him. Sen had been a little cruel toward Solomon’s guild mates, but to be fair, they were trying to kill him. Even then, he hadn’t taken their lives despite the fact he easily could’ve. No, after speaking with Solomon and getting a glimpse of his memories, Sen didn’t think violence was the way forward.

When Sen didn’t speak, Solomon took the initiative. “You won our fight. How would you like to proceed? I’m afraid I can’t offer you my life. I’m not willing to part with that.”

“Call off your people and leave Vitree,” Sen said.

Solomon nodded. “That’s easily done. What of the dreadbeasts? Is there any way we can help?”

“No. We’ve got it from here.”

“This is just one town. There are many—” Solomon stopped talking as he scanned Sen’s face. “Oh. I see.” The man suddenly realized the depth of the operation he had intruded upon. He nodded slowly before looking to the Asteroid Eye on Sen’s forehead. “It’s a good spell. Take care of it.”

Sen was merciful. He didn’t believe Solomon needed to die, but he would not go back on the promise he’d made. He would not part with this power. Solomon still needed to pay a price for his actions tonight.

“Should we cross paths again, I should like to hear how it has helped you,” Solomon said.

Sen smiled faintly. “Very well. Good luck finding the one you hunt, Solomon.”

Solomon nodded. He held out a hand towards the East. “Meteor Message.”

A small meteor flew towards the battlefield where the Khans and members of Meteor Haven fought.

Sen raised a brow. “Shouldn’t there be another of those?” Bartholomew and Drussan were in a different direction.

Solomon laughed. “No. Bart would just ignore it. I’ll go fetch him myself.” He nodded one last time and then disappeared from the rooftop. Even without Asteroid Eye, his base stats and other abilities were still far stronger than most mages. It would still take quite a lot to take the leader of Meteor Haven down.

Sen focused his Asteroid Eye on the Khans, then jumped off the rooftop. He had a sniveling slaver turned dreadbeast to collect.

Micah wasn’t far.

Vlay

If Simeon struck Vlay, Vlay felt it on his chest.

If Vlay struck Simeon, Vlay felt it on his chest.

The boy wasn’t even defending himself anymore. He held his arms out with a cocky smile on his face. “Go on! Hit me some more!” Simeon said. Blood still poured from wounds and dribbled from the corner of his mouth from injuries he’d sustained earlier in the fight.

Vlay knew it wasn’t smart, but he obliged him. Hitting things made him feel better. It cleared his head. So he unleashed a barrage of punches at Simeon’s body, despite the fact that each one struck him through the impact point instead of hurting the brat.

He cursed and took a step back, huffing in air. Vlay could fight for days, but taking so many hits in one spot was brutal. Since Vlay had nearly dodged it, the impact spell wasn’t exactly on his skin. It hovered several inches away. He tried slipping a hand under the mark and punching Simeon.

To his surprise, he felt the strike on the back of his hand.

He blinked.

That had worked?
