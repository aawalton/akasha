Gone for a couple of days, and the mission of destroying an enemy guild turned into joining them in an alliance. Sure, protecting Earth was a good cause, but still. It didn't sit right with Luke, but Milla wouldn't hear of it. She was scared. They were all scared. It was understandable, of course, but Luke found that, in the end, he was not. Taking a step back and looking at the situation from a global perspective, things were dire, sure, but if there was any fear in him, it was for those around him, not for himself. An alliance was good, but not one with ISG. They got their second chance, and they blew it, as far as Luke was concerned. If Milla wanted him to make an appearance at some meeting, he would, though. He owed her that much, at least.

The school bus was gone, along with everyone in it, having returned to the guild headquarters. Through the Integrated Shop, Luke had found a repair option for the Shelter, and it now stood as good as new. Like most things in the shop, it was pricey, but he had a lot of stuff to sell. Decluttering the inventory was freeing, in a way, and made him feel light-footed even if the items didn't have actual weight. Being a guild member in good standing, he donated a few items to the guild, like the different swords he'd acquired fighting demons, a night-sight bracelet he didn't remember looting, along with some other scraps. The corpses he sold, netting him a tidy sum of credits. That left monster cores and a couple of infernal cores and even Meta-Hearts. Looking at the latter stirred uncomfortable feelings in his chest, and he ended up handing them over to Smudge. Feeding the remains of people to your pet might sound like it was in poor taste, but... yeah, maybe it was, but it felt even more wrong to let them go to waste by selling them or even throwing them away. This way, the little voidling would grow stronger.

Once they were by themselves, Smudge jumped up on the bed with Luke, wagging his tail. After eating the previous cores, he'd grown a little more, but what was more impressive was how he was now able to look so much like an actual dog. Or the outline of one, at least. It was a little weird, but he enjoyed having the strange pet back, and he had a feeling their connection would be important once they began dealing with the void in a more direct way. Luom had all but told him the next step on their journey of integration would come soon, so they needed to be ready.

Since he'd found a trace of Hannah out there and she, in no uncertain terms, had told him to stay away until she was done with whatever she was doing, Luke found himself without much in the way of pressing demands. The Singers, of course, but that needed to wait until he met with the DCP people, or whatever they were called. Other than that, the void mana in his Meta-Heart needed dealing with, and that would eat up a lot of time, as well as experimenting with infernal mana, but Luke felt like stretching his legs a little and doing something physical. Too much time had been spent sitting around and delving into the metaphysical realm. Nodding to himself, he grinned and stroked Smudge's back, marveling at how fur-like it felt as the strands of nothingness passed between his fingers.

"Hey, Smudge. Want to go for a walk in a dungeon?"

The little bastard whined and rolled over to his side, his stomach growling.

"There'll be more monster cores for you."

That made him shoot up and out of the bed, and before Luke knew it, his friend stood by the door to the Shelter, wagging his tail like he thought he was an actual dog. Hopefully, he wouldn't get fined for walking him in the city without a leash.

Luke: "Hey, Ray, want to go demolish a dungeon?"

Ray: "Our level difference is too big now, big fella. If you go in first, I'm dead meat. With the other way around, you'll get bored."

Luke: "I could hang back and heal you."

Ray: "Hah. You've never been able to hang back and heal, not even at the start."

Ray: "Besides, I have a job I'm doing later that Milla put me on after I demanded it."

Luke: "What job?"

Ray: "I'm going through a portal."

Luke: "You're what?"

Ray: "We need to learn more about the Singers. I'm going there to see what they're about."

Ray: "And if I get the chance, maybe I'll kill a general, king, or whatever, or two."

Ray: "Don't tell your sister I said that last part."

Luke: "Sounds dangerous."

Ray: "Danger is my middle name!"

Luke: "Your middle name is Mortimer."

Ray: "Shut up."

A no from Ray, then.

Luke: "Curtis!"

Curtis: "No."

Luke: "What? I was just saying hi."

Curtis: "I am following a group of Singers a good distance west of the city. Scouts, I think."

Curtis: "I don't want to be disturbed."

Luke: "Well, all right then."

Penny wasn't in the city at the moment, according to Milla, and Mateo had left after making sure Luke was well following the episode with the void mana. Luke understood the man wanted to be with his family, but he wished the Lancer had stayed a few more days.

Luke: "Hey, Lemira. I'm going into a dungeon with Smudge. Want to come?"

Lemira: "Luke! Good to see your recovery is progressing well. Are you sure you should enter a dungeon this soon after what you just experienced?"

Luke: "Probably not, but Smudge was whining about going for a walk. You know how pets are."

Lemira: "I am not sure that I follow, but unfortunately, I cannot join you at this moment. Our guildmistress has issued orders that we are all to give extra attention to our own progression, so I am engaged in a dungeon with another group."

Luke: "That's a good thing, I think, yeah. Next time, then."

Lemira: "If you wish it, I will return and go with you instead. You have saved my life more than once, and I will be forever in your debt."

Luke: "Don't do that. Stay with your group."

Lemira: "Very well. Next time."

Milla was keeping everyone active and engaged. It was a good strategy, and not only because people would need to get stronger if they wanted to keep up and have a shot at taking down the Singers, but also because it helped with the pesky emotions that had been creeping up in everyone ever since the Singer blast that knocked out the emotion blocker thing Guide put in place. As long as you were fighting, you weren't thinking about what you were doing as much, kind of like desensitization training. After the first few hours of struggling with mental clarity, Luke himself found he could manage it well enough. Decisions that had been obvious before, like who needed killing and who didn't, took a split second longer than with the block, but maybe that wasn't such a bad thing. As he saw with the demon battles, his ability to fight hadn't been affected much, if at all. Maybe that said something about him as a person, but at this point, he wasn't going to complain.

"Looks like it's just you and me, boy," Luke said, looking down at Smudge, who walked right next to him.

With everyone either injured or out, Luke found himself without a party. A healer without a party, what a novelty. Ray hadn't been wrong in saying that staying in the back wasn't Luke's thing, and this wouldn't be the first time he attempted to solo a dungeon. Last time, Hannah ended up joining him, and he blew up an undead dragon with a nuke. Hopefully, this time would be a little less eventful.

Pushing away the worry about Hannah being gone, Luke spread his senses in all directions, searching for and finding a portal. A welcome surprise was that he was now able to tell if someone had entered through the portal or not. The portals with people in them gave a different reading. Not that he got a status for every portal or anything, but it felt crowded in a way that, like with most of these things, he found difficult to put into words. The number of active portals was low compared to the lead-up to the system event, but with so few Integrated still around in New York, the supply outweighed the demand.

Sensing the portals was not the same as reading the system weave, but they were related concepts, and in doing one, he reached a sense of the other. Walking down the street with Smudge in tow, Luke felt light and airy, as if he might start levitating at any moment and move up and into the system weave, becoming one with it. Reading it was easier, too, and it was as if he had reached and crashed through some barrier, leading to a breakthrough. Was it perhaps the actual barrier between The Greater System and the void? Or the pure exposure to The Greater System Weave? He didn't know, but the sensation filled him with a confidence that had been missing for a little while. If he wasn't careful, though, that could turn into arrogance, and then he'd be on thin ice.

"There it is, Smudge," Luke said, pointing down the street.

A portal stood open next to what had once been a clothing store. Motes of light swirled in front of it in inviting patterns. Luke's shoes on the pavement were the only sound, except for some gulls screaming at each other on a roof somewhere. The sun was out, but the weather was still pleasant without being too warm. He drew in a deep breath as he approached the entrance to another dungeon, feeling a sense of appreciation for where life had taken him. A lot of messed-up things had happened, and more were sure to follow, but for this brief moment in time, all was well.

Reaching out to touch the portal before walking through it with Smudge in tow, Luke froze as a message appeared in his interface.

Curtis (Guild): "I killed them all, but I was too late."

Milla (Guild): "Curtis?"

Curtis (Guild): "They weren't scouts."

Curtis (Guild): "The Singers brought out a weapon and launched it before I could reach them."

Milla (Guild): "What are you saying, Curtis?"

Curtis (Guild): "Get out of New York."

Curtis (Guild): "You have to get out now!"
