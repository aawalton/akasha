Staring at what was left of her previous ‘tomb’ — or containment area, as she hadn’t been truly dead — Azure turned off her flight spell and sank down on top of a stone pillar, feeling suddenly much heavier. It was all too appropriate. Her sobbing abated, leaving her with a simple, hollow loathing. It felt like something of an old friend.

Chasing after the truth for how long? Now I have it, and wish I didn’t. She scoffed, shaking her head. “Whatever I’ve suffered, I deserved worse.”

“No one gets what they deserve,” Six offered idly. “Just what they take or don’t. Most take all that they can get; it’s just that some have it easier than others.”

Azure didn’t bother answering, as she didn’t want to hear philosophy right then.

Deathsting flew from one side of the lip above to the other, turning around and glancing at her. “You’re simply more formidable than you realized. No wonder I accepted you; you should do the same.”

Azure found herself shaking with laughter at that, more in bitterness than amusement. She still didn’t say anything, and the brief laughter died.

Her mind continued mining backward, recalling all those visions she’d had. Visions she’d sought out. Defying her mother and losing her tail, seeking out forbidden transformations, betraying her husband, fleeing to the tower, being tortured and driven mad inside, unleashing power within herself, gaining the aid of her angry, inner self, twisting reality to make herself into a monster fit to take ‘revenge’…

It wasn’t a prior self. I’m a mass murderer bundled up into a more aesthetic form, a mutant spiritual construct. It’s still these hands that did those things. To think, I thought I went from flesh to crystal… it was all a farce… It was just a little dial I’d forgotten how to turn…

She lifted an open hand up to stare at her palm. Were the lines the same as they once were, thousands and thousands of years ago?

When she’d gone below, in Itha Ranon, she’d literally faced the husband she’d tricked and abandoned, and his words had summoned up something from deep within her, not a past life. She could still taste the vicious resentment and hatred that overtook her in that moment, which suffused her to the bone, right before the vision took hold.

When she’d faced ‘the demon’ in the darkness, as stirred up by the oracle of that place and time, she had really faced herself, not a past life shadow. That was entirely why her victory had meant ascension. It felt somehow cheap, now. Unearned. She hadn’t understood anything, and yet she’d reached enlightenment?

It felt as if she’d gained that, just so she would not break against the truth she ‘needed,’ to really understand things. But she didn’t feel better about knowing, just worse.

“Azure-” Six began patiently.

“Please!” Azure snapped back, glaring over at the calm countenance of the gorgon. “I don’t need your platitudes, and I don’t need a pep talk! Save it.”

“But you clearly do-”

“Shut up!”

Six just talked right over, as Azure sighed in aggravation. “-Which is why I will start with a question: why did you begin this journey?” Azure didn’t answer, just shook her head balefully. Six continued, “I will answer for you, I suppose. You began it for the same reason I prepared for aeons to be here: to conclude reality. To make a satisfactory end.” Six pointed a finger. “You knew you were not strong enough mentally and spiritually to face that end as you were, so you sought out the truth like iron longs for the whetstone.”

Azure snorted. “A depressed, self-hating sack of shit with an identity crisis does not make for a sharp sword.”

“It can make for a wicked knife in the back,” Deathsting quipped. Azure ignored him.

Six had the gall to merely shrug. “You’ll be fine.”

Azure threw her hands up, staring at Six in incredulity. “Fine?! How can I even look Samantha in the eye after this?” She coughed a sound she intended as an exasperated laugh, but it came out like a pained sob. “How can I allow her to marry me?”

“She won’t use her natural eyes at all if you let her,” Six replied flippantly. Azure glared back at her, but Six maintained a shameless deadpan. “You don’t have to do those things; all you need to do is serve her.”

Azure scoffed and looked away. “You’re a real big help, thanks.”

The revelation remained like an anchor in her soul. Her whole life felt like a cheap trick. No wonder she had felt like such an outsider, no wonder she longed for things she couldn’t articulate, no wonder she suffered as a concubine for so long and just endured it to the deepest depths of submission. Why wouldn’t it be easy for someone so evil by nature to feel like she deserved punishment and abuse?

Her contrasting dominant tendencies after meeting Samantha, something at most a curiosity to play with by her old lover, suddenly had a more obvious source than mere past lives. She’d literally tortured people for years. Even the possibility that was the source of her urges was horrifying.

I would never hurt her! It’s only to please, to serve in a desired fashion… I have to believe this. I have to know that is the truth! I-I’ll go mad if I don’t.

And what of her upbringing? Was it a lie, or was she somehow refashioned into a child? Who did all of this, and why? Who broke her out? Why?

I had a childhood, I had friends — I had Jallee! We played in the mud, we-... I had a mother, damn it!

Instead of the ‘mother of Azure,’ though, her mind flashed to recall that moment framed by a giant tree in the background, her mother’s twisted expression as she looked down on her disappointing daughter, something between sadness and judgment, burning into Her retinas like a brand.

It rang as the greater truth deep in her soul, and she shuddered as she imagined, with sympathetic pain, her new tail being chopped off… again.

Incensed and frustrated, Azure launched herself up and stalked over to Six Moons Falling, getting right in her face and glaring up at the taller figure. “Tell me what you know, right now. I deserve to fucking know!”

Six stared back at her unflinchingly; her expression wasn’t even cold, just calm and unperturbed. Infuriatingly so. “Talk of ‘deserving’ again? Tsk, tsk. I will tell you what you need to hear, only: use the provision you already possess to get your precious answers.”

Shaking in anger, glaring, baring her teeth, Azure growled and spun away, casting another Flight spell and soaring away without another word. Deathsting, respecting her foul mood (more or less), said nothing as he followed silently, flanking her.

Six’s cheery voice followed her. Her waving hand could be felt, too. “You’re welcome, my cute and angry horned one!”

Azure reached out mentally and connected to the various Servitors as she mapped her way out of that accursed place. “Collapse on me as an escort out. We’re done here.”

Where she was going, she couldn’t say, but it wasn’t to Samantha. She couldn’t — not yet. The thought of that inevitability was a boulder in her gut.

And yet, she’d likely have to, to utilize the ‘provision’ Six spoke of. A token. Unless she should arrange to take it herself and go alone? Was it possible?

The last of the bloody naga. The one called ‘Goldy.’ What do you know about me, serpent? Serpents, serpents, serpents, always fucking serpents in our lives, meddling and cryptic! Damn them all. To hell with them!

Fury like an aura around her, Azure stormed out of the catacombs and made for St. Deikmorn, dead set on getting piss-ass drunk.

✦•············•✦•···········•✦

Six Moons Falling smiled and waved at Azure until she was long gone. Finally, she opened her eyes and sighed with amused exasperation. In ages past, she’d known that being by the now irrelevant, indirect title ‘Lovechild of Many Shapes.’ Indeed, she’d been the true spiritual offspring of the Changers and their sinful ways, rising amongst them in hatred and consuming almost all of them in her rebirth from the polluted fires of their arts.

In that violent glutting, she’d missed one of the most powerful — and certainly the most insidious — who’d escaped, but Six’s prior identity had taken care of that. It was all thanks to the Lovechild, technically. Without her, it would’ve never been possible.

You served our purposes well, Azure, Lover of the Apocalypse, however unwittingly and evilly. The destruction of many old orders? The gods scattered and diminished? Jump-starting the Sage long before the appropriate time? A troublesome, subtle super-entity slain, where otherwise he would’ve been the serpent in every empire’s ear, instead of a benign pretender like me? All the dirty work I too have sullied my hands with?

Six chuckled to herself. All to make the one you’d come to love more pure and concentrated to her purpose! You’ll never accept that things would’ve been worse, because you can’t know, can’t fathom that truth. It’s not for you, not now, anyway. Perhaps it's for your greater self.

Six walked idly through all the collapsed stone, admiring her precision handiwork conducted not long after landing, muddying the evidence of extraction. Azure was too upset to consider the details of ‘how,’ which was probably a good thing, because there were still things worth hiding. In any case, the act hadn’t been Six’s idea.

Mother knows best.

Smiling faintly to herself, Six hopped back up from the debris pit and strolled through the catacombs ostensibly toward the eventual exit, feeling high on life and optimistic — as usual, of late.
