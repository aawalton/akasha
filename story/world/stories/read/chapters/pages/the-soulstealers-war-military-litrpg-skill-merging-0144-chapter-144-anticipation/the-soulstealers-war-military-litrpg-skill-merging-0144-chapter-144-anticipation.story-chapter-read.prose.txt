In the evening, while I trained with Yesman and Habit’s squads, the riders from the 294th approached us. Romance led a few of them, and I broke off from our formation practice to see what they were looking for.

“They’re letting you stay in the castle?” I asked.

“And keep our horses in the stable,” Romance replied. “The castle’s stable, not the tournament grounds’ stables.”

“That’s for the best. You don’t want them to spontaneously combust,” I joked.

“What?”

“Nevermind,” I said. “I’ll explain later.”

“Sure,” Romance said, rolling his eyes. “Atoning things, surely…”

“What do they need regulars here for?” Habit asked.

“Word is there’s orc activity in the area,” a different Dupe provided, one of the sappers from Yesman’s squad. “They’re probably here to help out.”

“You’ve seen signs of orc activity?” I asked. I didn’t want to sound hopeful, because it would be for the best if orcs didn’t turn up. But having no sign was even worse.

“Just rumours,” Habit provided.

“There’s nothing concrete yet.” Elf stepped up to us as well, still carrying most of his gear with him. “But they want us to support the scouts and check for any movement on the perimeter, check if the scouts missed anything.”

I gulped. “If Lord Ceonwin’s scouts haven’t found anything…”

What if Hinlaw was just trying to get me scared? Maybe I was supposed to embarrass myself with the notion of an orc attack, start shouting like a madman, and then when it didn’t happen, no one would trust me or my information again. Or worse, maybe they’d think I was trying to postpone the tournament for my own gain, potentially revealing myself as a competitor within it.

But I held my ground. Hinlaw hadn’t known I was listening, and he’d still spoken about his attack.

The only question was where it would begin.

“It’s possible there’s nothing,” Romance said. “But Lord Ceonwin seemed to think there was something out there.”

I nodded. “If there is, I’m sure you guys will find it.”

“We were hoping, perhaps, we could take part in some training with your squads,” Romance said. “My riders and I would die to see the sappers in action, even if most of us will probably lose.”

I glanced at Habit and Yesman, and both of them shrugged. Adding a shrug myself, I said, “There’s not much more we training can do to prepare to take down the level guardian. It can’t hurt to give the regulars a little more experience.”

All throughout the evening, I worked with Romance and his squad, as well as a few others from the 294th, sparring with them and checking if they’d got any of the skills we’d recommended during our last mission with them.

Our sparring matches were easy, and though the regulars were professional, they didn’t have the same level of training as us. They usually lost within fifteen seconds. If I was going to take over the 294th one day, or command them in any way, I’d need to improve their general combat proficiency.

But that was a problem for another time. Future me was doing a lot of heavy lifting lately. It couldn’t be avoided, though.

For now, I just had to make sure I was ready for my tournament match, and I used the time just as much for myself as for them, continuing to stabilize in my newly enhanced body.

~ ~ ~

When I returned to my room, I immediately checked my supply of infused food. I’d been using as much as I could, practically choking it down even when I didn’t feel hungry, but I’d also been rationing it out over the days until I reached the next tournament fight.

Even while the others were trying to rest, I continued working, eating what I could, even though my body wanted me to reject it. Once or twice, I gagged, and I lost a meal’s worth when my body rejected all the infused food by throwing up—apparently, you could have too much of it. But I cleaned it up as quickly as I could.

In the morning, I woke up early, heading to the jousting training track with Lord Ceonwin, but before I could leave, Anchor stopped me. He said, “Levi, you look awful.”

“I’m just doing what I can,” I replied.

“If you’re exhausted, you won’t ride well. And if you lose the joust before he even has a chance to draw you into a duel, what’s the point?”

I sighed, then said, “I don’t know. But I have to push myself—”

“You have been pushing yourself.”

“And it’s not going to be enough. Everyone’s counting on me. The squad. Lord Ceonwin, probably. The entire Domain. If I lose here, we’re—”

“There’s nothing more you can do to meaningfully change the outcome of that fight, except by being as mentally ready as possible,” Anchor said. “Believe me, I’m terrible at aiming when I’m tired.”

I hung my head. “Alright, alright. Tonight. I’ll make sure to sleep properly.”

~ ~ ~

After breakfast that same day, Brawl and Anchor advanced to Titanium around the same time as each other. They were growing at the same rate, and although Brawl had a higher total attribute count, their Presence was what held them back—just the same as it had for me.

I congratulated them both, but there wasn’t much time to celebrate, not with our usual training session.

I didn’t push myself any harder today than yesterday, but I still managed to push my riftrock in a circle around the outer wall—and it still exhausted me. At the same time, though, I kept my kit bag full of all the Presences I had. All throughout the day, I used them, steadily increasing my Presence number and pushing toward Silver.

All throughout the day, whether in training or in evening sparring practice, I counted about sixteen uses of [Mass Hammer], which I counted as a victory. At six points each use, it meant my Presence was in the high nineties, now.

After training, I bathed, then returned to our room. I couldn’t afford to skip out some of my infused food, but I also didn’t stay up as late as I could to do it.

As I went to sleep, around the same time as Anchor and Brawl, I told them, “Thanks for looking out for me.”

“Squadmates don’t let squadmates get in blood feuds with thegns while tired,” Anchor replied. “Drunk, on the other hand? Maybe we can spare some funds for some liquor.”

“Brother, do not go drunk,” Brawl insisted.

“Are all the top thegns like Hinfic?” I asked as I pulled off my tunic and unlaced my boots. “Those with power, course.” I’d met thegns like Sir Sidal and Sir Aldhelm, but they were different. They were hand-picked military men, who served Lord Ceonwin closely. But from what I’d seen of men like Hinfic and his father, or even Leoheard? I didn’t want to know what the thegns up north, closer to the capital, were like.

Anchor shrugged. “Most likely.”

“You think there’s really anything we can do about them, then? To stop them from rotting Gate inside-out?”

“I know it will only take one good thegn, who’s willing to use power the right way, to set things right,” Anchor said. “And then, I think the other good thegns who are too afraid to speak up will climb out of the woodwork.”

“You know that?”

“I’ve met a few good thegns in my deployment before here, too,” Anchor said. “They exist, no matter how hard the self-interested ones try to beat them down.”

“Is it just self-interest?” I asked. Hinlaw…he was a human. And supposedly, we could choose how we’d act. But I hadn’t even seen a glimmer of remorse from him.

I guess he’d already made his choice.

“Maybe,” Anchor said. “And maybe, you’ll never know. Are you going to let that stand in your way?”

“No,” I said. “No, I won’t.”

I flopped down on my bed and rolled over, and as soon as I let out the last breath in my lungs, I was asleep.

~ ~ ~

My schedule remained the same for the next two days until the morning of the tournament—except I made sure to spread out my extra intake of infused food throughout the day as well as my Presence, and I made sure to catch up on as many sleep debts as I could.

On the night before, it took me a few hours to fall asleep, but thankfully, I didn’t need to retreat to the rooftops to clear my mind.

The morning of the match arrived with the sounding of a horn. I rolled out of bed. The horn signalled that the final match was about to begin, but I didn’t have to be there until much later. The audience was still filing into the stands or surrounding the arena, and Lord Ceonwin would have to provide opening remarks.

I put on my tunic and boots, then grabbed my illusion pendants, but didn’t activate them yet. Anchor and Brawl woke up the same time as me, and they got themselves ready as well. I told them, “Any chance you could gather up my armour and stuff it in your kit bags? And maybe carry my spear, too?”

“Of course,” Anchor said. “I assume we’re gearing up with our own equipment?”

“Yeah,” I said. “And Sage, too. Tell her to be ready for a fight.”

Anchor and Brawl nodded. Brawl said, “We’ll stay on the lookout for orcs?”

I nodded, then checked my reading slate once more before departing. Now, it read:

After checking over the status, I hid the slate. A Silver. Finally. And just in time for the final fight.

I gathered Burr from the stable, left the castle, then headed across the tournament grounds. I didn’t need to sneak today—it was an unofficial holiday, and no one, even among the sappers, was going to miss the final fight of the tournament for anything.

As I walked through the tournament grounds, a patrol of soldiers from the 294th rode past, and though I didn’t recognize the soldier, I asked, “Any sign of orcs?”

“Nothing,” the Dupe said. “Romance is out on patrol, looking for them. But it doesn’t seem like there’s going to be an attack.”

I grimaced, then said, “Stay on alert, yeah?”

“Yes, Sarge.”

“And if you see Habit or Yesman, tell them to get their squads in their gear.” I was pretty sure I could count on their help if there was an orc attack, and I needed our best in their top fighting condition. “And if you see anything, tell my squad immediately. Find Anchor. He’ll know what to do.”

The soldier nodded, and I dismissed him. Something was wrong, but I couldn’t quite put my finger on it.

Still, I had a tournament to take part in, and I couldn’t wait any longer. I snuck to Sir Ricdine’s tent with Burr, activated my illusion pendants, then put on my armour and pulled on my helmet.

It was now or never. I had to make this worth it. I had to make this work.
