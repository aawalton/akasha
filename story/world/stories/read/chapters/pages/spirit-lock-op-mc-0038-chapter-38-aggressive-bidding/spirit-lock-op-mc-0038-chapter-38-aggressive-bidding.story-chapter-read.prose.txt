As Ritrix lectured Kayline, Leo got a call from his father. He swiped it away, yearning for the awkward moment Kayline had to leave. To his bewilderment, she sat down beside Hayden—again—and said, “Sorry about that. Politics.”

Hayden smiled faintly. “I understand.”

Leo’s eyes widened, and he looked toward Ritrix, who had disappeared into a private room—a room Kayline and Lina should have joined him in. What the hell’s going on? he wondered.

Suddenly, the lights dimmed, and Leo listened in.

“Welcome to the Phoenix Room auction,” the auctioneer said. “This is a special night focusing on providing resources and spirits to rifters in preparation for a grand rift we believe to open in the near future.”

The crowd stirred with anticipation as Nara wheeled out boxes containing relics, elixirs, and catalysts.

“Before we begin, we want to remind patrons that ending bids are final; there is no backing out. So, if you don’t want an object, do not bid. With that reminder, let’s begin with our first spirit of the night.”

Two beautiful women wearing mid-thigh business skirts opened up a box, revealing a glowing rock.

“This relic contains the Forest Gale spirit. It’s a support spirit that creates immersive gusts of wind. While it doesn’t have direct combat applications, it can massively augment dispersal-type spirits, especially those that spread gaseous poison or fire. That makes this spirit valuable to higher-level rifters, so they may hire you. And when they do, they’ll give you opportunities and contacts for growth.”

The audience reflected on the profound usefulness of the innocuous spirit, sparking a sudden interest throughout the crowd.

“The spirit is also easy to evolve to the second evolution,” the auctioneer added. “It only requires cora and a gas created by decomposing vegetation. You can get it by walking through a forest.”

Evolving a spirit ranged from straightforward to notoriously difficult. Sometimes they required certain foods or elixirs, or tempering. Other spirits required the host to feed them other spirits, unique resources, or perform a wide array of attacks. It was different for every spirit, and the requirements for evolution were often unconfirmed or unknown. That’s why it was valuable to buy spirits with straightforward requirements. Otherwise, a rifter could be stunted—perhaps forever.

“As always, the spirit comes with a basic catalyst for usage,” the auctioneer said. “And tonight, we’re also giving buyers core expansion elixirs to the third spirit lock—a special gift from Jeela Rian herself.”

A raucous roar of applause followed—as expected. Expansion elixirs increased the size of one’s core, opening “spirit locks” that allowed the rifter to have more spirits. The first elixir cost about twenty thousand credits, and the third cost around eighty thousand. Dual expansion elixirs were rarer and of higher quality. Those went for over 100,000. That was in addition to obtaining a catalyst. The fact that the poories were getting a hundred and fifty thousand credits’ worth of resources—no matter how much they spent—was a cause for celebration.

Or so one would think. Hayden’s bored expression didn’t even twitch.

Leo clicked his tongue. That guy’s unbearable!

“Bidding starts at ten thousand credits,” the auctioneer said. “You may begin.”

Hayden instantly placed a bid for twenty thousand. The number reflected on the digital bid counter in the corner of Leo’s eye.

For that? Leo thought. How desperate.

Leo doubled it. The bid clicked to forty thousand.

Hayden looked conflicted, and then bid one hundred thousand.

“What are you doing?” Kayline whispered.

“I’m not going to get what I want—so it’s good to get something,” Hayden said. He was quiet, but Leo heard it and smirked.

Yeah, good luck with that. Leo doubled the bid. The bid counter read: “200,000.”

Hayden grimaced. It was the first time Leo had seen anything other than a stone-cut expression or a charming smile. That made Leo’s heart sing, so he didn’t let up.

Hayden bid 250,000.

Leo doubled it, going to 500,000.

By that point, the audience had erupted into a flurry of whispers.

“What’s going on?”

“I don’t know.”

“They know that’s F-rank, right?”

“Yeah, they’d have to. Do you think they know something we don’t?”

“Can’t imagine so.”

Those people were wrong—Leo did know something they didn’t. He knew that Hayden Reskco was an unbearable asshat. He also knew that this wasn’t about a spirit. Leo was buying Hayden’s misery—

And he wasn’t holding back.

Leo grinned and spoke the bid aloud. “One-point-one million.”

That was one hundred thousand more than Hayden’s famous one million.

Leo yearned to record Hayden’s expression at that moment. His slouching shoulders—his bitterness. The dread was etched onto his face. As pretentious pricks say about their wine—it was exquisite.

“Sold for 1.1 million,” the auctioneer said. “Onto the next spirit. This relic contains the connection-type spirit Air Hop. An F-rank variant of the C-rank Airwalk spirit, Air Hop allows a person to temporarily jump onto the air for up to two hops. It starts weak, but once it reaches the second evolution, the user can hop twenty times, allowing them to scale small cliffs or break serious falls. It’s a must-have survival spirit.”

Hayden buzzed with excitement, clenching his jaw. It was subtle—but Leo saw it. Oh, yes, he saw it. And he couldn’t wait for what followed.

“The evolution requirements are famously low as well,” the auctioneer added. “It only requires spirit usage, giving people a quick route to more functionality. Bidding starts at twenty-five thousand. You may begin.”

Hayden immediately bid fifty thousand.

Leo laughed and doubled it.

Nara turned to him and sneered. He smirked and doubled it again when Hayden added more.

Lina scoffed and glowered at Leo, but Kayline had her eyes focused on the stage with a poker face. That woman was Team Hayden until her daddy talked to her. Now, she wasn’t even speaking to him. That made Leo happy. To celebrate, he raised the bid to 1.1 million.

“Sold for 1.1 million.”

Leo scoffed when his father called again. Leave me alone! It’s my money!

Leo was using his bank account, so it didn’t matter what his father wanted. Leo was good with money, constantly saving. If he wanted to waste it on petty feuds, he would do it. He selected the Do Not Disturb option in his link and leaned back.

Annoyed, he bid 1.1 million out of the gate for the next spirit. Hayden slouched, Lina snorted, Nara sneered, and it all made him feel a bit better. No, it made him feel a lot better, so he kept playing Hayden.

Torque Fist.

Resonance.

Buckle.

Searing Blade.

One after the other, Leo won every spirit, and Hayden became more and more dejected. Then, the D-rank spirits came out, and Hayden bid frantically, but he always lost after the one million mark.

One million was far too much for D-rank spirits, so Leo won all of those, too.

Now he had twenty spirits, and the night was still young.

Then the C-rank spirits came out—and the real pain began.

“Razor Wire is one of the most iconic spirits, a line-type spirit that wraps walkers in thin, razor-sharp tendrils,” the auctioneer explained. “It’s a versatile spirit that is often used for trapping, confining, and slaughtering walkers of any grade. It’s an essential spirit welcomed on any team.

“As for evolution, it’s a passive evolver that feeds on… as macabre it is… blood. So one only needs to use it in battle to evolve.

“Bidding starts at 100,000 credits.”

Hayden doubled the price—and Leo shot it up to 1.1 million. Hayden ran his fingers through his hair, clearly indicating his desire, so Leo kept bidding for it, eventually spending 1.7 million.

And why not?

Leo’s monthly allowance was five million. Monthly. He couldn’t spend that much if he wanted to, so he just accumulated it. Now, he had a reason to use it—so he spent it without regret.

Thunder Dome.

Spectral Roar.

Intimidation.

Domain.

Leo obtained seven more spirits in a flash. At some point, Hayden’s despair put him in such a good mood that he continued to bid on everything just to prove what true power was.

“Sold for 4.7 million.”

The whole audience was now glowering at Leo, but he didn’t care. So he kept going on and on until he got a call from his mother via his business line. He swiped it out of the way, but his family was like a cloud of gnats. He put that link on Do Not Disturb, as well.

Fine, I’ll stop! Leo thought. Not like he could buy anything, anyway.

The richer patrons in the audience relaxed when Leo stopped bidding, and the auction continued. A rank-three catalyst went for ten million, a fourth core expansion elixir went for eight. A mildly interesting spirit went for six. It was suddenly boring—

Until something incredible happened.

“Oh,” the auctioneer said, opening up a box. “This was supposed to be the Scarlet Vine spirit, but I guess it’s the…” She frowned. “It seems that it’s the Clear Line spirit. It’s… a pure-type spirit that has transparent tendrils that don’t do anything of note. It’s primarily used for training children how to use spirits, since they don’t require an open gate. I’m not sure why this is here. Let me check.”
