In coding, there’s something called a recursive loop. It’s essentially a function that calls itself over and over again. Typically, there’s some stopping criteria to it: a number of times run, some sort of convergence, or a solution being reached. Usually, there are multiple stopping criteria so a program doesn’t stall out and run forever.

What I had effectively done was create a recursive loop between my mind and that of Maerin. Before, the communication was a sort of gate. Maerin could root around in my head and speak there. I could respond through my thoughts, but couldn’t put them in her head like she could mine.

I copied her ritual in part, splashing a bit of my blood on her and creating my own bridge, which allowed me to hear her thoughts directly. I read those thoughts in my mind, and she picked up those thoughts in her own, which I then picked up. Recursively, over and over again, transferred at the speed of thought.

By the time we moved to stop the relay, we were already spasming on the ground. I could feel how my brain split and tore. My forehead instantly rocketed up to fever temperatures and grew beyond that. Neural pathways fried at the flurry of synapses firing. The whole system heated, overheated, and caused wires to cross, melt, combine, or vanish entirely. I smelt toast. Vaguely understood, I fell to the floor. Then promptly died.

I woke up on the couch in Joey’s living room. The connection, thankfully, had been cut when we entered the real world. Maerin and I were both writhing on the floor. Blood leaked from my nose and eyes as something ruptured. Somehow, I managed not to throw up on the carpet. Maerin wasn’t bleeding nearly as much as me, but she hadn’t managed to keep the alcohol down.

We caught our breath, and the next time I looked up, Maerin was leaning against the couch, glaring at me. She shakily stumbled to her feet before navigating to the kitchen, turning on the faucet, and dunking her head underneath the stream.

I held my hands up, my chest shivering from the sensation of dying. “Yes. My bad. I’ll be more careful.” I meant it too. If I had tested that in the real world instead of the Dream, we would just be dead. Real dead, with no second chances.

“Sorry, your word kind of means jack shit to me at the moment,” Maerin said after gulping down water straight from the faucet.

I paused and thought about that. I hadn’t exactly kept to the spirit of the deal, but then again, I wasn’t about to let some crazed girl do whatever she wanted in my head without retaliation. I was against it. I gave what I was comfortable with, and she had crossed a line. I’d be more careful, because the two-way connection was fucking stupid of me, but only because I had acted hastily. The core idea itself wasn’t bad. Communicating at the speed of thought could be the difference between life and death in an Instance, especially if miscommunication could be quickly handled.

“Let’s go back in the Dream and try again. I think there’s a lot we can do with that connection. I agreed that you could make the bridge. Fine. But I’m not your servant and you don’t get free reign of my mind just because I’ve decided I owe you. We stay cordial. You don’t push and I don’t push back.”

“Okay,” Maerin agreed after a small pause. “No two way connection though, not fully. The favor is pointless if you have the exact same thing I have. We have a barrier on your end of the connection, and I can allow things through if I choose.”

I considered the offer. “I want a kill switch. Something able to block you in case of an emergency. We both know how much the Dream and Instance Holders like taking away autonomy. I don’t want this biting me in the ass if you get mind controlled.”

“Agreed. Timed. One day cool down. No trying to break it.”

“Sure,” I agreed in turn. I was fine with this much. I frowned as I reviewed our conversation so far. “You’re planning on going into more Instances?” I asked.

“Yeah.”

“Why?” I asked lamely. It’s a question I have constantly asked. It made no sense why people would willingly go into the Dream. I had my reasons, but the idea that anyone else had theirs just didn’t make sense to me.

Maerin controlled a sigh and pivoted to look me in the eyes. “Control.”

“Control? The Dream isn’t going to give you control.”

She stared at me and then summoned motes of glowing lights over her head. “Your world doesn’t have magic, yet here I am using it.”

“Point taken, but you have enough now you could become ruler of a country if you wanted. Hell, even the entire world.”

Maerin shook her head, “There’s Instances eating away at reality. Who knows what happens if they aren’t handled.”

I still didn’t get it. “Right, but that’s what I was going to do, regardless.”

“Alone?”

“Yes.”

“I admire your confidence, but I’m starting to think it’s a form of mania.”

“Point is, there’s no reason for you to get involved. So why? You could have a safe life, find people to love and who love you, get a job and relax. Hell you can just wave your hand and make yourself a house or piles of cash.”

“I have nothing here.”

“You have a clean slate.”

“That may be true, but as long as there exists something that could take it from me I have nothing. We are very small fish in a very large ocean. I don’t want to not exist one day because Ananke forgets about me. I refuse that reality.”

I stared at Maerin and sorted through my own memories. It was confusing looking at her. I had two sets of memories. One where I met her in the Dream, where she was a Survivor, and one where we met in college. Where she helped construct sets for stage plays, loved her passion projects, and wanted to work in horticulture. I didn’t even know what the fuck horticulture meant. I had to look it up to talk to her about it, but I had forgotten her, and so had everyone else. Maerin had been slotted into that role. It was a strange, painful feeling that spread through my chest. My emotions for my friend were bleeding over to Maerin. How awful.

“What a dumb reason,” the words slipped from my mouth, but I couldn’t help it.

“What?”

“You’ll miss your whole life, fighting against forces you can’t possibly control or beat and then die. For what? To say you could? To have tried?”

“You’re doing the exact same thing,” she was getting angry again, and not hiding it well.

“I’m trying to get my brother back, not control reality. Once I do I’m coming right back here and living my life however I please. An asteroid or black hole could hurtle through space tomorrow and obliterate everything here. There’s always something out of people’s control. You can’t obsess over it.”

“You’re such a hypocrite, you know that?”

“Everyone’s a hypocrite, and those like Jeren who aren’t are hell to deal with. People do what feels right in the moment, everything else is just rationalizing and posturing.”

“I swear you’re just saying shit sometimes. Do you have a single relationship that isn’t built on pissing people off?”

“Now you’re just trying to be hurtful.”

“Is it working?”

“No.”

Another hour passed before Joey returned. He looked exhausted, and his eyes were puffy. I could tell he’d been crying, and it made my heart ache. I spent one Presence to summon pizza, one Hawaiian, one pepperoni, when he wasn’t looking. It felt like a waste of Presence, like I could have done more with it, but reality still took my request. We ate at the dining table in a semi-awkward silence.

“Do you want to go into the Dream with us?” Maerin asked.

I almost dropped my food.

“No,” my voice cut out like a whip. I wasn’t about to let anyone I cared about near the Dream. I had only told him about it, so Maerin didn’t have to pretend to be his girlfriend and then hurt him with the play acting. I was going to kill the Instances and the Instance Holder, chipping away at the world, and I couldn’t do that while trying to take care of Joey.

“Yes,” Joey replied evenly.

I glared at Maerin to stop talking, but she didn’t balk, “He should get a say in it I’d think. We told him about the Dream. Trust me, it can be maddening knowing about it, yet unable to go.”

“It’s not safe,” I said.

Joey cleared his throat to get our attention, “I don’t think much is safe, Dorian. The Spiders are a good example. In the Dream if I die I’ll be back, and at least then I’m not waiting around for things to happen. Plus, if the Dream took … since the Dream took someone I care about, maybe it can give her back.”

“I can look into that, you don’t need to,” I argued.

Joey just gave me a sad smile, “I know, but I want to do this.”

I didn’t argue. Any more would just be selfish. I was a selfish person, but there’d be no winning this fight, and all it would get me is a broken friendship. The words sat in the air for a long time before I broke the silence, “I got dragged in by the Dream, and Maerin was forced in by her Instance Holder.” I turned to the girl in question, “any ideas on this?”

Maerin nodded, “Yeah, you have to give him some Presence, people naturally have some, but something about giving someone Presence attaches them to the Dream.”

I turned to Joey, “You sure about this? It’s not pleasant there. I wouldn’t be there unless I had to.”

Joey just nodded and gave me a pat on the shoulder, “Thanks for the warning, but I’m sure. Even if … if someone I cared for wasn’t gone I’d still go. I wouldn’t let you go through this alone. You know that.”

I nodded. It was true. Joey was probably the best person I had ever met. One of the rare people who wasn’t a hypocrite, and rarer still, one of them that didn’t manage to piss me off by existing. If I had told him about the Dream from the start, maybe things would’ve been different.

I turned away, but nodded. “I know, just wanted to warn you.” I held out my hand and let static take my vision, “Whenever you’re ready, take my hand.”
