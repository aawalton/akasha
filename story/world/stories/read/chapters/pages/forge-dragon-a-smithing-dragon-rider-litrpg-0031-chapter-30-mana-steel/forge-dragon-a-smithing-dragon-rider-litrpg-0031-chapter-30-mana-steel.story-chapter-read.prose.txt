“What are you going to name it?” Grisim asked Caleb as he and Kelia were looking over his new hammer.

Caleb, who disliked naming things, hadn’t thought of it. He’d made a lot of items over the years, and naming them felt a little grandiose. He did admit, though, of all the things he’d ever crafted, if any deserved a name, this hammer did.

“If you think of a good name, go ahead,” Caleb said, putting off the decision.

“Smasher!” Grisim said enthusiastically,

“He said a good name,” Kelia said.

A knock came at the door and all four pairs of eyes shot to it.

“Hide Bog!” Caleb whispered, urging the dragon to be quiet through the bond.

Bog, to his credit, tried to move quietly, but the iron beast travelling across a stone floor sounded much the same as Caleb dragging his anvil across it.

“One second!” Caleb yelled, going to the door to forestall whoever it was.

Eventually, Bog made it to the back room, and Kelia threw a blanket over him.

“Hello?” Caleb asked through the thick mill door.

“Caleb? What’s all that commotion?” Master Dimel’s voice came back.

“Sorry,” Caleb said, opening the door. “Grisim was moving the anvil.”

“Okay…” Dimel said. “Well, I brought you the steel delivery as promised…”

His voice trailed off as he saw the hammer sitting on Caleb’s anvil.

“Is that it?”

Caleb nodded, gesturing for his master to inspect it.

Gingerly Dimel lifted the hammer.

“Elders,” he whispered. “It’s beautiful.”

He continued to inspect it, tapping it against the anvil and listening to it ring.

“Tell me everything,” his master asked more than demanded, and Caleb obliged.

***

“Amazing,” Dimel said for more than the first time.

He was inspecting Bog, who Caleb had called out once he’d begun his explanation. To Dimel’s credit, he’d only jumped slightly at the appearance of the dreaded “iron salamander” and had taken Caleb’s explanation in stride.

As Caleb explained his process of discovering the art of enchanting, Dimel sat enraptured as the truths he’d only seen glimpses of were revealed before him.

“Explain again how you activate this sight,” Dimel asked. “It sounds… familiar.”

Dimel asked a lot of very detailed and pointed questions, and Caleb had renewed respect for his master’s abilities. Once he’d learned the secret of enchanting, he’d thought his master’s forging to be blind guesswork. Blind it might have been, but it hadn’t been guesswork. Somehow Dimel had intuited the truths that had been laid bare before Caleb’s eyes.

A few hours later, Dimel left Caleb with much on his mind, and Caleb suspected his master would be locking himself away for the next few weeks as he practiced what they discussed.

“Oh! I almost forgot!” Master Dimel said at the door. “Word is a legion is coming by to recruit.”

This was a regular occurrence. The empire didn’t publicly schedule when the legions would recruit, lest people make themselves scarce in times of conscription, but it happened with enough frequency that Caleb had lived through three visits.

With one notable exception—the day they took his mother—the visits were routine. The legion would offer contracts to any who signed up. Initialized received a tidy sum of gold for a five-year commitment while anyone else was promised fair pay, an eventual pension, and citizenship in the empire—something his village lacked. Every visit saw some young fools signing up for adventure. Those who had failed to initialize after partaking in a dragon hunt were the most common takers. There were few known paths to initialization, but the empire’s Thousand Blade Legion was one for sure.

The Thousand Blade Legion never came to recruit, but that legion recruited the best of the best from the others. If one could get taken into that legion as one of their irregulars, initialization was only a matter of time. Though most died before their time came.

The empire came to their village when they needed scouts. The Frost Dragon Hunters made great scouts, and the work on the battlefronts led to leveling. Any initialized in the village above level 10 had spent at least five years in the legions, returning home after getting more than their fill of empire life.

While it was unlikely a conscription would occur, most found it best to lie low when the empire was in town.

Caleb fought the urge to begin working on his armor, as he had a promise to himself to keep and finally the tools to keep it.

***

The rhythmic thump thump thump of an axe striking a tree was the constant backdrop of Caleb’s work, occasionally broken up by the creaking of a tree falling. Outside, Grisim was taking up his old trade as a wedding gift to his brother and new sister.

He’d promised the couple that he’d add a house onto the mill-turned-forge. And Caleb, while skeptical of Grisim’s ability to build a house, had agreed to let him at least try. At worst, Caleb would need to help out at some point.

Inside he was looking at the blue ring he had in front of him, astounded at what he had, but still thinking it was missing something. With Bog’s help, Caleb had forged the small lump of blue metal into a ring. When Caleb’s forge had refused to reach the temperature needed to work the steel, Bog had snatched it out of the coals and curled up in the burning fuel as if taking a nap. After a half hour, the dragon opened his mouth to show a red-hot lump of steel. The dragon had been growing since their departure from the cave, and soon he’d no longer fit inside the forge, but for now, the method worked.

Caleb had drawn the metal out into a thick wire and wrapped it around a ring form, occasionally giving it back to Bog to reheat. Even with his new hammer and its greater aspect for steel, he had to use the full power of his class and Imperium to work the small piece. As he worked, he forced the natural mana out of the ring. He’d expected that by the time the ring was forged, the mana would be gone from such a small object.

He’d been wrong.

The little ring was full of mana, and Caleb found he couldn’t force it out via his usual methods. Normally, he simply forced mana out with each strike in the forging process, but that wasn’t enough. The mana didn’t leave when he struck it, it only moved deeper in.

To counter this, Caleb flattened the metal out into a thin sheet, hammered out what he could, rolled it up, forge welded it together, and hammered it out into a sheet once again. By the time he was done, Caleb was certain he’d removed more mana from the bit of blue steel than he had total in all his forging thus far. One side effect of all this work was that the simple ring had a watered pattern to match his hammer.

He held the finished ring in front of him, but felt something was missing. As pretty as the pattern of the ring was, it was lacking somehow.

Caleb went over to the quenching trough. Now, though it still contained quenching steel, the trough was empty of water. He’d discovered after his quench that the water was cooling rapidly and beginning to freeze. He’d dumped the water out to prevent it, but by then some of the larger pieces had grown ice crystals around them.

Reaching out with his Imperium, he lifted the largest fleck out of the trough and to his hand. While most of the metal they’d retrieved from the basilisk had been little more than dust particles, this one had been larger—relatively. It was about the size of a grain of sand.

Or it had been. Now it was encapsulated in an ice crystal that refused to turn to metal. Caleb had tried, thinking to inlay the grain into the ring. When that refused to melt, he’d begun filing the crystal down into a faceted stone. As he worked the ice, the glow that had been absent slowly returned—faintly, but enough to make the crystal sparkle. He got it down to the size of an apple seed, was happy with it, and moved on to forging claw prongs onto his ring.

When he was done, he had a blue and white watered steel ring with a diamond of twinkling light.

He slid the ring onto his pinky and willed the system to recognize it.
