Everything was set in place.

Max waited for the perfect moment when the stall was busiest.

Illias gave him a massive, gap toothed grin from the other side of the street.

It didn’t long until the moment they had been waiting for arrived.

A rush of noble kids and the large entourages that followed surged toward the merchant stall. All dressed in finery, silk, gold, jewelry, and gaudy everything. Even their shoes were likely worth more than everything Max had in his possession, including the borrowed cloak.

Each one demanded attention from the merchant and the guards.

He could hear them laugh and bemoan how bad the Skill Shards were, one going so far as to call the Class Shards the last thing he would consider even if it came at the cost of having no class at all.

Max’s anger grew with every word they said.

I need to do it now or I’m going to do something stupid to these sheltered idiots.

He raised his hand to catch Illias’s attention and then dropped it. The boy practically fistpumped out of joy and launched himself toward the mass of kid nobles and their entourage of more kids around their age. They likely had protectors around them, but none that were exactly close enough to stop someone as near as Illias and as non-threatening as him.

The red-headed boy crashed into the group, sending four of them tumbling into the merchant’s stall.

Max moved without waiting to make out what the shouts and curses were exactly or to watch the guards reaction. His eyes were solely focused on the shiny crystals that had scattered on the ground. He targeted the furthest one between them all.

Never breaking his stride as he got low and swept his target off the ground.

He felt his heart beat out of his chest.

Max kept his head down and felt the pulsing cold of the Skill Shard in his palms. He activated his System Inventory and sent it in without another second of hesitation–

A hand grabbed his shoulder.

Max tried to shrug it off, but found it impossible. He turned around to find a guard standing above him.

“Let me see your hands,” the guard said, his eyes narrowed dangerously.

Max shook and trembled. He struggled to bring his hands up even if they were empty. At any second the guard would take his head off, stab him through the chest, or worse, drag him off to the jail house like Caretaker Isabella had said.

His palms were empty.

The guard frowned and began to pat Max down. He found nothing. The man shook his head and left.

Max felt relief flood his senses.

He turned around and ran into the alleyway, swinging around for another attempt. Confidence flooded his senses as his System Inventory skill actually worked. He just hoped he had enough space to fit two of the large crystals. They were bigger than he expected once he finally got close enough to grab one.

By the time he made it back, everyone was looking away from the stall, including the guards and the merchant.

Shit…

The nobles were beating Illias.

Max considered helping. Illias was a small boy and probably couldn’t take much more of the hits, yet on the other side, his whole life depended on this moment. Him grabbing the Skill and Class Shards would allow him to become more than what he was right now. It was an opportunity he could not let escape him. He moved closer and slunk in low behind the stall instead of in front like last time. His eyes found the section that had Class Shards written on it in calligraphy and grabbed one.

No one noticed him vanish back through the alley.

He tried to send it to his System Inventory–

System Inventory full

Please level System Inventory skill to increase allotted Spacial Pocket:

Current Size: Six cubic inches

Next Level: Eight Cubic inches

Max cursed and hid it in his cloak. He had to figure out how to use it as soon as possible before someone caught him. He knew it was unrealistic to think, but his mind kept screaming that something wrong was going to happen the second he stepped away. He wanted to use the Class Shard now. There were a million possibilities of things going wrong this instant if he wasn’t careful.

He shook his head and kept running in a full sprint to get away from the scene of his crime.

I got them! I actually got them! Now I have to figure out how to use them…

Hope Illias is fine.

Max stopped for a brief moment and looked back.

He swore to somehow pay Illias for what he did for him. This was not a moment he would forget from the smaller kid for a long time coming.

Max turned around–

He grunted as he crunched his nose into what felt like a solid wall. He fell to the ground covering his face in pain, tears swelling in his eyes and streaking down his face.

“Don’t you know it’s impossible to hide a Class or a Skill Shard?” a feminine voice said.

That sobered Max up real quick.

His eyes widened and cleared, focusing on the figure standing before him. A tall lady in leather armor and a black cape stood in front of him. Her cold black pupils studied him for a moment without a single expression on her face.

“Did you really think you could escape?”

Max gaped.

Her armor was not pristine like the guards and fake guardians. They were covered in cuts and damage, yet they were high enough tier to still be preferable to new armor. Max had crashed into the armor and smashed his nose into her body hard enough to bleed and yet she hadn’t even shifted slightly.

That meant only one thing.

She was strong, impossibly strong.

A small smile cracked her stoic expression, nothing more than a tug on the corner of her lips. “Scared aren’t you? Should have thought about it before doing something so stupid. Who is dumb enough to steal a Class Shard? You apparently.”

Max blinked.

C-Class Shard…? She didn’t mention the Skill Shard.

“What Class Shard?” he tried to fish for information in case she was setting him up.

“The one in your pocket. That thing radiates mana like a ball of fire, and unless you happen to be a Fire Mage or have a particularly powerful Fire Patron Inheritance… The Class Shard’s in your cloak right now. The merchant was complaining about a Skill Shard too, but you don’t have it… and, quite frankly, I think he’s trying to get one on them noble brats.”

Max ignored the scrolling system notification.

She doesn’t know I have the Skill Shard… It worked. The System Inventory actually worked! If only I had enough space to put both of them I would have… Oh God… I’m screwed.

The magnitude of what had just happened hit Max without mercy.

He was so dead when Isabella found out.

Four legionaries marched into the alley just as he thought that. The Merchant was behind them.

“I want them hanged! No! Tortured and then hanged! Steal from me?! From me of all people? Don’t they know who I–”

The merchant’s eyes found the lady, they went wide. His jaw snapped shut.

Max tried to get up, but was manhandled by the legionaries as they searched his person. One of them found the Class Shard and pulled it out for everyone to see. The legionaries gave him incredulous looks, the merchant turned bright red at the sight of his prized item, and the lady’s expression did not change one bit. She already knew it was there.

The merchant tried to take it, but the legionary stopped him.

“Evidence.”

The guy's face morphed into a dozen expressions before he finally settled on being exhausted. “Fine, but I better get it back or I’m raising hell! Hell! You understand what I’m saying? Hell, I say! Hell!”

Max was picked up by a legionary by the collar and set down on his feet.

“C’mon. Don’t try to run.”

Max was trying to find any opportunity to escape as he was being led away. He never found the chance he had been looking for. He was led down the alley back toward where the stall was. His eyes widened as he saw a familiar figure on their knees and waiting there surrounded by legionaries and a crowd gathering around him.

Illias looked like a mess, face swollen and bruised.

The boy picked his head up and saw Max, he tried to hide the massive smile that grew on his face but failed.

Idiot! What the hell is he still doing here?

Max was thrown to the ground next to him.

A single, older legionary with pale white skin and stubble on his chin, sat on a crate. He studied the two of them for a moment. “A party of two thieves…? I don’t get it? What was the point in doing something like this? Or do you just want to cause me a headache?” He paused and looked introspective. “I’ve heard of something like this. Assisted death is what they call it. Do something ridiculous and hope you get killed in a final hurrah and epic glory. Is that what you two were trying to do?”

Max’s face darkened.

I shouldn’t have involved Illias. Shit! What the hell do I do now?

Max looked up and into the crowd. His eyes widened as he saw a familiar figure in between the masses pressing themselves closer to the scene. Old Goat, with a goat beard and wispy hair like his moniker, was as pale as milk. The ancient man would not survive seeing his little grandchild imprisoned and sentenced to life or execution.

Those were the only two outcomes he knew of for something like this.

He took a deep breath and collected his courage.

“Two? Ha!” He laughed loudly, channeling every villain he had read in the story books his mother forced him to read as a child that were clearly out of his age range. “You think this child is my equal? Are you mad? Idiot was used and didn’t even know what was happening for a piece of bread!”

“Bread?” Illias perked up, not understanding what was happening.

Max elbowed him hard. The little boy still thought this was a game or fun event he could escape from. “All he had to do was cause some noise and he did his role perfectly! Dumb–”

“I’m not dumb!”

“Stupid–”

“M-Max!”

“Naive child that I took advantage of to get what I wanted! And if it wasn’t for the whore–”

The lady frowned.

The captain of the legionaries gave him a sad look.

“–I would have escaped and no one would have been the wiser!”

Silence followed his declaration. The entire mob watched in stunned, tense moments as Max played his role flawlessly. He could see kids he knew look at him differently, parents pulling their children away, and older seniors shaking their heads and giving him looks as though they just knew that he had been this monster ever since he was a child.

Max refused to look toward Old Goat lest he breaks out of his character.

He kept his eyes on the captain.

“Is this the final story you want to give?” the man asked him.

“Story? Ha! These red-haired, soulless bastards are nothing but tools! They are only worth what they can be used for. I knew he was following me the entire time and I waited for the perfect moment to put him to perfect use.”

Illias gasped beside him. “D-Do you really think that, Max?”

Maximilian felt his heart shatter as he kept his head forward and refused to address the crying boy next to him.

It made it more believable if Illias truly thought Max was telling the truth.

“Let the little boy go,” the captain stood up and stepped forward to tower over the kneeling Max. “Honor among thieves? You see something new every day. Drag him to the jail house. The judges will decide his fate.”
