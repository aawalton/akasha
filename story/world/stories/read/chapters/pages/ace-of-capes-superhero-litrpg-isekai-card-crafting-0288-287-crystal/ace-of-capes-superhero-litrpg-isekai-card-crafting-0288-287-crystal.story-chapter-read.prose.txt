Lexie swerved to avoid the dragon’s breath for what felt like the umpteenth time. It was starting to get tedious.

The first time she'd used magic, she'd noticed something about the atmosphere.

It had some of the same restrictions that Earth had when it came to suppressing Eldritch mana.

Using too much made her nauseous, but she had enough human in her that it didn't give her the same breathless feeling as she'd had when she'd first landed on Earth.

As she activated her cards, she soul-scooped to see how much human mana was used with each skill versus Eldritch.

There was certainly quite a bit, but it confused her because the human mana seemed intertwined with the Eldritch, wrapping around it like a sash and resisting being taken apart.

Strange. So it appeared her human mana had incorporated well with the Eldritch. Did that mean that it would also grow if she kept growing her Eldritch?

Somehow that didn't seem right.

She was using the dragon to find out. She'd activated her VOID card and used it to eliminate the flames more than ten times before she got tired of the exercise.

The dragon kept doing the same thing, attacking her with oversized gusts of fire that were swallowed up by VOID each time. Pvilycht had built this up too much in his head. Lexie had been expecting more of a fight from the dragon, but so far, it seemed more scared of her than she was of it, hence why it didn’t try to move out of the cave that it had cramped itself within. Instead, it retreated deeper as Lexie countered all its fire-breathing attacks.

It was starting to get tedious.

“Listen, I’m not here to hurt you,” she said, then wondered. Does it even understand what I’m saying?

Probably not.

She should make a card to communicate with the dragon. Probably adjusting a couple she already had would probably work.

She got to work as she one-handedly swallowed the attacks, while pulling out the cards <Can I Have Your Attention>, <Compelling Argument>, <Easy Comprehension> (a card she'd made back in her dungeon, which allowed researchers to boil down large data points and comprehend complex jargon), and <Fairest of them All>, for a touch of charisma.

Some combination of those should give her a good enough translation effect.

It took her a few minutes to work out the pathway formula, and then she wove her mana through it, hitting the dragon with a directed beam from the tip of her finger.

She did that while dodging and blocking the fire from the monster. Either it didn't know very many tricks, or Pvilycht had forgotten to mention how docile they were.

After the skill took effect, she asked, “Do you hear what I’m saying to you right now?”

The Dragon snorted. That didn't translate to a response Lexie could comprehend. Or was it that dragons weren't as intelligent as she'd thought? She'd assumed they would be, thanks to all the stories she'd heard and how ancient they were.

But what if they were just animals with no thoughts and only instinct?

Heck, she didn’t even know anymore.

Just then, a shadow fell over her, creeping up behind her.

She used fire flight to sweep away at the last minute, as a barrage of flames nearly burned her to a crisp. Mother loomed over her, about ten times the size of the dragon in the cave and with more menace in her glowing yellow eyes.

“Can we talk?” Lexie asked right before another blaze was sent her way.

Man, I have gotten weak, Lexie thought as she swallowed the next attack with VOID once more.

For the next eruption, she decided to fight fire with fire, sending her own flames to meet it halfway. They collided in a stunning blast of heat, mushrooming to the top.

If they were there, would they eventually join the fight?

She'd better end it quickly before they could.

Look how shiny.” She didn't know what the gem was called, but it was indeed very shiny.
