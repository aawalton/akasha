Standing there, glaring at the still blood-covered spot on the floor where Ray had just turned into light and disappeared, Curtis let out a grunt. It was strange, the emptiness of it all. Was he supposed to cry or yell about the injustice? No, that would change nothing. Ray was dead. That was that.

Curtis sat down on the floor. Rain pattered against the windows to the left, and thunder rumbled over and over again. So stupid. He'd raced up the stairs, wanting to show the progress he'd made in the fight just now. The risk of Ray not being able to deal with a Singer without dying hadn't crossed his mind.

Now, Curtis was alone. Again. The familiar, and admittedly comfortable, cold emptiness spread throughout his chest. Ray left. In the end, that was how it always was. The right way of it. Status quo. Loneliness.

"So be it," he grunted, and got to his feet. Even so, for the longest time, he couldn't look away from the spot of blood on the floor. He tried summoning some emotion, any emotion, but it didn't work. Nothing but weariness. Ray deserved better. Curtis didn't.

Milla (Guild): "We have several casualties already. Please return to guild headquarters, where I can keep you safe. Phoenix Phalanx reports they've lost members fighting against the Singers, and many Integrated have been killed by demons. We must regroup. Return here now, that's an order!"

Curtis sighed and went over to the window, leaving footprints of darkness which surged toward him. The cloak of emptiness unfurled along his back, and the hood rose over his head to cover his face. Embraced by darkness. It felt good. Safe. Comfortable.

Looking out across the city, Curtis saw signs of chaos everywhere. In the distance, another huge demon ran amok. Winged-demons filled the sky, though some looked like they were being struck by lightning. Singers were pouring into the streets from the portal they had opened. Intruders. He'd known for so long, but nothing he'd done made any difference. What was the point of being a Seeker when the things he found were beyond his control?

Speaking of things to be found.

"You're alone," Curtis said, turning to face the interior of the office.

"I am looking for someone," the Singer said. "I have a list. Give me your name, human, and I might spare you."

Even after the Intruders revealed themselves to be Singers, Curtis had been able to track their locations. All their locations. Those who came after, through the portal, weren't as powerful. Perhaps now he would have been able to stand up to one of the original Intruders. And one of these? No problem.

A glimmer of excitement broke through the emptiness as Curtis spun up a few orbs of darkness to hover around him. The Singer was a short, for one of their kind, female with black hair and eyes that darted this way and that, never resting on one point for more than a second. Judging by her build, she was a quick one. A good test of what his abilities were becoming.

"My name is not something you should concern yourself with," Curtis said, spinning up the orbs so they would catch the Singer's attention. The motion did nothing more, but it allowed him to move an unseen patch of darkness along the floor, stretching out of his shadow. Hannah would have been proud.

The Singer shrugged, and her oval face was overcome with a sneer that told him just how little regard she had for humans. These Intruders couldn't be allowed to take root, no matter what. "So be it," she said, and leapt forward.

Darkness reached out from Curtis's shadow and grabbed hold of the Singer's leg. She fell and attempted a forward roll toward him, but cried out in pain when darkness pierced her flesh. Without stopping their spinning, Curtis formed spears of darkness out of the orbs surrounding him, then hurled them straight at the Singer. To his surprise, they missed.

She had some sort of blink skill that even let her tear free of his hold on her leg. Reappearing to his right, she landed on her good foot and then teleported again. This time, she appeared right in front of him, close enough that he could feel her panting breath on his face as she buried a hatchet in his chest, cleaving it open.

"You should have given me your name," she whispered, almost as if she regretted the killing blow.

"You shouldn't have come this close," Curtis whispered back. "I like my personal space."

Her eyes widened, and her hand left the handle of the hatchet, but by then it was too late. Far too late. Rather than blood, darkness seeped from the wound in Curtis's chest, and it clung to both her weapon and hand, climbing up the limb.

The Singer let out a scream as she teleported again, escaping his grasp. Unfortunately for her, the moment of hesitation when she froze from fear had been a little too long, and when she appeared again, the fear turned to horror as she tried to hold her arm up again. It was gone.

"It's mine now," Curtis said. Feeding on her mana, the darkness had consumed the limb, and that wasn't all.

Shifting her weight, the Singer went down in a heap. Screaming, she lifted her leg. Everything below the knee was gone. She might've escaped the hold, but wherever there is light, there is also darkness. Curtis had kept some of his unfeeling, uncaring darkness affixed to her even as she teleported, and now the mana fed him, converting most of it to the stamina he needed to keep the darkness going.

His initial assumptions about the Reaver class had been wrong. It wasn't a caster class, nor a fighter class. Trying to fit into either of those boxes had been nothing but a detriment to his strength. The true power in darkness was not in how one could form it into bolts for attack, or a weapon, like a two-handed sword for cutting. No, the true strength in darkness is in the terror it evokes in your opponent when they realize that it is an all-consuming nightmare that will come for them.

Curtis kept his gaze fixed on the still screaming Singer as he walked toward her, his features drooping as his body turned into the absence of light, the antithesis of emotion.

In doing so, coming to terms with what he was and what that allowed him to do, he got a notification in his interface. Ignoring it, he spoke, his voice coming from every path of darkness in the room at once, not just from himself. "Darkness will come to every Singer on Earth. There is nothing for you here, nothing but emptiness."

As a formless wave of darkness, Curtis cascaded over the horror-struck Singer. It took a moment before her screams cut off. In that moment, Curtis savored her emotions as they fed the darkness, and in that darkness, he dreamed of the moment when he caught the original Intruders and fed them to the growing pit inside him.

Once she was dead and nothing remained, Curtis sat in the spot where Ray had disappeared. Reforming himself was always a challenge, but it was worth it. Worth it for the power. The blood was already drying. A pang of something in his chest gave him pause. What was that? A heavy feeling. Rage? No, he couldn't summon that even if he tried. Fear? No. Sadness.

Curtis pushed it down. He had seen enough of that in his life already. Far too much. He stood and wiped his fingers on his pants, getting most of the smeared blood off. Ray's blood.

The notification blinked in his interface. Annoyed and wanting to get rid of it, he opened it, expecting yet another message from Milla. Perhaps joining the guild had been a mistake. Now, with Ray gone, there was little keeping him there. Luke and Hannah. At least he still had those two. If they made it back out of the portals. Curtis glanced back down at the blood-soaked carpet. These days, there were no guarantees.

Blinking, he saw the notification wasn't a message at all, unless those from the system counted.

Congratulations! Your class has evolved from Reaver. New designation: Hollow. See added notes, skills, and other changes throughout your interface.

"Hollow, huh?" Curtis said, shaking his head.

It fit. There was no question about that, but it felt like the system was mocking him. Like it was saying the emptiness inside him was so great that his old class wasn't enough to contain it. Perhaps it was true. Curtis sure felt empty sitting there. Killing the Singer hadn't even given him an ounce of satisfaction. Even so, there was an undeniable hunger growing now, and this one wasn't sadness, or in his stomach. Almost as if the emptiness inside him wanted to be fed. Perhaps if he did so, he might find it within himself to give a shit about something again, something other than making the Intruders see the mistake they had made in invading Earth.

He returned to the open window. The storm had grown even worse than before, and the clouds were dark enough that they were cutting the reach of the dwindling sun down to almost nothing. Night had come early to New York.

Unsmiling, unfeeling, Curtis stepped out through the window and became one with the night. It was time to feed the growing hunger.
