Ferris headed for the center of Hollowfield. The clan’s compound was arranged with six districts all surrounding the first, which was managed by the first branch. They oversaw administration for the clan, the merit store, and organized the delver groups.

Unlike moving between the fourth and fifth districts, entering the first required passing through a guarded checkpoint. A wall, taller and thicker than the one surrounding the clan, protected the inner district. Ferris and Jade had no trouble passing through the gate after showing his clan identity token.

The merit store wasn’t hard to find, but Ferris had a little trouble staying on track. The buildings were all grand, elaborate, and artistically elegant. Cultivators—evident by their various bonded weapons—were present in higher numbers than he had ever seen. They were still outnumbered by mundane people, but it was surprising to see so many.

Ferris lingered for a moment outside the building used to register for the clan delve groups, but he knew there was no point in entering… yet. Some day.

Entering the merit store, the entrance was arranged much like the library, with a series of lines leading to assistants. The clan did not allow browsing where the valuable resources, weapons, and other treasures were stored. Instead, you would speak to an attendant that could show you a catalog. A specific item could be inspected before purchase, but there were no halls full of treasures on display.

Ferris soon found himself at a desk across from an older lady. Much like Gilbert at the library, she appeared to take pride in her position and carried herself with dignity.

“Adept, welcome to the Hollowfield Hall of Merits, how may we assist you today?”

The woman didn’t give her name, much less her family name, simply presenting herself as a representative of the merit store. Ferris didn’t mind, as it suited his current needs for a speedy business transaction. Still, it was an interesting contrast to Gilbert’s mixture of friendliness and pride.

“Greetings, I am Adept Ferris Bluereef, retainer of Hollowfield. This is my first time visiting the hall, and I am interested in tier one treasures or techniques suitable for use in cultivation, particularly weapons usable in my soul.”

The lady nodded and pulled a large binder out from under the desk. It contained a lot of paper, which she started flipping through.

“May I have an idea of your expected budget?”

“I am willing to spend nine merit points today, if there is an appropriate option.”

She stopped flipping through the book and glanced at him.

“It would be recommended to save up more. I’m afraid the minimum price of a forged construct weapon starts at twelve. Techniques might be cheaper, although you would have to visit the library to view their selection.”

“I see,” Ferris sighed. He was not surprised, as any weapons they sold here would have been made with resources gathered from delves and crafted by special techniques requiring years of training. They would be on an entirely different level of power than the simple spear Ferris had formed with melted demons and qi.

It was a pity they didn’t have cheaper options, but he wouldn’t be surprised if they had good reasons. It just didn’t help him at the moment.

He took the time to familiarize himself with the standard prices. The lowest cost of a tier one resource was three merit points. Any forged tier one magical item, either physical or a construct, started at twelve. Ferris considered the magical chain wrapped protectively around Jade’s core. Foreman Carter had sold it to him ‘at cost’ for six. The man had truly given him a deal, as the specific design wasn’t even listed in the standard catalog, increasing the price to a special-order job.

The minimum reward for job assignments was one merit point per day. If Ferris worked the standard three days per week, it would take a month to earn twelve merit points. He already had nine, so he could have twelve in just three days. If he could wait that long, he could acquire a powerful weapon that would likely overwhelm the chain demons.

Yet Ferris doubted he had three days.

Thanking the lady, Ferris took Jade and left empty handed. Heading toward the library, he wondered if he had any other ways to acquire merit points. Asking for a loan or handout would be instantly denied, and his standing in Elder Harper’s eyes would be damaged by the attempt.

Ferris considered if he could acquire a mundane weapon, and then transfer it to his soul. Non-magical weapons and armor weren’t exactly cheap, but Ferris could certainly afford them. There were many techniques to transfer objects to his soulspace, but he didn’t know any.

The closest thing he had was the Consume Pool, which enlarged and destroyed everything Jade ate. He believed the ability was making a spiritual copy, not transferring. Maybe with some time and experimentation, he could find a way to convert the ability to transfer instead. Unfortunately, he didn’t have the luxury of time.

Even if he could get the weapon into his spirit, it would likely not compare to something created with his qi. In the end, a mundane weapon likely wouldn’t provide a big enough benefit.

It really came back to time. He could try to track the amount of damage the statue was taking. There was a chance it would take the chain demons longer than three days to destroy the statue. He wouldn’t be able to watch while working, but he could check it before and after. It would be risky, as the statue might deteriorate faster than expected.

Even waiting today might be too long, he thought, maybe I should have checked the statue this morning?

Ferris shook his head. That would have taken at least an hour, and had its own risks. Perhaps it was a little frustrating that he hadn’t even considered it, but he had a lot on his mind. There was no use second guessing himself at this point. Better to focus on the path forward.

After making his way to the library, Ferris stood in line. He saw Gilbert among the attendants while he was waiting, and the man stepped forward when Ferris reached the front.

“Welcome back to the library, Adept Ferris.”

“Hello again, Gilbert. Thank you for your assistance last time, it seems I will be in your care again.”

“We find that continuity of service can be beneficial, when possible, especially in the beginning. I am pleased to see you take an interest in the library. How may we be of service?”

“Today I am interested in tier one techniques, specifically any that can aid in combat in my soul. Additionally, I would like to view First Steps of the Soul.”

Gilbert nodded, and then led Ferris and Jade to another private reading room. They didn’t have to wait long before he returned with another list, thankfully longer than the options the day before.

“Once again, these two are considered foundational and are recommended to all Adepts. The other techniques will reference concepts covered in these volumes.” Gilbert pointed to the top two names: Spiritual Harmony, and Mental Materials. “Neither of them is directly intended to aid in combat, but both will indirectly accomplish the goal.”

They sounded like something he needed eventually, but not today. It was painful to ignore them, but they wouldn’t help his immediate problems.

Gilbert moved on to the next section, which contained over two dozen titles. “Here we have a list of construct crafting techniques catered to specific types of weapons. I have brought a list of common options, although more may be available if you have a specific request.”

“All of the titles on this list are priced at the minimum for tier one techniques, which is six merit points. There are more expensive techniques available, but they usually build upon previous volumes or have prerequisite abilities.”

Ferris read through the construct crafting techniques. There were standard weapons like a sword, spear, and bow, but there were also listings for siege weapons, fortified defenses, and traps. The traps were particularly appealing to him, although potentially less useful for assaulting a fortified position like Ferris planned.

Near the end of the list, there was a single volume on creating armor, with a note. It stated that this manual was a generic introduction, but more specialized volumes existed for particular armor types. The idea of constructing armor for himself was appealing, but it likely wouldn’t help against the chain demons. It would just slow him down, making it easier for the demons to grapple and constrict him in chains.

“Gilbert,” Ferris said, while reading the list, “I visited the merit hall today. They sell finished constructs, can you tell me the difference between these crafting techniques and those weapons?”

“Yes,” Gilbert answered, “the cheapest of those weapons are twice the price of one of these volumes. Typically, the knowledge to create something is worth more than the product, but the clan encourages new Adepts to learn self-sufficiency. Beyond the expensive materials used, those weapons were made by skilled Adepts with advanced knowledge of their craft. They cannot be compared to what a novice could make.”

Ferris frowned and looked at the list again. “Would these techniques require additional resources before I could use them?” It made sense, but made them potentially useless for his current problems.

Thankfully, Gilbert shook his head. “No, these all contain beginner techniques that don’t require additional materials. Special resources are eventually included to empower the constructed weapons, but it is recommended to start just using qi.”

Ferris eyed the book titled Basic Spear Construct Crafting. ‘Basic’ it may be, but it would likely provide a weapon much better than his demon reinforced spear. It could even provide magical abilities, although that would likely cost magical resources.

But… his spear had not helped against the chain demons. He needed something that countered their strength.

Ferris read through the ranged weapon options. He didn’t have much training with any of them, as his clan only taught weapons beyond the spear after you become a cultivator.

There were several thrown weapons, but he excluded those due to supply and time. The fortress had dozens of enemies, and if he only had two or three throwing axes it would be hard to assault. He needed something that he could create ammo cheaply and quickly; a large thrown weapon would require more qi per shot.

There were three types of bows listed: short, long, and crossbow. While he wasn’t an expert, he believed the short bow would be the quickest to fire, the long bow would have more distance and power, while the crossbow would take longer to load, but be easier to use.

One entry confused Ferris, and he asked Gilbert about it.

“This book, Basic Snake Sword Construct Crafting, what is a snake sword?”

Gilbert shook his head and said, “I cannot give many details beyond the title, but there is a note that this technique requires more magical resources than typical.”

This ‘snake sword’ also sounded interesting, but not immediately helpful.

Ferris was leaning towards one of the three types of bows. The chain demons were slow and couldn’t reach farther than medium range, so the advantages of the short and long bow reload time weren’t important. The crossbow would be easier to aim, and he could run away to reload. A crossbow might be more complex than a typical bow, but that was why he was paying for the technique. If he had no merit points at all, he might have attempted to create a short bow without guidance.

“I would like Basic Crossbow Construct Crafting, please.” Ferris was decided. He hadn’t found any better option to spend the merit points on.

Gilbert looked… disappointed. Ferris was confused at first, but then remembered the man had suggested Ferris start with the two foundational books. Gilbert probably thought Ferris was ignoring his advice and chasing quick power.

No matter his feelings, Gilbert promptly stood to fulfil Ferris’ request. The man was a professional, after all.

Soon, he returned with an identity reader, much like the one Foreman Carter had used to charge him merit points for the magical chain.

“As this is the first technique manual you have purchased with merit points, please understand that nothing is allowed to leave the library. You may view the volumes here at your convenience at any point in the future.” Gilbert looked him in the eyes. “I understand there are techniques that can copy texts and store them spiritually. This is generally permitted, so long as they don’t damage the source. Keep in mind that it is strictly forbidden for some key volumes. You will be informed if you are granted access to such a technique. It is also forbidden to duplicate any knowledge for sale or distribution.”

Ferris nodded, expecting the rules. He didn’t currently have a way to copy books into his soulspace, but he wanted one. He was giving a decade of time to Hollowfield, he intended to take full advantage of the payment. Amassing a library of knowledge in his soulspace would help him long after his contract ended.

“Now,” Gilbert continued, “All information related to cultivation is stored on jade slips. The library can provide a device to view the information, if necessary.”

Ferris frowned; he had no idea how to actually read a jade slip. He had heard of them in stories, and watched Elder Harper read them in her research. In the tales, delvers would find a jade slip deep within a rift. They would hold it up to their head, and then learn the secrets to some amazing lost technique. Watching Elder Harper hadn’t shown him how to read one himself though.

“Yes, please.” If some people needed a device, then he probably would as well.

After taking Ferris’ identity token and charging six merit points, Gilbert left once again. Soon, he returned with a flat disk slightly smaller than a dinner plate and two pieces of pale green jade. A pair of assistants followed carrying a sleeping cot, and placed it on the ground. Gilbert placed the flat disk on the table. The device had a clamp in the center.

Ferris looked at the device on the table, the two pieces of jade, and back to Gilbert.

“Ahh, Gilbert,” Ferris asked a bit meekly, “I am afraid I have never used such a device before. Are there… instructions?”

Thankfully, Gilbert nodded like this was normal, and explained without further embarrassing Ferris.

“A spiritual gate device, like this jade slip reader, should be natural to use for any cultivator after bonding, albeit slow.” The man glanced briefly at Jade. “This is the manual for First Steps of the Soul,” he picked up one of the pieces of jade, “slot it into the device like so.” The man placed the jade inside a small indent and closed a clamp to secure it.

“Meditate while touching the device’s gate,” Gilbert gestured to a blue gemstone embedded in the device, with a small picture of a hand drawn next to it. The gate, Ferris presumed. “Then avoid your soul. Instead, seek the gate and enter it.”

Ferris understood why the man referenced bonding. That had also involved ‘avoiding your soul.’ Hopefully the next part made more sense in practice.

“Thank you, Gilbert. I believe I can do that,” Ferris said.

Gilbert nodded and left, leaving Ferris and Jade alone in the room with the device. The intention of the sleeping cot was now obvious after the instruction to meditate, so Ferris laid down with the device in his lap. He would have liked to bring Jade with him, but the slime’s mental body was still recovering.

Ferris did unwind Jade’s chain, then wrap it around the table. It wasn’t as good as his dresser, but he thought it would do.

A small voice in his head suggested not bothering to confine his familiar. He’d survived last night, after all. Ferris smothered that voice with a vindictive fury. His bandaged hand ached.

After a final review of the safety lessons with Jade, Ferris finally laid down with the jade slip reader on his chest. His hand rested on the blue gemstone on the device, which Gilbert had called a ‘gate.’

It took longer than normal for Ferris to find the emptiness within meditation to see his soul. Last night had some emotional highs and lows for him. He didn’t like crying; he wasn’t a kid anymore… but he still missed home. Thankfully, time helped quiet the thoughts, no matter how painful. Before he knew it, Ferris saw his soul appear before him.

Instead of entering his soulspace, Ferris did as Gilbert had suggested and looked beyond, seeking that connection through his hand. A faint pulse called out, and he followed it to come upon a doorway—or perhaps it was best called a gate—in his mind.

Entering the gate, Ferris beheld the world inside the jade slip.
