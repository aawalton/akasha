The settlement was smaller than any Veronica had been to. Its population was probably only around one hundred, maybe two hundred people. It was only after they drove farther past the gate that they began seeing the first signs of life.

Villagers walked through the streets, all dressed in worn rags and loose clothing riddled with holes and tears. Though, there weren’t actually any paved roads or sidewalks. Each one looked up as the wagons rolled past.

“I’m not the only one feeling… a bit off-put by this, am I?” Gwen whispered quietly.

“It is certainly odd that there are this many people living out here despite the conditions,” Nasha replied.

Veronica couldn’t help but agree.

Everywhere they looked, they were met with an overwhelming sense of poverty. Not just a lack of wealth, either. None of the villagers looked remotely healthy. Each man and woman was little more than skin and bones, as if famine had taken hold of the settlement. Veronica frowned when she saw that the children looked the same.

“And… am I crazy, or is everyone just… staring at us?” Gwen murmured. “I mean… I get that our wagon probably looks expensive and is worth thousands, but…”

“Yeah, I know what you mean,” Veronica said softly, looking through the curtains.

Being gazed at in awe was not unexpected. However, everyone they spotted simply… stood still. They watched as the wagon passed, rolling deeper into town. The villagers stopped in their tracks, frozen in place just to stare at them. Even after they passed by, many kept their eyes glued to the group for a long while before finally returning to what they had been doing.

They continued their search, looking for any sign of a building that might belong to the village head.

However, they didn’t need to look for long.

The elder had found them instead.

Gwen stopped the wagon as a man in a dirty white robe stepped in front of them. He appeared to be in his late seventies, hunched forward and gripping a cane. He was bald, with a gray beard, and, strangely enough, he never blinked. There was also an ominous presence about him, though nothing that immediately alerted Sage.

Veronica reached out, tapping both Nasha’s and Gwen’s shoulders. They all dismounted the wagon.

“Hello,” Veronica said calmly. “Are you perhaps the one in charge of this settlement?”

The man paused for a moment, taking in a deep inhale before answering. “That is me, wanderer. My name is Lae’varetin. I am the elder of this bustling town.”

Gwen winced upon hearing the word “bustling.”

“My name is Veronica. And this is Nasha, and this is Gwen,” she said, gesturing to the others.

“Mm. For what purpose have you traveled this far to reach our village?”

The man’s voice was unsettling, laced with an edge. Almost like he didn’t want them there.

“We are adventurers planning to explore the Valley of Silence. We were hoping there might be someone in this village who could guide us through it.”

“A guide?” he said, tilting his head, almost… a bit too far. “I see. A guide to the Valley of Silence. The Valley of Silence…”

The man’s voice grew quiet. The three of them grew slightly more anxious, unsure of what was going on. Nasha’s hands slowly drifted toward the daggers hidden around her waistband. Veronica already had her mana barrier active around the three of them, prepared for any sudden attack.

Gwen just looked like she was on the verge of running and jumping back onto the wagon.

“The three of you seem ill-prepared for such a journey,” he finally said.

Veronica’s brows lowered. “Ill-prepared? What do you mean?”

The elder turned around. “Come with me. We shall talk elsewhere.”

The three of them looked at one another as the man began walking ahead.

“Gwen, drive the wagon. Nasha and I will walk,” Veronica said.

“Got it.”

A couple of minutes passed as they slowly drove through town, matching the old man’s pace as he guided them. Soon, they reached a building that looked slightly better kept than the others.

Though, that was not saying much.

The walls were made from rough planks of mismatched wood, some newer than others. Patches of dried mud filled the gaps, while a roof of old thatch sagged under its own weight. A few charms hung above the doorway, made from twine, feathers, and small pieces of bone.

Gwen stared at them.

The elder stopped at the door and slowly turned back to them. “Your wagon may remain here. No one will touch it.”

Veronica glanced around.

Several villagers stood nearby, staring from a distance. None of them spoke. None of them moved closer. Even when her eyes passed over them, they simply watched, their hollow faces unreadable.

“Somehow, I believe that,” Veronica muttered.

Gwen gave her a nervous look, then climbed down from the wagon.

The three of them followed the elder inside.

The interior was dim, lit by a few narrow beams of sunlight slipping through gaps in the walls. A small fire burned in the hearth, though it gave off more smoke than warmth. The smell of old wood, dried herbs, and something faintly sour hung in the air.

There were no decorations. No signs of comfort. Only a table, several rough stools, and shelves lined with clay jars. Perhaps there was a bed somewhere they couldn’t see.

The elder lowered himself onto a stool with a slow breath, both hands resting over the head of his cane.

“Sit,” he said.

Each of them took a stool and sat.

The elder looked at them in turn. His gaze settled on Veronica last.

“You said you intend to enter the Valley of Silence,” he said.

“That’s right.”

“For what reason?”

“Do we need a reason?”

The elder hummed. His eyes still never blinked. Perhaps he was blind?

“The Valley of Silence is treacherous land. No one enters without losing something,” he said. “Many lose their way. Some lose their voices. Others lose their minds. The unlucky ones lose something else.”

“Excuse me, sir, but are you…?” Gwen asked.

“I am indeed blind,” he said, before she could finish the question.

“Was it because of the Valley of Silence?” Veronica asked.

“I was one of the fortunate ones. You can live without sight.”

Everyone’s expressions darkened. Silence enveloped the room, broken only by the crackling embers in the hearth.

“Why do you all live here?” Veronica asked. “The conditions in this village are too poor. Even worse than the poorest rural towns. Not to mention where this place is located, straight in the middle of arid, dry land. There aren’t any trees or vegetation here. How do you all even survive?”

“We are all cursed ones,” the man said slowly. “We stay here not out of willingness, but out of consideration for others.”

“Cursed ones?”

“The ones who remain in this village have all lost something precious to them. That is the curse of the Valley of Silence. Wherever we go, the calamity follows. This village was built to isolate us, so that no one else may befall our curse.”

“Wait… then… is it contagious?” Gwen asked nervously.

The man shook his head. “Fret not, little one.”

“I’m not that little—”

“Short exposure to our presence does no harm. It is only once others live among us for a time that our burden of misfortune befalls them.”

“Even the children?” Veronica asked with a frown.

“The young ones have already been cursed since birth. There is nowhere else for them to go. It is best that we all stay here.”

“Then us asking for a guide…” Nasha chimed in. “Is that impossible?”

He lifted his head, as if speaking to the edge of the ceiling. “If you truly wish to explore the Valley of Silence, we will not stop you.” His gaze returned to Veronica. “We can prepare you a guide. However… you must be prepared to lose something you hold dear.”

Both Nasha and Gwen turned to look at Veronica. For a moment, she was completely silent.

Then—

“We are sure. Prepare us a guide, if you have one,” she finally said.

The elder hummed. “Very well. I have given you the warning. It is up to you to heed it.”

“Hey,” Gwen murmured. “What was all of that about? Curses? Losing something we hold dear? That was all a lie, right?”

“Didn’t look that way to me,” Nasha said.

The three of them were currently back at the gate of the “bustling” settlement. Nasha leaned against the wagon wheels, while Gwen sat in the coach. Veronica herself stood off to the side.

“No, what he said is most likely true,” Veronica said.

“Wait, seriously?” Gwen asked. “There’s an actual curse that we’ll be inflicted with?”

“Aren’t you a healing mage? Isn’t dispelling curses kind of your thing?” Nasha asked.

Gwen leaned back, almost taken aback. “Well, yes, but I’m still just a mage in training. Things like dispelling curses are outside my skill set. I can heal wounds, alleviate minor poisons, and barely make purified water. I don’t know any cleansing magic for curses.”

“We’ll probably be fine,” Veronica said.

“What do you mean?” Gwen asked. Nasha also looked over at her.

“I think I know what type of curse is befalling the Valley of Silence.”

“What is it?” Nasha asked.

“Mm. It’s most likely a demon’s curse.”

“What?! There’s a demon in the Valley of Silence?!” Gwen exclaimed. She turned to look at the town. Several villagers near the edges of their houses were still staring at them. “Isn’t this… like, really dangerous, then?”

“It depends,” Veronica said.

“What do you mean?” Nasha asked.

Veronica crossed her arms, pursing her lips. “The curse left behind in the Valley of Silence may exist because a demon has taken residence somewhere inside. It’s probably a manipulative type, and not a fighter. That’s likely why the ‘curses’ the elder mentioned are things meant to torment them. The demon probably revels in their suffering.”

“What’s the point of that?” Gwen asked.

“What’s the point of bullies bullying someone else? To them, it’s fun.”

“But… if it’s just… a ‘bully’—didn’t they also say that people sometimes lost their loved ones?” Gwen asked.

Veronica shrugged. “Demons are still demons, you know. Even the weakest one can wipe out a small, unarmed town without much effort. If the demon truly wanted to cause harm, they would all be dead.”

“Does that mean the demon will probably be on the weaker side, then?” Nasha asked. “One that we actually have a chance of fighting, I mean.”

“Maybe. It depends on whether we encounter it in the first place. The Valley of Silence should be pretty large. And manipulative types won’t risk attacking us if they think we might be able to harm or kill them.”

“And if we do encounter it?”

“You and Gwen should be able to handle a lesser demon quite easily. I’ve already defeated a demonic soldier back in Greystone, and I’m much stronger now. But if it’s a demon centurion… that will be a bit trickier. I’d say the odds are probably fifty-fifty.”

“What’s the chance it’s a centurion?”

“I don’t know. Manipulative types can be of any tier, so it’s hard to say. But according to Sage, even Tier-3 mages and aura users have conducted expeditions into the valley and survived. That goes for the man who originally found the Tears of Selene,” Veronica answered.

She examined her palm, then balled her hand into a fist. “At best, I can probably push myself into using a fifth-tier spell to defeat a centurion. But only as a trump card. It would most likely damage my mana cores. And considering my tier, the damage won’t be just a minor headache.”

Veronica turned to face Gwen. “I’ll have Sage look up spells for purification and curse removal.”

Gwen nodded, her expression serious.

All three of them turned as they heard the approaching footsteps of a villager. It was the head elder. But next to them was someone that didn’t exactly look like the ‘guide’ type.

“There’s… no way, right?” Gwen murmured. “I was expecting someone more…”

Veronica narrowed her eyes. The taste in her mouth turned sour.

“I have brought your guide,” the elder said. He guided them forward with his palm.

“Hello,” the voice came. “My name is Dusk. I’ll be your guide through the Valley of Silence.”

It was a young, frail boy, no older than probably ten years old.
