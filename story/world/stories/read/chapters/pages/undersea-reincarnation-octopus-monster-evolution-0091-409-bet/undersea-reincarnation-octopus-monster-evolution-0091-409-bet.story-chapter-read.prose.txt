Wolf’s Den was an underground city, hidden below a mountain whose peak Henry couldn’t see past the clouds. From the whole look of the area and the width of its entrance, it might have once been the den of some massive creature. At least, that was the impression Henry was getting as he floated invisibly from across the No Man’s Land that separated the sparse vegetation hiding him from the city entrance. And it was no mere settlement. It was a city. He could see the lights and buildings stretch deep within the mountain’s bowels, until he couldn’t even make the shapes out.

This place might actually be larger than First Landing. How were they even sustaining so many people? Was it just from hunting? Or did they have some hidden agricultural infrastructure hidden within?

Henry wanted to go in and investigate. He was definitely curious. But he didn’t want to attract attention and in there, there was supposed to be a Warden. Someone of Velistraine and Attal’s caliber. Henry didn’t want to deal with someone of that level just yet. So for now, he just pulled his attention back to the fire he and Maurice were sitting around.

They had traveled for the last couple of hours, and they didn’t stop for fights, just to create some extra distance between them and the folks from Wolf’s Den. Hopefully, Henry’s posted clone would let him know if that Omar came back out with more people, then again, with how large this place was, Henry wouldn’t be surprised that there was another exit. So he couldn’t rely only on this clone to keep tabs on them. And if they sent someone after him, they’d have to find him. Though that was likely not that difficult if they had someone with a decent divination capability. While Henry hadn’t shed any blood or limbs that he saw the humans pick up, they could technically pick up some of the wood or dirt he had hit with Toxic Ray, and that might be enough of a scent for divination skills to track him.

“We might need some defense against divination, now that I’m thinking of it,” he muttered, turning the eagle steak over the fire, feeling the heat on his hands. Henry didn’t remember ever camping in his old life. He had to admit, he didn’t hate the experience.

Across the campfire from him, Maurice nodded, tearing into his steak raw before he swallowed the fist-sized chunk in one gulp. “The turtle we tried to find probably knows how to do it,” he said, mouth horrifyingly bloody before he dove back in.

Henry hummed, the fire reflecting off of his eyes. “It did, didn’t it? And I’ve been thinking… we want a way to communicate with the clones I’ve sent with Velistraine. A way to either see past the System’s barrier or send something through it. Guess who can send distress signals and travel through said barrier?”

At first, Henry had thought he’d needed to find a way to cut a portal out of this world to have a chance of reaching the clones, and as they traveled after the eagle fight, he had broken a small chunk out, but it had given him nothing.

Then he remembered; there was one of the turtles still alive on this world. They had tried divining its location to learn about how it had come to this world, back when they knew much less about what was happening, and it had rebuffed their attempt, breaking Henry’s Find the Path, which, back then, had been empowered by Velistraine’s Aspects as well.

Well, ever since the world’s situation became slightly clearer, and once they learned that they’d get their answers by collecting the orbs, the living Rainbow turtle was pushed down the priority ladder. But now that they were thinking of communicating with the outside universe and all that, the turtle once more became important.

“The problem is, can we contact it without it running? Our Aspects are stronger, but it did manage to shrug off our attempt the last time,” Henry said, thinking out loud.

Too busy eating, Maurice sent his next message with Telepathy.“[On one hand, back then our own skills and Aspects had been C-rank. I think. But also, Velistraine had boosted the magic. Now though, our skills are stronger. Our Aspects are also much stronger. But we have no Velistraine…]”

“So fifty-fifty,” Henry concluded, lips pursed as he pulled his steak from the fire, and Maurice nodded, looking as if he’d just murdered someone with his bare teeth. “Fifty-fifty,” he confirmed.

The two ate in silence for a few moments, then Henry grimaced. “That’s not good enough. And I don’t want us to scare it off. What if it could hop away to another world? Or hide better? Might be better to wait until we hit A-rank. Maybe even have the Aspect that’d let us locate it up to A-rank as well. That way we could find it, and really capitalize on it. By then… my Avatar’s range should be much wider. I don’t think I’ll ever be able to cover the whole world in my network, but it’ll at least be a little wider than this. Unless…”

Henry trailed off, eyes staring in the distance. He still had Blink, which was at B-rank. He also had Find the Path, which was part of Trickster’s Pathfinding, at C-rank.

Could he fuse the two? Could he divine a path toward something, then teleport toward it?

Henry straightened up, the skewer hanging limply in his hand as he stared vacantly into the fire. That sounded too good to be true.

“What? Unless what?” Maurice asked, noticing Henry’s sudden alertness. “Wait! Don’t tell me!”

Henry looked up to find Maurice squinting at him. His eyes flicked to the side for a moment, probably to re-play the conversation, then his eyes widened before flicking back to Henry. Then his bloodied mouth spread into wide grin, eyes glittering in the dark. But before he could say anything, Henry raised a hand and cast two skills at once. One retrieved water out of Maw of the Trickster, pouring it out for only a fraction of a second before Control Water grabbed it and shaped it an orb. The next moment, a spray shot out of the orb and sprayed Maurice’s nightmare-fueling appearance.

“Clean your damn face.”

Now that his face couldn’t be used to scare adults and children alike, Maurice came to the same conclusion as he did. A Find the Path fusion with Blink could be incredibly powerful, and it so happened that they’d gotten both skills from the same being. Could that have been the actual, originating skill? Could the turtle have been using a fusion of both, but because of their low level back then, they’d gotten a much weaker, broken down version?

It was possible. But now that this option was on the table, Henry and Maurice grinned at each other. “I bet I could figure it out first,” Maurice taunted.

“I’d like to see you try,” Henry smirked back. “But let’s set some ground rules: we still got some work tonight, so let’s start at dawn. Whomever finishes the fusion first wins.”

“Deal.”

Henry raised a brow. “You don’t want to bet anything?”

It was Maurice’s turn to smirk back at him, raising his chin. “Bragging rights are enough for me.”

Henry barked out a laugh. “You’re on, then.”

With their little bet decided, the two continued eating, in good humor. Dawn was still a few hours away, so they just had some time to wind-down before they found themselves some new monster to train their skills on.

And speaking of training, Henry thought, pulling up his notifications. Ever since the day had started back in that cave, he’d been pushing his newly created Aspect and cornerstone, creating clones, sending them around, coordinating, and widening his territory. He used them plenty in the fight against the eagles, even if he hadn’t relied on them too much.

Still, that had been enough to give him back a level from the regressed Aspect.

1x [Storm Sovereign of Sky And Sea (A) - Lvl 21] core was collected.

Unfettered Echoes of the Trickster (B): Level 7 → Level 8

That in and of itself was good news enough. But then, naturally, Henry took a few minutes to summon more Avatars until he couldn’t, and considering the cost in mana of these new clones, he found himself needing to have nearly half his Octominds on meditation-duty, just to keep up with the expense.

His clones could meditate as well to regain mana so they wouldn’t dip into his own reserves, but only a trickle came back to him. Maybe once he could field thousands it’d make a difference, but for now, what his Octominds could regenerate was good enough. And it was needed. Because three hundred and twenty new clones didn’t come up cheap.

Holy crap… that’s nearly 30% growth, he thought, eyes wide. Then, with a little more calculations, he deduced that the Lifeblood clones’ range grew by 10%, while the Arcane grew by 20%, making it so the latter could maintain a perfect connection for up to 5.3 miles. As for the limit where it would go out of range, that was slightly past 7 miles.

Suffice it to say, his network was growing fast, and Henry could only wonder how much wider it would get at A-rank.

I just need to stockpile A-rank cores until it’s ready to take the next step, he thought, glancing up at the starry sky, which raised another question.

Should he prioritize the upgrade of the Aspect to A-rank first, or should he push for A-rank himself?
