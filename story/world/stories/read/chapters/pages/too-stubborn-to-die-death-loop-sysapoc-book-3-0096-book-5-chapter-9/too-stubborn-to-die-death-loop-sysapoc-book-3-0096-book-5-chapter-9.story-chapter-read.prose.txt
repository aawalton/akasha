Ryan’s Bar

Ryan had heard the news. Aaron and Talia had returned from the dungeon, and of course, he wanted to catch up with them. He had a special brew to share with them, after all.

But he was following a case of particular importance to him.

It had all started not too long ago, when a mysterious magazine had appeared in his bar.

At first, it had raised a brow, but nothing more. And in fact, it had given him a good idea. Wanting to return a little normalcy to the world, the magazine inspired him to set up a book exchange within his bar.

Of course, there wasn’t a lot of written material in their new world, but more was being created by the day. Especially self-written biographies. Apparently, everyone wanted to tell their story from the apocalypse; unfortunately, fewer wanted to read them, having experienced more or less the same thing for themselves.

But that didn’t bother Ryan. More books were good, regardless of what they were. They provided company to the other books, and made his little bookstand look less depressing.

However, there were limits to his tolerance. Limits that were being tested by a barrage of deranged smut that kept turning up. Sure, his establishment was a bar, one that could get rather rowdy at times. But that didn’t mean he wanted his book exchange filled with explicit material, especially because it was starting to attract a certain clientele.

“Heya, Ryan,” Sooty waved as he wandered through the door and made his way to the book exchange. “Oi! Watchya do with all the good ones?”

“What do you mean, good ones?" Ryan said, crossing his arms.

“Err, I ah. Nothing. This is what I meant! A Housewife’s Journey Through the Apocalypse."

Ryan’s gaze followed Sooty as he left, but he could only sigh when the perverted little soot goblin left.

He needed to do something about this, and grabbed his trenchcoat and fedora.

“Hey, hold down the fort while I’m gone, will you,” he called out to his staffer, and made it across the road.

A small cafe had popped up. Unfortunately, everything was gluten-free, but he hadn’t come for stodgy bread. He had come to solve a mystery.

Pulling his fedora low to obscure his face, he leaned into his chair, sipped on a responsibly sourced latte, and waited.

He knew they would act, and he wasn’t wrong. Less than an hour into his stakeout, a figure came walking past.

They looked away from him, head low, and he couldn’t make them out. They briefly entered his bar and exited, turning down an adjoining street.

Ryan had to be fast. He noted where they were going, and quickly ducked back into the bar to confirm that yet another obscene magazine had been placed in the book exchange, and took chase.

Zigzagging down the narrow alleys that had hastily been built for Dober’s growing population, he chased after the figure, doing his best not to be seen.

I’ve got you now. I won’t let you escape.

He followed them to an unsuspecting house, and then waited for a moment as they let themselves in.

Now!

Not a second could be wasted, and Ryan stormed straight up to the little home and swung the door open.
