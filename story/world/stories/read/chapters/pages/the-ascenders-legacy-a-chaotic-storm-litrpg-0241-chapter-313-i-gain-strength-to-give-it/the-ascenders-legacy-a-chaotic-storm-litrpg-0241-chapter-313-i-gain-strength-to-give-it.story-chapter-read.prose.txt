Down at the base of the mountain, mouths hung open as the disciples watched Aodhán push past the 1050th step, his pace brisker than it had been in the last hundred stairs.

Each step he took elicited gasps of amazement and head shakes, as disciples wondered where this sudden surge of strength had come from. Some attributed it to the rain, others to yet another skill from his seemingly endless arsenal.

Even the Specials speculated, their expressions scrunched in amazement. Reaching the 1000th step was already impressive. Continuing forward was just mind-blowing. No one could explain it. But there was no doubt that they respected it. Admired it even. And when Aodhán crossed the 1060th step, they found themselves unable to keep that admiration quiet any longer.

Cheers, screams, and applause rang out for each step—tens of thousands of cultivators cheering Aodhán on, and though he barely heard them over the violent trembling of his spirit and the booms of thunder, he felt them—his core sense unable to ignore the immense amount of admiration, awe, and amazement rising from the crowd.

Those emotions pushed him forward, bolstering his strength and endurance greatly.

But there was only so much the cheers and the refreshing effects of his Aspect could do against the escalating power of the mountain's aura, and nearly an hour later, Aodhán was forced to a final, insurmountable stop at the 1097th step, his entire body rattling and spirit groaning.

Cheers rose from below, urging him to take one more step—to force himself to the 1100th step and round it up to a nice little whole.

But Aodhán couldn't.

Already, he could feel what seemed to be micro-fractures forming across his spirit, not physical but painfully perceptible—like glass splintering under too much pressure. Chaos bled into his spirit without control, seeping in through those cracks like blood from a hundred tiny cuts.

Aodhán glanced at the last three steps, wanting to take them. He could take them. He still had enough will and stubbornness to push himself forward. But he knew that doing so would only put his spirit under more stress, widening the fractures already marring it.

And so, for his own safety, Aodhán gave up and took a step backward.

Cries of disappointment filled the air instantly even as people cheered and applauded him, completely amazed at how far he had made it.

And when he turned to take in the crowd, those cheers became so loud the earth and skies shuddered with them.

Still shuddering under the weight of the mountain, Aodhán was unable to suppress a shaky smile as an immense feeling of belonging bloomed within him. For over five hours, these people had gathered and waited to see how far he would go. They had cheered him on when he'd been faltering. Encouraged him when his competitive spirit had given up. He could feel their admiration. Their love and amazement as they roared below him—a river of storm cores all just like his yet so vastly different—all cheering for him, all accepting him.

Tears pooled in his eyes as joy bloomed within him, so deep and full Aodhán was certain he'd only felt it once before—a time so long ago he could barely remember.

The joy of a house full of family. The joy of uncles, aunts, nieces, and cousins all present. The joy of food and warmth. The joy of a mother's love and tight hugs. The joy of family.

The emotion bloomed larger within him like a tangible thing, and just when he felt as if his heart were about to burst, a memory fragment from the deepest recesses of his being suddenly rose to the surface of his mind.

A cozy room washed in soft gold light. The faint scent of pine and cinnamon. Glimmers of colored lights reflecting off ornaments. Laughter. High, bright, childish. Off-key voices singing. Gift wrappers tearing. Fireplace crackling. And warmth—so much warmth it hurt.

The memory vanished almost as soon as it came, flashing across his mind like lightning across the surface of a storm. It wasn't a memory Aodhán remembered, but he recognized it regardless.

The decoration of lights. The music. The food. The people. The joy. The gifts.

It was undoubtedly a holiday. A holiday with his family back on Earth before the fire, and though he recognized no faces, he recognized a voice. The same voice that had screamed for him to run on the night of the fire.

The voice of his mother.

The tears pooling in his eyes fell in that moment as a deep longing for the family he had lost rose within him. He hadn't known it before, but it seemed he'd had a large, loving family before the fire.

And now you have another.

The thought rose in his mind with the voice of his mother, and Aodhán's smile turned bittersweet as he took in the new family the thought referred to. Emotions surged within him, unrestrained, and with a shuddering breath, he fixed his gaze on the base of the mountain and teleported, eager to meet them.

He appeared at the base of the mountain an instant later, a wide smile on his face, but he hadn't taken into consideration the reduction in pressure. The oppressive aura of the mountain that had pressed down upon his spirit for the last five hours suddenly vanished, leaving him as light as a feather, and his body wasn't prepared for it.

He collapsed, unable to hold himself up.

The judges rushed toward him immediately, their expressions a mixture of admiration and concern. Before they could speak, though, a profound silence suddenly descended on the mountain, so complete that Aodhán felt as if he'd gone completely deaf.

He looked up immediately, recognizing the sensation as the same one he'd sensed back at the arena. And in the air, floating down from among the clouds, were the six High-Elders, each one wreathed in lightning that encircled them like a galaxy of stars.

This time, however, he wasn't the only one who could see them.

The whole sect could see them, and without hesitation, everyone bowed—whether in the air or on the earth. Aodhán tried to do the same, but his body was too weak, so he pushed himself to his knees instead, panting as he tried to get his breathing under control.

For nearly a minute, no one spoke or moved until one of the High Elders—a woman with flowing crimson locks and an aura of majestic destruction—spoke, her voice reminiscent of the crackle of lightning. "Lift up your heads, disciples, and bear witness. The First Seat has an announcement."

Every eye shifted to the First Seat instantly—a mountain of a man with flowing white hair that reached down to his knees and runic irises of such intensity they shone like a sun among stars. The First Seat was the highest-tiered cultivator in the entire sect and was considered the closest person to divinity for members of the sect.

He was god in the eyes of many, the servant of god in the eyes of others—and Aodhán quickly understood why the moment he spoke. Unlike the other High Elders, his voice didn't emanate from his mouth when he spoke. Instead, it emanated from the storm roiling above, a boom of thunder so fearsome it shook the earth to its core.

"Today, you have witnessed a trial unlike any other in our sect's history. Aodhán Brystion, at the thirty-sixth tier of advancement, has ascended to step one thousand and ninety-seven, cementing himself as a special among specials." He smiled and glanced at Aodhán, who had finally managed to pull himself to his feet. "Come up, child. I want everyone to see you."

Aodhán hesitated a moment, and with an exhale, he rose into the air, draconic wings unfurling from his back in a burst of chaos and electricity. They flapped once, twice, until he stood only a few meters below the High-Elders but above everyone else—even the elders who had arrived in the last few minutes.

Tens of thousands of eyes fell on him in that moment, their voices completely silent yet loud with admiration. The weight of that admiration descended upon him like a cloak—neither physical nor spiritual, yet tangible regardless.

Expectation simmered in the air as the crowd waited for an explanation of the impossibility that he was, and Aodhán wondered if they would still gaze at him with such admiration and respect when they found out who he was and what he represented.

Zarxan had mentioned that transmigrants and double inheritors were praised and respected in Gordon. He could see it in their reaction to the Specials. But Aodhán wasn't just any transmigrant. He was a transmigrant whose presence within the sect would spark a war, and he couldn't help but fear he would suffer a different fate than the other transmigrants.

After living in fear and caution for nearly a year, he couldn't bring himself to believe he would truly be accepted when they realized what he represented.

And so, with his heart thundering within his chest, Aodhán glanced up at the First Seat, half-expecting soldiers to rush out from nowhere and bind him in shackles or for the crowd beneath him to morph into a mob of violent disciples the moment his status was revealed.

His fear must have been obvious in his expression, because the First Seat's expression sobered, and he said, "Aodhán here is a foreigner from the continent of Lutia—a continent once powerful, now crippled by their own foolishness." He looked straight at Aodhán then, runic irises sharp. "But this is not Lutia, and here you will not be hunted for what you represent. Rather, you will be respected, because we know your value and understand the workings of fate and karma."

Aodhán felt a wave of relief at the man's words, and he nodded in acknowledgment.

The First Seat smiled and glanced back at the crowd. "Many of you may have guessed correctly what Aodhán is, but for those who haven't, I'm happy to announce to you that the Storm Sect has just gained its fourth transmigrant, becoming the only sect to ever do so in the last century."

Cheers erupted once more, and contrary to Aodhán's fears, the crowd didn't pull out knives and pitchforks to hunt him down, nor did soldiers bearing shackles descend from the sky.

Instead, they erupted with a thundering wave of whistles, screams, and applause that shook the earth with its intensity. Their excitement filled the air so tangibly Aodhán felt goosebumps rise on his skin. His heart thumped loudly within him—not with fear, but with an overwhelming sense of belonging and pride so full it threatened to burst from his chest.

So this was what it felt like to be accepted. To be praised for living his truth rather than hunted for his strength. These people didn't want him dead. They were simply happy he was part of the sect. Power wasn't individual to them like it had been in Lutia. Rather, it was collective. The sect was one, and the strength of anyone was simply the strength of the sect.

And his inclusion in the sect had just granted them all prestige. A win for one was a win for all.

Slowly, a smile bloomed on his face, growing steadily until it lit up his whole face. Within his spirit, Varéc growled, excitement surging from him to Aodhán as if he wasn't already dealing with more than enough.

The cheers rose higher into the air, becoming a chant of his name, and Aodhán's smile soon broke into laughter, wings flapping excitedly behind him in response.

That excitement didn't last long though, as the First Seat raised a hand to quiet the crowd. The chanting quieted immediately, and the First Seat continued. "We are more than overjoyed to have Aodhán as a disciple of this great sect. But our rise in power will not go unnoticed for long." His gaze hardened, and his voice gained a sharper edge. "Sooner or later, the other sects will notice, and they will seek to pull us down and rip us apart. But shall we hide our heads in the sand out of fear like cowards?"

A resounding NO echoed from the crowd, so loud and unified it stabbed right through Aodhán's mind like a spear of conviction.

The First Seat smiled widely and asked again. "Shall we reject this transmigrant because we fear a war?"

Another NO!

But the First Seat wasn't finished. "Does the storm fear the chaos of war?"

Another NO resounded, and the First Seat's smile broke into a wide grin. "Then we shall not fear. Any sect that comes to us seeking war will find it. Anyone who moves against one of us moves against us all. We have seized ourselves a great position in Gordon, and we will not let it go—not for the blood of our enemies or their enemies."

"A war is coming, disciples. And if we are to win it, we must all become strong. All of us, from the outer sect to the high elders, including myself, and together, we will become the most powerful sect in Gordon. Who's with me?"

This time, what erupted wasn't just cheers or screams, but a feverish, bloodthirsty cry of agreement that rose into the air like a declaration itself. That declaration soon became a chant of allegiance and of support, and Aodhán shuddered at the sight, goosebumps erupting across his body as thousands of cultivators declared with one voice their readiness for war.

It was incredible how unified they all were in a decision that would have taken Lutia months, and despite his earlier hesitance, Aodhán couldn't help the excitement that bubbled within him in that moment.

His smile returned, and he looked up at the High Elders, a hand on his chest as he echoed the chant rising from below. "I am with you. I am strong. And I will give my all to protect the sect."

His gaze met Zarxan's, and the High Elder nodded in acknowledgment. Aodhán's smile widened in response, and he looked back down at the chanting crowd below him.

He hadn't been in support of the war before, and he still wasn't. But with the fervor of the crowd below, it was obvious he wouldn't be fighting this war alone—not physically, emotionally, or mentally. He would be fighting alongside thousands of brothers and sisters—each wielding different truths of the storm.

He would be fighting alongside his new family. And together they would be unstoppable. Invincible. Forged anew.

He may have fled Lutia in search of peace and safety. But he knew now that the Storm had brought him here to spark a new age. To scorch the earth in lightning and smite their enemies with thunder.

This time, however, he would be doing so with an army at his back.
