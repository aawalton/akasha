Chapter 37: Restless, Part 2

The funniest part of being woken up by Raeva was realizing just how well he’d been sleeping.

After a big day like yesterday, Ambrose honestly expected to be too worked up and defeated to sleep, but he was out almost as soon as his head hit the pillow. Raeva’s knock on his door had woken him with a jolt, and it took him a few seconds to remember where he was. He turned the lamp up and answered the door without bothering to get dressed. When he saw it was her, he couldn’t help but tease.

And yet, she still came inside, and paced while he sat on his torturously soft and smooth bed. For her, he’d resist the temptation to slide back to sleep.

“So,” said Ambrose, “something on your mind, or just a bit of everything?”

Raeva ran a hand through her hair. Her braids were coming apart, and frizzy black hair poked out between beads. “A bit of everything, but…It was the fights. I was almost useless.”

It sounded like it cost her something to admit that, so Ambrose decided to be gracious and not torment her over it. “You weren’t useless. No one was, but I bet we all felt it. Well, other than me. Everyone else had a rough time.”

She paused mid-step and looked at him like he was a disgusting fungus. “Really?”

“I’m not bragging,” he protested. “I got a legendary card and it turned out to be actually pretty great. I’m not tooting my own horn here, I’m grateful for our good luck and am just acknowledging that it probably kept us alive. But so did you, and so did your brother. The closest we had to someone not pulling their weight was Jessica, but I think we both know she made up for it in the end.”

Raeva relaxed a little. “Did you have to put it in the most dickish way possible?”

He smiled ruefully. “At this point, it’s habit. Besides, I thought you saps all liked me the way I was? Did something change, or were you lying so I wouldn’t betray you?”

Raeva was taken aback, and even Ambrose felt a bit of surprise at how hurt he sounded. “Actually, forget I said that. I’m choosing to believe that you all suddenly regained your sanity and realized what a great, powerful, smart, attractive -- “

“You done?” Raeva asked.

“I notice you only objected when I said attractive.”

She groaned and threw her hands up in the air. “Could you please be serious for a minute?”

He sobered up instantly and nodded. Patting the spot on the bed next to him, Ambrose said, “C’mon. For at least five minutes, I’ll be the charming, good version of me you all seem to think exists.”

Raeva relented and joined him, tucking her legs underneath her. For all of his jokes, Ambrose had never once lied or exaggerated about the fact that she was a fiery, vibrant woman whose company he enjoyed. Sharp, beautiful, and just as acidic as he was. He wracked his brain for something good to say.

“It was a bad matchup for you,” he said finally. “That fight. Your cantrip was basically useless in a big brawl, and we got overrun. But between your brother and Luthor keeping things at bay, and you and me taking out their creatures, we did get stuff done. We lasted long enough for the Edict to end, right? And here we are, still alive, thanks to Jessica. Teamwork, right?”

“I guess,” said Raeva, hugging her chest. “I thought we were all a lot stronger than we are. We’re the best in our class. Next year, we were going to graduate and become important. And Vanderborn slapped us down like we were nothing. Where do we go from here?”

Ambrose smiled and risked scootching over closer. He tentatively put his arm around Raeva’s shoulder. She allowed it, and leaned against him, not quite relaxing, but that was okay.

“The big thing to remember is that we’re still using starter decks. They’re meant to be balanced and give us a taste for things, but now that we’re all Affinity Level 4, we have better options. We’ll customize our decks to be as strong as possible, and good for working with each other. Give it some time and you all might even catch up to me.”

Raeva laughed again and poked his bare chest. “Lord of the imps. It did come in very useful last night. You probably are the most dangerous one out of us now.”

“I was before, too. Well, maybe second-most,” he said before she could protest. “I guess we’ll never know now, but it’s fine. We have bigger things to worry about. And in the morning, we can make plans and take our first steps towards…whatever we choose to do. Harassment and mild terrorism, probably.”

Finally, she let out a breath and leaned against him. She was warm, and a strange mixture of wiry and strong, and soft. In spite of enjoying the moment, Ambrose understood it for what it was…And what it wasn’t.

“Thanks, Amby-lamb. This helped.” Raeva turned and kissed him on the cheek. Then she slid off the bed and headed toward the door.

“What?” Ambrose said, grinning like a fool, “You got what you wanted and now you just leave? I feel so used.”

“The money’s on the dresser,” she said, before closing the door.

He burst out laughing, and laid back down. The lights were still dim, and sleep wasn’t too far off…

But her visit got him thinking, and soon it was all he could do. After ten minutes, he huffed and got dressed in his ratty, formerly exquisite formalwear. It was the only outfit he had. He left his cabin behind and headed to the galley, only to find that he wasn’t the only one up.

Raeleq sat in his spot from the night before, eating slices of buttered bread.

“Is everyone having trouble sleeping?” Ambrose groused.

*****

As painful as the last day had been, Raeleq Ransa found himself pretty cheerful, overall. Sure, they were on the run, and might not ever see their families again…but this was as good a fight as they were likely to get, and they were alive and together. Things were daunting, but overall, they were okay for the moment. It was something worth remembering and being grateful for.

That, and the abundance of food they had. Their eating area was small, but the Wanderer’s storage capacity was ridiculous, and it felt like they had enough supplies for months. So why not eat some bread that SNAC had cooked up but hadn’t served with dinner?

When things got tough, the younger Ransa tended to get hungry. Hurt? Snack on something. Tired? Get a bite to eat, then nap. Angry? Well, eating definitely helped. There were few things a good meal couldn’t solve. Well, there was a lot it wouldn’t fix, but it made him feel better, and it gave him time to think things over.

Despite what others might believe, Raeleq wasn’t stupid. He might have different priorities and skills, but that didn’t make him any less than anyone else. That was the good thing about being on a team: everyone got to contribute and help out with what others were bad about. They were different, but everyone was equal.

It was during this thought that Ambrose Adams stumbled over to his table and gave him a look. “Is everyone having trouble sleeping?” he complained, but it seemed half-hearted.

“Nope,” said Raeleq. “I slept for like seven hours, and now I’m eating before getting up for the day. Not that there’s much to do, I think. A day after a loss like that is best spent resting and thinking about how to do better.”

Ambrose paused, blinking, before he sat down across from him and stole a slice of bread. “And what do you think we could’ve done better or differently?”

Raeleq took a bite full of delicious, salted butter on sourdough and thought about it. “I wouldn’t have sent [Reggie] out first if I thought we’d need to last longer to survive. If he hadn’t been taken out early, I think we could’ve stopped Vanderborn’s thralls from getting out of hand.”

To his surprise, the shadow wizard looked thoughtful rather than full of his usual disdain. “You might be right. But would that have changed anything in the end?”

He shrugged and drank some water. “Who knows? Can’t really say one way or the other, but I can think about being better for next time. What about you? What would you do differently?”

Ambrose didn’t respond at first, and that was fine. Big questions weren’t meant to be answered fast. They were best chewed over, like paash leaves on a long journey. Raeleq busied himself with grapes in a bowl, popping them into his mouth one by one. Patience was free.

“Maybe I would’ve run out of the office and jumped off the gardens before it could turn into an argument,” he said finally. “If I was gone, you all would’ve left too, and maybe we could’ve bought ourselves another year before he screwed us over.”

“Not being there tends to win fights before they start,” Raeleq agreed. “But this one was probably coming, no matter what. You said he was watching us, right? He knew we were going to be trouble.”

The thought didn’t bother him as much as it could have. It made sense that the paranoid de facto leader of the world would keep an eye on troublemakers. If Raeleq was bothered by anything, it was the lack of opportunity to actually cause trouble. But then, they had that now, greater than ever before. Time, space, and a ship to get around, everything they needed to be a thorn in the empire’s paw.

“Yes, he was watching you, and I saw a note about using you and Raeva against your father. Now they might try to do the reverse. Are you going to be okay if they do that?”

It was a thought he tried to avoid. Raeleq pushed the mostly empty plate of bread away and hummed thoughtfully. “I don’t see that we have much of a choice. Our father is a powerful man, with many allies. He wouldn’t want us to give up to protect him. A warrior trusts his peers to do the right thing and get the job done, no matter what. He trusts us, so we must trust him.”

Ambrose’s eyes widened, and he leaned back slowly, an unreadable expression on his face.

“What?” he asked, suddenly self-conscious.

“It’s just…I think this is the longest conversation we’ve had that didn’t involve lecturing me about torturing your pet’s soul, or whatever.” Ambrose smirked, but it didn’t contain the usual malice behind it. “Our lives really have been turned upside down, haven’t they?”

Raeleq laughed, deep, booming, and free. “They have. But I can think of worse people to have on our side.” A horrible thought occurred to him. “We could’ve had Kim instead of you.”

It was Ambrose’s turn to be surprised. “Damn, maybe you do have teeth after all. Brutal, but fair. She would’ve surrendered instantly, wouldn’t she? Thanks for the perspective.”

The Bedamin prince stood, wiping his mouth off with the back of his hand. Everything was still sore, but food and talk helped. Now, he needed to move a little to help with digestion. He nodded to Ambrose, and carefully maneuvered his way out of the cramped space, and above deck.

SNAC piloted the ship, but he wasn’t the only one up and about. Luthor knelt on the deck, hands raised in deference to an invisible god, looking relatively kempt despite being in the same damaged formal clothes as everyone else. They’d need to figure out a way to get clothes and other things, but that was a problem for later.

Raeleq waited until his best friend got close, then stood beside him, waiting for the ritual to end.

*****

Out of all the group, Luthor Graves was perhaps the least affected by their current predicament. At least, he was the one most suited to adapting to it and accepting their circumstances. And he did it with the same tenacity and focus he approached nearly everything with.

The most important things were understanding, and planning. They didn’t plan on being on the run, but here they were. And since they were, Luthor adjusted the plans smoothly, and would keep changing to meet the challenge, no matter how bad things got. Victory wasn’t just a matter of skill and power. More than anything, it was about will and endurance.

It started with going to bed early, and waking up early, as he always did. One of his most prized skills was the ability to fall asleep nearly anywhere, and catch rest in smaller doses during times of stress and danger. This definitely counted.

The first thing he did was get dressed and go above deck, to face the expanse. It was unsettling but beautiful, and it raised all kinds of questions.

For example, if there were countless worlds out there, independent from one another, what did that mean for the gods of Amaria? There were a good dozen in the Amaric pantheon, but Luthor worshiped Solum, god of light, healing, and protection. He woke up early most days to greet the dawn and say his prayers and devotions.

But how did you do that in a place where there was nothing but dark skies and no sun to shine its light on them? Did Solum exist only on Amaria, or was he just one aspect of a greater god who appeared in all worlds? The question was an inescapable itch in the back of his mind.

“What time is it?” he asked SNAC after a few minutes of silent thinking. “On Amaria, I mean.”

SNAC tilted his head in Luthor’s direction. “It is nearly five thirty in the morning. Sunrise will be soon. Do you require food, Master Graves?”

A humorless smile spread across his face. He didn’t like the title, but the damned golem refused to be anything less than deferential. “No, that is not necessary, thank you. I’ll wait until everyone else is up. Tell me, is this ship stable and safe to move around on? If I were to run around the edges, would I be at risk of falling off into…whatever is out there?”

“No Master Graves, there is no risk of ‘falling’ into the expanse. Our ride is smooth, and there is a shield protecting the ship and keeping everyone on it and not floating out into nothingness. There are dangers in the Aetheric Expanse, but Master Vanderborn designed this ship well. You are safe.”

It was mostly reassuring, at least. The golem bothered him in some ways, but Luthor knew better than to turn down free intelligence and service. SNAC would be a liability someday, but at the moment, he was their greatest resource outside of the ship itself.

He nodded once, climbed down the stairs, and began jogging around the edges of the ship.

There was just enough time for him to get a good stretch in. Every day, without fail, starting with some physical activity and prayer helped him get his head on straight. So for about fifteen minutes, he bent and twisted himself, holding straining poses until he felt himself limber and loosen up.

When Luthor had a light sweat, he picked a spot on the fore of the ship, facing the expanse directly, and knelt. He raised his hands, palms facing forward and slightly upwards in supplication. There was no sun, no fellow worshipers, no temple, nothing but his own imagination and faith.

But what did he believe, when it came down to it? What did that belief even mean, in a universe like this? He didn’t know, but that didn’t stop him from praying anyway.

“From the rise of dawn,” he whispered, “to the descent of dusk, hear my prayers, Lord Solum. Provide your warmth to my mother, who is about to face her greatest trial. Shine your light on my friends, give us guidance to get through this ordeal and come out the other end stronger. Give us life, to enjoy and defend, and give us the righteous fire to burn away injustice and impurity.”

Luthor rarely asked for fire, but it seemed appropriate now. When Vanderborn came after them, it would lead to a second confrontation. They’d need to be ready. Maybe Solum couldn’t hear his prayers from so far away, but it didn’t matter. The point of prayer wasn’t just to be heard. It was to speak from the heart. To open one up to the possibilities of grace, strength, and wisdom. It wasn’t about tangible results, it was about one’s ideals, and striving to be worthy of them.

“Here in this endless darkness, please, keep us in your heart and hands, for we carry your spark, and we work to light up the world with our words and deeds.”

Dimly, he was aware of Raeleq behind him, up early as well. The Ransas, like most Bedamin, venerated their ancestors and prayed for guidance and strength, just as he did from Solum. Who was to say they weren’t the same source? Luthor bowed his head and finished, standing when he was done.

“Did He answer this time?” Raeleq asked.

“Hm. If He did, it was quiet. It doesn’t matter. I’ll answer with my own fire, and share it with as many as I can. How are your wounds? My card isn’t quite ready yet.” Luthor had an uncommon healing non-combat card. It wasn’t too strong, and only had a few charges each day, but it had been enough to keep them from needing serious attention.

“Well enough. I don’t expect any of us are going to be willing to do anything today. We’ll rest, eat up, and maybe tomorrow we can make some plans.”

Luthor nodded, then motioned with his head. He started jogging again, and Raeleq fell in step beside him. They did a few laps in familiar, comfortable silence. Their conversation wasn’t over, only paused for when they were ready to pick it up again.

It was how they worked, and always had. So much of their communication was silent understanding, similar values, and a decade of friendship at work. They jogged until they both worked up a good sweat, the ever-present chill of the expanse held at bay for just a little longer.

Until movement caught his eye, and they stopped at the fore of the ship. Luthor pointed at a silver streak, moving across their field of vision. “Do you see that?”

Raeleq stopped and screwed up his eyes. “I think so. What is that?”

That’s when SNAC’s voice called out, amplified but still soft in tone, “Excuse me, Master Graves. I see ships on the horizon. Should we hail them?”
