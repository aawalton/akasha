Chapter 122

Darkness took the sky and fell through the trees.

Riley watched the light change like she was staring at the second hand move on a clock, ticking down the seconds before her force sprang into action.

She raised her fist in the air, and once the light finally disappeared, she dropped it. Her army moved. Quickly but quietly.

As they closed in, Riley opened the HUD.

Opening the HUD to plan the battle was a familiar feeling, one she'd truly missed. Her eyes narrowed and she got to work, creating two fighting groups.

Group B: a mix of archers, infantry, and cavalry, twenty in total. Riley nodded to herself and ran through the tactical steps of how she thought the battle would unfold.

Group A: eighty soldiers, a mix as well. She smiled slightly, thinking of the meat of her army pushing the final thrust to victory. The south gate would fall, and so would the Clawborn.

Relax, Napoleon. You're getting ahead of yourself.

She assigned Group A to line the trees facing the south wall inside the tree line, then looked at Thorne and filled in the mental checkbox. Thorne looked back at her, waiting for her to say something. She just turned her attention back to the HUD, thinking she had actually said something to Thorne. She was in flow. Verbal reconciliation would come shortly, once her tactical mind and the rest of her brain merged back together.

She focused on Group B, assigning them to face the east wall inside the tree line.

Riley turned her head and whispered to Thorne and Valrik on either side of her. "We move to inside the tree line in front of the main gate."

Both nodded and moved.

They approached in silence until one of the soldiers stepped on a stick hidden in the darkness. Everyone stopped. Riley's hand went up again and she froze the movement in the HUD.

The Clawborn archer in the southeast tower looked in her general direction. The cool night breeze hit her face and did nothing for the drop of sweat rolling down her back. She'd burned Pop-Tarts faster than this scout was taking to assess the twig-snapping situation.

Finally the archer's attention was redirected when another guard tapped him on the shoulder and he turned. Riley and her group exhaled in silence, and she signaled to move.

Both groups reached their assigned points. Riley focused on the HUD and targeted the two guard posts on the east wall with Group B's archers.

"Let's light these clowns up."

The archers lit their arrows and loosed them. The small pouches of wildfire attached to each arrow splatted on target and the fire caught.

The lookouts turned and desperately flailed their arms, but the Clawborn couldn't easily put the small fires out.

The east wall guard posts quickly started to look like a game of Whack-a-Mole as Clawborn heads popped up and down, trying to put out the fire that was now spreading and starting to engulf the lower part of the guard posts' roofs.

Riley, half enjoying the first attack, set up similar attacks on the southeast and southwest guard towers with Group A's archers. It wasn't long before all four fire targets, the two east wall posts and the two south wall corner towers, looked like horrible birthday candles on a Clawborn shit-depot cake. The northwest tower, out of reach on the far corner, stayed dark and manned.

Clawborn started to yell, and what sounded like a cowbell began to ring as desperately as the hands trying to put out the fires.

Riley gave direct orders to Valrik: take a handful of soldiers to the main gate in the turtle formation with buckets of wildfire fuel, paint the main gate under the archers' cover, then retreat to the tree line.

As Valrik turned to move, Riley grabbed his arm. "You are not painting a picture. Just cover a bunch and get out of there. Got it?"

Valrik looked down at her hand, then back into her eyes. He nodded slowly. "Yes, Warden."

The rotten egg smell finally reached Riley as the fire continued to spread in the guard towers and posts. The roofs were ablaze and unmanned as the guards moved down to the walls. The fire spread light in and around the depot, an unfortunate consequence of the attack.

The walls provided less cover for the guards. Riley redirected her archers to start picking the Clawborn off the walls where they could.

"That's right. Now you can't hide your ugly faces behind cover as easily."

One of the Clawborn archers started to scream as the fire on his back overtook him. His arms flailed and he ran toward other guards further down the wall. The receiving guards, not wanting to become part of the new Clawborn lighting system, pushed the flaming guard back. He fell over the wall, flailing frantically to the ground, and landed with a thud. His arms stopped moving soon after.

Riley's face flashed a grimace for a second, and then her focus turned to Valrik and the turtle now on the move. The formation's shields caught the orange light as it advanced with each grunt the soldiers gave in unison. They crouched low and moved with purpose, crossing the road straight for the main gate.

Clawborn arrows started to shower down on the top and front of the armored turtle. Ting, ting, the arrows sounded, testing the turtle's shell. The turtle stalled for a moment. Valrik grunted and half turned his head. "Move! The shields will hold!"

The men in the turtle started to move again, and more arrows showered down, banging louder from under the shields than the tinging sound they made when they hit the outside. Sweat dripped down the backs and legs of all inside as they pressed forward.

Volleys of retaliatory arrows flew from the trees back toward the Clawborn archers, who were now exposed and frantically trying to decide where to train their next arrows. One arrow found and buried itself into its Clawborn target. Landing with a squish, it took the archer's eye, then his life. The body half-hung over the wall, now being used as additional cover by another.

Clawborn arrows divided their next volley between the immediate threat of the turtle and the tree line beyond. The next arrows were loosed and hissed through the trees past Riley. Several small thuds sounded behind her as most of the arrows found their targets in trees and the ground. Two of Riley's soldiers were the unfortunate recipients. One archer fell as an arrow buried itself in his arm between the armor plates, and another in his neck. He coughed and spit blood from his mouth, and it poured down his cheeks. The second, an infantry soldier, caught an arrow in the face through his eye and was dead before he hit the ground.

Riley turned and made a half-move toward the wounded soldiers as Thorne's hand grabbed her arm.

"Warden. Leave them. The battle first."

Riley looked at Thorne, her face pale from shock and stunned into silence. Thorne gave a single nod, and her eyes blinked back to life. She half-nodded back before turning to face the battle once more.

Focus on the live ones now. Heal the fallen later.

She ordered the bodies moved to the back of the line to be collected after the battle.

The east wall had double the number of Clawborn archers now, since the guard posts on that side were full of flame and unmanned, and the men who had been on them had come down to the wall. Archers of both sides shot back and forth, neither bending to the other's attacks. Riley turned the HUD's focus on the east wall and adjusted the attack. She made the arrows pinpoint a quarter of the wall instead of the entire wall. The next round was more concentrated and peppered two archers, one of whom dropped his bow and covered his face with his hands as three arrows hit him. He started to scream and fell backwards into the depot.

The turtle was closing on its south gate target when a loud boom ripped across the battlefield. The Clawborn depot main gate started to open. Riley's face dropped as the wood she was targeting slowly disappeared behind the depot walls, and fully armored Clawborn guards started to pour out in front of the south wall, taking up a defensive formation. In one short moment the Clawborn guard had formed two lines. The back line held long ten-foot pikes; the front line was manned with large shields and broad, shiny but worn swords. The long pikes lowered between the front line, creating an almost living Clawborn tank.

Between the shields, Valrik watched the formation take shape and brought the turtle to a stop. It was a standoff. The Clawborn had just castled their king, and Riley was left with the next move.
