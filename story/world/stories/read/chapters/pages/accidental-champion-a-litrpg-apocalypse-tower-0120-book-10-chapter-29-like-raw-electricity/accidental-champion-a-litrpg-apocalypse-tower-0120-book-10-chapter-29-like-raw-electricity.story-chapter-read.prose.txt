The song of energy was beautiful.

He listened to it for what might have been months before turning to the next sense. It frightened him, every time he blocked a sense to use another, but he was looking forward to it. He was fidgeting with expectation, awaiting the change.

For the first time in a long time, Xavier could feel again. He reactivated his sense of touch, and there was so much to touch. The feeling overwhelmed him. He quickly switched off his hearing. The deafness was profound, leaving him with a vast emptiness after the beauty of the music of energy he’d been listening to for so long.

His heart felt like it was hammering against his chest. He could finally feel it again. The stone floor he sat on felt colder and harder than ever. His breath—oh, he could feel his breath! For so long, despite first being able to see the breath come in and out of his chest, or being able to hear it, he’d felt almost as though he must be suffocating.

Not so anymore.

Every little sensation was too much and yet not enough at the same time. He’d been starving for touch and he hadn’t even realised, because he couldn’t feel his hunger for it. Every bodily sensation, a shiver up his spine, the feel of his fabric on his skin, the weight of his body as it was pushed down by the intense gravity of the Void Dungeon—all of it was… exquisite.

But there was one thing he couldn’t feel, one thing he was used to feeling—or, at least, sensing—and that was the energy flowing and cycling inside his body. He was dulled, completely, to the energy channels, as though needles had been shoved into them and a local anaesthetic had been applied. For so long he’d been able to feel his energy channels with the use of spiritual sense that it had almost felt like an extension of his touch.

Yet it wasn’t. It was a different sense altogether.

Still, without feeling his energy his sense of touch felt muted.

Once the feeling of overwhelm passed, he was hit by another feeling—one of vulnerability. This was a feeling he’d grown familiar with, especially when all he’d had was hearing. But being only able to hear was very different to only being able to touch.

What if there was a beast running at my right now, and I couldn’t hear it coming? What if its jaws were about to clamp down on my head, and I was blind to it?

What if… what if… what if…

He sunk into the feeling of fear, and that emotion was so much stronger than it had been without his ability to touch, because without touch he hadn’t felt the things inside his body either. The beat of his heart of the breath coming in and out. He also hadn’t felt what fear did to him. It had been a mental emotion. Now, it was physical. Visceral. Loud.

Xavier couldn’t ignore it. Not in his current state. So, instead, he did everything he could to feel it. He accepted the emotion and let it rush over him. He did the same with everything else he felt. And there was so much there. He wasn’t sure how long it took before he raised his head and opened his eyes—though he couldn’t see anything, eyes open or otherwise—and released a long breath.

It was time to get to work.

Being left with only the sense of touch wasn’t nearly as disorientating as he’d thought it would be. Feeling his way around was easy enough. It wasn’t long before he made it to the wall. Even that short trek from the middle of the Dark Tunnel to the side, he’d already felt his sense of touch expanding. It wasn’t only the sensations of his body he could feel, or his foot touching the ground, or his hand reaching out and his fingers brushing the stone wall.

He felt the very air in this place. Everyone could always feel the air. When they moved their arm a little fast, and there was that woosh, as though wind was gusting at you. Or when there was a breeze, or someone breathed on you…

But when the air was still and the body was still, it was much harder to feel.

Xavier felt it intensely, as though before it had been hidden from him, like his brain had cancelled it out because it was nothing more than white noise.

Better able to feel the air, he could actually feel the shift he made in the air as he moved, and the more he moved the more he understood those shifts. And there were other things he could feel. Shifts in the air that seemed to come from far away, down the Dark Tunnel. When he’d only had his sense of hearing, he’d strained it as hard as he could to stretch down the Dark Tunnel, and yet he hadn’t heard a single thing. There were Void Dungeon-created beasts down there for sure, and yet none of them made a sound. It was odd.

The shift of air, however, was very telling. There could have been a draught, of course. An open door somewhere… not that it would open to the “outside,” as all there was outside with an airless void.

Something’s moving down there, and I can sense it, which means if something were to come close, I’d be able to sense that too. Well before it got close enough for me to touch.

His sense of touch seemed to expand faster than his sense of hearing or sight. And hearing had expanded faster than sight had. It was as though each time he did this his brain cottoned on to what he was trying to do and helped him along with it.

When he touched the wall, brushing his fingers along it, he found himself searching for the things that he’d been able to hear. Whatever was on the wall—a wall that had looked smooth to his natural eyesight—he’d been able to feel instantly. Once more, he ran his hand along the wall. What he felt were odd shapes, shapes that felt familiar somehow just as they had when he’d heard them.

Xavier frowned. He worked on this for hours. Then the hours turned to days, then weeks. He mapped out what these things felt like in his mind—a process that was much easier than it had been with hearing—and it wasn’t long before he realised exactly why they were familiar. He’d dealt with them before. He’d even looked for them when he’d first arrived in this place, but he hadn’t been able to sense them anywhere.

They were, of course, runes.

Yet, there was something different about them. They weren’t the exact same shapes that he was used to. Weren’t drawn in the exact same ways. He couldn’t understand what the difference was, and as much as he wanted to experiment with them, now wasn’t the time.

There would be time for that later.

Still, he was exceptionally curious about the runes. He wondered why it was he hadn’t been able to see them, even though he’d been able to hear them—at least when he brushed his hands along them—and now he could feel them.

Are they different because they’re in a different language? Runes are the language of the universe, but there are many universes, and here we are in the void…

A startling thought occurred to him.

Could there be a language of the void? Could these be… void runes?

His spell patterns always worked in the void, something that he’d never questioned. Now, it made him wonder, as universes were where that energy came from… that and himself, of course.

And if there were void runes, did they run on Void Energy?

He shook his head. There would be a lot of experimentation in his future.

When he’d mapped out a thousand of the different runes, he made his way back to the centre of the tunnel and sat back down. He could tell it was the centre from an easy flap of his wings that sent a gust of air all around him. The way the air shifted and returned told him the exact distance to the walls.

He rested his hands on his knees, and it was lovely to actually be able to feel that sensation, then began pouring energy out of him in that same criss-crossing pattern of strings as before. He could have started by trying to feel his energy channels inside his body, but something told him that would be harder, though he couldn’t say he was right about that.

Once he’d started pouring strings of energy outward, he raised his arms, holding them in the air where the energy flowed. He needed to be able to feel them, after all.

It didn’t take as long as he expected.

Where the energy had looked like clumps of gas hanging in the air, had sounded like magical notes, it felt like raw electricity. The hairs on his skin didn’t stand on end and yet it felt as though they should. The electricity was different to electricity he’d felt in the past. And perhaps electricity wasn’t even the right word for it. It was just the best word that his mind could reach for.

He supposed it felt like energy, but not in the same way that it felt to his spiritual sense.

Still, it was remarkably familiar to him, as though he’d felt its brush in the past, or all along, and hadn’t even realised he’d been feeling it. Perhaps his sense of touch was the most susceptible to sensing energy after his spiritual sense, or perhaps his brain had simply become accustomed to reaching into the deep past of its evolutionary programming and altering his senses—or rather, expanding them—to be able to feel energy as they always should have.

It wasn’t long before Xavier felt he was ready for the next sense. And if he’d been frightened to switch to touch, he was even more frightened to only have a sense of smell.

He opened his sense of smell and rather abruptly shut off his sense of touch.

Instantly, he was overwhelmed once more. Every aroma in the Dark Tunnel was far more intense than it ever had been before, and the stench of the Gorgotlin blood he’d spilled in the tunnel still sat in the air after all the time that had passed. The rest of the air was… stale, though there were hints of freshness, and he wondered if that was coming from farther down the tunnel.

He tilted his head to the side and breathed deeply through his nose.

Smell and taste were the two senses he was the least confident about. Taste especially—what was he going to do, lick energy?

He pushed that thought away and focused. The feeling of vulnerability slammed into him as it had in the past, but it was easier to brush aside. He had been safe inside this Dark Tunnel near the start of the Void Dungeon for a long while now, and something told him that wasn’t about to change.

Besides, he was confident he’d be able to smell any beast who tried to come at him.

It wasn’t long before he started being able to single out the different smells. In a way, everything he smelled was very familiar to him, but none of it was all that distinct. When he separated the different smells, he realised there was more still attached to him than he’d thought. He could smell a faint hint of Adranial’s perfume, and even a little bit of her sweat—her natural scent—that he’d been carrying along with him all this time. That brought a smile to his lips, as vivid memories of the last time he’d held her played in his mind.

Xavier sat there a long while, discerning different smells. But he didn’t wait as long as he had with the other senses to bring out the energy. He was confident in this expansion of senses. Confident that he was nearing the end of this path.

And he wanted to see what they would all feel like when he finally brought them together. Then, he would finally look at the runes on the wall with every sense that he had.

Not only that, he’d be able to look down the tunnel again. Maybe he’d be better able to discern whatever it was down there that was watching him…

Then there was the Void Dungeon itself—the last smell he’d separated before working on detecting energy had been the smell of the dungeon itself.

There was something off about it.

Something… festering.
