Zaiden didn’t bid me farewell. I guess to him we’d always be together. I still couldn’t get how he worked so differently from my other two thralls. I understood that he wasn’t a monster like Dusty and Weedrill, but his freedom to move about and disconnect from my will was strange. It made me wonder if there was something more to this bond we shared, something I hadn’t discovered yet. As Noland and I walked away from Typha and Zaiden, I could still feel the lingering connection to him, like a gentle tug on an invisible thread. It wasn’t uncomfortable, just... there.

‘You’re awfully quiet,’ Nolan observed as we made our way through the wheat fields, the golden stalks swaying gently in the breeze.

‘Just thinking,’ I replied, my claws leaving tiny impressions in the soft earth. ‘How are we going to make it to Esme without a teleporter? The feather I have isn’t going to take us there.’

‘Don’t worry. I got that covered.’ He pulled out a crystal from his inventory and tossed it in his palm. ‘One of the fae priestesses gave it to me. Shit, I already forgot her name.’

‘What did you do for it?’

‘Quit smoking in her courtyard.’

I deadpanned him.

‘Apparently I was dropping ash on her prize-winning flowers. She was pretty pissed about that.’

I couldn’t help but find the whole situation amusing. Only Nolan would accidentally anger a fae priestess over cigarette ash and somehow walk away with a teleportation crystal.

In no time, we made it to the outskirts of Ebor to meet up with Esme. Her sister Mesalla wasn’t around this time, Esme mentioning she was out collecting herbs and spices for their potions. Her shelves were pretty empty, so that was understandable. Her stock usually dries up around this time, Esme explaining that the closer they were to the Tournament of Champions, the more potions people needed. “Unfortunately, they aren’t for champions,” she mentioned as she brewed on her work bench. “Common-folk are looking for an edge, something to protect themselves when we are auctioned off to the highest bidder.”

“You don’t have much faith in our future, do you?”

“I’m not the type to encourage high hopes where there isn’t a lick of light at the end of the tunnel.”

Nolan chuckled. “That’s where you two differ.”

“But, I’m happy to see your dragon has evolved,” Esme said as she looked down at me with a soft smile.

“She’s not my dragon, just Freya.”

“And fairly quickly too, if I might add. Someone’s been very lucky. We need a little of that around here.”

“Well then, I’d say my timing is impeccable. Because here’s some luck,” Nolan said as he pulled out Myrim’s Magical Base Elixir I’d given him back before we teleported and settled it on her bench.

“What’s this?” Esme asked, looking at the vial funny.

“You don’t recognize it?”

Esme picked it up and brought it to eye level, her fingers twirling the neck of the bottle around before she gasped. Immediately, the neko mage dropped what she was working on, her ears perking up straight. “This is... this can’t be...” Her voice trailed. “Myrim’s base elixir?” She looked up to Nolan through those big glasses confused. “Where did you get this?”

“Let’s just say we had a very productive dungeon run,” Nolan said, crossing his arms. “The question is, can you work with it?”

Esme bought the bottle up to the light streaming through her workshop window. The liquid inside shimmered, leaving the girl speechless for a long while before she asked, “Work with it? What do you mean?”

“We need a cure for Khunum’s curse.”

Her eyes ballooned up. “Nolan?”

“I know you’re the only one who can do it, Esme.”

“You expect too much from me! Maybe Esomi—she was the one who had the expertise for this type of potion crafting,” Esme said, shaking her head. “I’ve never attempted anything on this scale before. I’m just her apprentice.”

“Was,” Nolan corrected gently. “And now you’re the master alchemist of Ebor, whether you feel ready for it or not.”

Esme’s tail swished anxiously behind her as she carefully placed the vial back on the workbench. Her fingers trembled slightly.

“This is... Nolan, do you understand what you’re asking? Creating a cure for a curse of this magnitude…. It’s a two part problem. The parasite, and then the faux antidote that turns the victims into ghouls.”

Nolan nodded gravely. “I know it’s a tall order. But if anyone can do it, it’s you.”

Esme’s ears flattened against her head as she paced the workshop. “Even with Myrim’s base, this is complex alchemy. The parasite alone is unlike anything I’ve studied—it adapts, mutates. And the faux antidote was designed specifically to work with it, creating that... that horrible transformation.”

I watched her carefully. Despite her protests, I could see the wheels turning in her mind, the way her eyes kept darting back to the vial. She was already working through the problem.

“I’ll need samples,” she finally said, her tail still flicking anxiously. “Fresh samples of both the parasite and the antidote. And time. This isn’t something I can brew overnight.”

“How much time are we talking about?” Nolan asked, leaning against the workbench.

Esme ran her fingers through her hair, and then said, “Weeks, maybe a month. And that’s if everything goes perfectly.”

I looked up at Nolan, concerned. And then he said, “Esme, we don’t have that kind of time. The tournament is right around the corner and we don’t have champions. Not to mention, the infected are spreading faster than we can contain them.”

“Then what do you suggest? Magic? A miracle? I can’t break the laws of alchemy just because we’re in a hurry!”

“No, but maybe we can bend them,” he suggested. “What if we focus on something that slows the progression rather than a complete cure to start with? Buy us time.”

“That’s a waste of an elixir. Besides, testing the duration of temporary relief would take longer than finalizing a complete cure.” She paused for a moment and contemplated. “I’m not saying it’s impossible, just... difficult. I’ll need to work day and night. And I’ll need help gathering the components.”

“Anything you need,” Nolan offered. “We have a schedule of our own, but this is important. We’ll make time to fetch you ingredients.”

“Let me fetch my books,” she said and then let out a deep sigh. “You’ve given me a major assignment in the busiest season of the year.” Esme disappeared into the back room, returning moments later with an armful of old, leather-bound tomes. She dropped them onto the workbench with a heavy thud, dust particles dancing in the air.

“Our family archives and references,” she explained, her fingers reverently tracing the worn spines. “Father’s research journals on advanced transmutation and curse remedies… they could lend me some insight on how to tackle this task.” She opened the top book, revealing pages filled with intricate diagrams and cramped tiny handwriting. “If there’s anything that can help us adapt Myrim’s base to counter Khunum’s curse, it’ll be in here.”

I peered over the edge of the workbench, trying to make sense of the complex alchemical formulas scattered across the yellowed pages. The text was dense, filled with symbols I didn’t recognize and calculations that made my head spin.

“Let me skim through these. Give me about two hours in silence.”

“No problem.”

Nolan nudged me to give Esme some space before she sent us out to fetch her materials she needed for this miracle cure. It was nice and sunny out, so waiting out by the stream was perfectly fine by me. The water babbled peacefully over smooth stones, and I found myself stretching out on the grassy bank, letting the warmth soak into my claws. Nolan walked over and plopped right next to me, with a slice of that honey cake he swore was poisonous! I gave him an odd look and he shrugged, wanting me to believe he was taste testing it. Heck, if he wanted the cake for himself, he could have just said so!

After a while, I became anxious. It was always good vibes between Nolan and me. We rarely fought, and when we did, it was always temporary. So after he downed two slices of cake, I felt this intense itch to tell him what had happened last night. He pulled out a cigarette, and for a brief while, there was a moment of absolute tranquility before I blurted out through our private link, ‘So, heh, funny story…I think I’m a god.’

Nolan scoffed. “Well, ain’t that something.”

I turned to him sternly. ‘I’m serious, Nolan.’

“Same here,” he said before he took a drag and rolled the fumes out his nose. “Kind of already suspected that when you turned on an entire dungeon core.”

‘Then why didn’t you say anything?!’

He shrugged. “I don’t know. Maybe I was waiting for you to find out for yourself.” He finally turned to me. “So, how did you find out?”

‘Zaiden brought me to a hidden shrine yesterday and… I heard them, Nolan. The gods that are trapped in the infinity realm.’

“What the hell is an infinity realm?'“

‘The realm where the Infinity Tree is. It’s this massive tree that connects all the realms together—at least, that’s what it looked like. That’s where the gods are imprisoned.’

Nolan took another long drag, contemplating this information. “And these gods... they spoke to you? Like, directly?”

‘Not exactly. It was more like whispers, fragments of thoughts.’

He exhaled a plume of smoke that curled upward into the clear blue sky. “Well, shit. That’s quite a discovery. So which one is it?”

‘Which what?’

He leaned his arm over his knee as he hunched his back and clarified, “Which god are you related to?”

‘Oh, um, I don’t know. But if I had to guess, it’s not one of the good ones. And, well, I heard my dad’s voice, and—’

“Your dad?”

‘Yeah. One of them sounded like him. Exactly like him. But again, there wasn’t any presentation of him or the other celestial being besides their voices. I don’t think they even knew I was around.’

“How did you get to that plane?”

‘I am not sure. The godly shrine’s powers were faint but they were still there and I channeled it. I couldn’t keep a stable enough connection I guess, because I was only there for a few seconds. I was… contemplating telling you because I was afraid you’d look at me differently for being an offspring of an infernal god.’

“Well, we already knew you had dark roots from somewhere. Your trip to this realm only confirmed suspicions. But that’s no reason to hate you, Freya. I don’t want you to be afraid of telling me anything. We’re a team, and we’ve been through too much together for me to judge you based on your parentage. Hell, I’ve seen what you can do, and I’m still here, aren’t I?”

“You didn’t like what I did in the dungeon, when I inducted Zaiden. I saw that look on your face, Nolan. You doubted my intentions.”

“No, I doubted you knew what you were doing. Because if you couldn’t get him out, his death would be on your shoulders, and I knew you couldn’t handle that. I trust your morals. I trust you’ll do whatever it takes to keep away from the dark side. But when I see shit that could go south, it’s my job to warn you about it, no matter how it’d make you feel. My job isn’t to babysit your emotions and make you feel good about every decision you make. My job is to keep you grounded.”

I considered this, feeling the knot of anxiety in my chest begin to loosen up. ‘So you’re not afraid of me?’

“Afraid? No. Concerned about what powers you might develop without proper guidance? Sure.”

‘Prince Theron’s powers… it had called upon him, the darkness, promising him wealth, power, and immortality… the whole nine yards. It was deep and evil, Nolan. I went in there not knowing if I could contain him, if I could suppress his powers, but I was desperate. I went from not being able to face my own darkness to overcoming a level five dungeon boss’ with the help of Zaiden.’

“Shit, I guess having him as your slave wasn’t a bad idea after all, huh?” He smiled. “But besides his help in being a suitable conduit, you did what you had to do. When someone’s cornered, they become another beast entirely. You’d be surprised what you can do when it comes down to the wire.”

‘I’m sorry, Nolan. I didn’t know how else to stop Theron.’

“Hey, why the hell are you apologizing for?” he said to me sternly. “You did what had to be done. I heard what he said, the prince. While up there as raw energy, I heard everything. Very few defeat that dungeon. We didn’t have the tools to defeat a powerful entity like him. You thought outside the box and handled business, and I’m proud of you.”

I smiled, feeling tears begin to mist in my eyes. ‘You don’t know how much hearing that means to me. When we first met, I was so scared of telling you why I was special. I was afraid of embracing my darkness, because I had no idea what it truly meant. If it was something I was destined for, something that I’d eventually embrace no matter what I did. Even with the confidence Wilna gave me, there was still this subconscious thought of turning, and I think that’s what stopped me from conquering that other part of me. And I’m sure that’s what stopped me from summoning my thralls.’

“Well, you can relax now, can’t you? I know all of your dirty secrets in your closet.”

I laughed. ‘You know, I realize that necromancy proficiency should only increase when I do necro stuff. But I think my bloodline carved a path to allow my codex to offer it to me through quests. Now with this darkness, I doubt that would still be the case. I can’t ignore it anymore, Nolan. It’s stronger than I’m willing to admit, and I can’t keep pretending like it doesn’t mean something more. If it wasn’t for my connection to darkness, I wouldn’t have saved Zaiden, Lun, Typha, or you, Nolan.’

“Oh yeah. I saw what you did there,” Nolan said with a grin on his face. “You bound a level 120 tier five dungeon boss. Your necro proficiency isn’t even that high, and you managed to not only bind him, but trap him in your domain. I’m impressed.”

I giggled. ‘Well, don’t give me all of the credit. You guys helped me, too.’

He snickered. “A dragon god, now that’s certainly interesting.” He paused. “I almost lost you a couple of times, didn’t I?” Nolan said as he took a draft of his cigarette. Then he chuckled to himself, his expression lost in thought. “To think I’d become this attached to some random baby dragon in the woods.”

I gave him a playful glare. ‘Why does it sound like you’re regretting it?! Be honest, I’m the best thing that’s ever happened to you.’

He paused for a long while before he admitted, ‘I had a daughter, once….’

I froze.

“Zev killed her, and my wife, Esomi….”
