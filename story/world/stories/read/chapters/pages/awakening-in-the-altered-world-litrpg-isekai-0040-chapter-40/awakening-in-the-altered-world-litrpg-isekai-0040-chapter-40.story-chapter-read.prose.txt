A few minutes later, Alex and Beckett had cleared the ice beneath them. Beckett was cross-legged, while Alex was leaning back in a similarly relaxed posture. There was still a chance there were other rift creatures around, which was why they still had their weapons sunk into nearby patches of snow.

Manas flowed all around them, in the air, as with Beckett’s tinkering, and in their bodies.

Alex winced as his forearm seized up again; he started cycling Pure mana to clear the Force mana in that limb.

As he did, he glanced up at the skies.

It feels like we were moving so fast the past half-hour, there almost wasn’t time to stop and think about it all.

Jesus, that was a lot of fights.

His eyes flickered to the giant chunk of ice in the distance, half-hidden from this angle by the trees, but still gleaming in the light.

“What’s up?” Beckett glanced over, seeing his expression.

“Are we really about to do this?” said Alex.

Beckett grinned guilelessly. “I guess, yeah. If you want to.”

“What do you mean, ‘I guess’?” said Alex, a bit amused. “You were the one who said we’d clear this entire thing, right?”

“Yeah, but like—sometimes I just say things,” said Beckett. “I mean, I believed it, but it was on a whim. I said it because it felt right.”

“I was thinking initially it’d probably be fun to try, at least,” Alex said, reflecting. “And take it as it comes.”

It all felt quite sudden, like he didn’t have a chance to fully appreciate things until now.

“That is a giant glacier,” said Beckett, glancing over. “I might go check it out in a bit, maybe see if I can identify it.”

“Just don’t get too close, you might trigger it,” Alex said quickly. “The boss’s supposed to have a ‘zone’ it’s supposed to stay in. You might be able to feel it with your soul senses.”

“Gotcha.”

Another few seconds.

“I was thinking about maybe fighting the final boss before I’d started the rift,” said Alex. It seemed kind of quaint. “Five minutes into the rift, and I was pretty sure we’d get like halfway in.”

“If it makes you feel any better,” said Beckett. “I didn’t have any idea what it’d really be like either. And we’re here now, right?”

Alex glanced at him. “If you told me what we’d had to get through to get here, from the start…”

“You’d be sure we’d fail, and you’d be wrong,” said Beckett easily. “See, this is why I think it’s better to be a little oblivious.”

“Okay, I still think this is how you get yourself killed.”

“I didn’t say totally oblivious,” said Beckett. “I’m not saying you should be an idiot, I’m saying, like—I just think you have to give yourself a chance to surprise yourself. If you always know what kinds of challenges you’ll face, all the terrible things that might happen to you, you’d never start anything, right?”

“Very wise,” said Alex, only somewhat joking. “In all seriousness, I can see the value in it.”

At least he could see why it worked for Beckett; he still felt like he’d want to go into situations clear-eyed.

He liked feeling secure in fights in particular, he’d realized, like he had a clear sense of what he wanted, what his enemies were capable of, and such.

Gleaning anything from those giant icebergs without triggering the boss fight radius will be an interesting challenge, to say the least.

A few minutes passed, and Alex was pretty much recovered physically. The ice was long cleared from his body. Beckett was likely a few minutes from clearing all his own ice.

“I think I’m missing something with this,” said Alex, studying his extremely tight forearm, lips pursed.

“Like with Force augmentation?”

“It’s actually acting on my muscles now, which is nice, but I’m trying to get it to be responsive to my body’s natural force,” he told Beckett. “So it acts as kind of a force assist.“

“But it’s still just seizing up,” said Beckett, realizing.

“Yeah, it’s endlessly contracting the muscle.” Alex knew he wanted Force to act a certain way, but somehow actually structuring it to act like that felt a bit beyond him.

He was pretty sure it was possible with his proficiency level; it wasn’t like producing shrapnel with Pure mana. Force augmentation was relatively complex, but he felt like it was within his current abilities. It feels more like a lack of understanding, maybe, of like—how I’d actually achieve it, precisely?

Alex decided he’d switch to the Force sinks aspect of Force cycling, which he was having a bit more success with. As he poured mana into his right arm, he wasn’t using Force sinks, exactly. He’d realized it’d be too difficult to ‘sink’ only the Force he wanted to; it felt easy to accidentally also sink the increased forces he’d hopefully achieve with Force augmentation.

His mental model of what he was trying to achieve was closer to ‘reinforcement’—kind of like sinks, as in a reactive, absorptive kind of Force, but running through the structures of his right arm; he was almost thinking of it as a Force brace within his body, which he’d use to absorb a percentage of incoming force. It felt like a bit of a synthesis of his earlier thinking about Force braces, as well as his experiments with Force sinks.

As Alex worked, Beckett seemed to be having increasing success getting his Fire and Lightning mana to coexist. Several strands of Lightning crackled around a dense line of Fire, but it all seemed decently stable.

Alex watched as it all vanished. Beckett had a few beads of sweat on his head.

“Quick break,” Beckett explained, producing a water bottle from his Interspatial Ring.

After drinking, a few seconds of silence.

“Well,” said Beckett finally. “We’re here.”

“We are.”

“I’m still getting whiplash, honestly.” Beckett stood, looking up at the temporary skies, and Alex recognized his expression. “Four days ago, I was supposed to start work the pool at this private club nearby, and now it’s like—”

“Wait,” said Alex, to clarify. “You were going to be a pool boy?”

“It would’ve been lifeguard work, occasional cleaning, just, be in charge of the pool in general,” said Beckett. “It wasn’t used much. That’s not—what I’m trying to say is, it’s all so sudden—what’s so funny?”

Beckett grinned, bemused.

“Sorry,” said Alex, collecting himself. “It’s just, the image caught me off guard—a little out of the blue. Please, continue.”

“What I mean is,” Beckett continued. “It’s weird, isn’t it? It’s incredible, but like—the fact we just switched universes...”

“Yeah,” said Alex, sobering up a bit more. “It’d be weird if it didn’t feel weird.”

After a pause—“There isn’t much you can do about it,” said Alex. “At least, that’s what I’ve been telling myself.”

Another few seconds. “The magic is—it’s magic, so there’s these upsides, but there is this terrible sense of vertigo I keep feeling when I think about it a little too much.”

“That’s exactly it.”

“There’s a few implications,” said Alex. “I’ve been trying not to… like, what about my self in that other Universe? I guess I’m just gone?”

Beckett was silent as well; Alex’s guess was, it was an aspect of all this Beckett had been thinking about as well, subconsciously if not consciously.

“I guess,” said Beckett slowly. “I’m just grateful my folks on this side, at least, have been—it’s been weird, but they’ve been pretty accepting. If they weren’t…I really don’t know.”

“Yeah,” said Alex, who couldn’t seem to stop saying the word. “There’s this pretty well-supported theory that you’re more likely to be universe-swapped if you’re very similar to your other-universe self, at the time of the transfer. The ‘swapped’ people tend to be almost identical, if not identical, where it counts, but if I’m honest—I’d be pretty weirded out if my sister was replaced with an identical sister.”

Beckett’s eyes flickered over. “Maybe we’re getting a little deep with this?”

“I mean,” said Alex, a bit uncomfortable. “It is what it is, that’s reality.”

A few seconds. “I get this is personal,” said Beckett, glancing at him. “But have your folks—?”

“I haven’t said anything about it yet,” said Alex.

Beckett looked at him very weirdly. “What do you mean you haven’t said anything yet?”

“I mean, I haven’t seen them yet,” said Alex awkwardly. “I’ve tried to set things up, and I’ve texted them, but I felt like—it’s something you should talk about in-person.”

A weak smile from Beckett. “But you’re, like—”

“Being a bitch about it?” said Alex, returning the weak smile.

“Scared,” said Beckett, after a hesitation. “Was what I was going to say.”

“It’s probably going to be fine,” Alex continued. “I checked our text histories a bit, but... yeah.”

And with this new world, if Alex was totally honest, it had been kind of easy, and even reasonable, as he’d convinced himself, to set things up, and wait a little to tell them, and get lost in all this magic he was genuinely into. The incentives were aligned.

He felt pretty guilty, which he was aware didn’t actually make things any better.

A silence.

“I’m not sure if this is helpful,” said Beckett finally. “If this happened to someone I cared about, I’d realize they weren’t exactly the same person, but I’d get that they’re as disoriented as I was.”

“I appreciate it,” said Alex lightly. “I’ll be seeing them in two days, anyway. We’ll see.”

“It might be that it’s all fine?”

Alex glanced at the time.

Fifteen minutes, he realized. We’ll have to start scouting the boss, and finish up whatever magic we have left.

“Are you sure?” Beckett met his eyes. “Alex, when I said we didn’t have to finish this, I was serious.”

“Let’s at least check it out,” said Alex. “We’ll give it a try if it seems reasonable.”

He smiled a bit. “If we’re honest, I could use a boss fight right now.”
