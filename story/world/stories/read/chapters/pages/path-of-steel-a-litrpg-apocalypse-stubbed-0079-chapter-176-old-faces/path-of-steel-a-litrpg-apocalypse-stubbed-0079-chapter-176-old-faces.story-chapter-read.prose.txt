“Yin Yang Collapse!” Feng Shen yelled.

“Pimp Slap,” Alpha replied.

Their attacks clashed in the sky, releasing extreme busts of aura. Gary’s robes billowed as he rushed towards the battle.

Boulderus Johnus, Feng Shen, and two champs clashed against the four brothers and Liu Dong, the swordsman Gary had fought just half an hour ago. Avernus the witch fought the leader of the Academy forces not far to the side.

The brothers, by themselves, were half of the puppeteer’s elite forces. Gary hadn’t realized they were so extreme. They also seemed to have replaced Zink with a white-robed, hooded figure that was chanting endless buffs and heals. Or maybe that was the Church leader—Gary couldn’t be sure.

What he did know was that his side was losing, as they were four to five. His addition balanced the scales.

“Thunder Thrust!” he said, dashing towards the healer. An web of red chains appeared in front of him, and he slid to a stop. Had he kept going, his spear would have gotten trapped, and he might be squeezed by the chains too. He jumped away as more of them emerged from the ground. The healer hadn’t even flinched.

“You think too highly of yourself,” Moe said. He’d thrown back his hood, revealing his serpentine face. A forked tongue flicked in and out of his mouth.

“Highly or not, I’m here,” Gary said.

“Watch your flank, Steel! There’s an assassin!” Boulderus Johnus yelled. Gary braced himself, though he’d already known that, but no attack came. Jin was missing.

Up above, Feng Shen clashed with Alpha again, both flung back. The boxing champ dashed towards Alpha, but red chains wrapped around his arms and legs. The swordsman flashed close. Boulderus Johnus appeared in front of the swordsman, holding his shield up, blocking a hundred quick slashes.

“Testudo!” he roared. “Wedge!” He lunged forward on his massive glyan, tearing through a batch of chains.

The champ flexed his muscles and shattered the chains binding him. They both assaulted Moe’s position, but he smoothly glided back, and Alpha took his place. “Bunch of nerds,” he grunted. “Head in the Shitter.”

His foot attempted to smash the glyan’s head into the ground, but Boulderus jumped off to block. They exchanged strikes. The swordsman ran to meet the boxing champ, and the two engaged in an extremely high-speed battle where none seemed to hit the other.

The healer chanted something. A white light covered the swordsman, making him even faster, and suddenly the champ was pushed back. His sliced gloves revealed cement inside. The wrestling champ dove to help him. Moe’s chains resurged, keeping them at bay. Feng Shen lunged at Moe, the two embroiled in a war of spear and chain.

That left Gary alone. “Thunder Step!” he roared, charging over the chains to reach the healer. His instinct suddenly blared. He stomped against the air in a backward Thunder Step, the sudden arrest of his momentum threatening to snap his legs. A cyan glow ran through where he would have been, revealing Jin, so fast he seemed to appear out of thin air.

In that split-second, Gary crossed gazes with the assassin. He saw the coldness in the other’s eyes. Jin must have seen the wrath in his.

“Nail Gun!”

Both exploded into motion at once, clashing repeatedly. Gary found himself pressured. Like when he fought the swordsman, he was lacking in speed, but he was durable enough to shrug off surface wounds. That gave him more room to push. Jin moved through the air in rapid straight lines, leaping from the floor to the walls of the base to the air itself, which he sometimes used as a springboard. He wove a lattice of neon blue around Gary. The whirring of his gears under the leather armor filled Gary’s ears. Unable to keep up, he was pushed into retreat.

The ten sector bosses, five from each side, engaged in a melee of extreme violence. Colossal aura bursts rang out. Clashes echoes. People went flying. They were ten blurs moving impossibly fast and striking impossibly hard.

But the Gymnasium side wasn’t making any headway. And the timer ticked.

Time to Floor Boss Showdown: 01:29

Gary gritted his teeth. The door was right there, behind the enemy healer, but he couldn’t even reach the healer, let alone the door.

Liu Dong suddenly arrived before Gary. He got frightened for a moment, thinking he’d need to fight two geniuses at the same time, but then Jin leapt away to meet the boxing champ. It immediately became clear that he was a better match than Liu Dong. Narrow wounds were carved over the champ’s body. He wouldn’t hold forever.

“We meet again,” Liu Dong said with a casual smile. “It might be time to recover my gift, don’t you think?”

Gary didn’t share the other’s jovialty. He retrieved the bag of ten thousand aura stones Liu Dong had given him and tossed it to the man, who grabbed it with a confused expression.

“What are you doing? I was joking. I obviously can’t let you past,” he said.

“I don’t need you to let me,” Gary replied. He raised his spear and charged. Liu Dong put away the stones with a frown and met Gary in heated combat. Spear and sword clashed repeatedly. Steel echoed. Sparks flew. They accelerated harder and harder, and slowly, Gary found himself gaining the upper hand.

He’d leveled up since the last time they fought. He could keep up with Liu Dong’s speed now. He could strike back.

He’d also gained a new vibration.

Nemesis echoed in Gary’s bones. It was a dark, vengeful thirst for violence. A calling to murder. He tore viciously into Liu Dong, his spear snapping for vital spots, clearly aiming to kill the other man.

“Take it easy,” Liu Dong growled, focusing on defense. “Are you out of your mind?”

“I fucking hate hypocrites like you,” Gary said.

“What?”

“You slaughter weaklings but take it easy against people of your level. You fear death while handing it out freely. That’s not how the world works, Liu Dong. Since you kill, be prepared to die. I’m ashamed for accepting your stones before.”

“What the fuck are you saying?” Liu Dong roared. “Do you take me for a coward, you piece of shit? Fine then. Taste my blade. Starry Night!”

“Nail Gun!”

They clashed a dozen times, their battle fiercer than before. Both were going for the kill now. Gary parried a blade headed for his throat.

This is how it should be, he thought, darkness boiling inside. Now it’s fair.

The other fighters glanced at Gary. His statement had changed the atmosphere. Where before they’d been fighting with passion, aiming to injure rather than kill, the current battle was a dark, tense, defensive thing. Different emotions brewed in everyone’s mind. Hard eyes gazed at Gary.

They knew that things were different now. All it took was one person fighting for real. They had to play Gary’s game.

He actually did it, Feng Shen thought, his gaze complicated.

Gary tore into Liu Dong. His spear slithered and bit like an angry snake, pushing the other man back. As the pressure rose, Liu Dong’s brows fell, and his blade lost its edge. He realized how equally matched they were, and that the battle could go either way. He focused on defense. When that didn’t stop Gary’s onslaught, he gritted his teeth and jumped aside, opening Gary’s path to the door.

Gary rushed past. They crossed gazes. Gary’s was dark and fierce—Liu Dong’s was humiliated and outraged.

“You fucking animal!” he roared.

“Coward,” Gary replied, and he saw Liu Dong’s eyes redden.

He sprinted past the swordsman.

“Call Momon!” Moe yelled.

A red chain speared at Gary as he’d almost reached the door, forcing him to slow down to dodge. Alpha was upon him. “Pimp slap,” he said, bringing his massive palm down at Gary. He gritted and charged ahead. The palm met him on the back, slamming him into the ground hard enough to crater it. An invasive, rowdy vibration permeated his body, but his black lightning quickly burned it off.

“Head in the Shitter,” came a voice.

Gary thunderstepped, reaching the side of the door just as Alpha landed boot-first where Gary had been, smashing the ground into tiny bits.

That would have smashed my head, Gary realized. He still wasn’t the brothers’ equal, but at least he could fight. A black-and-white wheel fell on Alpha, who crossed his arms to defend, while Gary sprinted into the puppeteer’s base. Finally, he was inside.

The door they were supposed to close was the same steel door that had laid off its hinges the last time he stormed this base, but had somehow been repaired in the teleportation.

Gary moved at one of the double doors. It took a lot of his strength, but he managed to kick it closed. It slid into place with a click. He rushed to the door’s other half. The brothers tried to reach him, but the positions had been reversed, and the two climbers and two champions gave it everything they had to keep them at bay. Gary made it to the door. He thunder-stepped a kick into it, almost breaking his knee, and watched it slam closed.

Almost.

A green tendril stopped the door midway. Gary braced himself. When the assailant became clear, however, he forgot all about the door.

Because the person who’d stopped him was his mentor, Kyle Mercy.
