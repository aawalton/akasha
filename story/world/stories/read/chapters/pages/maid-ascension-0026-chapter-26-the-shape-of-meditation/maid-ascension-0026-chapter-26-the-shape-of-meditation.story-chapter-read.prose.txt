Lira’s next objective was clear. She needed to create three simple cantrips tied to mind magic, something practical that would prepare her for the far more demanding task of absorbing a book with magic.

She refused to waste time on trivial spells that served no real purpose. Each cantrip had to earn its place, something she would actually rely on rather than abandon after a single success.

The first idea came to her almost immediately. If every spell required a trance to learn it, then the most obvious tool she lacked was a reliable way to enter that state. Up until now, she had relied on primers, environmental immersion, or emotional alignment. All of those were inconsistent in some way or another. A cantrip that guided her mind into stillness would not only help her learn future spells much more easily, but it would also help during monotonous, boring work.

She opened her father’s ledger and began to search through it again, looking for words that resonated with the desired concept.

Pika was obvious, the anchor of everything she had been building. Mind was the center of this path, not just a component of it.

Beyond that, she searched for something that captured the quiet she reached when her thoughts settled. Her eyes stopped on Selan, meaning stillness, or quieting. It was the calming of noise and the narrowing of focus that mattered when meditating, not its complete absence.

Then she found Tureth, meaning balance.

She lingered on that one longer. Meditation was a state where thoughts, sensations, and awareness aligned without conflict. Balance between attention and relaxation, between control and letting go, and between holding a thought and allowing it to pass.

Together, the three words formed something complete.

Pika. Selan. Tureth.

She closed the ledger and pulled a blank sheet from her stack. If she had learned anything from her attempt with wind, it was that words alone were not enough. She needed structure, connection, feeling, and understanding. Something that would guide her into the right state before the spell itself could take hold.

A primer could help with that, even if she had to build it herself.

She dipped her pen into ink and began to write.

The martial arts book described meditation as a discipline of awareness, a way to sharpen the mind by removing distractions using repetition and breath. It spoke of posture, stillness, and attention directed inward until the body and mind moved as one.

But that was only part of it.

Meditation, to her, was not some distant, mystical practice reserved for monks or sages. It was something she had brushed against many times without naming it. she had always been closest to it during calligraphy. There were moments when the world narrowed to the movement of her hand, the flow of ink across paper, and the shape of each stroke forming without hesitation. In those moments, she was not thinking about the next line or the previous one. She simply acted in the present, trusting in her skill to do the rest. She didn’t doubt when she breathed or when she walked; why would she doubt writing? She was Lira, writing was her nature.

When she finished the first section, she paused, then carefully wrote the incantation beneath it.

Pika. Selan. Tureth.

She continued writing, reinforcing the idea, layering understanding with experience. She wrote about the way her breathing would slow without her noticing, how distractions faded as they lost importance. She described the feeling of being present within a single action, how time seemed to stretch and compress at once, measured only by the rhythm of her work.

Between each section, she repeated the incantation, spacing it evenly, letting it sink into the structure of the text rather than standing apart from it.

When she was done, she set the pen aside and read through everything once, slowly, letting the meaning settle. It felt different from her previous attempts. Less forced and more complete.

She adjusted her position, sitting cross-legged on the floor, the paper resting in front of her. Her back straightened. She placed her hands on her knees and closed her eyes.

At first, nothing happened.

Her thoughts drifted as they always did, small distractions pulling at the edges of her attention. The quiet creak of wood, the faint movement of air through the room, the lingering awareness of the ink drying on the page, or the smell of the burning tallow candle. She acknowledged them and let them pass.

She recalled the text she had just written, not word for word, but the meaning behind it, contemplating on what she wrote and exploring her feelings on the matter.

Her breathing slowed.

Mind was the source of the self.

Stillness was a state of potential.

Balance was alignment.

Her thoughts began to settle, losing their urgency. Each breath rose and fell, taking away all resistance. Her awareness narrowed, and the tension she had not noticed began to loosen.

Then she reached for the final piece.

Pika.

The word did not feel like something she spoke, but something she recognized. The center of it all, the place where everything else connected.

Selan.

The quiet deepened, like being underwater.

Tureth.

Everything aligned in balance.

Click.

Her eyes opened.

For a moment, she did not move. The world felt the same, yet different in a way that was difficult to describe. Clearer and calmer, but not dull. Her thoughts were present, active, and singular in their focus rather than the mismatch of jumping ideas that usually resonated inside her head.

It had worked.

The realization came with a quiet satisfaction rather than excitement; she doubted she could feel excitement in this state.

It had taken longer than the ink spell, but far less time than the wind.

That made sense. This was closer to her domain. It was the domain she wished to claim next.

She let out a slow breath and allowed the state to fade naturally, paying attention to the transition. The stillness receded, replaced by the usual movement of thought.

With her thoughts returning, so did her feelings. She was elated, then slightly worried. If she got addicted to that mind state, she would no longer be Lira.

Completely losing her emotions was not an option. She needed her curiosity to keep pushing forward, she needed her empathy to interact with her friends, she needed her joy so she could be happy, and she needed her hate so she could avenge her father.

No, it was better to use this spell sparingly.

Lira looked down at the paper in front of her, at the carefully written lines and the repeated incantation woven between them.

One cantrip completed.

Two more to go.
