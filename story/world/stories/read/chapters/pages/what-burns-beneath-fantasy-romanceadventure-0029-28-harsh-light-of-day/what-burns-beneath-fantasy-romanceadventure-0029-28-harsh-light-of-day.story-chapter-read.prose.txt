Renner affixed his leathers and weapons before stepping outside, leaving me to dress.

I immediately grabbed my waterskin, splashed its contents onto my hand, and scrubbed away all evidence of my dream. Shaking fingers peeled free my sweat-damp gown and, shivering in the cold morning air, I yanked on fresh clothes. When I opened the door, my companion was nowhere to be seen. I adjusted my pack, clutched my book, and headed down the stairs.

Lewen, still yawning, fussed over a large black kettle beside the fireplace.

Renner leaned against the wall nearby, arms crossed. “Which street?” he was saying.

“Oh, let’s see… if you head west out the door, past the bakery- it’s got a yellow signpost outside, can’t miss it- then up past the mill. Gil’s house is just behind. Tucked near the wall, with red shutters.”

I cleared my throat. “And what about your town Watch, sir? Do they have a building somewhere?”

He nearly dropped the kettle.

“The Watch? Ah. Well, we’re a small town, you know. Peaceful. Davith Rell acts as constable, and you can find him right near the town square. His office is beside our temple.”

“Peaceful, huh? So, not a lot of excitement?” My companion’s voice was deceptively mild as he rubbed his jaw. But there was something in his eyes… calculating. Weighing. Watching our host with an almost predatory intensity.

Lewen barked a laugh, red jowls trembling. “Saints, no! Sometimes there’s a boundary dispute off on one of the farms, of course, or some of the younger lads stay out too late and cause a ruckus. But, by my bones, this is a safe place.”

My fingers tightened around the book. “Our friend is missing.”

The innkeeper rubbed his neck. “Right. What you said earlier… Miss, I do recall some of the festivities last night. Perhaps your friend found some, ah, company?”

“She slept in the same room as us. We came down and asked for a new key, remember?” Renner cocked his head to one side, watching the larger man closely.

Lewen blinked. “Oh. Ah. Right. I’m… I’m terribly sorry, folks, I’m just not quite myself this morning.” He gave an abashed little grunt and paused, staring down into the contents of the kettle. Whatever was inside of it smelled like earth and herbs. “Could’ve sworn I only had one cup, too.”

I shivered, taking in the innkeeper’s distant demeanor as he stirred. If Renner’s right about there being Fae magic at work… why does Master Lewen seem confused, but not the two of us?

My ‘immunity,’ for lack of a better word, could perhaps be explained by my own magic. Perhaps even by my pact. I had no idea how it all worked, but those were at least two possibilities.

Renner, though… Why wouldn’t it have affected him? He seems clear-headed.

Master Lewen gave his head a little shake. “Sorry, folks. Can I fix either of you some porridge?”

“No.” I winced immediately after the reply, very near a snarl, escaped. I ducked my head, stepping towards the door. “Sorry. No… thank you. Perhaps later.”

“As you will.” He nodded a farewell, yawning again.

The street lay empty, dull cobblestones shadowed pale silver and dulcet pink with the dawn. Frost glittered on nearby patches of grass.

Crisp air nipped my nose. “Where should we start? The Watch, or the stablemaster?”

Renner pulled his cloak tight. “Stablemaster. We’re not going to the Watch.”

“Alright. Wait, what? Why not?”

He started up the street, shoulders hunched against the wind. “Because this might get ugly.”

I stood shivering for a moment, parsing through all the things that statement could possibly mean.

And then, with dawning horror, I recalled Renner’s admissions to Teela back in the cave.

He’s killed people. He didn’t even acknowledge they were people. And he was going to let those awful men hurt Teela, and he tried to stab me and…

What am I doing? Saints, what am I doing?!

I couldn’t deny the warm, nervous little flutters I’d felt last night. The kinds of feelings from years ago, when Royce’s mouth was warm against mine and his hands were strong and gentle and… and I’d felt like that again. Only for a few moments, not enough to truly consider or be distracted by, but… enough to forget, just for last night, the kind of man standing before me.

I shouldn’t have.

“Are you coming?” He stopped several paces ahead.

“What…” My voice sounded very small in the frosty space between us. “What do you mean by that?’”

“What?”

I didn’t answer.

His face hardened. The silence stretched on until he shifted his cloak just enough for me to see him thumb his sword hilt.

I stepped back. “You can’t mean to-”

“I mean to do whatever is necessary.”

My shoulders tightened. “But… look, we have no idea what’s going on! And we aren’t… even if he’s involved, somehow, we’re not going to…” I held my breath, unable to voice the terrible thought.

Renner waited a while before responding. Then, words heavy with steel, “Maybe you should wait at the inn.” He turned and stalked away, cloak sweeping at his heels.

A different kind of cold sank into my skin. I hurried after him, boots tapping against the cobblestones.

The town lay silent around us, aside from occasional gusts of frosty air rustling at fallen leaves and creaking signposts. I found myself glancing warily down every alleyway, clutching my book like a shield.

Fellbrook would be alive with sounds, even at this hour. There’d be chickens, hounds, horses, songbirds… but here, it’s just so quiet.

Past the mill- which smelled of earthy, sweet flour and made my stomach grumble- and down uneven cobblestones, we found the house Lewen had described. It was made of dull wood, dark and almost damp-looking around the base, with chipped red shutters framing mud-smeared windows.

The door was closed, but there was a little brass bell affixed to its frame. I reached up to tap the metal.

“Wait.”

The wary edge to his tone made me feel as though I was doing something unsavory. As though I should be careful not to be seen. Which was silly, of course, but the hairs along my neck still prickled.

I glanced around. This particular area, tucked behind the mill and nestled against the town wall, was deserted. There was no one nearby. No witnesses, I thought with a grimace, side-eyeing my stone-faced companion. No one to intervene if this… ‘gets ugly.’

“Why?”

Renner moved to stand sideways beside a window. He peered at the glass, mouth twisted downwards.

“I take it you’ve never met someone pacted. At least, not the kind of meeting that ends in fire and blood.”

I swallowed, shaking my head.

He fixed me with a hard stare. “Whatever this guy may seem like, if he is bound to a Fae, then… well, then that trumps everything else. Maybe he’s a wonderful person, deep down, or maybe he’s scum. Doesn’t matter. His pact is gonna dictate what he does, at least when it matters.”

“You mean… what, it forces him to… to do what the monster wants?”

“Something like that.” He prowled along, boots slow and soft on the dirt.

I followed, fingers twisting at my cloak. That isn’t… that’s not how it’s been for me. I agreed to answer his questions, and I have, but… it’s been of my own free will. Nothing’s forced me to answer, save my fear of what will happen to Royce!

And that fear was mine. I was certain of it. It wasn’t a feeling or sensation caused by some sinister, magical bond; it was born of love. I could choose to go against it; the Fae had told me so, himself.

Not that that means anything. The thought twisted through the back of my mind, bitter and angry and… ultimately, impotent. I’m sure he’s not telling me the truth about things. Not that he’s even told me very much, but there’s… ashes, I don’t know. Something about the way he says everything; so arrogant and sure of himself. Like he’s telling me half-truths, because of course I don’t know a salted thing about his awful Court and the monsters in it and what they want and I have no way to refute any of what he-

“Something on your mind?”

I nearly walked right into him.

Renner had paused in front of me, one eyebrow cocked. His face had that odd, blank cast to it. He’d been looking at me like that a lot lately. Like he was trying to appear uninterested, but was actually gauging every facet of my expression with fierce attentiveness.

It’s almost like he… like he knows. He can’t, of course, it’s impossible, but… that’s what it feels like. Like he knows exactly what kind of dreadful things I’m thinking, and he’s just daring me to say them aloud.

I swallowed. “No.

He continued watching me.

I leaned against the dingy wood, sighing. “Renner, we don’t know for certain that this man isn’t a… a victim of some kind. I’m not saying you’re wrong; maybe he is pacted, or maybe he’s a horrible criminal who kidnapped Teela, or maybe… salt, maybe lots of things!”

He snorted and continued on, then paused to squint through another dirt-smudged window.

“But he also might not have any idea what’s going on. Or he might be confused, like Master Lewen-”

“Who also might be lying.”

“We don’t know that! He seemed genuinely confused, not like he had something to do with this!”

Renner slipped past the window and turned, moving around the back of the house. “The world you live in sounds nice, ladyship. Real quaint. Rose-colored. Isn’t that the saying?” His eyes cut towards me, lip curled in obvious scorn.

I bristled, fighting the urge to stamp one foot into the soft earth. Then my gaze swept past Renner, towards the back door. It was propped open ever so slightly, and lying there in the shadows beyond…

What is that? It looks like a log, or perhaps some kind of animal. Or… oh.

“But if you ask me, growing up in that cozy little village didn’t do you any-”

I shoved past him with a cry. My shoulder slammed into the door, nearly knocking the half-rotted wood off its hinges.

The stablemaster lay facedown on the floor. He wasn’t moving.
