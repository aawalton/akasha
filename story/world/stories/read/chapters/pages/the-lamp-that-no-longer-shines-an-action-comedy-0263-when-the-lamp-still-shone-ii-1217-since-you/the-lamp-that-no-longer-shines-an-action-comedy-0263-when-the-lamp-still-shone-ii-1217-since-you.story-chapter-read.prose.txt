[Time]: Summer, 1981[Location]: Yggdrasil Academy / The Umbral Cathedral

The high summer of 1981 lay over the spires of Yggdrasil Academy like a wet wool blanket, but inside Irene Habsburg's dormitory, the air was cool.

The administrative paperwork had been finalized, stamped, and archived that morning, and she was packing. She had bled the Academy dry with the brisk, affectionate efficiency of a woman draining the last of an excellent vintage and setting the bottle aside.

Kneeling beside her open trunk, Irene went through the objects that had accumulated in her spatial ring over the past year, and every one of them had come from the same place. The legendary, rune-carved pebble, sitting heavy with dormant spatial gravity. The winter cloak with its gemstone-threaded enchantments humming at a frequency that made the air around it taste of crushed starlight. A stack of grimoires dense enough to cost a lesser witch her sanity. And, scattered among them, an assortment of shiny, trivial trinkets: objects a certain cat had tossed her way over the year with the casual indifference of a creature batting a bottle cap off a table and immediately losing interest in where it landed.

The hollow was glaringly obvious once she looked for it. Not a single photograph.

Images of the Head of the Grand Witches were famously scarce. A photograph taken without her consent simply failed to exist: the film developed into static, the magical crystal resolved into white noise. Even photographs she had permitted carried an expiration date, the paper dissolving slowly into sepia and then nothing, on a schedule set by nobody but her.

Irene set down the last of the grimoires, her golden eyes narrowing. She was Ovelia's sole acknowledged disciple; Ovelia had said so herself, in the offhand way she kept for facts she considered embarrassingly obvious. Outside the warded corridors of the Umbral Cathedral, not a living soul in the Inner Sea knew it.

Very well, Irene thought. If she will not step into the light of her own accord, I will bring her out into it.

She snapped her trunk shut and stepped into the shadows.

That evening, the Shared Void was cycling its third shuffle when Allison reviewed her hand and regarded the table with the displeasure of a woman who already knew she had lost.

Irene drew from the Void.

[2nd Seat · Famia Schüder] (Cost: 9 / Passive: Arms Corridor. All opponents receive [Tariff] each time they deploy a unit costing 5 or higher.)

She set it face-down and looked at Ovelia. "It occurs to me," she said, her tone carrying the breezy, unstudied innocence of a prepared observation, "that you are the only Grand Witch alive with no card in this game."

On the cushions opposite, Ovelia's tail executed a single, sharp, agitated thwack against the leather. She abandoned her hand entirely, bristling. "Because Cat detests being photographed!"

Irene exhaled a long, theatrical sigh. "But you are the First Seat. Witch Cards travel to the far edges of the universe. For whatever exists beyond the reaches of the Inner Sea and cannot enter it, this deck is the only window they have into Witch civilization." She set the Famia card face-up on the table, voice gentle. "They believe Famia Schüder or Ash leads the Witch civilization."

Ovelia froze. It was the specific, classical expression of a cat who had been walking with complete confidence across a room and simply stopped, mid-step, to stare at an invisible concept on the wall, eyes wide and tail rigid.

Then she poked Allison sharply in the ribs with her tail. "Meow! Allison handles all public appearances! Let her be on the card!"

Allison let her ears flatten. "You keep handing me every task you can't be bothered with..." she muttered into her cards.

"Obviously," Ovelia replied, waving a hand with the magnanimous logic of a sovereign who had never once questioned her own reasoning. "If Cat had to sit through every tiresome social obligation personally, what would be the point of having Aspects?"

Irene looked across the table. "Ovelia," she said. "Do you know what day today is?"

Ovelia's brow furrowed. "What day?"

Beside her, Allison's eyes were cutting between Irene and Ovelia with the frantic energy of a woman trying to say something vital with her eyebrows alone. Ovelia noticed nothing whatsoever.

Allison cracked first. "Today is Irene's graduation day, is it not?"

Ovelia's confusion resolved immediately into a look of profound, condescending unimpressedness. "Is that all? You could have graduated any day these past several months. That hardly constitutes a milestone."

Smack.

Kara's tail described a precise, lazy arc and delivered a heavy strike directly against Ovelia's ribcage. She folded her hands in her lap, her smile brimming with a quiet, maternal certainty.

"Today," Kara said, "is Irene's birthday."

Irene had never told a soul the date, and somebody in this house had kept it anyway. She lowered her eyes, her thumb slowly tracing the edge of the card, and the corner of her mouth would not stay level. "I have never cared much for birthdays," Irene said quietly. "They were always either a weakness, or a headline." She looked up, her eyes meeting Ovelia's directly. "To me, today is the one-year anniversary of the day we met."

"Met?" Ovelia repeated. Her ears flattened, and stayed pinned. "You know perfectly well I have been waiting since you called me—"

The Aspects moved before she cleared the sentence. Allison's hand closed over her mouth. Shaar pinned her arms. Daena, for once acting on her own initiative, caught her ankles from below.

Beneath Allison's palm, the fight went out of her all at once. Ovelia slumped back into the cushions, her starry eyes dropping to the floor.

Irene sat with the unfinished sentence lodged somewhere under her ribs, vibrating like a plucked wire. Since you called me.

She had never called her anything. Three Aspects had thrown themselves across the cushions to stop a single sentence, which left only one Irene it could belong to: the one still somewhere ahead of her on this road. That Irene would call, and Ovelia had heard it already, and had been waiting for her ever since.

Then the fault is entirely mine, Irene thought, and something warm and wholly unrepentant spread through her chest. I have kept you waiting.

Ovelia sat muffled and undone in the cushions, carrying a grievance too old for either of them to name out loud, and Irene meant to make up every day of it.

"Today," Irene said. "Let's go out. Together."

The grip around her eased, and the instant the last hand let go, Ovelia was already pushing upright, her ears snapping back to their usual imperious angle with the transparent haste of someone determined to pretend the last minute had never happened.

She narrowed her eyes, radiating the petulant, defensive displeasure of a cat who suspects the furniture has been rearranged specifically to inconvenience her. "Cat is incredibly busy! Cat is not like some idle girl who has nothing better to do than wander the streets!"

Irene was in far too good a mood to take no for an answer. She bit her lower lip, blanching the flesh just enough. She channeled a precisely calibrated surge of mana into her tear ducts, allowing a thin, trembling film of moisture to pool over her golden irises and catch the amber light of the chandeliers at the ideal angle.

Ovelia's tail shot straight up into the air. "Fine! Fine!" she stammered, abandoning the scaffolding of her sovereign dignity entirely. "Where? Where do you want to go?"

The manufactured tears evaporated instantly, replaced by a smile that could have powered the Cathedral's lighting system. "The Dragon Era Park. They recently opened a premier beef-and-seafood pavilion, and the chef is a Greater Lantern Cat. And the park has displays of dragons unavailable anywhere else."

She saw Ovelia draw a breath and struck first. "Living dragons. Roaming a sky-island. Not the taxidermy collection in your dimensional vault."

Ovelia clamped her mouth shut with an audible, thwarted click, cheeks puffing out, arms crossing over her chest with the locked certainty of a closing argument.

"Come with me," Irene coaxed, dropping her voice into the warmest register she owned.

"Hmph." Ovelia looked away. "Cat has sworn never to step foot onto the territory of a Dragon Park in this lifetime."

Irene let her shoulders drop in a portrait of quiet, measured disappointment. "I see. Very well. We can review the itinerary and find an alternative—"

"However," Ovelia interrupted, twirling a lock of raven hair around one finger with studied, aggressive nonchalance, addressing the far wall and not Irene. "If someone were to arrange a small, private touring coach... Cat supposes that would not technically constitute stepping on the park grounds."

Irene beamed. "You are too good to me."

Ovelia turned her head away entirely, her tongue darting out to briefly, nervously groom the tip of her own tail. "Whatever."

"I'll go change then." Irene was already standing, smoothing her skirt. "Two o'clock. Foyer."

Clatter. Crash. Clang.

Vila, who rarely stayed awake long enough to take part in household politics, lowered her magazine. She frowned at the whirlwind tearing through the dimensional wardrobe.

Cat completely fails to understand the necessity of this level of devastation, Vila concluded.

She cast a desperate glance toward Kara for help. Kara was curled in a velvet armchair, emitting a high-pitched, deeply unsettling feline giggle while aggressively consuming a comic magazine. The cover featured a scantily clad young witch and the bold title: Sweet Mama Special: Vol. 133.

Cat was on my own.

Vila closed her copy of Daily Life of the Sword-Forging Cat and raised her voice. "Ovelia! You are disturbing the household!"

The crashing ceased. Ovelia poked her head out from between a row of gothic gowns with the expression of someone who had been in the middle of a very serious altercation with a coat rack. She marched out, sleeves rolled to her elbows.

Half a second later, Vila rubbed a phantom bump on her head. "If you are simply selecting an outfit, would it not be simpler to wear our standard attire? Irene is very familiar with us."

She wisely did not add: You look a little desperate.

"Idiot."

Ovelia hurled the word at her own Aspect without hesitation. "It is precisely because of moments like this that Cat's supreme prosperity and unparalleled excellence must be visually asserted! Have you no grasp of basic aesthetics?"

She pointed at the wardrobe. "Why else did Cat acquire this staggering volume of textiles? To look at them? Meow-ow!"

Vila crossed her arms and let her cat ears twitch once in silent, aggrieved judgment.

Satisfied that she had crushed the internal dissent, Ovelia swished her tail and tore open the next wardrobe. There was enough fabric inside to dress a small principality, gathered over decades with the indiscriminate hoarding of a cat that had simply seen many pretty things, and half of it still wore its enchanted purchase tags.

Ovelia pulled out a dress, held it against her frame, grimaced, and shoved it back.

A yellow sundress? Meow-ow! Cat hates it!A dark violet tulle skirt? Interesting, but not the correct kind of interesting. Rejected.A pink— Cat will face the heat death of the universe before wearing pink.

Paralyzed by infinite choice, Ovelia abandoned her pride and began barking orders. With a long, synchronized sigh, Vila and Kara set down their respective reading materials and waded into the textile catastrophe to assist.

Irene materialized in the Cathedral foyer five minutes ahead of schedule, having allocated a full thirty-minute buffer for Ovelia to dawdle, stall, and complain.

Ovelia was already there. Irene stopped walking.

Ovelia had set aside the gothic black she wore the way other people wore skin, and put on a black casual hiking vest layered over a crisp white athletic button-down. A pleated skirt, fitted safety shorts, and knee-high riding boots finished it, and a sharply angled beret hung from her belt. The whole ensemble was devastatingly, improbably cute, the exact inverse of her usual lethargic majesty, and somehow more dangerous for it.

Irene herself was dressed in a red-and-gold tailored cloak draped over a high-waisted navy petal coat, a delicate chiffon blouse, and supple driving gloves.

Noticing Irene's arrested, evaluating gaze, Ovelia immediately tilted her chin upward. It was the specific, silent, unbearably smug expression of a cat waiting to receive what it considers its rightful due in the form of verbal adoration. Irene exhaled quietly. "How long were you waiting before I arrived?"

Denied her immediate compliment, Ovelia huffed. "Are you feeling the appropriate degree of remorse now? Forcing Cat to wait."

Something between a sigh and a laugh escaped Irene. "I am."

She closed the distance. The charm was available to her, and she left it where it was. "I would hate to keep you waiting any longer than I already have."

Ovelia's ears went back. Then the laugh came, soft and involuntary, and she looked away, and the color rose faintly in her cheeks. "Smooth-talking girl."
