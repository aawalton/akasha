“[So. That’ll be our first target,]” Velistraine said for the third time, dread and worry wafting off of her in equal measure, and just below that, Henry caught a hint of a small, growing nugget of frustration and embarrassment, which she was doggedly ignoring. It was as if she was trying to work herself up for what was to come, and while it wasn’t too apparent, Henry and Maurice were familiar enough with her and had a sharp enough Telepathic sense to have front seats to the confusing maelstrom in their midst. The two shared a look, but neither of them said anything about it. Instead, they just focused on their target.

Some trauma was understandable. Expected, even. Henry would keep an eye on things and see if she’d rather retreat, but he’d wait a little and see how she handled things.

The pillbug ship they were stalking was a few hundred yards away from where they’d been hidden. It wasn’t the one closest to them, but the one Henry had noticed while Velistraine and Maurice were out for the count. After discussing it, they opted to start with this one, just so they didn’t pick up a fight near their shelter. Well. One of their shelters. And once they got the measure of this one, they could clear out the other.

The trio hovered just below the edge that separated the road from the emptiness of space. It was almost a hundred yards up from the walkable platform and right now, Henry was making sure none of them drifted outside this area’s mysterious effect. Any further and he’d lose sight of the road, and they’d lose access to breathable air. He wouldn’t even sense the different flow of mana of this spot. In fact, if it wasn’t for his many Avatars tucked and hidden all around the road, it might be impossible to find the road again if he were to lose sight of it and for some reason get himself turned around, to the point that Henry wondered how it had even been found at all.

Even Find the Path couldn’t pick it up. At least, not at its current level.

Who had made this place? And why? Frankly, the more he thought of it, the more curious he was getting. Henry really hoped the System would shed some light on whatever this place was once they got all the orbs in a row. He was looking forward to figuring out where this road led, and what its exact nature was. For now, though, he focused on the shape below them, while Velistraine kept anxiously shifting her posture over and over. While Henry could definitely understand where she was coming from, he was finding Velistraine to be more and more distracting. It was her growing certainty that this was a bad idea and that Henry and Maurice might not be ready. Which might be true. The doubt wasn’t as prevalent as her general anxiety, but it was there, and it was somehow becoming contagious. Then again, he couldn’t blame her. She wasn’t up-to-date on what they were capable of, and the last time she’d seen them seriously fight, it was when they’d stepped into B-rank. Back then she had toyed with them, but they weren’t the same duo, and Henry had plenty of redundancies that she didn’t know about. Velistraine was mostly a martial individual. She didn’t have clones and Avatars to hop between seamlessly. She couldn’t heal very well. And she couldn’t retreat at a moment’s notice. All the weaknesses that had made it hard for her to survive out there were utterly nullified by Henry’s presence. He was ready.

Henry wanted to gorge himself on these creatures and head back home in a couple of days, ideally. It wasn’t going to happen, most likely, but he also believed some recklessness was required in this scenario.

With that said, Henry didn’t let any of his frustration show, and he tried to comfort her a little. “[We will engage this one, reduce it into dust, then clear out until they’re less alert. I told you we have plenty of spots to hide in, far away from this spot, and all are both invisible and their mana-signature is veiled. This will be fine. I promise,]” he sent, voice smooth and confident.

“[And if they call for help, we leave, so we don’t get bogged down, then we try again later,]” continued Maurice, picking up on what he was doing. “[Don’t worry about it. It’ll be fine!]”

Velistraine nodded along for a moment, then froze, realizing what was happening. That her two juniors were propping her up and encouraging her, when it should be the other way around. Embarrassment erupted out of her in waves, making her throat and face turn red. Henry obviously noticed as he had plenty of Avatars around watching everything, but his main body didn’t turn and he acted as if he saw nothing while the nugget of frustration grew and shone below her worries. As for Maurice, he didn’t need to act. He was too focused on the target below, rubbing his claws together as he barely kept his predatory aura in check.

Henry sensed her frustration beginning to bubble, and that he could work with. He just had to help her direct it outward rather than inward.

“[Now, one last thing before we pop this can of worms open,]” Henry said, and he could feel the giddiness emanating out of Maurice while Velistraine’s anxiety mellowed. “[You’re sure there’s no talking or negotiation with these creatures? There’s no way to work with them in any way whatsoever?]”

Henry knew the answer already, but he just needed to do his due diligence. He just wanted to make sure that the war crimes all three of them were about to commit were justified.

Velistraine glowered at the ship, then shook her head. There was not an ounce of doubt in her, and he could feel Predation of the Trickster sing at the prospect, while Unfettered Echoes continued to hungrily pulse for more fuel for its transformation.

“[None,]” Velistraine sent back, knuckles white around the pommels of her swords. For this occasion, she had pulled a second bone sword. Henry wondered if dual-wielding was her specialty or if she was acting out of the ordinary. “[They might as well be rabid,]” she continued. “[You’re about to see for yourself.]”

Eyes swinging back down, Henry hummed. He could definitely work with that and at once, he ordered some of his Avatars closer, all of which began producing more of their toxins. For this, Henry prioritized the mana-burning Aetherbane toxin of the seahorse, as well as the tranquilizing cocktail of the Mesmer eel. These would be the second volley. And with their preparations done, Henry gave Maurice a look, then nodded.

Maurice immediately began casting. The black bubble appeared as a pinprick of darkness at first, but very quickly, it swelled, growing more ominous and foreboding as it did. It sucked in their surrounding light, and a low, barely audible hum filled the air, like barely perceptible static. In only seconds, it was nearly the size of a beach ball, and Henry was pretty sure that if Maurice injected the damn thing with some of his Aspect, it’d be at the same power level that the crab had used against the beast-master. Enough to severely injure an above-average A-rank.

But Maurice didn’t inject it with his Aspect. Maybe he was testing himself, or maybe he didn’t want to give them away too soon. Whichever case it was, Henry was glad.

He didn’t want the fight to end too fast, and it was a good thing that Maurice didn’t hold on to it for too long, because as soon as he flicked his claw and sent the quiet orb flitting forward, the back of the pillbug ship unfurled, and Henry barely had the time to see a chitinous foot and the glimmer of a scythe-like arm before the bubble made contact.

Like in a silent video, the ship, along with whatever had been attempting to slip out, crumpled upon itself with nary a sound, like half-inflated balloons sucked in by a vacuum though considering its size, not all of it was engulfed by the implosion. For a moment, everything was still and quiet, but Henry could sense the spikes of mana and rage beyond. As if delayed, the crunch of chitin and the screech of the things within came nearly a second after the impact, once the black orb had allowed the sound to travel away, and with it gone, the creatures roiled out of the carcass of the ship like a swarm of frenzied eels, scythe-like arms and mandibles trembling and thrashing as they tore themselves out of the vehicle, sending shredded limbs and dark ichor flying as they pulled their sharp appendages out of their companions, screeching their madness out into the world.

It lasted for barely half a second, at which point Henry’s first Avatar detonated in their midst.
