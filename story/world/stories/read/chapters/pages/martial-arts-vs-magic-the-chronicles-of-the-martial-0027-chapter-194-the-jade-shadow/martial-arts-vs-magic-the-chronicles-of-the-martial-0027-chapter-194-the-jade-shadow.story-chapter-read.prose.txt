On the grand horizon, the sun rose slowly over Hwangcheon. The markets were already awake and open, with people bustling around, as the gentle heat washed over them.

Today, the weather was good.

Vendors argued over fish prices, and carts still rolled toward the palace with vegetables, fabric, and firewood. It was the typical Hwangcheon on the surface, perhaps even better given the weather, but if someone paid attention, they’d notice that even the arguments stayed low. Guards had doubled at every major crossing. Their hands stayed near their weapons, and whenever a cart slowed beside them, one guard checked the cargo while the other watched the driver.

People were tense. Everyone knew a Baolian demigod had spent the night behind the western palace wall. The western inns had emptied first, followed by three streets of homes after a rumor claimed Fang Yizhen disliked the view from his rooms.

The rumor was probably nonsense, people knew, and yet they left anyway. The merchants who remained kept glancing at the palace whenever a cart wheel cracked too loudly against the road.

Lately, strange things have been happening in Hwangcheon. A miracle clinic had opened where people received free treatment, treatment that was far better than the most expensive healer around. According to some rumors, a demon ran the place.

Truly, Hwangcheon was going through turmoil. This morning was special despite all that. Just after the sun rose, a shadow passed over the market and covered the light. A child pointed before anyone else found their voice.

“A Dragon!”

His mother caught his wrist and pulled his arm down, but half the market had already looked up.

Across the grand sky, a huge jade-green dragon passed over the city, its long body cutting through the morning light. Green scales flashed along its frame, and the horns above its head curved back toward the clouds. Its wings beat once, sending a gust through the market that overturned awnings and scattered paper charms across the road.

Some of those charms had been painted with the Green Scale King’s name, exactly. An old woman caught one against her chest and bowed while the dragon circled the palace. Shopkeepers lowered their heads after her. A pair of soldiers dropped to one knee, and the child who had pointed tried to copy them until his mother pulled him close.

Ao’kai of the Shan Gui Highlands, the Green Scale King, descended in front of people who had left rice, jade beads, and spring water at his mountain shrines for generations.

He did not land gently, for his claws struck the palace approach and cracked the stone, sending dust across the boots of the royal guards waiting there. By the time the dust settled, his gigantic serpentine body had folded into green light, revealing a large man, shirtless but wearing simple, baggy gray-green pants, standing where the dragon had been. Black hair fell behind his shoulders, while green scales remained visible along his neck and hands.

Their captain hurried forward with a nervous demeanor and stopped several paces away, then lowered his sword to the ground with both hands.

“Green Scale King,” he said, his head bowed. “Hwangcheon welcomes you.”

The rest of Goryeo’s guards began to bow with him, only for a different set of soldiers to push through their line. They belonged to Baolian, wearing rich dark gold armor beneath formal robes, their hands resting near swords and spears, and one of them shouldered the kneeling captain far enough that his sword scraped across the stone.

Commander Yuwen Bao walked at their center. He was broad through the shoulders, with a long spear strapped over his back, and he did not look at the royal captain or the stones Ao’kai had broken. His attention remained on the Dragon God as he stopped where the captain had been standing and bowed at exactly the angle courtesy required.

“Lord Fang expected you sooner,” Yuwen Bao said. “He is waiting.”

Even in his human form, Ao’kai’s dragon eyes narrowed. Wind still moved through the market from his landing, stirring sleeves and prayer papers, but nobody spoke while their guardian god looked down at the commander.

The few scales that remained along Ao’kai’s jaw drew tight. His feet remained planted in the broken road for another breath. Then Ao’kai glanced once at the royal captain on the ground before walking toward the guest compound, with Yuwen Bao taking the place beside him where Goryeo’s escort should have been.

The old woman still held his prayer charm against her chest. Nearby, the royal captain picked his sword up from the stones and waited until the guest-compound gate closed before raising his head.

Where is he?

Ha-Yun couldn’t help but glare as he replied, “Lord Fang is discussing a matter between the Dominion and the Green Scale King. The palace is not involved.”

Ha-Yun felt her father’s aura move. The flagstones beneath her shoes began to hum as golden light gathered around the Monarch’s shoulders. Goryeo’s guards straightened behind him, while several Baolian soldiers widened their stance and reached for their weapons.

Yuwen Bao did neither. The hem of his robe stirred in the rising pressure, but his boots stayed where they were, blocking the Monarch from entering a guest residence inside his own palace.

Ha-Yun couldn’t help but speak now. “You are standing in the palace while saying that, Commander,” she said before her father could answer. “The Green Scaled King is no subject of Shengzong’s to be summoned behind closed doors, and definitely not within Goryeo.”

Yuwen Bao looked at her in silence as if identifying who had spoken. “If my Emperor has no authority over Ao’kai’s matters, then Princess Jin Ha-Yun too has no reason to concern herself with it.”

Ha-Yun’s hand moved toward her sword, Cheongmyeong, as her father’s aura pressed harder. The golden light entered the cracks between the flagstones and spread beneath Yuwen Bao’s feet. One of the Baolian guards behind him swallowed loudly, but the commander only let his hand fall to the spear strapped across his back.

Before either side took another step, Fang Yizhen’s voice came from inside the compound.

“Let them in.”

Yuwen Bao waited until Fang had finished speaking, then stepped aside without bowing to the Monarch. Ha-Yun followed her father through the gate and glanced again toward the roofs beyond the western wall. It was empty, with not even a pigeon resting on top.

So where is Iskandaar?
