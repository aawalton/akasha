The reins of Nigel's war steed had never felt so heavy. It felt like he was dragging the lives of his men to the afterlife himself.

Nigel looked over his soldiers. He saw a sea of dull iron. Half of the soldiers were wearing worn and scored armor, their battered plate matching his own perfectly. His knight order was the most ill-equipped among the entirety of Cinderfall's army. Nigel felt a sharp pang of guilt. During the grant allocation to the different knight orders, he couldn't secure his soldiers the ore they needed to reforge their gear. The high lords of the Phoenix had seen to that, ensuring the Acards received only scraps. The guilt was just piling up, one layer over the other.

He had led his men in many battles. Some were perilous, like delving into rogue dungeons to cull the hordes before they spilled into the farmlands. But this time felt entirely wrong. His men were unaware they were charging to their deaths. This time, unlike the others, there was absolutely zero hope for survival.

Nigel gripped the reins tighter. He knew he was lying to himself. There was a way to survive. The men just needed to forsake their honor as knights and surrender. But Nigel knew this was a tall order to ask. Some of the men here had failed to call upon a phoenix in the royal hatcheries. Most of them were serving here solely for the honor of being able to be called a knight, enduring the grueling training for the distant opportunity to visit the royal hatchery, holding onto the desperate hope that one of the numerous eggs would finally glow for them.

Nigel remembered his own last visit to the hatchery. When his turn was over, the next kid in line had several eggs light up for him. In that case, the knight could pick any of their choosing and bond with them. How does he always end up with the short end of the stick?

Nigel pulled on the reins and turned his steed around, looking at the sea of iron bearing the crest of his house.

He took his helmet off and cradled it in his right arm. He let out a deep sigh and looked up, his heavy cape billowing in the freezing wind.

With all the strength in his lungs, he shouted, “My knights! You have served me admirably all these years, and so I have always been frank with you all.”

Nigel paused and took a deep inhale. “We are charging to our death this time.”

A few knights jeered back from the front ranks. “When have we not?”

Others chimed in, banging their gauntlets against their shields. “That’s nothing new, Knight Commander!”

A loud voice carried from the back of the formation. “You warned us when we signed up!”

Nigel felt something warm well up inside him. A small ember of pride pushed back against the freezing dread. No, I am quite blessed. I am lucky to have these men at my back. It was a sharp contrast to the humiliating memory of the hatchery he had just relived.

Nigel raised his free hand, calling for silence. “As I was saying, this will probably be my last charge with you all. I will not lie to you. There is a way to survive this ordeal.”

He leaned forward in his saddle. “All you must do is surrender. The enemy didn't lie. What you have been told at the camps is fabrication and propaganda. They don't kill people who surrender. They are escorted out of the battlefield, allowed to walk freely to the other side. I have seen so. That is all I know. I know not the fate of those who make it to the other side, but what the command has told you is simply untrue.”

He swept his gaze over the worn, tired faces of his men. “I, for one, won't surrender. I shall die with the honor of a knight intact. But any who do choose to surrender, you are taking a gamble. The lives of your family back home may be in peril if you do so. I am not discouraging you all, just giving you the facts. If you have nothing to lose, I implore you to not waste your lives in vain. To live to fight another day is greater than an unsung death.”

He set his jaw, staring out at the distant, silent trench lines of the Aegis Legion. “I for one don't have that option open to me. I have something I need to honor and protect. But if you do... consider taking it.”

The men all looked around at each other, the weight of his words settling over the iron ranks. Then, a low chant started from the back.

“We bleed, we sweat, we endure.”

The line was very familiar to Nigel. It was the chant of the Acard knights. They didn't know the whole phrase, the bitter mantra of his grandfather, but they knew the end.

Soon more people chimed in, their voices rising over the wind. “We sweat, we endure.”

More and more people joined in, building until it became a roaring, defiant sound that shook the ground.

“WE BLEED, WE SWEAT, WE ENDURE.”

“WE BLEED, WE SWEAT, WE ENDURE.”

“WE BLEED, WE SWEAT, WE ENDURE.”

Nigel knew he didn't have to say anything else. His heart felt lighter. He jammed his helmet back onto his head, locking the seals, and drew his iron sword.

He bellowed at the top of his lungs, "CHARGE!"

He spurred his war steed forward. All fifty thousand of his knights followed, kicking up a massive dust stream behind them. Nigel welcomed this death with open arms. This was it.

All hell broke loose the moment they crossed no man’s zone. The ground erupted with roaring fire and billowing smoke. Hidden claymores shredded the front ranks, turning horses and men into tangled heaps of screaming metal.

Streaking beams of light tore through his ranks from the distant trenches. It was absolute madness. Metal beasts rained hell from the skies, tearing through the formation with ruthless efficiency.

Nigel saw from the corner of his eye one of the knights drop his blade and raise his hands high in the sky.

Other knights also charged in front of him. It was utter chaos. Some raised their hands, shedding their weapons and dropping to their knees in the mud. Others continued to charge, screaming their defiance as the plasma tore them apart.

There were about five thousand people surrendering in the middle of a massive, hostile charge. It was mayhem. It was an algorithmic anomaly.

Even Tes was struggling to solve the localized variables. Several firing angles became invalid as the automated turrets calculated they might hit non-combatants who had surrendered. The ground mines needed to be disabled in specific grid squares, and the wide-area aerial bombardment had to be halted to prevent massacring the men waving empty hands.

Tes, realizing this was a dire situation, stopped the automated defense grid. The tactical display in the CIC lit up in green, warning that all of the red icons could not be targeted as the crossfire would hit a bystander.

Out of the fifty thousand, fifteen thousand actually made it to the enemy trenches.

Nigel himself was shocked at this turn of events. Who would have thought the knights around him surrendering would create a wall of flesh protecting him? The automated guns fell silent, refusing to fire through the crowd of surrendering men.

Nigel acted without thinking. He spurred his horse over the lip of the trench, unsheathing his blade and hitting the automated turret closest to him. The impact produced a dull thud against the metal plating. The blade sparked, but didn't bite.

Nigel looked around. All of his charging knights were facing the same problem. They couldn't cut through the enemy's armor. Their rusted iron swords just bent against the advanced alloys of the automata and the turrets.

If only the king had plated just our blades with arcanum or mithril. Just the edge.

Meanwhile, the automata surged forward. They sifted through the combatants and non-combatants, manually sorting them. They escorted the refugees backward into the holding zones while swiftly and brutally ending the enemy combatants who still carried steel.

Nigel saw one figure that looked different walking toward him slowly. The man looked odd and out of place amidst the bulky automata. He was carrying just the hilt of a blade on his hip. His armor was sleek, having narrow slits for eyes instead of the glowing blue optical sensors of the machines. There was one obsidian star pinned to his chest plate.

The man rushed forward suddenly at an astonishing speed, punching Nigel's war steed squarely in the chest. Nigel jumped off before the beast pinned him under it. The animal collapsed, not moving from the single hit.

Nigel raised his blade in a guarding stance, holding the iron in front of him, tracking the man in the sleek black armor.

The man reached for the bladeless hilt.

VWOOM.

A brilliant blade of azure condensed light erupted from the hilt, casting a cold glow over the trench. What is this? Nigel thought, panic threading through his focus. I sense no magical output from this man. How is he doing that? Usually, people channeling magic exuded a specific aura, a pressure in the air. A warrior could clearly tell the difference. This man radiated nothing but pure, unadulterated hostility.

The man raised the azure blade. Nigel brought his iron sword forward for a parry, bracing for the impact.

What happened next was a scene ripped straight from his nightmare. The memory of facing the Phoenix Knights in the sparring rings, the humiliating realization that his physical sword skills were completely obsolete against overwhelming magical power.

This man, this man with no magical aura, did the exact same thing.

The azure blade hissed through it, shearing the heavy broadsword in two like a hot knife through butter.

Nigel felt broken. His grandfather's teachings, his lifetime of sweat and endurance, shattered.

Valen halted.

The silver-haired general retracted his visor. The sleek faceplate folded away, revealing a stunning face and long, silver hair contrasted by dark skin. The man was immaculate-looking, but his face was currently twisted with an ugly, visceral anger.

The Dark Elf grabbed Nigel by the collar of his armor, lifting him slightly off the ground.

“YOU PIECE OF SHIT! HOW DARE YOU SPIT ON THE LORD COMMANDER'S KINDNESS!”

Valen was furious. The one thing that made the blood in his veins boil was when someone misused or mistreated Alarion's mercy. He didn't demand the rescued peasants show gratitude, and he accepted ignorance from those who didn't understand the scale of what Alarion had done for them. But if someone abused that generosity it rubbed Valen the wrong way.

Valen slammed the man into the ground, the impact rattling Nigel's teeth. "What if he loses faith now? What then? Without his kindness, I wouldn't be standing here. I would have starved to death in the ash. My people would be suffering under the thumb of the demon lords."

Valen slammed his heavy boot down on Nigel’s breastplate, pinning him to the ground.

Nigel was shocked at the man's strength. This was inhuman for a warrior without active magic enhancement. But he couldn't understand why this elf was so enraged at him. Even though Nigel couldn't sense mana, he could sense raw, suffocating bloodlust.

“Death is mercy for scum like YOU.”

On the ground, Nigel looked around, the harsh reality sinking in. He had made it to this side by inadvertently using refugees as meat shields. Incorrectly, he would be seen as a coward who used underhanded and vile tactics. He had sullied the Acard name. Even worse, Cinderfall command would see this outcome. It wouldn't be a stretch for the King to reuse this tactic, to force innocent civilians into the vanguard to act as living shields to bypass the automated defenses. This engagement just proved the enemy was too soft.

Nigel couldn't bring himself to explain. He held his tongue tight, accepting the disgrace and his impending death as Valen lifted his humming plasma blade to behead him.

Suddenly, a red light flickered on the side of Valen's suit. The light blinked from the collar housing where the helmet had retracted. It spoke with a soul-less, calm, inhuman voice.

[Commander Valen. He is Nigel Acard. Cousin to the King of Cinderfall. The battle has ended, and the man is unarmed. According to the laws set forth by Dragon Valley to prevent blood feuds, the man cannot be executed for his high noble lineage and standing. He can be ransomed back. If you execute him, his family can file a claim against us, and we would have to pay reparations according to the law. The man needs to be taken in as a prisoner. There are too many witnesses to this act. Receiving ransom is more beneficial for us.]

Valen clenched his teeth and looked around. The automated voice was correct. The battle had ended. All the combatants had been neutralized or separated from the surrendered forces. And because Valen had cut his blade in half, the man was technically unarmed. Valen deeply regretted disarming him. He should have deflected the blow with his pauldron and taken the head then.

Nigel scrambled in the ash, his breath coming in ragged, desperate gasps. He lunged forward, his fingers digging into the dirt as he reached frantically for the ruined, sheared-off half of his own iron broadsword that lay discarded near Valen's boots. He intended to drag the jagged edge across his own throat. He absolutely refused to accept the ultimate disgrace of being ransomed back to a King who had sent his men here to die. To be bought back like a piece of broken cattle would ruin the Acard name forever.

Valen grabbed his arm, stopping the motion easily. His augmented strength completely overpowered Nigel. "Looks like it may be my lucky day after all. You really seem to prefer death."

A few heavily armored automata marched forward. They tackled Nigel from behind, hauling him backward and holding him down on the ground.

Nigel begged, his voice tearing from his throat, reaching desperately toward Valen's boots as he was dragged away.

“PLEASE KILL ME! LET ME DIE A KNIGHT'S DEATH ALONG WITH MY MEN!”
