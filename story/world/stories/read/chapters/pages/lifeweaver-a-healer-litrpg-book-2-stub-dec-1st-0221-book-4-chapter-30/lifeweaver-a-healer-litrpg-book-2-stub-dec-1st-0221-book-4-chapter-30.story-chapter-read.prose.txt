When he came to, Hannah was no longer in his lap, and the grass had taken on a damp quality he associated with early morning. Sun streamed through the tall trees, and birds chirped somewhere out of sight. Despite the fact that he must've slept through the night, Luke felt tired and ragged. Another eight hours of sleep would do the trick, maybe, but that wasn't an option, judging by the many messages in his interface. Ignoring those for a moment, he listened for more sounds and heard footsteps and rustling coming from within the small, derelict cabin. Rather than shout her name, since that was sure to worsen his headache, he got to his feet and swayed a little. Hunger. For the first time in a long, long while, he needed something to eat. The battle in the void had taken a lot out of him. Dried blood still covered his eyes, forcing him to keep blinking to get flakes out, and more was on his cheeks and down the sides of his neck, having poured out of his ears. The Integrated Shop provided a couple of water bottles, and he used one to clean himself off as he took small, controlled steps toward the structure.

Smudge pushed and wanted out, and Luke allowed it. The little voidling - not so little anymore - loped off into the forest, jumping and barking at butterflies and lizards. Despite acting more and more like a dog, he was now starting to look more like a wolf, and a large one at that. Smiling at the excitement radiating from his companion, Luke looked back up at the cabin as he approached the steps. As he'd seen before, it wasn't in good shape. The whole thing tilted to the side, looking like a gentle breeze might knock it over. He still entered. In the grand scheme of things, a small wooden building collapsing on his head would be a vacation compared to what he'd endured since Earth's integration. The door swung open on hinges that cried out, as if in pain.

"Hannah?" he called into the dim light inside.

"Over here."

Crossing the entryway, he entered a small kitchen with a stiff-looking wooden bench that looked like it could fold out into a bed. The whole room smelled of damp wood and dust. No one had lived there for a long while. Continuing on, he found Hannah standing over a small bed tucked into the corner of a bedroom. A couple of books in terrible condition stood on the nightstand, and a small, grime-covered lamp in the shape of a unicorn stood next to them, facing the bed.

"This is where I used to sleep," she said as she turned around to face him.

For a split second, Luke feared seeing those pitch-black eyes again, but when her face came into view, he saw she was herself.

"You used to sleep here?" Luke asked. "In the woods?"

Hannah stepped up to him, wrapped her arms around his waist, and looked up into his face. He might be a clueless idiot a lot of the time, but even he knew what that meant. Luke bent over a little and kissed her. They stayed like that for several too-short minutes, basking in each other's presence.

"You made it back," he said after a while.

"I did," she replied, a little out of breath. "You came looking for me. Thank you."

"Yeah, but you weren't ready to go."

She shook her head. "I wasn't. And when I did, I almost ruined everything."

"You mean with the void? I fixed that."

"You've grown stronger," Hannah said as she took a step back to look him over with a smile. "But you look like you could use a shower."

"I'm guessing there isn't one here? Someone stole my Shelter."

That made the good humor bleed out of her expression, and she glanced at the bed again. "No shower here."

"Wha-"

The smile returned, bigger this time. "But there is a lake! Let's go swimming!"

"But I don't have swimming trunks with me," Luke said, frowning.

Hannah took his hand and pulled him out of the room, turning with a knowing grin on her face. "You think I brought a bathing suit?"

"Oh," Luke said, frowning, before his eyes widened. "Oh!"

The lake was small and surrounded by trees without a sign of a single other person. Sunlight glittered over the tranquil surface, and Luke hunched down to touch the water. Freezing cold climbed all the way up his hand and forearm, and he pulled away with a shudder. If he were by himself, there was no way he would jump into that, no matter how much he needed a good wash, but he wasn't by himself. Turning, he saw that Hannah had changed out of her clothes and that she had indeed not brought a swimsuit. Luke swallowed hard, heat coming into his face.

"What are you waiting for?" she asked, wriggling her eyebrows. Not waiting for an answer, she waded out and then plunged down under the surface, coming up with a shriek of joy. "Come on!"

"To hell with it," Luke muttered with a smile, tearing his blood and grime-stained clothes off before joining her, heroically braving the cold water.

For a moment, he felt free of all the burdens on his shoulders, as if they were just two people swimming and having fun playing in the water. Luke knew it wouldn't last, but he allowed himself to cling to that tranquility for a little while. The world outside could wait.

After a while, Hannah swam up to him and wrapped her arms around his shoulders, pressing herself close enough to whisper in his ear. "Why don't we get out of the water and you pop your Shelter?"

Her low whisper made him shudder, and he wanted nothing more, but there was a slight problem. "I don't have it anymore. The Singers stole everything in my inventory."

"Oh," she said. "I wondered where your robe went. I'm sure there's a story there. Save it for later."

"What about your Shelter?"

She sighed. "I lost everything, too."

"I'm sure there's a story there," he echoed.

Hannah wrapped her legs around his waist and used that to push herself a little higher up as she peered around the lake, her small smile growing larger. "I'll save that for later. There's no one here. Not for miles around."

"Oh, yeah?" Luke said. This time, he understood her meaning right away, and he grabbed her waist and stood, lifting them both out of the water. Hannah weighed almost nothing at all in his arms, and he carried her out of the water and onto the grass with ease as she alternated between kissing him and laughing in delight.

Later, when both of them were spent and had gone into the water a second time to clean the last couple of days off, they lay on the grass in the sun, watching the clouds. Luke filled her in on what happened with the pink dungeon, the demons, how the Singer invasion was going, and how he'd battled the void creatures while repairing the weave. So much had happened since they entered the portals. A lifetime of experiences and near world-ending catastrophes, all in the span of a couple of weeks. He had feared that Hannah's experiences had changed her, and the episode when she returned reinforced that, but right then and there, she seemed more easygoing and happy than he'd seen her in a long while.

"When did you live here?" he asked.

That took the edge off her bright expression. Smooth, Luke. Real smooth.

"My parents weren't the best," she began, and by the look on her, that was an understatement for the ages. "Sometimes they disappeared, or were sent to jail. I'd spend time here then, with grandma and grandpa. They died almost ten years ago now, within days of each other. Haven't been here since."

"We can fix the place up," Luke said.

She chuckled. "Are you asking me to move in with you, Luke?"

"Uh," he said, clearing his throat. "Maybe?"

Laughing, she pulled clean clothes out of her inventory and threw them at him. "Let's talk about that once the void isn't threatening to end our existence, shall we? Not that I mind looking at you naked, but I think it's time we got dressed. You said we're going to fight the Singers, right?"

She stood, and he watched her start dressing, not minding the scenery one bit. "You don't have to join that, Hannah. I mean, you just got back from... something? You still haven't told me what you went through."

Hannah paused, looked down at her feet, then back up at him with an expression of determination masking some real fear beneath it all. "Yeah. It would probably be a good idea to tell you. I'd rather forget most of it, but it'll probably be relevant at some point, when I, you know, almost destroy Earth again."

"Come sit," he said, patting the grass beside him as he took the clothes into his inventory and equipped them from there, getting fully dressed in less than a second.

She looked at him, still wearing nothing but pants. "That's cheating."

"You're fine like that, if you want," he said in his most magnanimous voice.

"Right," she snorted as she opted to put her bra and dark gray T-shirt on before joining him. Following that, she sat down and drew in a deep breath. "All right. This might get a little dark."

'A little dark' was a gentle way of putting it, as it turned out. Hannah went back a lot further in her telling of events, all the way back to when she absorbed The Deep Dweller. She hadn't admitted it at the time, but it hadn't been a quick and simple fight for dominion, but a prolonged war where the monster fought with vicious glee to take control of her body. Fighting back to keep control of her own body, Hannah had thrown everything at the beast while outwardly acting like nothing was going on. How lonely that must've been. In the end, she had come through victorious. Honing her will had been the only way to win, which meant she had learned to wield it even before Luke, who nodded as she spoke, impressed at her tenacity and spirit. He took her hand and squeezed it as she continued.

Taking the power of The Deep Dweller for herself, Hannah had learned that it wasn't just any monster, nor one of shadows, really, but an anomaly of sorts, a being of distant relationship to the void, like a second cousin twice removed. Void mana had been part of it. Not much, but enough that it bled over into her. Rather than feel pain when it surged through her mana channels, it allowed her to see a version of the weave, her version. The way she described it was a lot like a sea of inverted shadows, like soft shapes of moonlight swaying together as one. It made her uncomfortable, but she couldn't say why, because she didn't quite understand it herself.

Void mana didn't harm her in any way, as she told it. It made her shadows stronger when she infused them with the strange non-system substance, like the two were feeding on each other. The tattoos appeared when she used it, and Luke understood from her earlier explanation that they were the same shapes as the weave in her mind. That was significant, somehow, but neither of them knew how. That brought them to the most recent event, the pink gate. Her pink gate.

"This is where it gets... interesting," she said, gathering herself to continue.
