Born from despair and vengeance, the Skalk is not a natural creature of AEFLYM, but an object of folklore from another world given form through death and wind. Its existence burns bright and brief. It lives no longer than seventy-two hours, yet within that span, it evolves with terrifying speed—feeding on the essence of death and despair around it to fuel its unnatural advancement. Its purpose, however, is singular: Vengeance. And when that vengeance is fulfilled, so too does the Skalk wither, fading back into the essence that birthed it.

Zarxan watched curiously as Az’marthon’s legacy and his companions raced toward the Fated’s location—a blistering desire for vengeance burning in the young man’s heart. A vengeance that tasted all too familiar.

Az’marthon had reeked of that same desire after his parents’ deaths and had carried that scent up until his ascension. There was no doubt about it anymore. Aodhán was so like his patron it was unnerving.

Their mannerisms were slightly different, and the legacy looked nothing like the Storm Lord Zarxan had known, but those were the only differences he could see.

Perhaps, by the time they spoke, he would see more. Though he highly doubted it.

Sighing, Zarxan walked toward the broken console, his fingers lightly tracing its edges to extract whatever energy remained. The console sparked, electricity fizzing across its surface until the whole thing went up in flames—the acrid smell of damaged runes and burning wires filling the air.

The flames licked his fingers, but Zarxan barely noticed, his attention fixed on Aodhán—who had long since left his line of sight—as he wondered what to do next. Now that he had saved the legacy and seen him with his own eyes, Zarxan wasn’t quite sure what to do after examining him.

If Aodhán turned out to be weak, could he simply leave him here? After all he had gone through to save him and his friends? And if he turned out to be strong—

Zarxan shook his head, hating the emotion that erupted within him at the thought. His fingers tightened on the burning console, and the whole thing was instantly crushed to powder.

The explosion of metal finally got his attention, and Zarxan grimaced at the sight. He wasn’t weak. Not by a long shot. But Az’marthon had refused him as a legacy—his own apprentice—only to choose this child. If Aodhán was stronger—stronger than Zarxan had been at the same tier—then that would mean Az’marthon had found him unworthy.

And that was a pill Zarxan was certain he would find hard to swallow. For his own sanity, he should simply leave and be done with the whole thing, but deep down he knew that whether the boy turned out to be strong or not, leaving now was tantamount to signing the boy’s death certificate.

Unlike Gordonians, Lutians were no friends of transmigrants, and it was only a matter of time before they began hunting Aodhán—if they hadn’t begun already.

Zarxan grunted at that, a part of him not entirely averse to the idea.

The boy had gotten what Zarxan had spent decades begging for, handed to him on a platter of gold. He hadn’t worked for it. Neither did he deserve it. Leaving him to rot in Lutia would serve Az’marthon right.

He had come and he had satisfied his curiosity already. He didn’t need to be responsible for it.

His gaze hardened in that moment, but despite the rationalization, Zarxan couldn’t bring himself to leave knowing fully well what that would mean for Aodhán. A fellow storm cultivator and the legacy of his master—the young man was practically family—

His mind stuttered to a halt, and he frowned as the word family echoed in his mind. His attention shifted to the legacy racing off in the distance, and his mind began cataloguing the eerie similarities between Aodhán and Az’marthon.

The conclusion he arrived at was frightening.

Aodhán was too much like Az’marthon—bearing the same dogged determination, seamless leadership qualities, and heroism that his master had possessed. Those qualities could have been a product of receiving the legacy, but Zarxan doubted it.

It was too early for such synchronized behavioral patterns and personalities.

And if that was the case, then this child could be Az’marthon’s…

Zarxan shook his head, unwilling to voice that possibility aloud—as if speaking it might make it real, might make his own inadequacy undeniable. If what he was thinking was true, then this child was even more special than he’d thought. He couldn’t leave him here to suffer at the hands of Lutia’s nobility. He had to protect him, and the only way to do that was to take him to Gordon, where he could start afresh with a clean slate and a new identity—far from the chaotic cesspit that was Lutia.

Zarxan himself had done the same thing—changing his entire identity after Az’marthon’s ascension and the realization that his master had denied him what he’d coveted most. He had become a new person in Gordon, growing to heights of status and power he’d never thought imaginable.

Aodhán could do the same. He too could become someone new.

Zarxan glanced back at the source of the explosion as the Skalk screeched once again, despair and fury billowing out of it in concentric circles as it went on a rampage against the Fated and his entourage of Mythics.

It certainly couldn’t defeat them on its own, but it wouldn’t have to.

Help was on its way in the form of dozens of former captives, a host of telepaths, and a family of infinity awakened.

The Fated was about to get what was coming for him, and with something almost resembling excitement, Zarxan rose into the air, eager to see how Az’marthon’s legacy doled out punishment.

Regardless of Aodhán’s strength, there was no doubt in Zarxan’s heart that it would be impressive.

He was Az’marthon’s legacy, after all.

And Az’marthon had been the Calamity of judgment and vengeance of his generation.

Even if the legacy had inherited nothing else from his patron, he most certainly would have inherited that.

A few minutes earlier, just after Jian’s death, Jethro sat amidst a dozen alert elders, eyes closed and lips parted in ecstasy as he consolidated his newfound strength. Jian’s spirit was much stronger than Kira’s, and as it melded seamlessly with his, Jethro couldn’t help but groan in contentment.

After absorbing the spiritual strength of two transmigrants, his spirit had nearly tripled in size and power, placing his spiritual strength above that of any normal transmigrant. He felt as light as a feather, his spirit humming with a potential he’d never felt be—

The world shuddered.

Not the ground or the building. But reality itself—light dimming as space ripped and time came to a sudden halt.

It was only for a moment, but in that single terrible moment, Jethro felt a blistering pain lance through his chest where his link to the Time Watcher had lain.

A link that was suddenly no more.

Jethro gasped in pain and horror, doubling over as his stomach lurched. Around him, elders collapsed to their knees, faces pale, hands clutching at their chests as if their hearts had stopped beating.

“What—” someone tried to speak, but the words died on their lips as they felt their link to the Watcher also vanish.

The Time Watcher was dead.

“No,” Jethro whispered in dawning dread, heart pounding as he lurched to his feet, eyes wide with denial. “No, that’s not possible.”

He raced toward the nearest window, wanting to confirm with his own eyes what he could already feel, but he’d barely managed a few steps before reality shuddered again.

And this time, the pain was far worse.

Causality itself groaned as another link vanished, forcing them all back to their knees. Colors bled wrong. Sounds echoed backward. For a fraction of a second, Jethro saw his own hand move before he’d commanded it.

The Karmic Watcher was gone.

“By Sárán!” Jethro screamed, whirling toward the first elder. “I thought you said they would survive!”

“I… I thought—” the elder stammered, face ashen, but before he could get the words out, reality shuddered a third time.

And this one felt like the end of everything.

Pain ripped through his spirit like a flaming lance, tearing a ghastly wound through all three links, and Jethro very nearly blacked out from the agony. His spirit burned. His vision blurred, and in that moment, Jethro realized there was no winning this war.

The Fate Watcher was dead.

All three Watchers were dead.

And there was no doubt that whatever had killed them was coming for him next.

Vision swimming, Jethro stumbled away from the window in terror, eyes wide as he commanded, “Get me out of here right now. My survival is what matters—”

The rest of his words were drowned out by an abominable screech as a creature straight out of nightmare appeared before them all—half skeleton, half ghost, vaguely resembling someone he knew

“Kira?” he stammered in confusion, and then his gaze strayed to the text floating above its head.

[Skalk—Tier 71]

“Get behind us, Fated!” an elder shouted as he and several others rushed to form a protective circle around Jethro. But as dangerous as the Skalk seemed, it was nothing compared to whatever had killed the Watchers.

The Skalk was a distraction. And that was the last thing he needed right now.

Groaning at the stupidity of the supposed elders, he shouted, “Leave it and get me out of here. There’s a bigger threat coming for us.”

The elders complied immediately, and Elder Connor rushed to stand beside him, a crimson talisman clutched tightly between his fingers. Face tight with pain—no doubt from the wound in his spirit—he raised the talisman into the air, cut a finger, and let his blood drip onto it.

Immediately, a swirling black portal appeared a few feet away from them, and without hesitation, Jethro and Isobá rushed toward it. However, just as they reached the opening, the Skalk screeched again—this time with so much malice it was tangible.

Against his better instincts, Jethro looked back to see the creature standing above Jian’s decapitated body, a maelstrom of despair and death billowing out of its translucent form in waves like smoke from a factory chimney.

Their gazes locked, and in that moment, Jethro wished he had ignored his instincts.

Despair billowed out of the Skalk in an overwhelming wave, and though the first elder raised a shield of wind, the wave wasn’t an attack, neither was it physical. It was a domain.

The wave spread outward like smoke, the air rippling, thick and heavy as though space itself was suffocating. The moment it reached them, the talisman crackled violently, and the swirling portal collapsed, devoured by the essence of despair itself.

“It’s a spatial disruption domain!” Isobá shouted as she lurched away from the collapsing portal, flames erupting around her as she turned to glare at the Skalk. “Go with the Fated, elders. I’ll hold it off.”

But Jethro was already moving, fleeing the spreading cloud in haste. The elders rushed after him, their Mythic speed helping them catch up almost instantly, but the Skalk wasn’t content to let them simply escape.

And Isobá wasn’t nearly enough to hold it back.

It lunged, blurring across the distance with speed nearly too great for Isobá to follow.

But she followed regardless, dozens of flame lances erupting from her palms in a wall of searing fire. Each lance burned white-hot, capable of melting steel in seconds, and they converged on the Skalk’s form like divine judgment.

The lances struck true.

And passed straight through.

The Skalk’s body rippled, becoming translucent—no, not translucent. *Immaterial*. The flames cut through empty air where its chest should have been, finding nothing but smoke and despair.

“What—” Isobá’s eyes widened in horror, but that single word was all she managed before the Skalk closed the distance in a single fluid motion—skeletal hands wreathed in wisps of death essence piercing straight through her chest to close around her heart.

And then it became solid, all within the span of a blink.

Isobá barely had the chance to gasp before the Skalk pulled, ripping through ribs and tissue without resistance—her still-beating heart clutched in its bony grip.

For a heartbeat, she remained standing, a terrible sensation of cold spreading through her body.

Then she crumpled like a puppet with cut strings, the light fading from her eyes before her body hit the ground. Her heart hit the ground an instant later, but the Skalk was already gone, its screech echoing down the hallway as it raced toward the elders frantically preparing another portal.

Its domain spread out again, even faster than before, and just as the portal began to form, it collapsed once more.

From up ahead, Jethro growled, “Kill that thing and get me out of here, Connor.”

Half the elders rushed toward the Skalk to carry out Jethro’s command, rules and skills exploding within the narrow space in an attempt to rip it apart.

But beyond being immaterial, the Skalk did not fully exist where they struck. It was a phantom. A story told to children on another world to frighten them. It was a thought given life—an existence created from memory and fear. Nothing could touch or harm it, and despite its lower tier, a few non-elite Mythics weren’t enough to stop it.

Multiple rules of absolute severance rang out, and with a violent heave, reality obeyed—space cleaving into flawless divisions where continuity itself was told to end. Explosions ripped the hallway apart, the air itself stilled, and causality buckled as rules of erasure and exclusion echoed out with Mythic authority.

All that power was more than enough to obliterate an army of hundreds in an instant.

And yet, the Skalk passed through with minimal harm, rippling across the fabric of the immaterial without fully resolving, its presence stretched thin until the rules completed themselves.

And even in the immaterial, it never stopped hunting.

Never stopped drawing closer, abyssal eyes fixed on Jethro with burning hatred until an elder suddenly called out, their voice echoing with an authority the others lacked.

{MY DOMAIN, MY RULES: ALL BORN OF WIND SHALL BE HELD BY IT}

Reality heaved violently, and almost immediately the air thickened. Wind that had once been free-flowing turned viscous, currents coiling inward to wrap around the Skalk like inescapable bands.

The Skalk shrieked, and for the first time since its appearance, its form failed to blur completely—its attempts at phasing faltering as skeletal contours snapped in and out of the immaterial.

The other elders didn’t hesitate to capitalize on that moment of forced coherence, and with snarls, they unleashed an unnecessary barrage of skills.

Metal spikes the size of towers shot toward the Skalk even as wooden and earthen spikes erupted from walls and floors to skewer its form. Light and darkness churned into constructs. Space fractured into blades, and blood spears congealed around it.

But perhaps a rule would have been more effective this time.

The metal spikes ripped through its form from every direction, tearing through its half-coherent body to pin it in place. Space-blades sliced its skeletal form apart, faultless edges carving clean through ribs of bone and vapor without resistance.

Light and darkness crushed inward, and what remained of the Skalk exploded violently, scattering in every direction. Despair bloomed in a cloud above its scattered remains, obscuring the hallway in smoke.

“It’s dead,” Elder Connor called out, and Jethro growled.

“Do you still have another talisman?”

Elder Connor nodded. “Just two more, Fated.”

Jethro nodded. “Then get me out of here. We’ve wasted enough time already.”

But perhaps, had they encountered a creature like the Skalk before, they would have realized that a creature of death and despair couldn’t die with so much death and despair around it.

Elder Connor raised the talisman and dripped his blood onto its surface. However, just as another swirling portal began to appear, a screech echoed from within the cloud of despair. Abyssal eyes opened, and before any of the elders could react, the Skalk blinked forward, phasing through the circle of elders to appear right before Jethro, its domain spreading out in a suffocating wave.

The portal collapsed, and with a snarl, it drove a skeletal arm toward Jethro’s chest. The elders shouted, fear apparent in their eyes, but in Jethro’s, there was only impatience and irritation.

In less than a blink, he harnessed the quintessence of three wind seals at once. Heat erupted within him like lava from a volcanic eruption, but this time, his spirit barely even groaned as he spoke.

{MY DOMAIN, MY RULES: WIND BECOMES DESTRUCTION}

The Skalk barely had time to react before it was violently thrown backward, its form nearly crushed to powder from the force of the attack.

Its domain flickered for a moment before dissipating, its ghostly form still spasming as its regeneration worked to piece itself back together. It still wasn’t dead, but Jethro barely spared it a glance. Instead, his gaze scanned the skies for the Calamity that could arrive any moment as he urged Elder Connor to hurry.

The elder took out the last talisman and activated it with his blood. Another swirling black portal appeared.

But it was too late.

Aodhán and his companions had arrived.

And with a single flick of his fingers, Damien Rune ripped the swirling portal to shreds.

“Where do you think you’re going, Jethro?” Aodhán snarled, lightning crackling violently around him as he levitated into the air alongside many others, an unforgiving fury etched into all their faces.

Jethro’s eyes widened in shock at the sight of them, and Aodhán’s snarl deepened into something even more terrible. “I promised to kill you, Jethro. I will not let you rob me of the chance to keep that promise.”

“Nor I,” Daruk echoed.

“Nor I,” Mirith echoed.

“Nor I,” everyone echoed.

A screech rang out from behind Jethro as the Skalk rose back to its feet, far larger than before. Its domain erupted out once again, and with a snarl echoing the deepest hatred and malice, it too spoke.

“Nor I.”

What followed was a clash so violent it brought the entire structure down, grinding the rubble to powder.

The Runes, the telepaths, and the Champions met the elders head-on, while everyone else turned on Jethro—every one of the seventy-nine survivors, including Tyrus, Aldric, and the cultists he had bound to himself.

Vengeance bound them all together—both weak and strong, all converging furiously on Jethro, more than ready to tear him to pieces.

And at the helm was Aodhán, his expression a storm of fury and resolve.

He would not rest until he had Jethro’s dead heart in his hands.

He would not rest until he had avenged Brennon and Baxter.

He would not rest until he had avenged himself.

And with a roar, he levitated into the air, raised his hands to the heavens, and called down a judgment of liquid lightning!

It was the same judgment Az’marthon had delivered in his first vision. And for this enemy, it seemed perfectly fitting.
