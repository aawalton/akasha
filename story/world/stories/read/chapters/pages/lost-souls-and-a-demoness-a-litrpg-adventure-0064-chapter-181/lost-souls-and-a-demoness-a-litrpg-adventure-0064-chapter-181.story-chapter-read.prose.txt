Once they'd gotten all the information they could out of their unexpected ally, the question then became: What were they going to do with her? Elyx wasted no time in making her position clear.

"I want to go with you."

"Go with us?" Jade asked. "To confront your emperor?"

Yeah, that wasn't about to happen. Jade's empathic senses hadn't detected any malice or deception in her so far, but that didn't mean they were going to trust her completely.

"What? No! No, no, no." Elyx shook her head so fervently that, under other circumstances, it would have been comical. "I want out of the Labyrinth."

There was a moment of silence while everybody processed that.

"You want us to take you with us when we leave?"

"Assuming you survive whatever insane thing you're about to do, yes. I definitely don't want to stay here. If anybody ever finds out what I did… and even if they don't, there'll be questions about why I came back. I'm supposed to be in the Labyrinth under the command of the man we just killed."

"I'm going to survive." Seris said, his voice echoing back from the cockpit. "I'll be staying here ready to blast off at the first sign of trouble."

"You mean, blast off when we all make it back." Tara scowled in his general direction.

"I'm already assuming I'll have to go up there and rescue you when everything starts going wrong."

Jade couldn't argue with that perspective, but they were getting off track again.

"I'm fine with that, but we'll have to talk more once we get out of here.""Deal."

Nobody else voiced an objection. Frankly, this was all secondary. They'd spent too much time down here already. At any moment, this lady Ira could detect them and summon reinforcements. Or just smite them with a bolt from the heavens with her angelic powers. It was time to get moving. Seris reluctantly agreed to let Elyx remain aboard, although he insisted she be rendered incapacitated for the duration of their mission. The demon wasn't thrilled by that stipulation but accepted it readily enough. It wasn't like she had much of a choice. Siora used one of her new Wayfarer abilities to quickly prepare a sleeping elixir and then administered it with characteristic efficiency. The demon slumped in her chair, the seat restraints holding her in place. And that was that. One problem down.

"One last push." Jade led the way back outside, emerging in the chill, twilit wastes. She took a deep breath, steadying her nerves. "Everyone ready?"

A few of her friends murmured quiet affirmatives and the rest nodded. Anticipation and anxiety crackled amongst them, mingling with stoic resolve. As quietly as they could, the group set off up the long, steep stairs set into the base of the plateau.

The climb was longer than it looked. With each step, tension rose in the group. Jade felt it as a persistent buzz in the back of her mind, constantly tugging at her attention. She did her best to ignore it, focusing on the climb. No demons leapt down to confront them, nor did they encounter any traps. The climb was mundane and uneventful, which only served to heighten Jade's anxiety. They continued. Ten minutes. Twenty minutes. Step after step, until even Jade was feeling the strain in her legs.

Almost there.

They were nearing the top. Another few minutes, and they'd reach the palace. The trail afforded them glimpses of the imposing structure and its towering spires. It was at once grand and decrepit. Time had taken its toll on the palace, and more than one section was in outright disrepair. To Jade, it looked more like an archeological ruin than a place anybody lived in. Yet the font of darkness spiraling into the sky from the Palace's central tower told a different story.

"Ahh…"

Something bumped into Jade's back. She caught Siora mid-stumble, steadying her.

"You okay?"

"Y-yes, sorry." The elf sounded embarrassed. "Just missed a step."

"Need us to call a break?"

Siora had levelled quickly, but she was still ten levels behind everyone else in the party. Without as many stat points as the rest of them, she tended to run out of stamina first.

"No." Siora surprised Jade with a firm shake of her head. There was a fire in her eyes the succubus had seldom seen. "You've always been pulling me along. Helping me every step of the way. But I'm stronger now. Today, I'll keep pace with my own strength."

"Siora…"

"Don't worry about me, Jade." The elf flashed her a bright smile. "I have your back."

How could she smile like that now, when the pressure was mounting with every step? She felt it too, Jade was sure of it, but not a hint of it showed in her expression. They'd all grown over these past months, and Siora was no different. Strength and resolve burned bright in her spirit, rebelling against the encroaching darkness that drew closer with each passing moment.

They resumed their climb and, only a few minutes later, finally reached the peak.

"Now that's a sight…"

Luis crouched near the top of the long, winding stairway, peering over the lip of the plateau. Jade joined him, the others close behind. The palace was enormous. It wasn't just one building, but an entire complex of structures built from a stone so black, it seemed to consume the light around it. One building loomed larger than the rest. It stood near the center of the plateau, looking more like a giant cathedral than a fortress or castle. Tall, arched windows lined its walls, twinkling with the shattered remnants of the stained glass that had once filled them. It was from a spire in this structure that the darkness rose from, stretching to blanket the sky in a false twilight.

"Stay focused." Jade said, reminding herself as much as Luis. "We can admire it later."

"Or never." Naomi whispered. "How about never? This place is creeping me out."

Indeed, an eerie silence hung over the plateau. There was neither sound nor movement, not even a flicker of torchlight. A pair of huge gates stood before them, leading to a large courtyard in front of the large, central building. Jade wouldn't quite say that the gates were open, it was more that they'd fallen apart. One of the huge double doors was completely missing, and the other was listing to the side, half broken away from the hinges. The only light came in the form of a diffuse red glow emanating from the sky, the meagre remains of the sunlight that pierced the blanket of darkness. Jade found herself clutching her dimensional pouch. Her fingers itched to withdraw the divine relic, but it was too soon. If she did it now, the angel and the demon lord would almost certainly sense it. No, to play this right, they had to wait for the right moment.

"Wait here." She whispered to the others. "I'll scout ahead."

"Jade…" Naomi began, but she held up a hand.

"I'm not going far, just a quick look around. We don't know where they are. Not the demon lord, and not the angel Elyx said was here."

"I'll give you two guesses." Luis muttered, looking at the giant cathedral.

"We need to be sure."

Naomi wasn't convinced.

"Promise you aren't running off on your own again." She pressed.

Jade suppressed a flash of annoyance. She'd probably earned that, if she were being honest with herself.

"I promise."

The others nodded their agreement, and Jade whispered an incantation. She faded from view a moment later, her invisibility spell taking effect. There was no time to waste. The spell drained her mana at a constant rate, and even with the lengthy climb to reach the palace she was still missing almost a quarter of her total supply. She moved quickly and quietly, slipping into the courtyard and lightly probing at her surroundings with her aura. Unfortunately, it was difficult to discern much of anything with magical senses with such a powerful source of dark essence spilling from the cathedral. That was fine. If it shrouded her sight, then perhaps it did the same for-

The doors to the cathedral swung open, immediately followed by a burst of radiant energy that lit the courtyard like the break of dawn. Something hot flashed over Jade, and she flinched as her invisibility was stripped away. She froze, crouched behind the remains of a shattered pillar lying in the rear third of the courtyard, hardly daring to breathe.

"There is only one that I summoned this day, and you are not him." A loud, feminine voice boomed through the courtyard, magically enhanced. "Whoever it is that has come and disturbed Lord Vastrozun's sacred home, allow me to teach you the error of your ways."

Spiritual pressure closed in around Jade's spirit like a vice. Hiding was pointless, now. It probably had been from the start. But, if this was going to work, they had to…

She stood and met the angel's gaze. Ira was tall, like Seraphine had been. She had the beauty of a celestial, with sharp features and pale skin that glowed with inner radiance. It gave her a gentle, ethereal look, completely at odds with her expression and aura. Her feathered wings were black as midnight, and she wore a full set of matching armor. A sheathed sword hung from her waist, and her hard eyes were locked on Jade. She heard her companions coming up behind her, perhaps having felt the same pressure Jade had.

Now. It had to be now. With a silent prayer, Jade pulled free the divine relic from her pouch. The dark crystal was brimming with untapped power and was practically buzzing in Jade's hand. She held it up and spread her wings in what she hoped was an imposing stance.

"Let us pass, Ira." She said, as if she hadn't just learned of this woman's existence less than an hour before. "We're here on behalf of Lady Elaria herself."

A flicker of surprise crossed the angel's face. Jade hadn't thought she'd believe her, but apparently, she had.

Can she sense truth?

While she waited for a response, she pulled up her information.

Ira Starsword

Level: ???

Creature Type: Celestial (Growth)

Origin: Restricted

Growth? Did that mean--

"Then," Ira intoned, leaping from the cathedral's top step, "that is all the more reason why I cannot let you through."

The angel slammed into the cobblestones of the courtyard with a loud crack, throwing up a cloud of dust. She put a hand on the hilt of her sword and Jade tensed. This wasn't an opponent they could defeat conventionally, that much was immediately apparent. Seraphine had been an angel sapped of strength and bereft of purpose, and they'd barely survived an encounter with her. That was not the case with Ira. Her aura was a blazing sun, and her eyes were sharper than a razor's edge.

And then a new voice filled the air. It rumbled across the courtyard, deep and resonating, freezing them all in place.

"I would grant these divine messengers an audience, Lady Ira." Lord Vastrozun rumbled from within the cathedral. "Please, show them in."
