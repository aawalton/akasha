[Expedition Day 44]

On the Horizon, Pryce began synthesizing penicillin early in the morning despite the fact that none of the mold colonies were fully grown yet. Fathom needed the medicine now, and they simply didn’t have the time to wait any longer.

This didn’t mean that he had to extract it all at once, however. Pryce just needed to make enough for today’s dose, which would give the rest of the batch another day to mature. He planned to do the same thing tomorrow as well, and by the day after tomorrow the entire batch would likely be ‘ripe’ for harvest.

The process of extracting and purifying penicillin from the broth was not a very difficult one, and Pryce would have remembered it well even if he had not spent the last week practicing and reviewing the steps.

First, the mold broth was acidified with phosphoric acid, then it was dripped down onto a solution of amyl acetate, which separated the penicillin substrate from the broth.

The overflow from this process was a penicillin-rich solution of amyl acetate, which was then mixed with water along with ammonia and ammonium chloride to act as a buffer.

This step forced the penicillin-rich solution to the bottom of the vessel, where it could then be drawn off with a tap once the liquid was given time to separate into layers.

This solution of concentrated penicillin was then purified with filters and activated carbon before being reduced to a powder.

This last step would have normally been done through freeze-drying, but Pryce had no access to such a device, and so he settled with a Sprengel pump, which simply used dripping mercury to pull air out of the connected vessel one bit at a time.

It took only 20 minutes to fully evacuate the bottle of concentrate, which had naturally been purified into an unremarkable-looking brown powder.

It was darker than Pryce had hoped, which indicated the presence of some impurities, but it was adequate when one considered his suboptimal setup. The equipment aboard the Horizon was indeed excellent in terms of quality, but it was meant to be a multi-purpose lab to conduct preliminary analyses, not an antibiotic production plant.

Pryce decided to wash and filter the powder a few more times, and that lightened the color enough that he deemed it acceptable.

A final weighing showed that he had synthesized 6.81 grams of penicillin-G – more than enough for a full day’s dose.

“Ready?” Pryce asked, half an hour later.

“Ready,” Fathom rasped.

As with before, Pryce slid a needle underneath Fathom’s scales, but Penicillin-G was best delivered as an intramuscular injection, so Pryce had to make sure that he didn't hit a vein.

Fortunately for the both of them, Pryce was successful on his first attempt – only a few drops of blood dribbled out of the perforated tissue, confirming that this spot was safe for an intramuscular injection.

“Just one more…and…done,” Pryce said, breathing a sigh of relief as he extracted the needle.

“This will…cure me?” Fathom tentatively asked.

“I don’t know, but this is the best I can do. You need four doses each day until you are healed, so I give you three more later today, okay?”

“Under…stand,” Fathom panted, his eyes hazy and unfocused as he laid his head back down to rest.
