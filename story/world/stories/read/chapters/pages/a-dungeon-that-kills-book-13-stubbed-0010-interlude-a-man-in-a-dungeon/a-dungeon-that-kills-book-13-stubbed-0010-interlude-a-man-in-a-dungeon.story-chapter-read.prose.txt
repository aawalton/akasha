How had it come to this?

Ekon let out a long, weary sigh as his gaze drifted across the cramped stone chamber—his current living quarters, damp and cold and devoid of any comfort. He saw the narrow bed, he saw the barred window, he saw the foul-smelling bucket in the corner. The place was indistinguishable from a prison cell.

Because it was one.

Technically, he was not locked inside this room. He could wander the corridors if he ever wished. But, in the end, all it meant was that instead of being confined to a single cell, he had an entire prison to roam at his leisure. And he failed to see how that made anything better.

Even now, after everything that had happened, he still couldn’t decide if fate had been kind to him or just cruel in a different way.

On the one hand, he had been saved. If the Sovereign hadn’t intervened at the very last moment, the masked men would have captured him, and he would almost certainly have suffered a fate worse than death. Also, he had done what Anastrasza requested, and that should have marked the end of their agreement. By all logic, he should have finally been free. He should have been able to leave Daelin together with his friends and return to some semblance of a normal life.

Except things had not unfolded that way at all.

To begin with, the one who had saved his life was a mysterious being who called himself the Sovereign, which meant he now owed him a debt. Someday, a repayment would be demanded of him.

But that was the small problem. The real disaster began the moment Anastrasza decided to speak again.

She had been silent for a good long while, only to become unexpectedly talkative after the Sovereign appeared. Then, she casually told him this was the very master of the dungeon she had asked him to find in the first place. Such a revelation had shocked him so badly that he reacted without thinking, blurting it out loud before he could stop himself.

Naturally, it caught the attention of the Sovereign, who immediately demanded to know how he got that information. One thing led to another, and he ended up becoming the intermediary between the two powerful and dangerous entities, relaying words back and forth like an unwilling messenger trapped between forces far beyond his understanding.

And that, without question, was the worst mistake he had made in his entire life.

Because by acting as Anastrasza’s spokesperson, he had learned many things he would have preferred not to know.

As it turned out, the masked men who had been pursuing him were actually members of a secret organization known as the Children of the Abyss, and the Abyss itself, the great chasm of the southwest, was apparently not just a landmark, but an ancient, monstrous existence that had controlled the world from the shadows for thousands of years.

And the Valiant Bank served the Abyss as well.

And the Abyss was involved in the Dark Emperor’s death.

And one of the legendary Six Heroes was an agent of the Abyss all along.

What?

Seriously, what?

The more he listened, the more his head throbbed from the unbearable weight of information being forced down upon him.

Then, the last revelation dropped.

Anastrasza said, as though she was just discussing the weather, that she was the Eternal Flame, the sacred divine fire worshipped throughout the North for countless generations.

At that point, he genuinely did not know how he was supposed to react anymore.

Yet, the true dilemma only arrived at the very end of the conversation, when the Sovereign offered him refuge within the dungeon.

Every fiber of his being screamed at him to decline the offer. He wanted nothing more than to leave at once, to run as far away from the Sovereign, from Anastrasza, from the Abyss, from every horrifying secret he had just learned as humanly possible.

But then he realized: where could he even go?

Anastrasza would still live inside his head, the Sovereign would still hold his debt, the Children of the Abyss would still want him captured. And, after learning so many forbidden truths, he doubted he could ever truly remove himself from this conflict, no matter how hard he tried.

So, in the end, the only remotely rational choice available to him was to stay close to the monster powerful enough to scare off other monsters. As terrifying as he was, the Sovereign was perhaps the only one who could protect him from the enemies now lurking in the shadows.

Of course, he was not naïve enough to believe such protection was free. Sooner or later, he would have to do something in return. Still, for the time being, this was the best option he could hope for.

There was a knock on the door.

His lunch had arrived.

Ekon let out a quiet breath. This was one of the very few things he still looked forward to in this glorified prison. More important than the food itself, these delivered meals were his only reliable way of keeping track of time in a place where there was no sun, no moon, no changing sky. And above all, it provided—

—companionship.

“Come in,” he said.

The door creaked open, and a familiar figure stepped in with a tray of food.

A raven’s head sat on top of a human body, a pair of black wings folded behind the white robe. This was the one who had been attending to him ever since his arrival in the dungeon, bringing him meals without fail three times a day. His full name was Hakuro-no-Kanemitsu, though he had told Ekon to simply call him Haku, just as the Sovereign always did.

When he first met the bird-man, he had admittedly felt a bit uneasy, as he recognized Haku as one of those who had descended from the sky to abduct Dagnar during the chaos of battle in the sun-scorched courtyard. However, after several conversations, he had found the bird-man was surprisingly approachable, and he had become the closest thing to a friend Ekon had in this forsaken place.

“Here,” Haku said after placing the tray onto the narrow bed, reaching into his robe and pulling out a thick book. “Being stuck here alone all day has to be dull, so I thought you could use something to pass the time.”

“Thank you. Was this brought from outside the dungeon too?”

“No. It’s from the third floor. You can borrow it for as long as you want, but make absolutely sure you don’t damage it even a little. Lord Sebekton wouldn’t be pleased about that.”

So they even have a library down here, Ekon thought, letting out a quiet sigh.

In truth, when he engaged in conversation with Haku, he had another motive besides the desire for companionship, one that was more pragmatic and less noble. He saw this as an opportunity to extract whatever fragments of information he could about the Sovereign and the mysterious dungeon he controlled.

It was not that he had suddenly become eager to seek out forbidden knowledge about the world. Of course not, after everything he had already been forced to endure. Nevertheless, he believed closing his mind to all information was unwise. Some of it was absolutely essential. After all, if he intended to rely on the Sovereign to protect him from the Abyss, and perhaps one day even help him get rid of Anastrasza, then he needed to understand the nature of this being and the extent of his power.

Haku had been cautious at first, skillfully avoiding sensitive topics and redirecting conversations whenever Ekon edged too close to anything important. But as the days went by and they spoke more often, the bird-man’s guard began to ease. Ekon, in turn, had learned not to ask questions outright, instead making casual small talk and allowing Haku to occasionally let things slip on his own. Sometimes, the bird-man even drifted into long-winded ramblings, and all Ekon had to do was nod along without interruption. Thanks to that, he had finally begun to grasp the true scale of the place he was currently living.

As it turned out, beneath this floor, the second floor, existed more than two dozen others descending deeper into the unknown, each one ruled by a powerful Guardian who had pledged allegiance to the Sovereign. In other words, the bandaged undead and the monstrous crocodile-man he had previously encountered were merely two among so many lieutenants the master of the dungeon commanded.

Also, the massive structure that dominated the landscape of the desert—which he had recently learned was actually a tomb—was apparently insignificant compared to many other constructions hidden further below. On one occasion, Haku had mentioned a so-called colosseum existing somewhere, an arena so gigantic it could supposedly hold one hundred thousand spectators at once.

Ekon genuinely struggled to imagine what possible purpose such a ridiculous structure could serve inside a dungeon. Then again, history was filled with stories of great kings who commissioned absurdly extravagant projects simply because they possessed the wealth to indulge themselves. So if the Sovereign could casually have something like that built for pure entertainment, then the amount of resources he had at his disposal was obviously far beyond anyone’s comprehension.

They chatted with each other for a while. Eventually, Haku gave a polite nod, then left the room as quietly as he had entered.

Once alone again, Ekon finished his meal in silence, and when he was done, he stacked the empty utensils back onto the tray and slid it outside the door. Then he lay down on his bed, one arm resting across his forehead. He was going to take a nap.

And that was when the all-too-familiar, unwanted voice rang out inside his head.

“How are you doing, my dear Ekon?”

Damn it.

His jaw clenched as his eyes opened again, staring up at the cold, hard ceiling. He had always detested that voice, but that feeling had become something even more complicated now, twisted by a new kind of fear.

Before, he hated her because she lied, because she manipulated him. Now, he was afraid of her speaking the truth, of her casually dropping yet another terrible secret threatening to shatter his worldview once again.

“What do you want?” he grumbled.

“Just checking on you. To see if you’re comfortable with your new, well... home?”

“I’m fine. Thank you. Now go away.”

“I can tell this room leaves much to be desired. And while I can’t exactly change reality for you, I can make you dream of that little haven of yours again. Personally, I find that dirty shack no different from this place, but to each their own, who am I to judge? So if you want it, just say the word.”

“No, thank you,” Ekon said immediately. He had no desire to ever go back to that creepy world again.

“Don’t be like that. I’m offering you something for free this time instead of, you know, making you go through another deal. I was being so nice, and this is how you’re treating me?”

Why won’t she shut up?

“On your high horse, are you?” Ekon muttered. “Everything went exactly as you planned, didn’t it? Damn it... How the hell did you even predict the Sovereign would show up at the Dark Emperor’s old castle anyway?”

“Because he is the Dark Emperor, silly.”

...What?

Ekon froze. His mind simply stopped working altogether.

Then, slowly, he nodded.

Yes, that makes sense.

To be honest, when he heard Haku talk about the dungeon, he had still been a bit skeptical, thinking the bird-man might have exaggerated certain details. There were even moments when he thought that Haku might have been pulling his leg.

But now, all those doubts had completely vanished.

This dungeon, and the man behind it, were truly terrifying.
