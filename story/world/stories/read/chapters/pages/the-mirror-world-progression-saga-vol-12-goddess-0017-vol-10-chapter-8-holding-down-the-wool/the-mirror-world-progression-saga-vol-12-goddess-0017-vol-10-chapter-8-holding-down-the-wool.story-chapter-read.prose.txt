Samantha returned to the dungeon and delivered the news to ‘The Shapeshifter Known as Nyomel’ that she could go about her business and dedicate herself to her current role as the Naugite. Only she couldn’t, as they needed to cover for the ‘punishment’ she was supposed to receive for her disobedience to the goddess, i.e., why she was in the dungeon in the first place.

That obviously couldn’t officially be because she was a shapeshifting snake. In any case, the reasons could be private from a superior to a subordinate, but doing so with the punishment was less likely. They both had the same ready answer for what it should be: a fine. This was by far the most common penalty imposed on anyone who had even the smidgiest smidge of money to their name, and even if not, petty violations of contracts or laws would commonly saddle even a peasant or common soldier with debt to pay off.

Nyomel insisted on a hefty fine, which Samantha initially balked at, but Nyomel knew her weakness: she offered jewelry. Not a surefire ‘bribe’ when they couldn’t immediately be waved in her face, Samantha nonetheless caved after practically being begged to take shinies. Furthermore, Nyomel looked like a weight had been taken off her shoulders when Samantha agreed.

She feels she owes a debt, and this eases it on some level. Seems rather Naugite of her. In any case, it’ll just go right back into the war effort.

Washing her hands of the matter, she had various other crap to get to. The new thing to go on the itinerary — figuring out how to fuck up Matrazere’s wards — had other shit in front of it, but she felt it would be foolhardy to ignore Endless Templar’s advice. The entity was a bit exasperating, like most ‘deity-level’ types she’d dealt with, but she held ancient wisdom in that strange mind of hers, and seemed to have a strong connection to Fate.

The entity tickled her brain with mystery. She felt like she was missing multiple somethings. One that suddenly struck her was Endless Templar’s apparent memory. She didn’t talk about ancient things in that typical, forgetful, vague way. It sounded more like she was summarizing specific memories that were clear.

She’s special in some way, that’s for sure.

Shrugging it off, Samantha moved on — something for another time.

When she peeked on her captive in the Mirror Maze, Hestrae Beth, she was amazed for perhaps the dozenth time, as she found her still completely ensnared by the false reality Samantha had woven. The Goddess of Mirrors & Other Shit had gotten a lot better at fooling people within it since the early days. Her mind could partition a sort of semi-subconscious, continuous control that adapted quite favorably to keep the manipulation going. She didn’t have to spend time on it.

It helped that Samantha was a Grandmaster in [Acting], now, slinging out a vast menagerie of believable, fast-talking characters. There was no question that HB was weirded out by things, especially the ways people around her had changed, and the vast numbers of flat-out strangers in the mix, but it was at least partially believable due to the catastrophe of the Rivermount failure, the rampant death it had dealt, and the bloated scope of the Dominion itself.

Other than that… HB was quite a masterpiece of self-deception already. She refused to entirely face her suspicions or paranoia about the reality around her. She just threw herself forward, her ‘itchiness’ always relieved when she fought. When she killed people.

For the false reality, Samantha gave the Dominion more of a draw in the battle. Rivermount got ruined by the golem, which hadn’t been stopped except at great cost and far too late to save the city. The Dominion, meanwhile, exacted more casualties, and Palthennius had come out much better, which HB hated with a passion but also readily accepted as the norm to strive against.

HB’s days were consumed by the ongoing war as the forces clashed and skirmished instead of the Dominion bitching out and hiding behind Matrazere’s walls. Palthennius and her commanded different competing groups. This sort of thing, HB fell right into, her competitiveness fueling her.

Samantha’s ruse to obtain intel on her ability worked without a hitch: an Inquisitor showed up, grilling her about everything under the sun that had happened, and though she scoffed about answering things about her ability that they ‘already knew,’ she accepted it as typical of Inquisitor behavior. The very curtly-provided explanation of ‘leaving no stone unturned’ in exploiting her ability going forward in the worst war faced in centuries was good enough.

And so Samantha discovered that, indeed, HB had thousands of Dominion peasants sworn into the ability. HB knew that many had dropped dead during the final altercation. It was verified that nothing bypassed the ‘trade’ — the source was irrelevant. Physical, mental, and spiritual wounds were translated through the curse.

That’s what it technically was, too — a curse on those people, to die in her stead. This wasn’t how it was framed, but Samantha figured it out.

And she calls me a witch.

To ‘fix’ this arrangement, she’d have to locate all the people and lift the curses, which was probably impossible because they’d agreed to it. It was essentially contractual.

Samantha felt like she was kinda stuck with HB. She would not ‘go through’ a bunch of other people’s lives just to kill the woman. She wasn’t entirely convinced either way that she could hold her indefinitely in the maze, but she’d try. It seemed the best option.

The mirror she was connected to was taken to the prison Samantha had constructed west of Itha Ranon — Bahamas — and dumped into a deep, dark cell with enchanted, warded steel surrounding it. Just in case. Fortunately, she had no innate intangibility powers. She had not even tried anything like that again in the false reality, though she was quite sullen and morose about losing her armor.

To keep with realism, the Dominion provided her with new elites to swear into her ability and protect her. Samantha designed a mix of personalities to try and give her potential confidantes. It didn’t work very well. She kept them at arm’s length.

Another aspect of her depression seemed to be losing a certain person, so bad that she cried herself to sleep at times — her maid, Erise Halder. She’d asked after her multiple times, to which Samantha had to report she was ‘still missing.’ Increasingly, HB assumed she was dead or captured. The last she’d seen her was to send her with the escape personnel, but she suspected Erise’s personality was not to leave her, and she’d tried to hide somewhere, only to be killed or captured.

HB even sent letters to the opposing force, asking for hostage exchanges and a list of identities and occupations of those held. Though she avoided asking after Erise specifically, it was clear what she was doing.

Samantha struggled with what to do about it. She wanted a confidante for multiple reasons. Sanity preservation and prying more secrets out of the obscenely private woman. Her appearance was asked about, but what were the odds she could get someone so personal correct without knowing her?

So a search was ordered in the real world for one Erise Halder, lady’s maid. There was no immediate dice for anyone captured. Perhaps she’d been killed, but perhaps she’d in fact obeyed her lady and fled initially through the tunnels. In that case, she was back on the Dominion side. Unless she was tucked away in Matrazere’s fortress, she might be accessible; a maid wasn’t likely to be well-protected, after all.

At the very least, if she were found, she could be observed and learned about. Samantha didn’t rule out capture, though that did feel a bit nefarious. But it wouldn’t matter if she couldn’t be located.

Samantha did clarify the relationship, however. The probing questions of the Inquisitor infuriated and offended HB, but it either wasn’t a lover type situation, or HB denied it and was a vow breaker, because she cited her vow of complete chastity with a great deal of anger. She even insulted the Inquisitor as a ‘disgusting reprobate’ for so much as suggesting she would have ‘uncouth, abusive relations’ with her maid. That was literally the only time she went so far as to insult an Inquisitor.

She was so annoyed, and her status so relatively high, that she sent a complaint through other channels, asking the Inquisitor to be reprimanded and possibly investigated for ‘degeneracy of imagination.’

So, she was a damned warrior-nun with a completely pure friendship with her maid? Okay, then.

Samantha continued to punt her situation down the road. HB remaining neutralized was the key thing. She also studied her various magic items and even identified them, discovering that her general omnipotence in the Maze realm meant she could take the actual items, then simulate them…

Except for the sword. It was bound to her. With a thought, she could summon it back to her. Well, they’d figure something out, but it would need special handling, because HB would sense shit happening to it. Samantha could use her disabling ability on it, then switch it out, then… eh. She’d figure it out later.

That crazy armor of hers came through without a hitch, by way of the special effect that had stolen it. It was also a bound item, but that had been cleared by the special effect of the ‘honorable’ HB refusing to submit to the geas, and was ready for re-binding to someone else.

Though Dax wanted it given to Canmore, Samantha thought it was pretty obvious who earned it, deserved it, and needed it: Bast. Nothing was stopping his ‘caster’ type class from wearing armor under the rules of Calrenazzod, and intangibility seemed a great way for Mr. Reckless to continue avoiding death. Besides, what was more ‘unstoppable’ than phasing through all physical matter?

Just as she was going to give it to him, Tashome contacted her and insisted he should ‘impart and improve’ the item for Bast’s use. Though reluctant of the never-ending workload dumped on the man, she relented, unable to resist the idea of a powerful, legendary artifact getting even stronger.

The temptation of power is one hell of a drug, ain’t it?
