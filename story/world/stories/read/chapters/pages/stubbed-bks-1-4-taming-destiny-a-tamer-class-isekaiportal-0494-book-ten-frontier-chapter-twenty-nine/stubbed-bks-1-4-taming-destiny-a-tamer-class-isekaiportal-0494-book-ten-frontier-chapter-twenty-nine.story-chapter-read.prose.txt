“Looks like we’re the furthest north of all of the settlements,” I remark as we pore over the map that we traded with Gulnis for.

“Perhaps the merchant company just hasn't sailed that way yet,” suggests Valence thoughtfully. “There aren’t nearly as many competitors on this map as there should be.”

“You have a point,” I agree. I know that Jareon Flameform was intending on coming, and there were at least thirteen other competitors at the time I signed up, not all who have been accounted for. Kyrion and his retinue haven’t appeared either, though it’s possible they turned back for Moriax after our clash. “Maybe they were blown off course like we were.” Or dead. That’s also a strong possibility.

“Perhaps,” Valence agrees, though looks troubled, perhaps thinking along the same lines as me.

“We’ll just have to keep an eye out for them,” I say. “Captain Sor’az went north – hopefully he’ll be back soon with news. He may have found more colonies in that direction.”

“If he does, we’ll have something else to trade with the merchant,” Layton notes. He gives Elarion an appreciative nod. “Clearly your tactics increased the value of the growing method – he was practically willing to sell his ship in return for it.”

“Although Elarion’s tactics were certainly…effective,” I comment before Elarion can speak, “I don’t think that’s the only reason for Gulnis’s desperation.”

“You think that the other settlements are struggling for food as much as we were?” Valence asks with a frown on his face.

I nod, but before I can speak, Josiah leans forward, also frowning.

“In that case, we allowed the method to go too cheaply. We could have become the new bread basket of the Lost Continent.”

“And if our trading lines were ever cut off, we’d be responsible for potentially hundreds of people starving – just because we were unwilling to share our discovery,” I reply pointedly.

Layton makes a small noise of disagreement.

“It’s more likely that spies would have been deployed to steal the secret. Eventually someone would have learned it.”

“All the better to have sold it first, then,” Valence comments, eyeing Josiah challengingly.

The lord of Glimgrove leans back in his chair.

“I still feel we could have sold it more dearly,” he grumbles. “A secret is not like most wares – it can only ever be sold once. And the more who know it, the lower its value is.”

Layton makes an exasperated noise.

“Then you could have asked for something more during the actual negotiations,” he exclaims. “As it is, it is too late now.”

“Personally, I’m happy with what we have gained,” I cut in with a firm note in my voice. “I know that new metal is currently a bottleneck for expansion – hopefully this new influx will alleviate that a little. And although the herbs aren’t quite as necessary, they ease the pressure on the alchemists to replace their entire recipe book all in one go.”

I shrug. “Honestly, I would have been willing to give the secret away for free – with what we now know about the local inhabitants, we need to band together to ensure that our attempt at colonisation doesn’t become merely a footnote in history. Helping the settlements improve their food production is just the first step in that. As it is, we’ve gained resources and some valuable information. Now we need to work out how that information changes our approach to diplomacy in this region – the main objective of this meeting.” I look slowly around the table, meeting the gazes that are fixed on me.

Josiah subsides, Sarran gives me a nod, Valence and Layton look at me expectantly. Elarion is the only one who remains studying the map, as he has during most of the discussion.

The map shows a section of continent, with our settlement being the most north-eastern point. Gulnis apparently has some sort of cartography Skill and was able to draw the map freehand with both confidence and artistry. I hope that the accuracy is as good as he promises, but even if it’s not perfectly to scale, the amount of new information is useful.

Though the coasts are far more detailed than the inland areas, the map goes as far as the range of mountains that extend further than the eye can see in both directions, roughly parallel to the coast. From what I can see of the map, the distance between coast and map increases as they go further south. The closest mountain is the same one that we can see from where we are – a lone mountain almost entirely detached from the rest of the range.

Directly between the mountain and the coast is a massive oval shape that is the forest of magical trees. They stand between us and our nearest neighbours who seem to mostly be hemmed in between the forest, the mountains, and a large river. Interestingly, that has its source in the same mountain as our closest river – Tilathis’ territorial seat.

“Who wants to bet that this is the origin of the massive network of roots that you detected?” I ask, glancing at Elarion while tapping the magical trees marked on the map.

“No bet,” Elarion replies grimly.

Valence makes a curious noise, tapping a small green flag with a black axe emblazoned across a yellow tree. It’s right next to the magical forest, inland at the end of a small river that runs into the forest.

“I don’t recognise this flag.”

“House Woodfell,” two voices speak at once. Layton and Elarion exchange glances, then Layton gestures for Elarion to go ahead.

“They rule Forestedge in Crownseat,” he explains, a faint twist of his lips betraying distaste. “Their primary export is lumber. It is unsurprising that they would attempt to corner the market of magical lumber.” He leans into where Gulnis has written the name of the settlement and snorts disparagingly. “New Forestedge. How…creative.”

I hide a smile as I realise the source of Elarion’s dislike – they’re competitors.

“How about these two? I don’t recognise them.” I tap a blue flag with a white mountain and a black bow that’s decided to go inland for some reason, due west of New Forestedge, and then another one that’s bright green with a white tree on it, a sickle in green nestled in the tree’s white canopy. Unlike the blue flag, the green one is on the coast, at the mouth of the same river as Woodfell has used.

“This is House Hunter of Stagrun,” Layton identifies immediately, tapping the blue flag. “They’re a small House – I’m a little surprised they’re in this competition at all.”

“I’ve met the current heir – Thayne,” Valence responds. “He’s obsessed with killing beasts more powerful than he is. And from what I know, his mother was as bad when she was the heir. They probably aren’t interested in the crown at all – it’ll be the powerful beasts here that they want.”

“That makes sense,” Layton agrees. “Sandend’s had several deliveries of potent monster parts from them – though always at unpredictable intervals.”

Valence nods.

“Thayne goes on regular hunting trips – into Nethyar sometimes. It seems that this time he’s gone rather further afield.”

“Alright, hunting maniac, noted,” I interject. “Hopefully they don’t bite off more than they can chew. What about this green one?”

“It belongs to House Greentoe,” Elarion says firmly. “They rule over Orchard in my family’s territory. I’m a little surprised to see them here – they didn’t indicate that they wished to take part in the expedition.”

“Perhaps they feared that if they expressed interest, they might be obliged to join your retinue,” suggests Valence tentatively.

Elarion nods at the younger lord, conceding the point.

“Given that they know where my family’s…loyalties lie,” he agrees, flicking me a telling look, “they may have wished to be independent.”

I clench my jaw, but nod stiffly in acknowledgment of his theory.

“I recognise this one,” I say to move the conversation on. I point at a black flag with a white mountain depicted on it, a pickaxe emblazoned across it in red. It’s placed on the southern side of the wide river that connects to Tilathis’ mountain.

“Everyone recognises that one,” Josiah replies with a wrinkle to his nose. “Lord Rockfall has a habit of making himself heard during every senate meeting he attends.”

“Which is all of them,” Layton adds, and the two cousins exchange long-suffering glances. From what I’ve heard, their exasperation is well-earned – when Lord Rockfall takes a position, it’s almost impossible to move him. And since he’s the leader of the bloc of Houses that export mining products, that makes him a rather important voice in quite a few matters.

By reading between the lines of what the merchants did – and didn’t say – I gather that Rockfall’s main objective is to make it safely up-river and monopolise the metal deposits they have identified as definitely being present in the mountain. That would usually be a problem for us – the best we have discovered in the area around the settlement are some poor-quality iron sites. The poor quality is of little concern – Blacksmiths have Skills specifically designed to improve the quality of their creations. The quantity is more of an issue. But in this case, we have something that he needs.

“Though he didn’t mention it, Gulnis and his partners were apparently thinking about a white Beast King that is apparently keeping Lord Rockfall’s expeditions from doing more than surveying the land,” I muse.

Some might consider it cheating to have my telepathic leopard companion listen in on their thoughts and send me what she could interpret. I just call it using my advantages wisely. Unfortunately, Kalanthia would have probably scared the merchants too much so I had to give the task to Lathani who’s a bit less practiced than her mother. At least Kalanthia was willing to help me throw the merchants off-balance. And it’s paid dividends if my guess is correct.

“You mean…” Valence asks, his eyes wide.

I nod.

“That’s my guess, anyway.”

A sudden sharp bark of laughter erupts from Josiah’s direction.

“Forget my complaints about selling the secret of growing Moriaxar plants here too cheaply – this is what we should leverage.”

“I entirely agree,” Valence emphasises, leaning forwards. “Ores will be essential for the development of the colony. If we can gain a controlling interest before Lord Rockfall even manages to get close….”

“I’d rather organise an agreement between our Houses,” I disagree. When I see several mouths open to argue, I hold up a closed fist. “I recognise that controlling the mines would be more profitable, but that would risk Rockfall trying to kill Tilathis. While he’s Kalanthia’s mate, and is at least neutral towards us, we’ll need to stop that happening. So, as I see it, it would be better to have an agreement with Rockfall – access in return for ore.”

Though Layton and Valence look thoughtful, Josiah doesn’t appear convinced.

“They are better suited to large-scale mining operations than we are,” Elarion muses.

I nod at him gratefully.

“That too.” I tap on the map at the next two settlements – their position very close to each other and on the north bank of the river mouth, opposite that of Rockfall. “Ultimately, I just want to make sure that we don’t end up dependent on these.”

One of the flags is the solid yellow with three differently coloured circles on it that represents the merchant’s guild. Almost directly next to it is another – one that’s very familiar. It bears a shield like my own does – black on a gold background with segments showing three stalks of the Moriax version of wheat, a set of scales, and, below all of it, something that looks like a large sand cat.

“Goldmine,” Layton identifies thoughtfully. “I assume it is no coincidence that her flag is so close to that of the merchants. They must have come as part of her expedition.”

“Most probably,” I agree. “And that they’re probably operating under her auspices is just another reason not to rely on them too much.” Elarion and I exchange a look, then I turn back to the map. “Which leaves only one party we haven’t yet identified. Anyone recognise this flag?”

The final flag is bright orange with a circular pattern that looks a bit like the VW logo in dark blue, underlined by a scimitar-like blade in an identical shade. It’s far to the south, about as far away from Rockfall’s settlement as is Woodfell’s. Even the land looks distinctly different down there – the ever-present forest has given way to plains of grassland on an area of land that’s high above sea level, cliffs separating it from the rest of the map’s area.

The others lean in, but no one immediately jumps in with a name. And, as each of them lean back with a shake of their heads, or a quiet ‘no’, the mystery grows.

Elarion eyes the merchants’ flag.

“Thinking about it, was there any limit on non-nobles joining the colonisation as their own parties?”

That leads to a thoughtful silence.

“I don’t believe so,” Josiah is the first to reply with a thoughtful look on his face. “Only in joining the competition.”

“Then perhaps it’s another merchant party?” Valence suggests.

Layton makes a noise of disagreement.

“A non-noble party would have fewer devices – and no weapon unless they were a mercenary guild. In which case, that would be the only device on it.”

I swallow as another explanation seems most likely.

“Perhaps it’s the flag of one of the native groups,” I suggest quietly.

Everyone goes still, the atmosphere becoming heavy.

“Then we must prepare to make contact with them sooner rather than later,” Elarion replies, his tone level, but concern in his eyes.

I nod, then lean back in my seat, my thoughts racing.

“So’raz should probably be back soon,” I muse. “After he returns, we should see if he’s willing to scout down south and get more information about all of these settlements. If not, maybe we can send another ship – perhaps with Nessie as a guard.”

“That would impact the fishing,” Josiah warns.

“Hopefully that will be offset by the new food production,” I return. He glances at Elarion thoughtfully, then nods and leans back.

“Reasonable.”

“In the meantime, natives or not, we have three main priorities that we’ll need to focus on concurrently. First, stabilising and growing our settlement here. We’re already well on our way to doing this, but there’s still a long way to go. Second, exploring this lead on metal ores. And third, reaching out in diplomacy to the other settlements.”

I glance at my watch. It’s late. We’ve been talking for quite a while.

“Instead of talking about it all now, why don’t we call an end to the meeting and go and think how we can each contribute to the three objectives. Then we can chat over breakfast or dinner tomorrow and combine our ideas.”

That meets with unanimous approval. In the chaos while everyone’s getting up and exchanging words on different topics – some relevant to the earlier discussion, some less so, I make a subtle gesture to Sarran to remain behind. I find myself gazing thoughtfully at the map. Somehow, seeing so many flags brings the challenge home to me. Until now, we've been building in relative isolation. Looking at the map, I realise those days are already coming to an end.

When the room is clear, Sarran approaches me, curiosity in his eyes.

“Markus? Why are my instincts telling me to be wary of whatever you’re up to?” He asks accusingly.

I give him an innocent smile that just makes his eyes narrow further.

“I just wanted to ask a favour.”

“A favour,” he repeats flatly.

“Yes. You see, Loran has Evolved, and I need someone who can better gauge his new stats to evaluate him.”

I’m gratified when Sarran’s jaw drops.
