Ernie gasped, nearly shoving Aida as he rushed to his wife’s side.

Agnes was beginning to stir, her thin eyelashes fluttering. She took in a shaky breath, her chest lifting more than they had ever seen it.

“My light! You’re awake.” The old, nearly decrepit man, who had just moments ago held himself so tightly, so carefully, to prevent himself from cracking in front of guests, was now draped over his wife, shoulders heaving as he sobbed with heavy relief.

“What’s going on?” Agnes rasped out. She fell into a violent fit of coughing, making Ernie grab her hands, as if he was terrified she would pass out again.

“I’ll go get something for her to drink,” Levi offered, backing out of the bedroom. It had been cramped before, but now that Agnes was moving again the room seemed absolutely suffocating.

“You’ve been asleep for eight days,” Ernie said brokenly. “I didn’t know what to do—but then this young lady, she did something and woke you up.”

Aida backed up as Ernie stood up to face her, his face shining with tears. “Thank you so much.”

“You’re welcome,” Aida said uncertainly. She didn’t know if she was the direct cause of Agnes waking up—it certainly didn’t feel like it. She Detoxed the woman, sure; but just because she pushed a button didn’t mean it was the cause of a miracle.

“Pardon me, warm tea incoming,” Levi said behind her. She squeezed out of the way, letting Levi approach to proffer Agnes the fourth cup of tea Ernie had set up earlier.

“We’ll wait outside,” Aida offered as Ernie held the cup for his wife. Her hands were still unsteady from disuse.

“Thank you,” Ernie repeated, trying to bow his head at the same time as he tipped the cup for Agnes to sip. “Don’t go yet; please wait for me.”

The two of them edged out of the room as quickly as they could, trying to give Ernie and his wife some privacy.

Aida sat back in her bamboo chair blankly, trying to figure out what had happened.

Agnes’s awakening had happened too abruptly; one moment, her life force was invisible. The next, it was there, as if it had never left. As if Aida had merely overlooked it.

There was no slow growth of Agnes’s spirit awakening from deep within her body. There wasn’t even any sense that her spirit had returned from somewhere—like how Professor Bruce had said he was able to detect Ezra’s soul in the vicinity of his body.

Am I just too untrained to be able to have felt her return?

“Good job.” Levi pressed Aida’s untouched tea into her hands. “You saved someone.”

She sipped, letting the brew settle itself over her tongue. Light floral sweetness, along with the milky, warm taste associated with tea leaves. Completely harmless.

Swallowing, Aida looked up at Levi. “Did you feel her come back?”

Levi cocked his head.

“I mean, she wasn’t there. At all. And then all of a sudden she was. It’s like… I skipped the frame where her spirit comes on screen. There’s no continuity.”

Bronze eyes blinked in bafflement. “I don’t really understand your analogy.”

Aida sighed. “Maybe I just don’t have enough experience. But did you think her awakening was odd?”

“You mean, odder than this whole situation in the first place?” He shrugged. “I really can’t say.”

Of course. How do you evaluate an outlier of an outlier? Aida dropped her eyes back to the cup in her hands.

“Be careful, honey, take it slow.” The two of them looked up to see Ernie supporting Agnes through the doorway. “You’re doing great! Almost there…”

Levi stood up instinctively and began pulling furniture aside, clearing a wider path for Ernie and Agnes to the sitting area where she finally settled into a seat.

“Here, drink some more…” Ernie fetched his own cup of half-drunk tea and quickly refilled it before raising it to Agnes’s lips. She drank greedily, her fingers clinging loosely to her husband’s hand.

“I wanted to thank you two again for helping this foolish old man,” Ernie said, looking between Levi and Aida gratefully as he dabbed at his eyes with the back of his wrist. “I’m grateful you didn’t just walk away after my pathetic plea for help.”

“How are you feeling?” Aida asked Agnes.

“I’m feeling better,” she said shakily, her voice hoarse. “This man—my husband, he told me a little bit about what happened. Thank you for saving me.”

Aida felt something tingle on the back of her neck at the same time she felt Levi stiffen beside her. Surely that’s not how you talk about the person you’ve spent most of your life with.

Nodding carefully, Aida looked at Ernie to see if he noticed the oddity, but he was so preoccupied with keeping her cup full he didn’t seem to notice.

Maybe it’s just an aftereffect of being in a coma.

“Do you know your name?” Aida asked gently.

The old woman’s gray eyes flicked briefly upwards, as if she was trying to recall a distant memory. “Agnes.”

“Do you know where you are?”

“I—I must be in my home.”

Aida nodded encouragingly at the old woman’s response, though something still seemed off about the way she answered.

But I’m not a medical professional… am I just reading too much into it?

“And do you remember your husband’s name?”

Agnes looked immediately at Ernie, and Aida caught a brief glimpse of something that unsettled her far more than her previous hesitancy. “His name is Ernie.”

Ernie beamed back at Agnes, wetness at the corners of his eyes.

“I’m glad to see you’re doing well,” Aida said. She looked at Levi, trying to signal that she had a lot of questions. “I think we should get going now. Ernie, do you mind—?”

“Ah, let me walk you out,” Ernie said quickly, clambering up from where he was crouched beside his wife’s chair. He dropped a quick, yet tender, kiss on her thin gray hair before he gestured towards the door. “Please.”

Ernie walked the two of them out of the alley maze to the main road in silence, punctuated by heartfelt sniffles from the old man.

Finally, he turned to them with an air of formality, looking at the two of them with his hands pressed to his heart.

“I want to thank you again. I regret I don’t have anything I can give you in exchange for all your help and time—but perhaps I can return the favor someday. I don’t have much, but I am skilled with the needle and thread. If you have any clothing items you need repaired or altered, my door is always open. I have a small stall in Buddington Market you can drop anything off at.”

“That’s not necessary,” Aida said. “But I wanted to ask—does Agnes seem… normal to you?”

Ernie blinked quizzically. “Normal?”

Aida looked at Levi, unsure how to frame her question without offending the old man. He looked back helplessly.

“I guess this is a bit of an in-depth conversation,” Aida said hesitantly. “But… I was curious how your relationship with Agnes was.”

Ernie chuckled. “Well, we’ve been married for fifty suns now. It wasn’t perfect the entire time, but we’ve worked through enough troubles to have a strong and loving relationship.”

He gave a grandfatherly smile to the two of them standing in front of him. “I suppose that’s why I felt comfortable reaching out to the two of you. You remind me of myself and Agnes when we were your age.”

Aida and Levi studiously avoided each other’s eyes, trying to appear untroubled by an old man’s preconceptions.

“That’s good, I suppose,” Aida said awkwardly. “Um… the reason I asked in the first place was because Agnes didn’t seem…”

“It may just be our misinterpretation, since we don’t know how your relationship is, but she didn’t seem to be quite as… loving… as we would have expected from someone you’ve been married to for fifty suns,” Levi said carefully.

Ernie waved a gnarled hand clumsily. “Oh, goodness, I’m quite embarrassed. We don’t normally show our affection so overtly to each other, especially in front of strangers. I was such a blubbering mess because I really thought I was going to lose her—even in my old age, I haven’t learned enough from my previous regrets to avoid new ones. Take it from me, life is too short to hold back.”

He wagged his finger sternly at the two of them after imparting his life lesson, though his face was crinkled in a smile. “Now, if you don’t mind, I’m going to head back home and put action to my words. I will spoil my heart with all that she wants.”

Waving cheerfully at Aida and Levi, Ernie hurried back into the cluster of buildings, leaving the two of them standing there forlornly.

“You saw it, right?” Aida finally asked. “She doesn’t seem like she loves him. Or even thinks of him as her husband.”

“Yeah, contrary to what he’s saying, it seems like she thinks of him worse than you think of me,” Levi said casually. He elbowed her. “You heard the elder’s wisdom. Don’t try to play it cool and create a lifelong misunderstanding.”

She elbowed him back, sticking her tongue out at him before looking back down the alley Ernie had disappeared into.

I hope we’re just overthinking it.
