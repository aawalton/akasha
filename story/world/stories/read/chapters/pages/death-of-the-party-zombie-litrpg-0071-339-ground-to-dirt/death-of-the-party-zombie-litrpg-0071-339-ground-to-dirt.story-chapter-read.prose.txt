Dark tendrils of energy snaked around the impact area as a shadowed figure picked himself up from piles of bodies.

“Not quite a last-minute entrance,” Sally said with a wide grin. “Things are only starting to get worse.”

Hunter doffed his hat toward the zombie before turning his attention to the surrounding war. “I don't know about that. Things are looking mighty dire as it is.”

“We are just a decoy force, or something.” She waved her hand at the space where Chuck used to be. “I'm about to reposition while the real army gathers its senses. Was worrying we wouldn't see you.”

The shadowed man adjusted his white glasses. “Well, the Sea didn't take too kindly to me destroying a planet. The only reason I made it here was the sudden spike in energy. A distress signal?”

Sally frowned for a moment. “Ah, it must have been from the bugs changing their System to the STAR+, if that’s how you were tracking me.”

“Sounds about right. Doesn't look like it's doing much for them.”

“We gave up our ability to respawn indefinitely. We’re slightly outnumbered, but that just makes for a target-rich environment, right?”

Hunter pulled out his revolver and spun the cylinder. “I'll need time to restore my full power, but I can still kill.”

“Music to my ears.” With a grin, Sally found a place on the map to teleport to. Her undead army vanished with Endless Rest+, before the pair also disappeared in a flash of blue.

Sanctuary's Champion still stood, albeit barely. Although his health wavered close to zero constantly, the basic Radoch troops had been unable to get through his defenses enough to kill him.

Eventually, there were too many corpses for him to move. Humphrey strained against the press of dozens of roaches, until he was pinned in place. Energy running low, he succumbed to the needling damage and expired.

Theo's Aspect form had lost a foreleg, and was partially blind. The elite Radochs had some manner of flak cannon, powerful enough to shred away at his dragon bones. One shot was all they were able to get off before he tore through them.

Rather than seek out better alternatives, he turned to thrash about the horde of plain roaches. Claws tore them apart. He gulped down droves in his sharp maw. Deathly energy radiated from him, burning away at life itself.

He was a wild animal. A force of nature. Unreachable.

Ren shelled the freshly deployed armies. Each empowered crystal soared through the air from atop the tower, traveling several miles before exploding on their target. It was a bucketful of death against an ocean, but each strike was taking out powerful Radoch technology.

The collector ship had safely landed on one of the new landmasses in Sanctuary - the appropriately named Tropical Isles, connected by a partial land bridge to the Forest area. It became their base of operations, and a gathering point for their several fighting fronts.

Only one person had eyes on the island now. Chuck watched as the collector ship spewed forth threat after threat. Now that they were part of the same System, he could see what everything was, even if he couldn’t directly manipulate things under Radoch control.

The more elite roaches were out in droves, setting up pylons around the Isles. Technology that prevented teleportation around the area, among other things. That ruled out one of his plans. Taking out the leadership with an unexpected drop of all five Outsiders would have been nice, if only to sow discord into the remaining army.

But they looked far too organized.

Time was not on their side. Fighting head-to-head was just treading water, and any miracle required his full attention. With a sigh, the Architect closed his eyes and dove deep into the System, allowing a projection to keep an eye on the Radochs.

Sally waved her hand, casting Endless Dead+ from her tome to bring back her undead army.

“Almost two hundred zombies,” she noted. Plus Big Joe and her squad of dwarves. “It seemed like more.”

Hunter glanced up at the pyramids. “What is our plan here?”

While this area of the Wastelands had clearly seen some combat, it was now deserted. Two dozen corpses lay strewn around the sand just outside of the pyramid perimeter. None seemed human or Radoch.

After producing a golden-hued potion from her Inventory, Sally grinned at the shadowy man. “I’m going to stand here and cast Zombie Apocalypse until my body can’t take it anymore. Then, it’s dinnertime.”

“There’s certainly a feast out there. What sort of numbers are we dealing with?”

She shrugged. “Half a million, maybe? I stopped tracking the numbers once I saw Theo was using his Aspect. Such a try-hard. Oh!” She clicked her fingers. “You get to meet my better half soon. I’ll even give you a tour of my village, assuming it survives this war.”

“Peachy. I assume you don’t need me to babysit you, though?”

“No, but I don’t want you running off and getting overwhelmed either. I’m not sure what rules you play by, bub, but if I can’t give you items and you don’t have abilities… you’re a bit of an oddity.”

Hunter nodded. “In good company, it seems. Perhaps you can give Archie a call, and I can keep myself busy elsewhere.”

Archie looked away from his System screens. “Excellent news, for a change. Hunter will soon be on his way.”

Max wiped his brow as several Radochs fell over dead. “The roaches seem attracted to power, and we’ve already brought several scouting groups our way. Not that I am turning away a proficient hand, but I wouldn't want to drag him into a losing battle.”

“Worry not.” The wizard shrugged, but didn’t elaborate further.

From what information Chuck had been feeding him, several other plans were now out the proverbial window. The strongest Radochs were still living and functional, and the world ran thick with hundreds of thousands of the weaker ranks.

The Architect had several last-ditch emergency plans, but the trouble was he could only focus on one of them. A choice had to be made, and it may affect every life on Sanctuary. He didn’t envy the man.

A flash of blue light illuminated the top of the structure where the pair stood. Hunter adjusted his hat and gave the two a nod. “We meet again.”

Archie smiled. “Good timing. This is Max, one of the Outsiders. We could use your abilities.”

After shaking hands with the magician, Hunter turned his attention to the tall stone platform they were standing on. “Keeping Sally and Sanctuary alive helps me fulfill my purpose, as self-serving as that sounds. What do we have going on here?”

The wizard tapped his cane on the platform. “The Radochs seem attracted to powerful people. This is what you’d call bait.”

“Hmm, a trap.”

“Not exactly.” Archie waved a hand dismissively. “Regardless, this is my current role. The two of you would be better suited elsewhere, although I’ll admit I’m not the best tactician.”

With the Architect preoccupied, he could only reach projections. They weren’t exactly helpful outside of their given roles. Sally was busy growing the biggest undead army she could. Theo was making a beeline for the collector ship, leaving a trail of death in his wake. What few defenders remained, led by Dent, were holding a literal fortress against constant waves of the aliens.

“Perhaps it is best you aid the last stand of our Adventurers,” he eventually concluded. “The Radoch numbers are still too great for individuals to make any sort of impact.”

Max stepped away from the edge of the platform. “I’ll ask Ren to join us once her Aspect runs out of energy. You already have several splinter groups making their way here, so I hope you are ready to entertain guests.”

“I’m sure I can create a path for my destiny.” Archie held out a hand. “Allow me to teleport you both to Gladstone Fort.”

Dent swept his light sword through the air, leaving trails of red energy behind the weapon. Sweat soaked through his clothes, burning at several healing wounds. Crossbow bolts bounced from the stonework around the gateway into the fort. Raising the drawbridge was no longer an option, and Radoch bodies filled the emptied moat surrounding the fortified stronghold.

It was only a matter of time before the roaches broke through the chokepoint. Most Adventurers here were injured or exhausted from constant fighting. Some of them weren’t even strong enough to be going up against these bugs individually, let alone an army.

As he slashed through the next bug, one to the side slid past him and put a halberd halfway through the woman fighting beside him. Another Adventurer moved in with a shield to keep the line, but they were being pushed back by inches every second, and losing lives along the way.

Perhaps that was it for this life. Dent adjusted his grip on his sword and prepared to use one of his Guardian abilities to earn a little breathing room.

A ray of darkness shot down from above, from the ramparts. It struck past the wave of Radochs and struck the burgeoning drawbridge. With a sharp crack, the bridge broke, disintegrating.

Dent stumbled back onto the solid ground of the fortress interior as the roaches fell into the remaining moat space. Purple cards flickered through the air, cutting down several bugs trying to clamber back up to the Adventurers.

A burst of confetti preceded Max’s appearance, and the magician gave the swordsman a brief nod before sending his cards out into the milling crowd outside.

“You’re a sight for sore eyes,” Dent said.

“I wouldn’t breathe easily just yet.” Max offered an apologetic grin. “Upon seeing how surrounded this fort is, the best Hunter and I can provide is some camaraderie as we march toward the grave.”

“He made it here, huh? Shame Chuck is otherwise busy.”

The magician summoned a cannon in between them, pointed toward the Radochs struggling to reach the open gateway. Occasionally one would fall, decaying to dust after being blasted by a thin beam of dark energy from the top of the fort.

“You’ve held their attention for a while,” Max said, as he pulled on a pair of white gloves. “But an audience such as this needs something really spectacular to stop them in their tracks.”

As the cannon blew a cloud of confetti out onto the clambering roaches, Max vanished, only to appear in midair - higher even than the fort itself.

For a split second, he took in the scenery. The nearby forest burned. Grass churned up, replaced by bloody soil. Tens of thousands encircled the small fortress like ants spiraling around a cube of sugar. It was both overwhelming and delightful.

Aspect of Chaos ran through his body, illuminating his limbs with sparkling purple light. While Max radiated like a sun, twinkling stars fell from the sky, drawing the attention of the huge army below.

Of course, that was their mistake.

Blinded by the intense glow, each observer received between one and twelve random status effects. Those that weren’t Stunned or Blinded witnessed a massive top-hat emerge from the purple sun. Six long arms stretched out, each with a spiral of arcane cards circling around it. His powerful voice thundered through the area, shaking the ground.
