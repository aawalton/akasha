Chapter 190 / B3-35: Gone to the Dogs

Waking late next morning, Joe found half a dozen notes had been slid under his door. The first one he opened was from Myllo stating that Dorian Barrburrow would meet with the other aspirants at 4-Bells-Day, or 3:00 PM, as Joe still thought of it.

Added to the meeting details were two small notations. The first was that they were keeping the details of Glen Cumha under wraps for the time being. Myllo penned that Joe should refrain from talking about Margen and the Spring King until further notice.

The second directive was that formal dress was expected. Joe was not sure what constituted formal attire, and Yuk, who had pooled himself into a bucket by the window, would almost surely be of no help in that category. Some time this morning, he’d see if one of his other friends could give him some advice.

He had spoken with Mercy Suku during their run last evening, and she had promised she would treat Margen to the best of her ability. Joe was a little concerned that she had any doubts about being able to remove the mythic mummy rot, but he assumed that no physician wanted to give absolute assurances, even a centilevel saint. Her note stated she would attempt her healing of Margen at sunrise tomorrow, and she wished for him to attend. Joe would certainly be there.

There were two invitations from prominent families in Fort Coral, inviting Joe to join them for dinner at his earliest convenience. These, he was unsure about. He had had a bad experience with the nobility of Duskrug, and so was leery of meeting face to face with anyone of similar station here in his new home. Then again, Fort Coral was far more laid back than Duskrug was. He’d ask one of his friends about what they thought he should do or not do before answering.

The most ornate envelope held a letter from the Paladin of Blu, Lady Yalaeth Blackgrove, who commended Joe for his bravery and for returning to Fort Coral the legendary lost founder. It was her sash that Joe had used. She explained how the relic was on loan to the guild, and surprisingly, it was powered by the very god Joe had had so many issues with back in Duskrug, Phealti. Turned out that the God of Order was still a bit judgmental over Joe's odd nature, but it seems he was willing to accept him enough to lend aid. Joe sent a small prayer of thanks to Azbekt, wherever he was. The dwarf’s sacrifice may just have been the thing that allowed Joe to don the white silk relic.

The last message was from the Fort Coral Harbor Master, Auggux Hasthauk. An akhlut pod was being a nuisance in the bay. Since the beasts had not yet attacked anyone, and one such creature had received notoriety during the Cauldrakon event as a guilder’s companion, the harbor master had held off calling for a hunting party. For now. If Joe wanted to keep the akhlut’s from being forcibly expelled from the harbor, he had better rein them in as soon as possible.

“Ah, man. So much for taking it easy today.”

As he was still out of cleansing charges, Joe had to settle for a quick wash. He would have preferred to fully clean off the sweat and dust from last night's run, but that would have to wait until after dealing with Finn and the rest of his pod. He scribbled a note for Yuk, who was still non-responsive, and grabbed Mo, before jogging through Seaside to reach Netting, the fishing district. He left the cù-sìth within the medallion for this first part of their trip, as they had garnered a fair amount of attention on last evening's run. Once the akhluts were squared away, he’d let them out.

As he approached the shore, it was obvious that the orcaish chimeras were being full-fledged assholes. Swearing sailors were holding up shredded nets, their catches having either been devoured by the ravenous brutes or just let back out to sea for the fun of it. He could feel Finn was enjoying the hell out of his antics with his pack of seahunters, but as Joe reached the wharves, it was clear the ahklut prime was quickly making enemies out of those who had sung his praises a little while ago when Cauldrakon’s crabs were the ones wreaking havoc.

‘Hey! Stop that!’ Joe had the wildness project towards Finn to give the command a bit more oomph. The biggest of the brutes stopped swimming and popped his head above the waves. With a huge toothy grin, the chimera leapt sideways through the surf and made a beeline straight for the dock Joe was on.

In Finn’s mind was a proud sense of accomplishment, since he had kept his packmates from sinking a boat and eating any of the fishermen. With a sigh, Joe had to concede that small victory to the big guy. Finn had remembered that Joe had told him not to break the houses or eat anyone during the battle after the legendary tempest. Messing with their catches was not against any of the rules Joe had laid out for him.

After the whale-skinned brute hopped up onto the pier, causing the logs to creak under the ton of akhlut, Joe thumped his hand against the big man’s shoulder. “Good boy,” Joe praised.

“What ya mean, good boy. Is you daft? Them bastards have ruined today’s catch,” an angry voice snapped from around Joe’s knees. Joe looked down to find a dwarf wearing a large, odd leather hat. It kind of looked like a jester’s cap with three pointed sections, but it was a dull gray color and lacked any ornamentation, like bells.

Auggux Hasthauk: Dwarf: Mariner/Tempest-Rider/Roger Scourge 46

“You think ripping through our nets be something ya praise?”

“That’s my fault, sir. I only told him not to eat any people or destroy any houses. I’m pretty impressed that he translated ‘house’,” Joe stated, pointing at the buildings further inland, “into ‘boat’,” he added, switching arms to indicate the sailing vessels. “Even though we haven’t gotten to the finer points of property damage yet, I’m damn proud of him. He is a wild beast who, in one lesson, learned the biggest things he needs not to do. And he got his whole pod to follow him on this. That damn straight is praiseworthy,” Joe declared, quickly following it up with a “Sir,” just to give the harbor master respect.

Auggux ran a hand down his salt-and-pepper beard before huffing, “Well, when ya put this that way, I’ll give it to em. I was a mite surprised them damn louts hadn’t rammed any of them boats. If'n yer gonna have them around, though, they gotta behave. I can unruffle the fisher folk this time, but that ain’t gonna last long if they are spending their coin on nets and not bringing in any catches.”

“How about as an apology, I pay for today’s losses. What ya think it will cost to mend the nets and pay for a day of lost fish?”

“That ain’t necessary,” the dwarven harbor master began, only to be interrupted by Joe.

“A pretty huge deal will be announced today, and because of that, I’m about to have a pretty amazing windfall. I can afford to drop a hundred gold for goodwill and to give the fisherfolk a reason to celebrate today instead of being pissed off.”

“YER THAT JOE?” the man barked. Joe realized that the Harbor Master would surely be a member of the city council, and so he knew of Margen’s return already. “I know yer about to get paid from the One Above, but that don’t matter. Yer coin ain’t no good here today. I’ll cover the cost and be happy to do it. Just wanna shake the hand of the man who brought him back home,” Auggux stated, thrusting out his hand. The way the dwarven captain said ‘him’ was with a low conspiratorial tone, as if he too was not supposed to let the founder secret out of the bag yet.

Joe grabbed the man’s blocky hand and gave it a good shake. “Thank you, sir. I’ll start sorting them out now,” Joe replied, dipping his head towards the chimera-filled bay.

“Good idea. If ye can get them louts over to Selkie Cove for the time being, I’ll deal with the folks here.”

“Sure thing.” Joe projected the wish for Finn to get back in the water. Once that happened, he used the ring of the Grimwald's Legacy set to teleport onto Finn’s back, appearing just in front of the akhlut’s dorsal fin.

[Cavalier's Seal] (Finger - Uncommon - Set: Grimwald's Legacy) If you are in Close range of your animal companion, you gain [Automount] and they receive the benefit of any helpful buff you apply to yourself. {Leadership / Pact}

He could have used the [Talisman of the Medic], but the seal had advantages in this case. First off, Joe did not have to lock eyes with a specific location. Also, he did not jump to that spot with his feet on the target. The [Cavalier's Seal] allowed him to appear already seated, right in the best spot for him to ride the big brute.

The only difference was poor Mojo. With a surprised squawk, the shug monkey found himself suddenly standing on nothing but air five feet over the pier. [Slipstream] allowed him to turn his fall into a glide down to the logs. The dirty look the changeling shot off elicited a chuckle and an apology from Joe. The seal allowed Joe to share effects with his animal companions, so he could probably take Mo with him on the automount. He would have to remember to include the pup in his desire to teleport next time.

With another mental prompting, they took off, skimming through the waves heading west. The shoreline of Netting ran from the sandy strand of Warder’s Beach to the Point. The latter was at the west end of the coastline, where cliffs rose up to the High Park district. Once around the Point was a small inlet known as Selkie Cove.

The rest of the pod followed their leader to the quickly emptied lagoon. This area was a good spot for both oysters and the clay the Bracey’s used to make glass. There were a few people here for one or the other of those, or just to swim in the calm waters; all of whom decided to find somewhere else to be with the arrival of a dozen or so one-ton seahunters.

In the middle of the shallow cove was a small island, not much more than a bugle of sand with a pair of palm trees. It was a favorite spot of the bayers, so much so that it had a hammock strung between the two palms and a semi-permanent firepit. Joe brought the pod there. He planned to get into the finer points of Coral Bay etiquette in a bit, but first, he wanted to introduce the fey hounds to Finn.

Joe summoned forth the three cù-sìth, feeling both his and Mojo’s expectant excitement only to have everything go horribly wrong. The spectral fey hounds took one look at the akhluts and immediately their hackles went up, teeth were bared, and angry snarls emerged from phantasmal throats.

The reactions from the orcaesque beasts were equally as hostile. The two-tone voices of the akhluts lost all their trilling notes, leaving only the deep chest-rummbling throb of their growls. Finn stood his ground, but the rest of his packmates began to advance on the ethereal hounds of Annwn.

“Whoa. Whoa. Whoa! Everybody stop!” Joe yelled.

Your skill [Pack Master] has increased to rank 17.

Joe’s spirit-based skill slammed down over the two combative packs. Whatever their beef was, Joe was not going to have any of it. The fey hounds yielded without question. The wild chimeras resisted for a brief moment, but Finn's assent to Joe’s demand tipped the scales fully.

Everyone stopped and settled, eyes locked on others.

Even though both sides were no longer actively preparing for battle, Joe could feel the waves of animosity pour through the connections to his companions. Except Mo, who was utterly baffled.

‘No idea, bud,’ Joe thought soothingly to the little guy. ‘This did not go at all according to plan.’

Joe tried to understand the sharp emotions flowing off the aklhuts and the cù-sìth, but as far as he could perceive, it had no explanation. It seemed to be an instinctive loathing that each group had for the other.

It wasn’t until Joe flipped to [Witch Sight] that he finally found the first clue. Joe, looking at Finn, could see the constant hunger that was always churning within the brute like a constant ache, but this time, he finally looked close enough to view the curse itself. The mass of red irritation shifted to a purple hue, becoming a weave of a million threads.

Examining one of those strands, Joe found that it looked a whole lot like something else he had just seen. The curse looked like a line of poetry.

Just like the death bane the Erlking had painted across his scattered trove, the writhing violet threads inside Finn were clearly fey in nature. Yet if the death bane was a clever bit of verse, the deep-seated tendrils woven through Finn were an entire aria. The level of complexity was a hundred times more intricate. It was not splashed across the sea-beast the way it was with the dirk, either. This hex was stitched through every bit of Finn’s form, most heavily concentrated in his gut.

Joe checked the other akhluts and found they, too, were cursed with the exact same bane. Actually, they were worse. The purple threats inside Finn were thinner and had a pale hue compared to the dark plum strings inside his podmates. The akhlut prime’s connection to Joe diminished the severity of the hex. The other beasts did not have that bond to ease the bane.

Returning the hounds to the amulet calmed the situation down, but the damage had been done. Neither Finn nor the wild akhluts were in any frame of mind to develop the right habits regarding the harbor.

Joe asked the big man to keep his kin out of the harbor for a while. At least until Joe called them back. He did not want Finn to head too far away. He wanted to study the curse, to try and figure out a way to break it if he could. This would allow the akhluts to finally have some peace from the endless hunger they had been inflicted with.

He thought back to the mural in the hall of the Erlking, the one where Wen had told him the story of the chimera. She had said that the winter Queen Morrigu was the one who had cursed the akhluts and the rest of the chimeras. That frieze had gotten the Mark of Death all worked up.

Maybe this is what he was supposed to do with the whole Aspirant of Spring thing. Joe still did not want to get involved in politics, but ending a centuries-old torture on every chimera would be a goal worth pursuing.
