The tower, always seeming to rise up right out of the sea, gleamed in the sun in the distance, with islands scattered around it. Estara, flying on a Pegasus for her first trip to Itha Ranon, smiled ear to ear. It was beautiful, in so, so many ways.

She’d come somewhat the ‘long’ way to Geirkos, as her mother was preoccupied and couldn’t do the mirror maze trick to make it nigh instant right to Itha Ranon. She was more than happy for the chance to see the tower coming from over the sea like this, in any case. Though certainly a necessary and desirable pilgrimage on multiple levels, the practical matter was a temporary need to fill in for Orswyth as the head of the entire church for a while. That was increasingly better done from Itha Ranon and Geirkos as the combination central nexus of the order.

He’s here, but elbow deep in another project.

Her eyes noted part of that already ongoing, right next to the tower, in painstaking stonework being done. A new extension of the tower. This would house a giant crystal yet to arrive by ship, which the catfolk and their mysterious deity would provide. It did something a bit unusual: absorbed divine source energy, once attuned to a Domain. The ways in which it could be filled were just one issue. Orswyth was working on what it would be used for, once it was…

She landed next to the cathedral, as that was the first thing she wanted to see. At the top of the steps leading up to it, just in front of the entrance, her sisters waited, wearing attractive dresses, one blue, one red. Ana and Ash.

It was the first time they met physically.

Estara could feel the gravitas build with each step up to them. A powerful alignment was growing. Somehow, she knew it would be like this, and somehow it still struck her in surprise. The expressions of her sisters watching her approach said they were feeling the same thing.

It seemed as if reality was spinning faster around them, like all the chaos of the world was tumbling more and more, with every step. It was like spiritual music. Though a bit terrifying, she couldn’t stop wanting more — could no more turn away than wind pulled into a storm. This crescendo, this sign, this image had to be realized and completed…

When she finally reached the top of the steps, the violent maelstrom rapidly abated, leaving utter serenity. Balance. All was right and all would be well.

Smiling in happiness to be in each other’s presence once more, the three sisters held hands, making a triangle. Forging an alignment and becoming in tune with one another in a way no other could possibly understand.

They well understood this moment as an omen for the world of Calrenazzod, and a hope to pass on, even beyond, etched into reality forever. One that never failed. A gateway was blown open once more, to make way for the end.

So they were destined to lead every soul in existence to it, and one day…

One day, the necessity will finally be relieved. Even if we aren’t remembered? It’s alright. We made our contribution to Fate.

Ana smiled at her. “But I still hope we are!”

Wondrous Implement hustled and bustled through his day as usual, helping clients, ordering around lazy imps and employees, and scribbling new invention ideas in Notebook #447, which was getting near to full capacity. There were also too many notebooks, yes, yes, too many. He technically had #2-200, minus a few missing books, compiled into a helpful, pared-down compendium of only twenty volumes. Not for the first time, he chided himself for not getting around to Compendium 2, but he was so, so, so very busy all the time, it would be a terrible drain to do it-

Wait… didn’t I already start that? What did we get to? Bah! Confound it! I’ll have to check the logs… I did fix those errors on the search function, didn’t I? Gah. I hope so…

He was interrupted by a messenger imp making itself very annoying in his immediate geometric field-of-view, flapping around and gesticulating with a letter bigger than its body.

“Yes, yes, you buffoon, I see it!” Wondy declared in heated tones. “Now away with you! Throw it on the pile with the rest. I’ve certainly got more important things to do than-”

The gesticulation of the letter made the pretty, ornate words, written in sparkling gold upon the front, flash into focus inside Wondy’s mindspace, which caused him to freeze.

To: Wondrous Implement

Fr: The One Behind a Dancer’s Face

Just as the imp sagged and began to turn away, crestfallen, Wondy reached out and snatched the letter out of the Imp’s grip, causing the imp to spin through the air for a moment before righting itself and blinking in confusion.

“Give me that, you fool! You should’ve said it was from someone important right off the bat!” He began tearing the envelope to pull out the letter for reading. “I’m surrounded by idiots! Why I put up with you all, I cannot fathom…”

Ignoring the messenger, he anxiously read through the aesthetic, delicate script:

Wondy,

Though I felt a little hurt about what you withheld from us, I know you had your reasons. Things turned out well enough in the end. I’m not going to hold it against you.

I am going to call in your debt, however, as solidified in the scroll contract you gave into my possession. One free commission, and I fear I must request that it be your top priority, besides. I doubt it is an easy one. What I need is a ‘converter.’ Mana into divine energy, specifically attuned to [Light]. I have a crystal battery as a receptacle.

If you can do this, great. The acceptance seal is provided below, per your instructions. I regret that I cannot come to you in person. Delivery, I leave to your preferences. If further instructions are necessary, let me know.

Regards,

You Know Who

Wondy sighed as he read over it. He inspected the contract seal carefully etched into the paper, pressed in via precise and perfect magical lines. It was a sound rune.

A first-order Calrenazzod energy converter. It’s been a long, long time since I’ve thought about such things. I’ll have to relearn old principles. At least she’s chosen a divine aspect that is feasible for it…

There was no question he’d do it, so he immediately pressed the seal, locking in the contract and helping to empower both him and the task. As always, it felt good to reinforce his purpose. This one felt good on multiple levels, though. He could vindicate himself. Clear not just debt, but guilt.

Necessary or not, well concluded or not, I still betrayed her. I did as he wanted. Never again will I allow debt to him. We are done.

A customer tried to wave him down, but Wondy turned away, rapidly floating himself to the back rooms, ignoring everything else.

“I’ve got to find that damned notebook!” he exclaimed, feeling a sense of despair. He’d been unable to, even for the compendium. He’d given up. But he would not give up this time. He’d need it to complete this commission.

I’ll tear the whole place apart if I have to! This is something I have to do. I know it in the core gears. To not be forgotten in a grand work…

“One must simply play their part in supreme excellence, no matter how small…” This, he spoke only deep within the framework, as the small, metallic voice of a very, very, very old, decrepit creature, almost entirely, but not quite, a clockwork machine. A cyborg. A fusion of contrary energies, particularly in Shadowset.

A few moments later, he was barking orders left and right, telling everyone to drop whatever they were doing to begin the greatest re-ordering and cleanup he’d ever conducted. One employee looked at him in shock and horror as he declared that, indeed, the store would close.

“Yes, you fools!” he yelled, thrusting his hands up emphatically. “All else has become secondary to finding the notebook! Whoever finds it will get…” — he paused for the sheer gravitas — “a day off.”

The great lot of them stopped and breathed in a collective gasp, utterly awed.

Wondy raised a finger. “No! It’s even more important than that! Not just a day — three days off!” He gestured with three fingers in emphasis.

Deeper gasps, somehow. An imp fainted.

Tashome drummed his fingers on his desk, scowling as he stared at the item in front of him, kept in a rectangular box of solidified blue energy. A containment field, more or less. He’d been studying the contents — a sword — carefully for a long time, poking and prodding it on the peripheries of its aura. Now, he was just staring at it.

This bloody thing… How in the hell? How?

He became aware of someone clearing their throat. He blinked and looked up to see Uriel there, glancing down at the sword and back to Tashome.

“Yes?” Tashome asked.

“Uh…” Uriel rubbed the back of his neck and looked back down at the sword. Winced. “They kinda asked me to check up on you…”

Tashome frowned at him. “They?”

Uriel gestured upward vaguely. “The whole shebang, Tashome. The Brain Trust? Jeeves, in particular, I suppose.”

“Jeeves.” Tashome sniffed. “He could just ask himself…”

“Yeah, that’s the thing — you’re ignoring everyone while you’ve been researching this thing. No responses.”

“I have?” He blinked and realized he indeed had been consumed. He stared back at the sword. “I have, haven’t I? How long?”

“Almost four hours.”

“What?!” Tashome sat up in alarm, swallowing and checking the time. It caused a nasty, deep gut-punch feeling. Like that of a busy man who realized he’d dozed off. “Son of a fu-...”

He sighed and sat back, running a hand through his hair and rolling his neck. He glanced at the sword, then Uriel, finally throwing a hand up. “And I’ve got nothing.”

Uriel’s eyes widened. “What do you mean?”

“Nothing to show for four hours! I can’t modify this gods-forsaken piece of shit. It simply refuses to define itself to me, as if it were no more magical than a fucking chamber pot. Even if I wanted to pour my very soul into it, it would be like trying to pour water into a flat piece of stone.”

Uriel appeared just as surprised and confused as Tashome as he looked down at it. “Remarkable… Well. She won’t be happy to hear that. Isn’t part of the enchantment a restriction of the user? Doesn’t that mean-”

“Yes!” Tashome interrupted, voice a bit more raised than he wanted it directed at his compatriot, whom he deeply respected. He tamped it down and took a deep breath. “Cloudbreaker, the wondrous sword of the wondrously great and noble Palthennius, cannot be used by anyone other than him, and there isn’t a damned thing anyone can do about it. Full stop. This sword is a fucking paperweight to us.” He looked back down at it and grimaced. “Until the bastard is dead, anyway.”
