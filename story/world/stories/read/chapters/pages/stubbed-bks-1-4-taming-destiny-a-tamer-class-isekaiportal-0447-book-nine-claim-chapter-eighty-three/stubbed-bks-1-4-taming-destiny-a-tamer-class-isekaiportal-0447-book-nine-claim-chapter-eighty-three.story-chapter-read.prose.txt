Got you, I say to myself with tired satisfaction, wrapping another parasite in my mana. After a good deal of trial and error, I've learned that they need to vibrate for a moment before they detonate – and that if I wrap one tightly enough and quickly enough, it doesn't get the opportunity. That means I can remove it alive.

This was a major breakthrough. It's enabled the alchemists to test their treatments on a live parasite rather than just the remnants of one. The last I heard, they were preparing for human trials, with a healer on standby to intervene if the treatment starts dissolving the patient along with the parasite's network.

Pulling my consciousness out of the patient, I smile at him, his eyes slowly showing his returning clarity of thought.

"This won't hurt," I promise him, then numb an area on his neck and make a small cut.

Watching a red, slimy, baby-fist-sized parasite emerge from a cut less than a centimetre across will never stop being strange. Its ability to squeeze through flesh, ignoring what should be physically possible, is matched only by its ability to hide itself.

Once it's fully out, I take a special storage box from the bag beside me and drop the parasite inside, sealing it quickly. The boxes are designed to keep live creatures in stasis for tamers – usually used for eggs – but the supply we brought from Moriax has proven invaluable for handling these parasites.

"Here," I say, holding it out to Raul, the boy standing nearby.

The children who aren't in apprenticeships immediately useful to our current needs have been pressed into service as messengers along with those who had already had that role. My Bonds, though incredibly useful for quick communication, are sadly unable to allow physical transference of goods. Raul, having done this several times already, takes the box, gives me a quick nod, and scampers off toward the Alchemists.

Another parasite coming your way, River, I warn her. She sends me distracted acceptance so I don’t press her for information on how the Alchemists are coming on.

I straighten from where I've been kneeling beside the mattress and step back, allowing Misa – an older teenager – to move in and begin explaining what's been happening to the newly-freed patient. That's also become standard procedure.

I glance ahead, seeing a line of other feral patients waiting impatiently for their turn, fighting their restraints and growling like animals. A healer apprentice is prowling up and down the corridor – we ran out of rooms long ago – and keeping a watchful eye on them. That became standard after two cases where the person managed to get a limb free of their restraints. In one case, it was a petite woman whose hand was able to slip through the shackles – at the cost of almost tearing off her thumb. In the other, it was a strong man who was actually able to break the bar on the bed frame he was chained to.

Fortunately, it wasn’t long after that when we swapped to Lady Keshaka’s new design of restraints. The member of House Artifice, though usually reserved enough to make hermits look outgoing, has really come through for us. She took a design for enchanted restraints that can be thrown like a ball and react automatically when they hit the targeted beast, and modified them so they work on humans.

The first iterations had the patients arrive on their stomachs with their hands and feet bound up in a hog-tie behind them, but, upon request, she modified the enchantments so they bind the limbs in front of the person and with a little more give for comfort. They also now include a gag to prevent the person from hurting themselves or others.

Honestly, I’ve been humbled at how well everyone has come together to help – from young children to crafters, to mages, to fighters, everyone has been pulling their weight.

Sensing that my mana pool is getting a little low, I take a moment to recuperate, drinking some water and eating some meat from my Inventory to help me regenerate my resources and fend off my fatigue. I’d rather not use a potion – not only are they limited but I’ve already used a couple in the last few hours. I’ve also been using Willpower Leverage to boost my Wisdom. While most people have accepted Bonds so I can remove the parasite in their bodies, it still takes a good deal of effort to scour every trace of the parasite out of them.

As for those who have refused the Bond, they take so much more mana that I’ve been putting them at the bottom of the priority list. Fortunately, they’re few in number, but, as always seems to be among a large group of humans, there are always some who put their fears or prejudices above even their own benefit.

And it is against their own benefit – my techniques are considerably less effective when I'm working against resistance without a Bond, as Ker Himlan learned to his detriment.

In the end, it was of both benefit and disadvantage that Himlan refused the Bond – benefit for the healers; disadvantage for him. Apparently, watching me operate without a Bond was more useful for the healers than the next few sessions combined. Apparently my actions were more obvious with Himlan – which makes sense.

However, as a result of my clumsy attempts to pull the parasite from him, he has been paralysed from the neck downwards – only able to breathe without aid because it was harder to grab the parasite and therefore it travelled further. He was devastated when the healers informed him that they are unable to heal him.

Yssel explained it to me afterwards. Spinal injuries are tricky enough for even magic-wielding healers to deal with, and the way the parasite inflicted the injury makes it a worst-case scenario. The best they could do would be to do earth-style surgery to remove the biggest bits of the vertebrae shattered in the explosion and then use magic to regrow them. However, that wouldn’t heal the injury as the spinal cord itself has been severed and with the parasite’s remnants infusing it, their magic can’t touch that.

I might be able to help – my Flesh-Shaping is more flexible than any of the Skills they seem to have access to, but I could only do it with a Bond. Ultimately not accepting one is his choice. I'm doing my best not to feel guilty about it.

However, I’m grateful that at least it helped the healers learn how to detect and remove the parasite. It took more than a few more experiments, but they have now learned to detect the parasites with reasonable reliability and can remove them in the early stages of infection. The difficulties arise when the parasite's network has become extensive – the healers' techniques tend to be even blunter than my own, even without a Bond in place.

I’ve still been maintaining Bonds with everyone who accepted one. The Bonds give me a way to monitor for complications, though I’m grateful that, apart from a few secondary infections due to lowered immune systems and the way they were attacking people, there haven't been any of those.

It’s probably fine for me to release everyone’s Bond, but with my current Willpower, holding Bonds with this many non-Classers barely registers. I'm not even approaching my limit. If I were back in the Samuran world, I suspect I could Bind an entire village without straining these days. Here, I’d rather maintain them until I know they’re unnecessary. For similar reasons, the restraints have been staying on until those who have been cleansed of an infection have been monitored for at least a day to ensure no resurgence. And then I expect we’ll need to organise systematic check-ups to ensure nothing slips through the cracks.

As I take another swig of water, I hope idly that we can repurpose or recycle all the restraints – the blacksmiths have no doubt gone through a large portion of our metal stock to create all of these.

Thinking about other restraint measures, I reach out to one of those who has been essential for the organisation of everything.

Are you at a point where you can give me an update, Loran?

There’s a moment’s pause, and then I feel him turn his attention to me.

I am, Markus. What would you like to know?

How are the populace taking the lockdown?

Well enough, he reports. We have the Water Mages who didn’t go with Lord Softrain walking through the streets with guard parties, supplying safe water to everyone. They’ve covered most of the city by this point.

Water Mages – of course.

That’s good thinking, I praise, while privately thinking I should have had the idea. I can Shape water, for heaven’s sake. I haven’t tried creating it from thin air, but I probably could if I spent a bit of time working on it. And that water would be sure to be clear of the parasites.

I force myself to stop the self-chastisement. I can’t think of everything – that’s why I’ve delegated to competent people.

What about food? I check. Are the kitchens operating?

The kitchen staff have been checked, and those who were infected with a parasite were taken to quarantine. Some of them have returned – cleared by the healers. Other healers are going door to door, checking for infections and delivering food at the same time.

And all of those who are infected are being taken to quarantine? I feel his wave of confirmation. Do we have enough space for all those people? I worry, thinking about how few buildings we had free to begin with.

The Earth Mages have been creating extra buildings to house the infected, Loran assures me and I relax a little.

Good. Have there been any more uncontrolled feral outbreaks?

Not since we finished taking in all of those from Healer Thessalanor’s list who reported symptoms before the storm hit. The other infections have all been earlier stage.

That’s a big improvement. Slowly, I feel my stress beginning to ease. We haven’t won – far from it – but I feel like we’re slowly getting things under control.

Thanks Loran. Is there anything else you think is necessary to report?

I feel him thinking.

The last news I had from the wall was that they are holding and that things have been better since Lord Heatwave was able to join. However, it has been almost half a candlemark since I last had a report from them.

I’ll check in with them, then, I reply, accepting his implied suggestion.

Reaching out through the Alliance Bond, I ask whether Valence or Layton are available to talk. After a moment, I feel Valence’s attention turn my way.

What is it? he asks curtly, his usual politeness completely absent. Given the situation, I don’t hold it against him in the slightest.

I was hoping for a report if you have a moment.

We're holding and slowly whittling them down, Valence replies, his mental voice strained, but this Tier three keeps interfering. It's less aggressive than the others – less reckless. It keeps absorbing blows meant for the fodder. Until we get the numbers down, we can't risk sending a team out – they'd be overwhelmed. We will win eventually – we have the advantage in this battle of attrition, but more help would go faster. Can you spare any of the mages you’re currently using?

I'm almost back, Josiah reports through the Bond. When we return we can attack from the rear. I have a good number of mages with me.

Maybe you can link up with Elarion and work together, I suggest, considering the matter. You’d be stronger together – and the vulnerable farmers could shelter on the ship. Would that work with you, Elarion?

There’s silence.

Elarion?

No reply. A flicker of unease moves through me. Perhaps he's deeply focused on his investigation. I don't want to entertain the idea that he might have got stuck like Artio has.

What are those? Valence asks, the non-sequitur throwing me off balance. For a moment I think he’s referring to Elarion, but then I realise he can’t be.

What are what? I ask.

Markus, Valence continues, worry suddenly in his voice. I think your baby lizards have got out of wherever you were keeping them. There are six. No, eight of them out there.

My heart sinks inside me. There can only be one group he’s referring to. All that stress that had been easing suddenly ties my stomach in a knot again. Trouble was supposed to be looking after them!
