BANG!

The Ether Blade in Esther’s hand unleashed a colossal surge of energy all at once. It transformed from a blunt, unremarkable iron bar into a sleek one-handed longsword. What drew the eye was its peculiar design—a small mouth and a copper human head affixed to the center of the blade. Esther raised it and gave it a single flick. A bone-chilling pressure gnawed at Jinyo’s very being.

“Heretic Inquisitor.”

Through the Eye of the Sage, Jinyo could clearly see that the sword and Esther had already become one. The synchronization rate between user and Divine Punishment was as high as 98 percent—an extraordinarily high figure. This was going to be bad. Jinyo had no experience dealing with a Divine Punishment wielder. Fighting one was so notoriously difficult that people used to say: if you want to beat Divine Punishment, you need Divine Punishment of your own.

The gap between someone without Divine Punishment and someone who had it was immense. Jinyo possessed an Ether Blade—and a high-purity one at that, the Sonneteer—but the sword hadn’t chosen him, so he couldn’t access its Divine Punishment.

“Reveal your true form, demon.”

The red eyes Esther fixed on Jinyo shifted. Three crest-like symbols reflected across his corneas. Jinyo felt as though some power had enveloped him—no, worse than that. It was like being swallowed whole from head to toe. The sensation was sickening and suffocating, making him want to flee.

“Jinyo Southerncross. Male. Age twenty-seven. Suspect in criminal cases: one hundred and eleven counts of homicide. Seventeen civil cases. Charges of research theft resulting in intellectual property damages. Verdict: guilty. Sentenced to two thousand three hundred and ninety years of imprisonment, or execution.” The mouth on the sword moved, its voice impossible to identify as male or female. But it was genuinely spine-chilling—both supremely authoritative and utterly devoid of emotion.

Jinyo understood now why he felt so terrible. It was the exact same sensation as being interrogated.

BANG!

The power within Esther’s body surged explosively. This was no longer the level of an ordinary power practitioner. It was as though he had transcended Knight Tier 2 into something far beyond. Jinyo hadn’t even steeled himself against the enemy’s combat power, because the words that sword had spoken were like a nightmare he’d been trying to bury for years.

Esther detonated his power and closed the distance to Jinyo in mere steps. The sword, engulfed in the flames of justice that incinerated wicked demons to ash, swung straight at Jinyo, who had no room to run. He snatched up the Sonneteer—its Divine Punishment still unreleased—and braced for impact.

The collision sent shockwaves ripping through Jinyo’s body, throwing his internal energy into chaos. He staggered back to regain his footing, but Esther shuffled forward in hot pursuit, refusing to let his prey escape. The techniques Esther employed were ferocious, savage, and reeked of murder with every escalating strike.

‘Too heavy! The power, the speed—no, the elemental force too. It’s increased nearly tenfold.’ Jinyo had never heard of a Divine Punishment that granted its wielder this much power. He used the Eye of the Sage to probe the red blade in Esther’s hand, and the data appeared.

[Heretic Inquisitor]

[Attack Power: ???] [Magic Attack: ???]

[Conducts an inquisition on the target and increases the wielder’s power according to the target’s legal offenses. 10 years per 5 Attack Power. 10 years per 2 Magic Attack.]

What kind of insane Divine Punishment was this?! There was no upper limit. As long as the target carried a heavy criminal record, the power boost was limitless.

The Heretic Inquisitor had assessed Jinyo’s combined criminal and civil sentences at 2,390 years. In other words, while wielding the sword, Esther’s Attack Power increased by 1,195—and his Magic Attack, which affected spellcraft and technique-based Planar abilities, rose by another 478.

‘An absolute nightmare.’ Jinyo heard his own bones screaming. Waves of heat and the acrid stench of charring clothes assaulted him. The crimson blade carved through flesh, sending blood splattering. His handsome face contorted in agony, yet he couldn’t tear his eyes from his opponent.

If not for Nature Synthesis and the Realm of Paradise accelerating his energy recovery, Jinyo would have collapsed within the first ten exchanges. Esther, already formidable at his base level, became more terrifying than any demon once buffed by Divine Punishment.

“My Divine Punishment never lies. Even if you are human, your soul is too wicked. One hundred and eleven murders—got anything to say for yourself, demon?” Esther drove Jinyo into a corner through both blade and word. His sword never lied. Even if Jinyo was human, a human with blood-soaked hands deserved to die a hundred times over.

The sword Jinyo raised to strike grew weaker. His breathing was ragged, yet his eyes remained sharp and clear. He looked up at Esther, who was seething with fury. It reminded him of memories from just a few years ago.

“Your Divine Punishment hasn’t lied about a single thing, and I won’t deny the sins I’ve committed. But I’m not an evil demon. I’m just an ordinary human who once lost his way—the same as you.”

“The same as me? Don’t talk as if you know me.”

“Fair enough. But let me say one thing as someone who’s been through the fire before you: don’t cling so blindly to any belief that it makes you deaf and blind. See things with your own eyes first.” Jinyo found a flaw in Esther’s technique and launched himself out of striking range. He tried to sprint away to regroup, but after only a few steps, he slammed into an invisible wall.

Jinyo’s arm throbbed from the impact—no different from when Esther had crashed into the cottage wall. He threw himself into a rolling dodge to avoid the incoming slash. Esther pivoted his blade and slashed at the airborne Jinyo. The staggering force nearly knocked the sword clean out of his grip.

He could already see what would become of him if he didn’t act soon. Jinyo planted his feet and steadied himself, but Esther wound up and hacked at him like a relentless specter. Both Ether Blades clashed, throwing sparks—but it was Jinyo who was shoved backward by the sheer difference in power.

“Running is useless. No one escapes unless I release the Divine Punishment. Just like you can never outrun your own sins. The fate of sinners like you is to die by my hand. I’ve sent hundreds of human scum like you down to meet the judge of the dead.”

“You really are a bounty hunter, aren’t you? If so, you must’ve seen people like me before.”

“Who knows? Everyone the Heretic Inquisitor declares guilty looks the same to me. Serial killers. Rapists. Thieves. And I’ve never once felt guilty about killing any of them.” Esther spoke without a shred of hesitation. What one might call compassion for a fellow human being seemed to not exist for those whom the Heretic Inquisitor had condemned.

He believed in the power of Divine Punishment so absolutely that there was no turning back. No—given his age, it made sense. Esther was only about nineteen or twenty. How much life experience could a man that young possibly have? The look Jinyo fixed on Esther was one of utter helplessness.

“What if—hypothetically—among the hundred people you’ve killed, even one of them was innocent?”

“Doesn’t matter. If the Divine Punishment declares them guilty, I kill them. Better than letting the wicked roam free.” Esther’s answer didn’t surprise Jinyo. Those ruby eyes narrowed, followed by a quiet sigh. His heart felt so heavy that words failed him.

“Why are you looking at me like that?” Esther felt a pang in his chest. This vile demon with its terrifying aura—yet its eyes were gentle. Was this demon pitying him? In all his years, Esther had never received a look like this from a target marked for death. All he ever got in return was fear or hatred.

That’s how it was supposed to be. No one bore goodwill toward the person trying to kill them. Esther had worked as a bounty hunter for a long time, but this was the first time he’d been shown compassion—compassion he didn’t even understand what part of him warranted.

“Your resolve is terrifying. I wish I could be even half as decisive as you. But I can’t be like you. My heart can’t take it.” Jinyo opened his bag and pulled something out—a peeled potato, its pale white flesh exposed. A raw potato, uncooked and unprocessed in any way.

Even if Jinyo couldn’t release Divine Punishment, that didn’t mean he had no trump card.

“A potato?” Esther was confused. What was Jinyo going to do with a potato? A bad feeling crept over him.

Esther stomped forward, unleashing a barrage of strikes to prevent Jinyo from doing whatever he intended. Jinyo tossed the potato, letting the Heretic Inquisitor’s flames roast it. The potato split into two large pieces for Jinyo to eat in two bites. He grabbed one and shoved it into his mouth, teeth grinding it to a pulp before swallowing. His body responded instantly.

[You have entered Moonlight Status]

Jinyo’s eyes flew wide. Two forms of Planar overflowed from his body. He used Nature Synthesis to draw Spirit Essence from the surroundings, circulating Floral and Lunar Planar and channeling them into the blade. Sword Spirit seized control of the sword and brought it crashing against the Heretic Inquisitor. Esther could still block comfortably—but the weight behind Jinyo’s strikes had nearly doubled.

That wasn’t all. Jinyo launched into a continuous flurry of sword dances. Pink flower petals swirled and harried Esther’s vision. He tried releasing waves of heat to incinerate them, but the petals refused to vanish—they only multiplied exponentially.

Punishment Wave!

CRACK!

At last, the blade found its mark. Esther shuddered from an attack that struck his meridians directly. Numbness spread from his hand to his heart. He instinctively retreated—but that single misstep created a critical opening.

“I see it.” Jinyo poured power into his blade. The sword art Esther used had a flaw: it demanded continuous, unbroken execution of its forms. The moment the user hesitated or paused, they had to restart from scratch. The instant Esther flinched and stepped back a single pace was a golden opportunity the likes of which came once in a lifetime.

Jinyo’s Planar control wasn’t refined by any means—he’d only regained his powers less than a month ago. But the experience of countless battles had given him the instinct to adapt. The stronger the enemy, the calmer he grew. The more focused he became, the more Jinyo could make the extraordinary happen.

Sonnet of a Hundred Blossoms — First Form: Peach Blossom Dance

CRACK! Punishment Wave!

The blade, dense with dual-element Planar, slashed into the enemy. Though the sword was too slender to carve a gaping wound, the Planar pulse injected through it attacked Esther’s meridians directly, throwing his internal energy into disarray. Esther gritted his teeth and suppressed a scream, only to find that when the second strike arrived, the force hadn’t diminished—it had intensified.

Punishment Wave!

Two consecutive Punishment Waves. Jinyo had found the rhythm. With the Eye of the Sage’s aid, he could see the flow of energy and the meridians inside Esther with perfect clarity. All he had to do was find the moment a blind spot appeared—a split-second flaw in the enemy’s energy circulation. Strike that point at the right time, and the damage bypassed all defenses entirely.

Esther scrambled to circulate Planar and plug the gap, but his panic only opened a new one elsewhere.

Sonnet of a Hundred Blossoms — Second Form: Mirror Blossom

Jinyo vanished from Esther’s sight. He reappeared at the blind spot behind Esther and struck the opening.

CRACK! Punishment Wave!

Two consecutive Punishment Waves.

“Urk—” Esther coughed up a mouthful of blood. The Punishment Waves were too much for his body. He began losing control of the energy within him. Jinyo’s blade lashed him again and again. By the time he tried to steady himself and search for Jinyo, Jinyo was already gone. All he could see was an illusion of ten thousand flower petals.

‘Ah… I’m screwed.’

It was the Bewitched status!

Esther swung on instinct, slashing into what seemed like empty air. But it wasn’t empty—Jinyo was there, lunging in for the finishing blow, and Esther’s instincts had caught it just in time. He roared. Flames erupted across his blade.

Solar Flame Sword Art — First Form: Roaring Blaze

“Your technique has too much raw power. You lack the soul of a swordsman.” Jinyo neither dodged nor slowed his approach. Esther’s blade came down before Jinyo was even within range. Esther’s face went pale, his lips trembling—he hadn’t realized that his spatial perception had already been hijacked.

The two elements of Planar fused together in perfect harmony. This was the first time since Jinyo had learned this art that he felt absolute certainty he could execute it at 100 percent. He willed himself to find the moment when Esther’s energy was at its most chaotic—the one and only gap Esther had left wide open. Jinyo thrust the Sonneteer into that precise point, just below the heart, near the large intestine.

Wandering Butterflies — Double Impact!

The blade thrust and twisted so fast it was almost an illusion. Two incompatible types of Planar slammed into Esther’s meridians, nearly knocking him unconscious. The hand gripping his sword went slack. Esther clenched his teeth, refusing to yield. He would seize his fallen blade and counterattack—

But then—

A pale palm that didn’t look like it belonged to someone who’d done hard labor struck Esther square in the face. Its name was Wilting Blossom Palm—an unarmed combat technique of the Southerncross clan. Because it was such a pathetically weak palm art compared to those of other schools, even practitioners of the Sonnet of a Hundred Blossoms refused to use it. But it was remarkably useful in a situation where the opponent was regaining their bearings and there was no time to set up a proper sword stance.

Esther spun. Jinyo used the opening to snatch the Heretic Inquisitor from his grasp.

“Haah!” Esther drew a great, heaving breath. The pain from multiple Punishment Waves and the Double Impact was like a water balloon stretched to bursting. He watched Jinyo holding his beloved sword but felt no fear at having his weapon taken—Divine Punishment would not obey anyone but its bonded wielder. Jinyo, unrecognized by the blade, could not access its power.

Esther ordered his body to rise. It refused to respond.

What?

“The reason you lost comes down to one thing: you relied on Divine Punishment’s power too much. This sword is extraordinarily powerful—both its attack force and its ability to amplify your techniques. But your own mastery of combat arts is still unrefined. The only things truly excellent about you are your Planar and your Divine Punishment.” Jinyo tossed the Heretic Inquisitor back. The weight of the blade dropping under gravity landed squarely on Esther’s stomach, drawing a grunt.

“Why… are you trying to humiliate me?”

“I have no evidence to convince you. All I can do is prove I’m not a bloodthirsty killer. If returning your sword isn’t enough, we can go another round—but I’ll do the same thing again.”

“….” Esther lay there, breathing hard. His sword had been returned without him having to fight for it. But the feeling of total defeat—body and spirit alike—caused him to involuntarily release the Divine Punishment, returning the blade to its dormant state. When the wielder’s conviction wavered, Divine Punishment could not sustain itself. It was as though his own heart had accepted defeat.

“Thank you. I’d be even more grateful if you’re willing to hear me out.” Jinyo extended his hand to the young man who had tried to kill him. Esther stared at it with wavering eyes, but the decision wasn’t a hard one.

“Better than being killed, I suppose.” Esther grasped the hand and let Jinyo pull him to his feet.
