Asher gasped, clutching a hand to his heart as he desperately willed it to stop actively trying to smash its way out of his chest. His head swam as his incredibly high blood pressure suddenly dropped back to normal, and he staggered sideways, barely keeping himself from falling over with a hand on the wall. All that anger, all that random hatred that he only now realized had come out of nowhere, gone in an instant.

Some sort of… Rage element, Asher realized, growing angry at himself for not noticing sooner. That’s such a bull—

Startled as he realized his anger was already beginning to build once more, he slipped into the astral, taking a few deep breaths to calm himself down. The fresh waves of anger were already subsiding, confirming his initial theory.

An aura of rage, or something along those lines… What a horrifying skill. Six built the second floor to be incredibly durable, and then the forced anger prevents anyone from thinking of how to actually bypass it. Though I find it hard to believe my heart would freak out to that degree just from anger… maybe a Blood element on top of Rage, to forcibly increase blood pressure? And Sound in order to project his voice and taunt his enemies, or at least an artifact to do it for him. God, that’s one trifecta of elements.

Despite being safe from the Rage and Blood effects while in the astral realm, Asher could still hear Six’s voice. It was warbled and distorted from the split between realms, but it was still there.

“I’ll admit, you caught me off guard there with those bold claims,” Six laughed, not seeming the slightest bit concerned that Asher had vanished. “I’ve heard plenty about your skills, Reaper. I know you’re still there. I’ve also heard that you can’t keep your vanishing act up forever, which means sooner or later, you’ll have to return to the wonderful world of anger and hatred once more. Why bother drawing it out? There are so many things in this world to be mad about! Come on, we can be mad about them together!”

Good news, Six doesn’t know I actually can stay in here forever, Asher realized, letting out a small sigh before triggering his Ring of False Death. Bad news, I can’t exactly find the hidden ladder up to the next level without walking around, which will disrupt False Death and burn my energy. And I can’t do anything about listening to this idiot all the while.

“Dealing with people more powerful than you, that always makes me angry,” Six continued, seeming to love the sound of his own voice. “Have you ever thought you were the top of the food chain, only to be put in your place by someone far stronger? Nothing makes me angrier! Oh, other than lies! Lies are infuriating! Here you are, claiming to have bested the Head’s ten Knuckles, but anyone who knows a damn thing about the Head knows there’s no way in all nine hells that’s true!”

Rolling his eyes, Asher did his best to filter out Six’s ranting as the man continued to talk and talk. While he transitioned from discussing his hatred of lying to his anger toward underlings who couldn’t handle simple tasks and how the senior assassins were all jokes, Asher continued scanning the room, trying to rely on Secret Seeker’s primary effect to find any markings that might indicate a ladder heading up. Unfortunately, that soon proved impossible, and Asher realized he was going to have to return to the material realm once more if he wanted to figure out a way to the third floor. Steeling himself for what was to come, Asher dropped Astral Dip, interrupting Six’s rant about senior assassins as he appeared again.

“There you are! You can hold that skill for far longer than I expected!” Six laughed. The man’s shrill, invisible laughter caused Asher’s blood pressure to spike as he gritted his teeth.

It’s not you, it’s the skill, Asher told himself, doing a quick lap around the room even as Six spoke.

“Have you ever seen a person’s heart burst from sheer rage? It’s quite the sight, let me tell you! Sometimes I grow angry at the fact that I’m not as physically powerful as some of my underlings, but physical might means nothing in the grand scheme of things. The ability to control anger, to force others to feel how you want them to feel and dance to your tune. That is power. Power beyond what those pathetic worms would ever understand!”

Asher finished his lap, still doing his best to ignore Six. He would have summoned a Spatial Lock to try and block out the sound, but Six was somehow making his voice echo directly within Asher’s ears, at a far too-loud volume that just worked to stoke the fires of Asher’s anger even further. Before things got out of hand like they had before, Asher purged the effects yet again, shaking his head to clear the lingering anger even as fresh, foreign rage continued to bubble up within him.

That was the whole room, but I couldn’t find any sort of ladder upward! Either Six doesn’t have one at all and he simply teleports up there somehow, or I’m missing something.

Asher didn’t think Rage, Blood, or Sound would really lend themselves to a teleportation-based skill, which meant Six was probably relying on an artifact of some sort. Or, there was another trick at play here. Hell, with the Sound element in his back pocket, there was no guarantee Six was even on the third floor at all. He could be hiding down in the bar somewhere, concealing his class with a skill and laughing at Asher’s struggles right overhead. At the very least, he had to be somewhat close in order for his aura skills to take effect, which meant he wasn’t doing all this from across the street or half a mile away.

Asher’s Shard Detection skill wasn’t picking anything up from the room above, but there was every chance that Six was using a concealing artifact, just like he was. He’d certainly have the wealth to purchase one.

“You know, I heard a rumor that you had someone important to you,” Six said, causing a fresh spike of anger to shoot through Asher’s chest. “Oh, hit a nerve there, did I?” Six laughed. “You do know they’ll die sooner or later, don’t you? No matter how hard you try, everyone close to you will die, and it will all be your fault. I’ve seen it happen time and time again. The strongest of warriors always seem to think that their loved ones are special, but they’re just as weak and fragile as everyone else. One day you’ll be kneeling in a puddle of their blood, crying and wondering what you could have done differently.”

Asher purged the unnatural anger once again, slipping into the astral as he caught his breath. Six continued ranting about his mysterious ‘loved one,’ but now that his head was clear, it was obvious the man didn’t actually know anything. The Finger was being careful not to use any sort of details about them, not even referring to them as a man or woman as he continued to go into a rather descriptive scene about how they’d regret ever coming to know Asher and would inevitably end up cursing him with their dying breath.

Damn, I wonder how much time and effort Six puts into coming up with these speeches he uses in tandem with his Rage and Blood Auras… Regardless, I think I’m done listening to this idiot’s voice.

With a single thought, Asher fell through the floor, landing among the bar-goers down below. He was just barely able to make it to the edge of the room before he snapped back into the material, scaring the ever-loving hell out of three tough-looking folks playing a round of dice.

“Mind if I borrow this?” Asher asked, snatching an empty beer tankard before any of them could object. Walking outside, Asher nodded to the bouncer as he exited, heading a good thirty feet away before turning around and eyeing up the distance to the top of the third floor. Mentally thanking his past self for dropping two million shards on his Belt of Strength, Asher imbued an Eternal Mark into the empty tankard, before hurling it with all his might up at the roof of the bar.

The bouncer shouted something at him, but he ignored her, watching the tankard arc through the air far higher than he could ever hope to jump. At the apex of its arc, he activated Recall, twisting the very fabric of reality to move all of fifty feet to the side.

And forty feet straight up as he popped into existence beside the soaring tankard.

Pulling out his daggers, Asher dropped straight down, briefly noting how he didn’t pick up any of the mug’s momentum when he appeared. He and Samantha had practiced this maneuver with her arrows, but they always waited until her arrow hit something. He’d never really thought about trying it on his own. Honestly, he was so stupid not to have the idea first! What kind of assassin—

Asher frowned as he realized he was once again within range of Six’s Rage aura, only above the man’s position now rather than below it. He barely even had time to be affected, however, as he’d only Recalled about ten feet above the top of the roof. Rather than try to land on the heavily sloped surface, Asher slipped into the astral and fell straight on through, landing in a well-furnished bedroom of sorts.

The bar’s third floor didn’t contain much. There was a massive, luxurious bed that took up a good quarter of the room, where an equally massive man was currently lounging, puffing on some sort of cigar as he leaned back against a wall of pillows. Six, as Asher had no idea who else it could be, looked like he was around five hundred pounds of pure fat. There were currently two unconscious women in bed with him, both sound asleep and presumably excluded from his overlapping auras. Asher thought it was a safe assumption, as rather than attempting to claw each other’s eyes out, their naked chests were rising and falling peacefully as they slept. Beyond the bed, there was a gigantic armchair that looked like it had been created for Rulfar in his battle form, and a large assortment of wines and finger foods left out for Six to consume at his leisure.

Asher took all this in in an instant, confirming there weren’t any sort of traps or hidden guards he needed to worry about. He supposed Six’s set up made a lot of sense. The Finger’s aura skills penetrated the floor below, driving any attackers into a mindless rage, eventually causing their own heart to give out. And based on how little damage his dagger had done to the wall downstairs, the two rooms were probably highly reinforced and just about impossible to break into.

For anyone other than Asher.

Standing up from his crouched landing and turning toward Six, Asher tightened his grip on his daggers as he returned to the material realm. Six must have thought he’d run away, as the man took one look at him and nearly inhaled his entire cigar. Wheezing and coughing his lungs out, the man slapped at his own chest, choking as he looked at Asher in horror.

“What’s the matter?” Asher asked, waiting for the man to finish coughing as he pointed Crescent at Six’s heart. He’d already thrown up a Spatial Lock to prevent teleportation skills from working, so Six didn’t have any hope of escape. “Don’t want to try using your Rage Aura on me anymore?”

“Please… I can… help you!” Six begged, finally hacking up the cigar he’d inhaled as he stared at him with watering eyes. “You’re up against the Head, right? I have intel! I know things about his Knuckles nobody else does!”

“What, that they’re actually just a skill of the Head's?” Asher asked, chuckling as Six’s eyes widened even further in shock. “I guess it probably wasn’t too hard for you to figure that out, seeing as they have to be immune to your skills, huh? I have to say, I’m impressed you even had the balls to actually try in the first place.”

“I can get you shards, tools, whatever you need!” Six blubbered, his eyes darting around the room as if searching for a way out. “I practically run Dormaul, I can get you anything! Just tell me what you want!”

Rather than look at the man begging for his life, Asher looked past him, letting Judgement reveal Six’s past to him. The Finger’s aura looked like creeping, pulsing tendrils of red so dark they were nearly black. Even from the center of the room, Asher could see just how frequently Six stoked the anger and rage of his victims, making them hate the world and everything around them to the point of their own bodies giving out. It was a cruel way to go, and Six reveled in it.

Judgement - Lvl 20

“You’ve already given me exactly what I wanted,” Asher grinned, slashing out with a quick use of Distortion and slicing Six’s head clean off for good measure. If he truly did have the Blood element, then decapitation was really the only way to make sure he was dead for sure. Snatching up the Finger’s shards, he even went so far as to shove Six’s body into his rift, half so the two sleeping women wouldn’t have to wake up next to a dead man, and half so he could search it for artifacts later. He’d look now, but he was far too excited to waste any time on that.

At long last, it was time for Asher to evolve his final skill.

The Stage of Bleed awaited.
