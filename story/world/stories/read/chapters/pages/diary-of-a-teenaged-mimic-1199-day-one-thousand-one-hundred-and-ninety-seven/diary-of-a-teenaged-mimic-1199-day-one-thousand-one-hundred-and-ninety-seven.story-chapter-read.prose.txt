At any rate, I know I was kinda wrapped up in my own head thinking about how I try not to think of people as resources, and I think I finally realized why.

Pratchett. Granny Weatherwax, specifically. That bit where she's talking with the missionary, and she says that there's 'no grey, just white what's got grubby'. Which... Holy shit I'm actually disagreeing with Pratchett and not because I'm being a contrary little bitch, but the fact that people who choose evil are always trying to point out worse evil and saying that they're gray do not negate the fact that gray in fact exists. Not only that, but in the real world so does brown, and blue, and the rest of the fuckin' rainbow. Shit, from what I remember green and magenta are the same thing, and one of them isn't real, and I can't remember which, but even then both of them are still real.

Because black and white thinking is a binary solution set, and there are precious few of those in the real world.

But, and this is the thing that had me feeling guilty about thinking about people as 'resources', I fully agree that evil starts with thinking of people as things. Shit, a nonzero amount of what I consider my greatest successes here and now are the exact opposite. Specifically, forcing those in power to think of people they'd thought of as things to think of them as people, or they'd wind up as one of the particular category of things known as 'corpses'.

Yeah, 'recognize people as people or die' is kinda, I dunno. But then, Justified Homicide. Justified explicitly by denial or destruction of Agency. So I'm not gonna be super proud of the method, but I'm not gonna disavow it, and I'm not gonna be ashamed of the results either.

Bag are people. Maenads are people. Thralls are people. Fae are people. Locals are people. Foreigners are people. Kraken are people. Trolls are people.

Fuck. Deities are people too, I guess. I'm vaguely embarrassed that it took me so long to realize that last one. The Troll one, not the Deities one. Also, something that I think suffused everything I've done so much that I forgot it when I made that list just then, Children are people.

Yeah, I'm pretty sure if somebody passes the Harkness Test, I'm gonna label them people. If the only reason they don't is because of the whole 'not of age' part, because they're Children, they're not just people, they're people who need to be protected and nurtured until they're old enough to protect and nurture themselves.

Which, y'know, I still do that to my ladies every chance I get, and they do that to me, so maybe it's more 'until they're old enough to find someone to exchange protection and nurturing with'.

At any rate, as everybody settled in to eat Furtim arrived. Didn't even sit down, just clacked her jaw and stood there as our little Trolls... Then there was the one who'd been hanging with Davey. She stood up, one hand behind her back holding Davey's, and made a couple clacking noises back. I realized right then that what they were doing wasn't even proper 'language', just the Troll equivalent of, I dunno, grunts and hand gestures. But the meaning was clear; Furtim had shown and said 'time to go home, kids', at which point four of our girls did the 'okay, ma, one last thing before I go', while one of them straight up hit her with the one two combo of 'why' and 'I don't wanna'.

Before anything could explode, I said, "have you had dinner yet?"

She paused mid-snap to focus her attention on me. "No?"

I realized right then that while Furtim might be the Prefect of the Legion, she'd also been what amounted to a junior officer two years ago, and had been for who the fuck knows how long before that. Because when the Queen of the Murderfuck Cult said, "Sit. Eat." Furtim sat and reached for a bowl. Which resulted in all five of our little Troll girls planting their asses back in their seats, spooning down stew as fast as they could.

I brought over a pair of cauldrons. My Sneaky Tail is not a small lady, and something told me Troll BMRs are kinda high when they're active. Once she was eating, I said, "kids, huh?"

She frowned, but kept eating, and I let her think about it. As quietly as I had, she replied, "replacing me?"

"What? No!" I kinda whisper barked. "What made you think that?"

Her frown stayed put, even as her lips tried to curl up at the taste of my bisque. "Encouraging their disobedience."

I sighed. "Furtim?" She nodded. "Trolls are people, right?"

She frowned and said, "yes?" stretching the word out to two syllables.

"Which means Troll kids are people too." Her jaw dropped open a little, then closed again when she took another ladle of bisque. Priorities in order, and soldiers don't waste food. "Agency, Furtim. Agency and Children. You know that."

Funny, Trolls get kinda dun colored when they blush. "I do."

"Hey, no harm done yet. And I'd be lying if I never told my kids to do shit and expected it to get done. But I think you're maybe being a little blind if you think any kid of mine is gonna be perfectly 'obedient', or any kid of yours is gonna be less than 'strong willed'."

She clapped her mouth, and this time I realized it was the Troll equivalent of a snort. "True."

"Legion back in Troll Home?" She nodded. "Want to get our girls back there with the Legion?" Another nod. I could have thought she was being close mouthed about it, but she kept spooning bisque in, which was the best kind of compliment for a Kitchen Witch. "Y'know how I get my kids to do things?"

I waited, letting her finish the whole tureen until she said, "tell me?"

I smiled. "I let them know why. In every case where there's time, I tell them not just what I want them to do, or want them to avoid, but why I want them doing it or avoiding it." I paused for a bit, thinking about the time the girls insisted on visiting Muscogee to get the refugee kids out and playing instead of sitting around in half-finished houses in shock at their village burning down. "Yeah, they get up to stuff I don't expect some times, but very little of it is stuff I don't approve of. Because I've told them what I do and don't approve of, and why." The memories of every time they'd surprised me in a good way brought a smile to my face. "That's the goal, y'know."

"What is?"

"For them to be better than us. Not just little imitations of us, but... Better. Kinder. Gentler." She frowned a little. "Stronger. Smarter. Because as T told me once, you've got to be pretty fucking strong if you want to be gentle, if you want to safely appear weak."

She sat there stewing as she ate her stew. Eventually, as she was nearing the bottom of the cauldron, she quietly said, "how?"
