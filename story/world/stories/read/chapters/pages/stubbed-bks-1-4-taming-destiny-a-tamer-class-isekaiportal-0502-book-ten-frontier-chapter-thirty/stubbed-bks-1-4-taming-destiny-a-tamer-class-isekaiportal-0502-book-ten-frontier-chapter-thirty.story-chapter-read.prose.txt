Artio managing to Evolve to the next Tier seems to have lit a fire under several of my companions – and allies. Though Layton and Valence have both returned to the settlement, Elarion has been completely distracted from his farming duties, instead sitting next to Artio cross-legged with his eyes closed as the owlbear tests his control over his new ability to switch between flesh and plant at will. Plant Meld, it turns out. Mould is what he used on the plant to force it to produce wheat instead of its natural berries like some odd sort of grafting technique.

Trouble and his two male shadows are there too, as are the two deris. Sirocco sits on the branch above his head. Surprisingly, Pride has detached himself from Catch’s side and is watching Artio thoughtfully. I wonder if two of the most standoffish of the Pack might have decided to become friends due to their shared attitude.

I’m also watching Artio, though for different reasons than most – I’m not trying to Evolve. Instead, I’m certain that if I watch Artio’s shifts enough, I’ll finally make that link I need in order to gain Plant-Shaping. So far, though, it’s just been frustrating – I can feel that understanding lingers only a short distance away, but I can’t bridge the gap between me and it.

Finally, I sigh and push myself to my feet. Fenrir, who has been keeping quiet watch next to me, looks up. His movement prompts Earth Fenrir next to him to mimic his movement. I have to admit that it’s more than a little endearing.

“Let’s go somewhere else and continue the testing we were doing earlier,” I suggest quietly to him.

He glances at Artio for a moment and hesitates. “If you’d prefer to stay here and watch Artio, that’s fine,” I quickly offer.

After a brief moment of thought, he sends me a sense of negation.

Come with you, he answers firmly. Seen what I need to. Plant not interesting.

Though his words are bare, I understand what he’s trying to convey – that the Evolution he watched Artio pursue was far more interesting for him and his path than watching Artio play with his new abilities.

“Alright, let’s go, then,” I smile at him. Most of our companions who weren’t interested in Artio’s abilities have already left – either back to the settlement or to go hunting. Sirocco is the only one who pays attention to Fenrir and me as we leave.

What are you testing? she asks curiously.

I’ve been working on my teleportation ability. Now, I should be able to teleport to one of you as well as teleporting you to me.

She chirps with interest.

I’ll come with you and help test it.

Are you sure? I check. Don’t you want to watch Artio for longer? Plant-Shaping isn’t really something I saw as valuable for her, but she’s stuck around so she must have found some inspiration somewhere.

No, she dismisses, flicking her wings and diving off the branch where she’s been sitting, landing heavily on my shoulder, her back feet slamming into my back. If I didn’t have a superhuman Constitution, I’d have probably been knocked flying. I hoped to learn how to transform myself into fire as the half-bird transforms himself into a plant, but so far I have gained little inspiration.

That’s where you want to take your journey? Interesting, I comment even as we start walking away. I wonder to myself whether she'd gain inspiration if I made a Fire Sirocco.

When I raise the idea with her, she seems interested, so I focus on summoning my fire, pouring in all my memories and impressions of Sirocco to give it form and character. The fire twists and shifts into a four-legged bird identical to the real Sirocco.

Flesh-and-blood Sirocco chirps in interest and inspects the Fire Sirocco from every angle, fluttering about and peering past my head to where it's perched on my other shoulder. I nudge it, and it takes flight into the air, flickering as it flaps lightly through the breeze. Sirocco takes flight as well, and the two of them dance together, just as Angel and the fire dragon had on the ship.

Is this helping? I ask Sirocco.

Yes, she answers firmly.

Satisfied, I leave Fire Sirocco dancing through the air, even topping it up with mana whenever it starts running low. It seems that the smaller a beast, the more quickly it loses mana.

When we reach a clearing, I ask my companions to stop.

"Let's test the new function of Bond Teleport here," I suggest, then consider exactly how we're going to go about doing so.

Actually, thinking about it, it's rather fortuitous that Sirocco decided she wanted to come with us, because now I can test whether I can take someone with me in my spatial tattoo.

But first, I need to see how the Skill works at all.

I direct Fenrir to stand on one side of the clearing, then focus on my Skill.

This is the first time I've teleported myself, but I figure it can't be that different from teleporting others. The first time I used Bond Teleport, I focused on Ninja and on pulling her towards me. My mana did the rest.

This time, I don't want to teleport Fenrir to me, so instead of focusing on Fenrir and pulling him towards me, I focus on Fenrir while concentrating on the idea of me moving towards him.

Then I activate Bond Teleport.

Mana pours down my connection to Fenrir like before, but, for the first time, I feel the magic wrap around me.

At first it feels skintight, and then it constricts a lot more tightly than that. As my vision goes black, I feel crushed and then sucked through a very tight tube.

Though I don’t actually feel in actual pain, or unsafe, it's still distinctly uncomfortable, and I wonder whether it's always been like this for my companions whenever I've teleported them. If so, I wonder why none of them have ever mentioned it.

When my vision clears again only a short moment later, I'm standing beside Fenrir on the other side of the clearing. He regards me with mild surprise, but also obvious pleasure, accepting my new position as though it were the most natural thing in the world.

Curious to see how much teleporting myself costs, I check my mana reserves.

I'm surprised by how little mana the teleport has actually taken. Yes, it consumed more than half my pool in a single journey, but considering how much more powerful I am now compared to some of my companions who also use more than half my pool to summon, it seems remarkably efficient. Though I do recognise that my estimations are possibly a little off – as my experiments with Fenrir earlier proved, the Skill has become more efficient to use for everyone.

Still, I'm not going to look a gift horse in the mouth – the less mana this consumes, the more often I can use it. As it is, I still have to meditate for a little while before attempting it again.

This time, as I teleport, I do my best to pay attention to exactly what's happening.

I soon realise why it feels as though I'm moving through a narrow tunnel – because I am. As I'd noticed when improving the skill, Bond Teleport is exactly what its name implies: teleportation through the Bond itself. And though my Bond with Fenrir is strong, it’s still a relatively tiny part of my internal matrix. Actually, the idea that the whole of my being can pass through that thin tendril is mind-boggling. I start to realise why Nicholas indicated that becoming a spatial-focused mage was a challenge.

After more reflection, I think I have also identified why teleporting myself is more efficient than I thought it would be – there's no resistance.

When I'm the one making the decision, my body and soul are completely aligned with the action. With my companions, even those who trust me absolutely, and despite Dominate controlling their bodies, their souls may still unconsciously resist being moved. That unconscious resistance would naturally increase the cost.

Once I've recovered, I do it again.

And again.

And again, until I'm confident I understand exactly how the Skill functions.

The Skill itself makes the teleport remarkably safe. I wouldn't dream of attempting anything like this without it – the chances of something going catastrophically wrong seem far too high – but with the Skill guiding the process, I feel confident enough to move on to the next test.

That's where Sirocco comes in.

She's currently perched on a branch with the Fire Sirocco beside her, preening itself.

I can't help smiling at how lifelike it is.

Sirocco, would you be willing to go into my storage space while I teleport? I ask.

Is it safe? she asks dubiously.

"I don’t know," I admit, not wanting to lie and pretend that I know more than I do. The fact is that I’m a bit nervous about this one – Bond Teleport is designed to pull one of my Bonded to me, and has now changed to allow me to travel to one of my Bonded. But the spatial tattoo isn’t anything to do with the Skill – or to do with the Bond.

Still, given that it only works for those who are Bound to me, and Lizil indicated that my companions are stored in a layer of my soul, I have enough confidence that it should work to be willing to risk Sirocco and myself in this experiment. The implications if it’s successful are game-changing, and, anyway, I’d rather test it out before it becomes a question of life or death.

Sirocco eyes me for a moment longer, still unconvinced, but eventually gives me the benefit of the doubt. She flutters over and allows me to place her inside my spatial storage, though I can feel her nervousness through the Bond.

Once she's inside, I check that my mana is full. It is – I took a mana regeneration potion recently in preparation. Then, butterflies in my stomach, I activate Bond Teleport again.

Immediately, I feel resistance that hadn't been there before. It’s like instead of just pulling myself through the Bond, I’m also pulling a massive block of concrete.

More and more mana is drained from me with every passing second. It rapidly surpasses what the previous teleports consumed and continues climbing to the point that I begin to worry I might not have enough to complete the journey, despite being full before.

Uneasy that something has gone wrong, I try to cancel the teleport.

I can't.

The skill continues relentlessly, draining the last of my mana.

Then it begins searching for more.

With no mana left, it starts drawing on another source – my health.

Heart pounding and my health bar quickly plummeting as I feel a soul-deep painful drain, I quickly quaff a second powerful mana regeneration potion, flooding my mana pool with fresh energy. The Skill immediately switches back to drawing mana instead of life force.

To my immense relief, the teleport finally completes.

My vision blacks out as I'm pulled through the familiar narrow tunnel, and a moment later I materialise beside Fenrir.

Immediately, I pull Sirocco out of my spatial storage.

Are you all right? I ask, checking both her health and mana.

I'm fine, she replies, sounding puzzled. Why wouldn't I be? Did something go wrong?

"Not exactly," I admit, feeling stupid as I consider that I should have realised that transporting someone inside my spatial storage isn't free.

They still exist, even while stored in whatever strange extradimensional space the tattoo creates. Just because they're inside my soul – or however Master Mirena would explain it – doesn't mean the teleport can, or will, ignore their presence. And it’s always possible that more Bonded will multiply the costs, not just add them.

I have to admit I'm disappointed. Being able to smuggle an entire army behind enemy lines would have been incredibly useful. Still...

Thinking about it, the result doesn’t mean I can’t use it for mass transportation – only that it will require a lot more preparation. After all, the mana was consumed before the teleport, not afterwards.

Arguably, I could use the same kind of techniques I employed when pulling Kalanthia halfway across the continent – drawing on external mana sources beforehand – to power a teleport with an entire force hidden inside my storage space.

The only real problem is that I wouldn't know beforehand exactly how much mana I'd need.

Then again, I hadn't known how much I needed for Kalanthia either; I'd simply known that it would be a lot. And now I know that if there isn’t enough mana, it will draw on my health instead. Which isn’t the end of the world either – if I’d thought instead of panicking, I would have drawn on the health pool all of my companions share. With Kalanthia, Artio, and Fenrir part of the pack, I’m sure there would have been enough health to complete the teleportation without needing a potion.

The way Bond Teleport used to be, I’d have feared that it would have become overwhelmed by that sort of demand.

Not anymore.

It's far more streamlined now. The channels are wider, there are fewer bottlenecks, and the pathways between me and those I'm closest to are much stronger.

I'll probably want my scout to be someone with whom I share one of my deepest Bonds, but I'd almost certainly choose Ivor, Sirocco, or Noir anyway to go into enemy territory like that. Maybe Lathani if it was a short distance. So that’s not an issue.

Ultimately, the greatest success here is that I’ve proven that I can safely bring someone through Bond Teleport with me. The only question is that of price, and that’s far easier to answer than if Sirocco was left behind – or worse.

Satisfied, I’m about to ask Sirocco to return to my storage space for another test when a bolt of terror shoots across the Bond network, bringing me to full alertness. I search for where the alarm came from, and find out which Bond it is just as Nessie reaches out to me with a stomach-curdling sense of fear and desperation.

Entangler! Please! My mate! Help my mate! I swear, whatever you need, I will give it. Or he will. Just help him!
