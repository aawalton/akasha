"Scorching Purge!"

Ning formed a series of hand seals, gathering spiritual qi around his palm until a thin wisp of flame sprang into existence and descended upon the stubborn weed in front of him.

The weed blackened almost instantly before crumbling into ash.

Ning examined the empty patch of soil with more satisfaction than the accomplishment probably deserved. After months of pulling weeds by hand, watching one of those tenacious bastards get erased from existence was almost euphoric.

"This technique is quite useful," he murmured, checking the untouched soil around the ashes.

Unlike the usual throw-a-fireball-and-blow-something-up type of fire spell, Scorching Purge concentrated heat on unwanted plants and harmful organisms while minimizing damage to the surrounding crops.

That precision was important because weeds in this world were no ordinary plants. Some grew quickly enough to overrun a field of spiritual grain within days, while others developed extensive root systems that absorbed the spiritual qi meant for cultivated crops. The more troublesome varieties could even release toxins when disturbed, making them especially unpleasant to remove by hand.

Ning crouched beside the small pile of ash, feeling the lingering warmth against his palm before raising his hand to admire the fading flame above his fingertips.

"Power of the sun in the palm of my hand..."

He admired his first genuinely offensive spell for another moment before extinguishing the flame.

Alright, that was enough theatrics.

With Scorching Purge added to his repertoire, Ning had now learned three of the fundamental spiritual farming techniques. More importantly, he was getting closer to the one he was actually looking forward to.

The Withering and Flourishing Technique.

Much like the Small Cloud Rain Technique, it was considered one of the most valuable techniques available to spiritual farmers. It allowed a cultivator to extract wood qi from one plant and transfer it into another, accelerating the growth of the receiving plant.

For spiritual plants that normally required years to mature, the usefulness was obvious.

The problem was its enormous spiritual energy consumption. A cultivator needed to reach at least the middle stage of Qi Condensation before they could properly practice it.

Speaking of cultivation...

[Cultivation Method: Pure Qi Sutra (1st Floor: 96/100)]

Ning's attention settled on the number.

The Pure Qi Sutra was the Pure Qi Sect's foundational cultivation method, known for its stability and compatibility with nearly every spiritual root.

Its greatest advantage lay in its name: Pure Qi.

The technique was attribute-less, allowing cultivators to practice it regardless of their spiritual attributes. It could also support them all the way to the late stage of Qi Condensation without requiring any special supplementary materials.

For new disciples without wealth or powerful backgrounds, it was difficult to ask for anything better.

The technique itself was divided into three floors, with each successive floor refining the cultivator's spiritual qi to a greater degree while improving the efficiency with which they absorbed spiritual energy.

Ning was finally approaching the second floor.

He sat down cross-legged and took out one of his remaining Star-Marked Red Bud Pills. Without hesitation, he swallowed it.

The pill dissolved rapidly, releasing medicinal energy that spread through his abdomen before entering his meridians. Ning steadied his breathing and began circulating the Pure Qi Sutra, guiding the energy through the familiar route while recalling everything he'd come to understand about the technique.

Purity was the core of the Pure Qi Sutra.

But what exactly did purity mean?

The sect's explanation focused on repeatedly circulating spiritual qi, separating the clear from the turbid and gradually refining away impurities until only pure spiritual energy remained.

Ning's own understanding had developed in a slightly different direction.

Compression.

If spiritual qi could be refined and compressed into a denser state, the same amount of space within his dantian could contain qi of greater quality. The process would also help separate unstable impurities, leaving behind more concentrated spiritual energy.

At least, that was his theory.

He certainly wasn't foolish enough to crush all the qi in his dantian together and hope for the best. Compressing spiritual energy without understanding how the resulting pressure interacted with his meridians sounded like an excellent way to become the cultivation world's first human pipe bomb.

So Ning had proceeded carefully over the past several months, experimenting with small changes to the Pure Qi Sutra's circulation and observing how each adjustment affected his cultivation. The panel had been invaluable throughout this process.

Ning inhaled slowly and guided spiritual energy into his meridians, allowing the Pure Qi Sutra to refine it before gradually increasing the pressure within his dantian.

In his mind, he pictured a sealed chamber filled with vapor. When the available space contracted, the particles were forced closer together, increasing their density until the vapor eventually reached the conditions necessary to condense into liquid.

Spiritual qi obviously wasn't literal gas, but the visualization gave him a framework for understanding the process.

He continued circulating the energy, refining it with each cycle before compressing it further. Traces of turbid qi were separated and expelled through his breathing while the remaining spiritual energy grew denser.

Ning lost track of how long he remained there.

Eventually, the compressed qi within his dantian reached a point where maintaining the previous circulation became difficult. He carefully guided the energy into the route he'd spent months developing, allowing it to surge into his meridians.

His body trembled as the pressure threatened to escape his control, but Ning adjusted the circulation and directed the overflowing energy back into the sutra's established pathways.

He repeated the process, gradually reducing the turbulence until the spiritual energy began circulating smoothly at its new density.

Within his dantian, the once-faint qi had taken on a noticeably denser, milky-white appearance.

Then the panel appeared.

[Pure Qi Sutra (1st Floor: 96/100) → 2nd Floor (1/400)]

Ning opened his eyes, a smile spreading across his face.

"It's done."

The difference was immediately noticeable. His spiritual qi flowed more smoothly through his meridians and felt denser than before. Even the rate at which he absorbed spiritual energy from his surroundings had increased by nearly half.

More importantly, his theory had worked.

Ning exhaled and allowed his circulation to settle.

Compression itself wasn't some amazing realization. What pleased him was that he'd been able to develop his own understanding of the technique and confirm that it worked.

The panel had played a large part in that.

Over the past several months, Ning had gradually come to understand its function. It wasn't some absurd game system that allowed him to ignore bottlenecks and endlessly increase his skills by grinding experience points. The panel couldn't cultivate for him or magically turn an incorrect method into a correct one simply because he'd practiced it enough.

It merely reflected his progress, which was far more useful than it sounded.

Whenever Ning experimented with a technique, he could observe how the changes affected his proficiency. If his progress stalled, he knew he needed to reconsider his approach. If it improved, he had a reason to continue exploring that direction.

Through repeated experimentation, he could gradually refine his understanding without having to rely entirely on intuition.

The panel gave him something cultivators rarely possessed: immediate feedback on his progress. For someone like Ning, who enjoyed figuring out the principles behind cultivation techniques, that was more than enough.

Over the following month, Ning settled into a familiar routine of tending his fields and practicing his spells whenever he wasn't cultivating.

His days passed with the dedication of a man possessed by the sacred desire to make his numbers go up.

Little by little, his cultivation climbed until he finally reached the bottleneck separating the second and third stages of Qi Condensation.

One evening, Ning sat cross-legged inside his hut, his breathing slow and controlled as spiritual qi circulated steadily through his body.

The second floor of the Pure Qi Sutra had made his qi much purer and denser than before. Now, he gathered that refined spiritual energy within his dantian and began compressing it, carefully building pressure while maintaining a stable circulation.

His meridians gradually tightened under the strain, but Ning remained calm. He had prepared for this.

He continued compressing the accumulated qi until it approached the highest pressure his meridians could safely withstand. Only then did he guide the gathered energy through the Pure Qi Sutra's circulation route and direct it toward the bottleneck.

The compressed qi struck, sending a jolt through his body, but the bottleneck held firm.

Ning gritted his teeth and steadied the turbulent spiritual energy before gathering it for another attempt. The second impact was stronger, and a faint cracking sensation spread through his body as the invisible barrier began to give way.

Seizing the opportunity, Ning drove the remaining spiritual energy forward.

Boom!

The bottleneck shattered.

Spiritual qi rushed through his meridians like water released from a broken dam. The pathways that had constrained his cultivation seemed to open all at once, allowing the accumulated energy to circulate more freely than before.

Ning's body trembled beneath the sudden influx, his meridians swelling with a sense of fullness that bordered on painful. Yet beneath that discomfort was an unmistakable feeling of strength.

The amount of spiritual energy within him hadn't literally doubled, but the difference in how much qi his body could contain and circulate was enormous.

He had broken through.

Ning resisted the urge to celebrate immediately. A breakthrough wasn't finished simply because the bottleneck had fallen, so he remained seated and continued circulating the Pure Qi Sutra, guiding the turbulent spiritual energy through his newly expanded meridians until it gradually settled.

Only then did he open his eyes.

A faint spiritual light flashed through them before fading.

[Cultivation: 3rd Stage of Qi Condensation (Consolidating)]

Ning looked at the words for several seconds before allowing himself to relax.

For the past few months, he'd focused almost entirely on cultivation, rarely leaving his fields except to purchase supplies or deal with necessary matters. The only major interruption had been the second harvest of his spiritual grain.

He liked it that way.

He wasn't the protagonist. He didn't need to fight bandits or fall off cliffs, much less offend some arrogant young master every time he wanted to advance a single stage.

He had pills and enough resources to support his progress, so he simply stayed home and cultivated.

Who would've thought that sitting safely at home and practicing was such an effective way to improve one's cultivation?

A shame it was a mob-only skill.

"Seven months..."

Ning glanced toward the faint evening light streaming through the window.

It had already been seven months since he'd arrived in this world. He'd spent that time farming and cultivating, earning spirit stones only to spend them almost immediately, gradually building himself up from an ordinary peripheral disciple who'd barely stepped onto the path of cultivation.

Now, the words floating before his eyes finally read:

[3rd Stage of Qi Condensation.]

A satisfied smile spread across Ning's face.

"Finally. I've reached my goal."
