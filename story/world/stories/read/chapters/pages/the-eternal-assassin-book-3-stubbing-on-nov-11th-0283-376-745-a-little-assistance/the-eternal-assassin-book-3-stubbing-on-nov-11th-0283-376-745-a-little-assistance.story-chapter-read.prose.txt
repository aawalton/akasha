Asher crouched upon the surface of the mountain Dormaul was set inside, carefully helping set Donvath down on the ground. The High Prince was shaking and ghostly pale, and Asher’s breath caught as he realized what his condition reminded him of.

Donvath looked like he was suffering tritessence debt.

“I’ve unwound a little too much time for one day,” Donvath muttered, giving Asher a shaky smile even as his teeth clattered together. “I’ll be fine… but you’re not getting any more do overs.”

“What the hell happened?” Asher asked, taking a moment to confirm that the wall of mud Ploryn had summoned had dissipated along with the lich’s death. Cora and Jameston were hacking their lungs up off to the side, but at least they were both still alive. “One second Ploryn was about to finish the ritual, and the next… You came back to stop him?”

“It was your plan,” Donvath admitted, tapping his belt and causing the blue wings along his back to vanish. “A tad reckless for my tastes, but we really didn’t have any other options. According to future you, that lich was bad news. You shared your memories with me and showed me exactly where I needed to hop back in order to surprise him.”

“Wait, I thought you were only capable of hopping back to your past body,” Asher said, scrunching up his face as he realized Donvath was far more deadly than he’d given the High Prince credit for. “What happened to the you of this time period? Are there two of you now?”

“No, nothing so crazy,” Donvath chuckled. “I will have vanished from within the war room. The royal guards are going to be in a tizzy once they realize, but it won’t have been the first time I had a vanishing act. I don’t often jump to the past like this because, as you can see, it takes a lot more out of me. Time and relative location play large factors in the energy expenditure. I barely had enough in the tank to pull this off.”

“Why are you telling me any of this? These feel like national secrets.”

“Call it a thank you for helping me save all of Dormaul,” Donvath said, giving him a strange, sad look as he laid a trembling hand on his shoulder. “Now, that’s enough of that. Unless you’ve forgotten, there are still three liches trying to destroy my kingdom, and I’ll see them dead for it. Deader? More dead. Whatever, you get the idea. Can you help me stand? I’m in something of a bad way.”

“You and me both,” Asher grunted, his own muscles still spasming from the lingering effects of being struck by a giant lightning bolt. Even so, after removing the Eternal Mark on Mako’s corpse and placing it instead on the ground, Asher took Donvath and Recalled back to the capital. Reappearing in his and Samantha’s room, he gave the High Prince one last look. “Am I okay to leave you here? I need to get back into the fight.”

“I think I can hobble down the hall on my own,” Donvath grinned. “Go save my kingdom.”

“Once the Council is dead, I'm going to loot the ever-loving-hell out of your treasury,” Asher said, smiling at Donvath’s laugh before Recalling back to the top of the mountain. Chugging a quick potion, he jogged over to Cora and Jameston. “Are you guys okay?”

“Yeah,” Cora rasped, still rubbing at her throat as she grimaced. “What happened?”

“Donvath came through for us,” Asher said, helping the two of them to their feet. “But the fight’s not done. There are still three liches left. You guys got anything more in you? I promise you as soon as this is all done, resurrecting Mako is the first thing I’m planning to do.”

“In that case, we better help wrap things up sooner than later,” Cora grinned, before pausing to take a swig from her canteen. Letting out a hearty sigh, she passed it to Jameston. “What do you say?”

“Let’s finish this,” Jameston projected into their minds as he drank. There was something odd about hearing the man speak while actively drinking water, which Asher thought was more than a little entertaining. This world was filled with so many wild, unbelievable powers, and yet he had to pause for a moment to think about Jameston’s surprise ventriloquist act.

“Focus, Asher,” Cora said, causing him to flinch when she snapped electrified fingers in front of his face. “What’s the plan?”

“Right. Sorry,” Asher said, shaking his head. Maybe that bolt of lightning I took head on did more damage than I thought. “Whikoga still has Winged Death protecting them right now, along with members from Amanda’s Order backing her up… Let’s head to Furora. If we can take down the lich raining icy death over the city, that frees up Amanda. I think of all of us, she’s the one with the most explosive power. Her and Groln combined can probably finish taking down that giant stone golem they’re fighting over in Juhstain.”

“Works for us,” Cora said, getting a confirming nod from Jameston as well. “I can use my Fire element to help melt the ice, and Jameston can work on healing injured civilians. When you find that lich, give them a nice love tap for me, would you?”

“You and Moxy have got to try hanging out sometime,” Asher laughed. Forming a Spatial Lock around them, he concentrated and dragged them halfway across the kingdom.

Instantly, the calm, quiet mountaintop was replaced with screams of panic and powerful crashes of car-sized chunks of hail raining down all around them. The ground shook with each impact and buildings exploded as they were struck, and Asher’s eyes widened when he spotted an ice chunk headed straight for them.

“Cora!”

“On it!” she shouted, raising her arms and launching an equally large gout of fire up at the projectile. The ice chunk exploded and the pieces melted away, spraying them with a few heavy drops of water. “What in the nine hells are the nobles in the city doing?! I thought they were protecting people!”

“We’re on the outskirts right now!” Asher explained. When Samantha had taken him around to replace some of his Eternal Marks, they hadn’t bothered going too far into the city because they figured the outskirts would need the most help. “Donvath said the nobles were protecting the central part!”

“Well, gee, thanks for that!” Cora shouted, blasting apart another giant ice chunk before it could crush a family running through the street.

“Are you going to be okay?” Jameston asked, giving him a concerned look. “How will you find the lich?”

“Don’t you worry about me,” Asher chuckled, turning and already starting to run off as he shouted over his shoulder. “I’ve got an acquaintance who should be able to help with that!”

It felt like the world was ending as buildings exploded and people died all around him, but Asher gritted his teeth and tried to focus on the task at hand. As much as he wanted to run around and do what he could to save people, his skillset simply wasn’t well suited to something like this. Running into burning buildings and teleporting people to safety was one thing. Stopping giant chunks of ice raining down from the sky before they could splatter people?

That was a tad beyond him.

In fact, Asher needed to be careful himself. Many of the chunks of ice were too dense for him to phase through with astral dip, and he wasn’t about to trigger the skill’s third-tier effect just to dodge something. It was easily his most energy hungry skill, and he didn’t have the tritessence to throw around right now. With that in mind, he kept his focus on the sky as he ran, using Distortion to dart out from underneath certain death time and time again while he made his way to one particular farm tucked a little ways into the city.

It wasn’t long before he reached his destination, and Asher let out a sigh of relief at the sight of the undisturbed building. Rows of crops had already been destroyed and the ground churned up, but the tiny cabin seemed to have gotten lucky. Yet as he got closer, he blinked when he realized luck had nothing to do with it.

“You’re gonna have to do better than that if you want to claim my bounty!” a small, elderly woman cackled from up on the roof of the cabin as she raised a crossbow almost as large as she was up to the sky and let loose a bolt. The bolt shot up through the air before impacting a massive chunk of ice, shattering the thing to pieces and sending the far smaller chunks raining down all across the farm. “Thanks for watering my crops! Is that the best you got?!”

“Mama! Get down from there!” a large man shouted up at her. His face was panicked as he alternated between looking at the giant chunks of ice raining down across the city and the ninety-year-old woman all but dancing on the roof. “It’s not safe!”

“Not safe for them, maybe!” Mama Fouger cackled as she shot down another boulder of ice. “I’m just getting started!”

“Reggy!” Asher shouted, causing the man to jump and spin around. It clearly took him a second to place Asher’s face, but his eyes lit up soon enough.

“Asher?! What in the world is going on?!”

“There’s a lich somewhere nearby who is trying to destroy the city!” Asher shouted, causing Reggy’s already worried face to pale even further. “It’s alright, I can stop them! But I need Mama Fouger to help me find them!”

“She’s a little busy at the moment!” Reggy admitted, turning back to the dancing old woman as she shot down a third boulder. “Mama, please! It’s dangerous up there!”

“Of course it is! I’m up here!” Mama Fouger cackled, slapping another bolt into her crossbow and yanking it back with surprising strength. “Hey, you’re that brat who shot up some of my vegetables and then asked me to find someone off the map! Did you ever end up finding him?”

“Yeah, we did,” Asher called up, surprised to find she actually remembered him. The last time they were here she’d seemed far less lucid than this. “And for the record, you were the one who shot up your own vegetables!”

“Bah, that doesn’t sound like me!” Mama Fouger said as she shot down another boulder aimed right for Reggy. “A lich, huh? Council of Death?”

“You know about the Council?” Asher asked, now even further taken aback as he stared up at the semi-senile woman. As much as he wanted to grill her on everything she knew, the constant shaking of the ground and screams being cut off forced him to focus, and he shook his head. “Yes, a lich from the Council! Can you help me find them?”

“Of course I can, but it’s going to cost you!” Mama Fouger said, letting her crossbow fall as she turned to stare down at him. “Reggy here needs some help getting his final element. He’s a good boy, and I want to make sure he’s well taken care of once I’m gone.”

“Mama, I told you, I’m fine!” Reggy snapped. Before he could keep arguing, his eyes widened, and he jumped while pointing toward the sky. “Mamaaa!”

Rolling her eyes, Mama Fouger didn’t even look as she lifted her crossbow with one hand and shattered another boulder of ice right before it could crush her. “Hush, Reggy dear, Mama’s negotiating. What do you say, brat? Do we have a deal?”

“I’ll help Reggy get whatever element he wants!” Asher agreed, thankful that she wasn’t demanding he hand it over right this instance. “Where’s the lich?!”

“Where do you think it is?” Mama Fouger snorted. Narrowing her eyes, she looked back up to the dark storm swirling around overhead. “It’s right up there, above the dead center of those clouds. It’s moving around something fierce though, I think someone else is trying to steal my kill. I keep trying to shoot the bastard down before that happens, but the undead sack of guts keeps blocking my shots! I tell you, they don’t make bolts as tough as they used to!”

“Can you seriously shoot that far?” Asher asked, doing his best to judge the distance between them and the storm far up above. He was pretty certain a regular crossbow shouldn’t be able to get anywhere near that distance, but Mama Fouger’s was clearly something special. Not to mention he was all but certain at this point that she had a Crossbow element to her name.

“If I wasn’t relying on you to get Reggy that third element of his, I’d shoot you just for asking that!” Mama Fouger snapped. “Of course I can shoot that far! I once shot down a pegasus courier flying at speed because she thought she could get away without paying me what I was owed!”

“It’s true, she did do that,” Reggy admitted, grimacing at the memory. “The courier guild was not happy about that. Doubly so when they learned she ate it afterward.”

“I’m not about to let perfectly good meat go to waste!” Mama Fouger scoffed.

Trying not to imagine the old woman bursting out of a pegasus’ chest like some sort of parasite as she ate it from the inside out, Asher looked between Mama Fouger and the sky. If the lich really was up there…

“Hey, Mama Fouger…” he said, grinning as the woman raised an eyebrow at him. “…How would you like to pull off the most impressive trick shot in all of history?”
