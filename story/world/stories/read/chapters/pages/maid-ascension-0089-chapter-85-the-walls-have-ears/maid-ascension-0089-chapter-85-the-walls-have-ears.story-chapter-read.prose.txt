Lira spent the better part of the following morning pretending to read a history book in one of the castle's outer gardens. The location had been chosen very carefully. Unlike the secluded inner gardens reserved for the royal family, or even the larger gardens at the heart of the palace where nobles liked to linger, this one sat between the outer bailey and the gatehouse leading into the inner castle. Every noble, knight, official, and wealthy visitor eventually passed through it on their way toward the king's audience hall or one of the countless meetings that seemed to occupy the kingdom's upper class. Better still, the garden overlooked several busy walkways while remaining quiet enough that a lone librarian reading beneath a tree attracted no attention whatsoever.

More importantly, the area was saturated with magic. Through her Mage Sight spell, Lira could see countless thin streams of disturbed mana drifting lazily through the air. Every enchanted sword, protective charm, cleaning cantrip, or active spell left behind faint traces that lingered for several minutes before dissolving back into the ambient mana surrounding the castle. Servants contributed their own disturbances as they carried magically chilled wine across the courtyards or hauled rune-inscribed trunks between buildings, while a pair of gardeners passed nearby pushing enchanted wheelbarrows that practically glowed with lingering magic. Compared to that ever-shifting web of magical activity, the thread left behind by her Focused Hearing spell became almost impossible to distinguish. It blended so perfectly into the background that even she occasionally struggled to pick it out. Lira would usually avoid this spot as her Mana Stabilization bookmarker could be more visible here especially if she was the only person surrounded with a bubble of clean mana. In this case however, she couldn't have asked for a better testing ground.

She opened her book to a random page and settled comfortably onto the bench, occasionally turning a page simply to maintain appearances. To anyone passing by, she looked like nothing more than a diligent librarian enjoying the pleasant spring weather.

"Pika. Cipos. Hishar. Kaldris. Velar."

The spell settled over her thoughts with the now familiar sensation of an invisible ear waiting patiently for instructions.

Unlike when she had tested the spell inside the library tower, she couldn't simply point it somewhere she could see. Here she had to rely entirely on memory. The spell could only travel toward places she could accurately picture, which ruled out much of the inner bailey, the royal apartments, and the audience chambers. She had never been inside those places, making any visualization vague and unreliable. Fortunately, she knew enough of Castle Elthram to work with. She pictured one of the shaded courtyards near the noble guest quarters, imagining the stone benches beneath flowering trees where visiting ladies often gathered to escape the crowds.

A few heartbeats later, voices drifted into her ears as though she were standing only a few steps away.

"...and then he actually asked me for a dance."

Several young women giggled.

"You danced with Prince Aric?"

"Yes, twice."

"Not at the same ball," another girl corrected immediately. "One was yesterday's spring ball. The other was Lady Jacline's banquet."

"I didn't say they were at the same ball."

"You implied it."

"I did not."

"You did!"

"Oh? Then how many times have you danced with the prince?"

"..."

"That's what I thought."

The girls burst into laughter again.

Lira smiled faintly. Noble daughters, reminiscing about a recent ball. She had stumbled into exactly the sort of conversation she expected to find near the guest gardens.

"He was exactly like everyone says," the first girl sighed dreamily.

"What does that even mean?"

"So polite... so handsome... and the way he held me..." Another wistful sigh escaped her. "Such a strong grip. He even thanked me for allowing him the dance."

"Oh, please." Another girl laughed. "You're only saying that because he's a prince."

"I am not."

"You absolutely are."

A third voice joined the conversation, sounding far more confident than the others.

"I thought Sir Deren was much more handsome."

"Oh?"

"I wouldn't mind marrying him."

The surrounding girls immediately dissolved into another fit of laughter.

"As if your father would ever allow that."

"Why not?"

"Because he's poor, even for a baron."

"He's still one of Prince Aric's closest companions."

"So? You're an earl's daughter."

The girl huffed indignantly.

"I don't care."

Someone else snickered.

"You'd really marry a poor baron?"

"Did you see the muscles on his arms?" she answered without hesitation. "Yum."

The others laughed so hard that one nearly choked.

Lira almost snorted herself.

Apparently Sir Deren had quite the following.

She couldn't say she was surprised. The man was young, handsome, courteous, and spent half his life standing beside the second prince. Once Kavard died and Aric inherited the title of Crown Prince, Deren's own status would almost certainly rise with him. Assuming, of course, everything went according to her plans.

Still, amusing as the conversation was, there wasn't much practical value in noble girls discussing handsome men.

She gently redirected the spell.

The invisible point of hearing drifted through the castle as she visualized the military keep instead. Almost immediately, the cheerful girly laughter gave way to the rhythmic clang of steel, boots striking packed earth, shouted orders, and the familiar sounds of soldiers drilling in the training yard. Rather than linger near the sparring knights, she guided the spell toward a shaded corner where off-duty guards usually gathered to watch the exercises.

"...I'm telling you, sergeant Salvek wasn't nearly this strict."

Several guards immediately snorted.

"He was if he didn't like you."

"That's true enough."

"The new captain actually inspects everyone."

"As he should."

"Oh? You enjoy someone searching your belongings every week?"

"I enjoy knowing he's searching everyone's belongings instead of just mine."

"Easy for you to say."

"What?"

"Salvek hated your guts."

Several men laughed.

"What did you even do to him?"

"I spent two months pulling double shifts on the north wall because I told him his paperwork was wrong."

"You should've apologized."

"I wasn't wrong."

"Didn't matter. He was a noble. You weren't."

Another guard spoke up.

"I'll take the new captain any day."

"Really?"

"He actually rewards people who do their jobs instead of the ones who polish his boots."

"Aye."

A chorus of agreement followed.

"The old man only promoted his drinking companions."

"And cousins."

"And gambling partners."

Laughter spread through the group once again before a younger guard lowered his voice.

"You think they'll investigate him?"

"Nah."

"Why not?"

"Because nobody important cares. His greatest crime was making young Jonny here lose some sleep."

The group chuckled again.

Lira frowned thoughtfully.

Interesting.

None of them mentioned the missing maid, the brothel, or the investigation surrounding Thadwick's death. Most likely, Salvek's personal followers had left alongside him after his dismissal, leaving behind only the ordinary soldiers who viewed him as an unpleasant superior rather than a co-conspirator.

From the sound of things, Salvek had certainly abused his authority, but mostly through petty favoritism and vindictiveness. The murders had probably been hidden well enough that even the guards serving under him never suspected anything. That, in itself, was useful information.

She quietly filed the observation away before steering her magical hearing toward the palace kitchens.

Instantly she regretted it.

"More onions!" Old Marga bellowed.

"I already cut the onions!"

"Then cut more! And who burned the bread?"

"It wasn't me!"

"Move!"

A deafening clang rang through the kitchen.

"I said move!"

"Sorry!"

Lira couldn't help smiling as she redirected the spell elsewhere. She liked Old Marga well enough, but she certainly didn't miss working beneath her.

Over the next hour she continued experimenting, moving her invisible point of hearing from one corner of the palace to another. With every successful attempt, the spell became easier to guide, and her confidence grew alongside it. The only true limitation wasn't the magic itself but her own familiarity with Castle Elthram. She could listen almost anywhere she could accurately picture.

Which naturally led to a dangerous thought.

If she wanted to spy on Prince Kavard...

She first needed to see his chambers with her own eyes.

That, unfortunately, was going to be far more difficult than inventing the spell had been.
