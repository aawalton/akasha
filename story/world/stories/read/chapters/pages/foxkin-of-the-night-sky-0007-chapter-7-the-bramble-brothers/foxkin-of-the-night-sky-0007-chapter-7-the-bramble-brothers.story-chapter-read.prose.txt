My system window flickers into existence.

“A duo fight? For the first boss? Fantastic. I’ve spawned into a clearance-bin Souls-like.”

They don’t say anything, but I can feel the stars agreeing with me.

I nod solemnly.

“Exactly. Ridiculous design choice. Someone should be fired.”

One of the two beasts shifts, just enough to break the stillness.

That’s all the warning I get.

The badger's muscles coil, and the squirrel’s tail twitches like an over-caffeinated fast-food worker. The air tightens, charged and expectant.

Right. They’re not here for my critique.

“Okay then,” I sigh, rolling my shoulders as I unsheathe my sword. “Let’s get this over with.”

The squirrel launches first, a blur of fur and murderous enthusiasm. I see the intent, the angle, the trajectory. And it… is completely off mark?

It closes the distance fast—shockingly fast—but not accurate. The squirrel sails past me by a generous few feet and slams its claws into the ground like it’s punishing the grass for existing. Soil sprays. Leaves scatter.

The squirrel freezes.

Slowly… the squirrel swivels its head left. Then right. Then down. Then up.

Offended. Betrayed. Possibly questioning its life choices.

I blink at it.

Then, with all the dignity of someone pretending they meant to trip, it goes perfectly still… like maybe if it stops moving, reality will undo the embarrassment.

I glance up at the sky.

Ah. Right.

Squirrels don’t have night vision.

I turn my attention to the badger.

It is staring at the squirrel with bone-deep disappointment.

Then it feels my stare and looks at me.

We stare. We judge. We experience a moment of cross-species mutual suffering.

Then we both sigh.

At the same time.

It’d be funny, if it weren’t so pathetic.

Then the badger rolls its shoulders, claws extending with a metallic scrape. The mood shifts, annoyance evaporating into threat.

I exhale and slip into stance, gravity curling around me like a well-trained dog waiting for a command. The night sky responds, quiet power humming under my skin. Everything feels sharper. Lighter. Easier.

The night is my domain.

The badger lowers its head.

It moves.

Slow at first—heavy, deliberate steps—

Then faster.

Faster.

Faster.

Charging like a living battering ram aimed directly at my chest.

And finally—finally—the real fight begins.

A grin quickly adorns my facial features.

Ignoring the squirrel entirely—because embarrassment is punishment enough—I sprint toward the badger with the confidence of someone absolutely certain they’re about to win.

Turns out confidence does not equal wisdom.

The squirrel goes from statue-still to ballistic missile in a single frame, exploding forward in a blur. I barely clock the motion before instinct takes over and I vault upward, gravity enthusiastically enabling my bad habits.

I soar almost two meters into the air.

World record? Probably. Someone notify the Olympic committee.

I land lightly, smug and graceful.

…for about half a second.

Because the squirrel bolts at me again, slower this time. Definitely a movement skill earlier.

I pirouette out of the way—dramatic, stylish, better form than half the ballerinas I’ve seen online—and prepare to punish it for missing.

Then the badger arrives with the energy of a poorly maintained truck skidding down a mountain, choosing violence by way of roadkill physics.

It tries to run me over.

Absolutely not.

I refuse to die to the fantasy equivalent of a drunk driver. I dash out of its path, barely escaping getting converted into Lyn jam.

The squirrel is waiting for that opening and blitzes forward again, fast again, because of course it can spam its skill. I abandon retaliation, throw myself to the ground, and narrowly avoid becoming squirrel chow.

The badger skids. The squirrel charges. Again.

I roll away, then push off the ground and spring back to my feet.

My skirt flutters dramatically.

I judge myself.

“Eight out of ten.”

Barely passing.

The duo stare. Unimpressed.

Rude.

It’s not seeing me.

It’s sensing me.

Vibrations.

It heard my voice earlier, sure, but couldn’t lock onto direction. Every hesitation before each dash… it’s recalibrating through the ground.

Great. Love that. My enemy is essentially a fluffy seismometer.

My ear twitches.

I spin around immediately, swinging my sword in a wide arc.

“Slash!”

My guess was right, and my aim is true. My sword sweeps towards the squirrel like the reaper's scythe.

There are rules to reality and that thing just violated at least seven.

A dozen strategies whirl through my head like frantic birds trapped in a chimney, some clever, some stupid, some deeply concerning, and all of them wildly risky.

Naturally, I pick the one with the highest chance of success.

Not the least painful though. I’m not that optimistic.

A fox girl takes flight—gracefully, majestically—like a gamer launching their controller after a rage-inducing PvP loss.

I am, after all, a flawless prodigy who has never lost, never raged, and has certainly never thrown anything in a perfectly controlled display of emotion.

My tail flicks with regal superiority.

My dignity?

Still barely.

Unstoppable aggression from the squirrel.

Overwhelming strength from the badger.

Starlight.
