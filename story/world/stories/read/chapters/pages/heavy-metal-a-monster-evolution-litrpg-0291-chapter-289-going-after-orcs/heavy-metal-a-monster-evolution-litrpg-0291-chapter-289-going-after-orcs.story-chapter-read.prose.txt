“This mission, have you already accepted it, boss?”

Rusty asked, since he still didn’t know the details.

“Well, I tried looking for you, but you were gone, so I just accepted it. How bad could it be, right? It’s just some orcs that we already defeated. They didn’t look that strong!”

Galiena grinned as she leaned back in her chair. It was clear that she wasn’t the type to think things through before signing a contract. He was, in a sense, her advisor at the moment, so it would also fall on him if she agreed to something that ended up being disastrous. His guides immediately had something to say about it.

“Did she just decide on it without considering the number of mercenaries available to her? Does she even comprehend the logistics of an expedition into an unexplored forest filled with orcs?”

Alexander, who had been through his fair share of adventures against monsters, was well aware of how dangerous such an undertaking could be without proper preparation.

“What did you expect from a brute who thinks with her muscles rather than her head?”

Aburdon scoffed in agreement.

“Though, in truth, her idiocy plays right into our hands. Let her march her men straight into the grinder. The resulting chaos will give you the perfect cover to pick off high-value targets and harvest experience uninterrupted.”

Rusty chose to ignore Aburdon’s usual schemes for the moment and focused instead on the task at hand. He looked down at the route map attached to the contract. The terrain marked on the parchment consisted of thick, dense woodland with only a few clearings, making it perfect for ambushes.

“I’ve been there before, but this is going to be different from walking in by myself…”

In the past, he had attacked one of the orc camps while they were resting after a recent raid. He had used the silence of night to eliminate the orcs and only engaged in direct combat when necessary.

That wouldn’t be possible this time. They would be moving as a group of mercenaries and adventurers, which meant the element of surprise would no longer be on his side.

“The orcs we fought at the gates were merely a raiding party.”

Rusty said, keeping his voice steady as he placed the document back on the cluttered desk.

“Deep inside the forest, they’ll have the advantage of knowing the territory. Their chieftains are likely C-rank, and if we encounter a shaman, we’ll be at a serious disadvantage since we don’t have any mages in our group. If we march in without a concrete strategy, we risk being surrounded.”

Galiena waved a hand dismissively, though she paused long enough to drop her heavy boots from the desk with a loud thud.

“That’s why I have you, Second. You handle the planning, the maps, and the orders for those skittish adventurers. I’ll handle the frontline!”

She let out a booming laugh and slapped her thigh before taking another long swig from her tankard.

“Besides, the payout from the city is massive. If we pull this off, the Steel Wolves will be set for months.”

“I see. When do we depart?”

“Two days from now, at dawn. That should give you enough time to get the men in line and review whatever supplies we need.”

Rusty wasn’t happy about all the responsibility for planning being dropped on him, but he also had a secret weapon in the form of Aburdon and Alexander. One had a perfect memory, so he could easily handle the maps and deduce where the best place for an ambush would be. Alexander, on the other hand, had plenty of field experience. He would more than likely know how many monsters they would be up against and how best to ration their supplies.

“Very well, but I will need at least two of our men to stay behind. They don’t need to be that strong. E-ranks are fine.”

If he was leaving to attack Orcs, he needed Gleam to come with him. For that, he needed someone to guard his shop and the orphans there. Now that he was part of the mercenary guild, there wouldn’t be many problems, but they still needed protection from the occasional drunk or rowdy adventurer who wandered in.

“Oh, that’s fine. We’ve gotten some new recruits now that the mercenary band has become more official, so I’ll leave that to you.”

“Good, then I’ll organize everything, Boss…”

“Ha, that’s why you are my second. So reliable!”

Galiena laughed again, clearly not understanding how vulnerable she was making herself. In a way, Rusty had become the real leader of this mercenary band. He had been given the authority to handle supply chains, troop distribution, and tactical positioning. He was now the mercenary strategist who could probably decide on almost anything, with their leader simply agreeing to his decisions.

“Just leave the paperwork to me, then…”

Rusty said as he picked up the stack of administrative forms and target reports from the table.

“I will prepare the deployment route and deliver the final march timetable to your desk by tomorrow evening.”

“Sounds like a plan! Now get to it, Second. I have a drink to finish, and then I have to train with the boys.”

Galiena cheered, already refilling her silver tankard before he had even stepped out the door. Once he returned to the main hallway, Rusty rolled the parchment maps tightly and deposited them into his system storage.

The corridor was quiet now, though he could hear Jakub and his cohort whispering near the back exit of the building. He ignored them completely. They were small fish compared to the threat of an entire Orc-infested forest, and if they attempted to stage a mutiny during the expedition, it would only make disposing of them that much simpler.

‘They would be good experience…’

While he wasn’t planning on killing these humanoids, he would show them no mercy if they dared to attack him. His current main goal was to get through this final D-rank evolution and finally reach C-rank. Once he reached that rank, he would perhaps be equal to the strongest knight in the settlement and maybe even the Orc leader. At that point, his plans would shift, but first he needed to get there.

“Now then, it’s time for you two to earn your keep!”

He said to Aburdon and Alexander in a smug tone as he left, knowing that the two of them would be doing the bulk of the work for him while he tended to other matters. With that, he walked through the mercenary camp, with some people nodding at him while others simply watched without saying anything.

He wasn’t sure about his standing among these people, and that wasn’t strange. Their group had not been active for long, and just as with Gwen, humanoids tended to be wary of someone who kept his face hidden behind a helmet at all times.

Still, authority was built on competence and strength, two traits he possessed in abundance compared to the average mercenary under Galiena’s command.

“Earning our keep?”

Alexander asked.

“Yes, isn’t that what the humanoids always say? I wanted to say it too!”

He replied as he moved along and headed back toward his home, where he planned to have the paperwork completed while he took care of some other chores.

“I’m back for now!”

He announced himself to the mostly empty shop. He knew, however, that once he finished with some of his new work, this would not be the case for long.

“Welcome back, Mister Edmund. Are you done for the day? Not going to the dungeon?”

Rolo asked while trying not to yawn. The boy was starting to enter a growth spurt, but he was stuck tending to a store that did not have much going for it.

“Maybe later. I’ll be down in the basement for now.”

“Oh? Is Mister Oswald going to come? Haven’t seen him for a few days.”

“Um... He should. I gave him a few days off, but he managed to leave some things to sell for later...”

His secondary persona, Oswald the blacksmith, was somewhat underutilized here and unable to function while he was going through his evolution. He was supposed to be the sole blacksmith in this place, but he was rarely seen, even by Rolo, who was stuck at the main desk.

“That boy, I think he is suspecting something. We might have to...”

Aburdon whispered, but before he could finish the sentence, Alexander interrupted.

“Don’t even think about it. We will not do anything to that boy.”

“What? If the boy finds out our secret, he will clearly tell on Rusty to the guild.”

“He wouldn’t!”

“Why wouldn’t he? Rusty is a monster, the boy is a human. He will abandon him the moment it serves him.”

Aburdon sneered at Alexander’s reaction, but the former hero was having none of it.

“Not everyone is like you. Rolo is a kind boy. He wouldn’t betray Rusty so easily, not after Rusty gave him and his siblings a home to live in.”

Rusty listened to the two while walking down into the basement. In truth, he had wondered how the children would react if he revealed his true form. Would they be scared and run away, or perhaps accept him for what he was? Their relationship had started with the lie that he was human, so he was not sure how they would react if they ever found out.

He pushed the heavy wooden door open and descended the steps into the cool, dimly lit basement. The various scents lingering in the room, such as iron and oil, did not bother him, as he had no sense of smell.

“(„• ֊ •„)”

“Gleam, we’ll be going to the forest in two days, so don’t worry. We’ll have some fun soon.”

“(˶ˆᗜˆ˵)”

Gleam was here with him, and judging by how much she was fidgeting, it seemed she was getting somewhat bored with simply staying around the house. Yet, there were suspiciously no rodents anywhere around the whole block, and he was afraid to ask what she had done to all of them.

“Now then, you two. Time to get to work!”

He proclaimed before opening his storage space and bringing out the improved versions of the darkness and light armor belonging to his guides. They were made from the older metal that had composed his body while he was in the middle of D-rank, which gave the two a boost to both their strength and dexterity.

“I made sure that you have more articulate hands. Now you won’t have trouble writing up all those reports!”

“Oh… how generous of you, Rusty…”

Aburdon responded as his wisp of black light entered the improved darkness armor. The armor twitched a few times before taking proper form, moving its newly refined, jointed fingers to test their flexibility.

“This will certainly make scribing faster… oh, what a joyous occasion…”

Aburdon said the words in such a monotonous tone that Rusty immediately understood them as sarcasm.

“You’ll be fine. Weren’t you just complaining that you were bored? Now you can go through all of these!”

Rusty took out a few notebooks along with multiple parchments from his storage. Even before today, he had become responsible for a lot of paperwork within the mercenary group. He had gathered all the information required to formulate the plan, along with the names and jobs of all their members. Now, it was up to Alexander and Aburdon to come up with a good battle plan for the coming expedition.

“Don’t worry, Rusty. I’ll make sure that he doesn’t slack off.”

Alexander said smoothly as his white light glided into the refined set of white, silvery armor. He flexed his metal fingers, admiring the precise craftsmanship.

“A proper military logistics plan requires strict attention to detail, and Aburdon’s administrative memory will prove quite useful despite his attitude.”

“Good, I’ll leave it to you then. I’ll take care of a few other things!”

With that out of the way and his two guides working on the plan, he could safely visit the crafting portion of the Soul Forge to work on a few pieces of superior enchanted high steel. He still needed to figure out a good way to balance the other elements with his twilight steel, and the forge within his lair was ill-suited for that.

The amount of time he could spend in this space had grown to a whole five hours now, which would allow him to progress through magically intensive enchanting processes much more efficiently. Some of these required delicate mana control and preheating materials that could take hours on their own.

However, while the time he could spend inside the mini Soul Forge had increased, he noticed one drawback after he returned.

“Oh? I was gone for two minutes? Not just a few seconds?”

“(˶ᵔ ᵕ ᵔ˶)”

Gleam informed him that his body had remained stationary for much longer than it had after his last evolution. This indicated that while he could stay longer inside the Soul Forge, the time dilation ratio was starting to shift as well.

Where hours inside had previously equated to only a few seconds in this world, it was now turning into minutes. While this ratio was not particularly bad, it meant that he might not be able to abuse this feature as much in the future as he would have liked.

‘The more powerful I become, the more difficult it’s going to get to progress further…’

He was aware of this truth and did not shy away from the challenge. His progress was already remarkably fast, as he was already close to reaching C-rank before even reaching the age of two.

“Okay then, take the time for this one too, Gleam!”

With this new information in mind, he headed into his last Soul Forge related chamber once again, the summoning chamber. Even though the adventures he had gone through with Gwen were still vivid in his mind, that did not mean he could not have new ones.

“Now then, let’s see what we can do…”

After his arrival, he looked at the summoning console, only to be disappointed by the sight of a single available mission.

“Only the Dungeon is available?”

Quest:

Dungeon Defender II

Difficulty:

Medium

Description:

The dungeon summons you again to be its defender. Protect the core from the invading raiders. Drive out all enemies or slay them.

Neither Gwen, Teral, nor even the Necromancer were available to him. Instead, he was left with the option of becoming a minion of the dungeon he had previously visited, and the difficulty was only Medium.

“It’s okay for the talismans, but I won’t get many summoner coins from this one…”

Rusty had spent the bulk of his summoning coins on other things, so buying another summoner slot was impossible for now. He could delete this mission and try for a new one, but there was no guarantee that the next mission wouldn’t be something even worse than a dungeon.

“Well… It’ll probably be a good warmup for my new body, and with two loadout slots, I’ll have an easier time!”

Without giving it much thought, he selected the mission and prepared to enter the dungeon.

The time dilation issue was still there, but for the time being, there was nothing out there trying to reach him. Aburdon and Alexander could continue moving as long as they remained near his body, and Gleam could always grab his helmet and escape through the secret passage if necessary.

It was time to become a dungeon monster again. Rusty wondered if the adventurers would offer more resistance this time around, or if they would simply be slaughtered as easily as they had been during his previous visit.
