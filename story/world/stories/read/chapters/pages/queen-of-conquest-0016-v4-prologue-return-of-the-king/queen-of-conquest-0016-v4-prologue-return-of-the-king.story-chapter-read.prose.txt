General Alexei Talbane sat cross-legged atop a pile of silk blankets. A knee-high lacquered table before him groaned beneath the weight of the empire’s most decadent cuisine, dishes far too refined for his common-born palate.

Still, he made an effort. Each time a servant presented a new dish with practiced formality, Alexei listened attentively, committing the names to memory. He had little interest in the food himself, but his wife adored this kind of fare. Perhaps he’d visit the kitchens later and request a sampling to bring home.

As always, no other generals or officials had brought their spouses to the Emperor’s luncheon. Like him, they sat along the throne room’s outer walls, careful not to encroach upon the red carpet that bisected the chamber. Eight seats in total—one for each of the emperor’s elite commanders.

Aside from the obvious fact that the casual spilling of blood tended to ruin one’s appetite, it was common knowledge: one did not bring his wife anywhere near the Emperor. It wasn’t that Alexei opposed the divine mandate that charged the God-King with spreading his seed across the Empire. On the contrary, he revered it. Let the Emperor populate every province with his lineage. But even so, like most men, Alexei would prefer his own wife be excluded from such sacred duties.

It was considered a tremendous honor to be chosen as a concubine of the living god. Scripture demanded rejoicing, gratitude, even celebration. But the thought of the Emperor laying claim to his wife unsettled Alexei in a way he dared not speak aloud. There would be nothing he could do but accept her fate. And smile while doing it.

His daughter, though, now that was another matter. When she came of age, he would present her to the Emperor’s court for consideration. If she were chosen, that would bring glory and honor to the family name for generations.

Alexei frowned, realizing he’d been glaring down at his untouched food. With a quick thanks to the gods for the emperor not yet being present in the throne room, he shifted his gaze to the crimson carpet where the day's entertainment had already begun.

As was tradition, the luncheon featured a public interrogation. An enemy soldier sat on a broken, rickety wooden chair beneath the inquisitor’s hands while the elite dined. Occasionally, someone would ask a question, and that would become the inquisitor’s god-given mission for the next few minutes as he dismembered the day’s guest.

The inquisitor was a thin, bony man with a gaunt, bird-like face who never wore anything but a single spotless white robe and white gloves. By the end of his work, both whites would be stained red.

Years ago, the sight had sickened Alexei. Now, it barely registered. With time and age came understanding. The ritual served a purpose: it reinforced loyalty, warning of what happened to traitors and enemies of the empire, while also ensuring a synchronized flow of intelligence among the empire’s highest command.

Today’s prisoner was different than usual. Despite half an hour of torment, the man had uttered not a sound. He stared forward, unblinking, eyes aglow with golden light, a fierce, unnatural magic that radiated from an otherwise lifeless face. The dim lighting of the throne room worked to amplify the prisoner’s eerie aura, allowing the golden glare to glance across the walls like the flickering of a candle.

Alexei’s frown deepened as he released an inaudible sigh. It wasn’t that he disliked the throne room, but he’d never felt at ease anywhere with such gaudy appearances, especially when it was portrayed as casual wealth.

The room, the throne, their silk blankets, the food, even the tables were monuments to the empire’s unimaginable wealth. When Alexei had been but a child, his father had told him stories of the Emperor and the Imperial Palace. His father had spun tales of how the very foundation of the palace was built not with mere stones, but with the bones of a fallen celestial beast which had been eventually wrapped in living stone.

Having spent much of his life in that very palace, walking its halls each day, he’d come to believe there’d been some truth to his father’s stories. There was simply something… unnatural about the palace that was unlike anywhere else he’d ever tread. Every step through the vast, echoing halls felt as if it carried the weight of some forgotten ancient memory, as if the air and stones themselves remembered those that walked there long ago.

The throne room was the most oppressive location of the castle, sparing none but the imperial family, who seemed immune to the innate bloodlust of the palace. At the far end, raised high above a procession of black-marble steps, sat the Soulforged Throne - an ironclad monstrosity adorned with molten sigils that pulsed faintly with crimson and violet lights. The throne, Alexei had always thought, was less a seat and more some fusion of architecture and power. If his father’s and the church of light’s stories were to be believed, and believe he did, the throne was forged of shattered godsteel, bound with soul-threaded veins that Alexei himself had seen twitch when the emperor spoke.

Though the emperor had never confirmed his divine status, Alexei believed to his very core that the emperor was no man. He was a god descended among mortals to set the world right. Alexei’s great-grandfather might have been nothing but a newborn babe when the emperor had first descended to the mortal plane and slain the Emperor of Laxon, freeing the continent from the tyrant emperor’s grasp, but Alexei belief in his emperor’s righteous and divine nature was reaffirmed whenever Alexei was in his presence.

Behind the throne, a colossal mural of shifting metals and energy-infused stone depicted a great war between seemingly titanic figures. At one side stood the emperor himself in some long-forgotten war at the helm of a massive army. At the other stood a woman with a strong likeness to the imperial princess, including cold, red eyes, dark brown hair, a slim jaw line, and even a scar tracing down her right eye. All that separated them was a crescent moon mark etched to the mural woman's brow.

At the center of the mural was a man and a young girl. No one knew who they were, though Alexei suspected it was the emperor and the same woman he faced off against, though many years before the depicted war.

The girl was young, no older than eight, gazing up at the man with a sorrowful awe that haunted Alexei since the first time he laid eyes upon the image.

Alexei was shaken abruptly from his examination of the mural by the groaning of the twin obsidian entrance doors. Two heavily armored soldiers stepped forth first, banging the butt of their spears against the stone floor in perfect unison.

“The Imperial Family enters! Show your respect,” they both roared, and Alexei immediately moved from the silk blankets to the floor, kowtowing perpendicular to the red carpet that ran through the center of the room to the throne.

The emperor’s presence struck like a hammer. His aura blanketed the room with an iron grip around Alexei's throat. Even the torches fell silent, flames frozen. Only the emperor's footsteps echoed, each one heavy and deliberate. Behind him walked the empress and the princess, their movements too graceful to make a sound.

Some days, the emperor's aura felt tolerable. Most days, it hung in the air like lead. But today, his heart core was open, releasing a surge of energy so vast it threatened to drown even the present commanders who all bore gold level heart cores. Everyone in the room had attained that secret rank before twenty, an unspoken requirement for their positions. To the rest of the empire, however, only the emperor had achieved the gold realm.

The emperor, however, had long since surpassed the gold realm, having ascended to the platinum realm. Recently, it was rumored that the man had reached his second heart ring within that transcendental realm, a feat that Alexei had never believed possible. Part of him still struggled to believe it.

As the emperor passed through the room, Alexei felt as though a mountain had been dropped onto his back. He didn’t move. Didn’t breathe. He must never show weakness before the emperor lest he cover his family with dishonor and disgrace. Never. He would remain strong. Stoic. Unmovable.

The silence stretched until at last, the emperor sat and retracted his aura's pressure.

“You may return to your seats,” he said, voice calm, yet drenched in absolute command.

As one, the leaders rose to a knee and struck their chests with a clenched fist.

“All hail the Emperor of Pandoria, Niro Aedronir, the Soulforged, the Eternal, the God of Conquest, and true ruler of the continental Pularea.”

When Alexei opened his eyes after the third recitation of the quasi-prayer, he found the emperor watching the prisoner with a rare, thin smile. For a moment, Alexei nearly forgot to return to his seat. The Emperor rarely wore any expression at all, let alone one approaching amusement.

He was clad in black and red robes, adorned with gems more numerous and valuable than anything Alexei could hope to afford in a dozen lifetimes. His face, hardened by battle and rule, was marked by old scars, his square jaw framed in a neatly trimmed beard. His peppered hair was cropped short in the classic military style of the Pandorian special forces.

The emperor’s crimson eyes, the unmistakable sign of imperial blood, swept across the room with the weight of his innate divine authority. Only those blessed by the gods themselves were born with such eyes, said to be borne of the very blood spilled to found the empire.

Beside him, the empress took her usual seat, her expression remote and distant. Her long blonde hair had been braided by careful hands, unlike the curt, uncaring style worn by the emperor.

But it was the princess who stole Alexei’s attention.

She was not her usual icy self. Rage twisted beneath the surface of her expression, barely contained. He could see it in the tightness of her jaw, the stiffness in her shoulders, the way her hands trembled against her dress despite her effort to appear calm. When their eyes met, he gave her a polite nod, but she only responded with a scowl.

He would need to discuss this with the emperor. At nearly eighteen, the girl should have long since learned the composure expected of a ruler. Cold and ruthless she might be, but Alexei saw in her the makings of a tyrant or, worse, a warmonger blind to duty. Over the years, he'd hoped to see improvement of some kind, but there never was. She'd only become increasingly cold and manipulative.

“Tell me,” Emperor Niro said, his voice carrying a surge of heart-energy that made the air itself hum. “What news does the golden-eyed soldier bring?”

The inquisitor responded first, bowing so deeply Alexei thought he might kiss the floor. “Your Imperial Majesty, I am honored that you have graced this lowly soul with your presence.” He turned to the chair where the captured soldier sat, bleeding, and dragged it forward so the man faced the throne. “Speak.”

The golden-eyed soldier remained silent for only a heartbeat before something squirmed beneath the skin above his ear. Alexei watched, half in awe, half in horror, as he realized it was a blood worm writhing along the man's skull like a pulsing, swollen vein.

The worm pulsed again, no doubt boring deeper into the soldier's brain, devouring whatever lingering protections remained in the soldier’s mind.

Then, like a damn bursting, the words spilled forth. Choppy and unwilling as they were, dragged out by the worm’s influence, they rang true to Alexei.

“My name is Martin Tulia, Second Lieutenant in service to His Grace, Collin Alistar, Duke of Lysoria.”

Emperor Niro’s smile widened. “I already know you serve Lysoria, but I suspect you have named the wrong master. Who do you now serve, Lieutenant?"

The soldier opened his mouth as if to speak, then closed it a moment later. His jaw worked silently for a few seconds before it opened again.

No words came.

Instead, an ear-piercing, agonizing scream tore itself from the man’s lips. Blood, bile, and spittle accompanied the inhuman cry, splattering unseemingly upon the red imperial carpet.

“My apologies, Your Imperial Majesty,” said the inquisitor, bowing again. “It appears the golden magic has not yet been completely dismantled.” He drew fresh symbols across the man’s neck in his own blood that writhed beneath the man's flesh like the bloodworm had done.

At last, the soldier resumed. “My… new liege… is Duchess Lilliana Alistar.”

A rustle went through the room as people recognized the name as being the prospective saintess the Church of Light had been droning on about recently.

“And what is her core level, Lieutenant Tulia?”

“G-g-gold,” the soldier stammered through a tightly clenched jaw.

“Her age?”

“Some… say… she is not yet fifteen.”

“Has she undergone full physical reformation?”

The soldier nodded, his entire body trembling and spasming with the effort of resisting the bloodworm. “Despite… her age… she has… reformed… to her true self.”

“Describe her,” the emperor growled, now leaning forward, white-knuckled fingers gripping the godsteel armrest of his throne. His fingers were clenched around the armrest so hard that Alexei was surprised the godking hadn't shattered it. “What does she look like? Tell me!” When the soldier stuttered simple physical descriptions such as the woman being tall and muscled, Alexei visibly watched his Lord's patience run out. “Have the blood worm pull the image from his mind,” the emperor snapped toward the inquisitor.

“That will kill him,” the empress said softly, placing a gentle hand on his shoulder. "He may still have infor-"

The emperor shrugged her off without a glance. “Do it, inquisitor. Now.”

Without raising his head from where it rested against the stone floor, the inquisitor released a pulse of magic from his mana core, and the blood worm cried out in pain. The soldier tilted his head back toward the sky, and golden-blue light exploded from his eyes, forming an image midair. It was clear. Radiant. Like starlight shaped into memory.

“This is what her appearance was during the fight with the monstrosity some months ago,” the inquisitor said, but Alexei doubted anyone was still listening.

Alexei might have considered her rather attractive if it wasn't for the cold ruthlessness radiating from her eyes like the heat of an exploding sun. Instead, just looking into them sent a shiver of true fear down his spine.

He blinked, his mind catching up to what he’d seen—and his jaw dropped.

No, not red eyes.

Crimson eyes.

And a scar, just like the princesses and the woman on the mural, tracked down her right eye.

“That’s her,” Emperor Niro breathed. The words were so quiet Alexei wouldn’t have caught them if he hadn’t been actively listening. Sometimes it paid to be only three seats from the emperor.

“Lilith.” The emperor’s eyes were wide, as if caught between disbelief and inevitability. “They were right,” he muttered. “She is here.”

Without hesitation, he slammed a closed fist into his armrest, the sharp crack shaking the room from whatever thoughts had gripped it.

“Tell our men to begin the planned insurrections in Tenebris, Lysoria, and Noctaron early.” He motioned to the princess. “You will head to Velora with the 4th and 5th battalions. Send word to Cael. They are to begin marching on Velora as well. I will handle our neighbors.”

Everyone present would know he was referring to the Holy Kingdom. Though typically an ally, the rumors of Duchess Lilliana Alistar being a Saintess of the Goddess of Light could complicate things. Killing her outright would not go unnoticed, though from the emperor's reaction to her appearance, Alexei doubted the emperor desired to kill the girl.

“Your Imperial Majesty,” Field Marshal Rubeus asked carefully, “what will we do if this woman is, in fact, a saintess?”

The emperor began to laugh.

It was not a merry sound. It reminded Alexei of an axe grinding against flesh and bone. No one interrupted as the godking laughed harder than Alexei thought a man could. When at last the laughter faded, the emperor leaned back into his throne, a low chuckle still rumbling from his chest. His eyes had narrowed to slits.

“This woman is no more a saintess than a demon of the nine hells could be,” he said. “I made certain of that.”
