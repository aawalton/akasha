Clark was just finishing his shower when the dragons attacked.

For some time, they had been washing in the muddy Tower Stream, bathing in their fenced-off section of the river as best as they had been capable with the mosses they had gathered for cloths and the crude soaps the Commander had alchemized into existence. Clark had been able to help with that sometimes, using [Unblemish] to cleanse certain stains or to get rid of any accumulated muck from his teammates. But other than for himself, that was not the most frequent means of cleaning.

Though once the Metalpress had been upgraded, Oliver had created a water tank out of copper, kept above head level and with a short pipe leading to a showerhead. With just some lashed reeds to make a privacy screen and some rough stones covered with a woven reed mat to make a floor, and they had an actual shower, with slightly warm water! Warmer than the stream, at least. The day heated things, but they rarely got hot like they had been sitting out in the sun. The nook that had been adapted for the shower was a little out of the way, for privacy, and that meant the view was not the best, but it was still quite possible to see the beautiful Jungle if Clark merely leaned to the side slightly.

The water itself was clean, of course. The copper tank had the same water purification enchantment that their water pool had, and with the more refined soaps the Commander had been making as of late, the entire experience was quite pleasant by any standard. Clark truly felt like he was at a tropical resort. Yes, it could be slightly inconvenient to refill the tank bucket by bucket, but that would probably go away soon, because Jacob was planning to relocate the tank to somewhere that the spider-bots could reach, just extending the pipes a bit to compensate.

Running water was truly a luxury most divine.

Their towel situation might have been less developed, but how was simply waiting a few minutes in such natural beauty to naturally dry off a true detriment? Clark spent his time stroking Tully, who absolutely hated the shower despite having no issues with swimming more generally and as such always came to obtain her due the moment Clark escaped from the terrifying rain-room.

Clark's first warning that something was off came from Tully, when her head jerked up and she looked off into the distance, towards the Junglewaste. Then she shook herself off and dove into a nearby shadow, vanishing except for the faint glimmer of her eyes, which themselves went away a moment later.

It took a moment for Clark to properly process the stunning betrayal of being abandoned mid-pat, but swiftly thereafter realized that he ought to investigate what was happening. A quick sweep of [Unblemish] finished drying him off, then he pulled on his clothes and jogged out to the main area, where Oliver was hastily tinkering with some clay tablet that had multiple crystals embedded in it.

"Clark!" Oliver jumped in surprise upon seeing him. "I think we're under attack?"

"I believe as much myself," Clark agreed. "Tully seems worried."

"I'm getting a mass of Dragon incoming that's consistent with some of Jacob's bigger fights, but..."

"We lack our Warrior. And our Ranger. And our Commander."

Oliver winced. "Yeah... hopefully Jacob can make it back here soon? Assuming his skill works when he's that far away? I don't know if we've tested range limits."

"At least we are not defenseless," Clark nodded, thinking of the ballistae and the inklings still patrolling the area.

"Yep. I don't like our odds, though."

"Ought we to retreat to the Spire?" Clark asked. "It would be the conservative action to take, and it is incredibly important that we do not die."

Clark paused for a moment, then added onto that. "Because this world is not Chained, we-"

"Yeah, yeah, I know," Oliver waved away Clark. "Okay, okay. What do we do. What.... Defend, because... we have more weapons at the walls?" he asked.

"Sounds good!" Clark readily agreed. "And we are always able to retreat afterwards, and if we can defend First Tower there will be less rebuilding."

Oliver inhaled sharply. "Right. The last time we had dragons overrun First Tower, a ton of magic went bad. And I don't want Dragon contaminating the wards. Definitely need to defend, then."

"Will your tablet aid us?" Clark asked. If Oliver sought to work on it while knowing they were in danger, did that make it some great and powerful weapon that they could use for their defense?

Oliver glanced down at it. "Not here. I got distracted trying to fix the audible alarm I made for this..."

Both of their attentions were drawn to The Jungle, as a big dragon like the Commander's inklings roared in the middle of the Junglewaste corridor.

"Dawdled too long," Oliver muttered, looking half frozen. Clark gave him a tug on the arm, getting them moving to the defenses.

"We must hurry!"

They ran over the creaking bridge across Tower Stream, veered away from the iron-smelting area, and got to the Ironroad Depot quickly. spider-bots scattered out of their way, and the heat from the Volcanic Crucible was notable but manageable.

By the time they reached the walls, the enemy dragon was being fought by three of the Commander's own - slightly smaller - inkling equivalents. It really was like watching a clash of titans, as all four of the big dragons had legs long enough step over the walls of First Tower and barely even notice them, and they were as tall as the Volcanic Crucible. Their sheer mass and wingspans was incredible, even if the attacking dragon would not be able to fly with its wings.

As one of the inklings lunged in for a bite, the attacking beast reacted more quickly, sinking its teeth into the inkling's throat, then shook its head back and forth, killing several of the scalewolf inklings that were also hassling it as well a handful of smaller dragons accompanying this attack, and only stopped when the inkling vanished into smokey shadows as it died.

THUNK

Oliver was surrounded by streams of magic, connecting him to the dozen artillery weapons lining the walls. He had his hands raised and was making finger guns at the fighting dragons. He was chanting something, and the magic frothed from his mouth like so much foam, dripping to the ground and coating his arms while he adjusted his aim and flicked his thumb.

THUNK

Another ballista fired, and this one struck the dragon in the chest. The scales on its chest imploded, and blood rushed from the wound. It did not sink in far enough though, and the weapon's shaft snapped a moment later. Clark, not wanting to leave things entirely to Oliver, grabbed the latest version of his steam cannon from the weapons platform - it was so great that it was now small enough that he could actually hold it in his hands - and ⟨Restore⟩d it to fully charged.

BOOMTHUNK

Both of them fired at the same time, and while Oliver's attack struck one of the inklings and instantly dispersed it, Clark's shot struck the attacking dragon in the head, which snapped back fast enough Clark would have worried about a broken neck and whiplash if it had been human. Its eye bled, and it was disoriented long enough for the only surviving inkling opponent to grab it by the neck and tear into its throat. Another THUNK, and a ballista bolt tore a hole clear through its neck, a jagged hole large enough for daylight to be visible through it.

It went down, and Clark inwardly cheered.

But before it fully died, a bunch of magic, drawn from the sandy ground it stood upon, flowed into its body and the holes in its throat began to glow with a vivid violet-red light. With a final dying roar, the big dragon breathed a torrent of incredibly potent Dragonfire into the sky, killing the inkling holding it down, then down more horizontally. Clark caught the briefest glimpse of runes appearing in midair where Oliver's wards tried to defend against the attack, only for them to shatter like so much broken glass.

Both he and Oliver ran, sheltering on the far side of the Volcanic Crucible as the fire collided with the Ironpile, melting much of the iron ore in it to slag, before punching through and taking out half the ballistae.

Then, the fire cut off and they cautiously emerged from their hiding place. The attacking dragon was dead, as were most of its smaller brethren. There were still some scalewolves able to engage with the survivors, and Clark reloaded his steam cannon to give them a bit of assistance.

"I guess we won?" Oliver hesitantly said. "I didn't want to jinx it earlier, but... I think we managed to defend successfully? Even if this is such a mess now..." He sighed. "We're going to need to trash so much hematite. And those wards are going to be a pain to fix..."

A dust plume along the Junglewaste caused them both to prepare for another fight, but when it resolved into the form of Warrior Veeran, Clark sighed in relief and happily waved to him.

"Are the two of you safe?" was the first thing he asked, and Oliver gave a thumbs-up while Clark verbally echoed the agreement.

"You were right about the heavy artillery!" Clark added. "It really is the best weapon!"

Cleanup was long and involved.

The wards breaking meant there was much less guiding of dragons and other threats to the front gates and the Ironroad Depot, and that meant he and Jacob needed to do patrols around the edges of First Tower to actually defend against those kinds of creatures, while Oliver worked on repairing the enchantments. After a bit of time, Alyssa and the Commander returned from their excursion to check in on how they were doing - Commander Inq had felt her inklings die and returned to ensure that everyone was still alive.

Having two more people able to fight and guard made it so that Clark could be more free to help with the cleanup processes. He spent a lot of time flushing out Dragon mana contamination from everything the dragon's breath had struck, and frequently helped Oliver as he fixed up everything both magically and physically. Oliver was annoyed, of course, so Clark let him be - needing to redo all of the work he had done to protect the camp had to be annoying. But he also kept upgrading things, which was great!

"Alright," Oliver eventually said when he slumped into dinner, nearly two weeks after the big dragon attack. "The walls are fully back online. I did do the thing you suggested, Alyssa. So now we have actual steel walls that are enchanted appropriately. I don't think they'll withstand anything too massive, nothing like those huge windstorms we got when the Junglewaste was first being made, but probably any creatures up to the size of the big dragons? Assuming they don't pull out anything stronger than that dragonbreath attack."

"It's almost inevitable that there'll be something eventually, right?" Alyssa asked. "Like, the dragonbreath thing was new, and we think the dragons are getting stronger. So this won't hold out forever."

"No," the Commander agreed. "We do need some better defenses, more active ones. Have you thought about what we discussed, Smith?"

"You need to be so much more specific than that."

"Regarding defenses," she began.

"That does not help," Oliver interrupted.

"Regarding turning spider-bots into a part of the defense, with weapons or the like."

"Oh," Oliver paused. "Still no, actually. Not much of a chance. I can probably manage it though, once we've got the Manufactorium working?"

"I assume we will have lost some progress by the time we return there," Henrietta replied. "But it should be nearly ready for your part, and you can begin your assembly there."

"That'll be useful, but yeah, this very much threw me off my groove. I have been thinking about modular systems of various types, so I'll probably run through a few sets of prototypes to see what works best. Oh, I want to get the helmets finished first, and then I need to retest to see what happens if I leave Broadcast Network range, make sure it doesn't seriously damage my spelloom..."

Clark gave Tully some scritches. He was glad he didn't need to think about those complicated things. He just needed to keep people functioning, and that was so much easier because there was never any question about what should be done. If someone was hurt or hurting, he needed to make them better. No complications. All of this might not have been busyness for the sake of busyness like his family so often liked to work with, but needing to figure out how things should be working and then try to get them working seemed so much harder.

"Gliders too," Oliver mused. "Lots of things to do, so little time to do it in."

"How can we help you?" Henrietta prompted. "Which things do you specifically need to do?"

"Pretty much all of it?" Oliver said. "I'm the only real enchanter, both in a designing and manufacturing sense, and basically everything I'm working on is enchantment-work. Except for the parts which involve me testing how my own body will act outside of the Network."

"Are there any parts which aren't directly enchanting-related? Things which... you could do a part of, the piece which actually requires [Cogniprint], and make that be as quick and as simple as possible, then let someone else do the remaining parts?"

Clark had a partial memory of the Commander encouraging Oliver to do similar things in the past, but it seemed to be an ongoing battle. Which he understood. Even if he could see how much better Oliver had gotten at adapting to his situation since they had first landed, old habits certainly could be hard to break!

Oliver paused for a moment. "No, I don't think so?"

"Are you certain?" Clark noticed the exact moment the Commander thought of something as her posture shifted, and she began trying to coax it out of their Artificer. "You were talking about modularity. Couldn't someone design some pieces, and then connect them via some common interface? Could someone else make that interface, or those interfaces if there's multiple?"

Oliver shook his head. "No, I especially need to be the one to create the standard, because I don't think anyone knows all of the weird things that could pop up in an improperly designed abstraction layer."

"Okay. So then what if you first make the interface, and then let people work with that?"

Oliver paused. "That... that's risky. I don't know what might be needed? Not until I build something and work through it properly. I can't design something like that in the abstract."

"Then what about designing something really generic, that you know you can make work? And then we can use that as the basis. Just the mechanical coupling, for example."

"Oh!" Oliver brightened. "So like the crates. I was thinking about how we could make standard-sized boxes, that we could then use for better transportation of things."

The Commander's face flashed through a couple of emotions as she processed what clearly hadn't been her idea, but Clark got to see as she settled on following through with Oliver's idea. "Yeah. Like that. Though I don't know how there could be any issues with magical compatibility there? And did you have any notes about what it would need?"

"I was thinking it might be useful if each larger size could fit the smaller size within it, for easy packing. So if we had one size that was a meter cubed, the next size would be two meters cubed, and that could hold four... eight of the smaller size."

"Alright, that makes sense. Haleford, would you be able to work on that? It seems well-suited for your skills." It took Clark a moment to realize he was being addressed, but he perked up.

"Certainly! So I should make nesting boxes out of wood? Steel?"

"Yes to both, I think. Don't forget that we need to pick them up afterwards."

"Yep!" Clark agreed, thinking about things. He grabbed a bit of clay and began to play with it, trying to get a feel for what he might do. "How big do you want them?"

"Yes? But if you mean what size should be the smallest, I don't know." Oliver answered. "Maybe like... this big?" he held his hands a little less than shoulder-width apart. "I really need to make a proper meterstick."

"I believe Veeran should be capable of that," Henrietta commented. "Perhaps some calipers?"

Jacob nodded. "I believe some screw calipers may be quite useful in that regard, to create a source of truth. And I can work on them while I oversee the railway construction."

"Good. For now, Smith, focus on preparing for an outing to make the Manufactorium, whatever that may entail. Ride and I shall finish preparing it, and Haleford and Veeran may continue expanding our logistics capacity. Sound good to everyone?"

It did, and so - after a most delightful night's sleep of course - they got back to work.
