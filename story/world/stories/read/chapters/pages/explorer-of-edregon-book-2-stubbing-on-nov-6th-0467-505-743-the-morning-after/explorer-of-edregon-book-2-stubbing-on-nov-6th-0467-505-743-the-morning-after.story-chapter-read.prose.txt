“Yo, Vin. This isn’t the first time I’ve left a party the next morning to find someone passed out on the ground. But I’ll admit, they don’t normally have such a nice set up going for themselves.”

Vin yawned, rubbing at his eyes as he spotted Alka standing in the doorway to the room Brian had shown him last night. After giving all the money over to the Battle Mage for her spell, Vin had decided to just pull out his shimmerwing mattress and spend the night in the basement. He figured everyone would be far too busy drinking themselves silly to even notice he was squatting, and if it was already morning, that meant he’d been right.

“How was the party?” he asked. Stretching his back, he got up and fished out a new set of clothing from his pack to change into. After letting Alka inhabit his own body for the better part of a month, there wasn’t anything he could show her she hadn’t seen a hundred times already.

“Probably would have been more fun if I could have actually drunk anything,” she admitted, glancing up toward the floor above them. “Most of them are still unconscious. Though credit where credit is due, these guys really know how to party. I think it’s closer to midday than morning. I’m sort of surprised I found you still in bed.”

“I had a late night working on magic,” Vin admitted, tucking his mattress away with Dimensional Sheathe while he smiled. “The Battle Mage gave me a tier-3 spell to work on, and I wanted to get some progress in. I’m already working on my own water-based tier-3 spell, and I don’t want to keep collecting more magic while I still have so many big spells unfinished.”

“Fair enough. What did she give you anyway?”

“Flaming Body,” Vin said excitedly. “It does exactly what it sounds like, causing the caster’s body to erupt in literal fire. Though unlike Self Destruct, the fire doesn’t hurt the mage! It’s designed to keep monsters from attacking them, and to prevent enemies from getting close.”

“Wow, a spell that doesn’t kill the one casting it,” Alka drawled, shaking her head. “Consider me impressed. She must be a talented mage indeed. But you really only got a single spell for all those gemstone-chip things? I don’t know how much they are worth any more than you do, but I got the feeling it wasn’t a small amount.”

“Tier-3 spells aren’t exactly easy to come by,” Vin argued. “I only have four at the moment, and I had to put them all together myself. I do need to be a little bit careful though, this will officially be my ninth fire spell. Too many more and I risk giving myself an affinity toward fire, even with my giant mana pool.”

“Huh. Didn’t realize you had so many fire spells at this point,” Alka admitted. “Anyway, what’s the plan? We’ve still got two more fragments left to hit up for the third ring. Want to head out before everyone wakes up, or would you rather keep working on your magic?”

“As much as I want to crack down and get these two spells finished… finishing the third ring should probably be the priority,” Vin sighed. “Though I will admit, this fragment has been nice. Been a while since we encountered a regular town of folk without anything dire going on.”

“Let’s hope the trend continues. At the very least, we know there’s a fragment of minotaurs and dwarves somewhere nearby.”

Decision made, Vin finished packing up his gear before heading upstairs. Just like Alka had said, the room that had held the party last night was a mess. While a good third or so of the partygoers looked like they’d managed to stumble on home at some point during the night, there were still dozens of people slumped and scattered all over the room. Warriors hung half-off fallen barrels that were now presumably empty of any sort of alcohol, and scrawnier towns folk were draped over one another in a pile that looked suspiciously man made. Snoring, coughing, and groaning filled the room as a few people started to rouse here and there, and Vin shook his head while they wove their way through the mess.

It’s times like this that make me sort of glad I’m not a big drinker.

Once they were outside, Vin took the lead and brought the two of them to the edge of town. He wished he’d saved at least a few of those gemstone chips for a hot meal, but he had plenty of rations to tide himself over.

“Let’s seek out the dungeon before we head into the next fragment,” Vin decided, focusing on Dungeoneering as he took off running. He knew his class would want him to spend the time needed to more thoroughly explore the dungeon, but they weren’t too pressed for time just yet.

As they ran, Vin took a look at his interface. Specifically at the fact that he was now up to three skill points again. He’d been keeping one in reserve for when Dungeoneering managed to hit level 20, and he wanted to be able to evolve Swimming when it hit level 20 as well. But three skill points meant he officially had one left over, and he had a skill that had been sitting at level 20 for a bit now that he was admittedly curious as to what it would turn into.

Level 20 Running is capable of skill evolution. Spend a skill point to evolve skill?

Don’t mind if I do, he smiled, accepting the prompt and tossing his newest skill point at the skill.

Running lvl 20 has evolved into Advanced Running lvl 1! 200 exp gained.

“Huh. That’s kind of a letdown,” Vin muttered, dismissing the notification as he kept moving. He knew from his friends that plenty of skill evolutions weren’t all that glamorous, but still, he’d been hoping for something a little more interesting. Scule himself had Advanced Herbalism as a skill, so he’d been prepared for something like this. Yet while the skill didn’t turn into anything crazy like Spellcraft to Runecraft or Meditation to Introspection, Vin couldn’t deny that he felt its effects in action. After he’d first picked up the running skill and had proper form and balance beamed into his head by the Gods, he’d thought he was an expert. As it turned out, there was still more to running he had to learn, such as breathing correctly and shifting his weight with the angle of the ground he was running over.

“I am prepared to look at your friend’s soul again whenever you are,” Ovord said, nearly causing Vin to trip and crash into a tree rather than run around it.

“Damn it, Ovord, I keep forgetting you’re around,” Vin said, motioning for Alka to come to a stop. “Ovord is ready to take another crack at your soul. Let’s pause for a second.”

Just like the last time, Vin conjured up a stone table for her to lay on before allowing Ovord control over his body. And also just like the last time, Ovord spent the entirety of his three minutes simply staring at Alka, not doing so much as laying a finger on her before he was pulled back into the recesses of Vin’s mana pool.

“Interesting. Allow me to sit on this until tomorrow. Another day or two of study with your Beyond the Veil, and I believe I will be prepared to attempt brushing against her soul.”

Vin relayed the news to Alka, who shook her head as she hopped off the table.

“Talk about a tease. Seriously, I didn’t realize this process was going to take so long.”

“Better safe than sorry,” Vin shrugged, using Stone Shape to wipe away his table and push the stone into the earth. “With that done, back to the dungeon!”

The start of the dungeon didn’t end up being anything too exciting, revealing itself to be another forest-type dungeon similar to Terra’s own and even filled with beast-type monsters as well. Yet while Vin and Alka didn’t have any difficulty scything and blasting their way through small swarms of what looked like tiny bears with spiked tails, the dungeon ended up having at least one surprise for them.

A powerful screech rang out through the forest, and Vin glanced up from the latest tiny bear he’d just blasted apart with a punch from his golem arm. Across the clearing, staring at them with large, dark eyes, was a rat the size of a small bus. The monster had spiked quills covering its body and two powerful tails thrashing around in the grass behind it, and it screeched again before it began running at them.

New elite monster discovered! 500 exp gained.

“Now we’re talking!” Alka laughed, eagerly racing to meet this new foe. Holding back for the moment, Vin watched as Alka twisted to avoid a powerful swipe from one of the monster’s clawed hands before bringing her sword down to cut deeply into the tail that swept out from behind it and tried to send her flying. Yet while she’d cut into the first tail, the second one hidden right behind the first smashed into her, knocking her back and slamming her into a tree.

Vin blinked as Alka ripped herself free from the tree, surprised that she’d taken a hit at all. It wasn’t often Alka struggled with any sort of monster, though he had to admit the giant rat’s tails were practically cracking like whips. The fact that she’d managed to keep up with them at all with her golem body was a testament to her skill.

With one tail cut in half, the monster screeched again before surprising them both by turning and making a mad dash for Vin.

“Stone Wall,” Vin started with. His eyes widened when the rat leapt over the wall, landing on top before lunging the rest of the distance between the two of them. Summoning his staff to his hand, Vin planted it onto the ground with a grin. The full weight of the rat crashed into him, and promptly came to a dead stop when he refused to budge so much as an inch. While the rat recoiled and tried to recover, Vin lifted his staff again, freeing him from his own inability to move before pulling back and punching the rat in the chest with his golem arm. His fist flashed a brilliant blue and the rat was sent flying back into the wall he’d created, shattering it as it tumbled to the ground.

“That thing is tough,” Vin called out, watching in amazement as the rat somehow got back to its feet and glared at him. Part of its chest was caved in and blood was still pouring out of where Alka had severed one of its tails, but the rat didn’t look done in the slightest. Of course, by that point Alka had managed to catch back up to it, and the Slayer took advantage of the rat getting its feet back under it in order to carve deep into its flank, severing both tails from the root this time around.

Screeching, the rat twisted and tried to sink its jagged teeth the size of Vin’s own arm into Alka’s head, but the Slayer was too fast for it. Ducking under the blow, Dream Eater gleamed as it twisted and spun upward, cleaving straight through the rat’s neck and severing its head in a single slash. Just like that the battle was over, and Alka grunted as the rat and its head crashed to the ground in two pieces.

“Talk about a durable one,” she agreed, wiping Dream Eater clean of blood on the monster’s fur before sheathing it. “You good?”

“Better than ever,” Vin said, walking over and poking at the monster. “What about you? Haven’t seen you take a hit like that in a while.”

“This body can’t keep up with my skill, nor some of the deadlier monsters we fight,” Alka sighed. “Not that I’m complaining, as being all but indestructible is a pretty worthy trade off. But this is why I’m so excited to get reconnected to the System. If I could just start raising my attributes again…”

“We’ll get there,” Vin said, laying a reassuring hand on her shoulder as he smiled. “No matter what it takes, we won’t stop until we get you connected and leveling up again alongside the rest of us.”
