Elion watched the warlocks coming. They must have gone to Boff first, who’d told them where Keyla was. They’d probably brought her bondhold.

“Where are you going with her?” Yaret asked, eyes wide.

“Away,” Elion said. “We need to stay hidden. You should probably leave too; or else they’ll want to question you about us.”

“I need to get back to my job,” Yaret said. “But it’s up in the refinery. They’re in the way. Though, there is another way out of the pit, over on the other side. The pit is dangerous at night, because it’s easy to stumble into a pool. But I think I’ll take the far path to get back up to the refinery.” He slipped into the darkness, gesturing for Elion to follow. Elion smiled at the man’s strange, motivated reasoning, which would allow him to help them and still follow his orders.

Elion and Yaret moved along the bottom of the acid pit. Some light from the refinery dimly lit the gravel around them, but most of the space was covered in thick darkness. Toxic fumes burned at Elion’s face, stinging his nostrils.

“We probably should have grabbed suits,” Yaret muttered.

“Too late for that now.”

“I think there are a few spares in the emergency locker by the pumps.”

*How far does the preservation of 'Save a Friend’ go?* Elion wondered. *If I dropped Keyla into one of these pools, would she be protected from acid until the ability wore off?*

They picked their way slowly along the path, two dark shadows moving between the pools of acid at the bottom of the pit.

They reached the lidding area. The long rows of barrels had been mostly moved up to the refinery, leaving the area broad and flat.

“Yaret,” Elion whispered. “Why doesn’t the acid dissolve the barrels. How can the machines pump it up, without melting down all the time?”

“It’s a special lining,” Yaret said. “They put it inside the barrels. It doesn’t dissolve in the acid. And they use those vats of grease over there to protect the outsides of the pumps.”

“So if you dropped a barrel into an acid pool, it would dissolve the metal and leave behind a liner?”

“No,” Yaret said. “The dissolving metal would heat up and melt the liner away.”

Elion shifted Keyla’s unconscious body on his shoulder. He felt bad for abducting her like this, but it was better than letting Venya get ahold of her. *Once she wakes up, will she feel like she needs to go back to the warehouse? Her order was ‘don’t leave,’ not ‘go back.’*

Behind them the warlocks and their arachnatronics reached the warehouse. They seemed ready for a fight.

Elion picked out the machine that had captured his knife. A man beside it was gesticulating wildly, leaning over the control panel of the box, and then pointing deeper into the pit, toward Elion.

“We’re not going to make it out of here,” Elion said. “They’re going to search for us here.”

“Hide in the barrels?” Yaret suggested.

“I already tried that,” Elion said. “Keyla sealed me in. I was almost suffocated.”

“Don’t seal the lids then?”

“What if they look?” Elion said. He coughed, and pulled his shirt over his mouth, trying to protect his lungs from the fumes. “They have a machine with them that can track my location, so it won’t work for me.”

“Got a better idea?”

“Maybe,” Elion said. “But it might be a bad one.”

Working quickly, he placed Keyla into an empty barrel, then set a lid over the top of it. He moved over to where the pumps were mounted at the side of the acid pool.

He ducked behind the stationary pumps, hoping to avoid attracting attention by lighting up, then he activated his armor. His breastplate materialized, new gold etched designs swirling around its perimeter.

“Ah,” Yaret said. “That makes sense! You’re a knight! An Ascendent, like her!”

Elion nodded, then pulled on a spare protective jumpsuit over his armor, feeling a sense of relief as he breathed in partially filtered air. He pulled open the crate of anti-acid grease and dipped his arms into it. They came out smeared in a thick layer of goo.

He knelt beside the edge of the acid pool and leaned forward, carefully dipping one hand into the liquid. The suit did not hiss or steam, the way that it would if the acid was dissolving it. The pool may as well have just been water.

“This will work,” Elion said, pulling his hand from the pool. The acid wicked off of his greased hand, beading up and dripping away like water off a duck’s back. No droplets tried to stick or stay behind.

He started smearing a thick layer of grease over his body.

Yaret helped rub the anti-acid grease over Elion. “Every knight needs a squire to help him with his armor,” he said.

“This is pretty untraditional armor,” Elion said.

Once he’d been thoroughly coated, Elion disarmed. The protective suit covered in grease disappeared. *I hope the grease stays on it.*

The burning sting of the acid pits returned to his nostrils.

“I need to get my knife back,” Elion said. “This is the best way to do it.”

“You surprise someone who thinks they know where you are,” Yaret mused. “Brilliant.”

“You have to start heading back. Keep walking, they may not even run into you.”

“And miss out on all the fun?” Yaret asked. “I’d love to see you beat up some warlocks. Ever since they burned my house down, I’ve longed to see someone put them in their place.

Elion grinned. “Watch from a distance,” he said. “Besides, don’t you have orders?”

“I’m on my way to do my job,” Yaret said. “This is the scenic route.” He shrugged. “I’ll get there. I’m not disobeying anything yet.” *Yaret has figured out how to find wiggle room in orders, probably from his years of working as a bondhold.*

Still, Yaret started walking slowly deeper into the pit. Elion watched him go, grateful for the man’s help.

Angry shouts came from the warehouse as warlocks stormed in, and found the space vacant. Boff was probably getting an earful. The warlocks gathered, outside the warehouse. They weren’t wearing protective suits. Elion wondered if they’d even try searching for him right now, in the dark, without protection.

Most of the group headed back to the elevator. One Arachnatronic remained, and the machine was loaded up onto it. They started heading toward Elion.

Elion scowled. They weren’t going to make this easy for him. He moved back over to the acid pool, watching the group as they tried to find the path through the pools in the dark. He spotted three warlocks on foot, and the man riding on the arachnatronic with the machine.

He took a deep breath, and choked on the tangy reek of the acid pool beside him, stifling a cough. Elion knew he couldn’t take three warlocks in a fair fight without his knife. But if he could get the drop on them, and recover his blade, then maybe he stood a chance.

For a half second he considered running off with Yaret. But he knew that they’d just follow him. And he wasn’t just going to leave Keyla down here, alone and unprotected.

He pulled open the control panel of the pump near him. The machine had a few switches on it. He examined it. The whole pump appeared to be made of reclaimed parts from other machines. Come to think of it, none of the pumps that Elion had seen looked alike. Probably repaired and patched up over decades.

There also weren’t any of the safety mechanisms on the pump that Elion would have expected. The pump had a receptacle for an empty barrel, which would be filled with acid. But as far as he could tell, nothing would stop him from running the pump without a barrel there, letting acid flow onto the ground and over the pump mounting points.

He grabbed an empty barrel and pushed it into position, then fired up the machine. It ran with a low whirr, liquid splattering down. The pump might not have been very safe, but it did run fast, quickly filling the barrel.

Elion stopped pumping when the barrel was half filled, and moved it out of the machine and over to a ledge near the path. He repeated the process three times, watching as the warlocks approached with their arachnatronic.

He wondered if they had one of the special acid pit arachnatronics with them; one that had it’s joints treated with the grease to prevent the caustic vapors in the air from interfering with the machinery inside the machine.

They had probably seen him at this point, but he ducked behind the pump. He wanted them confused.

*Okay, you can do this.* He pulled his shirt over his mouth, breathing slowly, trying to calm himself without choking on the acid vapors. His eyes stung and watered.

The warlocks approached.

“I swear I saw someone over here,” one of the robed figures was saying.

“I’m telling you, he’s hiding underground,” called the man from he back of the arachnatronic. “He’s got to be in the tunnels. When we triangulate his position—” Elion recognized the whiny voice. He’d heard it before, when he’d hidden in a barrel from the same man.

“The maps didn’t align with any of the positions that you indicated,” the warlock said.

“They’re not complete,” the man whined. “I’m almost done with my vertical indication gage, and then we’ll be able to see exactly where he is. We shouldn’t be out here at night, risking the equipment in all these fumes!” He coughed loudly, to make his point.

“Dave, quit complaining.”

Elion crept around behind his prepared barrels of acid. Light shone off of Dave’s head, as he rode the arachnatronic. He didn’t look around, but remained hunched over his device, the one he used to trace Elion.

The search party approached, beams of light from the arachnatronic sweeping across the path.

*Just a little closer…*

When they walked into range, Elion sprang into action.
