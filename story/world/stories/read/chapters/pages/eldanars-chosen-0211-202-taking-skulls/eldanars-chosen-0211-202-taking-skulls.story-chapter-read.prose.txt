The initial, explosive momentum of the rescue charge had slowly evaporated. It was violently replaced by a grinding, suffocating war of pure attrition. Josh stood at the centre of the defensive circle, his heavy steel boots sinking deep into the foul-smelling sludge. The sheer, physical pressure of the undead horde pressing inward was astronomical. It felt exactly like trying to hold back a collapsing stone wall with his bare hands.

Every single breath he drew tasted of dust, stagnant water, and the sharp, coppery tang of old blood. The noise was absolutely deafening. It was a cacophony of rusted iron scraping against pristine steel, the wet tearing of rotting meat, and the relentless, unified clicking of a thousand skeletal jaws.

"Do not let the circle break!" Josh roared. His voice strained painfully to carry over the din of battle, echoing off the sheer limestone walls of the gorge. "Keep your shields locked! Brace your boots!"

To his immediate right, a heavily scarred man let out a sharp, breathless cry of pain. A corrupted, skeletal dire wolf had managed to slip its rotting, fleshless jaws past the rim of his heavy iron shield. The beast had used the slippery mud to its advantage, sliding low under a sweeping mace strike. Its jagged fangs clamped down brutally on the man's forearm. The iron chainmail crumpled under the immense, unnatural bite force.

The man stumbled backwards, his boots losing their purchase in the freezing slime. His shield dropped a crucial few inches, breaking the solid wall of steel that was keeping them alive.

Instantly, the horde reacted to the momentary gap. Three more ghouls surged forward, their bodies a terrifying mass of bruised, purple flesh and elongated limbs. They scrambled over the back of the clamped wolf, their jagged claws swiping wildly at the fighter's exposed chest and throat.

Before the fatal blows could land, the dark, smoky air above them practically split open.

A searing, highly compressed lance of white-hot plasma struck the ground directly in front of the faltering mercenary. Brett was operating with laser-focused precision from the ridge thirty feet above. The elemental heat was terrifying. It instantly vapourised the three lunging ghouls, turning their purple flesh and brittle bones into fine, glowing grey ash that washed warmly over the mercenary's terrified face. The freezing mud beneath the strike flash-boiled, sending up a thick plume of foul-smelling steam.

A split second later, a brilliant orb of concentrated light dropped from the sky, courtesy of Carcan. It did not explode like a typical offensive spell. Instead, it expanded into a translucent, shimmering golden dome that briefly covered the injured fighter. The holy energy hissed violently against the skeletal wolf still clamped to his arm, burning the dark necrotic magic animating the beast. The wolf shrieked a hollow, rattling sound, its jaws loosening just enough for the mercenary to rip his bleeding arm free. With a roar of pure adrenaline, the man violently crushed the beast's skull with the heavy, spiked pommel of his mace.

"Close the gap!" Ronald hissed, sliding smoothly into the space beside the injured man before another wave of undead could exploit it.

The feline beastkin was entirely in his element despite the horrific conditions. He did not possess the heavy, thick armour or the brute, anchoring strength of the shield-bearers, but he possessed an impossible, liquid agility. The mud that bogged down the heavy fighters seemed to barely affect him.

Ronald ducked gracefully under a sweeping, decapitating strike from a rusted halberd wielded by a towering skeletal orc. His twin hunting daggers flashed with lethal intent in the dim light. He did not aim for the thick bone of the torso. He drove the pristine steel blades deep into the knee joints of the massive creature, twisting his wrists sharply to completely sever the magical anchors holding the bones together.

As the massive undead orc collapsed forward into the sludge, Ronald used its falling, rusted shoulder plate as a springboard. He vaulted backward, flipping through the freezing air to land perfectly within the safety of the shield wall just as a fresh wave of thick, grey necrotic webbing slapped heavily into the mud where he had been standing a fraction of a second before.

"The webs are too thick!" a dual-wielding rogue shouted from the left flank. She was frantically trying to slice through a sticky, calcifying net that had draped heavily over her leather-clad shoulders. Her razor-sharp daggers were struggling to cut the unnatural material. "We are getting bogged down! If we stay here, they will bury us!"

Josh knew she was absolutely right. They could not afford to remain static in the centre of the pit. If they stayed in a defensive circle, the massive Crypt Weavers clinging to the sheer limestone walls above would simply entomb them in necrotic silk, wrapping them up for the horde to slaughter at their leisure. They had to keep moving towards the trapped Iron Gauntlet party, who were still fighting a desperate, losing battle against the rock face twenty yards away.

"On my mark, we push the line!" Josh commanded, raising his heavy steel longsword high above his head to signal the advance.

He closed his eyes for a fraction of a second, tuning out the horrific noise of the gorge. He drew upon the deep, humming reservoir of his mana. The energy felt denser now, more potent and responsive to his will. He triggered Stormforged Adamantite.

Raw, blinding blue electricity surged violently down his right arm, completely engulfing the steel blade in a cage of high-voltage static. The air in the immediate vicinity of the sinkhole filled with the sharp, acidic tang of ozone, cutting cleanly through the overwhelming stench of decay and rotting meat.

"Now!" Josh bellowed, his voice echoing with absolute authority.

He dropped his shoulder and triggered Adamantine Breach. He did not aim for a single target. He launched himself directly into the thickest, most densely packed section of the horde blocking their path to the trapped party.

He hit the wall of rotting bodies like a runaway siege engine. The concussive shockwave of the skill threw a dozen minor skeletons and corrupted hounds entirely off their feet, shattering their brittle bones against the sheer limestone walls with a deafening series of cracks. Josh swept his electrified blade in a massive, horizontal arc.

The static discharge exploded outward. It violently scrambled the dark magic holding the dungeon spawn together, causing them to collapse into loose piles of bone, whilst simultaneously cauterising the rotting flesh of the native beasts in a blinding flash of blue light.

The shield wall behind him surged forward into the newly created gap. Their heavy boots churned the black mud as they fought bitterly for every single inch of ground.

But the horde absorbed the devastating punishment with terrifying, mindless apathy. For every rotting beast Josh cut down, two more scrambled wildly over the smoking corpses to take its place. They were a tidal wave of bone and iron, pressing constantly against the small pocket of living fighters.

A massive, mutated Plague Bear, easily twice the size of a normal dire bear, reared up on its hind legs directly in Josh's path.

This beast was far older and more decayed than the one they had fought in the forest. Its flesh was actively sloughing off its exposed, yellowed ribs in wet, sickening clumps. Highly toxic, bright green gas vented continuously from deep, open wounds across its chest and neck. The smell alone was enough to make Josh’s eyes water and his stomach heave. The monstrosity brought two massive, skeletal paws down in a devastating, crushing strike aimed squarely at Josh's helmet.

Josh planted his steel boots deep into the mud and instantly activated Titan's Anchor.

The shimmering, high-voltage shield of raw blue energy flared to life, rooting him entirely to the earth. He became a fixed, immovable point in reality. The massive, rotting bear slammed heavily into the magical barrier.

The kinetic force was staggering. The impact blew a ring of black mud outwards in a perfect circle. Josh's boots were driven a full inch deeper into the freezing sludge, the thick slurry rising past his ankles. His teeth ground together so hard his jaw ached, and his shoulder screamed in protest, but the magical anchor held firm. He did not yield a single inch of ground.

The magical shield lashed back instantly. A massive jolt of raw electricity blasted up the bear's exposed skeletal arms, causing its dead muscles to violently seize and spasm, temporarily stunning the behemoth and halting its toxic breath.

Before Josh could drop the anchor to strike back with his sword, a heavy, steel-tipped broadhead arrow descended from the darkness high above.

Perberos had found his angle. The elven ranger was operating with chilling, mechanical patience on the ridge. The arrow struck the Plague Bear directly in the centre of its glowing green eye socket, burying itself deep into the necrotic core hidden within the shattered skull. The massive beast crumbled instantly, its bulk collapsing heavily into the mud and sending up a massive splash of freezing water.

"Keep moving!" Brett's voice echoed loudly from the ridge, magnified clearly by a simple acoustic spell. "The Weavers are shifting position! You have seconds before the next volley of webs falls!"

Josh dropped his energy shield and stepped over the massive, smoking corpse of the bear, leading the wedge forward once more.

They were only ten yards away from the trapped party now. He could clearly see the exhausted, towering tank of the Iron Gauntlet group through the swirling mist and falling ash. The man was down on one knee in the freezing mud, his heavy rectangular shield buckling dangerously under the relentless, snapping assault of a dozen skeletal hounds. The cleric standing behind him was completely out of mana, resorting to frantically swinging a small, silver-plated mace to keep the beasts at bay.

The rescue squad was taking heavy punishment to cross those final ten yards.

A heavy fighter to Josh's left took a glancing blow from a rusted iron club wielded by a towering ghoul. The rusted weapon struck the side of his steel helmet. The metal dented inward with a sickening crunch. The man staggered sideways, his vision swimming dizzily, dark blood pouring freely from a deep gash above his eyebrow.

Instantly, a concentrated beam of soothing golden light shot down from the ridge, piercing the gloom.

Carcan had not missed a beat. The healing magic hit the staggering fighter perfectly, forcefully knitting the torn skin on his forehead and clearing the blinding concussion from his eyes. It allowed the veteran to raise his iron shield just in time to block a thrusting, rusted spear that would have otherwise impaled his throat.

They were surviving purely on the flawless, highly coordinated support of the ranged fighters maintaining their overwatch above them.

Perberos and the other archers were a relentless, mechanical force. They ignored the chaotic, bloody melee on the ground entirely. Their bowstrings hummed a constant, deadly melody. They spent their time tracking the massive, skull-fused Crypt Weavers scuttling along the vertical limestone walls of the gorge.

Every single time a Weaver turned its bulbous, glowing abdomen outward to fire a heavy net of necrotic webbing, a coordinated volley of heavy steel arrows would strike its body, seeking the magical core. The massive, bone-plated spiders would shriek, lose their grip on the wet, slippery rock, and plummet into the pit. Their massive, bony weight would crush dozens of their own lesser undead beneath them as they crashed into the mud.

"Five yards!" Ronald yelled, his voice cutting through the noise.

His twin daggers were a blur of muted green motion. The beastkin vaulted cleanly over the snapping jaws of a rotting wolf, landing directly behind the creature to cleanly sever its spine before kicking the corpse into the path of an advancing ghoul. "Push through! Do not stop your momentum!"

Josh battered a path through the final, desperate ranks of the horde blocking their way.

The physical toll was immense. His lungs burned with every breath of the freezing, foul air. His sword arm ached with a dull, persistent agony from the constant, jarring impacts against rusted iron and thick bone. His heavy steel plate armour, once polished and pristine, was completely covered in a thick, freezing layer of black mud and foul-smelling, congealed gore.

He stepped heavily over a dissolving pile of golden dust, smashing a skeletal warrior aside with his shield, and finally broke out of the dense, suffocating pack.

He was standing right beside the towering tank of the Iron Gauntlet party.

The exhausted man looked up. His face was pale and drawn, his eyes wide with a chaotic mixture of absolute disbelief and overwhelming relief. His massive rectangular shield was terribly dented, bearing the deep scratches of a hundred claws. His breath was coming in ragged, painful gasps, a thin line of blood trickling from his nose. The rogue and the cleric behind him were leaning heavily against the freezing limestone wall, their legs shaking, barely able to stand under their own power.

"You took your sweet time," the tank wheezed, his voice hoarse. He forced a weak, bloody grin onto his face as he pushed himself painfully up from his knee, refusing to show weakness now that salvation had arrived.

"We got caught in the traffic. You know how it is, the roads are backed up with carts!" Josh replied grimly, offering no smile in return.

Josh stepped firmly into the mud beside the stranger. He slammed his own deeply gouged steel shield against the rim of the man's rectangular one, physically linking their defensive line with a loud, resounding clang of metal.

The rest of the rescue squad poured into the small, concave pocket against the sheer rock face. They moved with practised military efficiency, quickly forming a tight, heavily armoured half-circle around the injured and exhausted Iron Gauntlet members.

For a brief, fleeting second, a triumphant cheer rippled through the exhausted adventurers on the ground.

They had reached their primary objective. They had successfully navigated the horrific, freezing mud. They had survived the rain of necrotic webs, and they had broken through an impossible, writhing swarm of undead to save their own people. The adrenaline of the successful charge flooded their veins.

But the cheering died almost instantly, replaced by a cold, settling dread.

Josh looked out over the rim of his heavy shield. The sea of clicking bone, snapping jaws, and glowing green eyes had not thinned in the slightest. The horde had simply absorbed their heavy, punishing charge. They had stepped over their own dead, re-formed their chaotic ranks, and completely closed the circle around the newly combined group.

They were pressed hard against the sheer, vertical rock wall of the sinkhole. There was no back exit. The black mud beneath their boots was thick, freezing, and treacherous, severely limiting their mobility. The dark, necrotic fog continued to leak heavily into the pit, making it difficult to breathe, and worse, more Crypt Weavers were beginning to crawl over the lip of the gorge high above them, replacing the ones Perberos had shot down.

They had successfully reached the trapped party, but the reality of their situation was utterly horrific. They were now simply a slightly larger group of people trapped at the bottom of a very deep, very deadly hole.

"Status?" Josh demanded, his storm-grey eyes fixed intently on the writhing wall of monsters currently preparing to launch a fresh, coordinated assault against their newly formed shield wall.

"We have two critically injured," the Iron Gauntlet cleric reported from the back of the pocket. The man's voice was shaking badly, his hands trembling as he clutched a useless wooden holy symbol. "Our mage is completely spent, and my healing spells are entirely depleted. We have nothing left to give."

"Then you stay in the very centre and keep your heads down," Josh ordered, adjusting his grip on the leather handle of his electrified sword, feeling the familiar, reassuring hum of the magic coursing through the steel.

He looked up at the ridge, seeking the faint, pulsing glow of Carcan's staff and the bright, dancing white flames around Brett's hands. The ranged support was their only lifeline right now, but even they could not keep the immense horde at bay forever. Brett’s mana pool, while massive, was not infinite, and Perberos only had so many arrows in his quiver.

The rescue mission was only half complete. They had found their people in the dark, bleeding and exhausted, but now, they had to fight their way back out of the pit.
