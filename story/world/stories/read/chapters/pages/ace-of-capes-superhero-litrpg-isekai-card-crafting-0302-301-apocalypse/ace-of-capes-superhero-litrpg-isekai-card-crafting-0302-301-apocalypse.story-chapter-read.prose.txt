Something was about to happen.

Max could practically feel it in the air, his keen senses tingling. Luke would call it superstition, but Max preferred to call it his extra-sensory perception, which he believed the system was somehow still ignorant of.

He always knew when trouble was brewing, and right now he could sense something coming closer.

It didn't shock him. These days, he would be hard-pressed to have a day without some kind of calamity visiting his doorstep.

He cracked his neck to release the tension and then sniffed the air once more. No smoke. His wards weren’t going off either to warn of intruders. He’d created new ones, with separate alarms for the glowing humans that called themselves Lightlarks, but the bastards could be sneaky when they wanted to.

Xena had promised they wouldn't ambush Lexie again, but Max didn't trust them worth a dadmn.

Speaking of Xena…

He sighed.

He really wished she hadn’t done what she did. He understood she was worried, but going to the Lightlarks about Lexie was not the right move. And he was surprised she'd done it. The Xena he knew hadn't been the type to kowtow to authority and betray their best friend for a bunch of practical strangers who called themselves her family. As far as Max was concerned, those people weren't her family.

Emma was her family. And Luke would be too once they got married, which should probably be by next spring. Though they didn't say it, he knew they were putting it off till he was in a better mindspace, till he could be around regular people again without wanting to chew his skin off.

Max would have to wear a suit. He wasn't looking forward to it.

But hopefully Xena and Lexie would be friends again by the wedding. He hated when they fought. Though he tried to stay out of preteen drama, he cared about both of them too much not to care that their friendship was fracturing.

Hopefully, though, it would mend soon again.

And hopefully, this ordeal had taught Xena that the Lightlarks were nothing but the Fae's puppets, doing whatever theywanted.

Xena likely regretted what she’d done, but she was too stubborn to turn her back on the people who'd set her on that path in the first place. She was now making Emma eat dinner with them at their fancy embassy so they could get to know each other and mend bridges.

Ridiculous. As long as they didn't come near the Sparrowfoots, Max would let them keep living.

He yawned and stared out at the grass. He didn't know how long he'd been sitting on the porch, but he was waiting for Monty to arrive. He'd finally completed the gun he wanted, and he was ready for the rest of his pay.

But instead of Monty, he saw Frank stalking towards him, with that determined look in his eyes.

Probably here to rant about the Fae again.

“Hey Frank,” he said. “Listen, this really isn’t a good time–" He didn't manage to get all the words out of his mouth before another man launched himself at him.

Monty spotted Dee at the back of the semi-crowded bar and grinned.

She saw him and her face dropped.

“You look terrible,” she said,

"You’re one to talk. You look just as terrible as I do.”

“No, I don’t. I’m a saintess, I couldn’t look terrible if I tried.”

He chuckled, sliding into the booth opposite her. She'd already ordered fries and beer for him and was looking at him expectantly.

"You haven't been sleeping," Monty said as he picked up a fry and handed it to her.

She glanced down at the fry hungrily but was already shaking her head in denial. "You know I–"

"Dee, the world could be ending in five minutes," he said. "Literally and figuratively. Everything's going to shit. The association's falling apart. My father is out in the wild again. The Emperor could drop a large bomb on all of us right now and wipe us out completely. So, I think you can get away with one fry today."

She grinned weakly, and instead of taking it with her hand, she leaned forward and playfully closed her mouth over the end of the fry, tugging it away.

She held eye contact the entire time, and he swallowed and looked away. Monty knew what that was. She was flirting with him, and it wasn't the first time.

Ever since that 'not a date' two months ago, she'd been doing it more often.

But he wasn't ready. He might never be ready.

He might never get rid of the demon that haunted him, and he didn't want Dee getting dragged into that.

"I can't stay long," he told her as she washed down with beer. "I have to go pick up a gun from..."

He trailed off when he noticed a shift in the air. It had gone still.

Everyone stopped talking at the same time, including Dee, who froze with the beer on her mouth dripping down onto her shirt.

Her eyes were wide and panicked.

Monty's world narrowed into tunnel vision. His heart stopped, his mind clamoring.

A feeling of vertigo made him feel nauseous, his muscles clenching, urging him to run while he forced himself to sit still.

You can't hide from him.

Oh no.

You never could.

Please no.

But it was undeniable. His father was here.

Lexie and Aiden stared as the fog outside the window thickened.

It was painful to think of how easy it would have been to avoid this. How easily they'd been tricked into coming in here, with just a little careless invitation. And now they were in a shack with a maniac.

Not a shack. Some kind of temple. That was part of why it felt familiar. It was Janus'.

Her father came over to her and put his hand on her shoulder, grounding her chaotic thoughts. She met his eyes, which were calm and determined as though to say, No matter what, I'll protect you.

She released a breath.

At least there was some slight comfort to be had in the fact that they were here together. Lexie could only imagine how panicked she would be if she were here alone.

Even though she was still pretty panicked. The fact that she couldn’t access most of her cards was driving her up the wall, and so was the fact that they couldn’t get out of this place. She was sure there was something she could do, some kind ofmechanism she could provoke. But she didn’t know enough about Janus to know for sure.

They needed time to figure it out. But she couldn't help but think, while they were in here, what was happening out there?

“What’s happening?” Lexie demanded of Mikhail. “What are you doing to people?”

Mikhail glanced pointedly at the couch she'd been sitting on, and she felt the overwhelming urge to rip off his head or do something equally heinous to him.

The Elridtch bloodlust spiked, and she felt more rage than she'd felt in a long time.

If she knew it would kill him, she would have ripped him apart in a heartbeat with her own bare claws.

Because she knew what he was doing to people outside, as angry and heartsick as it made her to think it.

She knew what his stupid apology meant.

But she couldn't think about that. She needed him to keep talking so she could get some clue that would get them out of her.

Lexie stomped over and planted herself on the seat. Aiden soon followed, although his gaze was calmer, eyeing Mikhail like a panther eyeing a gazelle before he struck.

Except that Mikhail was no gazelle. He was the worst predator there was.

“In just a few moments, a lot of people will be dead,” he said. “A lot of them will be people you love and care about. Not all, of course...I'm not a monster.”

Lexie bit her tongue to keep from raging even as heat rose in her chest. She didn't scream. She didn't cry. She refused to think about it right now.

That was what he wanted her to do, to lose hope and lose her mind. She wouldn't give him the satisfaction.

He stared at her dispassionately, and the near sympathy she detected in his gaze made her even angrier.

“If it makes you feel any better, I don’t take pleasure in doing this,” he said. “But as I said, it was the only way.”

“No, it wasn’t!” Lexie screamed. “I was already going to help you!’

“No, you weren't, because the Fae would not have let you. Do you think they didn’t have an idea of your plans?”

“You said they couldn’t predict me.”

“And they had a way around that. Your friend Xena.”

Lexie hated the spike of pain that went through her chest. “Xena would never.”

“You mean she would never report to the Fae about the fact that you were making strange living cards from Eldritch?’

“I don’t think I ever told her I was making them.”

“No, but you told Dewie,” he said. “She did it for the right reasons because she was worried about you, and she never told them everything. Just enough that they knew long ago what direction you were going in. The ambassador is not a stupid man, and he's not one to act rashly. So he plotted and waited behind the scenes while you took yourselves closer to the edge."

"What do you mean?"

"The conflict in the association wasn't totally natural. The Fae were surreptitiously pushing Kya to take power. That missile hadn't been meant for Stella, but it had been deflected there thanks to Kya. The Fae knew that without Stella, Jerry would hit the red button, giving them control over Earth before you were done creating this new system of yours, and they captured you.”

"You could have avoided all of that by not letting the Emperor start the war in the first place."

"They would have found another way," he said. "At least we controlled the chaos so we could somewhat control the outcome."

“They wouldn’t have been able to contain me.”

“You severely underestimate them, then. You have no idea the kind of magic that they've had millennia to cultivate. You might be closer to chaos than they are, but they're more knowledgeable about how to wield it than you. The ambassador didn't let you go because he was weak. He did it because your presence on Earth further drove dissent and helped their plan in a way. Your living cards would have done the same thing. Either way, he would have had you. And he was very prepared for you, Lexie Sparrowfoot. I couldn't let that happen." He stared out the window where the fog was thick enough to obscure vision.

"They’re arriving today. They might already be here after the massacre and are likely trying to contain the situation. They thought they'd won, and they needed to think that. The only thing they don't have is you."

“And what if I hadn’t come here?" Lexie snarled, despising the fact that he thought he made sense. "Did you have a plan B?”

“Yes,” he said simply. “Aiden would have brought you.”

Her fatther narrowed his eyes. “No, I wouldn’t.”

“Yes, you would, although you might not have known it. But I suppose we can get into that later."

Lexie's nose flared, and she vaguely wondered if the Fae coming was what Naem had been trying to warn her about. Was it why he insisted she had to take the lordship test?

The test wouldn’t have protected her, though.

Or would it?

Would it have at least protected her from being trapped in this room with this maniac?

Was Naem trying to stop her from coming here? In that case, why couldn't he warn her? Mikhail wasn't the Fae, so Naem wasn't restricted from talking about him. Why had Naem remained silent?

And how exactly had Mikhail used Neqal? What was their relationship?

Lexie decided she had to know, so as he opened his mouth to spew more bullshit, she asked, “How do you and Naem know each other?”

He didn't answer until he'd taken three more sips of tea.

“Well, I know of him,” he said. “Mostly through Neqal. But I didn't use him. Eldritch are hard to predict, and Naem is harder than most, no matter how much they try to control him. However, Neqal is more predictable.”

Lexie frowned. She would have guessed it was the other way around. Naem was the constant, and Neqal was the variable.

“All beings start from chaos,” he said. “What differentiates them is how they deal with that Chaos. Some, like humans, contain it. Some, like the Fae, eliminate it. And others find a way to make it part of their soul. Chaos and power are inexorably linked, and for the Fae to gain power, they must siphon it from a creature who embodies chaos. The Eldritch seemed like the best option at the time. They were directly connected to the Chaos mother and were simplistic creatures easy to control, and didn't evolve fast enough to gain awareness.

But they were evolving, with each iteration, they were gaining things, remembering things. And the nature of the arrangement made the Fae more reliant on them than otherwise. Very soon, there might come an Eldritch who could resist his baser impulses and outwit the Fae. At least one of them almost did.”

“Naem.”

“No, Naem’s progenitor and an Eldritch who nearly severed the Fae’s connection to all magic, rendering them weak forever. They managed to defeat him with great loss to themselves and trap him, but they needed him as well, so they created Neqal and Naem. Two of the same. Neqal was allowed to evolve naturally as an Eldritch. The control. And Naem, the stronger of the two, was experimented on. “

“To what end?”

“Well, at first, it was to find a way to gain control of the eldritch evolution, find new ways to bind them. But then, after some time, they noticed that Naem was able to contain his chaos, while retaining power, unlike any other Eldritch they had met. So they started to do that, restrict more chaos, removing negatives, while observing the power dip. They believed they could create something new. A new chaos mother that they could form orderly chaos from. Or something of that sort."

Lexie didn't know what to believe. On one hand, she didn't want to believe this murderous psychopath; on the other hand, there was no reason for him to lie to her about Naem. If Naem was going through all that....how did he not have animosity toward the Fae? Did he lie? Or simply repress his feelings the whole time.

Or was he just not allowed to say that he hated them?

And his asking Lexie to take the lordship test...perhaps it could have helped her. Maybe once she was the King of Eldritch, she would have been able to do what his father did and separate the Fae from the magic. Maybe it wasn't a trap after all, just a trick he desperately wanted her to see through, but she didn't trust him enough to give him the benefit of the doubt.

“So how did you discover this?” Aiden asked.

“The same way you should have Archmage. Context clues. You should know that the Guardians and the Eldritch aren’ttoo far off from each other."

"What does that mean?"

"I'm sure you'll find out when you two get where you're going.”

Her jaw clenched. "What makes you think that even if we go to the tower and get the key, we would help you? I can still achieve my goal without you, you know."

"I guess. But I have a feeling you'll see things my way. After all, the contract states that if you knowingly cause my death, then your father dies. Isn't that so, Aiden?"

Aiden flinched and paled as Mikhail continued, "I suppose this is the part where I tell you what role you play in all this, how it all ties together. You see, golems are a wonderful creation. One of the best things that Rafi and I made. And we'vetested them over the years to see what we can do with it, how far we can push it. At what point does a creation gain consciousness, does a soul split into two, or a half begin to exist as a whole?"

Aiden inhaled, closed his eyes, his expression full of disappointment. "You're the Alchemist."

"No." He shook his head. "The Alchemist is me. My very first independent golem."
