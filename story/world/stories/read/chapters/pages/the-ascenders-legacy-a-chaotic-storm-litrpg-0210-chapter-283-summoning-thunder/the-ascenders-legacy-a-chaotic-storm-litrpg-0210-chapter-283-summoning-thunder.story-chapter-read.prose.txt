While news of a Way rippled across Lutia like a whirlwind of shock, Daruk battled a growing impatience to be reunited with Aodhán.

They had promised to find each other the moment they arrived in this realm, but neither of them had realized just how large the shivering plains would be.

Sighing, Daruk yanked a second grand abyssal pin out of the frozen earth, his expression completely neutral even as the ground shuddered beneath him and a plume of null essence rose into the air.

He studied the pin carefully, noting the color difference between it and the previous one. Yuna's expression turned into a grimace.

The pin was crimson.

"We've just set the blood phoenix free, haven't we?" Mira asked, and Daruk cracked a small smile.

"We can't know that for sure. We've only felt eight tremors so far. There's a fifty percent chance that it's still bound, only with half the chains."

Yuna shuddered in displeasure. "I hate this. It's like we're digging our own grave."

Daruk twirled the pin and handed it to her. "Would you rather they did it for us?"

"I'd rather it not be dug at all," she retorted with a glare as she accepted the pin and placed it in her bag alongside the other one.

It had been four hours since Daruk's battle with the thread awakened, and since then so much had happened—most prominent of which was the dream they'd all had. At first, Daruk had been unwilling to sleep, but after Yuna and Mira had woken up suddenly with the same dream and prophecies of catastrophe, he'd forced himself to get some shut-eye.

Arkhan hadn't delayed in visiting his dreams with the terrible news.

An hour had passed since then, and though a part of him was relieved that the unease he'd felt so far was finally justified, a larger part of him wished his instincts had been wrong. No matter how many times he tried to calculate their chances of survival, it never fell in their favor, and the fact that Sebastian was having the same issue only made it worse.

As self-serving as Sebastian had proved, Daruk doubted he would lie about something like this. But then again, this was a noble he was talking about.

Exhaling deeply, he raised his gaze to the horizon, searching for the highest hill he could find. He had waited long enough. He had to find Aodhán as soon as possible before time elapsed, and if that meant putting himself in a bit of danger, then he was willing to take the risk.

Fate obviously wasn't eager to bring them closer, which meant he would have to force things.

"Are we still heading to the twin-peaked mountain?" Yuna asked, scanning the horizon alongside him.

"I doubt we have much of a choice in that decision," Daruk grimaced. "But before that, I have to find my brother first."

Yuna tensed behind him, exchanged a glance with Mira, and cleared her throat loudly, obviously uncomfortable.

Daruk kept his expression carefully blank.

Arkhan had asked them to make their way to the twin-peaked mountain so they could fight off the monsters together, but aside from the fact that such an atmosphere was the furthest from safe for Yuna and Mira, it was also unsafe for him and Aodhán. Aodhán most especially, and he highly doubted that Aodhán was keen on the idea at all.

Unfortunately, Daruk had failed to come up with a better plan. He had a few ideas on how to maximize their survivability, but nothing concrete yet. However, even his best ideas hinged on a few of Aodhán's skills, and for that, he needed to find Aodhán as soon as possible.

There was only one way to do that.

His gaze fell on a shimmering ice mountain in the distance, and without hesitation, he zoomed toward it, a blur of white and blue against the endless expanse of snow and ice. Yuna and Mira followed behind him, floating on their own platform constructs, expressions of confusion on their faces.

When Daruk reached the top of the mountain, he glanced back at the girls and cautioned, "Step back."

"What are you planning to do?" Yuna asked him warily.

Daruk glanced back at her and smiled. "I thought it was obvious. I want to summon my brother."

"What?!" Yuna's expression went white immediately, and her eyes widened in fear. She stumbled back, knuckles white as she clenched Mira in a tight grip.

"Do you have to do that?" Mira asked, her voice shaky. "Your… your brother is scary."

"No, he's not." Daruk looked Mira in the eye, a strange feeling coming upon him. "He's literally the nicest person I know. At the very least, he's nicer than me."

But the girls didn't seem convinced.

They had explained their run-in with Aodhán in the last level, and though Daruk knew Aodhán could seem scary at a distance, he also knew the girls weren't telling him the whole story. Now, more than ever, he was certain one of the girls—Yuna—had taken the cultist oath against her will.

Summoning Aodhán here was both for his benefit and for hers. Whatever evil the cultists would make her do, it was better it happened here where they could manage it than at the twin-peaked mountain, where chaos would be the order of the day.

Glancing away from the girls, he levitated to the top of the mountain and inhaled deeply. Chaos and willpower surged within his spirit as he raised a hand to the sky to activate {Frost Beam}.

But this was no ordinary beam of frost.

Willpower erupted out of his palms so densely reality screamed, space warped, and the temperature plummeted to abyssal levels. The beam speared through the clouds above—so white, so bright that it was blinding.

Thunder boomed loudly, and an instant later, the world went completely white.

Seojun Sōsei stirred, his mind slowly crawling its way to consciousness from the depths of the sleep into which it had been forced. He had no idea how long he'd slept, but he felt far more refreshed than he had in years.

Seojun suspected he'd been asleep for too long.

He'd certainly needed the sleep. He hadn't had the chance to have one since—his mind stuttered, and the memories of the last few hours slammed into his mind at once.

He jerked awake immediately, eyes flying open to reveal a beautiful night sky peeking through a dense canopy of blue trees. There was, however, no earth beneath him. Instead, he floated in the air on a bed of azure mental energy.

He glanced to the side urgently, eyes widening slightly when he found Mirith glaring at him from a similar floating construct.

"You're finally awake," she drawled, an emotion he couldn't quite recognize dripping from her tone like venom. "What a mighty fine messenger you make. If I had known you were so sleep deprived, I would have found someone else to get the dream for me."

Mention of the dream caused another surge of remembrance, and Seojun's eyes widened in panic. "The dream—there are four—"

"I already know," Mirith cut him off with a wave of her hand. "I dug it out of your subconscious an hour ago while you snored blissfully."

Seojun scowled, panic from the dream and anger at the invasion of privacy warring within him.

"Calm down," Mirith snorted. "I stayed away from any core memories. Even I have boundaries."

Seojun closed his eyes against a fresh wave of embarrassment. If his core memories were the only thing she'd stayed away from, then his mind had practically been laid bare before her.

Just how long had he been asleep?

"Seven hours, give or take." Mirith replied to his unasked question.

"What?" Seojun's eyes widened. "Why didn't you wake me?"

Mirith glared at him. "I had more important things to deal with. Like the monsters sleeping beneath us."

Seojun stilled. "You… you're dealing with them?"

Mirith grimaced. "I have certainly tried, but they're too deep underground, and the interference keeps hampering me."

Seojun frowned. "Are you not scared?"

Mirith glanced up at him and smiled. "I am. I'm terrified. But I've had hours to freak out before now, and I've never been one to let my fears get in my way."

Seojun swallowed, realizing that he had been the exact opposite all his life. "What's your plan, then?"

Mirith exhaled. "A single creature at the 75th tier is already a menace, its mind just barely within reach. I can try to control it, but I'm not sure how well that will work." She grimaced. "Even if it does, there are still three other creatures to worry about."

"But with one out of the picture," Seojun spoke, his mind already racing, "we could set it up against another, so we'll only have to deal with the two remaining—"

"Did you hear me well?" Mirith cut him off with a sharp glare. "You're speaking as if I were certain of controlling any of the creatures. I am not. Each one of those creatures is at least twenty-five advanced tiers above me, and if your dream is right, further enhanced by some mysterious energy. It's a strong possibility that I'm completely useless against them."

Seojun grimaced, realizing with a heavy sigh that if a telepath far more powerful than anyone he'd seen so far felt useless against the creatures, what chance of survival did they truly have?

Silence stretched between them for a moment until Seojun asked, "Are we heading to the twin-peaked mountain, then?"

"Yes," Mirith nodded. A moment later she added, "And so are your sisters."

The mention of his sisters brought a wide smile to Seojun's face, and he collapsed back onto the floating construct of mental energy in relief. Closing his eyes, he raised a silent prayer of thanks to Aeloria before turning to ask Mirith, "Where are they?"

Mirith shrugged, feeling a bit subdued. "A mile or two ahead of us."

Seojun's smile widened, feeling excitement begin to bubble within him. If they were only a mile ahead of him, then he had to catch up with them quickly. It had been so long since he'd seen them. He needed to make sure they were safe.

Mirith snorted. "They're with Daruk Brystion. They're safe." And then she glared up at him. "At least for now."

Seojun paused. "What do you mean, for now?"

Mirith's expression tightened. "One of your sisters has tainted herself with an oath to the cultists. She did it to protect the other."

"No," Seojun breathed, his excitement morphing into cold dread as his worst fear was presented before him. Tears stung his eyes, and he opened his mouth to speak, but no words would come. Mirith, however, didn't need words to understand him perfectly.

"Yuna may be safe for now, but she's in grave danger, especially since she's traveling alongside Daruk Brystion, who will no doubt meet up with his brother soon." Mirith shook her head. "I wouldn't put it past the cultists to try something…"

"That something will lead to her death," Seojun whispered. It wasn't a speculation but a statement of fact.

Aodhán had been very kind to him, but he didn't know the man well enough to know how he might respond to betrayal or an attack like that.

Mirith hummed in speculation. "I'm not sure he'll kill her. He knows she's your sister, and he does regard you somewhat. But I guess it depends on what she's forced to do."

"No," Seojun shook his head, already thinking ahead. "I must prevent this. We need to catch up with them and separate them before they meet up with Aodhán."

Mirith smiled. "Unless you plan to tie down your sisters, I highly doubt you can keep them away from Aodhán for long."

"I'll find a way," Seojun snapped, already rushing to his feet. Yuna couldn't be forced to do anything against Aodhán if she was far away from him. That was the only way he could keep her safe and maintain his relationship with Aodhán.

Mirith cocked her head. "You really do hold him in high regard for a Sunstonian."

Seojun grimaced. "He saved my life. I owe him."

"Right," Mirith frowned. "I saw that in your memories."

Seojun turned his gaze forward, determination rising within him. He needed to reach Yuna fast before she could meet up with Aodhán. However, before he could even move, an explosion of willpower so powerful rose into the sky—many times more than the one he'd felt in the last level.

Even from the distance, Seojun felt it like a rattling in his bones. Dread pooled in his belly as he realized the implication of the blast. Daruk had just summoned his brother.

If he intended to save his sister, now was the time.

Seojun was a blur of azure and gold before he could even think.

He would be damned if he let his sisters die just when he'd been given the chance to see them again.

At the base of the twin-peaked mountain, over two dozen individuals were already gathered, most of them ranging between the 30th and 42nd tiers. Most prominent among them were Monica, Orin Draviksson—a powerful sound awakened—Serenya Serenity, Arkhan, and Sebastian himself.

They were 'the leaders' of this budding alliance, standing at the peak of power among those already present, except Arkhan, who had made himself indispensable with the excuse that there were still so many people to inform about the impending danger.

He wasn't truly lying.

The five of them stood on one of the peaks, overlooking the small number of people they had gathered so far. There were over three dozen of them, with more still on the way, but it wasn't a particularly encouraging sight.

Most of them sat alone, meditating, while others clustered together to share in the comfort of their numbers. A few of them were even sleeping, but with less than a day left until the monsters were fully unleashed on them, people were growing antsy.

Already, they'd broken up two fights in the last hour, and they still had more than twenty hours left.

"What are we going to do about the resurrected and sleeper agents among us?" Orin asked quietly. "Leaving them to roam freely puts us all in danger."

Monica grimaced. "I vote we kill them as soon as we can identify them, that is." Her gaze narrowed as she scrutinized the people below. "The creatures we're about to face are enough danger already. We don't need anyone else adding to it."

"That's a terrible idea." Serenya glared at the entropy awakened. "The solution to every problem is not to bring it to an immediate end—"

"Then it must be brought to an eventual one," Monica stated without remorse, and Serenya's expression darkened.

She inhaled deeply, most likely drawing from a drying pool of patience, before continuing. "One thing is certain: we need numbers. How many people would we have to eliminate to weed out the sleepers and resurrected from among us? Besides, we have yet to prove that the resurrected would be a danger to us."

Orin frowned. "As right as you are, Serenya, Monica isn't wrong either. Even if the resurrected aren't a danger, the agents most certainly are."

"That's right," Sebastian nodded. "We don't know for sure that the resurrected are working for the cultists. Unlike those who directly took the oath." He glared down at the growing number of people beneath them and sighed. "All of this would be so much easier if the telepath were here. Any updates, Arkhan?"

"None," Arkhan muttered with his eyes closed. "She doesn't seem to need sleep like the rest of us."

Serenya shook her head. "We'll need her if we are to reason in any way with these creatures. Is there no way we could find her location and go to her? We still have a whole day."

"There is one way," Arkhan muttered, and glanced at Sebastian. "But we would need Aodhán Brystion for that, and he's not here yet."

Monica grimaced. "The storm awakened? Of what use would he be?"

"He can sense people's cores from afar," Sebastian replied grimly. "I'm not sure how wide his range is, but he could certainly find the telepath if he tried."

Monica's grimace deepened as she realized how the boy always managed to see her coming. And then Baxter had betrayed her, siding with the boy and standing in her way—

"We need a healing tent," Orin spoke again, his voice breaking into her thoughts like a knife. "Do we even have any healers among us?"

"If we had, then they'd all be dead by now," Sebastian replied with a grimace. No one with a pure healing ability could have survived the first level on their own. "We, however, have people with minor healing abilities. Can we afford to put them in tents, though, when we still need people to fight?"

"We'll just have to spare one or two," Serenya sighed. "People will no doubt sustain injuries. If we can get them back into battle as quickly as possible, then those healers would have been worth it."

Arkhan snorted. "We're speaking as if this will be some drawn-out battle or something." He glanced at Sebastian. "Tell us, seer. How many minutes do you reckon this battle will last?"

Sebastian scowled at him, but before he could respond, a loud boom shook the earth to its core, and in the distance—a beam of willpower so large it rocked his senses shot into the sky, so bright it was blinding.

"That explosion…" Monica stammered, recognizing it immediately. "That is Daruk Brystion. What could he be battling this time to unleash so much willpower at once?"

"He's not fighting," Arkhan whispered from behind them, his eyes open in awe. "He's declaring his location to someone. I suspect it might be Aodhán."

"How do you know that?" Orin asked, but Arkhan didn't respond. Instead, he glanced at Sebastian. "What's going on?"

"I don't…" Sebastian stuttered, his mind racing and blooming with hundreds of myriad possibilities. One shone brighter than the others in his mind's eye, and without hesitation, he latched onto it, scanning the parallel for what was happening.

What he saw didn't inspire any confidence within him, and with a scowl, he asked Arkhan, "Didn't you say they were coming to the mountain?"

"They told me they would," Arkhan replied slowly, and then his eyes widened. "But what are the chances that they have a different plan?"

Sebastian felt a chill descend upon him in that moment as a string of new possibilities suddenly bloomed in his mind, entirely new—not even a branch, but a shift so drastic it could only have been orchestrated by chaos.

He scanned one of the new possibility threads and felt his stomach sink.

The chances were exceedingly high.
