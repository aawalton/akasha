I obeyed. I hated it. Every bit of it. I hated the way my knees trembled and my chest heaved with helpless sobs.

But I didn’t know what else to do. So, after looking desperately between the glowering Fae and my best friend’s ashen face, I obediently sank onto the bedspread.

“Good. And, you…” Velidan cast a scowl towards Royce. “Go sit in the chair. Say nothing.”

Royce’s fingers spasmed and his jaw worked, but the only sound that came out was a strangled moan. He took a step, boot dragging against the floorboard, and swung one hand back to the drapes. Clumsily trying to pull them down.

Velidan watched the movement and, with something almost like approval flashing across his features, seized my friend by the throat.

“Don’t-!” I clapped a hand over my mouth, stifling the scream.

Royce was sent stumbling to the chair. He sat down hard, every vein and muscle taut. Both hands were clenched so hard that his knuckles shone bone-white beneath the skin.

His eyes stayed fixed on me the whole time. Round, shining, and terrified. And beneath that… sorry. As if he was trying to apologize for not fighting his way free.

“Just let him leave,” I whispered.

“I was going to, before you set me on fire.”

“I thought…” My voice cracked. Half in fear, half in helpless fury that I wasn’t even allowed to defend someone I loved. “I thought you were going to kill him.”

“I told you I wouldn’t.” He scoffed, lip curling in a fresh snarl. “I don’t kill things needlessly, Brin.”

I don’t believe you, I wanted to say, but bit my tongue. Angering him further would only make this worse.

After another moment spent observing Royce the Fae shook his head and said wryly, “Imagine how simple this all would have been if you’d been this compliant.”

I shuddered at the thought. “This is what you tried to do to me? The first night?”

“Oh, I’ve tried several times.”

Frost snaked across my skin at that. “Is he alright?”

“Well,” glittering eyes cut between me and my ashen-faced friend, “I imagine he’s quite unhappy.”

“That’s not what I-”

“He’s fine. Better than you or I, at the moment.”

That caught my attention. I tore my gaze away from Royce’s terrified expression and looked up.

All the scorching, violent red had faded to ivory, but a thin sheen of sweat still shone on Velidan’s skin. His vibrant clothes were singed black in several places. His lips were parted and his chest swelled with each breath.

He looked like that before… on the balcony. After Baltha, after the cave…

“It hurts you. Healing yourself. Healing me and my friends and-”

“Hurt is not the right word. Suffice to say I’d rather not fix any more mortal wounds in the next few moments. And again I ask,” dark hair swayed as he shook his head, looking down at me in obvious consternation, “how have you survived doing this for years?

“It’s not usually that bad.” I lowered my lashes, then collected a swath of my gray hem and used it to clean my face. The cloth came away crimson.

Well, it was already ruined. Saints, it looks like I coughed blood all over my bodice.

“How…” I stared at the fabric. “How long was I… unconscious?”

“Long enough to be concerning. Don’t use that rune again if I’m not present.”

Ice crawled down my spine. Ashes. Lying there, bleeding, choking… what would’ve happened if he hadn’t been there?

My voice hitched as I whispered again, searching futilely for comfort in the words, “It’s not usually that bad.”

Velidan said nothing, but I could just feel the weight of his attention. My skin practically crawled beneath it.

I cut my gaze to Royce. He was still ramrod straight, though the naked terror was slowly settling into… confused terror. “I did the rune. I answered all your questions. What else… when will you…”

“When will I leave?” He chuckled, pacing towards Royce. The movement made my stomach flip and my friend’s eyes widen, but the Fae just picked up the shard and moved back towards me. “Soon. I have some instructions for you, first. There’s a city several hours away, deeper in the valley.”

“Sedgewick Vale?”

“I’m not familiar with the name. It’s not far beyond the lake. Is that your current destination, or are you residing… here?” He looked around the room, frowning.

“We’re going to the city.” Pressure closed over my chest at the sudden thought that he might press for why, so I quickly added, “Just a quick stop to buy horses. And supplies. Before we leave.” There was a short silence. “Horses are, um, big animals that carry-”

“I know what a horse is, Brin. I’m not an idiot.”

“Well,” I muttered before I could stop myself, “you didn’t know what a tomato was.”

He snorted. “I’m still unclear. And your plans have changed.”

“They have?”

“You’re going to stay in the city for several days.”

“What? Why?”

“Because I’m going to be in the area, and I’d like my weapon to be nearby. I can’t seem to bring it across the realms, but I can find you quite easily. I’ll come collect it when I’m ready.”

The blood drained from my face. I looked down, fingers twisting together.

There was a short silence. My skin crawled beneath a heavy, piercing stare. Then, evenly, “Is there a problem?”

I swallowed. “No.”

Velidan stepped near, very slowly, and stopped just in front of me. The quiet turned frosty as I stared hard at his black boots, heart slamming against my chest.

His voice grew soft. “You look frightened when you’re hiding something. Did you know that?”

My mouth went dry. “I’m not hiding-”

“Your breath catches and you look down.” Strong fingers clasped my chin, tilting my face up. “It’s almost… endearing.”

I tried to jerk away, but he held tight. “I’m not-”

“Royce.” The Fae’s voice sank to velvet once more, gaze drifting to the chair. “Speak freely and honestly. Why was she heading to the city?”

“We were… going to buy horses. So we could… get to Pinnacle faster.” My friend’s baritone quavered. The words sounded strained, as if he was biting his very tongue to try and hold them back. For a moment I dared to hope that he’d stop there, but he pressed on through gritted teeth. “And we heard there were… paladins in the city. She wants to go find them.”

“Paladins? The warriors who serve Thalessa?”

“Yes.”

He frowned. “Why?”

“To… to give them…” The muscles of his neck bulged. His eyes closed, head shaking back and forth, before he finally burst out, “The shard. And… and to ask them for… help with… you.”

“Helpful. Be silent again.” Dark hair hissed against burnt silk as Velidan cocked his head to one side. “ See how easy honesty is, Brin?”

I glared up at him.

Too-sharp teeth flashed in a smile. “I’ll say this once. If you give that weapon away, I’ll return it to you buried in the skull of whoever had it.”

Ice flooded my insides. The face looming over me held only calm, cold promise. I closed my eyes, nails digging into my palms and breath hitching.

His grip tightened nearly to the point of pain. “Am I clear?”

“Y-yes.” He released me and I quickly shifted away. “What… what are you even going to do with it?”

“Think about it, Brin.” He lifted the sun-bright steel, eyes glittering wickedly in its light. “What does one need a weapon for?”

My brows knit together and I spent a long moment in thought. “You said things that could kill you were hard to find.”

“Oh, very hard. I know of only three.”

“And I assume you’re not going to use that on yourself.”

He chuckled.

“So…” I caught my breath, eying him in wary disbelief. “You’re going to kill a Fae? Why?”

“More than one.” One brow rose. “You look so shocked. I told you there was dissent among my kind. It’s… escalating.”

“Dissent… among High Fae. That’s what you said; that the ones like you were fighting about coming back to this realm.” I shook my head again, glancing towards Royce’s baffled face as if he could offer some insight. “You’re going to kill your own kind? So that they don’t come to the mortal realm?” The words sounded foreign as they poured out. They didn’t make sense. “I don’t believe… Why would you… why?”

“Perhaps I’m feeling altruistic. Perhaps I’m developing a soft spot for you poor humans.” At my stunned silence, rich laughter filled the room. “Or perhaps not. Perhaps I’m just a pragmatist. Does the reason matter?”

I shook my head. “A pragmatist? What does that have to do-”

“Some of my peers are planning to do something incredibly foolish. It’s likely going to get me killed. Obviously I’m going to stop them.” He chuckled again before adding, quite sternly, “And I don’t think you need to know any more than that.”

The deluge of confusion and questions was so heavy it made my head throb. “So… is that what was happening the other night? In your house? You were… ashes, killing Fae?” I shuddered at the memory. “I saw blood on the floor, I heard the screams…”

He went still for a moment. Then, tone smoothing into silk, “That was just me… rectifying a mistake.”

Pained, muffled wails echoed from a corner of my mind. My stomach roiled and my shoulders tightened. “What kind of mistake?”

“Something of mine was damaged.” Endless, impossibly dark eyes held me with an unsettling weight. “I felt it necessary to do some damage in turn.” The little curve to one corner of his mouth made me recoil even further. The obvious satisfaction as he spoke of… saints, whatever he’d been doing to some poor creature.

Part of me whispered that maybe my sympathy was misplaced. That Velidan murdering some bloodthirsty monster, perhaps something like Baltha, perhaps something worse, in the bowels of his house was an event I should celebrate.

Slip’s expressive, scarred face flickered behind my eyes. I imagined a creature like her on the receiving end of such violence and shivered again, inching back along the bed.

He spent a long moment looking at me, seemingly lost in thought, then murmured, “Your friends should leave.”

I blinked, casting an uncertain glance towards Royce. “My… what?”

He blinked, too, as though his own words had surprised him. Then his mouth curled into a familiar little sneer. “Consider this a kindness on my part, mortal. Anyone you love, anyone whose life you value, should flee this area immediately. Today.”

“Flee? Why?”

“Sedgewick Vale is about to be the site of a ritual. One that, if successful, will turn this area into the most dangerous place in the mortal realm.” He fixed me with a hard stare. “You will be safe. Every other mortal is free for the taking.”

My heartbeat sped up. I looked towards Royce again, eyes wide, and he mirrored the expression. “I don’t understand. What kind of ritual?”

“I’m not offering explanations, just advice.”

“But there’s hundreds of people there! Maybe thousands! How can we stop-”

“You can’t.” Velidan stepped back, twisting one hand in a familiar gesture. Beckoning forth a ripple of moonlight along the very air. He smirked, features illuminated by the silver glow, and said, “One other piece of advice, Brin. Next time you stay at a house of rest, ensure you’re given a door that locks. Anyone could just walk in.”
