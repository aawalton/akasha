◈◈◈

"No mantles," Jack announced, looking at two muscleheads he had decided to referee. Shaking his head, he held a recording crystal loosely between two fingers. “Gleeward, Rudy—no active skills. No essence. You hit each other with your weapons until one of you stops moving."

Rudy rolled his shoulders, working the stiffness out of his neck.

"A bloody inspiring speech, aye," Gleeward muttered, staring straight ahead.

"I have a shorter version if you prefer," Jack offered.

"Piss off."

Rudy smothered a grin. He was glad Jin had pointed these two out for sparring, and at first he was a bit hesitant, but over the few hours they had been training, Rudy couldn’t help but like them.

He wanted to train with his best friend, but Jin was still recovering from his risky endeavors. Asking Reyana was completely out of the question. She had nearly folded Rudy in half yesterday over a misunderstood joke. He rubbed his ribs just thinking about it. He would never understand women or why they treated mild sarcasm as a capital offense.

He hefted the blunt training greatsword. The weapon was a dead slab of metal in his grip. It possessed the same length and weight as his own sword, but it lacked the comforting, thrumming connection of his soulbound Greatsword of Forlorn.

Focus, Rudy chided himself, shaking out his arms. Pay attention.

Across the scuffed concrete, Gleeward stood with his spear loosely in one hand, the blunt tip angled toward the floor. Jack constantly jabbed at the man for being a drunken mess, and watching the slight sway in Gleeward's stance, Rudy might actually have believed it.

Might. If not for the eyes. Gleeward's bright green eyes and his aura told Rudy all he needed to know.

"Ye ready?" Gleeward called out.

"Born ready." Rudy dropped his center of gravity and started circling left.

Gleeward didn't step to match him. He just rotated his torso fractionally, keeping the spear point centered on Rudy's mass.

"At least let me signal the start!" Jack yelled from the sidelines. "Also, Gleeward, you daft prick, you're dragging your back foot. You look like a wounded duck."

"Shut up, Jack," both combatants barked in unison.

Rudy lunged.

He crossed the ten-foot gap in two massive strides. Dropping his shoulder, he drove his entire body weight into a horizontal cleave aimed directly at Gleeward's midsection.

Gleeward didn't retreat. He stepped half a pace to the left—moving straight into the swing's arc. As his boots shifted, his hands snapped up the spear shaft, instantly shortening his reach. He brought the iron haft up vertically, catching Rudy's greatsword cleanly on the flat of the blade, right above the crossguard.

The exact point of least leverage.

CLANG.

The impact rattled straight up Rudy's forearms and ground his molars together. His forward momentum dragged his heavy swing wide, ripping his posture completely open on his right side.

Gleeward pivoted smoothly on his heel. Using Rudy's own force, he whipped the shortened spear around. The blunt iron butt cracked flush against Rudy's exposed ribs.

Rudy choked on a gasp—stumbling sideways—his ribs throbbing from the spear butt. Leaning into the lost balance, he let gravity drag him into a spin and whipped the blunt greatsword in a savage backhand aimed right at Gleeward’s temple.

Gleeward simply dropped to one knee. The heavy slab of iron whistled over his scalp, displacing enough air to ruffle the veteran's hair.

Before Rudy could even register the miss, the iron-banded spear shaft blurred toward his shins in a sweeping arc.

Rudy jumped. He pulled his knees up, feeling the rush of displaced air scrape against his boots. While airborne, he shifted his grip and committed his entire falling body weight into a massive, two-handed overhead crush.

Gleeward planted the butt of his spear against the concrete floor. He angled the shaft upward, bracing it like a pike against a cavalry charge.

CLANG.

Rudy’s greatsword hammered into the angled iron. The spear didn't break. It acted as a ramp. The descending force sheared cleanly sideways, driving Rudy's blade into the concrete floor with a violent shower of sparks. The redirected momentum folded Rudy completely forward, pinning him over his own weapon.

Gleeward released his lower grip. He stepped into Rudy's personal space, rendering the greatsword entirely useless, and drove a short, piston-fast jab with the blunt spearhead directly into the center of Rudy's chest.

Rudy barely managed to redirect the attack with his armguard, but the impact still lifted Rudy off his feet. His back slammed against the cold concrete. The air left his lungs in a single, violent rush.

The blunt iron tip of the spear stopped exactly one inch from his Adam's apple.

"Dead," Gleeward stated.

Rudy lay flat against the floor, staring up at the harsh, buzzing lights. His lungs completely forgot how to function for a terrifying three seconds before finally sucking in a ragged breath.

"You telegraphed the overhead," Jack observed from his perch on the supply crates. "Three inches, every time. Your guard drops exactly three inches when you load a downward strike. I've seen you do it four times just in the past few hours, mate." Jack casually tapped his recording crystal. "Also, you're favouring your right side. Makes you look bloody ridiculous. I think and I’ll say it again this shortcoming is natural for your age. If you lean on essence and its abilities, the situation might have been different."

"I am going," Rudy wheezed, "to find a woodchipper and introduce you to it."

"Yeah, I've had that threat before. Nobody ever follows through." Jack pocketed the crystal. "Gleeward, brilliant use of the angled pike intercept. You were a fraction early on the pivot in round two, but you recovered well enough."

Gleeward pulled the spear back and offered a calloused hand. Rudy grabbed it and hauled himself upright. His ribs throbbed in a steady, agonizing rhythm.

"Raw power isnae the problem," Gleeward said. The young man’s voice held no malice. "Ye hit like a collapsing building, laddie. But ye're swinging that sword like it's a club. Every time ye go wide, ye hand me the distance. Against a spear, distance is absolutely everything."

“And to reiterate. Rudy, you are what we call a raw power combatant,” Jack said. “It’s pretty self-explanatory.  Whereas Gleeward and your friend Sir Jin are skill-based combatants.”

"Yeah. So you are saying I’m simple in my approach while Gleeward and Jin go for all complex approaches?" Rudy recovered the training sword from where it had clattered into the corner, rolling his wrist to test how annoyed his tendons were.

“Yup. There are again variations with Gleeward being a martial skill-based while Sir Jin is well…” Jack made an ‘I don’t even know face,’ and Rudy laughed.

"Yeah, agreed.” He said, hefting his greatsword up. “Good thing I'm simple but stubborn. Let's go again."

◈◈◈

The second round lasted eight brutal minutes. The third dragged on for eleven. It ended when Gleeward slipped a thrust and drove the blunt iron butt of the training spear directly into Rudy’s jaw.

The fourth ended when finally Rudy drove his head into Gleeward’s—skull against skull and in a contest of thick skulls… Rudy won. Though not without a blinding flash of white, which washed out both of thier vision. They stayed slumped against the cold masonry, laughing loudly while they waited patiently for the training hall to stop spinning.

"Time," Jack called out. The word echoed in the ringing silence of Rudy's ears. “Seriously, you two… I’m glad I didn’t sign up for this.”

A plastic canteen sailed through the air. Gleeward snatched his out of the arc, and Rudy, who was turned around, had his canteen bounce off his head and clatter to the dust-slick floor.

“Hey!” He scowled, rubbing his head, and dragged the canteen up with numb fingers, twisted the cap, and dumped half the icy water directly over his head. The runoff washed sweat and the metallic tang of blood past his split lip.

"Ye got faster," Gleeward stated. His heavy accent made his speech even funnier. "Third round. That initial charge off the pivot. I caught only half the parry. Drill that transition for another week, and it becomes a real bloody problem."

"Can confirm," Jack said. "Reaction window on the third round dropped by forty percent, mate. You still broadcast those overheads like a bloody lighthouse, but your approach variance is improving, which we saw in the fourth. Pretty devastating and self-harming, but can’t say it won’t work."

Rudy smiled. His breath came in ragged gasps, but deep in his chest, the Asura heart was already doing the heavy lifting. The violent, war-drum rhythm—thump-thump, thump-thump—was steadily stepping down from its peak. It pumped pure madra through bruised muscles and fractured capillaries, burning away the lactic acid.

He rested his head against the floor, focusing on the heavy throb. He still wasn't used to the sheer physical weight of it. His old heart had just pumped blood. This new organ operated like a localized tectonic plate vibrating behind his sternum—a massive, unyielding engine built specifically to keep him conscious when he had every right to pass out.

And it made his decision not to call upon his mantle and his powers in the spar a mighty annoyance.

"So," Jack said. "What exactly is our new commander up to? I put in a request for a job. Got a message back five minutes later that said—" Jack checked his tempad "—'give me two hours, or just watch, it'll be faster.'"

Rudy chuckled. "I asked him the same thing before I came down here. He told me, and I'm quoting directly: 'It's better you see it with your own eyes.'"

Gleeward paused mid-drink, lowering his canteen. His pale green eyes narrowed. "What exactly does that mean?"

"Knowing Jin?" Rudy wiped a smear of bloody water from his chin. "It means he's either doing something incredibly stupid, or he's currently breaking what we know of reality. Usually, it's a mix of both, assuming you guys saw last night’s footage?"

“We did.” Pride and hope were clearly visible on both their faces.

Rudy wiped the sweat from his eyes, smearing dust across his forehead. "What about you two? You aren't just sitting around waiting for the final war to drop on our heads."

Gleeward rotated his right shoulder. A dull pop echoed in the quiet hall. "Veric, the old bastard's running a session for me in an hour. I hit Order III, aye, but my channels are still bleeding essence at the edges. My combat reflexes are solid enough. The depth is lacking. I need to close that gap before the final push."

"I've been going through the—” Jack made a face. “—the guides."

"Oh yeah, those," Rudy laughed.

"I thought they were standard stuff to keep the recruits busy. Going from the terrible naming scheme," Jack said. "But… they're very helpful, mate. The efficient techniques alone for mantle and spellcasting... I’m just glad he made those publicly available."

Jack narrowed his eyes at Rudy. "And we recently got the clearance for the skill merging documentation. Is that possible?"

Even Gleeward had a serious expression.

"Yeah, it works." He said seriously. "As for how he knows… well, I don't know, Jack. I've watched him tear through many things I thought impossible.”

"Makes sense," Jack muttered, tapping the peg against his thigh.

"If you've got doubts about what marked skills to merge and what skills to get… just go find him," Rudy said. "He won't mind. Really. He has a skill that lets him process information faster than a human brain physically can. Then he just applies it. To everything. Constantly."

“So you just ask him a question, and he would have the answers soon, and if he told me to find you guys for training. You can bet he probably has a spreadsheet with your names on it queued up."

Gleeward stopped massaging his shoulder. His pale green eyes locked onto Rudy. "He'd do that? For us? He's the acting commander, laddie. We're grunts."

"Yes," Rudy said. "He would."

Both their tempads chimed at the same moment. Gleeward checked his first. "Speak of the devil. Veric."

"Mine too." Jack was already swiping through his display. "He wants to see us. Looks like we're actually ahead of schedule."

Rudy pushed himself off the floor, his knee joints popping in protest. "Go. Good session. Same time tomorrow?"

Jack tossed the wooden peg over his shoulder. It clattered into a dark corner of the room. "Assuming the bloody city is still standing."

"Aye," Gleeward grunted, wiping sweat from his brow with a rag before hoisting his training spear. "Same time! Today was fun."

Then they were gone, and the training hall fell completely silent.

“They were fun… if only Jin were here too,” Rudy sighed and tapped the silver band of his spatial ring. A brief distortion rippled the air, depositing a massive, heavily worn beanbag. Rudy dragged himself over to it and simply let his knees give out until the synthetic fabric swallowed him whole.

A ragged, exhausted groan tore its way out of his throat. He flexed his neck, wincing at the sharp, echoing crack of the joints releasing tension. He pressed a bruised, calloused palm flat against his chest.

Beneath the leather, muscle, and bone, his Asura heart thumped against his ribs.

He kept his hand there for several minutes, focusing entirely on the physical throb to quiet his racing thoughts.

Eventually, he dug his tempad out of a thigh pocket. His thumbs felt thick and clumsy as he tapped the screen, firing off a series of brief messages. He pinged Reyana to ask how her follow-up drills with the 1st Company had gone. He sent a quick, standard proof-of-life check-in to his dad. He messaged Salvatore to request another master-disciple session specifically focused on shoring up his pure martial skills.

Joe replied before Rudy even closed the messaging app.

Sure. Give me an hour. You want to talk ZAC and stuff?

Rudy typed back, his thumbs clicking rapidly against the glass.

Yeah. Aura and skills mostly. I wanna know how to have a good build.

Good timing, Joe replied. Come find me.

Rudy locked the screen and let the device rest on his chest. He tipped his head back against the beanbag, staring blankly at the ceiling.

The lights continued their steady hum. Deep in the concrete walls, the entire Bastion vibrated with a low-frequency rumble. Thousands of people moved through the corridors above and below him. Recruits drilling, logistics teams hauling supplies, artificers forging armor. Jin’s improvised army was operating at a terrifying, breakneck pace that would have seemed impossible just two weeks ago.

Rudy forced his mind back to the spar. He pictured Gleeward’s spear redirecting his overhead strike. He analyzed the three-inch guard drop Jack had so annoyingly documented. Gleeward had stepped directly into the swing’s arc, shortening the distance to deny Rudy’s greatsword any leverage. That single, counter-intuitive decision to move closer to a descending blade had reversed the entire momentum of the exchange.

He shifted his weight, his spine groaning in protest as he hauled himself upright. He picked up his tempad.

He had been avoiding this. He had put it off with the lingering apprehension of someone poking a sleeping predator with a stick, half-expecting it to bite his hand off.

"How does it work again?" Rudy muttered to the empty room, feeling vaguely ridiculous. He cleared his throat. "System."

A crisp blue text box opened in his vision.

"Wow," Rudy said.
