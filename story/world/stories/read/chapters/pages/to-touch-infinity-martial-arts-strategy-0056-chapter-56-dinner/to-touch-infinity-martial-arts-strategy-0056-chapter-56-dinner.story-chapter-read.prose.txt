Wi Mo hauled back hard on his stallion’s leather reins, wrestling the panicked beast into submission as it whinnied and struck out in fear with its hooves.

Behind him, the Meng-shan vanguard had collapsed into chaos. Broken spear shafts littered the ruined earth alongside crushed bodies, bright blood, and flecks of frothing foam. A confused mess of purple-robed disciples were driving their spearheads into the enraged oxen, but the wounded beasts refused to go down. Their thick hides and deep layers of muscle absorbed the shallow thrusts, and though bright red gashes appeared on their ribs, the animals continued to thrash.

The strategist didn’t need the Tai-shan Young Master’s ridiculous, booming shout to know the cavalry would soon be bearing down on him. He could see the flow of the battle with his own eyes, and he was already shouting commands. Wi Mo dug his heels firmly into his mount’s flanks, pulling the bit tight until the black horse finally ceased its flailing and stood firm.

“Hurry up and brace yourselves! FORM A LINE!”

Directly ahead of him, a small fraction of his army was still holding their nerve. Obeying his commands, a double line of spearmen locked their bucklers together, jamming the wooden butts of their weapons deep into the soil and lowering the deadly tips to meet the oncoming charge.

The Tai-shan had destroyed his ballista and scattered his front ranks, but Wi Mo was far from finished. The cavalry wouldn’t be able to charge a prepared spear wall. The braced iron points would impale the horses, hurling their riders into the mud. Ultimately, they would be forced to jump down and fight on foot.

And once they were grounded, he would turn their own momentum against them.

Spotting four spearmen scrambling away from the chaos, the Lesser Elder snapped his fan towards a large, bleeding ox that was stumbling near the edge of road.

“You four! Isolate that beast, and keep it boxed in. Drive your spearpoints into its left flank; keep stabbing until the pain makes it turn around. And the moment their cavalry is forced to stop – release it straight down the center!”

They had panicked his oxen in order to disrupt his formation, but two could play at that game. Soon, one of those half-ton beasts would be plowing straight over the Tai-shan Young Master. As well as that cursed old monster who kept ruining his plans.

Wi Mo began shouting for more disciples to fill the gaps. There were only a few breaths to go before the two sides collided.

The seven Meng-shan spearmen who had burst through the abatis grid continued to rush forwards, their weapons leveled as they charged at the weakened section of wall. They were fully ready to engage the small contingent of Tai-shan disciples blocking their path, while trampling over the mysterious scholar who appeared to be in their midst.

That is, until the inevitable happened.

Unaware of the hazards that lay in wait, one of the spearmen drove his full weight straight onto the needle-sharp point of a steel caltrop. The iron spike drove straight through his leather footwear, piercing the sole of his foot and eliciting a howl of pain. A second soldier, slightly further down the line, suffered the same fate.

The five remaining spearmen flinched involuntarily at the sudden cries of their compatriots. The group's momentum collapsed as they each looked over to the side, tripping and sliding as they tried to halt their own rush, and figure out what had just happened.

One spearman, unable to stop himself in time, stumbled forwards past his crippled comrades, close to the outer rim of the spike field. He never did get a chance to set his feet.

Sergeant Noh Gu-shim and one his guards stepped forwards in perfect unison, right to the very edge of the danger zone. A pair of twin greatswords flashed in blinding arcs. The spearman raised his weapon to parry, but the blades shattered the wooden shaft, smashed his buckler aside, and carved a deep, fatal trench across his chest.

Exactly as they’d practiced, the two then retreated several paces backwards, just outside the reach of the remaining spears.

The four remaining Meng-shan soldiers looked desperate. They began to surge forwards again, anger and fear on their faces as they tried to escape the unexpected trap they’d found themselves in.

One spearman singled out Chae-ha, driving three lightning-fast thrusts towards her throat and ribs. But the young girl barely reacted as the iron tip went sailing past. Her hips continued to swing; her feet meticulously mapped out the carefully planned choreography of her 108 Phantom Steps. The wide, jade-green panels of her scholar’s robes did their job, and the spearhead punched through empty air – snagging nothing but a bit of cloth as her body glided effortlessly out of the weapon’s line of travel.

Beside her, Noh Gu-shim and his men weren’t looking for a duel. They were focused on keeping their opponents boxed in; herding the enemy soldiers, so they stayed inside the boundaries of the spike field as they moved. Every breath the spearmen stayed inside that zone, the likelihood of one stepping on a steel caltrop increased.

Another surprised howl confirmed the strategy worked. The leftmost spearman took a step to brace for a thrust, only to drive a half-inch iron needle straight into the sole of his foot. He tripped and fell, dropping to the earth with a gasp of agony. Chae-ha's guards moved in; their greatswords flashed once more.

The spearman targeting Chae-ha herself began to grow frustrated by the floating, elusive motion of her robes. Desperate to create an opening, the man lunged at her to try and close the distance.

The young Inspector caught his foot with the panel of her robe before his boot had even hit the ground. This was the moment she had been waiting for. It was time to show off the true power of her I-ju-do – the technique Mu-cheon had taught her, which she had adapted and renamed the Silk-Whip Sweep.

“Geuk-neung-mae-jang,” Chae-ha breathed softly, her voice eerily calm. “Combination Arts: Mausoleum of Thorns.”

Chae-ha tugged sharply, yanking the man’s foot aside right as he tried to transfer his weight onto it. The spearman collapsed onto the ground, falling hard onto his back and side – directly in the center of the bed of caltrops.

Instinctively, the soldier threw his left hand out to try and catch himself – only to scream in terror and pain as a four-pointed iron needle drove itself completely through his palm. Another spike bit deeply into his thigh, while a third sank into his lower ribs. Overcome by panic, he tried to roll away, but that was the worst possible instinct. The movement caused four more hidden spikes to bury themselves deeply into his shoulders, back, and hip, causing him to gasp in agony.

Circling the edge of the spike field like a pack of hungry wolves, Noh Gu-shim and his three companions stepped in to finish the wounded soldier off. The spearmen who remained standing finally understood just how dire their situation was, gave up on trying to maintain their reach advantage, and executed a final, reckless charge.

But Chae-ha was there to stop them. As they passed by her on either side, the young girl’s Phantom Steps abruptly stopped. She flicked her wrists, and jade panels spread out to the left and right like the folds of an opening fan.

“Pye-ju-jin,” the young Inspector whispered dispassionately, as if in a trance. “Formation Arts: Blinding Silk Array.”

The rising silk panels floated directly into the two men’s faces. The gentle impact caused no damage, but the presence of the broad panels completely obscured their vision. The soldiers both cursed, stumbled, and tripped, all while clawing with one hand to try and remove the obnoxious fabric.

Their blindness only lasted a moment. But a moment was more than enough.

Noh Gu-shim stepped back, wiping the bloodied edge of his blade on his sleeve. He turned his head towards Chae-ha, his gaze filled with a silent, profound respect. He brought his right fist firmly against his open left palm, offering her a formal, martial salute.

Chae-ha allowed the panels of her robes to settle, placing her hands back into her oversized sleeves as her personal guards returned to their defensive positioning around her. The young girl nodded in satisfaction.

The area in front of the gatehouse was quiet once more. The breach had been dealt with, the choke point was secure, and the integrity of the wall was protected.

All that remained now was for Mu-cheon to do his part.

Seeing the braced wall of spears waiting for them, Mu-cheon pulled hard on his reins, bringing his stallion to a sliding halt ten paces short of the hedge of iron tips. Beside him, Eom Tae-seok executed the same maneuver, their horses’ hooves throwing up clods of damp earth. The rest of their group remained mounted.

Across the short gap, the Meng-shan Lesser Elder stood behind his double rank of spearmen, a cold, triumphant sneer spreading across his face.

“I must admit, Young Master. You’ve been much more resourceful than I anticipated. But it’s only a matter of time until the oxen bleed out, and you are all swept away before the endless tide. This ends here.”

“Oh, shut it. Didn’t anyone tell you I’m the one who’s supposed to have the loud mouth?”

Mu-cheon stooped low, his large hands wrapping around the belt of a dead spearman. Invoking the first mnemonic of the Chuk-ap-gyeol, he let out a sharp grunt as he swung the body around like a hammer thrower. With a roaring exhale, he launched the corpse straight into the center of the Meng-shan spear wall.

The body slammed directly into the cluster of pointed wooden shafts, knocking men apart, snapping a few shafts, and opening a hole in their formation.

“Throwing bodies?” Tae-seok grunted, his single eye glinting with amusement. “When all of this is around?”

The Outer Elder stepped over to a piece of iron shrapnel that had broken off the siege ballista. Without breaking stride, he reached down and picked up an oversized, bent crank handle. With a fierce overhand throw, he hurled the oversized iron bar at the spearmen who were still standing.

The bent iron pole whistled through the air and slammed into the left side of what remained of the spear wall. Bucklers splintered into kindling, spear shafts sheared in half, and two spearmen were killed outright by the impact.

The remaining Tai-shan cavalry resumed their charge, ready to engage a spear wall that no longer looked like much of a threat.

Wi Mo cursed. “Stand your ground!” he demanded of his men, backing his horse up as much as he could. His eyes trailed off to the side.

Before Mu-cheon and Tae-seok could take advantage of the carnage they’d created and reach the strategist, an unexpected, agony-filled bellow echoed from nearby.

Driven by four Meng-shan spearmen repeatedly thrusting their weapons into its left flank, a gigantic, bleeding ox bolted straight down the lane. Overcome by blinding pain, the half-ton beast came bearing down on Mu-cheon and Tae-seok like a battering ram.

Mu-cheon turned his head just in time to see the beast’s horns closing in on him from three paces away. There was no time to jump clear.

Dropping his Dae-jik-do into the dirt, Mu-cheon reached out with both hands to catch the horns before the sharp tips could impale his chest.

The impact hit him like a rolling granite boulder. The ox’s immense momentum shoved the Young Master backwards, his footwear digging two deep trenches into the soil as his heels skidded helplessly through the gravel. The beast thrashed its thick neck upwards, trying to toss him aside and yanking his arms around with bone-rattling force.

Muscle strained against muscle. Mu-cheon’s teeth ground together as he fought to keep from being thrown under the trampling hooves. Recognizing he was about to be crushed, the young man pivoted to one side, turning the ox’s head as he did so.

The beast’s unstoppable momentum carried it straight past where he was standing. But as the animal stumbled past his chest, Mu-cheon pulled his face right up against its ear – and let the voice of the Great Bell ring out. At maximum volume.

A red spray burst from the animal’s nostrils and ears as the acoustic pressure ruptured delicate tissue. The ox’s eyes rolled back in its head in shock. Its front legs lost coordination, and it began to stumble around as if drunk.

All of a sudden, Eom Tae-seok appeared again underneath the beast, shrouded in fog.

“Je-un-pa,” the veteran general breathed, as his Cham-ma-do rocketed towards the sky. “Cloud-Dispersing Strike!”

The shockwave from the collapsing vacuum in the wake of his slash blew the fog away. And where once there had been an ox, there was now only two raw sides of beef, sliding against each other as they spurted blood and bile onto the ground.

Silence settled over the area.

Mu-cheon caught his breath. The young man wiped a smudge of soot from his cheek and retrieved his Dae-jik-do from the dirt. Then he looked over at the enemy strategist.

The Inner Disciple offered him a slow, wide grin that showed all his teeth.

“So, now,” the Young Master said, stepping over the remains of what would be that night’s dinner. “Where were we?”

The resulting look of disbelief remained stuck on Wi Mo’s face long after his head had been separated from his shoulders.
