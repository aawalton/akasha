With the Rift being at the far end of the chamber and the demon's grievous injuries, Rowan thought he’d be able to catch it.

He thought wrong.

The creature used every single point of Dexterity at its disposal—its almost seventy levels granting it speed Rowan wasn’t able to match.

Mist erupted from its feet in a thin line, a sharp contrast to the pseudo Domain it exhibited earlier.

It’s almost out of mana.

Even still, the spell activated.

The distance between them grew even larger as the demon used [Mist Step], and in response, Rowan started casting.

[Firebolt] was his quickest spell by far, but even that didn’t seem like it would be enough.

The demon reappeared in reach of the Rift. It glanced back, extending its remaining hand toward the spatial tear. The expression on its face was one of contempt and confusion, but when it noticed the rapidly forming spell in the palm of Rowan’s hand, it morphed into terror.

A clawed finger just barely grazed the edge, and the Rift pulsed red.

Rowan forced his mana to move faster. The orb of flame filled rapidly, his Intent following a heartbeat later.

With a flick of his wrist, he sent the spell flying.

The demon disappeared.

“FUCK!” Rowan yelled, watching his spell pass harmlessly through the Rift and impacting the wall behind it. An explosion followed, but he wasn’t paying attention. Instead, his gaze was entirely focused on the pulsing redness of the tear in space floating just a dozen feet away.

Rowan’s muscles tensed, the fight leaving him antsy and hesitant to believe it was truly over.

Maybe it’s another illusion.

He glanced around the chamber, seeing nothing but charred stone walls and a faint light coming from the staircase leading towards the surface. There was no way to know for sure, but Rowan had an inkling that the demon hadn’t faked its escape.

For one, its injuries were definitely real. The empowered [Fireball] wasn’t something it could shrug off with tricks and deceit. It might have been a much higher level than Rowan, but that didn’t mean it could perform miracles.

His eyes returned to the Rift.

Its purple glow had morphed into an angry red at the creature's touch, almost as if it had reacted to it.

But that wasn’t possible.

In fact, just it being here was impossible.

Rowan had never heard of a Rift forming outside the Far Wilds—that’s why they were called that in the first place. The mana density required was magnitudes higher than anywhere in the Stormspire Heights, and beyond, besides past the Northern Reaches.

He’d seen one only once before. A year before his twelfth birthday.

His mother had taken him to an outpost their family controlled that sprang up around one that was close to breaking. Only that one had been much, much larger. It had filled Rowan with a sense of awe that few things he’d witnessed could match—and he’d seen Archmages duel.

There was just something inherently wondrous about them—about knowing that beyond that edge lay a whole other world. Or at least a fraction of one. So even if this one was smaller and stranger, Rowan still felt that same wonder. Only this time it was combined with the thrill still pumping through his veins.

He took a step back, letting out a slow, drawn-out breath.

With one last glance around the chamber, Rowan dropped his spells.

His muscles immediately tensed, a sense of foreboding washing over him, but [Iron Will] dealt with it easily enough.

In and out, he told himself. It’s over, for now.

Slowly, Rowan relaxed.

It started at his shoulders and moved all the way down to his legs. A comfortable ache settled in his muscles, the familiar exhaustion of a battle well fought. Rowan tested his channels, feeling a slight hitch.

The empowered [Fireball] had been different from any spell he’d cast so far, and Rowan had a feeling it had something to do with it almost becoming a Hymn.

He closed his eyes, trying to recall the moment.

The demon had been pinned down under his onslaught of Wind, the Crystal Barrier the only thing keeping it protected. Rowan remembered the feeling of determination that filled him, vowing that the Demon wouldn’t end his Path.

Something important had happened, and considering it had strained his channels, Rowan was pretty sure it was connected to his trait.

His lips curled up into a smile.

For the first time since the Plateau, Rowan made some tangible progress in that regard.

I might have tapped into my Wellspring without knowing it, he thought. Which probably means I used Spirit.

That just made his smile widen.

He had some thoughts about what Spirit did, but with only one example of its use, Rowan hadn’t wanted to make assumptions. Especially considering what they represented.

Spirit is most likely some kind of resource. And that sea is my repository of it. Horus’s [Arcane Soul] gives him a seemingly unlimited pool of mana, so it’s entirely possible my [Immortal Soul] gives me the same for Spirit.

But that wasn’t what sent his mind racing.

When Rowan fought the Wyrmlings, he’d wanted them to be gone, and his trait made it happen. The soundless explosion that followed did just that, hurling the monsters away and splattering them against the walls, [Immortal Soul] effectively turning his desires into reality.

This time, he wanted to win, and that desire empowered his spell.

Priest Aegar said Spirit wasn’t something people used. That it wasn’t possible to regenerate.

The kindly man had helped Rowan by freely offering his knowledge, but there was a distinct possibility he was wrong about that particular fact.

When the team gets here, I need to have a conversation with Zoe.

If there was someone who could shed some light on the situation, it was probably the person sent by a literal Goddess. All of them already knew who he was, so there wasn’t really a point in hiding his Trait. Or well, Traits.

But he didn’t need someone to state the obvious.

Spirit allowed him to manifest his will.

There was certainly a whole lot more to it—with depths he probably couldn’t begin to imagine—but that felt like a good starting point.

He let Fire and Wind trace through his channels. Rowan didn’t move them in any particular pattern, simply guiding them along his body and feeling the familiar ache. It wasn’t debilitating, but it was certainly there.

Still, better than after the Plateau. He turned around, walking a few steps away from the Rift and summoning a chair from the Vault.

I wonder why that is?

Rowan did the same with a canteen and drank his fill. As he did so, a black-feathered raven flew into the chamber, letting out an excited trill when he saw him.

Kai barreled into Rowan, feet finding purchase on his arm and hopping onto his shoulder, nuzzling against his cheek.

“Told you I’d be fine,” Rowan smiled, scratching his beak. “But we have a problem.”

Kai glanced at the Rift, curiously tilting his head to the side.

“The bastard went in there, and I think I have to follow him.”

With a wary trill, Kai nodded towards the staircase, claws tightening slightly around Rowan’s shoulder.

“I beat him once,” he replied. “And besides, leaving the Demon alive feels like a very, very bad idea.”

Rowan pulled out a treat, feeding it to Kai. “It’s cornered,” he said, eyes firmly planted on the Rift. “If I fight it in there, it won’t be able to run away. And if I manage to wear it down, I might even get some answers about what it’s doing here.”

He pushed away his musings about [Immortal Soul] for now. It wasn’t something he’d figure out in the next few minutes, and while he was certainly excited about finally making some progress, right now, his priority was the Demon.

Standing back up, Rowan walked over to the Rift, circling it with a critical eye.

The red flashing hadn’t subsided, ethereal sparks bleeding off the edge as the spatial tear pulsed and writhed. His heartbeat quickened, weariness warring with excitement.

With a deep breath, Rowan scanned it.

Name: The Blackened Keep

Type: Rift - [CORRUPTED]

Rank: Tier 2

“Well, that explains the red,” he muttered, brows furrowed in thought. “I’ve never heard of a Corrupted Rift before,” Rowan glanced at Kai. “You?”

His familiar shook his head.

Rowan sighed. “Yeah, thought so.”

For a moment, an urge to reach out welled up inside him—a desire to feel and see what this next challenge had in store for him. But Rowan didn’t move, forcing his hands to remain steady at his side. He’d find out what lay beyond this spatial anomaly soon enough, and jumping headfirst into it was as dangerous as it was stupid.

It was a fair assumption that the Rift was the Demon’s true lair, with this chamber only serving as an entrance. Rowan’s best bet would have probably been to jump in right after it, but that would have meant fighting another battle at less than his best. Something he was loath to do, considering who his opponent was.

Despite his enlarged reserves and newly acquired efficiency, there was no telling what kind of dangers lurked behind the sundered veil.

Rowan closed his eyes, shifting his focus inward, and started meditating.

Around half an hour later, he stood before the Rift, a river of mana coursing through his channels. It had taken him less than he thought to refill his Core—which was another thing he needed to test once this was over—but it wasn’t like he would complain about it.

“So, what do we know?” he asked, absentmindedly stroking Kai’s feathers. “It’s called the Blackened Keep, so a castle of some kind. Now the only question is where it’s going to place me.”

As a Tier-2 Rift, it wasn’t going to be large enough to include a whole keep, which thankfully meant Rowan didn’t need to siege a castle today.

Tier-1 Rifts were suited for anyone below Silver to delve, with monsters being either Bronze or Iron. But Tier-2 Rifts were different. Peak Gold-rank threats could appear as Dungeon Bosses, and they rarely didn’t have an entourage.

“And that’s not including the Demon,” Rowan frowned, rubbing his chin. “Or does it? I mean, the System still classifies it as Tier-2, and if there was another monster like that inside, I doubt it’d still say that.”

Kai cawed in agreement, scraping his claws against Rowan’s coat.

“Well, no point in giving it more time to prepare,” he took a deep, calming breath, letting his lungs press against his ribs before slowly exhaling. “What kind of Duke would I be if I let a threat like this linger, right?”

Kai trilled.

Rowan knew he needed to be careful. He won that first fight for a multitude of reasons, but the monster was cunning—intelligent, even. This time, it would be prepared.

It thought I was weak, and slow, that’s why it played around, his gaze lingered on the Rift. It’s going to attack me right as I enter. When I’m still disoriented.

Rowan cracked his neck, shaking out the tension from his hands.

“Wait for me outside, alright?”

Kai bumped his head against Rowan's, hopping off his shoulder.

“I’ll try to be quick.”

Rowan pulled at his Core, an eager smile tugging at his lips.

Mana coursed through his channels, following the familiar circuit for [Fire Shield]. The spell bloomed to life a heartbeat later. It started on his arms, flowing down his back, before finally covering his legs. Rowan curled it around himself like a suit of armor, the fine control he had over the working filling him with glee.

And then, without hesitation, he reached out.

His finger touched the edge.

The Rift flashed.
