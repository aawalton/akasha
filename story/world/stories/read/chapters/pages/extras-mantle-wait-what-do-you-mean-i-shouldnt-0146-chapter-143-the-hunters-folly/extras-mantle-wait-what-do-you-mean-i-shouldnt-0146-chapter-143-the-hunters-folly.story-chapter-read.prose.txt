◈◈◈

Vienna was a city drowning in its own blood, but to Zachariah, the scent of the burning capital was nothing short of intoxicating.

For the past twenty-four hours, he hadn't slept. Hadn't stopped moving. His robes—woven from the dark blessings of his particular branch of the faith—were stained with ash from collapsed buildings and the blood of his own lesser brethren, mixed together. He stood atop the ruined steeple of a Cathedral Ward watchtower, dark eyes scanning the smoke-choked streets below, and felt something close to joy.

He was hunting a ghost. Or, at least, a man who thought he was one.

The doorman—the one the lower ranks had started whispering about in terrified, hushed tones, like saying the name too loudly might summon him faster—had been tearing through their outer perimeters for a full day and night. Pale skin, red eyes, a grin that promised violence wrapped in something almost cheerful. He killed anything and anyone short of Order IV with the kind of effortless, contemptuous precision that made grown cultists soil themselves before they died.

But Zachariah was an Underlord and not just anyone with the blessings of the Darkened One, the one who could hear his lord's voices.

He was not a prey.

The rat is slowing down. A cruel, jagged smile pulled at his gaunt face. He thinks he's clever. He thinks he can bleed our numbers dry and slip back into the dark before we close the net.

He replayed the last day in his head, and his ego swelled a little more with each pass. Three times now, he'd caught the doorman off guard. Three times they'd clashed, however briefly. The man was famous for his doors—slipping through reality itself to dodge anything lethal—but during their last exchange, Zachariah had pushed harder than ever before, landing hits of his darkened powers onto the man.

And the doorman hadn't opened a single door since then. He had taken pride and now anticipation as the one who would bring down the devil.

His spatial essence is running dry. Zachariah's grip tightened on his cleaver's hilt. He's exhausted himself. Burned through his reserves, culling the weak, and now he has nothing left for the strong.

A pulse of darkened essence flared in his communication ring.

« Favored One! The doorman is here! He's breached the armory at Out—Hold him!—he's slaughtering the vanguard! »

"Hold him there." Zachariah's Lord Flame ignited around his shoulders, a halo of dark, rotting light. "I'm coming to claim his soul."

He leapt from the steeple, letting his essence cushion the fall, and felt something he hadn't felt in years.

Finally, the hunt ends tonight.

◈◈◈

By the time Zachariah and the two Underlords who'd answered his summons reached Outpost Gamma, the courtyard was a slaughterhouse.

Dozens of bodies—Order II, Order III, the cult's expendable middle layer—lay broken among the rubble in configurations that made it clear they'd died running, not fighting. Standing in the center of the carnage was the figure Zachariah had spent a day chasing across half of Vienna's smoking ruins.

A dark trench coat, weather-beaten and billowing in the unnatural wind that the veil generated, whether anyone wanted it to or not. A scarf wound up over the lower half of his face. Shadows are doing the rest of the obscuring work.

Something was wrong with the picture, though, and it took Zachariah a beat to place it.

The doorman—elegant, lethal, known across every report for his spears—wasn't holding a spear.

Resting against his shoulder, instead, was a massive, unadorned slab of iron shaped vaguely like a greatsword, the kind of weapon that looked less like a tool and more like a punishment somebody had decided to carry around with them.

Zachariah's sneer widened as he stepped into the courtyard. "Lost your parlor tricks, rat?" His voice came out heavy with the resonant authority that high-rankers couldn't help projecting even when they were just talking shit. "Where's your spear? Did you drain your pathetic little core so completely that you're stuck swinging a slab of crude iron like some common barbarian?"

The figure didn't answer.

No witty retort. No cheerful mockery, no dramatic flourish, none of the things the reports had described in increasingly hysterical detail over the past day.

The man simply shifted his stance, weight low, both hands finding the greatsword's grip.

The silence unnerved him for about half a second. He dismissed it as the grim resignation of a man who already knew he was dead.

"Take him! He dies today!"

The two flanking Underlords surged forward first, blood-magic and shadow-lances pouring from outstretched hands, and Zachariah followed a half-step behind with his cleaver raised, aiming to take the exhausted, stupid faithless head off in one clean stroke.

The doorman planted his back foot and swung the greatsword in a single, devastating horizontal arc.

CLANG.

The force alone shattered the incoming blood-lances into crimson mist, and the shockwave from the parry cracked the pavement under everyone's feet hard enough that one of the flanking Underlords stumbled, his arcane shield disintegrating from pure blunt trauma rather than anything resembling magic.

Zachariah capitalized on the opening without hesitation, bringing his cleaver down in a vicious necrotic strike aimed at the man's exposed chest.

The doorman twisted, took the blow on the flat of his blade—and the sheer weight behind Zachariah's attack drove him backward several feet—and his boots gouging deep trenches into the stone.

A grunt escaped him.

He's struggling, Zachariah thought, triumph settling into his chest like a warm meal. His strength is failing.

Before he could press the advantage, the doorman’s legs coiled.

Without a single spark of essence—no shimmer, no fold in reality, nothing Zachariah's senses could detect as anything other than muscle—the man leapt. The force of it shattered the remaining cobblestones beneath his feet and launched him three stories into the night air. He landed on the adjacent warehouse roof without so much as a stumble, and was running again before his second boot had finished settling.

"He's running!" one of the other Underlords snarled.

"Let him." Zachariah's laugh came out ugly and wet. "Look at him leap like a frightened animal. No essence left for portals. Hunt him down—drive him toward the Cathedral Plaza! I want his head and skin flayed to leave it at the bastion as a gift.”

◈◈◈

The chase lasted another grueling hour.

Five Underlords now, Zachariah at the head of the pack, moving across rooftops like wolves who'd finally cornered something worth the effort. They rained destruction down at every angle, methodically cutting off escape routes, forcing the dark-coated figure into a corridor that bent inexorably east—directly into the heart of the cult's territory.

Every time the man deflected a strike with that absurd greatsword, he lost ground. Zachariah watched him stumble once, a glancing shadow-bolt clipping his thigh hard enough to draw another one of those low, wordless grunts.

Ecstasy was the only word for what Zachariah felt. He was orchestrating something close to perfect.

"Keep pushing him!" he barked into the ring. "He's heading straight for the Plaza. Lord Icas is waiting."

The target broke through a ruined archway and dropped heavily into the center of Cathedral Plaza—a massive open square ringed by towering spires of corrupted architecture.

A trap with the lid already half-closed.

The five Underlords descended, landing atop gargoyles and broken walls, sealing off every visible exit, and Zachariah felt the particular satisfaction of watching prey realize—a half-second too late—exactly how thoroughly it had been herded.

The doorman stood in the center of the plaza, chest heaving, the greatsword's tip dragging against stone.

Surrounded. Spent. Done.

"It is over." A voice—not loud, but carrying the chilling, razor-edged authority that came from someone who'd never once had to raise it to be obeyed.

His High Excellency Vakan stepped from the shadows of the cathedral's main doors, blood-red robes immaculate despite the carnage they'd all walked through to get here, his Lord Ember’s presence settling over the plaza like a physical weight pressing down on everyone's shoulders at once. Lord Icas Vasht walked beside him, dark robes pooling like spilled ink, a cruel smile already finding its way onto his face.

Zachariah felt the reverence and pull to his faith as he inclined his head and kept a formal, somber expression. Inside his mind, however, he was elated. The presence of Lord Icas was expected, but not of the High Excellency himself. This was his chance to shine, rise through the ranks.

"You have caused us much grief, little rat." Vakan stepped into the dim moonlight, raising a pale hand. "But your tricks end here. Activate the seals."

Icas snapped his fingers.

The plaza's perimeter erupted in violet light. Runic arrays and rituals carved into the surrounding pillars flared to full brightness.

"We have sealed off the place," Vakan said. "The fabric of reality within this plaza is locked. Not even the Hierarch could open a portal here, and the only way to escape is over my dead body."

He took another step forward. "And the essence… Icas, if you would."

Icas twisted his wrist. A secondary array hummed to life, and the ambient essence in the plaza curdled into something turbulent and wrong, the kind of poisoned atmosphere that made casting anything feel like swallowing broken glass. The Underlords positioned around the perimeter grimaced as their own auras flickered uncomfortably under the same effect—a cost they were all willing to pay to neuter whatever this man actually was.

"Your doors are gone. Your magic is poisoned." Vakan's eyes gleamed with absolute, settled victory. "You're cut off from your power entirely. Drop the sword and kneel, and I may allow you to keep your mind before I present you to the lord."

Zachariah dropped into the plaza behind the target, landing a dozen feet back, hefting his cleaver with a grin that showed too many teeth. "Let me take his legs, Your Highness. He made me run for a full day. He owes me a toll."

The figure in the center of the plaza didn't drop his sword.

Didn't kneel.

He let out a slow, heavy sigh instead—a sound entirely devoid of fear, which should have meant something to everyone present and apparently meant nothing at all.

Then he reached up with his free hand, pulled the scarf away from his face, and shrugged the dark coat off his shoulders, and just like that, his figure also shimmered.

Zachariah frowned.

The man standing in front of them was not the lithe, pale youth with the manic grin and the crimson eyes that every field report had described in exhausting, increasingly terrified detail.

This man was old. His face was a road map of scars, and a single terrible scar traveled diagonally from his upper lip down through his neck. His hair was silver, pulled back tight against his skull.

And his eyes—cold, metallic, the color of a blade right before it's used—locked onto Vakan with the unhurried, predatory calm of something ancient looking at an insect.

Zachariah didn't recognize the face but…

Vakan did.

Vakan stopped dead in his tracks. The smug, triumphant smile slid off his face like it had been physically struck loose, and the color drained out of his cheeks until he looked the shade of old parchment left too long in the sun. His Order V presence—normally so suffocating it pressed conversations flat before they started—shivered with something raw and instinctual.

"No," Vakan whispered. The word barely cleared his throat. "It—it cannot be."

"My Lord?" Icas stepped forward, confused by the sudden paralysis. "What is it?"

Vakan took a staggering step back. "The Destabilizers. The Spatial Seals—When!"

"Everything is functioning perfectly." Icas gestured at the glowing runes with the patient confusion of a man explaining the obvious. "He has no essence. He cannot cast. He's trapped."

"You fools." Vakan's voice cracked, climbing toward something close to hysteria. "He doesn't use essence."

Zachariah's eyes narrowed, his grip tightening on the cleaver. "What does it matter who he is? He's just a man with a sword. I'll end him."

He surged forward, channeling the last dregs of his dark essence into the blade, aiming a lethal strike directly at the exposed line of the old man's spine.

The silver-haired man didn't turn his head.

He simply shifted his weight and swung the greatsword backward with one hand.

No incantation. No flare of astral light, no frost, no detonation of essence. Just pure, unadulterated martial perfection—the kind that came from thousands upon thousands of hours of agonizing physical discipline distilled down into a single motion that had nothing to do with rank and everything to do with competence.

The iron blade struck Zachariah's cleaver.

His weapon didn't just break. It exploded into shrapnel, a hundred fragments of enchanted steel scattering across the plaza, and the greatsword continued its arc uninterrupted, burying itself deep into Zachariah's ribcage like the cleaver had never been there at all.

Zachariah's eyes went wide. He felt ribs shatter, organs liquefy under physical pressure that shouldn't have been possible from a man with no essence behind the swing, and the last coherent thought he managed before his body left the ground was a confused, panicked attempt to understand how.

He flew fifty yards across the plaza and hit a stone pillar with a sound that ended any further questions. As the world dimmed around the edges and finally went out, the last thing Zachariah's failing eyes registered was the old man calmly resting the greatsword back against his shoulder, as he'd just finished a chore rather than ended a life.

The four remaining Underlords froze. Their bravado—the entire structure of confidence that had carried them through a day-long hunt—shattered in the same instant their leader hit the pillar.

Icas stared, horror creeping into his voice. "Who are you?"

Vakan's hands trembled.

"Salvatore Silvers." Vakan's voice barely carried the name, like saying it any louder might draw the man's attention more fully than it already had. "The Starkiller."

Salvatore rolled his shoulders. A chorus of pops and cracks echoed through the magically silenced plaza, loud in the absence of anything else, and he looked up at the glowing spatial seals overhead, then down at the essence destabilizers still humming away on the stone beneath his boots.

A slow, feral grin spread across his scarred face—a smile carrying no magic at all, and somehow radiating an overlord’s aura of pure, suffocating bloodlust that made the surrounding air feel thinner and colder all at once.

"Gotta admit, scum." His gravelly voice carried across the plaza without any arcane amplification; it didn't need any. "When Joe told me you idiots would try to trap him by locking down space and poisoning the essence..."

He gripped the greatsword with both hands, lowering his stance.

"...he forgot to mention you were actually locking yourselves in a cage with me."

“Besides, Revenant may have escaped my hands. You, however…”

Vakan felt a chill race down his spine, freezing something in his blood that hadn't been frozen in decades.

Salvatore's silver eyes flashed in the moonlight.

"Now," the Overlord said, voice dropping to something almost gentle. "Let me show you what a real warrior looks like."
