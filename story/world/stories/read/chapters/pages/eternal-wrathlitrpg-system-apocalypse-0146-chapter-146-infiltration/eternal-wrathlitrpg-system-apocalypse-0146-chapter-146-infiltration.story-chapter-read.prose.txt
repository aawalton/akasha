Charging through the basement was akin to charging through a police station. There were guards everywhere. That was why Andrew relied on James’ magic—his mind magic. Illusion could distort what the opponent saw.

Illusions could make them invisible or create bait out of thin air. It worked by distorting what the opponent saw. But James had to see the opponent before casting the spell.

The Census Bureau was a four-story building with two basement floors, spanning almost a soccer field wide.

“Even if I hit them with an illusion, those guards are literally standing in front of the door,” James muttered.

“Kouki can distract them. Try to hand them food. Then we can slip in from the side. Ask about the trader’s location and give them food as thanks. Afterward, you leave, and in three hours you need to do the same thing again,” Andrew said.

“Understood. I can do that.” Kouki nodded.

The plan worked wonders. First, James cast an illusion on the two guards in front of the door. The illusion didn’t harm them, merely erasing James and Andrew from their sight. Then, after he gave the okay to Kouki, Kouki went to the guards.

“Ah, guards. I want to ask you a question. Do you know where the trader’s location is?” Kouki didn’t ask them to come closer. That would have been too suspicious, but he positioned himself a little farther away, prompting the guards to move closer. It was just three meters, so the guards thought nothing of it. The distance was more than enough for James and Andrew to move in.

Andrew opened the door, and both slipped in.

“From here on out, any soldiers we meet are hostile. Last time I was here, a guard was with me. If we come here without a guard, it would be suspicious.” Andrew paused. “Wait, can you turn me or yourself into a guard?”

“I could. But I need to see them first,” James nodded.

“That’s a bummer. The basement is filled with narrow corridors. I don’t think we can avoid eye contact. Will your illusion still work then?”

“Imagine if you were a guard, then you found a hostile enemy inside your camp, and then a second later the enemy was gone. Would you ignore it?” James answered with a question. “Illusions are not brainwashing, Andrew.”

“They won’t conveniently think it was just wind or a hallucination, will they?”

“I don’t know,” James answered.

“Then, if worse comes to worst, we have to kill any guard we come across.” Andrew’s face tightened. James had killed a human before, both in real life and in dungeons, so he merely answered with a nod.

Luckily, the basement was practically empty. James only met three guards; all were alone and distracted. After casting illusions, he and Andrew went past them and reached the prison area.

The good news ended there as cries of anguish echoed.

“Argh!! You’ve got to be kidding me! I am innocent! The trial is bullshit!” A man cried out in despair. The two infiltrators hastened their steps. They felt dread in their hearts. The atmosphere here was filled with fear and blood.

“Were they being tortured? I don’t recall them being that malicious,” James muttered.

“Hurry, something might happen to them.” The scream stopped, and a dead silence filled the corridors. Andrew raced to the source, fearing for Anderson’s life.

James followed him. Andrew had clearly forgotten that this was a stealth mission as he slammed the door open. There, he found a man without his feet. His eyes followed the trail of blood to a guard—the solid evidence in his hand.

“What are you doing here? This area is off-limits! You are violating the law!” The guard glared at Andrew, picking up a meat cleaver from the table.

“You are violating the law! Why are you killing him?” Andrew pointed his spear at him while the butcher drew his cleaver. A golden glow shone from it. The butcher wasted no time. Unlike most civilians or even guards in the city, these men attacked Andrew on sight. No words of accusation or threats of trial.

Their decisive action stunned Andrew for a moment, but James was not fazed. He had drawn his sword and activated his sword skill the moment the butcher had picked up a weapon. The two sword skills clashed, the sound reverberating across the room. Recalling that they were on a stealth mission sent dread down Andrew’s spine.

“Let’s hurry and kill them all!” Andrew picked up his spear.

“Already done!” James said as he slashed the butcher across the chest. Thanks to his level-ups and stats, he won the skill clash. And thanks to his superior weapon, as soon as the clash ended, his sword went through the meat cleaver and into the man’s torso. The blade bisected him across the torso. His upper and lower body separated before he perished.

The three guards with him picked up their weapons as well—carving knives and meat cleavers. They charged with a cry. James was bewildered. These soldiers didn’t activate their weapon skills and merely charged like fools. He hesitated to meet them head-on and sniped them with his horn instead.

Lightning bolts carved holes through their bodies. He then swung his sword at the last approaching soldier. The soldier was pushed back, and the blade lacerated the charging man across the torso. The stench of blood thickened as James beat the last soldier. These soldiers wore no armor, so even James’ normal attacks proved effective.

“Argh…” The soldiers groaned. James glanced over his shoulder and found Andrew, spear at the ready, but he hadn’t gotten a chance to do anything. James lifted his sword, ready to deliver the coup de grâce, but Andrew stopped him.

“Wait a minute. Let’s interrogate these soldiers. Hey! What’s happening here? Who ordered you to torture the man? That’s not what the verdict of the trial said! The verdict said these men were supposed to be executed in two weeks!”

“…Andrew, I don’t think they were here to torture the man. He was already dead, with a knife at his neck,” James said.

James didn’t want to think about it, but there was only one conclusion. James had spent some time in the dungeon without supplies, where hunger was constant. He had never eaten a human, but he had eaten a humanoid monster before.

“Huh? What are you talking about? What else was it if it was not torture? He cut off his feet and arms! This is nothing else… but… torture.” Andrew’s sentence trailed off. Realization dawned on him at what James meant. He pulled back from the fallen soldier.

“No way…”

“Shit! We are under direct orders from the Count! We must do this if we are to save the town! Don’t interrupt us!” the soldier who was downed by a lightning bolt argued.

“Was the food situation truly that dire? Anyway, I don’t think ten people could feed an entire town, though. How many people were here anyway? Five thousand? Yeah, there is no way ten people could save them,” James muttered. In the trial dungeon, one orc could only last him two or three days. There was no way ten men could feed an entire town.

“Shut up and begone! If you leave us alone, we will turn a blind eye to your transgression!”

“Like hell I would trust you. You attacked me unprovoked, remember?” James said.

“Die! Even in our death, we will do our duty and save this town!” The guard cursed for the last time before making a desperate charge. Without any armor, weapon skills, or stats, he didn’t have any chance of victory.

James looked over at another guard with a lacerated torso. Unlike the brave soldier from earlier, this one tried to flee. He slammed open the door on the other side and ran without even trying to close it. Seeing his defenseless back prompted James to shoot him with his bow, but Andrew stopped him.

“Wait! Let’s follow him. We might learn who the mastermind of this whole thing is.”

“…Alright.” James stopped. It was in times like this that he relied on Andrew’s calm and calculating manner. James then jogged, following the running soldier with Andrew in tow.

“Have you been here before, Andrew?”

“That room before was the meeting room, not the torture room. I had met Anderson there before. The jail is that way.” Andrew pointed to the room on the left.

“But he is running past it. Should we stop giving chase?”

“Let’s free Anderson first. We can chase him after Anderson is free.”

James rammed through the wooden door. It shattered into pieces as he found people tied up like livestock.

“James! What are you doing here?” Silas said.

“We came to save you, Anderson. You too, Silas. Let’s bring everyone out of here. I feel something fishy is going on here. The people have gone nuts.” Andrew came over. He then brought out a knife and cut the restraints. “Where are the others?”

“The guards took them, and they never returned.”

“Geez, this town is crazy. Why did they put everything in one building? Yes, it was easier to guard and manage, but look at the mess they caused. A single candle and it all burned up. This kind of thing would never pass a safety inspection,” Silas sighed. “And they put all the blame on us.”

James eyed the government official, tilting his head. How did Silas know it was the fault of a candle? From the town guard’s testimony, it was supposed to have been caused by a torch.

James wasn’t the sharpest tool in the shed, but if there was one thing he gained from living alone, it was a sharpened instinct. His instinct told him Silas was suspicious. He couldn’t put his finger on it, but somehow, he knew Silas had dragged them through the mud.

“It was your fault, wasn’t it?” James muttered. His eyes were on Silas.

“Huh? What are you talking about, James? What is my fault? This town and its authoritarian law are the ones at fault!”

“You’re the one who burned the granary, right?” James accused him.

“What good would it do if you knew? It’s too late already. The verdict is out. But the answer is no. I did not do it,” Silas denied.

However, James was not convinced. He glared.

“You knew something, didn’t you? Tell me,” James said. His sword was at Silas’ neck.
