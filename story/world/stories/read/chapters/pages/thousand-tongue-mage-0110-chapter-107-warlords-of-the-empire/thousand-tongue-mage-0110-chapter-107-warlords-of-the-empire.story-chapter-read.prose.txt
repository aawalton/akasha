Back in the Hagi’Shar Forward Army he’d been assigned to, Enki had been taught to count every last one of his kills. They were all told it was for organization’s sake—that they needed to know how many scouts to send out to retrieve the bug carcasses in a timely manner—but mostly, counting was for morale. It was for men who needed proof they were doing something to whittle down the Swarm.

He hadn’t counted the number of bugs he’d crushed under his boot in years, and he wasn’t going to start now.

A wormhole tore open before him, and he dashed through it, coming out sideways three streets over with his bayonet already swinging. The blade punched through a Giant-Class ant’s eye ridge and out the back of its skull with a clean, bored shove. He twisted, yanked, and stepped on its falling body as he fired his rifle one-handed behind him. Another giant ant’s head exploded, chitin punched through by whatever projectile he’d shoved into the chamber.

By the time the other ants responded to his appearance, he’d already warped away again. Wormhole. Street. Wormhole. Rooftop. Wormhole. Alley.

The city tried to swallow him with noise—civilians shrieking and flames popping as they ate timber across the cit—but he kept his mind focused on the sensation of every bug’s aura around him.

Noise didn’t matter. Motion did. Half-inorganic as his body was, he could fight like this for a very, very long time. Maybe even forever, since he didn’t have to eat or drink or sleep… but forever wasn’t fast enough. Every time he thought he found an opportunity to warp straight to the southern wall, some cluster of civilians would appear like bait thrown into the road. There were people crouched under broken carts, stuffed into small houses, and pressed behind cabinets and closets everywhere. He heard their breathing before he saw them, and the bugs heard them too.

His jaw set as he warped, drove his bayonet through another giant ant, and used its corpse as a shield against a clawed slash from another ant.

He could ignore the civilians. He could choose the southern wall, and he could choose Regalia, who was the only target that really mattered here, but…

A girl’s voice cut through his wicked thoughts as cleanly as a blade.

‘You are an organic human, in every definition of the word, and you are an inorganic god, in every tongue there exists in this world.’

‘You will stand between all of humanity and the Swarm as the most powerful, most beautiful being there is.’

‘Don’t give up on anyone.’

… And all of humanity meant every single one.

No matter who they were, and no matter what they’d done, as long as there was a bug in the same room as them, he would save the human and kill the bug.

That was what he’d promised the Worm Children of Immanu.

He warped again. He found a family pinned in a collapsed shop, their backs pressed to splintered shelves while a giant ant clawed at the doorway. One shot to the back of the head took it down, and then he opened a wormhole straight inside the shop, yanking each member of the family out like sacks of grain.

“Go north,” he said flatly.

There was no time to see if they’d heard him, because there were more civilians to warp out and even more bugs to kill. The southern wall was still far away. Too far, with this many interruptions—every second he spent saving one alley was another second Regalia gained at the wall—but what he wanted was an absolute victory. Not a half-baked one.

Suddenly, a four-armed Mutant-Class fire ant burst through a windowframe before him in a spray of brick and ash, already cutting two of its own palms as it landed. Then it activated its Art and pointed its palms at him, and a gush of crimson blood shot out its woudns—turning into tens of thousands of tiny fire ants that ignited into a wave of flames.

He was about to warp out of the way when he caught a glimpse of movement behind him. They were hard to spot, but there were two children in the house behind him, hiding under a table.

The thought of dodging ended there. He planted his feet instead of moving, brought both forearms up, and expelled cryogenic mist from his skin in a violent, sustained burst. Blue cold tore out of his limbs and met the oncoming fire head-on. The wave of burning ants detonated into steam as frost and heat collided, but the wave didn’t stop. The Mutant-Class ant cut two more of its palms on its mandibles and pointed them at him, shooting even more fire ants at him.

So there are even Mutant-Class ants in the capital now.

He skidded back across the street, boots carving gouges through soot and rubble as the pressure shoved him several meters back. Heat licked through his cold barrier and bit into his skin, and he grimaced as two more heavy impacts hit the roofs on either side of the street. Two more Mutant-Class fire ants had landed on them, slitting their own palms open as well before firing even more waves of tiny fire ants at him.

The three-way wave of fire overlapped into sheets of living flame that swallowed the entire street. He grit his teeth as he biomineralized his forearms and expelled even more cryogenic mist, thicker now, denser now, the cold biting deep enough that the ground behind him fractured into screaming layers of steam. His boots slid another step back as pressure mounted.

Even this was starting to hurt his half-inorganic body.

Tch.

I need more levels of toughness.

Still, that could come later after he killed these fire ants. He simply couldn’t afford to get tripped up by them for much longer.

But just as he was about to open a wormhole beneath his feet, drop through, execute all three ants with three rapid shots from behind, before warping back to his current location to intercept the rest of the fire ants to protect the house behind him—a maneuver that’d cost him more stamina than he liked, but which might be necessary—a fourth figure tore across the roofs before him.

Steel and sawteeth flashed, and the first Mutant-Class ant on the left roof jerked as its head separated cleanly from its body.

The shadow didn’t stop. It dashed across the gap between roofs in a single bound, boots striking stone once before the second decapitation landed, then the third. Three cuts. Three heads hit the street in the same second, followed a moment later by their bodies collapsing in different directions.

After five more seconds, their living fire ant waves sputtered out, allowing him to lower his arms and stop releasing cryogenic mist.

He looked up, scratching his steaming forearms as he did.

Kita Salaqa stood at the edge of the roof, both sawtooth blades dripping dark. Her armor was scratched and torn in more places than it wasn’t, soot smeared across her cheek and jaw, and her breaths were fast and uneven. She looked exhausted—her cape did not. The living mass of muddy red army ants billowed behind her, and the embroidered crest of the Attini Empire flashed whenever firelight caught it.

It looked a little ridiculous.

And as she stared down at him, chest heaving, only one thought came to his mind:

“Are you copying me?” he said.

Kita blinked.

Color rushed to her face instantly.

“Of… of course not!” she said. “It’s just… this cape is a gift from…” Then she cut herself off, groaned, and scrubbed a hand through her hair like she was trying to rip the thought out by force. “Oh, never mind. You’re not the only one allowed to wear a cape, are you?”

Enki didn’t blink. “Okay.”

“More importantly,” she dropped down from the roof, a small chunk of army ants breaking from her cape to form a pillow beneath her, cushioning the landing, “I’m here to help you reach Regalia. Conserve your strength and stamina for her. I can take care of the small ones.”

And she meant it. For as long as Enki had known her, he had never seen her as cool and composed as she was now.

Maybe it’s because of the cape.

Do I… like capes?

Then a roar called down from the north, and both of them turned their heads to look.

Far in the distance, shouts and screams echoed across the city as the northern ramparts emptied themselves. Ropes dropped. Mushroom scaffoldings bloomed down the northern walls. Tens of thousands of soldiers dropped down into the city in waves with blades and rifles in hands, and even the cannons on the walls were turning. Artillery that’d spent the entire morning firing outward finally pivoted inward, thundering into major bug breaches across the city with less than perfect precision. Entire clusters of giant ants still vanished in concussive fire, because with enough shells, anything could be hit.

And leading the descending army at the very front was a particular Magicicada Mage, whose fiery explosions blossomed again and again down the streets, erupting into pillars so massive even Enki could feel their heat from here.

Kita grinned as she watched her empire follow her into war. Enki didn’t. He watched and understood, with cold clarity, that he could never do something like that. He could never stand somewhere high and make men move with his words alone.

Back when he’d first arrived in Immanu as a soldier abandoned by his Forward Army, he’d had a lot of trouble communicating with the Worm Children of Immanu, who’d given him his new Whiteworm Class.

… Zora Fabre.

If there is one man who can unite all of humanity against the Swarm…

For starters, it would be a man who could speak every human tongue.

Just then, a new sound cut through the roars and shouts in the distance. Both Enki and Kita turned once again. A giant ant-pulled carriage barreled around the corner, crushing debris beneath its wheels and running over one of the Mutant-Class carcasses Kita had made. As it screeched to a halt before the two of them, Ifas waved at them with the reins loose in one hand.

“I got strips of ant meat cooking in a pot inside the carriage!” Ifas called cheerfully. “Get in! Eat to your heart’s content while I get you to the southern wall!”

Kita stared at him. “But how did you even get here so—”

“Get in,” Enki said plainly, shoving Kita through a wormhole and putting her inside the carriage. Then he hopped onto the roof of the carriage himself, giving Ifas a quick nod.

The moment they were both in place, Ifas cracked the reins, and the giant silver ant surged forward, barreling straight down the main street toward the southern walls.
