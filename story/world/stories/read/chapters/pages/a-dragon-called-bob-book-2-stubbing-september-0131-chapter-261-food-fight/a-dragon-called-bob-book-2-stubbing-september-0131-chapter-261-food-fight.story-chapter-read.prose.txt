Splinterpick moved slowly backwards, his cleaver held out to his side, eyes locked on my own. He smiled ever so slightly, a smug sense of superiority I planned to disabuse him of shortly.

“You know they call me the Dreadchef?” he asked.

“A habit of causing food poisoning?”

“Never! I am the Dreadchef because none can match my culinary skills. My knife work is supreme, my flavour palette is exquisite, and my mouth feel is perfection.”

“I’ve never really understood the whole mouth feel thing. I mean, I’ve eaten some pretty funky stuff, in some cases both raw and alive, but it doesn’t matter much when the fangs close down.”

“Perhaps I can enlighten you?”

A chopping board appeared in front of him, and he was suddenly surrounded by ingredients floating about him like a nutrient-rich halo. Vegetables, cuts of meat, pots filled with what I assumed were spices, and various cooking utensils drifted in rings around him as though he were a gas giant that had gathered edible moons.

“As an opener, flash-fried borgon fish, with a bouquet of spring vegetables.” The ingredients passed across his floating chopping board, and his knife flickered. Slices flew up and landed in a pot hovering behind his right shoulder.

I tried to move, to lunge at him and punch the smug look off his face, but my body didn’t respond. My mouth began to fill with saliva. As the raw materials landed in the pot, the base of it glowed red, and the air began to taste divine.

“It smells good,” I admitted grudgingly. It really did, better than anything Gorboon had ever produced. How the hell could I get out of this? Some power was holding me entranced, something that had only happened once since I’d been on Helstat.

The high priestess of Karen that the dungeon had thrown me at, way back when I was still clearing floors. It wasn’t the dungeon, it was IMPS. As I recalled, the first words I’d said to the wonderful and all-powerful system that governed reality had been to demand to speak to its supervisor.

Had I set myself on a path of conflict with the one goddess you do not want to piss off right from the start? A tutorial would have been nice.

If Splinterpick were some kind of priest… dammit, using the identify spell would be pretty handy about now, but I was supposed to avoid using any of my magic. Katprick had said it was too human. Elves didn’t need to announce their spells; they just thought, and the magic did the rest.

“Now for the spices. It’s a proprietary blend, so I won’t be announcing their names. Astute viewers may recognise some of my signature picks, but the key is in the proportions. Seeing as we have a captive client, we can give it a few minutes to really let the flavours develop through the fish, adding to the dusky flavour and improving the mouth feel.”

“Mouth feel is just pretentious. It’s like sommeliers talking about hints of oak and raspberry. It’s fucking wine, you drink it to get pissed. Same with food, except it’s even lamer to be all priggish about what amounts to future-poo.”

He blanched. “You are truly a savage creature, unworthy of being called an elf. Why are you here if fine dining is a joke to you?”

“Every day something lives, something else has to die. Cow, gamblepric or aubergine; it has to die for you to live. Hell, usually we eat the young of other species, a potato on your plate is a potato plant that will never germinate.”

“The hells are you blabbering about? Has House Evergreen sent a literal madman to the Fallimpiad?”

“You can dress it up as fine dining, but what it comes down to is killing and eating.” The way I’d dealt with that Priestess of Karen had been to play into her bit, but the same approach didn’t seem to be working this time.

“There is something of the divine in fine food. Something that apparently you are completely blind to,” he sneered.

“Oh, there’s something divine in it, all right. What doesn’t kill you makes you stronger.” Biomass, despite my current issue of over-gathering the stuff whenever I killed anything, was certainly useful.

“Let’s see how you feel about my borgon caldeirada especial!” He flicked his knife in my direction, and the pot flew over to hover over my mouth. Some force tilted my head back and forced my jaws open. The steaming liquid tipped down my throat, all in one go. Force feeding was not on my to-do list this morning, but the thing about force is it doesn’t give you a bloody choice.

Swallowing convulsively, the boiling liquid vanished into the cavern of my stomach.

19kg of biomass added to storage.

That was a lot of biomass for one pot of admittedly tasty fish stew. I coughed and sucked in a deep breath, hoping I could find a way out before he put the Hamster Cheeks evolution to a real test.

“Needs more salt.”

Spinterpick stumbled towards me, his orbiting ingredients trailing along behind him. “It was perfectly seasoned!” he snarled.

“The paprika was barely noticeable.”

“There wasn’t any paprika in it, fool!” He stopped inching reluctantly towards me. Well, at least I’d found out how to break his system.

“There should have been,” I replied. He took another step forward.

“Bullshit! I will not be critiqued by an elf that lives on raw food!”

“Hell, dude, I eat the hooves and horns as well. But I prefer a good roast.” I wasn’t lying.

“Let’s see how you handle a hotpot!”

The knife flickered as more ingredients passed between it and the board, then circled back up to land in the pan. Everything moved so quickly that I could hardly follow the chopped chunks of food flying around the short elf. He grinned evilly at me, no doubt enjoying my shocked expression.

“You had no idea what you were facing? I’m the Dreadchef, a master of my trade! Perhaps a taste of flambe nimblebonk will help you realise my culinary superiority!”

The pot burst into flame and moved to hover over my mouth again. I felt like a goddamn sex doll, mouth locked open in a perpetually silent scream at the abuse I was subjected to. The meal, fire and all disappeared down my gullet.

24.7kg of biomass added to storage.

“Needs more heat. That was barely over nine thousand on the Scoville scale. Your powers are weak, old man.”

“I am the master!”

“You were the master. I am no longer an apprentice.” I was running on fumes. That shit had been hot as Nyal’s lair. I was manually closing my pores with the shapeshifting power to stop from dripping sweat.

His foot rose, and he stopped closer to me. My hand moved slightly, straining for his throat.

“You think you are strong with the taste. But you’ve fallen to the dark side. Let’s see how you fare against the Phaalis.”

“A curry? Or was that a proposition?”

“I’ve never heard that term. It’s a proposition to embrace your doom.”

Another pot appeared, and something that looked like rice but was probably albino rat crap, based on how IMPS treated food, flowed into it, and the pot began to steam almost immediately. White meat was swiftly diced, along with what looked like a mix of tomato and cucumber. The spice pots floating above emptied themselves into the saucepot, all of them.

I swallowed nervously. All the spice? I was a dragon, and my armour stat was insane, but I suspected anything added to that pot would dissolve like it had offended Heisenberg.

“You can’t intimidate me, cook.”

“COOK!” More spice pots appeared from nowhere and emptied themselves into the pot. “This will show you why I’m the chef of death.”

“That isn’t as good marketing as you might think. When I eat, I don’t want to shit my intestines as liquid organs out the next morning.”

“Can’t take the heat, stay out of the kitchen. The wildlife—the ingredients—aren’t the real threat here. The cooks are the thing you need to worry about.”

“You aren’t even a cook. I bet this is just as mild as your banter.” Pot to kettle on that one, but he winced and inched closer to me, so I must have scored a point.

“Eat my mighty phaalis! It’s hotter than anything you can imagine! Death peppers, lizard peeper powder, if you survive, you’ll be shitting fire for a week!”

The pots flew and boiling rice, plus the water, and the hottest damn concoction I’d ever tasted poured down my throat. I coughed, and despite the removal of my pores, sweat began to pour out all over my body. A fire burned in my belly, which was weird. My flamebreath came from specialised organs housed along my back, not my stomach or lungs.

41.6kg of biomass added to storage.

I belched, the noise ringing out across the quiet headland, blending with the waves crashing against the bottom of the cliffs behind me. Control eluded me for a moment, my stomach rebelling against what had been inflicted on it, but I somehow retained control of the furnace in my guts.

There were a lot of whole animals in my storage space. I pulled up a list of contents and boggled at how much random crap I had stored away. I scanned down until I found something suitable. Varn had short fur, the same colour as Splitnerpick’s hair. Perfect.

I summoned a tiny part of a varn body into my mouth, praying that it wouldn’t betray my use of magic. It didn’t really count as magic, as far as I understood it. According to Tim, everything other than my actual spells counted as spell-like abilities, so hopefully it wouldn’t blow my cover.

My right arm was suddenly free, and I brought it up to pluck the scraps of hair from between my teeth. I spat to try to clear the rest away, although they didn’t really bother me. I’d had swords, daggers, and armour wedged between my fangs, so a scrap of fur was no big deal.

I threw the hair at Splinterpick with a sneer.

“At least clean your meat properly.”

“The Dreadchef has allowed hair into his broth! This is a tragic turn of fortune!” I was going to kill that announcer, despite this comment helping me out.

“What? I didn’t! That’s insane!”

My fingers closed around his throat as he involuntarily stepped forward.

“It was good stuff. I’m going to shit blood for a week, but it won’t kill me,” I whispered before my forehead smashed into his nose and he dropped unconscious at my feet.

“Bob the Bumpkin takes the win? What the hell is happening? Is it still on? Bob wins!” The announcer suddenly sounded a lot less confident than before. “Now for the kill. A great talent has been lost to the world this day. The culinary arts will mourn, a genius leaves the profession, killed by a dumbass with an iron stomach.”

“I’ll kill you slowly, announcer guy. But I’d sooner destroy a stained glass window than the Dreadchef. That shit was hot. His talent shouldn’t be allowed to pass from the world.” With no idea if my pandering to the audience would work, I left it at that and lowered Splinterpick gently to the ground. “If you want a job when you wake, look me up.”

I glanced around, but the plateau was still empty. If I had to do this eating competition bullshit with the other four, this was going to get old fast. Those assholes who shot me with arrows would just die, assuming I could dodge the wildlife.

The woods enveloped me, and I disappeared like a ghost, determined to kill or incapacitate the rest of them before any more force-feeding could be allowed to happen. Maybe I could skip most of the competition via mass murder. More than three at a time counts as mass murder, right?
