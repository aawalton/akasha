"What do you think, Wiggles? Will I be able to go?"  Callie asked her mireling friend as she searched the chicken coop for eggs.

At this point, Wiggles was a frequent visitor to the coop, and the chickens weren't bothered by him anymore. Mom likely knew about him, too, but had chosen not to comment on the matter. As long as Wiggles stayed out of sight, Callie could spend her morning peacefully playing with the mireling.

Go? Go where?

"To town! There's a library there with more books than one can imagine. I bet one of those will have something to do with magic," Callie said excitedly.

Lab-ry? Baaks?

Mireling seemed confused by Callie's enthusiasm.

Friend not return?

"I'll return. I'm not sure how long we'll be gone. Maybe three days. If I'm allowed to go. I really hope I am," Callie said. She just had to read those books about magic.

Miss friend…

Wiggles drooped low to the floor into a sad puddle.

"Don't worry, I'll be back before you know. Just please don't go around burning shoes…" Callie patted the mireling consolingly.

No burn! Wiggles good!

"Really? I'm proud of you, Wiggles!"

"Callie!" Callen called from outside the coop. Wiggles disappeared instantly, sliding in between the narrow hole in the corner. Seeing him squeeze through like that, Callie noted that the hole was smaller than Wiggles's core appeared to be. Was it squishy just like the rest of him?

"I'm here," Callie peeked her head out of the coop.

"Mom and Dad are back," Callen announced, a smile on his lips signifying good news.

Callie hurriedly retrieved the remaining eggs, placing them gingerly into the basket, and then ran inside.

Mom and Dad wore gentle smiles. They didn't even have to say anything for Callie to know the chief's answer. After placing down the basket with eggs, she jumped in the air and yelled. "Wohoo! I'm going to town for the first time!"

"Easy, there. There are some conditions," Dad said sternly.

"Conditions?" Callie asked.

"Yes. Mister Blackthorn was very much against your going. While you understand all the basics of survival, you are far too reckless when it comes to arcane beasts," Dad gave Callie a significant look.

"They aren't evil, you know!" Callie insisted.

"We all witnessed that they can be reasonable, but it doesn't mean you can throw all caution to the wind. The chief's condition is that this will be the only time you're allowed to go until you pass Blackthorn's test properly. If you wish to come along with us to town after that, then that will have to wait until spring," Mom said firmly.

Callie wanted to protest, but she held herself back. She would just make the most out of this one trip. It wasn't like there would be much travelling to do until spring anyway, with harvest season coming, which would be followed by intense preparations for winter and then the winter itself.

"Okay, when are we going?" Callie asked instead.

"Tomorrow. Callen will be heading to the town together with merchant Silas. I talked to him, and he was kind enough to let us ride on his wagon. In exchange, he'll get to use Gale free of charge," Mom said.

Callie nodded. During the summer, Callen and this merchant, Silas, had become fast friends. The merchant had taken Callen with him to the town twice now, and each time he returned with tales of all that he had seen—colorful market stalls, large houses, and more people than Callie could imagine.

She was still a bit suspicious of the merchant. He seemed… too friendly in her opinion, and he was most definitely an outsider. But Callie wouldn't dare to suggest this to Callen. His eyes sparkled with life and joy each time he spoke of his travels. Callie couldn't bring herself to say anything bad about the merchant.

Besides, it was nothing more than a gut feeling. Perhaps it was wrong. If somebody asked her to point at the egg thief, Callie was more likely to say that it was Mister Blackthorn. Especially since he seemed so keen not to let her go.

Thankfully, the chief understood the importance of this trip, so with a spring in her step, Callie hurried to pack her bag for tomorrow.

"Good morning," the merchant Silas greeted them enthusiastically.

"Morning, Silas," Callen shook his hand, an excited smile on his face.

"You're really saving my hide here. The horse I bought this summer got so excited that he sprained his leg. Nothing a bit of rest won't fix, but I can't exactly strain him now," Silas said, politely inclining his head towards Mom, who was leading Gale by the reins.

"It's the least we could do since you're allowing us to ride your wagon. It would be rather difficult for us to travel on a single horse since Callie doesn't yet know how to ride one," Mom said.

The only reason I don't know it is because you won't let me.

Callie would be more than eager to learn how to ride, but everyone in her family would always say that she was too small. They worried that Gale would throw her off, although she didn't believe that he would ever throw her off on purpose. After all, she would sneak him a carrot or an apple on occasion.

"So you must be Callie? Your brother has told me a lot about you, but we've never actually met before," Silas said with a friendly smile, extending his hand toward her.

Callie regarded his hand dubiously, then hid behind Mom. Why should she shake hands with him?

"My apologies. She's quite shy around strangers. I'm sure she'll warm up to you in time," Mom said.

"No worries. For a little girl, a stranger, a man no less, must be quite intimidating. But I'm sure that by the end of this journey we'll be fast friends," Silas said with a smile.

I rather doubt it.

Callie remained by Mom's side while Silas and Callen got everything necessary loaded into the wagon and Gale secured to its front. Once that was done, Silas and Callen sat at the bench in front while Callie and Mom sat in the wagon itself.

While she didn't like Silas, his wagon was quite amazing. It was nothing like the flimsy cart that they had at home. It had an actual roof, which would protect them from rain. On one side of it, there was a comfortable cushion where one could sit, while the other side was lined with shelves, drawers, and boxes. There were even curtains on the sides to prevent others from seeing inside.

After they did the last check to make sure that nothing was forgotten, they were off. Silas and Callen chatted amicably, like good friends would, speaking about the stuff Silas needed to buy and where he would need to go to buy them. Mom would occasionally chime in, but Callie remained silent.

She didn't understand what they were talking about. It felt like she was being left out. Callen hadn't even so much as glanced at her, although normally he would definitely try to involve Callie in conversation.

"Callie, are you alright?" Mom asked, likely noticing that Callie was pouting.

"I am fine," she muttered and decided not to speak at all during the whole journey.

"Oh, sorry. We were talking about boring adult things. Is there something you would like to talk about?" Silas asked.

"No," she replied stubbornly.

"Sorry, Callie. Silas, perhaps you could tell us about the library. It's where Callie and Mom are heading," Callen suggested.

"Oh yes! Drymon has a quaint little library. I was frankly surprised to find one in these parts. It has a nice collection of books," Silas said.

"You've been inside?" Callie, unable to withhold her curiosity, asked.

"Yes, of course. I'd be a fool not to visit it at least once. Let me tell you, you will not be disappointed."

"What about the library card? Is it not very expensive?" Mom asked.

"I have the card from the Central Library, so I didn't have to pay for a new one. How about I lend it to you?"

"Really?" Callie asked, thinking that perhaps Silas wasn't so bad after all.

"Oh, no. We couldn't possibly accept something so expensive. A card from the Central Library surely must have cost a lot…" Mom said, shaking her head.

"It did cost a pretty penny, but there's not much point to it if it's not being used. I won't have the time to visit the library this time, so I'd much rather see it used, rather than sitting in my pocket. Besides, you're helping me out quite a bit by lending me your horse for free. I insist," Silas said.

Mom looked a bit uncomfortable. "Very well, but then I insist that you take Gale for free next time as well."

"I won't say no to that kind offer. I'm sure my poor mare would appreciate extra rest as well," Silas laughed.

"What's the Central Library?" Callie asked. Callen looked just as curious about the topic.

"Oh, it's a grand place. Let me tell you all about it."

Silas told Callie and Callen many stories about his travels. The Central Library, a place so grand that one would barely see its roof, was only the first of the many stories. He told them of places Callie couldn't even imagine. Houses as tall as the top of trees and twice as wide, with walls made of white stone and adorned with gold.

"You're making it all up!" Callie accused, not believing something like that could exist.

"Oh, it's all real. I've seen it with my own two eyes!" Silas laughed at her little outburst.

"I wish I could see it for myself," Callen said, a spark of excitement dancing in his eyes.

"If those places really do exist, then I'm sure you could see them one day, big brother," Callie said.

The wagon seemed to be filled with merriment, except for Mom. She had gone oddly silent, gazing at Callen with an expression Callie didn't quite recognize. Was she sad?

"Is everything alright, Mom?" Callie tugged on her sleeve.

"It's alright, dear," Mom gently patted her head. "I was just wondering when my son grew up into a man."

Callie cocked her head. Callen was a man? He certainly was big, but whether he was a man or not, she couldn't tell. For her, Callen was and always would be her dependable big brother. Nothing could ever change that.

All of a sudden, the wagon rocked violently as Gale exclaimed a loud neigh.

"Wow, there! Calm down," Callen called.

"What's going on?" Mom asked, her hand reaching for the sword on her hip.

"Something seems to have spooked the horse, but I couldn't see what," Silas replied.

"Stay in the wagon, Callie. I'll go check," Mom said, withdrawing her sword.

At the same time, Callie felt a gentle tingle in her arm. Some weird noise came from the front of the wagon, once again spooking Gale. But alongside it, Callie heard a tiny child-like voice.

Where? Help… Help…

"Mom, I'll come with you!" Callie jumped to her feet, running after Mom. It was an arcane beast. A very scared one at that, Callie could feel it.

"Callie, it's too dangerous," Mom said fiercely.

"Please, Mom. I… " Callie didn't finish the sentence, instead touching her right vambrace.

Mom saw the gesture, then nodded, understanding her meaning. "You must stay behind me at all times. Is that clear? And if I tell you to run, you run!"

Callie nodded and hurried out of the wagon.

Callen and Silas had their hands busy trying to calm the horse, while Mom and Callie headed towards the source of the strange noise. It sounded… kind of like crackling, alongside the whispers, most of which Callie couldn't make out.

Scared… Where? Help

These three words seemed to repeat over and over. Callie followed these voices, leaning on the feeling her mark was exuding. It was no doubt glowing at this point.

Then she finally saw it. A little creature with grey fur, hiding among the foliage near the road. She couldn't make out much of it, but its face was distinctly fox-like and filled with fear.

"It's alright… I won't hurt you," Callie whispered, reaching out her hand to the creature, hoping that it would be able to sense her mark alongside her intention to help.

But the moment her hand moved forward, little sparks appeared on its fur. Mom grabbed and pulled Callie back, and then the bush exploded into pieces, torn apart by lightning.
