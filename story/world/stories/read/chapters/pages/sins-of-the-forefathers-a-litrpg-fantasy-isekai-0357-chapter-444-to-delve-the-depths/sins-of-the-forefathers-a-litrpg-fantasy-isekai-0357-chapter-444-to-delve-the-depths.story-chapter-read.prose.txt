I not only kept my eyes out, but my senses as well as Renauld, Sylvia, Fade and I descended the Long Road into the caldera of the Unchained. Fade’s words about the Death Aether that seemed to be present down here was enough to put me on edge. I trusted Bleddyn well enough not to lead our procession into a danger, but I was still wary.

I had been burned one too many times by trusting blindly and not investigating myself. I was long since past the point where I would prefer to trust, but verify.

Thus, I had a plan. Settling all of the refugees was going to take a while. There were just so many of them that the grim faced members of the Unchained down here in the apparently unnamed base had no time to meet. I had gotten word that Bleddyn had called for a period of settlement before we would all get together to formally decide on our next steps. I was gratified to learn that while we may have lost the Battle of Rhoscara, nobody was interested in outright giving up.

We were down, but not out.

This suited me just fine. I had things to do before I was willing to sit down and formally take command of the remnants of the Herztalian expedition. Not only did I need to seek out the Preceptor that apparently lived within the settlement and contract his services for my surgery, but…

I wanted to check this place out more. I was suspicious about this place.

I left my true body in the care of Renauld once we had come to a stop, within the designated area that the Unchained had directed us to. The Gnoll bid us farewell, and then Fade, Sylvia and I set out. Before I started my investigation, I wanted one more person with me. Someone who had chosen different travel arrangements, for a reason I understood well. I had left him with a lot to think about, after all.

Walter.

After our talk in the camp some days ago, my Squire had, oddly enough, chosen to make the journey here with the Elves of all people. That had…surprised me. I hadn’t known the young man was particularly close with any of them. Most Elves didn’t even speak the common Herztalian tongue.

I should have known better. There was at least one Elf that did speak the language, if not haltingly. Someone who he had grown somewhat well acquainted with, considering he had been their guard for a time.

I stopped some distance away from a particular Elven mobile home, lingering in the shadows of another. All around us, Elves were disembarking from their large, spherical wagons, stretching and tending to their exhausted Chamels. I was well known to these people, even with Alverons refusal to see me, so we didn’t get much more than a few side glances before they returned to their business.

Which was fine with me. I was paying more attention to the scene playing out a few dozen feet away from me

Walter was assisting with the Chamels that, presumably, belonged to the mobile home he had traveled on. He wasn’t alone in doing so, of course.

Kierla was with him.

I’m…not sure I had ever seen Walter like this. The young man was startlingly relaxed as he attached a leather feed back to the nuzzle of one particular Chamel. The smile on his lips didn’t even falter when the veritable dinosaur lunged forward to munch and crunch it’s way through it’s giblets and bones. He just kept a steady hand brushing through the frills upon it’s scaled head. Similarly, the normally excitable Kierla was far calmer than I had ever seen her. Most of the time, it was a struggle to keep her focused, whenever I had worked with her in the past.

Not so now.

If I didn’t know any better…

I exchanged a glance with Sylvia, standing close enough to me in the shadows we were nearly embracing. At my raised eyebrow, the Sculpted woman hummed a single clear note of amusement, and then turned back to study the unlikely pair in the distance. “We should leave them be,” She finally said in a whisper.

I nodded slowly, and shifted my eyes back to Walter, just in time to watch as he laughed out loud at something Kierla had said to him.

I…wasn’t used to seeing Walter like this. Normally, the young man alternated between a few different states. He was usually either anxious about something, or his self imposed duties hung so heavy on his shoulders that he existed in a state of grim determination. Most of the time, he existed in a state somewhere between the tow. It was rare to see him so…

“Happy…” I murmured. “He’s happy with her.”

I heard a lupine snort from below me, before a voice echoed through my mind. “I guess that’s one way to put it. Another would be he’s nearly ready to ru-”

Without even looking, I reached down and gently tapped Fade on his head, cutting off his mental words. “Don’t be rude,” I scolded with a small smile on my own lips. “But Sylvia is right. Walter…he deserves a bit of happiness in his life. I can talk to him later. For now, we can do this ourselves. C’mon, let’s go.”

I felt Fade nod under my hand, and Sylvia take my remaining one in hers…

And then we turned, and left.

………………………………..

It wasn’t long before our trio had exited the bounds of the Unchained’s settlement. The light from their torches only reached so far, and it wasn’t long before both Sylvia and I felt the need to activate out respective light abilities. Normally, Fade would have done the same, lighting up his antlers to glow a soft green.

I could tell he was a bit grumpy that he couldn’t.

Still, there was a reason I had wanted him to come along, and it wasn’t so he could be a flashlight. Once we had gotten a fair distance away from the settlement, we stopped in the middle of the unnervingly flat caldera. We had set such a fast pace sprinting away from the encampment that by now, the torches of the Unchained were only a slight flicker in the distance.

That seemed to be what Sylvia was looking for.

The Sculpted woman turned to look at me, the dim light of our Skills catching oddly off of her Mithril skin, and spoke bluntly. “This is about the Netherim, isn’t it?”

I couldn’t help but notice that Fade perked up at her words, suddenly paying rapt attention to me.

I hid a smile. Neither Sylvia or Fade had been with me, on any of my adventures into Netherim ruins. The two of them hadn’t been present for the events that had taken place on the island of Gorenzan. They hadn’t seen the colossal, haunted edifice within the heart of Mt. Goryuen where I had discovered Aveline. And as for the one that had been under Hollow Hill, Fade had been a puppy at the time, sleeping in my room in Grey’s tower, while Sylvia…had been inside it for all of half an hour.

At the time of my adventure that resulted in the death of Tatsugan, Sylvia had been recovering from her brush with death at Elderwyck. She had been half-amnesiac, and frankly scared of me from the weight of expectations. Fade, meanwhile, had been training with Taran within his hidden glade home.

Neither of the people who were arguably closest to me had first hand experience with the Netherim…but I had told them about the long dead people. Why wouldn’t I? I made no secret of Aveline’s people to those in my inner circle.

Fade had long been jealous of missing out on the experience at Gorenzan, and was determined not to miss out on the opportunity to delve haunted depths. He had never been daunted by me saying it was vanishingly rare that we would ever get the chance to do so again.

All he had to say on the subject was that with my luck? Somehow I would find a way.

As for Sylvia…my lover wasn’t the most adventurous woman at the end of the day. She was too pragmatic for that, for all of her playful side. A very goal oriented woman, my Sylvia was, not keen to wandering. She had never been all that bothered by missing out on the adventure which had taken place while she was an amnesiac.

It was just like her to ask so bluntly about this.

And…she was right. If my instincts were correct…

Fade might just be getting his wish.

“Yes,” I said, nodding patiently, setting Fade’s tail to wagging. “I suspect so. This, all of this?” I swept out an arm at the enormous cavern all around us, my voice echoing out from our position eerily. “This isn’t natural. It’s not really possible for caves like this form by themselves. The smoothness of the floor, the perfect dome overhead, the lack of things like stalactites and stalagmites…it has to have been done by the Netherim. It seems pretty obvious to me.”

“Yes…obvious,” Sylvia said dryly, eyes cutting away to the distant roof. We could just barely make it out near the settlement, from all the light they were sending up. “But what does it matter? Not only are they clearly gone, but any trace of them is as well. It’s just a cave, in the end.”

“Ah, but that’s where you’re wrong!” I held up a helpful finger, only to lower it at the unimpressed look Sylvia shot me. I instead coughed in my gloved, Primordium fist. “So to speak. Earlier, Fade was telling me something interesting. Very faintly, he can sense Death Aether somewhere within this cavern.”

Sylvia briefly turned an inquisitive eyebrow down towards said Spirit Wolf. In response, she received a lupine nod of agreement. “Hmm. Strange. I don’t feel anything, and it’s not as if I’m unfamiliar with Death Aether. We encountered plenty of it in the catacombs outside of Elderwyck.”

“Exactly,” I smiled broadly, to her surprise. “I don’t sense it as well. But if Fade does, I have no doubt it exists. His senses are far more adept than either of ours. And where does that leave us, hmm? If we can’t detect the Death Aether, and yet Fade can…” I prompted…

Only to receive two blank stares from my companions. Silence fell between the three of us for a moment, before Sylvia sighed loudly. “You’ve spent entirely too much time around my Father, Nathan,” She said in a tired, amused tone. “Neither Fade nor I have a deep background in Aetherology like you two do. Please, illuminate us. What does that mean?”

I laughed a little sheepishly. “Ah, well…it means that there’s a source to the Death Aether. Something that must be hidden, if neither one of us can detect it. Death Aether doesn’t just occur naturally. Environmental Aether has to be acted upon for it’s alignment to change like that. Normally, either from being within the presence of a great many dead bodies…”

“…or from a single, massive loss of life,” Sylvia finished quietly. At my suddenly somber nod, she continued. “I’m following your reasoning now. If such an event had occurred, it would have permeated the entire caldera. The Death Aether wouldn’t have a hidden source like you hypothesize.”

“Look at you, using big words like ‘hypothesize’,” I teased gently, causing Sylvia to roll her eyes at me. “Now who’s spent too much time with Grey. But yes, that’s my theory. I think that there’s a hidden source of the Death Aether somewhere in here. Likely something left behind by the Netherim…which means there might just be a Netherim cache of some kind down here.” I took a deep breath…and then turned to face Fade. “Do you think you can track it? The ‘scent’ of the Death Aether?”

Fade cocked his head at me, before closing his eyes and furrowing his lupine brow. I think, if he still had his antlers, they likely would have been glowing from the visible effort he was putting forth. Sylvia and I waited in silence as the Spirit Wolf visibly cast out with his senses, trying to catch the end of an ancient thread. Eventually….

He nodded, opening his eyes. “Yes, I think so. The feeling of it is faint and old, but I think I can feel where it’s coming from,” Fade hesitated for a moment. “But, uh…I think it’s pretty far away. I’m not sure how long it’ll take for us to reach it. Should we…go back and tell the others?”

Sylvia turned to me with a raised eyebrow, clearly willing to let me make the call on this. Which, fair.

I was the one with the most experience with the Netherim here, after all. And I hadn’t exactly painted the safest picture of the last Netherim structure I had encountered.

Still…

I shook my head. “It’ll be fine. I don’t think that, whatever this is, it’s one of the main bunkers the Netherim used. Sylvia, do you remember the map? The one down beneath Hollow Hill?”

Sylvia blinked. “Ah…vaguely. Why?”

“Well, I remember it much clearer. That map was for the location of all the main bunkers that the Netherim lived within, back when they still ruled Vereden,” I said, making a wild leap of logic based on nothing more than experience. Still, it felt right to me. “Back when, I specifically remember looking to see if there was a dot around Addersfield, because, well…things were much more raw back then. There was nothing there, though. So, I’m thinking that whatever’s down here? It must have been something secret.”

“But still a secret that’s generating a good amount of Death Aether.”

Quietly, I inclined my head, acknowledging the point. That was true…

But I still wanted to go find it.

This…this was low stakes. The weight of everything that was happening, from the loss at Rhoscara, to the looming crisis of the worsening Skyfall, the coming journey we had to make for the Dwarven Holds…those were all big things.

I just wanted something small. Something I could definitively solve. I know for most people that an ancient bunker from a race of long dead pseudo-ancestors would be a pretty damn big deal.

But honestly? It wasn’t for me.

I was jolted out of my thoughts when I felt Fade give me a gentle mental prod. I looked around, only to find the Spirit Wolf had already left Sylvia and I behind while we debated. He was looking over his shoulder a raised eyebrow, clearly ready for us to get a move on. “Well? Are you coming?”

I exchanged a glance with Sylvia, and then the two of us set off after him.

I sure as Hell was.
