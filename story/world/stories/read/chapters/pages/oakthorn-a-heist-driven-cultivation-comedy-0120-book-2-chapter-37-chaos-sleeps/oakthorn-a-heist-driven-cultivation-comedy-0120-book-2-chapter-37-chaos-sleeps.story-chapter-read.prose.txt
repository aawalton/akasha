Blood and brimstone clung to Cade Stormhollow like a burial shroud as he slumped against the curved wall of their temporary sanctuary. Every inch of his body sang with the exquisite pain of survival. Ten floors of the crypt’s twisted labyrinth had exacted their toll, each level more nightmarish than the last.

“Anyone else feeling like this place harbors a personal vendetta?” he muttered, examining a fresh gash on his forearm where a skeletal harpy had nearly claimed his hand as a trophy.

The small alcove they’d discovered was barely large enough for five people, but it was the first spot in hours that didn’t actively try to murder them. Ancient symbols etched into the stone glowed with faint amber light, creating a bubble of relative safety amid the crypt’s malevolence.

Nora sat cross-legged near the entrance, her massive blade across her knees, methodically honing an edge that never seemed to dull. “A ruthless crime syndicate leader got possessed by a cursed crown hidden within the deepest bowels of this world and now controls this very abyss,” she reminded him without looking up. “Of course it feels personal.”

Elena paced the limited space like a caged wolf, movements sharp and erratic. The rage pouring off her was almost tangible, a scorching heat that burned anyone who ventured too close. Since losing Jer to the flooding chamber, she’d transformed into something feral and vengeful with each floor they’d ascended. Her eyes constantly darted to the passages beyond their sanctuary, as if hoping Bernard would materialize so she could carve his heart out with her favorite knife.

“We should rest while we can,” Cade told the group, but kept his eyes on Elena.

Her red hair was a mess, and her hands seemed to tap incessantly against her various weapons to a beat only she could hear.

“Bernard isn’t resting. He’s probably torturing Chubbs right now,” Elena sneered.

“Seriously?” Rayka whispered.

“We don’t know that. Let’s focus on what we can do. We’ll get to the top. We’ll save her,” Cade said with a touch of steel to his tone. Elena sniffed but looked away. Her fingers didn’t stop twitching.

“And what about Orro?” Rayka asked quietly from her position beside Evie. Her usual warmth had dimmed to embers. “We don’t even know what happened to him in the confusion.”

Cade’s heart twisted. He’d replayed those chaotic moments a thousand times—the flood waters receding, the frantic headcount, the sickening realization that Orro was nowhere to be found. Had he been swept away to some other part of the labyrinth? Or had he met a fate even worse than Jer’s?

“We’ll find him,” Cade promised, knowing how hollow it sounded. “After we get Chubbs back.”

Gav shifted uncomfortably, amber eyes reflecting the glowing symbols. His gaze flicked toward Elena, then away, clearly measuring the wisdom of speaking. “The, uh, trail we’re following is still fresh. Bernard couldn’t have gotten far with the kid.”

Elena’s laugh was a shattered thing. “And when we find him, I’m going to make him wish for the death my brother received.” Her fingers traced the edge of her blade with disturbing tenderness. “Starting with whatever’s left of his eyes.”

The amber light pulsed softly, syncing with the crypt’s heartbeat. The living structure continued its rhythmic contractions. It was a constant reminder that they were effectively insects navigating the bowels of something vast and hungry.

“We move in four hours,” Nora said, finally looking up from her blade. Her dark eyes held the weight of everything they’d endured, but also the steel that had kept them alive. “Everyone should try to rest.”

Elena made a dismissive sound but stopped her relentless pacing, settling against the far wall with her back rigid and knees drawn up. She stared at the far wall, flipping a knife with mechanical regularity.

Exhaustion tugged at Cade’s eyelids, but sleep remained elusive. Every time he closed his eyes, he saw Chubbs’ terrified face as Bernard dragged her away. The creature inside their rival burned with such ancient hatred as it recognized Creation in the little girl.

He also saw Jer’s final moments—that sad smile as the manacles closed around his wrists, his quiet smile before the water swept them away. And Orro, who had simply vanished in the chaos, leaving a void in their ranks that seemed to grow with every hour.

“You should rest,” Nora said, moving closer to where Cade sat. “You haven’t slept since we entered the crypt.”

Cade nodded but made no effort to lie down. “You first.”

She gave him a look that plainly said she wasn’t fooled, but didn’t press the issue. Instead, she lowered her voice so only he could hear. “Your magic. It’s growing wild again.”

It wasn’t a question.

Since his momentary mastery against the fear creature, his power had regressed, becoming more volatile with each passing hour. During their last confrontation—a chamber filled with animated statues that bled mercury when shattered—Cade’s magic had nearly immolated both friend and foe. Only Nora’s quick shield of shadowy energy had contained the worst of the blast.

“I know,” he admitted, examining his palms where silver-gold energy occasionally sparked beneath the skin. “It’s like trying to harness a hurricane with cobwebs.”

“And the red threads?”

Cade flexed his fingers, watching as crimson light briefly wove through the silver-gold before disappearing. “Growing stronger. More... assertive.”

Nora considered this, her expression thoughtful rather than accusatory. “That moment when you gained control against the fear creature—I’m assuming you’ve already tried to recreate it?”

“Every waking second,” Cade sighed, frustration evident. “That’s the damnable irony, isn’t it? Perfect clarity in the heart of battle, then it slips through my fingers like sand.”

“The mind works strangely under duress,” Nora observed as she met his eyes with a steady gaze. “Sometimes we see most clearly when everything is falling apart.”

“Poetic, but not especially helpful for practicing.”

Nora’s lips twitched in what might have been a smile in another life. “Try again. I’ll keep watch.”

“It’s my shift,” Cade protested.

“I’ll keep watch while you practice, then you can trade me,” she countered, her tone brooking no argument. “We don’t need any more heroic sleepless vigils. We need you functional, not a martyr.”

Cade hesitated, then yielded. “Two hours. Not a minute more.”

“Good.” She adjusted her position so she could watch the corridor and him at the same time. “Now center yourself. Find your core. Map its landscape.”

Cade settled into a more comfortable position, back against the wall, legs crossed awkwardly. He closed his eyes, immediately regretting it as images from their journey flashed behind his eyelids: the chamber of singing crystals that shattered eardrums when touched; the corridor where gravity reversed every seventeen steps; the room filled with liquid shadow that solidified when exposed to light.

Focus.

He attempted to slow his breathing as Nora had taught him, counting inhalations and exhalations. One in, one out. Two in, two out.

By four, his mind had already cataloged seventeen potential escape routes from their alcove, calculated the probable number of hours until Bernard completed whatever ritual he was planning, and composed the beginning of a limerick about crypts that he’d never finish.

“Your mind is racing again,” Nora observed quietly.

“A thoroughbred that refuses the bridle,” Cade muttered without opening his eyes.

“Channel it,” she suggested. “Don’t fight against your nature, Cade. Work with it.”

The young thief tried again, this time picturing his thoughts as a rushing river. Instead of fighting the current, he visualized himself standing on the bank, observing its flow without being swept away.

The image helped. Gradually, the chaotic whirl of his consciousness settled into something more manageable. Not quiet—never quiet—but focused, directed.

“Better,” he murmured, feeling the first tendrils of calm.

“Now find your core,” Nora instructed, her voice becoming distant as he sank deeper into the meditative state. “Picture it as a physical space you can explore. You can do this.”

Cade reached inward, seeking the pulsing heart of his magic. For a moment, there was only darkness—then, like before, a sphere of silver-gold energy materialized in his mind’s eye. It wasn’t stable; the surface rippled and surged, occasionally throwing off sparks that dissipated into the void around it.

This time, however, he could see the red threads more clearly. They weren’t merely woven through the destruction magic as he’d first thought—they formed a complete secondary matrix, a delicate lacework of crimson energy that both contained and penetrated the silver-gold power.

Two distinct magics, occupying the same space.

“I see it,” he whispered, watching as the crimson threads pulsed in counterpoint to the silver-gold energy. “The red magic... it’s not part of my destruction core. It’s separate, but connected.”

Nora’s voice seemed to come from very far away. “Can you interact with it?”

Cade mentally reached toward the crimson threads as he had before. The moment his imagined fingers brushed against them, that same ancient awareness jolted through him—primordial, vast, and eerily familiar.

This time, he didn’t pull back.

Instead, he traced the path of a single red thread as it wound through the chaotic silver-gold energy of his destruction magic. Where the thread passed, the energy calmed, becoming more cohesive, more directed.

Order from chaos, he thought, his fascination overriding caution.

The Son of Ruin followed another thread, and another, mapping the intricate patterns they formed. They didn’t confine the destruction magic—rather, they shaped it, guided it, like channels directing the flow of a river.

“By the four hells,” he whispered into darkness.

The realization struck him with physical force. He’d been wrong. This whole time, he’d been wrong. How did he miss this? Cade has approached his magic like it was a wild beast to be tamed. To be controlled. He’d been trying to dominate it, to force the wild energy to obey through sheer strength of will.

But these red threads... they worked with the destruction magic, not against it. They didn’t conquer. They collaborated.

When chaos sleeps within the flame, And order breaks its chains...

Evie’s prophecy echoed in his mind with new significance. The chaos wasn’t just the Pandemonium Box as he’d assumed—maybe it could also be this foreign crimson magic, somehow integrated with his destruction core.

Chaos magic? No, that wasn’t quite right, Cade though. The crimson threads didn’t feel chaotic—quite the opposite. They brought order to the inherently chaotic nature of destruction.

Were chaos and order somehow... the same? Two sides of the same coin?

Here in the darkness of his mind, a jagged memory sliced through his concentration—Elena’s agonized scream as the water had swept them away from Jer. The image of his friend’s resigned smile as the manacles closed. The sound of rushing water that had haunted every moment since.

His concentration faltered, the mental landscape wavering like a reflection in troubled water.

Focus, damn it.

Tentatively, Cade tried a new approach. Instead of pushing against the wild fluctuations of his destruction magic, he imagined himself as one of the red threads—moving with the energy, guiding rather than forcing.

The effect was immediate and startling. His silver-gold core stabilized, the violent surges smoothing into controlled pulses. The crimson threads brightened in response, weaving themselves more intricately through the destruction magic until the two energies began to move in harmony.

“Holy mother of gods,” he breathed, his physical body suddenly aware of a warmth spreading outward from his chest. “I think I’m getting it.”

His concentration shattered as something collided with his shoulder.

“Cade,” Rayka was saying, shaking him gently. “Cade, wake up.”

He blinked, disoriented by the abrupt transition. “What? I wasn’t sleeping, I was—”

“My friend, you were glowing,” Gav supplied, his amber eyes wide. “You were like a beacon in a storm. I can sleep through most anything, but a miniature star ten feet away is difficult even for me.”

“I like stars,” Evie whispered from where she was curled up around her staff.

Cade looked down at his hands. Sure enough, silver-gold energy danced across his skin, but now distinct threads of crimson wove through the familiar destruction magic, creating complex patterns that reminded him of constellations.

“Is that... normal?” Rayka asked cautiously, concern etched in the fine lines around her eyes.

“Define normal,” Cade replied automatically, still transfixed by the display. “For me, I guess so?”

“What does that even mean?” Elena demanded from across the alcove, her voice sharp as the blade she still flipped between her fingers. “Another thing you’ve been keeping from us?”

The accusation stung, especially coming from Elena in her current state.

“No, it’s not—this is new. Or old. I’m still figuring it out,” he replied quickly.

“Figuring what out exactly?” she pressed, her grief making her merciless.

Nora intervened before Cade could respond. “He’s working on stabilizing his magic,” she said, her tone leaving no room for argument. “Which might help us save Chubbs and avenge Jer.”

The mention of her brother’s name hit Elena like a physical blow. She flinched, then nodded sharply, retreating back into her smoldering rage.

“The dual nature of his magic,” Nora continued, her dark eyes brimmed with a mixture of fascination and concern, “is more complex than we realized.”

“Wait. Hold on. A dual nature?” Rayka repeated, leaning closer to examine the fading patterns on Cade’s skin. “Since when does Cade have two kinds of magic?”

“Since always, apparently,” Cade murmured, watching as the crimson threads gradually faded back beneath his skin, leaving only the familiar silver-gold energy. “I just didn’t know it.”

He described what he’d seen in his meditative state—the crimson threads, the way they worked with, rather than against, his destruction magic, their ancient, primordial quality.

It felt good to finally tell them, even if this wasn’t the way he’d imagined it happening, especially since Orro wasn’t there to berate him for hosting so many secrets.

“So you’ve got some strange second magic,” Gav summarized when he finished. “That’s... actually, that makes sense in a twisted way. Your powers have never quite fit the destruction mold.”

“Could it be connected to dad?” Rayka asked, her brow furrowed in thought.

“Maybe,” Cade admitted, though something told him the truth went even deeper. “But it doesn’t feel... new. More like it’s always been there, and I’m just now seeing it.”

Evie, who had been nearly catatonic since Bernard had taken Chubbs, suddenly turned toward him. Her eyes held that distant, starry quality they took on before a prophecy.

“When chaos sleeps within the flame, and order breaks its chains,” she recited, her voice vacant. “The child of ruin speaks the name, that only one remains.”

Gav frowned. “She already sang that one, right?”

“Maybe we missed something,” Nora suggested, studying Cade intently. “Could the chaos be referring to your second magic?”

“That’s what I was thinking,” Cade confirmed. “But it doesn’t feel chaotic. If anything, it brings order to my destruction magic.”

“Opposites can be the same,” Evie said dreamily, before her eyes cleared and she blinked in confusion. “Why are you all staring at me? Is Guildford braiding my hair again?”

They stared at her.

“Opposites can be the same?” Rayka repeated, a small flicker of hope lighting her eyes for the first time since Orro had disappeared. “Like... life and death being part of the same cycle?”

“Or chaos and order being different expressions of the same force,” Cade suggested, following the intuition. “Maybe they’re not opposites at all.”

The theory felt right. The crimson threads brought coherence to his wild destruction magic not by opposing it but by channeling it, directing its natural flow rather than trying to contain it.

“I need to try again,” he decided. “I need to understand how these two energies work together.”

“Agreed, but the rest of you should still try to rest,” Nora told the others. “Cade and I will keep watch.”

There were murmurs of acknowledgement. Despite her obvious distrust, even Elena eventually closed her eyes, though her fingers remained wrapped around the hilt of her knife. Despite their reassurance, Gav settled near the entrance, his lycanthrope senses attuned to any approaching threat, while Rayka stayed close to the now-chatty Evie, a protective arm around the siren’s shoulders.

Cade closed his eyes again, finding it easier this time to slip into the meditative state. His rapid-fire thoughts still raced, but now he observed them with detachment rather than being swept along.

His core materialized more quickly in his mind’s eye—the silver-gold sphere now seething with activity, red threads weaving through it in hypnotic patterns. But instead of merely observing this time, he immersed himself in it, imagining his consciousness sinking into the very center of his magic.

The sensation was overwhelming—like diving into a maelstrom of raw energy. Destruction magic surged around him in violent eddies, power in its purest form. Not evil, not cruel, simply elemental—the fundamental forces of nature breaking all things down. It was inexorable.

And there, amidst the chaos, the crimson threads glowed like veins of fire through stone. They didn’t resist the destruction magic’s wild nature—they embraced it, channeled it, gave it purpose and direction.

Chaos and order. Destruction and... creation?

No, that wasn’t right. The crimson magic wasn’t creative in nature. It didn’t build or generate—it organized. It took the raw energy of destruction and made it coherent, coordinated, purposeful.

Unbidden, Jer’s face flashed through his mind—not the drowned horror from the Fearwalker’s vision, but Jer’s real face, alight with mischief and loyalty. “You’ve got this, Cade,” the memory seemed to say. “You always do.”

The grief threatened to pull him from his meditative state, but Cade used it instead—transforming the pain into determination, letting it fuel his focus rather than shatter it.

Following an intuition he couldn’t explain, Cade mentally grasped one of the crimson threads. Instead of merely touching it this time, he aligned himself with it, allowing his consciousness to flow along its path.

With growing confidence, Cade began to direct the flow of his magic. Not forcing, not controlling, but guiding—like a river finding its natural course. The crimson threads responded eagerly, brightening and multiplying, weaving themselves more intricately through the destruction magic until the two energies began to move as one.

His physical body felt the change—a deep and encouraging wave of flame expanding omnidirectionally from his core. The power flowed easily through his magical pathways. There, in the deep recesses of his sense, the energy felt like liquid fire.

It didn’t hurt. In fact, for the first time since his magic had awakened, it felt right.

The silver-gold energy of destruction condensed. It was like watching a titan take a mountain and ball it up into the size of a bead. None of the mass was lost, but the space it once occupied brimmed with potential. Meanwhile, the crimson threads formed an intricate lattice around the dense bead of power, containing and directing its ferocious energies rather than letting it dissipate chaotically.

Two cores, working as one.

Cade pushed deeper, seeking the source of the crimson magic. Where his destruction core had a clear origin—his encounter with Destruction’s Remnant—this other power seemed to have always been with him, dormant until recently.

He went deeper, zooming past his destruction core. Darkness swept over him. He could feel his breaths come slower and slower. The moonless midnight of his mind enveloped him. He followed the red cords farther down. He passed flashes of memory as he did. Cade saw the first time Hugh had taught him to manipulate someone’s emotions through subtle nonverbal cues and charisma. He witnessed him and Rayka outside of the Skyreach Academy’s walls, begging for coins from the passing scholars that used to teach him about the wonders of the world.

He didn’t linger, choosing instead to go even deeper.

Then he found something—a cluster of images that the red threads were woven through.

Images flashed through his mind: his father’s hands glowing with the same crimson energy as he traced symbols on infant Cade’s chest; a cave filled with ancient markings that pulsed with red light; a tree whose roots spread impossibly wide, branches extending into dimensions beyond perception.

The visions came too quickly to process, overwhelming his concentration. He pulled back, his consciousness returning to the surface level of his meditation.

Even so, something had fundamentally shifted. The two magics that had fought for dominance within him now coexisted in cautious harmony—not fully integrated, but no longer actively opposing each other.

His core had stabilized.

Cade opened his eyes, half-expecting to find himself transformed. But physically, nothing had changed—except that the erratic surges of magic beneath his skin had calmed to a steady pulse.

“Did it work?” Nora asked, studying him intently.

“I think so,” he replied, flexing his fingers experimentally. No wild bursts of energy, no uncontrolled flames. Just a contained, steady power that responded to his will rather than fighting it. “It’s like... like they’ve reached a truce.”

“Can you control it better now?”

Cade extended his hand, palm up, and summoned a small flame. Instead of the usual chaotic burst, a perfectly formed sphere of silver-gold fire materialized above his palm, threaded through with crimson veins that pulsed in rhythm with his heartbeat.

“By all that’s holy,” he whispered, watching as the fire moved exactly as he willed it, expanding and contracting with a fluidity he’d never before achieved. “I think I might actually be onto something.”

The sphere of flame dissipated as Cade closed his fist, fatigue suddenly washing over him in a wave. The meditative state had taken more out of him than he’d realized.

“Okay, your turn to keep us alive,” he told Nora, recognizing the wisdom in her earlier insistence. Sleep was no longer a luxury but a necessity if he wanted to maintain this newfound balance in his magic. “Wake me if—”

The entire alcove suddenly blazed with blinding light. The amber symbols etched into the walls flared brighter than the sun, pulsing in rapid, alarming sequence.

In the distance, a sound like thunder rolled through the crypt, followed by a high-pitched keening that set their teeth on edge.

The others jerked awake, weapons drawn before they were fully conscious.

“What in the four hells was that?” Gav demanded, his eyes already shifting to their lupine silver.

Rayka helped Evie up, the siren’s eyes wide with terror. “Cade, what’s happening?”

Before he could answer, the wall at the far end of the alcove shimmered like heat rising from stone. Runes in an ancient language appeared, glowing with the same sickly green light they’d seen when Bernard had first claimed the crown.

The crypt’s heartbeat accelerated, the rhythm irregular and frantic. The very stone beneath their feet vibrated with each pulse.

“Bernard’s started the ritual,” Cade realized, cold dread washing over him.

Elena’s expression hardened to stone. “Then we make him dead.”

“Let’s move,” Nora agreed, already heading toward the exit. “Now.”

Sleep forgotten, Cade gathered his power—now flowing in harmony rather than fighting itself—and followed. Whatever Bernard had unleashed, whatever ancient horror sought to bring into their world, they would stop it. They had to.

For Chubbs. For Jer. For Orro.

It was time to reach the top.
