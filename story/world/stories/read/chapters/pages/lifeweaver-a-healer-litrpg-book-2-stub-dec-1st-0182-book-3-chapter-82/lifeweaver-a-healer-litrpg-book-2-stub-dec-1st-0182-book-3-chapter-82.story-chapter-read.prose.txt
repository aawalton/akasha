Sending Splinter back home was the easy part, as it turned out. Not a single one of the other Lifeweavers had seen a system weave, not this dungeon's and not even their own. That posed a problem. While those gathered in the room all had echoes that gave them a vague general direction of the way home, their lack of understanding meant they couldn't reach out and find that echo's thread.

First, Luke attempted to teach them, but that idea was a bust. Perhaps his obvious irritation and the overwhelming need to get back and help his friends shone through during his teaching, but no one is perfect. Teaching wasn't his calling, though. That much was obvious.

Despite their lack of lifeweaverness, Luke didn't just want to leave them behind. Perhaps they'd get there, in time, but Milla would kill him if he turned the first meeting of what appeared to be at least ten different systems' worth of Lifeweaver into some sort of intergalactic incident where all of them came away with a burning hatred for Earth. Also, they had all pitched in to open the portal, so it would be kind of a dick move to leave them to fend for themselves, even if he hated giving The Fallen Shepherd even a second more than necessary to traipse around Earth. That bastard still being alive was still something Luke hadn't come to terms with, not really. It was like a bad dream come true.

Having to help the Lifeweavers left one solution, and by the time most of those sent back out into the corridors had returned, Luke was well underway with his first test subj... er, willing participant. The idea was that, like with Splinter, Luke would be able to reach in through their Meta-Heart and the system weave while they focused on their echo. Even the ratkin hadn't been able to touch the thread, or even see it on his own, so this would be more of the same, just a bit of a challenge, right? Wrong.

Okay, so after about an hour of intense concentration and searching the weave for the merest glint of an echo thread, Luke was about to give up the whole endeavour. That's when he got a bright idea. If the problem was the other Lifeweaver's connection, or lack thereof, to the system weave, why not do something to help build that connection?

Said and done, Luke grabbed hold of a system thread and shoved it into his own Meta-Heart. No, he wasn't about to test something like that on someone else before knowing it was at least somewhat safe. He, at least, could come back from almost any damage. As it turned out, lashing someone to the system weave didn't kill them, which was a positive, but Luke couldn't see how it made any difference at all, at least not with himself. The thread just hovered there.

"I'm going to try something. Hold still," he told Dot, whom he'd recruited as a willing participant. She glared at him but didn't protest, which he took as resounding acceptance.

Ignoring the sour look, he repeated the procedure. From what he could tell, Dot didn't notice anything at all, but from Luke's perspective, things changed quite a bit. Most of the changes, he couldn't even begin to describe because he just didn't understand them. Perhaps when he grew stronger as a Lifeweaver, he would, but for now, not so much. The one thing he was after, though, was very much present. Luke sensed Dot's echoes. He could do this.

"Do you want to go now?" he asked her.

She gave him a worried look and shook her head. "I don't know the ones I healed."

"Fair enough," he said with a shrug, and sliced through the system weave connected to her Meta-Heart. It wasn't until he'd done so that he realized it might have been a mistake, but from the look of things, that didn't hurt Dot either.

One by one, Luke brought the many different Lifeweavers to the portal. Identifying their respective echoes wasn't much of a challenge after the first one, and soon the room had a lot more free space to move around in.

"That's it," he said, extending Weaver's Perception throughout the dungeon to make sure no Lifeweavers remained, or at least no Lifeweavers he'd sent out. There were more out there, he was sure of that, but he couldn't take responsibility for all of them.

Luke looked over at Dot. "Your turn."

"I'm ready," she said, nodding.

Considering where to send her, Luke figured the best would be to Elijah. Curtis would be out and about, fighting Singers, and Ray, now resurrected, not far behind the Reaver. Elijah, at least, would follow Milla's orders, which, he guessed, meant staying near guild headquarters. The decision made, he went through the process of connecting the echo thread to the portal, giving it a destination.

"There," he said. "That should lead you to The Band headquarters.

"Thank you." She stood there for a moment, then looked as if she was about to say something more.

Luke pointed to the portal. "Just go."

Dot's face fell, but she didn't argue, and a moment later, Luke was alone. His first instinct had been to go in after her, but the thought of having to be in the same room as her made him wince. No, that wasn't what he needed in that moment. Curtis or Ray, then. They'd need his help, Luke was sure.

As it turned out, Luke was right. Sort of. Appearing in a mass of Singers, Luke saw Curtis, or a whirling mass of darkness that he thought was Curtis, fighting against a group of four or five mages who had erected barriers to keep him away from them. Something was different about the Reaver, but there wasn't enough time to give it a closer look, because there were others around, and they weren't faring as well. About fifteen human Integrated fought against what had to be at least thirty Singers, and that was not a recipe for success. Ray was around as well, moving about in stealth.

Several of the humans were injured, so Luke rushed over, killing a Singer as he went by, slamming his new staff into the side of the Singer's face, followed up by a thread of mana extending from the weapon and into the opponent's brain, scrambling it.

"Phoenix Phalanx," Luke said, coming over to Nick, who stood in front of the downed George and the new girl. Vicky, was it?

"Luke," Nick grunted, fending off a pair of Singers who moved with the grace of snakes. They darted in again and again, working in tandem to prod Nick's defenses. "Glad you could join us."

"Wouldn't miss a party like this," Luke said, giving Vicky a look. "Can you heal him?"

She shook her head. "The stupid restrictions won't let me."

George, the Psychebinder, looked paler than usual, and his pulse was weak. Probably on account of the large hole in his gut. Not wanting to use his own mana, Luke pulled from Vicky after extending a thread her way. With all those Singers around, they didn't have much time to mess around.

Even as Luke closed the gaping wound in George's stomach, another human Luke didn't recognize fell after a block of ice tore his head clean off. Pulling his now-wet hair, on account of the massive downpour, out of his face, he stood back up and surveyed the field of battle.

"We need to get out of here," he told Nick.

The guildmaster of Phoenix Phalanx shook his head. "Curtis won't listen to reason. I've tried to get him out of here, but he won't listen."

"I'll go get him," Luke said, hefting his staff as he ran into the chaos of battle again.

Ray appeared by his side. "You're back! About time!"

"Yeah," Luke said, looking over at his friend. "Did you die, by any chance?"

"Yup," Ray said, some of his good humor draining from his face. "I got your message to Milla."

Luke nodded. "Good. You give me a window, and I'll pull Curtis out. What's going on with him?"

"His class changed. Not sure why, but it's called Hollow now," Ray said, pointing to their friend. "You're seeing this, right? His darkness is kind of eating people. Kind of gross, if you ask me."

Luke: "Curtis! We need to retreat!"

Curtis: "I have to make the Intruders pay."

Luke: "Your brother's people are dying."

Luke: "There are too many of them."

Luke: "I have a way to make them regret ever coming here, but this is not the right time!"

Curtis: "Tell me."

Luke: "Trust me."

It took a moment longer, but Curtis stepped back, surprising Luke, who thought he would have to knock the Reaver, er... Hollow out, and drag him back to guild headquarters. Ray buried his dagger in a Singer's chest, took a blow of telekinetic force, and spun around. Rather than fall, he pivoted to the side and threw an explosion potion right into his attacker’s face. The Singer woman's head exploding bought them a few seconds to pull back into a fighting retreat. Nick gave Luke a relieved nod. He'd lost a few more people from his group, but not any core members of Phoenix Phalanx as far as Luke could tell.

More Singers streamed into the street, and the invaders began advancing. The humans fled. This was not the time for an all-out war. That day would come, and it would come soon, but there was a more important matter to deal with first, if the onslaught of messages Luke received was any indication. Lemira was frantic. That much was plain when Luke read her messages, but her message was clear. It was almost too late to deal with the demons, and if they failed, New York would fall, followed by the rest of the world.
