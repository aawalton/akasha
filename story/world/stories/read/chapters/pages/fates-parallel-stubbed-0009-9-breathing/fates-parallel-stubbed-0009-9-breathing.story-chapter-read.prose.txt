As they left the lecture hall, Eui gave Jia a sidelong glance.

“You’ve really got that boy whipped, huh? I feel like I’m seeing another side of you, Jia.”

“Who, Dae? What do you mean?”

Eui shook her head with a wry smile.

“You mean you aren’t doing that on purpose? I honestly don’t know which is more impressive.”

Jia was about to ask Eui to speak more clearly when Takeda Rika caught her attention and approached.

“Hey Lee! Are you free this afternoon?”

“Hi Takeda. I didn’t have anything planned, why?”

As Lee Jia greeted Takeda, she noticed Eui withdraw a bit, crossing her arms and looking down to let her hair fall in front of her face.

“I was wondering if you wanted to come over for tea again. To be perfectly honest, I’m struggling a bit with this mana sense stuff and was hoping we could go over each others’ notes to see if there’s anything we missed as fellow uh, amateurs.”

Lee Jia cast a glance in Eui’s direction as she recalled the last time she had practiced meditation with someone else. Well, she was already awakened now so it shouldn’t be a problem—probably. Takeda followed her gaze and misinterpreted the troubled expression on Lee Jia’s face.

“Oh! Your friend can come too if she likes. Nice to meet you, I’m Takeda Rika.”

Eui didn’t quite recoil as she took a half-step back from Takeda’s extended hand. She didn’t meet Takeda’s eyes as she mumbled her response.

“No thanks. I’ll see you at home, Jia.”

Lee Jia was a bit surprised as her friend hurried away. She had expected Eui to be antisocial, but she’d never seen the girl act shy before. She offered a conciliatory smile to Takeda, who had been left hanging.

“Sorry, I guess she’s a bit shy. Anyway, I guess I can join you. Though, isn’t Pri—Miss Seong going to be there? Couldn’t she tutor you?”

Takeda let out an exasperated sigh.

“She has been. I’ve been working on it since the first day, but Eun-eun is a terrible teacher. She’s like ‘just focus on your aura’ as if that’s supposed to help somehow. Imagine if Master Ienaga told you to ‘just focus on your ki’.”

Lee Jia looked askance at Takeda. Hadn’t Ienaga said almost exactly that? There was something else that caught her attention, though.

“Eun-eun?”

“Ugh, you know how she insists on informality and given names? I tried to get back at her a bit by giving her a cutesy nickname, but I think she loves it. It’s too easy to get caught up in that girl’s pace.”

Takeda frowned and kicked at the ground as she griped about her roommate. Lee Jia supposed it made sense that the princess would be willful, though that wasn’t really the way she would have imagined it.

“I see. Actually, speaking of Ienaga, weren’t you supposed to be in her advanced lesson today? Also, ‘master’?”

“Oh, I’m not her apprentice or anything! Master is a title—like Magus Hwang or Elder Qin. Master Ienaga is pretty famous back home, she’s said to be the strongest in the nation. Her dojo trains the best elite warriors in Yamato. As for the lessons, I went this morning. Martial arts isn’t always as grueling as the awakening, don’t worry.”

“Ah, well that’s good to know. Yesterday was...rough.”

Takeda gave Lee Jia a look of sympathy and nodded.

“It does get better after you awaken, but most drop out long before they get to that point. Stick with it!”

Lee Jia nodded resolutely—she had every intention to. Soon the pair arrived back at the girls' dorms and made their way to Takeda and Seong’s house. Seong Eunae met them at the door.

“Rika! Welcome back! Oh, and you’ve brought Jia with you. Come on in, I’ll put on some tea.”

Lee Jia bowed stiffly and greeted Seong.

“G-good afternoon Miss—”

“Eunae!”

Seong Eunae interrupted firmly. Lee Jia looked up at the girl’s bright smile. Somehow, that friendly expression was more terrifying than if she had been angry.

“—Eunae. Sorry for the intrusion.”

“Of course not! You’re always welcome here, Jia.”

Takeda was right, it was hard not to get caught up in Eunae’s pace. A few moments later they were seated around the tea table and Eunae once again brought out some tea and snacks.

“It’s so nice to see you again, Jia. I’m sorry we didn’t get a chance to talk yesterday, but there wasn’t much of a chance during Master Ienaga’s lesson.”

Lee Jia remembered that Eunae had been there as well, one of the last to exhaust themselves.

“She had the rest of us stop and meditate after you fell. Still, I was very surprised to find that you had already awakened!”

Takeda nearly spat out her tea.

“Wait, what!? It’s only been three days!”

“One, actually. She had already awakened yesterday morning, so she must have broken through the day after we met at the latest.”

Eunae commented calmly while sipping her tea. Lee Jia scratched her cheek and laughed awkwardly.

“Ahaha, y-yeah. I just got kinda lucky, I guess…”

Eunae placed a finger on her chin and tilted her head contemplatively.

“I suppose luck could be considered a sort of talent in itself. Still, I think this goes a bit beyond mere fortune. I wonder if I should ask that Hyeong Daesung for tutoring, myself.”

Lee Jia looked up from the biscuit she was munching on in surprise.

“You knew about that?”

“Well, you had to get help from somewhere. Besides, growing up in my family taught me to pay close attention to figures of import.”

“You know who Dae is?”

Eunae nodded, staring down at her tea.

“How could I not? The only apprentice of Grand Magus Do Hye is not someone I’m likely to miss. The important foreigners were harder to spot. I’m aware of Hayakawa Kaede and Yan Zhihao thanks to Rika, but I am sure there are others like Xin Wei who are keeping lower profiles.”

Lee Jia stared at Eunae. Nobles really did seem to live in another world entirely. She was surprised to hear Xin Wei’s name, and even more surprised that only one of those names was new to her. Was everybody in this place some kind of important person? Lee Jia turned nervously to Takeda.

“Takeda...you wouldn’t happen to actually be some super famous person from Yamato would you? I’m not sure I’d be able to handle it.”

Takeda patted Lee Jia on the shoulder and smiled sympathetically.

“I know what you mean. Don’t worry, I’m as common as dirt. I only got in because I was the best in my dojo, and even then only because the local lord didn’t have any family that qualified.”

Lee Jia sighed in relief, but then Eunae stepped in to ruin it.

“That said, I think you’ll find those like Rika and yourself to be in the minority. Immortal practitioners, especially as young as us, generally come from families with higher status.”

Lee Jia groaned. Oh well, just another way in which she was in over her head. It didn’t really change much, if she thought about it.

“Anyway, enough about the dumb machinations of idiotic nobles—no offense, Eun-eun. Lee Jia, you were gonna help me figure out this mana sense thing!”

Takeda interrupted the conversation to bring them back on track. Eunae puffed her cheeks out and pouted slightly.

“I take no offense. Political machinations are dumb and most nobles are idiotic, and it pains me that I have to keep track of them. That said, is my advice truly so inadequate, Rika?”

“Rather than inadequate, it’s more like I feel as if I would have made more progress in Magus Hwang’s lessons if I hadn’t suffered your sabotage beforehand!”

Eunae reeled as if she’d been struck by a physical blow before turning away and sulking.

“I was just trying to help…”

Ignoring her roommate’s muttering, Takeda turned back to Lee Jia and grinned.

“Don’t worry about her, she’s just being dramatic. Shall we?”

“R-right. Sure.”

A few hours later, Lee Jia was sulking next to Eunae. Both of them staring into their teacups with dead eyes while Takeda stood over them, shouting angrily.

“I cannot believe you two! You are both the worst tutors of all time. This is why talented people are—ugh! ‘Just breath like this’. Yeah thanks, Lee Jia, except I can’t tell how you’re breathing just by looking! ‘Let your awareness float on your aura’. What the hell is that even supposed to mean!?”

Takeda had been berating them for some time now. Lee Jia’s attempts to explain the feeling of awakening had not gone over well with Takeda, and it was made even worse when Eunae had picked up on it and started adding her own unhelpful observations.

In the end, Takeda had told both of them to shut up and stop distracting her. The snapping point had been when Lee Jia pointed out an error in Takeda’s breathing patterns without being able to describe what she was actually doing wrong.

Once Takeda finished rebuking them, they finished their tea and Lee Jia excused herself. Takeda and Eunae saw her out with almost completely flipped attitudes from only a few minutes ago.

“Bye Jia! Please come visit for tea again!”

“See you later, Lee Jia. Come hang out any time. As long as it’s not to help with tutoring.”

Lee Jia bowed lightly, blushing with embarrassment over her failure to help, but pleased to have made more friends.

“I will! Thank you for inviting me. And I’m sorry that I wasn’t more helpful.”

“Haha, don’t worry about it. It was mostly just an excuse to invite you over again anyway! Bring that shy friend of yours next time.”

Takeda’s grin made Lee Jia’s blush deepen. She wasn’t sure that bringing Eui would be a good idea, though.

The next morning, Jia and Eui were waiting with trepidation for their second lesson with Ienaga to begin. Eui was grumpy—well, more grumpy than usual.

“Why are you hanging around me, Jia? Shouldn’t you be off charming your new boyfriend? Or schmoozing it up with the fucking princess? I should be beneath your notice at this point.”

Jia sighed. This had been going on since she got back home last night. Previously she might have thought that Eui was just being her usual, bitter self. Perhaps even attributed her behavior to jealousy—she still thought that, a little. But she understood her friend better now, and knew that there was some deep-seated self-loathing there. As such, Lee Jia tried not to be too frustrated as she responded.

“Eui, I’m not leaving you behind by making other friends. I said it before, didn’t I? We need to make use of every resource we can, and that includes making allies. Dae and Eu—Princess Seong are important assets, but you are still my most important ally and my best friend.”

“That’s not what—I didn’t—I mean—”

Eui sputtered and averted her eyes, blushing for some reason. Jia regarded her friend curiously, but of course the intrusive thoughts that sometimes gave her insight into Eui’s thoughts were silent just when she needed them most. Her thoughts were interrupted by Master Ienaga’s voice as the lesson began.

“Last time, you learned how to turn your body into an empty vessel for ki. As you meditated, you learned what it felt like to fill that vessel. Unfortunately, it is not enough to simply allow the empty space to be filled naturally. You must learn to gather your ki proactively, to fill that vessel by your own will.

“When your meditation today begins I will guide each of you through the basic steps of the breathing technique. It is important that you focus on the feeling of the ki in your body. You must adjust your breathing until you feel the ki entering your body with each inhalation. Now, start running.”

Lee Jia didn’t hold anything back. Knowing that exhausting herself was the entire point, she focused on sustaining an all-out sprint for as long as she could through sheer force of will. A few of the other disciples scoffed, and she even heard someone comment that it wasn’t a race, but she didn’t care. Eui and a few others seemed to catch on to what she was doing, or come to the same conclusion themselves.

She wasn’t able to keep up the sprint for very long, but she continued to push herself as hard as she could, ignoring everything else and focusing only on her own efforts. Eventually, she felt that familiar wall and redoubled her efforts. Each step took monumental effort, and each breath burned in her lungs. For the barest moment she thought that she had made a mistake—that by exhausting herself too soon she had rendered herself unable to break through the wall—but then she finally made it through.

The wall vanished and a soothing feeling flooded her body. The air felt cool and refreshing in her lungs, and she could feel the energy spreading out from there, soothing her aching muscles. Lee Jia knew that when this high ended, her body would be completely empty of energy. This was when she had made her mistake last time—she’d fed her body by depleting her aura until both were completely exhausted. She didn’t have any intention of repeating that, but still…

Lee Jia began to push mana into her body. What had Ienaga said? It was a clever trick, but something was missing. The instructor had said she would explain later, but she never did—unless Lee Jia had missed some hint.

She focused on her breathing. Lee Jia had already learned three different breathing techniques for meditation, and using those as a base, she made small adjustments, concentrating on the way it affected the mana in her aura and the ki in her body.

Her steps faltered—this was difficult! Trying to adjust her breathing while running made each breath painful, trying to focus on her breathing would cause her to miss her steps, and focusing on running would break her concentration. She wasn’t even certain what she was trying to accomplish—she’d started experimenting on a hunch, but her mana was running low. She was running out of time to accomplish whatever it was she was trying to do.

Belatedly, Lee Jia realized that she had been trying to meditate while running. But in the past, whenever she meditated she would feel distant from her body—how could she run like that? Instead, Lee Jia decided to focus in the other direction. As she had in the first training session, she concentrated inward, focusing on every breath of air, every beat of her heart, and every straining muscle—she immediately wished she hadn’t.

Lee Jia was now hyper-aware of just how much pain she was in. Her lungs burned, her heart pounded painfully in her chest, her head was throbbing from dehydration, her eyes were stinging from the salt of her sweat, her ankle was sore from a misstep earlier—it went on and on, with every ache and pain now at the forefront of her mind. It was awful, but now she could focus on running and control her breathing at the same time. Unfortunately, this also meant she could no longer feel her mana sense, it was as distant and ethereal to her as her body was when she meditated before.

Without access to her aura, the ki in her body was rapidly beginning to deplete. She didn’t think she had more than a few moments before she collapsed. What was she trying to accomplish? She’d been running on pure intuition, but now it felt like she needed a clear idea. What had she been missing? She had tried to sustain herself by pushing mana into her body, but it failed when she ran out of mana. What if she meditated to replenish her mana? But she couldn’t do that and run at the same time unless she focused her awareness inward, but if she did that then she couldn’t push mana into her body.

“An empty vessel can’t help but be filled.”

Lee Jia nearly tripped as she remembered Ienaga’s words. Without any time to spare, she focused on the breathing patterns that would draw more mana into her aura. Without the ability to sense her own aura, she could only trust that she was doing it properly and pray that she hadn’t come to the wrong conclusion—it would be terribly embarrassing if she collapsed again. Before she could second guess herself, the ki in her body ran completely dry.

A burst of energy—the same as when she had broken through the wall earlier—flooded Lee Jia’s body. The runner’s high was intoxicating as all the aches and pains of exhaustion were temporarily relieved. It felt like it was more than the first time—the soothing feeling was stronger and lasted longer. As Lee Jia’s breathing became stronger, there was a sort of tug at her attention. At the edge of her mind, the faint feeling of her mana sense was becoming stronger.

Slowly, a little bit at a time, Lee Jia allowed that faint feeling to grow until she was acutely aware of both her body and her mana sense. It was jarring, as though she were in two places at once. She could feel her concentration wavering as she tried to maintain the state of dual-awareness. With some effort, she thought she should be able to push mana into her body again, but there wasn’t much point anymore.

As Lee Jia grew more accustomed to the feeling, she could spare enough attention to notice that she was now the only one remaining on the track—no, not the only one. Just ahead of her, Eui was desperately persisting. Eui’s aura had shrunk down to a thin film around her body, and Jia was certain that in a few moments she’d end up meeting the same fate that Jia had before.

Lee Jia wanted to tell Eui to stop—or tell her about the technique she had discovered—but her focus was already strained as it was and it felt like if she stopped breathing long enough to speak everything would fall apart. In another desperate moment of intuition, Jia reached out toward Eui with the mote of awareness within her aura. To her dismay, Eui’s aura just flowed around it like hers had when Dae had checked her aura before.

Just when Jia was about to give up and stop Eui, she seemed to notice what Jia was trying to do and furrowed her brows in concentration. Slowly, their auras began to flow together, and Jia’s mana sense felt a familiar existence tentatively reach out to—

Jia collapsed. She isn’t moving. She isn’t breathing. Eui stares as the instructor drags her roommate off the track. What is she going to do if Jia is dead?

Jia and Eui went down hard on the dirt track, snapping Jia from the vision. That had definitely been Eui’s memory. It was so much more clear this time—because it was more recent? Lee Jia pushed herself up shakily, glad that she was still able to move. Looking up, she met Eui’s eyes staring at her with shock and surprise—she understood.

Already, Lee Jia could see Ienaga approaching. Jia and Eui exchanged a glance and nodded at each other wordlessly. It had worked—they both knew what to do now. Quickly, the pair scrambled back to their feet and once again began to run.

Ienaga Yumi watched the reckless pair of girls as they continued to run, already long after they should have stopped. She crossed her arms and glared at them. She was annoyed that An Eui had kept running even after being told to stop. She had dropped some hints for Lee Jia, but she had never expected this. They were reckless, insubordinate, and borderline suicidal—Ienaga smiled privately—those were her favorite kinds of students.
