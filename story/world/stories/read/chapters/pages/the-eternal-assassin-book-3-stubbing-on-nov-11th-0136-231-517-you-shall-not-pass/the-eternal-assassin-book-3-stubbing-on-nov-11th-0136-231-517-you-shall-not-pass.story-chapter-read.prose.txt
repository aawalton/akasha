Samantha stared at the ivory door, waiting for Asher to reappear and let them know what was on the other side. They’d gone over countless plans and contingencies during their time training with one another, and they’d all agreed that using Asher’s Astral Dip to scout out the interior of new rooms before they barged in was the best use of his skill. Only during their discussions, Asher had agreed to try and take no more than a quick look before returning to the material realm and explaining what they were dealing with. No more than five or ten seconds at most.

He’d been gone for nearly thirty.

“Something’s wrong,” Samantha said, her grip tightening on her bow as she took a few steps farther away from the door. Her role in just about any combat situation was to distance herself from the threats in order to better utilize her archery. While she couldn’t really take to the skies, seeing as they were in a hallway, she could at the very least put some distance between her and whatever was on the other side of that door. “He should have been back by now.”

“Don’t worry so much, the runt probably saw something particularly stab-able and took advantage of the opportunity,” Moxy snorted, not looking the least bit concerned. “I’m sure he’ll pop back into existence in another few seconds and say something stupid, as usual.”

A few more seconds passed, and when Asher distinctly did not pop back into existence, Rulfar joined Samantha in frowning. If she hadn’t spent the last week training with Rulfar in his battle form, the sight of a giant, half-bear, half-man trying to frown would have sent a shudder down her spine. “Hold on a moment,” he said, raising his Ring of Lesser Realm Sight to his nose. That was the very same ring that Asher had brought back for them and had even used Permanence on, which meant he’d handled it for at least a few hours. Taking a deep breath, Rulfar’s frown deepened, and he shifted his stance as he faced the door with his battle axe at the ready. “It’s faint, but I have him. He’s moving farther into the lich’s base.”

“I guess that means he’s either running off on his own, or something got their hands on him,” Moxy said, rolling her neck and bringing up her fists as she faced the door as well. “We going after him?”

“Of course we’re going after him,” Rulfar said, as if the answer was obvious. “Stand back. Even if this door isn’t enchanted, ivory isn’t exactly a simple material to smash through.” But even as the bloodhound stepped forward, before he could swing his raised axe into the pure-white door, there was a ‘click’ on the other side.

Followed immediately by the ivory door getting blasted off its hinges, directly at them.

Thankfully, in his battle form, Rulfar’s already impressive figure was transformed into a ten-foot-tall wall of pure muscle. The bloodhound took the impact of the door head on, not even flinching as he knocked it to the side, just in time to cleave through the first of the lesser demons that attempted to storm into the hallway.

“It’s an ambush!” he shouted, activating the enchantment on his axe as he cut deep into the next demon’s arm, causing a powerful spray of blood to shoot out and blind two imps attempting to crawl along the wall into the hallway.

“No, really?!” Moxy shouted back, already laying into the doorway with a rapid flurry of air punches. Despite the lesser demons’ impressive durability, each air blast was still enough to tear small chunks of flesh from their forms, and the impacts hit hard enough to stop their forward momentum, forcing them back a step as they snarled and tried to force their way through.

“Where is Asher?!” Samantha shouted, firing into the mass of demons with her bow from farther down the hall. There were so many demons pressed up against one another, all trying to force their way through the single doorway into their hall, that it was functionally impossible to miss. Her piercing shots were even more effective than normal, each arrow managing to punch through two or even three demons before the enchantment wore off.

While firing arrow after arrow into the chaotic mass of demons, her mind went back to an interesting phrase Asher had taught her from his own world a while ago, back when Horntho village had still been around. He’d mentioned something about ‘shooting fish in a barrel,’ explaining the concept meant something was incredibly easy because the fish had nowhere to go. At the time, Samantha hadn’t thought hitting fish that were actively swimming around would be all that simple, even if they were trapped, but she finally understood what he meant.

There were so many demons and imps trying to force their way through the doorway, that their overwhelming numbers meant little. She didn’t even need to aim.

“Moxy, focus on the imps!” Rulfar ordered, once again cleaving through an outstretched arm from a demon who managed to free itself from the throng, before finishing his swing with an axe to the very same demon’s throat. “They’re crawling past the demon blockade!"

Sure enough, the imps were in fact doing just that. Almost like they were giant, humanoid spiders, the imps dug into the walls and ceiling with their claws, squeezing through gaps in the mess of lesser demons as they broke out into the hall. Samantha was afraid for a moment that Moxy wouldn’t listen, the warrior looking far more interested in continuing to throw air punches at the larger demons. Thankfully, Moxy merely nodded, turning and blasting the imps off the walls and ceiling before they could circle around and attack them from behind. Where a single lesser demon took over a dozen of her punches to go down, the imps were far more fragile. One air punch was all it took to rip their heads free from their bodies or cause their chests to explode from the force of her strikes.

Samantha withheld her shudder at the reminder of just how devastating an opponent Moxy truly was for anything that didn’t have enhanced durability. Whenever they sparred as a team, Moxy held back on her air punches out of concern of accidentally killing her. Something she’d only agreed to do in exchange for getting to go all out against Asher and Rulfar. In his battle form, Rulfar could actually absorb quite a lot of damage, and of course, Asher was far too slippery to get hit most of the time.

And even if she did accidentally blast his head off, he’d grow it back soon enough.

“Something’s not right!” Rulfar shouted, grunting as an imp squeezed through the door and latched its teeth into his ankle while he was busy killing another lesser demon. Ignoring the imp entirely, Rulfar kept cleaving away at the bigger threats. “This is the worst possible position the demons could have attacked us from!”

“Nobody ever said they were smart!” Moxy laughed, punching the imp off Rulfar and quickly healing the damage with a touch.

“No, but the lich is supposed to be!” Rulfar said, snarling as he punched a demon that tried to wrestle his battle axe from his hands, forcing the demon’s head to snap back as he tore the axe away from the demon’s clawed hands. Samantha doubted many of the lesser demons had experience fighting above their weight class, seeing as they were all close to eight-feet-tall. “They should have a better defensive set up than this!”

The moment Rulfar pointed that out, Samantha’s blood ran cold as she realized what was happening. Shooting an arrow through another one of the lesser demon’s heads, she shouted up the hall toward them.

“They’re not trying to kill us, they’re trying to buy time! What if they’re doing the extraction ritual to Asher right now?!”

“It certainly feels like they’re trying to kill us,” Moxy laughed, twisting out of the way of a lesser demon who managed to squeeze past Rulfar and the corpses piling the doorway. Leaping off the ground in an explosion of air, Moxy’s fist smashed into the demon’s chin, sending the monster stumbling backward and collapsing to the ground. Landing on its chest, she threw three punches in quick succession at its face, cracking straight through to the brain and killing it before it could recover.

“No, she’s right!” Rulfar said, snarling as more claws slipped past his defenses and cut gashes into his dense fur and muscle. “This is a diversion, plain and simple! They can’t effectively attack us, but we can't get past them either! They have the only doorway blocked!”

“We need to punch our way through!” Samantha shouted, letting fly yet another arrow before realizing she was nearly out. The downside of letting Asher be the team pack mule was that with him gone, that meant her additional quivers were as well. “Moxy, clear a path!”

“We’re in a hallway,” Moxy reminded them, grinning even as she punted an imp who had thrown itself at her. “This is gonna be fun!”

Not needing to be told twice, Moxy jumped back behind Rulfar and began compressing air into a tightly packed ball. Having seen the force of this skill send Asher and even Rulfar flying on more than one occasion, Samantha was glad she was farther down the hall as she watched Moxy prepare her attack. Nodding to herself, Moxy moved around to the side, laughing as she hurled the ball directly at the doorway. “Incoming!”

At the last second, Rulfar jerked out of the way with surprising speed for his current form, and the lesser demon who had been about to try and grab him blinked in surprise at the spinning ball of air that was flying toward it. Moxy’s ball hit the demon in the face…

And promptly exploded.

Even as far back as she was, Samantha narrowed her eyes as a powerful wall of wind pushed back against her, causing her hair to flutter as she stood her ground. Rulfar had moved to the side enough to escape the worst of the blast, but the same couldn’t be said about the throng of demons. What had to be a dozen demons all struggling to get through the doorway at once were blasted backward from the force of Moxy’s skill, hurled back into the room that they’d come from. Before they could recover, Rulfar and Moxy sprinted through the doorway and into the room one after another, taking advantage of the confusion to start scything their way through the remaining demons. Samantha charged after them, grabbing arrows as she went and using Razor Wings to bisect the occasional imp that leapt at her. Now that they’d gotten through the doorway, she could finally make out the sheer number of demons that they‘d actually been up against.

“There had to be at least fifty of them waiting for us!” Samantha called out, drawing back an already bloody arrow and firing it through a demon going to attack Moxy from behind.

“A lot less now!” Moxy chuckled, blasting herself into another demon and smashing her elbow into its throat. Samantha noted how the warrior’s semi-reckless style of close-range fighting hadn’t come without cost, but the nasty gashes across her back and face were already healing with a burst of violet light.

Moxy wasn’t wrong, either. At this point, the three of them had nearly finished off the entire room of demons and imps, and only the dregs remained. Samantha was a bit upset knowing that, unlike when Asher killed them, these monsters would simply reform down in the nine hells, ready to be summoned some distant day in the future to wreak havoc up on the material realm once again. The thought of the very demons she was killing potentially burning down another innocent village filled her with a cold, burning fury, which she channeled into her shots as she continued killing.

Eventually, the last of the lesser demons fell, and Samantha lowered her bow. Things had gone much faster after they’d blasted their way through the clogged doorway, and as much as Samantha wanted to catch her breath and grab all her arrows, they needed to keep moving. There was no telling how much longer Asher had left before they began the ritual.

“Let’s keep moving,” Rulfar said, clearly on the same wavelength as her as the bloodhound held out an arm for Moxy to heal. “Samantha, grab some arrows. Ten seconds, then we…”

Rulfar’s voice slowed to a stop, and Samantha looked up from the imp she was currently tearing an arrow out of. Across the room, slowly closing a door behind it, stood a demon not at all like the ones they’d just finished killing. Nine feet tall and with teeth and horns darker than night, the purple-skinned greater demon grinned at the three of them, its voice silencing the world around them and reaching down into their very souls as it spoke.

“Going somewhere?”
