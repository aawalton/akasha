Freddy’s first day at the university was going about as well as expected.

He stood there, face buried in the palm of his hand, wishing the earth would simply swallow him whole.

The three watchdogs marched up to them, with a lanky man taking the head. “We’ve received a report of badge forgery?”

“Right here, officer,” the rude bastard pointed at Freddy.

The watchdogs looked like they were going to arrest Freddy for all of a moment, and then, they appeared to realize what was happening. The man in the front coughed. “Ah. Mr. Stern. Apologies. It was unavoidable you would arouse suspicion. Not to worry, however. We were alerted of your arrival beforehand. Sorry for the disturbance."

“Well, it’s not like I didn’t expect this to happen, honestly.”

“Huh?” The redhead blurted out, his two brain cells rubbing really hard together as he tried to process what was happening.

“This aside, we would like to offer you a warm welcome. If there is anything you need help with, an assistant can be arranged for you.”

“No… no, I’ll just… figure it out. Thanks for the offer, though.”

“You’re welcome, Sir.” The man bowed a little. “Now, if that’s all, we should be on our way.” They turned and left.

The silence in the room was so thick a pin dropping would sound like a bomb going off. The insanity, however, was off the charts. One girl in particular was losing her fucking mind in the crowd, and Freddy tried his best not to glance at her. I could swear I’ve seen her somewhere before.

Freddy’s memory was pretty great, especially after he ascended to the second star. But it was in that awkward place where it was just good enough to remember random people he’d passed by on the street, but not good enough to be absolutely sure it was just a random passerby and not someone he had actually interacted with.

“What’s your name?” Freddy broke the awkward silence as he faced the red-haired young man before him.

The man gathered himself, squaring his shoulders. “I’m Shane Lawman.”

Freddy wagged his finger as he worked his memory. “Ah, you’re Overlord Lawman’s kid, right? I saw you in the arena a few times, you’re pretty good. Look, I can see you’ve got a strong sense of justice, and to be fair, you did first offer me a chance to take the badges off without having the cops called on me, so props for that. But if you’re done being a nuisance, I’d like to go on my way. Okay?”

Shane snorted kind of aggressively, a dark look crossing his eyes as he turned around wordlessly.

“Wait,” Freddy said, causing the man to halt. “What’s with that look?”

“What look?”

“You know what look. The ‘I’ll get you back for this’ look. Do you have a problem with me?”

Shane scoffed. “Severed powerhouses usually know better than to flaunt medals like that. It’s just common courtesy.”

“Huh?” Freddy scratched his head. “Severed? Buddy, I’m not sure who you think I am, but I assure you, this is my first time at the second star.”

“Hah!” Shane cackled, and he was joined by a number of laughs from the crowd.

Freddy frowned. “What?”

“Sir, there’s no need to embarrass yourself like this,” Shane jabbed with a smirk. “You’re evidently quite accomplished, and there’s no shame in giving ascension a second try. But pretending to be a young two-star is kinda creepy.”

Freddy’s anger bubbled up, and he revealed a grin as he wagged his finger again. “Okay, you’re really starting to piss me off now. Isn’t it enough that you embarrassed yourself once already?”

“I’m the one embarrassing myself?” Shane asked, putting on a mock surprised look.

Freddy looked around, and judging by the looks the others in the room were shooting at him, everyone thought Shane was in the right. “I’m twenty five years old!” he shouted in indignation. Everyone in the crowd winced in cringe, as if he’d said the most embarrassing thing ever.

The urge to pull his ID out and show it to everyone nearly overwhelmed him, but he took a deep breath instead. “You know what? Believe what you want. I don’t have to prove anything to anyone.” He tried walking away, but the muttering and muted chuckles pricked at him something ungodly. Suddenly, he stopped, taking his ID out of his storage ring. “Here you go!” He flashed it, showing it to everyone. “See the birthday? Twenty-five! I turned twenty-five just recently! So there you go. Show’s over, folks!”

“That’s fake!” someone from the crowd shouted.

“Fuck you mean ‘that’s fake’!? Who just said that!?”

A scrawny woman raised her hand, stepping forward. “I have a talent that lets me see certain information about people. And you, uh… You are over 125 years old.”

Freddy cringed. “That’s… technically sort of true.”

The crowd broke into laughter.

“I just spent a lot of time in time-dilated dungeons, okay!?”

The laughter grew louder.

Shane chuckled, pulling his phone back out. “What do you think the watchdogs would say about the fake ID?”

“That it isn’t fake!” Freddy said. “Call them and see!”

“Look,” Shane put the phone away. “You are no doubt an incredible individual. But what’s with this strange complex? Get yourself together, man. There is literally nothing wrong with being a geezer.”

“I’m not a geezer, you dumbass; call the watchdogs and you’ll see what they tell you!”

“I don’t feel the need to waste any more of their time on petty nonsense.”

Freddy guffawed. “Now that’s fuckin’ rich. You know what, I’m starting to think I hurt your pride.”

“Nah.”

“Nah? You called them and looked like an idiot. Now you’re trying to salvage your reputation by basically bullying me.”

“I would never bully the elderly.” Shane grinned.

“Okay.” Freddy clenched his jaw. “I think I’m gonna kick your ass.”

“Whoa, threatening violence, now?” Shane raised his hands and backed away. “That’s not very cool of you, gramps.” His grin widened. “Besides, I’d be careful where I’m stepping if I were you.”

A crisp vein showed itself on Freddy’s neck as he worked to calm down. “What? Scared?”

“Of you? Why would I be?” Shane leaned forward. “Let us be real here, champ. Those badges of yours are impressive, but all I’m smelling a whole lotta cheeeeeeeeeeeese,” he dragged the last word out as he bared his teeth at Freddy. “Over a million confirmed kills in under 10 battles? It’s obvious you either used a talent or a spirit ability to empower someone from outside the battlefield. That’s the only way to get credit for kills without getting credit for direct participation.

“And look at you. I don’t see anything even resembling a scar anywhere. How much time could you have realistically spent in action?”

“I have a healing talent, moron.”

“Of supreme quality? Okay, sure.”

Freddy gave the guy a flat look. Then, he pointed at the Solo Dungeoneer badge. “How’d I cheese this one, genius? Did I stand back and let a ghost of myself delve or something?”

“Hah! At your age, it would have been embarrassing if you hadn’t delved at least that many!”

“I already told you, my guy. You want proof of what I can do? Let’s take it to the arena.”

“And why the arena specifically?” Shane asked with a quirked eyebrow.

Freddy looked at the man like he was an idiot. “So you don’t die for good?”

“Oooh”-s echoed through the hall, with Shane balling his fist up, putting it to his mouth, and sucking air in. “Them’s fighting words, gramps. If you’re confident, then”—he bumped into Freddy with his chest—”let’s fight outside the arena.”

“Sure.”

“Hah. As if you’d really dare.”

“No, I’m serious. Let’s do it, then. Come on.”

“Look here…” Shane suddenly grew serious. “Out of respect for your service to the country, I won’t push things any further.”

“Are you fucking stupid? I said let’s fight.”

“You’ve had a rocky start today,” Shane continued. “I will do you a favor and let you save the last of your reputation. Stop bringing shame on those medals and show that you’re worthy of them.”

“Buddy, were you dropped on the head as a kid?” Freddy asked just as Shane turned around and started walking away. “Hey! You’re literally just running away!” Freddy laughed. “Look at this loser! You’re full of shit, Shane!” But Shane ignored him.

Freddy turned to face the room full of onlookers, where a blend of second-hand embarrassment and jeers welcomed his attention. “All right… Okay… You know what? I don’t even care. Why should I? You’ll all know you’re wrong soon enough.” He raised both middle fingers to the crowd as he maneuvered towards a nearby hall.
