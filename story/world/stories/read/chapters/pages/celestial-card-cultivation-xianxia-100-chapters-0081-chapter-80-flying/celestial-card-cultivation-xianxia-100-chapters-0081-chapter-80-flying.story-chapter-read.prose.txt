They were closing in on two weeks in the wilderness.

Jianyi would have expected to miss living in a nice home—any home really—and there were certainly aspects that he would love to transport into their camp, but overall, he enjoyed his time tremendously.

He was grateful for all the lectures in the sect and had learned a lot from them, but the lack of all constraints to do anything but progress on the things that mattered to him—and things like woodcraft that he hadn’t known to be important—was liberating.

Since the previous day, he had been trying to pick up on tracks that he had originally found near a small, clear stream that likely terminated in the same rivulet that fed the pond that the beaver had lived in.

The tracks were similar to the ones from the deer that Jianyi had defeated, if a bit wider and deeper than what he had seen so far.

It took another hour until he stumbled upon a fresh set of tracks that matched the ones he had been following for so long.

Minghao had taught the trick of carefully rubbing one’s finger along the track to get a sense for the humidity in it, and compared it to the surrounding ground. Based on that, he estimated that he was maybe an hour or so behind the deer.

Despite the apparent distance to his quarry, Jianyi progressed slowly and with caution—Minghao’s warning not to take unnecessary risks loomed large and fresh in his mind.

A few hundred paces later, he came upon a scene of utter chaos. Underbrush had been torn from the ground; the hoof marks were no longer following the rhythm of a beast walking; instead, the ground was torn up, and a small sapling was even broken in the middle, its thin branches scrubbing the forest floor.

Failing to identify the cause of the commotion, Jianyi tried to process each individual track, each scratch in a tree, and each torn flower. Hovering over a dark spot on the ground, he rubbed his finger across it—he came back with the brown of the earth—and the red of blood.

A fight had happened between those trees.

Now that he knew what to look for, he found more signs of a creature having been significantly wounded; he did not, however, find any tracks other than the one from the heavy deer.

Jianyi considered his options but ultimately decided to follow the tracks—he was committed to being careful, but there could be an opportunity at the end of this path.

The first opportunity was one of learning. He was slightly proud of himself when he deduced from the changed pattern of the tracks that the deer was favoring one leg, but at the same time, the tracks were deeper, more ground was thrown up by each step—he assumed this meant that the creature was rushing, fleeing.

He slightly accelerated his pursuit while still trying to apply all his stealth, or rather, presence, training.

A few minutes later, he spotted movement between the trees ahead of him.

Heavens! Fortunately, the slightly undignified curse played out only in his head. The creature he had been following was not a large deer; it was a Stone Boar.

Jianyi thought back to the interaction with a member of that species of spirit beasts when it had surprised him and his friends on the road to one of their merit missions. Back then, he had made the creature retreat with a Bolt of Fire—but he was pretty sure that was only the case because the sow had had two piglets with her.

The beast ahead of him was massive. Standing, it would certainly reach almost up to his chest, and with a thick layer of stone across its shoulders and most of its sides.

He sensed that it might be a smidgen smaller and younger than the one they had faced on the road; also, it wasn’t standing, but rather resting on the ground, eyes closed. The reason was apparent: a hand-width-wide wound close to its spine that had torn away some of the rock protection.

The wound was still weeping blood, but if Jianyi had to guess, he didn’t think it would be lethal—the creature had clearly managed to flee from whatever enemy had hurt it and was now trying to recover its strength.

A Stone Boar was clearly Earth-aligned. If he could best the beast and extract a card, he could take the first step on body cultivation.

His gut was churning—but it wasn’t fear. It was urgency, maybe a tinge of greed.

He reached out with his senses, trying to gauge the beast's progress. It was, without a doubt, in the Qi Gathering stage, which was good. But he was also fairly certain that it was at least two, if not three, stages ahead of him.

But… it was hurt. And… he could have surprise on his side. And… his techniques hit harder than normal.

Yes, there was risk; he acknowledged that. But there was also a reward. It was what he needed, and he might have to wait weeks to come across a creature with the right affinity that was on a lower stage of cultivation.

He took a deep breath, challenging his assumptions. Was his desire for more progress overwhelming his common sense? After a few moments, he concluded that no, it was a risk worth taking. He just needed to do it right.

One thing Minghao had suggested was that if one had the time to, it would be helpful to visualize how he wanted the fight to go; that way, at least the first moments of the fight required less decision-making and allowed for more focus.

After a moment, he nodded to himself, his heart started beating slightly faster, and adrenaline rushed through his veins.

He took a few more steps forward, got as close to the beast as he believed he could without alerting it to his presence, then he called upon his cards and was fortunate to see Bolt of Fire right away.

He leaped forward, spear in hand, and with the power of his intent, triggered the Fire-based attack, racing after the bolt to close the distance.

The boar opened its eyes and looked at him. Its eyes oozed viciousness.

For a moment, Jianyi threatened to freeze. The fire of his bolt reflected in the boar’s tusks.

His will asserted itself.

He would not be cowed by a Qi Gathering beast. Anything, really.

The bolt hit the beast, which had gotten on its legs, and burned through layers of stone, into its flesh. It squealed in a mix of anger and pain.

Jianyi pressed forward and lunged into a Piercing Fang. It was an attack with the sole purpose of killing his opponent. There was no hesitation; he didn’t limit himself. He just attacked—lining up his leg, hip, torso, and arms perfectly with the forward motion of the spear.

The attack landed, but the beast had moved slightly to the side. Instead of hitting its hide, the spear scratched over a chunk of stone until it landed on the creature’s back, where it did leave a deep but not fatal cut.

The beast did not care; it seemed after fleeing once that day, it now only cared about attacking. It catapulted forward from a standing start, and only by launching himself to the side with his still extended leg did he manage to evade. He landed heavily on the ground and rolled to the side until stopped by a tree in his path.

Still on all four, he needed to leap again as the beast passed through where he had just been. Jianyi’s hair stood up as he heard the loud thud with which the beast hit the tree, ripping some of its roots from the ground.

Not getting hit rose even further on his priority list. There was no coming back from that.

The close dodges had given him a better understanding of the state that the beast was in, and it looked like the fight had opened the wound on the boar’s back again. He needed to capitalize on that.

He managed to sidestep the next charge; this time, he had sufficient time to counterattack, though. A Blazing Arc slashed the creature close to where its original wound had been—and clearly that had been one step too much for the creature.

It squealed with a new level of hatred and rage that was far beyond what it had done before.

In parallel, the stone protrusions on its body started to glow in a faint yellow.

Jianyi’s gut dropped, and he knew, deep down in his being, that staying close to the creature would be the last mistake he ever made.

Two quick steps and an undignified jump behind the closest tree were all that saved him from an explosion that threatened to take out his eardrums, followed by a barrage of stones that flashed by him, through bushes, and even smaller trees.

He needed a second to get his heartbeat back under control and force his limbs to move again. A quick glance around the tree revealed the boar panting heavily, a lot of the stone from his hide gone, having been the ammunition for the blast that had shredded the environment.

The beast was exhausted, but not done.

The moment it spotted him, it charged again.

A toppled sapling to the right and the tree he had been hiding behind to the left limited his movement. He saw the beast coming in, willing to trample anything that stood between it and its mortal enemy.

There was no way. No left. No right. No back.

There was only up.

Summoning all his strength—and instinctively cycling some of his qi into his legs—Jianyi jumped.

Time seemed to slow down as he saw the tusks of the creature ripping through the air that he had just left behind him; then he focused all his core strength and control he had over his body to turn his leap into a wheel, which made him land heavily, but on one foot and knee.

Behind him, he heard the outrage and destruction wrought by the beast.

Quickly, he got back up on his feet and turned towards the boar.

The sequence had his pulse racing, but it had also given him an idea.

It was a risk.

But everything in this battle was.

In a moment, the sequence flashed through his mind.

It was doable.

Hopefully.

Jianyi took a quick look around him and took two steps to the side.

The boar announced its next charge with another squeal.

Jianyi waited.

The boar was twenty paces away.

Fifteen.

Ten.

He called upon his card.

Force of the Forest flooded his body with the strength and stability of trees much older and stronger than those around him.

Five.

He planted his spear—the leaf-shaped blade almost white, the haft almost black—into the ground, gritted his teeth, inhaled, and tensed every muscle in his body.

Impact.

Weightlessness.

Impact.

Pain.

Heaviness.

Silence.

Jianyi opened his eyes and found himself at the bottom of a tree, which was still shaking from his impact on it after being thrown away by the boar’s impact.

Said beast was lying draped over his legs and hips. The spear buried deep into its chest. Dead.

He had done it. He had defeated the boar. He…

A presence unfolded itself, stopping him mid-thought.

A presence much stronger than a Qi Gathering beast.

He saw something flying towards him.

A cat.

As large as a pony.

Spotted.

A leopard.

Jianyi sighed.
