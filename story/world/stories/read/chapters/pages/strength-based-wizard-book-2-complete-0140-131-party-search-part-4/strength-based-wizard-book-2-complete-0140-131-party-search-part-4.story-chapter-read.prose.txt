Chapter 131

Party Search, Part 4

A week later, I’m back in the Harvest Guild training facility, standing in the same room where I first tested both [Cast Fist] and [Magic Sapper]. The room has been repaired. Mostly. The observation glass is new, thicker, and faintly pink around the edges, which makes me suspect Labonte had a personal hand in further reinforcing the protective window. The floor panels have been replaced where Charlotte’s [Earth Thrust] tried to convert me into a human shish kabob. The scorch marks are gone, as is the robot debris.

I stand near the center of the room in street clothes, Full Metal Staff hovering at my side. I’m alone. Labonte is booked for the day, as is Jerome. The Vice Captain is actually off on business on my behalf. He promised me he’d meet me at HQ later in the day, and apologized for missing this moment. Charlotte is accompanying him.

I’m waiting for each of my [Matches] from [Party Search] to appear. This is the time I discussed with each of the Participants. Now all they need to do is follow through, I think, surprisingly nervous. After all, being stood up isn’t entirely out of the question. Each one has a [Party Gather Ticket]. Each one is supposed to use it to come to my location at exactly Noon my time.

The first [Match] I received had come from Lim Sang-hyeok, the Level 54 Spirit Commander from South Korea.

His blurb in the [Party Search] marketplace had been simple, but one that stuck with me:

I do not fight alone.

The second had come from Ivy Madison, one of the handful of Level 40-somethings I had decided to send requests to after I completed cycling through the high-leveler profiles. She’s a Level 40 Participant from San Francisco, with the ‘Architect’ Class. Her blurb was fairly straightforward.

I can build a variety of constructs. Let me know what you need and I can make it!

The third Match had happened only yesterday.  A man named Raul from Mexico. Just Raul. No last name provided in his Participant profile. He’s a Level 42 ‘Brainbarian.’  I had stared at that Class title for at least a full minute. Brainbarian. Not a typo. Naturally, I liked him immediately. Even if his Profile was missing a photo or a blurb. I’m still surprised he was actively monitoring the marketplace menu and opted to [Match] with me as well.

The air in front of me ripples.

“Here we go,” I whisper, rocking on my heels as I wait for my Party hopefuls to teleport into the training space.

A circular distortion opens above the training floor. The edges flicker with the pixelated geometry of System-generated light. The pixelated light forming the edges of the circle emit lines of light, stretching down towards the floor of Harvest’s training room. The sharp lines fold through the air like reality is unfolding a travel brochure, until three doors of blue-white light stand before me. Three figures step through the doors, appearing at once as shadowed silhouettes. As the doors of light close, each figure steps into the room, taking real form.

Is this is what it looks like when I use a Gate Ticket? I think. It’s strange being on the other side of the phenomenon. I take in each of the three newcomers as they materialize.

Sang-hyeok is exactly as he appeared in his [Party Search] profile. Young and unassuming, with straight black hair and dark eyes set in a round, pale face. He’s slender, dressed in a dark coat over simple travel clothes, with a large backpack strapped over his shoulders. He looks around with quiet attention rather than obvious awe. His posture is relaxed, hands deep in the pockets of his coat.

Ivy Madison stands beside him, tall and lanky, probably in her thirties if I had to guess. Her short dark brown hair ends just past her ears, slightly uneven in a way that looks either intentional or like she cut it herself during a breakdown after surviving a Gate exploration that could have gone a lot better. She has green eyes, a long nose, and the focused, slightly distracted look of someone who is always partly daydreaming. She wears dark pants, scuffed boots, and a gray jacket with too many pockets. She’s holding a large duffle bag in one hand.

Then there’s Raul. Raul is huge. Well, he’s on the shorter side. About a head shorter than me. But he’s wide as well. Absolutely ripped in a way that makes my inner gym rat sit up and salute. Even under the three-piece tan suit he’s wearing, the balance of his physique is obvious. He has shoulders like armored doors, a thick chest, and traps visible under the collar. His waist is tight, opening  to legs that look powerful enough to squat a dragon.

Respect. I nod in appreciation.

Raul’s skin is dark, his eyes hazel, and his hair is buzzed short to the scalp and bleached electric blonde, bright enough to qualify as an emergency beacon. A full black mustache sits on his upper lip in stark contrast to his bleached hair.

He looks at me and his face instantly lights up. Then he launches into Spanish.

“¡Hermano de hierro y magia! Cuando vi cómo combinaste músculo y hechicería para destruir a ese demonio, supe que nuestros destinos—”

A small notification blooms in the corner of my HUD.

Language Detected: Spanish

. . .

Ping!

Language Integration complete.

His voice shifts mid-sentence, the System smoothing meaning into English while leaving his accent intact.

“—were destined to meet as comrades! Joseph Sullivan, Muscle Mage, breaker of demons, lifter of impossible burdens! It is an honor. An honor, sir!”

He strides forward like we’re old friends meeting in the final act of an action movie. I don’t even have time to respond before he thrusts out his hand, forearm angled up. I clasp his hand. Our arms form a handshake oozing masculinity. The Predator handshake. Palm to palm, fingers locked, forearms vertical. Our biceps bulge. His sleeve strains. My sleeve strains. System-generated energy crackles around us.

Raul grins.

I grin back.

I hear Ivy mutter, “Oh my god.”

Sang-hyeok says something in Korean.

Raul squeezes. I squeeze back.

This is not a dominance contest. This is diplomacy. Pure muscular diplomacy.

Raul releases my hand and slaps my shoulder with a hard clap. A normal person would lose a tooth from the shockwave it generates. I barely move, which makes his grin widen.

“Yes,” Raul says. “Good. Very good.”

Ivy clears her throat. It is an awkward little sound, like someone stepping politely between two charging rhinos.

“Hi,” she says, raising one hand. “I’m Ivy. Madison. Ivy Madison. Obviously you know that. Because of the Profile. And the [Match]. So. Hello.” She winces at herself.

I smile. “Good to meet you,” I say.

She nods quickly, then looks at Raul. Her cheeks redden and her eyes dart away. She decides Sang-hyeok is a better choice to address next. Bowing slightly at the waist, she greets him too. “Ivy Madison. Nice to meet you too!”

Sang-hyeok inclines his head. He says something softly in Korean.

My System catches it.

Language Detected: Korean.

. . .

Ping!

Language Integration complete.

“Hello,” says Sang-hyeok, turning to me now. His hands remain in his pockets. “Where will we be staying?”

I take a half step back so I can see all three of them at once. “Welcome to Cleveland,” I say.

Raul places a hand over his heart. “A city of great trials as of late!”

“That’s one way to put it.”

“It smells of battle,” he says. He inhales, taking a massive breath in before sighing.

Ivy looks mildly concerned, but I catch her taking a cautious sniff of the air. She looks confused.

“That might just be the lake smell,” I say jokingly.

Sang-hyeok glances toward the walls, then up toward the observation deck, then back at me. He seems quiet, perhaps measuring. Or perhaps I’m just reading too much into it.

I shift into the closest thing I have to leader mode, which feels like wearing someone else’s jacket that’s two sizes too small.

“As I discussed in our chats after we matched, my Guild has set up full accommodations for all of you,” I say. “Rooms, food, medical access, training space, security, whatever you need. If you join my Party, Harvest will provide equipment, support, research, transportation logistics, legal cover, and anything else I forgot to mention.” Jerome had given me the official pitch with all details and benefits outlined. I forgot most of it, but am sure this is the gist of what he had laid out for me before this meeting.

Raul nods solemnly. “A prepared Guild is a strong Guild. I expect nothing less from someone as strong as yourself!”

I continue. “We all know time is moving fast. Phase Two is already underway.  I don’t want anyone joining blind. I don’t want anyone here because they watched a video of me punching something and thought that meant I know what I’m doing.”

Raul opens his mouth.

I point at him. “That includes you, my beautifully dressed friend.”

He closes his mouth, then nods.

“I’ve already got two Party members,” I say. “Charlotte Greenbauer. Level 32 Gourmand. And Jelly Boy, a Level 35 slime. Both a very versatile spellcasters.”

Raul whispers, “Magnificent.”

“Anyway,” I say. “This session is about getting to know each other. Capabilities. Weaknesses. How we think. How we fight. How we might work together as a Party. If after today, we all think it’s a good fit, we’ll join together as a Party. Then, we get to work.”

I take a breath and look at the three people standing in front of me.

A Spirit Commander from South Korea.

An Architect from San Francisco.

And a Brainbarian from Mexico.

“All right,” I say. “Let’s start simple.”

I look at Raul, then Ivy, then Sang-hyeok.

“What can you do?”

By the end of the day, I am in my full wizard gear: jorts, hat, boots, cape, and fanny pack of holding.  I am also covered in a thick layer of sweat. My hair is damp under my Behemoth Cap. The cape hangs dramatically from my naked shoulders.

Across from me stand my three official new Party members. No longer [Matches] or candidates. Full-fledged Party members.

After I had a chance to test each of their capabilities, I happily made the offer for each to stay as official members of my Party. All accepted.

Ivy accepted with a little too much anxious enthusiasm, as if she was worried the invitation would vanish if she didn’t mentally slap the yes button fast enough. Raul accepted like I had just asked him to co-star in the greatest action movie ever filmed. I’m pretty sure he almost hugged me hard enough to trigger my [Bludgeoning Resistance]. Sang-hyeok had stared at the Party invitation for several quiet seconds, then muttered something about limited options and survival before accepting the System-generated Party invitation.

The day had gone extremely well. All three of them are good.

Sang-hyeok can summon spirits. Various types, apparently, though he was careful with how much he showed. His most common summons are vaguely human-like mobs, translucent figures with smudged faces and long limbs, each one made of pale smoke and half-remembered bone. They move wrong. Like zombies do in a lot of movies.

During his demonstration, six of them had stepped out of the air around him. They surrounded him in a loose formation, heads tilted toward invisible sounds. When the training room generated another automaton, the mannequin-like machine emerging from the floor itself, the summoned spirits swarmed the training dummy. Not fast, exactly. But relentless. They grabbed limbs, blocked movement. And pulled its attention away from Sang-hyeok. One wrapped itself around a construct’s head while another latched onto its legs. A third simply stood in front of an incoming strike from the dummy and got obliterated, only for another to rise from the fading smoke seconds later.

When I examined these lower-leveled mob spirits, a System message had appeared in my HUD.

Monster Identified: Gui (Friendly, Summoned Spirit)

Crowd control. Aggro draw. Disposable bodies. Which sounds terrible when phrased that way, but the System is a terrible thing, and sometimes terrible math keeps living people alive. Sang-hyeok had watched the whole time with his hands in his coat pockets, expression flat.

When I asked if it bothered him when they got destroyed, he looked at me for a long moment and said, “They’re already dead. Who cares.” A pause. “But yes. A little.”

Sang-hyeok explained he also had access to Spells and Skills that affected his Summoned Spirits. He had also provided brief demonstrations of several of his stronger, higher leveled Spirits. I was impressed. This guy’s Class is a dream for the child in me who played countless hours of Pokemon and other monster tamer games. One such Spirit was called Taotie. It’s an eight foot tall frog-like creature with a mouth too wide for its face, filled with sword-like teeth. Above its mouth, covering the rest of its face is a red oni mask. Its belly is translucent, full of a white cloud-like substance.

“What does it do?” I had asked Sang-hyeok.

“Devour things.”

I had simply nodded at that.

“Nice,” said Raul.

Ivy is impressive in her own right. Her Skills let her build constructs and structures.

Nothing mobile. She made that clear right away, like she was apologizing for not being able to conjure murder robots on demand. I reassured her that it was alright, and probably for the best as she won’t be duplicative of Sang-hyeok’s role in the Party.

She built walls first. Simple ones. Knee-high barriers. Then chest-high cover. Then angled plates that shimmered with translucent gold lines, each one snapping into place with a sound like stone blocks being stacked by an invisible giant with OCD. Then bridges. Then stairs. Then a narrow tower rising straight out of the floor paneling, assembling itself layer by layer in seconds. The tower had looked like a cross between a siege engine and an unfinished apartment building made of blue-gray stone, with golden blueprint lines crawling across its surface.

Then the cannon unfolded at the top. That got everyone’s attention. Even Sang-hyeok raised an eyebrow. The cannon wasn’t metal. It was structural magic—braced rings, geometric supports, a barrel made of interlocking hexagonal plates. Ivy stood at the base, one hand raised, green eyes narrowed in concentration.

Then the cannon fired. A beam of magic punched across the training room and vaporized three training dummies, one after another.

The recoil made the tower groan.

Ivy winced like the structure’s discomfort was emotionally personal.

“That needs reinforcement,” she said apologetically.

I said, “That was awesome.”

She said, “It was under-braced.”

“That was awesome and under-braced.”

She seemed willing to accept that.

Tactical optionality is the phrase that came to my mind observing Ivy’s Skills and Spells.

Cover. Elevation. Barricades. Choke points. Firing platforms. Emergency walls. Traps. A literal magic cannon if we can keep enemies in the kill lane long enough. Ivy can shape the battlefield, and aid in exploration. In a team fight, she’ll be a huge asset.

Then there’s Raul.

Raul is exactly what I expected from a Barbarian, except also not at all.

Physical brute? Yes. Absolutely.

The man moves like someone taught a brick wall enthusiasm. He hits hard, takes hits well, and smiles the whole time like violence is a beloved family recipe.

But he’s not a barbarian. He’s a Brainbarian.

His Skills scale off Intelligence. Which is deeply unfair and also extremely funny. Who can relate better than a wizard who scales off his Strength?

He has battlefield analysis Skills. Enemy assessment Skills. Something he called [Cranial War Map], which briefly projected a floating tactical overlay around his head showing angles, predicted movement paths, weak points, and what I’m pretty sure was a tiny glowing diagram of Ivy’s tower and how to dismantle it in a single strike. He also has high psychic and mental resistances, which immediately makes him valuable because every major magical disaster seems to include at least one asshole trying to invade someone’s skull.

But the big thing? Raul can temporarily drain his Intelligence to enhance his Strength and Dexterity. The Trait is called [Brainless Brilliance].

I thought he was joking. He was not joking.

We decided to test. There was no universe where we didn’t eventually decide to answer the ancient philosophical question: what happens when a Muscle Mage grapples a Brainbarian in a tan suit? The answer is property damage and nudity-adjacent wardrobe failure.

Raul stood across from me, rolling his shoulders.  “You are certain, my friend?” he asks.

“Absolutely not,” I say. “Do it.”

His grin widens. Then his eyes change. The hazel goes hard and bright, then fades to white, like all the calculation in his head compresses into one diamond point. The light had drained from his once intelligent gleam, to a bestial dullness.

My [Perception] screamed as Raul cleared the space between us in a sonic boom. The floor cracked under his first step. The sound hit after his body did. Our hands met in a grapple at chest height. The impact was monstrous. A circular shockwave blew dust and loose debris away from us. Ivy yelped from somewhere off to my right.

Raul’s strength hit me like a dump truck with abs. I have to draw on a large amount of my Strength immediately. My muscles lock. He pushes, and my shoulders rock back half an inch. I pushed back, generating another massive shockwave. This one was strong enough to rip our clothes off our bodies, the shreds of fabric exploding into the air. We had remained locked in a naked test of might. But only for a fraction of a second, as pixelated light formed around our bodies, replacing our nakedness with our full suites of System equipment. For Raul, this meant something that looked like a brown fur kilt and golden neck and arm rings, as well as leather bracers on his forearms.

“Ha!” Raul bellowed.

The grapple continued. Raul pushed harder. I pushed back. The floor beneath us groaned. For several seconds, we were locked there, two idiots converting physical might into tectonic activity.

His Dexterity boost made him a tricky grappler. He shifted angles fast, trying to break my wrist position, then my elbow, then drove his shoulder under mine. There’s real technique under the brute force. Real combat sense under the drooling, frothing mouth and dead eyes of the man turned brute force. I assume he hadn’t yet drained all of his Intelligence.

I almost lost the grip twice. My Stamina bar was dropping quickly in the bottom corner of my vision. I dug deeper. Strength flooded through me. I stepped forward. Raul’s grin widened into a mad smile, accompanied by a happy snarl. I took another step. His boots scraped backward. One inch. Then another. Then I twisted, dropped my center, and used the tiny shift in his balance to lever him sideways.

Raul hit the floor on one knee. The impact cracked another panel of the training room floor. I pressed down. He resisted for one glorious second. Then his other knee hit the ground, and I was able to feel him relent.

The grapple ended. I had won. Barely, I think, stepping away from the panting half-mad man. For a heartbeat, the room had remained silent.

Then Raul threw his head back and laughed a big, booming, joyous laugh.

“My friend! Magnificent!”

He surged up and grabbed me in a hug that would pulp a mailbox. I had noticed the hazel color return to his eyes in that moment. I patted his back because I was too winded to do anything else.

“Jesus,” I had said to the man. “You’re strong.”

He pulled back, still laughing, eyes a little less sharp than before but otherwise back to normal.

“I am already duller from that exchange,” he said cheerfully, tapping the side of his temple. “But I’ll be back to full brain might soon!”

Now, hours later, we stand in the aftermath of everyone’s demonstrations, including some displays of my own, even if the three generally knew what I am capable of.

Sang-hyeok is near the wall, his spirits gone but a faint chill lingering around him. Ivy is sitting on a conjured waist-high platform, elbows on knees, looking tired and pleased with her performance. Raul stands with arms crossed, still shirtless in System combat gear, mustache wet with sweat, smiling like he just had the best day of his life.

My Party interface hovers in front of me.

Current Party Count: 6

Joseph Sullivan (Muscle Mage)

Jelly Boy (Arcane Juggler) [Ally]

Charlotte Greenbauer (Gourmand)

Lim Sang-hyeok (Spirit Commander)

Ivy Madison (Architect)

Raul (Brainbarian)

I grin, looking back up to the three through the translucent window of the System message.

“Welcome to Harvest,” I say. “Welcome to the Party!”

Jerome and Charlotte meet the Party in the training facility a little less than an hour later. Charlotte arrives first. She walks in carrying a fresh cup of tea in one hand and a little paper plate stacked with cookies in the other.

She approaches the others with a warm smile, all freckles and calm confidence.

“So,” Charlotte says, looking between Ivy, Raul, and Sang-hyeok. “I hear we’re officially working together. I’m Charlotte.”

Raul laughs. “The Gourmand! Pleasure to meet you. My name is Raul.”

Charlotte’s eyebrows knit together for a moment, before melting away to a the warmth of understanding as I can tell her System just handled translation of Raul’s speech. “Yup! These cookies here are tactical, so watch out.”

Ivy gives a nervous half-smile. “Ivy. Ivy Madison.”

Sang-hyeok mutters something to Charlotte that I don’t catch.

Charlotte pauses. Then she nods. “Fair.”

Raul claps Sang-hyeok on the shoulder laughing. Sang-hyeok seems uncomfortable with the contact, but doesn’t move or say anything. He just slightly stiffens.

Jerome enters the room at that moment and approaches me while Charlotte begins the social portion of our new-party disaster.

“There is catering in the third-floor conference room,” he says to the group, greeting them all with a wave.

I look past him at Raul, who is now listening with profound seriousness as Charlotte explains that Funfetti can be combat-relevant under specific circumstances.

“Good,” I say. “I’m starving.” I step away from the group, giving Jerome and myself a little more privacy for our conversation.

Jerome glances at the others before lowering his voice slightly.

“We have also had some success finding Gates for acquisition.”

That gets my full attention.

“How much success?”

“Enough that we should have one ready soon for use. Perhaps even by the end of the week.”

My stomach tightens. Is that fear? No. More like my body recognizes the start of a thing. The edge of the diving board before taking the plunge into a pool. The moment before the first rep when the bar is still on the rack and every bad decision you made leading here is sitting across your traps.

“What kind of Gate?” I ask. A day prior, Jerome, Labonte and I discussed how we would go about accomplishing the tasks required by Stage Two of the God Game once I formed a Party. The Quest required obtaining a Divine Tear—whatever that is—from a Gate generated by a Silver or a Gold Gate Ticket. Jerome’s intelligence gathering informed him that Silver and Gold Gate Tickets are non-transferable items that can be generated as Quest Rewards while clearing high-leveled randomly generated Gates. Quests were generated by those first entering a new Gate. This, of course, placed a higher premium on high level Gates, as any Guild with Parties still participating in the God Game will want to maximize their chances of obtaining a Silver or Gold Ticket.

“We are still confirming details,” Jerome says.

“That sounds like lawyer for ‘dangerous and expensive.’”

“Most things are dangerous and expensive.” He gives me a look.

“Fair enough.”

Jerome taps his tablet, and a faint blue reflection moves across his glasses. He glances up at the others. “Will this be your entire Party?”

I look at the group.

Charlotte. Sang-hyeok. Ivy. Raul.

With me and Jelly Boy, that makes six. The minimum requirement to proceed with the next Phase of the God Game.

“Probably.”

Jerome’s expression remains perfectly neutral.

“There’s one more potential addition I’m waiting to get back to me,” I add.

Jerome’s eyebrow lifts.

“Not through [Party Search]?”

“No.”

“Participant? Or Ally?”

“Participant.”

I reach into my fanny pack of holding. I pull out my phone and look down. One unread message. I unlock my screen and it’s there. A response.

My chest tightens.

Then I read it.

A smile forms on my face before I can stop it.

Jerome notices.

“Good news?” he asks.

I turn the phone off and slide it back into the fanny pack.

“Yeah,” I say. My smile stays glued onto my face. “Good news.”
