Grizzle slid into the water, not even causing a ripple. Shy followed him, a little slower. He took off swimming in a direction different from what I’d expected, but I trusted him.

Keeping pace with him wasn’t hard, and I glided through the water almost like I had been swimming for my entire life. Which just wasn’t true.

Still, I’d take it. Especially as I tracked Strange's location via our bond. Finally, I gave up and just used his radar to figure out where he was. They were near the waterfall, but hadn’t exited the tunnel yet.

We cut through the water and didn’t stop. A few other creatures registered on my radar, but all fled when we moved in their direction. My chest didn’t even hurt this time, and I didn’t feel pressed for air at all.

The group of Clan Vielether, with Strange, moved just as they appeared on the edges of my radar.

Grizzle suddenly swam up and disappeared out of the water, Shy following.

I almost didn’t even notice the pool on the roof. The edges of it shimmered in the darkness, and I swam up and broke the surface before pulling myself onto the edge of the stone.

The dungeon archway had vanished, but this was definitely the cavern. I recognized the rocky walls.

“Thanks,” I said, as I rushed to the tunnel entrance and around the corner, leaving Grizzle and Shy behind.

Smoky air drifted into the tunnel, lingering even as daylight tried to blind me.

I stuttered to a halt at the sight of the grasslands. Or rather, what remained of them.

Blackened earth was all that was left, with smoke drifting up in lazy spires. The once-massive trees in the far distance appeared like someone had dipped them in black chocolate.

A breeze suddenly swept through the air, causing even more of the soot to spread across everything. It almost blacked out the sun. Meanwhile, the dots of the three Clan Vielether shuddered to a stop in the distance.

Close to the forest, I guessed, based on the distance.

As soon as the dust settled down, I leaped down the rock, bypassing the trail entirely, before landing in the burnt remains of grass. Black soot puffed up as I landed, coating my damp skin and clothing from my dip in the pond. It formed a weird, thin layer of paste, which dried fast, and hardened.

Thinking fast, I flexed, stretching up and making several sections crack, but that was all.

Triggering my mana shield, I used that to push away from my skin, and entire chunks came off. From there, I brushed it off as fast as I could. It didn’t take any of my skin off with it, but the thought that it might creeped me out enough that a full body shiver ran down my spine.

Once I was clean, I took off again. Dust rose behind me as I sprinted through the grasslands. Thankfully, none of it stayed on me, though it made my eyes water. Shaking my head and giving in, my form shifted, turning as dark as the soot.

My speed increased, and I reached the stick-trees in record time. I barely paid any attention to the tall jagged spires. All the leaves had burned away from the sun. Yet, even as I traveled through the forest, things changed. Some trunks grew wider.

This was such a weird place.

The bond stretched even higher. They had to be at the cliff already.

I needed to catch up before they reached the portal. The gift from Nessie meant that I wouldn’t go wherever it normally went; I’d end up back at Lakeside Landing. It’d helped in getting here, but now…

I couldn’t even think of it.

Instead, I focused on putting one foot in front of the other, and reaching the cliff as fast as I could.

A massive roar split through the air in the distance, and I hoped it’d be something that would slow them down. A small part of me knew it wouldn’t, but I hoped.

The black dust floated in a line behind me as I rushed forward. Several trees had toppled, and I jumped over them with ease. Two creatures appeared on my map, more of those spider-tree things from before. One of them froze, partially out of the ground, as soon as it sensed me. The other plopped back down, like I wouldn’t notice.

I didn’t have time for that as I pushed myself. The ground sped by, until the trees started to thin. At the same time, nibs formed along the ground, much like when the cold had lessened and the light had first started to dawn.

Ferns burst forth as I ran, making my path more complicated as I had to shift and avoid the quick-growing plants. I practically floated as I dashed across the thinning forest, which made my thoughts break for a second.

I pulled up the description for my new skill Metamorphic Assimilation. One manifestation made me shiver in delight: Flash Wings (Apochas). Wings. It said I had wings.

As I crossed the treeline, I resisted triggering it. Right now, speed mattered, and I didn’t know how long it’d take me to learn how to fly. Up until now, all of the adjustments to my body were on limbs I already knew how to use.

The path up the side of the cliff switched back several times. That wouldn’t work. Already, they had gained more distance than I was comfortable with. I leaped, aiming for about a quarter up the side of the cliff.

To my shock, I flew past the point I wanted, and barely caught myself on the trail closer to the halfway point. I didn’t wait, though, and leaped again.

More roars filled the air, and I peeked in with Strange. He remained in the backpack, comfortable as could be.

I landed at the top of the cliff face, and rolled forward as a gust of wind pushed at me. More craggy spires covered the landscape, but in the distance, three figures sped away from me in the air.

Catching up like this wasn’t gonna happen.

Without thinking too hard, I triggered the Apochas manifestation.

A pulling sensation at my shoulder blades made me shiver, as my height reduced, losing the added bulk the Shadowstalker form normally gave me. Instead, that pulled into long extensions from my shoulder blades.

I tried to twist to see them, but I couldn’t.

My center of gravity shifted in a weird way, and something coated my eyes. I blinked several times, and the dust cleared.

Up!

Nothing happened.

Mentally, I tried to trigger the wings to work, watching the dots in the distance move farther away. Then I jumped in the air. As soon as I left the ground, a buzzing sound started behind me, and the pull on my shoulders increased.

The sensation made me nauseous for a few moments, before it settled. Unfortunately, I had no idea how to direct where I wanted to go. A rocky crag loomed in front of me, and I skidded across the side of it.

My armor tanked the hit, but the noise echoed through the air.

Something large took a few steps in the distance, but I ignored it as I tried to turn away from the next rocky structure. This time, if I failed, it wouldn’t be a skid.

I reached out as if to grab the rocky spire. As soon as I braced myself, my wings stopped buzzing. I pulled myself up to the top of the rock.

The dots in the distance were too far away. I wasn’t going to reach them in time.

My eyes itched as they sped away before aiming back toward the ground.

I leaned my head back and stared at the sky, ignoring my itchy eyes. Then, I blamed the dust, as they watered even more.

The bond between Strange and I flickered for a moment as he went through the portal.

Why did he go with them? Why was he happy to go with them?

I stood like that for several moments.

The heavy footsteps headed in my direction, but I didn’t care.

My stomach grumbled slightly, and I needed to head back eventually.

Grizzle and Shy would need to come this way to the portal, to go back to Lakeside Landing. I had agreed to help them leave.

I’d stick by that.

I cracked my eyes open as an enormous creature loomed into view between two pillars.

[Toxic T-Rex, Poisonous coating, Level 210, Predator, Unknown.]

Two eyes stared at me as it roared in my direction. Its massive teeth filled its mouth, though a few were broken off. Scars covered its neck like something had wrapped around it multiple times, but didn’t finish the job.

Rock Breakers were my bet.

Still, it didn’t move. Instead, it stared, waiting for something to happen after its roar.

The last time I’d faced a creature like this was the Giganotosaurous, and it’d helped my stealth. Still, I felt no drive to attack this creature.

Its mouth opened again, but this time it didn’t roar. Instead, it turned sideways a little to eye me better with an eye. “Not breaker.”

The voice echoed around the area.

“No, I am not,” I replied, seeing no problem with talking with it.

“Breakers riled up from buzzing creatures,” It added.

“They were not my friends, I hoped to stop them.” The words slipped out without my meaning for them too.

It shook its head. “Too late.”

“I know,” I said. I let out a sigh. “I’ll be coming back through here with some friends, heading for the portal.”

Its gaze increased. “You leave?”

“That’s the goal, to get my friends and I off this planet,” I said, seeing no reason to lie.

“How?” it asked, leaning closer. “Portal locked.”

“The portals are only locked for those that…” I trailed off. ‘Aren’t citizens’ had echoed in my head. But this T-Rex had already blown past level 200. “Aren’t citizen’s,” I finally said, deciding to go with it.

Its head tilted, then its eyes fluttered for a moment. Everything near it froze.

I triggered my second sight, and found the T-Rex's head completely covered with mana. It flowed as threads from the sky and touched it in various places.

Then it shook its head, the threads vanishing.

“Oh…” It turned away. “Citizen.”

“Maybe you can figure out what you need to do,” I added, wondering what that had been.

“Quest given,” It said with a shuffle. “Hard one… I think.”

“Good luck.” I turned back to face the cliff edge that I had jumped up. Now would be a great time to get some flying practice in.

With another sigh, I jumped off the rock and into the air. Thankfully, my wings buzzed as soon as my feet left the ground. Now, to figure out how to steer, to get where I wanted to go.

The higher I went, the hotter the air became, and I tried to let my body relax. That didn’t work, so I leaned in the direction I wanted to go.

I lowered every so lightly, then experimented with leaning different ways.

I got this… right?
