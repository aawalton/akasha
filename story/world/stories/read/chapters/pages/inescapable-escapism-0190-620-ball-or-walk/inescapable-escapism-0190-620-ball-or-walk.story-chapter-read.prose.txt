“Are you sure you don’t want me to walk you home?” Duncan asked, his voice soft and his face centimetres from mine.

I couldn’t stop myself from stretching up onto my tiptoes and pressing my lips to his before replying.

“No, it’s okay. I’ll be fine.”

Duncan still didn’t look particularly comfortable, but I kissed him again, hoping to distract him. As much as I did want to steal another few minutes with him, I also knew that I needed to be alone for a little bit before I got home. I had to give myself some time to stop grinning like a fool. Otherwise, my mom would immediately guess something was up, and I didn’t want that to happen.

“Okay,” Duncan said reluctantly. “But text me when you get home?”

“I will,” I told him.

Duncan’s lips found mine again, and I let my eyes flutter shut as I leaned into the kiss.

“And I’ll see you on Wednesday?” he asked once he’d pulled away, his voice slightly breathless.

“Yeah,” I replied.

I bit my lip before kissing him one last time and turning towards the front door. Pulling it open, I glanced back at Duncan, and the urge to kiss him again pulled at me, but before I could act upon it, his dad appeared in the hallway behind him.

“Oh, are you heading off now, Grace?” he called as he walked towards us.

“Umm, yeah. I told my mom I’d only be a few hours,” I replied, immediately feeling awkward.

“Ahh, would you like a lift home?”

“No, no, it’s okay,” I said before quickly adding, “But thank you.”

“Are you sure? I don’t mind. I assume you still live over on Main Street?”

I was a little surprised that he’d remembered after so long, but I tried to keep that from my face.

“I do, but honestly, it’s okay. It’s only a quick walk.”

Duncan’s dad nodded.

“Okay. Do you want Duncan to walk you home?” he asked, resting a hand on Duncan’s shoulder. “I’m sure he’s already offered, but—”

“I did,” Duncan told him. “Grace said she’s fine.”

His dad paused for a moment, glancing at his son before looking back at me.

“Alright. Well, it was great to see you again, Grace.”

“You too,” I said, trying to ignore how hot my face felt. “Ummm… see you soon, Duncan.”

“Bye,” he said, shooting me an apologetic look.

I smiled at him before sending an awkward smile towards his dad and walking away. Duncan’s family was just so nice, and I didn’t quite know how to respond to that. I think that’s why I felt so uncomfortable and out of place when his dad offered to drive me home. I always did when people went out of their way to do things for me, and I always felt like my reaction was wrong. I was either being too grateful or not grateful enough. I couldn’t quite find a balance.

But I did like Duncan. I’d had such a nice time at his house, and I felt almost like I was floating as I walked home. My body felt too light, like it wasn’t fully held down by gravity, and I could take to the skies at any moment.

That sounded kind of nice, though. Part of me longed to leave the earth behind and let my body float away, to be untethered to the ground. I’d dance from side to side with the breeze, entirely at the mercy of the wind as I explored the world from above.

Actually, I realised, that sounded terrifying. That wasn’t what I wanted at all. It wasn’t how I felt. I felt more like… I could break into dance at any moment. Like I could be swept into a waltz or a tango, ignoring the fact that I knew neither dance.

My feet started to move on their own accord, my body swaying rhythmically as music swelled in my head. My arms began to rise. I started to take a step to the side before a bramble caught my ankle, and I crashed down to reality again.

Embarrassment slammed into me, and I looked around quickly. Luckily, I’d made it halfway along the shaded path before getting entranced by my own thoughts. Duncan’s house was almost out of sight, and thankfully, there was no chance anyone could have seen me from there.

That realisation filled me with such relief that my knees felt weak as I bent down to unhook the thorn from my skin. It would have been absolutely awful if Duncan or either of his parents had seen me. They would have thought I’d lost my mind, and Duncan would probably never want to see me again. His parents were fairly relaxed, but I assumed they’d probably ban him from hanging out with me. I’d never be able to go to his house and make out with him all day again, and the thought of that made my heart sink.

Despite the kind of embarrassing end to it, I’d had such a nice time with him. With my boyfriend. That’s what he was, and I still couldn’t quite believe it. It was what I’d been hoping would happen, what I hoped he was, but I didn’t think that was how it would go. I assumed he’d tell me he didn’t feel the same way or that he wasn’t sure, but instead, he wanted me. He asked me to be his girlfriend. I no longer needed to wonder what we were. I knew.

As I turned onto the main road that cut through the village, the smile started to slip from my face. I could feel my steps starting to quicken as anxiety edged into my heart, and I reached into my pocket for my phone.

There were still no messages from my mom. I’d been keeping an eye on it all day. I’d even switched my phone off silent to make sure I didn’t miss it if she texted me, but she hadn’t, and I wasn’t sure what to make of that. It worried me, though. I expected her to text me about Rex at least once, and the fact that she hadn’t concerned me. It made me worry that something had happened, something bad enough that she wanted to wait until I got home to break the news to me.

Perhaps she just wanted to see the look on my face when she told me she’d taken him back to the shelter because I was neglecting him. I’d left him alone with Mom whilst I’d gone out, gallivanting around the village. I should have done better. I should have taken him with me, but now, it was too late.

By the time I reached the bottom of my drive, I was almost at a run. My heart was racing, and I could feel a layer of sweat slicking my palms as I forced myself to slow. It was painful. It felt wrong, but I knew I had to do it.

If Mom was looking out the window and waiting for me to arrive, I needed to appear calm. Otherwise, I might just make things worse. There was a chance, a small, minuscule chance, that nothing bad had happened, and if Mom saw me sprinting towards the house, she’d see it as a sign that I didn’t trust her. She would assume that my anxiety was because I didn’t believe she could cope with looking after Rex, that I didn’t think she was capable of it. That was what she accused me of before I’d gone out, and it wasn’t that I doubted her, it was just…

My hands were trembling so badly as I fished my keys out of my bag that I struggled to get them into the lock. It took me multiple attempts to actually slot them into place, but finally, I managed it. I opened the door as quickly as I could, throwing it open and stepping inside.

And I was met with a disconcerting quiet. There was some noise coming from the lounge, but the volume was so low that it took me a few seconds to actually notice it, and it just caused my anxiety to spike. Mom never listened to the television that quietly. Not unless she was listening out for me, waiting for me to get home.

I slipped further into the house, padding towards the lounge door. I peered inside, my heart in my throat, but my eyes immediately found Rex. He was curled up, fast asleep on the armchair my dad normally sat in, and my mom was seated in her usual spot on the sofa. They were both there, and they seemed okay.

Rex moved suddenly. Somehow, despite being asleep, he’d noticed my arrival. His head snapped up, his eyes finding me, and he leapt from the chair. I bent down, kneeling as I stroked his head before throwing my arms around him.

“Hello, Rexie. I missed you,” I told him before pulling back, my eyes finding my mom. “Hi, Mom.”

She looked away from the television slowly, her expression somewhat bored, as if she’d already grown tired of our conversation before it had even begun.

“Hello. How was… Phoebe’s?” she asked.

The hesitation before she’d said Phoebe’s name made me think she’d forgotten where I’d said I was going, and I couldn’t work out whether it was just an act or if she actually paid that little attention to what I said.

“Yeah, it was great!” I replied.

Mom’s eyebrows rose, and I realised half a second too late that I’d sounded too happy. I’d made her suspicious, and I needed to dial it back.

“Oh really?” she asked. “What was so great about it?”

I felt my smile falter, and I hurried to respond.

“Nothing really. I mean… it was just good to see her again because she was away all summer, you know?”

Mom sighed and looked away, her eyes returning to the television.

“If you say so. What did you get up to?”

The brief spark of hope that I’d felt when Mom had stopped looking at me died and was immediately replaced by anxiety. For a second, I’d thought that was the end of it. I’d hoped that she’d believed my response, but it was clear she hadn’t. I needed to be careful, to convince her that I’d been at Phoebe’s and not anywhere else.

“We just watched a movie,” I said. “We were going to go to the cinema, but we decided not to in the end. There was a movie Phoebe wanted to watch on Netflix, so we just stayed in and watched that.”

“Oh? Which movie?” Mom quizzed, glancing at me again.

I was glad Rex was still sitting in front of me. Petting him gave me an excuse not to meet Mom’s eye, and that made it easier to lie to her. I felt less anxiety as I kept my expression carefully neutral and let the words pour out smoothly.

“Some horror thing Phoebe found a while back. It was called something like Beneath Her Lies, I think?”

Mom’s eyebrows furrowed.

“You don’t know the title of the movie you just watched?” she asked.

Her tone was incredulous, but I just shrugged, pretending to be unfazed.

“I’m pretty sure that was it,” I lied. I couldn’t remember whether Phoebe ever actually mentioned the movie she’d watched this morning, but I was certain that was the name of the movie in the snippet of the other trailer she’d sent me by the same director. “It was pretty good, though.”

“Oh?” Mom asked.

She still seemed somewhat suspicious of me, but I was too well practised. I had far too much experience with hiding something from her, and I knew what worked best to make her stop caring about what I was saying.

“Yeah! There was this wild twist at the end that I didn’t see coming at all,” I said, injecting more enthusiasm into my voice. “One of the main characters had been replaced by another like halfway through the movie, but I had no clue right up until the reveal!”

“You didn’t notice the character… changing?” Mom asked, but her eyes had already begun to flit back to the television.

I ducked my head to hide my expression, pretending to just be focusing on petting Rex, as a smile pulled at my lips. It was working. Mom always got bored quickly if I was talking about something I really enjoyed or was clearly passionate about, so I just needed to do more. I needed to seem even more into the film, and she’d start ignoring me again.

That wouldn’t be too hard. Phoebe had told me so much about it, so I definitely knew enough to blag it to my mom. I doubted she would look up the details later. She rarely did, and I knew she didn’t like horror movies, so as long as I played that up, it was less likely that she would.

“No!” I cried. “The actress didn’t change because the girl who replaced her skinned her and was wearing her face, so I had no clue!”

Mom’s expression was disgusted as she glanced at me again.

“That seems… unrealistic.”

“Right? But then there was this flashback where they actually showed it happening, and it was so gory and realistic-looking. Like, there was this bit where the girl had to use a bone saw, and—” I broke off, shuddering. “It was so gross.”

I laughed, but Mom did not join in. I’d been expecting that, though. She never really did, and I was a little worried that I was overdoing it, but Mom hadn’t noticed. A flare of triumph burst in my heart as she spoke without even looking away from the television.

“Right.”

That was all she said, and I was glad she didn’t so much as glance in my direction, because I couldn’t hide the grin that appeared on my lips. She had acted exactly as I’d expected her to.

“How’s it been here?” I asked after a pause. “Has Rex been okay?”

Rex’s expression appeared almost offended at my question, but his tail continued to wag. I pressed a kiss to his forehead in apology, and he allowed it before quickly licking my cheek as I started to pull away.

“Fine,” Mom said, actually glancing away from the television for a second to look at Rex. “His last owners may have been completely useless, but at least they trained him well.”

I didn’t know how to respond to that. It didn’t really seem to warrant a response, but I knew I had to say something. Mom would be annoyed if I didn’t. She’d accuse me of being rude.

“Yeah…” was the best I could do. I trailed off, hesitating for a moment before looking at Rex and speaking again. “Well, I’m going to take Rex to the toilet, and then we’ll go for a walk.”

Rex was on his feet immediately, clearly excited about the prospect of going for a walk.

“Whilst you’re out there, make sure to pick up the poo from earlier,” Mom told me.

I’d started to stand before she spoke, but at that, I paused and looked at her.

“There isn’t any out there. He didn’t poop when I took him out this morning.”

Mom looked at me as if I’d said something stupid.

“He did when I took him out whilst you were at Phoebe’s,” she told me before sighing.

“Oh, okay.”

Mom muttered something under her breath as I finished straightening up and turned towards my lounge, but I didn’t catch it. That was probably a good thing, though. I assume it was probably something insulting, but I didn’t really care. Mom had bought my lie, and Rex seemed fine. It seemed as though Mom had done a pretty good job of looking after him. She’d even taken him outside, and I’d hoped she would, but it was good to know.

“Go toilet quickly, and then we’ll go for a walk. Okay, boy?” I asked Rex as we reached the French windows.

I unlocked them, sliding the door back before grabbing one of the bags I’d left nearby and following Rex into the garden. My eyes scoured the ground as I walked, searching for the poop that Mom had mentioned, before remembering something. I needed to text Duncan. I’d told him I would when I got home, but I’d gotten distracted by Mom, so I hadn’t been able to.

Glancing over my shoulder, I scanned the windows to make sure Mom wasn’t watching me. I half-expected her to be there, making sure that I was actually doing as she’d said, but I couldn’t see her. I slipped my phone out of my pocket as I spotted the poop, sending a quick message to Duncan before replacing it and bending down.

Rex trotted over as I stood again, tying the now-filled bag. A ball was held in his mouth, and he stared at me.

“One sec, Rex,” I told him. “I need to put this in the bin and wash my hands first.”

He continued to look at me expectantly as I started to walk towards the wheelie bin near the back gate before turning away. I dropped the bag inside, glancing at Rex quickly before going back into the house.

My phone chimed loudly as I turned the tap on, and I flinched at the unexpected sound. I’d completely forgotten that I’d turned it off silent earlier, and I washed my hands as quickly as I could, whilst also glancing over my shoulder to make sure Mom wasn’t coming in to ask me who I was texting. Thankfully, my phone didn’t make any more noise before I was able to silence it again, and I scanned Duncan’s response before returning to the garden.

By the time I got back, Rex had dropped the ball, but the moment he saw me again, he ran towards it. I did the same, lunging for the ball, but he got there first. It had been closer to me, but somehow, he’d managed to cross the garden before my hand had closed around it.

He pulled back, his tail wagging playfully as he held it in his mouth, and I laughed.

“Good boy!” I told him. “But you’re going to have to drop it if you want me to throw it.”

Rex seemed to consider that for a moment before reluctantly letting go of the tennis ball and stepping back. A hint of anxiety pulled at me as I reached for the ball. For a split second, I was worried he’d change his mind and snap at me, his sharp teeth sinking into my hand, but he didn’t.

I lobbed the ball as hard as I could towards the back of the garden, and Rex bolted after it. He leapt into the air, catching it before it could even hit the ground, and I laughed in surprise. Had his past owners taught him to do that? Or was catching a ball in midair just something a dog could innately do? I had no clue, but it was impressive and worrying how high he’d managed to jump.

Rex ran back to me, dropping the ball at my feet again, and staring up at me until I threw it for him again. My throw had been slightly weaker that time, and Rex let it hit the grass and bounce before pouncing on it.

“Good boy,” I told him as he returned the ball again. “Do you want to keep playing fetch or go for a walk now?”

I wasn’t sure whether he’d really understand my question, but it had slipped out before I could stop it. Rex stared at me, the ball at my feet, and his expression unchanged as he sat. I wasn’t sure if he was just staring at me for no reason or trying to figure out what I’d said, so I tried again.

“Ball?” I asked. “Or walk?”

Rex barked excitedly, getting to his feet again, and I laughed.

“Do you want me to throw it again?” I tried, but Rex did not respond. “No? So… you want to go for a walk?”

Once more, Rex barked. He trotted past me, moving towards the French windows before looking back.

“Okay, okay. I got it, boy,” I told him, reaching down to stroke his back as we went into the house again.

Rex didn’t wait for me to lock the windows again; he just continued walking through the room when I stopped, and I couldn’t help but smile. He seemed excited to go for a walk, and I was too. I’d always enjoyed walks. It was nice to get out of the house for a little while, but taking Rex made them even better. Plus, he meant I actually had an excuse to go that didn’t make Mom suspicious.

I pulled the curtains shut and turned away from the window, starting to follow Rex before confusion washed over me, and I came to a stop again. I stared down at the floor near the doorway, my eyebrows pulling together.

His bed was gone. I was certain that I’d left it right there, but it was gone. I looked around my lounge, wondering whether Rex had pulled it into a different spot for some reason, but it was nowhere to be seen. It couldn’t have just disappeared, though. He must have taken it somewhere, but I had no clue where.

Peering into the kitchen, where Rex was drinking loudly from his bowl, I scanned the room for his bed, but it wasn’t in there either. I turned towards Mom and Dad’s lounge, hesitating for a moment before walking towards it.

“Mom, do you know where Rex’s bed is?” I asked. “I thought I left it in the lounge, but…”

I trailed off as I spotted it in front of the fireplace, and my confusion grew stronger. I knew dogs loved sleeping in front of fires. That was a stereotype, but the fire wasn’t even lit. It was the middle of summer, and I had no idea how Rex had even managed to get it in there without Mom spotting him. Maybe he’d waited until she’d gone to the toilet or to get a drink or something? Or perhaps she’d been so distracted by her phone that he’d managed to sneak it in there without her noticing.

“Yes, it’s here,” Mom said.

My eyes flicked towards her, but she wasn’t looking at me. She was pointedly staring at the television, but I couldn’t look away as a realisation came to me. Rex hadn’t brought his bed in. He wasn’t even using it when I got home, but I was pretty sure Mom had done it. Perhaps she was trying to make him more comfortable or something. I wasn’t sure, but the thought made me smile.

She’d turned the television up too, I noticed. It was significantly louder than it had been when I’d first gotten home, and I felt like that was intentional. Had she had the volume low so as not to wake him up? That didn’t seem right. She’d never thought to do that with me. I’d been woken up by her shows more times than I could remember, but I couldn’t think of another reason as to why she would have done it.

It was kind of sweet, though. I hadn’t expected her to care so much, to try so hard, but the fact that she did made me smile, and suddenly, I knew it would be fine to leave Rex with her when I went to school.

Mom looked up, spotting me still standing in the doorway. A confused expression pulled at her face before being replaced by one of irritation.

“Did you wear your shoes through the house?” she demanded.

“Uhh… yeah?” I said, looking down at my feet.

I hadn’t even thought to take them off when I got home earlier. I’d been too distracted by the need to make sure Rex and Mom were okay, and it hadn’t even occurred to me.

“They’d better be clean. If you’ve traipsed mud into the carpet…” she trailed off threateningly.

“Don’t worry! They’re clean!” I told her as I turned away, my eyes scanning the floor.
