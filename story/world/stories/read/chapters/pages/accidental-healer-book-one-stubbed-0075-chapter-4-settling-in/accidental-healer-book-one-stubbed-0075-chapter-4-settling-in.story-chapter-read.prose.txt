I started at the beginning. Even before mana ingulfed Earth.

“How in the world does anyone forget to bring all their camping gear to go camping?” Mom’s voice was laced with concern, and a twinge of what I guessed was. Did I really raise this dork?

My poor mother. If she thought that was bad, she had better buckle up, because the ride on the dumb dumb train was just barely leaving the station.

Norso’s mouth hung open revealing half chewed cookie, mom shook her head, and dad just laughed.

“You’re telling me, you chose your healers class sarcastically?” Dad wheezed between laughing.

“How was I supposed to know the system couldn’t read subtext?” I defended weakly.

“It probably would’ve at least asked if you were sure if you didn’t get too excited during the tutorial.” Mom shrugged. “But you’ve always been that way. Do you remember nearly drowning during swimming lessons?”

I stuck my nose up indignantly. “You mean when I bravely lead the charge to the deep end like a hero?”

“I don’t see many heroes spluttering water and crying they will never get in a pool again.” She teased me.

“We still don’t know if I cried for sure. It could’ve been pool water. Besides, I did get in the pool again.”

“Two years later!” Dad pointed out laughing again. He wiped his eyes. “Alright, so now you’re a healer. What happened next?”

“Well...I left the tutorial with some highly questionable titles and no weapons because, again, the system has no sense of humor.”

My dad facepalmed again and Norso’s mouth still hung open.

I could only imagine what the dark elf might be thinking. He was familiar with the young man who cut his leader in half. I doubted my origin story was doing many favors in maintaining whatever mystique I may have gained.

“If you thought that was bad, only like five seconds after I was let free, Mischief showed up and literally tried to eat me.” I paused for dramatic effect before forging on. “Fortunately, I’ve always been a peak physical specimen, and I was easily able to fend him off with merely my bare hands.”

I held my hands out in front of myself.

“A mountain lion!” My mom exclaimed. “You fought a mountain lion unarmed?”

“Pretty much....”

Without warning, dad shot out of his seat, bumping the table causing cookie crumbs to scatter.

He spun around, scanning the small cabin. My hand reached my side ready to draw tranquility when I realized what was happening.

I rested my hand on dad’s forearm and urged him to sit back down, which he did reluctantly, eyes still searching the corners of the room.

“Don’t listen to that cat.” I said guessing it was Mischief. “He’s a master of manipulation. What did he tell you?”

“He said that you should tell the truth.” I could tell he was rattled.

“Oh, really?” I called out. “Who completed the first wave all on his own? Why was that again?”

“Because you got lucky. The devil is in the details, Layton. I respect you and them too much to allow misinformation.”

“Fine. I might’ve been a little lucky.” I conceded, then I folded completely. “The truth is, Mischief would’ve killed me had he not tackled me into the wave dungeon and landed on some kind of metal shard. It was really like a one in a million chance.”

My mom covered her mouth. “Was it really that close?”

“Like I said, I got lucky. More than once.”

I should’ve figured Mischief was prowling somewhere outside the small cabin. In fact, if I paid close enough attention I could feel the subtle pressure of his mana bias. It still felt proud and domineering, something that might unnerve others, but it was a comfort to me.

“After the wave was over, Mischief was injured. My first instinct was to put him out of his misery.” I smiled. “It would’ve been the worst mistake of my life.”

“You saved him? Even after he tried to kill you?” Norso asked, apparently snapping out of his stupefaction.

I tipped my head in his direction. The question felt ironic coming from Norso, who had not all that long ago conspired to kill me as well.

“It’s a weird habit of mine.” I teased. I could tell Norso understood, and his shoulders sagged.

“But he returned the favor a hundred times over.” I added sincerely.

I proceeded to tell them about the rest of the waves, the dungeons, meeting Jared, and the rest of his faction.

“Wow. Pretty gutsy of Jared to come after you like that.” Dad mused.

“Especially after you basically just saved their lives and welcomed them into your faction.” Mom added.

“Agreed.” Mischief added sardonically.

Norso just listened.

“I guess. But I kind of get it. Plus he just witnessed Mischief, and I delete an entire raid and he still put himself between me and one of his faction members.” I shrugged. “That took guts, I respected that.”

To put their mind at ease about the steward of my faction I told them how he’d taken time to apologize later even going so far as to explain why he did it.

We talked the entire night. About the Guildians, the Red Orcs, the dark elves, the scourge trial, everything. By the time I reached the three Powerful Chaos spawn at the end, my dad was scratching his head.

“And all they said was someone, ‘He’, was tampering with the natural order?”

“That’s all they said.” I confirmed.

“I wonder what that was supposed to mean?” Mom asked.

“My gut tells me that it probably has to do with the cryptic notes, like the message you had. Plus, the way that we were thrown into the scourge trial also felt off.”

“How so?” Dad pressed.

“It just felt like we had no choice. Like someone far away just decided we’d do it. Maybe they took advantage of me never being allowed to confirm anything. You know, since I skipped the tutorial. It’s just a hunch though.” I said, shrugging.

“I really don’t like the sound of that.” Dad rubbing his chin.

“Well, they’re all dead, so I guess it’s ok now.”

My dad didn’t seem as convinced. “In the future, it might be worth thinking twice when accepting help from the strange system messages.”

Everyone at the table nodded along.

A silence fell over the cabin, the night growing old. It was then that I finally felt the full weight of the past days, and fatigue dragged at me, eyes drooping.

“I’m so glad you’re both alive.”

My parents gave each other knowing looks.

“We wouldn’t be here if you and your faction weren’t so strong. We’ve both got a lot of catching up to do.”

That’s when I realized, I still had no idea what my parents’ levels or classes were.

“You’ve heard everything about me, what about you guys? What are your classes?”

My dad spoke up first.

“I’m a level 33 Frontline Brawler. I fight using a shield and a mace.”

“It suits you. But yeah...were going to need to get you some more levels.” I grinned. “Actually, I have something that might be a nice boost for you!”

I rummaged through my storage excitedly. Then pulled out a deep emerald crystal and placed it on the table with a heavy clunk.

Dad peered down at the softball-sized emerald.

“What is it?” He asked, lifting it up.

“It’s called an armament shard.” I used my hands to try and imitate mana flowing. “Try forcing some mana through it, I want to see what happens.”

Norso, who I was a little surprised was still here, perked up from laying on the table.

“You're sure it's safe?” He asked, turning it over in his hands.

“Pretty sure. I think you’ll like it.”

“Just try it honey, were all so curious!” Mom chimed in.

That was all the encouragement he needed, and he concentrated on the emerald, which in turn began to ignite in brilliant light. Beams of green shot forth like a disco ball in the cramped cabin.

Then all at once the lights focused on Martins chest. In a flash, the emerald zipped towards his chest, directly over his sternum, eliciting a soft grunt.

He blinked and the light seemed to filter into his body, turning his skin into a green hue.

His eyes seemed to gloss over, and he appeared to be reading something on his status screen.

“Follow my chosen path.” He spoke to the invisible screen.

“Yes.” He confirmed.

Once the word was uttered, the green light flowed turning to liquid that engulfed my dad. The liquid hardened, much of it turning a dark almost matte black-green color with softer green hues at each of the joints.

The liquid didn’t stop there however, and slim strands flowed to his arms, one forming a solid looking kite shield, the other a heavy looking spiked mace.

Finally, the last of the liquid covered his head, fitting the ensemble off with a slitted helmet.

“Where did you get an armament shard?” Norso asked in wonder.

“Took it from one of the chaos spawns.” Then I spun my finger in a circle. “Gives us a twirl why don’t you?”

The floor creaked at each fall of his boots as he complied with my request.

“Layton, this is incredible. I barely feel it covering my body, it’s almost as though it hovers over my skin.”

“Do you like it?”

“Like it? I love it.”

“You look so handsome!” Mom cheered.

“His face is covered with a helmet mom.”

“Yes, but I just know it he’s handsome underneath.” She said in a mock, dreamy voice. The helmet melted away, revealing my dad's face, that looked more youthful it seemed each day.

“Just putting it on gave me a new title.” He marveled. “It's called ‘I Am Armament man’. It gives me plus 10 stats to both constitution and strength.”

“It looks like a natural fit. Can you withdraw it at will?”

To answer, the suit of matte black and green armor slid away into the emerald at his chest, which then faded away to nothing.

“Looks like it.” Mom said.

“Now we just need to go find some mana spawns to give it a real test run.” I said, leaning back in my chair.

“And what about you mom? What’s your class?” She also looked more youthful. She’d always been a beautiful woman, even into her fifties, but like dad her hair was already mostly grey before the change. Now it was returning to its vibrant curly red.

“I’m a level twenty-seven soother.” She pursed her lips. “It's kind of a weird class. All I have to do is listen to people talk about their problems, but it doesn’t level up very fast.”

I cocked my head. “You just listen to people?”

“As far as I can tell.”

“And what happens?”

She shrugged. “I’m not sure really. Nothing that noticeable. Maybe a bit calmer by the time they leave.”

“A soother? What did your class title say when you were choosing it?”

“It said ‘lift the heads of the heavy laden and downtrodden.’ I only chose it because I didn’t want to choose a fighter class.”

“It sounds like something from the bible.” I noted.

Then again, the system seemed to use familiar language from our world. Maybe it was based on the individual?

“It really does fit you though, you’ve always been a great listener.”

“Thank you, Layton.” She smiled sweetly. “I wish I could’ve been more help though during the fights.”

“I’m sure that you helped a lot in your own way.” I said with a big yawn. Even though I didn’t need nearly as much sleep anymore, I couldn’t remember the last time I closed my eyes.

The floor creaked and my mom walked around to give me a side hug and kissed me on the forehead.

“Why don’t we all get some rest. In the morning I want to see where you’ve been living.”

I stretched my legs under the table and reached my hands over my head, yawning again.

“Sounds like a great idea.” I agreed.

We chatted a bit more, Norso finally excusing himself. He wouldn’t go far. I suspected he’d probably find a place to curl up outside to rest. It wouldn’t take long if I decided I wanted to sleep in my own bed. But I really didn’t want to leave.

As if my mom was reading my mind, a blanket and pillow appeared in her hand, which I took gratefully. She fussed over me sleeping on the floor, but I assured her that it would be more than enough.

The moment my head hit the pillow, I was swallowed up in a dreamless sleep.

***

I woke up with a start, to the sound of knocking at the door of the cabin. With a grunt, I pulled my blanket over my head and rolled away from the intrusive knocking.

Knock, knock, knock.

With a grumble, I sat up and rubbed my eyes looking around the empty cabin. It seemed empty.

My parents were probably already out and about.

At least they had the decency to let me sleep. The same couldn’t be said for whoever was wrapping at the cabin door.

I kicked the blanket off and rolled to my feet, blearily stumbling my way to the door.

Grey sunlight and a cool breeze rushed into the room as I cracked the door open squinting against the light.

“Oh, hey Jared.” I said, rubbing my eyes. “Good morning.”

“Good afternoon, you mean.” He replied with a grin. “Finally decided to get a decent night rest?”

I peeked outside the cabin to see people wandering around the streets. Some even risked sideway my way as they passed, trying to seem inconspicuous.

Admittedly, it had been a very long time since I was able to simply pass out and sleep.

“What time is it?”

“Just a little past noon.”

I stepped outside and sucked in the fresh air. “Where are my parents?”

“Your mom and dad are meeting with a group of people from New Boise. I can take you to them, besides I have a few things to report.”

Feeling more awake by the second, I gestured for him to lead the way with a swing of my arm.

The streets were much busier than the previous day.

“So, you said you had things to report?”

Jared nodded. “Just a few peculiarities.”

“Peculiarities?”

“Well Samson and some of the wolves were scouting the perimeter of the faction once the barriers dropped and they found what looked to be the site of a battle just outside our borders.”

“Oh, really? Do we know which factions were involved?”

Jared shook his head. “Samson couldn’t tell. There were obvious signs of battle, he followed one of the trails and it led to the faction just north of us in Jordan’s old territory. There was another trail, but it led away and went cold.”

I scratched my chin. “Do you think we should try and make contact?”

“It couldn’t hurt to try.”

“Alright, what else?”

“Nothing too concerning. We are still comfortably the leader in both regions we occupy. Oh, would you mind opening your status?”

At the mention of my status screen I realized there was a flashing notice waiting for me to open. Without waiting, I pulled it to my attention.

Help has been requested. The order of Tranquility has been offered an objective.

I grinned.

The first mission for the order of Tranquility had come.
