Arthur moved deeper into the forest, hunting his next Grubtusk Devourer. The sun had dipped behind the trees and shadows lengthened. But with Mana Sight active, the woods lit up like a twisted Christmas tree. Every mana signature glowed, pulsing faintly in the dark. Navigation was easy. He kept one rule in mind: always move against the wind.

He wasn’t about to let his scent spoil a surprise.

After a few minutes, he found his first target. Slumped against a tree, snoring like a content pig-beast, completely unaware of the threat creeping toward it.

Arthur didn’t go for the eye this time. Instead, he crept closer, slow and silent, and lightly touched the beast’s flank. Then he willed it into the ring.

Fwoosh.

Gone.

He shut his eyes, focused inward, and checked the ring’s interior.

The Grubtusk was there, still asleep. Suspended in stasis.

Arthur grinned like a mad scientist discovering caffeine.

“Groooaaan.”

Drogan’s voice echoed with theatrical disappointment.“Just when I thought you had it in you to kill something, you’re back to storing monsters like a packrat. You make progress by walking forward, not backward.”

Arthur didn’t respond immediately.“Give me a couple minutes, teacher. I want to test something.”

Before Drogan could argue, Arthur willed the Grubtusk back out. It appeared on the ground, still snoring, completely unaware it had ever left.

He gave it another poke. Fwoosh. Back into the ring.

Again. Fwoosh. Back out.

“Are you, are you playing fetch with a monster!?” Drogan groaned. “What the hell are you doing, boy?”

Arthur didn’t answer right away. He touched the beast again and stored it once more.

Then he explained, “They’re active during the day, which makes it hard to get the jump on them. If I go in swinging, I’m risking everything. I got lucky earlier. No other monsters showed up. That won’t always be the case.”

“So I need a safer option. I hunt them at night, when they’re asleep and isolated. Store them. Then fight them one-on-one when I’m prepared. No noise. No witnesses. No risk of drawing other monsters.”

Drogan was silent for a moment.“Hmph. Where’s the fun if you’re not breaking bones?, but… you’ve only got noodles for arms anyway. I’ll allow it. For now.”

---

With a quiet smirk, Arthur slipped back into the trees. He continued hunting into the night, capturing sleeping Grubtusks and avoiding anything unfamiliar. But soon, a thought struck him:

Why limit it to just Grubtusks?

If it was asleep and glowing on Mana Sight, he could deal with it later.

He approached one particularly large mana signature. Definitely not a Grubtusk and activated [Inspect].

[Inspect Successful]Name: Stonejaw HoundRank: ELevel: 61Affinities: Earth / Vibration

[Inspect has leveled up to 3]

Stonejaw Hound. Sounded bitey.

Arthur crept closer. But as he neared, the beast stirred. Its heavy head lifted. Its nostrils flared. Slowly, it turned toward him, growling low.

Arthur froze.

The hound sniffed the air. Then, with a final snarl, it backed away and bolted into the underbrush.

Arthur let out a slow, shaking breath.“Heightened smell. Got it,” he whispered. “Note to self, don’t sneak up on that one again.”

---

He resumed his pattern, avoiding anything remotely awake, capturing another dozen or so monsters before finally heading back to his creek. When he arrived, he flopped down beside the water and called out:

“Teacher? Any idea how many of these things I can store?”

Drogan sounded more amused than annoyed now.

“Kid, that ring is a legendary artifact. Even I didn’t have one of those. Should hold several thousand E-grade monsters, easy. When it gets close to 80% capacity, you’ll start to feel it - like a weight pressing against your soul. You’ll know.”

“Thousands, huh?”

“Don’t get greedy,” Drogan muttered. “You want to train, not collect souvenirs. Get some sleep. Tomorrow, I want you killing the monsters you caught. No more fetch.”

Arthur nodded. He curled up under his ragged cloak, thoughts racing with all the things he could try next.

Tomorrow, he wasn’t just going to fight.

He was going to test everything.

---

Arthur woke the next morning feeling… not terrible.

He rinsed his face in the creek, drank deeply, then hiked about half a mile away. Just to be sure no monsters would crash the party. Once he found a quiet clearing, he summoned the first Grubtusk from his ring.

It was still asleep.

Arthur didn’t waste time.

He stepped forward and drove one of the sharpened tusk bones straight into the monster’s eye.

It screamed.

The thing didn’t die instantly. It thrashed, blinded on one side, blood spewing from the punctured socket. But Arthur was ready. He circled to its blind side, dodged the sluggish swipes, and brought it down with the same routine as before - only cleaner, tighter, and more efficient.

[Mana: 102/210]

He harvested the core and stashed the corpse in his ring, resisting the urge to gnaw on it like a starving wolf. Instead, he focused.

Mana Body on. Push the muscles. Burn the reserves.

Mana Heal. Recover. Reset.

Again. And again.

Just before his mana bottomed out, a satisfying chime echoed in his mind.

[Stats Increased]Strength: 11Agility: 11Dexterity: 11Endurance: 11

---

Satisfied, Arthur returned to the creek. He washed the sticky core off, gave it a quick glance, then swallowed it whole like a disgusting glowing vitamin.

The moment the core hit his stomach, Arthur began the process - drawing mana out gradually and channeling it toward his chest. He guided the energy in slow, deliberate loops around his spark, each pass feeding his mana pool. Warmth spread through his chest, building with every careful cycle.

Then came the gross part.

He belched. Hard.A burst of sour, oily air escaped his throat, black mist hissing between his teeth.

“Blegh! Gordon Rosay would slap me with a raw steak,” Arthur muttered, fanning the air like he’d just exorcised a sulfur demon.

Water was still the only thing that filled his stomach, but monster cores? They were a game-changer. Packed with concentrated mana, far more than moss or meat. They let him restore his mana, fuel his body, and even boost his stats. Efficient, potent... and only mildly revolting.

Still, way better than choking down moss.

---

He repeated the cycle over the next several days, though he didn’t bother tracking time.

Summon. Kill. Exercise. Heal. Absorb. Repeat.

With each kill, the gains slowed. Diminishing returns kicked in hard. But he kept pushing until everything plateaued.

Arthur flexed his fingers.

He could feel it - raw speed, strength, and control humming beneath his skin. Compared to his old life, he now moved like a gymnast on combat stimulants. Olympic-tier reflexes and that wasn’t even counting Mana Body. Which nearly doubled his stats when active.

But even with all that power… his eyes drifted toward the ring on his finger.

The Chimera was still in there.

Still waiting.

Arthur narrowed his gaze.

“Not yet,” he muttered. “But soon.”
