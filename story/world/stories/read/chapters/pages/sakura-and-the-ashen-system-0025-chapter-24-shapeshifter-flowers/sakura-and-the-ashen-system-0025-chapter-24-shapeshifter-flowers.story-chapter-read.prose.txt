They climbed onto Pampli again. They had to keep moving no matter how bad things felt inside.

Even the forest itself seemed darker now, and the gray clouds had sunk lower. Sakura sat in front in silence, more hunched than usual, staring ahead but barely noticing anything. Brein still stood before her eyes. His bloodied body. And his shaking voice telling her that Lir had been taken.

Pampli moved a little faster than before, but without his usual mischief. Now and then he only let out a low snort and flicked an ear in irritation when thorny branches brushed against him.

Gilbert said nothing for a long time. Maybe he was simply giving Sakura time to live through that storm of grief. But he himself did not relax for even a moment. He kept watching the forest carefully.

At one point, the birds above them suddenly went silent, then burst from the dry branches and flew off. Somewhere deeper in the woods, another thin forest chirring sound died away too.

Gilbert spotted the patrol before Sakura even realized something was wrong.

He touched her elbow sharply.

“Not a sound,” he said almost voicelessly.

Pampli froze at once. Sakura tensed automatically, and only then did she see movement between the trees ahead and a little to the left. Three. No, four riders.

A patrol.

Sakura felt everything inside her tighten.

“Easy,” Gilbert whispered.

Sakura had already turned Pampli to the right, leading him away smoothly and quickly. The beast lowered himself toward the ground, trying to make himself as unnoticeable as possible, though Sakura was not sure how unnoticeable a huge tame obsidian monster could realistically be. He slipped between two broad trees, then behind a rock outcrop overgrown with moss, and froze there. Sakura bent low too, almost pressing herself to his neck.

The patrol rode past nearby. Close enough to hear the creak of leather straps and low conversation.

“Damn. I need to piss so bad.”

“You’re hopeless. Shouldn’t have drunk that much.”

One of the riders stopped his horse, and Sakura felt Pampli tense beneath her. If the beast sneezed now, snorted, or simply decided he was tired of hiding, they would be spotted immediately.

But it passed.

The rider only spat to the side, muttered something about rot and a useless forest, and the patrol moved on.

They waited another minute. Maybe two. To Sakura, it felt longer than the whole morning ride. Only when the sounds finally died away did Gilbert quietly breathe out.

“All right. We’ll go around.”

“Were those Merren’s men?” Sakura asked just as quietly.

“Most likely. Or someone who already got orders to sweep the area. The difference doesn’t matter much right now.”

Pampli started forward again, now taking another route through a tighter stretch of forest where they had to duck under low branches and steer around jutting stones.

“They’re looking for us,” she said dully.

“Yes.”

Sakura gripped the front of the saddle so hard her knuckles turned white. Gilbert seemed to notice, because after a while he suddenly started talking about something else entirely.

“Veyr likes to remind people that it isn’t only monsters and humans that are dangerous here.”

Sakura did not understand at first what he meant, and she was just about to ask when she saw it herself.

To the right, at the foot of a low slope, a long snake was winding through the grass. It was not large, and it moved sharply, beautifully, with the shine of black scales. Sakura only had time to notice that its head was strangely broad when the roots of the nearest plant suddenly moved.

Thick roots, which until then had looked like nothing more than part of the ground, jerked and coiled around the snake so fast that Sakura did not understand at first what was happening. The snake thrashed violently, trying to twist free, but the roots only tightened harder. The low plant, with its fleshy dark green leaves and large gray-lilac bud, seemed to come fully alive. Two thinner roots broke through the top layer of soil and clamped around the middle of the snake’s body.

A few seconds later, it was over.

The snake twitched a couple more times, then went limp. The roots slowly dragged it closer to the base of the stem.

Sakura stared at it with near disgust. Goosebumps ran down her back.

“That plant just... ate a snake?”

“Almost,” Gilbert answered. “First it strangled it. Then it’ll start digesting.”

Sakura kept staring at the place where the snake’s tail disappeared for a few more seconds.

“How disgusting.”

“That’s a rootsnare,” Gilbert went on, as if he were giving a lecture on a walk and not trying to stay hidden from a patrol while watching the road. “Not the most dangerous kind. It rarely goes after humans unless it’s very hungry or unless someone steps right into its core. If we’d run into choking ivy, that would’ve been much worse.”

Sakura turned her head.

“Choking ivy?”

“Don’t ask.”

“Now I have to ask.”

Gilbert sighed. Trying to distract Sakura from her dark thoughts, he had signed himself up for another lecture.

“There are plants in Veyr that don’t strangle or sting. They just release such a thick sleeping gas into the air that a person falls asleep standing next to them. Then they drop. And that’s when the worst part starts.”

“They eat them?”

“Some do. Some just root themselves into them.”

Sakura grimaced.

“At some point I’ll stop being surprised, but apparently not today.”

They rode on a little farther. Sakura was already about to ask something else about Ogrom when Gilbert suddenly straightened sharply.

“Stop.”

Pampli halted at once. Sakura tensed, turning to Gilbert and waiting for an explanation. The mage’s gaze was directed to the side, where beyond the sparse trees a small field of flowers spread out. They looked unexpectedly cute. Low, with thin green stems, pink petals, and bright yellow centers.

Gilbert jumped down from Pampli first.

“A short stop,” he said. “Sakura, come here. Help me.”

Sakura got down too, though she eyed the field warily.

“Don’t tell me these flowers eat people too.”

“No. These are useful flowers.”

He had already stepped into the grass, carefully choosing where to place his feet. Sakura followed him.

Gilbert crouched by the nearest flower and touched the petals.

“This is what will help us stay hidden in Ogrom.”

Sakura blinked.

“Botany?”

Gilbert did not even look back.

“Pick only the flowers. Don’t touch the stems. If you snap a stem and sap starts coming out, drop it right away.”

Sakura frowned harder.

“Why?”

“Because the shapeshifter’s poison is in the stem sap. And in the young leaves too. The thing we need is in the petals. They collect a volatile pigment and bind it to the background magical field. That’s why the flower works as a temporary distortion agent.”

Sakura slowly crouched beside another flower, touching the bright petals. They felt pleasant under her fingers.

“You just said that like I was supposed to understand all of it.”

“You understood the important part: don’t touch the stems.”

“That part, yes.”

Sakura helped Gilbert pick them, following his instructions carefully so she would not damage the stems. By the time they finished, Gilbert had a whole handful of pink petals. Pampli came closer and nudged one of the flowers with his nose, but Gilbert snapped his fingers at him at once.

“Don’t eat it.”

Pampli snorted in offense.

“Don’t even think about it,” Gilbert added. “The effect could be far too unpredictable with you.”

Then Gilbert picked a flat, even stone at the edge of the field, poured some of the petals onto it, and took a short metal rod with a flat tip from his bag.

“Watch,” he said. “If you do it fast, the petals keep the pigment longer.”

He began grinding them in circular motions. The pink color darkened quickly, first turning into a thick raspberry mash, then into a dense glossy mass shifting between red and purple. Gilbert worked with the confidence of someone who had done this more than once. He did not crush everything into a shapeless mess. He was pressing the juice out properly, separating the fine fibers.

“The shapeshifter flower is usually considered poisonous,” he went on while he worked. “That’s why most sane people try not to touch it. And they’re right to. If someone foolish starts chewing the stems or brews the whole plant, they’ll vomit first, then their vision will start blacking out, and after that they probably won’t even make it to a healer.”

Sakura listened closely, watching as he gathered the finished mass into a small wooden cup.

“But the petals are different?”

“Yes. There’s no poison in the petals themselves. They have a different makeup. They store a coloring ether. It doesn’t affect blood or bones, but it temporarily throws off external markers: eye color, hair shade, sometimes facial contours, more rarely the nose shape or the lines of the cheekbones. The effect is unstable, which is exactly why it’s useful. Hard to predict. Which means hard to trace too.”

He splashed a little water from his flask into the cup and stirred again.

“How long does it last?” Sakura asked.

“A few hours if we’re lucky. Less if we’re not. So we’ll have to move quickly in the city.”

He lifted the cup to the light, judging the thickness.

“Not bad.”

Then he held it out to Sakura.

“Drink.”

Sakura narrowed her eyes immediately.

“No way. You first.”

One of Gilbert’s brows twitched. He gave a quiet huff, then took the cup back and drank several swallows himself.

“Happy now?”

“Not yet. I’m waiting for the result.”

Pampli was staring at him too with open interest. Nothing happened for a few seconds, until Gilbert’s white hair began to change shade. It darkened more and more with each second. His features shifted too, though almost too subtly to catch. His cheekbones softened, his nose straightened, and his jaw lost some of its old sharpness. He was still Gilbert, but like a rougher copy of him from another city and another life.

Sakura stared at him, eyes wide.

“That’s so creepy!”

“Now you,” Gilbert told her.

He quickly crushed fresh petals again, mixed them with water, and handed the cup to her. Without much hesitation, Sakura drank it. A tart sweetness hit her tongue at once, followed by an unpleasant prickling, as if tiny needles had scraped down her throat. Sakura grimaced.

At first, she felt nothing. Then the skin on her face tightened slightly. Sakura lifted a hand to her hair and almost immediately noticed that several strands at her temple were no longer black. They gleamed with a rich raspberry shade, as if someone had drawn berry juice through them. Her eyes started to itch from the inside. She blinked several times.

“There’s something wrong with my eyes.”

“That’s normal. Don’t rub your face yet.”

He was watching her too closely now, and it was starting to get on her nerves.

“Well?”

Gilbert tipped his head, judging the result.

“Amber,” he said. “Your eyes are amber now. And some of your hair strands are raspberry.”

Sakura breathed out and tried to smooth her hair down. Gilbert stepped back, still examining her with a professional eye.

“Better,” he said. “But not enough.”

Sakura did not like the sound of that.

“What now?”

Gilbert reached into his Inventory and pulled out a small tin jar. It looked old and badly dented, with a dark lid and no label Sakura could read.

“What is that?” she asked.

“Gray Veil.”

“Well, that tells me absolutely nothing.”

He opened the jar. A sharp, bitter smell rose from it at once, and Sakura made a face. The substance inside looked like some kind of swamp ointment mixed with ash.

“What is it?” Sakura repeated.

“A temporary interference for System recognition,” Gilbert said. “Everyone knows about it, and it isn’t hard to get. But for Ogrom, that’s an advantage, not a flaw. The ointment interferes with the reading of your personal information.”

“That sounds very legal,” Sakura said sarcastically.

Gilbert did not answer. He dipped two fingers into the grayish paste and rubbed a thin streak over the inside of one wrist, then the other. After a moment, he handed the jar to Sakura.

“Apply it to your wrists in a thin layer. Don’t waste it.”

Sakura took the jar with visible distrust, but did as he said. The ointment felt cold and unpleasantly slick against her skin. Part of it soaked in, and part of it remained on the surface with a faint gray sheen.

“Don’t get your wrists wet,” Gilbert ordered. “And don’t rub it off. Otherwise the effect may disappear.”

Sakura gave a short nod.

“So now what?” she asked.

“Check me.”

Sakura blinked. It felt strange to do it like this, on command. In general, it still felt strange to read information from someone so easily. It was like peeking into another person’s passport, and that often made her uncomfortable, because there had been nothing like it in the world she came from.

Still, she did it. Sakura looked at Gilbert and focused, the way she usually did.

For a second, nothing happened. Then the pale text flickered before her eyes.

Name: ????????Race: Unreadable

Sakura stared at the lines.

“Well,” she said slowly. “That looks completely not suspicious.”

“In Ogrom, the suspicious ones are the people whose information reads too easily.”

“That’s comforting.”

“If we weren’t wanted and were heading into a respectable city, they’d make us wash this off right at the gate,” Gilbert said. “But in Ogrom, plenty of people don’t want anyone seeing their names, and everyone is used to it.”

Sakura looked down at the gray trace on her wrist. She exhaled through her nose.

“Wonderful. I always dreamed of becoming respectable by Ogrom standards.”

Gilbert closed the jar and put it back into his Inventory.

“But appearance alone isn’t enough. For a disguise, we need roles.”

At that point, both of them were distracted, turning toward Pampli. It was as if they had thought the same thing at once.

“And him we’ll have to leave. We’ll hide him in the forest,” Gilbert said.

Pampli snorted in outrage. He did not like that information at all, and he looked at Sakura, searching for support in her. But this time Sakura agreed with Gilbert. She let out a heavy sigh.

“I’m sorry, Pampli. But he’s right. You really can’t go into the city. I promise we’ll come back quickly. All right?”

Sakura stroked his head, then scratched behind his ear. Pampli looked upset, but he stopped grumbling.

Gilbert gave a low huff and went back to the subject of roles.

“The two of us will have to look like a pair of lowlife drug peddlers. Small-time drug dealers. That’ll be convenient enough and forgettable enough.”

“Out of all possible options, you picked drugs?”

“For Ogrom, it’s the best option. You’ll understand soon enough.”

Gilbert adjusted the strap on his shoulder and looked through the trees, as if he could already see the city ahead.

“We’re almost there. Time to move.”
