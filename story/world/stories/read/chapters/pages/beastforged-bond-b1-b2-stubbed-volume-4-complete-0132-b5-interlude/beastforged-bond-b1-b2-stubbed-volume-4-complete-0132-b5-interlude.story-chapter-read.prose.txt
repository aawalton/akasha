Max stared at the relaxed human in silence. Surrounded by the Spirit Guards and threatened by one of the more battle-hungry Caldera, the human didn’t appear affected in the slightest. It even looked like he was having a good time, which was weird, to say the least.

But if that was already confusing, seeing the sacred beast was even more so. It was not the first time he had seen a sacred beast, though it was definitely his first time encountering one bound to a human. A young one at that.

Max tried to remember the last time he had seen someone like the young human but failed miserably.

Had a human ever successfully bonded with a sacred beast before? He couldn’t recall his master ever mentioning something like that.

Binding Vows with Spirits was far more common than sacred beasts binding themselves to anyone.

Using his traits Truth and Inspection had been enough to tell Max all he needed, yet no matter how truthful the young human’s words sounded, he couldn’t help but feel like Adam’s words weren’t true.

But Truth did not lie. Adam, the young human, spoke truthfully. He didn’t even bother adding half-truths, nor did he see a need to omit anything. As irritating as that was, Max retained his decorum and proceeded with the interrogation while the Spirit Guards split up to inspect and investigate the surroundings.

Interrogating Adam turned out to be not all that helpful. His retelling of the story already contained all the information at his disposal, including the intel that there was a Fissure but that Adam had been nowhere near it yet.

For someone who had just fought and defeated a Spawn, Adam sounded too calm. Not only did he know about the Spawns, but it almost felt like he’d fought them before.

His Soulkins were special. To sense a Spawn and Fissure before Nadja.Max’s Soulkin voiced the same concern he had.

Defeating a Spawn was impressive, but detecting and defeating it before the Spirit Guards arrived at the scene was extraordinary. It could only mean that Adam’s Soulkin was either a Life-attuned sacred beast or something greater. Likely an Overlord sacred beast… or so Max would have guessed if Adam had been anywhere near the Grandmaster rank.

Inspection put Adam’s Mind at the Master Rank and his Body on the threshold of the Grandmaster Rank, both of which were impressive, yet it was the Soul that caught him by surprise.

Inspection failed to determine what Rank Adam’s Soul was. Only the best Ranker Charms could block Inspection, yet Adam had none of those charms. Still, Max couldn’t get a sense of Adam’s Soul – his Inner World.

Something about that kid was special, and that did not directly involve the dragon-like sacred beast or the other Soulkin that had sensed the Spawn ahead of time.

Or it does. If he learned to channel the Spirit’s power through the sacred beast…Max’s Soulkin spoke again, but he shook his head.

To use a Spirit’s power, one had to be a Ruler, also known as a Ranker. Even those so-called Pseudo-Rankers could barely imbue their blades with the power of the Spirit they made a Vow with.

If he learned to channel his sacred beast’s Spirit power, their soulshare would have to be at the 3rd Stage, Max reminded his Soulkin, though it only nodded in agreement.

When the Spirit Guards returned, Max dismissed Adam, thanking him for his cooperation.

“I can stay and help if you need a helping hand,” Adam offered with a light smile, though it was apparent that he wanted to leave and sleep. His sacred beast had already closed its eyes as well.

“We can manage,” Max responded with a smile of his own.

One moment later, the sacred beast disappeared, returning to Adam’s Inner World.

Wings similar to the sacred beast’s sprouted from Adam’s back, and he shot into the air with surprising speed.

“See you.”

His words reverberated through the air right before he vanished in a flash.

“What a strange human,” one of the Guards said. “Then again, it might be your kind that’s weird.”

Max raised an eyebrow at the Shika, only to be faced with rows of razor-sharp teeth.

“Says the guy who almost got eaten by his lover.”

“For the sake of our children!” the Shika responded, sounding far too proud for Max’s liking.

“Anyway…” Max’s tone turned more serious as he changed the topic. “What did you guys find out?”

“There’s a Fissure. A Newborn-threshold one,” one of the Caldera reported.

The Fissure was not supposed to be there. In fact, no Fissure ought to appear anywhere close to Dracos.

“The Farmer’s death has been confirmed. The death signature is fresh, and we should be able to retrieve his soul, but it will be damaged. Recovering it fully intact won’t be possible.”

“Souls are not our field of expertise. We can leave that issue to Death.” Max waved dismissively, which earned him a frown from the Blessed vowed to Death. It hadn’t been long since she returned, and the old man would have much rather spent his last decades on Razarn with her remaining wherever she had spent the last few centuries.

Alas, that wasn’t feasible. Death was back, and she seemed to like her place in Dracos quite a bit.

“Give me the full report. I will forward the issue to the Spirit Governance,” Max ordered. “After that, remove the Voidtrails… and yes, you will be reimbursed for the Essence used in the process.”

He’d been hoping for a calm week, but that was impossible now. A Newborn Fissure near Dracos promised trouble. And a field day with the Spirits.

I hate this job…

Death stirred awake. She let out a realm-shattering yawn and pulled the bedsheets aside. Even if she didn’t need sleep – never had – sleep was something magical. It was understandable why everyone liked it so much. Who wouldn’t enjoy it?

Clearly, that did not include the Spiritborne who had decided to interrupt her sleep.

She floated out of bed while her spirit power retrieved the report that had been forwarded to the Spirit Governance network.

“Adam Savier?” Death mused, her sonorous voice reverberating through the penthouse she called home – as short-lived as it was.

Spirit power flowed outward, and she faded away. One moment she floated in her penthouse, and in the next she levitated above the Devourer’s Fissure outside Dracos.

It should not have been possible for the Devourer to create a Fissure anywhere close, but that was not her problem. The Spirits responsible for protecting Dracos this century ought to take care of this issue.

But since she was already there, she may as well deal with it herself, lest it devolve into something more threatening.

Her Death Essence surged outward as she reached toward the violet tear in the space before her. It radiated neither power nor life, let alone ether. It was nothing, yet it was also the most dangerous thing in existence. Death mantled the Fissure and forcefully pressed it together.

If Thyria were here, we wouldn’t have to waste so much of our Essence. Death let out a heavy sigh. Sometimes she regretted agreeing to seal her sister away, especially considering that she had only sought revenge for all the things Thyria had done to her back in their old home.

She did not regret the punishment, yet Death agreed that some things would have been easier with Thyria present.

Putting that thought aside for the moment, Death Essence erupted from the Death Spirit. It tore through the air to forcefully shut the Fissure and seal it permanently. While that was a little over the top for a Newborn Fissure, even the Apex Essence of an Emperor beast might not have permanently sealed it otherwise.

Killing Spawns with Apex Essence was simple. It was one of the most efficient methods of killing even the older Spawns. Removing the Voidtrails spread by the Spawns and Fissures was possible with weaker Essence as well, yet the same could not be said about Fissures themselves. Only the Spirits could close them safely and permanently.

Recovering a Spirit’s Essence, however, was not possible. Not without devouring the Core Essences of other beings. Humans preferably, for their Core Essence was far more nutritious than that of others. That was also why Thyria had done what she did.

Maybe it was cruel, but the fall of one civilization would have ensured the survival of all the others. To Death, that was an acceptable sacrifice.

Death glanced back at the Spirit Guards. They were using their own Essence to remove the Voidtrails left behind by the Spawns when she suddenly stopped.

To the mortal eye, the Spawn had been eradicated. That was true in every visible and invisible sense, including spirit sense. The Spawn and everything belonging to it had been removed from existence. That only left the power of the Primal. Sensing it again after hundreds of years was strange, yet heartwarming. If she had a heart.

Eliminating the Spawn was effortless for you, wasn’t it, Adam? She mused to herself.

While she hadn’t expected Adam to learn how to wield the Source of the Insignia anytime soon, he had already done so many decades earlier than she had expected. But the reason behind that was simple, wasn’t it?

Bound to three sacred beasts, the Insignia likely accumulated enough power to awaken.

Two now, one of which shows signs of evolving into a Spirit, Death corrected herself, though that changed nothing about the final result.

Adam was the Primal Heir. There was no doubt about it.

With that in mind, Death focused on the Spirit Guards once more. Volca may have plans for the kid, but since Adam was already involving himself in Dracos’ protection, she may as well involve herself too.

“Send Adam Savier an invitation.”
