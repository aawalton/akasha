I stared blankly up at the empty skull before, while I quietly tried to just…process what I’d just been told.

Hermus.

The apparent Great Spirit of Death. Someone who I had never once heard an inkling about. All I had ever known about the Great Spirits was that there were five of them.

Sun, Moon, Sea, Earth…

And Life.

Yet, here was some kind of creature claiming that they were a sixth Great Spirit. A potential counterpart to the Great Spirit that I personally counted as an ally. Someone who had never once said a word about having a potential equal and opposite.

I couldn’t help but feel just the tiniest bit betrayed.

Still, though…

“You said ‘once’,” I said quietly, my words carrying almost unnaturally in this strange, Aetheric void. “Once you were known as the Great Spirit of Death. Which implies…”

‘That I am no longer,’ This being, this…Hermus affirmed easily. ‘I carry no titles, no prestige. Any office or position I once held within the Concordian Hierarchy is no longer. Such a post was vacated long before the tallest of trees was rooted, and the first laws of Vereden were set to papyrus. The essence of such a position no longer exists. Once, there were seven. Mourned, was the sixth. Now, there are only five.”

My brow furrowed, but it was Sylvia who voiced what I was thinking. “Sixth?” She asked in a quiet, respectful tone. “Lord Hermus, what do you mean by that? Vereden has only ever known five Great Spirits, though ‘known’ isn’t quite the right word.”

“More like hidden,” Fade muttered to me, sounding unusually subdued. For some reason, he shivered right next to me.

‘That, at least, is no grand conspiracy,’ Hermus said kindly, his wispy voice still coming from no discernible source. ‘There once was a beloved brother who girded the Sea and the Mountain, the free spirit who was the third peak of their triumvirate. For he was the Sky, the breath of whimsy to tempestuous wrath and bitter revanchism. Alas but for his death at the hands of those who were meant to aid, and yet chose the yoke. Alas for poor, scattered Vaelis, Lord of the Sky. He is mourned to this day, in no small way.’

…how much did I really know about the history of the Great Spirits? About Spiritkind in general?

Before now, I would have said I had a pretty good grasp on it, but that wasn’t true, was it? There were no books on the history of the Spirits, because they weren’t physical existences in the first place. There were no libraries I could go to. There were no experts on the past of the Spirits I could consult. Or at least, none I had access to.

It occurred to me that I had little to no contact with Spirits beyond the outliers. The leaders of their kind, and the half-physical children of them that were, in the grand scheme of things, young in the total history of their people.

I had never met or spoken to a Spirit outside of the Great Spirits or the Spirit Beasts. Partly, I think, that had been because I had deliberately been trying to keep out of Spirit matters. Beyond things that were directly related to people I cared about, like Azarus and Fade, I’d tried to keep away from it all. I had thought I already had a lot on my plate; why add to that by involving myself in other matters?

Now, I was starting to realize that had been…a bit naïve of me, wasn’t it?

I may not have wanted to involve myself with the Spirits, but they sure as Hell seemed to find me.

Alright.

If even Anima wasn’t going to be totally honest with me, then I’d try my luck with this guy.

“Taran never told me any of this,” Fade told me quietly, sitting down on the stone. He rapidly stood back up, though, as if it had burned his hindquarters, and instead leaned against my leg.

It was only because of experience with these powerful beings that I wasn’t surprised when Hermus spoke again.

‘Taran…’ Hermus mused, apparently having heard Fade. ‘That would be the eldest son of the Moon, is that not correct? This one never had the chance to meet him, but I have heard whispers of his honor and integrity, foremost among your kind. Worry not, littlest nephew. It is entirely possible that not even he knows of my once-upon-a-morrow existence.’

Fade…wasn’t that surprised either that Hermus could eavesdrop on our bond. He was far from the first person to intrude on our privacy. “You…know about us?” Fade asked hesitantly.

Hermus somehow chuckled, and the sound of it was so unintentionally eerie that I shivered slightly. ‘Indeed, young one, indeed. Though I have dwelt beneath the stone and the soil since time immemorial, news does occasionally reach me. Either from those who have desired to become my disciple, as young Rafael has-’

Off to the side, where he had been standing patiently next to one of the kneeling corpses with his hands folded patiently at his waist, Tlazo inclined his head respectfully.

‘-or through…other means.’

This time, the way Hermus chuckled was irritatingly mysterious.

‘Besides, from where do you believe the idea for a half-physical, half-spiritual existence such as yourself arose?’ Hermus continued pointedly. ‘Though my siblings, Lady Life foremost amongst them, fought most vehemently against my decision to assume the shell you see before you, it was ultimately respected and known. When their time of great struggle was upon them, and they sought means upon which they could birth new progeny, I believe their inspiration was my ages past sacrifice.’

I held up a hand, both to stop the disembodied Spirit as well as to catch his attention, feeling like a foolish grade schooler as I did. I was getting tired of this.

I wanted some damn answers.

“What do you mean by that?” I said, pushing my frustration aside with practice. It wouldn’t help me here. I couldn’t help but notice that compared to the other Great Spirits I’d met, this guy was oddly formal. “Who…no, what are you really? Tlazo’s introduction said something about you being the ‘father of Necromancy’? What does that mean? And most importantly…” I frowned, straightened my spine, and in the absence of anything else to look at, did my best to meet the enormous skull’s blank sockets. “Why is the apparent chamber where the Netherim usurpers ascended to divinity right above you?”

Out of the corner of my eye, it wasn’t hard for me to see it when Sylvia jerked in place at my words. She shot a brief glance up at the ceiling above us, and then turned her wide sapphire eyes on me with a wordless question. I only nodded in response, but that seemed to be enough for her.

In the silence that followed, the only thing that pierced it was a low, contemplative whisper from Tlazo.

“Netherim…?”

Finally, when this ‘Hermus’ spoke once more, the almost jovial note that had filled his whispery voice was absent.

In its place was an ancient, deep, all-consuming weariness.

‘You know a great deal more than I might have expected, young man,’ Hermus said softly. ‘You must have been quite busy indeed to have uncovered so much in so short a time. When last Rafael was dispersed and returned to this resting place, he told me of his meeting with you. The latest in a long, unbroken line of Precursors. I am…gladdened indeed to see that you triumphed over that misbegotten creature.’

“You speak as if Rafael-”

I ignored the sharp look Rafael sent me then.

Look, man. If the ancient Spirit of Death can refer to you as your real name, I should be able to as well, and not…whatever it is that ‘Tlazo’ refers to.

“Was your only source of information about the outside world, down here.”

‘Because he is,’ Hermus said simply. ‘Rafael is my latest and only current disciple. My only student. I have gone long stretches of years, in ages past, where I have known only these walls and their peace. It can sometimes take a great deal of time indeed, before a new, prospective student can find me.’

“But…” Fade spoke up tentatively. “Aren’t you a Spirit? Can’t you just…cross over into the Concord and talk to someone there?”

Even with no true features, for a moment, it almost looked like the immobile skull was smiling down at Fade.

‘I am not, actually, a Spirit any longer…except in the most literal sense, of course,’ Hermus chuckled, then. ‘I am dead, young one. The Concord has been lost to me for many, many years now. As has been the physical.’

“But you’re speaking to us right now in the physical world.” Sylvia pointed out, cocking her head.

‘Am I now?’ Hermus answered mysteriously, once more sounding like he was almost grinning. ‘Is that what you think? I’m afraid not, young one. Both the physical realm as well as the spiritual are for those who cling to life. Such is why my dear sister Life was always so much stronger than I, when I was counted among the Great Spirits. What use does an existence offer, that has no influence over their own dominion? What good can they bring to the world, when they cannot inherently understand it? No, I chose to die, so that I might better understand the intricacies of death…and so I remain. I am dead, and thus am…elsewhere.”

I opened my mouth to ask a question, only to slowly close it.

What the Hell did that mean? What was elsewhere? He couldn’t possibly be talking about the afterlife…

Right?

If…something like that even existed…how was he even speaking to us?

“You’re not a Lich…” I said slowly. “You can’t be…so what are you?”

Hermus chuckled at my words, which did nothing to calm the creeping feeling running up and down my spine. ‘Oh, but I am…in a fashion. What you know of Lichhood is…limited, I’m afraid. No doubt you believe the entirety of it is binding one’s soul to an object, so that they might cling to life there in the land of the living. But it can be so much more than that.’

The creeping feeling in my spine transformed into outright shudders at the fascination in his voice.

‘Imagine, instead…if you loosened that bond in some ways, and tightened it in others,’ Hermus continued, almost breathily. ‘Instead, one adds another component to their phylactery. A tether, if you will. And so, that phylactery turns into an anchor that connects to the soul. Thus, if a Lich is brave enough, confident enough, and powerful enough…they may choose to let go.’

“And maintain their phylactery at the same time,” Rafael continued quietly.

My lips parted as I started to understand. “If the phylactery is the anchor in the sea that is the physical, and this tether is what ties the soul to it…what’s the ship in this analogy?”

‘You’ll find out…’ Hermus answered in a calm, even voice. ‘One day. Perhaps tomorrow, perhaps centuries or even millennia from now. But eventually, you will find out…eventually. All things die, in the end.’

Rafael closed his flaming eyes, then. “Immortality is a lie,” He droned, almost as if he were reciting a teaching. “All things end.”

I took a deep, deep breath, then…

And held it for several seconds.

Ooookay then. I…didn’t really have the mental strength to contemplate that right now. Just the implication that there was a definitive afterlife of some kind, and I was talking to an almost…almost…

What, reverse Lich, currently hanging halfway in, and halfway out of it?

No. No, I just…couldn’t think about that right now.

I could have a breakdown later. Hopefully, way later, when my real body was fixed up.

Right now, I had more…I mean, I guess they were more pertinent questions, but goddamn was it hard to focus on them.

I wasn’t the only one, of course. Fade had tucked himself deeper into my side, closed his eyes, and flattened his ears as if to block out the world. While Sylvia…

Sylvia had frozen completely stiff at the words of Hermus. She hadn’t moved, shifted, or said a word in the last several minutes. Her hand was equally as immobile in mine. It almost felt like I was trying to comfort a metal statue, honestly.

I’d…just let her process that at her own pace.

There were other questions that needed answering.

“And…” I swallowed on a dry throat, despite my lack of need for water. “About you being the Father of Necromancy?”

‘Hmm. Because, I suppose in a word, I am,’ Hermus admitted casually. ‘I’ve already told you about my desire to better understand death. To do so, I sacrificed a great deal of my personal power and authority as a Great Spirit, to fully incarnate into a physical form, the remains of which lie before you. I chose a mortal existence, though an admittedly extended one, so that I may experience the full breadth of a physical life. During that life, I…attracted many people, drawn to the naturally powerful Death Aether I radiated.’

He chuckled then.

‘I chose the form of a mole, though one large enough to defend itself, because I had hoped that after much study, an underground life would dampen such a thing. I was of course much younger then, and much more naïve. Still…I chose not to drive these people away. Instead, we worked together to find a new understanding. I shared with them all that I knew, and they did the same. In that age, we created a new form of Sorcery, which you call Magic. Thousands of years ago, Necromancy was born in these very halls…’

Something…something about what he was saying sounded off to me. My instincts were shouting at me, screaming that something was wrong. But I couldn’t quite put my finger on what.

It only began to dawn on me, when Hermus spoke in an especially wistful tone.

‘You ask, young man, why the chamber that allowed the…‘Netherim Usurpers’, as you call them, to ascend to Divinity is directly above me.’

‘That would be because I taught them.’
