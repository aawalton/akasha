Edge shook his head to clear his mind as his focus returned to the present.

The team was ready to move on as soon as they finished harvesting the valuable monster parts Dragon’s Greed had identified. After extracting the fangs and claws, he sprayed the gory pile with Repel Water and stored everything in his vault.

He was drawing near the weight limit of his Spatial Storage Rune, so he decided to pop into his inner world to organize the haul, say hello to avatars living in his village, and check on his Companion. It wouldn’t take long thanks to the time dilation, and Edge wanted to make sure Rue had fully recovered from his brief exposure to the toxic taint filling the monsters’ world while severing the dimensional anchor from the lobster’s shell, saving the crew and everyone living in World’s End in the bargain.

“Hello, boy.” He dropped to one knee while running his fingers through the fox’s exquisitely soft fur. “It looks like you’re feeling better.”

The beast grumbled as the impression that he was recovering quickly but not back to 100% flowed through their bond. “I suppose that means you aren’t interested in some of the spiced sausages I’ve been saving as a special treat?”

The two-tailed fox’s ears twitched when they heard the word “sausage,” and he let out an enthusiastic yip. Rue followed Edge across his central chamber, which had taken the form of his father’s workshop after evolving to stage two. They walked past his reservoir, circled around his reactor, and came to a stop in front of the armored door that led to his Spatial Storage Room, which had manifested as a bank vault within his inner world.

The interior was jampacked with monster parts—just shy of the rune’s two-thousand-pound limit. Fortunately, the vault was spacious, and there was plenty of room to organize and manipulate the objects it contained. He walked over to a pair of magitech stasis boxes, smiling at the memory of Lilly gifting him the one with a defective seal during the early days of his adventure.

Edge cracked open the box that was in perfect condition, grinning at the surprise on Rue’s face when he realized he’d been tricked. The stasis box had blocked the scent of the sausages stuffed inside, or the beast would have discovered them right away. “How many do you think you earned for that stunt? One? Two?”

He laughed when he felt gluttony coursing through his Companion bond, along with a sentiment that translated roughly to, “You had better give me all of them if you want me to save your ass next time.”

“Fair enough, boy. You’ve earned it.” Edge handed the beast the entire string, watching him devour the tasty meat so fast it was like he had a spatial storage in his stomach. As far as sausage goes, that isn’t too far from the truth.

While the beast enjoyed his hero’s feast, Edge sorted the contents of his vault, using Dragon’s Greed to arrange everything by value. It wasn’t a perfect tool, since the skill couldn’t account for the economic forces at play within World’s End, but it was a great way to decide which bits and pieces of monsters and beasts were worth keeping, along with some random resources he’d found along the way.

Party of the way through, Chibime and Sea decided to join him. The Epic avatars were far more human than most of the gang. They supervised his efforts in a comical fashion, muttering to each other in sounds that almost sounded like speech, but were concepts and emotions rather than language.

He spent a long while staring at the dimensional anchor he had picked up on instinct. It was a bizarre amalgamation of purple metal that endlessly folded in on itself to form impossible angles that hurt his brain to contemplate. His gut told him pulling it out of his vault was a bad idea, and he made everyone promise not to go anywhere near it until he learned more.

When he was done, he bid his skills farewell, gave Rue a pat on the head, and promised to return in the morning for their daily training session. With that, Edge stepped out his core, removed the pile of resources he’d flagged, and set them on the ground.

The team picked up some of the materials and left the rest in a sheltered location. They were planning to post a job at the resource hunters’ society when they made it back to town, trading the location of the parts they’d left behind in exchange for a share of the profit. Since the rift had devoured all the corruption before it closed, the sub-biome was free of the toxic taint. Better still, the monsters had driven the local predators away, which would make the Haunted Vale unusually safe for at least a few more days.

Before they moved on, Edge summoned his Guide. He had been too distracted by everything that had happened in the monsters’ dimension and his conversation with Skill-Eater to focus on them until now, but it was time to peruse the enormous batch of updates he had accrued over the last six hours.

When the wisp of golden light that only he could see floated up to his face and flattened to form a screen, he eagerly began to read.

For defeating multiple cored opponents Stage 2 or above, your team has earned 90 Mortium. Your share is 17 Mortium.

That brought him to forty-nine lifecoins, even after spending twelve to steal the Rare and Epic powers that were digesting in his core. It was an astonishing amount of Mortium, highlighting just how extraordinary the series of events he had survived by the skin of his teeth truly was.

I shouldn’t have to worry about running out for a while, even if find more Rare skills to steal. I’ll consider making a few purchases from the Mortium exchange when we get back to World’s End.

He pulled up the quest-completion notice next.

You have completed the quest: Close the Interdimensional Portal.

Your individual contribution has been rated as: Extraordinary.

In addition to the trait detailed below, all participants have been granted the following reward:

1 Trait-Mutation Token.

Additionally, you have been granted the following individual reward:

500,000 Credits.

You have accomplished a notable feat and been awarded the following trait:

Monster Slayer

You have repelled an incursion from another world, slaughtering scores of monsters along the way. We’ve already established this wasn’t a natural event. Something did this on purpose, and if you want to live through the winter, you need to figure out what was responsible, how they did it, and make sure it doesn’t happen again.

I can’t gaze into a scion’s core or peer into another dimension, and there are forces at play obfuscating my senses. Right now, you know more about this situation than I do, so I’m counting on you to handle the matter until I can take direct action. On that note, it’s vital to our mutual survival that you don’t mention you noticed anything out of the ordinary in your quest and trait descriptions.

I will do my best to find a safe way for us to communicate directly. I’ll contact you when I do and explain everything. In the meantime, be careful. The planet’s movers and shakers are gearing up for war and any of the factions’ heavy hitters could erase you in the blink of an eye.

-Neo

Effects: +25% increased damage dealt to monstrous life forms. +25% reduced damage received from monstrous life forms. Enhanced ability to sense tainted magicytes. (This bonus damage does not augment magitech weapons, traps, and spellshots).

Edge had no idea what to make of the strange messages he had received from the being that referred to itself as Neo. Thus far, the warnings had helped him survive, and he intended to keep Neo’s existence a secret until he had a better grasp of the situation.

“What did you guys get for completing the System’s quest,” he asked after mulling the matter over.

It turned out that everyone had received the token and trait, but only Edge had been awarded Credits, which must have been a bonus for entering the rift and destroying the anchor. No one mentioned anything unusual about the quest-completion notice, which meant his text was unique.

“This trait is awesome,” Jumo said. “It’s like Crusader, but for monsters, with a detection bonus as a cherry on top.”

“Until we defeated the kaiju,” Mel added, “I had never heard of a trait that reduces damage. The bonus damage is fantastic too, since we fight monsters more often than we encounter artificial life forms.”

Trapper rubbed her the back of her neck as she stretched. “A heightened ability to sense corruption will help us hunt monsters and make it harder for them to ambush us. Not to mention, find rifts faster if we ever wind up in a situation like this again, although, gods’ willing, we won’t.”

“Professional shadowkillers would give their left arm for that trait,” Sakura said. “And the token is interesting too. Just like the Trait-Merger Token we received for wiping out the stampede, I’m almost positive this item didn’t exist before the anomaly. I have no idea why the System is creating new tokens after all these centuries, but I strongly suspect they are even more powerful than they appear at first glance.”

“I feel the same way,” Edge agreed. “That Trait-Merger Token gave me the full benefits of both components plus an additional effect, on top of freeing up a trait slot to equip something else.”

He would have to wait until he evolved to equip his new trait, as would Jumo, Sasha, and Mel. Sakura and Trapper had just hit stage three which meant they had plenty of free trait and skill slots to work with, along with some additional implant points. They had equipped Monster Slayer earlier in the day, and it was already making a big difference.

On that note, Edge confirmed the prompt at the bottom of his updates, causing a box to materialize by his boots. Inside was the token, along with a scrap of paper that listed its full description. He read it before turning his attention to the item itself.

Trait-Mutation Token

Unpredictably alter the effects of one of your traits. It might ruin a strong trait or make it even better, but that would be a risky move, and you would be foolish to try it. Using this token on a weak trait, however…

“Is it just me, or is the System getting stranger by the day?” Jumo asked when Edge read the description aloud.

You have no idea. He bit his tongue to keep himself from voicing that thought, since the token’s text wasn’t nearly as bizarre as the messages from Neo.

“That hint seems pretty straightforward.” Edge rubbed his chin. “I don’t want to wreck one of my traits, but I’m not using Fit and Toned. Even if the token makes it worse, I wasn’t planning to equip it again anyway.

“Using the Skill-Mutation Token felt risky as hell, since the results were irrevocably bound to a skill slot, even if the skill was worthless. But there isn’t a limit to how many traits I can earn, so I’m willing to find out what the token can do here and now.”

First, he pulled up his profile to read the current version.

Fit and Toned (unequipped)

You’ve managed to push your new body to the limit, maxing your conditioning across the board. I must admit that you’re looking pretty good. The monsters will have some eye candy to appreciate while they’re tearing you apart. That being said, you aren’t the baddest mofo in the dungeon. Not by a long shot.

Oh, and be careful—I don’t think the bone slime will be alive for much longer.

Effects: +1 to all physical attributes.

The description reminded that the System had started acting strange several months prior. It had begun with a few snarky comments and an uncomfortable interest in placing Edge in life-and-death dilemmas.

During his solo survival vacation in the Savage Garden, the messages had grown increasingly personal, and in some cases, incredibly helpful. They had displayed empathy by letting him know he hadn’t killed any of his allies when the dungeon sealed itself, a stark deviation from the clinical detachment the System had shown everyone during his decades’ long obsession with the Prison World feeds.

As unusual as that was, it was nothing compared to the most recent development. When the rift to the monsters’ homeworld opened, the text in his quest’s description sounded like it was written by a person who was terrified that the apocalypse had begun ahead of schedule.

That was the first time the author had referred to itself as Neo. While they were clearly related, Neo implied it was distinct from the System, warning him that no one else could see these communications, and it would be destroyed if he revealed its existence. Now, Neo wanted to find a safe way to meet and claimed to be actively helping Edge to the extent that it could.

There was no way that he was going to trust the entity that had seemed so eager to watch him die in a spectacularly gruesome manner without learning more, but there was one thing that made him willing to give it the benefit of the doubt. Whatever Neo was, it had thwarted the Ancients’ plans by sending him through the rift to destroy the anchor, saving World’s End in the process.

And after what Edge had experienced during his battle with the dark passenger and his blessedly brief visit to a parallel dimension, anyone with the power to stand in the Ancients’ way was someone he wanted on his side.

“Are you okay?” Melenia walked over and entwined her fingers within his own. “You looked like you were a million miles away.”

He put his arm around her shoulder. “Just reflecting on all the crazy shit that happened over the last few days. Let’s swap stories over dinner, but first, it’s time to find out what this token is all about.”

With that, he reached into the box and removed the disc that was sitting inside. It was the most extravagant token yet. It looked like someone had carved a slice out of a vibrant rainbow and trapped it within a crystal, creating a multifaceted gem that made iridescence seem drab by comparison.

“I would like to use this token on Fit and Toned, please,” Edge addressed the System. No matter what was going on, it never hurt to be polite to an entity that controlled not only the planet he lived on, but the most profitable entertainment industry in human history prior to the disaster.

When those words left his lips, the token rose from his fingers and started to spin. As it whirled faster, miniature bolts of many-hued lightning shot out, engulfing the canyon in a shimmering spectacle. It was gorgeous and utterly entrancing. The jaw-dropping display would have revealed their position to every predator in the Vale if the monsters hadn’t already driven them away. Sasha offered a round of applause while Mel oohed and Jumo let out a low whistle of appreciation.

Traits were magical boons granted directly by the System, so there wasn’t anything to sense inside his core, and since the trait wasn’t equipped right now, there weren’t any changes to his attributes either.

When the ostentatious but undeniably impressive light show was over, Edge pulled up his profile.

The trait Fit and Toned has become:

Not a Moron

Congratulations. You were able to solve a puzzle that might well have stumped a small child. As a reward, you get to make the most out of these new tokens I’ve been tinkering with.

Effect: +10% to all physical attributes.

Back to the snark I see. Maybe Neo is trying to stay in character after breaking it so spectacularly earlier. Whatever. That trait is badass and enduring a little verbal abuse is more than worth what I got in exchange. Traits that provide a percentage boost to attributes are rare and incredibly powerful. I can’t wait to equip it the moment I evolve.

“Well?” Trapper asked.

“Don’t leave me hanging,” Sakura added. “Did it give you something worthwhile.”

“Indeed, it did.” Edge smiled wide and shared the details.

“Damn.” Jumo reached into his belt pouch and pulled out his token. “That trait would be perfect for me.”

They didn’t know if the mutation tokens would always produce the same result when used on the same trait, but the warrior had maxed his maxed out his physical conditioning shortly after Edge, clearing the requirements to receive Fit and Toned. Jumo really wanted that trait, so he was willing to give it a try.

To his delight, it worked, although his mutated trait was called “Brawny” for some inexplicable reason, and the description was cut and dried. After being treated to a second pyrotechnic performance that was every bit as wonderful as the first, Jumo began to laugh.

“Fucking awesome. I had Fit and Toned equipped, and I just netted so many cycles’ worth of physical attributes that it’s hard to control my body. Brawny is already worth its weight in gold, and it will keep on scaling as I continue to grow.”

“This discovery is huge.” Sakura nodded. “I don’t know how to get more of those tokens but being able to turn weak traits into strong ones is amazing, particularly if the outcome is predictable after the first person takes the plunge.”

“Earning Fit and Toned takes some effort,” Trapper added, “unless you use Regeneration to streamline the process like Edge. But it’s a trait anyone can obtain if they try. If we can figure out how our people back home can earn these tokens, it will give Puppet Town an edge during this critical juncture.”

Mel chuckled at the unexpected pun and soon everyone was laughing too. “It’s been far too long since we heard a good edge joke. I can’t wait to share that one with Earl. I hope everyone in the Ivory Plains is doing all right.”

Me too, Melenia.” Edge wiped away tears from the corners of his eyes. “Me too.”

“I found another monster before I got distracted,” Sakura said when she stopped giggling. “We should be able to ambush it and take it down with an alpha strike.”

“You heard the woman.” Trapper slipped on her pack and unlimbered her crossbow. “Daylight’s burning and there’s a pile of Mortium out there with our names on it.” Everyone grabbed their gear and fell into formation as the longest monster hunt of Edge’s career entered its final hours.
