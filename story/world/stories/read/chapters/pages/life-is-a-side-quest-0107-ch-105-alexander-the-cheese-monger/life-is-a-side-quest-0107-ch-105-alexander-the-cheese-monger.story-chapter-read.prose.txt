Alexander was forced several times to crawl on hands and knees to get past the doorways and waddled like a duck up a flight of stone stairs. He now sat on a tiny stool on one side of a tiny long table. The prince, flanked by half a dozen guards, sat at the far end. Min Min stood nervously on the side of the room, glancing from Flick perched upon Alexander’s head to the Prince.

“Please help yourself to some snack cheese as we wait for my uncle to accompany join us.”

A tiny plate with a few bites of what appeared to be several cubed pieces of a soft white cheese was set before him. A spoonful of a clear red jelly was on another tiny plate beside it.

The prince took a bite of the cheese and squeaked in delight, “A fine year making!”

Alexander, not wanting to be rude, took the cheese, dipped it in the red jelly, and took a bite. The creamy flavor of a decent gouda hit his tongue along with a raspberry jam. It was standard fare for a charcuterie board back home. He lifted a piece to Flick, who shrank back and shrugged his nose.

“Min Min! Come sit and take eat some.” The Prince patted the seat next to himself.

Min Min shook her head, “No thank you my Prince.” Alexander noticed she lifted her nose in the air. Turning down some advances, perhaps?

“Hmph, so what is your name calling outlander?” The prince asked.

“Alexander, your grace.”

“I am Prince Wensley, well met. How do you find like the cheese?”

“It is excellent. I imagine cheese is harder to come by in these parts. Thank you.”

“Good cheese is. We can make cheeses easily enough, it is quality we have lost. We hear legends tell of the cheeses of our homeland. They had access to more cultures for the age making.”

Seeing an opportunity, Alexander reached into his fanny pack. Several spears were lowered in his direction as he did so. The prince waved his hand and the guards relaxed.

“I would be pleased if you would like to try this vintage, your grace.” Alexander pulled out a brick of his aged white cheddar and handed it to a guard who took it with a suspicious look.

The guard marched it to the prince.

“I warn you Alexander giant, I am immune to poisons.”

“It is still wrapped in the condition I purchased it from the market. I have never used poisons either, so I wouldn’t know the first thing about blending it with cheese.”

The prince nodded, utterly confident Alexander was speaking the truth. He cut the plastic and waved it in front of his nose.

“This! What is this!? I cannot make read the words!”

“Barber’s 1833 Vintage Reserve aged cheddar. It comes to market after two years of aging.”

The prince attempted to carve off a slice, but it crumbled into several pieces.

“The salt crystals make it less manageable to cut evenly, but the dryness and creaminess blend well with the salt,” Alexander explained.

The prince took a bite.

“Heavens! This!” The prince took a sharp breath. “This is the best and worst bad experience of Wensley's life! Is this the levels of cheese we have lost? My heart hurt aches.”

“What is this commotion tears I hear from nephew?” A cool warm voice said moving in from the side door.

“Uncle! This outlander has a cheese that you must try!”

The uncle went from a composed stride to a darting pace and picked up the brick of cheese to give it a smell.

“CHEDDAR!?” He exclaimed taking a piece off the plate. He nibbled it softly and closed his eyes. Tears started streaming from his face. “My home, my farmlands! What weight of emotion I feel in this small nibble bite of history. I feel the love poured into the make crafting.”

Alexander was sure they had industrial metal barrels of the stuff being made at all times, but didn’t spoil the mood. He was certain the original recipe at least was a heartfelt craft.

“Uncle! Please settle calm yourself! This giant is come to these lands but hours ago. He has found a portal to return to his land home before and seeks it again!”

“Truly?”

“He has not told a lie false yet.”

“Forgive pardon this old mouse. The cheese has taken over my faculties. I am Count Manchego, a pleasure to meet you giant.”

“A pleasure. And you appreciate it more than most, I am sure, as it is a part of your history and culture.” Alexander bowed his head. Seeing an opportunity, he pulled out a catalogue. “If you are interested, we have a fresh market delivery system at my place of work. You could order any number of cheeses and buttons once we establish shipping routes.”

“Hah! A thoughtful calm giant this one is and shrewd. Not like the brutes that roam the other lands.” The older mouse said combing his hands through his tuft of hair under his chin.

“The quality of the paper and view images make me feel as if I could reach out and pluck eat it off the page! I would love to go over this with you but…” the count said once again regaining his composure, “…first I would hear your tale story so we can gain information on the portal way.”

“Certainly, it would be my pleasure.” Alexander said, again with a small bow.

Alexander told them briefly of the first time he had entered the land of shadows. The mice looked surprised to learn the way Alexander could not see the end clearly and how distances stretched for him. Squeaks sounded about the room when he mentioned meeting the Warlock. He left the artifact he had taken vague, as he didn’t want more questions about his class of tower. When he told them about his return through the portal their mouths stood agape.

“How long!? When did you travel this way portal?” Min Min almost shouted.

“I am not sure about time dilation here, but in my world’s time it may have been close to four months ago.”

Min Min scrunched her face and grabbed her chin. “It may be enough.”

“I have discovered a way to disrupt all magic making in and area… by… explode destroying magic resources.”

“Or of course the palace dungeon for sneak hiding and destroying crystal magics.”
