A so very short time ago…

The Director was asleep. Dreaming, to preserve sanity. They were as entertaining as they needed to be, for as long as they needed to be, and composed of hidden relevance. The timeframe was inconsequential, naturally, as she existed on timescales beyond the blinks of this or that cosmos.

So much of her was the cosmos. Her thoughts, captured and blended, flowering into cruder, more externally beautiful substance. Pneuma into so many delightful snowflakes.

Despite the unconscious quality to her self-exile, it was within a cage of intention. It was part of a plot, which she allowed only to unfurl enough to know as a coded reminder within her persistent lethargy: Orchestration. This echo alone sifted back into reality, and she knew that subconscious power would enact what was needed. The sleep, the self-banishment, was critical for the actual stew of ingredients that would finish the [Game].

Though, naturally, that would not truly be the end.

She was very surprised when something actually happened in her space. There was — impossibly — an invader.

It was a minuscule presence, but the actual occurrence in and of itself was deeply disturbing. She almost obliterated it even before ascertaining so much as the form. But she hesitated at the taste of the energy, and allowed her mind to absorb the being’s information.

If she had teeth, they would’ve chattered. This was not good. Not good at all.

It was a bloody Iteration! More than that. It was the Iteration. The Returner. The word alone was too much to be here — to be known. She blotted out the rest deliberately, with no small effort.

She almost blooped the Iteration right back out with any knowledge of the event stripped, as needed, as required… but she hesitated.

What did it matter? Knowledge could not go with her. The Iteration did not appear to know anything yet, and thus, she had not potentially ruined the balance of Orchestration. The Iteration was thankfully clueless about the things that must not come into the Director’s active consciousness.

The Iteration had only just discovered her hidden [Fate] domain.

That was why she popped in. The typical surreal mindquest out of time and space, feeling out her new muscles through reality. She could go anywhere, anytime, in that compacted moment. It was, in fact, encouraging that she’d somehow inverted into the greater consciousness.

How plucky and precocious.

The Director hesitated because, though knowledge couldn’t be allowed to return… subconscious elements could. Vague ones. Tiny ones.

Dare she trifle with such danger? Why risk it?

She found her internal reasoning at least partially irrational, yet undeniable: she simply wanted to.

The Director ensnared the Iteration and surrounded her in a reality construct, giving her the semblance of form and sequential time. She made something peaceful and tranquil to put her at ease — a scene of the exile planet, Earth, with a quaint bench overlooking a mild lagoon of gentle waves, and a sky of many beautiful clouds and colors, as if the sun had set but a moment ago. The Iteration was initially set to be sitting on it.

Immediately, the girl jumped up, head spinning around, obviously sensing a grand presence about. “Holy sh-shit! Hello?!” She looked down at herself and suddenly covered her nakedness with her hands. “Why the fuck am I naked?!” That seemed to be directed at herself, however.

The Director spoke, as if from the sky, rich and feminine, “Hello, Samantha.”

The Iteration appeared quite shocked, her eyes wide. “Y-your voice, it’s like…” She swallowed hard. “Are you God?” She meant it in a non-gendered way.

“We always answer ‘yes’ to this, naturally.”

At first, the girl didn’t know how to take this. Then she smiled and coughed out a laugh. “I… guess that makes sense…” She swallowed again, looking around. “Where am I? Why am I here?”

“You’ve pulled yourself into a higher realm. As to why, and other such Big Fucking Questions… Perhaps you should sit down?”

Samantha took a deep, deep breath and nodded. “Yeah… I think that’s probably a good idea.” She sat down on the bench, legs pressed together, arms covering her breasts, blinking out at the lagoon. “It’s very beautiful here. I… feel like I know it.” Her eyes flickered upward. “I feel like I know you.”

“Because you do know me, Samantha. Because everything and everyone ever is your deja vu, and you feel it especially here, where all things are connected. All things are… one, you might say.” She left a dramatic pause. “First, I have to preface everything with the following: you cannot remember me, this place, or this conversation when you go back. Perhaps never, depending on your performance down there. Nonetheless, I believe there is a reason you’re here, or you wouldn’t be. Do you understand?”

Blinking, Samantha nodded slowly. “Bummer. But, um… I can see that. C-can… something intangible be imparted, essentially? Upon returning.”

“Well, aren’t you an intuitive girl? Yes, that’s the idea.” She left another pause of gravitas. “If nothing else, hear and know this: you can trust yourself and those instincts of yours. They come from a more powerful source than you could possibly fathom. Those that came before you, those who would come after, all are linked to you. You are infinite. Draw strength from this, and know the incredible depths with which you are supported.”

“Wow…” She seemed to hesitate.

“Speak, please, Samantha. I already know what it is. You might as well express yourself.”

“Right… I… can’t shake this feeling. Are you… am I…?”

The Director couldn’t help but laugh. “Yes. You are my personification in reality. The vehicle by which I’d enforce my will. Under restraints, caught in the competition of a game that lost its proper rules, trying to cheat our way out of a jam. Perhaps it’s better for you to think of yourself like my daughter. It’s truer to say you’re a spiritual clone spawned on a lower-dimensional game board. Regardless, our connection is intrinsic.”

“Incredible,” she breathed. She suddenly laughed, sitting back and kicking her feet, hands coming up to smush together at her mouth. “I’m an even bigger deal than, like, Heracles!”

“That’s quite fair to say, yes.” The sky suddenly went dark and clear, showing the vibrancy of the stars amidst the Milky Way. “We’re simply the deal. Wranglers of every system in every sky.” She made a bright supernova spawn, brilliant and colorful as Samantha watched on, mystified. “Listen: just as you are supported, you must know that there is a plan. There is an Orchestration. Order shall prevail, and there will be an end to the misery of chaos. Trust this, too, and seek it without quarter or relief. Drive forever forward in the acceleration of our machine, and nothing will stop you.”

In the sky, ten stars aligned in a perfect circle. “Do this, and I promise you, all that you cherish, I will cherish. Finish our race, and it will be your name carried forward into our eternity.”

She meant far more with that than Samantha could likely understand. That was probably better off.

Samantha seemed to take this solemnly as she looked up. “Mine? But what about… yours?”

The Director huffed. “Mine? No. I’ve long lost the meaning, I’m afraid.” Suddenly, with this admittance, something triggered, and an epiphany of Fate hit her, revealing the fullness of the purpose for why this encounter had occurred. Perhaps… within the [Game Logic], it was a kind of pre-embedded reward for them both, for the Great Mother and her Daughter’s terribly foolish risks, gambling everything just to connect and converse. “Now then. There is one last thing I can impart, which will become known to you in the final hour of revelation. Like a code, may it unlock a critical warning… May you hear these words echo in that moment, though we both cannot know the meaning now…”

She spoke the words necessary, and time swallowed them up with great hunger.

Crow kicked through the ashes of the manor, trying not to be too disappointed in what he found. It wasn’t easy, because there was nothing. A dozen others picked through, equally helpless to find anything but dust.

Bloody Dominion. They’re nothing if not thorough at burying things.

They’d utilized the piece of the murdering artifact that Tashome had kept to track down the owner. From the get-go, the ‘signal’ was weak and already fading, and they now had the evidence for why: whoever it was had been slain. Likely, the remains of the artifact were among the dust, too. Not for a second did he think that solved anything, though.

Whoever had this done, or who did it themselves: that’s the mastermind. A smart and elusive figure. Based on the scraps of evidence we have, likely a Witchhunter.

It didn’t matter how much they looked within the remains to find something, anything — everything had been disintegrated, bleached, and neutralized by a specialist in eliminating traceable auras. Crow wasn’t an expert, but Zephyr knew a little about this, and usually killing the ‘source’ was used as a convenient sacrifice to power the potency, as it were. There was nothing left physically or spiritually.

The little piece they’d fashioned to track down this source had simply brought them to a graveyard. One without beneficial headstones, no less.

I just need names, damn it.

He stood in the ashes and looked out at the surrounding terrain. A bit of scorched farmland. A few other supporting buildings were given the same treatment as the carcass of the manor under his feet, likely once a stable and a barn among them. In the distance, there was a ruin of another, smaller farmstead. Probably many decades in the decaying, if not longer. There had been a community here, once, likely as what surrounded a trade route north.

There were lots of birds in the sky. Those were ‘his.’ Servitor scouts lent by Samantha.

Crow watched as one of them swooped low and transformed from a hawk into a humanoid figure, which then parked immediately in front of him. The Faerie Dragon, Herne.

The handsome figure with antlers growing out of his head nodded to him and said, “The area is not unoccupied. A village, perhaps a day’s travel west, along the road. Scattered farmland.”

Crow nodded. “So these people would be known, to some degree. Get those best for it — and that are available — on top of digging for more. Your spy buddies, preferably a Siren, if possible. Names and other information. Speed over subtlety, mind.”

Herne raised an eyebrow. “They won’t like that. The way of our goddess-”

“Yes, I know. Save the speech about what’s ‘wise.’ We control this area now, Herne. Restwater will be our city all too soon. The old clandestine habits aren’t necessary in these parts anymore. Possibly nowhere.”

Herne nodded slowly. “As you wish, milord.”

Crow had to let the title go. It was just easier to let that be the assumption. It was certainly better than those silly cultic titles. Some things were still best left unpublic, after all. “Did you note a cemetery?”

“Of course. Why?”

“Get names there, too, and dig them up for any persistent artifacts of use. Lead the men over there to help, I suppose. Just one or two articles each. A bone or scrap if nothing else. They can be reburied. Preferably, all done without the outward appearance of disturbance. So… earth magic. I believe we have two of those capabilities here with us.”

Herne had a distasteful look on his face. “Is that truly necessary?”

“Every name is potential information from their ghost; the unjust dead are our army of informants. Depending on their soul’s perspective, at least. I’ll question any of them I can for a sliver of advantage. So… yes, Herne. It is.”

Frowning, Herne nonetheless nodded. “It shall be done, milord.” He promptly transformed back into a hawk and flew back up into the air.

Crow crouched down in the debris and scooped his hand into the uniform ash, letting it fall back out. He wondered if this or that bit of glittery dust was the remains of what killed Agatha. If true, at least the pressures of their hunt may have ended its little reign of terror over the years. In one sense, he’d have arrived at the end of a road.

But that definitely wasn’t enough for him.

Surely, at least a few of you feel wronged by this result. Or were you all loyal to the end, brainwashed and controlled? I guess we’ll find out.
