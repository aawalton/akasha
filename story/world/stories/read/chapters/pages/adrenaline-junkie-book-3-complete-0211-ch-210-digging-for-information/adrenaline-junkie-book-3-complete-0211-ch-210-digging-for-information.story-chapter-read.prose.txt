“Thank you,” Archie said to the waitress – Dina, as she’d introduced herself, and also the one responsible for soaking both him and the table in lemonade earlier – as she handed over a cardboard tray holding five medium-sized cups of lemonade and two small cartons of fries.

Archie passed her a Silver Coin as payment, watching as she dropped it into her coin waist bag and rifled through it, calculating his total amount he needed to receive as change faster than himself before then pressing his change into his palm: a Half-Silver Coin and fifteen Copper Coins. That now brought his total wealth to one Half-Silver Coin, sixteen Copper Coins, and a Half-Copper Coin.

“I’m so sorry once again,” Dina said, the words tumbling out almost as fast as she handed over his change. “I never do that normally, it's just last night was exhausting and seeing your–”

“It’s fine, water under the bridge and all,” Archie waved her off as he stored away the drinks, the cartons of fries, and the fresh stack of coins into his Spatial Storage.

Considering he’d been cleaned up not long after, thanks to a rather thorough deep cleaning skill from Dina, plus the half-off discount she’d given as an apology, Archie didn’t think much of the sticky downpour anymore. If anything, her accident had worked in his favor: the spill revealed that his journal wasn’t blank at all, but written in invisible ink. And really, he understood perfectly well that not everyone would find Volos and Arsenic as adorable as he did.

Aoife had thought they were cute, having worked as a miner and seen her fair share of multi-legged critters crawling out of cracks in the rock. Tim, on the other hand, insisted they were basically just wingless, multilegged land-dragons and liked to treat them with the sort of awe normally reserved for apex predators. Henry had taken longer, a full week, but eventually grew accustomed to them after spending enough time around the two hatchlings to vet them properly, ensuring they wouldn’t be a danger to Camilla.

Just as Archie was about to head out of the tavern after Dina said she had to get back to waitressing, he stopped mid-stride as a sudden thought came to him and turned on his heel, to where he spotted Dina weaving between tables with a tray tucked under one arm and beelined towards her immediately.

“Hey, Dina,” Archie called, lifting his hand in an awkward wave. “Sorry for bothering you again so soon, but I was wondering if you could help me out with something?”

She glanced up, adjusting the new set of dirty dishes she added beside the ones already on the tray she was carrying before turning to face Archie. “Depends,” she said, eyeing him warily yet still embarrassed over their initial encounter. She’d already given him an apology and a half-off sale after her little mess-up, but after he’d supposedly forgiven her, he came back asking for help. “What can I help with?”

“Simple question,” Archie said, leaning slightly against the counter as she set the tray down. “You’ve lived here long?”

That earned him a confused look. She blinked a couple of times before tilting her head. “My whole life,” she replied slowly. Then, narrowing her eyes as if studying him, she added, “And judging by that question, you’re definitely a newcomer who came for the New Year's Festival last night. As I’m pretty sure there isn’t a soul in Creavenboor who I don’t know or haven’t served before.”

Dina froze for a beat, then her eyes went wide as realization and embarrassment hit. “In the tavern! Serving food in the tavern,” she blurted, words tumbling over each other before her voice became barely audible, even to his own ears as a D-Grade. “Not… not anything else.”

Archie chuckled. “Hole in one.” He rolled his shoulders back, then followed up, “I’ve been hearing about a dungeon in the Primeval Forest and was wondering which gate I’d need to take to get there. Any chance you know the way?”

“Oh~,” her face brightened with recognition. “You mean the Hollowed Redwood Dungeon? The new dungeon that was formed by the System a couple months ago?”

“Yeah, that’s the one,” Archie answered, but was slightly confused by what Dina meant by formed by the System; didn’t people create dungeons? I really need to find and go through some library, and or access the list of books Bralmir downloaded onto my Aetherglass while I was doing my D-Grade Trials, and see if I can find something about it.

“The closest gate to reach the Hollowed Redwood Dungeon is the South Gate.” Dina lifted a finger as if pointing in the direction. “Just head straight down the main road after you leave the city, and you’ll eventually spot it. You’ll know you’re there when you see this massive boulder shaped like a hog with a tortoise shell strapped to its back.”

“Ah, also, it’s a four-stage dungeon with a lot of strong creatures inside, so you should go with a team,” she added.

“…Huh. Weird landmark,” Archie nodded, storing the mental picture away. “I appreciate it.”

He made to turn away but hesitated, then leaned back on the counter again. “Actually, one last thing. Do you know where I could find Baron Alric Velstrome?”

The response to his question was immediate as Dina’s brow furrowed in confusion as she moved to place more glass bottles in the tray she was holding. “…That’s… an odd request,” she said carefully. “Why do you want to know?”

Archie raised his hands as if to ward off suspicion. “I know, I know, it sounds weird. But there’s a reason. My boss back in Marl’geth, who’s a Magitech merchant, sent me here to hand-deliver documents for the Baron. The only issue is, the bastard thought it’d be hilarious to not tell me where to find him once I got to Creavenboor. The guy was pissed at me for something I did years ago and still hasn’t gotten over it.”

Her suspicion softened a little, though not fully gone. “Ah. That explains it, I guess. Well… you’ll probably find him at the City Hall. He’s the Financial Advisor here and works directly under the City Lord.” She leaned in slightly and lowered her voice. “But be careful around him. I’ve heard he’s got a temper. Nasty one.”

Archie hummed, filing that away. “Duly noted. Do you know what he looks like? I’d hate to confuse him with some other guy and get flogged or something for messing up.”

That actually made her chuckle for a brief moment before fading back into disbelief. “Wait, seriously? Your boss didn’t even give you a description?”

Archie let out a long, defeated sigh and spread his arms helplessly.

“…Unbelievable.” Dina shook her head, sympathy sneaking past her skepticism. “Your boss sounds like my old one. Always thought it was funny to make people scramble.” She said before letting out a deep sigh and continuing, “I’ve only seen the Baron once, during a public execution where the City Lord paraded traitors out into the square and made an example of them. The Baron was there on the platform beside him and listing out each and every tax violation they made and their embezzling before the City Lord carried out their execution.”

Archie tilted his head, waiting for the details.

“He’s… rather portly, four chins and all. Bald on the top of his head, though he’s still got these tufts of orange hair on the sides. His face was cleanly shaven during the time I saw him. He is also shorter than you by maybe a palm’s length, and he has this big, purple, bulbous nose.” She wrinkled her nose. “Hard to miss him, honestly.”

Archie smirked faintly. “That’s more than enough, thanks.”

Dina shrugged, brushing down her apron as a couple drops of some liquid from a mug she was holding dripped down it. “Just… don’t get on his bad side. I’ve seen what happens when people do.”

“Duly noted,” Archie repeated, giving her a short nod. He adjusted his cloak, pushed off the counter, and finally stepped toward the tavern door for real this time.

Outside, the streets of Creavenboor greeted him with the sounds of vendors that seemed to have just finished setting up shop while he went in to grab a drink.

After having gathered information from Dina, the Hollowed Redwood Dungeon had just jumped to the top of his to-do list, not just because it’d be a good place to make some coin and grind, but because it would give him the opportunity to crack open that damn journal and pour lemonade on it to allow him to read the invisible ink it was written in.

It was better to do it in the dungeon, as he would be away from crowds and away from prying eyes. As from what he’d picked up in the Group Tutorial, Dungeons were instanced, which meant that while he was in the dungeon, only he would be inside it, unless of course he was entering the dungeon with a team.

Archie adjusted his stride, heading south through the crowd with his hands crossed and tucked behind his head. Primeval Forest it is.

The noise of Creavenboor followed him the entire length of the main street that led towards the South Gate was filled with vendors shouting out various and varying prices about their wares, people going about their day and chatting either on their communication devices or with others about varying topics, and he sounds of horns blaring at other vehicles to move when the traffic lamp swapped to a green go sign. He was so caught up in the bustling about that it felt like, in an instant, he arrived at the South Gate.

Following a quick interrogation with the guards, he was asked for his name, identification card, place of residency prior, and how long he planned to be gone, among many other things. After scrawling into their logbook that he’d be back before sundown, they waved him on and moved onto the next person waiting in line to leave the city.

Writing down that he’d be back by sundown gave him approximately thirteen hours, give or take an hour or so, to roam the Primeval Forest and locate the dungeon. If it took longer than two hours to find the dungeon, then he would continue to explore the forest instead and clear the dungeon tomorrow.

One of the guards, a broad-shouldered man with a scar down his left cheek, gave a curt nod after Archie handed him back the logbook. “Remember, last night was a festival exception. When you come back into the city, it’s five Copper for reentry.”

“Got it,” Archie said, offering a short wave before stepping through the gates and out into open country.

Archie rolled his shoulders after he passed through the gates and walked a couple of yards away from the gate before breaking into a sprint. The rush of air across his face reminded him just how much faster his body had become since his D-Grade Evolution.

However, not even two minutes into his sprint, sudden, frantic tapping on his skull  from hundreds of legs pulled him from his musings.

Archie grimaced, already knowing what that meant as the taps became even more frantic. Alright, alright, I get it. Just give me a second.

He slowed his pace enough to reach into his Spatial Storage and fish out a carton of fries. Without missing a step, he pinched two between his fingers, held them up, and immediately felt two sets of tiny claws scramble from within his hair.

Arsenic, the more impatient of the two, snapped both right out of his fingers, scuttling back up to hide in his hair while greedily munching. Volos wasn’t far behind, tapping his skull insistently until Archie handed over another pair of fries.

“There,” Archie muttered under his breath, watching the two hatchlings crunch down like starved wolves. “Happy now?”

Volos chirped once, muffled by the fry still clamped between his mandibles, while Arsenic gave an approving clicking noise before also hiding inside his hair.

Archie huffed out a laugh through his nose, shaking his head as he picked up speed again.
