The more skyfoxes I killed, the more of the rest would splinter off from the group. Taking advantage of their fear, I doubled down on my attacks, combining magic with swordplay. If anyone with a mage card could see me now, they’d probably be disgusted by my lack of skills. Even a Magician could achieve way more with half of what I had. Then again, I doubted they would last ten seconds in a dream realm.

What exactly did you do, Marcus? I couldn’t stop thinking about what he had said.

Even if he had become his rule-master’s favorite, it was way too soon to earn favors of that nature. Or was that the way the game was played? Right now, I was also a rule-master’s favorite, and that was just the beginning. Despite all the new tricks Marcus and the pact holders had obtained, I still had a few things up my sleeve.

“Flee!” I ordered, using the Emperor card.

As before, the effect lasted less than a second, but that amount of time was enough to cast a mega circle. This time when the stone spires emerged, they weren’t in the shape of a ring, but specifically shot up beneath eight dream beasts that had the misfortune of coming too close to the ground.

“Is that all?” I shouted, raising both divine swords in the air. “Come at me!”

The action seemed to have been the final straw. From here on, there was no doubt that I had bested the entire skulk. Only a few of the stronger sky foxes remained behind for a few seconds, estimating their odds.

“Serp!” I put my divine blades in my inventory, then summoned him.

The sword glistened in my hand. It had lost its previous shape, but still emanated bloodlust at a level that surpassed even Marcus’ blood weapons. After all, he remained a snake and, now that he had grown, was eager to test his fangs.

With a snarl and a hiss, the sky foxes flew off towards the horizon, leaving only one behind.

“I guess it’s you and me.” I pointed the tip of my saber at the cerulean-white creature. “Do you find me interesting enough?”

Part of me regretted having to deliver such a good specimen to a deity. It wasn’t like I had much of a choice. I didn’t have the Tamer, and my Beastmaster was too low to do anything about it.

“Leave, Pup,” I said in a calm tone. “You’ll just get in the way.”

The creature whined. It had been incredibly loyal since I had captured it. All I had to do was say the word, and it would transform into a sabertooth monster and charge at my enemy regardless of the likelihood of success.

“Pup,” I repeated the order.

There was one final whine before the beast dissolved into mist. A moment later, I unsummoned Serp as well.

“Well, now we’re all alone.” I took a step in the skyfox’s direction. “You’ve seen what I’m capable of. Think you can match it?”

There was no warning. Dozens of participants had died, uttering those words. All of them had become cocky after surviving half a year in the games, to the point that they thought they could master it all. I knew precisely what the skyfox was thinking. It had observed most of my attacks, so it was fully aware that I wasn’t to be trifled with.

In contrast, I hadn’t seen any of its cards. If I had to guess based on its memory stat, there was a good chance it had a lot of them.

In the blink of an eye, the monster vanished.

Speed cards, I thought as I leaped to the side.

A blast struck the spot I had been a moment ago, hitting the ground with the force of a falling meteor.

Quicksilver? I wondered. No, that was a Grand Arcana card. This felt a lot more like a Minor Arcana, possibly from a house I couldn’t recall right now.

The attack was immediately followed up by a slash of the tail. Striking me like a steel bat, it attempted to crack my protective stone shell, though only managed to thrust me backwards.

Magic circles appeared on my back, countering the force. The shock was substantial, as if someone had knocked the air out of my lungs.

Stone shape, I thought, causing spikes to emerge from my stone armor and not a moment too soon.

A giant paw slammed into my chest. The knives had pierced through, causing grey vapors to trickle out.

Stone shape!

The spikes rapidly extended, yet before they could clamp on the creature’s paw, it pulled it away, disappearing a split second later.

“Stop!” I shouted, using my Emperor’s card.

A mega spell circle appeared on the ground, shooting out a column of water into the air. Then, I saw it. Despite its speed, the skyfox wasn’t able to move through water—not faster than any normal creature. The same cards that let it split through air now were powerless, granting me a golden opportunity.

I drew my divine blades and threw them at its hind legs. Unhindered by water, they zipped on, wounding the skyfox just as I planned.

Zap! I ordered, casting a wind circle beneath me.

The beast spun around, shaken by the dose of pain. That didn’t shatter its spirit. Instead of flying, it slashed the water with its front legs. Claws the size of swords had emerged from its paws, slicing through everything until they struck the ground.

Instantly, my spell circle faded, causing all the existing water to splash back down.

I summoned Serp and darted forwards. Descending upon it with a butterfly strike.

“You were toying with me?” I used a multi-attack, instantly followed by a force thrust.

With its hind legs wounded, the sky fox parried all my attacks with its front paws.

Restrict! Restrict! Restrict! I kept up with the attack. This ability was one it hadn’t seen me use.

The exchange of strikes continued. Dozens of restrictions were implied, and yet that barely seemed to faze it. The beast was constantly changing its attacks, using different skill cards. Not only that, but it would often counterattack as well.

Parts of my stone armor shattered, barely protecting me from a lethal blow. The tips of its claws ripped through my clothes, slicing into my skin.

Instantly, I used the Two of Chalice to heal my wounds and the Stoneweaver to reform my armor. More spell circles formed on my hands and arms, launching a wave of fire at the skyfox. One breath of icy chill and they were instantly negated.

You can use magic? Instinctively, I leaped back.

I hadn’t seen any spell circles, so it had to be a single ability card.

As I stood, a stir in the air caught my attention. Any normal person would have ignored it, but the Listener care made it stand out from the background. Without thinking, I leaped to the side, performing a circular slash.

The serpent blade bounced off a set of teeth on the side of a dark blue skyfox that flew by. Petty as a fox could be, the creature had taken the opportunity to attack me from behind. I could bet that there were others like it, circling me high above like vultures, waiting for their moment to strike.

“Piece of shit!” I spun around.

The blue skyfox was flying straight at my opponent. Before anyone could react, the cearulean-white beast leaped forward. It had no intention of attacking me, choosing to devour the other creature instead.

I just watched as the size of the monster doubled. The divine blades in its legs trembled, though it wasn’t able to push them out. Even such a powerful creature couldn’t negate a piece of divinity.

“No, you don’t!” I dashed at it.

No matter what it took, I had no intention of letting it escape. The creature was one in a million. I could go to sleep on the skyfox hill a thousand times and still not find anything like it.

The skyfox looked at me. It had every opportunity to fly away. Even wounded by the divine blades, it had gained enough strength to escape, and there was nothing I could do about it.

“Zap!” I shouted, aiming to gain some time rather than anything else.

A moment later, the creature was gone. One second after that, it reappeared right in front of me. Its massive mouth, three times larger than myself, opened, eager to devour me in one bite.

They always fuck up in the end. I tightened my grip round Serp.

“Everything but the heart,” I said as the jaws slammed shut.

For a split second, the skyfox might have even believed that it had won. Given its speed, there was no way for me to counteract. Unfortunately for it, Serp was a lot faster. As he drew blood and flesh from the sleeping me back in the trial-world, the blade spontaneously grew, coiling around me to form a protective cocoon. It didn’t stop there. The head of the platinum snake grew further into the monster’s flesh, shredding everything around it.

I had no basic concept of skyfox anatomy. I suspected that Serp didn’t either, but if there was one thing he could do without fail, it was finding the heart of a living creature.

Standing calmly in the darkness, I did my best to ignore the growing numbness in my arm. I could feel Serp extend, just as I could feel the skyfox trying to resist. Two monsters were fighting around me now, and it could be said that I was in the belly of both of them. There was little doubt that this was my win, though not one I had achieved.

Strength is more than a weapon, I thought.

Avon used to say it a lot. It was an amusing concept, but he was wrong, at least to a degree. Strength was the refusal to die and use anything at one’s disposal to achieve victory.

After a few seconds, Serp uncoiled. I was back in the bleakness of the dream again. It seemed a lot smaller now, only a patch of barren soil thirty feet wide, surrounded by a thick wall of fog on all sides. Serp’s platinum body was the only thing left, taking up all the space and even going into the fog.

It wasn’t anything much, he said in his typical boastful fashion. Just fast. And still slower than me!

“Yes, Serp.” I stepped out of him, making my way to the only other place thing he had coiled around. “You’re much better.”

Without asking, I reached forward. The blade segments moved just enough to form a hole as large as my hand. When I reached in, I could feel a cool sensation as if my hand was in cold jelly. Then, I squeezed.

I woke up.

I was back in the Crystal Fields, holding Serp in my hand. My chest and hand were bleeding. One set of wounds was from the serpent blade. Even now, Serp had his fangs into my hand. Knowing him, he probably wanted praise for being so reserved when it came to consuming parts of me.

The other wounds were a result of the fight. Despite occurring in a dream, the skyfox’s dreambite ensured that any wounds were transferred to my awakened state.

Your have received five booster spheres for fulfilling the request.

A message appeared before my eyes.

The deity has offered you a further booster sphere for the uniqueness of the specimen.

The spheres appeared in my hand. There were six of them, all different colors.

Just one bonus? I thought, though I didn’t say it out loud.

That was rather disappointing. I had expected a free skywolf to be worth considerably more. Sadly, beggars were no choosers. If six spheres were what was given, that was what I’d have to make do with.

I better get one of those! Serp was quick to say. You’d never have caught it if it wasn’t for me!

“Neither would I without any of the other cards,” I said, not paying much attention.

Six spheres. If I were lucky, I could claim the second skill of both my Observer and Stoneweaver cards. The Observer was the priority, naturally. After that, if there was anything left, I might as well give it to Serp.

Suddenly, I heard a strange noise in my mind.

“Pup?” I asked. It definitely didn’t sound like him.
