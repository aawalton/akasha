“Okay.” Lexie steeled herself, too, as she activated the card. The ghoul eyes floated in front of her, and once she looked into them, she was transported back into the mind of the monster, with images whizzing by like sports cars.

Luckily, it seemed that her body had put a bookmark on the last memory she’d been in, and it took her there instantly. So she just had to flash forward through more years of memories, after which she landed on a scene of Vulcan bleeding out on the floor.

She paused there and scrubbed back by a few more hours to give herself the context.

John was about in his early twenties, on his way to work.

It was set up to be another predictable day with another predictable set of outcomes, working in a cubicle amongst predictable people.

He worked at a Sytec, one of the best security agencies in the country, and though it sounded great, most of what he did was monitoring algorithms, sending emails, and getting yelled at for things that were not his fault.

Yesterday had been the cherry on top. He had been yelled at for three hours because a security breach had happened during the night, from a threat he’d warned his supervisor of weeks before, but they told him that it was just a small bug.

He'd known that was just a lie. The truth was that the fix, a total system overhaul, would have cost more money than the company was willing to spend, and the middle management man was worried it might come out of his bonus. So he’d simply kept it under wraps, and when the breach had predictably happened, well, guess who took the axe for it?

It felt like his entire life was like this. He constantly warned people of the consequences of their actions, but they paid him no mind. And somehow, when everything fell apart, he was the one whom they all blamed.

Like when Mr. Calder killed Mrs. Calder.

He'd told them it would happen because he could see the violence in Mr. Calder's eyes. He'd told his mother to tell Mrs. Calder to finish packing up and leave quickly. Not only did she not do that, but she was also careless and left evidence everywhere she went.

And then when it happened, his mother blamed him for not calling the police when he'd heard Mrs. Calder's screams. He'd been the only one in the complex at the time, and he'd tried to call the police. But his mother had forgotten to pay the phone bill, so they'd been cut off. By the time she got home and paid it, well, Mrs. Calder was already dead, and there didn't seem to be any point in wasting the minutes and calling at that point.

He'd gotten grounded for a week because of that, when the problem had all been because they hadn't listened to him.

And that cruel history kept repeating itself when it came to him.

He had to understand, though.

After all, why should they listen to him? They were all big, important people who ran the world, and he was no one special, just another cog in the wheel, a hamster running and running, yet getting nowhere. And he would forever get nowhere.

It was starting to be unbearable.

He'd thought he'd gotten used to the restlessness, the gnawing annoyance, like he was on the verge of screaming day by day. Like a rubber band continuously stretched and released, snapping back into place but never the same, always a little loose each time.

The snapping had intensified in the last few months. He was getting looser and looser.

The only thing that had given him some semblance of pleasure was the new game he was beta-testing, but even that wasn't worth it anymore.

He didn’t want to keep doing this, going through endless cycles of gnawing annoyance just for a chance at happiness.

If this were all there was to life, then it might as well be over sooner rather than later.

As he walked into his office building, he scanned his ID like normal. He nodded at passersby and entered the elevator like normal, too.

But when he got to his desk, what he did next wasn’t normal.

He didn’t slide into a seat and pull up an incident report, updating everything he’d figured out about the system. He didn’t write the apology letter he was supposed to write to his boss about how he was sorry about the mistake that wasn't his fault, and it wouldn't happen again.

Instead, he slotted in a pendrive and injected a complex virus that would take out the entire system for at least the whole day.

Then he opened his briefcase and pulled out the handgun he’d brought to work with him.

He got up and began to walk.

The first unfortunate person to cross his path was Mrs. Hollister, headed to the breakroom for what was probably her third cup of coffee this morning. She smiled her greeting to him as she passed.

The smile dropped when he pointed the gun at her.

The second person to go was Ken, sitting nearby. He'd jerked out of his seat when he saw John shoot Mrs. Hollister, and paled when the gun was turned his way.

By that point, there was chaos. The shots had people screaming and running to the elevators.

A brave soul tried to tackle John and got shot in the process.

People got trampled on their way to the emergency exit.

He calmly tracked above their heads and found his next target.

His boss. Mr. Furuya.

Perfect.

By the time security came up to stop him, he'd already killed Furuya and about three more people.

He then died by gunshot wound with a smile on his face.

He woke up in a void, sitting in front of a short humanoid thing with a crafty-looking face, who smiled at him.

“Not bad, John," he said. “I mean, I thought my way would be more spectacular, but I guess your way works too."

"Do I know you?" His head hurt, where he'd been shot, but he didn't feel the wound anymore. Well, he didn't feel anything, seeing as how he didn't have a body.

"I am Cael, your Guide. Welcome to Earth 9.”

He'd known this would happen. He didn’t know how he knew, but somehow he'd known.

“Am I dead?” he asked.

“For now,” Cael said. “But you’re about to be reborn so we can see if you have what it takes."

“For what?”

“You are one of the Chosen souls to be sent to Earth 9. Your task is very simple. Create the end that you want to see. Accumulate fate points and be the last Chosen standing."

“What?”

"You will be given power to attain your vision,” Cael continued. “Or rather, I should clarify that you will be given the opportunity to attain power, and you must take advantage of that to earn even more power and fate points than any other Chosen. And in the end, if you are the last Chosen standing, you will be able to shape the world into whatever you want.”

“How?”

“That’s not something you should bother with unless you win.” He said. “If you’re not even close to winning, then know that you're close to dying. Death is always watching, and the more opportunities you miss, the closer it gets to your window. And once you die this time, that's it."

Those words struck fear in his heart. He still wasn't clear on what was happening, but the little he was hearing sounded exciting to him.

Exciting and also familiar.

“We’ve had this conversation before,” he said. “Haven’t we?”

“Yes. Sometimes life is a game, and sometimes, a game is life.”

It was only after he landed on Earth 9 that he realized what that phrase meant.

He was born to a family in District 5, a bit far away from the main players. Even as a baby, he had awareness and could access his welcome screen. He knew what he had to do.

Most of the action took place in Districts 8 and 9. Which means that he had to get there somehow.

While the thought ran through his head, a big-busted woman picked him up from the crib and burped him.

Lexie began to fast-forward from that point because she didn't want to go through his entire life story.

She vaguely saw his pre-awakening at the age of 9 as a Mercenary Mage. She wanted to know how he'd met and accessed the Mountain, but that would come later.

She kept going until she saw her mother in a dark forest, fighting off what looked to be jaguars.

Her mind, or rather his mind, filled her in on the context of the scene.

The woman they were staring at was a total wildcard.

And it was starting to get frustrating.

This was the third plan he’d had for her demise that didn’t work.

He’d ensured she was assigned to this mission and had gotten separated from the healer and the rest of her team. He'd tipped the shifter assassins off, and the plan was to have them maul her and for the healers not to get to her in time.

Yet here she was stabbing them in the chest and riddling them with bullets, completely eviscerating the poor creatures.

She was supposed to have died in a dungeon, according to the narrative.

He’d tried to kill her earlier, just to get things started, because her death was supposed to be the thing that triggered the Archmage’s rage and turn against the machine.

Yet every time he’d set her up, something happened. Either it didn't work, or the daughter fell into the trap instead.

A daughter that he didn't really remember existing back in the old game.

Which was basically his coded way of saying she was a Chosen. But how?

Though Vulcan had gotten rid of the few Chosen he’d found out about throughout the years, he'd never actually met any of them.

Nevertheless, they were easy enough to find.

All he had to do was approach one of the major characters, typically other villains or heroes, and observe them for some time.

There was usually someone in their inner circle that he would always hear about but could never meet for whatever reason.

He would then go into simulations to find out more about this person by infiltrating the group and talking to them, making friends.

Using the information he found, he would put things in place to kill the ghost he could not see.

It didn't always work the first or second time.

But typically by the fourth try, he was able to get rid of them, giving himself an advantage.

This was the first time he'd ever seen another Chosen. How could she be a Chosen?

And how was she still alive even after falling in his trap?

“Fascinating,” he said, rubbing his chin. Thus began his obsession with Lexie Sparrowfoot.

Lexie paused on that and wondered how Aiden was dealing with this, not only with seeing her mother again, but also with seeing Vulcan plotting against her in real time.
