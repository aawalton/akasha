Chapter 2 – The Most Chaotic Guild in the World

ARC I: THE ACADEMIC EXILE

SYNOPSIS: Livia Quinn, the Emotional Buffer 2.0, tried desperately to hide amidst the chaos, but the universe wouldn’t allow it. The arrival of Bloodbath & Beyond, the server’s #1 Guild, drags her into the most chaotic group therapy session in the fantasy world. She must confront Bron the Immortal Tanker who weeps over an existential crisis, and a Guild Master who seems far more dangerous than any dragon.

Livia Marcelline Quinn, the newly minted Emotional Buffer 2.0, sat on her creaky bench in the heart of the MMORPG camp. Her Therapist’s Log was open to a blank page.

The camp was a circus of chaos—players sprinted past, yelling about “raid wipes” and “OP builds.” A mage set a crate ablaze with a misfired spell. A paladin tripped over his own cape. Someone, somewhere, was eating a sandwich with the intensity of a man who had not eaten in three days and had earned every bite.

This gray robe screams “I’m here, deal with it.”

She doodled a frowny face in her notepad, muttering, “If I’m stuck in this game, can’t I at least get a stealth skill?”

[Achievement Unlocked: Wallflower’s Lament. +10 Invisibility Points (Non-Functional)]

“Oh, come on!” Livia swatted at the air, her dry sarcasm flaring. “Even the system’s trolling me now.”No logout button. No tutorial skip. Just a notepad and a role she never asked for.

I flunked counseling practicum.Now I’m supposed to fix heroes who can kill gods.

She watched a nearby player—a warrior with shoulders like a barn door—receive a quest notification and immediately sprint toward the horizon, sword raised, screaming something about honor. No hesitation. No second-guessing. Just momentum.

Must be nice, Livia thought. to know exactly what you’re for.

The ground shook, snapping her from her mental spiral.

It started at the edges of the camp.

Players slowed. Conversations dropped mid-sentence. A merchant stopped his looping sales pitch for the first time Livia had observed—which, given that NPCs didn’t technically stop, meant the game itself was paying attention. The ambient noise of the camp—the clanging, the shouting, the endless background hum of a world that never slept—pulled back half a step, the way a crowd goes quiet before something large enters the room.

Livia looked up from her notepad.

The players near the camp entrance had moved. Not run—moved. The subtle sideways shift of people who want to give something space without looking like they’re afraid. A gap had opened in the crowd, unplanned, organic, the kind of gap that forms around a thing that could break you without noticing.

Oh no.

A warhorn blared, loud enough to rattle her teeth and vibrate her notepad off her knee. The sound didn’t echo so much as it landed—a physical thing, a declaration. And then they came through the gate.

They moved like a weather system.Not organized—nothing about them suggested drilled formation or rehearsed entry. It was the opposite of that. They moved the way a storm moves: with complete indifference to whether you were ready for them. Their banner came first, carried by nobody in particular, floating on some enchantment or just on the force of their collective ego: a bloody sword crossed with a sarcastic grin, and beneath it, the words

BLOODBATH & BEYOND

Guild #1. The kind of reputation that preceded itself by three rooms.A floating leaderboard materialized overhead as they entered, the way leaderboards always did when entities of sufficient notoriety crossed a threshold—the game announcing them to anyone who hadn’t already felt the ground shake. Their names burned gold against the air:

1. Miles Vexley — Psychoblade Commander — Lv. 992. Seraphina Duskveil — Eldritch Archmage — Lv. 973. Bron Ironslam — Immortal Wall — Lv. 954. Nyx Shadowmint — Ghost Rogue — Lv. 935. Alaric Dawnblade — Holy Paladin — Lv. 91

Level 99. Livia stared at that number for a moment. She was Level 1. She was, in the hierarchy of this world, the lowest possible classification of sentient entity. She was to Miles Vexley what a houseplant was to a nuclear warhead.

Behind the five names on the banner, more of them kept filtering through the gate—not headline numbers, just guild. A rogue in armor two tiers behind current meta. A mage who looked barely past her starter quests, trailing after the others like she’d been adopted rather than recruited. Someone in gear so old it had probably been patched out of the loot tables entirely, still wearing it like a medal.

It wasn’t the roster Livia expected from the server’s #1 guild. She’d pictured something sleek. Matched. Optimized down to the decimal.

This looked more like a family that had grown by accident.

Blend in. You are furniture. Furniture does not make eye contact.

She watched them fan out through the camp with the ease of people who had never, in their entire lives, wondered whether they belonged somewhere.

Miles Vexley came through last, and he came through slowly—the exact pace of someone who understood that arriving last, at that speed, in that silence, was its own kind of statement. His armor was dark, absorbing light rather than reflecting it, the kind of gear that had been upgraded so many times it had developed opinions. A single enchanted blade hung at his hip, its edge faintly luminous. His face was composed: not expressionless, but controlled, every micro-expression deliberate. The smirk was there—it was always there—but it wasn’t the smirk of someone amused. It was the smirk of someone who was always, on some level, three moves ahead.

He scanned the camp.

His eyes crossed Livia’s.

She looked down at her notepad immediately.

[System Note: Guild Master’s engagement duration: 2.3 seconds.][System Note: Deviation from standard interaction protocol detected.]

I am a bench. I am part of the scenery. I am not here.

Behind him, Seraphina Duskveil glided forward, her robes shimmering with arcane runes that rewrote themselves every few seconds, like a spellbook that couldn’t settle on a single argument. She was looking at something to Livia’s left with an expression of deep, academic interest. Livia tracked her gaze.

A slime. She was looking at a slime.

Okay.

Bron Ironslam came next, which was its own experience. He was enormous in the way that made the word enormous feel insufficient—a human being who had, through sheer accumulated mass and enchanted armor, become more of a geographical feature than a person. His dented breastplate bore the marks of at least forty major encounters and a suspicious number of small ones. He was currently eating something. Nobody near him was asking what.

Nyx Shadowmint materialized—that was the only word for it—near the edge of the group, already partially behind a crate. His hood was up. His hands were visible only because one of them was holding, with a grip that suggested it was not optional, a small flashlight featuring a cartoon cat in a bow. It was on. Fully, resolutely, non-negotiably on—a steady pink beam cutting through the afternoon light with complete indifference to the fact that it was the middle of the day. Livia’s psychology instincts noted this involuntarily and she told them to stop.

Alaric Dawnblade arrived glowing. This was not metaphorical. A genuine holy aura radiated from his armor, pulsing softly gold—which would have been impressive if it weren’t currently competing with a faint but unmistakable smell of cheap ale. He was holding a flask. His expression was the expression of a man who had made several decisions recently and was not yet prepared to evaluate them.

Livia shrank into her bench.

I’m just furniture. I’m just furniture. I’m just furniture.

But the universe, or the sadistic game dev running this world, had other plans.

Bron “Bronie” Ironslam, the hulking tanker of the guild, stopped dead in front of her, his massive frame blocking the sun. His dented armor clanked as he pointed a gauntleted finger.

“You!” he boomed, his voice like a landslide. “You’re the new NPC, right? Emotional Buffer 2.0 or whatever?”

“Uh… yeah?” Livia squeaked, clutching her notepad like a shield. “I’m, um, Mental Supporter. I listen to… problems?”

Bron’s eyes lit up, a manic glint in them. “Perfect! I’ve got problems.”

He dropped onto the bench, splintering it into kindling, and launched into a rant.

“I’m immortal, see? Can’t die. Sounds epic, right? WRONG. It’s boring as hell! I tanked a dragon last week—the biggest raid of the year—and it felt like filing taxes. No thrill, no stakes. What’s the point of living forever if I’m just… existing?”

Livia blinked, her half-forgotten psych training kicking in. “So… you’re saying you feel purposeless because you’re too strong?”

Great job, Livia. Real Freud-level insight there.

Bron froze, his scarred face crumpling. “Yeah. Exactly. It’s like… what am I even doing here?” To her horror, the mountain of a man began to sob, tears streaming down his cheeks like a broken faucet.

The camp fell silent—or as silent as a place full of screaming players could get. The guild’s other members gathered, their expressions ranging from shock to amusement.

Miles Vexley leaned against a pillar, his armor radiating menace and his smirk dripping sarcasm. “Well, damn, Bronie. Didn’t know you had feelings under all that metal.” His voice was smooth, laced with mockery, but his eyes flicked to Livia with a glint of curiosity.

Livia, panicking, fumbled for a technique from a lecture she’d half-slept through. “Okay, Bron, um, try this: breathe in for four, out for four.” She demonstrated, her voice shaky. Bron followed, his massive chest heaving like a bellows. His sobs slowed, then stopped, leaving him sniffling like an oversized toddler.

[You calmed an immortal tanker suffering existential dread. +800 EXP. Passive Skill Unlocked: Soothing Aura Lv. 1]

Livia gaped at the text. “Wait… I get EXP for this?” She glanced at her HUD, where a tiny progress bar had ticked up.

Mental Supporter Lv. 1: 800/1000 EXP.

Tears as an energy source.

This game is more absurd than my thesis.———————————————

The guild erupted into chaos.

Seraphina “Phina” Duskveil, the Eldritch Archmage, glided forward, her robes shimmering with arcane runes. “Fascinating,” she purred, her voice like a velvet blade. “Tell me, Emotional Buffer, can you mend a heart enthralled by… unconventional desires?” Her eyes flicked to a nearby slime, which jiggled innocently.

Livia’s jaw dropped. “Uh, what?”

Before she could process, Nyx Shadowmint, the Ghost Rogue, slunk forward, clutching his Hello Kitty flashlight—still on, its pink beam sweeping the ground as he moved. “I… I need to talk,” he mumbled, barely audible. “It’s about… the dark.” His hood hid his face, but his trembling hands and the white-knuckle grip on that flashlight betrayed him.

Miles clapped, slow and mocking. “Well, look at that. Our newbie NPC’s got the whole guild spilling their guts.” He stepped closer, his smirk widening. “What’s next, Buffer? You gonna fix my trust issues?”

Livia’s panic spiked, but she masked it with a deadpan glare. “I’m not a miracle worker. And stop calling me Buffer.” Her sarcasm earned a chuckle from Miles, who raised an eyebrow as if impressed.

Another guild member, a paladin glowing with holy light but reeking of cheap ale, staggered forward.

“Hey, Buffer! Can you cure a gambling addiction? I bet my sacred sword on a goblin race last night. Lost it to a slime.” He hiccuped, swaying dangerously.

Livia’s head spun. This is a nightmare. I’m surrounded by overpowered lunatics with therapy-level baggage.

She opened her notepad, scribbling: Patient: Bron. Issue: Existential dread. Approach: Breathing exercise. Result: Crying tanker. She paused, then added, Note to self: I’m screwed.

The guild’s chaos escalated. Phina began reciting poetry to the slime, Nyx hid behind a crate, and the paladin tried to bet his boots on Livia’s next sentence. Bron, still sniffling, patted her shoulder, nearly knocking her over. “You’re good, Buffer. I feel… lighter.”

[You prevented a guild meltdown. Skill Unlocked: Group Therapy Lv. 1. Achievement Unlocked: Therapist by Accident]

Livia stared at the text, her dry humor resurfacing. “Great. I’m the accidental shrink for the world’s most unhinged guild.” She glanced at Miles, who was watching her with an unreadable expression.

“What’s your deal, huh? Gonna cry about your childhood too?”

Miles laughed, sharp and

dangerous. “Stick around, rookie. You might just survive us.”

A promise that sounded more like a threat.

But as the guild dragged Bron off to celebrate, Livia felt a chill. These weren’t just players—they were a walking disaster, and she was their only lifeline.

A lifeline made of frayed thread and questionable psychological theories.
