When Edge and Melenia made it to the administrative annex, they registered for their first official match.

Today’s low-rung brawl was taking place in the main arena, which meant the duo would be making their debut on the galaxy’s most famous fighting floor. Jumo wished them luck and left to grab some snacks before finding a seat in the stands. Admission was free, giving the settlement’s less fortunate residents an opportunity to enjoy the venue and help build a following for new gladiators before they moved on to bigger and better things.

Krix was overseeing the proceedings with an experienced eye. When the cut-off for registration arrived, he took stock of the participants and conferred with his associates. There were enough contenders for two separate matches. By luck of the draw, Edge was placed in the first fight, while Mel was assigned to the second. An attendant ran the list over to the bookies, giving everyone time to place their wagers while Krix addressed the gladiators.

“Welcome, one and all. I see a lot of familiar faces from this week’s assessment. For many of you, this is your first brawl. We have time, so I’ll explain a few details you might not be aware of. The challenge system is how we determine rankings for the upper echelon of gladiators, but it isn’t practical for fighters who are at the dawn of their careers. Instead, we host events like this on days when there aren’t any major matches scheduled.

“This is your chance to climb the ladders and qualify for various exhibition events hosted throughout the season. Your rungs will rise and fall based on your performance weighted against the quality of your competition. Additionally, you will receive a small percentage of today’s profits, which is also dependent on your performance.

“I hope you enjoy your first true taste of fighting before a roaring crowd. With any luck, this will only be a small footnote in your long and illustrious careers. When we’re finished here, I will assign each of you a starting gate. When they open, wait for the announcer to officially start the match, then fight until you are eliminated or your enemies are no more.

“I have one final matter to discuss before the fun begins. This is serious, so pay attention. The arena’s magic will teleport you out of the ring before you take a fatal blow or if you are rendered unconscious, but it will not prevent any damage you receive before that point. You may also tap out, but doing so comes with a hefty penalty to your ranking unless your injuries are severe enough that you are unable to continue.

“The wounds you receive will be painful. They can cripple or kill unless you pay for a healer’s services. Your earnings are generally sufficient to cover the cost, even for the losers, but that isn’t always the case. I highly recommend establishing an emergency-treatment account now, as you might not be able to authorize payment after your match. Either way, you have been warned.

“In a similar vein, your gear can be damaged during these contests, which is another expense you need to learn how to manage if you want to succeed in the arena. One final note. While it is frustrating to lose, attacking your fellow gladiators outside of an official match will be severely reprimanded, along with our patrons and staff. Does anyone have any questions?”

Edge raised his hand. “Is there a way for us to view the other brawl without heading into the stands?”

“Yes, there will be a screen available in the waiting room below. You are welcome to watch from there.”

With that, Krix divided the gladiators into two groups. Mel pulled Edge in for a parting kiss, which resulted in some good-natured teasing by their peers. He followed an attendant into the central arena, down several flights of stairs, and onto the bottom floor.

He didn’t get a chance to check out the waiting area, since his match would begin in just a few minutes. Instead, he walked around the perimeter hallway until he arrived at his assigned stall and stepped inside. It was a small changing room with a door on one side and a massive gate on the other. When the gate opened, he would approach a mark on the ground and wait for the announcer to warm up the crowd. When they gave the signal, his fight would begin.

Edge was so excited that his hands were shaking. His avatars and Rue offered encouragement through their bonds, and he appreciated the sentiment. He passed the remaining minutes at his disposal by reviewing his impressions of the other fighters in his group, several of whom he had met during his evaluation.

Before long, the crowd began to cheer, followed by Krix’s booming voice addressing the masses. Apparently, the man was the announcer for today’s event. The vibrations conducted through the stonework, setting his heart pounding as adrenaline penetrated his veins. With theatrical slowness, the gate began to rise, revealing the fighting floor of the Grand Arena of World’s End.

He savored the moment as he stepped onto the stonework, grinning from ear to ear while staring at the crowd above. There were about five hundred people watching, mostly poorer residents who couldn’t afford the main events, along with some people scouting for various organizations, but even that felt fucking incredible.

He forced himself to lower his gaze after waving to Jumo, evaluating his competition as Krix arrived at the end of his spiel.

“Welcome, esteemed patrons, to today’s low-run brawl. We have some fresh talent for your viewing pleasure, including some promising gladiators who have never graced our establishment before. Who knows? You might be bearing witness to the first chapter of a new legend in the making. A story to share with your family once we return home. But enough about the future. It’s time for some gratuitous violence here and now. Without further ado, gladiators may activate their auras and passive skills. Refrain from moving or using active skills while I flip a coin. When it strikes the ground, your match will begin!”

The man pulled out a ceremonial coin—a ritual whose origins were lost in the murky mist of ancient history—then placed it over his thumb and flicked it high into the air.

A horn bleated when the disc struck the ground in case any of the fighters had missed the moment, but Edge had already exploded into action. He decided to reveal a few new skills, which weren’t really secrets anyway, as he’d used them while hunting with Widow’s crew. He dodged some stray spells while circling the arena’s perimeter and searching for his first victim.

Someone used a skill that filled the air with smoke. He appreciated the cover, as it would restrict his enemies’ vision but wasn’t an obstruction to All-Seeing Gaze. He knew the audience could see through the haze, since the area’s magic prevented such obfuscations from ruining the show, but it reduced the attacks headed his way while he made his opening move.

As Edge ran, he fired a volley of Vacuum Blasts, targeting the melee specialists going at it in the middle of the fighting floor. The wedges of compressed air were hard to spot, and with magic flying on all sides, they were difficult to track with energetic senses too. The Blasts that struck the floor produced a grenade-like explosion that sent people flying, much to the crowd’s delight. The ones that landed on target were even more effective.

One unfortunate swordsman was struck between the shoulder blades. He was teleported to safety before the spell blew him to bits, but an illusion was generated in his place to simulate the results, so the audience wasn’t deprived. The body erupted into a rain of viscera as a ding chimed in Edge’s ears, confirming he’d dealt a killing blow.

He was pleased that his magic was effective, and Blast’s avatar was thrilled by a chance to cut loose and create as much carnage as possible. He kept one eye on the fighters trading blows in the center of the ring, but most of his attention was focused on the people standing near the wall. They were a blend of cautious gladiators waiting for their opponents to soften each other up and ranged specialists hoping to cull the competition from a distance.

He spotted a dark-skinned woman firing jets of acid from both palms, reducing the closest warrior to sludge before he knew what hit him. She turned when she saw Edge coming… which meant she was staring straight at the chakram he’d flung a few seconds earlier. She ducked beneath the bone-and-brass ring at the last possible second. Before she had time to recover, Edge summoned Foebinder.

The left chain went streaking through the air, wrapping around the acid-wielder and pinning her arms to her side. She started casting a different spell, but her concentration faltered when he planted his feet, and the living links yanked as hard as they could. By now, Foebinder was strong enough to drag her with no appreciable effort. She screamed as she came sailing toward the spot where he was waiting with his polearm at the ready.

He took aim and thrust, skewering the gladiator as Opulence disemboweled the illusion the arena generated after portaling her to safety. Some of the blood was real. Judging by the volume, the spell hadn’t kicked in until the blade reached a vital organ, which meant she had suffered a nasty cut, but nothing that would threaten her life.

Edge used the same trick to pick off an archer, who had chosen a poor weapon for this type of fight. By the time he was done perforating the man’s throat, most of the gladiators standing by the wall were down, taken out by warriors who had the same idea as him. He fired a fresh round of Blasts while entering the center of the ring, which caused a few minor wounds but didn’t kill anyone.

It was only four minutes into the brawl, and half the combatants had been eliminated—everyone who was unprepared for a contest of this nature. He wasn’t above stabbing someone in the back under the circumstances, but since he suspected it wouldn’t earn as many rungs, he looked for the toughest opponent he could find and approached her head-on.

It was the same muscle-bound woman he’d fought during his evaluation, who wielded a gigantic club as if it were paper mâché. He was curious about what kind of skills she used, since their duel had been weapons-only. He learned the answer when a spearman came charging from the flank, trying to skewer her while her attention was divided.

Far faster than Edge anticipated, she turned and let loose with a powerful swipe. The spear had decent range, but her mammoth club was longer. The colossal weapon caught the man in the chest, shattering several ribs before he was teleported to safety and his illusion burst like a meat piñata.

He winced at the sight. Healing that wound isn’t going to be cheap. I think she’s got some passives that boost her attributes, a skill that amplifies the force of her strikes, and an aura that helps her anticipate attacks.

Regardless of the details, Edge didn’t want to get hit by that club. Even with Harden, absorbing that much momentum would deal some serious damage. He fired a volley of Blasts while spraying a water saw into her eyes, switching to her fingers when she raised the club to block. The instant her line of sight was impeded, he came charging in, manifesting an iceblade for Foebinder while leaping over a blind swipe of her oversized bludgeon.

He brought Opulence whipping around in a vicious arc while the chain struck from the rear. While ducking below the stinging spray, the woman batted Foebinder out of the way while spinning to avoid the glaive. It would have worked if he hadn’t activated Quadra Slash and flared the skill, causing four offset blades to extend in the blink of an eye.

He felt his weapon bite into flesh before the resistance vanished and her image was severed into five separate pieces. The left chain reached out and grabbed a gladiator by the neck, strangling him to death while Edge dodged a volley of glass shards manifested by one skill or another.

The glass caster kept him on his toes, forcing him to duck and weave as he sent a few Blasts back the other way. The crystal shattered when it struck the ground, creating shrapnel that was sharp enough to slice through his armor and difficult to dodge while dealing with the prolonged barrage. The assault was dangerous and distracting. If he didn’t deal with it fast, someone would take advantage of his impairment and land a killing blow.

Instead of evading, he charged straight in, just as a razored spire of glass came streaking for his throat. He stumbled when it hit him, like he was in severe pain… then took a big step forward and impaled the unlucky gladiator, who was unaware that Edge had Hardened his body in time to block the blow.

When he turned around, there was only one gladiator still standing, a slender man wielding a short sword who only came up to his waist. He’d never fought someone so small before, but his instincts were telling him this man was more dangerous than anyone else he’d faced. Edge fired a few air wedges, but the fighter slipped around them effortlessly, shrugging off the explosions like the force couldn’t touch him.

He evaded Foebinder’s attacks just as easily, sizing Edge up as he went in for the kill. Edge wasn’t sure what was going on, but it was clear that this warrior was a Speed and Reflex specialist, and his Perception was on par with what Edge could manage with Gaze’s assistance. They traded some probing blows, which the warrior parried and dodged but never blocked.

Something feels off. While the man was remarkably agile, he hadn’t revealed anything that explained why he’d outlasted the competition. Edge smiled when his skill senses detected a spell he was familiar with, because he knew what was coming next.

When the gladiator abruptly spun to move inside his guard, Edge Hardened his entire body, using his diamond scales to block the poisoned blade lunging for his throat… and the duplicate coming from behind that had been manifested by Hidden Strike. The warrior intuited something was wrong. Before he had time to pull back, Edge cast Repel Water.

Fast as thought, the repellent field pressed his foe against the wall. The warrior used a barrier skill to block Opulence and Foebinder, but it didn’t stop the water saw Edge fired at the same time. The high-pressure spray slit the man’s throat, nearly beheading him before the illusory body hit the ground.

With that, Edge found himself alone on the arena floor, staring up at the roaring crowd as Jumo offered a grinning thumbs-up. Krix had been narrating the entire time, but the announcer’s words must have been muted to the fighters while the bout was underway, because his voice suddenly kicked in at full volume.

“There you have it, folks. After a brief but brutal match that was even more thrilling than I expected, the winner is Edge Vasher. This was his debut bout, and I have no doubt this victory is only a prelude to what Edge has in store for us later in the season.”

Krix poured on the praise for a few more minutes, winking as Edge basked in the adulation of the audience and some maintenance staff ran out to clean up the mess. When the announcer pointed to an open archway called the winner’s gate, he left the fighting floor and entered the lobby.

The second brawl would begin in just a few seconds, so he sat down on a bench, draining his canteen and wiping off sweat while watching Mel’s debut on the massive magitech display suspended from the ceiling. She didn’t have as many tools at her disposal or a reason to conceal her powers. Thus, the lethal guardswoman decided to take a different approach to boosting her ranking.

When the coin struck the ground, she waited for the melee specialists to congregate in the middle of the ring. When the fighters were fully engaged, she ignited her core and brought out the big guns right from the get-go.

Mana erupted from Melenia’s reactor and encased Extinction’s blade, expanding the cutting surface to a size that would give even a kaiju pause. Heads turned, and the crowd went wild. Before her competition had time to react, Mel hurled her weapon with every scrap of strength contained within her powerful body.

Extinction went streaking toward the brawling warriors, guided by the Epic skill’s rank-two upgrade. Some of the more perceptive gladiators darted out of the way, but four didn’t see the attack coming in time. The primal axe cut through them with no appreciable resistance, arcing like a boomerang while returning to her hands.

It was a devastating blow, but the redheaded juggernaut was just getting started. Mel waited until the spinning blade was facing the way she wanted, then activated Force Cleave, powering a combo that drained half her reserves in a single go. A clone of the axeblade shot off at a different angle, cutting down two more gladiators who thought they had moved out of the way.

By the time she caught Extinction’s shaft, six warriors were dead. The crowd had gone dead silent during the second half of the exchange, and even Krix seemed stunned. They exploded into cheers as Mel sprinted toward the closest opponent. While he was sure the man would be ridiculed for it later, Edge couldn’t blame him for turning around and running the other way.

He moved fast, but it didn’t matter at all.

The warrior’s retreat carried him past a small stone resting on the floor. Edge could hear his scream from here when Mel Swapped with the rock she’d thrown, ginormous axe already coming down. The man’s cry cut off when he was bisected the long way, taking the crowd’s adulation up another notch.

Holy shit. Edge whistled in appreciation. He’d done quite well for his first match, but the guardswoman was putting on a performance that put his to shame.

The final fighters might have given her trouble under other circumstances, but they were demoralized by how quickly she had taken out the competition. A few well-timed Swaps and three Force Cleaves later, Mel was crowned the victor, having eliminated two-thirds of the gladiators herself.

When Krix was done singing her praises, she appeared through the winner’s gate. Edge came running over to give her a bear hug, picking her up and then spinning in circles. His Guide appeared to deliver a message from the System, and judging by the look in her eyes, hers had done the same.

They had both earned a decent pile of Credits for winning the brawl and, more importantly, shot up quite a few rungs at once. “If we can keep up the pace, we should qualify for the Carnage Cup,” he said.

“Good,” she replied. “That was even more fun than I expected.”

He was ready to head back to the Lost to share the good news, but Melenia was feeling frisky after cutting loose. They told Jumo they would catch up a little later, then stopped by a pleasure house that rented rooms by the hour. The duo enjoyed a private bath that was big enough to swim in, followed by some of their most vigorous lovemaking to date.

When they were finished, they took another dip in the tub and climbed onto a pair of tables to receive a skill-enhanced muscle rub from the staff. It was the best massage of his life. The aromatic oils smelled divine, and he left the establishment so relaxed he thought he would melt and flow down the drain, grinning as several fans asked for autographs on their way to the inn.

Edge’s first day as a gladiator had been immensely thrilling and thoroughly satisfying. He just wished his other problems could be resolved so simply.
