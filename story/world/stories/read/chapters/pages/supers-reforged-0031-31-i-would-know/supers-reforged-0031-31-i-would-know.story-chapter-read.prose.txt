“Alright,” Nadia said. “Let’s go over what that man said one last time.”

I groaned. “Do we really have to do this?” I asked.

“Yep,” she said. “We do. It’s training.”

I looked at her and internally debated whether to concede her point or try to fight it. We had spent all breakfast going through what I remembered of my non-interaction with the strange man at the hardware store, which wasn’t much at all, and it didn’t help that most of the focus was on the part that I missed due to Kimberly’s phone call.

“Well,” she said after the round was over and she seemed satisfied that she could not dredge up any more information out of me. “It’s certainly problematic.”

“I could have told you that without the need for all this.”

“Smartass. It’s not what I mean.”

“What are you thinking about? Something related to your past?”

She nodded, and I grimaced. I wished I’d been wrong about this particular hunch, but of course not. Of course Nadia’s past was going to bite us in the ass eventually, and that eventuality was approaching fast.

“What about other options. I don’t know, Latnium or Vera or…”

“Vera? No,” she said dismissively. “I mean, it could be Latnium, but why? The other option is much more solid and you know it. We need to be wary, they could be beginning to move.”

She had, after all, defected while on a mission. Her old group, while they had not officially sanctioned the search that led to her meeting me and cutting ties with them, was aware of my house and of her disappearance. They could probably draw the connection pretty easily. And now someone was asking questions.

“I should have paid more attention,” I grumbled. “We could have known much more by now.”

Nadia scooted closer to me. “Not your fault, my prince. I can’t expect you to do everything yourself.”

“I wish I could, sometimes. I wish I could just snap my finger and fix–”

“Hey,” she said. “We are fixing things. One at a time. And, most importantly, together. What’s the use being without problems if you are alone? I’d choose a life full of problems but with you over being fine but alone. And I know you’d do the same.”

“Of course. In a heartbeat.”

She gave me a kiss. “That’s my prince. And, speaking of the fact that we aren’t alone. I sent a text to Vera, who called me in turn. We talked, and she agreed to look into it. She’s coming over right now.”

“Which means we should tidy up,” I said as I got up. “The living room is a mess.”

Nadia smiled. “Our mess. Finally the house is beginning to look like someone actually lives in it!”

Indeed, ever since we finally bought the wood-burning heaters, we were beginning to use the house like an actual house. And while it showed in the general untidiness of the place, it also no longer looked like a haunted mansion. And I agreed with her sentiment. Better this way, even if it was a bit of work whenever we had guests over.

Well, not like we had many people who we could invite over. In fact, Vera was pretty much the only one. Haha.

When our manager arrived, looking sharp but comfortable, she set up her laptop inside and began to unload some supplies from her trunk. Before we could ask her about her findings, however, she informed us that Kimberly would also be arriving within minutes.

Nadia blinked. “Right,” she said. “For the cameras. I just wasn’t expecting him so soon.”

Vera grinned. “There he is.”

His car… reflected his personality well. It was eccentric to say the least, with trinkets on every surface like they were fungi sprouting out of a mossy substrate. It was colorful–an eyesore, really–but surprisingly silent as it rolled up the hill and parked in our driveway. The same couldn’t be said about the man, who looked at the supplies Vera had prepared for him, nodded and then proceeded to really show us the real meaning of supplies. Box after box came out of his car’s trunk, then from the back seats, the passenger seat, and a couple tools even came from his own driver’s seat.

“Are you sure he doesn’t have some sort of spatial power?” I whisper-asked Vera.

“Not that I know,” she said. “And I would know.”

Nadia eyed the shed, which we had reason to believe did have some spatial shenanigans going on. “Better we keep him away from the shed, then.”

“Agreed,” I said.

“Well hello!” Kimberly yelled from halfway down the driveway. “Nice place you got here.”

He waddled over, studying us as we stood in a line ready to greet him. We did look a bit off, with Nadia nervously studying the road and the fields, me in the middle, and Vera standing there like a businesswoman with her hands behind her back.

“Kimberly,” I said. “I find you… well.”

In my defense, I really tried to be polite. But the man looked like a goblin, with his fat belly threatening to throw the sad little buttons holding his winter coat together into the stratosphere. For all his trinkets were strange and polished and looked after, his clothes were barely functional. Not that he seemed to mind the cold.

His eyes studied the house, noticed the current camera setup, and he grinned. “Lots of work to do, it seems. I take it there are no changes to the agreed-upon plan, yes?”

“Plan?” I asked.

Vera took over. “Yes. He and I agreed on a plan, which translates to a conservative overhaul of the camera and monitoring systems ONLY. Someone will, however, need to keep an eye on him in case he notices other problems with the house and decides to deal with them in his own style.”

“Ye woman of little faith,” Kimberly stated. “I gave you no reason to doubt my adherence to your plans.”

Vera cocked an eyebrow and the man withered a little.

“Okay maybe once.”

She cocked the other.

“Twice, and that’s all I’m going to concede. And the second time was only because your desk was crooked.”

She shook her head. “It did not need the kind of work you applied to it, and I would have appreciated the gesture more had you asked first instead of working on it for twenty minutes without me able to stop you or even talk to you. I had to wait until you were done before I could start talking to you about the actual reason I called you to my office.”

“Yes, well,” he stammered. “ANYWAY, I can start working, yes? I have all the tools needed and, as agreed, no creative extravaganzas and, I quote, anatomically accurate eyeballs anywhere. Okay? I go?”

I nodded.

“Very well,” he said, turned around, picked his stuff up, climbed a ladder and began to disassemble my cameras.

I noticed he decided to start from that one camera that made me almost fall off the ladder when I installed it. I also noticed that while his ladder seemed utterly unstable, he looked completely at ease perched on top of it with a mountain of stuff he carried up the steps.

“While he works,” Nadia said impatiently. “Vera, you said you have some info about that surveyor I talked to you about?”

“Yes,” Vera said.

“Good, let’s talk inside,” she said, leading the way.

I pulled the door closed behind me as I followed them, but since Kimberly was working outside, I did not lock it. Nadia looked at it, then seemed to realize the same thing, although her eyes lingered on the lock for a moment, then on the key around my neck, hidden by my clothes.

She was still on high alert, I could tell. This was the most nervous I had seen her since she and I finally stopped being jailer and prisoner inside my home. And I hated seeing her like this.

“Make yourself at home,” Nadia told Vera, directing her to the sofa while she dragged a small glass table she set in front of it.

Our manager placed the laptop on top, and opened its lid and began to type.

“Here’s what I found. Which, I’ll be honest, isn’t much at all. Firstly, I called the store and got the name of the realtor they sent the man to. I called them next, and that’s where things got strange. The realtor never got a call from any surveyor.”

“Really?” Nadia asked.

“Really,” she said. “Who did call was a man who did not ask for any house, did not care about any listings, and just asked questions about Gus Hale, and a certain house on a hill near the city. This house, Adrian.”

Nadia’s eyes darted to a window, where a shadow had just passed. It was Kimberly, dragging his ladder, and she relaxed a fraction. “I don’t like this.”

Vera, I noticed, had stopped typing. Her eyes were on Nadia, not the window, and they stayed on her for a moment before she turned back to me.

“Who is, or… was… Gus Hale, Adrian?” Vera asked. “A relative? I understand if you don’t want to tell me, but…”

I grimaced. “It’s fine. The connection is obvious. He’s not my father, if you were wondering.”

She didn’t look like she was, but she said nothing.

“He was my grandpa. This house was his, even though he’d been living near Central City for the last two decades, give or take. When he died, they took almost everything he had away, save for this house.”

“They took his stuff? Who?”

“The banks,” I said tiredly. “Turns out, he had bad debt. I don’t know if he was thinking I’d never learn about it, or he didn’t care as long as he wasn’t around when it finally came out, but…”

“Hey, Adrian,” Nadia said softly, squeezing my hand. “You don’t know what happened. Don’t let bad thoughts ruin your memory of him. Besides, look at what you do have.”

Which was this house, and all the good memories we were making inside of it. My face lost some of its tension, even though my mind was cast to the lab hiding beneath the cellar, which might be the real reason people were snooping around here, or testing what information the Hale name might reveal to them.

But we couldn’t tell Vera about that, could we?

“The plan remains the same,” Nadia said. “We fixed all the locks, Kimberly is working on the cameras, and we will have to play our part. Be vigilant, always. This goes for you too, Vera.”

“Of course,” she said professionally. “It is part of my training as a manager. I did not expect it would come in handy this soon, but I will show you that I am more than up for this task. In fact, I am glad you chose to trust me with this.”

“You gave us reason to trust you,” I told her.

I swear she almost blushed before she looked away.

“Speaking of,” she said, coughing. “We should have a feed from the first few cameras. Do you mind if I wire it to my laptop for now?”

“Wait, are the cameras broadcasting?” Nadia asked quickly.

“Only to a private local wireless network they broadcast around the house. Nothing touches the open internet, don’t worry. Kimberly told me the credentials, and you will change them when I leave.”

“Oh,” Nadia said, settling. She asked a couple more questions, being less tech-savvy than Vera or, God forbid, Kimberly. I understood the gist and was happy to see she found the solution satisfactory. “That’s good to know. And…” she paused, looking at me and biting her lip. “If you can only access the network when you are here, then you can keep the credentials. This way, next time you come over… you can help us keep watch.”

She smiled at the end of it, and Nero chose to show up for some belly rubs and we closed the laptop for the time being. Lunch followed, and we witnessed Kimberly make a show of despising traditional fork and knife setups to eat, favoring a contraption of his own instead.

“I made this with elephant ivory and titanium,” he said, quite proud of himself.

“Elephant,” I said. “Elephant ivory.”

“Well, replicated. It’s not like we can go and kill elephants these days, can we? Most of them have mutated and would rather kill us instead, haha!”

“I see…”

“And what exactly does it do?” Nadia asked.

The man grinned and pressed a hidden button. The object began to spin, and several different tips came out one after the other. Like a Swiss knife, except it was a fork, a spork, a butter knife, and other kitchen implements. Soon, they went from normal to weird, to bizarre, to outright nonsensical but hey, if the man liked it, we could at least make a show of being interested. He was, after all, installing cameras for free all around the house.

He went back to it immediately after eating, refusing our invite to hang out together for a bit (we were secretly thankful for that. Asking was polite, but we had no idea what we’d do if he accepted). There was still quite some work to do, he claimed, but he was sure he’d be done before darkness came.

“He doesn’t seem to mind the cold at all, did you all notice?” I asked. “Myself, I’m dreading outside work.”

Nadia gave me the side eye. “Are you trying to tell me you don’t look forward to building the greenhouse together?”

“Oh,” Vera said. “That’s what the supplies were for, wasn’t it?”

“Indeed,” Nadia said with a wink in my direction. “And you are free to stay inside, enjoying the warmth, while my Adrian and I try to figure out how to actually build it. What do you say?”

“I don’t know, I wouldn’t want to impose.”

“We insist,” I said.

“Well,” she said. “It is rather cozy in here.”

“It is, isn’t it?” Nadia’s eyes lit up. “See, Adrian? Those heaters were the thing we were missing.”

“That, and actual centralized heating, my love. We can’t put a heater in every room.”

“We could if we had the stuff to run flue pipes out through the walls.”

“Oh yeah?” I asked, tickling her. “And who would go around feeding a million heaters, dragging all the wood everywhere and cleaning all the dust they leave behind, huh? You?”

She laughed, succumbing to my ministrations. “You?”

“Nope.”

Vera chuckled, amused by our antics. “Yes, I think I can definitely keep working on some managerial stuff and keep an eye on Kimberly, since you insist. What’s the word… the… vibes here are quite nice.”

I blinked.

“What?” she asked. “I’m not an old woman. I keep pace with current trends.”

“Let’s,” Nadia began, stifling some giggles. “Let’s park it for now, shall we? Vera, make yourself comfortable. There’s coffee in the kitchen, and yell at us if you need anything.”

“Thanks,” she said, smiling. “As for you, I’ll keep an eye on you through the cameras, no need to yell. I’ll follow your building adventures very closely. Who knows, maybe you discover another vocation other than heroing. Rumors say they pay superpowered builders a lot, these days.”
