The words were like a disk-scratch halting the sound of cutlery on glass.

The follow-up was instantaneous. An explosion of reaction, demands, outcry, and quiet sobs from Tamsin.

“What?”

“–the fuck is he doing at the Emperor’s?”

“When did he leave?”

"You should have been watching him, Theo!"

"I was. How was I supposed to know he would disappear on me?"

“What’s he doing there?”

“I’m going to get him.”

“Calm down, everyone,” Aiden, the sole voice of reason, injected his soothing energy into the cacophony. “One at a time.”

That didn’t do much to reduce the tension still bristling in the air, as Mane frantically stalked to where his coat hung, then he doubled back and picked up Tamsin.

“We need to alert the association,” Mane said as he rocked his weeping daughter, and Luther grabbed Thor before he could jump on the table and cause chaos. He must have summoned Ralph because he appeared out of nowhere, a silent presence, and ushered the two children away.

“No,” Aiden, Lexie, Theo, and heck even Luther responded.

“The Association can’t be trusted right now,” Luther said. “I’m not entirely sure one of them wasn’t behind Stella'saccident.”

“I think the same,” Mane swallowed, his skin grey with panic. "But how else are we going to get Torin out of there?"

"Are you sure he’s at the Emperor’s palace?" Luther asked.

“Positive. All the A-Rank and S-Rank senior heroes were briefed on it, in case any of us got captured. We were given Vacek’s schematics for both the palace itself and the outer buildings, including the penitentiary.”

“I  have some experience with the penitentiary,” Lexie admitted. “Vacek had me complete a mission there recently. The palace isn’t too far off.”

“But I don’t understand what Torin’s even doing there.” Mane attempted to run his hands through his hair in frustration, but only managed to dislodge his hairband and get his fingers stuck in the tangles. “What would possess him to go there?”

“Revenge,” Aiden said simply, and when everyone looked at him, he shrugged. “Isn’t it obvious that he would want revenge on the man who nearly killed his mother and a bunch of other innocent people?"

“No. Torin's not like that. He’s not a vigilante. He’s very principled, very rule-abiding.”

“So was I at one point in time,” Aiden gave a wry look. “It’s different when your family’s at stake.”

Mane shook his head slowly, as though unable to believe it himself.

“Killing the Emperor doesn't necessarily go against Torin's ethics,” Lexie said. "Actually, it might play right into his sense of duty. Getting rid of a great evil to save the world."

After all, that had been partially why Lexie herself had gotten rid of Vulcan. So that he couldn’t hurt anyone else.

Torin likely had the same thought, except with a mountain of hero's guilt packing onto it.

So though it was unexpected to see him go rogue, it wasn't at all out of the realm of possibilities for Torin. He likely viewed killing the Emperor as his duty, and thought perhaps he was the only one who could do it, as the Emperor was becoming a threat that the association could no longer contain or win against.

Just like Aiden had been in the original timeline.

Torin had killed him after Aiden had inadvertently caused Stella's death.

Lexie was guessing that the original game didn’t delve much into Torin’s motivations and feelings, how much of it was revenge versus a deep-seated duty.

Game Torin was probably a self-insert character, all power, no emotion. But Torin in this world was full of emotion, full of flaws that he so desperately tried to hide. He’d been pushed to the breaking point already so many times. It wasn’t a surprise to have him snap.

“Call him,” Mane ordered.

“That’s what I’m trying to do," Theo said. "He’s impossible to reach.”

“What about the frog card? Can you talk to him through that?”

“That’s not how it works. I can track him using it, and it can show me what he sees, but that’s about as far as I can take it.”

Mane’s features were tensed, and his forehead wrinkled.

“I’ll go get him,” Lexie said.

Aiden’s head turned sharply, and she shrugged. “I’m the best person to get in and out of there without being seen. I have the tools and the experience." She could even have Pvilycht do it on her behalf, but that would be slightly more complicated since Torin didn’t know Pvilycht and would be less likely to listen to him. Also, she wanted Pvilycht to focus on his mission.

“I don’t think that’s a good idea,” Mane said. “I know you’re powerful, Lexie, but I don’t want you to risk your life for my–”

“Torin's family to me,” Lexie said as she got to her feet. “Actually, I consider you all family, even if we’re not blood-related. That makes your problems my problems too.”

“I’m going with you,” Theo said.

“We haven’t even decided if she’s going at all,” Luther said, with his hands laced in front of him as he stared at Lexie. “I appreciate the sentiment, but if there’s anyone who should go and fetch Torin, it’s me. It’s my job after all.”

“You have bigger responsibilities, and the Firebringers can’t afford to lose their interim leader after what happened to Stella. You should stay here. You too, Theo.”

“No way.” Theo was already gearing up, pulling his weapons out of inventory, and slipping on rings. “No way am I letting you go alone.”

“If you go with me, you’re going to slow me down and put me in even more danger,” she said. “I told you I know what I’m doing. I have a good disguise.”

“Well then, give me one of your cards, and I can disguise myself, too.”

Lexie could do that, but she didn’t want to because Theo being there would complicate everything. She went to him and put her hand on his arm. “If you really want to help, try reaching Torin again. Try talking him down from whatever he’sthere to do. In the meantime, I'll go get him out myself, and I'll make sure he doesn't get caught. Or worse."

The true danger here, which no one but Lexie and Aiden understood, wasn’t Torin getting captured. It was Torin succeeding.

Torin was intelligent. Even if he’d gone off to murder the Emperor in a fit of rage, he wouldn't do it half-cocked. He would sit and plan it out, weigh it in his mind. He would ensure that even if he didn’t make it out alive, he would complete his mission successfully.

And if he did, then whatever critters the Emperor was hiding would be unleashed on the world, and it would be impossible to get rid of them.

“Let her go, Theo,” Aiden said, much to Lexie’s surprise. Despite everything they’d been through, she could have sworn that her father would be on the verge of telling her she couldn’t go or telling her that he had to come with her.

“You’re not going to stop me?” she asked.

His eyes twinkled as he cocked his head. “I know better than to do that. If you get stuck, I know where to find you.”

He winked, and she grinned.

Theo finally sighed in resignation. “Do you need this?” He asked as he held out his card to her. She hesitated and shook her head.

“No worries. I’ll find him on my own.”

With that, she disappeared and appeared in her dungeon. Seconds later, she was looking at an image of Torin…but not really.

He was wearing all black, down to a balaclava that covered everything but his eyes. He'd also done something to his body. It was hard to tell exactly what at first. The hallway was dimly lit by fiery lanterns that hung every few feet. The path was thin, and there were no windows to allow the heat out, so a haze of smoke hung in the air.

And somehow, Torin was part of that haze. His body looked like it blended in with the smoke, and when he moved, it was only a flash of darkness cutting through the fog.

Probably why the guards couldn't see him.

That and his hiding in corners, in shadows. He would wait and only move when they blinked.

His speed reminded Lexie of when he’d fought Top Dog, how he’d disappeared and appeared within the blink of an eye.

Now she finally knew that it wasn’t just his natural speed. He’d used heat to somehow bend his body particles, such that they faded into the atmosphere, almost like a mirage.

Or perhaps he did, indeed, create a mirage.

He'd also somehow transformed the heat into kinetic energy that boosted his speed, such that he'd travelled the entire length of the winding hallways in three breaths.

Lexie stared in shock.

She shouldn't be surprised, considering it was Torin...but she'd never seen someone use fire in such a way before.

It wasn’t just mana-intensive; it was extremely technical, and one wrong move could either have his body implode or break the illusion and get picked up by surveillance.

Lexie wasn’t confident she could do it as well as he, and so she tried to think ahead, to guess where he was headed.

She saw soon enough.

Now she knew exactly what this building was and why the few windows she spotted were closed.

It was some kind of bathhouse.

The naked statues gave it away.

So did the towel rack at the end of the halls with the hanging robes and slippers.

Two guards stood outside a thick door, where Torin was headed.

How Torin knew that the Emperor would be in a bathhouse at this time was a mystery. Maybe he could have gotten the intelligence from Theo, but Theo himself probably didn't know.

Could it be that Torin had been spying on the Emperor on his own? How?

Lexie didn't have time to ponder.

Torin had stopped at the corner, ducked behind the wall, watching the guards.

Lexie saw her chance.

She instantly teleported, cloaking herself in darkness.

She appeared beside him, taking care not to startle him too much when she whispered, “Hey.”

He still jumped, and she slapped her hand over his mouth to keep him from talking.

“It's Lexie,” she whispered. “You can't do this. We have to go."

Talking to him first was a mistake because when she tried to grab him and teleport him out of there, he moved so fast it was like he phased through her as well.

In another blink, he was gone.

Just in time, the guards at the other end of the hall in front of the door turned away at a stray sound, and Torin once more pushed open the door slightly and slipped into a crack that he realistically shouldn't have been able to slip through.

So the heat gave him a partial phasing ability, too? Incredible.

When did he learn to bend his body like that?

With no other choice, Lexie followed him in.

The Emperor was in a huge pool of heated water that took up most of the space.

For a second, Lexie stared at him and was overcome with enough hatred to make her spit.

He looked irritatingly healthy. While he caused deaths, while Stella lay in a healing center fighting for her life, he was in here with his long beard and his broad body, looking as healthy as ever.

He was so close. She was so tempted to let Torin finish the job.

Or to just do it for him.

Above them was what looked to be a ventilation system. There was a closed window at the top of the steps, a painted mural on thickened glass.

The Emperor murmured something, and Lexie started, but he didn't otherwise move.

He had his head leaning back against the terrazzo, his body languid.

At first, it didn't seem like anything was amiss. He even had a smile on his face.

“Why are you interrupting?” The Emperor murmured, but his voice sounded strange. Slurred, almost like he was drunk or sleepy. Or maybe dizzied by the ungodly temperature in the room.

Torin was still in the corner, sticking to the shadows. The Emperor was still alive.

Lexie faced a second of confusion.

Was he not here to kill the Emperor? Why was he just standing around then? She'd thought he'd rushed in here to burn him to death or shoot him or something.

But Torin was simply standing still, just glaring at the man.

The room was getting hotter. The Emperor should have been calling for help or for someone to reduce the temperature, but he didn’t seem like he was able to. It hit Lexie then.

It was Torin.

He'd increased the heat of the room.

Not only that, he was boiling him from the inside out. She flashed to him.

"Torin, stop."

He ignored her. She tried to grab him, and he flashed away. She tried again, and he did the same thing. He was faster than the Lightlarks. It was annoying.

"If you kill him, more people will get hurt."

Once more, he said nothing. Meanwhile, Lexie activated her DECODE cards and used them to decipher the magic by which he was phasing.

She nullified it, and right as he became solid, she grabbed him and teleported him to her dungeon.

“Why did you do that?” Torin raged in her face, and Lexie caught her breath, her heart still racing from how close they’dbeen to getting caught. Also, because Torin had never yelled at her before. She was pissed now.

“Why did I do that?’ Lexie glared at him. “Why did you? How on Earth did you think that you were going to kill the Emperor and get away free?”

“I almost did. I would have. It would have looked like an accident.”

“Sure. He accidentally spent too much time in a hot bath and boiled from the inside out. Death by severe heatstroke. Is that what you were going for?"

“Yes. And I would have been successful if you hadn't ruined it.”

"You were being watched."

"I already took care of the PHORBS with my mirage. I made sure the heat was the perfect temperature for it. Even the guards couldn't see me when I was right next to them, much less the surveillance. I had everything handled."

The scary part was that he was right. Torin would have been totally successful if Lexie hadn’t stopped him.

“Just how long have you been planning this?” Lexie asked him as he ran his hand over his face. “It had to have been a long time. This isn’t just about your mom, is it?”

“Every week the death toll rises by tens, twenties, hundreds sometimes,” he said. “It’s been like that for months now, even when the heroes were fighting it. After Vacek died, everything just got much worse. The Emperor is funding several wars, do you know that? Not only that, but he's funding villains, too. Every day, people are dying because of him. How am I supposed to just sit back and let that slide?”

Awareness dawned on her. “So you have been spying on him.”

He didn’t answer, but he didn’t have to.

“How did you even find the time?” Between school, AFC, and training his powers to unbelievable heights, how did he find the time to spy on a man as elusive as the Emperor as well?

He stared. "Is that really what you want to ask me?"

"No, sorry. I actually want to know where you got your intel from."

"From myself and a small team I'm working with."

She narrowed her eyes. "What team?"

"You wouldn't know them."

"I should if they're good enough to get you this close."

"Most of that is me. I've stalked him, predicted his movements. Blackmailed someone in his court to feed me information. I've planned this. I know his whereabouts. I knew he would be in the sauna today, knew how to take him out myself. All of it ruined."

"Listen, I know you don't realize it now, but you wanted me to get you out of there. I've done something like that in the past, killed someone without thinking, and it cost me my best friend."

Torin didn't even look a little sympathetic. "Small price to pay for saving the world."

"Yeah, except you wouldn't have saved it. You would have doomed it?"

"What do you mean?"

Lexie chewed her lip, then made a quick decision. She used the dungeon to identify where Sir Hoppington was, casting red sand to show his obvious shimmer.

Then she trapped him in an opaque forcefield so he couldn't see or hear them.

Torin stared at the frog that had shimmered into view and sighed. "So that's how you found me."

"Yeah." It was a little comforting to know that, even with all his meticulous planning, he'd gotten one thing wrong. Lexie didn't feel as bad about her own half-cocked, clumsy murder now. "Now I'm going to tell you something. Can you keep a secret?"

"Obviously."

She exhaled. "The Emperor planted sleeper agents in case of his death. That's why we can't attack him outright. Because then the contingencies activate."

"Can't we just get rid of the contingencies?"

"No. It's an army of void-resistant re-animated souls that can take over any body, dead or alive. I would find it difficult to get rid of them."

"You mean like zombies?"

"Worse. Zombies that are smart, and war-ready, and whose souls can possess even plants."

"Oh." Torin's face went through a series of emotions, the wrath attempting to fight the new perspective he'd been given."Shit."

"Yeah. Shit."

"Why doesn't he just activate them now?"

"Maybe he can't. Or maybe he doesn't have enough yet. There's only one confirmed one we know about, and his name is Saint-0. He was formed in a lab from the soul of child soldiers, and we've been searching for him to no avail..."

Lexie realized that Torin was no longer listening to her. He was staring into space, but not in the way of just realizing that he was in a dungeon.

More so, his mind was snagged onto something she'd said and refused to let go.

"What?" Lexie asked.

"Saint-0," he said. "I think I might know something about it."
