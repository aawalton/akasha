The days of travel passed by quickly, with most of his time spent with either Skippy or Garth.  The ship’s training facilities didn’t lend themselves to any sort of real sparring with C Grade levels of power, so he and Skippy spent time talking battlefield tactics.  The skrell’s abilities had only improved since their brief spar on Guancious, and Skippy enjoyed animatedly discussing different strategies for using his spatial manipulation to complement Kyle’s battle plan.

Garth, on the other hand, was much more interested in talking about the Earthling resettlement on Kertan Six.  Kyle hadn’t fully appreciated all the challenges that the refugees had run into, nor how many petty criminal convictions they’d accumulated after Corthian Mining’s fall from grace.  Nor had he expected the level of advocacy Garth demonstrated for them.

“I’m telling you, Kyle, there are plenty of good people who could be a real asset if we could secure a pardon,” Garth said, pointing his fork toward Kyle between bites.

They were at lunch, just hours away from their destination.  Even though he didn’t show it, Kyle knew that Garth was nervous, which was largely why the man brought up the familiar argument.  He shook his head as he took a bite of steamed rice and vegetables, talking as he chewed.

“The last thing we need is to introduce criminal elements, Garth.  We’ll be inviting trouble.”

“I’m not talking about violent offenders, Kyle.  And we’ll see what Marcus’ legal team thinks.  Joseph was going to get a proposal developed and sent over soon.”

“If they bite, power to them.  But don’t forget, you’re the one who signed up to police and lead the refugees.  Don’t bite off more than you can chew.”

“We’re talking about handfuls of E Grades, Kyle.  Hell, I doubt most of them could have taken you when we first crossed paths.  My team and I will be just fine.”

“So long as you know what you’re signing up for,” Kyle said, the rest of his statement trailing off as he saw Tillienne and Rochelle rising to leave the mess hall, talking animatedly.  They’d become fast friends during the journey, much to Kyle’s chagrin.  The duo took every opportunity to tease him, with Rochelle sharing stories of the “Onyx Warrior” moniker, while Tillienne spread the stories of Kyle’s disastrous Practicum.  The fae woman shot Kyle a devilish grin from across the room, a twinkle in her eye.  All that means is trouble, he thought.

Garth let out a snort.  “Your ladyfriends sure are having a good time.”

“Tell me about it,” Kyle sighed.

“You know, settling down isn’t such a bad thing.  You can still pick up the occasional job, but it’s not a bad life.”

“It wouldn’t be,” Kyle agreed.  “But it’s not the life for me, not yet.”  He held himself back from sharing some of his goals with Garth.  Pursuing A Grade secrets wasn’t for the faint of heart, and Kyle didn’t want to drag anybody in unnecessarily.  Even his friend.

Garth shook his head slowly.  “I know there’s still probably a lot of hurt when it comes to your relationship with the survivors.  Just try to have some grace for them.”

Kyle blinked at the older man, the comment taking him by surprise.  He leaned forward and met his friend’s eyes.  “Garth.  I’m not hurt by them.  I don’t stay separated because I’m afraid they’ll judge me, or because I’m worried I won’t fit in.  I don’t fit in.  I don’t belong with most of them.  And that’s fine.  I’ve found things I’m good at, met people who support me, and have been making the Collective my home.  There are only two survivors from Earth that I’m  carrying a grudge against, and hopefully that number is cut in half by the end of the day.”

“That’s one thing we can definitely agree on,” Garth said, though his expression told Kyle he wanted to say more.  Instead, he finished the last couple of bites on his plate, and rose to leave.  “I’ll see you when we disembark, going to go get my head put on straight.”

“Sounds good, Garth.  I’ll do the same.”

Kyle cleared his plate and headed back to his chambers, and was surprised to see Tillienne and Skippy talking outside his door.  “We’ve still got some time until we land, what’s going on?”

Tillienne’s expression was serious, and she gestured to Skippy.  “We’ve been talking, and we’re concerned about the expedition’s ability to keep up.  And when I say that, I mean it literally.  The three of us take pride in our speed, both inside and outside of combat.  If we are going to have to get beneath the moon’s surface to find our target, I am doubtful that the expedition can keep up in any meaningful way.  And if they try, even to secure our passage out, they risk overextending.”

Skippy nodded.  “Even the strongest D Grades don’t have much to help with long-distance movement.  Chester has some stone manipulation he can use, but it’s not nearly enough to keep pace.  When I watched your swordsman friend, he can get around a battlefield well, but that’s not sustainable for the kind of travel we’ll need to do.”

Kyle nodded, seeing the issue.  “What do you suggest?”

The ghost of a smile crept across Tillienne’s face.  “I suggest that we either get dropped off, just the three of us, or that the D Grade forces only focus a perimeter on protecting the ship.  Should we need evacuation, we have local communication enabled on the nav bracelets.  However, if the enemy is dangerous enough that the three of us are unable to defeat him or escape, it’s probably for the best that they leave.”

“Besides, that leaves more fun for us,” Skippy added, pointed teeth showing in a wide grin.  “It’s been a while since I’ve been able to let loose and blow off some steam.”

“You realize that we’re about to enter pitched combat against a mid-tier C Grade inside his own territory, that he’s been reinforcing for years, right?” Kyle asked flatly.

Skippy’s smile just widened.  “Like I said, it’s been a while since I’ve been able to blow off some steam.”

Tillienne folded her arms and gave Skippy a stern look, then turned back toward Kyle.  “While we will certainly treat the mission seriously, I also think that we’re better suited to handling whatever comes if it’s the three of us.  I don’t want to speak for you, but raw numbers start to mean less and less when the power disparity is greater.”

“I could fight E Grades for days without breaking a sweat,” Skippy confirmed.  “Though that would be pretty boring.”

“I don’t disagree with any of the points you made,” Kyle said.  “Just remember that this isn’t a vacation.  Just because the others can’t keep up with us doesn’t mean that we go running off independently.  We stick together and fight as a unit.”

He said the last part staring right at Skippy, his expression mirrored on Tillienne’s face.

“What?” the skrell asked in mock outrage.  “You both know that I’ll take it seriously.”

“If you spent less time talking about fighting endless hordes of E Grades, and more time talking about the C Grade we’re hunting, I might be inclined to believe that,” Tillienne replied, though her smile belied her true feelings.

“Then we’re in agreement.  Do you need me to message it out?” Kyle asked.

“I already have an update drafted,” Tillienne said, lifting her nav bracelet.  “I just wanted your go-ahead before sending it.  After all, you’re the mission leader.  I wouldn’t want to cross the great Onyx Warrior.”

Skippy gave an exaggerated sigh.  “I remember hearing about how powerful the Onyx Warrior was on an expedition once.  I got so excited thinking I’d have a new sparring partner, only to find out it was just Mayhew.  Your alter ego sounded much more impressive, you know.”

Kyle felt a note of smug satisfaction and amusement through Pack Leader’s Instinct, and sent back his annoyance.  To Tillienne and Skippy, he gave his best withering glare.  It didn’t seem to have much effect.  Finally, he gave a resigned shrug.  “It’s a good plan.  Now go make whatever final preparations you need.  We’re almost there.”

Skippy nodded and took off, but Tillienne lingered a moment, watching the skrell go before meeting Kyle’s eyes again.  “How are you holding up?” she asked, once Skippy turned the corner.

“I’m as good as can be expected.  Ready to get this over with, if I’m honest.”

“I understand that.  It’ll be good to have closure, for both of us.  Who knows what kind of abominations we’ll be facing this time.”  She had a thoughtful look on her face, her eyes growing distant.  In a softer tone, she continued.  “I remember them, you know.  The faces of the people he infected.  The relief in their expressions when they were finally set free, allowed to rest.  It’s haunting.  Power that oppresses like this, that takes freedom… it shouldn’t exist, Kyle.”

“He’s a monster, no doubt about it.  People like us get the chance to right the wrong, and we have to take it.”

“Have you ever wondered why evil like that is able to get so powerful?  Why it’s even a valid path for mana cultivation?”

“I’ve always heard that mana is neutral, and it’s up to us to decide what we do with it.”

Tillienne looked like she wanted to say more, but instead just leaned in and hugged him.  “I suppose that’s enough for now.  I’ll be ready to fight when we land, but promise me you’ll be careful too.  The faces of the infected aren’t the only thing I remember, and I don’t want to relive watching you die again.”

Kyle pulled her close.  Despite her overwhelming physical power, in this moment, she felt smaller.  Vulnerable.  Of course, if he made a comment to that effect, she’d put him through a wall without issue.

“You, too.  We’ve got a date waiting for us on the Hub, and I don’t want either of us to be stood up this time.”

She smiled, some warmth returning to her expression.  “It’s a deal.  See you soon, Warrior.”

Kyle just shook his head before stepping back into his chambers.  He ensured the C.H.A.D.D. pack was in working order, then made his way to the hangar.  When he arrived, people were already reviewing the brief that Tillienne sent out.  The expressions were mixed, but more than anything, he saw relief on their faces.  Not that he could blame them.  Their pay was the same regardless, and if it meant less risk trying to defend both the ship and the entry point, all the better.

As the final groups gathered, Chester Drake walked up to Kyle.  He gave a quick nod, then surveyed the group.  “Any words you’d like to share, Mr. Mayhew?”

Kyle thought for a moment, then shook his head.  He’d never really been one for rousing speeches.  “Nothing on my end.  Just be safe, be smart, and we’ll all get through this and give DeRosa what’s coming.”

Over Chester’s shoulder, Kyle could see Garth reading the brief with a frown, and Kyle excused himself to walk over.

“You sure this is smart, Kyle?  Shouldn’t you take at least a few of us with you?”

“It wasn’t my idea, but I think Tillienne has the right of it.  If we get into circumstances the three of us can’t handle, adding more people will just be adding casualties.”

Garth sighed.  “It’s your call.  Just let this be my formal disagreement.”

Their conversation was cut short by a rumbling as the shuttle entered the atmosphere.  Another message came through the nav bracelets, echoing an announcement from the overhead speakers.

We will reach the landing zone in ten seconds.  Nine.  Eight.

Kyle looked back to Garth.  “We’ll be safe, you do the same.”

Garth nodded in return, right as the shuttle touched down.

The group got into their formation, just as the hangar doors opened.  Kyle was the first off the ramp, with Chester following right behind.  The moon itself seemed to be nothing more than barren stone, though Auric Perception gave Kyle a persistent sense of wrongness.  Familiar, wicked mana seemed to permeate the air and ground itself.  DeRosa.

“C.H.A.D.D., can you detect anything?”

[MY SENSORS ARE FACING AN ODD BLOCKAGE BENEATH THE GROUND’S SURFACE, DR. MAYHEW.  IT IS AS IF A STEALTH SKILL HAS BEEN DEPLOYED JUST BELOW THE STONE.]

Kyle took a few steps, gesturing for the expedition to follow.  The moment Chester set foot on the stone, his eyes went wide.  “They’re coming!  We’re under attack!”

Kyle trusted the man’s Seismic Sense, and he allowed his skills to activate as the ground beneath erupted in a storm of fungal tendrils, wrapping around the ship’s landing gear and releasing hundreds of rushing E Grade fungal constructs.  What a warm welcome.
