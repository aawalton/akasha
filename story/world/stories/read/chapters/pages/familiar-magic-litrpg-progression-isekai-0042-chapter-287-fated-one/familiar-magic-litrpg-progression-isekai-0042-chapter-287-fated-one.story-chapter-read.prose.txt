Ethan spent several quiet days in Bastion after Steelbinder shut herself down. Apparently using the greatest creation of her long life as little more than a distraction, and getting it destroyed in the process, had put her in something of a mood.

She still spoke to Allyara–when she was awake, at least. The raven-haired Champion was at least able to convince Steelbinder to contact Apollo. The nearly-manic Holy Chosen had been left alone, and bound to Fable for over a week with no word, and the army had long-since passed the forest in which Bastion was hidden. He’d been sending demons scouting in every direction, but achieved little beyond magnifying his own worries.

The army was now moving to a secluded canyon to the north, not wanting to risk bringing the Followers down on Bastion. Apollo and an off-shoot of Fable would be arriving soon, and Allyara had promised Steelbinder would be ready to speak again when they did.

Ethan was grateful for that. They needed a plan. They needed something, anything to help them hold onto hope.

His time in Bastion had done little to lift his spirits. He’d tried, and failed, a half-dozen times to heal the Nator. As he’d feared, they weren’t sick or injured, they were exactly as they were meant to be–twisted and broken as that was. Ethan simply didn’t have anything to restore them to.

He’d even begun using Selina as a template, trying to see if he could copy parts of her own physiology, in an attempt to create some kind of “baseline” Nator. The attempt had failed so thoroughly that he and the Runemistress had needed to work together to understand why. The answer had left Selina confused and quiet.

Her form was corrupted by Potentia as well.

They’d been operating under the false assumption that those Nator who escaped to Nexum had been unchanged by their world, but it seemed that wasn’t the case. Selina, and by extension, every Nator back on Nexum, had likely been changing in subtle ways for centuries before they left the corruption behind.

It wasn’t precisely like those in Bastion, however. Her form wasn’t “wrong”, instead, it was more like a transitional state. As if the Nator had been becoming something else, but that evolution was interrupted.

She’d gotten over the revelation with her typical resilience, and as Ethan watched her from the roof of a building in the town square, he doubted anyone could see the pain behind her eyes.

She was reading to a group of small children. There were very few so young, and they were fascinated by Selina and her stories. The Runemistress had countless books, and was somehow managing to make one on magical theory seem a fairytale.

Equally amusing was the crowd on the other side of the square. There, adults were gathered to hear stories as well, these told by Tomo, the “Fated One”. The ancient Nator Familiar spoke of Potentia as it had been, and seemed to be recalling more of his previous life every day. He was becoming the shaman he’d been long ago, so much so that Ethan worried at treating him like a Familiar much longer.

Tomo had called such notions “foolishness”, and insisted that they long-since become a true partnership. As happy as the demon was to be reunited with his people, he saw his nature as Ethan’s Familiar as his path toward saving the Nator, and valued it more than ever. Still, seeing the man–and he was a man, Ethan had to remind himself–seeing him in his element, talking to an enthralled crowd, it was hard to imagine pulling Tomo away.

“Did you speak to this…Apollo?” a voice asked.

Ethan turned to see Allyara standing behind him on the flat stone roof, her silver eyes as tired as ever. “I did,” Ethan said. “He’s close enough that our communication runes function again. I’ve updated him on what happened, and he’ll be here soon.”

Allyara shook her head slightly. “I still struggle to believe a Twilight has managed a Champion Ascendency. Master Ulric has taken remarkable liberties, but I suppose he’s equally made remarkable achievements.”

Ethan shrugged as the woman sat down next to him–she did so with impressive grace for someone still missing an arm. “We just did what we had to,” he said. “It was the only way to get the Masters to acknowledge me. They operate under disturbingly strict rules.”

“Merely a step on the path,” Allyara said. “Dedicating oneself with the entirety of one’s being to a goal is a necessary stopgap. Overcoming one’s limitations requires a level of discipline far beyond what the mortal mind is normally capable of. Still, the ‘Old Masters’ are a perversion of such things.”

Ethan’s eyebrows rose. “What do you mean by that?”

Allyara shook her head in disapproval. “No one is meant to stay a Master. Arguably, it is the worst place to end one’s path. You lack agency, and choice. As you no doubt saw, some are unable to act, even in their own best interest. Or in the interest of survival itself. The rank is called ‘Master’, largely because it is a phase of self-mastery. Not attempting to move beyond it is to make a mockery of its very purpose. Using Masters as tools, the way you say Noctem has, is not far beyond slavery.”

Ethan turned away, frowning. He hadn’t considered what a Champion might think of the Old Masters. Someone like Allyara might have spent centuries learning how to leave the rigid structure behind, and seeing others “trapped” there must be difficult. Ethan had merely felt the frustrations of not being able to harness the Masters’ power, as he couldn’t help but think of them as nearly all-powerful.

Allyara spoke of them like children.

It was the Champion who changed the subject. “What you’ve done for your Familiar is remarkable.”

Ethan looked back at Tomo, who was gesturing dramatically with his shaman’s staff, thumping it to emphasize key parts of whatever story he told. “He’s come a long way,” Ethan said. “He’s been a guide, and a friend to me since we met, but I never could have imagined all that he was. Is, I suppose.”

“A true Nator shaman,” Allyara said, something like wonder in her tone. “I’ve spoken with Vetta and Oreth about this. I was curious when the others called him ‘Fated One’.”

Ethan turned to her, curiosity piqued. “And what did you learn? Vetta told me it wasn’t my business. She doesn’t exactly hide her disgust at the fact that I’ve Bonded one of her people.”

Allyara shrugged her single shoulder. “Hard to blame her for that. She isn’t likely to understand what he’d been before he became…this. But those like Tomo are still spoken of, even a thousand years or more since the last one walked this world.”

“What do you mean? I thought all Nator drew power from their Soul Runes, how are Shaman any different?” he asked.

“The Runemistress is the best example, I think. You likely know that most Nator are enhanced physically from their Soul Runes, but the rarest among them are more like her. She has only the smallest ability to extend her power beyond her own body. She can create Rune Circles, but they’re merely catalysts for monster cores. I’ve known a few others like her, and only the rarest can project any magic beyond themselves.”

Ethan thought back to what he’d seen in the Nator on Nexum. Some few did operate like Selina, but it was a pale shred of what could be achieved from the weakest Bond. “I think I understand. But what Tomo can do…”

“Illusions, conjurations, metal attacks. They were as far above most Nator as a Bonded is above an Unbound. It is thought that they represented the next step in Nator evolution.”

“And the people here, they recognized that in Tomo, just from a glance?”

Allyara shook her head. “No. It’s not his power that makes him special. It is a remnant of a time long-past, certainly, but it’s what he’s becoming that they see. While Shaman may have been the greatest expression of Nator power, Tomo is more than just what remains of his Soul Rune. Cleansed of the corruption as he is–” She paused, shaking her head. “I still find that hard to believe, though I see it for myself. But with the corruption cleansed, and his physiology evolved through his Bond with you, he’s becoming something more. They believe he’s becoming what the Nator were always meant to be.”

Ethan looked back to his Familiar–his friend–and considered his appearance. He was still Nator, clearly. His horns, height, and skin-tone were alike with his people. Yet he’d changed much since reaching Twilight, and even before that, after the interaction with Dawn. Ethan had often dismissed his features as demonic, but there was more to it. The word “draconic” had often come to mind as well.

Tomo was scaled, and thick-thimbed, his face slightly pointed, and far less human than someone like Selina. And those protrusions on his back… He’d seen Tomo use them like spears, which had always tickled something in Ethan’s mind.

Telathamir.

The black dragon that had Bonded with Zakiel, and likely the very same one that Ethan and his friends had faced, near-death in the Forgotten City. It used its wings in the same way. When folded up, they looked much like what Tomo had, though the Familiar’s were far smaller and less developed. But what would they look like after another evolution?

“Fated One,” Ethan whispered. “Some of those more aggressive Nator in the city, they almost look like lesser versions of Tomo.”

“Failed versions, I think,” Allyara said sadly. “But you’re right about the connection. What they see in Tomo is what they believe to be their peoples’ true path, and I cannot disagree.”

Ethan watched his friend, still gesturing wildly at the crescendo of his latest tale, and found himself wondering once again where the “Shaman” truly belonged.

His eyes flicked away when he heard a commotion in the distance. He could just make out a crowd forming at the city entrance. Ethan quickly got to his feet, letting out a relieved sigh as tension left his body.

“Apollo and Fable are here,” he said. “It’s time to wake Steelbinder. It’s time to figure out our next steps.”
