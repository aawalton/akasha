The door opened into a room of white walls stained in black. It was chaotic and very much confusing—the color not the layout. As for the layout of the inside of the building, that was surprising, for lack of a better word.

For one, they walked into a long hallway that only allowed five people to walk side by side. Considering the size of the building, Melmarc had expected more. Maybe offices on either side. Melmarc had expected a small portion of this, though, but not all of it. He had allowed [Knowledge Is Power] to venture a little into the building during their small bout with the guards outside, but he had not allowed it to venture too far inside.

When it had returned, he had sensed the hallway but hadn’t really paid much attention to it since he had been looking out for people and not layouts. Still, he turned his head one way, then the other. He took the white walls stained in black like some kind of blood splatter.

“Looks as if they just walked around shooting people,” Ark commented as he closed the door behind them, banishing the unconscious guards out of sight. He looked around, eyes careful, taking in more than just the walls. “More like they just let a monster rampage through a room of too many people.”

Melmarc nodded absently, then pointed at the ground beneath them as they walked slowly. His eyes were kept attentive, listening to the slightest bit of sound that implied anything. The sound of Veebee would be more than welcome, but the sound of another person would at least lead them somewhere.

Ark looked down at the ground and his brows raised a little in surprise. “Really?” he asked in distaste. “You’d think their interior decorator would know better than to use the same design for the walls and the tiles. At least they didn’t do this in the classrooms.”

Melmarc agreed, but he had something else to say. “I don’t think there’s an interior decorator involved.”

“You don’t say,” Ark deadpanned, their voices kept low. “Just in case you don’t know, I feel it too.”

His words made Ark blink. “You feel what exactly, too?”

“Whatever it is you’re feeling.” Ark gestured at the walls and around them in equal distaste. “Oh, look. They got the ceiling, too.”

“How exactly are you feeling?” Melmarc asked, not sure he had the strength to move the conversation to the ceiling.

Ark sighed. “You keep fidgeting,” he said. “You do it in school sometimes, like some kind of a tick. It’s not enough times to be obvious, but I see it. You do it less in the apartments, though.”

Melmarc nodded. He didn’t know that his discomfort had managed to evolve into a physical display of discomfort. He thought he’d been keeping it in his head all this time.

“So,” he pressed as they finally found themselves coming to the end of the hallway. “How does it feel?”

The hallway split into a T-junction. Left and right, the building mocked them to choose. They came to a simple stop on the right wall and Ark peeked out. He moved his head, looking from side to side.

“Kind of boring,” he commented. “Just more hallways.”

Melmarc peeked out as well. Ark was right. On both sides were nothing but hallways of the same design. Straight, long, and black and white.

“Think we should split up?” he asked.

Ark pretended to think about it. “Let’s see. I have no idea which way Veebee could possibly be. And in the movies, splitting up is always the way to go when you want one of the characters to get got.”

Melmarc cocked a brow at him. “You’d rather we both got got?”

“Yep.” Ark nodded.

Melmarc sighed. At least it was better for them to get caught together than for one of them to get caught. Something told him that they would handle things better if they were together.

“Left it is,” he said.

Ark paused. “Why? Whatever happened to right?”

“You want to go right?”

“Maybe.”

“Then let’s go right.”

Melmarc started moving only for Ark to grab him by the arm. “You know what,” Ark said with an apologetic smile. “Let’s do left.”

Melmarc smothered a chuckle and went left. Ark walked calmly beside him. The part continued on. It took Melmarc a moment to notice another thing that was strange about the building. From where they were standing, just as had been the case when they’d entered the building, he could not see the end of the hallway.

This place isn’t wide enough for that, right? he thought to himself as they walked, peering forward to see if he could see anything at all. Nope, just hallway.

It didn’t make sense.

“Ark.”

“Yes, brother.”

“Can you see the end of the hallway?”

“Nope.”

“And that does not bother you?”

Ark shrugged. “Nope. Wouldn’t be the first type of hallway I’m coming across in my life.”

Melmarc paused for a moment, then reminded himself that he wasn’t the only one who had been in two portals before. Ark had been in at least two portals as well.

“How did you deal with them?” Melmarc asked, since this was a new experience for him.

Ark shrugged once more, unperturbed. “I winged it.”

Melmarc wasn’t sure how that would possibly help him as his discomfort increased, making him want to scratch at the back of his neck. With the news of how his discomfort tended to manifest itself in physical actions, he fought to keep it at a low as they walked.

“I almost forgot,” he blurted, remembering something. “How does it feel to you?”

“This place?” Ark asked.

“Yes.”

“Well,” Ark put a thoughtful finger to his lips, looking up and away as if trying to find the words to fit the answer. “It feels like lying down in your bed and realizing someone that’s not you has chosen to lay next to me.” He shivered visibly. “I definitely don’t like it. Feels far worse than the world’s been feeling since it became your property.”

“I’m sorry, what?” Melmarc sputtered. “What do you mean the way the world feels since it became my property.”

Ark stopped to give him a look that Melmarc couldn’t quite place. He stood there for a beat, forcing Melmarc to stand too for a little longer than he would’ve liked.

“What?” he asked when Ark said nothing.

“Nothing important,” Ark said in the end, then resumed walking. “It’s just good to see that you still have emotions like surprise. Good to see you can still be shocked. Also, try to keep your voice down. For all we know there could be someone watching or listening.”

“Why are you glad I can be surprised?” Melmarc asked, confused.

Ark sighed. “In case you haven’t noticed, Mel, you’ve been somewhat… bored? I think that’s the word I’m looking for. You’ve been looking as if you don’t care about anything for the longest time. You don’t really laugh with anybody. You don’t pay attention to your phone. You’ve just been like all those protagonist soldiers in movies that just focus on the mission. Bland!” He snapped his finger in triumph. “You’ve been looking bland.”

Because the world has been bland, Melmarc thought but didn’t say the words. “I noticed.”

“And have you noticed why?” Ark asked, raising his hand to halt their movements. Then he sniffed the air.

Melmarc shook his head. “I’m not entirely sure. I think it’s the Oa—aspect thing that’s getting to me. The whole [Optimum Existence].”

Ark looked at him. His eyes assessed him for a moment before letting the matter lie. “You asked how exactly the world feels to me now. I want to say the world feels like I’m walking into your room, but we share a room. So, to be more precise, the world feels like I’m lying down on your bed. I’m here, but it’s not my bed. If you catch my drift.”

Melmarc almost felt bad. It had to be a horrible feeling, like wearing another person’s clothes when you didn’t like sharing.

“It must be horrible,” he muttered, feeling a little sad to be the cause of Ark’s discomfort.

Ark blinked at him. “I’m confused.”

“I don’t like lying down in another person’s bed,” Melmarc explained, then paused as the end of the hallway was suddenly in front of him. He had no idea when he’d noticed it. “It must be tough.”

“Oh, that.” Ark made a sound that implied it didn’t really bother him much. “In case you haven’t noticed, I lie down on your bed a lot. Mostly when you’re not around. In truth, I kind of like lying in your bed.” He shrugged. “You always keep the sheets neat and lay it well.”

Melmarc pressed his lips into a thin line, unsure of what to say to that. He had never noticed Ark lying in his bed unless he was around, and never for a long period.

“But this place?” Ark looked around, suddenly angry. “It’s like walking into the room and finding some buffoon on your bed. It’s like, nobody bullies my brother but me. Nobody lies in your bed but me… and you, obviously.”

“Oh.” It was all Melmarc had to say.

“Left or right?” Ark asked.

Melmarc tried to think about it and realized that he didn’t really have any deductive way to make a choice. “Right.”

Ark paused. “Wait. Are you even feeling anything at all?”

“The building is actually very—”

“Not that.” Ark waved his answer aside with a gesture. “I meant Veebee. The thing that I showed you that helped us find this building was supposed to increase your awareness of Veebee. I thought the last choice was because you felt Veebee was in this direction.”

“No.” Melmarc shook his head. “I still can’t feel Veebee. Also, what exactly did you teach me?”

“Something I think Veebee should’ve taught you by now.” Ark went left.

Melmarc wasn’t sure why Ark went the wrong way, but he followed Ark, regardless.

“It’s some kind of telepathic technique,” Ark continued as they walked. “It helps you… it’s supposed to help you connect with Veebee’s mind better, access its current memories too. But to do that, you have to kind of let go of your own.”

“Let go of my memories?” Melmarc asked, not sure how that was supposed to work.

“You ever just stop thinking and do?” Ark asked. “You know, just leap without looking?”

Melmarc gave him a flat look and Ark rolled his eyes.

“Of course not,” he answered for Melmarc. “You do most of the thinking and I do most of the doing. Anyway, the way Spitfire taught me is to let go of my opinions of a shared memory he and I had and just focus on the facts of the memory.”

“The dinner had nothing to do with Veebee, though,” Melmarc pointed out, noticing how Ark’s steps were beginning to slow down significantly. “It was even before we became Gifted.”

“I know.” Ark was looking straight ahead now, not even bothering to look to the sides. “Spitfire wanted it to be a shared memory because that way he would be a part of the memory. He said my mind is quite chaotic so it’ll be harder to do if I am not fully aware of him in my mind.”

“And you chose one without Veebee in mine because?”

“Because your mind is too organized. If I put Veebee in it, you’d be too focused on Veebee because you’re trying to find Veebee.”

“And the lack of judgement?”

“To get rid of your perspective.” Ark placed a sudden hand on Melmarc’s chest, halting him in place. His voice dropped a decibel lower. “You remember what they say about perspectives and memories.”

They was actually their childhood therapist.

“There are four memories,” Melmarc answered, mirroring Ark’s decibel. “The one you were actually in, the one you actually experienced, the one the world keeps on telling you that you experienced.”

“And the one you keep telling yourself you experienced,” Ark finished for him.

Apparently, it was one of the ways the mind could block traumatic experiences or exaggerate normal experiences to make it seem traumatic. For example, running into a snake late at night and being attacked by it. Your mind could remember it as a two-headed snake, if the lighting and vision was poor enough. Or, over a couple of months or years, it could make you believe that there was no snake at all, that something startled you and you don’t really remember what it was.

“So you needed me to remove my perspective as much as possible and focus on the facts. How did that help?” Melmarc asked.

“Because,” Ark was still peering forward, looking at what wasn’t there, nothing but an endless hallway of black and white, “according to Spitfire, the human mind cannot exist without bias. We cannot remember a thing at a hundred percent accuracy. We cannot experience something if our bias is not a part of the experience.”

“Oh.” Melmarc wasn’t in complete agreement, but maybe it was just his stubbornness. Then again, maybe Spitfire was judging based on Ark’s mind.

“So,” Ark continued. “I needed you to try your best to observe factually without the bias, that way, Veebee’s mindset can finally sneak in. That will increase your connection to…” His words trailed off suddenly. “Fuck.” He sniffed the air like an animal once more. “Mel.”

“Yes.”

“[Knowledge Is Power].”

Melmarc did not hesitate.

[You have used Knowledge Is Power]

“How far out?” he asked, head on a swivel. “What am I looking for?”

Ark turned and pointed at the wall. “That way.”

Melmarc focused, hoping to push the wave of mana faster even though it had never happened before.

“Pull it back,” Ark commanded. “That should be far enough.”

He can see the wave? Melmarc thought, obeying. He pulled back on the skill, forcing it to him. Now, he experienced once more the downside of [Knowledge Is Power]. Knowledge was only power when he had it, and he only had it when the wave of mana finally returned to him.

The wave returned to him and Melmarc’s eyes widened.

“What’s it?” Ark asked, worry in his voice. He already knew that something was wrong, he only needed Melmarc to confirm what it was.

“A woman,” he answered, staring at the indicator behind the wall, and also knowing that there was no physical wall.

[Edith Talwort (Teacher)(S)]

As confusing as an S-rank teacher was, because what exactly was the difference between the ranks for a class like teacher, Melmarc was focused on something else.

He turned and looked at Ark. “I think we should run.”

Ark didn’t hesitate, pointing off to the opposite wall and grabbing him by the arm. “This way.”

They bolted, slipping into the wall as if it didn’t exist.

“Also,” Ark continued, “what are we running from?”

“A [Teacher].”

“We just beat up two soldiers and we’re running from a teacher,” Ark laughed.

“The soldiers were grey,” Melmarc pointed out. “They didn’t care.”

“Let me guess,” Ark sighed, pulling to a stop and taking a left before they slipped through another wall. “This teacher is red.”

Melmarc wished she was red.

“No,” he answered, “it’s black.”
