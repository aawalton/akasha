Chapter 31Canon bridges don't stay down

Craft had stuck his head out beside Dane. The smoke swelling from the gorge ahead was impenetrably thick, and the smell of charcoal and tar, industrially pungent.

It would keep them from reaching Monstera. He told himself it wasn’t a big deal — Nightshade hoarded patience if nothing else — but it didn’t make the sinking feeling in his chest go away. Was he going to fail right from the outset?

A green-skinned humanoid figure waved at them. “O-oi, boss!” he called out with a deep, grumbling voice. His muscles bulged, built like a tank. He wore a plate of banged-up scrap iron over a ripped tuxedo, and he carried a machine gun under his arm. It was more like a sheet of metal banged together into something like a gun.

Its appearance sent his heart racing. Sweat dripped off his cheek. “S-should we run?”

Dane looked at him funny. “What’re you talking about? It’s just a gork.”

“H-huh?”

Lei-rei stuck her head out beside Craft. “They’re a monster species. They’re always looking for a fight, but if you turn them down, they won’t try to make you pay for the privilege.” She looked at him. “Honestly, they might be more reasonable than Dane sometimes.”

Dane looked at her like he’d bitten a lemon. “Hey!”

Craft took a deep breath. I’ll just have to take their word for it. Still, the green juggernaut reminded him way too much of a certain army of berserkers. At some point in humanity’s existence, genetically-engineered giants and heavy-caliber berserkers had been the only thing standing between an army of androids and humanity’s total annihilation. Later on, said army of androids was the only thing standing between said berserkers and total annihilation. Between the two, the kill-death optimizers had been more open to reason. Who’d want to fight a gun-god-summoning cult, anyway?

Dane stopped the carriage. Shovelfuls of steel cartridges, each the size of a soda can, littered the road. “Hey! What happened here!” he said, leaning in from the driver’s seat. His eyes were level with the gork, who was tall enough to be eye-to-eye with Dane.

“Boss, boss, git dis!”

He seemed overly excited, like a puppy meeting its owner again. With the burning bridge in the background, though, he was one dangerous puppy.

“Me wuz stompin’ down da road ta me cuzin’s weddin’, yeah? Den me see dis grotty humie in da middle of da bridge! Me wuz gettin’ a bit bored, an’ da sun wuz shinin’ bright, thought it wuz a good day fer a good foite! Me wuz about ta ask ‘im wot ’e wuz doin’, den ‘e starts shootin’ at me! Me didn’t even do nuffin’ yet, wot’s da matter wit’ ’im?”

Save for the accent and implication of family trees, Craft thought he was pretty well-spoken, and importantly, reasonable. He’s called a ‘gork,’ right? Maybe he could lower his guard a little.

That aside, if he understood him correctly, he’d encountered a stranger on the bridge, and before he’d even proposed a fight, the stranger opened fire. “Hey, isn’t that serious?” he muttered.

Only Dane and Lei-rei had heard him. “Wouldn’t say serious,” Dane whispered, “but it’s up there on rudeness. Even monsters know consent.” He scrunched his nose. “Could be an outsider who doesn’t know the local guidelines.”

One person immediately came to Craft’s mind, but it was just a baseless suspicion — an obsession by this point. It couldn’t be them. There must’ve been a different story to this.

Lei-rei sighed. “More work.”

He’d had almost forgotten she was a town guard of sorts. He’d help her out, but that’d be like trying to take her job. Maybe if she asked, then.

“Den me starts blazin’ back at ’im!” the monster continued. “But ‘e wuz a tough one, y’know? Not enuf dakka, so me busts out da big shootas an’ starts poundin’ ‘im wit’ more dakka!”

That got Dane to turn right back at him. “Ha?” he blurted. “Why’d you blow up the only bridge headin’ into the village!”

The gork showed his fangs. “Me’s offended, yeah! Me knows me’s a bit gobsmacked, but me ain’t dat stupid!” He adjusted the tie on his tux. “Ain’t nun o’ dis dakka me’s used got burny bits! Me wouldn’t blow up da bridge me’s about ta cross, wot’s ‘e takin’ me fer?”

“Ugh.” Dane winced. “Sorry ’bout this mouth.”

“You’ze a good humie, boss. Ain’t a matter ta me.”

Dane sighed in relief. “Thanks. That’s good to hear.”

He waved as the gork returned to his roadside encampment. Craft stared at him. “You know that guy?”

Dane faced him and raised his eyebrows. “What? Well, not specifically.” He squinted. “What’re you on about?”

Craft had never been able to gauge Dane until now. “Seems you’re well-liked.” It made sense for a traveling merchant to be liked, but if it’s on the level of being liked by someone he hadn’t even met before, wasn’t Dane’s reputation just virally good?

But he replied with something strange. “Huh? What’re you on about?”

…Did he have selective hearing or something? “Didn’t the gork just say you’re a good guy?”

Dane chuckled. “Gorks say that to everyone.”

Craft looked at Lei-rei. She subtly nodded no. Did that mean Dane…didn’t believe others’ compliments? Craft might not have been close with him, and neither did he fully trust him, but he could tell he was at least a reliable partner. To have such a partner undervalue himself didn’t sit right with him at all.

“Well, whatever’s gotta happen,” Dane continued, “we ain’t gettin’ into the village today.”

Craft shelved his concern. If Dane didn’t have any problems living like this until now, then he could stay as he was for a longer time still.

“Aren’t there any alternate routes?” Craft asked.

“Ain’t any!” Dane said. He started making four different types of smiles.

“You don’t seem bothered.”

“What? Nah. It’ll be fine! The bridge’ll fix itself up by tomorrow.”

“ ‘Fix itself’ ?”

“Huh? Yeah! It’s an angel-insured part of the map, so it’s got auto-repair to make sure it stays part of the map.” He took out a mirror. “Anyway, don’t mind me. I gotta practice smilin’ so my contacts don’t get too pissed about me being late.”

“Angel-what? ” Craft blurted, but Dane was too busy.

“The Holy Bureau of Geography. It’s usually shortened to just ‘Geo-Bureau,’ ” Lei-rei explained. It didn’t actually explain anything. He gave her a blank stare.

“Have you never met an angel?” she asked.

“Well, I have.” A pretty strict one.

“When the Geo-Bureau canonizes an area, they’ll do everything they can to—” She looked to the sky. “Oh, they’re already here.”

Who’s here? He tracked her gaze just in time to find the sky had opened up, sucking in the smoke, turning it from a pillar into a cone. The speed at which it happened made him feel small — as if he was watching a sea get sucked dry in mere seconds.

But it was smoke, not water, and while the fire still burned, the sea was infinitely regenerating.

Red-winged valkyries in fire jackets descended with skyborne firehoses. They doused the raging inferno in oceans, turning the black smoke white. Yellow-winged kids in full PPE came next, their sorties leaving behind light beams that outlined the original bridge’s dimensions. Hook blocks descended from cranes beyond the clouds, bringing with them gifts of steel and cement. A white-winged angel came last, her weapons: a permanent smile and a clipboard. The other angels, their tasks complete, fell in line.

This was all rather far away, locked behind a hardlight barricade that looked like a giant cancel sign.

“I don’t know what I expected,” he said. That said, the sight was strangely easy to accept compared to everything so far — or was it because of everything so far? “It’s kinda funny how they’re color-coded. Red, yellow, white — what’s next? Blue?”

“…Well.”

“Huh, I was right?”

“They’re the ones responsible for maintaining System functions, so you won’t see them. There’s also the green-winged who take care of our avatars and accept consultations on anything related to Anima. They’re easy to meet. They usually stay in temples in big cities. …Then there’s the one black-winged. You wouldn’t normally want to meet her.”

But he did. She was going to show up tomorrow. He vividly and ashamedly remembered the tone he’d used on her the last time they’d seen each other. He had been in the wrong, and to make up for it, he wanted to show her results — that he wasn’t just beginning to open up, but also helping people out.

But if he couldn’t even handle one broken bridge, then what good would he be anymore? He just wanted to get something right for once, then maybe he could feel more justified in his judgement, and Amacus would trust him with himself.

“Is there really no other way?” he said. “I’d rather get Nightshade’s grass ASAP.”

Dane choked. He put away his mirror and raised an eyebrow at Craft. “Hey, pal, I hear ya, but she ain’t in that bad of a shape. What’s the rush?”

I just want to get something right. He couldn’t possibly say that.

“It’s the outsider,” Lei-rei said. She glanced knowingly at Craft, but she looked at Dane again. “This concerns me too. I can’t let someone like that slip by without giving them a remedial lesson.”

It was a two-on-one. Dane shrugged. “Alright, alright, I get it! I still don’t know what to tell ya, though. This is the only road headin’ into Monstera. Maybe that gork outside’ll know a way around, but if it ain’t a road, I ain’t hitting it.”

Craft took a deep breath. He didn’t know what Lei-rei had meant with her glance, but at least they had a lead. Alright, gotta talk to the not-berserker.
