Caleb pulled his hammer to him and studied it.

Meta Steel Warhammer (Rare)

Weapon type: Hammer

Material: Steel

Aspect: Steel (greater), Cold (lesser), Earth (poor),

Effects: Greatly improved ability to pierce steel and retain its edge while doing so. Lesser resistance to cold based attacks and poor resistance to earth based attacks

The item had improved its cold aspect from minor to lesser and the earth aspect had been added entirely. He checked his spatha and found the same effect.

His mind immediately began racing through the possibilities, and the mage steel and other rare metals he’d requested Mettius source shot higher up on his wish list.

He was going to need to find a way to make more money.

***

After thoroughly examining his equipment to make sure it was ready he called Kelia, Mettius, and Grisim to see his results.

The three gathered eagerly waiting for what Caleb had been laboring on for so long.

“You look like Bog’s shits,” Grisim said as he took in his soot covered brother.

Caleb looked to Kelia for confirmation, and she winced.

“I wouldn’t have put it that way, but you’re taking a bath as soon as you leave this forge,” she said.

Without fanfare, Caleb used his Imperium to lift his armor off the table and bring it over to him. The lorica slid over his head first and then the arm pieces rose over his arms. As each piece was in place, a metal click could be heard as he locked the pins in place with his magic. The next landed gracefully on his head, closing with a satisfying click and then he was left with the leg armor hovering in front of him.

“I’m still working on this part,” Caleb said, a little embarrassed.

He used Jump, going high up into the tall ceiling of his mill turned forge, and pulled the armor up over his legs as he landed and then once on the ground slid his feet into the foot covers.

Then he stood awkwardly waiting for some sort of response.

Finally, Grisim broke the silence.

“How do you poop?”

***

Grisim’s comment had broken the standoff, and Caleb told them all about what he’d done, starting with the mechanism for pivoting armor, and going on to the final trait he’d unlocked at the end,

During the explanation, Mettius pointed out that armor like this existed and was popular in some of the armies the legion did battle with. Caleb suddenly felt foolish for not consulting Mettius or Drusus about potential armor designs, but was happy to learn that his own efforts had settled on a design that others were actively using in battle. It only served to validate his own beliefs in the soundness of the design.

Mettius also saw the benefit of Caleb’s new trait. As an experiment, Mettius slotted the armor onto himself, and while the armor status page retained its cohesive identity and listed the aspects as affecting the whole set, each aspect was a rank lower.

“Forge Dragon?” Mettius asked, noticing the name.

“Yeah, I was going to ask about that,” Caleb said. “I didn’t pick that. How does the system pick item names?”

“No idea,” Mettius said. “And I’ve never heard of a forge dragon, but I can ask around discretely the next time we are in civilization.”

Mettius looked around the mill turned forge with a small level of disdain he had whenever he remembered he was out in the middle of nowhere surrounded by “barbarians.”

“But, now that you’re done,” Mettius said. “You should see about that bath. Canus got orders us got orders yesterday to pull out, and it was only thanks to your wife here that you had this extra day to finish.”

***

Caleb quickly based in the frigid river behind the mill and returned to find his meager possession had been packed up already by his wife.

“What would I do without you?” he asked, going in for a kiss now that he was clean.

“Smell like Bog,” she answered.

He helped load the wagon they’d been given and they road back to the join up with the departing legion.

As they approached the camp, they saw a small contingent on the distinctly dressed Frigidi in their white clothes standing uncomfortably to the side of the muster both in the way and unsure of what to do with themselves.

“What’s the story behind that?” Caleb asked.

“That,” she said meaningfully, “If how I bought you more time to finish your little project.”

Caleb looked at her, inviting an explanation.

“I wasn’t going to help them at first,” she admitted after a while. “But… you weren’t done, and we got the orders to leave. And I’d had a few days alone with my thoughts. When the orders came to Canus to move out, I just spoke without thinking. I told him I needed time because the Frigidi had just asked to negotiate with the legion. He believed me, and he sent Mettius, myself… and you, out to discuss what they had to offer. I suggested maybe you wouldn’t be the most welcomed site and he agreed.”

Kelia went on to detail the encounter. How some had treated her coldly—which didn’t have some revered Frigidi meaning—while others acted as if she were some lost daughter. She didn’t provide any explanation for her existence, but she sensed she was a known quantity to them, as if they always knew of her and were only now seeing what had once been a tale.

The Frigidi wanted a treaty. They knew the legion could wipe them out—and they didn’t know the legion had no desire to do so. They asked what the legion would want in exchange for supplies for the coming summer and assurances of peace. Kelia brought the news back to Canus, and he requested four combat capable initialized of uncommon rank or higher.

“I asked him if we could make a trainer for myself a condition of the deal, and he readily agreed,” Kelia explained. “So, I brought the demands back to the Frigidi. They tried to negotiate, but Mettius took over and got them to agree. They took volunteers and found four young Frost Warriors—their uncommon warrior variants—willing to join. None of them had the requisite skills to train me though, so they agreed to send some elders as representatives to them empire. Canus wasn’t too happy about that but he couldn’t really say no after we demanded a trainer. So four young hot head warriors have joined us along with four elders who I suspect were sent because they weren’t expected to survive the coming summer.”

“That’s a lot,” Caleb observed.

“Mhmm,” she agreed.

“Are you okay with this all?”

“Yes? No? Maybe. I’m still working that out,” she said, laying her head on his shoulder as the drove the wagon into the legion camp.

Caleb’s father was there waiting for them outside the perimeter. Grisim was besides them, and the father there with two wrapped bundles.

“You smell a little better,” Grisim said to Caleb as they pulled up.

Kavil gave his elder son a look, and Grisim raised is hands in surrender.

“I wanted to give these to you both,” Kavil said, handing each a bundles package.

The brothers both unwrapped the bundles to find old woven pieces of cloth.

“Are these our baby blankets?” Caleb asked, holding it up to see it was in fact his blanket from childhood.

It was a dark blue quilted fabric with light blue lines running through it. It felt warm like wool but was softer than wool had any right to be.

Kavil nodded and give Caleb a meaningful look.

Picking up on it, Caleb reached out to the blanket with his sense for the system.

Willowweave Scarf of Hope(Rare)

Material: Willowweave

Effects: The wearer of this scarf is protected from mental attacks. Increase effective willpower by 5 when resisting outside influence. This effect also increases the wearer’s ability to control their fear.

“I knew it was magic!” Caleb said loudly.

At the proclamation, Grisim examined his similarly patterned green scarf and then his eyes grew wide.

“You made fun of me for using it for years until I put it away!” Caleb said, pointing at his brother.

“Ummm,” Grisim said, uncharacteristically chagrin. “I was wrong.”

Caleb handed the scarf over to Kelia so she could examine it, and then moved to embrace his father.

“This is why she was taken,” Kavil said. “She couldn’t help but help others with her gifts, and eventually word got out. If…”

Kavil began, but couldn’t get the words out.

“We’ll look,” Grisim said, patting his father on the shoulder. “We promise. If she’s out there, we’ll find her.”

Caleb had wanted to promise the same but couldn’t bear to give his father hope that might be false. Grisim was not so reserved.

Their heartfelt goodbye was interrupted when Mettius came with a scroll in hand.

“I’ve got our orders,” Mettius said, holding them up.

Kavil said his goodbyes and then Grisim was shooed away as he was not a member of the equites or privy to this order

On them was a seal Caleb recognized which was saying something because Caleb only recognized a single seal and only that one because it was emblazoned on everything in the camp.

The personal seal of the Emperor.

“Father—may he reign forever—has a mission,” Mettius said, unrolling it and his eyebrows raising as he read. “He asked for us specifically, and he wants us to… find the Benefactor and aid in quelling the uprisings that are popping up over the empire.”

“Oh, just that?” Kelia asked.

“No offence, Mettius,” Caleb said. “But I think my father gives better gifts.”
