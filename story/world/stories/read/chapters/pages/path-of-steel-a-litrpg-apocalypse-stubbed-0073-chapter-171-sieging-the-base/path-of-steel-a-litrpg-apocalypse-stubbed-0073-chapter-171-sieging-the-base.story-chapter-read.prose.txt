The army rolled down the hill like a truckload of rubber balls. War cries echoed. The disciples of each clan came together, especially the Legion, while the rogues banded together loosely. Beef boys tumbled down the hill behind the pro boxers, wrestlers, and callisthenics athletes.

Gary had quickly located his team. He ran with Asim, Delilah, Cog, Alicia, his eight hired beef boys, Evil Shrek with his donkey, and the Jester of Hearts. “Stay close!” he yelled. “And if you see Chloe, make sure she’s safe!”

“Yes, boss!” the beef boys replied as one. As they approached the bottom of the hill, Gary leaped, sailing through the air to reach the front line, which was almost exclusively made up of Core Formation people. The army charging to meet them on the plain was a mix of tentacle monsters, Core Formation field bosses, and climbers from the beast taming and swordsman clans. The other two enemy clans, the Academy and the Church, stayed at the back, providing buffs and ranged firepower.

Magical projectiles rained at the middle of the gymnasium army. Witches chanted spells, using the power of nature to block them, but they were one clan against two, and the dead terrain didn’t favor them. More than half of the projectiles broke through to land on the army. Explosions echoed. There was chaos. There were screams.

Gary spotted a blue bolt heading for his team, trailing smoke. “Fan the Spear!” he yelled, jumping high. His twisting aura field grabbed the projectile and sent it high, where it would fly over their army and land on the hill they’d vacated.

From above, he briefly scanned the enemy casters, not finding the face he was looking for. Chloe was probably here somewhere. He couldn’t possibly find her in this chaos, but he hoped she’d be okay.

When Gary landed, the enemy was in front of him.

Multi-Pronged Mosquito, Level 26

You never met these guys on the third floor, did you? They’re agile, annoying motherfuckers who suck the blood out of anyone sleeping unprotected. Or protected. Or not sleeping. Anyway, they suck, and they can release that blood at extreme pressure.

A flock of these things flew before Gary. They resembled tiger mosquitos, except as large as a grown man’s torso and bursting at the seams with blood. Despite the obvious bloating, they were extremely fast, zooming around so rapidly Gary could barely keep track of them.

As he arrived before the mosquitoes, they adopted dodging maneuvers, flying unpredictably to surround him. Gary’s eyes tracked them. He thrust.

“Nail Gun!”

The mosquitoes were fast and turning constantly. He couldn’t predict their next move, but he didn’t need to. His spear could reach its target instantly. If he could see them, he could hit them. Four mosquitoes exploded in a shower of blood, and the rest of them flew up and away, looking for easier targets. One unleashed a torrent of blood at him, fast and razor-sharp. He couldn’t dodge it, but it only stang against his skin, causing no real damage. The mosquito buzzed unhappily as he stabbed it.

Level up! You have reached Level 29. +8 Free Points

Merit Points: 500

He didn’t know what to say. He’d made five hundred merit points in seconds—as much as in the entire first mission. Was this the power of Core Formation?

More enemies rushed him. Gary danced with his spear, breaking past the enemy’s front line, thrusting left and right, spinning, slashing, blocking. Everything around him was slow and weak. He straight-up ignored the attacks of Birth of Aura opponents, since they couldn’t hurt him. He reaped them like hay.

Meanwhile, Goldie was focused on the chat and mini-map, directing Gary to help his team when they got stuck. Only a minute later, his stash was abnormal.

Merit Points: 1700

The battle had just begun, and he’d almost made enough for another chunk of Star-Forged Steel. His eyes turned into dollar signs, but then a strong hit to the back sent him rolling. He blasted through a Birth of Aura monster and turned to look at his assailant.

Lieutenant Lickmaster, Level 33 (Field Boss)

This boy has skills. He’d won the title of most desired chameleon three years in a row before the puppeteer turned them all into tentacle zombies. Now fully integrated with its tentacles, this swell guy wants to dominate much more than just your genitals.

You can probably take him, though.

The chameleon from the orchard stared at him, on the ground this time. Its tongue had clipped him but didn’t break a bone. Gary frowned. A stronger monster might have injured him. Suddenly, he remembered there were sector bosses gunning for him. It was possible that every single monster had a secret order to assassinate him.

I can’t drop my guard, he thought.

His Void Lightning core pulsed. A powerful aura field expanded from him, reaching almost fifteen feet away. The volume of information was staggering, especially as his aura field clashed with many others, and keeping it up was a significant aura expenditure, but that was a sacrifice he was willing to make. Safety came first.

The chameleon turned invisible. He only caught the dirt rising as it stepped around at high speed, then jabbed an also invisible tongue at him.

“Thunder Step!”

He veered off, blasting through the battlefield himself. He and the chameleon circled each other with weaker monsters caught in between and eviscerated by their strikes. Thankfully, Gary was in enemy lines, so he didn’t need to worry. He smoothly angled towards his army while duking it out with the chameleon.

Back when he’d broken through, he’d thrust into the chameleon’s tongue and injured it heavily. The monster remembered that, so it avoided head-on collisions. It used invisibility and high speed to dart around Gary and snap at him from inconvenient angles just outside the range of his aura field. Gary defended calmly. He couldn’t parry the tongue, but knowing where it came from, he could dodge. Or take it.

The tongue blasted again, and he took the strike to the waist to wrap his arm around the tongue. When the chameleon pulled it back, he held on. He flew to the monster. A panicked squeal reached him as the chameleon entered his aura field, his vibration clashing against a colorful, elusive kind. He used his momentum to plant a Black Thrust in its side, sending it flying, then thunderstepped in rapid succession to appear over it before it could escape. The chameleon released a multi-colored shield.

“Fourth Song: Lightning!” Gary yelled, crashing down his spear like the god of thunder, penetrating the shield and the chameleon as if both were made of paper. The field boss gave a final death throe with the Golden Bolt still embedded in its midsection.

Level up! You have reached Level 30. +8 Free Points

Gary got eight points on every level-up, as well as an extra four in Vitality from the Seed of Eternal Flesh. He opened his status screen and quickly distributed the free points between Strength and Speed, equally.

Strength: 84

Speed: 104

Vitality: 144

Vitality was the most important stat in keeping him alive, but it was already absurdly high. Speed was a close second in importance, while Gary felt comfortable with his low Strength for now. His techniques and vibration were destructive enough that he never felt he lacked the power.

“Asim needs help. Behind you!” Goldie yelled, and Gary immediately rushed there—after retrieving his spear from the corpse. He found Asim and Evil Shrek beset by a Core Formation swordsman. Edgar, Donkey, and the three hellhounds—Ain, Akila, and Anippe—were trying to protect them, but the swordsman easily ran circles around them. A shotgun fired and missed. Evil Shrek cursed as he reloaded, a deep gash running down his side.

Gary thunderstepped into the enemy. The swordsman reacted in time, and the two mixed in a dance of sword and spear.

This enemy wasn’t half-bad. Gary found himself challenged. He tried to keep the distance while the swordsman moved like a fly, even faster than the mosquitoes before, to slip into Gary’s range. He used the same technique as against the boxing champion, angling his strikes so the best way to dodge them was away from Gary. They fought for some time.

Finally, eyeing the gash on Evil Shrek, Gary pretended to miss a thrust. The swordsman’s eyes brightened. “River of Stars!” he yelled, arriving immediately at Gary’s side, slashing at his ribs, tearing up his robes, and drawing a thin line of blood on his skin.

“What?” the swordsman said, seeing his attack’s ineffectiveness. Gary had been ready. The butt of his spear cracked the swordsman’s skull, and a slash separated his head from his shoulder. The purple-robed swordsman with the silver sword died with regrets.

Gary used his aura to inspect the wound. It wasn’t too heavy, as he expected when he saw that the swordsman hadn’t been able to cleave through a Birth of Aura field boss, and it was already regenerating. His only strong suit had been his speed and technique, at which Gary was evenly matched.

It put his sky-high stats under a new perspective.

“Are you okay?” he asked.

“Yeah,” Asim replied, panting. Evil Shrek cocked his shotgun.

“It’s not my first siege,” he said, jumping on Donkey’s back and riding to battle. Gary shook his head. He was surprised to find his merit points increased by six hundred—the man he’d killed must have been a field boss.

Gary scanned the battlefield. They were winning. The enemy climbers weren’t fighting, but staying at the edge of the battlefield to survive. A line of heavyweight tentacle monsters circled the battlefield, not letting anyone desert. There also weren’t many chimeras.

Even with these advantages, however, the battle was far from easy.

Suddenly, the ground shook. Gary lost his balance, and then he spotted a gigantic fist falling towards the front of their army.

“Shit!” he said, grabbing Asim and thunderstepping away.

The punch never landed.

“Testudo!”

“Blossoming Dawn!”

“Rags to Riches!”

Three voices merged as one. Boulderus Johnus and the two rogue sector bosses—the golden-robed woman and the man in rags—took to the air, clashing directly against the fist in a shower of colors. Even at this distance, Gary was rebuffed by winds of aura.

The punch was stopped, the entire titan reeling back. He took a step to steady himself and accidentally squashed a group of tentacle monsters. He harrumphed. “Mother will be displeased,” he said, staring at the three climbers who’d stopped him.

“Mate, we’re going to tear her a new one,” the ragged guy said.

They leaped, summoning more of their aura against the titan. Explosions came from the side, where Strok the boxing champ ducked and weaved between General Whipfrog’s tongue and whips. Every punch he threw was apocalyptic. When not fighting for his belt, the champ was a level 50 sector boss, and it showed.

A huge, red bird cage sprang near the entrance of the puppeteer’s base. Gary was suddenly reminded of the final battle in the Floor of Trials, where Kyle had been killed. Booms echoed from inside the cage, and he spotted the signature black and white traces of Feng Shen’s attacks.

The brothers were here, too, and they’d always been strong. But now, so was Gary.

“Well then,” he said, breaking into a grin, “I better go say hi.”
