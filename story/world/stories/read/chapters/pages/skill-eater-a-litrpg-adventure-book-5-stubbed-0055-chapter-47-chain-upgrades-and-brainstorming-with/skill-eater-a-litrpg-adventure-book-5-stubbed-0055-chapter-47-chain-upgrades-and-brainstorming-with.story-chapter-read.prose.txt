When Edge made it back to World’s End, he stopped by the beast hunters’ society to sell his parts, then walked to the harvesters’ tower to offload the resources he’d found while exploring the forest.

His next stop was the Association’s vault. He added the gladiator’s gear to the resources King had given him—bound for Puppet Town whenever the Wave-Bound Warrior returned—and the next caravan was ready to head out. There wasn’t anything he wanted to keep, and while the man’s demise had resulted from his murderous inclinations and poor choice of opponent, Edge didn’t want to be associated with the deed.

He arrived at the Lost Traveler in time for the crew’s midday meeting and let his friends know about the new powers that were his to command.

“Wait,” Sasha said. “You can fucking fly now? I am so jealous.”

“Those skills you stole from the gladiator sound awesome too,” Jumo added. “Shortsighted fool.”

“I’m always happy when the team picks up more sensory-boosting magic, and a Rare power to boot.” Trapper nodded her agreement. “I can’t wait to see what upgrades are offered when you take Enhance Senses to rank two. If the situation permits, I’d like to help you think the matter through before pulling the trigger.”

Mel and Sakura shared their thoughts, and everyone helped Edge brainstorm on how to make the most of his skills. As they spoke, they prepared lunch, sipping from frosty bottles of beer while grilling up a storm in the kitchen.

When the food was ready, everyone sat around the table, bringing him up to speed on what had happened while he was away. Sakura had made considerable progress hacking into the auction’s database. She didn’t think she would finish before they met with the Coalition, but another few days should do it.

The information broker Trapper had spoken to was convinced the Legendary lens had been won by a high-ranking member of the Hunter’s Association, but she hadn’t been able to confirm the information or determine the other participants of the bidding war.

At Jumo’s request, Sakura had used her ultimate abilities on Frederick and Kira. They were both stage three and didn’t have access to the VIP balconies at the auction house, which meant neither of them was the Ancient. As they were in desperate need of allies and trusted Jumo’s judgment, the crew decided to form an alliance with Syndicate’s leaders. They shared everything they had learned about the monsters thus far, including the results of Sakura’s investigation into the serial killer.

Going forward, they would be working together to protect Lowtown and thwart the Ancient’s activities. Frederick promised to share anything he learned about the Coalition or the Wardens, along with any promising opportunities that might benefit Puppet Town.

When Sasha’s turn came, she told him the First had worked out an arrangement with the hunting societies that was inspired by Edge’s actions.

“They’ve set up a donation station where people can deposit low-grade materials and used gear. It’s only a drop in the bucket, but it’s helping a handful of people who are strong enough to survive in the Lakelands but lacked the requisite resources to start hunting in the field, releasing a bit of the tension simmering in the free city.”

Mel reported that monster sightings in the Lakelands had been on a steady decline since the incident in the Haunted Vale. Whatever the Ancient that crafted the dimensional anchor was up to, it hadn’t created any rifts in the region recently. That just left the Coalition and the Wardens. There were rumors that one faction or the other had wiped out a prominent jailbird gang on the far side of the biome, but they hadn’t been confirmed, and the factions’ current whereabouts and objectives were unknown.

When the crew went their separate ways, Edge went looking for Beelo to get his take on Kinetic Evasion and Polearm Mastery. He was hoping to get some tips on incorporating them into his training regimen and wanted to see how useful the defensive skill would be when battling a weapon expert.

He stopped by the training annex, but Beelo wasn’t there. However, one of the warrior’s friends let Edge know that his mentor was having lunch at a nearby tavern. When he arrived, Beelo was finishing the final bites of his meal. They stayed long enough to enjoy a delicious dessert, then walked to the arena together.

After stepping into a private room, Edge let the gladiator know about the skills in question. “Damn. I’m surprised you managed to find such good gems for sale outside of the gold-ring auction. They must have cost a fortune.”

He was spared the effort of devising a lie when the warrior grabbed his glaive and gestured for Edge to step into the ring. “Well then. Let’s find out what they can do.”

Kinetic Evasion was amazing right out of the gate, although he had a long way to go before he could interpret every sensation it provided quickly enough to process the information mid-battle. Even so, it helped him deal with some tricks he hadn’t seen before, preventing him from being disarmed by maneuvers that would have taken him out otherwise.

Although Edge knew he wasn’t ready yet, he tried using Evasion in conjunction with Gaze’s combined mode. Beelo started off slow before picking up the pace until their glaives were meeting so fast he couldn’t track them with his eyes.

For a single, glorious second, everything came together like a lens sliding into focus. He dodged one attack, parried another, and counterattacked with a seamless combination that forced Beelo to take a few hasty steps back before Edge’s brain was overwhelmed and the warrior smacked him in the head with the shaft of his weapon.

Mastery wasn’t well-suited to sparring full tilt, but it was amazing for combat drilling, letting Edge correct his mistakes before the gladiator had time to point them out. Instead of providing direct feedback past that point, the warrior taught him a dozen routines he could practice on his own, each designed to instill a single concept.

Beelo was deeply impressed and offered some tips that would help Edge get more out of powers as time went on.

“That was great,” the gladiator said. “Both of those skills are amazing. Polearm Mastery is no substitute for expert instruction and experience, but you’re learning considerably faster than before. Kinetic Evasion is already making you more difficult to hit, and I can tell you’ve only scratched the surface of what it can do.

“When you rank up those skills and integrate their feedback, you’ll be a nightmare to face in the ring. Keep up the good work. I can’t wait to find out what you’re capable of when you have a few seasons under your belt.”

They used the rest of the session training Foebinder, alternating between Opulence and the other glaive. When they were finished, Edge thanked the man for his time, paying Beelo double the regular fee for helping him make the most of his new skills on top of their usual routine. They clasped hands and parted ways after agreeing to meet again soon for another lesson.

Extraction’s cooldown would expire around midnight, and he wanted to maximize its use before speaking with King. After the crew’s evening meeting, Edge ate dinner and stepped into the Lakelands, eager to take his new Rare powers to rank two—maybe earn another cycle first if he encountered promising prey.

While he was searching for a beast to hunt, he decided to try out Manifest Chain’s upgrades, starting with the adjustable anchors. The first thing he learned was he couldn’t move them before summoning the chains, which meant Foebinder still split the skin of his left wrist before emerging into the moonlight.

After greeting his metallic ally, he began to experiment. When he ordered the anchor to travel to his palm, the links tore through his flesh. It wasn’t a lot of damage, since the strange place where the chain appeared was just below his skin, but it was painful and would cause problems if he moved it anywhere his armor was in the way.

Instead, he willed the anchor to emerge from his body and spent a long while examining it in depth. He’d been manifesting it for months by this point, but this was the first time he was able to get a good look at it. It was a circular hole in spacetime that looked like an oil slick when viewed from the far side, shimmering with a rainbow sheen.

Edge had only experienced portal transportation a few times, since the System had shut long-range transportation down in the aftermath of the anomaly. However, this seemed more like the rift that led to the monsters’ homeworld, although it was connected to his core instead of another dimension. Actually, inner worlds could exist in other dimensions for all I know, and Skill-Eater’s is even more unusual.

Regardless of the details, once the anchor point was separated from his flesh, the chain no longer tugged on his wrist when it grabbed onto objects, which meant he couldn’t use it to reposition, but he couldn’t be pulled off-balance if something yanked on it either.

The rift moved in time with his body and provided a rock-solid attachment. Since the chains of oblivion were incredibly long and connected to the bedrock of Skill-Eater’s realm, Foebinder could bear loads up to its capacity without any chance of the far end coming free.

It was easier to swing Opulence without the left chain attached to his arm, although Foebinder had gotten pretty good at minimizing the impact from that arrangement. Magebane was another story. Separating the anchor point from his right wrist would make melee combat a lot easier going forward. The chain would benefit too—able to extend its antimagic touch and wield the spell-severing scythe without having to coordinate with the movements of his arm.

He tried attaching the anchor to other parts of his body, which worked more or less how he’d expected. Edge wasn’t sure what advantage connecting the living links to anywhere other than his wrists would provide, but he was certain it would come in handy eventually.

Last on his list, he tried out the Mana Enhancement upgrade. It was considerably more expensive than flaring his other skills, but Foebinder received a decent boost to its Power and Speed, which would be useful when the left chain was wielding a weapon, carrying a shield, or subduing a powerful opponent.

When he tried boosting Magebane, the differences weren’t nearly as pronounced, although the links seemed more dexterous than before. When he focused, he realized the Disruption field around the right chain was significantly more robust, which meant enhancing Magebane improved its ability to interfere with the skills of whatever it touched. It looks like it receives a modest boost to Control and a big one to Disruption.

When he enhanced both chains at once, it drained a ton of mana, in part because manifesting both chains counted as a combo. He wouldn’t be boosting them at the same time too often outside of Overdrive, although it was always nice to have more options.

By the time he was done testing out Chain’s new powers, Extraction’s cooldown had nearly expired. Edge entered stealth mode and began hunting in earnest, grinning in anticipation while wondering what upgrades were on the menu.
