Zora didn’t consider himself one of those mountain nomads in the Mori Masif Front who measured time in seasons. He didn’t think he was an old man, in any case, to be counting days like that, but…

It was the dawn of summer—one month after the siege on the Divine Capital, and exactly three years since he stepped foot in the Attini Empire.

The siege ended the moment Regalia was forced out of the city. Every Giant-Class and Mutant-Class bug hammering streets and collapsing districts retreated all at once, and even the undead went slack. As he’d predicted, there was a range to how far Regalia could sustain her summoned zombie ants, so the moment she was too far away from the city, her ants simply dissolved. Tens of thousands of corpses stopped moving in the same breadth.

Afterwards, stabilizing the city was no small issue. It was less heroic and more honest. Frankly, it was pure logistics. Kita and the Salaqa Household’s soldiers held the southern wall together with their palms on the stone for three days and three nights straight, pouring army ants into the cracks and chasms while thousands of architects and engineers threw themselves into repairing the damages. He had to admit, he was impressed. Regalia had decayed more of the wall than he’d initially noticed, but the architects were able to seal the cracks up with mycelium growth and make the wall look even sturdier in just three days. If that wasn’t the power of pure, overwhelming numbers, he didn’t know what was.

And the month afterwards was tumultuous in ways that the civil war never was. With the zombified Divine Empress torn from the throne and Her Four Households completely decimated, there was nothing left in the capital but a power vacuum for the Reunification Army to fill… but there was no real opposition to the question of who would sit on the throne of ant heads next. He’d heard the new government was planning to drop the ‘Divine’ part of both the title and the capital. They were going to call their capital something else now, and probably for the better.

Kita Salaqa did not believe in the gods, and she would never wait for them to bless her empire.

After her display of valor and utmost bravery on the day of the siege—being both the first person to rush down the northern wall and place her hands on the crumbling southern wall—no Noble-Blood still worth a damn could deny she had the strength of heart to lead the empire. Even more indisputable and irrefutable, of course, was her bloodline. After Zora’s great speech on the northern wall that day, many scholars and historians did more digging into the Salaqa Household’s bloodline, and they realized the old records did not lie. Kita Salaqa was, indeed, a direct descendent of the child of the First Divine Empress. She had both the strength of heart to lead and the royal blood in her, so who could oppose her claiming the throne now? Who would dare oppose a new Walking Legend?

In any case, they hadn’t held the coronation ceremony yet. There was still a decade’s worth of diplomacy, bookkeeping errors, and economic instability for the government to deal with. Kita Saalqa may be supported by her uncle Yiru and several of her father’s close Outer Regional Lords serving as her advisors—and Machi, her new royal attendant who followed her wherever she went, was also as much of a topic as she was—but it’d still take time for the Reunification Army to distribute aid to the Outer Regions. It’d take time for the resources that’d been hoarded in the capital to be released back to the valleys and coasts and highlands they’d been drained from, and it’d take time for the capital itself to be properly rebuilt and repaired. It simply wouldn’t be ‘right’ to hold a coronation while half of the empire was still reeling from the siege.

But Zora had heard rumors that even the Spore Knights fighting on the World’s End Beach were going to be sending a few representatives into the Royal Temple tonight to witness the coronation ceremony, and to formally address their new Empress.

She’s got lots to do from now on.

Not only does she have to manage politics and grain and diplomacy on that uncomfortable-looking throne, but she’ll also have to supply the Spore Knights with her own ants through her Swarmblood Art.

The previous Divine Empress had a completely different Ant Class, so now that she was dead, the new Empress had to take control of the Spore Knights and give them her new ants. The act of blessing the empire’s strongest warriors was a major part of the coronation ceremony, so Zora was just thankful he didn’t have to go talk to the Spore Knights themselves to convince them to come. They must’ve heard of her exploits even while they were fending off the endless Crawling Seas.

Now…

Now, there was nothing for Zora to do but sit at the top of the northern wall, lifting his chin up at the warm morning sun.

Enki was beside him, cross-legged with his rifle in his lap. Zora’s own staff was laid on the stone beside him. A hundred meters above the city and the World’s End Beach, they had the single greatest view any man could afford in the empire—barring perhaps the Great Fungus Tree in the middle of the capital, of course, though that giant mushroom tree was only about eighty meters tall—which meant the top of the northern wall was also the windiest and coolest spot in the entire empire.

It was a good morning for a funeral.

Today, Kita Salaqa and the Outer Regional Lords were honoring those who fought and died in the civil war. The procession was passing in the fields just south of the northern walls, and right about now, it should be coming to an end. The moment of speeches would fade into the moment of silence, and the silence would thicken into individual grief as everyone scattered to visit their own graves.

Right about now, Kita Salaqa should be visiting the graves of her family. Her sister, her mother, and now her father.

Zora had offered to go with her, but she’d smiled and shook her head, so now he was stuck up here.

Wind carried three kinds of sounds up to the top of the wall: the hammering of reconstruction down in the capital, the faraway roar of the Crawling Seas beyond the southern edge of the continent, and the heavy, laborious breaths of the driver climbing up the wall behind him and Enki.

No effort was made to hide the last sound. Ifas had been climbing up the hard way for the better part of the past ten minutes, but now he finally hauled himself over the edge, mildly annoyed as he plopped down next to them.

“You two really made me come up here the hard way, huh?” he grumbled.

Amusement tugged at Zora’s mouth as he kept listening out at the Crawling Seas. “There is an old proverb from the Mori Masif’s valley cooks: ‘A man who complains while climbing is simply announcing he fully intends on reaching the top’.”

“Hah! Whatever that means.” Ifas stretched until his joints popped in a line. “I’m famished, though. Anyone want some fresh fruit?”

“No,” Enki said plainly.

“Gladly,” Zora said.

Ifas was going to eat either way, so he plucked out a few pears from under his coat and began peeling them with a small knife. Despite his refusal, Enki caught his slices and munched on them quietly. They were soft, juicy, and crunch—just how Zora liked his fruits.

Chewing filled the silence. Wind came and went. The Crawling Seas kept rasping beyond the horizon, and as they ate, Ifas squinted at the wall of dark clouds that shrouded the world past the horizon.

“Seeing it with your own two eyes really is different than hearing it in the stories,” he said, crunching down on another slice of pear. “You know there are bugs as far as the horizon is long, and then you come here and realize… there really is that many. Feels impossible to beat them, don’t it? There may even be bugs out there that nobody’s ever even seen on this continent, just waiting for this first wave to crumble.”

No answer came from either of them.

“... Don’t matter, I suppose.” Ifas chuckled. “That just means there’s more types of bugs I can turn into delicious meals.”

With that, all three of them finished their pears. It’d been a while since Zora ate breakfast consistently, so this small amount of fruit was more than enough for him to get on with the rest of his morning.

Usually, that meant a little bit of exercise.

Without another word, all three of them stood up together, and direction separated them. Zora and Enki went left along the wall, weapons in hand. Right carries Ifas, unhurried, and he whistled as if he were strolling through a market. Once they opened twenty paces between them, they all turned around to face each other.

Zora raised his staff. Enki raised his rifle. Both of them pointed their weapons at Ifas.

“Alright.” Zora sighed. “We’ve pushed this off long enough. Let’s just get it over wi—”

Ifas disappeared so completely that it wasn’t even a blur in Zora’s senses. Sound didn’t trail him. The air didn’t shift. Even the small betrayals that typically meant movement—cloth shifting, breath catching—vanished in an instant.

Then something behind Zora sounded like wind being removed from the world.

Enki reacted first, snapping his rifle around. Impact followed instantly—kitchen knife against bayonet—and the collision cracked the top of the wall beneath their feet. A shockwave rolled out, rattling stone shards out of the newly sealed cracks and chasms.

Ifas laughed, gripping his knife in one hand.

Speed became violence as the chef dashed and blurred circles around the two of them, footsteps skipping the way a pebble skipped across water. Sound arrived in fragments, too fast for normal ears to stitch together, and even Zora couldn’t quite tell where he was at any given point in time. Ifas knew this. That was why he kept trying to close in on the blind man, and not the boy warping at equally rapid speeds, clashing his bayonet against Ifas’ knife time and time again.

Left alone, the two of them would completely annihilate the top of the wall with the force of their melee, so Zora cracked his neck and tapped his staff against the ground.

“Shards of stone, get up, ignite, and fly.”

The damaged wall obeyed. Hundreds of stone splinters tore free around his feet, lifted into the air, and ignited along their edges before launching out in every direction in a perfect sphere. Enki dodged them easily enough, warping in next to Zora, but Ifas—caught behind the two of them—simply swept his knife in a blur of arcs to cut down the few dozen shards flying his way, letting the rest pass harmlessly into open air.

Then his knife left his hand as he chucked it at Zora.

Zora didn’t flinch. He angled his staff.

“Catch it.”

The knife stopped dead inches from his face, but before he could pluck it out of the air, smoke exploded from the hilt. That made him flinch a little as the smoke bit his throat, making his voice come out just a little weaker than he would’ve liked.

“Six stone javelins, track him.”

More stone shards shattered up, forming six long javelins that immediately screamed at Ifas as he charged straight at them through the smoke. He didn’t bother cutting them. He dashed around them with insulting ease, forcing them to curve and adjust mid-flight to chase after him, and even Enki seemed a little taken aback as he suddenly appeared before both of them.

Enki stabbed forward with his rifle and fired, but Ifas coiled around the barrel like a serpent and grabbed Enki’s face.

A trap.

A wormhole opened under the two of them, and they plummeted before reappearing far above Zora. Enki shrugged Ifas’ hand off his face and raised his rifle, firing seven shots in a second. Zora had seen those bullets take out a Mutant-Class like it was nothing, but Ifas flicked seven smaller knives out of his sleeves to destroy all seven bullets before taking out a pot lid and a frying pan, grinning down at the two of them.

The six stone javelins flew past the falling Enki and shot up at Ifas. The chef was armed now. He batted and slashed and crushed every single javelin into shards, then kicked off solid air above him to propel himself down.

Zora scoffed.

A cloud of stone dust exploded as Ifas and Enki both crashed back down onto the wall. All of them recovered, moved, and by the time Zora swept his staff around to clear the cloud, a tie was already formed.

The three of them were standing in a triangle. Ifas’ frying pan and pot lid were pressed against their necks on both sides. Enki’s bayonet was pointed straight at Ifas’ heart, while the sharpened tip of Zora’s staff was pointed straight at the man’s neck in return.

One more step from any one of them would draw blood, so nobody moved.

“... How much were you offered?” Zora asked, humor returning to him first.

“More than you can even comprehend,” Ifas replied, grinning without easing his pressure. “The previous Divine Empress and Her Four Households really wanted you dead, you see. They weren’t stingy at all.”

“But for the Death God, the so-called greatest assassin in the world, it’s not just about the money, is it?”

Ifas’ grin widened. “I have all the money I ever need. I’d simply heard too many curious things about the infamous Thousand Tongue and the Worm Mage, and guess what? Intercepting the letter the late Salaqa Lord sent to the driver who was supposed to drive you around was easy enough for how much fun this past year has been.”

Enki raised a slow brow. “Fun?”

“Oh, yes. If the two of you were boring, I’d have killed you in your sleep ages ago and collected the bounty, but then you guys just kept escalating things over and over again and… hah! I got to cook so many new bugs I’ve never had the chance to cook before thanks to you!”

Zora smiled. “What now, then? The late Divine Empress who hired you and all those other assassins to kill us is dead. I doubt the new Empress would want us dead, so there’s no reason for you to keep going, right?”

Silence for a moment.

Then the pressure eased. Ifas withdrew both lid and pan casually, stepped back, and sat down cross-legged.

“Well, yeah. That usually happens when my employer gets offed.” He patted the ground next to him, gesturing for them to sit. “That was a decent bit of stretching and warming up. Want some tea now?”

Reluctance sat heavy on Enki’s shoulders, but Zora sat first. Enki followed, because the boy probably trusted his judgement, even if it was never said aloud.

Kettle and cups emerged from Ifas’s coat like a magician pulling out frogs and birds, and he started humming as he assembled a small brewing set.

“... You don’t even have a system,” Enki said finally, watching the brewing process carefully. “How are you that strong?”

Ifas opened his mouth, Zora spoke first.

“He’s a Hayawari.”

Ifas paused for a moment and glanced at Zora. “You know of us?”

“I did a bit of research when I first left Amadeus Academy,” he replied. “The word is old. It means ‘Blessed Man’ in the Sharaji tongue, but the Hayawari are simply exceptionally rare people born with the ability to resist mutating bug parts and losing their humanity even after consuming bioarcanic essence. Their bodies are… innately capable of converting any essence they consume into raw physical refinement: increased muscle density, faster nervous reaction, perception, and the like. The Hayawari are like if Enki and I poured every single point we ever obtained into our physical attributes instead of unlocking new mutations.”

“That’s a nice way to put it.” Ifas sipped his own tea and nodded. “In that case, you just saved me a ton of explaining. Here, have a bit of this as well.”

Steam curled between their hands as Ifas passed the cups over. Zora accepted his with both palms and drank lightly. Hayawari were rare, yes, but not so rare that one could simply stumble into becoming the ‘Death God’ by accident. The ability to suppress his own bioarcanic arc the way Ifas did must’ve required years of training—but Zora didn’t feel like prying now.

The cup of tea was good. It was warm and grounding, and it was the only thing making him feel just slightly comfortable as he continued listening out at the Crawling Seas.

“It’s not over yet,” he muttered. “The Swarm still lives, and Morta is still out there somewhere. If Decima was any indication, the very last Magicicada Witch will not be easy to find.” He tilted his head slightly and lifted his staff, listening for a vibration that never came. “Even now, my staff is giving me nothing. I’ll likely have to scour every inch of the continent to find her… which is why I’ll be needing a driver. And a cook.”

Ifas hummed thoughtfully, taking another sip of tea. Zora turned just enough for his blind gaze to settle in the man’s direction.

“I promise you it’ll be fun,” he said, “but you’ll have to promise me one thing as well, if you wish to follow me.”

“What is it?”

“Never kill another human again.”

“Sure,” Ifas said casually. “It’s about time I retired the Death God title anyways. Never liked it to begin with. I’ve been in the killing business long enough.”

“... You don’t look that old.”

Ifas laughed. “Bioarcanic essence does wonders for keeping the years off. However old you think I am, I’m probably just a little bit older than that.”

Before Zora could reply, the city bells began to ring. Eight clear chimes rolled across the capital, echoing off stone and scaffold and newly rebuilt districts. Zora turned his face towards the city and let the moment settle.

“We can talk about our travel plans tonight,” he said. “First, we have an heiress to turn into an Empress.”
