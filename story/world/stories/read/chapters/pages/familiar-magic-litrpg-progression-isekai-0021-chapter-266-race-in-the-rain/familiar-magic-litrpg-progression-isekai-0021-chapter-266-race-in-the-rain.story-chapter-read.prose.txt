In a dark crevice within the red mountains of Potentia, Ethan faced a horror from his past that he’d hoped never to encounter again. Undeath was a unique phenomenon that he now understood was tied to the endless cycle of death and rebirth across this world. Some few demons had somehow gained the ability to  interact with this cycle, and even corrupt it further. Ethan was looking at that corruption now.

He knew that he had little time, and reacted as swiftly as he could. As the Follower reanimated in front of him, Last Resort exploded from its scabbard. Ethan slashed again and again, holding nothing back, and layering on his Mystic enhancement at the same time.

Thankfully, the Follower had been killed already, and undead didn’t equal up to their living counterparts. Ethan sliced the Follower into dozens of pieces, then turned, and raced away.

“This is very, very bad,” he said.

“I never encountered undead on my world,” Tomo said. “This horrific affinity is something that came later, when the corruption had grown much stronger. To hold the dead in this realm when they should be reabsorbed into the Astral is remarkable enough, but to reanimate their bodies is extraordinary.”

“Extraordinarily concerning,” Ethan said. “I only faced one of those creatures before, and at Dusk rank it had raised an army thousands strong. At Twilight…well, I’m starting to realize that Apollo’s ability to control large forces–and the Followers for that matter–may not be quite so rare as we might hope.”

Tomo growled in acknowledgement. “Followers…”

“Yes,” Ethan said, moving back through the crevice. “They’re here. I don’t know what that means, but it’ll be the next problem we deal with after this unexpected apocalypse in the making.”

His words were punctuated by more thunder, as the storm continued moving closer. Ethan could feel the rain now, and suspected it was far worse back on the open plains with the army. He frantically tried his communication rune, but they were unreliable on Potentia in the best of times, and no one answered his panicked calls.

Ethan slid to a halt when he reached the dead gigas he had passed earlier. They were now less dead, and dragging themselves toward him, enormous hands grasping. But they were Dusk and Dawn rank, and Ethan was in a hurry. Their reaching hands found only Revan’s flames, obliterating them entirely.

“Thanks, buddy,” Ethan said, resuming his sprint as the Familiar disappeared in a burst of flames.

As he ran, he tried to remember everything he’d been taught about the undead. He’d faced them in his first year on Nexum, and that felt like a lifetime ago. The most important thing was that their true vulnerability lay in the only actual undead, those few demons that were, themselves, very much alive, and merely possessing the so-called undead affinity.

These demons would have full autonomy, be exceptionally powerful, and when slayed, take their minions with them. Furthermore, it was their abilities which brought defeated creatures back to a false life, preserved them, and controlled them directly. That meant they needed to stay relatively close. It wasn’t like a zombie movie where one stray undead could get a few lucky bites and ruin everything.

That said, any demon that did die within the true undead’s sphere of influence would rise again. And while the dead weren’t as powerful as they had been while living, they were relentless, and felt no pain. Demons already tended to be terrifying combinations of strength, claws, and fury, and Ethan wouldn’t underestimate how deadly a combination that could be. Combine it with an enemy that didn’t react to injury, and it was easy to understand how pervasive undeath could become.

Ethan kept moving. Now rushing uphill through what was quickly becoming a river, as he expected. He idly cut down any undead he found, focusing on rapid, debilitating strikes, primarily aiming for legs on the smaller ones, and tendons on the larger. The undead didn’t heal, and those injuries would mean it would be hours before they reached the army.

But when Ethan reached the monstrous forest he’d passed through on the way here, it was clear that other undead were already far ahead of him. The forest was going wild, limbs and roots snapping crazily in every direction. Ethan didn’t know if the trees themselves were vulnerable to the undead affliction, but they were clearly agitated, and many had been damaged, leaking sap-like blood.

Ethan tried his communication rune again. “Apollo! Selina! Fable! Can any of you hear me?”

He kept racing forward, feet splashing through puddles as the dark clouds transformed day into night. Ethan considered sending Deevee ahead, although at Twilight rank he was nearly as fast on foot as he was using rifts. More importantly, he continued to encounter undead–there were hundreds of them in the forest, and each one he crippled was one fewer reaching his army.

He was nearly halfway back when he finally heard something.

“Ethan?” Apollo said. “We kept getting broken messages, but Fable was able to enhance communication. She suggested we set up relay towers going forward, kind of like cell towers–”

“Apollo!” Ethan bellowed.

“Yeah, yeah,” he said. “The storm is here, but don’t worry: I’m all tucked away inside the train like I promised. The army is tucked away too, this is one hell of a downpour.”

“Damn it, Apollo. Undead!” Ethan shouted.

“What? Where?”

“Coming for the army. They could already be there for all I know. That’s what was killing the scouts, and I found Dusk ranked gigas already down, which means–”

“That there’s something stronger than Dusk rank taking them out, in all likelihood. Damn it. I’ve never encountered the undead personally. How bad is this?”

“Under normal circumstances? Extremely bad. Anything that dies will become one of them, and they’re very difficult to take down. But under the circumstances? With the storm, and the army buried…”

“They’re sitting ducks. Shit. Ethan, the lightning out here is insane. Fable has raised dozens of lightning rods, but the plains are still being struck like crazy. If I wake the army, we’ll lose a lot of them.”

“Which in turn, will become undead. Yeah, there’s really no good options here. But we can’t do nothing. If our army stays underground, they’ll all get turned. Our only chance to fight back while I search out the leaders. Hopefully there aren’t many, and they aren’t too powerful.”

“That’s a lot of unknowns,” Apollo said. “Maybe we should–”

He cut off, and Ethan worried that communication might have been severed again, until the voice came back, deep and pitiless.

“These abominations must be brought to heel!”

“Hello, Angelos,” Ethan said. “I couldn’t agree more, but can you please put Apollo back on?”

“The fighting has already begun. I am losing my connection to the outermost reaches of my crusade. I must attend to this.”

“Angelos, wait! You can’t risk yourself, everything falls apart without you!” True he could reconjure Apollo after some time, not unlike losing one of his Bonds, but the army would be lost.

When silence was all that came back to him, Ethan switched to contacting Selina. It was more than a minute before she answered.

“Ethan, you have to hurry! Exactly what you’re picturing is happening!”

“Angelos is out in a lightning storm getting his army killed?”

“While blowing around like a kite and being struck by a dozen lightning bolts. We couldn’t stop him!”

“I’ll be there soon, I’m about ten minutes out. Help Fable come up with ways to help, this is going to get very bad.”

“We’ll do our best, Ethan.” She paused. “This is a bit like old times, but I’m realizing a lot of our old times were terrible.”

“You’re not wrong,” Ethan said. “You be careful too, Selina, and stay with Fable. There’s something very powerful out there, Twilight rank and dangerous.”

“Understood, good luck Ethan.”

Ethan kept running, pushing his body even harder. Tomo’s Astral form floated next to him, looking curious.

“You did not tell them about the Follower,” he said.

“One problem at a time, Tomo. Besides, I doubt they’re connected to the immediate issue. We need to get control of this situation as quickly as possible. Then we can worry about what one of those bastards was doing on Potentia.”

“And how many more there may be,” Tomo put forward.

Ethan tried not to think about it. His mind was already racing through tactics that might work against an undead army, while they were in the midst of his own demon army. Even acknowledging the situation was crazy enough to make his head spin.

If they met on equal footing, the more powerful force would eventually win–provided they had enough of a power advantage that their own losses, and subsequent increase to the undead ranks wouldn’t tip the scales. Ethan was fairly confident he’d have the larger army in this situation. It wasn’t easy to gather demons together, especially as the locals had to be more aware of the undead threat than on Nexum. If they weren’t, if they didn’t have some kind of instinct to run, or target the controlling demons, Potentia would have fallen to the undead long ago.

But with his army largely buried and helpful, or alternatively emerging from the ground and being decimated by wind and lightning, the circumstances were very different. Each of his demons could potentially be targeted individually, and if they started with some of his Twilights, Ethan’s options would drop quickly.

He was considering how to best use his powers when the forest seemed to vanish all at once, leaving him sprinting across the planes through rain and darkness. And there was his army, waiting for him.

In utter chaos.

The first thing that stood out was the Fable Train itself–though it didn’t have much in common with a train at the moment. It looked more like a wide steel wall, likely meant to shelter the army from the rain. Lightning struck countless rods rising upward, and each flash illuminated thousands of demons, huddling in the shadows of the train. Hovering above it all was a glowing orb of light, which Ethan recognized at Apollo, in all his glory.

But Ethan’s attention was quickly drawn to the madness closer by. Enormous shadows moved across a battlefield he could make little sense of. He realized by the hulking, humanoid shapes that many were gigas. Even at only Dusk rank they were roughly thirty feet tall, and crashing through anything in their path.

At their feet, rushing around like cockroaches when a light turned on, were hundreds of smaller demons. Ethan had no idea which belonged to him, and which were undead. They were only visible in the flashes of lightning, which had a strobing effect that ruined his nightvision.

He moved toward where the fighting was densest, hoping to get lucky and spot one of the true undead. They would be moving more naturally, and smoothly, and not likely involved directly in combat.

He wasn’t surprised not to spot one easily, but he did slide to a halt when something else caught his attention. While it had been crouched before, a gigas was now standing back up, and stretching to its true height, easily eighty feet or more, and looming over the battlefield. It had three heads and four arms, and was currently slamming two of the army’s twilights against the ground over and over.

“Perfect,” Ethan muttered. “I’ve always wanted to see what those things evolved into.”

The battle with the undead had truly begun.
