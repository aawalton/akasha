The thing lunged.

Cale barely had time to move before it was on them—a blur of muscle, bone, and raw malice. A razor-limbed blur tore through the dim light, slamming into Galvon and sending him crashing into the wall like a ragdoll.

No hesitation. No warning. Just pure, murderous intent.

Risto fired first. The twin blasts of golden energy hammered into the creature’s side, burning clean through its outer flesh—but it didn’t slow down. The wounds sealed almost instantly, the muscle knitting back together with a sickening squelch.

Cale moved. No thought, just instinct. His hoverboard whined as he kicked off, juking left just as a serrated limb ripped through the air where his chest had been. The thing was fast—faster than a murderbot, faster than the manafiends.

It went for Artan next. The swordsman met its charge with a clean, horizontal slash—his blade flaring with silver light. The cut connected, shearing through the creature’s torso—

—only for it to twist its body mid-motion.

It didn’t bleed. Instead, the cut unraveled, flesh slithering like strands of thread, pulling itself back together.

It lashed out. Artan barely had time to lean back before a bone blade sliced across his chest, carving a deep red line. He hit the ground with a choked grunt.

“Shit,” Madia hissed. She spun her staff, slamming it into the creature’s temple like a sledgehammer. A crack rang out, the force of the impact sending it stumbling—

Ragnak was already there.

With a bellowing roar, he came down on the thing like a streaking meteor. He slammed a punch into its side, the sheer force sending a shockwave through the air. The ground shuddered from the impact.

The creature skidded back, slamming into a wall. The whole power room trembled. The creature hunched forward and snarled.

Ugly bastard.

It crouched low, limbs trembling. The mutant was slowing down. The damage wasn’t healing as fast.

We can kill it.

Cale rushed in. He vaulted over a fallen support beam, eyes locked on the monster’s exposed flank. His body burned, lingering fever not entirely gone, but it didn’t matter. He flexed every muscle—then relaxed.

Ghost Step.

His form flickered—his body turning translucent for a fraction of a second—just long enough for the creature’s desperate counterstrike to pass through him.

Then he materialized behind it.

Cale drove his elbow down into the creature’s back with all the force he could muster. Bone cracked. It staggered forward and groaned.

Risto fired again. Artan struck again. Madia struck again.

The thing screeched, its body beginning to fail.

They were winning. But now all of the mutant’s anger was directed at Cale. There was murder in its eyes, as it clearly prepared to charge. Cale was out of breath, hoping he could make it out of this alive.

And that’s when Sintra made her move.

She leapt forward, planting a foot against a fallen slab of stone and using it to propel herself higher. The creature was stumbling, exposed. She raised a long dagger high, aiming for its head.

Cale saw it coming.

“Sintra—!”

Too slow.

The creature’s limbs twisted unnaturally. The second Sintra was airborne, it reversed direction, its body snapping back at an impossible angle.

A spike of bone shot out from its chest.

Straight into her stomach.

Her momentum carried her forward another foot before she froze—her body twitching in the air.

Then the thing flung her off like nothing.

She hit the ground hard, coughing blood. Her hands clutched at the wound, but the damage was already done.

The room fell silent.

A single breath passed.

Then Cale moved.

He called the last dregs of his mana pool. He constricted the energy inside him. This wasn’t ghost step, but he was using the same principles to attack.

He flexed every muscle in his body, his mana contracting into a tight, hot coil. Then—just as he reached the creature—

He let it go.

A surge of power erupted through him as his fist connected with the thing’s skull. The force sent a boom through the room, shaking the walls. The monster jerked violently, its body twisting, convulsing.

Artan followed up. One last strike.

His blade sang through the air.

A clean cut.

The creature’s head separated from its body. It toppled. Twitched. Then went still.

The fight was over.

But Sintra was dying.

Cale stumbled to her side, kneeling, hands moving instinctively to apply pressure. “Sintra, stay awake. Look at me.”

She tried to smile. “Did we… win?”

Cale swallowed hard. “Yeah. We won.”

She exhaled, soft and shaking. “Good…”

Her grip on his wrist weakened.

Then it faded entirely. The light left Sintra’s eyes and she went limp.

Cale’s jaw clenched.

He stayed there, kneeling, until the others came closer. No one spoke or moved.

Sintra was gone.

*

A heavy silence hung in the room. Cale just watched Sintra and held her hand until warmth started fading from it. Not even Madia demanded they immediately get back to work. But all of the other recruits dispersed quickly after noting Sintra dead. Cale felt a chill.

“Just like that…” Cale said and closed Sintra’s eyes.

Just like that Sintra was dead. She had come all this way just to make a single fatal mistake. End of the line. No redo’s. Brutal. Unfair.

And all I can think is that I’m glad it wasn’t me…

None of the other recruits seemed to care. Maybe they were used to death. Maybe they thought she’d been unfit and this was natural. Cale didn’t know or didn’t care.

Did she have parents? Friends?

Cale looked at Fiara, the closest thing he had for a friend here. She caught his eye, gave him a shrug and a flat look and turned away.

Anger was building up. Cale wanted to shout at her, at all of them. Shake them into caring. It could have been any of them.

But it wasn’t.

That was a cold thought. It had been Sintra because she was weak. Because she was wounded. Had Cale been naive to carry her on his back? Had her fate been sealed, and the others had seen it?

No… She should have stayed back. She attacked the mutated manafiend because it was attacking Cale.

A pang of realization struck Cale. It wasn’t guilt, but he felt sad.

She wanted to repay me.

And just like that… Her moment of softness had cost her everything.

It was a hard pill to swallow but the other recruits weren’t completely wrong. Sintra’s actions and weakness had led to the combined effect of her death. Softness, carelessness, weakness.

I have two options. Rage against the way of the world, or learn from this.

Cale felt a bit of warmth die inside him. When he got up and looked around at the other recruits, he did not see potential friends or peers. He saw equations that he had to manage.

Then his eyes passed over Fiara. She looked at him, gave him a wan smile and beckoned him closer.

Cale sighed. There was something in Fiara’s blue eyes. A speck of sympathy. Cale understood.

She had gone through the same emotions he had. And she had come to the same conclusion. The one that he hadn’t understood and Sintra certainly had not. It wasn’t that the pity and sadness wasn’t there for Fiara. It was that she gave it no quarter. There was no room for it.

Cale looked at Sintra. His pity and sadness were fading. Would he hold on to them? Kindle them?

Sintra had been driven by emotion. She had died.

She had played the game poorly and she had lost.

Cale wiped his bloody hands on Sintra’s shirt and sighed as he got up. In that sigh he let go of his feelings. They slid away like water off a roof. They would not help him. He walked over to Fiara, hoping that this world would not take more from him.

He knew it was a fool’s hope.

When Cale got to Fiara, he didn’t look at her.

“It gets easier, you know,” Fiara said quietly.

“Not caring?” Cale asked, harsher than he intended.

“Knowing when to care,” Fiara said.

“And when is that?”

“I’m caring right now, idiot…” she muttered, leaning into him just slightly.

“Oh,” Cale said and looked at her.

She nudged him softly in the ribs. “If you want to talk about it, we’ll do that later. I need you to focus now. You good?”

Cale nodded. “Thanks.”

Fiara gave him a little smile.

“I’m good. What do you need me to do?”

*

The recruits bounced back into action, led by Fiara. She had a blueprint she was working with and instructions from Darius. It was simple really. They needed to unload the copious amounts of mana crystals from the payload into the braziers in each corner of the room. There was a large sigil in the center of the room. Aura told Cale it was the nexus of power. In the sigil there were five outer circles all connecting to a single inner circle.

Aura told Cale immediately that the recruits were supposed to be conduits for the power, channeling it into the middle circle which would transmit all of the power to the top of the spire.

Cale contemplated whether to tell this information, but Fiara managed to figure it out.

After they loaded all of the mana crystals in the braziers, with Fiara reminding them that there were exactly eight thousand, six hundred and forty four crystals, and if there were less when they were finished, there would be heads on plates.

Risto pushed a fist into his pocket and dropped a handful of crystals onto a brazier, muttering a curse.

Soon enough Madia, Risto, Artan, Ragnak, Galvan and Cale were sitting cross-legged in the outer circles of the sigil. Each of the sigils connected to a brazier. Fiara was figuring out how to start the ritual and light the braziers.

“I can’t believe how slow she is. Why did you befriend this dolt?” Aura asked and huffed.

Cale stifled a chuckle and waited in his circle.

“Alright, I got it,” Fiara said and put away her managlass tablet. “We need to pour mana to the circle in the middle.”

Ragnak got up and started working with his mana.

“Not yet, dumbass,” Fiara snapped. “This room will be sealed by a powerful mana array when we start. We have to tell Darius we’re ready. I’ll call him.”

Fiara took out her wrist device and tapped on it. Nothing happened and her brow furrowed.

“Ugh… Why now?” Fiara said.

“What’s wrong?” Madia asked.

“The signal is not going through.”

Artan raised an eyebrow. “Odd. Why?”

“It’s as if it’s being jammed.”

“Jammed?” Ragnak asked loudly. “Enemies?”

“We should be good,” Fiara said, but Cale heard a worry creeping up in her voice. “Maybe the Citadel is somehow interfering with the connection. Cale, I need you to go tell Darius that we’re ready.”

Cale hesitated for half a second. The timing was too clean. Too convenient. If something was off, he was walking straight into it. Either way it was best to keep Darius in the loop.

He exhaled sharply, shook it off, and took out his pink hoverboard from the wrist device. "I’ll be back soon. If the manafiends don’t eat me first."
