Chapter 364

Eik cleared his throat. “Uuh, okay, so… Say that again.”

“Before Toxin created me, I lived a life here on this world… I was born on Toxin.” Profound Toxin had gotten more used to speaking out loud and could get the sentences out much more smoothly now than it could before. It made it considerably less annoying to listen to it speak.

But the answer was stated so matter-of-factly that Eik still had to take a few more moments to process what he had just heard.

“What?” he breathed. “So you were essentially a native of this world? A native of Toxin?”

“Yes.”

“But-But…” Eik trailed off as his eyes scanned along the horizon. He could feel the signatures of every single Profound Toxic beast here. “They’re all made of Profound Toxin. Even if you were a native, I still think that counts as being created by Toxin.”

Profound Toxin followed Eik’s gaze, taking a second to gather its thoughts. “It… wasn’t always like this here. I was just another organism trying to… survive a harsh existence here. We all were. But in the sense that I am an offspring of other organisms native to this world, I suppose it’s not entirely… incorrect to say that even that version of me was created by Toxin. Just like how you were created by Earth.”

“So the Profound Toxic beasts are a new phenomenon here?”

His copy shook its head. “Not new. Very old, But, yes, in the distant past, they were not here.”

“What happened?”

“Everything… changed.”

“No, yeah, I gathered as much, but what changed?”

Profound Toxin laid its head on its shoulder. “I was not yet a part of Toxin in such a way that I had the privilege of… experiencing what happened. But there was an Awakening of some kind. An Awakening that brought with it a formless and diaphanous consciousness,” The avatar said, clearly half absorbed by distant memories. “It brought with it a… will. A will capable of changing and shaping.”

“And that will changed you? Made you into what you are now?”

“No,” it began but paused and changed its mind. “Well, I suppose it did, but it didn’t happen… right away. There was a long time—ages—during which I was but a remnant of a life, buried deep within Toxin. The environment here has always been toxic relative to a place like Earth, but it wasn’t until the Awakening that such a property was written into the fabric of the world itself. It has been a long process.”

“So there are more Profound Toxins, then? More like you, who were changed by this will of Toxin?”

“No. I am the only one. Of that I am certain. I am an extension of the consciousness of Toxin and of its will. There need not be another.”

Eik’s brow creased at that. “What am I then?”

Mouth hanging slightly ajar, Profound Toxin hesitated. “I might have… misspoken. In a way. Yes, you are also an extension of Toxin, albeit not yet connected in same way that I am. But the truth is that we are one… you and I. Even when you learned to take on my power—Toxin’s power—there was never another. It has always been me. And now it has always been you. You are me. And I am you.”

“Why were you chosen then?”

The blue figure shrugged. “That memory faded long ago, if it ever existed in the first place.”

“All right, fair enough. Then why was I chosen?”

Another shrug. “I already told you that. There was never… another.”

“That doesn’t make sense,” Eik insisted, feeling himself become confused and frustrated. “I was just some guy on Earth. There must have been a reason. There must have been something! Are you telling me that there weren’t some kind of sequence of hidden requirements that I fulfilled or something like that? Something to make the System notice me?”

In what might have been the most animated and natural expression of emotion Eik had ever seen come from the Worldbreaker, Profound Toxin sneered loudly. “I don’t give a fuck… what that System thinks. You might have been Awakened by the System, but you were chosen solely by me. By us.”

Eik didn’t know what to say to that. It was… Well, it was insane, to put it mildly.

The wording made it all sound like a prophecy, but Eik knew Profound Toxin well enough to know that it didn’t deal in prophecies. Maybe it had just been told in the best way it could be told, but the story left a lot to be desired and Eik’s brain didn’t like that.

Obviously, if he wasn’t going to be told any more, the best thing to do would be to just put it out of his mind, but he also knew himself well enough to realize that he wouldn’t be able to do that. It would sit in the back of his mind and it would fester.

Dang it…

“So this is a real world, after all?” Eik asked, crossing his legs in the hanging chair as he looked out over the windswept landscape. “It’s not just a space within my spirit or something along those lines?”

“Of course not. Toxin is as real as Earth. No, even more real.”

Well, that settled that then. Gul and Andihar would be curious to know this as well. “So when I died here in the early days and woke up on Earth in a decidedly not dead state, I was just healed up from near death?”

“Is that not obvious?” Profound Toxin questioned.

“Yeah… Sure,” Eik grumbled. “So, what, you were a bird in your previous life?”

“No. There were no birds on Toxin. At least not when I was alive.”

“But when I activate Omnicide, which, by the way, I still think is a wildly misleading name for what it does, I grow feathers.”

“That’s because I had something that developed in a similar fashion to feathers on Earth. And therefore you also have feathers. I was… incapable of flight, however.” Eik’s complaint about the naming of the ability was readily ignored.

“So what were the feathers for?”

“Camouflage, I suppose,” the Worldbreaker said. That made sense. The feathers Eik grew had been a primarily vibrant, shifting greens and yellows interspersed with light browns. It would have probably concealed the wearer pretty effectively in the forest or even on grasslands. “And for attracting a mate. You know… for fornicating.”

“Yes, I get that. Did you look like I do?”

“No. I was not… a humanoid creature. Just because you take on an aspect of my being, doesn’t mean you let go of your own. We share an existence.”

Eik frowned. “But I just don’t understand why this capstone evolution grants me a form similar to what you used to be. For all intents and purposes, whatever you were before, you are now wholly Profound Toxin, isn’t that right? Why am I not connecting even more strongly with the blue toxin?”

“It is simply the natural way of things,” the clone said and scratched its chin in a a way it must have picked up from Eik. “Since your connection to the world soul of Toxin has deepened further with this evolution, you will subconsciously reach deeper into the story and history of the world, and in extension mine. That appearance is nothing but a manifestation of the connection. As far as I understand, that form itself holds no power and is a mere byproduct of the might you are capable of wielding with Omnicide.”

The story of Profound Toxin’s past self filled Eik with curiosity. “Would you mind showing me what you looked like back then? Your true self, so to speak.”

Profound Toxin studied its own blue fingers as it answered. “What you see is my true self, but I understand what you are referring to. I would not mind.”

Steeped in anticipation, Eik watched, waiting for the first signs of a transformation. This was so exciting! After ten seconds, Profound Toxin shifted in its seat and Eik thought it was time, but all his copy did was push off the porch floor lightly with his feet to set the hanging chair into a rocking motion.

Another ten second after that, Eik was beginning to get peeved. “Well?” he asked.

“What?”

“I asked if you would mind showing me what you used to look like.”

“And I told you I wouldn’t mind.”

“So do it!” Eik barked.

“All right, you could have just asked…”

“I di—! No, actually, you know what? I know what you’re doing here, you little shit! And, guess what, now I don’t even want to see it anymore!”

Profound Toxin stood up and walked into the garden. “I’m going to do it now.”

Eik snorted, leaning back into the hanging chair as he crossed his arms over his chest. “Fine, whatever. See if I care.”

“All right… Get ready… Here it comes,”

“Screw you, dog breath.”

Smoothly, the gooey Eik clone began to shift and undulate as he grew. And grew. Almost immediately, it lost its human shape and flowed out into a formless mass that lengthened gradually until it reached a length of a little over fifteen meters.

“Damn, dude, you were enormous! No wonder I grow as well if that size is what my body is drawing inspiration from.”

In seconds, six legs rippling with muscles sprouted along the trunk of the body as a angular head formed on a thick neck. Several rows of deadly fangs lined not one but two powerful maws, stacked on top of each other essentially. The lower jaw of the upper mouth acted as the upper jaw of the lower mouth, forming a grotesque, two-story death trap.

A pair of long, oval eyes opened slowly, while massive antlers shot up from Profound Toxin’s skull like trees growing in extreme fast motion. The beast let out an ear-splitting howl as a powerful, whip-like tail lashed through the air with a crack.

Finally, multicolored feather surged out in thick clusters, beginning just behind its eyes and all the way to the tip of its tail. In much the same way Goo could, the creature shifted from the luminescent blue into natural colors that must have been identical to what Profound Toxin’s original species looked like.

“That is terrifying,” Eik concluded.

“Thank you,” Profound Toxin said sincerely, nodding its gratitude.

“Don’t mention it. So, does that form drain you as much as it drains me?”

“Omnicide is very powerful, and you will have to grow stronger if you want to keep it active in perpetuity. In the beginning, you could barely manage Backflow for a few seconds and now you could keep it on until you die of old age. Same thing here. Even at X-rank, your reservoir of spiritual energy isn’t endless.”

Eik sighed. “Great…”

Four days later, Eik had been back to the remote world three more times to test Omnicide further.

Just like Profound Toxin had said, it was frighteningly powerful but the energy required was comparable, making it more of a final stretch kind of thing than a start to finish staple of any combat. At least until he got more powerful. The additional destructive capabilities of Omnicidal Profound Toxin was well deserving of its name, now that he had had more time to work with it.

It could kill anything in moments and would surely be a boon in the fight against the Lord of the Moon.

The two rods of metal unearthed on the world previously flooded with miasma had been handed over to a skilled team of geologists, ritualists, and other hopefully useful experts. Apparently, it wasn't nearly as easy to understand as the effigies, so they hadn’t been able to figure out anything yet, but they were being careful.

The grade seven alchemical recipe, however, had seen some development—if it could be described as development.

“You’re kidding,” Eik groaned, leaning over the desk in the laboratory.

The expression on Ganyak hir Gawak’s face was one of pure shame and embarrassment. “Nothing worked…”

“Oh, man… This is not good.”

“Ask him about that thing we talked about, grandpa,” Meri piped up, determination still shining in her eyes.

The guild master chewed on it for a moment, grimacing. “We’ve had an… idea,” he began.

“I like ideas,” Eik said, standing up straight. “Go on.”

“The parts from the X-ranked spawn didn’t work, but there is one more thing we might be able to use.”

“What?”

Ganyak hir Gawak swallowed hard. “You.”
