I ACCIDENTALLY BECAME A GUILD THERAPIST

Chapter 19 – The Wedding of the Century

ARC II: THE WORLD OUTSIDE THE CAMP

SYNOPSIS: Today is the day. Twelve pages of vows. One dragon in the guest section. Forty-three guild members who were told business casual and interpreted this very liberally. Livia has a script, a signed certificate, and absolutely no idea what she is doing. Glimmer is ready. Phina has been ready for eighteen months.

The morning of the wedding, Livia woke up to a notification.

[System Note: Today is the day of the Phina/Glimmer union. You are the officiating party. You have twelve pages of vows to stand through. The certificate is signed. Elder Tobren has confirmed attendance and has brought his own chair as stated. The dragon confirmed last night. The system wishes to note that when you agreed to this eight weeks ago it was not entirely clear it would reach this point. The system is glad it has. The system will be watching. The system is also, privately, a little excited.]

The system is excited.

The system never says it’s excited.

Today is going to be something.

She got dressed. Her robe was clean, the blue diagonal threading catching the morning light in a way that had grown familiar over the past weeks. She picked up her notepad, the official certificate of union (stamped, sealed, Elder Tobren’s signature taking up an impressive amount of space), and the officiating script she had written and revised four times.

The script was one page.

Phina’s vows were twelve..

This is going to be a long ceremony.

I said that to Phina.

Phina said: "A union of this magnitude deserves duration."

I cannot argue with that.

The guild garden had been transformed.

Not by any single grand gesture but by the accumulated contributions of forty-three people who had been given the instruction "business casual" and had interpreted it according to their individual understanding of what that phrase meant in a world where some people’s armor glowed and Bron owned exactly one non-combat outfit that was technically a blacksmith’s apron with decorative trim.

The result was, as Livia stood at the garden entrance and surveyed it, genuinely beautiful in the way that things are beautiful when nobody tried too hard and everyone tried a little.

Chairs in even rows, slightly uneven because Bron had helped set them up. Flowers from the guild’s own garden, arranged by Phina the previous evening with the focused attention she usually reserved for arcane research. Glimmer’s regular path through the garden had been marked with small glowing stones, so it had somewhere to process in if it needed a moment.

That last detail had been Nyx’s contribution.

He didn’t mention it to anyone.

Livia noticed.

Filed.

The dragon was in the back row.

It had, to its credit, done its best to look appropriate. It was sitting with its wings folded and its head lowered to roughly the level of the surrounding seats, which still meant it was blocking the view of six chairs behind it, the occupants of which had relocated to the sides without complaint. Someone had placed a small flower arrangement on its snout, which it was wearing with the dignified acceptance of an entity that understood it was a guest at someone’s wedding and had elected to be on best behavior.

Elder Tobren was in the front row. His own chair, as promised. He had a small recording crystal in his lap and the composed expression of someone attending their fifteenth unusual union and finding this one appropriately satisfying.

Alaric had set up a discrete betting station behind the rose bushes. Livia had pretended not to see it.

[Current attendance: 43 guild members, 1 dragon, 1 Elder (with chair), 1 slime (bride), 12 guests from other guilds, 4 NPCs who had heard about it and arrived without being invited but were welcomed because the seating chart had collapsed anyway. Total: 62. The system notes the garden was rated for 40. The system is not going to say anything about this.]

Phina was not nervous.

Livia had checked, because it was her job to check, and what she found was not nervousness but a quality of settled certainty that was rarer than nervousness and considerably more moving. Phina had been thinking about this for eighteen months. She had assembled the evidence, done the work, made the case to herself and to Elder Tobren and to every bureaucratic obstacle between love and legal recognition. She was not arriving uncertain. She was arriving prepared.

She was also wearing twelve pages of vows in a slim case tucked under her arm, which she had decorated with small arcane runes that pulsed gently, which Livia had been informed were "a visual representation of the emotional resonance of each stanza."

“You look wonderful,” Livia said..

“I know,” Phina said, with the calm of someone who had spent considerable time on her appearance and felt this was simply an accurate assessment..

“Are you ready?”

Phina looked out at the garden. At the chairs. At the dragon in the back row wearing its flower arrangement. At Glimmer, who was already at the altar end, pulsing a steady, warm gold that it had, as far as anyone could tell, produced entirely on its own without being asked to.

“Yes,” Phina said..

She is.

Eighteen months.

She has been ready for eighteen months.

Livia had officiated nothing before this.

She had been a patient’s companion. A note-taker. A door-holder for feelings that needed somewhere to go. She had guided people through confessions and crises and the particular kind of session that was just sitting with someone in their own silence until it became something they could carry.

But she had never stood at the front of a room full of people and said: we are gathered here, because this is real and it deserves to be witnessed.

She stood at the altar end of the garden. The certificate in her hand. The script in her other hand. Sixty-two people and one dragon looking at her.

She took one breath.

“We’re here,” she said, “because Phina asked a slime if it wanted to stay. And it stayed. And she came back, and it stayed again. And she built a case for it, seventeen months of data and color-coded charts, because she is who she is and love, for Seraphina Duskveil, means being certain before you ask.”

Glimmer pulsed warmly..

“We’re here because what Glimmer offers is something that does not require explanation: it is simply, consistently present. And that turned out to be exactly what Phina needed. Not because presence is a small thing. Because it’s the largest thing.”

In the third row, Bron put his arm around the plushie.

Nyx was in his designated shadow corridor. She could see the faint pink of his flashlight..

“This ceremony is unusual,” Livia continued. “The paperwork alone took eight weeks and required the cooperation of an NPC who has been on this server since launch and has opinions about loot balance.”

Elder Tobren inclined his head slightly..

“The world this union exists in has no established category for it. The registry call it an unusual union. We can call it whatever we want.” She looked at Phina, then at Glimmer. “I call it a promise. Between two beings who chose each other when they could have chosen otherwise.”

Phina’s eyes were bright..

“Phina,” Livia said. “Your vows.”

The vows took thirty-seven minutes.

Livia had prepared for forty. The twelve pages turned out to run slightly shorter than anticipated because Phina, midway through the seventh page, set the vow case down and spoke the remaining five pages from memory.

This was, in retrospect, not surprising. She had been composing them for eighteen months. Of course she had memorized them..

The vows covered: the first observation (day four, initial jiggle data), the incident of the arcane experiment (day thirty-one, when Glimmer had absorbed a misfired spell and refracted it as light rather than releasing it as energy, which Phina had noted was "anomalous and suggesting internal processing capacity beyond established slime parameters", the seventeen months of chart data and what the chart data meant, and the moment at which Phina had understood that she was not studying something but loving it.

She described that moment plainly, without the poetic register she usually deployed for everything:

“I was sitting in the guild hall at three in the morning unable to sleep, which I do sometimes, and Glimmer came and sat beside me. Not because I called it. Not because it needed anything. Just because I was there and it wanted to be there too. I had spent seventeen months documenting what Glimmer was. That night I understood what Glimmer is. It is the thing that stays.”

Glimmer glowed very brightly..

Livia did not cry.

I did not cry.

I was very professional.

My eyes were wet because of the ambient mana in the garden.

That is my official account and I am filing it.

Behind her, she heard the dragon make a sound that was almost certainly a sniff..

When Phina finished, Livia looked at Glimmer..

The script did not have a section for Glimmer’s vow, because Glimmer could not speak in the conventional sense and Livia had not known how to write around that. She had left a note to herself in the margin of the script: figure this out.

She had not, precisely, figured it out. But standing here now, she thought she understood what the answer was..

“Glimmer,” she said, “you’ve been asked a question. You don’t have to answer in words.”

The garden was very quiet..

Glimmer’s glow, which had been warm and steady throughout, shifted. It changed color slowly—from the gold it had been producing all morning to something deeper, more complex, the same amber of Phina’s mana-light, and then something that was entirely its own: a color that Livia did not have a name for, warm and particular, the specific shade of something that had made up its mind a long time ago and had simply been waiting to be asked.

The glow settled..

Stayed..

I think that counts.

I think that absolutely counts.

“By the authority vested in me by Elder Tobren, the Guild Registry of Aethelgard, Form 7-C, and approximately eight weeks of paperwork,” Livia said, and her voice was entirely steady, “I declare this union recognized.”

She pressed the officiating seal to the certificate.

The garden erupted..

Forty-three guild members, a dragon, an elder in his own chair, twelve guests, and four gate-crashing NPCs produced a volume of celebration that was, by any reasonable metric, excessive for a garden rated for forty people. The dragon contributed a controlled plume of celebratory fire, which was impressive and which nobody had explicitly authorized. The NPCs, having arrived without invitation, felt no particular restraint about throwing the confetti they had also brought without invitation. Alaric’s betting station emerged from behind the rose bushes.

Bron lifted Livia off the ground in an embrace that briefly removed her ability to breathe, which she accepted as the cost of the occasion..

Phina and Glimmer were at the center of all of it, and both were glowing..

Someone had set up a small holographic broadcast.

Livia did not know who had done this. She was going to find out later that it was Morty..

The ceremony was live on the server’s main community feed.

43,000 players watching live..

Livia did not know this yet either..

Elder Tobren found Livia at the edge of the garden during the reception, while the main chaos was happening twenty meters away and Glimmer was being introduced to the dragon, who appeared to find Glimmer fascinating.

He sat in his chair. She sat on the garden wall beside him..

“Fifteen,” he said..

“Sorry?”

“This is my fifteenth unusual union. I have been approving them since the server launched.” He watched Phina laughing at something Bron had said, Glimmer pulsing contentedly on her shoulder. “This is the first one where I was certain before the ceremony started.”

“How do you usually feel?”

“Cautiously optimistic. Love is—” he considered the word, —“not always what it presents as. I have approved unions that ended badly. I have blocked unions that would have been good. The certainty is unusual.”

“What made you certain?”

He looked at her with the particular attention of someone who was about to say something he had been considering for a while.

“Glimmer,” he said, “has been sitting beside Phina every night for three weeks. In the guild garden, after everyone else has gone in. I observed this on my walks. Glimmer did not need to be there. Phina did not call for it. It simply—” he paused, —“preferred to be present with her. That kind of preference does not usually expire.”

Livia looked at Phina and Glimmer across the garden..

It simply preferred to be present with her.

Eighteen months of chart data and it comes down to that.

It preferred to be present.

“You observed this on your walks,” she said..

“I walk at night,” Elder Tobren said, without inflection. “I have been on this server for eleven years. I observe things.”

A pause..

“I observed,” he said, “the boot prints in my garden path three days before you arrived. Different tread from any player I have on record. I thought you should know that I noticed. That I have been noticing for some time.”

Livia looked at him.

“You knew about the boot prints before Aldous told me?”

“Aldous is thorough,” Elder Tobren said. “But he was not the first to notice. I was.” He stood, picking up his chair. “I wanted you to know that someone on this server has been watching for you for a long time. I do not know who. But the watching has been—” he searched for the right word, —“careful. Attentive. The way you watch something you want to protect, not something you want to catch.”

He nodded once, with the gravity of someone who had been alive long enough to distinguish between kinds of attention.

“Enjoy the reception,” he said. And walked back to the main celebration, carrying his own chair..

Livia sat on the garden wall for a moment.

Watching something you want to protect.

Not something you want to catch.

T.S. has been watching since before I arrived.

Elder Tobren noticed.

The boot prints were in his garden too.

She opened her notepad. Added to the T.S. page:

Elder Tobren noticed the boot prints before Aldous. He describes the watching as careful. Attentive. Protective, not predatory. He wanted me to know this.

She closed the notepad.

I will sort this.

Not today.

Today is Phina and Glimmer’s day.

Miles found her on the garden wall at some point during the reception, when the noise had reached its peak and the dragon had begun a low harmonizing rumble that was either celebratory or digestive and nobody was asking.

He sat beside her. He did not say anything for a while. Across the garden, Phina was attempting to teach Glimmer to dance, which Glimmer was interpreting as an invitation to expand its form in all directions simultaneously. Bron was watching this with the expression of someone witnessing something that made the world feel larger..

“Twelve pages,” Miles said..

“She memorized the last five,” Livia said..

He made a sound that was the cousin of a laugh..

They sat for a while in the noise of the celebration.

Then Miles said, without particular preamble: “I found the video.”

At Phina and Glimmer and Bron and Nyx at the edge of the shadow corridor and Alaric running the betting station and the dragon and Elder Tobren with his chair and sixty-two people who had come because someone had asked a slime if it wanted to stay. “It was—” he stopped..

The reception wound down in the way that things wind down when they have been genuinely good: not abruptly, but slowly, people drifting and returning and drifting again, the energy becoming something quieter without becoming less.

Phina and Glimmer were the last to leave the garden. Glimmer had returned to its regular size but was still glowing, softly and consistently, the particular color from the ceremony that Livia had no name for. Phina walked with the quality of someone who had set something down and discovered the ground was still there.

She stopped beside Livia on her way out..

“Thank you,” she said. “For the vows. For standing there. For all of it.”

“Thank you for trusting me with it,” Livia said..

Phina looked at her for a moment with the assessment of someone who had been observing carefully..

“You’ve been different lately,” she said. “Since the echo dungeon. Since the Aldous messages. Since—” she paused, —“whatever is happening that you’re carrying.”

Livia looked at her..

“I’m okay,” she said..

“I know you are,” Phina said. “I’m not asking if you’re okay. I’m saying I’ve noticed. And if it becomes something you need to put somewhere—” she looked at Glimmer, who pulsed once, in agreement, —“we’re here.”

Phina just offered to listen.

Phina, who I have been treating for slime-related romantic complexity for months.

She offered to listen.

Livia opened her mouth and closed it again..

“Thank you,” she said, and meant it entirely..

Phina nodded, with dignity. Glimmer jiggled, with warmth. They walked inside..

Livia stayed in the garden a moment longer..

The chairs were still there, slightly uneven. The dragon’s flower arrangement had fallen off but was still on the ground nearby. Elder Tobren’s chair had been neatly stacked against the wall. Nyx’s small glowing stones still marked Glimmer’s path..

She wrote in her notepad:

Today: Phina and Glimmer married. Thirty-seven minutes of vows. Glimmer’s answer was a color I don’t have a name for. Elder Tobren told me someone has been watching for me carefully. Protective, not predatory. Miles said the meeting was good. Phina offered to listen.

It was a good day.

I think I’m allowed to just say that.
