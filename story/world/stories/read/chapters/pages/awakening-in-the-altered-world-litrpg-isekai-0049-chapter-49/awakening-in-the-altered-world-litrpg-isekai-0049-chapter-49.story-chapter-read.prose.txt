What’d resulted was an almost nine foot tall arc of pure, incredibly potent Swordforce.

And after seeing it, and feeling precisely how much this blade helped him cast, Alex felt like he was beginning to get how the Frigid Knight could execute that kind of monstrous ice arc.

Alex was staring at his sword in wonder, tilting it, watching the light glaze off of it.

It works like a hybrid sword and staff, kind of?

Alex turned it around.

I think I’m supposed to be able to cast everything through this thing, if I want.

He channeled Pure through it, and willed a Mana Spear into existence, and inspected the Spear.

He wasn’t sure the Spear itself was any better, but it did require less willpower.

He raised his sword, and with concentration, created.

Glancing up, he saw five forbidding-looking, Swordforce-tipped Mana Spears hanging in the air above.

He marveled at them. “Okay,” he breathed. “I…I can certainly work with this.”

Wait. Alex paused. I wonder.

He went over to the steel pillar next to the station, pulled up the v-station menu, and selected an option.

Two items materialized in the center of the stage. The first was a T1 Whetslab.

It was at this T1 Whetslab that Alex directed his Mana Spears.

CRSH-CRSH!

And he saw the percentages leap.

12.4% -> 37.5% -> 49.6%.

Nice. He smiled a bit, turning to the second item—a faceless figure that’d materialized at the center of the stage, with graph paper-like lines running across its body.

Alex began collecting mana into his sword, and after almost ten seconds—‘Start.’

It went right at him, and Alex slashed.

A brilliant, nine-foot-tall arc of Swordforce sliced through the air, leaving a pure, bright, ringing sound in its wake.

And Alex saw the figure reeling, limbs sprawling back, as the figure on its chest went—

0% -> 71.9%.

Jesus Christ.

Actually seeing it represented was something else.

It went at him again, and Alex slashed three times, collecting mana and releasing it as his arm swung; the slashes went through it in the space of less than two seconds.

71.9% -> 83.4% -> 97.3% -> 100%.

I guess it really is a Tier 1 starter bot, he thought, blinking. It’s that way for a reason.

In less than 5 seconds, he’d crushed what’d taken him 7-and-a-half minutes a few days ago.

That was 4 Class levels ago, to be fair. And a lot of Skill upgrades ago. At some level it’s not surprising, it’s just—

Clearly I’ve severely underestimated how much having good gear does.

Kennedy and Adam Devereaux, whose names he’d belatedly learned, had felt like superhumans. Some of that was likely training, some of that was passives like Adam said, and some of it was probably Alex’s perception playing tricks on him as a newbie.

But with clarity, their gear was surely at least half of the reason. Which felt obvious in retrospect; Alex suspected it’d be the case with any truly elite Guild talent.

Which also might have implications for the kinds of fighters I’ll be facing this weekend, he was realizing.

Also, this sword was better than Beckett’s gear. Beckett’s spear wasn’t mana conductive. Alex recalled Beckett’s gear was the placeholder starter gear he’d been given, before his Guild suited him up in custom, specialized gear.

Alex’s assumption was, most of the people showing up for this ‘Rising Stars Cup’ were typical local talents, and he didn’t expect they’d have anything crazy. Maybe around this gear level, or less.

But it was entirely possible higher-level Guilders had gear items like his sword, or potentially even better; he was feeling strong right now, but he also realized these advantages weren’t only his. It just likely felt especially significant right now because he was finally being awakened to it.

Well—I might’ve been aware, like intellectually, that of course gear matters, but I didn’t really feel it, I guess—

A pause.

“Am I going to have this same revelation, all over again, about Skill Shards?” he wondered aloud. “Once I get them, that is?”

He could easily see himself getting the Mana Spear Skill Shard, and realizing he’d underestimated the extent to which having a sophisticated, precise, structured Skill made his attempts look utterly amateurish.

Also a little early to be thinking about Skill Shards; I’d actually be able to add supports to a Mana Spear Skill Shard, make deeper modifications instead of just throwing other mana types on it, but—very much for the future.

He smiled a bit to himself, glanced over the mess of treasures still remaining. A quarter of an hour had already passed. I should get to brass tacks, he decided.

As much fun as he was having playing with this glass cannon build, balancing things was probably wise—he might be able to get away with it in 1v1 duels this weekend against talents, but it’d also have to get him through high level rifts. And he couldn’t see these slippers sufficing.

To begin with, Alex decided on his ring.

Bloodruby Ring [Uncommon]

A ring featuring a ruby taken from a rift rich in blood mana. This ring has been enhanced by a skilled artificer using rift creature cores derived from this very rift. Has a minor beneficial effect on the vital energies of its user.

Requirements: Level 3.

+2 Str, +2 Con.

The ring felt quite potent to his soul senses, and he liked it as a balancing choice, reinforcing his base physique.

The Manasilk Gossamer Robes gave such a giant stat boost and were so pleasant-feeling, he was reluctant to part with them. But after fifteen indecisive minutes he decided on a second option which he felt did very similar things, but was a little more useful as protection than what was essentially a glorified nightgown.

Twin Gem Plate [Uncommon]

A plate of high quality tempered steel, embedded with an Intelligence-boosting Emerald and a Wisdom-boosting Sapphire. Its steel is mildly mana conductive, highly resistant to foreign mana penetration, and highly resistant to slashing and piercing.

Requirements: Level 5.

+3 Wis, +3 Int

It had a reassuring weight to it as he walked around. He immediately felt much less exposed—it felt nice to be able to take a hit if you were forced to.

His pants, he kept.

Observatorium Acolyte’s Slacks [Common]

Slacks for freshan Mages at the Observatorium, designed for light daily use. Appropriate for affairs magical and non-magical.

+4 Int, +2 Wis.

Enchantments: Self-repair.

The Observatorium was a specialized Guild in LA, he was pretty sure. Or it might’ve been a Professions association? Whichever the case, clearly felt like it wasn’t meant to be taken into rifts. But since it had Self-repair—it’ll be fine.

He also kept his pendant, the Common-rarity Teardrop Azure Pendant with its +4 Wis.

His final armor decision was his footwear.

Steel Sabatons [Common]

Steel sabatons highly resistant to all-type physical attacks. Highly resistant to mana penetration.

Requirements: Level 3.

And that was it. This he’d chosen mostly because he wanted footwear that could survive pretty extreme forces, especially as he was Force Augmenting, and also he’d run the numbers and realized he had about a Credit left to work with.

So, plain, durable steel it was; it looked like something a knight might wear, steel greaves connected to steel boots, but it was quite lightweight, and it was designed with a series of interlocking plates such that he felt surprisingly mobile in it. If he didn’t have Pure cycling he would’ve gotten frostbite last rift; this solved that issue nicely.

When he summoned a mirror and saw an image of himself, he saw a pretty blinged-out guy in essentially half plate. The bracers, chestpiece and sabatons gave him a sense of relative sturdiness; I look like an actual fighter now.

A few of the items weren’t the most spectacular, he’d admit, but they were also relatively starter gear, and in his mind they fit his vision of his fighting style pretty well. High Wis, high Int, but he also wasn’t reliant on kiting or avoidance, nor on damage-soaking. A balanced mana swordsman. It was the style he felt like he was naturally gravitating toward across his Duels, and especially in the rift.

With what was left of his time, Alex tried to decide on a sword.

There were twin swords. There was a sword that he could channel Pure mana into which would create a bigger image of itself, increasing its reach, and the onyx glassy sword, which featured a Sharpness enchant. There was a sword enchanted to have a minor mana siphoning effect whenever it sliced an enemy.

But Alex couldn’t tear himself from the sheer conductivity of his first choice.

Fluxsteel Longsword [Uncommon]

A sword created from Fluxsteel, an extremely mana-conductive material for F-rank. It also features a very sharp edge of refined Fluxsteel that takes especially well to mana, allowing for mana concentrations along its edge higher than most steels would naturally allow.

Unenchanted, unlike the other swords, but that also meant he could always enchant it in the future with whatever he wanted.

As he waited on an Uber, he checked his phone, and saw a few notifications; Beckett followed him. As did… Clarissa St. Claire, for some reason.

It was kind of amusing the only three people following him, this brand-new, 3-follower account, were all verified, with decently big followings—with two being bona fide regional celebrities.
