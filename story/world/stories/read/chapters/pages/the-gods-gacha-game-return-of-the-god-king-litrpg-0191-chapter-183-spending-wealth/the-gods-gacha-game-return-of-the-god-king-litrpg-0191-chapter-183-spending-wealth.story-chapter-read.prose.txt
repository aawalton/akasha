Since most of our equipment had been left behind at Tuilë’s workshop, we could forget about challenging a new scenario or even entering a dungeon. Not only were we now down a member, but there were also only about two days left before the Battle of Divine Will. Under these circumstances, the best use of our remaining time was to train and prepare as thoroughly as possible.

I shared this with the others, and they all agreed. It seemed they planned to go around purchasing supplies before heading to the training center. Knowing that I hadn’t undergone my class advancement yet, they decided to use District D’s training center. As for me, there were still a few things I needed to take care of before going to train.

First things first, I needed to pay a visit to a certain fox merchant to retrieve the skill book I’d been asking her to get for me.

Without wasting any time, I headed for the skill bookstore alone. It didn’t take long before I arrived. Pushing the door open, the familiar chime rang out—and sure enough, the fox merchant was already there, as if she had been waiting for me.

Mira looked up from behind the counter with amused eyes. “My, my. You’re punctual. I was just wondering when you’d show up.”

“I don’t like leaving things hanging,” I replied as I stepped inside.

“Heehee. That does sound like you.” Her tail swayed once before she reached beneath the counter. “Good thing, too. I’ve already prepared what you asked for.”

She placed a sealed skill book on the counter, its cover etched with shifting runes that seemed to blur the longer I looked at them. Even without opening it, I could feel a subtle distortion emanating from the book itself, as if it didn’t quite want to be perceived clearly.

“This one wasn’t easy to acquire,” Mira said dramatically, pressing the back of her hand to her forehead. “I went through great lengths to obtain it. If not for the materials from that powerful Daleth-rank monster you brought me, I wouldn’t have been able to secure it. Go on, take a look.”

I picked it up, and the holographic screen containing the book’s description immediately appeared before my eyes.

Skill Book: [Veil of False Providence]

Rank: EpicType: Active

A concealment skill that distorts fate recognition and status perception directed at the user. Actively masks the true details of the user’s status screen, replacing sensitive information with false or incomplete data. Ineffective against direct physical inspection but highly resistant to divine-level observation.

This was exactly what I needed.

Grant Plausibility alone wouldn’t be enough in the upcoming Battle of Divine Will—not when gods themselves would be watching. This skill didn’t just hide information; it lied convincingly, feeding observers what they expected to see rather than the truth.

Mira watched my reaction closely, a knowing smile tugging at her lips. “Good choice, isn’t it? This concealment skill can even slip past divine authorities. Of course, it isn’t completely foolproof, but it should deceive most divine beings—unless they’re willing to expend considerable effort.”

“Thanks. This is going to help me a lot,” I said, giving her a small nod.

“Of course it will.” She leaned forward slightly, deepening her smile. “And since a promise was fulfilled on both sides, I’d say this transaction has been… very pleasant indeed.”

“It sure is.”

As I said that, I tapped the skill book and activated it.

You have acquired a skill: [Veil of False Providence].

“Oh-ho! By the way,” Mira added suddenly, tail swishing with unmistakable pride, “I’ll be moving up to District C starting tomorrow.”

“Oh? Did you manage to secure a promotion?” I asked.

“Mmm-hmm!” She puffed out her chest slightly. “Thanks in no small part to you, my dear customer. Honestly, I could’ve moved up yesterday if I hadn’t been waiting for someone to come claim his precious skill book.”

“I see. Congratulations,” I said sincerely. “You’ve earned your reputation, Mira.”

She laughed softly, clearly pleased. “Heehee. Of course I have. And I fully intend to keep earning it.” She leaned in a little, lowering her voice conspiratorially. “Be sure to look me up once you reach Daleth rank. I’ll give you a very special price… though I probably won’t be running a skill bookstore there.”

“Really?” I raised an eyebrow. “Since you’ll be in District C, does that mean you might be able to get your hands on legendary-rank skills?”

“L-Legendary-rank skills?!” Mira recoiled dramatically, clutching her chest. “My, my, my! Dear customer, are you already indulging in such extravagant daydreams?”

“You don’t have to worry about the payment. I have the capital to afford it.”

At the mention of that, her eyes flashed with naked greed—only for her to sigh and shake her head a moment later. “Still, those aren’t things you can simply buy, you know? They’re treasures that rarely appear, even in auctions held in the higher districts…”

Just as I thought, legendary-rank skills existed in a league of their own. It couldn’t be helped. The number of Daleth-rank divine warriors in Fantasia was simply too small. Most likely, no one had ever obtained a legendary-rank skill book so far.

Mira then smiled slyly, flicking her tail. “That said… who knows what the future might hold? With a tiny bit of luck, it may become possible. If that day comes, I’ll be sure to inform you immediately, my dear customer.”

I gave her a final nod before turning to leave. With this secured, my preparations for the Battle of Divine Will had taken a decisive step forward.

Since acquiring a legendary-rank skill with soul coins was effectively impossible, I had no immediate way to spend my absurd amount of wealth. However, I already had a backup plan.

Rather than letting the soul coins sit idle, I intended to invest them in relocating the Genesis Order. District F had served its purpose well as a starting point, but its location and limitations were becoming increasingly apparent. Of course, that didn’t mean abandoning it entirely. The current base could continue functioning as a secondary headquarters. But for what was coming, we needed something better.

With that decision made, I headed straight for the Genesis Order after putting on the Mask of a Thousand Races and changing my appearance back into Lord Genesis. The moment I arrived, the followers knelt in unison, and Rex greeted me with reverence. Since time was tight, I had no intention of holding a sermon today. Instead, I gestured for him to follow me into a quieter side room.

Once the door closed, Rex dropped to one knee and asked, “Is there something I may assist you with, my Lord?”

“Rex,” I said evenly, “the Genesis Order will be moving.”

His eyes widened. “Moving… the headquarters?”

“Yes. District F will remain operational, but it will no longer serve as our main base. The Genesis Order has outgrown this place.”

Rex hesitated for only a moment before nodding. “I understand. Where will we relocate to?”

“Near the main plaza,” I said. “There are large buildings there, and the location is far more strategic. I’ll inform you of the exact building later. For now, I want you to make preparations and notify the followers.”

Rex’s expression shifted from surprise to quiet excitement. “A location like that… won’t it require a great deal of soul coins? The Order’s current reserves amount to around twenty thousand. Will that be enough?”

“Don’t concern yourself with the cost,” I replied calmly. “I’ll handle the expenses. You handle the logistics. Start by selecting trusted members to oversee the transition. I want this done quickly but cleanly.”

Rex straightened, resolve hardening in his eyes. “As you command, my Lord.”

After that, I left the headquarters and headed straight for the main plaza to look for a suitable building. With the amount of soul coins I had on hand, buying several buildings outright wouldn’t have been an issue, let alone renting one. However, since I was looking for a headquarters rather than a storefront, it was better to choose something more secure and slightly secluded—close enough to the plaza to remain influential, but not so exposed that anyone could wander in freely.

It didn’t take long to find a promising candidate. Tucked just off one of the wider plaza streets stood a large stone building with a reinforced structure and limited entry points. It had clearly been designed with defense in mind—thick walls, narrow windows, and a spacious interior that could be repurposed with minimal effort. More importantly, the surrounding buildings provided just enough cover to keep it from drawing constant attention.

Once I made my decision, the next step was straightforward. To purchase property within Fantasia, one had to go through the City Registry Hall—a neutral administrative institution responsible for land ownership, transfers, and long-term leases. Each district maintained one such hall, with the main headquarters located at the main plaza.

As I entered, I found the place bustling with activity. Clerks moved briskly between counters while buyers and sellers alike filled the halls. It was strange to see this kind of scene, considering that this was a divine city rather than a mortal city. After submitting the necessary documents, I was ushered into a small negotiation room. The clerk in charge initially quoted a price that was, frankly, excessive. Fortunately, I possessed the Negotiation skill—and at a fairly high level—so the discussion proceeded smoothly.

You have used Negotiation.

After a brief but firm negotiation, the final agreement was reached. The building was officially transferred into my ownership after I paid a grand total of 65,000 soul coins. The deed was finalized on the spot, complete with magical authentication to prevent any disputes or tampering.

Soul Coins: 144,275 → 79,275

The ownership of Plaza Compound C has been successfully transferred to you.

Negotiation has leveled up.

With that settled, all that remained was to inform Rex and begin preparations for the move.

After informing Rex about the building, I made my way to the training center. With the Battle of Divine Will only two days away—and the commander of Istellia’s divine army now decided—the only thing left for me to do was train. Train, and then train some more. There was no room for complacency.

As usual, I first consumed a bottle of the Elixir of Mana Concentrate and began with the basics—doing some warmups before moving on to strength and stamina training in the gym area, pushing my body until every muscle screamed in protest. After that came weapon practice, honing my proficiency with sword, spear, and dagger. Even if my base stats were already comparable to those of newly promoted Daleth-rank divine warriors, that advantage would mean nothing without the skills to support it.

Not stopping there, I moved on to sparring sessions with Boris and the others inside the dueling cubes, testing my limits in live combat. Afterward, I focused on refining my existing skills. Even a fraction of a second could decide life or death in the Battle of Divine Will, so I repeated the same sequences again and again until my movements became instinctive.

By the time I stopped, my clothes were soaked through with sweat, and my breathing came in ragged gasps, as if I had just been dragged fifty meters underwater.

Still not enough.

After a brief respite, I turned my attention to magic. I circulated my mana and refined it under sustained pressure, forcing it to remain stable no matter the strain. After repeating the process hundreds of times, I moved on to training each of my spells in the magic training area.

If the other gods believed Istellia’s side would be easy prey, they were in for a rude awakening. When the Battle of Divine Will began, I wouldn’t just survive, but I would make sure that our side would win with flying colors.
