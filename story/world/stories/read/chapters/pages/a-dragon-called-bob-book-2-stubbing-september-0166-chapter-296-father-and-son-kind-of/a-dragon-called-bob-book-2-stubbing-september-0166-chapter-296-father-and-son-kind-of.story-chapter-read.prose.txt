“No.  We need to talk.”

“I can do both, Bob,” Esme muttered, treating me to a wan smile.  “Help me up.”

She was naked, having resurrected without a thread of clothing on her.

Noticing my gaze, she winked at me when I looked up.  “He was kind, Bob.  Nobody hurt me… or anything else.  Your face isn’t right, love.”

“Charming,” I chuckled.  I produced a hand mirror from storage and struggled to re-humanise my features.  My cheeks and jaw slid back, reducing the impression of a snout, and I smoothed the faintly scale-like ridges that had run across my forehead.  “Now I don’t look like a Klingon.”

“A what?”

“Never mind.”  I had control again.  My mental menagerie had stayed merged, and now I had Esme back; a sense of calm flooded through me.  “I need to get you microchipped.  Make you easier to find next time you run off with some random emperor.”

“I will assume from context I should slap you for that,” she said, gently patting my cheek as I pulled her carefully to her feet.  “Thanks.  Now, clothes please?”

“How about I just take mine off so we’re even?”

That earned me a less gentle pat on the cheek.  “Bob,” she said in a warning tone.

I pulled out a couple of dresses I kept in storage.  For the record, I kept them for exactly this kind of situation.  Well, not this specific one; on a few occasions our clothes had failed to survive our more intimate moments.  My point is they weren’t for me or anything like that.

There is a widely held belief that if men had boobs, they’d never get anything done.  Too easily distracted.  I won’t provide details, but I can confirm.  It had been like learning to put my phone away back on Earth.

“Fine fine.”

“Did you get Reg?” she asked as she slipped the dress over her head.

“Ah, about that.”

“You didn’t?  Dammit.  That’s a pickle.”

I sighed.  “The Changer took him.”

She paused, her fingers tightening hard enough to rip off the button she had been doing up down her side.  “That’s a big problem.”

“Yeah.  I need to get to the unicorns.”

“And the other dragons.  Dragon’s blood and unicorn horns are the tricky ingredients for the Injections.  Speaking of?” She nodded at the unused injections on the table.

“Help yourself.  I think I’ll have Cyrus keep us well supplied here so when we respawn we can re-inject.  Since when did we become life junkies?”

“Junkies?”

“Never mind.”  At some point, I would have to get Kat to remake all my favourite movies so I could share some more cultural references with my beloved.

“It’s a good idea.  Where is Cyrus?” she asked, pulling on a pair of leather boots.

“On the industrial floor.  I think?”

“Might be worth checking.”

“KAT!  Oh wait, she’s still back in the throne room.”

A note appeared hovering in front of me.  Then a second and a third.  I snatched them up and read them.

“Dear individual known as Bob.  Cyrus is currently in his alchemy lab, on the second alchemy floor—the one you purchased exclusively for his use.  Kind regards in triplicate, Karen,” I read aloud.

“The others?” Esme asked.

“Same thing.  The triplicate bit really is her jam.”  I crumpled the notes up and put them in storage.  I would not litter in my vault.  This space was sacred.  Albeit depressingly empty.

My hoard had shrunk significantly.  A young Smaug would still be able to bury himself in the stacks of shinies, but I was too big to manage it now.  I needed more.

“How much did you spend?” she asked.

“Not counting the injections, about sixty million.  If you add those in…” my voice shook as my words stumbled to a halt.

“How many times did you die?” she asked.

“Twice.  Same for the rest of the strike team.  It was billions!” I wailed as I finally processed my feelings of loss.

“It wasn’t fun for me either,” she said softly.

“You were so brave!”

“It hurt, but knowing I wouldn’t die kind of robs me of the brave thing.”  She rubbed carefully at her throat, an unhappy expression on her face.

“I need a break,” I declared.  “Come on.”

A portal appeared; beyond it was the lake where we had first… anyway, it was that lake.  I stepped through and produced a bottle of wine and a pair of glasses, then turned to look back.

“There’s no time, Bob.”

“There’s always time for love.  I need to get someone to put Barry White’s back catalogue into memory crystals.”

She stepped through and took a deep breath of the fresh air.  The orlics weren’t in this area.  Soon they’d have recovered from their fights with Vik, and even more of their warriors would be sent against the Changer.  But for now this little part of the world, with Mount Bob looming on the horizon, was peaceful.

“Some people, not me, might say you were compensating for something by naming that giant rocky prick after yourself,” she chuckled, accepting a glass that I proceeded to fill.

“Mount Danglethorn or whatever it was… shit name,” I grumped, taking a sip from my own glass.

I lowered myself to the grass, sitting cross-legged and stared in silence at the azure waters for a moment.  This victory was at best hollow.  The Changer had access to portals.  He could be anywhere at this very moment, subsuming people and animals to fuel his army.

Esme sat next to me, stretching her legs out.  “However far you go, there’re always more problems,” she said gently.

“A firebreak,” I muttered.  “In order to live, we’re going to have to kill.”

She was quiet for a second.  “You mean… burn everything around a, what’s the word, incursion?”

“Outbreak.  It’s a disease.”

“And now it can go where it wants.”

“Metastasis.”

“What?”

“Cancer.  When it starts spreading, it’s metastatised. That’s like level four?  I think.”

“Four?”

“The death sentence.  It’s only a matter of time at that point.”

We fell silent and watched the wind shaping ripples on pristine water.  Nothing like the sludge that passed for water in the city; this was pure and clean.  And soon enough it would be gone—just another victim of the Changer’s expansion.  To be fair, the water wouldn’t feel it, unlike all the people and animals.

My thoughts churned.  Maintaining the network was mana-intensive.  I could only get away with it because of the mana crystal floor in the dungeon constantly recharging the portal stones.  Without that, or something similar, the Impression would only have limited uses at any one time.  He—it—could open a handful of portals and spread himself out, starting new infections, but he couldn’t abuse it as I had with my transit system.

“We could cut a deal,” I said reluctantly.

“What?” she snapped.

“Get ourselves and a few others out of this world.”

“Bob.”

“I know.”  I hung my head.  I’d had to at least suggest the idea, even though I knew she would never go for it.  “So what do we do?” I whispered.

Her hand found my arm, gripping my bicep hard.  “We fight.”

I turned my head to her and leaned forward.  My lips reached out for hers, metaphorically; I wasn’t shapeshifting.  As they met, a thrill ran through me, and I pulled her close.

“Bob.  We will get through this,” she mumbled around my tongue.

“Shuddup.”

I reached down and shaped a finger into a claw to carefully remove her dress.  Careful in the sense that she wouldn’t get cut, not that the dress would be functional afterwards.  As my tip found the seam, there was a blast of air that made me open my eyes and growl.

“Ooh.  Sorry to interrupt.”

“Morris,” I said flatly.  “You understand the concept of privacy?”

“Only as far as invading it goes.  Hey mum!”

“She’s not your mother!  You’re a middle-aged thief in a dragon's body.”

“More of a canman than a thief, Dad.”

“I’m not your father.”

“Noooo!  No, that’s not true!  It’s impossible,” the red dragon wailed.

“Search your feelings,” I snorted.

“Fuck off old man.”

“I’m younger than you!”

“Meh.  Only physically.  I’m young at heart.  I’ve come to say goodbye.”

He had grown up fast.  Or at the least he’d taken a lot of Increase Mass evolutions.  He was twenty-five metres from nose to the end of his tail.  The light danced on his scales as he moved, twisting his serpentine neck to shift his head back and forth.

Esme jumped up and rushed to him.  “You’re too small!”

“I’ll get bigger.  There’s plenty of good hunting out there.  And opportunities.  The old man… he’s got to do his own thing, mum.”

“You don’t have to.  We could use you,” I said, standing up.

“And that’s the problem, Bob.  I use, I don’t get used.  It’s just who I am.”

“You can remake yourself,” Esme said.  “You don’t have to be what you were back on Earth.”

“I liked who I was.”  His crimson-scaled shoulders shrugged.  “I don’t want to change, and being a dragon gives me a lot of room to indulge my greed.”

“But—”

“Let it go, love,” I said.  “There’s a goblin in him.  A greedy little beast, and he needs to learn to deal with it on his own.”

“A dragon thing?” she asked.

“Yes,” Morris replied.  “Do you know how big this world is?  It’s probably three times the size of Earth.  I’ve only flown over a few parts of it, but there is much more than this little empire.  And I’m going to slice off a chunk of it for myself.”

“As a courtesy to your old man?  It’s the least you could do after the damage you did to my cloaca when I laid your egg,” I chuckled.

“I won’t step on your toes…claws,” Morris said seriously.  “Also, I want to get as far away from the Changer as I can.  Maybe I’ll live a bit longer that way.”

“If we lose, you won’t live long,” I growled.

“You won’t lose.  Probably.  You’ve got too many gods on your side.  Also, I hate Karen.  What kind of god is that?” Morris said with a toothy smile.

A series of notes appeared in front of me.  I read them.  More appeared, which I also read.  The screwed-up bits of paper began to gather at my feet, somehow unmoved by the breeze.  Then a wall of notes appeared around me that I grabbed like I was in one of those tanks where they use a blower to make money fly around.

“I don’t think she’s very happy with you,” I offered.

“You bent the knee to her, not me.  I don’t need to worry about it,” he replied.

“Morris, she’s not someone you want as an enemy,” Esme muttered, miming stamping a form in triplicate.

Another wall of paper materialised in front of me.  “I get it, Karen!” I yelled at the sky.  “Kid, don’t piss her off.  Some of these threats are weirdly specific.”

“I’ll be around if you need me.  Mum, You’re a good mum.  I’ll make you proud.  Bob, you’re a bit of an ass.  Try to stop that.”

I snorted as Esme rushed over and hugged his chest.  He raised an armoured paw and rested it gently on her back.

“I’ll be around if you need me,” he said softly.  She backed off as he spread his wings, then her hair streamed out behind her as he took to the sky and flew east into the rising sun.

“He’s a weird kid,” I muttered.

“You didn’t try to make him stay!” she accused.

“Can’t clip a dragon’s wings, love.  You know that.”

She crossed her arms and glared at me.  It was a level three, nothing to worry too much about.

“You need to be crowned,” she said, smiling faintly as her pupils dilated.  I knew that look, she was looking at a status screen.

Harald Hardprick has fulfilled the prophecy.  The rebirth of the Primal Empire is at hand.  He has chosen to cede his throne to a dragon called Bob for reasons unknown but that may have involved violent threats from a certain pixie.
