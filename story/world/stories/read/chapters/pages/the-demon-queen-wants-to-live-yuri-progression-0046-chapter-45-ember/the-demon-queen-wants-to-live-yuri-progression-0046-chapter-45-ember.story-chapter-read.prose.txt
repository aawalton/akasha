"Do you think I can't feel that?" she said.

Her words made me go still, just for a moment. Then I straightened. I did not pull away from her, but something inside me almost did. "It is nothing. Nothing more than the consequence of a process. One that will end, in time," I said.

Ash looked at me. It was one of her looks that told me she was seeing more than I had meant for her to. It almost made me look away, except that would be a retreat. "Lys."

"It is a minor discomfort. One that is hardly worth-"

"Lys."

I closed my mind. The humming in my chest was still there, just as it had been there since I had first woken. I suspected it would be there still on my dying day. If she could feel the hum from where she sat, she had been feeling it for some time. I had forgotten how sharp her senses were. Far sharper than my own. Ash continued to wait without saying anything. I would have preferred she say something. This silent siege of hers was effective. The Hero shifted on the bed, just enough that her knee pressed against my thigh. And still she just waited without saying anything at all. I stared past her, at the far wall.

"I wanted to keep both of my marks. Your suggestions to fire them at each other…showed me a third thing I had not realized was there. Something…in between the Cradle and the Requiem." I did not know why I was explaining, only that I had started and could now not stop. "So, I thought that if I forced my two marks to war inside my core, this third thing would carve my core into an Essence Core. It did." Even as I explained, the humming grew louder.

The Hero slowly nodded. She looked back at my chest, to the place beneath that hummed. When she spoke, her voice was very quiet. "So this third thing that's inside you…It's still doing something, isn't it? It will keep doing something. I can hear it from here and-" She shook her head. "It's hurting you, and it will keep hurting you."

"Yes," I said. It was the truth. Or at least, what I suspected was the truth.

The Hero kept staring at me. "And you did this alone. You told no one about this. You asked no one about this first. You just did it." Her voice had not increased in volume. It was still sharper than it had been.

"There was no one to ask," I said at once. Something told me I had said it too fast.

"There was me."

I met her gaze and looked past it, again at the far wall. There was a crack that ran near the ceiling. I had never seen it before. It was a large crack, and I looked at it with far more focus than a single crack on a wall deserved. "What would you have said?" I asked. "If I had told you."

"I would have told you that you were about to do something that could kill you. That there might be another way, if you had just waited. You didn't have to wait long. Just a day, or two would have been enough."

"And if I had done it anyway?"

Ash paused. Then her head tilted, just slightly. "Then I would have sat next to you. So that if something went wrong, someone would know," she said simply, as though it were the most obvious thing in the world.

That was not obvious. And Ash saying it in the way of Heroes did not make it so. I looked at Ash and opened my mouth to say just that. The look on her face made me close it without saying anything at all. My attention returned to the crack. It seemed larger now. When I spoke again my voice was stiff. It matched the rest of me. "I have never needed to run my decisions by another. I am not in the habit of seeking counsel for my decisions."

Ash was quiet for a long time. I looked back at her face, and saw the hard look on her face gone. In its place was something almost soft. Almost sad, I think. She spoke, and her voice was softer too, little more than a whisper. "Now you can."

The silence between us stretched again. I had nothing for what the Hero had just said. But something needed to be said, this much I knew. "The danger was minimal, Ash. The chance of death was-"

The softness left Ash's face. "You are sitting on a bed in pain that will never stop. Lysanthia, do spare me the logic, just this once."

She had said my own name. The one I had carried for centuries. It should not have stabbed me so.  My own name should not have stabbed me so. My jaw tightened and I opened my mouth to argue. The pain would dull with time. Perhaps it would not dull exactly, but like all things, I would grow used to-

"You would have told Sara not to do what you just did." The words made me still. "If she came to you," Ash continued, "and said she was going to hurt herself, possibly permanently, because she thought it was the only way…what would you have said? Tell me."

It was an answer I did not have to think about. Not even for the shortest moment. I would have told her no. No matter how much she protested, and no matter what reason she gave, I would have told her no. I would have taken the sharp thing from her hands and held it away from her until she stopped reaching for it. I would not have let her choose pain. "That is different," I said.

"It's not different at all," Ash said. She shifted on the bed, until the little distance between us became littler still. "Promise me something."

The Hero's jaw was set and her eyes were steady. I had seen Heroes look like this often. And so I knew that this promise of hers would be more demand than request. "The next time you are about to do something that will hurt you," she said, "you talk to me first. You come to me and you tell me what you're thinking, and together, we find a different way, if there is one to be found." She held my gaze. "Promise me that much."

Her demand was unreasonable, and for more reasons than one. The first reason was one I had already voiced. I was a Queen, and never before had I needed another's approval for anything. Second, there were some choices that could not wait. One had to make a choice, and make it quickly, before they could wait for counsel. I looked at Ash's eyes, and I found both objections lacking. "Yes," I murmured.

Ash nodded once. Just once. "Good…Lys." She let out a breath she must have been holding. "I do not like to see you hurt yourself."

I looked at her. Ash was not looking at me. She was looking at the lantern, glowing dim. "Why?"

She frowned. She considered the question for a long time. "Because…I suppose it hurts me too…to know that you're hurting, I mean." She glanced at me. Her eyes widened, her face reddened. It was I who looked away this time.

I had nothing to say to the foolishness she had just said. Nothing to say at all. Ash shifted, or perhaps I did. Her hand touched mine. I did not pull mine away. Calluses aside, hers were very soft. "Why did you do this?" Ash asked. "This is not just about keeping both marks. Is it? There were other ways."

I thought of the demonbloods I had seen. The way they had cowered, not just from me, but from everyone and everything. I thought of the System I might have broken, and the way the world might have changed in a thousand years. Changed in a way I could have never foreseen.

"You do not owe them your pain, Lys," Ash said, her voice soft.

I stared at her. She stared back. I was sure I had not said anything. And yet.

"Perhaps not." They were not the reason. Not the whole reason. The Ash in front of me was replaced by a different one. One who hung in the air, held up by her throat. If there was power to be had, then I would take it. This was the surest path. That had been all that mattered.

Ash said nothing for a time. Neither did I. I said nothing, but something in her gaze softened anyway. She could not have known. She did not have any of her [Skills]. Nothing that would have let her read thoughts.

"So, did you name it?" Ash finally asked. "Your core. It's an Essence Core but…that's clearly not all it is, right? I don't think they'd classify it as just that, at least. I was wondering if you had a name for it."

"You assume I would want to name it?"

"Yes," Ash said. "Wasn't the first thing you did after getting Lines to name your techniques?"

I could have pointed out that Ash herself had done the same thing, but that would be petty. "Very well. And, I have given it some thought."

"Oh?"

I nodded slowly, staring at the lantern. Perhaps I had given it more than some thought. "I had considered calling it the Eclipse Core."

"That's a grand name," Ash agreed. At some point, the hand that had brushed mine now sat atop it. It was not an uncomfortable weight.

"It is a grand name." I looked at the lantern again. At its flickering flame. Now, it reminded me of another. One that had given off less light, but had felt far warmer for it. "But I have come up with one I like better," I declared, to no-one. "An Ember Core."

Ash said nothing. I frowned and looked at her, only to see her smile. "That is a much better name," she said.

I was glad I hadn't decided on that first name.

Eventually, we retired. The lantern had burned so low it barely gave off any light at all. Neither Ash nor I moved to tend it. There was one bed, and there were two of us. It had been the same in Hamel. And on the road, in the Inker's tent. It was a matter of simple practicality. It did not feel so tonight.

I was aware of every subtle movement of the bedding as Ash lay beside me. I was even more aware of the warmth along my side where her body pressed against mine. I was aware of the egg that sat between us, ever pulsing. I was aware of the sound of Ash's breathing in the dark.

The Ember Core hummed beneath my ribs. It was more noticeable now in the silence of night. I lay on my back and stared at the ceiling and made my breathing slow and deliberate.

Some minutes later, I turned onto my side, facing the wall. The thing was still humming, of course. Both the Cradle and the Requiem were silent, but between them, the thing I had made hummed and hummed and it would never stop humming. I shifted again, and this time the accursed bed frame groaned beneath me.

Ash's breathing was still slow and even. The stupid Hero, who could fall asleep anywhere, in any circumstance, had fallen asleep here too. It was only annoying before. Now it was frustrating. I should have learned the trick to this from her before.

Again I twisted and turned, trying to find some position on the bed where the humming was at its lowest. Or at least, where I could hear it less. Each time I thought I found it, the Core's rhythm changed, just slightly. As if to mock me. I tried not to sigh, but I wanted to.

Tomorrow night would be the same, I knew. The night after that would be the same too. Perhaps every night would be, until my second end found me. I had done all of this for power, and for power I had paid far worse prices before. This thing was nothing. Compared to everything I had endured, it was the smallest splinter. The splinter would not let me sleep. I shifted again, and the bed groaned again, and-

"You are an idiot." I froze. Ash's voice came from right beside me. Her breathing had not changed, and so I had not realized she was awake.

"Excuse me?"

I felt Ash turn on the bed, and then her breath was warm against the back of my neck. "It's been half an hour, Lys. You are not sleeping. I doubt you're going to sleep all night."

"I am merely resting."

"You are in pain."

The Ember Core's rhythm shifted again. It was louder now. The slightest bit more painful. It had been so much more tolerable when there had been other distractions. There was movement behind me. Ash sat up, and then her hand found my shoulder. "Come on," she said.

"What are you-"

"Get up. We're going for a walk." Her hand was firm. "Neither of us is sleeping like this anyway. We may as well walk. It might help you relax. Maybe that will help."

I should have argued. I did not need to 'relax' as she had said. And I did not need help. I sat up instead, and lifted the egg from the pillow. Ash watched me cradle it against my chest. She smiled. "You're not bringing the satchel?"

"No. The hour is late, is it not?" I said. "It is fine."

The hallway outside was empty, as I had expected. There had been no sounds at all over the last hour. The lanterns that hung along the walls were all burning low. We walked amidst shadows. Our feet made little sound.

We walked aimlessly for some time. Past closed doors and darkened hallways, past windows where the dark sky opened up beyond the glass. Ash walked beside me. Close enough that our arms nearly touched. We did not speak. Not for several turns.

We passed one of the large windows, one that was half open. A breeze blew from somewhere out past it, and I stopped. I held the egg tighter against my chest. The sky was very dark, but it was clear. The stars were bright out tonight.

"The sky is very blue here," I said quietly. Ash turned to look at me. She saw that I was speaking to the egg. I pretended I did not see her smile. "During the day, I mean. You will see it soon enough. It is…quite something. You will be a winged creature, and so you will travel it more freely than most."

I was speaking to an egg. Something that did not and could not- The egg pulsed in my arms. It pulsed twice. I paused. I chose to interpret this pulsing as an interest in what I was saying. So, I continued. "I would describe the food in this Academy as adequate. Adequate for one to sustain themselves. I do not know if beasts are fed the same, but I will ensure you are. Or perhaps I will force the Hero to teach me this cooking." I glanced at Ash. "She is passable at it. Though, it has been some time. Perhaps her skills have dulled."

The Hero made a choked sound that was very close to a laugh. Her lips pressed tight together, and her eyes sparkled in the dim light. We kept walking. Past more closed rooms, behind which were doubtless more sleeping students. Past large doors, perhaps for one class or another. We turned a corner, and came into a hallway curved with yet more windows. I saw my own reflection in one of them. A dark shape with two jutting horns, an egg pressed to her chest. A Hero beside her, soft-edged in the dark.

I looked away from the reflection. "I never thought I would have a child," I said. I did not know why I was saying them. I had certainly not decided to. But I said them anyway.

Ash was silent for a few steps. Then she spoke. It was the first time she'd spoken since we'd started walking. "Why is that? Is it because you didn't want one? You lived for many, many years, didn't you?"

It was a reasonable question. Humans had little knowledge of my kind, save for what it took to slay them. They had even less knowledge of me. "Because I could not. The Demon Queen's Class did not allow for the concept. Children were…not a possibility the System permitted. I do not know why."

"And now?"

Now. Now that we lived in a world where the chains had far more slack to them. I looked down at the egg. Moonlight caught it from the window, and in this darkness, the whole of it seemed to glow. "Now I have this thing. Though, you are not quite a child, are you?" I said to it, holding it up. "You are a beast. A creature born from whatever fool thing I did to save you. When you hatch, you will have wings, most likely. And claws. You will not look like me, and I believe that is the main requirement to be my kin."

The egg could not answer me, but it pulsed in my hands all the same. "I do not know what you will need from me, and I am sure you will need many things. I have never been so responsible for another." I paused, correcting myself. "That- I suppose that is not quite true. I was responsible for an entire realm, once. Millions of lives were defined by their proximity to my power." I lowered the egg back against my chest, cradling it in one arm. "That went poorly. That went…very poorly."

There was no reason to think about this. I was no longer the Demon Queen, and the power which had defined an entire realm once was gone. Thinking about it would achieve nothing. I thought about it anyway.  Something inside of me ached. Something that was not the Ember Core. There were footsteps. I turned. Ash reached out and took my free hand with her own. Her fingers laced with mine. They were warm. And soft. They were not only that. I could feel the callouses on her palm, ones she had gotten from a lifetime with the blade. It did not matter. I did not pull away from her, though I could have.

We kept walking after that, hand in hand. Our fingers intertwined. "I will do better this time," I whispered to the egg. Ash's fingers tightened around mine.

We turned a corner, and came to another hall, with another long row of windows. There was moonlight here, streaking through the floor in long bands of silver. A thought was building in me. Had been building in me since we had begun this walk. Ash had read something of my old life, much as thinking of it pained me.

"Tell me about your friends, Ash," I said. "You told me once…about a river, and the glimmering tree you wished to see. I would-" I stopped. Tried again. "I would hear more. If you are willing. Of things you have seen, or want to see."

The Hero looked at me. She wore an expression I had not often seen on her. Something careful. For a moment, I wondered if she would answer me at all. I wanted her to. That surprised me. Ash smiled. "Ronan took me to a tavern, once," she said.

"Tavern," I murmured. "What is this 'tavern'?" I vaguely knew of such buildings being present in cities. Their purpose eluded me.

"A tavern is…." Ash paused for a heartbeat. "Like the common room of that Inn, but bigger. It's where people go for…pleasure. Two kinds of pleasure, usually."

Two kinds of pleasure. I had not the faintest idea what Ash meant. Ash must have seen it written across my face, because she continued. And when she did, her voice was very gentle. "Ronan said he loved three things. He liked charging in first to every fight. Then, he liked drinking. Like what we drank at the Inn, remember?" I did remember. That vile liquid that had made me cough. "And he liked women." She said this last part with a particular strangeness that I noted. Her cheeks had color to them. "He said I needed to- in his words- 'stop being so hopeless.'" She cleared her throat. "At- attracting women. And uh- at everything that might come after. This was long before I was…." She trailed off.

I looked at the hero. She was staring at the hallway ahead. I followed her gaze. There was nothing there, nothing that warranted her staring with so much focus. I could feel Ash's pulse through her fingers, and it was getting faster and faster by the second. "He took me to this tavern he knew. It was very loud, and very crowded. He said it was 'the perfect place.' I'd never been to such a place before. My training had never allowed it. Anyway, he bought the first round of drinks and the second and by the third he was surrounded. There were just…women everywhere. I still don't know how he did it. He could listen to five women at once, and made all of them feel heard. Perhaps that was some [Skill]."

I nodded slowly. This was a kind of ritual, and I was starting to understand some of it at least. A few of my own Generals had engaged in similar behavior, when they thought I could not see them. "And you? Were you surrounded by any of these women?" I paused. Ash paused too. We had both stopped walking. The words had a sharper edge than I had planned them to.

"No! No, Gods no." Ash's free hand was making some kind of frantic gesture in front of her face. "He tried, he really did, but it never happened!" Ash exhaled through her nose. "I sat in the corner with my drink and spoke to no one for three hours."

I laughed. It was the second time I had laughed today.

Ash groaned. "It's not funny."

"I do not recall saying it was."

"You do not need to say it!" Ash protested. We started walking again. "He came to check on me thrice. Thrice. All three times, he looked at me like I was a lost cause. The third time, he brought one of the women over himself and introduced her and I said-" Ash pressed her free hand to her face. "'Hello, [Inspect] tells me you chose a nice sub-class. I haven't seen it often.' That is what I said." Ash groaned. "She did not appreciate the question. She left very quickly after that," Ash finished.

It had almost been enough to make me laugh again. Some of it was what Ash had said, much of it was the way Ash had said it. As if she still did not understand how or why it had happened. "Surrounded by women," I said sharply. That was odd, I had not wanted the words to come out sharp, but they had come out sharp all the same. "Your Ronan was quite popular, then."

"He had a talent for it."

"And you have none at all, it would seem. My understanding is that this is quite the failing in humans. Three hours, and not a single woman of any quality saw fit to approach my retainer. I believe that is unfortunate."

"Well," Ash said. Her voice sounded strange. "That was…all of that was a long time ago. Things have- I mean, circumstances are-" She stopped. Her fingers had tightened around mine. Not enough to bring any discomfort, but certainly enough to notice. "I think it's for the best that none of them did. I wouldn't have liked them anyway. I have always been very er- particular. About what I like."

"What do you like?" I asked. It was genuine curiosity. I wondered what her answer would be.

Ash made a strange choking sound, and her pulse, which was already quite quick, somehow quickened even more. "If I had to say then, uh, t-tall," she finally said. "Among other things." Then she walked faster. So fast I had to hurry to keep pace with her, lest I be pulled. I matched her pace, though I did not understand the urgency.

The night air was cool and pleasant, and yet I felt warm. I did not know why, only that Ash was very warm too. It was not nearly enough to do anything about the humming inside me, but the pain of the Ember Core felt more tolerable now. That was a strange thing.

We walked in silence. During our walk, we turned a corner and found a room that spilled out light. It seemed we were not the only ones awake. We turned a different corner, and came to a hallway that again had many mirrors. We passed by them, and in one, I saw my reflection again.

A dark figure with two jutting horns and eyes that glowed in the dark. An egg cradled against my chest. I saw more than just myself in the mirror.

Ash stood next to me. She was standing so close that it was difficult to tell where my reflection ended and hers began. In the moonlight, her blonde hair looked almost silver-pale.

I stared at that reflection for far longer than was necessary. Some part of me was committing it to memory. "We should go back, Ash."

Ash slowly nodded, and we began our walk. She did not let go of my hand the entire way back.

Eventually, we made our way back to our room. It took little time, not with Ash leading the way. It seemed she could tell directions even in the dark. The door opened with a small creak that carried in the otherwise silence of the Academy. The place was exactly as we had left it. I placed the egg back on the bed, close to the wall. I checked it once and adjusted the pillow beneath it.

Then I lay down first, and Ash joined me.

I was on my side, facing the wall. The egg was cradled against my chest. I braced myself and closed my eyes. I had braced for the long night. For the hours of wakefulness that would surely come. The Ember Core still hummed, and I suspected I would find sleep no easier now than I had before, when Ash had taken me on our walk. But it was not just the humming that kept me awake. I had read a journal, one that contained only the musings of a fool. No, that was not all they were. Even without my new Core, sleep might have been impossible to find.

The bed creaked, and I felt Ash move. Her arm came around me from behind, pressed flat against my stomach, just beneath my chest. She pulled me back against her, just slightly. There was still distance between us, however small. I had gone rigid. Slowly, I crossed the last of the distance between us. Until the little distance became none at all. There was warmth, and no small amount of it. I could feel all of Ash pressing against me.

The firm line of her stomach, the edge of her hip against the base of my spine. The soft, full weight of her considerable chest pressed against my shoulder blades. I was keenly aware of all of it, though I had no reason to be. Ash's arm moved again, until her fingers spread and pressed against my bare skin, where my shirt had ridden up. The warmth of her palm sat directly over the place that hurt.

The Ember Core hummed beneath her hand. It would always hum. But for a moment- just a moment- the warmth drowned out the humming. And my body released, bit by bit and one muscle at a time. My breathing started to slow of its own accord.

I should have said something. Ash's thumb traced a small, absent circle against my skin. I closed my eyes and said nothing at all.

For the very first time, I fell asleep before the Hero.
