“Not that we don’t believe you,” Adya said, looking the old man up and down as she repeated the same questioning tone Cass had had only moments before, “But can you prove it?”

“Why would I ever want to do something like that?” Alan replied, his fingers drumming against the table as he took another long draw off his drink.

As it refilled after nearing emptiness, Cass said, “You may want to take it easy there.”

Alan rolled his eyes, then snapped his fingers. The drink refilled, and he took another long hit, draining it dry. Then, he snapped his fingers again. This went on enough that Cass looked over at the man behind the bar.

Will was a study of economic motion as he filled drinks from a tap, made them disappear by poking them, then set aside the next empty glass to appear. His face never changed from the same expression of boredom he’d had when first meeting Cass and his friends.

By the 11th quick-swapping drink, Alan belched, long and loud. The smell that came from him, yellow teeth glinting from his open mouth, was enough to cause the hairs on the back of his neck to stand up and vomit to roll in the back of his throat.

Adya took it even worse.

“That’s disgusting! I don’t care if you are 8th tier, cover your mouth, you foul-”

Alan’s hands snapped out as his lifted drink crashed and broke on the floor. Holding Adya’s head perfectly still, he spoke in a low, commanding voice that sounded close to a growl.

“Adya Korring, Tier 2 Close-Quarters Scout, barely. You hold two upgraded combat titles relating to low-grade monsters like Scroungers and Skreels, as well as a contract title. Low-scale proficiency with the spear, an incomplete gate relating to Observation,” He spat to the side, “disgusting. And a strange ability from your contract called Profile. That’s a new one.”

She tried to pull away, but the man’s thin, rag-covered arms held her without shaking as he looked deeply into her eyes

“Let me go!”

“Let her go!” Cass yelled at the same time.

“No,” Alan said with a smile. “You’re barely a Scout, and you picked an upgrade that will slow down your growth. Sure, combat will get you levels, but it’s slow and forces you to constantly move. You’re a stupid girl, Adya Korring.”

Cass reached into his bag and pulled out the hatchet, “I may not be anywhere near your level, Alan, but don’t think I won’t stop you from hurting her.”

“Relax, kid. This is just something I need to do to look a little deeper, nothing will happen to her. It’s completely nonviolent, promise.”

“Then why did you grab her so fast?”

“Because how would people feel if I said, Hey, let me grab your head real quick? That’s a stupid question.”

“Cass,” Adya said, both of her hands wrapped around the man’s unbudging forearms.

“Why does she need to constantly move?” Cass asked while putting a hand on Adya’s shoulder. She looked at him in a near panic, and he knew from the feelings she was sending him that she was about to attack the man.

Speaking slowly, he said, “I don’t think he’s going to hurt us, Adya. He’s had all the time in the world for that, and he hasn’t done so yet.” Just to prove his point, he put the hatchet away.

She took a deep breath, her panic slowly ratcheting down as she put her trust in him. Alan gave her another smile as he released the grip he had on her.

“Good, you can control yourself. Maybe there’s some hope for you.”

Looking over at Cass, his hands disappeared before Cass felt a pressure settle in the center of his skull, “To answer your question, little survivor, killing different monsters unlocks different titles. Titles mean abilities and increased experience, not to mention it makes perfect gatting easier. So you slay a few hundred, then you have to move to the next location to find more, or be relegated to only gaining small amounts of experience each time. That’s why combatants who stay in one place slow down in leveling so fast. The System demands they travel.”

The man’s blue eyes settled on Cass’s brown as they stared unblinkingly at each other. In the back of Cass’s mind, he felt something like a warning as the old man in front of him breathed slowly. Before he knew it, the vicehold on his skull was released.

“There’s more than a little strangeness to your connection with the System, Cassio Vale. I’d say it’s almost watching you to see what you’ll do next. I’ve only met a few people in the world with that much attention on them.”

“Like, who?”

“Like me,” Alan replied, then snapped his fingers twice. Sound returned to them as whatever Will had done ceased to exist. He snapped a single time after and his drink was replaced, this time with a smoothie.

“Are you trying to be funny?”

“You need it, boss. Been too much beer, even for someone like you.”

“Beer has everything you need. This,” He looked at the pink drink in front of him, “This is what happens when you grow soft.”

Adya glared at him, “Aside from your alcoholism, is there anything else we need to know?”

He took a sip, then made a horrific face and continued drinking until half of it was gone. Cass couldn’t understand where it all went, given how skinny the man continued to look. Putting down the drink, he sniffed.

“You’re this man’s Guardian, are you not?”

“I am,” Adya said, sitting up straighter in her chair.

“And it’s your job to protect him?”

“Mine, and uh, Bella’s.” She said. When he looked confused, she nodded the draft-horse’s way.

He gave a harsh laugh, “Hah, a Guardian horse. Will the wonders of this world never cease? Fine. I'll take on the both of you.”

“Take us on? Wait a second. We don’t need you to do anything for us!”

Cass put a hand on her shoulder, “Just listen for a moment.”

Alan smiled and nodded slowly, “The way you are now isn’t good enough to protect this young man. He’s precious cargo. A QuestWright with the attention of the System is something that should be guarded as if your life depends on it. The way you are now, he’ll keel over and die the first time a mid-grade comes rolling around the corner.”

“What’s it involve?” Cass said, leaning forward, “Wait, hold that thought. Orla! Can you come here for a moment?”

The merchant-girl glared their way, then cradled her drink and came over to them, “What’s up? Who’s the old man?”

“I’m only seventy-two,” He spat out.

“That’s old as shit,” Orla said with a raised eyebrow. “You know, I’ve always been curious, and I bet you were around back then. What was dirt like when it was born?”

“Now hold on a moment…” Alan’s face began to turn red, but at this point, Orla couldn’t be stopped. She was always a truthsayer at the worst possible moments with the worst possible people.

“I bet holding is all you can do when going to the bathroom now, am I right? How’s that gotta feel?” She crinkled her nose, “My guess would be wrinkly and limp.”

“You…you…are hilarious, girl!” Alan said with a loud guffaw. “I like this one, Vale. We need more women who speak their mind in Liberty, merchants or not. She can kick around, too.”

“I bet that’s what he says to…hold on. Are we staying here?” She looked around the mostly empty building, Will giving her a wave behind the bar. “There’s not much to talk about.”

“Pfah, this is just the front, and I mean that in two ways. Everything in Liberty is revealed layer by layer. Come along, but leave the horses.” Standing up, the old man stretched, belched a second time, glared at the half-drank smoothie, then started to walk quickly away. Cass hurriedly called over Gary and Pellin as the five of them followed Alan to the back area.

Alan stopped in the small field of grass in back and waved his hands around, “Behold, the true value of Caligula’s End.”

“Grass?” Gary asked with his hand up.

“Put your hand down, boy. I’m no teacher. And no, that’s a stupid thing to say.” Reaching down, he seemed to find one piece of green shrubbery and pulled. And pulled. And kept on pulling as it lengthened the more he worked at it. Once it was at his hip, a large square of grass lifted with it, revealed a set of stairs and bright lights down below.

“Come along.” He waved at them to follow, then headed down.

Adya looked at Gary, who looked at Pellin, who looked at Cass, who then looked at Orla. Orla shrugged.

Together, the five of them followed Alan down the stairs. Gary had to bend over, his tall height forcing him down, but it was almost comfortable for the rest of them. Along the sides were several thick pieces of timber holding up long, sturdy planes of metal. The stairs went down quite a bit of the way before ending abruptly in a hexagonal room filled with people.

Cass tried to breathe through the existential terror of what was above him.

It’s fine. There’s enough rock and dirt up there to bury us a hundred times over, but it’s fine. You’re a big, strong QuestWright, right?

As he mentally talked himself up, he’d failed to notice the room.

To the right were several beds stacked on top of one another, a modest kitchenette, and a group of people playing cards at a table. On the left were several dummies that looked similar to what Cass had seen in the GoldenCrown compound, each holding different weapons or in the general shape of a large, feral monster. But it was the center that caught his attention.

Toward the back was a massive metal gate built into the very wall. The dull silver of it minutely reflected the light as Cass noted several dents marring its surface. By the way they were pushing out rather than in, they could only have come from the other side.

“Welcome to Caligula’s End.” Alan said grandiosely. “The number one training and leveling location in Liberty.” He pantomimed buffing his rags as he said, “I bet you young people haven’t seen-”

“There are monsters past the door, aren’t there?” Orla interrupted.

“I wonder what kind there are. Do you remember those weird, giant Moles we fought? It’d make sense if they’re underground like this.” Gary asked, bouncing on his toes.

“You’re taking this all a little too well,” Pellin commented, his face having lost some blood. “I have no interest in diving into whatever is out there.”

The tendon in Alan’s neck distended before he yelled, “As I was saying! This is the best training and leveling location in the city, that’s hidden! We don’t talk about it anywhere but here, and the only people who find out about it are those I have personally vetted.”

Cass raised his hand, asking, “Why?” When Alan looked at him.

“Why do you people keep raising your hands?”

Gary shrugged, “It’s polite.”

“Training,” Cass said right after.

Alan rubbed his eyes and spoke slowly, as if they were five years old, “We don’t tell people about this because if the Guildtower found out that we were poaching on their hunting fields, we’d be screwed. I would be hunted down, which ain’t so bad as they’d never catch me, but all the people I help would be as well. I can’t protect everyone if that happens. We’re not the only group that does this, but we’re one of the more successful ones, partly because of me and partly because we follow protocol.”

Gary raised a hand, “How many-”

“No more questions!” Alan said while cutting the air with a hand. He clapped twice, the sound loud in the room, “Now, you’re all idiots, but you’re not like the troglodytes who live in the Guildtower, no offense meant, Vale.”

“What’s a troglodyte?” Gary quietly asked Orla.

“Cavepeople,” Pellin answered for her.

Gary scratched his chin as he looked at Cass, “But we’re the ones in a cave right now? Right?”

Alan disappeared, and in the next moment, every one of them was sitting in a chair with no memory of having gotten there. Breathing fast, Alan pointed a finger at them, “Stay there and shut up while I explain.” He waited a moment, but Gary leaned over and violently threw up on the floor.

“Ah, music to my ears. Good. Listen up, because I hate repeating myself. This is a secret, with a big S. If you go around telling people what we’re doing here, we’re all dead and you too, just for having this information.”

Gary sat up and wiped his mouth with a sleeve, “but what-”

Orla pulled a Crest out of her pocket and threw it at him. It bounced off his head, causing the large baker to glare at her as she put a finger up to her lips.

“I really do like you, girl. So, here are the rules. No talking about what’s beneath Caligula’s End to anyone, on threat of total banishment. If you think you’ve found someone trustworthy, bring them to Will at the bar, then wait. I’m never too far away. Another rule, no entering the tunnels unless you’re accompanied by at least a tier 5. Will counts, if barely, but everyone over there is safe to travel with as well. You’ll meet them all later.”

The people at the table looked up from their cards and exchanged various greetings before looking back down.

“The last rule, no going into the tunnels during the Lottery.” Gary froze with his mouth open as Alan’s thin finger pointed his way. They held that pose until the Baker closed it with a light click.

“The reason we don’t go into the tunnels during the Lottery is that it’s a cover. While the whole crowd is looking at which poor bastards the Guildtower is swooping up, the Liberty military is conducting drills below their feet. That’s why everyone is expected to watch the Lottery. Or at least, enough people that it wouldn’t be strange to see how few Privates are running around the city on their highways.”

They all sat in utter silence as Alan looked at each of them. He paused an extra second on Gary before saying, “Any questions?”

Every hand went up.
