The last line was done. Amy could finally stop working on the ritual and look at it.

Crow lay unconscious inside the circle, with blood in the grooves around him and more drawn across his bare torso. The mana made it glow with each heartbeat. Complicated as it had been to carve all that into obsidian, Amy almost preferred doing it to looking at the result.

Everyone had given blood, so she hadn't needed to drain herself doing this. Unfortunately, gathering around him afterward hadn't done much to make the whole thing look less like a sacrifice.

Lyra was shivering, Lain had blood on her face, and Iris was smiling at a time when Amy would have preferred her not to. Ash stared down at Crow while Zayd studied the ritual. Nobody seemed inclined to say anything.

The red light reflected off the walls and made them all look worse. Amy shifted where she stood. Could they please get past this part?

"Well," Iris was the first to break the silence, gesturing at the unconscious boy surrounded by glowing symbols, "so do we start by forcibly making him drink the blood of a thousand virgins, or continue by eating his intestines and then gifting the rest to the Dark Lord below?"

"Hilarious," Ash muttered, looking as though the circle might make him sick.

"What? If we're going to sacrifice our friend to save ourselves, I think I'm allowed one joke."

"We're not sacrificing him. It's a deterrent." Amy would have liked to sound a bit more convincing.

"Of course. Whatever helps you sleep, boss." Iris rolled her eyes.

Amy ignored that and went over the activation in her head again. It was supposed to be the easy part. For some reason, that wasn't helping her feel any better about doing it.

She stepped back to check the circle. "Alright. Everything's ready." Lain had handed out cloths from her storage ring, and Amy used hers to wipe the blood off her hands.

Zayd moved closer. "I'd very much like to know how your ability managed this. Pity we haven't the time." He studied the markings a little longer before looking up at her. "Either way, I must ask Miss Stake—how will we know when the barrier has successfully linked with Crow? Surely we need some form of visual confirmation that the ritual has taken effect."

Amy froze, realizing she hadn't actually considered that aspect. Her ability had shown her how to create the link, and she had faith in it. But still, overconfidence could lead her to ruin very easily; this whole nightmare had shown her time and time again that she could not become complacent. Zayd was right, she needed to—

"The balcony…" Lain suddenly interjected, her quiet voice cutting through Amy's thoughts. "I saw one during exploration…"

"That works," Amy nodded, grateful for the solution. "Then let's get moving—"

"Wait," Lyra suddenly interjected. "Is it safe to leave Crow unguarded? What if something happens while we're gone?"

She gestured helplessly at the glowing circle. Lain was quiet for a moment, considering. Then she said, "Amy and I should go..."

"I want to come too—" Iris started, taking a step forward. Lain looked at her, and Iris stopped.

Amy couldn't work out what that look meant. Apparently Iris couldn't either.

"What?" she asked, tilting her head.

Lain turned to Amy without answering. "Let's go."

"Oh. Sure..." Amy glanced at Iris, then back at Lain. "Let's go."

Iris opened her mouth again as they left, but Lain kept walking. Amy had to hurry to keep up with her.

"Through here. It isn't far." Lain led her into a passage she'd missed when they arrived.

It was narrow enough that Amy had to walk behind her. Soon she couldn't see the red light from Crow's circle anymore; the runes along this corridor shone a faint blue.

As they climbed a winding staircase carved directly into the academy's walls, Amy found herself stealing glances at the other girl's profile; it was pretty obvious that she wanted the two of them alone for whatever reason… either that, or she really did not want Iris to come.

Have I done something wrong?

Amy's mind raced through the recent events, then it looked to the most obvious reason. Maybe Lain was upset about the ritual itself. Amy knew it wasn't exactly... ethical. But they didn't have a choice, did they? They needed time for the trials, and Crow had volunteered—sort of… Actually, he hadn't….

Maybe she blames me for all of this, Maybe she's leading me somewhere isolated to—

No. That was ridiculous. Lain might appear cold and emotionless most of the time, but she wasn't a killer, just timid. Then again, at least that's how the Lain in the manga had been, and Amy had learned the hard way that she couldn't blindly trust her knowledge.

They kept climbing around and around, and Amy's legs were beginning to object. Lain still hadn't said anything. If she wanted them alone for a conversation, she was taking her time starting it.

Should I, like, say anything…?

Amy debated in her mind whether opening her mouth right now would bring more trouble than answers. And eventually concluded that, if she did nothing, at least things could not get any worse.

After a lot of internal debate and walking, they finally reached a heavy wooden door reinforced with iron bands. Lain pushed it open, and cold air rushed in, carrying with it the distant sounds of beast growls.

They stepped out onto a stone balcony. The structure really did look like a medieval castle from out here, all towering spires and battlements. Amy almost felt like she was a character preparing for a war in a dark fantasy movie set, and for better or worse, a damn detailed one.

Thousands upon thousands of chaos creatures swarmed around the academy's perimeter. From this height, they looked like black dots, but Amy could make out some of their forms: they looked just as ugly as the first time she saw them, and the fact that they hurled themselves against the academy's invisible barrier did not do them any favours.

"Christ," Amy muttered, gripping the stone railing. She'd known they were out there, but seeing the sheer number of them…

How are we supposed to survive that…?

A little bit of fear started creeping in, until she shut it down with a shake of her head, forcing herself to focus. Either she'd survive or wouldn't; it didn't change that she needed to give her all for the opportunity to live another day.

Amy closed her eyes and reached out for the connection between the ritual circle below and the barrier surrounding the academy. She could sense it there, waiting. The process was kind of reminiscent of the exercises she did to augment her control over mana and spell casting. Only a ginormous bit harder. Thankfully, her broken skill and all the training that had pushed her magic to B (D) rank came to the rescue.

Okay, she thought, raising her hands. Let's see if this actually works.

But just as she was about to channel her power into the ritual's activation, Lain's quiet voice cut through her concentration.

"Amy."

She stopped with both hands still raised. Lain had said her name so quietly that she'd almost missed it.

The girl stood a few feet away, looking straight at her. She seemed unsure about something, which wasn't an expression Amy saw on her very often.

"I wanted to talk to you."

At least she didn't sound angry. Amy lowered her hands, though she wasn't sure she felt any better about this.

"What about?"

Lain looked down at the creatures below them. Amy waited until she looked back.

"You're changing."

"...What do you mean by that?"

Lain kept looking at her. No explanation, nothing. Amy could hear the creatures growling down below while she waited for the girl to elaborate.

"Well..." Amy tried again when no answer came, "I think it's rather like I'm acting more like myself. You know? Like I'm finally being a little more honest about who I really am."

"It's both," Lain said quietly, her voice barely audible over the wind. "And you should be careful."

Amy felt a chill that had nothing to do with the winter air. "Careful about what?"

"I know good people. And it only ends in two ways for them in places like this."

"Which are?"

"Corruption," Lain said simply. "Or death."

Amy stared at her for a moment, then let out a short, bitter laugh. "Well, you don't have to worry about that. I'm not 'good people.'"

Lain gave her a look that made Amy wish she'd kept that to herself.

"I wouldn't be worried about you if I thought that was true. I wouldn't have asked to talk."

Amy had no idea what she was supposed to do with that. She started to answer, gave up, then faced the barrier again. There was still a ritual to activate.

Lain was watching her. Amy raised her hands and tried to concentrate on the circle downstairs, and on the link she'd prepared between it and the barrier.

She fed mana into it. The barrier resisted, and the circle kept taking more power without getting any closer to connecting. After several attempts, Amy gave in and used her ability. She could sense where she needed to direct the mana now, though actually doing it was another matter.

Sweat ran down her face. So much for all that practice; nothing she'd done in class had prepared her for this.

Come on. Amy pushed more mana into the circle. Work already, damn it.

Then it caught. The resistance stopped so abruptly that she nearly lost her concentration. She could feel Crow's heartbeat through the link, and the barrier was pulsing with it.

"It worked." Amy tried to lower her hands. Her knees gave out instead, and for a horrible second she thought she was going over the railing.

Lain caught her by the arms, then shifted her hold to Amy's hips while she tried to get her feet under her again. The balcony refused to stay still.

"Thanks..." Amy swallowed and tasted blood. Had she bitten her tongue?

Lain didn't respond, just held her steady.

"We should get back," Amy said, trying to take a step toward the door.

But Lain didn't move. Her hands were still on Amy's hips, and her silver eyes had shifted downward, staring intently at something beyond the balcony's edge.

"Lain?" Amy frowned, following the other girl's gaze.

Far below, almost invisible against the dark landscape and the seething mass of chaos creatures, stood a figure. Amy could barely see it from up here. It wasn't moving, though. Everything else down there was.

She narrowed her eyes and tried to get a better look. The figure was humanoid, and it was looking directly up at them.

"Abaddon," Amy whispered the name like a curse. "How long has he been there?" She asked, her heart rate increasing.

"I don't know," Lain replied quietly, "But he's been there for a while."

"..." Creepy fuck… I wonder what’s going through his mind right after what he just saw us do…

"Amy," Lain said, turning to her, "Are you certain Abaddon won't simply... work around this obstacle…? He claims to be a true prophet after all…"

Amy looked up from the figure in the distance, meeting Lain's gaze. The question was reasonable, logical even. "The only thing I can promise is that I will try my best," she said simply. "It wouldn't be the first time I beat a stronger seer, either way."

Just like Zayd's uncle—Professor Kaelen—Abaddon was way stronger than her, and his mastery over his ability put Amy's in the water. And yet, Amy had won during the tea meeting, not because she was stronger or luckier, but because her ability was in a way a kind of counter against future sight; what did it matter for him to see the future if she was still the one choosing it?

Still, she couldn't grow confident. He was still Abaddon, and he was still a true prophet.

"I trust you," Lain muttered, turning to Amy. And before she could respond, Lain turned once again toward the distant figure and raised her hand. Ice crystallized in the air around her fingers, forming into a perfectly sharp spear of frozen magic.

Amy jumped back, startled by the conjuration. "Lain, what—"

Without hesitation, the girl launched the spear toward Abaddon's position. But Abaddon moved the moment it left her hand. By the time it reached his position, only the chaos creatures were left.

"Fast," Lain said, lowering her hand. "But he's hurt. He was favoring his left side when he moved, and his shoulders were hunched. You and your book did quite a bit of damage."

Amy was still staring at where he'd been. "Maybe warn me before you do that?"

"Sorry..."

"Oh, no, don't worry." Amy rapidly said, not expecting an apology. "It's alright. Let's just get going."

They both walked towards the stairway, Lain still holding the dizzy Amy, whose head was still on the fleeing man.

"He'll be back," Amy said as they descended the winding stairs.

"I know," Lain replied quietly. "We need to prepare."

They walked in silence after that. And as they neared the bottom of the staircase, Lain suddenly stopped.

"Amy," Lain said, not turning to see her. "Think about what I said earlier… I meant it."

"...I will." Amy wasn't sure what Lain wanted her to do about it, but she could at least think about it.

"Good." Lain started walking again. "I've already lost good people, Amy. I don't want you to be next."

They sat around Crow, who remained unconscious by the door. His breathing was steady. If the ritual hurt, he wasn't showing it.

Amy found a space between Iris and Lyra. Lain sat a little farther off. Zayd was sitting straight, looking as if he might get up at any moment, while Ash had put his jacket under his head and stretched out on the floor.

"Well?" Iris asked. "Neither of you came back screaming, so I'm taking that as good news."

"It worked." Amy wiped the blood from her lip. "The link is holding. His life is tied to the barrier now."

"Good. Then we can discuss the defenses," Zayd said. "And who takes their trial when."

Amy rested her head against the wall. She hadn't expected activating the ritual to take that much out of her. Her head was still buzzing, and she wished she could get rid of the taste of blood.

"Defenses first. We can't assume Abaddon will leave us alone forever. He could find a way through without killing Crow." She made herself sit up. "We need this room ready before he does."

Ash sat up too, leaving his jacket on the floor. "How many did you see? Still thousands? They were bad enough when we were running from them. I don't particularly want to fight them standing still."

"More than before," Lain said.

Iris let out a breath. "Lovely. Six of us, then, against that. Ideas?"

"We only have to last until Crow finishes his trial," Amy said.

"How long would that be?" Zayd asked. "And what would he have to do?"

Amy tried to remember anything that gave a sense of time in those chapters. She couldn't. "I don't know. It could be a few minutes. It could be hours."

Nobody answered her.

"Hours?" Lyra looked toward the doors. "We're supposed to keep them out for hours? With Abaddon out there too?"

Amy didn't blame her. Saying it aloud wasn't doing much for her own confidence. They probably couldn't manage it.

Probably.

"We can do it," she told them anyway. Even if she did not believe they could do it, she would still say the same words. "We have advantages. This place is easily defensible—lots of hallways, solid walls, if we map it correctly, we will know exactly where they are coming from. We're not being ambushed."

"You're right," Iris said, standing up and pacing to one of the obsidian doors. "Look how thick these are. Reinforced, too. If we keep them from breaking the doors down, they won't all be able to come at us at once."

Zayd went to the other door. "Obsidian resists magic. We may be able to use that. With some reinforcement..."

"I can reinforce the doors with ice, maybe the walls and cover the ground too," Lain offered quietly. "It will slow them down."

"We should also make traps," Ash said. "I know how to make stone spikes and pressure plates. Only need the right material."

"I have many," Lain spoke, "I will give you some."

"We block most of the entrances," Iris said, feeling along a door frame. "Leave them a few ways in, where we're ready for them. Make them come through those."

"That gives us somewhere to start," Zayd said as he rejoined them. "Now, the trials. We need to decide who goes when, before something happens and we have to choose in a hurry."

Amy nodded. She really didn't want to think that Abaddon might find a way around the barrier, but it was wise to keep it in mind. This decision was important.

"Logically," she began, then stopped. It was easy to call a decision logical until she had to tell someone to do something that might kill them.

"Amy?" Lyra prompted.

"There's more to it than who we need here to fight. These trials will... do things to us." Amy hesitated. "You could come back changed. A little, maybe, but changed. And tired. That goes for whoever goes first as much as whoever goes before Crow."

"Changed how?" Iris asked.

Amy thought back to the manga and winced. "They make you face things you don't want to. It's going to hurt, even if nothing physically touches you."

"Oh, good," Ash said. "Torture. The psychological kind."

“I assume this is something you also saw in your book, right?” Iris asked with suspicion clear in her voice.

Amy just rolled her eyes, ignoring her, then looked around the circle at each of them. Coincidentally, not a single person here had it easy in life, not even her. The trials would definitely hurt hard and deep.

"I think..." she started, then stopped. The weight of the decision felt enormous. It made her feel bad, dropping her companions into their traumas… And yet… "I'll go first and then Zayd second," she said finally. "We are both the only seers, the faster we go back, the better; the easiest way to fight against divination is with more divination, so if Abaddon does something, we can expect that it will take a little time to set up, with luck, we both will be here by then."

Zayd nodded. Amy checked what her ability was showing her before continuing. "Ash after you. His trial will probably be physical as well as mental. He should recover quickly enough."

"I'll be fine," Ash said. Amy wished she could be that confident.

"Then Iris."

"Sure." Iris shrugged. "Can't be much worse than the demons I've already dealt with."

"Lain, you're after Iris. That gives you time to finish the reinforcements."

Lain nodded once.

"Then Lyra and Crow?" she asked. "Yes," Amy said. "Someone could come back hurt. I want Lyra here if that happens. Crow stays in the circle until we've all finished, so he's last."

No complaints. Good. Amy wasn't sure she could explain it all again.

Lyra looked at Crow. "So this really is what we're doing."

"I know. So let's get to work." Amy stood up. At least she knew what to do now. "Abaddon's still out there. I want these defenses ready, and we have no idea how much time the barrier's bought us."

While the others got up, Amy looked over at Lain. The girl gave her a nod. That didn't tell Amy much, but apparently she was satisfied for now.

Amy returned it, then turned toward the doors. They had a lot of work to do before this room would be ready to keep anything out.

She hoped they had enough time.
