Amy shook her head. "He won't be able to move. Even when he wakes up, he'll need us to protect him until we undo it."

"Shit." Iris pushed her hair back. "So we go one at a time, with Crow last. Then, when it's his turn, you have to..."

"Break the connection, yes. After that, the creatures will probably bring the barrier down in a few minutes. Maybe an hour, if we're lucky. We'll have to stay and fight until he's done."

Lain stared at her. "Thousands of them. While we wait for Crow."

"I don't have another way," Amy said.

None of them answered. Amy waited, though she wasn't sure what she wanted them to say. She'd heard herself explain it. It sounded awful.

"Well," Iris said eventually, "if we mess this up, at least nobody has to die alone."

"That's... not particularly comforting," Lyra muttered.

"We need to go and tell the others about it," Iris said with exasperation. "Speaking of which, why are they not with you two? It could be dangerous here, you know?"

"I doubt there is any danger here, so that's why I left them behind, so they guarded the teleporter," Amy explained. "Abaddon puts nothing in."

"That sounds dangerous…" Lyra muttered.

Amy simply sighed, "Again, no other option. We had to make sure—"

"I'll correct you on that one, Miss Stake. There was one other option." A male voice suddenly spoke from Amy's left side. They turned. Zayd walked out of the shadows by the entrance with Ash slung over his shoulder, still unconscious. He looked around at them all.

"Zayd?" Amy hadn't expected him to leave the teleporter. "What are you—how did you—"

"Wake up," Zayd said curtly, unceremoniously dumping Ash onto the obsidian floor with a solid thud.

"Ow! Zayd, what the fuck?" Ash pushed himself up on his elbows and looked around. "Did you just drop me? Wait. Where are we?" He caught sight of Crow. "Why's his shirt open?"

"Long story," Iris said.

Ash looked at the others, presumably hoping someone would give him a little more than that.

Amy ignored him and instead turned to Zayd. "What are you doing here? And what do you mean by 'another option'…?"

Zayd brushed dust off his clothes before responding. "I must confess, I was unaware of your specific plan, Miss Stake." He looked at her. "But I didn't care for the prospect of being ambushed, either. While you were busy here, I dealt with a rather obvious problem."

"Which one?" Iris asked.

"The teleporter. The only way into this building. I wasn't comfortable leaving it available for anyone to use. So I spent considerable effort tampering with its activation mechanisms."

Iris stared at him with a confused expression. "You what?"

"I modified the teleporter's core matrix," Zayd continued, seemingly unbothered by everyone's shocked expressions. "Anyone using it now has to keep a hand on it and channel for quite some time. They'd also need to understand my modifications. Abaddon cannot activate it from elsewhere. Neither can anyone else."

Ash raised a hand from where he sat on the floor. "Sorry. What happened to the teleporter? Why are we worried about it? And again, why is Crow—"

"Later," Iris cut him off.

"How…?" Lain's voice was sharp with interest. "How did you manage to modify Academy-grade teleportation magic…?"

Zayd seemed rather pleased to have been asked, though his expression barely changed. "I started when we met Crow's father. While he was occupied with all of you, I was trying to get the teleporter working. I had to learn quite a bit about it."

"You were trying to get us out," Amy said.

"Yes. I didn't manage it before the creatures arrived, but the work wasn't wasted. When we reached this building, I went back to it. I spent the time after you left finishing the security link."

"Hold on." Ash got to his feet, swayed, and steadied himself. "You've been rebuilding a teleporter while I was asleep? And again, what the hell are we doing here? And again, why is Crow half-naked—" He gestured helplessly at their unconscious friend. "What is going on?"

"The world is ending and we're all going to die unless we do morally questionable things to survive," Iris summarized bluntly. "Catch up."

Zayd nodded approvingly at the summary before continuing. "Once I completed the modifications, it occurred to me that your decision to leave me behind was likely strategic, Miss Stake. You needed someone to defend the teleporter. So once that problem was taken care of, I decided to leave."

"And you brought Ash with you because...?" Lyra asked.

"Was I supposed to leave him there unconscious?" Zayd asked. "Besides, whatever you were planning, I thought you might need him."

Ash threw his hands up in exasperation. "Someone please explain what's happening before my head explodes."

"Amy's going to use Crow as a hostage to prevent the Academy's barrier from falling," Lyra summarized.

"She's WHAT?"

"It's complicated," Amy said, already feeling tired of the topic. "Either way, we should stop losing time with explanations and actually get to work."

Ash looked around the room at everyone's faces. He spent a little longer looking at Crow before turning to Amy.

"Alright. So things are bad enough that we're using him as a hostage, and you've all agreed to it. I'll take that to mean we're fucked if we don't." Ash rubbed his head. "What do you need me to do?"

Amy straightened up, feeling a weight settling on her shoulders once again. She glanced around at the faces circling her—Lyra still uncomfortable, Iris grudgingly supportive, Lain calculating, Zayd surprisingly helpful, Crow still unconscious, and Ash ready to act despite his confusion.

Everyone was here one way or another…all except for one.

Amy looked down at the book in her satchel, silent and damaged. A pang of sadness hit her, but was rapidly exterminated by the feeling of numbness. The emotions were happening deep down; she could feel them, but it was almost like another person was experiencing them. It was a level of dissociation she had never experienced before.

"A ritual, that's what we are going to do." She suddenly responded, her voice sounding kind of emotionless. "Then we'll put up defenses. After that, if there's time..." Amy looked around the obsidian room. They still needed to talk before any of them went into those trials. "Well, we'll talk."
