As he walked, he kept his eyes open for patches of dense mana. More than a new safe zone, he really needed a new dungeon. There was nothing wrong with the gas station, after all (aside from him getting tired of roller dogs and donuts); it was just that the nearest dungeon was currently problematic. If there was one within walking distance of his previous safe zone, he was happy to keep staying at the gas station.

Though… if the Game is using established buildings as safe rooms, isn’t it possible I’ll walk for miles and find nothing? It’s not like there was a whole lot down this highway. I mean, keep going and I’ll hit something, but it might be twenty, thirty, fifty miles out. That’s a long way to walk. He paused. Okay, I think my home city is within fifty miles. But the point stands!

He’d been climbing for some time now, and as he turned, he came to a natural overlook, a place where the thick forest fell away to reveal the world around him. He stepped forward and gazed out over the world. A thick old-growth forest spread as far as the eye could see, interrupted occasionally by raw rock jabbing through the earth like broken bones. A new mountain range loomed on the horizon, wreathed in cloudy blue mist.

Eric crossed his arms and shook his head at himself. “Or they could completely terraform the world and do whatever they like with the buildings, I guess.” If they could form new mountains, why not move a few gas stations around?

Or… grocery stores, or anything. He’d kill for some fresh produce and raw meat, even if he had to cook it over a fire. It wouldn’t be his first time doing camp cooking. That, and his back ached after so long on a hard tile floor. One of those furniture stores where they had display beds or even just a superstore with things like pillows and comforters that he could stack up to make a thicker nest than a few thin, cheap t-shirts sounded like heaven.

From the overlook, he had a better view of the world around him. There were several dense sections of mana, and usually, in a clearing near them, a building of some sort—safe rooms, he could only assume. He checked his map, and found that it had populated with everything he could see, though in vague, generic terms, whereas the stuff he’d walked was clear and crisp. Basically, the stuff he could see on the overlook was blobbed out—safe room, dungeon—according to his guesses, whereas the stuff he’d walked was clearly fixed, with paths and details like spawns and even plant life if he zoomed enough. The dungeon he’d cleared had a red triangle with an exclamation mark in it hovering over it, and when he focused on it, it warned: Dungeon Over Level – Extreme Danger!

Guess I could’ve looked at it on the map, too. Joe really wasn’t kidding; he wasn’t helping me out, just pointing out the obvious.

None of the other dungeons had any warnings on them, nor any other indication of level. He could kind-of eyeball the density of the mana, but he didn’t know if that was a good indication or not, or even if his readings were reasonable; one dungeon was heavily wooded, and he couldn’t make out much mana past the trees’ own mana, and another appeared to be underground, its entryway appearing as a bright, ultra-dense mana spot while the rest of the area was empty—or at least at normal mana density. Without specific knowledge of any of the dungeons, he turned his feet toward the closest one and walked on.

At least he had a feel for the layout of the area he occupied now. It was most useful to think of it in video game terms, as if this was the map of some fantasy land. Right now, he was in the overworld. The monsters here weren’t particularly strong, but if he was careless, they could still take him out. Still, for the most part, he was just getting from point A to point B out here.

The points of interest were the dungeons. He wanted to get to and clear a few of them before the next monthly reset, and at least visit all the ones on his map before he moved on. Each dungeon had its attendant safe zone within a reasonable walking distance; or at least a safe zone within walking distance, he amended, looking at his map. Several of them didn’t have a dedicated safe zone, but there was always a safe zone within range; some of them supported multiple dungeons, was all.

I should make sure to try and get a high vantage point on a new area I’m about to explore, he noted. If he could always have the information he’d just gained from his little hike, he’d have a much better idea of where to go and what to do next wherever he decided to set up shop.

There were the faint impressions of paths between the safe zones and dungeons, when seen from on-high; places where the trees thinned or where the path itself was visible through the trees. Those weren’t recorded on his map, but he’d made a mental note of them anyways. It meant there would always (or at least often) be a “proper route” connecting any two places, and going off that route was probably akin to overworlding in an open-world game; a here there be dragons kind of vibe, or at least here there be lots of wolves and annoying enemies that make it not worth leaving the path.

He didn’t know it was the case, but he did strongly suspect it. He’d caught a glimpse of scary things moving around in the woods every now and again from his clearing stronghold, but they’d never actually materialized into a threat. Now, he had retreated into the gas station every time one got too close, but the point was, if he’d been out there in the woods, he would have encountered more of those scary monsters.

Amendment: the overworld is only not scary if you stick to the path.

A few hours passed while he made his way down the rise and into the valley. It was lunchtime by the time he reached the bottom. He had the I Love Truckin’ bag out and was snacking on some trail mix from its depths as he walked, but put it away and slid the bag back over his shoulder. He swallowed his mouthful of peanuts and chocolate and dusted his hands off. “Well.”

Looming over the far side of the clearing, a huge gate blocked his path forward. Heavy iron bands bound wooden slats that stretched high overhead, single solid planks of wood that seemed to have been hewn from the hearts of trees many centuries older than any tree he’d ever seen, because each plank was easily two hundred feet tall. The double-doors weren’t latched or locked, but stood ready to swing wide at the slightest touch. Two equally massive posts secured the gate in place, and extending from those posts, a thick mana fog clad the forest all around him. If there be dragons anywhere, they were probably in that fog.

Curious more than anything, Eric took an empty can out of his bag and tossed it at the mist to the right of the gate. It flew into the mist and vanished. A moment later, it came rolling out of the fog to the gate’s left, crumpled, with a gash missing from its side and droplets of soda leaking out of it.

Right. Not going in there. He turned to the gate. He hadn’t seen it from above, but if the mana fog extended around this whole area—and it looked like it did—then the gate was the only way in. Did that mean that the dungeons in this area were connected, somehow? Did he get some kind of bonus when he beat them?

Yeah, a bonus boss, probably, he noted. The way things had been going, it’d definitely try to kill him before it rewarded him.

Still, there was only one way to find out. He sent the Truckin’ bag to his inventory and drew his Blackfang Sabre he’d gotten from the last boss. He wasn’t a sword expert, but he could keep the pointy end toward his enemy with the best of them. The baseball bat was an excellent walking-around weapon—after all, if he tripped, he just fell on a stick—but the sword was good for situations like this, where he was likely to face something big in a nice, open, relatively flat arena.

There’s no way nothing comes out of a gate that big, he thought, looking it up and down. It was too huge. Too impressive. Too… boss-door-y.

Only one way to find out. He stepped forward and pushed the doors open.

They creaked wide, hinges squealing and heavy wooden slats groaning. Eric stepped back, making some room between himself and whatever waited on the other side of the doors. Dense, opaque mist laced with strands of golden mana spilled out, wafting into the clearing. Eric called out his Magical Wand and tucked it into his belt just in case. Firebolt was a work in progress, but the kiddie wand still had batteries. Those star-shaped bolts got the job done with the best of them. It didn’t matter how silly it looked; it could save his life. The 10% poison knife joined it a second later. He hadn’t found a use for it last fight, but maybe this one would be different. He was still wearing his snakeskin coat, and for whatever it counted: he pulled the snakeskin bandanna up over his nose.

The earth trembled underfoot. Something howled in the near distance. A shadow loomed in the thick fog. Eric braced himself.

A wind blew through the gate, stirring the fog into a frenzy. It rushed at Eric, sending the tail of his coat flying, pasting his clothes up against his chest. He lifted his hands, defending himself from it, whatever it was.

What is the shape of your heart?

The wind died. Eric looked around, startled, but he was still alone, just as he’d been this whole time. Cy continued to snooze on his shoulder without noticing a thing. He touched his ear and frowned. Had he heard those words? Felt them? He wasn’t sure. It was like they’d been spoken by the wind itself, or put in his mind without ever being spoken.

He looked at the gates with more trepidation than before. Was this some kind of trial? Something like that? He could turn back, but it also made him want to walk forward. Trials meant rewards. And he already knew there were plenty of baby steps beyond the gate, little dungeons he could use to get stronger.

Eric stepped forward, toward the gate. The fog shivered again, and a massive beast hurtled out of it, eyes glowing red. It looked like a wolf, but uglier and a good five or six times bigger, big enough its lower jaw was level with his forehead—not that Eric had long to process this. It pounced on Eric. Fangs bore down on him, streaked with thick saliva.

“Shit!” Out of pure panic, Eric met the open mouth with a swing of the saber, but the blade struck its rubbery gums and bounced right out of his hands. The teeth snapped shut around him, and the beast skidded to a halt at the end of the clearing and shook hard, like a dog with a chew toy. Eric bounced around in its grip, struggling against its shakes. It had him by the midriff, with his upper body and legs both free, though his arms were bound in its grasp along with most of his lower abdomen and waist. All he could do was flex or fight as it shook him and swing his legs to try and wriggle free.

But just because his hands were in its mouth didn’t mean they were useless. A good chunk of the rest of him was in its mouth too, and it was the chunk where he'd put all his weapons, at that. With one hand, Eric fished around at his belt; with the other, he grabbed ahold of its thick, wet tongue and cast Firebolt. It was barely more than a handful of sparks, but a handful of sparks still burned on the tongue. The monster jerked to a halt where it stood and jerked its tongue out of his grasp, then went back to shaking. Before it could shake him into a concussion, Eric twisted his wrist and started pushing the Firebolts directly into the soft flesh under its tongue instead.

At the same time, he finally closed his other hand around a familiar shaft. Blindly, Eric drew the poisoned dagger, then started stabbing the monster’s upper palate. He could only swing the lower part of his arm, and only a few inches, but it didn’t matter. He prioritized speed over depth or weak points; the dagger only had a 10% chance of poison, after all. Better to get lots of little stabs and go for that 10% rather than trying to somehow critically wound it through its upper palate. If he managed to poison it, poison would more valuable than any strike he could manage from in its jaws, bouncing around like a chew toy. Before long, a beautiful word appeared before his eyes, then another, and another:

Poisoned

Poisoned

Poisoned

On the third proc, the beast finally growled and spat him out. The force of the spit sent Eric flying, and he struck the ground and rolled until he hit a tree with a thump. Soaked in beast spittle, one leg bleeding badly, he climbed unsteadily to his feet. The beast circled him, its hackles raised, red eyes half-narrowed. Eric turned with it, keeping the poisoned dagger between himself and the beast. The saber laid discarded on the earth halfway between them and the gate, well out of Eric’s reach.

He tested his leg. The adrenaline suppressed most of the pain, but even so, he’d had to mostly use his other leg to climb upright. The injured leg took his weight, but it trembled fiercely, and he could feel the pressure of impending pain pressing up against his adrenaline-dulled senses. No fancy maneuvers. No crazy swordfighting. His back was up against a wall here, literally as much as figuratively. Behind him was the forest, thick with trees and danger. Ahead of him, the beast, the clearing, and the gate.

It’s poisoned. I just need to stall it out. Eric tossed the poisoned knife back onto his belt and pulled out the Magical Wand instead. Or do I?

The beast roared and pawed the ground, then charged him again. Eric flipped the switch on the Magical Wand. Tinny speakers played that familiar public domain song… and then the notes twisted and faded, the lights flickered, and the whole thing went dark.

Not now! There was no more time. Eric threw himself to the side, rolling head over heels over the dusty ground, and the beast hurtled past and struck the trees behind him. It laid there for a second, stunned, and Eric used that time to limp around the edge of the clearing as fast as he could—which wasn’t very. He put the Magical Wand back away and drew out the poisoned dagger again instead. Poison it was.

He activated Regeneration as he limped along. If it was any other limb, he probably would’ve left it be, but legs were essential both for running and attacking. Most of the body’s power came from the legs, or so every sports teacher had taught him. Whether he wanted to stand his ground or run away, he needed legs first.

The monster finally climbed to its feet and backed up from the trees it had crashed into and shook its head, looking around. It was poisoned, and the poison ate away at it steadily, but it still looked healthy for now, with plenty of health in the bucket. Its eyes latched on to something on the other side of the clearing, and Eric breathed a sigh of relief. If it could just keep being distracted by whatever that was—

A tiny white shape hopped toward the trees as fast as its little legs could carry it. Bright red horns shone on its forehead. It shot a panicked look over its shoulder at Eric and kept hopping toward the forest. The beast took another step toward the little rabbit and extended its muzzle, nostrils flaring as it sniffed.

Cy! That’s Cy! Eric checked his shoulder instinctively, but no, the rabbit wasn’t there; he must have fallen free during the attack. He gritted his teeth. He couldn’t let Cy die. That dang Anomaly still had to teach him magic! Using all the strength he could muster, he hobbled toward the beast. Eric lifted his hand and mustered all the mana he could wrangle, pushing it into his hand until the extremity glowed gold in his vision. Exhaustion fell over him, but he pushed anyways. It’s poisoned. It won’t last much longer anyways. I just have to distract it, just for a few seconds!

With one great push, he shoved all the mana out of his hand, forming a Firebolt out of the raw energy in the split second before it left his body. Instead of the fizzling sparks, a roaring bolt of fire burst out of his palm and soared toward the beast. It struck the monster on the shoulder and set its fur aflame, then burst, sending the beast stumbling to the side.

“Cy, run!” Eric shouted.

The beast turned, fixing those glowing red eyes on Eric. Eric stumbled to a halt, then began to back up. He called on his mana, trying to call up another Firebolt, but it was empty: he was tapped out. Fully tapped out. His body ached like he’d run a marathon, and it was a struggle just to keep lifting his legs and walking. Blood seeped down his injured leg where the wound had reopened and started to bleed again.

I hope I don’t die for that dumb rabbit. I hope it actually teaches me magic, dammit, Eric thought as he locked eyes with the beast and slowly backed toward the trees behind him. He drew his poisoned dagger and pointed it at the beast, keeping it between him and it, for whatever that counted. Come on. You’re poisoned. Die already!

The beast lowered its head. It growled, low and dangerous, and pawed the ground, a second from charging.

Eric’s back hit the trees. He adjusted his stance, putting all his weight on his good leg so he could move as fast as possible right now. Here goes nothing.

Behind the beast, a tiny rabbit lifted its paw. “Swarm.”
