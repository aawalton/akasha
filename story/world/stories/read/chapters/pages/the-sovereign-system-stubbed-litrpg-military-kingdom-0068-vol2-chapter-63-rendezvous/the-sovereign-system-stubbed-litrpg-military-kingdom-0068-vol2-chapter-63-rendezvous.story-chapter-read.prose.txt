The soft patter of rain drummed against the thatched roof, each drop echoing through the silence like a persistent reminder. One stray droplet slipped through the roof and landed on Luke’s face, making him flinch.

His eyes snapped open, a flicker of irritation passing through him. With a quiet, miserable sigh, he sat up and rubbed the fatigue from his eyes, though it clung stubbornly to his bones.

A glance around the room revealed a bare and grimy interior. The floor was packed dirt, littered with bits of straw. Two straw-filled mattresses lay on opposite sides—one of which he had just risen from. Other than a pair of rickety chairs and a soot-stained fireplace in the hearth, the room was empty.

Luke swung his legs over the edge of the bed, wincing slightly. His muscles ached, stiff from the cold and poor rest. Stretching his arms overhead, he tried to loosen the soreness buried deep in his body.

It had been two days since their escape from the Jade Silk Pavilion, and Hilda had yet to return with any word of Kayson or Sebastian. Though the delay wasn’t outside his calculations, a creeping sense of helplessness had begun to settle in—burrowing into his mind and souring his mood.

Coupled with the dismal living conditions, it made for a perfect storm of anxiety and spiraling thoughts. He couldn’t afford to remain idle—not while a coup was likely unfolding in the palace.

A pang of guilt struck as his gaze shifted to the second prince, curled into a ball on the other mattress, knees drawn tight to his chest. Luke had heard him crying the night before—a soft, broken sound that lingered long after the boy fell silent.

It’s going to get worse before it gets better, Luke thought grimly, pressing two fingers to his temples.

He pulled on his boots, grimacing at the damp stench wafting up from them. For a brief moment, he considered tossing the wretched things into the fireplace—if only there had been a fire. But he shook the thought away.

They were the only boots he had.

Throwing his cloak over his shoulders, he stepped outside into the drizzle. Cold rain immediately kissed his skin, and with a mutter of irritation, he pulled the hood over his head and trudged through the narrow alleyway.

The sky hung low, blanketed in dark clouds that cast a melancholic shadow over the city. The alleys, already grimy and narrow, felt even more oppressive beneath the grey veil.

He passed slumped figures huddled beneath awnings, seeking whatever shelter they could. Their clothes were soaked, their limbs drawn in tight as they tried to catch fragments of sleep. The sight twisted something in Luke’s gut.

Helplessness again. It crept up like a tide, threatening to drown him.

He shook his head, forcing the feeling down.

There are more important things to do right now.

Winding his way through the twisting backstreets, Luke finally emerged onto the main thoroughfare. Even in the rain, the city was awake. Merchants dragged carts loaded with produce, workers hauled goods into storefronts, and the clang of labor echoed through the wet air.

The scent of freshly baked bread mingled with the earthy smell of rain-soaked stone. His stomach growled in protest.

He ignored it, eyes scanning the street ahead.

He was looking for a particular building.

His eyes halted on a wooden signboard swinging gently in the rain, hanging from iron hooks embedded in the stone wall of a large building at the far end of the street. Faded yet elegant lettering read: “Wandering Inn”, beneath a hand-painted image of a bed.

Without a word, Luke crossed the street, his hood drawn low against the drizzle. As he stepped inside, a wave of warmth greeted him, seeping into his skin and making him shiver involuntarily.

“We don’t got any vacancies,” a gruff voice called out.

Luke turned toward the speaker—a short, broad-shouldered man with a thick beard streaked with red. His single eye glared suspiciously beneath a tangled mess of dark hair, the other hidden beneath a strip of cloth.

The sight made Luke think of pirates from old Earth tales. His gaze instinctively dropped to the man’s legs, almost expecting one of them to be a wooden peg.

He was almost disappointed to see they were both intact.

“I’m not here for a room—just a meal,” Luke said plainly.

The stocky man grunted. “Breakfast ain’t for another hour. You pay up front.”

“That’s fine. I’ll wait.”

The man jabbed a thumb toward the room behind him. “Sit wherever’s free. Just don’t wake the bastards sleepin’. I ain’t cleanin’ your teeth off the floor later.”

Luke offered a curt nod and scanned the room. The scent of stale ale and wet wood filled the air. Tables were scattered throughout, most occupied by slumped figures—likely drunk from the night before. Only one man was awake.

A subtle breath of relief escaped Luke’s lips as his eyes landed on the bald, scarred head.

“You came, Christian,” he said, sliding into the seat across from the man.

Christian looked up and grinned, flashing a toothy grin. “I gave you my word, Gen—” He caught himself, clearing his throat awkwardly. “I probably shouldn’t call you that…”

Luke waved it off with a smile. “I’m surprised you managed to get some time off. Wasn’t Tristan supposed to notify the Master of Arms about your promotion to Lieutenant Commander?”

“I asked him not to,” Christian replied, his tone serious. “I swore my loyalty to you, not the Kingdom.”

Luke blinked, caught off guard. “Huh?”

His mouth opened in surprise before curling into a wry smile. The sentiment was unexpected—but deeply appreciated. Warmth stirred in his chest.

“How about the others?” he asked. “Don’t tell me they’re just as stupid as you.”

Christian let out a soft laugh. “They wanted to do the same. But the Vice General talked them out of it—said he needed some trustworthy men to stay behind and help.”

At the mention of Tristan, a shadow crossed Christian’s face. Luke noticed it immediately, and a flicker of unease stirred within him.

“And Tristan?” Luke asked gently. “Is he okay? Did they question him?”

Christian’s expression turned grim, making him look years older.“He was sent to the dungeon… Even with Richard pushing hard, he couldn’t convince his uncle to reverse the order.”

He fidgeted as he spoke, cracking his knuckles absently.“They’re saying he might be executed for treason—dereliction of duty or something like that.”

Luke’s brows furrowed, a pang tightening in his chest. He had expected Tristan to be interrogated, perhaps even demoted—but not this.His plan had been simple: have Tristan shift the blame onto him. After all, he was presumed dead, and with a war looming, executing a capable Vice General should have been out of the question.

If they’re distracted, maybe we can… The thought never finished. He shook his head.

All hell is about to break loose.We’ll need to focus everything on reclaiming the throne and rooting out the traitors.

Even so, a sliver of guilt wormed its way in.

Sorry, old friend. You’ll have to wait a little longer before we can come for you.

Pushing down the unease, Luke steered the conversation forward.“Any changes in the palace?”

Christian shook his head.“Everything looks normal. The king’s retreated to the inner palace for mourning with the noble consort. Supposedly, the ritual will last a few more days.”

Luke’s eyes briefly lit up, before narrowing.“Keep your wits about you. Do you still have contact with Zion and the others?”

Christian nodded. “Yeah. They’ve been busy since getting promoted to Lieutenant Commander. Seems like there’s a real shortage of officers in the army right now. But we’ve still got our ways of staying in touch.”

“Good,” Luke said, voice low. “Tell them to monitor any movements within the army. We probably only have a few days before things go sideways.”

Christian raised an eyebrow.“You think they’ll move while the king’s locked away in the inner palace? I thought only eunuchs and maids were allowed in there.”

Luke leaned in.“I think… he might already be dead,” he said, barely above a whisper.

Christian stared, wide-eyed. The shock was obvious, but not a trace of doubt crossed his face. He didn’t question the claim—he accepted it, simply because Luke said it.

“How will I reach you if I learn something?”

“There’s a shack—if you can call it that—just a few minutes from here. Right before the alley intersection. I’ll be staying there for the next few days.”

Christian gave a sharp nod.“Alright. I’ll keep my ears open and only contact you if I’ve got something. Otherwise, I’ll be back here every morning around this time if you need me for anything, boss. Stay safe, yeah?”

With that, he stood and gave a respectful fist-over-palm gesture, careful not to draw too much attention.

But just as he turned to leave, Luke reached out and placed a hand on his shoulder.

“Wait…”

Christian turned back, blinking in surprise.

“I, uh…” Luke hesitated, awkwardly scratching the back of his neck. He couldn’t quite meet the man’s eyes.

Before he could say anything else, a loud growl erupted from his stomach—deep, guttural, and embarrassingly loud in the quiet room. It echoed off the walls like a lion’s roar.

His face flushed with heat as he struggled to preserve what little dignity he had left.

“…I need a loan,” he admitted flatly.

A crooked grin stretched across Christian’s face. For a moment, it looked like he was about to burst out laughing—but instead, he reached into his robe and pulled out a coin pouch, holding it out without a word.

“That’s my bonus,” he said with a chuckle. “The one you promised me. Don’t spend it all in one place.”

Luke took the pouch with a grateful nod. “Thanks.”

Christian spared him any teasing and turned to leave without another word. Luke watched him go, waiting until he left the room before letting out a weary sigh. He slumped back into his seat.

What he’d heard wasn’t entirely unexpected… but it was far from what he had hoped for.

Tristan’s imprisonment would complicate everything. Without him, Luke was down a key asset—one that extended far beyond brute strength.

Tristan may not have been the greatest fighter, but he had a sharp mind and even sharper instincts. If Luke didn’t have the Scholarly Pursuit skill backing him, he was certain that Tristan would outclass him as a strategist.

Executing this plan without him is going to be rough, Luke thought, massaging his temples. There are too many moving parts.

There were the traitors hiding in plain sight. The loyalists scattered and unaware. And now, Ming Hu—an unpredictable variable with his own agenda.

Worst of all, Luke had no idea who belonged to which faction.

That meant—for now—he could only wait.

Wait, and plan with what little he had.

His forces were limited. His intel was threadbare. And his entire war chest consisted of a single coin pouch.

Things were looking grim.

I really need to see Sebastian and Kayson soon, he thought, anxiety twisting in his gut.

The traitors wouldn’t wait forever. In fact, he was certain they were already making preparations. They’d been laying the groundwork for years. All he’d done was force their hand to act sooner.

They had power, resources, and influence.

He had nothing… except Prince Lyon—the last surviving heir to the throne.

But even that wouldn’t be enough.

A low, angry rumble cut through his thoughts.

His stomach again.

He let out a hollow chuckle, running a hand down his face.

“Who the hell’s makin’ that noise!?” a gravelly voice slurred from somewhere nearby.

Luke winced. He turned toward the sound—and froze.

A broad chest filled his vision, above which sat a head of wild, red-braided hair and a face full of freckles.

Youthful. Familiar. Wide-eyed.

“…Brian!?”
