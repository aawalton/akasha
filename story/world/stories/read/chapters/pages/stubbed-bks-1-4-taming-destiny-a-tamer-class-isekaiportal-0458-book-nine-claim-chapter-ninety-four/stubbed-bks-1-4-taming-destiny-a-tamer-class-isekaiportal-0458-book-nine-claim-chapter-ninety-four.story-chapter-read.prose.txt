My fervent hope that I haven’t just doomed Maxen or any of the fighters who volunteered to come here with me makes the Battle with the elk-like creature feel like walking through molasses. The weight that was dragging on my soul earlier has lessened significantly, but I know that it will be back in place as soon as I complete this Bond.

I don’t care, though – all I care about is getting this done and then finding out what’s been happening with the rest of my retinue.

Finally, I‘m out.

It’s done, I tell Elarion shortly, flying up into the air while ordering my newest Bonded to freeze in place, using the Bond to ensure it obeys my instructions. Fortunately, it seems my command overrides the parasite as the mole and bull are still in place and silent.

I was expecting to see the lion-like Tier three, newly freed from the Bond, taking advantage and attacking my retinue, no matter how illogical that might actually be for it considering everything. Therefore, I’m a little surprised to see the lion continuing to work its way through the army of Tier twos, studiously ignoring Maxen and his fighters.

Instead, it seems the crocodilian has managed to work free from the spike of earth I lanced into its guts. Not without consequence – the amount of blood left in a trail behind it indicates that it’s taken a serious wound – but the parasite-controlled beast is hardly slowed down despite that.

Even as I order the elk-beast to freeze and update Elarion quickly, I’m already zooming through the air to join the fight.

On the way, I take in more details of what’s happening and am soon more than a little impressed with how well Maxen and his fighters are doing. I know that part of their training has been figuring out how to combine their Skills so both offensive and defensive Skills complement each other. It seems to be working as a quick glance doesn’t indicate any gaping holes in their ranks – perhaps it hasn’t been enough time for the Tier three to get through their defences.

However, the Tier three’s attack doesn’t stop other parasite-controlled beasts from continuing to press the fighters, and its corrosive breath is clearly difficult to defend against as only magical shields work. As I experienced myself, it’s also a challenge to do any damage to it due to the combination of its thickly-armoured hide and the corrosive, fire-retardant that coats its scales.

This isn’t going to end well. And I can sense that trying to force another Bond with a Tier three will be the straw that breaks the camel’s back. Best-case scenario, I lose one of them. Worst case, I lose all of them. And sustain soul damage into the bargain.

Anyone available to help Maxen, please come, I send out through the Bond network. If anyone can kill the crocodilian…go ahead.

As much as I want to free these Tier threes, give them another chance, I can’t put their wellbeing above that of those I’m sworn to protect.

Thinking quickly through what I might be able to do personally to help, I dive down to land next to the man himself, behind the shields.

He startles, but quickly regains his stoic demeanour when I drop Stealth and Fade.

“I’ll try to drop it into the earth,” I tell him quickly – it’s the only idea that’s occurred to me that might deal with the beast without killing everyone else around. “Keep the other beasts off me while I do, please.”

“Of course, sir,” Maxen agrees. As I knock back my third – and last – mana potion of the battle, I hear him ordering those around me to ‘protect the lord with your lives’. Reassured, I close my eyes and start pouring my regenerated mana into the earth. At least Battle of Wills doesn't take mana and counts as Deep Meditation, so I’ve actually regenerated a fair bit – the potion helps that even more.

Creating a pit would take too much mana, but creating a bog is another question – adding my water-magic into the mix should help with that. And even if the crocodilian can swim, which it probably can, once it’s in up to its neck, I can pull the water-mana out of it and turn it back to dry earth.

Even as I feel the earth starting to soften, the crocodilian’s feet starting to sink in further with every step, sudden shouting grabs my attention.

I open my eyes to see the crocodilian’s jaws wide open, a thick corrosive mist billowing out of it. Cursing, I switch tacks, pulling at the air around us and deflecting the corrosive mist upwards – I would deflect it into the other beasts, but I’m not certain of where my allies are right now.

There’s an odd flash of orange light, a ball shape that follows the line of greenish-yellow mist back to its source.

I realise what’s happened only as the ball disappears into the crocodilian’s mouth.

Aingeal! No! I cry, deadly afraid that the little fire elemental has just sacrificed itself – if the gas were flammable, this battle would have been over far sooner.

I watch the Tier three anxiously, my heart sinking as it claps its jaws shut with an audible snapping noise and reptilian satisfaction.

Then my fear turns to wariness as the crocodilian expression twists. It looks distinctly…surprised.

A moment later, it looks uncomfortable.

And then pained.

At that moment, I notice that its body is beginning to swell up, particularly around the midsection.

“Everyone! Take cover!” I scream, repeating my message through the network and urging all of my Bonded to get into a protected position.

Personally, I tug at my earth-magic in the ground and pull it into a wall between us at the crocodilian, then activate my armour shield – it’s regenerated a few seconds’ worth of protection. Around me, the rest of Maxen’s forces work quickly and methodically to raise thick walls of steel and magic. I’m pushed to the blood-soaked earth as Maxen lies bodily across me.

And then the world explodes.

Bastet, driven by Packleader’s command, had abandoned the battle with the male and had sought cover behind a large boulder on the hill. The male had followed her, biting at her tail and heels, maddened by the thought his quarry was escaping him.

However, when the wave of wind strikes, it is the male who is picked up by it and dashed to the ground, covered in blood, bits of bone, and flecks of greenish liquid.

Bastet does not waste a moment, pouncing on the downed male and setting her teeth around his throat. She does not wish to kill him. He fought well and her instincts say he would make a good mate. Yet she has won and refuses to let him go until he acknowledges that, no matter how much the greenish liquid burns her lips or the flesh between her claws.

However, he continues to fight mindlessly, the creature living inside him driving him to continue to battle even when a self-respecting male would accept defeat and run to live another day.

Need help? Bastet hears. She lifts her chin a little until she can see the packmate who spoke. It is Artio. He, Artemis, and Orion clearly took refuge behind a wall of vines that are only now untangling.

Yes, she responds, sending him a sense of gratitude. Her vine-weaving packmate directs his plants to wrap around the male.

The male continues to fight, but eventually he is so tightly wrapped that he cannot. They both ran out of resources to power their magic even before the explosion, and until he has managed to regain them, he will not be able to use them to free himself.

Sending another pulse of gratitude to Artio, Bastet rubs her burning mouth against one leg, then, finding that only means it starts burning her leg as well, she beats her wings and rises into the air to gain a better perspective and find Packleader.

The reptilian beast is gone, only blood, scraps of hide, and the smoking bones of a most-intact skeleton proving that it ever existed. Beasts have been knocked flat around it, and painted in blood and green even further than that.

Fear briefly making her wings falter, Bastet scans the field for her cubs – she knows Packleader lives, but her cubs are another matter.

There. Coming out of the shadow behind a boulder, Lathani. And next to her, Ninja, though her cub is worrisomely limp and looks like she has been picked up by a giant hand and tossed.

Gliding over to them, Bastet wishes she had more fire available to power her flight.

She lands, barely noticing the large green stone that glints in the sun – a Core from the reptile.

Instead, she rushes over to her cubs, pulling back from the greeting with Lathani before they make contact.

I have burning water on me, she explains when the nunda flicks her ears back with minor hurt. Then, not wasting time, she nudges at Ninja with a burning-water-free nose.

Her wings sag in relief as she realises her cub is still breathing. Bleeding, yes, but breathing. And covered in burning liquid that is already beginning to make her whimper despite being asleep.

Markus! she calls. Ninja needs you.

I’m coming, she hears, just as a shadow falls on them and the maned Enlightened so recently expelled from the pack stares in at them, his lips pulled back from his teeth in a snarl.

Bastet crouches, meeting him snarl for snarl, preparing herself to battle once more. She’s tired, but there’s nothing she will not do in defence of her cubs, grown or not.

She projects her domain, ensuring the other Enlightened understands that fully – that she will fight to the death for her pack.

The Enlightened hesitates, not projecting his own domain the way he would if he intended on challenging her.

The moment hangs in the air. And then Bastet’s sharp ears pick up the sound of Packleader coming to join them.

The other Enlightened obviously hears the same as he looks up, nerves clear in his body language.

Packleader lands, looking at the frozen Enlightened and dismissing him in the same moment. He immediately strides towards Ninja, dropping to his knees next to her and placing his paw on her shoulder.

He’s silent and Bastet sees Ninja’s wounds heal before her eyes. She’s aware of the Enlightened watching, but since he does not make a move to approach, she ignores him – for now.

Finally, Packleader opens his eyes.

“She’s fine and should come back to consciousness in a moment. Your turn.” Bastet steps next to Packleader and is grateful as warmth removes the pain of wounds from her battle with the male, and coolness alleviates the burn of the green water.

“Well,” Packleader reflects as he pulls away from her and moves over to Lathani. “At least it looks like Aingeal pretty much won the battle for us – however reckless that was,” he adds, giving a displeased look at the fire ball bobbing by his head. The ball bobs in a way that looks distinctly abashed. Bastet joins Packleader in glaring at him – it was very reckless. Ninja could have been killed.

The strange Enlightened shifts and their eyes shoot towards him.

Bastet sees the moment he makes a decision. He rises quickly from his crouch, stepping sideways to grab the green Core in his mouth, and then high-tails it away from the battle.

Packleader does not move to pursue, but stares off towards the beast with lines forming between his eyes.

“Did he just steal the Core and run?” Packleader asks, one of the hairy patches above his eyes lifting.
