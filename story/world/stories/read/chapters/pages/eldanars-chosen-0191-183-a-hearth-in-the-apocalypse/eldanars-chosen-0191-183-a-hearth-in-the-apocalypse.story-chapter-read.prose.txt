The air inside the Adventurers' Guild remained thick and oppressive. Josh stood by the heavy structural pillar, listening to the murmurs of exhausted fighters and sobbing clerics. He looked at Brett. The mage was swaying slightly on his feet, his pale hands tucked deep into the folds of his massive fur-lined cloak to conserve heat.

"We need to leave," Josh stated quietly, breaking the heavy silence that had fallen over their group. "If we stay in here much longer, the despair is going to seep directly into our bones. We need to sleep before we completely collapse."

Bhel grunted in agreement, resting his heavy chin on the iron head of one of his twin axes. "Sleep sounds wonderful, lad. But look around. If the guild hall is this packed, the inns will be overflowing. We will be lucky to find a dry patch of mud in an alley."

"We know a place," Brett murmured, rubbing his tired, bloodshot eyes. "Or at least, we did. The Bull's Head Inn. It is where Josh and I usually stay when we are in the city."

"Garrik and Barb run it," Josh explained, turning to Perberos and Carcan. "Garrik is a retired fighter, and Barb is a healer. They have always looked out for us since we arrived here. If anyone can find us a quiet corner to rest in, it will be them."

Perberos gave a short, pragmatic nod. "Lead the way. Any shelter is preferable to standing in this crowd."

They pushed their way back out of the heavy oak doors, leaving the stifling heat and the stench of old blood behind. The night air of Ashenfall was freezing, biting through their layers of clothing and battered armour.

The walk through the city was a sobering experience. Ashenfall was usually a labyrinth of lively taverns, loudly haggling merchants, and singing bards. Tonight, the narrow, winding alleys were choked with the physical reality of the disaster.

Every available inch of space had been claimed. Flimsy lean-tos constructed from broken merchant carts and torn canvas lined the cobblestone streets. Families huddled tightly together around tiny, smokeless fires, sharing meagre scraps of salted meat and stale bread. The gutters were overflowing, and the sharp scent of unwashed bodies mingled heavily with the metallic tang of woodsmoke.

Josh walked at the front of the group, his heavy boots carefully navigating the small spaces between the sleeping refugees. He kept a hand resting casually on the hilt of his longsword. The desperate, hungry eyes that tracked their progress belonged to ordinary people pushed to their absolute limits.

"It is significantly worse here than in the market square," Brett observed quietly, stepping carefully over a puddle of murky water to protect his fine robes. "The logistical strain on the city must be monumental. If the guard does not secure safe supply lines soon, starvation and disease will kill far more people than the monsters."

"The city guard is stretched too thin," Perberos noted, his silver eyes scanning the dark, shifting shadows on the rooftops above them. "I have counted only two patrols since we left the guild hall. The outer walls are probably taking priority."

They turned down a familiar, narrow side street, moving away from the affluent central district. The buildings here were older, constructed from dark, weathered timber and heavy stone foundations.

At the end of the street, sitting squat and familiar against the gloom, was the Bull's Head. The aged, greying timber of its exterior looked exactly as Josh remembered it. The painted wooden sign, depicting a scowling bull with iron horns, swung lazily in the freezing wind.

Normally, the sounds of raucous laughter, clinking tankards, and poorly tuned lutes spilled out of the frosted windows into the street. Tonight, the inn was completely silent.

Josh pushed the heavy wooden door open. The small bell attached to the frame let out a dull, muffled clatter.

The interior of the inn was completely transformed. The spacious common room, usually a haven of joy and drunken revelry, felt like a triage ward. The large hearth fire was burning low to conserve precious wood. The heavy oak tables had been pushed tightly together to create maximum seating.

The room was packed with quiet, sullen locals and a large number of unfamiliar, ragged refugees. People were nursing watery bowls of stew, speaking in hushed, nervous whispers. The sombre atmosphere was incredibly heavy, crushing the usual warmth of the tavern.

The party stepped inside, the door clicking shut behind them. Josh scanned the room. The long wooden bar at the back was entirely unmanned.

"They might be asleep," Brett whispered, shivering slightly. "It is very late."

Before Josh could respond, a small wooden door behind the bar swung open. Branna, the sturdy dwarven barmaid, stepped out carrying a stack of slightly chipped wooden bowls. She looked incredibly tired. Her usually pristine braided hair was unravelling, and dark circles bruised the skin beneath her eyes.

She placed the bowls on the counter and looked up. Her eyes went wide. She dropped a wooden spoon onto the floor with a loud clatter.

"Garrik!" Branna shouted, her loud, brassy voice completely shattering the quiet misery of the room. Several patrons jumped in their seats. "Garrik, get out here now!"

Heavy, thudding footsteps echoed instantly from the back room. The door flew open so hard it rebounded off the stone wall. Garrik Stonebrook came steaming out, his bald head gleaming with sweat, his grizzled beard practically bristling with tension. He held a heavy iron fireplace poker gripped tightly in his massive fist, clearly expecting a brawl.

"Who is causing trouble?" Garrik bellowed, his eyes sweeping the crowded room.

Then, he saw them.

The heavy iron poker clattered uselessly onto the wooden floorboards. Garrik’s weathered face broke into a massive, blinding smile that completely erased his exhaustion. He let out a booming laugh of pure, unfiltered joy.

"By the gods!" Garrik roared, vaulting over the low counter with surprising agility for a man his size. He marched across the room and pulled Josh into a crushing, bear-like embrace, his thick arms wrapping entirely around the heavy steel plates. "You beautiful, foolish bastards! You are alive!"

He released Josh and grabbed Brett, nearly lifting the protesting mage entirely off the floor. "I told Barb! I told her the Kobolds couldn't kill you!"

"Garrik, please," Brett wheezed, his face turning slightly purple. "My ribs are fragile."

"Barb!" Garrik yelled over his shoulder, releasing the mage. "Barb, get out here! The boys are back!"

A moment later, a striking elven woman emerged from the back room, wiping her hands on a heavily stained apron, her rich brown hair falling loosely around her face. Her gentle, empathetic eyes widened as she took in the sight of the battered party. She rushed forward, pulling Josh and Brett into tight, motherly hugs.

"Oh, thank the Light," Barb breathed, stepping back to examine them with a highly critical, professional eye. She noted Josh's skewed, acid-burnt armour and the pale, deeply exhausted complexion of Brett's face. "You look terrible. You both look like you aged five years since we last saw you."

"It has been a very long few weeks, Barb," Josh smiled genuinely, feeling a true sense of warmth for the first time in days. "Allow me to introduce the rest of our party. This is Bhel. And these are Perberos and Carcan."

Garrik offered Bhel a firm handshake, noting the dwarf's heavy twin axes with a retired fighter's appreciation. Barb immediately gravitated towards Carcan, recognising the amber-topped staff and the subtle holy symbols of a fellow healer.

"You look exhausted, child," Barb said softly to Carcan. "I can smell the magical ozone on you. You have been burning your mana continuously."

"It was a difficult road," Carcan admitted quietly, her silver eyes reflecting her deep fatigue.

Garrik clapped his hands together, gesturing towards a small, relatively empty table near the hearth. "Sit! Sit down, all of you. Branna, fetch them some ale. Real ale, from the good keg. We have cause for a small celebration tonight."

The party collapsed into the wooden chairs, the heat from the hearth slowly seeping into their frozen bones.

"We need to hear it," Garrik insisted, pulling up a stool opposite Josh. His expression turned deadly serious. "The whole city is talking about the portals tearing open. They are calling it the Shattering. Everyone says the Kobold town was completely wiped off the map. How did you get out?"

Josh and Brett spent the next hour recounting the nightmare. They told the innkeeper about the sudden surge, the endless tide of scaly bodies, the desperate defence of the town square, and the gruelling, terrifying escort mission back through the native beast territory. They spared no details, describing the huge kobold bosses and massive alpha beast and the acid that had nearly melted Josh's ribs.

As Josh finished describing the final, desperate clash in the pine valley, he reached up and tapped the solid steel helmet resting on the table beside him.

"I need to thank you, Garrik," Josh said sincerely, looking the old fighter directly in the eye. "When you gave me this helm before we left, you said it would keep my head on my shoulders. You were absolutely right. The defensive boost it gave my stats saved my life against more monsters than I can count."

Garrik reached out and tapped the dented steel with a thick finger, a proud smile touching his lips. "A good helmet is worth more than a magic sword, lad. I am glad it served you well."

"Garrik," Brett interjected softly, leaning forward. "We have a rather pressing logistical issue. We need beds. Do you have any rooms available for the five of us?"

Garrik’s joyful expression instantly vanished. He pulled a deep, painful grimace, rubbing his bald head with a heavy hand.

"I am so sorry, boys," Garrik sighed, looking genuinely guilty. "I don't have a single bed left. The inn has been full to the rafters for three days. Your old room on the second floor? I currently have a displaced farming family of seven sleeping in there. They have infants."

Josh nodded slowly, completely understanding. "The guards at the gate told us the city was at breaking point."

"It is getting worse every hour," Barb added quietly, setting five wooden tankards of dark ale onto the table. "Food is getting incredibly tight. The market stalls are quiet because the merchants cannot travel the dangerous roads to bring in fresh supplies. The city granaries are being rationed by the council. People are getting desperate."

"We won't turn you away, though," Garrik insisted firmly. He pointed to the open space in front of the stone hearth. "You can stay in the common room. I will bring out some fresh straw pallets and some heavy woollen blankets. It isn't a feather bed, but it is warm, and the door is locked."

"That is more than enough, Garrik," Josh smiled gratefully. "We are used to sleeping on frozen mud. A floor next to a fire is a luxury right now."

"Hopefully," Garrik muttered, taking a sip from his own tankard, "with capable adventurers like you returning to the city, things might get shaken up. If the guild can organise a proper cull of the beasts clogging the roads, maybe these families can finally go back to their own homes."

The rest of the evening was spent in the warm, flickering glow of the hearth fire. As the other patrons slowly drifted off to sleep in their chairs or trudged upstairs to their crowded rooms, the party shared the final details of their exploits.

"It wasn't all just running and hiding," Bhel grunted, taking a massive gulp of his ale. The dwarf wiped the foam from his intricately braided iron-brown beard. "The fighting was brutal, but the system rewarded us for it. We hit a major milestone after the siege."

Garrik leaned forward, his eyes gleaming with professional curiosity. "A milestone?"

"Level twenty-five," Brett stated, a hint of scholarly pride cutting through his exhaustion. "We finally crossed the threshold during the fighting in the pine forest."

"Tier three," Garrik breathed, his eyes widening with genuine, profound respect. He looked around the table, taking in the battered, bruised, but undeniably powerful adventurers before him. "By the gods. That is no small feat. Most fighters spend years slogging through the levels before they even sniff the third tier."

"It feels significantly different," Josh admitted, flexing his fingers and feeling the latent static electricity humming just beneath his skin. "The physical density of my muscles. The volume of mana sitting in my core. It is an entirely new bracket of power."

"My flames burn twice as hot," Brett added, staring thoughtfully into his tankard. "I do not even need a focus staff anymore. I can channel the raw elemental energy directly through my own body with incredible precision."

"Congratulations are in order," Barb smiled warmly, raising her cup. Branna echoed the sentiment from behind the bar with a tired cheer. "You have earned every single scrap of that power. It takes true grit to survive the Shattering and come out stronger."

"We are going to need every ounce of it," Perberos said quietly from the shadows near the edge of the firelight. "The threats we face tomorrow will not care about our level."

The sombre reminder brought the conversation back down to earth. Garrik eventually fetched the promised straw pallets, laying them out in a neat row before the dying embers of the fire.

As Josh lay down on the makeshift bed, wrapping a thick woollen blanket tightly around his shoulders, he listened to the soft, rhythmic breathing of his friends. The floorboards were incredibly hard, and his chemical burns throbbed with a dull, persistent ache.

But as the heat from the hearth washed over his exhausted body, he finally allowed his eyes to close. They were safe. They were inside the walls, surrounded by people they trusted. For tonight, at least, the horrors of the past week were locked outside the heavy timber doors.
