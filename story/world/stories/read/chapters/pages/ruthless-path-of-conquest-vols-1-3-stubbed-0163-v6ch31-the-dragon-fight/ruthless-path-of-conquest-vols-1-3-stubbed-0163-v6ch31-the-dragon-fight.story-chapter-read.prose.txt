The human and the dragon exchanged another flurry of blows too quick for most of those watching to follow.

The pair had been fighting for ten minutes, and in that time, they had gathered a small group of spectators. Neither combatant paid them any mind. The observers were too distant to affect—or, perhaps more importantly, be affected by—the fight, so they were unimportant.

Precision Gravity Control.

The dragon’s weight suddenly multiplied, rendering him too slow to avoid James’s next punch. He had used Full Body Control earlier to render his fingerbones thicker and to add spikes to his knuckles, emulating knuckle-dusters, after he broke his hand the second time from punching the dragon’s thick hide. Now James’s right fist struck struck hard. He thought he heard a little pop of a bone fracturing, even if it was just a hairline fracture.

He counted it as a victory. It wasn’t a big, dramatic injury, but he had learned not to expect that in this fight. This creature was exceptionally powerful and would have to be whittled down slowly.

James thought he was doing well, until he felt the dragon's tail around his ankle. Then he tumbled to the ground and had to kick off the dragon’s side to get a bit of distance before he righted himself.

Sparring with the young dragon was pushing his instincts and senses to their limits, to the point that he occasionally lost track of one limb, usually the tail.

He got up, just as a claw raked the front of his chest, sending little rivulets of blood flowing for a handful of seconds. Then the wounds closed. In less than a minute, James’s health was as good as new.

That was one thing he had going for him. The dragon’s regeneration was good—James had tested that—but the Fisher Emperor’s was even faster. So far, it was the only advantage he’d found he had besides a significantly greater diversity of Skills. But Skill diversity wasn’t much of an advantage when almost none of his Skills were damaging. The scales were almost fully magic-proof.

By contrast, the dragon’s one signature Skill had proven incredibly effective.

The incredibly high speed of James’s regeneration, coupled with the difficulty of substantially injuring the dragon meant that James and the dragon could keep fighting each other for a long time, perhaps indefinitely.

Zigfried opened his jaws and began gathering energy for his best attack once again.

All James could do was leap away, try to get distance.

He managed to dodge at the last second—that was the best way, he’d found, to avoid landing in the direct path of the flames—but the cone of fire caught one of his toes.

God damn it!

He kicked off the shoe. He’d already lost the other one, so this left him barefoot. If his feet got caught in the next fire attack, he’d have to chop them off, as he had already done with one arm. Losing clothing items wasn’t objectively a big deal. The System was restoring all ruined or lost equipment at the end of the melee, after all. Even losing body parts hadn’t been a big deal for James, because he could regrow lost limbs almost as quickly as he lost them now.

But it was annoying, because it showed that he hadn’t found any way of properly counteracting the fire breath yet. All he could do was run away or limit the damage he took to a specific area.

James frowned. It was one thing to be able to harm the dragon. It was another problem entirely to avoid being burned to a crisp for long enough to actually finish the fight. Already, he knew, the barely broken bone that he had inflicted would have repaired itself.

How the hell do you defeat a dragon’s fire?!

He spun and ducked under another burst of flame, rolled, and kept going until the dragon stopped to inhale. James stopped and took a breath as well, then decided to go ahead and ask the question out loud. He’d been thinking about it for almost the whole fight, but he had nothing.

What the hell? The worst that can happen is he laughs in my face.

“Hey Zigfried, how does a non-dragon usually deal with Conceptual Fire?” he asked.

“They usually burn to death, I’m pretty sure,” Zigfried said. “I figured you could sort of handle it, though.”

“But not always?” James said.

“Umm…”

“What would you do if another, older dragon used it on you?”

“I would try to run away,” the dragon said. “Use physical obstacles to shield myself. Probably die, though.”

“Oh.”

“Yeah, you’ve been doing a great job of dealing with it! You only lost some equipment. Most opponents would be a little pile of ash by now.”

“Great,” James said.

Master, I could help you with this, Roscuro sent, his voice coming across like a whisper in James’s ear.

Roscuro, I told you earlier that I didn’t want to eat his soul, James replied. He’s a… surprisingly nice dragon. He might even help us with the Holy Kings if I really needed him to, at this point. You already said the flames he shoots are not soul-destroying or something, so there’s no real need to treat this like a fight to the death. And I’m not a wanton murderer.

Yes, very well, master, I recall your command, the Soul Eater replied. He sounded disappointed, despite what he said. I meant that I could help you with the fighting. The fact that the flames destroy physical matter but do not attack the soul or intangible objects means that they cannot harm my form. With my most recent enhancements, I can now grow to almost any size you would wish to use and shapeshift very flexibly. I can even split my form into more than one solid object.

I see, James sent. Very well. Sword and shield.

The Soul Eater transformed and then armed its master as if he was an ancient warrior, wielding blade and aegis to fight a mythical beast. The shield was almost James’s own height, so that it could protect him from head to toe, yet it weighed a fraction of what a metal shield of the same size would.

He attacked the dragon, and this time, when Zigfried blasted him with flames, James held Roscuro up to take the blow for him. The Soul Eater was unaffected, though the physical force of the flames bursting forth blew James backward a few steps.

But the Fisher Emperor was able to hold his ground, the shield thrust out in front of him. The fire poured off of it in all directions but backward, which meant James remained safe.

Even the heat somehow failed to transfer through the Soul Eater’s body into James’s hands. Roscuro’s body did not exhibit the properties of metal, or indeed of any solid object known to man before the System.

James simply remained in place until Zigfried had to stop and take a breath again.

With the flames out of the way, James charged in, thrusting with the Soul Eater sword held in the hand opposite the shield. The blade stabbed into Zigfried’s scales and just barely penetrated. James saw the Juvenile Red Dragon wince slightly with the pain.

Then the dragon opened its mouth and loosed another blast of flames.

I would think you would know this isn’t going to work after last time, James thought.

But it did force him to step back and break off the offensive for a few seconds.

Again, he used the shield to protect himself, and once more, the fire attack lasted until the dragon grew short of breath.

When the fire breath paused again, the dragon and James began a new dance, much more dangerous for the dragon this time.

The wound from the Soul Eater had closed during the flamethrower assault, but James resumed what he had just done. He lunged forward, hacked and stabbed at the dragon, then found himself forced onto the back foot once the dragon got its breath back and managed to unleash another burst of fire.

James shielded himself with Roscuro and waited for the attack to be over.

When he attacked again, the dragon’s wounds had healed thanks to the time James was forced to spend hiding from Conceptual Fire.

Rinse and repeat.

There was just one problem, from the dragon’s point of view. Or at least, there was a problem that the dragon gradually became aware of.

It was the tenth or eleventh time that they had repeated the dance.

“What have you done?” the dragon asked as James darted in, not bothering to defend itself from the blade that hacked at its wing.

James could see now that the last several stab and slash wounds he had inflicted on the monster had not quite healed. In fact, the flesh around those wounds was turning slightly black, as well as in a few of the areas James had stabbed where the wounds had closed. He allowed himself a smile.

“My body naturally produces venom that I can use to coat my weapons,” he explained. “It will naturally slow your body’s healing down if it has to cope with multiple wounds all over, and it’s even worse when you’re also fighting off poison.”

“Clever.” Ziegfried nodded in approval.

The dragon lunged across the gap that separated them.

Roscuro bent, but his blade ultimately tore through the wing the Soul Eater had been embedded in. Then the dragon was upon James. Its thick claws reached around the shield, tore at James’s arms and legs, and tried anything to separate one of his limbs from his body. At the same time, he craned his neck over the shield, opened his mouth wide, and began gathering flames again.

Soul-Freezing Gaze!

The dragon froze in place for a moment. At the same time, a massive headache struck James, but without the pain. He felt the tightness and could tell he should be suffering migraine levels of pain, but he had deactivated his capacity to feel it.

So instead of being debilitated, he slipped out from under the dragon, the shield morphing slightly to pull out of the dragon’s grip. James had moved fully to one side of the beast before the flames finally burst forward.

As Zigfried aimed his fire blast directly at James, the Fisher Emperor used Roscuro to shield himself again, and James also gathered Mana for his final move.

It had been a fun fight, and educational about how to defeat someone who was fundamentally slightly stronger than James on a few levels, but this should be the killing stroke.

He waited for the dragon to take his breath and then activated it.

Proficient Silk Fabrication.

It was one of James’s most boring Skills, in theory. It just allowed him to create silk. The key detail was that the crafted silk could come not just from biological material, but even from traces of his own Mana in the air. And James had not used the Skill yet at all.

The dragon froze for a moment, confused at why it was suddenly impossible to move. Silk encased its jaws, keeping them from fully opening, and held its tattered wings in place.

Warp Step!

James shot across the distance that separated him from the dragon, his sword ready, his eyes on Zigfried’s neck.

The blade swung.

“Time out!” the dragon called, speaking through the little gap the silk allowed his jaws to make.

Of course, we’re just sparring, James reminded himself, forcing a smile onto his lips. Killing time. Actually killing each other is entirely unnecessary. I wonder if another Holy King is nearby.

He forced his arm to jerk to a stop, the Soul Eater sword just inches from Zigfried’s neck.

As James stopped, his senses, on hyper alert because of the fight, alerted him to an object coming from behind him. The Fisher Emperor threw himself down and to the side and turned his head just in time to see something strange.

A blurry blade streaked just past, covered in Mana the color of a burning photo negative—Is that Soul Magic?—and stabbed at the place where James had just been.

It struck the dragon instead.
