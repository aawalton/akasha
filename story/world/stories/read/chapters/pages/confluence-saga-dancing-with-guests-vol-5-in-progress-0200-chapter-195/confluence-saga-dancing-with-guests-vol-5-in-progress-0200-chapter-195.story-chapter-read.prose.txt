Dean,

After six months at Scattered Creek I find myself needing to make a report and requesting what I hope is not an overreach.

I am aware that the Knowledge curriculum has been traditionally set prior to the arrival of the students. I have reviewed what my predecessor submitted and compared it to the reports of what was taught during the first year and I am afraid I must bring up several irregularities.

The original plan is identical to what he has taught every other year, and closely aligns to the outlined material going back through the last dozen Seers. The deviations, minor though they appear, could be considered extremely prejudicial and biased. I hesitate to call it antagonistic to all Paths but Seer and Arbiter ones.

Last year however the bias went further. Hereditary Families over newly successful ones. Central Ponds over fringe or feudal. Treating everything as remedial lessons to correct those who were not brought up in the most privileged of states, while clearly pointing, often in the lessons themselves, at a specific team as the reason it was necessary.

Many of the lessons in the standard plan would require treating the majority of the first year as wasted, delaying future lessons even further.

With that in mind I formally request that I be permitted to research each team myself the way they would have been upon enrollment and arrival. In addition to re-organize the classes to account for any abnormalities in background or experience, grouping teams to allow lessons to be provided in the most useful manner.

Sarna glanced up from her work as Leo let their bedroom door shut forcefully.

"Must you?"

"If I didn't you'd spent the night looking over your reports, or your plans, or whatever has your focus tonight."

"You know I wouldn't spend half this time if you bothered to do your own," she said.

"But then you wouldn't have a reason to complain about how I never do my part," Leo countered with a grin. "And the Dean would complain how it isn't done right."

She sighed. He was right, as usual. Last time he'd submitted a report it had been… questionable. There hadn't been anything missing, or incorrectly stated. It had however been almost illegible and filled with tangents. The worst part was she knew that he could provide all the information and make it comprehensible, he'd done so for years, if only in their late night discussions.

So she spent some extra time making sure his reports were properly submitted, that his ideas were stored in a coherent manner and he had decent notes to remind him what he was planning to lecture about. And of course she got to complain about how much time he made her waste doing his work for him.

"Coordinators again?" she asked.

"You know as well as I do who's going. And you know it'll keep me extra busy."

"Couldn't you let one of the guides head out with Mithrax?"

"If that one loses focus and lets loose… Several of them probably could avoid the worst of the situation, but if anything were to happen to them…"

"You'd blame yourself, same as you have in the past," she finished for him. "Don't bother telling me Mithrax is different. I know they are."

"What do you think the odds your darlings pull it off?"

"I'm not entirely sure why they didn't the first time around. And you know they spend less time dueling after the first year until they hit level 18. Lord Percivus has invited me to watch some of their training, even asked for my thoughts."

"And?" Leo said as he pressed his palms against her back, his fingers curling around her arms to guide her into stretching.

"A bit more," she said as she arched her spine.

She felt the series of small pops that she knew he'd be able to hear as her spine relaxed, then the sharper crack as her shoulders reset themselves. There were benefits to pushing yourself to the extreme along your Path and Affinity. She could stretch and contort in ways that had left more than one opponent at her mercy while thinking they had the stronger position. On the other hand without regular exertion, or at least careful assistance, she could find herself hobbling around stiffly.

"They aren't the same group as they were six months ago, or even three. Every pairing coordinates as though they'd worked together for years. They haven't had another dueling rotation so they haven't had a proper challenge as a full team."

She gasped as he ran his knuckles down her spine, a second series of pops following soon after. She stood, twisting at her hips so she was nearly facing him before whipping her leg around for him to catch, her hips releasing their tension as she completed the rotation.

"Thank you," she said.

"You did my reports," he grunted as he stepped away from her. "But maybe find someone to spar with, you aren't normally so locked in."

"Who? None of the other instructors will, not to the point that would help."

"Why not Lord Percivus? From what he admitted about his past he can't be weak."

"I suppose I can ask," she said. "He might enjoy the challenge."

"Yes, Bern," Morgan said, suppressing a sigh. "The plan makes sense."

This was the second time they'd gone over the 'plan' as Bern called it since they'd entered the Depths. He didn't want to think about how many times, eleven? twelve? no, eleven, in the two days since Gwen had formally let her parents know of her decision.

Technically since she'd let Bern know, because despite their being at the same elite academy and having several shared lessons, she had managed to avoid any and all interactions with her twin in the six months since she'd shared those ritualistic words. Morgan had said nothing to Bern at the time, or any time since. If he had the day off Bern would have raged, and every day from that point onward would simply make the situation worse.

Of course Bern assumed she'd told Lord Percivus, an assumption Morgan had no intention of correcting. History and precedent were on his side, he was not involved in the statement, he was simply the intermediary for the bloodline to recognize her words. Had she spoken to him in public it might be seen differently, but she had not.

That wasn't to say Bern would see it that way, that he'd care about precedent. His mother certainly hadn't. She'd been outraged that it had happened at all, especially when Cara had ignored her outright. Their father on the other hand had taken the information calmly, at least as far as he could tell.

Morgan was fairly certain he'd spoken more with Lord Bern on this visit than he had in all the years since he'd first been introduced combined. Which was not to say they'd had much to say to one another, merely the appropriate pleasantries about his time at the academy, about the Lord's latest projects and interests, and the mention that they were to be visiting his branch of the family for someone or other's child fully Awakening.

Lord Bern couldn't remember the names of the parents, nor of the child. They were to stay at the main Holding of course, Lady Phila was even going to get to tour the gardens as a Guest. A sign of things to come, at least so his wife had claimed. Now of course that was likely thrown into disarray, or would be if anyone heard of the humiliation before hand.

"We push for five tomorrow," Bern said, again. "Six the next, within a week we'll be at seven and they'll be forced to-"

"Recognize that you and Keldren are pre-eminent warriors capable of feats that no other team can match. That the rankings were incorrect from the start. That you were held back by your twin."

"Excuse me?"

"Bern," Morgan said. "You have repeated this multiple times, you have explained how Keldren will handle the front and you will deal with all the gathered foes while I ensure none are overlooked."

"Exactly-"

"That Keldren is strong enough to withstand any stray effects of your techniques since he will never be anywhere but at the periphery." He continued dryly. "That the goblins are too weak individually to stand against anything you throw at them."

"And if any stragglers should find their way too close I will be there to deal with them," he finished when Bern opened his mouth. "Did I miss anything?"

"That we will impress our guide sufficiently that she will finally explain how they determine which chambers we face."

Morgan had ideas as to how they made that decision. They alternated tunnels, switched how far they proceeded on any given day before beginning, but once the first set of opponents was faced the progression seemed almost predictable. He'd guessed correctly a handful of times, been off by only one or two entrances several others. Not that he'd ever discussed what he'd noticed.

"Assuming she acknowledges you beyond pointing out our next destination."

"She will when we prove we can clear the seventh cavern with time to spare."

Morgan had his doubts, their escort had never shown the slightest sign of wishing to interact with Bern, and only minimal interest when anyone else had spoken to her. But Bern was determined.
