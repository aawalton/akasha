Locked away within a private club booth sat two men, an awkward silence filling the air between them. They each slowly sipped on drinks of their choice, the uneasy feeling growing worse every time glass met the table surface, the clinks punctuating every unsaid word.

Freddy simply waited there, still a bit unused to his hulking form. It was a comfortable thing, having his muscle back, but he really didn’t miss taking up so much space. He glanced over at Vladimir.

The man had changed. He’d kept changing every time they’d see each other. In small ways, from how he held himself, to work on his physique, to the many new scars he was collecting by exposing himself to the dangers of a delver lifestyle.

Usually, these changes were so small Freddy had to take a minute to even notice them. But it had been a while since the last time they’d seen each other, and since then, much had changed.

Vlad had collected a couple of particularly unpleasant scars. One along the side of his head, a bit of another showing under his shirt, just beside his neck, and a third—

There was a long, pale line across Vladimir’s entire forearm, starting from the elbow and moving up. Something had slashed him across the arm, and while most of the wound seemed to be pretty clean, the way it ended was anything but. Most of the ring finger on Vladimir’s left hand was a sickly shade paler than the skin around it, a tell-tale sign of having been regrown.

Deciding it had been quiet enough, Freddy gave a brief nod in the direction of the wound. “Sorry about your finger. That looks like it was pretty bad.”

“Yeah…” Vladimir raised it to the light, snorting. “Made a pretty big mistake on a risky delve. Well, it wasn’t like I didn’t expect this kind of stuff to happen. Doubt it’s the last big wound I take.” He clenched his fist.

Freddy couldn’t help but wince in sympathy. “That really sucks, dude.”

Vlad laughed. “It really doesn’t.” With a shit-eating grin, he moved the finger closer for Freddy to see. “This is some of the best healing money can buy in Starhold. It’s the same shape, I haven’t lost any strength, and it’s only a touch more numb than a regular finger. You’re a bit spoiled, so this might look scary to you, but there are plenty of others you should direct your sympathy at, instead.”

“Like the people who had their organs harvested under your watch in Repentawa?” Freddy jabbed.

Vladimir flinched, pulling his arm back. He snorted. “Is that what this is about? Why you haven’t been talking to me? Dude…”

Freddy shrugged, feeling a bit awkward about the way he brought it up. “Yeah… A small thing, really. What’s a couple thousand lives ruined, anyway?”

“Why are you suddenly bringing this up now? Hasn’t bothered you before.”

“That kind of stuff happens when your soul is too shredded to feel things, Vlad.”

“Ah… Congratulations on your recovery, by the way.” He carefully poked Freddy’s massive arm. “Don’t you think this is a bit much, though?”

“Don’t change the subject.”

Vladimir sighed. “What do you want me to say? But really? That I was forced to do it by the empire? That after my soul damage, I’m a changed man, who is now far more merciful to my lessers?”

“Let me be clear with you, Vlad. The only reason you’re ever gonna see me again is because I watched you put your life on the line to try and stop Nahar. The empire would have punished you if you hadn’t, but that couldn’t have been worse than what happened to you, anyway. I’m willing to believe you. I just need you to tell me why.”

Vladimir maintained eye contact for several long seconds, before suddenly turning away, his form visibly deflating. In these moments, it became so hard to see the man for the ancient fart he really was, and not a beaten-up twenty-something. “I’m not exactly at liberty to disclose some details.”

“Convenient.”

“I’m not saying I won’t tell you. But I’ll need you to keep your mouth shut. I mean it.”

“When have I ever spilled a secret?”

“I mean it, Freddy.”

Freddy snorted before leaning back into his seat, miming zipping his mouth shut.

Vladimir took several sips of drinks, all while clearly hesitating to speak. Just as the silence was about to stretch too long, he opened his mouth. “Being a four-star in the empire isn’t all it’s cracked up to be. In theory, the higher you go, the more power you have.” He scoffed. “But when you get high enough, you will inevitably be reminded who that power really belongs to.”

The silence returned for another couple breaths. Vladimir shook his head. “Those who reach the fourth star aren’t allowed to exist in peace. Every four-star must take the Lord Trial. Fail, and your options become miserable. You can take as many Lord Trials as you can survive, but… Trust me when I say it’s better to just eat the loss when that happens.

“The empire offers a relatively safe way to bring yourself back to the third star, but nobody sane would take that option. The others aren’t much better, though. If you’re proficient in a non-combat field, you can enter direct employ with the empress, but otherwise, the main choices are either between becoming some shade of super soldier or an adjudicator. I picked the latter.” His head dropped, a deep scowl settling between his brows. “That was one of the worst decisions I’ve ever made in my entire life.”

The quiet, that pesky thing, returned yet again, staying far past its welcome this time. After a long while, the former adjudicator took a deep breath, then downed an entire glass of whiskey. “Being an adjudicator sucks. On paper, it’s fantastic. Excellent money, lots of opportunities for growth. It’s a safe option if you want to take your time to prepare yourself to take another trial.

“In theory, you uphold the sanctity of law within whatever territory you’re assigned to.” His gaze darkened. “In reality, it’s a job. You work for whoever you’re put under, and often, it’s some dirtbag Lord who will use you as an instrument of cruelty upon whoever is unlucky enough to be living under them.

“My first assigned territory was absolutely amazing. I just didn’t know it then. It was a poor, relatively fresh area, which meant that I didn’t get all that much access to resources. Besides, it was a boring backwater shithole. I was spoiled back then, admittedly. I come from a rather big family, who… Well, they haven’t been around for nearly half a century at this point, but still. I was a proud scion, and said pride had recently been fatally wounded by my failure to complete my trial.

“I grew impatient. It felt like it would take decades to grow enough to be able to handle a trial. I couldn’t wait that long! No, I was in a hurry! The clock was ticking, and I’d been made a mockery among my family members. So I wanted to move to a different territory, somewhere where I could make progress in a more reasonable time.” He laughed darkly. “That was eighty years ago.

“I was assigned under a fresh Lordling, the son of an Overlord, who had just cleared their trial.” He spat on the ground. “Drunk on power, that son of a bitch abused his status at every turn. I had no idea what I was walking into. When I read the missive detailing a new policy that was just a roundabout way to force young women into the Lord’s personal harem, mere days after getting there, I was livid.

“You see, Adjudicators, in theory, have a certain degree of power over the law they supervise. This, theoretically, includes a veto power for laws that heavily clash with the moral tastes of the adjudicator in question. So, naturally, I used my given right to file an official complaint to the Lord’s office, who then forwarded it to the appropriate authorities, which stepped in and corrected the situation.”

“Really?” Freddy asked.

“Fuck no. He beat me within an inch of my life, breaking damn-near every bone in my body. After that, I just narrowly avoided getting executed for insubordination, and that was because his Overlord father stepped in and informed him that getting new adjudicators becomes a little difficult if you have one executed. And that was it, really. He was given a very mild slap on the wrist, while I was transferred to a new territory with life-altering injuries.

“I had already been a very mediocre four-star on ascension. After that beating, I was weak even by adjudicator standards. Let alone a Lord Trial, I could barely beat the stronger among the criminals I was forced to deal with. And the laws I was forced to uphold didn’t get any better. I had considered swapping to the military, but at that point, I was too weak to handle that, so I gritted my teeth and stuck it out, hoping to one day land a territory that wasn’t utterly miserable.

“The last bit of hope I had was crushed when I landed a frontline territory.” He paused, the look in his eye growing distant. “If you think the empire is cruel to its own citizens, you should see how it treats those in freshly conquered territories. I simply couldn’t bring myself to enforce half the shit my Lord told me to, and eventually, he reported me for my ‘terrible work ethic.’

“That was how I landed my job in the Northern Belt. As a penalty for having been horrible at the last one.” He went silent again. Freddy thought of saying something, but nothing productive came to mind. So he waited. Minutes later, the adjudicator ran a hand through his hair and sighed. “I wish I could say that I was some innocent victim who got swept up, but you’re not an idiot, so I’ll spare you the bullshit.

“The whole time, I had the choice of severing back at the third star, or even lower, and trying again. I was disadvantaged, but it was nothing that couldn’t be overcome with time and effort. In the end, if I had made different choices, I never would have been put in a position to do any of this stuff. But I won’t apologize.” He turned, meeting Freddy’s gaze. Then, with a puff of air, he extracted a small stack of papers and handed it to him.

“What’s this?”

“Even if I hadn’t been perfect, I wasn’t going out of my way to bring harm to anyone. The empire, by design, heavily limits legal interference in the Northern Belt, which makes it rather hard to moderate criminal activity.” He pointed at the papers. “Just take a look.”

Taking the papers, Freddy listed through them, skimming their contents.

It was a huge pile of legal contracts made between the adjudicator and the many businesses of the general territory he was assigned to. At first, they seemed like random agreements, many of which were for the organ trade. Freddy’s expression darkened. He’d read many similar contracts. They made the process seem quite a bit cleaner than it really was. The reality, as Freddy had seen first-hand, was a lot more gruesome than any legal document could possibly describe.

However, as he kept moving through the contracts, a pattern emerged. For starters, the deals were pretty awful—for both parties. The going rates were terrible, and there were countless ways the process was made infinitely more complicated than it needed to be, drastically slowing down the rate at which organs could be collected and sold.

There were other rather suspicious clauses as well.

For starters, these operations could only be run in relatively abandoned areas of the city, and only inside the city. Then, they were also forbidden from taking many measures that would have helped them obscure the locations. In fact, some clauses basically made it impossible for the companies in question to hide the locations from anyone who was curious enough to take a look.

On top of that, there were specific agreements in the case of “vigilante attacks and other acts of God,” which pretty much declared that, should the sites in question be attacked, they were on their own, with the adjudicator taking no responsibility for taking care of the attackers.

More details piled up. Small ways that made the sites more vulnerable, slowed the process down, and made the trade just barely lucrative enough to even really bother with in the first place.

It all slowly slid into place.

Repentawa had been a relatively pleasant place by Northern Belt standards. It was the very reason Freddy had decided to move there in the first place. But all this time, he had thought it was Valhalla that had built that city into what it was.

And he had been wrong.

Freddy returned the papers, a torn expression on his face.

Vladimir accepted them back, making them vanish with a pop. He turned away, looking at some random drink on the table, a breath caught in his lungs, waiting to be turned into the right words. “Look, I… I’ve met a lot of people in power over the years. It’s tempting to assume that everyone is a monster—and some people definitely are—but not everyone is. But…” He released a tired breath. “The system is designed to break you.

“It is designed to sap every bit of patience, kindness, and warmth out of everyone stuck under it, until you’re nothing but a bitter, hollow husk of a human being. Unless you’re a Lord—and oftentimes, even then—you don’t get a real say in how things work. You have some serious power, Freddy, so I don’t know if you’ll ever have to learn these same lessons. I hope you don’t. I hope you, at least, get to keep that innocence of yours until the very end. Until then, I…” He got up. “I think it would be best to give you some time.”

Vladimir gave Freddy a strained smile. “Hope to see you around.” And with that, he walked out.

Freddy was left there, the music failing to overpower the deafening silence left behind. He picked up a drink, and downed it, throwing the glass at the wall. Slowly, he got up, and started walking. At first, he had no real destination, but before long, the titanic construct of the Starhold Arena found itself in his path. He booked a VVIP lounge, and spectated.

For hours, he watched people get broken for the entertainment of others, everyone involved happily playing along. At times, a particularly nasty person found themselves in the spotlight, and the crowds booed. Not because they disliked the violence, no, but because of the ever-growing risk that they would find themselves on the receiving end of it, out on the street, in yet another gladiator suicide attack.

He gritted his teeth, a simmering anger suffusing every cell in his entire body. And then, he got up. He moved through his contacts, commissioning a peculiar kind of armor, and then, he visited an office where he could commission a custom-built map, one for his first and last non-anonymous match.

After waiting for about a day, he set the terms of the match. The anonymous category was very straightforward, but for the custom matches, there were countless settings, which took him a good bit to figure out.

He slowly set the terms of the match.

There were 10 slots. He’d pay 10 million dollars to anyone who joined a slot, with an option to bid for a lower pay. The theme? A party of ten heroes gathers to kill a great evil hiding within an old, abandoned fortress.

Or, well, they gather to try.
