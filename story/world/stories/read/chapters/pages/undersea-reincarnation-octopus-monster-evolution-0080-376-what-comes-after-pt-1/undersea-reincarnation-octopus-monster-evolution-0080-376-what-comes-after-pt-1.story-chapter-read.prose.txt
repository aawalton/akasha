Pulling Attal out of the crumbling basement was manageable. It was almost mechanical. The man knew the building was about to come down on top of their heads, and Henry’s well-known teleporting capabilities made the solution obvious. So getting him to comply and let Henry whisk him away was easy enough.

That was much easier of a task to wrap his mind around than trying to understand what had just taken place. The rift. The creature against which even the System seemed to struggle.

Velistraine disappearing in the great beyond as the rift was sewed shut behind her.

Henry reflexively activated his teleportation. One moment, blocks of cement and stone were dropping around them, and the next they were a few feet above the choppy waters, a hundred or so yards away from the light show that was the clone’s continuous bombardment of the parasite while the latter continued tracking Henry and Maurice’s real bodies.

For a moment, Attal just watched vacantly, still breathing hard from what had just taken place, but quickly enough, his gaze sharpened, settling on the small hovering creature encased in its defensive bubble. The change of scenery and focus didn’t do much to either their mental wellbeing. The image of Velistraine jumping through the rift to wrestle the creature away was still on the forefront of Henry’s thoughts, and he’d bet Attal felt the same. But there was still a real, serious battle going on around them, so after a couple more seconds, Attal finally managed to speak.

“It’ll try to run before it dies,” the man spoke softly, reaching into his pockets before retrieving a small vial that he downed. There was a quick grimace, then Attal spoke up once more, a little more fire in his voice. “Keep it busy and I’ll try to gut it in one shot,” the A-rank said before he tapped his belt. Something activated, and the man faded away, dissipating as if he had been made of smoke.

From a short distance away, Henry watched as the figure faded away, leaving two idle clones Henry then converted to artillery.

“[Henry? What happened?]”

Maurice was still doing his best to fend off the parasite’s attempt at latching on to them, and with both of them on it, it was manageable, though Henry couldn’t keep going for long. He was finding himself need to dip into his conceptual essences too frequently to shove the attempts away. As for Maurice’s question… Henry didn’t really have the words to explain. He was still a little too scatterbrained and before he realized it, he found himself looking inwardly toward the Octomind.

It had lost its physical manifestation, but the intelligence was back with him, dormant, and right as the large shadow fell on him, the frustration and disappointment ignited.

“Goddammit,” he roared, whipping an empowered, bladed arm up and cutting half-way through the starfish limb that tried to intercept them. Even though it was nearly twice as wide as he was, his massive arm bit through it just fine. Blue blood sprayed out like a geyser as the impact shoved the limb away from him, coating him as he flew past it, but before the anger could take hold, he took a deep breath and focused on the situation at hand.

The parasite was still after him. He needed to distract it for—

For what?

Henry blinked, suddenly alarmed at the hole in his memory as he reviewed the last few moments, only to suddenly recall Attal and his disappearance.

Well, that’s not concerning at all. Impressive. But still fucking concerning, he thought, realizing whatever Attal had done to erase his presence had also pulled the man’s memories out of Henry’s mind. Or at least made them hard to access.

In any case, Henry needed to calm down. The ups-and-downs were too distracting, and now was not the time for distractions.

“Sorry, Maurice,” Henry said, swinging back around to finish the job and fully sever the massive arm while the parasite continued hounding them. “I already told you a little what was happening underground, but… Velistraine’s gone. She jumped into the rift to shove the creature back so that the rift could be closed. And the Octomind I sent with her got shunted back.”

The arm of the starfish was trying to go back underwater, but Henry wasn’t letting it. As for the parasite, it got blasted by three-hundred simultaneous Toxic Rays which Henry made sure to narrow as much as possible, and he was happy to see the little horror get blasted away while he painted the water with the starfish’s blood.

“Oh… that’s not great,” Maurice managed after a few moments, lending a few imploding bubble to finish the job and tear whatever tendons were left after Henry’s butchering. “Did you send it alone?”

“No…” Henry said, inhaling the bloodied water through his beak. The interface lit up, but he ignored it for now as he tried to sense the clones he’d sent with the Octomind. He was very much aware his clones should have survived the trip. So long as the skill’s description was still accurate. But he had no clue how functional they would be. Or how long they’ll manage to survive out there. Worst case scenario, they were catatonic and/or breaking down, rendering them completely useless to Velistraine. But best case scenario?

A little ember of hope lit up, but Henry didn’t want to give it too much weight just yet. “I sent some clones with her. Once we deal with this thing, I’ll run a couple of tests. See how well the clones take to orders when they’re out of my reach. Never had to know this… but now we do.”

Henry hoped they could still maintain some semblance of autonomy. The Shadow clones had never been as autonomous or smart as the Octominds, but he didn’t need them to be. He just needed them to obey simple orders. And they should be capable of that.

Also, what would happen if an unreachable clone stored something in its Maw? He had to find that out as well. And soon.

Back in the air, Henry glared at the parasite. Its timing had been awful, but it’s not like he could have contributed much against the massive, bipedal mantis-like thing.

“A lot of people would have been hurt if she hadn’t, right?” Maurice asked, peeking up from the massive arm that Henry had wrapped around him. Maurice was still in human form, but Henry had gone full-kraken. He wasn’t as large as the starfish, but suffice it to say, Maurice looked tiny in contrast with him. In fact, he was probably visible from the shore by now. Especially with the glowing, gold dots decorating his inky skin. Which, at the end of the day… didn’t really matter.

“Right,” Henry muttered, turning away from the shore and focusing on the parasite who’d somehow gone still, and as Henry tried to understand why, his mind whirred. Who knew what would have happened if that monstrous insect had managed to slip through. As far as they knew, it might have even had the capability of turning that rift permanent, and through it, the swarm of whatever insectile thing it was would pour through.

This whole world would have been done for.

“I suppose this might be what the System has been trying to prevent for a while,” Henry said, taking a moment to consider the Toxic Rays. Considering how many times he’d been casting the skill, he could already think of a couple of ways he could tweak it. But his heart wasn’t in it right now. As for why the parasite had gone still, he believed he knew the reason. And it helped that Attal spoke into his mind.

“[I’m ready, try to immobilize it.]”

Far above them, a dangerous amount of mana had been coalescing for a bit. Enough of it that Henry could feel it on his skin, and looking up with his mana vision, it was like he was staring up at a second angry sun. Which was why the parasite suddenly dropped its pursuits and plunged down to the water, to its body-guarding starfish most likely. Or it tried to.

From every Trickster’s Shadow clone, the force applied by the B-rank Telekinesis on the powerful, mid-A-rank parasite was like a moderate, finger-tip pressure. By channeling it through Rupture Casting, it was like firm shove with that same finger. Not enough to do much by its own. But applied by more than three hundred-clones simultaneously?

It stopped the disgusting thing. The backlash was tremendous, and Henry was seeing stars. But it gave them a second. Then another second was bought by the seven Octominds using every bit of Thaumaturgy and Predation left, combining it with Rupture Casting to greatly empower their telekinetic grip. The air around the parasite rippled with vibrant purple and blue lights, as if the air itself had ignited with mystical fire, and though he felt rage emanating out of it, the parasite couldn’t move.

Then the world went dark and silent as a single, pin-point beam of blinding white-orange light flew down from above and through the parasite, annihilating all color from their surroundings, sizzling away Henry’s Telekinetic grip like a drop on a hot stove.

The deathly quiet lasted for only half a second. But it felt longer. As for the backlash from holding the parasite and from having his skill disintegrated, well… Henry had felt worse. It was hard to see or think, but he has had worse, and he was pretty sure he was bleeding out of his eyes and lungs. But he’d walk it off.

Still, as the notification came though, Henry could only think of one thing.

How should he tell her crew and Zerathstra?
