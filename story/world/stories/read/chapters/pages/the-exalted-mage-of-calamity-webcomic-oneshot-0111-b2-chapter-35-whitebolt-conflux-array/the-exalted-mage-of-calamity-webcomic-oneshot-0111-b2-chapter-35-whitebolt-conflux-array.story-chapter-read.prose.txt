Veronica looked down at the small golden lump of resin.

It was dense. The badge inside was difficult to make out, buried beneath layers of amber light and darker veins. Even when she pressed two fingers against it, the resin barely gave.

Several scholars and archive visitors watched from a cautious distance. The roped-off appraisal area had fallen quiet enough that the faint hum of the table's enchantments stood out beneath the murmurs. The commotion had already spread through the archives, drawing more curious onlookers eager to see what gave this young mage the confidence to challenge the scholars.

“Care to explain what’s happening now, Veronica?” Gwen asked from beside her.

She stood a short distance from the refinement table with her arms loosely crossed, her gaze shifting between the resin and the watching mages. Nasha stood a little farther back, looking more amused than concerned.

“Why’d you get involved?” Gwen continued. Then she leaned in slightly and lowered her voice. “Also… why were you being so rude?”

“I wasn’t being rude,” Veronica replied plainly, making no effort to lower her own voice.

Gwen stared at her.

Veronica tapped the edge of the table once, studying the runes carved into the pale stone.

“That is simply how nobles speak and conduct themselves. It may seem rude to you, but words are their own form of battle. Among the nobility, the way you speak can carry as much weight as a sword drawn in public.”

Gwen glanced toward Lord Vaun, who stood several paces away with his hands clasped behind his back.

“I had to get his attention,” Veronica continued. “If I approached him like some random third-tier mage asking for a favor, he would have ignored me. But if I carried myself as someone accustomed to negotiating with people of standing, then he was forced to reassess me.”

“So you were being noble-rude.”

“I suppose that is one way to describe it.” Veronica looked back at the resin. “As for why I involved myself, that part is simple. We need access to the upper floors. Lord Vaun has the authority to bring us there. He needs this badge retrieved. If I solve his problem, he solves ours.”

“Oh?” Nasha said, walking forward until she was close enough to lean into Veronica's view. A smirk tugged at the corner of her lips. “And how exactly do you plan to solve it? That man sounded quite confident. A seventh-tier alchemist, wasn't he?” Her eyes gleamed with curiosity. “Do you have a surprise to show us?”

Veronica closed her eyes for a moment and let out a quiet chuckle.

“I’m not an alchemist. I don't possess an alchemical core, nor the comprehension required to produce true balefire. I do, however, have an alternative method. As long as they were telling the truth about balefire being able to retrieve this safely, then I'm confident it will work.”

“Alright, clear the area. I’m placing the last stone,” the female scholar said.

Veronica looked toward Gwen and Nasha.

They both nodded and stepped back.

The three scholars made their final adjustments to the barrier stones surrounding the refinement area. Small crystal anchors had been placed several feet apart, each one glowing with pale blue light. Thin strands of mana stretched between them, weaving together into a transparent dome that enclosed everything within a two-meter radius.

Only Veronica, the resin, and the refinement table remained inside the barrier.

“This barrier is powered by condensed, high-quality mana stones,” Harris said, his voice dripping with disdain. “Enough to withstand an attack from a seventh-tier ruin mage, as requested. You had better keep your promise.”

Veronica turned to face him. “I already outlined the terms before Lord Vaun. He will serve as our witness. If I retrieve the badge without damaging it, you will kneel and apologize.”

Harris scoffed. “Fine. But if you fail, you will pay for the mana stones, along with twenty thousand vix for wasting both Lord Vaun’s time and the archives’ time. Sworn upon Viscount Leopold’s house.”

“Fine with me.”

Veronica stood in front of the table and closed her eyes.

Of course, no matter how convincingly Veronica spoke like a noble, she wasn't one. So she borrowed the weight of House Ronswick instead. It was a risk, but not one she intended to lose. She never willingly entered a losing battle.

“Any day now,” Harris drawled.

Veronica ignored him and began. “Light to magic, their bonds come true,” she chanted.

She held both hands out in front of her, palms facing one another in a cupping motion, as though cradling an invisible sphere. Two spell circles materialized before her hands, perfectly straight and parallel.

Gwen and Nasha’s eyes widened.

Veronica was chanting before casting a spell.

The mage who could compress higher-tier spells into lower-tier ones had done so several times in front of them already. Whatever she was preparing now, it had to be something just as powerful.

Others nearby noticed the chant as well. They simply assumed she was struggling to cast a particularly potent third-tier spell. None of them knew who she truly was. But they were about to.

Something strange began to happen as her spell circles glowed. The murmurs surrounding the refinement area faded into silence.

Gwen glanced around. The crowd had suddenly become much more attentive. She moved to get a better look. Her eyes followed their stares before landing directly on Veronica’s spell circles.

“The magic circle…” someone whispered.

“It’s… bending,” another finished.

Both spell circles before Veronica’s hands began to warp. Or rather, they curved inward, folding into three-dimensional hemispheres. Their light grew brighter as more mana poured into them until, at last, the two halves fused into a perfect sphere.

Veronica’s eyes began to glow as her mana rings spun faster. The spell before her had already consumed half her mana. She only needed to activate it.

With a pulse, the barrier flashed. For an instant, the entire archive was swallowed by blinding white light.

“Argh!” Harris yelped, throwing an arm over his eyes. “What the hell is she doing?!”

He wasn’t the only one. Lord Vaun, Nasha, Gwen, and nearly everyone watching instinctively shielded their eyes from the sudden glare. Gasps rippled through the crowd, followed by the scrape of shoes as several people stumbled backward.

Only after several seconds did the light begin to fade.

The crowd slowly looked back toward the barrier.

Veronica still stood in the same position, her hands cupped before her. The spell circles had vanished. In their place floated a brilliant sphere of crackling light. Several people stared at it. It looked… almost like…

A compressed lightning bolt.

No larger than an apple, the sphere twisted violently against the force holding it together. Thin arcs of lightning snapped across its surface, striking the inner wall of the barrier with sharp cracks.

Each impact made the dome tremble.

Harris’s expression stiffened.

That barrier had been reinforced with high-quality mana stones. Even sixth-tier spells should have been absorbed. And yet it was shaking all the same.

Veronica paid the scholars no attention.

She stepped toward the corner of the refinement table, carrying the compressed lightning bolt between her hands. The air around her fingers shimmered beneath the strain of keeping it contained. Carefully, she lowered the sphere until it hovered just above one corner of the table.

The refinement table groaned. A thin circle flared beneath it, locking the lightning bolt in place.

Several people in the audience instinctively took another step back.

Veronica returned to the center of the table. She glanced once at the contained lightning, confirmed that it was stable, then raised her hands again.

Outside the barrier, Harris watched. His eyes narrowed as he tried to understand.

“She’s making another…?”

The first spell had not behaved like a third-tier spell. Its visible structure had seemed simple enough at first glance: two circles and a chant. But the pressure it released was completely wrong. Even sealed behind the barrier, its activation had caused people to feel a slight pressure.

A third-tier mage shouldn’t have been capable of that. Not unless she possessed an artifact powerful enough to amplify her magic. Or unless the spell itself had been dangerously unstable.

No…

That wasn’t right either. The barrier had been built to withstand a direct seventh-tier spell. So why did a single compressed lightning bolt place this much strain on it?

Harris’s fingers tightened inside his sleeves. “Marcen,” he said without taking his eyes off Veronica. “Give me your formula lens.”

The shorter mage blinked. “Senior?”

“Now.”

Marcen hurriedly reached into the inner pocket of his robe and withdrew a thin crystal lens set within a bronze frame. Unlike the Core Appraisal Lens from earlier, this one was broader and flatter, with a faint grid of silver markings etched around its rim.

A spell formula lens.

It could not fully interpret every path of magic. No artifact could, unless it had been created for a specific discipline. But it could reveal a spell’s shape, density, and layered structure as it formed.

It was a tool for study. With it, mages could analyze their teachers’ spells, examine the equations that shaped them, and observe the variables woven into their construction.

Harris snatched it from Marcen’s hand and raised it to his eye.

“Let me see what you’re up to,” he muttered.

Inside the barrier, Veronica began chanting again. “Light to magic, their bonds come true.”

At first, he expected what any third-tier spell formula should have shown. A compact structure. Perhaps a spell equation half a meter wide. A few interlocking circles. Chaotic and messy formulas, all built for power rather than stability or control, typical of ruin mages.

A focus on power and destruction, with no finesse or precision.

Instead, the moment Veronica’s spell circles began to bend, the world through the lens exploded into light.

Harris froze.

The formula was not one or two feet wide.

It was everywhere.

Layers of symbols expanded around Veronica in all directions, unfolding like a vast board of luminous script. Lines curved above her shoulders and stretched past them, since the spell itself was perpendicular to her body. They spread beneath the table, along the floor, and up against the inner wall of the barrier.

Dozens of rotating rings overlapped one another, each filled with marks he could barely follow. Of course, since he was not a ruin mage, he couldn’t understand the variables in any meaningful way. But even from a cursory glance, he could tell that the equation she was using was not sloppy or messy.

Far from it.

“Senior?” Marcen whispered, noticing his astonishment.

Harris didn’t hear him. The second pair of spell circles folded inward. The barrier rumbled once more, but it still held.

“This… this is impossible,” he said.

Lord Vaun turned toward him. “What is it? What is she doing?”

Harris gritted his teeth. He didn’t want to say it. He certainly didn’t want to explain it. That the woman in front of them was forming some type of spell array, using spells whose formulas could rival a seventh-tier spell.

And she was doing it four times over.

Inside the barrier, Veronica’s breathing began to lose some of its steadiness. She placed the second lightning bolt at another corner of the table, then returned to her position at the front.

By now, a larger crowd had formed. Many had stepped closer, nearing the boundary of the appraisal area for a better view. Even Nasha and Gwen were pushed slightly forward as people shifted around them, trying to make room.

Veronica continued.

She formed the remaining two lightning bolts in the same manner, one after another, and placed them in their proper locations.

The refinement table rumbled violently, and so did the barrier.

The four compressed lightning bolts hovered at each corner of the table, each one crackling with contained force. Thin arcs of energy snapped between them, not quite connecting, but reaching, as if the array itself were waiting for the final piece.

Veronica exhaled slowly.

The four compressed bolts each formed an anchor of Whitebolt Conflux, an array that imbued flame with the power of lightning. Performing the technique alone normally placed an enormous burden on the caster’s mana reserves. But for Veronica, she handled it with relative ease.

She moved toward the center of the refinement table and picked up the golden resin.

Even with the four lightning bolts contained around her, she kept her hands steady. The amber lump was warm beneath her fingers, though whether from the refinement table or the pressure of the array, she couldn’t tell.

She placed it in the center of the table. Then she reached toward the side of the refinement table and tapped one of the carved runes. The golden lump began to rise inch by inch, with Veronica adjusting it until it hovered around chest height.

“What is she doing now?” Marcen whispered.

Harris didn’t answer him. Instead, his eyes were completely fixed on the four lightning bolts at the corners. Each one was like an anchor of invisible pressure. The crackling energy they emitted would cause serious harm, even to someone like him. But strangely enough, they didn’t seem that potent. They could shake the barrier, but he had seen seventh-tier ruin spells before. How were hers not as strong, despite using a seventh-tier spell equation?

Veronica placed her hand beneath the resin and formed a flame above her palm. A small, ordinary flame. Then she looked toward the lightning bolts.

The array activated.

Lightning exploded inside the barrier, with arcs of white-blue energy snapping across the dome, the floor, and the edges of the table. Several people in the audience cried out and stepped back. One scholar nearly tripped over his own robe. Even Gwen flinched, her hands rising slightly before she stopped herself.

Nasha didn’t move, but her eyes sharpened.

The pale blue dome rippled again and again as electricity lashed against its inner surface. The mana stones placed around the area brightened, their light deepening as they fed more power into the barrier.

But despite the flashy effects, the barrier held.

The four lightning bolts began to unravel, several arcs snaking inward. Their power stretched around the center of the table, twisting and forming a ring of jagged, wild edges around Veronica’s flame. Every spark that reached out broke apart against the fire.

Then the flame changed. Its red and orange glow vanished. Instead, it turned bright white.

A harsh, radiant glow forced several people to shield their eyes again. The heat within the barrier intensified so quickly that the air began to distort around Veronica’s hand.

Harris raised one sleeve in front of his face, squinting through the glare.

“Impossible,” he muttered.

Lord Vaun said nothing. He only stared, eyes wide, as the golden resin began to soften.

At first, it was barely noticeable. A thin shimmer spread across the surface. Then one of the darker veins inside the amber shifted, stretching downward as the resin lost its shape.

A drop fell.

It struck the refinement table with a hiss.

Then another. And another.

The golden lump slowly sagged around the object within. Layer after layer began to melt away, running down in thick streams while the white flame continued to burn beneath it. The resin did not shatter. It did not crack. It did not explode or corrode inward.

It simply melted. Just as Veronica said it would.

Veronica’s fingers twitched slightly as she adjusted the flame, keeping the heat centered and controlled. The chaotic ring of lightning continued to feed into it, each spark sharpening the fire’s edge into something closer to an alchemical flame.

It wasn’t true balefire. But it was close enough. An imitation of it.

The resin continued to drip away until, at last, something brown appeared inside the golden mass.

The badge.

Veronica narrowed her eyes and lowered the flame by a fraction, letting the last layers of resin peel away from its surface. The melted remains slid down, leaving the badge suspended in the air by the table’s enchantment.

The four lightning bolts finally dispersed.

One after another, they collapsed into faint streams of light and vanished. The violent crackling inside the barrier faded with them. The white flame above Veronica’s palm dimmed, returning to orange for a brief moment before she closed her hand and extinguished it completely.

Silence filled the appraisal area. Only the faint hiss of cooling resin remained.

Veronica reached forward and took the brown badge into her palm. It was warm, but intact. She turned toward Lord Vaun, raising it between two fingers for everyone to see.

“Melted,” Veronica said. “And intact.”
