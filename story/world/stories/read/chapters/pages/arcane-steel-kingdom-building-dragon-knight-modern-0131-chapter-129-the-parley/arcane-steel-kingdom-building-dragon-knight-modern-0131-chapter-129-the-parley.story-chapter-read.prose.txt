The Cinderfall vanguard was an exercise in staggered panic. They had divided their massive force across the scarred, ash-choked valley, setting up in three distinct layers to counter the threat of aerial bombardment.

First came the rearguard camp. Situated on the far side of the artificially created, churning black river, this was their lifeline—a sprawling, heavily warded encampment holding their primary supply wagons, field hospitals, and reserve infantry.

A few miles closer to our lines lay the forward operations base. This was the command center, a fortified cluster of silk tents housing the generals, the nobility, and the heavy artillery batteries they were desperately trying to assemble in the mud.

And finally, stretching across the width of the valley, was the main battle line. Millions of conscripts and seasoned veterans huddled behind hasty earthworks, staring across "no man’s land" at our silent, unblinking turrets.

What the Hegemony generals didn't know—couldn't know, without an Oracle of their own—was that their meticulous staging played right into my preparations. The engineering automata of the Aegis Legion had seeded key choke points in this valley with remote-detonated claymore charges months ago. The rearguard, the forward base, the main lines—they were all sitting above hidden pockets of magitech explosives.

It wouldn't wipe out a fifty-million-strong army. A blast that size would crack the tectonic plate. But it would cripple their command structure, shatter their logistics, and immediately shave twenty percent off their combat capability the moment they tried to mount a coordinated charge. It was a massive, bloody speed bump.

But I didn't press the button. Not yet.

According to the archaic, frustratingly formal rules of engagement that governed warfare on this continent, a massive clash between recognized powers required a parley. Two representatives were supposed to meet in the center of the battlefield to negotiate the terms of the slaughter. If they couldn't reach an agreement on the rules—like fighting only during daylight hours or barring certain classes of magic—a formal duel was held. The winner set the terms.

Traditionally, the sun-up to sun-down rule was standard. It allowed armies to rest, recover, and retrieve their dead. It was a mutual benefit.

For me, it was a tactical disaster.

My army was largely mechanized. Automata didn't sleep. They didn't feel morale decay in the dark. My logistics operated on a shift structure designed for continuous, twenty-four-hour engagement. Pausing the war every evening would give the Hegemony’s mages time to recharge their shields and allow the Phoenix Knights to recover their strength without pressure.

I needed total war. Unending, exhausting, grinding combat that would break their spirit before it broke my machines. But more importantly, I needed time. Every hour wasted on archaic diplomacy was an hour closer to the completion of the Colossus-class Crawlers.

I watched the tactical feed from the CIC. On the Cinderfall side, the defensive wards flared to life, casting golden, shimmering domes over their command tents. A lone figure detached from their front lines.

He rode a massive, soot-black stallion that kicked up a storm of ash. His crimson cape billowed dramatically in the freezing wind, and his armor—a masterpiece of gilded steel and ruby inlays—caught the pale afternoon light perfectly. It was a calculated display of martial pageantry.

"Tes," I murmured, leaning over the console. "Scan the mount."

[Analyzing. The biological entity is a Burning Stallion. A specialized magical beast adapted to the high-ambient heat of the Cinderfall magma fields. High stamina, resistant to thermal magic.]

The knight halted his impressive steed just before the edge of no man’s land. Flying in lazy, protective circles above him was his contracted partner—a Tier 5 phoenix in its smaller, avian form.

The Cinderfall Hegemony, situated on harsh, volcanic soil where little could grow, had always relied on their abundant mineral wealth to buy food and pride from their neighbors. It was the reason they coveted the fertile lands of Aerthos so desperately. It was the main bottleneck holding back their rapid, endless expansion. And this knight sitting before us was a prime example of that bought pride.

He drew his sword, pointing the polished, rune-etched blade dramatically toward our silent, fortified trench lines. He took a deep breath.

And then, he began to shout.

His voice, magically enhanced to boom across the entire valley, was an auditory assault.

"I am Sir Dumaric Farthallow!" he roared, puffing out his chest until the armor creaked. "Scion of the Boiling River, Knight of the Everlasting Ember, and Chosen Shield of the Southern Wastes!"

He didn't stop there. He was just getting warmed up.

"I ride with the blessing of my grandfather, Lord Balthazar, who bathed in the blood of the ice trolls! I strike with the fury of my father, who broke the walls of the western rebellion! But all our glory pales before the eternal, burning radiance of the Mother Phoenix! Praise be to her golden wings, which shelter our glorious kingdom! Praise be to King Theron Flavius, the undisputed master of the continent! Praise be to Cinderfall, the eternal flame that shall consume your pathetic, cowardly scrap metal!"

I rubbed my temples. The magically enhanced voice was incredibly grating, echoing off the valley walls like a terrible, never-ending theatrical performance being played through a broken speaker.

I checked the secondary feeds showing our trenches. The Aegis Legionaries manning the frontline barricades looked just as annoyed. The Dark Elves were shaking their heads, hands covering their sensitive ears, while the automated turrets tracked the screaming knight with cold, silent indifference.

I keyed the main communications channel.

"Someone please press the skip button on this guy," I ordered.

A mile away, hidden in a camouflaged sniper nest carved into a rocky outcropping, a Mark IV-S Specter unit received the command. Its optical sensor flared a dull red. It didn't hesitate. It didn't calculate wind speed or trajectory; the suit's internal targeting matrix handled the physics in a microsecond.

The Specter pulled the trigger.

The weapon was a specialized, magnetically accelerated sniper rifle firing a solid, 15mm tungsten slug. It crossed the distance in the blink of an eye.

There was no magical flash. No heroic clash of steel.

Sir Dumaric Farthallow’s head simply exploded, a spray of red mist and shattered gold helmet painting the ash behind him.

The crack of the rifle shot arrived a second later, echoing sharply across the valley. The Burning Stallion shrieked in panic, bucking wildly before bolting back toward the Cinderfall lines, leaving the headless body of its rider to slump heavily into the mud.

A collective, horrified gasp went up from the Hegemony front lines.

Above the corpse, the Tier 5 phoenix shrieked. It dove instantly, bursting into a sphere of golden, restorative fire. The flames washed over the ruined body, and the magic of resurrection took hold. At the same time, the phoenix threw up a thick, shimmering barrier of heat and light to prevent a second shot from ending the process.

I keyed the comms again. "Valen, would you do the honor of being our representative for this archaic parley?"

A modified Revenant carrier dropped from the cloud layer, hovering directly over the meeting zone. The rear ramp slammed down.

Valen fell.

He plummeted toward the earth, his dark, sleek command armor cutting through the freezing air. Just before he hit the ground, his back thrusters ignited with a sharp, controlled hiss of blue plasma, arresting his momentum instantly. He landed in the ash with a soft, confident crouch, right outside the phoenix's protective barrier.

Inside the dome of fire, the resurrection was complete. Sir Dumaric staggered to his feet, gasping for air, his hands flying to his neck where his head had recently been missing. He looked around frantically for his sword, finding it half-buried in the mud. He snatched it up, pointing the blade at Valen with a trembling hand as the phoenix barrier flickered and died.

"How dare you!" Dumaric screamed, his face purple with outrage, spittle flying from his lips. "An unprovoked attack during a parley! It is cowardly! It is dishonorable! And why does the cur hiding behind that metal mask not show his face? Where is the honor in hiding?!"

Valen stood up slowly. He simply stood there, a picture of relaxed, lethal grace.

Valen smirked. A thought command pulsed through his suit's neural interface. With a soft hiss of depressurizing seals, his black helmet retracted, folding neatly back into his heavy collar.

He shook his head, revealing his long, silver hair and the sharp, pointed ears of a Dark Elf.

"I am General Valen of the Aegis Legions," he said, his voice smooth and dripping with aristocratic sarcasm. "And I do concur, dear sir. It is quite dishonorable to attack a man unprovoked. Especially when he is resting in his home, with his family, and is forced to protect them with their very lives."

I watched the feed from the CIC, leaning back in my chair. That hit a nerve.

Dumaric’s face went from purple to a mottled, ugly red. The Cinderfall nobility knew exactly how this war had started. Being reminded of their own cowardly, unprovoked assassination attempt on the Wight family right before complaining about honor was a bitter pill.

But Valen wasn't finished.

"Furthermore," the young elven general continued, taking a casual step forward, "the attack on you wasn't entirely unprovoked. Your voice should be classified as a weapon of mass destruction. An assault of that volume on our delicate elven ears before the war has even officially started was quite rude."

On the screen, Valen actually reached up and elegantly flicked one of his long, pointed ears for dramatic effect.

I had to bite the inside of my cheek to keep from laughing out loud on the open comms channel. I had chosen Valen for this job because he had a brilliant tactical mind, but his sharp, cutting tongue was a massive bonus.

The Cinderfall representative was completely gobsmacked. He stood there, his sword wavering, his mouth opening and closing like a landed fish.

Valen delivered the killing blow.

"So, Lord Dum-Fart. To what do I owe the pleasure of this meeting?"

Diplomatic meetings in Aethelgard have one golden rule: Keep your cool, maintain your control. The representative who lost their temper lost the high ground.

Sir Dumaric Farthallow was inches away from starting a fistfight. He lowered his blade, taking huge, heaving breaths, his chest expanding as he tried to cool his boiling anger. This entire exchange was taking place in the dead center of the valley, watched by millions of soldiers on both sides.

It played a huge role in morale. The Cinderfall soldiers, already battered, exhausted, and hungry, were watching their proud, noble representative get mocked by an elf after getting his head blown off. It was shameful.

Dumaric finally regained a shred of his composure, though his voice still shook with rage.

"I am here to set the terms of engagement," Dumaric spat. "We demand the traditional accords. Combat shall commence at sun-up and cease at sun-down. Night raids are strictly forbidden. And..." He hesitated, glancing nervously at the sky. "All attacks on the civilian populations and capital cities of the respective kingdoms are banned for the duration of the conflict."

I listened through Valen's open comms channel. Of course, I thought. They want to limit the war to this valley. They're terrified I'll drop another Icarus on Cinderfall while they're stuck here in the mud.

Valen, I projected over the secure link. Refuse the terms. Demand continuous combat. No safe zones.

Valen smiled, a cold, predatory expression. "My Lord Commander finds those terms entirely unacceptable. We demand continuous, uninterrupted combat operations. No rest periods. And no place in your kingdom, or the conquered lands of Aerthos, is off-limits. You brought the war to our home. Do not cry when we bring it to yours."

Dumaric’s eyes widened. "That is madness! You would turn the entire kingdom into a slaughterhouse!"

"We didn't start the fire, Sir Dumaric," Valen replied smoothly. "We're just providing the fuel."

"Then we have no agreement," Dumaric growled, stepping back and raising his sword into a guard position. "As is custom when terms cannot be met... I call for a formal duel. Tomorrow at noon. The victor shall set the terms of the war."

Valen looked into the camera drone hovering nearby, directly at me in the CIC.

Take it, I told him. It buys us another twenty-four hours. The Crawlers need the time.

Valen turned back to the fuming knight. "We accept. Tomorrow at noon. I hope you bring a better speech."

The parley was over. Dumaric turned and marched back toward the Cinderfall lines, his phoenix circling nervously above him. Valen’s helmet snapped back into place, and the Revenant carrier dropped low to extract him.

I leaned over the holographic table in the CIC, watching the data streams surrounding the two Colossus-class construction sites. The massive, spider-like legs of the mobile fortresses were nearly attached to the main hulls. The automated forges were humming at maximum capacity.

"Keep building," I whispered to the quiet room. "I'll play their little games as long as I have to."
