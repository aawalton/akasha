Chapter 15: Echoes of Home

Suspicion crawled along my skin like tiny insects as I looked at her, every instinct honed by weeks of Nyxen training screaming warnings. The woman stood just outside the roped boundary of my training arena, her posture relaxed but alert. Her dark hair was cropped short in a practical style, and her lean frame spoke of someone who had spent considerable time preparing for combat. But it was her eyes that caught my attention, green and bright with an intelligence that seemed familiar in a way I couldn't quite place.

"I...yes," I replied carefully, my voice guarded. How could she possibly know I was from Earth? She had said 'also,' implying she was from Earth as well. I had assumed I was the only one brought to this crimson world, but that clearly wasn't the case if I had heard her correctly. The implications of that realization sent my mind racing through possibilities, none of them particularly comforting.

She smiled then, and it was probably one of the few genuinely friendly expressions I had seen since boarding Vash's ship weeks ago. The warmth in her face was so at odds with the cold calculations and cruel amusement I had grown accustomed to from the Nyxen that it almost felt like a trap. Holding out her hand in a gesture so mundane and Earth-normal that it made my chest tighten, she said, "I'm Kari. Kari Smith."

It was such a simple name, so unremarkably human. It called up a homesickness in me I hadn't realized was even there, buried beneath layers of grief and rage and the desperate need to survive. The name conjured images of suburban neighborhoods and coffee shops, of normal people living normal lives without the constant threat of death hanging over their heads. I shook her offered hand, feeling the calluses on her palm that spoke of weapon training, but wariness continued to play in my mind like the soft, discordant notes of an out-of-tune instrument.

"How did you know?" I asked, releasing her hand and taking a small step back to maintain fighting distance. It was an unconscious movement, one that Vash's brutal training had beaten into my muscle memory.

"Oh, that you were from Earth?" Kari's smile widened, and she gestured vaguely at my posture and expression. "Well, you just have that look, you know? That look of a tourist seeing a new destination for the first time. Like all of this was not only new to you, but fundamentally weird and wrong. Most people who grew up in the multiverse take this kind of thing for granted, but us Earth folks? We stick out like sore thumbs for the first few months."

Her voice was light and musical, like wind chimes in a gentle breeze, the sound carrying easily across the training hall despite the constant noise of combat around us. I grunted, some of my suspicion bleeding away but still maintaining a watchful presence in the back of my mind. What she said made sense, and I probably did have exactly that look. Everything on this world seemed designed to be alien and unsettling, from the architecture to the social customs to the casual brutality that permeated daily life.

"You said 'also?'" I questioned, wanting to be absolutely certain I understood her correctly.

Kari nodded, her smile dipping slightly as a sad light entered her green eyes. For a moment, her carefully maintained composure cracked, and I caught a glimpse of something raw and painful beneath the surface.

"Yeah, I'm from there as well. Earth, I mean. I just arrived a few weeks ago, maybe a month now. Time moves strangely here, doesn't it? I've been training almost constantly since then, trying to catch up to everyone else who's had years to prepare for the games."

I frowned, processing this information and finding it difficult to reconcile with what I thought I knew about my situation. The idea that there were others like me, other survivors of Earth's transformation, was both comforting and deeply unsettling.

"But how? I thought I was the only one. My sponsor, Lady Vash, she made it seem like I was some kind of unique find."

Kari's expression darkened, and she glanced around the training hall before leaning slightly closer. "I was brought here by a Nyxen Lord named Caeldon Faer. He's not as well-known as Lady Vash, but he's got significant resources and influence. I'm not the only one he rescued, either. He brought more than a few people from Earth, anyone he thought showed talent or potential."

She paused, running a hand through her short hair in a gesture that seemed unconsciously nervous. "Earth isn't what it used to be anymore. The Awakening changed everything. There are monsters now, creatures born from the Flux that hunt anything that moves. Society is collapsing in most places, governments have fallen, and whole continents have been reshaped. Entire landmasses have been added where there was just ocean before. There are weird races establishing settlements now, and factions like the Ha'Ji have sent delegations to establish outposts and recruit survivors."

The mention of the Ha'Ji sent a small chill down my spine, though I wasn't entirely sure why. Vash had mentioned them only in passing, usually with a tone of contempt or dismissal.

Kari's voice caught slightly, and she looked away from me toward one of the training androids. "My brother..." Her throat tightened visibly, and she had to pause to collect herself.

She got ahold of herself a moment later, letting out a shaky breath that spoke of practiced emotional control. "He didn't make it out when Lord Caeldon's people came for us. The monsters got to our shelter before the rescue ship could extract everyone. I watched him die trying to buy time for others to escape."

Her hands clenched into fists at her sides, and I could see the muscles in her jaw working as she fought down whatever emotions threatened to overwhelm her. It was a sight I recognized intimately, having seen it in my own reflection countless times.

"This is my chance," she continued, her voice growing stronger and more determined. "My chance to make sure that never happens again. To become powerful enough that I'll never have to watch someone I care about die while I stand there helpless."

In her, I could see a kindred spirit. Someone who sought some of the same things I did, driven by loss and the desperate need to ensure it never happened again. The familiar weight of grief and rage that I carried seemed to resonate with whatever burden she bore, creating an understanding that went deeper than words.

Smiling again, though this time with more genuine warmth, she pointed at the deactivated hilt of my resonance blade. "I like the weapon choice. A lot of people here are going for exotic resonance weapons from what I've observed. Whips and tridents and all manner of elaborate constructs. But there's something to be said for the classics, don't you think? Simple, elegant, effective."

She gestured around the training hall, where I could see other initiates practicing with their various manifestations. "Want to spar? I could use the practice against someone with a more traditional fighting style, and you look like you know what you're doing."

I hesitated, weighing the risks and benefits. I had come here for training, and sparring with another initiate would give me a better sense of what I might face in the games tomorrow. But trusting someone I had just met went against every lesson Vash had drilled into me about survival and paranoia.

"Alright," I said finally. "Why not?"

We took positions across from one another in the center of the arena, the ropes humming softly with contained energy around us. Other initiates had stopped their own training to watch, apparently curious about the newcomer and how he might fare against one of their more established members.

"I know it isn't exactly the Nyxen way," Kari said, settling into a low combat stance that looked practiced and fluid, "but try to pull your blade before making contact with skin, hmm? With the games starting tomorrow, I'd rather not suffer any permanent injuries that might affect my performance."

The request rankled against everything Vash had taught me. Such considerations smacked of weakness, of the kind of soft thinking that got people killed when the stakes were real. Her training had thoroughly disabused me of such gentle notions about combat. But at the same time, Kari's reasoning made practical sense. I didn't want to find myself injured before the games either, and there was little to be gained from maiming a potential ally.

Sighing with resignation, I nodded my agreement before activating my resonance blade. The familiar weight settled into my hand as the blade of white light sprang from the crystal focus, touched by its distinctive crimson core and humming with barely contained energy. The weapon felt like an extension of my arm now, as natural as breathing after weeks of constant practice.

To my surprise, Kari activated not one but two resonance blades, each one manifesting as pure white light without any colored highlighting. The twin weapons appeared in her hands with practiced ease, and she settled into a stance that spoke of extensive training in dual-wielding techniques. Her grip was sure, her balance perfect, and there was a fierce grin spreading across her face that made her green eyes light up with anticipation.

With a sudden widening of her eyes that might have been surprise or excitement, she launched herself at me with explosive speed.

Vash had used a multitude of different weapons against me during my training, including a double-bladed style similar to what Kari was demonstrating. I knew from painful experience that the key to fighting against dual wielders was superior movement and footwork. The extra weapon gave them more options for attack and defense, but it also committed them to staying within a certain range to be effective.

I moved fluidly around her initial assault, my feet finding the proper positions almost automatically. Her attacks were a flowing combination of the basic ten-chi-jin forms and several more complex techniques I didn't immediately recognize. Her blades wove intricate patterns in the air, creating a web of light that would have been beautiful if it weren't designed to kill me.

Neither of us accessed the Flux directly at first, relying instead on pure physical skill and weapon technique. But that began to change as her attacks grew fiercer and more aggressive, forcing me to dig deeper into my reserves of speed and strength. The familiar ritual began almost without conscious thought: I called up the memories that fueled my connection to the Flux.

Lynn's dead eyes, glassy and empty in the ruins of our bedroom. My son Jackson's torn and broken body, small and fragile in death. The surprised expression on Lieutenant Zorin's face as my blade cut him in half, the way his body had crumpled to the training room floor. Each image was a key that unlocked another reservoir of power, another layer of the cold rage that had become my constant companion.

I didn't attempt to use the strange authority I had felt during my fight with Zorin, that moment of overwhelming presence that had frozen him in place. Mostly because I had little idea of how to actually access it consciously, and partly because this was meant to be a friendly sparring match rather than a fight to the death.

Kari's connection to the Resonant Flux appeared to be roughly equal to mine, her movements taking on the same enhanced speed and precision that marked someone drawing on the deeper currents of power. But I had certain advantages that began to tell as the fight progressed.

I was less restricted in my movements due to wielding only a single blade, able to flow between attack and defense with greater fluidity. My reach was longer, and I could commit more fully to strikes without worrying about coordinating multiple weapons. And frankly, despite her obvious skill and training, I was simply better than her.

She telegraphed her strikes just a little too much, a fraction of a second of preparation that warned me of where the attack was coming from. Her footwork was solid but predictable, following patterns that I began to recognize after the first few exchanges. Most importantly, she fought like someone who had learned techniques from a book or instructor, while I fought like someone who had been beaten into understanding every principle through pain and repetition.

The sparring match ended with my victory, though it was closer than I had expected. My blade stopped just short of her throat while her own weapons were trapped in a bind that left her unable to counter-attack effectively. We held the position for a moment, both breathing hard from the exertion, before disengaging and stepping back.

Kari scowled, though there was more frustration than anger in her expression. She deactivated her blades and wiped sweat from her forehead with the back of her hand.

"You're good," she said, her voice carrying a note of grudging respect. "Really good. How did you get to be so skilled in such a short time? Most of us have been training for months just to get basic competency with our weapons."

Before I could respond with anything more than a modest deflection, a new voice cut through the air between us. It was sharp and mocking, with the distinctive sibilant quality I had learned to associate with the draxi servants.

"He's not good," the voice said with casual cruelty. "You're just pathetic, Kari."
