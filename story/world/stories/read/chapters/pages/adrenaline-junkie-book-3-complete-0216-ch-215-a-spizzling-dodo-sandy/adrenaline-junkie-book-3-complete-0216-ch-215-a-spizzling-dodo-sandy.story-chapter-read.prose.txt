Archie pinched the bridge of his nose and exhaled slowly. “Not pear the fruit, Liv. Peer-to-peer connection, Peer. As in two people; two equals having an equal exchange… Like coworkers. That type of peer.”

Liv just stared at Archie blankly, as though he’d started reciting Shakespeare backwards and in Greek. “What does dat hav’ tah do wit mah Aetherglass ‘nd how we can talk wit it wit’out signal?”

After a couple more seconds of silence, Archie sighed and dug out his Aetherglass, thumbing it open as his eyes flicked across the interface and swiping through the many app pages that were added by default. “Alright, hold up– just give me a sec, I think I saw that this had a Help app when I was looking through it a couple days ago.”

For the next couple of minutes, he found himself scrolling through the Help app, muttering under his breath as the glowing pane flicked past his eyes. “P2P connection… no Aether Tower nearby… direct link in closed zones caused by and with the assistance of the System, they can act as their own towers for communication, but in turn limit their use…” he muttered out loud as he found what he was looking for.

Finally, he glanced up, waving the Aetherglass lazily in her direction. “See? Right here.” He said as he pointed to a specific paragraph labeled as P2P. “It explains a peer-to-peer network perfectly; It’s basically a closed circuit between our Aetherglasses, which allows them to act as both client and server when communicating with one another, meaning we can bounce messages and calls to each other without needing one of the relay towers.”

“I think they added this long ago when the makers of Aetherglass got complaints how their product didn’t work in Tutorial Events or other System Events so they made them able to bounce off each other while in a locked space and act as their own radio towers in a sense so everyone in said closed space can at the very least communicate with each other when and if certain requirements are met.”

Liv cocked her head, chewing on his words like they were gristle. “…All I heard was ye sayin’ ‘pear’ again.”

Archie let out a laugh that was more pained than amused and dragged a hand down his face. This is just like that time I tried to explain to my dad that it was perfectly safe to put his credit card information on the PC gaming platform I used for my favorite subscription game. All I got back was: “Just buy the gift cards, they work the same. Why does that platform need my credit card information anyways?”

“Okay, look, bottom line: because we tapped our Aetherglasses together, you’re in my contacts list now under something called the ‘Closed Channels’ tab. Same for you. I can call you. You can call me. Doesn’t matter if we’re stuck in some anti-signal dungeon or halfway across the goddamn sea. This works because our Aetherglasses work like their own radio towers now, and after tapping them together, they’ve memorized their frequencies and can communicate with each other now… Make sense?”

Liv’s lips curled into a grin that was equal parts smug and dismissive. “Aye, makes sense enough. I hit mine on yers, and now we can yak at each other like normal.” She said as she then began to tuck away her Aetherglass. “All dat magical computer chargin’ shite makes zero sense tah me, but ye gimme a hammer, an anvil, an’ mah scriber? I’ll whip ye up some good fookin’ armor an’ weapons. Why can’t it be as easy as dat, eh?”

Archie blinked at her, then barked a short laugh despite himself. “I mean, forging does get complicated if I am being honest, with all the mana and core stuff you can do while hammering. ”

She smirked, tugging her ponytail tighter. “Oh, aye? Funny, ’cos everythin’ I’ve ever smacked wi’ a hammer either starts workin’ or stops botherin’ me. Easy as tha’, yer just o’erreactin’.”

Archie stared at her for a good few seconds before he began to shake his head slowly, as lips twitching into the faintest smile. “You know what? Fair point.”

Shrugging helplessly at her response, Archie let his gaze drift around before stopping past Liv’s shoulder to the side table beside her stand, where the scent of savory roasted meat laced with herbs and spice caught his attention. Half-wrapped in paper and already bitten into sat a sandwich, though not just any sandwich. Juices and sauce had soaked through the wrapper, pooling onto the table in dark, greasy splotches.

Oh, my, my, my… Archie mused to himself as his eyes zeroed in on the sandwich and onto the mustached chef hat insignia on it.

Archie’s eyes flicked around the platform, and sure enough, more than a few people were holding the very same-looking sandwich. Adventurers planning out their plans for conquering the dungeon, smiths taking bites out of their larger-sized sandwiches while tending to their forge and the metal inside it, even vendors who were haggling prices with their customers had a couple by their side – all of them eating and having purchased the same sandwich that Liv had on her table.

Hell, even the elves are eating them, Archie thought to himself. And I thought elves were supposed to be vegetarian… goes to show what I know.

A little click went off in his head as a thought came to him.

“Say, Liv,” he called, his voice cutting through her thoughts as she intensely inspected the Aetherium ingot in her left hand while absentmindedly rotating the dagger with wolf-jaw tongs as it was in the forge.

“Wot?” she asked as her eyes remained fixed on the glowing metal he’d given her.

Archie jerked his chin toward the sandwich. “That thing you were eating. Do you know what kind of sandwich it is? Everyone else seems to have one… or five.”

She blinked, finally tearing her gaze from the ingot in her hand to glance at the half-eaten food she cast aside to focus on her forging, then back at him. “Uh… somethin’ called a Spizzling Dodo Sandy. Got it from me best mate – ’cordin’ to my Quest Log, she’s a chef. Dragged meh here at the crack o’ dawn tah set up shop, then shoved one in meh hand and said some’tin ‘bout it bein recentleh dis’overed.”

“Like the dinosaur?” Archie’s brows ticked up, but instead of getting a confirmation from her, she shrugged. Just like him, the description from Identify told her nothing beyond the name, Spizzling Dodo Sandy, and that it had been made by Chef Enzio.

A Spizzling Dodo Sandy… I guess I’ll be looking out for those while I’ll be exploring and leveling in the forest, he thought to himself as he added looking for Dodos to his list of things to do while leveling up.

Okay, so Dodo meat for the butchers because from what I’m seeing it's good chicken, and according to Liv, they’ve been recently found, which means it's new to the market and in turn rising in price. Other than Dodos, I need to find creatures with tough hides to sell to leathersmiths, and to do that, I need to be mindful about not damaging their hide. And finally, as I explore the forest, I can use Material Insight to try and find any good herbs and such to sell to alchemists.

“Is it any good?” he asked, eyes narrowing slightly as if he could taste-test just from watching her chew.

Liv made a face like she swallowed a lemon at the price her chef friend put up on his stand board after he handed her the sandwich. “…It’s expensive,” she grunted, then shrugged with a reluctant smirk, “but aye, it’s some damn good grub.” She admitted as she shoved the Aetherium into her Spatial Storage before turning back to Archie fully.

“An’ with everyone an’ their mam comin’ here for the dungeon? Business ‘as been lookin’ good fer a smith like meh. Been hammerin’ non-stop since I set up shop at dawn.” Her green eyes flicked over his armor, and began analyzing it’s craftsmanship as she was unable to see the runes he’d placed on it.

“If ye wanna set up shop,” she continued, smirking faintly, “don’t do it here. Too many smiths already. Ye’ll drown faster than a dwarf in open sea. Need a permit from the city hall anyway. I only got mine fer two months ‘cause o’ a Quest.”

Archie tilted his head, filing that away. Permits – looked like he’d have to deal with those in the System Event, too. As if it wasn’t bad enough that he’d once been forced to shut down his summer-break lemonade stand for not having a business permit, which meant waiting until his birthday to finally buy the game he wanted.

"Got it," Archie nodded as he gave a look around and counted at least three other smiths in sight with their Do Not Disturb plates facing down, unlike Liv, who seemed to be on break, judging by her plate being up and Archie only just noticing its existence now.

"Uh, sorry for disturbing you on your break."

"Only just noticed it now, eh?" Liv smirked at Archie's slightly embarrassed gaze. "It's fine, I'm just glad to meet another Earthling after all that happened."

"Same," Archie smiled as he stuck out his hand to shake hers. "You're the only other person I’ve met that’s from Earth other than Henry."

Shaking Archie's hand, Liv looked thoughtful, and just as she was about to say something, she held her tongue, giving him a solemn look of understanding as she picked up on what went unsaid, unbeknownst to Archie, quite easily, given her previous occupation.

"I'll see ya around, Archie," Liv said. “Give meh ah call if ya see any good metals.”

"Take care, Liv, and don't forget you have my Aetherglass number; so hit me up if shit ever hits the fan and you need help," Archie said with a wave as he turned toward the far end of the platform, heading into the deeper parts of the forest in the opposite direction from where he’d come.

As he walked towards the opposite edge of the platform, he could hear the murmuring of everyone talking about the creatures within the dungeon and the forest.

“…did ye see the size of that first Sapheart Sovereign? Just the big fucker’s pecker alone is taller and wider than Tall’brow,” a tall brown-haired mage said as he patted the shield-bearing dwarf beside him.

“Oi!” hissed the dwarf as he swung his fist into the lower half of his teammate and sent him doubling over and clutching his own family jewels. “I’m at least de size of two of ‘em.”

The group of seven, minus the mage, chuckled at the brown-haired mage’s jab and the dwarf’s quick reply, along with his antics in response.

A light, female voice chimed in as she held up her lockpicks that she took out from one of her many leather pouches she had strapped to her inner thigh: “If that chest in the east corridor is still there along with the other ones we found from before, then now that I have enough lock picks we should head there first before facing the third floor.”

From another group, Archie passed beside he heard another interesting-sounding murmur, “ –south wing of the forest has got some rare herbs. When I brought some to my brother, he said they were really good ones for Healing Potions, and if we bring more to him, he’ll give us a real good cut.”

Archie’s grin tugged faintly at the corners of his mouth as he stored the snippets of conversation into his mental ledger as he moved.

Reaching the edge of the platform, he launched himself in a blur of motion, branch to branch, disappearing into the forest canopy.
