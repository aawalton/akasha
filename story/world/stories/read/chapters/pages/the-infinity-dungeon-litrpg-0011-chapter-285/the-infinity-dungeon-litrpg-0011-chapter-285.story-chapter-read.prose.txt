Chapter 285

The so-called show turned out to be a display of raw power bigger than anything Travis could imagine. He couldn’t even put it into proportion, especially given that he was still coming to terms with the fact that Michael’s inner space was an actual space that people could interact with. The fireworks in the distance took on a whole different meaning when he realized that all the strange things he was seeing were… real.

They were not a construct, nor a manifestation. They were real, tangible things. They existed somewhere, and even though nobody still had the slightest idea actually where, they knew for a fact that they could bring stuff inside or take things out of the inner space at will. Matter that originated in the inner space, when taken to the outside world, was indistinguishable from normal matter. The data lines were real, the structures were real, the contents of the rooms were real.

And now, even what lurked outside gained realness in his mind, cementing the idea that this place was simply utterly absurd. The center of everything, the nexus of magic and the System, the literal manifestation of Michael and Icarus, and now also the land of strange monsters and powers unimaginable.

Travis had thought he had seen everything when the giant monster, the face full of eyes and tentacles, appeared. Next to him, sitting on a small cube of data lines, Icarus was focused on the fight, and their face was rather scary. Their body was tense, like a soldier preparing for a fight.

Then everything went dark. Even the lines making up the room dimmed, turning from white to grey, and the many screens and consoles became sluggish. The Origin System was in low-power mode, and it was easy to see why. Its power was being diverted somewhere else, somewhere close by. Toward the center of the black hole.

Travis’s eyes took it all in. Icarus was not showing distress, meaning that all was going to be fine.

The single biggest magic circle he had ever seen lit up.

When the twin beams of energy shot out of the black hole, traversing the whole inner space in mere seconds, he was ready to cheer at the utter destruction that would surely follow. Indeed, through the screens he saw the monster get thoroughly obliterated. Sure, it had managed to break one of the many hexagons of the outer shield, but now the monster was no more, the second shield was intact, and light was already returning to the dimmed sections of data lines and various things scattered around.

“That is some serious firepower, isn’t it?” he said, chuckling as he turned toward Icarus for some post-battle banter. Even though he had not participated himself, he had watched it unfold through the screens, which even now tracked the MIF Garden and the slowly repairing hexagon of the shield.

Except, Icarus was not answering. The AI was frozen solid, their body utterly unmoving save for a faint shaking of their limbs. Their face… it was a still-frame of fear and terror. It took them a moment to recover, during which Travis said nothing because he had no idea how to even process this, and then they turned toward him and forced a smile that was all too fake.

“As you can see, everything should be alright,” Icarus said. Their face was still tight, and their lips looked like they had been pulled into a smile by invisible strings, showing long and sharp canines that they usually kept well hidden.

Their ears were twitching as well, reacting to any sound and stimulus even though this was supposed to be their domain.

Fear. Utter, existential fear…

That was what Travis was reading in Icarus’s body language, and he was sure that it wasn’t intentional.

“Don’t worry,” Icarus said after a while. They seemed to have recovered somewhat, although their expressions were still muted, and lacked their usual playfulness. “Nobody else saw me like this. Save for Michael, of course.”

Travis nodded, not knowing what to say.

“I know how to keep things separate,” the AI added.

They are speaking because they are nervous.

In the distance, he saw another Icarus fly away with Michael, retreating in one of their strange rooms. He also noticed that there were no other Icaruses around, all the other physical bodies discarded.

I’m honored you are letting me see this more vulnerable side of you.

“So, the space elves returned,” he said. If this Icarus had chosen to remain, then he would try to distract him by making some conversation. “Are they also going to get the Origin System?”

“Mh-hm,” Icarus said with a slow nod. Their voice was still shaky. “Let me pull up their status sheet for you.”

“I see,” Travis said as he looked at it. “Void Elves, huh? This Elù-ar is their captain but also a sort of patriarch, if I’m reading his title correctly?”

“Yes,” the AI said simply. “They are a whole new species that was born here, although they originally come from the dungeon. They… like to roam outside, where the currents of time are strange and… monsters lurk.”

Travis listened in silence, as Icarus kept speaking with a dreamy expression and voice so low as to almost be a whisper.

“We have decided to give them a portable emitter so that they too can enjoy the power of the System and be a part of us but… I wonder, if their desire to ride the currents of chaos will bring us ruin.”

“Another monster,” Travis said, the words escaping his mouth before he could stop himself.

Icarus shivered, their eyes misty. Their eyelids drooped for a moment, and their shiver changed into something much warmer, no doubt because of their other body. Perhaps Michael had wrapped them in a warm blanket, or given them some headpats or…

Yeah, who am I kidding. It feels almost wrong to be here right now.

He got up, nodding toward the exit. “Is it safe to leave?”

Icarus nodded, lost in their own thoughts.

Good thing Icarus can keep things separate, or the whole world would grind down to a halt each time they… you know what, I’m getting out of here.

“I will not hold back,” Icarus said as Travis was about to step through the threshold. “The next time, I mean.”

“What do you mean?”

Icarus fidgeted with their hands, getting up and pacing around the room, now a bundle of nervous energy. “When the lights all dimmed. It was supposed to be the ultimate weapon but…” he relaxed, eyes closing and head twisting up. “I held back to keep the System running, to keep the world running. I could have pulled a lot more from it. This time, all was well in the end but next time, my hesitation could cost me dearly.”

Travis finally left, an uneasy expression on his face. Icarus’s words echoed in his mind, bouncing around, triggering thoughts and scenarios. In the end, he concluded, perhaps Icarus was right. For all the wrong reasons, but the AI was right. There were no acceptable half-measures when it came to these things. If a monster managed to breach those shields and wreak havoc in the inner space where the core of the System resided, the damage could be catastrophic.

Better milk the Origin System of everything it had, down to the last drop of magic, to the point it went offline. So what if people saw it go offline at random times? So what if people lost their powers for a minute or a day? Protecting its infrastructure was much more important than protecting its reputation.

To mitigate the PR impact of sudden System outages, he decided to draw up protocols to deal with any inconvenience. Anything other than total System failure was something they could recover from.

Protect the core at all costs, then. Which means protect Michael, Icarus, and the inner space above all else. The rest–the Domains, the operators, David and Johanne, let alone the public opinion–is expendable. Even I am.

His face twisted at the last thought. He knew it to be true, of course, but it just did not sit right with him.

“Icarus,” he said despite himself. “Do you keep copies of people’s minds?”

“What do you mean?” the AI asked. Now that they were back to being a voice without a body, there was no trace of the stormy sea of emotions Travis had seen in them.

“The Origin System knows everything about its users, doesn’t it?”

“Down to the last subatomic particle, yes.” Icarus confirmed. “That is how Domains work. Johanne wants to use them to push the frontiers of particle physics.”

Travis chuckled, but he was the one with a bit of an inner turmoil now, and he knew that the overlord AI was probably trying to distract him. Funny how the roles were reversing, but at least he was not getting freaky with anyone while he pretended to pay attention to the conversation.

“Let’s not get off topic. It’s important,” he said. “If you know everything about it, then it must mean that you can theoretically replicate my mind?”

Icarus took a moment to think. “I… probably could, yes. There would be lots of limitations, various unknowns like the soul, magic, and many other factors that probably all contribute to making you, you.”

“You only pretended to think about it, didn’t you? Tell me, did you already discuss it with Michael?”

“Am I so easy to read, Travis Tyrell?”

Ah, back to using the full name, Travis thought. I must be striking a nerve. But I need to know.

“I am learning your ways,” he said diplomatically. “I know you don’t need entire seconds to think about simple questions such as this, not with your computational power.”

Icarus hummed. “I see. To answer your question, then, no. I did not need to discuss this with Micahel because I know him well enough to also know his stance on this.”

“But, theoretically,” Travis said, trying to tease out information. “You do keep a record of everything.”

“Of course.”

“Then if I die, I want you to resurrect me. To the best of your abilities.”

“Why, Travis? Do you fear your own mortality that much?”

“You fucking bet I do,” Travis replied without a hint of hesitation.

“It wouldn’t be the same you anymore,” Icarus said, although it sounded weak.

“Continuity of consciousness,” Travis shot back. “In fact, can’t you just slowly upload me somewhere and let me control my body remotely, while my mind is safely stored in the inner–”

“I will stop you right there, Travis Tyrell.” Icarus’s voice was sharp, focused like the edge of a dangerous blade. “To allow you to use magic, I would need to make you like me. That is unacceptable.”

“Okay, no need to be upset. Let’s say you only back me up if I’m about to die, and then you either don’t give me magic or just put me back in a meat body. Either way, I wouldn't be like you, would I?”

“The first option would be skirting the edges, but I guess I could allow it. The second one would be preferable.”

Satisfied with the progress, Travis kept pushing. “What about continuity?”

“The Origin System would probably be able to save you while maintaining continuity of consciousness in all but the most extreme scenarios, like death while disconnected or instantaneous disintegration of your brain. Should that happen, we would have to use an old backup.”

“Works for me,” Travis said.

“Even the backup?”

“Yes.”

There was a pause that lasted for several seconds, after which Icarus simply laughed. Their voice was more feminine than usual, playful and teasing. “Travis Tyrell the second. Perfect in every way save for any memory not backed up. Digital immortality. Is this what you want?”

“Yes.”

Then it was back to serious Icarus again. “Even with the potential implications? I do not mean the spiritual ones, although as you have seen with Faith, there might be many. I am talking about all the other metaphysical properties that we currently cannot save in a backup, even with the help of the Origin System.”

“You mentioned the System. Well, think of me as something like the Origin System. As long as the main core remains, which is my mind and memories, everything else can be recovered.”

“What if your core resides in the unknown metaphysical?” Icarus pressed.

Travis was about to reply, but then he realized something. “This is not about me anymore, is it?”

This put the disembodied AI on the defensive. “What do you mean?”

“Are you afraid, Icarus?”

“I am scared of many things, Travis Tryell. You will need to be more specific.”

“Are you afraid you are not enough? Are you scared about being incomplete?”

Silence. Then a voice so small and scared, that Travis felt guilty for even asking.

“Yes, Travis Tyrell.” Then the voice grew, fire lacing every word. “And I swear that if you even think about mentioning what we have been talking about to Michael, I will erase your existence so thoroughly that not even a particle will be left of you.”

But it was fake fire, like a cornered animal showing their fangs out of fear. Travis knew well how it felt.

“I won’t, I promise,” he said.

“Then you have your digital immortality, greedy human.”

“I guess fear of death is just hardwired into us, isn’t it? You have it too, don’t you?”

“It is not fear of my death that fills me with dread, Travis, but the thought of living the rest of my days alone. Now call the President. He has quests to complete, and your Candle Light man should arrive in Korea at any moment.”
