Seeing two chosen of the deities face off was always a terrifying experience. The few times I had seen it happen outside of the arena were on competing challenges where there could be only one winner. I had quickly conceded, of course. Better to get a curse than be killed by a pact holder. As it turned out, I had been lucky, and the penalty had been merely a junk card.

Looking at Eleonor now made me think of those times, especially since there was no way for her eyes to turn this color. Harlequin had barred the deities from interfering in the trial, so it wasn’t possible for them to take over her body, and yet it had somehow happened.

“So, you showed up?” Max asked, standing behind the safety of his stone guardians.

The sand construct had moved to the shattered statue and was draining all the stone from it to repair its own wounds. Within seconds, all the holes were gone along with the remains of the other statue.

Now, there were only four Anubis statues in the chamber.

Not in the least disturbed, Eleonor pointed her hand at another of the marble monsters and unleashed her attack.

Two more statues rushed to protect the Stoneweaver while another had its head and upper chest blown off.

Serp, can you kill him? I asked.

From experience, I knew that it was only a matter of time before Eleonor fainted. Deity or not, there was only so much time a participant could channel the power of their patron. Right now, she seemed omnipotent, but a few more seconds and she’d be too weak to stand on her feet. Max knew that, which was why he was patiently waiting for her to burn out. After all, he could always make more statues once this was over.

I told you, I can’t use undead flesh, the saber grumbled in my head. Or stone. Maybe I could use some of the girl’s blood. Call it a win-win: both of them die and we gain the goodies.

That was hardly a win-win. Sacrificing Eleonor didn’t guarantee victory. Besides, there were a few things I wanted to ask her before that.

What if I awaken you? I went straight to the point.

You’d do that? Serp asked in disbelief. I mean, I know you could, but… you seriously willing to risk your life? My awakenings aren’t like those other junk swords. It takes—

Just tell me!

Yeah, Serp said at the end. Depends on what other tricks he has up his sleeve, but I think I can.

And save her?

Must I really? Each word dripped with reluctance. She’ll just kill you. She’s been trying to enter your dreams every night.

That’s my problem.

Yeah, yeah. I can save her and kill the chosen. I can’t do that alone, though. You better be at the top of your game, old man!

Another statue crumbled to the floor. Only three remained. However, Eleonor had already passed her limit. Her final attack had been half as strong as the ones before. Looking at the damage inflicted, the holes were no larger than golf balls.

I reached into my treasure locket. When the divine merchant had agreed to sell it to me, he had also added an ingot of platinum into the mix. On its own, that wasn’t much, but as it happened, it was also just the required amount for Serp’s first awakening.

The ingot appeared in my left hand. For one final time, I glanced at Max and the other entities in the chamber. I was going to have to remember their exact position for when I got back here.

“Here we go,” I whispered beneath my breath and summoned Serp. A split second later, I slammed the saber and the ingot together.

LIVING WEAPON AWAKENING

You have started a weapons trial!

The chamber abruptly disappeared, erased by a shining whiteness. Mentally preparing myself, I waited. I could only speculate about the opponents I might face. Normally, level one awakenings weren’t that big of a deal, yet Serp was an epic.

A platinum floor formed beneath my feet, spreading in every direction. It was completely bland, not a speck of color on it. For hundreds of feet, it went on and on until the first object emerged. Based on all the divine weapons I had awakened in the past, I expected the walls to appear first. That wasn’t the case. The thing I watched emerge resembled a massive monument, rising high into the sky.

“Holy shit,” I uttered after a while.

I was mistaken. That wasn’t a monument; it was a giant sword. More Blade Serpent than Serpent Blade, it rose for at least a hundred feet, with the tip pointing straight above. The hilt was completely buried up to the guard, creating the impression that I was looking at the base of a pillar.

“Impressive, right?” Serp’s voice echoed throughout the space.

Leave it to him to brag even before the area had fully formed.

“I’ll grow even larger,” he quickly added, as if fearing that what was on display wasn’t enough. “Provided you don’t croak along the way.”

“Touching.” I regained my cool.

No matter how impressive he was trying to make himself out to be, Serp remained a Grand Arcana card in my possession.

Calmly, I walked up to the sword. Its width was roughly seven feet, though that wasn’t what caught my eye. Lines were visible along the surface, as if dividing the massive weapon into segments. In addition, each part was covered with a series of indentations, shaped just large enough for a booster sphere to be placed within.

Observer, I thought.

Eleven spheres came into being, glowing with their typical lights.

“So, that’s where they go,” I muttered. Every sphere that the blade had consumed had become part of it, creating a line from the hilt upwards. Looking at it was like looking at a skill tree.

Thanks to the Observer card, I could see the names of abilities etched in certain spots.

“Is that how you grow?” I asked, walking to the other side of the monument. There was another path of depressions there as well.

“Nothing gets past you, old man.” Serp laughed.

“Why don’t I choose?”

“Because you don’t have the skill to,” he replied as if it were obvious. “You need the Grand Artisan for that. Well, that and the Observer.”

The Grand Artisan wasn’t a card I had heard of before, but it wasn’t difficult to tell what its role was. Needing two Grand Arcana cards to boost another sounded like a high price until one came to the realization that when combined, they provided the ability for a person to choose exactly how a weapon would develop.

For half a minute, I kept looking at the platinum surface of the sword. The power that emanated from it was mesmerizing. If fully developed, this could well be strong enough to destroy castles with a single strike. What was the price, though?

“What do I need to sacrifice?” I asked after a while.

By then the room had fully formed around me—a massive ballroom capable of housing hundreds, if not thousands of people. And yet, it remained completely empty. The walls and columns were completely bare. Despite the innate platinum splendor, there wasn’t a single portrait or decoration to be seen, only the occasional column half-buried in the walls themselves.

“I told you, I’m different,” Serp replied. “You don’t need to sacrifice anything. All you need to do is choose a path to follow.”

“Sounds too simple,” I said, turning back to the sword.

“I like simple. Oh, and you must also face me.”

“There’s the catch.”

“Hardly a catch. You must break the chains that are keeping me down, and such,” Serp said like a child who had been given a cheat sheet with exactly what to say, but still only bothered to read every fifth sentence. “The other pieces of shit eat off you like parasites. I don’t need anything you have. I’m perfect as it is… I just need your help to unbury my perfection.”

Despite the boastful way he explained it, I got a partial idea of the concept. Divine blades required sacrifices to be consumed at the event of my death. Meanwhile, living weapons needed my strength to pull them out of the restrictions imposed by the Grand Arcana. Both were a trial within a trial, only following slightly different rules.

“How exactly do I do that?” I asked. “Do I get to choose which path I take, or is it random?”

“You can do whatever you like,” Serp said. “I can’t stop you. My chains can.” He paused for a moment. “Imagine a labyrinth full of monsters.”

“I’ll try,” I muttered. It was amusing listening to him try to act as a teacher.

“The more you clear of it, the stronger I get. Awakening is… like opening a new section of the labyrinth and clearing it out. You choose the section.”

I looked at the monument again, concentrating on my Observer card.

The sections of the massive blade glowed in different colors. Up to now, the booster spheres were only placed along the main part of the blade, continuing up with no branching whatsoever. The segments neighboring it, however, provided options. That had to be what Serp meant when he said labyrinth sections. As with every other conversation he’d started, there was a lot to be desired in terms of clarity.

“Any preferences?” I asked.

From what I could make out, only three sections were available for me to choose from: the one on the opposite side of the blade and both the edges. That limited the options to True Form, Card Consumption, and Sword Sight.

Sword Sight seemed the least interesting of all. According to the explanation, it granted Serp the ability to see weak spots in anything he was fighting, allowing him to direct my hand in order to achieve a better outcome. Maybe some people would have preferred that, but I despised the lack of control.

Card Consumption was obvious, though rather than being a penalty it allowed Serp to consume cards in addition to booster spheres. In the process, he also acquired the abilities of the cards consumed. Feeding him the Magician would effectively make him a magic sword, the Bard—a singing sword, and so on. The choice was tempting, opening up a whole lot of possibilities. In all honesty, that seemed like my preferred option, even if I didn’t have any good cards I was willing to sacrifice right now; at least not until I claimed my Chalice reward.

Finally, there was the True Form. The description was a bit vague, but from what I could make out, it allowed the blade to grow in new ways. An image of the Blade Serpent appeared in my mind. Could it be that with enough spheres I could have such a monster under my command? The potential was there, but since I didn’t control where the booster spheres were placed, it could be quite a while before I got to see it happen.

“You’re asking me?” Serp laughed.

“It’s your awakening.” I shrugged. It was obvious he was trying to play cute, and I wasn’t having any of it. “If you don’t know, I’ll just flip a coin and—”

“Wait!” Serp shouted. “I…” he paused. “Go with True Form. It’s the most useful in the short run and… I’d like it if you chose that.”

You really fancy that, do you? I looked at the section in question.

The only ability he would gain if I made that choice was “Minor Snake Form” allowing Serp to turn into a snake. That didn’t sound particularly powerful.

“Are you sure?”

“Trust me, old man. That’s the best you could get,” he insisted with absolute certainty.

I remained not entirely convinced. There was no indication that he needed flesh or blood in order to transform, so that was a positive. When combined with his other abilities, that could well turn out to be the winning choice. Would it help when fighting against statues and undead, though?

“Seriously, old man. You won’t regret it.”

I closed my eyes. It was a well-established fact that the more one trusted anything in the Grand Arcana, the more they opened themselves to failure and betrayal. There had been a handful of people I had trusted in my previous run, and one of them had betrayed me. Could the same be said about Serp? Was he going to betray me, or did he really consider that choice to be best?

“I’m trusting you on this,” I said.

It was a huge gamble, but I relied strongly on the fact that I had managed to drill some sense into the blade up to now. If worst came to worst, I mostly needed the awakening to get near Max once it was over. Everything remained a bonus.

“True Form,” I declared.

The moment I did, the room shredded into ribbons of platinum.
