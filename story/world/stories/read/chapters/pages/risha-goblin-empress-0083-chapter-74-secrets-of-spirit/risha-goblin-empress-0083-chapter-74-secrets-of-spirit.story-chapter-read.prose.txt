The next hour had Risha’s brain dizzy with spiritual symbols and their meanings. It was an entirely new language, except one that her [Brood Mother] skill didn’t translate for her.

This wasn’t what she had expected when she found the spirit shaman. It reminded her of when the older hunters would take her out and teach her how to read tracks, except, much more boring, with no physical activity to help her stay focused. The Old Brother was clearly unused to teaching, but he had an infinite patience to him that made up for his lack of experience.

So she persevered, carving each symbol into the air with her spirit as she learned how to form her own contract with the spirits of the spirit realm.

Thankfully, after some time, the ancient shaman decided it had been enough.

He stood up from the bed he’d been sitting on as he watched her and stretched his arms over his head.

“So, little sister,” he walked towards the door, motioning for her to follow him. “What kind of spirit do you wish to bind to yourself?”

Risha frowned, thinking over all of the spirits she had seen so far. Many were simple representations of the elements. Water, wind, earth, fire, like Igni. But that wasn’t quite right, Igni was more than just fire. She’d also seen the spirits of the living, like her children, or the shaman in front of her. What else could be a spirit?

“What makes a spirit?” Risha asked.

The Old Brother smiled at her as he stepped through the door of the cabin and back into the void of stars, “That’s an excellent question. Spirits are life given form. Everything has a little bit of life to it, whether it’s the mountain that reaches for the sky, the stream that flows through the world, or the tiny goblin listening to the stories of her older brother. Maybe, even the worlds themselves have life to them, but such spirits only speak to us through the words of the system, if they speak to us at all.”

Risha stepped into the void, a pathway appearing under her feet as she followed the Shaman. “What spirits do you have bound to you?”

He placed a finger to his lips, a small smile showing, “That’s a secret.”

She stayed quiet as the two of them walked, stars shining both close and far in the distance. “I don’t know how to choose a spirit.”

“Hmm. A problem I should’ve expected, I suppose.” The older goblin looked up, his gaze moving through the stars as he searched for something Risha couldn’t see. “A spirit worthy of the Goblin Mother, where are you? What are you? Oh. Now isn’t this interesting.”

He grabbed Risha’s hand, and a spirit enveloped the both of them. Long wings extended above them as they were carried in purple talons. Risha looked up to see the scaled underbelly of the spirit as it flapped its wings, and they flew through the void, stars passing beside them in an instant as they traveled.

The spirit dropped them on the pathway, then disappeared into the ancient shaman.

Risha opened her mouth to ask questions, but a finger on her lips stopped her.

“It’s a secret, little sister.” He gave her a mischievous smile, then turned to one of the stars.

The star’s light wavered strangely, as if she were seeing it in the reflection of a pond.

“I don’t know your name.” The shaman said as he walked up to the star, peering at it curiously. He stood straight, and turned to Risha, “I think you’ll find what you’re looking for in here, but I won’t be able to follow you.”

Risha walked up, looking at the strange star as she tried to place why it seemed so strange. “Why not?”

“It has a rather strange method of keeping people out. A level cap.”

“Level cap?”

“Yep, any creature above level one hundred can’t enter.” He tilted, then shook his head “Who did they expect to travel around here at that low of a level?”

Risha turned to the other goblin, wondering what level he was at. She’d heard of skills that some scouts had gained that allowed them to see the status of others, but she didn’t have that kind of skill at her disposal. She turned back to the star, “What’s in there?”

“Hmm. No clue exactly, but my intuition points to it quite strongly.” His eyes flickered, reading something she couldn’t see. “There’s some danger, so if you don’t feel that you’re ready, we can continue our search.”

“No.” Risha held out her hand, her eyes narrowing. “There’s a spirit that suits me in there?”

“Could be, or it could be something else you need. Like I said,” He paused with a slight frown, “I don’t have a name for this star.”

Risha placed her hands on the swords at her waist, then stepped into the star.

“I might be gone when you get back, do not worry. I’ll leave something to lead you home.” The shaman’s last words reached her as her world was enveloped in light.

***

The world changed around her, until her feet landed on a carpeted stone floor. Daylight filtered in through crystal windows, illuminating the head of a stairwell.

Risha looked behind her, only to see a stone wall blocking the way. She turned back to the stairwell, and started down it. The light from the crystal windows quickly disappeared as the stairs spiraled into darkness.

Her eyes adjusted to the dark, seeing everything in pale hues of white and gray. Still, she went deeper, until the stairs opened up into a large hall. Pillars rose from the floor, supporting a roof that even her sharp vision couldn’t see.

Behind the pillars, things of pitch black stalked the darkness, watching her.

Risha wrapped the chains of her swords around her arms, then drew them. Crimson and Scarlet vibrated with bloodlust, ready for her.

Risha stepped forward and they struck.

Long spears of darkness flew through the darkness, seeking to pierce her.

Risha twisted under a limb, then threw Crimson at the pillar closest to one of the shadowy creatures, where it pierced deep.

She pulled herself to Crimson, then pushed off the pillar, pulling her sword with her as she sliced through one of the creatures.

Black blood fell as the creature went limp.

She threw Scarlet, piercing deep into one of them. Then Crimson followed, taking the life of yet another.

She sent spirit through the chains, pulling her swords free as more of the creatures ran through the darkness.

She smiled wide, the swords lighting up the darkness with the purple light of her spirit. She spun, the chains loud as her swords swung into her featureless enemies.

Blood fell and time lost meaning as she fought through them in an endless onslaught. [Mother’s Ferocity] and [Pack Leader: White] enhanced her movements as the battle continued, her sharp smile only growing wider.

Eventually, they stopped coming, and Risha stood alone in a pile of bodies, heaving with exertion.

She pulled her swords to her hands, and they hummed with happiness, sated on the blood of her enemies.

Risha walked forward, her leather boots squelching as she walked through the large hall to a pair of featureless stone doors.

Risha sheathed her swords, then braced her shoulder against the doors. They opened slowly, into a room lit with arcane symbols, only some of which Risha could read. A large creature sat in the middle of the room, chains holding it to the floor.

She recognized the arcane symbols for binding, holding the creature to this room.

It stood to its full height as she entered, looking much like the panther she’d killed not long ago, except larger. Its black fur was speckled with violet stars in the shape of the night sky, and on its forehead, a curved white moon shone.

As she studied it, it studied her, its eyes watching her with age-old exhaustion and anger.

“Another challenger seeking the treasure I guard. You run to your death, goblin.”

“You guard treasure?” Risha asked, images of shinies filling her mind.

The large panther’s eyes narrowed, “You know not what you seek? Yes, I have been trapped here, forced to guard that which mortals desire beyond all else.”

Risha paused at the creature’s words. She looked into the eyes of the monster, then sat down on the dusty floor. “Trapped? Forced? Why?”

The panther paused, taken aback “Do not think to fool me with your tricks, goblin. I have faced your kind before.”

Risha raised her eyebrows, then wrapped the chains of her swords around their handles, leaving her arms free. Then she unstrung the belt that held her swords, and set it behind her, all while the panther watched. “Risha likes tricks, but this isn’t one. What’s your name?”

The Panther didn’t answer right away, but the tenseness of its muscles left as it watched her. Finally, it sat on its haunches, “I am Nyx.”

“I am Risha.”

“Yes, you spoke your name already.”

“But that was not an introduction. Risha likes to slip into old talk when she wants to remind herself of who she is, or when she forgets to correct herself.”

“I see.”

“Why is Nyx trapped here?”

“Why do you care, goblin?”

“Not goblin. Risha. It’s a good name, you can call it.”

“Why do you care, Risha?”

Risha lifted her chin, her body stilling with seriousness. “Because I don’t like seeing any creature trapped.”

An old anger entered the panther’s eyes as its hackles rose. Still, it answered her question. “They needed a guardian. Who better than a beast? Do not think you can access the treasure by befriending me. It is only my life that will allow you into the vault.”

“Risha already has lots of shinies.” Risha thought back to her collection of stones and crystals back at the city, and smiled in joy. Would sleeping with them be too uncomfortable? No, it couldn’t be. She shook her head, and focused back on the moment. “I can free you, Nyx.”

The panther raised its head as its hackles lowered, “What do you ask for in exchange? If you seek to bind me yet again, shaman, I will not stand for it.”

Risha blinked, she hadn’t even thought about binding the creature as one of her spirits until it was mentioned. It wasn’t too great of a loss in her mind, she still didn’t know which spirit she wanted anyway, and the Shaman could help her search the stars for something else.

Risha nodded, “I won’t bind you then. Just don’t hurt me or mine.”

The cat stared at her, then huffed. “I cannot give you nothing.”

Risha hummed, “I am searching for a spirit to help me in my fight for my people. Perhaps you can help me in my search.”

“Perhaps.”

Risha smiled, then stood up. She raised her hand, “That’s good enough for me. Can I touch you?”

The cat narrowed its eyes, then nodded.

Risha stepped forward until she could place her hand on the soft fur of its cheek. This close, the maw of the creature loomed large, capable of eating half of her in one bite.

[Bond Breaker].

Risha’s spirit flowed through her, into the cat, then into the room. The space glowed with her power, as her own spirit extended out of her, taking on the shape that it did when she created one of her spirits.

Wings, scales, fur, made up of the features of her children, Risha’s spirit could not be held by the room, and the floor shook under her power.

The chains on the Nyx’s ankles shattered, then the mystic symbols of the room flickered. A force came from all around her, attempting to stop Risha.

Risha’s spirit roared, refusing to let anything bind her children. The force battled against her, forcing her to fight for every destroyed arcane symbol, until, all at once, the force left.

The mystic symbols shattered into nothing, and the room only remained lit by Risha’s spirit.

Risha’s spirit retreated into her once more, and she stepped back.

Nyx stared at her in surprise, her tail raised high.

Risha turned around, and started walking towards the hall she’d fought through. She trusted Nyx’s words that the vault could only be opened with the panther’s death, so the only way out was back up.

Risha grabbed her swords on her way, and wrapped the belt back around her.

“Wait.”

Risha stopped, and looked back to Nyx.

“I will not be trapped. But… you need a spirit to fight with you.”

Risha nodded.

Nyx bowed her head, and the stars left her black coat, floating through the room into another language of symbols. These ones too, were beyond Risha’s understanding.

“My power is yours, as a companion, and friend. In exchange, I will use your spirit to sustain myself in the physical realms.”

Risha fully turned to the panther, and walked over with confident steps. She placed her forehead against the moon on Nyx’s head. “That is a good pact. Risha promises that you will be protected and cared for as one of her children, and as one of her friends.”

The white mark of the moon glowed on Risha’s hand, a symbol of their contract.
