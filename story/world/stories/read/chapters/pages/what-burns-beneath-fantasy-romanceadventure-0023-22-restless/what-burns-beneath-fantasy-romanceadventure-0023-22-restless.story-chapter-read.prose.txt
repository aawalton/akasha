I tried going to sleep early. I was unsuccessful.

Our dinner had been tasty, though devoid of any meat. I’d managed to sniffle away my remaining tears. I was safe, at least for the moment, and in a genuinely lovely place with good food and some good company and music and I was going to enjoy myself, dammit.

Teela had chattered happily about her home city. It was a place she spoke of fondly, but with a dispirited twist to her lips. She told us that Fae-touched, rare though they were, used to be treated very kindly in Respite. Her mother had come into a large inheritance shortly before she was with child- Teela didn’t go into the specifics of any of this, and I didn’t pry- and had been able to purchase a small cottage in one of the city’s wealthier districts. Teela’s childhood had been a kind one. When she came of age to pursue a trade- or marriage, though she mumbled with pink cheeks and downcast eyes that courtship was very likely ‘not in the cards’ for her, to which Renner had once again muttered that ‘people were morons’- she found work in the primary temple to Virtue.

Apparently Respite had quite a few temples. But the grandest, the one formed of cascading waterfalls and stained glass and tiered crystal fountains, was a monument to the young goddess who had poured herself out as blessed water. Teela described the temple grounds in exquisite detail, her hands and expressions growing more animated with every word.

We’d been interrupted by a lanky, tawny-haired young man. Freckles dappled his nose and cheeks. He’d shyly asked Teela to dance.

I’d sat watching, biting down a smile, as the pair whirled and laughed amidst the townsfolk. Her hat stayed low and she kept her cloak on, but her eyes shone merrily in the dancing firelight.

It was a wonderful sight. My thoughts had turned to Respite, to the temple she’d spoken of with such awe, and for a moment I let myself think that perhaps one day I could see something so beautiful.

That had turned my musings maudlin again, as I considered the last truly beautiful place I had seen; his house. With its alabaster stairs and silver lights and dangling jewels. A prison moulded from cold, impassive grace.

I'd stared into my empty mug, tongue trapped between my teeth and knuckles growing white. Then I'd felt Renner's eyes on me, too-focused on my sudden distress. I'd shoved my plate away and practically fled up the stairs.

I bet the Respite temple is more wonderful than a thousand of his diamonds and carpets. I bet it’s warm and happy and full of life and safe.

I stared up at the ceiling. Dusk had fallen, and the only light in my small room came from the cloud-hazed moon and a few pale, distant stars. I’d changed and crept into bed, growing steadily numb with dread.

He hasn’t hurt me yet. And he… surely he won’t do anything too terrible until he has what he wants. And Teela will wake me up as soon as the sun rises. It’s going to be alright.

Whatever my mind said, my body was in full disagreement. Every calming thought was overpowered by a racing pulse and muscles that refused to relax.

After resigning myself to the fact that I simply wasn’t tired enough to fall asleep and face whatever horrors lay in wait, I opted to go back to the common room. Two cups of fragrant ciderwine had left my head fuzzy and pleasantly warm. Perhaps another would be enough to lull my racing heart.

It was only Master Lewen and a few older townsfolk still in the inn. The cheerful, lively atmosphere of evening had been replaced by quiet chatter and the gentle clink of mugs against tabletops.

I approached the innkeeper with a smile, which he returned warmly.

“Everything alright, Miss? I know those rooms can get a bit drafty; I can send one of the lads to fetch an extra quilt, if you like.”

“My room is very nice, thank you. I just can’t seem to fall asleep.”

“Ah.” He winked at me. “Well in that case, I’ve got a nice strong malt on tap. Not the best flavor, but it’d put a bull to rest.”

I grinned, shaking my head. “I think that sounds a bit too strong for me. But I’d love some more of your specialty, if you have any.”

“Of course, Miss.”

I settled near the fireplace with my mug and runebook. Looking at the familiar symbols and smooth parchment had been the last thing on my mind, lately, and it was time to rectify that.

The page after Veth was a criss-cross pattern of swirls. The word Luridel lay scrawled underneath. Looking at this one always made my skin tingle. I’d tried using it, once, on impulse, while tending one of Marion’s wilted tomato plants. Nothing had happened, aside from me vomiting onto the poor thing. I hadn’t tried it again.

But… looking at it now, my head wasn’t pounding. No spots danced behind my eyes, no blackness creeped at the edge of my vision. There was only a thrill of nervous excitement.

I promised Renner I wouldn’t do any magic in Snowmelt. Teela agreed that it was a bad idea. So I won’t try it. But… maybe soon. I wonder what it does.

My fingertips trembled against the page. I drank the image in, wondering… until a sniffle pulled my attention away.

The stablemaster sat alone in a corner, gnarled hands wrapped around a mug. He was staring into its contents, mouth twisted into a grimace. His gaunt head was slowly shaking back and forth.

He looks… miserable. I suppose it must be very difficult for his livestock to be gone. Especially if renting horses out is how he makes his living.

I watched as the man lifted his mug, taking a long drink. Dark brown liquid sloshed over the rim, dribbling down into his beard. He wiped at it, sniffling again. Moisture shone beneath his eyes. It almost looked like he’d been weeping.

My brows knotted together. I glanced towards the innkeeper, but he seemed engrossed in cleaning tables at the far end of the room and was paying no attention to this particular patron. My gaze drifted back towards the stablemaster just as he swiped his mangled hand at his eyes.

My heart sank. Hadn’t I made exactly the same gesture earlier, when trying to hide my own distress? I peered into my fragrant drink, uncertain of what to do.

He probably doesn’t want a stranger to come up and check on him. He didn’t seem like the type to confide in someone he doesn’t know.

All the same, I felt as though I had to do something. Another peek showed that his shaggy head was bowed, brows furrowed and thin lips a miserable rictus. He seemed to be deep in thought.

I sat quietly, sipping at my rich, warm drink until the last drop was gone, then approached the stablemaster’s table.

“Are you alright?”

He jolted back as surely as if I’d struck him, head snapping up and blue eyes widening. He almost seemed… frightened.

The look was gone in an instant, replaced by mottled anger. His good hand clenched around the mug. “What?”

I took another step back, already regretting my decision to approach. Oh, well done. Probably should have left him alone. But now I’ll look even more strange if I just walk away without saying anything, so…

I cleared my throat, offering a meek smile. “I-I’m sorry to bother you. I didn’t mean to… that is, I just… I thought you looked… unhappy.”

He searched my face, baleful eyes watering. “What?”

I hugged the book to my chest, heat rushing into my cheeks. “I’m sorry. I didn’t think… I’m sorry for interrupting. I shouldn’t have bothered you. Can I pay for your drink?”

His mangled hand twitched, knuckles growing white. “No,” he ground out through clenched teeth, not meeting my eyes, “No, you can’t pay for my… no.”

“Oh.” I edged backwards, feeling entirely foolish. “A-alright, then, I’m sorry, have a good…” The words died in my throat.

The stablemaster hunched over his mug sullenly. Behind him, wavering in the dwindling firelight, his shadow remained sitting upright.

I took another step back, breath catching in my throat. No. No. What in the realms… what is this?! What is wrong with me?

I stared, horrified and speechless, waiting for the shape to correct itself. Like Agnes’ had. It loomed, stark and motionless.

The stablemaster grunted and slashed his mangled hand through the air, clearly wanting me to leave. And behind him on the wall, the night-black arm remained still.

I clutched my book, white knuckles gripping the spine like a shield, and hurried towards the staircase on shaking legs. I nearly tripped twice on the way. I stopped at the top landing, heart racing.

There’s something wrong with me. There has to be. Or… or there’s something wrong with him. And Agnes. Salt, what do I do?

Ice blossomed in my belly as another possibility came to mind.

Or… or it’s not me. And it’s not him or her or anything human; there’s something else here.

The terrible thought grew like a pit beneath my feet, threatening to swallow me whole. I shrank against the wall, suddenly quite sure that this place was anything but safe.
