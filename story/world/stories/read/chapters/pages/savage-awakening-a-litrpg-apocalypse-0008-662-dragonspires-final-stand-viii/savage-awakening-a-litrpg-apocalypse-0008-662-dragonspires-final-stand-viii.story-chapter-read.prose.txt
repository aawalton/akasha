He'd been in the void before—he'd spent months there on his journey to the Ruins, and then to Boot Camp. He had no issues operating here.

He saw hundreds of Greater Endbringers in the far distance, all arranged in a single grand fleet. Half seemed to be of Prime hordes, the others miscellaneous Greater Endbringers.

It had to be the bulk of the Greater Endbringers that Malzareth had left—almost the entirety of their fighting force.

And beyond them were stationed the Primes. He couldn't see them from this distance but he could sense their souls in the Astral Plane.

And then, beyond even that—flickering at the end, a dimmed soul split wide open with the Sage's silver...

He felt an icy coldness in his chest.

It all receded incredibly fast, and he knew in a straight footrace, in the long term, there wasn't a chance he could catch them.

"Support me," he told the Lord-Commander. "I'll run a path straight down the center."

The Lord-Commander technically didn't take orders from him, but—

"I'll clear," said the Lord-Commander, nodding resolutely.

Then Zane stepped, and gave chase.

It was just him and the Lord-Commander, who drew his bow even as Zane rushed forth—they were still the only folk from Dragonspire who'd broken through.

At the same time, Endbringers rained down. A squadron of two dozen descended through that tunnel of night, hurling spells—

He was through them with two Supernova Steps, and he left a wall of Star-Crushing Slash light behind him. Twelve new souls joined his collection, adding to his silver bar.

He kept moving, faster now than ever—still using Supernova Steps instead of Annihilation Charge to prioritize speed. Behind him brilliant silver arrows machine-gunned around him—silver now that the Lord-Commander's Supreme had passed.

After seeing what he'd done to those twelve Endbringers, it seemed some executive decision had been made by either Malzareth or the Primes.

That fleet of Greater Endbringers was unleashed en masse.

Fifty, then a hundred, and more descending the tunnel—

There was only one way past, and that was through.

Mirror Drakes and Lesser Nightmare Dragons—shadows draped on bone, the minions of Gilgoroth—fell by the dozens, submerged in his Axe light. Wedges of Supernova white carving through the masses—

As he chased he felt quite clearly that he wasn't stable here. He couldn't be, as a True God, since he didn't have a Universe to anchor him in the void; sooner or later he'd fall out of the void. But he'd already known from the start that this wouldn't be a drawn-out race.

From that point on, Zane went on an absolute mission.

His Slashes fell at speed, cascading relentlessly; the silver in his eyes shone brilliantly, even brighter; in some ways it was quite useless to throw an army at him. It was simply more ammunition for his Supreme.

He just needed to get in range with a certain weapon.

And then he'd broken past their ranks. He was out in open space once more.

He felt the pace at which Malzareth was moving and realized it was slower than it'd been back when the dragon was still coming to Dragonspire.

He realized with the immensity of the damage that'd been done to its soul, it couldn't move as fast—it couldn't use its Laws nearly as well.

A realization which felt like a switch turning in his mind. He knew, in that moment, he was definitively capable of this.

With that, he changed his weapon.

He started down a sequence of Annihilation Charge steps.

It was at this point that a Prime broke free. Gilgoroth left its Master's side and dove.

Zane stepped up to meet it, stepped harder, even—then, in two hard Steps, carved past it. Simply went around the two giant, pink, dream-like claws descending on his head, moving in a clean right angle. Gilgoroth's partly ghostly form streaked past him with several miles separating them.

An instant later one of Stegar's bodies streaked for Zane, this one sleek, built for speed—as it neared it disassembled into a flood of components, and it was impossible to avoid being clasped by at least a few dozen of them.

But he just let loose a vast burst of pure essence, put on another burst of acceleration, and shrugged them right off—breaking free yet again. It was an immense waste of essence, using such vast quantities in this loose, omni-directional way, but essence was essentially free to him right now.

And then his harpoon was in hand and he knew, instinctively, this would likely be his one chance.

It was as clear an opening as he could've hoped for. Two Primes at his back, the next two in motion, but too far distant, and with that distant dot of golden blood in his sights—

He wound his fist back and it was so clear, in that moment in his mind's eye, the vision of it—

His harpoon began its forward motion, just.

In the same instant he felt a vast figure in the Astral Plane approaching at what should've been impossible speeds from behind; it'd strike him mid-throw. He stepped once more, a flicker of irritation crossing his expression, letting Gilgoroth's gleaming form sail by—

The Prime seemed fully a ghost right now, like it'd phased totally out of existence. Its appearance was translucent, quite pale. It moved like it had no body at all; he realized this when it whirled and dove at him again, and simply phased through the chains he threw at it, shimmering silver as it did.

And then he felt steel slam into him from behind, and the world went sideways.

It was like he'd been encased in power armor, binding his entire torso. He gritted his teeth, shucking more of Stegar's body parts away, but he was already thrown, and badly, and he'd had nothing to anchor himself to begin with—

He knew, even as he fell, that his chance had gone to a sliver.

In that instant a bone-laced shadow phased into him and materialized.

Bone claws snatched him over the arms, and he felt himself break through the walls of the void.

He howled as he did, still fighting to claw his way back in, fighting to keep that distant signature in his sights—

Then he was gone, the void shattering behind him—tumbling through open space, two Primes still binding his arms, holding him down.

Gilgoroth made a single, triumphant sound.

It'd won the exchange but it hadn't yet recognized the implications of this moment.

Its claw was forced apart. There was a series of bright snapping sounds as the steel of dozens of Stegar's body parts split at once.

An altogether different weapon fell to Zane's fists then.

Gilgoroth was known as an Elder Nightmare Dragon because its powers lay predominantly in matters of the soul. It'd grasp you with its cursed claws, or hypnotize you with its eyes, lull you into deep dreams, and there, eviscerate your psyche. Its Supreme was primarily active post-hypnosis, and it could easily kill a top Hegemon ranker in seconds. It had, in the past.

It was its misfortune that in this moment, it faced a man whose soul made all of that meaningless.

Its claws shattered against his mind without even scratching it, the way brittle bone might shatter against a steel cage—the guarantee of his Title.

Then it had to contend with the consequences of its actions.

120 slashes.

All chained, in a row, with no rest.

It spoke to the difference between a Greater Endbringer and a Prime that Gilgoroth was capable of taking all those strikes. It was a lesser Prime, but it was still a fully-fledged Prime, and it had ways to survive.

It drew up heavy, silvery shields—which fell in an instant to that brilliant immensity. It substituted itself for a specter of itself—but that specter was soon overwhelmed and it was forced back into reality. It tried sinking in its dream-pink claws again and again, even shattering his Diamondjade pagoda, but its curses, which were the true threat, simply wouldn't take.

It simply faced 120 Star-crushing Slashes in the other direction.

Stegar's body attempted to buy time for its brethren to flee—but if a full Prime could not contend with Zane in this moment, what hope did a fraction of a Prime have?

When the last slash fell, reality was a mess of clean scars afterward, a portrait ripping open so many voids that seemed a testament to the state of the man who'd made it, a pure expression of his feeling in that moment.

By the end of this fight, he was still brimming with life.

The evidence of it was there—that silver bar above his head, well over double the length it'd been at the start.

The notification popped up. Galaxy-wide, he'd later learn—

A Prime Endbringer has been slain.

And then the rest poured in.

Level up!

Level up!

Level up! ...

Essence Level 682 -> 699

Maxed True God, just like that, easily.

And then—

Skill up!

Star-Crushing Slashes VII -> VIII

It just kept coming, and the most startling notification was his Credits.

Calculating Credits...

Summing...

Credits earned!

+27,420 Credits

So many Credits, it practically made money lose meaning.

He didn't care about any of that in this moment.

He knew he'd lost it.

He had no way to enter the void here and he was so distant it took a little time simply for Command to locate him precisely.

He was the strongest he'd ever been in that moment, shining with essence and life, and there was nothing he could do.

He ended the fight kneeling there, in the middle of nowhere, hundreds of clicks from civilization. Too distant now to tap into the local speaking channels, even.

He made himself stand.

He bolted in the direction of the lines. He still tried, with all he had left—he spent the rest of his Supreme in pursuit.

It was hours later until Command could retrieve him.

By then he'd received the news—his try was, by far, the closest.

It was gone.

When he'd asked where, Reina said the Scryers only had an estimate of where they were heading, but they could all deduce where.

It was making for the single most defensible place it had in all of Dragonspire.

The Superdungeon, the seat of its power.

By this time, the Sage was lucid again, and Reina asked if he wanted to see the old fellow.

"We'll have to put him into an induced coma to stabilize him," she said. At his expression—"It'll only be temporary," she assured him. "For a few days. It's so the healers can extract the Destruction from his soul."

The Sage was a little grumbly when Zane came up to meet him—mostly because of all the bandages they had on him and the three Empyrean healers they had assigned. The Carved Saint had helped freeze portions of his chest.

He perked up when he saw Zane.

It took all the healers to get him down again, but not before he wrapped Zane in a hug—"A godsdamned Prime!"

The Sage seemed to want to talk about it a bit more, but now wasn't the time.

"Just seems like a big fuss," the Sage told Zane, glancing at the bandages. "S'pose I'll go through with it... well, hells."

He glanced back at the battlefield. "What a fight."

Zane nodded. "You're sure you feel fine?"

"I've felt better," chuckled the Sage. Then—"Lad, the only thing I regret about that fight is that that spear didn't go just a few inches farther. So you get that look off your face!"

He seemed in quite good spirits, which was a relief.

"They'll put me under for a bit. Then you let me know how it went down on your end," the Sage continued.

"I'll find you after this's done," Zane told him.

The last thing the Sage did before he went under was to say, "Oh. And lad..."

And his eyes hardened, and Zane saw a familiar brightness to them. The Sage grinned, but there was nothing light to it anymore; it was a grin that was pure Sage.

"You finish that fucker," said the Sage. "You understand?"

Zane set his jaw. "I will."

They clasped hands and sealed the promise.
