Samantha’s other big bit of news was simply the return on information from Crow, as gleaned from the Consolidator’s skull. She wasn’t exactly surprised that it required something from all of the Bridge of Edges locations. It made a lot of sense that history fell into these wells the Consolidator had created, making for important places and drawing important people. That it was tied to magic connected the dots back to her even more. The more it was carved into space as important, the more that tied to Azure, in turn. Thousands of years, on top of thousands of years? Yeah. That’ll do it.

Ten locations needed out of eleven, one false, one new. I don’t get anything intuitive about it at the moment. Perhaps we’re better off gathering them first. That is likely to be the safest method.

Many of the locations were now abandoned, ironically. Some were technically in the range of control of the Sage. Had he simply snubbed his nose at them?

She was confident that one of the locations was not the Capital. The Sage had mountain-sized pride, and he’d deliberately made his own, new thing there from the get-go. It was his, through and through, and no one else’s. Set apart. If anything, he pulled power away from many of the other existing locations. That definitely tracked if they had catacombs. He could’ve plundered them of their most powerful dead to take them to his twisted underground. And then, he deliberately hadn’t done so with such locations as the Consolidator’s tomb, because he wanted the reach in a faraway land.

The number ten was also interesting. An important number. Its association was not to common deity, such as three, seven, and nine. It had more commonality, but in a grand fashion. It was a number of the universe, of the cosmos. It was both divine and ever-present…

It struck her like a bell.

We’re ten, aren’t we? Ten spheres. Ten domains. One of ten, of all things. Azure, Crow, Bast, Tashome, Orswyth, Dax, my unnamed sister, Glae, the Sage… and me. With seven cards played. Death, The Lovers, The Chariot, The Hanged Man, The Hermit, The Emperor, The High Priestess. Presumably, Glae plays himself, and the Sage does the same. Right? I haven’t played myself. Can I?

She felt a weird sense of dread and uncertainty about it.

Holy hell, I don’t know! I was the Empress in the beginning, but how much is it a face? I look in the mirror, and I see the Magician, but perhaps that is the Sage? Am I just half of one card, The Lovers? Er. No. No, that’s not right. What else? At one time, I thought Tashome was the Fool. Perhaps I am.

It seemed clear that she couldn’t play herself. She felt that. Either not at all, or not yet. Whether that was because she was uncertain, there was a needed timing, or it was intrinsic already somehow and thus irrelevant, she didn’t know.

Samantha had to pause and sheathe this pondering mid-conversation with Crow, because he was still discussing what else the Consolidator spoke on.

The precursor-being knew nothing of the staff. That was odd. She was so certain it was his. In any case, she’d handed the damn thing off to Zephyr — not literally, of course, since it was cursed — and instructed him to glean what he could, resolve what to do with it, or hand it off to Tashome otherwise.

High-level magic items were such a pain compared to the mid-level ones.

The spell information was useful. She would strongly consider the forcefield skill, though she wasn’t often fighting solo against large hordes. Information on ‘mana armor’... that was damned tempting, though physical threats had become a teensy bit secondary compared to those of the mind and spirit.

Before she conspired with Azure a bit on the new ‘locations’ information, she fucked off to Heaven and a convenient empty field — except for cabbits… whoa, there were a lot of them… oh well… A convenient, mostly empty field to utilize [Mirroring Duplicates], specifically Strange Phantasms 0 and 1, i.e., Ozzy and Rally.

Rally simply nodded in greeting, floating just above the ground, her staff floating next to her.

Ozzy threw her hands out wide and made miniature grand fireworks detonate around her. “Ta-da! I’ve arrived again, Boss! Whatcha nee-” Her ears suddenly drooped as she saw Rally, her smile wiping off her face. “What’s she doing here?”

Rally crossed her arms. Her frigid gaze, set in an already inherently cold, alabaster face, was cast with derision at the other phantasm. “You took the words right out of my mouth, goblin.” Her staff seemed to ‘nod’ agreement.

Ozzy bared her fangs in a snarl. “Excuse you?! I’m a gremlin! Get it right! You do not get goblins and gremlins wrong — not to their face!”

“Yes, such a huge difference.” The staff somehow ‘twirled’ mockingly.

Ozzy glanced at the staff. “Th-that thing isn’t sapient! You’re just doing that!”

“Am I?” If a staff could make a rude gesture, it would have. Somehow, it gave the feeling that it had just made an extremely rude gesture.

Ozzy’s mouth fell open, and her eyes went wide with rage at the presumed insult. “Why, I oughta-!”

“Hey, hey, enough of that!” Samantha called. “Play nice, you two. I need to ask you both something, and I figured the confirmation either way would come from having two key perspectives. Okay?” She paused with insistent airs, making sure she had their undivided attention, and noted they were grudgingly pulling their eyes from each other to her. Both nodded curtly. “Alright. Well, it’s kinda simple. Can we, or have we ever… played ourselves? Our card? Like with the others.”

They both nodded confidently and said simultaneously, “You’re already doing it.”

Samantha blinked from one to the other phantasm. “Okay… elaborate, please?”

The other two Iterations glared at each other. Rally held up her finger and her eyebrows. “One at a time, now. Me first.” Though Ozzy rolled her eyes, she didn’t object as Rally turned to Samantha and continued, “The card play of the present Iteration is the conduct of her life. Either it reflects correctly, or we fail, yes? Succeed, and it is the capstone cementation. It’s as simple as that.”

Ozzy lifted her finger with a smug grin split between the two other Iterations. “No, it’s not! Haha, you’re so stupid, Rally. Primitive, really.”

Rally rose higher in the air, her eyes started glowing, and the staff went to her hand, while still shaking with rage. “You… dare to insult me?!”

Samantha used [Conjure] to put a thick wall between them. “No fighting, Rally! No insulting, Ozzy!”

“Bah. Fine!” Rally said, lowering back down.

“Fine, yes,” Ozzy said, still smug. “As I was saying… The card play is what you’re already doing, and how you do it. But at the end, at some point, you will actually make that final play. In relevance. It’s necessary. Get it?”

Samantha nodded slowly. “How will I know? When to do it.”

“You just will. You’ll get there so long as you execute who you are into reality. It leads itself up to that moment. Obviously, you’re not there yet. It isn’t easy getting there, either. You’re talking about the final victory. It’s not like you’re about to unleash it on Matrazere. All of those dominoes are set and ready to drop, right? Er, well, I’m mixing metaphors. I really wanted to include ‘drop.’ Uh. House of cards is better, maybe?”

Rally shook her head in exasperation. “Beware the play of your enemy. Or betrayals. At any time, your supposed allies could claim their card in play, now. If you do not maintain their loyalty, trust, and satisfaction, they will ultimately destroy your carefully laid plans at a key moment. Intuitively, the spite in their heart will lead them to inflict a crippling play, and all will be lost.”

“Metaphorically speaking,” Ozzy added, and made a thin smile.

Samantha pondered this. Currently, it didn’t seem likely she’d be betrayed by existing allies. Perhaps if she secured Glae? It seemed better not to. To keep him at arm’s length to avoid the backstab. Or constantly act while expecting it. But, aside from that… “So, does this essentially mean the Sage will have his own trump card?”

Rally sniffed. “Come now, dear, you didn’t expect less, did you? It will be a terrible altercation. Your greatest challenge by far — by an order of magnitude — and needing every sliver of advantage. Perhaps this… — she shrugged uncomfortably — “card play, this final act, is the ultimate attack to finish him… or somesuch.”

“One thing at a time, girlie!” Ozzy declared cheerfully as she put her fists on her hips. “Hey, you’re so much closer than the last time we talked! You’ll be closer still the next, I’m sure. The final answer to your question is that you can’t rush your life. It began somewhere, and it ends somewhere. The culmination is where the cards are all aligned — one way or another.”

Samantha returned Ozzy’s smile faintly and nodded to them both. “Alright, then. Thanks.” After a pause, she added, “I’m leaning toward The Virgin. What do you think?”

Ozzy laughed. Rally snickered and said, “Some ships sail yesterday.”

A cabbit came out of nowhere, jumping into Rally’s arms and startling her. The cabbit held up its hands and demanded, “Pets!”

Rally first blinked a few times, then frowned imperiously at the creature… and then began to pet it, at which point it settled down in her arms with its eyes closed and began to purr.

Samantha went invisible and hid to avoid roaming cabbit aggro, as she pondered things more. She wished she were the Magician. It nagged her that this was more logically her enemy.

When I reach the end, I’ll know. That’s when it’ll count. I don’t need it now. I have what I need to conquer what’s in front of me. I don’t fear the road to the end.

Eventually, Samantha rematerialized back to Itha Ranon in search of Azure, hoping to share some of the duties of the collection quest with her, and possibly get confirmation about her ideas on how to conclude the right locations to use. If anyone had a sense of those places, it was her.
