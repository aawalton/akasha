Master? I definitely didn’t remember that part of the ritual! Kauli hadn’t prepared me for this. Not only did I not expect this thing to talk, I didn’t expect it to regard me as its superior. The creature lowered itself, those violet orbs dimming slightly as it bowed before me.

I took a step back, completely caught off guard by this change of events! I came in here thinking I was going to be a meal!

‘Master... it has been so long.’ The creature’s voice resonated through my mind like dark water flowing into a deeper abyss. ‘You have returned in a new vessel.’

I stood frozen, my borrowed body trembling despite my best efforts to appear composed. This wasn't supposed to happen. The Dark One was meant to see me as Kauli, as a sacrifice, not... whatever this was!

Unfortunately for me, its kindness wouldn’t last for long. It was clear that it had mistaken me for someone else. When it realized that I wasn’t whoever it thought I was, things got ugly fast. One second I was being revered, and then the next, I was running for my life from a sonic blast!

Deep from its shadowy jowls, it let out a guttural roar that shook the entire cavern. Stalactites crashed around me as I dove behind the altar, narrowly avoiding being impaled. Waves of his sonic attack shook the ground, making my steps stagger. I tripped on my own foot and fell, nearly banging my head against a clutter of large rocks.

I turned around and hid behind those same rocks, covering my ears while thinking of a way to get close enough to it. Back to the original plan—nothing had changed. Well, besides the fact that I couldn’t hear Nolan anymore. It was a strange phenomenon, but not as strange as waking up the demon by just touching the ground. I didn’t even recite the prayer, my mere presence inviting the Dark One for dinner.

“Crap,” I seethed. I tried to summon my thralls again, but it was no use. “What’s the point of collecting monsters to serve my every command if I can't even summon them when I need them the most?!”

I’d deal with that problem later. Right now, I needed to focus on getting out of here alive.

As the Dark One’s roars subsided momentarily, I peeked around the rocks to assess my situation. Those violet eyes were scanning the chamber, searching for me. Shockingly enough, it had two forms—shadowy and smoky, and a more physical form that seemed to solidify when it was preparing to strike. I watched as it shifted between the two states, its bottom half extending into a pair of hind legs. When it finished transforming, all I saw was a fugly demonic looking hyena with a triple set of jaws and a back covered in bony spikes.

Patchy pale brown skin, a bony torso, and a stench that could knock a full sized bear to sleep. It was advancing toward the rocks where I hid, sniffing the air with nostrils that flared purple with each breath.

The Moonwater vial was still secured in my inventory, but I’d have to get close enough to use it. Those sonic blasts would wreck me if I charged in straight. So I pulled out the whip and the sword the shaman gave me, activated my armor, and gave myself a quick countdown before I burst from behind the rocks, my enhanced whip already swinging over my head as it readied to fire another sonic blast.

I tossed my special whip and snatched its snout, yanking it shut just as the blast was building. Its own sonic attack reverberated inside its jaws, releasing a satisfying crack that echoed through the cavern as the pressure built up within it. The Dark One thrashed wildly, trying to tug at my whip as purple mist leaked from between its clenched teeth. Honestly, I was hoping that its own sonic blast would tear it from the inside out, but I wasn’t that lucky. With a violent jerk of its massive head, the whip nearly yanked my arm from its socket. I dug my heels into the ground, opened my mouth, and let out an [Astral Blast].

With my recent skill level up, the blast was barely able to reach the beast. Unfortunately, it had dematerialized before my attack could do any real damage. The horror-show dispersed into a shadowy mist that swirled around the chamber like a sentient storm. My whip fell uselessly to the ground as there was nothing solid to grasp anymore.

My arm throbbed where it had nearly been dislocated. The shadows condensed in patches around the cavern, testing me while watching my movements. I could see those violet eyes forming and dissolving throughout the black haze, never staying in one place long enough for me to target it.

This was going to be annoying. It could shift between two states in the snap of a finger. How was I going to toss this Moonwater in its gullet?

All of a sudden, those violet eyes began to glow, the beast releasing two rays of beams at me!

I dropped and rolled, the beams cutting through the air where I had been standing. They hit the rocks behind me, turning them into a puddle of molten slag that hissed and bubbled.

“Oh come on!” I grumbled under my breath. “Sonic blasts and eye beams?!”

In my escape, my foot slipped into a crack on the ground, wedging my ankle at an awkward angle. Pain shot up my leg as I struggled to free myself. The shadowy demon quickly noticed, gathering more densely around me. Those violet eyes reappeared, multiplying throughout the swirling darkness, and then they unleashed another hyper beam blast.

I wrenched myself free and escaped, the beam following me as I ran for cover. I ran paths across the clearing, barely escaping its onslaught. My stamina was decent after the level up, so I had enough energy to spare. My only issue was trying to find out how to entice it to shift back to its solid form.

Forced behind rocks again, I scanned the area for options. I did a double take on the altar, the place where it’d first spawned. I furrowed my eyebrows—I just gave myself an idea….

I darted toward the altar with my sword still drawn. The beast immediately condensed, rushing toward me like a tidal wave. Perfect. I slashed at the stone surface of the altar, sending sparks flying as my blade scraped across the ancient markings.

It didn’t like that….

The shadowy mass coalesced rapidly, its violet eyes burning with rage as it began to reform into its physical state. Its jaws gnashed and foamed with purple spittle as it lunged at me, desperate to protect the altar.

I feinted left, then spun right, narrowly avoiding those massive teeth. The beast’s momentum carried past me, its claws scraping against the altar stone. I seized the moment, uncorking the Moonwater vial with my teeth while keeping my sword raised defensively. The hyena-demon whirled around, and it was then and there I realized I’d been impatient. It quickly materialized its tail and slapped the vial away from my hand, and then followed up with a rapid slash at my crossed arms over my face with its claws.

My armor took the brunt of the attack, but the force still sent me flying backwards. The Moonwater vial skittered across the cavern floor, stopping just shy of a deep fissure. I scrambled to my feet, cursing under my breath.

“No, no, no, no, no!” I watched as the beast ran parallel to me, heading for the vial.

Its muzzle curled into what could only be described as a grin; it knew exactly what I was after. I drew my sword, prepared to stop this prick at all costs!

“Oh no you don’t, asshole!” I used [Meteor Strike] to ward it from the vial before it could reach me.

The beast let out a shriek as my [Meteor Strike] landed, slamming into its flank with enough force to knock it sideways. I didn’t wait to see if it would recover. I sprinted toward the vial, my shoes sliding on the uneven stone floor.

Behind me, I heard the distinctive sound of the Dark One shifting forms again. Its shadows stretched across the cavern walls, racing to cut me off from the Moonwater.

I leapt and then dove into a roll, snatching the vial in one hand, and swinging my sword with the other as the demon materialized its large jaw to snap at me. The tip of my blade was caught in the roof of its mouth, and I used my feet to push its bottom jaw down to keep those razor-sharp teeth from closing on me. I begged my dragon strength to keep up with its resistance. There wasn’t a lot of Moonwater left in the vial, but I had only one shot at this. The beast’s hot, putrid breath washed over me as it struggled to bite down, and before it could hit me with a sonic blast, I tossed the vial down the hatch.

“Your days of ruining these people’s lives are over!”

The vial shattered between its teeth, releasing a burst of luminous liquid that poured down its throat. The beast froze, its jaws still locked around my sword. For a heartbeat, nothing happened.

Then its eyes bulged. Its jaws began to vibrate, and a sickening gurgle rose from deep within its chest. I yanked my sword free and scrambled backward just as the first wave of convulsions hit the creature.

The Dark One’s body began to glow from within, a violet light quickly replaced by silver brilliance that shone right through it. The beast looked like a glow stick before the lightshow died down, and all that was left was the unfiltered rage of an ancient undead hyena.

It glared me down, its muzzle low and its chewed up ears pulled back. Though weakened, the Dark One was far from defenseless. It had reverted to something more primal—a creature of muscle, bone, and instinct. And it was still multiple times my size.

It paced my way slowly, baring its yellow fangs. The hyena-demon growled, a normal sound this time—no sonic blast—no reality-warping vibrations. Just the threat of a very large, very angry predator. It charged, but something was different. Where before it had moved with supernatural speed, now it seemed... slower. The Moonwater had weakened it, cancelling out its dark powers, and hopefully, its hold on the village. It launched itself at me with its jaws wide, but I managed to sidestep and slash at its flank with my sword.

The blade bit deep, drawing a spray of dark blood. The creature snarled and then lunged, nearly catching my leg in its jaws. I brought my sword down on its back, but the bony spikes deflected the blade with a metallic screech. The impact sent vibrations up my arm, nearly causing me to drop my weapon.

The beast and I went back and forth. It seemed like it wasn’t able to transform into a shadow anymore, but what it lacked in black magic it made up for in stamina and resilience. With every slash I made on it, it answered back with more speed and determination. At one point it got close enough to me to ram my body into the wall. My armor was shot. I took massive damage, but I had my healer’s sword in my grip, the blade healing me gradually but surely.

With all of my tricks in my book, I was slowly eating through its HP. It did terribly against my acid breath, losing most of its adrenaline when it lost a chunk of its collar bone. I followed up with a [Vine Slash], wrangling its snout again. My vine released green misty toxin while providing me a way to keep the beast grounded. It tried to pull back with its hind legs, but my enhanced whip was ready for it. I roped it with a sharp snatch, making the Dark One topple onto its side. It thrashed and growled, but its movements grew steadily weaker as the toxin from my whip seeped into its system.

I approached cautiously. The beast's breathing was labored now, its once-violet eyes fading to a dull amber. It snapped weakly at me, but there was no real strength behind the attack.

“Not so tough without your fancy shadow tricks, are you?” I taunted, circling it.

The creature let out a pitiful whine that almost made me feel bad for it. Almost. I remembered the villagers' terrified faces, the devastation this thing had caused. No amount of pathetic whimpering would earn my sympathy.

“Time for your debut,” I raised my sword, ready to finish this once and for all. With a hard grunt, I impaled its neck, proceeding to cut through to decapitate it. The Dark One, the demon of Obayek village, was dead, and I was going to take its head as proof.
