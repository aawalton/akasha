With the decline of the church of Cruon, the Basilica Draconian had become more of a museum than a place of worship. There were still sermons, but they were much smaller than they had been back in the day. That didn’t mean that the trip was wasted even without winning the grand prize, though.

A wonderful mural took up the entire floor and could only be seen in its entirety from the pulpit. The artwork spiraled around and inward, much like Cruon’s seat outside, and showed off the history of the Paladona and the church.

It started with settlers in the area, hungry, dirty, and lost, seeing a dragon emerging from the ocean in the distance. The next one in line showed them offering Cruon fruits and vegetables. Then, teaching them how to fish. Building the settlement. Defending from monsters. Paladona growing along with the construction of the Basilica Draconian.

Cruon was at the very end, happily lounging in the ocean while surrounded by fishing boats. The city was in the background with the sun behind it, providing a wonderful scene for everyone who came to visit. There was nothing about her death, though that was only because the mural had been completed before then.

Feeling a newfound respect for how much the dragon had done for the city, they followed the queue that took them through the cathedral and back onto the streets. It was a great look back into history, even if it was one that was curated through a religious lens. The two thoroughly discussed how things might have gone if Cruon hadn’t been as helpful.

They wandered after that, as they were prone to do. There was far too much of Paladona to see in one day, and every aspect of the city grabbed their attention. New food demanded to be tried, street musicians called for them to dance, and shows in plazas celebrating Fustavele had them stopping to watch. They didn’t regret a single moment.

When the sky turned dark, they were finally ready to head back. Clay had conserved their energy by sticking to Trevor’s backpack for most of the day and had no problem getting their two riders back to their villa in record time.

This far from Paladona, the stars were bright enough to illuminate their surroundings well enough to see. The lights in the building that Lora was staying in were off and they found her lying on the beach and staring up at the sky with her arms behind her head.

“Are you doing alright, Lora?” Adabelle asked as Clay’s body shrunk low enough that they could walk out from underneath their rider’s legs. Which was exactly what they did.

“Oh, hello! You’re back!” she greeted, craning her neck to look towards the road. The woman grinned before relaxing again, throwing her arms into the sand. “Yup! Fine ‘n dandy, cotton candy. How was your trip into Paladona?”

“It went well,” the noblewoman replied as Trevor waited for Clay to shed their husk. “There was a little hiccup with the Quest Giver, but nothing that spoiled our mood too much.”

“Still a little indignant, though,” her husband muttered as he sent the stone slabs into his inventory one by one.

Lora flipped over in the sand before standing up. “I noticed that you two had a note on your door this morning. Surtla wasn’t supposed to skip out on the meeting. Did something happen?”

Adabelle was ready to let it go as just another outburst from a spellcaster who wasn’t used to being told no, but her husband was apparently more than just a little indignant. He wrapped his arms around Adabelle as Clay dove into the beach, and she wrapped her tail around his waist as far as it could go.

“My awesome and powerful wife won an extra reward from Tanazi. His hat, right?” Trevor asked, though it was clearly meant to be a rhetorical question.

“Right,” Lora answered anyway.

“We added it to the things we were giving away before I realized that, hey, maybe it didn’t count because it was something extra,” he continued. Adabelle rested her hands on his, patting them gently as she let him get it out of his system. “When I brought it up, he begrudgingly admitted that it was ours, but he didn’t have any intention of telling us that before.”

“What? No!” the woman gasped.

“Right? And then, get this, when we told him we wanted to keep it, he tried to buy it from us,” Trevor said, running a thumb along the back of Adabelle’s hand. “When that didn’t work, he insulted my wife for wanting a souvenir for our honeymoon. He even invoked Klar’s name!”

“Not the boss!”

“Yeah, saying that she was intent on making the worst choice possible, and that he can’t see what Klar sees in her but it can’t be much,” the courier responded, getting mad all over again.

“Honey,” Adabelle said, turning around so she could see him. He let her. “While I like that you’ll so readily defend me, this isn’t important. Surtla’s in the past now, we have the hat and his quests, and it doesn’t matter what he thinks of me because he’s just a little man who doesn’t know how to handle not getting what he wants.”

“It’s not just about you, though,” Trevor replied, frowning as he placed his hands on her waist. “I mean, that’s one of the big reasons why I got mad. The biggest one, really. But he’s also throwing Klar’s name around saying he’s his benefactor and questioning his judgment. Surtla just seems pretty two-faced to me.”

“Well, there’s nothing I can do about it right now, but I’ll let the boss know the next time I see him,” Lora said seriously. It was the first time they hadn’t seen a happy expression on her face. That abruptly changed as her frown turned into a smile. “Let’s move on to happier topics. I was waiting out here for you for a reason, even if I did get distracted by the stars.”

“What’s going on?” Adabelle asked.

Lora checked her pockets, frowned as she came up empty, and then held up a finger. She disappeared on the spot.

“I think she forgot something,” Trevor chuckled.

Adabelle turned her husband around to show him that the lights had been turned on inside Lora’s villa. They flickered from room to room, marking the Summoned’s progress in trying to find whatever it was they had lost.

“That might take a while,” the noblewoman said with a wry smile. “Stargaze with me?”

“Sure. Blanket or chairs?”

“Blanket.”

Nodding, Trevor pulled away from her and stepped into the beach. A stone shark fin appeared from the sand and began circling them, much to his amusement. He plucked a large blanket from his inventory and spread it out before laying down. Pillows came next, adjusting for comfort, and then Adabelle joined him. She snuggled close so that her head rested in the crook of his shoulder.

They stared up at the stars. The only time Trevor had seen them so bright was when he left Tosa behind to go to places where the light pollution wasn’t so bad. Up in the Oscatarn Mountain Range when he visited Vilderchtif, out on the Powered Through as it sailed across the ocean, and even on the outskirts of the Wildlands.

Trevor had long come to recognize the constellations in his new world, but this sight was always breathtaking to him. Like the Milky Way, there was a long disc of stars that traveled across the night sky. With the sound of the waves lapping against the beach filling the air, it was almost enough that he felt he could fall asleep right there.

Lora returned before he could. She approached from behind, holding two slips of parchment into the air triumphantly. “Behold!”

“What’s that?” Trevor asked.

“I’m glad you asked, dear friend,” she said with a flourish. “These are two tickets to the premier vessel, the Hello Sea, for Fustavele’s finale tomorrow at noon. Boarding ends at eleven, and while it’s not the premier premier vessel, it is still one of the best views you can get of Croal as he works his magic.”

“That’s certainly very thoughtful of you and Klarkartar, Lora. Thank you,” Adabelle replied, fully meaning her words. “Out of curiosity, have you ever heard of a ship called the Serendipity?”

“Have I?” she asked in return. “Of course! It belongs to the church of Cruon itself and that’s where all the big names go. They get the closest to the island, and even participate a bit in Croal’s dance. Most of the time there’s a little meet and greet section with the dragon, too! Why do you ask?”

The noblewoman gave her a thin smile before looking at her husband. “Well, I uh, kind of won us tickets to board the Serendipity tomorrow,” he admitted sheepishly before retrieving the two tickets from his inventory.

“Huh? What?” Lora asked, coming a little closer and leaning down to see what he had just produced. She brushed her rainbow hair out of her face as she stared. “Wuzzah? How did you get your hands on these!?”

“A bird was impressed by my coins, so I won it,” he said flatly.

Lora looked confused, and Adabelle giggled before giving an actual explanation. “Trevor here was the lucky visitor to the Basilica Draconian, so he won tickets to board the Serendipity. We have since attributed our luck to the alumrae, a bird that makes nests out of shiny coins. There’s a superstition here in Paladona that, if you toss a coin into the fountain and it catches it, then you have good luck. I’m not sure how factual that is, but it does seem to have worked out.”

“Actually, that's not a superstition, it's fact!” the teleporter explained. She moved over to Adabelle’s side before flopping backwards onto the beach, halfway on the blanket and halfway on the sand. “There’s been studies and everything. The alumrae is a magical beast, like dragons, and they can absolutely bless someone with good luck. That’s why they let them nest in the Cruon fountains instead of getting rid of them for Fustavele. They can also curse you, though, but that’s only if you throw some grimy coins.”

“Good thing my inventory has no grimy coins thanks to Clay,” Trevor said, putting his palm against the sand. His familiar was quick to high five it from underneath. “That’s really interesting, though. I made a joke about going back and offering it a platinum piece, but now I just might have to.”

“It was watching him very intensely,” Adabelle said, shifting onto her back so she wasn’t facing away from their guest. “Landed on his shoulder and everything.”

“Wow, that’s huge. Congrats!” Lora exclaimed before holding up the tickets. “But what am I supposed to do with these?”

“You could go,” Trevor offered. “Find someone interesting to take the other ticket, or sell it.”

“You really mean that?” she asked, the star-like pupils in her eyes seeming to light up as she turned.

“They’ll go to waste otherwise, so it’ll be better if you use them,” Adabelle answered, agreeing with her husband’s judgment. “Do you know anyone in Paladona that you’d like to take?”

“I have a few contacts here and there as the boss man’s speediest liaison,” Lora admitted. Her eyes glazed over for a moment as if she were thinking, then she started giggling in a way that Trevor would describe as diabolical. “I know just who to invite, too! Don’t you worry about a thing, chicken wing. These tickets will be put to great use.”

“Who are you thinking of?” the courier asked, not sure how he felt about this woman’s mirth.

“No one that concerns you, but if it works then you’ll find out,” she all but sang. The smile on her face softened as she looked up at the sky and shimmied a little deeper into the sand. “What I wouldn’t give for my sister to see this, though. Always loved boats, that one. And dragons. She’d probably be able to sneak on, given how little we were, but I’d still invite her.”

“You weren’t always a human?” Adabelle asked, though the answer was fairly obvious. The pupils alone gave her away.

Lora thought about the question for a moment before grinning. “I’ll tell you later,” she promised. “Until then, just enjoy your honeymoon. I’m sure you know when the boarding will be since you have your own tickets, so don’t be late! If you need a teleport to the port, let me know and I’ll get you there in a flash. So, have a good night!”

She gave them an awkward, laying salute before disappearing again, leaving a perfect indent of her body behind.

“Not a human, okay,” Trevor said slowly. “Are we placing bets on what she used to be?”

“We’re not betting on what our friend used to be,” Adabelle chastised lightly, her tail tapping his chest.

“Boo.”

“Betting is off the table, but I will allow wild speculation,” she continued.

The courier grinned. “Alright, so this is what I’m thinking…”
