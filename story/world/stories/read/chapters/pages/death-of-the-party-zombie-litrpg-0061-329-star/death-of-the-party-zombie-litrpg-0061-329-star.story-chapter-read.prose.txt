Thus, the Grand Survey was sent out to every living being on Sanctuary. Every psychologist, healer, and therapist chartered for their opinions and held data. Warnings, provisions, and step-by-step guides were distributed from corner to corner of the world.

The consensus was clear, by a large majority. Sanctuary gave its verdict: the new System was to be implemented.

As for the name? It was kept nice and simple as STAR+, the plus sign denotation just the same as an upcasted skill. It made sense and was easy to remember. The day of the change was marked as a global holiday, with no work, adventuring, or danger allowed.

For most in Sanctuary, the renewed System came by with little fanfare. They didn’t get to see the stress and pressure exerted by the Architect and those working in the tower. None knew of the guinea pigs or those put forward for early testing. Only one person felt the shift in energy - the basic magic that the planet was made of.

A change in System UI and the updated display of abilities would be the most common sign that things were different. Those living or camping out closer to the wilderness would have seen different creatures spawn. The world turned, and nothing seemed to go wrong.

Sanctuary was no longer an awkward game-based world with fantasy flair. It was a living, breathing fantasy world governed by System-based rules. The difference was granular in places, overt in others. But as the holiday rolled through to the second, then third day, the results of this risk were clear.

In a matter of 48 hours, the average power level of the populace increased by ten percent. No longer restrained by a level and experience-based System to grant new abilities, those with the passion for improving found their progress doubled in pace. Those wanting a more relaxed existence started gaining skills in crafts or trade skills.

And for a select few—outliers from the general statistics—this was free rein to push themselves to the limit. Sanctuary depended on it.

Sally flashed her dagger around, the blade sparking as she deflected the sword of the paladin. Holy light burned the ground in the area, scouring at her legs. Her zombies stumbled, partially melting as they tried in earnest to assist.

“I’m just saying,” she gasped as she stepped away from the follow-up attack. “We can be friends now, it doesn’t have to be like this.”

Beneath the shining plated helmet of the paladin, a chuckle emerged. “Your kind will never be tolerated by Temple Arisa.”

“What? Petite blonde chaos gremlins?”

Sally grinned. “Eh, the former would have been more understandable.” She held her open hand out, and her arcane tome floated beside her. Pages flicked open as the ground shook. Before the paladin could move to intercept, an enormous hand burst from the ground, picking him up along with it.

A second arm emerged from the earth, right before a head and the rest of the body, taking the captured warrior dozens of feet into the air.

With a flash of blue light, Theo appeared. He dusted down his burgundy suit and flashed Sally a smile, his fangs catching the light. “Sorry to intrude, my queen. I didn't realize you...” He paused for a moment to look up at the massive undead creature currently crushing the armored paladin.

“Summon Zombie Giant.” Sally beamed. “I think I'll call him Big Joe. I was worried the increased stakes here would stop most hostility, but some people can't help themselves.”

The vampire put his hands in his pockets. “Hopefully they left some of their order back home. People who can resurrect them from their folly.”

She jerked her thumb back toward a couple of plated corpses. “These guys might be Starborn, but they taste just as good as an Adventurer. It's a win-win. I'm sure you're not just here to spy on my new abilities though, pup?”

“No. I actually wanted to see if you'd like to spar.”

“Generally not our way of doing things.” Sally crossed her arms. “But sure. Taking pointers from our more dazzling equals, I guess?”

“Not that it's a competition.” The grin on his face said otherwise. “But, Ren doesn't even have an Avatar form yet.”

“Let me finish up here and I'll find you, Fangs. Archie said a normal adventurer would max out at three Ultimate skills, but we get double that as Aspects. I want to get another from the System before the bugs arrive.”

Big Joe tore the paladin in half as the man's resolve gave out. The giant zombie stuffed the top half of the body into his mouth while entrails splattered to the ground. The legs and hips of the holy fighter bounced from the ground twenty feet away, casually discarded.

A flaming skull appeared in Sally's hand as she cast Mortis Bomb, while the vampire vanished in a flash of blue light.

She sighed to herself as the weight of the situation bore down on her once more. Chuck had levied a request before hitting the new STAR button.

The Outsiders would reform and become a separate entity, distanced from Sanctuary.

At first, she was hurt. This was her home after all, and she'd bled and killed for it to survive as long as it had. But after Archie sat down with the Aspects, their roles and this reasoning became clearer.

If they wanted to travel the Sea, getting their hands dirty and fixing errors, they shouldn't be doing it on behalf of this world. They were not a police force, and any backlash shouldn't tie back to Sanctuary. The Outsiders were going to become just that. A neutral force for change, spreading the STAR+ and fighting injustice. Just at an arm’s length from what they had created here.

The Mortis Bomb blew up against the shield of a struggling knight, and several zombies clawed up from the ground around him.

Archie had created the Outsiders as a Guild, not a Party. So, while only five strong currently, it was reasonable to assume they'd find other important allies - perhaps even more Aspects during their travels.

Part mercenary rescue squad, part intergalactic detectives.

She grinned at the thought. Once they got over this small hurdle of the Radoch infestation, life would get a little more interesting.
