Elion hesitantly walked to the throne. He touched the ornate chair, examining the majestic workmanship. The beautiful designs were carved in gold, representing animals, plants and what looked like historical scenes. The entire throne was covered with them. Through elegant windows and gaps in the carvings, the intricate mechanisms of the throne showed, pulsing with multicolored light that suggested the work of each of the seven Sentinels.

Glancing down at the limp body of his sister lying on the floor, Elion cautiously pulled himself up and sat on the throne. As he sat, he prayed that Liora was still alive.

The throne did nothing. He looked around, examining the arm rests, which seemed to contain several knobs and controls. Touching them elicited no response.

"What's it supposed to do?" he asked.

Yaret shrugged.

"I don't think it's working. She must still be alive," Elion said.

Yaret frowned. “We need you on that throne; using its power to put an end to this,” Yaret said.

He slowly lifted his laserarm and pointed it at Liora’s still form.

"What are you doing?" Elion exclaimed, unable to believe his eyes.

"She's the current heir to the throne, but you're next in line. When she dies, you'll be heir to the throne, and you'll be able to use the throne’s power."

"No!" Elion exclaimed, jumping from the throne and pushing Yaret's rifle to the side. "Stop! I'm not going to kill her!"

Yarin’s voice rose. "The longer that we let Dorian run rampant with his power, the more people will die. You can stop all of this right now, Elion. The moment she dies, you can take the power of the throne. You can stop the Tephalians. You can strip Dorian of his power, and you can save this planet."

Elion gaped, the hugeness of what Yaret said washing over him like a wave. What mattered more—one person's life, or an entire planet?

Yaret tried to pull his rifle back up, but Elion still held the barrel with one hand.

A ripple of Tephalian power flickered through the throne room, and all Praxian light faded for a moment before coming back on.

"Can’t you see that this world is dying?" Yaret yelled. "The Tephalians are coming. They’ll destroy everything! You heard what Zev said; there’s a powerful Tephalian ship entering orbit. It’s dropping warriors out of the sky. They are ripping our Praxian fields apart!”

He tugged at his rifle again. Elion held firm.

“I’ll do it for you,” Yaret said. “I’ll shoot her. You get on that throne, and use it’s power to reform the Celestial Sphere!”

Elion shook his head, rubbing his temple with his free hand. "I don't understand," he said. "Why would that even work? Sometimes, the Tephalian distortion field completely wipes out Praxian power and breaks apart Artefin tools, and sometimes it's merely a ripple or an inconvenience. And when Zev captured that Tephalian, he managed to keep his power all the way through. How can the throne stop all of that?"

"It's the difference in the strength of the field," Yaret said. "The stronger field overcomes the weaker one, and the deeper you are in the Tephalian field, the stronger your Praxian field needs to be to be able to counter it. The Golden Throne of Erod is the source of the Praxian field. It’s lain dormant for many years, but now you’re here!

“You can use it and increase the power of the Praxian fields across the planet. You can prevent any more Tephalians from landing on Kaelios, and you can help all those still loyal to the throne in hunting down and capturing and destroying the remaining Tephalian invaders. But without that throne, you can't do anything."

Elion looked down at his sister. She looked peaceful, lying there on the floor, like she was just sleeping. She had been in so much pain earlier, it was a relief to see her quiet face. It wouldn't be a bad way to die—lying there, unconscious, in your sleep, obliterated by a blast from Yaret's laser gun.

“Even if she was conscious,” Yaret said, “She’s too weak to do it.”

Elion shook his head slowly. "One life for a whole planet," he said. He closed his eyes, and the words of his father came back to him—what his father had always told him when he was younger.

"You need to look out for her. You need to protect her. She needs you."

Elion's eyes snapped open. "I'm not going to kill her, Yaret. I swore oaths."

Yaret raised his rifle. "I'll do it for you."

"No," Elion pushed the rifle aside. "I swore to protect people, not to kill innocents."

"She's not innocent," Yaret said. "She was working with Dorian. She was helping him. She was trying to push forward his plan."

"Killing family members is what started this whole mess in the first place," Elion said. "I don't want to kill her. Isn't there any way that we can heal her? If she was conscious she could abdicate the throne to me. We just need her to wake up!"

Yaret shrugged. "What would Zev want you to do?" he said. "I think you know that he didn't expect your sister to survive this."

Yaret started to raise his rifle again.

Elion ripped the weapon out of Yaret’s hands. “You can't be trusted with this," Elion said, fire burning in his eyes. "We're not killing my sister."

“Elion, please, we’re so close to victory!” Yaret pleaded.

"Zev said that Isalyn and the others would be heading toward the safe house where Master Golin lives. Let's take her back there. They can heal her. We can come reclaim the throne. Liora will help us once she understands what's really going on. She’ll abdicate. She’s only helping Dorian because she doesn’t understand the truth. We don't need to kill her."

Yaret sighed. "I don't think that's a good decision.” He raised his hands in defeat. “But it's your decision to make. She is your sister. I don't like the idea, but I'll help you."

Cautiously, Elion handed the rifle back to Yaret. The man received it, and turned, scanning the halls of the throne room.

Elion bent down and picked up Liora in his arms. He carried her out of the throne room with the sinking feeling that he was making a terrible mistake. But how could not killing his sister be a mistake? This had to be the right thing to do, didn't it?

They headed through the halls, trying to go back the way they had come. As they did, it became clear that this was the way that Dorian and Zev had gone. Signs of their fighting scarred the ornate walls, the beautiful tapestries, and immaculately carved pillars.

The ground rumbled beneath their feet and sounds of fighting grew louder. They rounded a corner and a half-dozen warlocks of the Black Wall ran past them, heading toward the fighting, preparing to join in.

For a moment, the warlocks did register the intruders, continuing on past them.  But then they realized and shouted out in excitement as they turned and ran, chasing after Elion and Yaret.

Yaret fired off a few shots behind them as they ducked down a side hall. Running through twisting passageways, darting this way and that, they ran through the corridors of the Palace of a Million Stars until the sounds of pursuit faded.

They found a small door, which Yaret pushed open, and ducked inside. Within was a small office space, filing cabinets, a desk, and a chair, all cluttered with knicknacks.

Elion swept everything off the desk and laid Liora atop it.

"What do we do now?" he asked. "Where are we?"

Yaret shrugged. "I'm as lost as you are. I've never been in this palace before."

Elion drew his knife and held it over Liora's face, up against her nose and mouth. The faintest of mist appeared on the shiny surface of the knife, and Elion breathed a sigh of relief.

"She's still alive. We still have a chance to save her."

He reactivated ‘Save a Friend,’ breathing through his nose as he tried to calm down.

"We're lost," Yaret said. "What are we going to do now? This was a bad idea."

Just then, someone rattled the latch on the door, and an elderly man stepped in, wearing a black robe. He looked at Yaret, taking in his rifle, and then his eyes landed on Elion standing over Liora with his knife. His brows rose, mouth hanging open in shock. He screamed.

"They're trying to murder the princess!" he yelled as he ran out of the door. "They're trying to murder her!" He screamed Dorian's name.

Yaret ran to the door and squeezed off a pair of shots with his laser arm, but they did not stop the man screaming. Yaret stepped back inside and slammed the door shut, leaning against it, cursing loudly.

"What are we going to do now?"

The screaming of the man faded down the hall, and silence fell. Elion and Yaret listened tensely. Then outside in the hallway behind, the telltale pop pop pop sound of warlocks teleporting in sent chills down Elion's spine.

They were cornered.
