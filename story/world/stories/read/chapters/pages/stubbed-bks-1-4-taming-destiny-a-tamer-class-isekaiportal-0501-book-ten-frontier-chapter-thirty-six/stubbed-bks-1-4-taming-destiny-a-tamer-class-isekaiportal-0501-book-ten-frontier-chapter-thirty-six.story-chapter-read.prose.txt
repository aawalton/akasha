“I wish to remain part of the pack,” he tells me firmly. “However, I do not wish to be subordinate as before.”

I feel one of my eyebrows lift.

“You wish for a Tame Bond instead? That’s fine, but you must recognise that it will require giving up certain benefits. Healing will be more costly; you may not be able to enter my spatial tattoo. I certainly won’t be able to summon you with Bond Teleport.” I don’t mention that I wouldn’t be able to teleport to him either – this isn’t the time to reveal the new additions to my Skill.

Artio shifts uneasily from foot to foot.

“I do not wish to lose what I had before. Only to have more choice in the matter.”

I eye him thoughtfully. Artio has always been prickly, and I wonder how much of that was due to feeling aggrieved at how he’d been essentially forced into a Bond, to a large extent against his will, for all he technically agreed to it. Though part of me dislikes the loss of possibility, a larger part of me is telling me not to push Artio right now.

“I can offer you Companion Bond at this point – that offers you the ability to end the Bond at any time you choose, though I suspect the cost of that is steep. Alternatively, you could try a Tame Bond and see how you feel about it. We can always transition into a Companion Bond if you miss what you had before,” I offer in the end.

Artio ruffles his feathers up and down in thought, then snaps his beak decisively.

“I will accept a Tame Bond.”

“Then let’s negotiate.”

Offering him the Bond, I put in my own requirements. Companion, Tame, or Dominate Bond alike, if someone is involved in my settlement or a battle, they need to be willing to follow orders related to those. Equally, if someone is benefiting from what the settlement is providing, they must be contributing to its success. I make sure this is clear to Artio. If he just wishes to hang around in the forest near us with a truce between us, then that’s fine. But if he wishes us to intervene on his behalf, he must be willing to join battle on ours too.

Artio takes a while to contemplate his own limits. Then, I feel him add them. He agrees to follow orders in battle or when involved in any settlement-related activity, but only if they are not unreasonable. He also requires his freedom – the ability to leave when he chooses, regardless of the situation. In that way, he reminds me of Sirocco – her freedom was very important to her at first. Until she learned that the freedoms she needed to give up to be part of the pack weren’t any she cared about losing.

I accept the latter, but challenge the former. ‘Reasonable’ is a dangerous word – because what if I believe an order is reasonable and he disagrees? What if the only reason he thinks they are unreasonable is because he doesn’t know all of the context? And what if there isn’t the time to explain all the context he will need to know to consider it reasonable? Matters can move swiftly, especially in a battle.

He acknowledges that and takes a moment to think. Finally, he proposes that he will follow orders when they are given, but may ask for an explanation afterwards. And, if not happy with the explanation, he may require it before obeying orders in the future.

I accept that – it’s good for me to know that my orders may be questioned after the fact. It’s good motivation to stay honest.

Finally, the Bond snaps into place. My companions welcome Artio again, but I sense wariness from several of them. So does Artio, if his sense of confusion is anything to go by.

Why are you cold to me, friends, Artio asks with a faint note of hurt covered up quickly with affront. Is it because I spoke against your Binder–

It is not that, Bastet interrupts him immediately, her ears pinning back briefly. It is just that some of us feel that you have chosen not to commit to us. So we are more wary to commit to you in return.

I chose not to commit to being trapped! Artio replies hotly.

Yet in doing so, you turn away from the bonds of the pack, as much as you do from those of the Binder. Bond and bond, they are one and the same, Bastet replies, growling a little.

Alright, that’s enough, I interject, stepping between the two – Artio has responded to Bastet’s vocal aggression with a shift in posture, crouching down and glaring at her. I’m very interested in seeing Artio’s new abilities, but not like this.

Bastet, Artio has made a choice. We know you made a different one, but that does not invalidate his right to make the one he did.

I turn to face the owlbear. Artio, you have chosen to hold yourself back from everything that being a full part of the pack entails – and I do not fault you for it, nor do I wish to pressure you in any way to change it. But you can’t expect it to have no impact on your relationships with others in the pack.

Then I turn to present my back to Artio – deliberately – and face where most of the pack are gathered. And finally, everyone, Artio isn’t the first to prefer a Tame Bond over something more binding. Bastet, you sold your freedom dearly when we first met; Sirocco, you too wanted to ensure that you wouldn’t be caged. Let’s remember everything that Artio has done for us, and choose friendship without fear unless something happens that proves such faith to be foolish.

Several pairs of eyes look away from mine – the feline-types in particular – but I get a feeling of acknowledgement from them. They’ll try, at least, which is all I can really ask. Artemis and Orion just look at me steadily, almost defiantly – they were among those who didn’t change their affection towards Artio in the slightest.

Nor was Fenrir, who now steps forward to sniff curiously at the Enlightened owlbear.

Look stronger. What new can do? he asks Artio as if the previous confrontation had never happened. I wonder for a moment if he’s tone deaf to the current mood, or if he’s actually fully aware but choosing not to pay it any regard. Given the occasional stroke of brilliant emotional awareness he’s shown from time to time, I would put my money on the latter.

Either way, it’s a perfect way of breaking the mood. Artio fluffs up – in pride this time.

“All settled?” Layton asks me, drawing my attention back to him – and the crowd of onlookers who have been quietly paying attention, even if they weren’t able to hear any of the communication that was transmitted along the Bond network.

“Pretty much,” I respond noncommittedly.

Watch, Artio instructs the lizog, clearly intending on doing something.

Hang on a moment, I suggest and the yelna sends me an aggrieved look.

What?

Well, we’ve got an audience, I point out, flicking a look at the assortment of Plant Mages, lords, Stewards, Farmers, and other companions who are all still watching as if this is the best entertainment they’ve seen all day – which it probably is. I just wanted to make sure you’re fine with them seeing.

They can watch if they wish, Artio replies nonchalantly after a moment, though I sense the slight flash of pleasure that betrays he’s not quite so indifferent to their presence after all.

Alright. Feel free to go ahead then, I tell him with a hint of irony. “Artio is inclined to give a little demonstration of his recently gained or enhanced abilities,” I declare to the crowd around.

They shift, interest spiking.

For a moment, nothing seems to happen. Then I frown in interest as what feels like a pulse goes through the earth below me, though I only feel it as an aftershock – as if whatever it was only brushed through the earth rather than truly changing it. I exchange an interested glance with Kalanthia – she felt it too. From Elarion’s interested frown, so did he.

Then, before Artio, a plant starts growing. At first, it’s only a small sprout, but it very quickly grows much bigger. It isn’t long before I recognise it as a local species. It’s a bush-like plant that has pendulous red fruit – not edible, unfortunately, though the alchemists have found some uses for it in various recipes not intended for consumption. Right now, the bush in front of Artio is covered in the white star-like flowers that precede the berries

Almost glaring at it, Artio concentrates visibly. The bush itself doesn’t change much, but something grows out of the rapidly withering star-like flowers – and it doesn’t look like the usual fruit.

“Is that…?” I hear murmured from among the gathered humans.

“It is!” someone answers the voice in disbelief, and the whole group seems to move forward as one to inspect what the bush has produced.

I can’t blame them for their reaction – I can barely believe my eyes myself. Because instead of the poisonous red berries that I was expecting dangle what appear to be wheat stalks. They’re hanging in a way no wheat stalks do – extended straight downwards from the withered remains of the white flowers – but they look almost unmistakably like the grain growing in the fields nearby.

“Markus, is it edible?” Elarion asks urgently, moving so fast that he appears to teleport to my elbow. “Ask him – is it edible?”

I flick my eyes at Artio questioningly.

He stretches out the moment long enough for me to start feeling exasperated, then clicks his beak in affirmation.

“He says it is,” I report back to Elarion.

His usual blank mask is nowhere to be seen as he grips my upper arm almost tightly enough to bruise despite my Constitution.

“And can it breed?” he demands.

Again, I glance at Artio.

I…am unsure, he admits this time. However Elarion seems undaunted even when I’ve passed on the message. His eyes are alight, his knuckles whitening.

“Markus, do you know what this means? If he can make hybrid plants that breed?”

I raise an eyebrow.

“That our issues with food are a thing of the past?”

“Exactly! And more! There are so many challenges that farmers must contend with. If we can create plants that are resistant to them….”

He seems to be off in a dreamworld of his own. I almost regret puncturing it, but memories of issues that were caused by genetically modified plants on Earth make me rather less excited than he is.

“Let’s take it slowly,” I tell him. “And test it – lots and lots of tests.”

“Of course,” he murmurs. “We must test it.” And without a word of farewell, he steps forward, crouches by the plant, and then seems to go far away without actually moving.

I exchange an amused glance with Layton and Valence, who has come to join us.

“We’ve lost him,” I joke.

“It is impressive,” Valence admits, gazing at the bush thoughtfully. “My House has always struggled with planting the semi-arid land to the north of our territory. To do so requires a great deal of resources. Yet if we were able to develop plants that could cope with such conditions naturally….”

“Such things have to be approached carefully,” I counsel. “Areas like that often have a delicate balance of conditions – tipping them too far one way can cause other issues.”

An aggrieved chirp brings my attention back to Artio.

That is not all I can do.

“Oh?” I ask him, raising both eyebrows this time.

He stands tall and then…changes. Subtly. His colour shifts more to green, his feathers appear even rougher, his claws gleam just a little differently.

I blink, then step closer to him.

“May I?” I ask, only placing my hand on his shoulder when he assents.

Just as I thought, the feathers beneath my fingers now feel more like intricately-sculpted slivers of wood. Stroking them in the wrong direction, I feel their spikes scrape against my fingertips.

Closing my eyes, I sink my mind into his body. Since he has a Tame Bond and not Dominate now, I can’t see his internal matrix. Even entering his body is much harder too, but that isn’t only to do with the nature of our Bond.

“You’ve become a plant?” I ask him incredulously.

To an extent, he agrees proudly.

I take a moment to process that before digging in again. Sure enough, I see what he means. He’s not entirely plant, but he’s also not entirely flesh either. Nothing has changed about the shape or function of any of his body parts – his heart still pumps, his bones still act as his frame, his muscles still control his movement – but the very nature of the magic that suffuses them has changed. And…

“Can you photosynthesise?” He responds with a confused head tilt. “Get food from sunshine.”

Oh that. Yes.

I shake my head incredulously. Whether this is Mould or Plant-Meld – probably the latter – I don’t yet know the extent of what it’s capable of. But I can already tell that this is going to be a voyage of discovery. And I’m down for every minute of it.

As, from the excitement that has returned to Elarion’s eyes, is he.
