He did not know what the discussion was about or what was being said, but Melmarc read the decision in his father’s eyes. It was the most subtle of things. It carried with it a weight of finality, a decision to be made. It had some level of satisfaction in a task about to be done.

“Okay,” his father said, his deep baritone carrying with it a decision made.

Melmarc leapt to his feet before his father could rise from the ground. His father’s eyes moved from Pain to him, darted back, then did it again. There was hesitation.

“No,” Melmarc said. “We do not do that.”

Pain looked from him to their father, then to Ark. There were questions in his eyes. “We do not do what?” he asked.

Ark shrugged. “I think your presence is not very appreciated.”

“He spoke to his wife,” Pain said after a moment, realization dawning on him. “Didn’t he?”

Ark nodded, smiling. “And she doesn’t like you.” He paused. “Any of you, really.”

“So, I wasn’t imagining it.” Pain sighed, rubbing a hand along his face. “You all know about us.”

Ark shrugged. “Not personally and not enough. But yes.”

“What has she told you?”

Ark cocked his head to the side dramatically while Melmarc remained standing in front of their father. Something told him that if he moved, so would their father.

“Who is she?” Ark asked, a touch of command in his voice.

Pain gave him a curious look. “Madness’ wife.”

“Wrong answer.” Ark replied. “Try again.”

Pain paused, confused. “War?”

Ark sat back with a smile. “There it is. Now was that so hard?”

“You do know that she’s not the Oath of War anymore, right?” he asked, tone careful, eyes on Madness. “I hope that’s not what she’s telling you.”

Veebee’s expression frowned a little. <<No longer an Oath?>> he asked Melmarc. <<Oath no longer Oath? How?>>

“Don’t know,” Melmarc said, then carefully squatted in front of his father. “Can I talk to her?”

Their father looked at him then at the phone. After a moment, he held it out to Melmarc.

Melmarc took the phone. “Falling stones,” he said in gratitude, then paused. Shaking his head, he placed the phone to his ear. “War?”

“I see,” their mother answered calmly. “He doesn’t know we’re your parents, does he?”

“Not to the best of my knowledge. Madness can’t do what you want him to do.”

“I don’t want him to do it,” their mother corrected. “I am allowing him to do it.”

“He shouldn’t,” he insisted. “That’s not how we operate.”

His mother made a thoughtful sound. “Then how do we operate?”

“With less violence.”

“And in this case?”

“He already knows, I don’t see why we have to—”

“Mel love,” she said very carefully, “the Oaths are not the friends that you think they are.”

“They can’t all be evil.”

“I didn’t say that they were evil, my baby. I said that they are not your friends. The world and their safety are far more important to them than you.”

That made perfect sense to Melmarc. “The world is supposed to be more important to them than me.”

His mother sighed. “That level of importance will lead them to making mistakes, Mel. Mistakes that will cost you for some reason.”

“But what if they are not so bad?” Melmarc asked. “What if—”

“Oh, they are definitely so bad.”

Melmarc turned at the sound of Uncle Dorthna’s voice. Ark waved at their uncle as if this was the first time he was entering the room.

“Oaths,” Uncle Dorthna repeated, “are definitely so bad.”

Pain turned his eyes on him and Melmarc felt something he had felt earlier. It was the same feeling he had felt the first time Uncle Dorthna had entered the room. It was fear. It didn’t make much in the way of sense to Melmarc. Pain did not look at Uncle Dorthna like he knew him, he just looked at him as if he was scared of him.

He isn’t scared of dad, but he’s scared of Uncle Dorthna.

Melmarc knew it for a fact. It went far beyond a feeling. He knew it. It was the aspect of fear in him.

“However,” Uncle Dorthna continued, “it is no excuse to exclude them. So,” he clapped his hands, “how about we fix this problem with something more interesting.”

Their father’s attention moved to their uncle.

“Violence?” Ark asked. “Please say there will be violence.”

Uncle Dorthna smiled at him. “There will be violence.”

“Madness is not looking for violence,” Melmarc said very carefully.

“Madness?” Uncle Dorthna cocked a brow at that. “That’s the one we’re going with? You know you can just say it the way it is. It’s not like he’ll remember once he’s out of the house.”

“Uncle Dorthna,” Ninra’s voice came from the phone.

“Yes, dear.”

“Are you trying to get our… nope, I can’t do it.” She sighed. “It just feels kind of bad doing it knowing that he won’t remember. Anyway, are you trying to get someone killed?”

Uncle Dorthna raised his hands in surrender, as if Ninra was physically present. “Oh, no.” His eyes moved over to Veebee. “The renegade void-beast knows what I’m talking about.”

Veebee hovered over to Melmarc even as all eyes settled on him. <<What,>> he pointed at Uncle Dorthna with his arm, <<is he?>>

Melmarc shrugged. “An important friend.”

<<Important friend very powerful,>> he pointed out. <<Veebee not like. Friend too powerful.>>

It was good to know that Veebee could tell how powerful Uncle Dorthna was. It reduced the chances of Veebee causing any trouble.

“What’s he talking about?” Melmarc asked, returning the conversation to what it was supposed to be.

<<Oath duel,>> Veebee said simply. <<Strong [August Intruder] make Oaths duel to solve problems.>>

“Is Mel a strong [August Intruder]?” Ark asked.

Pain looked at Melmarc, suddenly assessing him. “If the things I’ve seen him do are anything to go by,” he said carefully, “then I would say yes.”

<<So you have been showing your strength,>> Veebee said in a sly tone. <<[August Intruder] has been bringing Oaths under command slowly.>>

Pain looked at Veebee. He pointed at it. “No one’s still told me what that thing is.”

Veebee looked at Melmarc. <<One bite.>> he begged.

“No,” Melmarc shook his head. “You can’t eat him.”

Veebee pouted. <<He’ll live.>>

Uncle Dorthna tilted his head in curiosity. “Are you always this hungry?”

Veebee nodded, then paused. <<Well, no. Veebee not always hungry. Not before.>> Veebee paused, frowned, looked at Melmarc. <<Why Veebee answer?>>

“Because you’re meant to answer,” Uncle Dorthna said dismissively. “Anyway, Veebee, tell your [August Intruder] how his Oaths can solve their problems and let’s get on with this.”

Veebee looked at him with a little worry in its eyes. Melmarc found it intriguing. With who Uncle Dorthna had been proving himself to be, he had no doubt that their uncle had powers that could compel a creature like Veebee to do whatever he wanted him to do. After all, Pain was scared of him in ways that seemed primal.

Still, he moved his attention to Veebee and asked, “How can we sort this whole thing out without anyone dying?”

<<Oath duel,>> Veebee said matter-of-factly. <<Oath fight under protection of [August Intruder] and no Oath die.>>

“And what do we need to make that happen?” he asked.

<<Veebee and large space.>>

Uncle Dorthna thumbed behind him, a cup that had not been there before suddenly in his hand. “We’ve got a very large space out back.”

Ark perked up. “The training ground?”

“It’s large enough,” Uncle Dorthna agreed.

Pain looked at Madness. “You’ve been training them here?”

It was as if he had already forgotten that Madness wanted his life. Or maybe the Oaths were simply like that, accustomed to Madness wanting someone’s life.

“War,” Melmarc said into the phone.

“Yes, Mel,” she answered.

“We’re taking da—Madness—and Pain to the training room for an Oath duel.”

“That sounds a little too safe.”

“Well, we’re not trying to kill Pain. I know you don’t like them, but Uncle Dorthna says I’ll need most of them if the world is going to survive. I want the world to survive.”

“Also,” Uncle Dorthna called out. “At some point he’s going to have to learn how to interact with them on his own without you holding his hands. What you’re doing isn’t good for his growth.”

“There are different directions he can grow in,” their mother opposed, “my way leads him in one way. Let’s not behave as if there is only one right way for him to grow.”

Even though the phone was not on speaker, and she was only in his ear, Melmarc was certain that Uncle Dorthna could hear her.

“He’s an [August Intruder], War,” Uncle Dorthna said. “There are a very limited number of ways for him to actually grow. Yours is not one of them. Do not allow your instinct to blind you.” He returned his attention to everyone else in the room. “So, what do you say? Shall we retire to the garden?”

Ark was already on his feet. “Oh, yes please.” In the blink of an eye, he was standing next to their uncle. He paused when their uncle turned around. “Hey, do you think I can take an Oath?”

Uncle Dorthna sized him up with his eyes. “Not without Spitfire you can’t. If you get Spitfire back, then we can ttalk. I’m sure there’s probably one or two Oatths you’re ready to test yourself against.”

With that, he turned and left.

Melmarc ushered his father forward. With displeasure written all over his face in a blank expression—Melmarc was understanding his father’s lack of expression more these days—he walked towards the kitchen, Veebee hovering behind him curiously.

Only when he had disappeared into the kitchen did Pain get up from the chair. He walked over to Melmarc.

“Are you really alright with the way you are being… guided?” he asked.

Melmarc suspected the Oath had been going for a word very different from the one he had used. He had probably intended on using the word ‘controlled.’ So why had he changed it? Was he being polite or was he being careful?

Regardless of which one he was being, Melmarc shrugged. “I’m alright with it.”

With that, he walked to the kitchen. Pain followed behind him.

It wasn’t long before everyone was standing in front of the garden. Pain looked at the entire place with a very curious expression.

“I can’t shake the feeling that I’ve been here before,” he muttered almost to himself. “It’s like I’ve seen this garden somewhere.”

“Maybe you gardened as a child,” Ark suggested nonchalantly.

Pain shook his head. “Not possible. I never gardened.”

“Then maybe you have been here before and you just forgot,” Uncle Dorthna offered. “I heard things like that can happen. Isn’t that right, Veebee?”

“No.” Pain shook his head once more. “Oaths never forget.”

Their uncle snorted. “Please. You guys forget every day. It’s almost amazing how you guys get anything done.”

Melmarc looked up at Veebee and noticed that he wasn’t paying attention to them. He had a hand on his jaw as he stared off into the distance. Melmarc leaned a little closer to Veebee.

“You good?” he asked.

Veebee looked at him slowly. <<Veebee checking which portal to open first. Very close portal, powerful portal or very far portal, powerful portal.>>

Pain stiffened to attention. “You will not be opening any portal.”

Veebee’s response was very… wrong. It levitated over to him, arms folded over its chest. There was something carefully ominous about it, as if it was proving a point. Its expression remained blank, empty. Uncle Dorthna watched with a smile and took a sip from his cup of probably hot water. Gently, Veebee came to a stop, hovering above Pain.

<<On whose authority do you speak?>> it asked, tone calm yet heavy. <<Veebee introduced you to the [August Intruder] you did not know was standing right in front of you.>> It lowered itself until it was eye level with Pain. <<Veebee can introduce you to the God you do not know is standing above you.>>

Melmarc almost laughed when he saw Pain swallow visibly. Uncle Dorthna had no such control. He burst into laughter and staggered forward, cup still safely in his hand.

“Oh, that was classic,” he said as he walked over to a specific spot in the garden. “The God you do not know is standing above you.” He laughed some more. “I’ve got to use that on somebody at some point.”

<< You,>> Veebee continued with Pain, <<are no stronger than me now, than you are behind a portal. So, Veebee asks again: on whose authority?>>

Pain looked at Melmarc, and Melmarc took pity on him.

“Veebee,” he called, “isn’t that enough?”

Veebee’s ominous nature disappeared. He suddenly looked like a happy child. Without any hesitation it hovered over to Melmarc very quickly. <<Was Veebee cool? Veebee was trying to be cool.>>

Melmarc couldn’t help the smile that touched his lips. “Yes,” he answered. “Veebee was very cool.”

Veebee puffed its chest out in pride. <<Veebee cool. Cooler than [Nenit].>>

“Uhuh,” Melmarc chuckled as the garden slowly grew brighter. “Veebee cooler than [Nenit].”

Ark looked at them. “What’s a [Nenit]?”

<<A [Nenit],>> Veebee explained carefully, <<is a piece of shit.>>

Voidbeasts must really hate [Nenit]s, Melmarc thought.

“And we’re gone,” Uncle Dorthna declared. His words were succeeded by a suddenly flash of light and they were in an open space.

Pain took a step back, then looked back. There was nothing but a white tiled wall behind him. He panned his gaze around him to the sight of white walls and what looked like tiles on the ground that were not tiles.

“Mass teleportation,” he muttered, as if talking to nobody. “Capable of carrying an Oath without their permission.” His eyes settled on Dorthna. “What—”

“Careful,” Uncle Dorthna warned. “I’m trying to keep you alive for the boy’s sake, but some questions can get you killed, regardless of my intentions.”

For the second time today, Melmarc watched someone who was supposed to be among the strongest in the world swallow in fear.

“Now,” Uncle Dorthna continued. “Veebee, take it from here.”

Veebee levitated to the center of the room, hovering right beside Uncle Dorthna. He looked at him, took him in, then nodded as if making a final decision. Once that was done, he turned back to Melmarc, Ark, Madness and Pain.

<<Oaths angry, Oaths want to fight,>> it said simply. <<Oaths fight like Oaths or like Gifted.>>

Uncle Dorthna gave Veebee a slightly irritated look. “Why are you talking like that?”

Veebee paused and actually looked chastised. <<Because [August Intruder] like.>>

Uncle Dorthna looked at Melmarc. Melmarc never actually said that he liked when Veebee talked like that, but it didn’t really bother him unless Veebee was explaining something important. So, he shrugged to Uncle Dorthna’s unasked question. “I don’t really mind it.”

Uncle Dorthna sighed. “I guess I’m okay with it if you are. You may continue, voidbeast.”

Veebee nodded. <<So, Oath fight as Oath or as Gifted?>>

“What’s the diff…” Pain’s words died on his lips as he saw their father standing in the corner, stretching.

That was new.

“What’s the difference?” Melmarc asked.

<<As Oaths, Oath have all Oath skills,>> Veebee explained. <<As Gifted, Oath revert to real rank and have one Oath skill.>>

Real rank?

“All Oaths are not the same rank?” Melmarc asked.

Veebee shook its head.

“Becoming an Oath is not subject to rank,” Uncle Dorthna said. “A lot of people think the stronger you are the easier it will be, but it really doesn’t matter. Take Madness for example. He’s been an Oath since he was A-rank.”

Pain’s jaw dropped and he looked at Madness who was now whining his waist against the wall in what Melmarc hoped was some type of advanced stretch.

“The lowest ranked Oath I know was C-rank with a B-rank growth potential.” Uncle Dorthna continued. “She was also an Oath of Pain.” Then he looked at Pain. “Between you and I, fight as Oaths. If you choose Gifted, Madness will wipe the floor with you.”

Then he winked.

Melmarc was having a difficult time figuring out if Uncle Dorthna liked Pain or if he was just having fun right now.

Veebee looked at Pain.

“Sorry, just one moment.” Melmarc raised his hand. “So all you have to do is embody the concept to be an Oath. That’s all?”

Uncle Dorthna nodded. “Yep. And be Gifted too. Rank doesn’t matter. You can do all the growing at your growth potential while you’re an Oath doing your Oath duties.”

“Did you know that?” Ark asked Pain.

Pain shook his head. “We don’t really know a lot about each other as Oaths except for the fact that we are Oaths and have where we generally operate.”

“Life knew,” Uncle Dorthna said. “Just letting you know in case it might cause some chaos.” He paused, looked thoughtful. “That said, Mel, you should really meet life. He’s a good teacher in some ways.”

Pain watched Uncle Dorthna through narrowed lids. “You know Life?”

“I know all of you,” he answered simply. “Veebee, you can take it from here.”

<<Now, steaks.>> Veebee paused, blinked. <<Sorry, stakes.>>

Uncle Dorthna gave him a side glance. “You really can’t be that hungry.”

<<[August Intruder] not feed Veebee since Veebee arrive.>>

Melmarc’s jaw dropped. Is he reporting me?

“Voidbeasts don’t need food,” Uncle Dorthna chastized. “Now do your job.”

Veebee obeyed. <<Stakes. If Pain win, Pain get to walk away free and do as Pain please. The end.>>

“No,” Pain shook his head. “What happens if Pain lose… sorry, loses?”

Veebee looked at him like he was stupid. <<Then Pain die.>>

“No,” Melmarc interrupted. “We are doing the duel specifically so that nobody dies.” He looked at Uncle Dorthna. “How binding are the rules. Is there a way to guarantee that the stakes are abided by.”

“They are Oaths,” Uncle Dorthna said simply. “If they agree to the terms and conditions, breaking it could get them killed. So you could say that they are pretty binding.”

“How exactly?” Pain said. “If you don’t mind my asking.”

Uncle Dorthna shrugged. “You for example would probably receive a pain so great that it kills you on the spot. Madness would probably just run mad and die. War would just go to war with herself. Grace would experience the full undiluted grace of the world.”

“I don’t get how that will kill Grace,” Melmarc said.

“Well, grace is about benevolence, favor and love. What greater love would there be than to relieve her of all pain and discomfort and suffering.”

“Still don’t get it,” Ark said.

Uncle Dorthna sighed. “To suffer, you have to be alive. If you’re dead, no more suffering or discomfort of any kind is possible.”

Melmarc and Ark paused. “Oh.”

“Yep. Your Oath can kill you as easily as it saves you. Now,” Uncle Dorthna placed his cup to the side and it hovered as if on a stool, “I believe Melmarc has an issue with the terms. As the [August Intruder] it is within your rights to alter the rules of engagement.”

That was easy.

“If Pain loses,” Melmarc said, “then he will have to teach me how to be an Oath of Pain and keep his knowledge of me a secret from all the other Oaths that don’t know who I am.”

Melmarc had always known that there was no better way to learn how to control the aspects he was gaining than through being taught by those who already embodied the aspects and lived with them.

Uncle Dorthna looked at Pain then at Veebee.

Veebee sighed. <<Pain agree?>>

Uncle Dorthna rolled his eyes at the grammatically poor sentence but said nothing.

Pain nodded. “I’m fine with those rules.”

“You kids may want to step aside,” Uncle Dorthna offered.

Melmarc turned and found their father standing like a menacing thing. Eyes fixed only on Pain, he was a man with a goal. Uncle Dorthna didn’t have to tell them twice before he and Ark backed away from Pain.

<<Everybody ready?>> Veebee asked.

Madness took an interesting stance. Knees bent slightly forward, he held his left hand straight and forward, palm facing Pain, then cocked his right hand back in a blow. His upper body assumed the stance of an archer drawing a bowstring back but without the bow.

Pain took a boxer’s stance, then bounced on the balls of his feet. It seemed he was prepared to be fast since Madness looked as if he was about to unleash a ranged attack.

<<Begin!>> Veebee declared.

Pain bounced forward. Madness was suddenly right in front of him, pinning him in place with a hand on his shoulder.

“Madness,” Melmarc's father said, “is a gift.”

Pain crumbled to the ground like a wet rag.
