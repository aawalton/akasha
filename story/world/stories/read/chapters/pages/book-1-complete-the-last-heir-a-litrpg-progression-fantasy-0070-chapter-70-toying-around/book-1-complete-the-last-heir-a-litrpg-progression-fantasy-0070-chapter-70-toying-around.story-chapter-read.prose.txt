Rowan’s [Blazing Chains] tore the Crystal shards out of the air, but as soon as his spell made contact with the demon’s, he knew something was wrong. There was almost no resistance. No battle of Intents. Before his mind even registered what he was doing, the [Fire Shield] covering his neck and shoulders grew to encompass his whole back. And just in time too, because a heartbeat later, a Crystal spear impacted his defenses.

The Demon’s attack shattered as Rowan’s Intent overpowered it, quickly followed by an ominous cackle echoing off the stone walls.

“Good, I would have been so disappointed if that got you.” The demon’s form faded into ethereal mist, spreading across the floor. “There is only so many times you can kill someone with the same trick before it grows stale.”

Rowan didn’t let its words shake his focus, but if the monster was being talkative, it didn’t seem like a bad idea to engage.

“You could always stop hiding,” he said, his eyes scanning the stone chamber with a sharp focus, his chains coiling protectively in front of him. “If you want fun, why not fight me head-on?”

“Because I am no fool.” The creature laughed. “I can feel your anger, mage. The raw fury coiling inside you, just waiting to be unleashed.”

To the left, a flicker of movement caught Rowan’s eye. Without hesitation, he launched a [Firebolt] at it.

The spell erupted from his hand, impacting the stone with a fiery explosion.

“Tsk, tsk, tsk,” the Demon taunted, still hidden by the mist. “So eager to end our little brawl.”

Its body reformed on the other end of the room, the swirling Mist coalescing into a shifting silhouette, flickering between stolen forms before finally solidifying. A tall figure emerged, its emerald-green skin gleaming under the dim light, but it was its eyes, glowing with a hungry, golden light, that unsettled him the most.

The demon’s form refused to stay still. Its features warped and twisted, shifting into a grotesque amalgamation of past victims—a knight’s gauntlet-clad hand, a rogue’s dagger-finger grip, a mage’s trailing robes of shifting crystal dust.

But beneath its ever-changing exterior, there was a constant—a cold, calculated amusement, as though it relished Rowan’s every move, every perceived struggle.

“Tell me, mage. Where does that anger you try so desperately to hide come from?” it asked, its voice overlapping with dozens of stolen tones. “Perhaps my kind has already taken something from you?”

Rowan gritted his teeth, clamping down on his emotions with an iron grip.

It’s trying to rile me up, he told himself. Don’t play into its hand.

The Demon smirked, dragging a forked tongue across its fangs. “I can almost taste it,” it whispered, eyes flashing from gold to blue.

Suddenly, a weight descended on Rowan. It disoriented him for barely a heartbeat, but his opponent took full advantage. It took him another second to realize what exactly it had done, and by the time he flared [Iron Will] to repulse the psychic attack, another spear blurred towards him.

The speed with which he’d shrugged off the attack seemed to momentarily surprise the demon, yet the only thing that changed was its smile growing wider.

Rowan lashed out with his chains, tearing the spear out of the air. It impacted the wall behind him, shattered into a cloud of diffuse shards that glimmered in the dull light of the chamber.

“You just keep getting better and better,” the demon cackled, tapping its claws. “Your only skill, and considering you managed to shrug off my probe with seemingly no effort, I’d guess it’s at least Expert.”

Rowan’s mana flared, a hot anger welling up inside him. This creature had just tried to invade his mind. To read his memories and defile his thoughts.

He pushed [Iron Will] harder, forcing his mind to sharpen.

Anger he could deal with. Being dead, not so much.

The shards from the shattered spear raced towards his back. Not even turning around, Rowan expanded his shield. They impacted like pebbles against glass, his Intent once again overpowering the Demons.

Rowan could admit that he felt apprehension when he first entered its lair. Fighting against someone with a tier-three affinity would have been an impossible task just a week prior, but in truth, this demon was no mage. It was a thief. A pillager of other people's Paths.

The way it used its stolen magic was basic at best, and downright bad at worst. There was no weight to its spells. Like a poor copy of something greater.

Rowan focused on his opponent, returning its toothy grin. “I’m going to make you beg.”

Pulling at his Core, Rowan traced the familiar circuit for [Fireball] through his pathways. Like a dam opening, his mana surged through his body, guided by countless hours spent practicing.

[Storm Touched] showed its worth once more, and half a heartbeat later, the spell bloomed to life in the palm of his hand.

It illuminated his surroundings, shining with a fierce red glow.

The amount of mana he managed to pack into the spell still surprised him. If he had to wager a guess, it was the equivalent of half a dozen Chants. But that wasn’t the only thing that made his spell so dangerous. It was the efficiency, combined with the strength of his Intent and his inherent connection to magic—one that had only grown since acquiring his second trait.

Not giving his opponent time to react, Rowan threw the [Fireball] and brought his shield to the front, protecting himself from the explosion he knew would follow.

The Demon hissed, vanishing into the swirling Mist blanketing the stone floor.

Rowan’s spell struck where it had stood moments before, a fiery explosion roaring to life. But before his flames could spread, something tugged at his instincts—a sharpened sense honed through countless battles.

My back is open.

He spun just in time to see the Demon emerge from the Mist, its emerald-green skin encased in jagged Crystal—a makeshift suit of armor. It moved with unnatural precision, a short sword appearing in its hand and flashing towards his throat.

Rowan’s chains reacted before his mind could catch up.

They lashed out, striking the Demon as Rowan wrenched himself backward. He felt the difference immediately—his opponent’s speed may have outstripped his own, but his Dexterity was high enough to make the gap manageable.

The sword deflected off the chain’s sudden strike, giving Rowan the opening he needed to create some distance.

The demon’s body contorted with unnatural grace to dodge his second chain, an enchanted dagger appearing in its other hand to deflect the strike.

Rowan attacked again, his spells whipping through the air with blistering speed.

Gods, I love [Storm Touched]. He grinned.

He’d already had an affinity for mana few could match, but his trait took it to an entirely different level. It didn’t just respond to him—it obeyed.

One of his chains raked across the Demon’s [Crystal Armor], and for the first time since their battle started, Rowan’s Intent truly clashed against its own.

Like shattering glass, he broke through.

For a split second, fear flickered in the Demon’s golden eyes. Rowan relished the sight and pressed forward, his spells crashing into its body. Flesh burned, and an agonized howl tore from its throat.

His second chain shot out, coiling around its legs, locking it in place.

A [Fireball] ignited in his palm.

Getting answers would have been ideal, but he wasn’t about to take that risk. This wasn't a monster he could afford to toy with.

The Demon had seen his level and assumed it could play around. That was a mistake, one Rowan didn’t plan on making and one he intended to punish it for. He might have been overly confident at times, but if his opponent fought seriously from the start, there was a very real chance he’d lose.

Better not risk it.

The [Fireball] flared to life as he hurled it forward, once again shifting his shield. Rowan gritted his teeth, squeezing his chains at the same time. The rest of the Demon’s armor shattered, splintering into Crystal dust. It barely had time to open its mouth before the explosion consumed it.

Heat licked at Rowan’s skin as his spell incinerated its target, leaving nothing but ash in its wake.

He stood still, breathing steady despite the lingering fire around him. His chains unraveled and withdrew, his shield settling back to a more manageable size.

For a moment, there was silence. No taunts. No stolen tones echoing off the walls.

Then, Rowan exhaled, a slow, controlled release of breath. His fingers flexed, mana still humming through his channels. Even after everything, his Core was still far from empty.

He curled his fingers into a fist, feeling the resonance of his own power—the way magic bent with nothing but a thought. His chains hung loosely around him, radiating their usual warmth, steady, reassuring.

A chuckle slipped past his lips, equal parts relief and exhilaration.

“Not bad,” he muttered.

His gaze swept the chamber, taking in the Rift at the back and the Mist still swirling across the floor.

…Wait.

His breath caught.

The Mist.

Why is it still—

Rowan’s mana surged, funneled into his shield, expanding it in an instant.

There was no scent of burning flesh. No lingering remains. His chains were strong, but it shouldn’t have been that easy to tear through the Demon’s body.

Demons of Envy weren’t the most durable of their kind, but even they weren’t this fragile. His opponent was almost level seventy. That meant stats. A lot of stats.

The moment his shield locked into place, spells slammed against it from all sides. Magic crashed into the barrier in rapid succession, each impact sending a tremor up his arms.

Laughter rang through the chamber. Sharp. Mocking.

Rowan winced, waiting for the barrage to die down before pulling back his shield, watching as a form emerged from the Mist.

Then came a slow clap.

“Oh, well done, well done.”

The voice slithered through the air, dripping with amusement.

“You really thought that was me, didn’t you?”

The Mist thickened, swirling at the edge of the room. The air vibrated with stolen magic, Crystal shards catching in the dim light.

More forms emerged.

“Have you forgotten what I am?” one asked, idly twirling a dagger.

“Did you think I’d commit on the first go?” another chimed in.

“Tsk, tsk, tsk,” the last one taunted, lips curling into a wicked grin. “We haven't even started yet.”
