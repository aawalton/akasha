Tia launched herself from the battlement, diving straight for the Raja's skull. He hadn't seen her, nor would he until it was too late. All the speed in the world would not save him now.

Her blade met its target, and the next thing she knew, she was slamming into the ground beside him. Face-first and eating mud.

Years of combat experience had her back on her feet and spitting out the dirt almost before her mind caught up to the fact that she’d been attacked.

Sure enough, what she found when she rose was not the Raja she’d meant to kill, but a Samsara twenty paces tall standing between her and Jagath. The Bairan giant held a war maul in one hand, his gravity art still pressing down on her.

He didn’t introduce himself, but he didn’t need to. Every soldier in the human camp knew of the Bairan Raja’s son. The leader of the Akh Nara’s Samsara. Bearer of the terrifying Ultimate bloodline art that could manipulate weight itself.

Balancer of Scales.

Jagath sneered down at her from his mount, addressing the Samsara without sparing Tia a word. “I had no need of your help against a human, Roshan.”

“Of course, Raja,” Roshan said, bowing his head. “My sincerest apologies. However, a Raja in danger is a Raja I will always move to protect. Please fall back. Allow us to deal with the wall and the Gate. I swear upon my name that you will have the honor of being the first to ride through.”

The Raja's sneer deepened, yet he gave the giant a curt nod all the same.

“Aindri, with me!” he commanded, turning his mount and spurring his beast, moving so quickly he vanished from Tia’s sight, as did his fellow riders.

“Commendable of you to come out alone,” Roshan said, cracking his neck. “And foolish.”

All around her, demons drew back, sniggering at her.

“Why don’t you use that maul of yours and fight me, then?” Tia asked, pointing her blade at the demon.

“I'm afraid I must do exactly that, human,” Roshan replied somberly. “But it will only end with your death. Then we will take that Gate behind you and your human allies will die. Your last stand, while noble, accomplishes naught.”

“I suppose we’ll see about that,” Tia muttered.

To either side of her, demons were climbing the wall faster than the defenders above could throw them off.

Even still, the gate was hers. For now. And duels, she knew, were sacred to demons. Even in the middle of a war, it seemed.

So long as she stood, the gate would as well.

It wasn’t even close to enough, but it was all she could do. If her actions saved but one life, if she bought their army even a mile, her death would have been worthy.

Roshan began slowly circling, unconcerned with the time lost. Perhaps he believed he could chase the army down on the other side at his leisure. Or maybe it was something else. Between his words earlier and his actions now... they struck her as almost reluctant.

“You don't have to do this,” Tia said, taking a gamble. “We could pretend to fight. Stall for as long as we can.”

“How curious, for you to come to such a conclusion,” Roshan responded with a sad smile. “Whatever you have read into my goals, however, I'm afraid you are mistaken. As Warrior, I am honor-bound to see this attack complete. Just as I am to vanquish you in battle.”

Well, it was worth a try, Tia thought.

What he did not know was that the army would split the moment they left the pass, thereby forcing the already smaller demon force to reduce their numbers even further. That if he lost this opportunity, he would have a much more difficult struggle ahead of him.

Tia activated Haste and slipped beneath his first swing well before it connected.

She kept herself in close, inside the long reach that made his maul so deadly and yet so useless at once.

A well-timed Leap carried her past his guard, and Blink saved her from his follow-up backhand, placing her in position for her next strike.

Her seric blade dug into the back of his knee, where the flesh was weakest on humans.

The same, it seemed, was true for demons, only the wound’s blood was stained black from his prana, like ink on his skin.

Superficial though it might’ve been, Tia had cut him. An immortal Warrior, said to be unkillable, and Tia had left a mark on his body.

Renewed purpose flooded her body, and when he swung for her, his maul met nothing but air.

For while his strength was monstrous, while he moved faster than anyone his size should have, Tia was faster still, enhanced by Haste and the ocean of prana she’d amassed in the Ash.

She was back inside his reach in an instant, slitting his wrist and retreating before he could respond.

He paid no heed to the wound and swept the maul again, but she dropped low, flattening herself against the dirt. Avoiding the blow yes, but also driving the Mythic Rank Talent Quake into the ground.

Roshan’s weight worked against him as the earth underneath gave way, and not even Giant's Grace could stop him from being thrown off balance.

The watching demons, who until now had been cheering Roshan and jeering at Tia, went suddenly quiet.

It meant nothing to her, of course, and she didn’t let up for even a moment.

Speed was her sole advantage, and the moment she stopped moving was the moment she died. Not for an instant did she delude herself that she held the upper hand in this duel or that she was winning.

How could she be when he was yet to use his mightiest power?

The giant went to a knee, and before he could right himself, she fired Blade Launch, taking a clean slice from his cheek.

The demon froze for a moment, touching his injured flesh, as if in disbelief.

“I owe you an apology, human. You fight like a demon and I shall disrespect you no longer.”

Before Tia had a chance to work through what he meant by that, an all-consuming heaviness crushed her, and this time it was she who took a knee, heaving under the pressure, sweat breaking out throughout her body.

It was as though the weight of a realm had suddenly been foisted upon her shoulders, and it was all Tia could do just to bear it.

With one art, her foe had entirely negated her greatest asset.

There would be no moving quickly under so much weight. Haste was all but useless to her now. Nor was there any escape, she knew. Not until the giant either ran out of prana or succumbed, and there was little chance of the former.

If the rumors were true and he did truly draw upon the Akh Nara’s reserves…

Tia swung for his leg, but her blade scarcely moved, as if trudging through water.

The maul came around again, but just as Tia had been burdened under the weight of Balancer of Scales, so too had his weapon been lightened. Its speed was incomparable, even if its mass currently was not.

That was of little comfort when the maul’s weight suddenly amplified upon crashing into her.

Rock Armor took the first blow, even if it rattled her skull, but against the second blow that followed a second later, it failed completely, shattering to bits and hurling Tia into the air.

Away she sailed before crashing into the ground and tumbling to a stop. Despite a body forged in the Ash, she knew she’d broken more than a few bones.

Spitting out a wad of blood, she forced herself up, leaning on her sword to support her shaky legs.

“Is that all you got?” Tia rasped, giving the demon a bloodied, crooked smile.

“No,” Roshan replied. The utter lack of emotion in his expression instantly set her on guard, but for what he launched at her next, there was no defense.

A metaphysical blade plunged through her armor and her flesh, his Warrior Chakra going straight for her soul where no blade could ever reach.

By every right she should have fallen there, empty and lifeless. Doomed to a crippled existence Maiya had called worse than death.

Surely, this was what Roshan had expected.

But though she disagreed with humanity’s Saint on the course of this war, they’d grown closer than most knew these past months.

Close enough that Maiya had taken her under her wing, teaching her everything she knew about her foray into the world of Chakras. Her stumbles and her victories, and they’d discovered together that Tia had quite the talent for it.

Something that galled the Saint to no end, for it seemed the journey had been far more difficult for her.

Even still, talent and effort could only go so far in the face of such little time. Tia had only managed to open the first three Chakras. Foundation, Life, and the Shield, and that last one only days ago.

Her Shield opened just for a sliver of an instant. Barely long enough to nullify the incoming attack… and she thanked all the gods that Roshan had yet to open his Crown.

For if he had, her meager effort would have failed entirely.

Despite that, she only barely managed the feat, and her Chakra closed immediately after. The cost of even that left her drenched in sweat and her ears ringing.

If Roshan followed up, she would have been helpless to defend against it.

And yet he didn’t.

He stood staring at her with an expression of shock.

Shock… and more than a little admiration.

The same expressions were written all over the faces of the observing demons.

No human had ever survived a strike like that.

“You truly are a worthy foe,” he said. “I would have your name.”

“Tia,” she said through gritted teeth. “Princess Tiyana Matali.”

“You are royalty?” Roshan replied with upturned brows. “Then we share something in common. I am Roshan Baira. Son of Raja Thaman of Baira, and it is my honor to end you.”

He raised his maul a final time, and the weight upon her grew heavier still, grinding her down flat against the stone and the dirt and the cold earth beneath them.

The earth…

The one place a Talent wielder of her caliber should never be driven.

For Talents were little more than Earth Affinity magic. So long as her feet touched the ground, Tia commanded the full potential of those arts.

And during her time in the Ash, she’d learned them all.

Had been forced to.

It was the single worst mistake the giant could have made.

Tia stopped struggling against the weight. She slammed both palms to the ground, reaching down through the dirt and into the cold mass of the rock below to pull with all she had left.

Adinat's Wrath.

The earth erupted beneath Roshan as a single column of solid rock, as thin as a spear and just as sharp at its point, surging upward with the full force of her amplified weight.

Not even Giant's Hide could defend against force of that caliber. The stone passed clean through the giant between the legs and through his torso, leaving him impaled, legs dangling in the air.

For a long moment, there was only a stunned silence.

Then his maul fell from his grip to land with a crash, and the oppressive weight finally lifted.

Tia sucked in a heaving breath, her heart pounding madly in her chest.

In trying to crush her, the giant had unwittingly powered her most powerful Talent Art far beyond anything she could have mustered on her own.

His own power had been his undoing.

Hundreds of demons stared in disbelief at the dead Samsara hanging from his spear of stone. At their so-called unkillable leader and the bleeding woman who had put him there.

Tia dragged herself back to her feet. Blood ran from a dozen or more wounds, and one of her legs would barely take her weight, but she stood all the same, making certain her voice carried over the din of the larger battle.

"There are a thousand more like me behind this gate!" she announced, turning so that every demon there could see her. "Come through it and find out for yourselves."

The words had their effect. The demons nearest her, who had witnessed the duel, gave ground and for an instant, she thought their fear might infect the rest of the army. To be enough to turn them back, or at the very least, make them regroup.

Then the body upon the spear began to move.

The wound sealed itself shut. The shaft of stone crumbled away beneath him, and Roshan fell, landing on his feet and standing once more.

Whole and unmarked, as if his battle with her had never happened.

Every demon who’d been on the verge of fleeing burst into a cheer that rippled in a wave across the whole army.

She had done the impossible. She’d fought at her absolute best. She’d given everything she had and killed the leader of the Samsara… and it had bought her nothing at all.

The walls had fallen to either side of her, their ramparts now firmly under demonic control.

Her people were being slaughtered behind her even now.

And before her, every rumor had been proven true.

The Samara could die and rise again without end, while she had only one life to give.

Tia realized then that this war would never be won with spears and swords.

No amount of blood spilled would stop this army of demons.

Nor would she allow it, as her people's leader. The armies looked to her for guidance. For a true resolution to this war.

So long as the Akh Nara lived, the Samsara would continue to prevail, and so long as they prevailed, the armies of demonkind would never yield.

The Akh Nara…

Tia’s expression darkened, and suddenly, she knew what had to be done.

For there was always only one answer. Only one way to win against foes who couldn’t die.

Cut off the source of their power, and the Samsara would fall…

Vir was reasonable. If he came back, even now, she believed he would end this war in the blink of an eye.

Yet he hadn't, and if Maiya wasn't willing to bring him back, then Tia would make him.

If he refused?

Then demons’ god would have to die.

And though she would hate herself after, Tia knew she was the only one who had any chance at all of doing it.
