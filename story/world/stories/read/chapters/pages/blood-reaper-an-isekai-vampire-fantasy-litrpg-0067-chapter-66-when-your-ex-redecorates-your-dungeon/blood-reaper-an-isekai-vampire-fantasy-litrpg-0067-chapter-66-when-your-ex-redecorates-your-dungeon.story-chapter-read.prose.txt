Morning mist coiled around ancient trees like serpents, clinging to the underbrush as Ace led his team through the outskirts of the Gloomvale Woods.

Judging by the map the System had given them, they were close to the Crucible. Any moment now, they would stumble across the entrance. What happened after that was anyone’s guess.

If Ace were a betting man, he would’ve assumed ample bloodshed to be involved in some way. Given everything they had endured in this place, the odds on that seemed pretty solid.

Three days had passed since they’d taken down the vampire bandits, and the gear they’d claimed had completely transformed their look. His ragtag band of rabble and misfits now wore matching leather armor, all of it perfectly adapted to their bodies as it shielded them from the elements. The shift in gear also seemed to have changed the way everyone carried themselves—each person walked with more confidence now, as though they each felt the part.

Gone were the frightened newcomers stumbling through an alien world. Instead, his team moved like hunters through a forest, barely making a sound as they cut through the wilderness.

It was a nice change of pace.

Ace’s boots sent vibrations up through his legs with each step, mapping the terrain better than his eyes ever could. He felt the subtle drops in elevation, the shifting density of the ground beneath them.

And, curiously, water nearby.

A lot of it.

Without a word, he raised his fist in warning. The clustered warriors behind him froze, instantly on high alert.

“There’s water ahead,” he said quietly, eyes still scanning the trees. “A big body of it, maybe a lake. There’s something flowing through the ground, as well, so I don’t know if it’s a spring or a river.”

“That’s freaky,” Marcus quipped. “You know all of that from your boots?”

“Dude.” Ace glanced at the man over one shoulder. “We’ve been over this.”

“It’s still weird.”

Ace shrugged.

He couldn’t really deny that.

Marcus fidgeted with the crystal amulet they’d looted, and its purple stone pulsed with life. “Anyone else think we look ridiculous?”

“Nope,” Tara said with a wry smile. “Just you.”

Ace chuckled.

“No, seriously. Look at us.” Marcus stretched his hands wide, as if making a point, and gestured to his new armor. “Four assholes in matching black leather with weapons that practically scream ‘we came to fuck shit up.’ Two weeks ago, we were begging not to die. Now, we’re a walking horror movie.”

“It’s an improvement,” Olivia acknowledged with a nod.

Tara smiled, but it didn’t reach her eyes. She pulled up beside Ace, and though her focus remained on the trees around them, she shifted closer to him. Her shoulder brushed against his arm as they walked, sending an electric awareness through his heightened senses. Her fingers traced the golden sigils etched into her Echo Staff, and he watched from the corner of his eye, recognizing the gesture for what it was—a soldier’s habit of checking their weapon before combat, transformed into something almost meditative.

She glanced up at him, and when their eyes met, her body relaxed. She smiled—for real this time.

Whatever this unspoken understanding between them was, it had no name yet. It pulled at something he’d thought dead since waking in this nightmare world.

He returned a wry smile and nudged her shoulder playfully. She chuckled and nudged back.

“Oh, just kiss already,” Marcus said from behind them.

Grinning, Ace cast a glance over his shoulder at the telepath, who was shaking his head and laughing.

Something in the air shifted, and Ace’s smile fell.

Through his boots, Ace detected a change in the dynamic of the forest ahead. Trees thinned. Gusts of wind shivered the grass along the edge of a large body of water. And, more importantly, the telltale tang of Shadow Realm energy thrummed beneath the surface of reality, like a heartbeat muffled under layers of scar tissue. His heightened senses picked up a subtle… wrongness… in the air. He felt a familiar pull in his chest, the same magnetic tug that came before his Shadow Realm Immersion activated.

Whatever lay ahead wasn’t just dangerous—it was a nexus point, a thin spot where the barrier between worlds had worn translucent. His jaw tightened. The System had led them here, and he had no idea what kind of game she was playing, especially since she so desperately feared this realm beyond her control.

“We’re here,” he said.

The group went painfully silent as Ace paused at the edge of the treeline and pulled back a low-hanging branch.

Sunbeams cut through the gaps in the leaves as they broke through the treeline onto a strip of blackened grass. Before them stretched a lake, its surface unnaturally still, and it served in sharp contrast to the purple sky overhead. At the far edge, a massive waterfall thundered down a sheer cliff, the water so dark that it swallowed light. The misty spray at the base of the falls caught the dim light and refracted into a blood-red mist.

“That’s...” Tara took a step closer to Ace, unable to finish her thought.

“...not right,” he agreed. “The water shouldn’t be this still with that waterfall hitting it.”

“Forget the lake,” Marcus said as he frowned at the shoreline. “Where’s this entrance? Map says it should be right here.”

As he had so many times on their travels these past few days, Ace summoned the map and compared it to what lay before them. “No, this is right. The entrance should be at the base of that waterfall.”

Tara knelt by the lake’s edge, her hand hovering over the surface without touching. “Maybe it’s underwater? Though I really don’t want to swim in... whatever the hell this is.”

“I don’t think this is water,” Olivia said suddenly. She pointed toward the falls. “Look at the falls. It’s parting in odd places. Something’s there, interrupting the flow.”

Ace squinted at the distant cascade. She was right—the waterfall split around shapes along the cliff that weren’t visible to the naked eye, creating odd gaps in the otherwise smooth curtain of darkness.

“Something’s definitely there,” he muttered. “The question is how do we get to it.”

“This is a trap,” Marcus said flatly.

“How can you tell?” Tara asked.

Marcus eyed the still lake. “Anything this fucking weird has to be a trap. The System wouldn’t just leave the door unlocked.”

Ace stared at the waterfall, calculating their options. Every instinct screamed that they were in danger despite the calm lake. But the treasures awaiting in the Crucible weren’t optional loot.

“Only one way to find out,” he decided. “I’m going to take a closer look.”

“Ace,” Tara snapped. “If you get in that water, I swear to God that—”

“Relax,” he said with a chuckle. “I’m just going into the Shadow Realm.”

“Oh.” A slight blush crept up her neck, and she crossed her arms as she pretended to study the lake with newfound focus.

Ace’s mind raced as he studied the unnatural waterfall. The evidence had been accumulating for days like pieces of a puzzle he’d been subconsciously assembling. Now, staring at this impossible lake, everything clicked into place.

Olivia shifted her cold gaze toward Ace. “What makes you think the Shadow Realm has anything to do with the entrance? Not everyone has Shadow Realm Immersion.”

“Maybe whatever is in there doesn’t want to let in folks that don’t have the Skill,” Ace said with a shrug.

“Rude,” Marcus said under his breath.

Ace gestured toward the waterfall, where liquid darkness parted around invisible structures. “Look at how the water behaves—it’s interacting with something that exists in another dimension. And remember what the System said when she gave us the map? That the Crucible exists ‘between worlds’ and ‘beyond her sight’?”

The memory surfaced sharp and clear—the System’s childish face contorting with momentary displeasure when discussing Darkmoor’s creation. Her careful avoidance of certain details. The way her form had flickered with rage whenever Ace pressed for information about the Shadow Realm.

“Every time I’ve mentioned the Shadow Realm, she changes the subject,” Ace continued.

Ace knelt, hovering his hand over the impossibly still lake surface. A familiar chill prickled at his fingertips—the same feeling that preceded Shadow Realm Immersion.

“What’s on your mind?” Tara asked, her voice low with concern.

Ace met her gaze, the weight of his conclusion settling into cold certainty. “The Crucible doesn’t just connect to the Shadow Realm—it’s in it. At least partially. That’s why the System can’t directly interfere with it. It’s why she needs people like us to go in.”

A thrum of warning pulsed beneath his ribs—not just from what might’ve been awaiting them inside the Crucible, but from what his growing connection to the Shadow Realm might mean. Each time he phased, the faceless entities grew more interested, more aware. This lake, this entrance, everything around this water—it all practically vibrated with their attention.

But they had come too far to turn back now. The Evolutionary Keystone represented their best chance at survival—at finding a way home. Whatever dangers the Shadow Realm posed, they paled against the certainty of the System’s planned destruction of Earth.

“Be careful,” Tara said quietly.

The concern in her eyes twisted something inside him—a reminder of his responsibility to protect this makeshift family he’d gathered. Whatever waited beyond that waterfall, he couldn’t face it alone.

He playfully tapped his finger on her chin. Then, without a word and without waiting for further discussion, Ace closed his eyes and reached for the cold thread of darkness that lived inside him now. The familiar sensation of icy tendrils wrapped around his consciousness as he triggered Shadow Realm Immersion.

The world bled away.

The transition hit harder than usual—like plunging into frigid water. Icy chills knifed through his lungs, stealing away his breath. When he opened his eyes, color had drained from the world, leaving only stark shades of gray—except for the waterfall. In the Shadow Realm, it glowed an intense crimson, pulsing like an exposed artery. The lake now appeared solid, its surface transformed into a black void that neither reflected light nor cast shadows.

Movement flickered in his peripheral vision. Faceless figures lingered at the edge of the treeline, more than he’d ever seen before. They didn’t approach, but their presence felt hungry—like sharks circling a wounded diver.

To his surprise, an altar stood at the edge of the lake—something that definitely hadn’t been in the physical realm. Before the altar knelt a man, or at least what remained of one. His body was frozen mid-fall, with one arm outstretched toward Ace. Pitch-black vines had grown through and around him, anchoring him to the spot like a grotesque statue. His expression, locked in a silent scream, contained such primal terror that Ace instinctively summoned a Duskblade.

“He was the last to enter, I think.”

Ace spun around and raised the Duskblade to attack.

Darkmoor’s echo stood just out of reach, more substantial than Ace had ever seen him. This close to the Crucible, his form had weight and presence. The edges of his silhouette were sharp rather than translucent. His eyes contained actual depth now, reflecting an ancient weariness.

Ace relaxed.

“What happened to him?” he asked.

“I can’t be sure,” Darkmoor confessed. He clasped his hands behind his back and slowly circled the altar and its captive. “I’d say he was trying to escape and failed.”

“What makes you say that?”

“It’s the kind of batshit crazy cruelty that Lysandra is known for.”

Ace tilted his head in annoyance. “Are you going to keep making me ask the obvious questions, or can we have a real conversation before I get kicked out of the Shadow Realm?”

Darkmoor chuckled. “I’m anchoring you here. It won’t drain your Soul Meter to stay.”

“How chivalrous.”

Darkmoor didn’t respond. Instead, he turned toward the waterfall, his profile cutting a stark silhouette against the fading sunlight of another day in the System’s brutal world. His eyes—ancient and weary—reflected the cascading darkness with an intensity that suggested he was seeing something beyond the present moment. The shadow-vines at his feet recoiled from his presence, curling away like living things.

For a moment, Ace glimpsed something raw in the ancient vampire’s expression—a wound that transcended death itself. The tension in the man’s shoulders worsened the longer he stared off into oblivion.

“I built this place as a sanctuary,” he said, so quietly Ace had to strain to hear him over the muffled roar of the falls. “Somewhere even the System couldn’t reach. Somewhere the shadows would protect instead of consume.” His gaze hardened as he stared at the altar. “And now look what they’ve done to it.”

A flash of emotion crossed Darkmoor’s face—so brief Ace almost missed it. Pain. Regret. Something deeper.

“Lysandra,” Darkmoor continued, and her name hit the air as though it were poison on his tongue. “Octavia. Vivienne. This has their names written all over it.”

“For Christ’s sake, man.” Ace rubbed his eyes in frustration. “Can you cut the cryptic bullshit for two minutes?”

Darkmoor cast an annoyed glare over his shoulder at Ace, but after a moment, the man simply nodded.

“The Sisters are triplets who made it into this world about six hundred years ago. They look innocent—well, everyone except Octavia—but don’t let them fool you. They’re not sweet. They’re not gentle. They’re not merciful. And by God, do they love games.”

“Let me guess,” Ace interjected. “These are the sort of games where people die.”

“Often,” Darkmoor said. “And from what I can gather, they’re the guardians of the Crucible now. After I died, they must’ve claimed it as their domain.” Darkmoor gestured toward the waterfall, his movements carrying a grace that reminded Ace of Olivia. “This isn’t how I designed it. The entrance was meant to be hidden, yes, but not... corrupted.”

Ace glanced at the altar, its runes pulsing with malevolent energy. “And this?”

“Not mine,” Darkmoor answered. “This was added after I died. It looks like a blood ritual site, designed to wake the sentinel.” Darkmoor nodded toward the kneeling figure. “Another of their twisted games.”

“Ah,” Ace said, piecing it all together. “So we have to fight him to earn our way in.”

“Bingo,” Darkmoor replied.

Great.

“How do we defeat him?”

Darkmoor’s expression turned grave. “All I know is how to wake him, and that requires a blood sacrifice on the altar. After that, I have no advice to give you. The Sisters have changed everything.”

“Helpful.”

Darkmoor just shrugged.

The shadows around them pulsed more intensely, writhing like living things. In Ace’s periphery, the faceless figures began to move closer, drawn by their conversation. Their hunger became a palpable pressure against his mind.

“I can’t keep you here much longer,” Darkmoor said with a brief glance toward the encroaching figures. “You shouldn’t remain here any longer than you need to. The Shadow Realm consumes people, Ace. Remember that. Remember not to linger here any longer than necessary.”

“Noted.”

Darkmoor grabbed Ace’s shoulder, and his eyes bore into him. “Trust nothing. The Sisters will test you in ways I cannot predict. They will find your weaknesses and use them against you.”

“They’re evil. Got it.” Ace tried to pull away, but Darkmoor’s grip held firm.

“One more thing,” Darkmoor said, his voice more urgent now. “Lysandra specializes in turning people against each other. Whatever happens in there, remember who you are—and make sure they remember who they are.”

Darkmoor nodded toward the color-bleached figures of his teammates, still waiting for him in reality. Through the monochrome veil of the Shadow Realm, Ace could see their growing anxiety—three statues caught in a tableau of mounting dread. Tara paced along the shoreline, her fingers white-knuckled around her staff, and she stopped every few seconds to stare at the spot where he’d vanished. Marcus had positioned himself as a lookout, his back to the lake as he scanned the forest perimeter with manic vigilance. Olivia, meanwhile, who normally maintained perfect stillness, betrayed her concern through the subtle shifting of her weight from foot to foot.

With that, Darkmoor shoved him backward. The Shadow Realm instantly dissolved around Ace, and colors bled back into his vision as he was violently ejected from the alternate dimension.

He hit the ground with a thud, and a jolt of pain shot down his thigh.

“Jackass,” Ace muttered under his breath.

The world snapped back into focus with painful intensity—the purple sky overhead, the too-black water, the muted roar of the waterfall. Colors that had been bleached to grayscale now burned his eyes and overwhelmed his heightened senses. The sudden transition left him momentarily disoriented.

“Ace!” Tara was at his side in an instant, her fingers already glowing with healing energy as she scanned him for injuries. Her touch anchored him, pulling him fully back into this reality.

Marcus hovered nearby, his shoulders relaxing even as his eyes darted between Ace and the surrounding forest. “Welcome back to the land of the undead,” he quipped.

Olivia remained where she stood, but her eyes tracked him with unnerving intensity. “Something followed you,” she said with clinical detachment. “It’s clinging to your shadow.”

Ace glanced down and saw she was right—his shadow pulsed unnaturally for a moment before settling back into a normal silhouette. The residual energy from the Shadow Realm clung to him like cobwebs.

He shook his head, trying to clear the lingering cold that had seeped into his bones. “I found the entrance,” he said, pushing himself to his feet. The weight of Darkmoor’s warnings pressed against his chest like a physical burden—revelations too heavy to carry alone and too dangerous to share. “But we’ve got a problem.”

“Just one?” Marcus raised an eyebrow. “That’s practically a vacation day in this place.”

“Fair.” Ace didn’t smile. His mind raced with fragmented images—the frozen sentinel, the blood altar, Darkmoor’s haunted eyes when he warned Ace about the Sisters.

How much should he tell them? How much could they handle? The thought of what awaited them in the Crucible—tests designed specifically to break them, to turn them against each other—made his jaw clench until his teeth ached.

But these people had followed him through hell already. They deserved the truth, or at least as much of it as he understood. He wouldn’t risk telling them about Darkmoor until he was certain the System couldn’t overhear, and that meant waiting until they were in the Crucible itself.

For now, it had to remain a secret.

“Do you trust me?” he asked them all.

“It’s that bad, huh?” Marcus laughed. “Fuck, why not. Yeah, I trust you.”

“Of course,” Tara said.

Olivia just nodded.

“Good,” Ace said with a small sigh of relief. “Because the Crucible has been taken over, and what we have to do to get inside is going to suck.”
