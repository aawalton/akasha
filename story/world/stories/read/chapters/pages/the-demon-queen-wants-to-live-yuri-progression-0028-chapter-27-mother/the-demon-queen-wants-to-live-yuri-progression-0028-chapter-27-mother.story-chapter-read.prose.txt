Ash stepped past the line of trees and into the clearing. She moved to my side, never taking her eyes off the silver woman. Her sword was drawn and raised. The clone she had cut fell before me in two halves. Each half fell to the ground and dissolved into silver mist. There was nothing at all to mark where they had fallen.

"What took you so long, Hero? I thought your kind would have better timing." I said.

Ash's eyes never left the silver woman. "Heroes are supposed to have terrible timing. We show up at the last second. That's how it usually went."

"I suppose that's right."

"You." Isabelle's violet eyes were fixed on Ash's sword. "Just how the hell did you do that? That didn't feel like any old power." There was no warmth in her voice at all now. "You're the other one then, aren't you? The other one that little man is dragging back to the Academy."

She waited for an answer, and Ash gave none. Ash only held her sword higher. The woman sneered.  "Silent, are you? No matter then." The mark on Isabelle's arm pulsed, and the three clones took a shared step forward. None of the clones so much as made a sound as they stalked closer. I shifted the satchel behind me.

The woman spoke again, and this time her voice sounded almost casual. "Y'know, I should be thanking you. You saved me a lot of trouble.  I was going to find you after this and kill you anyway. Now, you came here to me. That is much more convenient."

Ash's grip on her sword tightened, and her voice did not waver.

"You're welcome," Ash said. Isabelle only smiled. Her three clones spread outwards as they drew near. They were trying to encircle us. "I'll take the one in the center. You take one of the others." Ash said and rushed the first clone.

Ash darted forward, and crossed the distance in two steps. The clone's greatsword was moving towards her in an arc. It was too slow. Ash stepped in beneath the swing and traced her blade along the clone's right forearm. The arm fell away in silence. The blade's edge hit the ground with a thud. The clone tried to right itself, but Ash was already behind. Her blade was moving again.

This time, she traced the clone's spine with the edge of her sword. The clone's entire body came apart down the middle. There was no blood. No spilling entrails. Instead, the body seemed to dissolve as soon as Ash had severed it.

Another of the clones turned toward me. I raised my right hand -a wisp of Ruin, barely enough to be called an attack. Black wisps streaked and grey bloomed across the clone's throat. The neck folded inward. The clone fell.

The third was on me almost before the second had fallen. This one had a spear, and it was faster than the other had been. It led with a charge, one I sidestepped. It followed that up with three smaller thrusts, all of them fast. I would not have managed to dodge any of them if the clone's movements hadn't been so predictable. I twisted from another thrust as I darted inwards. I pressed my palm into its chest, and sent Ruin past the skin. The clone dropped with a shudder.

I pulled my hand back. That single attack had used more of my mana than I had wished. There were already more clones, rising from the earth. I counted four, then six. The number kept rising. Ash cut three with a single sweep of Parting, the air slicing open in front of her blade, and I felt the mana it cost her from where I stood.

I erased two with Ruin before they could charge me. I erased a third before it could attack me from behind. Another fell to Ash's sword. More and more of these bodies fell, and more and more clones rose to take their place. Isabelle had still not moved from where she stood. Her arms were folded, and all she did was watch.

I ducked beneath a greatsword swing and the blade missed my horns by less than an inch. I stumbled backward, and then I stopped fighting and looked. There. Or rather, there was nothing.

I had commanded armies. Had felt the weight of ten thousand demons at my back. Even the weakest Imp carried something. All who lived carried the desire to keep living. They all feared death. These clones swung with precision and speed enough, but their blows were empty. Up close, I could see it clearly. There was no will behind them. No survival instinct at all. They only bothered to have the clumsiest of defenses.

Isabelle's right hand was moving, just faintly. Before a swing came, one of her fingers would twitch. When the clones spread out to encircle me, her fingers spread wide too. I watched her, and I understood. Ash cut a clone across the chest, rushed back and raised her sword in the air. A clone rushed at her, but she didn't seem to care.

Ash raised her sword higher still. For a moment, I thought I saw it glow. She swung, and I felt the mana she used. She swung Parting into empty air. Three feet to the left of the nearest clone. At a height that would have passed over anyone's head. At nothing.

A half-second passed. Then the clones all dropped together, as if someone had cut their strings all at once. Most of them dissolved into silver mist before they even hit the ground. The few that remained faded into nothing moments later. The clearing was empty then, save for us.

Ash slowly lowered her sword. She stood in the center of the clearing, and her breathing came fast. She took a step and staggered. She planted the sword between her feet to steady herself. "How?" I demanded. She had turned the tide with a single swing of her blade.

Ash looked down at her own blade for a long moment. "You're the one who…the one who told me about Concept Magic. About…about the inside of the inside." She paused, as if struggling for words. "This was…it was like I could see the connections, I guess? The threads that ran through to all of them. So all I did was swing and sever." She said it like she wasn't sure she believed it herself. "Didn't know if it would work. Thought I might have just wasted almost everything for nothing."

The Sever mark. How clever. I had been meaning to tell her what I suspected about her power, but it seemed that was unnecessary. No wonder this was the Hero who finally killed me. I almost smiled.

Isabelle had gone rigid. Her hands hung at her sides, and for the very first time, she took a step. It was a single step forward before she righted herself. Hers was a look I had seen often. It was the look of someone who had thought themselves invincible, and had been proven wrong by mere insects. That gaze moved from me to Ash. The small smile she had carried from the start vanished in an instant. In the next, she moved. I was still staring at where she had been. Ash let out a choke. Isabelle was next to her, one hand around Ash's throat, and Ash's feet were no longer on the ground.

Isabelle tore Ash's sleeves with her free hand, exposing her marks to the moonlight. She regarded the Hero for a long moment. Ash let out a croak as Isabelle's grip on her throat tightened. "I see now. You have a Sovereign." The word came out flat. Then she sneered. "It's something to do with cutting, isn't it? I really should thank you. A human with a mark like this becomes a problem very quickly."

Ash hissed, her free right hand clawing at her throat. It did not do much.

"Let her go."

Isabelle looked at me. She was still holding Ash aloft. "Lys." Ash's voice was strained but steady.

I raised my right hand. Black flame curled from my fingers. Isabelle regarded the flames with all the boredom in the world.

"And just what is that tiny little Line you're hiding going to do to me?" Isabelle asked. "I don't even think you have Essence."

"Let. Her. Go." I held the Requiem ready. "Or I will end you."

The smile stayed, though something behind it flickered. "Well? Give it your best shot, Lys. I'll let you have one. Maybe you'll reconsider after this one is dead."

Isabelle was bored. She had expected more. She always did. The half-demon with the pretty eyes had been a real find, for the first time in years. A new Sister, precious enough to be worth the escort of half an army.

And so Isabelle had made the half-demon an offer, the only one that mattered. Isabelle had been refused. She had been refused…for a human.

They always refused at first. They always came around later, once the world had shown them the lesson it always showed their kind. Isabelle held the human by the throat.

The woman clawed at her fingers with a grip that might have troubled someone weaker.

The human's Sovereign mark was the problem -at Line, it was manageable, but people with marks like that could climb as high as the heavens. It was a true blessing that Isabelle could snuff her out here. The Archon should have come himself.

The woman in her grip twisted. Isabelle tightened her hold and let her gaze travel. She'd be killing this one, so she might as well see what she was killing. The human was muscled and toned. A woman whose body was used to fighting. Yet, that was not all she was. She was soft and generous in ways that might just explain why a half-demon would be stupid enough to refuse an offer from her kind. So, that was it then. This human slut truly was the reason.

Isabelle almost laughed. Desire made fools of everyone, and it seemed even half-demons were not immune.

She glanced at Lys. The half-demon stood ten paces away with her right hand raised and those pretty black flames guttering from her fingers. Impressive enough for what must have been a Pyre mark just at Line. Isabelle had never seen flames like those before, but they'd dealt with her clones. Isabelle had barely put any Essence into the clones, but still, the girl had dealt with them.

That was something. Then Isabelle looked at the girl's face, and her amusement died.

It died because there was no fear in those red eyes. None at all. That simply made no sense. Isabelle had fought countless battles. She had fought others with Runes. Had even survived a battle with a Script. Isabelle had killed more people than this Lys had ever even met, and every time, there had been fear, however finely suppressed. Some brief flicker of doubt, however half-buried. The survival instinct was a stubborn thing, and it never quite let go, not even of the strong.

There was none of that in this Lys. What Isabelle saw instead was certainty. A certainty that was so sure of itself that it had gone past belief and into knowledge. The half-demon's expression was something calm. Something that simply knew that it could destroy anything between itself and the thing it wanted to reach. Isabelle had served her Mistress for three hundred years. She had watched her break armies. She had watched her Mistress stare down people even Isabelle would run from.

But she had never seen this look on even her Mistress's face. It made no sense.  It was like reaching into a den and poking what you thought was a simple beast, and then the den moved, and you realized the beast was a dragon, and the den was its mouth, and the darkness above you was not the sky but the rest of it. Isabelle's hand almost loosened on the human's throat. Almost.

Isabelle smiled wider and tightened her grip on the human's throat. "Well? Give it your best shot, Lys! I'll let you have one."

The black flame came. It came as a thin burst of fire, one that was barely worth noticing at all. It crashed into her and broke across her skin. Her Tenet of Reflection shielded her as it always did. The only thing that burned was the small flicker of doubt Isabelle had had. See? It's just a Line, wielded by just a girl. Nothing to be afraid of.

A finger traced across her collarbone. Isabelle stared at the human, and at the hand that had traced that line on her skin. When had Isabelle stopped holding the human's arm? Then her body split open. There was blood, her blood. It ran down her from a wound that should not have been possible, not from a Line. Her Tenet was gone. More black fire came. It found the wound. It went through. The pain came from somewhere that was not her body.

"What-" Isabelle gasped. "What did you-" She screamed.

I raised my right hand and poured everything I had into a single lance of Ruin. Black flame crossed the distance and struck Isabelle's chest. It scattered across her skin without harming the woman at all.

Whatever defense her mark provided, my Requiem could not breach it. My mana was draining, and all for an attack that did nothing. This Isabelle did not seem bothered at all. Then Ash's left hand rose towards the woman's collarbone and traced a single line across that silver skin.

Blood welled and spurted from a cut that had not been there a moment before. Isabelle stared down at herself in genuine shock. I raised my hand, just slightly. Just enough to aim my Ruin at this new wound instead. There was no defense now. My Ruin breached the skin, to the thing beneath, to the concept that sat at the very core of the woman's strange defense. It was a concept, one that simply said Reflect. It was woven into the fabric of what she was. The Requiem found it, and erased it.

The woman let out a howl. My right hand fell to my side. My legs wobbled and nearly followed before I steadied them. That attack had used most of the mana I still had, and so I was now left with nothing. Isabelle hissed and stepped back, dropping Ash. The Hero rolled on the ground and came up beside me, her sword already raised. She coughed. The red marks circled her throat. I reached Ash before I had decided to. "Are you hurt?"

"I'm fine," she said. Her voice was hoarse. "Did it work?"

The answer stood before us. Isabelle clutched at her neck, where the blackened skin was slowly spreading.

Silver had turned to grey and grey was turning to black. The rot was slowly crawling through her. With her strange defense gone, she was as vulnerable as anyone. I was panting. I had little left for another attack like the first.

Oddly, Isabelle started to laugh. She touched her blackened skin, and her violet eyes narrowed. "You fucking traitor," she hissed, staring at Ash and then at me. "You would rather bleed for a human than stand with your own kind?!"

She laughed more. "I see," she said between breaths. "I see, I see." Her violet eyes found me, and they were wide. "I fucking see now! It's not just the one girl with the Sovereign." She looked at my right arm, at the black Line that hid underneath the cloth. "Both of you then. A half-demon traitor with a Sovereign. I should have fucking known."

Her laughter slowly died. She stared up at the sky, hand covering her face. She took a single very deep breath, one we shouldn't have been able to hear, and yet did. She looked back at us. "Fine," Isabelle said. She straightened. The blackened skin pulsed as she moved, and I saw her wince. "I've changed my mind. Both of you die here. My Mistress can forgive me later."

Ash and I set our feet. "You…you can't do that again, can you?" Ash asked.

"No." I did have some power left, but I suspected this woman would not let me use Ruin twice.

Isabelle raised her right hand in front of her face. Her index finger pointed up at the sky. The silver mirror on her arm blazed so bright it cast shadows across the entire clearing. The air grew heavier. The world all around us began to shimmer and gleam. The entire clearing began to sway, every color shifting and turning. Ash had to steady me lest I fall down to my knees. The woman was using this Essence, and a lot of it.

"Let me show you two fucking whores," Isabelle whispered, "the peak of fighting with ink. Those Covenant bastards never would. A damn shame you find out moments before death."

The pressure built. It pressed against my skull, against my chest, against the inside of my teeth. Ash shifted beside me, and I saw her jaw clench. The ground began to change. The air began to change. Then the pressure stopped so abruptly it almost made me stumble.

Isabelle's eyes went wide. She took a step back. I thought I saw her knees wobble.

"Mother," Isabelle said, dropping to one knee. Her jaw snapped shut. "M-Mistress," she said quickly. Her gaze fixed on the floor, and her head dipped lower still.

A presence came from the edge of the clearing and slowly moved over it. It was a weight. One that was heavier than Isabelle's power. Heavier than the Phoenix. Heavier than anything I had felt since waking in this world.

There, at the far edge of the clearing. Where I had looked and seen no-one. I looked now and saw a woman. As if the shadows had hidden her, and now had simply parted, just as they had once done for me. She stepped past the line of trees and the moonlight shone down on her. The very first thing I saw was her dress. It was a [Battle Dress], one in dark brown, fitted perfectly to her body.

Then I saw the horns -two dark arcs sweeping back from the temples, ending in points as sharp as any blade.

Then the woman's skin, the color of ash. Then her dark hair, falling past her shoulders to the ground. Finally, the two red wings, furled behind her.

The recognition came, and when it did it struck me as strong as any blow might have. I knew those amber eyes. I had given them orders. I had trusted them. I had wrung the life from them.

The demon's mark shone in the moonlight. The Ink was a deep red that covered most of her right arm. It was in the shape of a four legged beast, and it moved. It moved up and down the length of her arm. Even now, something in me catalogued the stage. It was beyond anything I had seen, such that I could only guess how far the gap between this woman and everyone else was.

Then I saw the darkness. I saw it from the gaps between her battle dress. Dark veins of black started at her left shoulder and moved all the way across her collarbone and down toward her chest. Everywhere that black touched looked sickly. As if the flesh was only just clinging to life.

A name came to me. One that would not leave. Morrigan. How? How could this be? It should not- I forced the thoughts away. It took far longer than it should have.

"Isabelle." The voice was low and yet it carried. Hers was a beautiful voice, one I knew. I had heard it sing, once, in the deepest halls of my fortress.

Isabelle flinched where she knelt. She was staring hard at the ground. Only at the ground. "Mistress, I had this under control. The half-demon has a Sovereign mark. I was just about to-"

"I felt what you were about to do. I also felt what they did to you. I told you that Sovereigns were different, didn't I? You cannot judge them just by their stage." Morrigan's gaze never left us.

Isabelle's hand went to her neck, where the blackened skin from my Requiem still spread. It looked to be spreading far slower now. Perhaps something of her power had returned.

Morrigan walked forward slowly. She passed Isabelle without a glance and stopped ten paces from us. Nothing in her posture was guarded. Her eyes never once so much as touched Ash's sword. Neither Ash nor I moved. I knew death when I saw it. I had been death, once. Her gaze moved over Ash first. It was a brief look, one that lasted no more than a half-second.

Then Morrigan looked at me, and my entire body went rigid. Morrigan studied me. She studied my face, my horns, and the arms where my Lines hid beneath cloth. Her gaze moved back up and lingered on my eyes. Something flickered at the corner of her mouth. Her head tilted, just slightly, and for one moment I was certain she saw me. Me, as I was, not as I am. "Interesting," she said quietly.  "So you two are the ones that old man sent so many soldiers to collect. I can see why." She glanced at my arms, though the sleeves covered them. "You have two Sovereigns, don't you?" she said. I said nothing. "On a half-demon, no less. That makes you the very first in this world."

"Mistress," Isabelle said again, her voice regaining some of its composure. "This one refused my offer. She sided with the human. She-"

"I heard you." Morrigan did not turn. "I also saw her hurt you. That is more noteworthy than anything you have said."

Isabelle fell silent. Morrigan looked at me for a moment longer. Her amber eyes held mine, and I stared back into a face I had known for centuries. The black veins at her shoulder pulsed once.

Her head tilted to one side. Something in her eyes shone. "You are talented, and clever. You understand a little of what makes Sovereign marks different from all the others." Her head tilted again, this time to the other side. "To have so much understanding at just a Line. That would be rare enough on its own. But you are also rather stubborn. Like someone I once served under, a very long time ago." My hands went still at my sides. She must have noticed. "That is not a flaw," Morrigan continued, and her voice softened in a way I had heard only once before. "Stubbornness is a virtue, in the right hands." She regarded Ash for a moment. "I do hope you do not waste too much of yours on things that will not last." She turned and was already walking.

"Isabelle. We are leaving."

"But-"

"She will come to us in time, or she will not. Either way, she is one of ours. We do not kill our family for refusing invitations."

"T-then the other one. Surely we can kill her now."

"We could," Morrigan agreed, turning back to me. "But I hope this Lys understands the…favor we are showing her by now." She turned back. "Come now. It seems that black flame was rather…destructive. You will need to be seen to."

She walked into the trees, out of sight. Isabelle stared at us. Her violet eyes burned, but she did not argue further. She spat on the ground between us, turned, and followed her Mistress into the dark. Then there was silence.

Ash was speaking. I knew this because her mouth was moving and sound was coming from it. I could not hear the words. There was a roaring in my ears, or perhaps it was only the absence of everything else.

My knees almost hit the earth. Ash was beside me in an instant. "Hey…steady now."

I looked at her. "Do you know who that was?" Ash asked softly.

My mouth was dry. The name sat in my throat like a stone. "Someone," I whispered, "who should not exist."

Mother. The word that half-demon had used echoed in my head.
