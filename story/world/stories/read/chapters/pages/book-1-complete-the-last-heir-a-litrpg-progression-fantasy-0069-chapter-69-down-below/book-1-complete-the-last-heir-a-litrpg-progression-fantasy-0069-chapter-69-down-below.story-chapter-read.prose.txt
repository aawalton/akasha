The barrier proved much easier to deal with than Rowan had expected. It was made by a tier-three affinity, something that should have made destroying it a monumental task, yet Rowan’s [Blazing Chains] made quick work of it.

The Crystal cracked after a particularly vicious lash, shattering the covering and exposing a staircase leading further down.

Rowan took a step back, flaring [Iron Will] to make sure he didn’t do anything particularly stupid. His first instinct was to just walk in and wreak havoc, but that was a fantastic way to get himself killed.

High overhead, Kai flew, the familiar beat of his wings audible even over the smoldering tavern behind him.

It’s time to start actively working on his advancement, he decided. This isn’t Litwick. If we encounter real trouble here, Kai can’t do much to help, and getting him killed is not an option.

The Storm Crown definitely had a Druid, and the bonding ritual was happening as soon as his familiar advanced to Silver. That would elevate him in strength quite a bit. Hopefully, to the point where Rowan wouldn’t need to worry.

Taking a deep breath, he pulled out a mana potion.

Rowan drank his fill, the cool liquid making its way down his throat and settling in his stomach. His reserves weren’t all that depleted—even with the amount of spells he’d cast—but every little bit helped.

[Iron Will] worked in tandem with his first trait to allow Rowan to replenish his mana at a monstrous pace. He wouldn’t be able to do this during a battle since it required focus, but the potion would still do its work in the background.

Next, he summoned the Minor Staff of Wind. The weapon had been helpful on more than one occasion, yet Rowan’s abilities had outgrown it. Instead of making his casting faster and cheaper, it did the opposite. Behaving as a stopgap between his magic and the outside world.

Storing it back, Rowan cracked his knuckles.

Buying another staff was something he’d have to think about. There was bound to be a magic shop in the region's capital, and he knew that getting one was the smart thing to do, but Rowan couldn’t deny there was something satisfying about using nothing but his own capabilities.

Still, it was definitely something to think about.

What do I know about the threat? he asked himself. It’s almost level seventy, and it has a tier-three affinity, meaning it devoured a Green-Core mage. Not to mention that definitely isn’t the only power it stole.

Demons of Envy were capable of consuming their prey and absorbing an aspect of their strength, be it skill or spell. But their true power lay in infiltration and subterfuge. They took on the forms of others through their inherent Illusion magic, combining it with their power-stealing to create monsters particularly suited for deception.

It wasn’t perfect, and there were ways to see through it, but they were still among the most dangerous of demonkind.

At least Demons of Wrath or Pride are straightforward, Rowan thought, smoothing out his trusty coat. Shapeshifters are just annoying.

The fact that this monster made its lair so close to a major city spoke ill of the times to come. Because if there was one, there were definitely more, and whatever they were planning couldn’t be good for the Kingdom of Vandral.

I’ll need to tell someone about this. Someone in charge.

Rowan sighed, running a hand through his hair. I guess that means no vacation for me.

He’d hoped to have some time to dedicate to the dozen or so things he needed to work on, but preventing the Demons' plans took precedence. Countless people called the Storm Crown their home. Innocent men, women, and children, all of them expecting safety from those capable of offering it.

It was a strange thought that Rowan was now one of those people, but he held to it with a fierce grip. This was exactly what he wanted his whole life. A way to shape his surroundings, and his future.

I’m the Duke of Eiseylth, he reminded himself, a wave of determination washing over him. I have a duty to the people of this kingdom, even if these aren’t my lands.

With his resolve set, Rowan prepared himself to enter the Demon's lair.

The first thing he did was cast [Fire Shield], covering his neck and back. Then he spent the next few minutes tweaking his Intent.

His mana flowed smoother than ever, following the familiar circuit of the Murmur-level spell like a rapid against a riverbank. He still hadn’t gotten completely used to the changes [Storm Touched] inflicted on his abilities, but Rowan felt confident enough in his own talent to tweak a few things on the fly.

[Fire Shield] was a costly spell. It guzzled mana like it was nobody's business, even when there was nothing to contend against. Just having it active was a drain, one Rowan could hopefully lessen—at least to a degree.

Alright, let’s try this.

His mind focused, [Iron Will] working to keep his thoughts on task.

Rowan started with a slight tweak to the circuit, changing a loop near his lower back to a straight line going to his hip.

The cost rose, and he quickly changed it back.

He frowned, not letting the setback discourage him.

Just the fact his spell didn’t destabilize was a victory in and of itself. Changing an active manifestation wasn’t something mages did—at least not if they wanted to live a long life—but it seemed Rowan’s new trait offered some leeway in that regard.

Pulling and prodding at his Intent ended the same way, upping the cost and lowering his efficiency. It took Rowan a few more minutes of trial and error before he managed something that could be generously described as a success.

The intensity of his spell lowered, but its cohesion stayed the same.

A grin tugged at his lips.

What he’d just done wasn’t a massive difference, but it was the first step in hopefully adding a condition to his spell. He’d created two states for his spell to be in, one with a lower mana draw, and the other at full power. It would take more work to fully integrate it, but this was good enough for now.

With [Fire Shield] covering his neck and back, Rowan stepped towards the staircase.

The entrance yawned before him like the jaws of a beast. The shattered remnants of Crystal still clung to the edges of the stone, jagged shards jutting out like broken teeth, reflecting the dim glow of his flames.

Beyond the shattered barrier, the air shifted, carrying with it a cold, stagnant stench that pressed against his skin.

A thick darkness pooled at the top of the stairs. Rowan couldn’t see further than a few feet down before shadows swallowed the light. The stone steps were worn smooth—not from erosion, but from use. Something had walked down this path countless times, its passage carving subtle dips into the stone.

With a thought, Rowan cast [Firebolt].

The spell flared to life in his palm, the ball of flame tightly contained by his will. Flicking his wrist, Rowan lobbed it down the stairs, observing as it illuminated the passage leading below.

It followed the path, impacting the bottom a few heartbeats later with a loud explosion. The power was similar to a [Fireball], Rowan’s new trait on full display.

A cackle echoed from somewhere below, “No, not a mageling,” the Demon spoke, its voice echoing strangely. “A fully fledged mage,” Rowan could almost hear it licking its lips. “Come, spellweaver, your prize waits below.”

He didn’t let its words disturb him, instead going over what his spell had revealed.

The walls bore faint markings, sigils that had long since faded into obscurity. This deeper part of the tavern seemed older, hewn from the same stone that made up the Imperial Road.

Rowan focused on the markings, recalling his lessons.

[Iron Will] allowed him to deepen his concentration, the faint etchings coming together into something resembling Old Imperial. Rowan had spent a summer learning the runes, but he hadn’t encountered them since then, meaning he was a bit out of practice. Still, it didn’t take him long to manage it.

House Thane, he translated, a kernel of suspicion planted. This is a Peak Duskridge compound.

A location like this was bound to be occupied, and considering the Demon had seemingly been here for quite a while, House Thane should have noticed. The fact that nothing had been done raised questions that Rowan planned on answering once he reached the Storm Crown.

Even if it isn’t House Thane, one of the three is definitely involved. Rowan’s frown deepened. Four, he realized. The Baroness too.

That would have been the worst possible scenario, and Rowan hoped that wasn’t the case. Last he knew, she was the only Mythril-ranked Yellow-Core warrior in the region—a powerhouse he wasn’t even close to matching. Not that the three Lords were all that much different, but the point still stood. If the Baroness was involved with Demons, things were not looking great.

Well, Rowan cracked his neck, taking a deep breath. I know someone who can probably answer a few of my questions.

His gaze settled on the staircase, two [Blazing Chains] manifesting from his shoulders. He curled them protectively at his sides, covering what his [Fire Shield] didn’t.

A faint drip, drip, drip echoed from below, the sound distant but sharp in the unnatural silence. It wasn’t water—Rowan had spent enough time in caves to recognize the difference. It was thicker. Heavier.

Without hesitation, he took a step forward.

Kai let out a loud caw from above, wishing him luck

Rowan allowed himself a small smile.

Don’t worry, buddy, he promised. I’ll bring you back its head.

The darkness receded as his spells illuminated the path, Rowan’s chains scraping against the walls and steps. He wasn’t stupid enough to just walk in there without checking for traps, and it wasn’t long before he found them.

An enchantment flared as a chain passed over it. A spear of Crystal manifested on the ground, shooting out towards the ceiling. Rowan was far enough away that it missed, a ‘Tsk’ echoing off the walls.

He took care of four more before he reached the bottom, his eyes widening in surprise. An expression of pure incomprehension flashed across Rowan’s face, the shock almost enough to make his spells falter.

The Demon stood in front of a floating tear in space, ethereal violet sparks flashing across its edge.

It caught his eye, a wicked grin stretching across its face. “Thank you for saving me the trouble of carrying you down here,” the Demon inclined his head. “Out of every activity in this wonderful realm, that one is among my least favourite.”

Rowan knew he should have been focusing on the monster standing in front of him, but his eyes were drawn to the impossibility floating behind it.

A Rift.

No, it can’t be, Rowan’s mind raced. Rifts only appear in the far Wilds, beyond the Walls.

But that obviously wasn’t true.

The fact that there was one here shattered Rowan’s understanding of the subject. Rifts were the way in which monster populations grew. They appeared out of seemingly nothing, and if one wasn’t cleared quickly enough, a Rift Break occurred. Those spewed out the beasts inhabiting it, fueling the usual surge cycle.

This definitely isn’t the only one, Rowan realized. If there’s one, there’s more of them.

His jaw clenched, the scale of what he’d uncovered growing.

“Uuu, a smart one,” the Demon chuckled, raising its clawed hand. “Tell you what, if you make this fun for me, I might be persuaded to shine some light on what you’re seeing.”

Gleaming shards of Crystal formed in its palm, quickly growing in number. “Here, catch.”

The spell erupted towards him, and Rowan pushed all the questions this encounter dredged up to the back of his mind. Without a doubt, this would be his toughest opponent yet. But even still, he felt more than up to the challenge.
