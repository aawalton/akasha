Chapter 82

A handful of villagers moved through the square of Hoshin, their shoulders hunched against a thin, grey wind that slipped between the huts. It tugged at loose strands of hair and whispered through the empty spaces.

They collected woven baskets and checked the edges of their knives, speaking in low voices as they prepared to head to the gathering grounds, hopeful for what the day might yield. Their steps dragged, weighted by a routine that no longer brought comfort.

Tama stood at the center of the village beside the piled tribute. Piles of ore, bundles of wood, and rough sacks of grain waited in neat rows on a broad mat of reeds. She kept her hands folded in front of her, fingers laced tight enough that the knuckles showed white. The wind carried the faint scent of damp earth and smoke from distant cookfires, but it did nothing to ease the tightness in her chest.

Her gaze drifted past the treeline to the south and west. Black smoke rose through the trees there, thick and oily, climbing into the overcast sky in slow, twisting columns. It billowed far enough away that the village itself lay untouched, yet close enough to take note. The plume did not come from Riley's compound. That direction held only the familiar green of the forest, this smoke came from somewhere else entirely, and its presence pressed against her thoughts like a warning.

Her stomach twisted. She shifted her weight from one foot to the other, the cool air raising goosebumps along her arms beneath her sleeves. What if the Clawborn arrived angry today? What if they decided the tribute was not enough, or that the village had hidden more away? She pictured their heavy boots kicking through the neat piles, their claws flashing as they dragged someone forward for an example. The memory of past visits lingered too clearly, the way voices had cracked and children had been pulled in for protection.

She swallowed hard and glanced toward the southern trail again. Riley's soldiers were supposed to meet the quota this time, to stand between the village and the worst of the demand. The idea had sounded solid in the planning stage, yet doubt crept in and made the whole thing seem thin. Would the soldiers keep their cover as Hoshins? Would the Clawborn see through this facade? Perhaps if the tribute looked full enough they would simply take it and leave without searching the huts or counting heads. Perhaps the smoke was nothing to do with them at all.

Tama drew a slow breath that did little to loosen the pressure behind her ribs. She kept her face still for the sake of the others still moving around her, but inside the questions circled, feeding the nervous flutter that refused to settle. The wind pushed again, carrying the distant smell of rotten eggs with it, and she tightened her fingers until the nails bit into her palms.

Tama turned slowly and let her gaze rest on Mali’s old house at the edge of the square. She remembered how children used to chase each other around that very threshold, their laughter bright against the morning air. Now the yard stood empty, the ground packed hard and silent. No small feet kicked up dust and no voices called out in games. The village felt smaller, as if it were quietly folding in on itself and fading away.

She wondered how Mali and the others were settling into their new home. Did the walls feel safe around them, or did the weight of their decision still keep them awake at night? The questions rose gently, without urgency, and she let them linger for a moment before pushing them away.

This is only transitional, she told herself. She would join them soon enough. The thought settle over her like a warm blanket against the cool wind, easing the tightness in her shoulders and the feeling of worry fading. That small comfort bloomed in her chest, quietly.

Nearby, one of the villagers knelt beside a woven pack, carefully folding a few spare garments and a small cooking pot into its depths. His movements were deliberate, almost reverent, as he prepared to leave for Riley’s community. Tama watched him secure the straps with steady hands. The corners of Tama's mouth curled slightly. Those who left would have a real chance at survival now, away from the constant shadow of the Clawborn. They would wake each morning without frantically counting baskets in anticipation of days like this one.

The same warmth deepened as she thought of the hope and safety the Hoshin people would soon feel. Children might run and play again without fear tightening their voices. Families could plan beyond the next tribute day. For the first time in their lives, her people had a genuine chance at a future.

Tama stood a little straighter, the cool wind brushing across her face. A quiet sense of accomplishment filled her. She had helped open this path for them. As their leader, she had made the hard choices and held the village together long enough for this change to take root. The feeling anchored her, steady and proud, even as the grey sky pressed low overhead and the black smoke still curled in the distance.

One of Riley’s soldiers, dressed in the simple tunic and trousers of a Hoshin villager, returned from the fields carrying another heavy full basket. He caught Tama’s eye across the square and gave a single, steady nod before continuing toward the tribute pile.

The old Hoshin healer stepped out from her low dwelling, her thin shawl pulled tight around her shoulders. She paused at the edge of the square and fixed her gaze on the stacked offerings, her lined face unreadable.

A thunderous sound of galloping horses rose from the northern trail, growing louder with every heartbeat. The few remaining villagers in the square exchanged quick glances and began to hurry away at a busy pace, their feet kicking up small clouds of dirt as they headed toward the work fields. One woman tripped over a loose stone and dropped her basket. Another reached down without a word, helped her to her feet, and steadied her arm. Both of them looked back at Tama for a brief moment, then turned and walked quickly away.

The disguised soldier remained unbothered by the noise. He placed his basket neatly with the others and calmly walked toward the pile of empty baskets stacked nearby.

The healer moved closer to the soldier and stopped beside him; her hands folded calmly in front of her.

The Clawborn horses thundered through the village gates and skidded to a halt, their hooves churning up a thick cloud of dust that rolled across the square. Tama lifted her hands to cover her face as the grit stung her eyes and coated her lips. Through the haze she saw Korr-Va scan the village with sharp, sweeping glances before he swung down from his saddle in one fluid motion.

Moments later a heavy Clawborn wagon, pulled by four broad horses, rumbled through the gate and came to a stop beside the riders. All the Clawborn dismounted at once. They stood in loose formation, their eyes fixed on Korr-Va as they waited for his instruction. The dust slowly began to settle around their boots and the wheels of the wagon.

Korr-Va moved forward with slow, deliberate steps, his heavy boots crunching over the packed earth of the square. His gaze swept across the stacked baskets and bundles without expression, taking in every detail of the tribute.

Tama felt her stomach begin to turn as he drew closer, a cold twist that rose toward her throat. She kept her feet planted even as the distance between them shrank.

The soldier stood perfectly still, his posture straight and his eyes fixed on Korr-Va. The old Hoshin healer remained just behind him, her small frame steady and her hands clasped in front of her.

Korr-Va kept his eyes on the tribute the entire way, never once meeting Tama’s gaze. When he finally stopped directly in front of her, he lifted his head and looked her dead in the eyes.

Tama jerked her arms upward from her sides to stomach height, an instinctive motion that pulled her shoulders inward as if to shield herself.

“We were able to meet the required tribute by working…” she began, her voice coming out weaker than she intended.

Korr-Va cut her off by shoving her aside with one rough push of his arm. She stumbled a step to the side as he leaned forward and looked down at the baskets that had been hidden behind where she had stood.

The soldier took a sharp step forward, his hands tightening at his sides. The old healer reached out quickly and caught his arm, her grip firm. She leaned close and whispered, “Don’t. Please.”

The soldier turned his head and looked at the old woman for a moment, then stepped back, his feet in line once again.

Korr-Va lifted his head and locked eyes with the soldier. For a long moment the two men stared at each other without moving. Korr-Va exhaled slowly through his nostrils. The soldier did not blink. Korr-Va narrowed his eyes and gave a low grunt.

Tama backed away a step and raised her hands to chest height with her palms facing outward toward Korr-Va. Her eyes widened.

Suddenly the old Hoshin healer began to sing in a thin, wavering voice. She moved forward in a slow walking dance, lifting her hands high and swaying as she approached Korr-Va in the manner of a tribal ceremony from long ago. She pointed both arms toward the tribute, then dropped to her knees and fell forward at Korr-Va’s feet as though worshiping him.

Korr-Va watched the old woman and broke eye contact with the soldier. When she finally stopped at his boots the entire village grew silent for a long moment. Tama felt her heart pounding in her throat. Every breath she took sent hot air curling over her lips while the rest of her body stayed frozen in place.

The old woman raised her head and looked up at Korr-Va. She offered a mostly toothless smile.

Korr-Va grunted, waved one hand in dismissal, and turned to shout something at the other Clawborn. They laughed and began loading the tribute into their wagon.

Korr-Va glanced back at the soldier. He tilted his head slightly, studying him as though weighing something in his mind. By then the soldier had already turned away with an empty basket in his hands and was walking calmly toward the gathering fields.

Korr-Va swung up onto his horse while the last of the tribute disappeared into the wagon. Moments later the entire group rode out of the village in a thunder of hooves and swirling dust.

Tama stepped forward and helped the old healer to her feet as the Clawborn disappeared through the gates. The cool wind continued to move across the now-empty square, carrying away the last traces of their presence.
