Saturday. Eastern Wing's infiltration day.

"Thirteen minutes to go before the meeting…"

As the time drew closer to the meeting, Amy couldn't help but feel nervous. She stood from her bed, placing her amateur painting she had been doing to pass time carefully on her desk. "Shit… Guess it's really time to go."

[…How are you feeling…?]

The simple question carried a kind of weight that she wasn’t able to describe. Amy's hand stilled completely. It took her more than a few minutes to respond.

"Terrified," she admitted quietly. "Somewhere in that wing..." She trailed off, her throat tightening.

[Bad memories…?]

"Yeah." Amy turned away from Libris, her back now facing the book. "My nightmares made flesh, probably. And I'm walking right toward them."

[Are you having second thoughts?]

After a slight second of hesitation, she nodded. "Yeah. I keep thinking about what you said… about that thing of leaving everything behind…"

[You can still do it.]

"I know…and I can't believe I'm considering it. But, for the moment, I don't think I will. I can do this." She squared her shoulders. "Besides, I've got you."

[Indeed you do. Though I can't support you directly. Hope you remember that.]

"Yet. Once I make the readers believe we share a special power…" Amy muttered, running through their practiced demonstrations in her mind.

[I'm certain you'll succeed. You can be quite convincing when you want to be.]

Amy smiled weakly at the book. "Thanks, I think." She checked the time again. "We should get going." She stretched, trying to ignore the subtle tremor in her hands. "Can you get me something to wear? Something..."

[Practical? Comfortable? Tactical?]

"Something iconic. But not too crazy. I want to look like someone they'll remember, but not like I'm trying too hard."

[Iconic?]

Amy grinned at the book's confusion. "Remember our conversation after having tea with Zayd? I told you I needed to be more proactive. I'm going to make the readers see me—actually see me. I'm going to make everyone fucking love me. Even the haters would have no choice but to care about me."

[Something iconic. Very well.]

Libris took a moment to consider it. Then gold spread from its pages and surrounded her. When Amy could see herself again, she was dressed in fitted black pants, a close-fitting top, and a black tunic. The boots looked expensive. Gold lines and details like little jewels ran through the outfit, with a cloak to finish it.

She turned in front of the mirror and tried moving her arms. "Oh. I can actually move in this. It looks good, too."

[You do have to get through tonight while wearing it.]

"Yeah, you did well." She adjusted the cloak and checked the mirror again.

[Naturally.] Libris rose until it was level with her face. [Shall we?]

Amy hesitantly nodded, taking one final deep breath. She wasn't ready—not really. But it didn't matter. Ready or not, her past was waiting, and this time she couldn't run away from it.

She stepped into the hallway of her dormitory building, Libris at her arms. There wasn't anyone around. Most people probably had better things to do with their Saturday. Amy tugged at her new clothes as she walked. "Third floor, right? For Crow's room?"

[Fourth. You want room 417.]

"Four...?" She stopped. "I just realized that's just one floor above me. Is it because we are both class S? But Zayd's room is far from here, no?"

[People living vertically live mere meters apart. Destiny is quite romantic.]

"Please don't start. I have enough to think about." Amy headed for the stairs. The elevator would be quicker, which was a good reason not to take it.

Her boots were surprisingly quiet on the steps. She almost wished the climb took longer.

At the fourth floor, she looked down both sides of the corridor. "Which way?"

[Left. Fifth door.]

She counted as she walked. Four doors went past much too quickly.

Then she was outside 417, with no more walking left to put off knocking. Amy raised her hand and looked at the wood.

[Nervous?]

"No." She sighed. "A bit."

She kept her hand there for a while without touching the door.

This was it—no turning back. Fuck it! Let's do this.

Amy knocked three times. Crow opened it so quickly she wondered if he'd been standing behind it. He looked at her, then at Libris beside her shoulder.

"Come in."

She stepped past him. The room hadn't changed since her last visit; there still wasn't much decoration. Most of the space on the table was covered with maps and loose notes, and everyone had gathered around it.

Lyra sat on the bed with some snacks, looking about as frightened as Amy felt. Ash had spread himself out in a chair, trying to look comfortable and not quite managing it. Lain followed a line on a map with her finger. In the corner, Zayd sat with his legs crossed and a cup of tea balanced on his knee. So far, exactly what Amy remembered reading.

Except... hang on.

At the other end of the table, a girl had looked up and was staring back at her. Iris.

Wait…what?

Amy stopped just inside the room.

Iris Wright. The tall, copper-skinned senior who kept appearing in the manga. Her braids were tied back, and she'd rolled up her uniform sleeves over muscular forearms. From the look she was giving Amy, neither of them had expected to see the other.

Crow cleared his throat. "That's everyone. We can start."

"At last." Ash put his feet on the table. "The golden princess has arrived."

"I'm not late." Amy checked the clock just to be sure. Exactly on time, actually.

"Stake?" Iris asked, looking her over. "You're coming with us?"

Amy nodded. She was still trying to work out what to say.

What the hell is she doing here?

There had been no Iris in this part of the manga. Amy was certain about that. Another thing that had changed.

[Perhaps this is another effect of your presence.]

Fuck me… can't things just be simple?

"She found out yesterday," Crow said. "Ash was talking in the training hall about our new companion." He glanced at Zayd, who smiled back at him.

"I didn't know she was in the equipment room," Ash protested. At least he looked embarrassed.

"Then she decided she was joining us."

"I didn't insist on anything." Iris folded her arms. "I gave you a choice. Let me come or explain the whole thing to Vanheim."

"That's worse," Lyra said from the bed.

"Well, I'm here now." Iris shrugged. "You aren't doing this without me."

Crow looked as though he had plenty to say about that, but kept it to himself. Ash was already smiling again. Lyra appeared nervous, while Lain remained as expressionless as ever. Zayd, seated cross-legged in a chair in the corner of the table, simply observed with quiet interest.

"Excuse her, Amy," Crow said, his expression in a perpetual scowl. "She's just annoying like that."

"And violent too!" Ash interjected with a grin. "Broke my arm during our assessment match last semester."

"You deserved it," Iris replied without looking at him.

"I did," he agreed easily.

Crow cleared his throat again, more forcefully this time. "We're wasting time. We need to finalize our plan before curfew."

Amy nodded, taking the empty chair between Lain and Zayd.

Lain greeted her with a nod, which Amy returned.

"Miss Stake," Zayd greeted her too, with a small bow of his head. "I trust you are well?"

"Well enough," she replied, studying him carefully; she still couldn't believe he was related to the Tribe of Onyx. Contrary to Kaelen, she couldn’t find anything suspicious about this seemingly kind and good-looking noble.

"And you?"

"Managing," he said with a hint of a smile. "Thank you for relaying my message to Crow. I suspect I wouldn't have been welcomed if it weren't for you."

Amy nodded, not really knowing how she had affected Crow's decision; in the original story, Zayd was with them, so even if she wasn’t the one who gave the paper, probably nothing would have changed.

"Alright. We should begin now; let's not waste any more time." Crow said, raising his voice slightly to get everyone's attention then turned toward the map spread across the table. "Our objective is to reach the Eastern Wing through the headmistress' office. There's a hidden passage there."

"And remind me again why we can't just use the main entrance?" Iris asked, trying to find it on the map.

"Have you seen it since Building B?" Ash said. "They've got professors watching the entrance now, on top of the extra guards."

"Okay. And the headmistress' office is a better option because…?" Iris said, looking just as confused as before.

"Because once we’re in, it would be smooth sailing toward our objective," Crow responded.

"Objective, huh. What exactly are we looking for in there? You haven't told me yet," Iris asked.

A momentary silence fell over the room as everyone looked at him.

"...A place called The Library," Crow finally answered, his voice measured. "We believe it contains information about recent events at the Academy."

"Building B," Zayd nodded, understanding. "You think there's a connection."

"Yes," Crow confirmed. "Among other things…"

"Alright then," Iris rolled her shoulders, seemingly satisfied with the explanation. "So what's the plan for getting into the headmistress' office?"

Crow spread out the plan of the administrative building. "Third floor, east side. That's her office. Two guards stay outside, with a new pair every six hours. Then there are the wards on the door."

Lyra looked at him. "How are we supposed to get through all that?"

"We can," Crow said. "Due to my extensive knowledge of artifacts, I can probably get rid of the wards with enough time. The only problem is the guards. I thought of some countermeasures, but nothing too concrete…"

"I can handle the guards," Iris offered confidently.

"No violence," Crow said sharply. "The last thing we need is Academy security hunting us down."

"Who said anything about violence?" Iris grinned. "I was thinking more along the lines of distraction. I create a big commotion close by—say, a few rooms away. Security responds, leaving their posts temporarily unattended."

"And what type of commotion are we talking about…?" Crow asked carefully.

"Well, it needs to be big enough to make sure the guards leave their posts, even if only temporarily. So I was thinking of a big BOOM. Like a big, big explosion."

Everyone stared at the girl in silence.

Ash was the one who responded first as he burst into laughter. "Gods, that's the stupidest idea I've heard. Real subtle, Wright. Why don't we just set the whole building on fire while we're at it?" he said while continuing to chuckle.

However, he didn't laugh for long as he soon caught sight of Crow's expression. The dark-haired boy was pondering that idea deeply.

"Wait, wait, wait, wait… Crow, you're not actually considering this, are you?"

"..." Crow didn't respond, too engrossed in his thoughts.

"Crow. An explosion." Ash wasn't laughing now. "They aren't going to give us extra homework for that. They'll throw us out. We could end up on trial, and then what? That stays on our records."

"I know." Crow looked up. "I was considering a distraction already. This might be a better way to do it."

He went over to his bed and knelt. When he pressed one of the floorboards, something clicked beneath it. A compartment opened, and he lifted out a large wooden box covered in carved symbols.

"Was that a secret compartment?" Lyra asked, leaning forward with interest.

Crow nodded. "For protection, mostly just in case some annoying companion sneaked into my room," he said, glancing pointedly at Ash, who looked confused in response.

Crow opened the box and took what appeared to be a smooth blue stone about the size of a golf ball before hiding the box once again.

"What's that?" Amy asked, unable to contain her curiosity.

"An illusion artifact," Crow explained, once again approaching the table. "I obtained it not too long ago. It only works over a short distance, but the illusions include sounds and smells. We can make an explosion without actually damaging anything."

"You could have said that earlier," Lyra muttered, sinking back into her seat.

"The explosion gets them away from the office. After that, I want them to find something obviously meant as a joke. Otherwise they'll keep searching for whoever attacked the building."

"Have you picked the joke?" Ash asked.

"Not yet."

"Put a dick there," Iris suggested. "Twenty feet tall. Make it glow, too." She gestured above the table, as though they needed help picturing it.

What…?

Amy looked at her for a few seconds. Her neck was getting warm again. Of all the things she could have suggested...

Ash was laughing. "Oh, I want to see their faces. Crow, please."

"Don't encourage her!" Lyra protested. She was turning red. "What if one of the professors sees it?"

"They're supposed to. That's how they'll know it's a stupid prank. Nobody's going to think the person who did that is busy breaking into the headmistress' office."

Lain raised one eyebrow and continued listening.

"Wouldn't any large illusion attract them?" Zayd asked. "I don't understand the advantage of this one."

"They'll want to catch whoever made it," Ash explained. "Badly. Enough to forget about standing outside a door for a while."

Amy waited for Crow to reject the idea. He took long enough answering that she started getting worried.

"It would make the prank believable."

Wait, what?

Iris stared at him. "You're actually considering it?"

"Yes. I want them to think that's the whole point of the distraction. They won't expect much planning from whoever puts a giant dick in a hallway."

"So we're doing this?" Lyra asked. "You're both serious?"

"Do you have something better?" Crow raised an eyebrow.

This can't be real…

"So it's settled then," Iris said, grinning triumphantly. "BOOM, then big dick. Classic distraction technique."

"I can't believe this is our plan," Amy couldn't help but say out loud, finding herself unable to understand the absurdity of it all.

Lyra's eyes crossed with Amy's, and a moment of silent understanding passed between them. Both girls wore identical expressions of disbelief tinged with slight embarrassment—though, Lyra seemed redder than Amy felt.

"Okay, so now that we have the general idea," Crow nodded. "The only thing left to discuss is how to actually set up the distraction. We can't be seen before or after placing the artifact."

"We could go through the service passages?" Zayd suggested. Everyone turned to look at him. He took a sip of his tea before continuing. "The older buildings have corridors for staff. Narrow ones, usually without much surveillance."

"And you know that how?" Ash asked.

"Family research."

Crow watched Zayd for a moment, then pointed to the plan. "There is a staff stairwell here. Basement to fifth floor, with a way out on every level. If we reach it without being seen, we can get to the third floor that way."

"I'm surprised you know so much, Mr. Thorne," Zayd commented.

"I've been researching the Academy for a while due to…reasons."

"I see." Zayd nodded, deciding not to press any further.

"Either way," Crow said, changing the topic. "There are two major issues with this plan. First, we can't set this to go off later. Someone has to put it in place and activate it at the right moment."

"I'll take it," Iris said.

Crow shook his head. "You don't know those passages well enough. Whoever goes needs to know how to get through them." His eyes shifted to Zayd.

Zayd inclined his head slightly. Then, after a second, he spoke. "I can handle it."

"Alone?" Lyra asked, suspicion evident in her voice.

Crow considered it. "Ash, you'll go too."

Ash sat up. "Why me?"

"You're the fastest when you use your enhancement. If it goes wrong, you can get Zayd out with you."

"Alright." Ash leaned back again.

"And the other problem?" Amy asked.

"The wards." Crow looked at her. "There are probably several layers, and they're meant to keep people out. My father's key will help, but I still need time to deactivate them. I don't know if the distraction will give me enough."

Nobody answered immediately.

"Could we go around them?" Lyra asked. "Through another door, maybe?"

"There isn't one. We have to get these wards down."

Lain had been listening without speaking. Now she looked up. "...If one person alone isn't enough, then two..."

Crow's eyes widened slightly, understanding dawning on his face. He turned to Amy. "Amy, could you help me navigate the magical defenses?"

What…?

Amy hesitated, not because she thought she couldn't do it—breaking a blood ritual done by the blood emperor sounded way harder— but because she didn't want to. Just thinking of the strain of using her ability…

I'm so tired of working all the time…

After a moment of consideration, she sighed before giving a nod. "I think so."

"Then work with me. The others can keep watch." Crow went back over the map. He put Lain by the main stairs and had Lyra and Iris hide where they could watch the corridor. Amy would stay with him at the wards.

"Once the door is open, we take the passage into the Eastern Wing. According to my…research…the Eastern Wing will lead us below the Academy, right into the Library."

"And assuming we find this Library," Iris interjected, "What then? Just grab some books and leave?"

"We look for information," Crow replied. "About the Blood Emperor rituals, about recent events at the Academy, and...other stuff… Any questions?"

Iris raised her hand high.

"We're not in class, Iris," Ash said with an eye roll. "Just speak."

She ignored him completely, keeping her hand raised until Crow acknowledged her with a nod.

"Yes?"

Iris lowered her hand and leaned forward, her golden eyes narrowing as she glanced between Zayd and Amy. "I get why Ash, Lain, and Lyra are here, but them?" She pointed first at Zayd then at Amy. "What's their stake in this? No pun intended," she added with a quick glance at Amy. "Because I doubt that they are just tagging along for fun like I am."

Everyone looked at Amy and Zayd. Neither answered at first.

Zayd finished a sip of tea. "My reasons haven't changed since you last asked."

"Yeah, that's not an answer, buddy," Iris pressed.

At this, Zayd merely shrugged, obviously not willing to respond.

Iris frowned at the non-answer but turned her attention to Amy. "What about you? What's worth getting expelled over?"

Amy already had the beginnings of an answer. Something vague enough to sound significant, with no actual information in it. She opened her mouth—

[Try telling them something true,] Libris said. [We discussed this. The readers need something about you besides unanswered questions.]

Right. That conversation. It had seemed like a better idea when she wasn't about to do it in front of six people.

They were all still looking at her. Amy wished at least one of them would find something else to do.

"I..." Nothing followed. For some reason, saying the truth felt wrong…

[C'mon, Amy. You can do it!]

Ughhh…

She took a deep breath then finally spoke. "My objective is to save as many people as possible," she announced, her voice quieter than intended but clear enough for everyone to hear.

Iris looked confused now. Ash raised his eyebrows, and Lyra glanced at Lain. Crow and Zayd hadn't stopped watching Amy at all.

"Save them from what?" Iris asked.

Amy held her gaze and said nothing. She'd answered the first question. That was enough for now.

Crow eventually broke the silence. "Everyone has a reason to be here. Right now, I need to know you all understand what you're doing tonight."

He drew their attention back to the maps. Amy still caught people looking at her, especially Lain. She couldn't tell what the girl was thinking, and that made it worse.

They spent another hour going over the route out, what to do if something went wrong, and when each person needed to act. Crow gave them their last preparations, then took the golden key from the box.

"Ready?" He looked at Amy first.

She swallowed before answering. "Ready." It came out better than she'd expected.

The others answered in turn.

"Born ready," Ash said, though he sounded more serious now.

"Ready." Lyra kept fiddling with her sleeve.

Lain nodded. "...yes."

"I am." Zayd put his empty cup aside.

Iris grinned. "Let's get on with it."

Crow looked around the group once more. "Then let's go."
