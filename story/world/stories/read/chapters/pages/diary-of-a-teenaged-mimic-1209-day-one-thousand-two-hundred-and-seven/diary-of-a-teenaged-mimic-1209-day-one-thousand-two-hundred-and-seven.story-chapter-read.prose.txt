Dear Diary,

Kinda funny, but I've just realized something. Or really, just got reminded of it.

The best way to learn something, or really the best way to push my knowledge to the next level, is to teach it to somebody else.

I'm not sure entirely why, either. I think maybe part of it is watching somebody else make mistakes that I don't make, or really more likely mistakes I do make, but don't realize when I'm making them. Because sometimes the thing that just feels right is also objectively the wrong thing to do, or is a factually incorrect piece of information no matter how much we want it to be true. Which means until I see somebody else making the same mistake I do, I don't realize that it's a mistake. Or maybe I realize that it's a mistake, but have no idea why or how to fix it, but looking at it from the outside makes it obvious.

Then there's the other part, where I have to explain the shit I do without thinking about it. I really caught that part when duBois brought me in to assist with teaching Combat Training, but I think I've hit it with Math at this point as well. I've definitely gone from 'I know Arithmetic pretty well, and Algebra, sort of' to 'I am a leading expert on Algebra here and now, as well as being one of the founders of Calculus'. Shit, we've got a couple 'founders' of Calculus here and now, and the other one named it after me. Maybe Leibniz should have just done the deed with Newton. Or the other way around. I got no dog in that fight, but I am all about making love not war.

Of course, I realized something else yesterday. Yeah, I'm getting that letting Ria and Maze teach the others is both taking some of the load off of me and helping them learn their Math, but there's another effect. I'm learning about teaching, because I'm having to teach them how to teach.

So weird. I've always thought of myself as kind of an idiot, but also thought of myself as smarter than most of my peers. I definitely wasn't any kind of intellectual or nerd, really. But here and now I've wound up having the opportunity to learn and teach and I've discovered I love both of those things.
