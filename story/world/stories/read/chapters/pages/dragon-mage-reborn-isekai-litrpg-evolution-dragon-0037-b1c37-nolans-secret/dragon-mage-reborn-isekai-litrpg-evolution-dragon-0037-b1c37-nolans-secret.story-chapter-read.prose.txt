I’d never felt so terribly blindsided in my entire life. So close to my dracuna stage, only to be pulled back into this infant dracapod body!

Not only did she steal our stuff, she stole our horse, too. The post where Nolan had tied him was still there, but he wasn’t. Ugh, this girl was just bad news all around!

We were close enough to the café for Nolan to suggest heading back. He said something about getting a few essentials from his guild so we could continue traveling. While I was persistent about trying to catch that girl, I knew deep down that the possibility of finding her was slim to none. We had no way of tracking her, and no idea where she’d headed off to.

I was bummed, no, beyond bummed! The bummiest of bums! Nolan noticed how defeated I looked as I paced next to him. I could feel him staring down at me before he said, ‘Look, I know it stings. That evolution was rightfully yours. But if there’s one thing I’ve learned in the trade, it’s that nothing’s ever truly gone for good.’

‘Easy for you to say,’ I grumbled. ‘You didn’t just lose your only chance at being bigger than an iguana!’

‘No, but I’ve lost more than you can imagine. Family, friends, guns...’ He paused, his eyes going distant for a moment. ‘Point is, we’ll find another way to find Scarlet.’

I squinted at him confused.

‘I mean, your evo stone, and the rest of our shit.’

‘But the stone was perfect! Complete! Do you have any idea how much time it’s going to take for us to collect three more stage one evolution fragments?! I was lucky, Nolan. You said it yourself!’

‘So then what do you suggest we do? Give up? Lose hope? Like I said, we’ll find a way. Just have a little faith, will ya?’ Nolan nudged me with his boot, a gesture that was probably meant to be encouraging but just made me topple sideways in the sand.

‘Now, that’s just pathetic,’ he jested. I righted myself, shaking sand from my scales. ‘Dramatic much?’ Nolan muttered, before he looked outward, scanning the horizon. ‘Look, thieves have patterns. They have hideouts, fences, and favorite spots. If she’s as good as she seems, she’s known in certain circles.’

‘And how exactly do we tap into those circles?’ I demanded. ‘In case you haven’t noticed, I’m a baby dragon, wanted dead or eaten, and you’re an old guy with no magic and no weapons!’

Nolan’s face darkened. ‘First off, I’m not that old. Second, thieves talk. And third...’ He reached into his boot and pulled out a small, worn dagger. ‘I always keep a backup.’

My eyes widened. ‘You had that the whole time?!’

‘Course I did. What kind of amateur do you take me for?’

‘Then why didn’t you knife her!?’

He snorted. ‘Wow, that’s awfully dark, even for a baby dragon.’

‘That fight was an important one to win, Nolan. And if that means we have to rough up a prissy blue-eyed thief to get our stuff back, then so be it!’

Nolan stopped walking, his expression serious. ‘Look, short fry, there’s a fine line between being tough and being stupid. That girl had more relics than an ol’ witch’s basement. If I had pushed too hard, her retaliation might have been deadly. And the last thing I want to do is croak before we settle the score.’

I took a moment to breathe. Nolan was right—what I’d said wasn’t like me. It wasn’t like me at all….

‘You need to be careful with a klepto,’ he warned me. ‘With that ring, she could snatch anything in your inventory without you even noticing it’s gone. And by the time you do, it’d be too late. If she could do that to you by just passing you, imagine all of the other suckers she’d crossed paths with, and all of the tools she has at her disposal. Facing a klepto expert becomes a battle that is impossible to win. While dragons are strong, there are still a few rare dungeon items that could knock them out cold.’

I was disappointed at how hasty I was. She wasn’t a monster, but an actual human being. I was letting my rage eat me up inside, and I needed to calm down and look at the positive side of things, like our wins and our progress.

Being optimistic was hard for me sometimes, but it was never late to learn. This was the second time Nolan had his horse stolen, and he was taking it pretty darn well. Not to mention, his beloved Scarlet was kidnapped, and he was still playing it cool. I could learn a thing or two from his poised demeanor in the face of adversity.

‘I’m sorry,’ I mumbled. ‘I’m not usually like this. I just...’ I trailed off, unable to find the words.

‘Frustration gets to us all,’ Nolan said with a shrug. ‘Just don’t let it cloud your judgment. That’s when mistakes happen.’

As we approached the café, I noticed someone along the horizon. Over the hump was a large green stump with a speck on a figure on top of it. I squinted as we came to a stop. After a few moments longer, I recognized that speck, as for the green stump, not so much.

The last thing I thought I’d see was Thea riding a giant toad!

Nolan grumbled. ‘Not another damn toad….’

‘You say that like she has a collection,’ I said with a smile on my face.

‘More like an animal sanctuary,’ Nolan sighed, running a hand through his hair. ‘That girl’s got a real bleeding heart for creatures most folks wouldn’t care about. Found her first giant toad with its leg caught in a hunter’s trap a few years back. Thing was nearly dead—poisonous, too. Most would’ve put it out of its misery, or let it rot there. But Thea spent three weeks nursing that toxic monstrosity back to health. Nearly killed herself when it secreted venom all over her while she was nursing it. She’s been collecting strays way before we met—the weirder and more dangerous they are, the better.’

Aww, that was sweet of the goth girl!

‘She bought acres of land back in her hometown just to house the creatures she’s rescued,’ Nolan continued. ‘Her parents were wealthy merchants who left everything to her when they passed. Instead of living the high life, she turned their estate into what she calls a sanctuary for the misunderstood.’

The toad hopped closer, each landing sending small tremors through the sand. Thea’s black hair whipped around her face as she guided the massive amphibian toward us. I didn’t realize I was sinking my claws into the ground the closer the beast got. It was already casting a shadow over me before Thea came to a complete stop mere inches from Nolan, who was too stubborn to move. It was a plausible, and funny, intimidation tactic

I swear they acted just like siblings.

“Standing in my way again, as usual.”

The toad croaked supportively.

“Pretty sure I was standing here first, princess of darkness,” Nolan hissed back playfully.

“Hmm,” Thea said as she tilted her chin. “Something about you looks different. Traveling light?”

Nolan’s annoyed face twitched at her. “Had a run-in with a thief. Took everything—my gun, our inventory, hell, even my damn horse.”

“Again?” Thea’s eyebrows shot up. “That’s the second horse in a month.”

“Not helping,” Nolan grumbled.

Was she keeping tabs? Because if so, it was actually his third horse!

Thea’s eyes widened slightly, the only indication of surprise on her otherwise stoic face. “A thief managed to get the drop on you? The great Nolan Reeves?”

“It’s Nolan Black,” he firmly corrected.

She clicked her tongue. “You must be losing your touch.” Then Thea’s eyes landed on me. “She took the dragon's stuff too, huh?”

‘My evolution stone,’ I projected miserably. ‘I was this close to evolving!’

“Yeah, dungeon bandits are the worst. Well, there’s no point in beating yourselves up about it. I am guessing you’ve come back for some replenishment.” She shrugged. “The both of you might as well stay for lunch.”

Thea parked her giant toad along the side of the building, where she then proceeded to pull out a basin of slugs for her pet to eat. We followed her inside, Elefre shocked to see us back so soon. He fixed us a table and came back shortly with tea for Thea and coffee for Nolan. Then Nolan ordered us some food. I selected my dishes, a few of them, actually, the loss of my precious items making me extra hungry.

“Thanks for this,” Nolan said as Thea settled a few items on the table between us. Mostly mana restoration and health vials.

“Sure. Wouldn’t be the first time you owed me,” Thea jested.

“Ugh, hey, try to keep this little incident between us,” Nolan said to Thea. “I don’t want Mable catching wind of this. You know how she gets.”

Thea smirked deviously.

“Hey! I mean it!”

She rolled her eyes. “Your secret is safe with me.”

I giggled. Her hands were under the table, and I couldn’t help but imagine her crossing those fingers together.

Nolan took the items and gave me a few. I bowed gratefully, thanking Thea for her generous donation. I then asked her if she’d crossed paths with some stage one evolution stones, to which she said if she had, she would have given them to me already.

“Well, maybe you can help us with something else,” Nolan said. “We need to find the thief. She isn’t your common variety type—short, blond, on the thin side, and she has some ego on her.”

“Hmm, she doesn’t ring a bell,” Thea said as she stirred her tea.

“Come on, Thea. Give me something. You’ve been around the deeper districts. You had to have heard about her.”

“If I did, I would have told you. Nothing saddens me more than a grown man losing his side piece.”

He deadpanned her.

“If she has no tracks, then that means she’s probably running solo. But if you want my advice, I’d start over. You’ll waste more time looking for her than raiding low tier dungeons and building your inventory back up.”

“It’s not just about my items. My scrolls….”

She shrugged. “You have friends, don’t you?”

“Yeah, but it’s not like they are dishing them out for free.”

“I’m sure she’ll make an exception for you,” Thea said, giving Nolan an interesting look. Was I missing something?

“I don’t want her getting used to me asking her for favors.”

“I’d put your pride aside and get the job done, Nolan. The Tournament of Champions is close, and Freya needs to level up.”

“I know….”

“So, priorities. You took on this responsibility. Now isn’t the time to come short.”

Elefre came back with our meals in an oversized tray. I ordered an entire meat pie for myself, some braised pork, and a helping of mixed fruits. Ever since I’d turned into a dragon, my taste in food had shifted a bit. I was more focused on meats than starchy foods. The bloodier the better. While this array was cooked, it still had me drooling.

Nolan got some kind of steak that smelled incredible, while Thea nibbled on what looked like a salad made of strange purple leaves.

I dove into my meal while the two continued their conversation. I looked over to Nolan and then to Thea. Did proper table etiquette still apply to me? I mean, I didn’t want to be rude. So I lifted the fork on the table. It was a trial and a half since I had no opposable thumbs, but managed to grasp it between my claws. I stabbed at a chunk of meat and tried my best to lift it to my mouth without looking like a complete savage.

Mission failed.

The fork, along with my succulent meat, slipped between my stubby digits, bounced on the cushion of our booth, and then clanked against the floor.

Nolan and Thea simultaneously turned their heads to me as I stayed there frozen with my mouth open wide and my arm hovering over it.

Nolan prompted his head up. “What the hell are you doing?”

‘Proper… etiquette?’ I replied sheepishly.

“We know you’re a dragon,” Thea said with a smirk. “You’re allowed to act like one.”

I straightened up, licking the corner of my mouth where some of that juicy sauce lingered, and then gazed down at my food. Well, if that was the case, I jumped on top of the table and went to chow town, burying my face into my meat pie. The warm, savory filling burst across my taste buds, and I couldn’t help the rumbling purr that escaped me. Thea watched me with a mix of amusement and mild horror as I tore into my meal, bits of meat and sauce flying in all directions.

“Well, I didn’t mean quite like that,” Thea murmured, discreetly sliding her salad away from my feeding frenzy.

Nolan just chuckled, leaning back to avoid the splatter zone. “Let the kid eat. Been a rough day.”

I wasn’t the only animal dining at the table this bright, sunny afternoon. Nolan was taking large chunks out of his steak, tearing it like a wolf would a carcass. I felt a connection there, a weird one….

For some strange reason, I thought about dad. Not to say that Nolan reminded me of him. I guess I was seeing traits in Nolan that I would have liked to see in my father. Nolan was supportive, kind, and most importantly… present.

It always seemed like Dad was trying to be someone he wasn’t, so fixated on being perfect to the point where he was so disattached from the family. He was creating a false reality around himself that neither I nor Mom could relate to. He thrived for the perfect home, the perfect job, and the perfect lifestyle. He needed us to live the perfect happy American dream, when all he was really doing was becoming a stranger to us. His true self was prideful, stubborn, and arrogant. He was there for his career, more than he was there for me….

I get that Nolan felt it his duty to help me anyway he could, you know, for the fate of two worlds and stuff, but being nice about it didn’t have to be part of the package. He didn’t see me as a chore, and I guess that’s what I wanted from Dad….

‘You okay, short stuff?’ Nolan asked.

I forced a smile, and nodded. ‘This stuff goes right through you. I need to go to the bathroom.’

‘Well, knock yourself out. We aren’t going anywhere.’

I hopped off my chair, leaving a messy pile of food scraps behind. As I made my way across the café floor, I couldn't help but notice how normal everything seemed. People eating, drinking, laughing—completely calm to the fact that a dragon was walking among them. I guess when you live in a world where giant toads serve as transportation, a small dragon isn’t that shocking.

The bathroom situation was... complicated. I stood in front of two doors—one with a silhouette of a woman, the other a man. As another woman walked out of the swinging door, I scurried inside. There was something about this idea that seemed unnatural….

I shrugged it off and waddled toward one of the stalls. The door was massive from my perspective—I had to stretch up on my hind legs just to push it open.

Inside, I stared at the toilet with growing despair. Even if I managed to climb up there, what then? Did dragons even use toilets? I hadn’t really thought about the mechanics of... well, dragon waste management within an establishment.

‘This is ridiculous,’ I muttered to myself, then dashed out of a slightly ajar backdoor where a delivery guy was dropping off boxes of merchandise. I turned the corner, eyed a long row of shrubs, and then parked my backside behind it. A nice spot to relieve myself.

A few moments of privacy later, I felt much better. I was about to head back out until I heard Thea’s voice from the other side of the wall as she said, “We need to think about this tournament, Nolan….”

Her voice was eerie, well, eerier than usual. I didn’t hear anything from Nolan for a while, beside the sound of him taking a sip of his coffee. The silence prompted Thea to continue on and say, “If that fat leech succeeds in scaring off our champions, then we’ll have no one to defend us.”

“We have Freya,” Nolan said promptly.

“Freya is the only contestant. We need two more.”

“Then Mable will fill the gaps if she has to.”

“And what if she can’t?”

He snorted. “You know, I remember you being a lot quieter.”

“I can’t help but try and sort these things out in my head. Being blindly optimistic only works sometimes. When the sun goes down and the lights are out, the reality of everything settles in like a storm. It’s real, vicious, and destructive.” She paused. “Everywhere you look, you see one of two faces: a face that is truly oblivious to it all, and a face wearing a mask to cover the pain of losing it all.”

“What happened to all of that faith you had in Freya?”

“It didn’t go anywhere, but I like to be realistic. If we lose this fight, we lose the war. There’s… no climbing back from that….”

“We’ll be fine.”

“Only if you’re letting her progress through this world on her own. You’re only a guide, not a facilitator. Freya is still a tadpole in a pond of sharks, I understand that, but she still needs to learn how to grow on her own.”

“She is far too young for that.”

“You can’t keep her sheltered. You’ll only stunt her growth. She needs to experience this world, learn its rules, adapt, and pedal through it. I understand you’re trying to protect her, but you’re being too cautious. Let her make mistakes, let her fall, and then help her get back up again.”

“We don’t have time for mistakes,” Nolan said, his voice strained. “And I’m not being cautious. I’m being practical.”

“Practical would be training her properly, not holding her hand through dungeons. She’s smarter than you give her credit for.”

“And what makes you think that I am babying her?”

“Because, I know you. And… I know how you see Freya….”

He paused, not saying one word. What did Thea mean by that?

“I get it. I’ve never experienced that kind of loss before, so I wouldn’t know how it feels. Regardless, you need to get your head straight about her situation, not yours. You can’t be selfish about this.”

Nolan scoffed. “I don’t remember agreeing to getting in each other’s business as an appetizer.”

“You tell Mable everything,” Thea sulked subtly. “I thought after all of those years, you’d tell me everything too….”
