« You ready? » Angel asked.

Born ready, Jin thought back, though the persistent, agonizing throb in his scorched essence channels strongly begged to differ.

Though we might have to do something about this pain.

« Oh, don’t worry about that. You are perfectly fine. That’s the result of assimilation of the harvest, bringing it closer to the next milestone. »

Ohh, to be honest with you, I totally forgot about that.

He sighed and leaned heavily over the WARCOM room's central tactical map, the holographic blue light casting harsh shadows across his face.

His focus was entirely locked on the projection in front of him.

Anyway, let’s focus… A lot is riding on today’s success.

« I know we will succeed, Jin. Have faith. »

Yeah.

On the holographic projection, a hundred crisp blue dots moved across the map in tight, disciplined formation. To anyone else, it looked like a well-trained military unit. Jin knew better. It was the terrifying synchronization of a hundred unawakened civilians who had a complex, two-verse sorcery violently hammered into their muscle memory.

Through his Whisperlink earpiece, he could hear the synchronized crunch of their boots on the frostbitten cobblestones above ground. No murmurs. No panicked gasps. Just the rhythmic breathing of people marching through their ruined city, fueled by a collective, burning rage that Jin could practically feel radiating through the comms.

"Jin, it is aggressively freezing out here," Rudy’s voice crackled over the secure channel, shattering the grim silence.

Jin tapped a rune on the console to adjust the audio feed. "That would be the cult’s localized environmental control. Probably amping the ambient decay to drop temperatures and hinder your movement." Jin paused, letting a small smirk touch his lips despite his exhaustion. "Tell you what, Rudy. If you survive this, I’ll personally requisition you some thermal underwear from Illiana. Maybe with little fire patterns on them."

"Ha-ha. Hilarious," Rudy shot back, the sound of his heavy boots crunching clearly audible. "But seriously, if you can actually get her to make those, I wouldn't say no."

"Rudy, focus on the perimeter," Reyana’s voice snapped through the link, her tone carrying the warmth of a drawn blade. "Or I’m telling Dad you've been skipping your physical conditioning to read those trashy romance novels you hide under your cot."

"Wait, what? When did I ever read those?" Rudy protested, genuine panic bleeding into his voice.

"Oh?" Reyana countered smoothly. "Then how exactly is your head so thoroughly filled with absolute garbage?"

Jin let out a chuckle, the sound scratching at his raw throat. It was good. The banter meant they were loose, managing the tension rather than letting the apocalyptic dread crush them.

But playtime was over.

Jin closed his eyes and breathed out. He called upon [Sovereign's Indifference].

The grey calm washed down through his mind, instantly burying his physical exhaustion, the residual anxiety, and the lingering warmth of the banter beneath a thick sheet of cold, frictionless logic. When he opened his eyes again, the silver halo of his mystic sight flared to life.

"Alright, that’s enough fun," Jin commanded, his voice dropping into the flat, absolute cadence of the Eternal Sovereign. "Maintain comms discipline. Hans, Rudy—keep your eyes strictly on the perimeter. Your job is containment, not participation. You don't engage unless an Underlord decides to crash the party."

"Yes, sir!" Rudy replied, all the previous humor stripped away.

"Joe, give me a position check."

"Door is primed and anchored," Joe’s voice whispered through the link. Even filtered through the comms, Jin could practically hear the man's shark-like grin. "I'm reading fifteen cultist signatures inside the outpost. Plus close to a hundred of those charming little flesh abominations. Completely oblivious to us. It's almost insulting how bad their perimeter security is."

"Understood," Reyana murmured. "Keep the exfil route open, Joe."

Jin watched the blue dots on the projection lock into position, forming a perfect circle around the red enemy markers of Outpost C2.

"1st Mage Company, hold positions. We execute exactly as drilled," Jin ordered, his hands resting flat against the edge of the tactical map. "Conductors. Signal your leads."

Jin leaned over the holomap. On the projection, five specific markers—the Conductors—slid seamlessly into their vanguard positions.

"Backbones," Jin said. "Begin the chant."

Through the Whisperlink earpiece, seventy-five voices whispered in unison.

« "O currents of power, converge upon this locus—weave the unbreakable shell of my will..." »

Even sitting miles away in the depths of Bastion, Jin could feel the ambient essence spike.

« By now all of Vienna knows that something big happened. »

Yeah, we should be finished by then. The recent damage to the cult would prevent them from sending reinforcements.

“Joe,” Jin said. “Keep an eye out for any suspicious activity or individuals.”

“Roger.”

On the visual feeds, the outpost's alarm wards suddenly flared an angry crimson. Frantic shouts echoed over the audio as the cultists finally realized they were getting attacked.

"Sustainers, balance the load," Jin ordered his company.

Twenty markers on his map flared pale green, and the turbulent, screaming essence surrounding the 1st Mage Company immediately settled into a stable flow.

The sustainers were a necessity for his plans to work. The Veil suffocating Vienna made casting without an awakened aura essentially a death sentence. Hell, it even made any attempts to call upon the mantle difficult.

To bypass this, each Sustainer carried a specialized buffer crystal designed to absorb the lethal feedback and provide their essence with a restorative effect that resisted the corruption of the veil.

Without sustainers, the auraless backbones would have melted their own nervous systems into magical fondue under the sheer strain of the sorcery they were attempting.

"Leads, chain them," Jin commanded, watching the data closely. "Conductors, pull the weave."

Backbone leads seized the fraying threads of raw sorcery spilling from their respective groups. It wasn't elegant spellcraft. What he was attempting was more of a brute-force magical engineering. Instead of quality, he was going for quantity.

The lead wrenched the sorcery into thick, glowing cables of coherent magic and hurled them forward.

The five Conductors caught the load, synergizing and chaining massive sorcery into one.

« "I trade the pulse of my core—let no force or flame breach this sanctuary!" »

The chant crested, and the sorcery snapped into alignment.

With a sound like the sky tearing open, a massive, shimmering blue dome of highly condensed essence erupted from the asphalt. It curved high over the cultist outpost before crashing down on the far side, sealing the perimeter.

Inside the cage, the panic was immediate. Cultists and dozens of mutated abominations slammed against the wall of magic from all sides.

Jin watched the visual feed with mild amusement as an Order III cultist hurled a lance of corrupted dark-fire directly at the barrier. The spell shattered into useless, fading embers against the dome, failing to leave so much as a scorch mark.

Jin tapped the console, checking the data pulled from each of the 100-person company’s gear. He was pleasantly surprised to find the Mage Company's output holding steady. At their current burn rate, they could sustain the dome for at least ten to twenty minutes nonstop.

Of course, if an Order III elite like Orodain or Mareth were trapped inside, it could probably chew through that time limit considerably, but containment was never the actual goal.

This was a test.

As for the cultists and monsters trapped inside the barrier, the horrifying reality of their situation was finally setting in.

Jin leaned over the tactical projection in the WARCOM room, watching the angry red dots scramble uselessly against the dome.

"Reyana, we are transitioning into the next stage. Deploy the payload." He tapped the console.

“Copy.” Reyana walked right up to the dome, and her heart felt more and more at ease knowing that she would be the one delivering death to these fiends.

"I'm at the delivery point," she murmured, placing her hands against the specialized intake rune woven into the dome’s surface.

For weeks, the death-essence generated by her mantle had been a slow, rotting poison eating her from the inside out. It was a curse she constantly suppressed, a terrifying affliction she had barely kept contained until Jin came along and started harvesting the lethal excess every once in a while. Then came Trish’s brutal lesson: she was a conduit, not a cup, and she just needed to let the death flow through her.

Tonight, she was done being afraid of her own hands.

Reyana closed her eyes. With a sharp, shuddering exhale, she deliberately broke the seal on her mantle.

The custom-crafted amplifier Joe had built flared to life on her wrist, weaponizing the pure death essence into the purest kind of necrotic flood.

The temperature around her plummeted instantly, the air turning brittle and sharp. The stone beneath her boots actively withered, cracking and turning to grey dust under the oppressive weight of death.

"Breathe, Rey," she whispered.

She let go of the seal holding down her mantle, and death flooded in.

Inside the dome, it became an instant, inescapable apocalypse.

A creeping dark fog with stark white outlines cascaded from one living entity to the next until death had swallowed the dome. The screaming started—a horrifying, discordant choir of pure agony—as cultists and mutated aberrations melted, withered and decayed where they stood. Even the Order III, attempting to use their aura to burn the fog of death away, found their spiritual pressure sputtering and dying like candles in a vacuum.

Reyana held the suffocating flow for a full minute, her face pale and sweat beading on her forehead.

Just as her knees began to tremble, Rudy stepped up, wrapping a steadying arm around her shoulder to ground her.

Reyana gasped, violently clamping the seal back down on her core.

Inside the dome, absolutely nothing moved. The screaming had mercifully died out somewhere around the ninety-second mark. The cultists and their horde of abominations lay rotting where they had fallen, their bodies already beginning to wither into desiccated husks.

Outside the dome, the hundred members of the 1st Mage Company stood in complete silence, having just eradicated an entire fortified outpost without a single Bastion soldier taking a scratch.

The heavy, ringing silence over the comms was finally broken by a low whistle.

"Damn," Joe said. "That was quick."

Back in WARCOM, Jin watched the holographic projection map the final moments of the raid.

« They did it. »

Of course they did.

“Joe, bring everyone back in,” Jin said.

“Aye,” Joe confirmed, and a loud snap echoed through the comms.

Multiple weathered oak doors materialized behind each group on the ground.

"Alright then," Joe's voice crackled over the secure Whisperlink, his tone drifting back into its customary lazy drawl. "Mission accomplished. Step right up. Let's exfil before the neighbours come round to complain about the noise."

10 doors opened behind the 1st Mage company, and the leads, without missing a beat, had the company moving through the doors in order.

Jin leaned back in his command chair, resting his boots against the edge of the table. He closed his eyes and let [Sovereign’s Indifference] finally drop.

"There are no neighbours left, Joe," Jin replied into the comms, rubbing the bridge of his nose to ward off a building headache. "But ten points for the customer service voice."

“Always happy to help.” Joe echoed back.

Jin tapped the console, muting the comms, shifting the drone telemetry and perimeter sensors to run quietly in the background.

With a flick of his wrist, he collapsed the sprawling holo projections that cluttered the air, leaving only a single holographic feed hovering above the center of the table.

The last few minutes of the attack were playing on a continuous loop.

Illiana—Jin thanked her quietly in his mind—had already set the footage to play on a continuous loop, highlighting the best parts.

Jin leaned his hips against the edge of the table and let the silence build. He watched the awestruck, slightly stunned expressions painted across the faces of Vienna’s highest command.

« Drama Queen »

Hey, I have to sell this, you know. We’re about to ask normal civilians… the bakers and accountants to march onto the surface and fight monsters. A little presentation goes a long way.

« You should add this footage to one of your tutorial videos. Name it ‘Hope for Dummies’ or something equally obnoxious. »

Good point. Hope sells.

Jin cleared his throat, and eight pairs of eyes snapped towards him.

"So," Jin said comfortably, gesturing at the looping hologram. "Thoughts? Questions? Concerns?"

Lieutenant Jorn was the first to break. The veteran soldier stared at the projection, his weathered face tight with disbelief. "Those… Those people…”

“Yeah?” Jin prompted the man.

“Those people were pulled yesterday. God fucking yesterday, and you're telling me we have a hundred civilians capable of using magic? and not just that they caged an outpost with minimal danger?"

"And exterminated everyone inside without a single casualty, or as Jorn said, minimal danger," Captain Lennon added, shaking his head slowly. "I've seen a lot of things that were a first."

Jin smiled, and his eyes moved to Illiana. He wanted to hear everyone's thoughts first, or at least that’s what he showed inside; he was giddy that his outright dumbest decision paid off.

« You should really keep [Sovereign’s Indifference] on during meetings… You are killing the serious vibes. »

Jin ignored Angel.

"Not to mention the discipline…” Illiana started.

“Okay, before any of you come to the wrong conclusion—” Jin interjected and gestured towards Vera. “—Vera here stands as a witness and a qualified specialist that apart from giving the capability to use one very specific sorcery. Nothing was done to thier mind.”

“Yes,” Vera said, standing. “Time was short, but I managed to conduct one-on-one interviews with each member of 1st mage company before and after the imprinting process. And no instances of mind manipulation were found.”

“Thank you,” Mathew said, meeting Jin's eyes. “I know the times are desperate, but thank you for not crossing moral lines.”

Jin nodded, but internally, he shook his head. Mathew was somewhere between lawful and neutral good morality, and Jin knew, if he had the capability to rewrite people’s minds, he would have done so in a heartbeat if it meant they would survive.

But he found no need to say that aloud.

« You know, before Orodain, you would have revolted... quite violently at the mention of manipulating people. »

Yeah, well, we all need to change with the times.

Fortunately, Illiana still had something to say, which shifted the topic.

“Also, the resonance stabilization doesn't make sense," Illiana said. "I know I helped you make those crystals for the sustainers… yet I can not understand how seventy-five untrained channels dumping raw essence into a mess of a sorcery output merged.”

"Well, your crystals, Sustainers, and the conductors are the answer." Jin supplied helpfully. "Sorcery chaining is… well, safe to say it's on par with a secret art on the rarity scale. The technique allows a skilled mage to weave threads of compatible sorcery into a single coherent one."

Elenor leaned forward, her eyes wide as she watched the Conductors on the feed weave the spell. "An Art!… but even then.. any attempts at harmonization on that scale. I'm a trained mage, Jin, and that level of synchronization would take me months to coordinate with a dedicated squad."

"Fear is a fantastic motivator, Elenor," Veric chimed in from the flank. He took a slow drag from his cigarette, exhaling a thin cloud toward the ceiling. He didn't look surprised; he just looked thoroughly entertained by everyone else's shock. "Turns out people learn very quickly when the alternative is getting eaten by a flesh abomination."

“And I’m a very good teacher.” Jin smiled and then added with a wink. “Watch my tutorials.”

"Even then, the mental strain on them must be astronomical," Vera said.

"It is," Jin agreed. The easy smile dropped from his face. "We are putting a gun in the hands of people who have spent the last three weeks waiting to die in the dark. It is going to be incredibly traumatic for them one way or another. That’s why I had those requirements for recruits. I just pray these people have what it takes to push through."

He looked down the length of the table, meeting Commander Mathew’s single, storm-grey eye.

"But as you can see, it works," Jin continued. "We have the blueprint. We just proved we can weaponize the population without sending them into a meat grinder. All we need now are numbers."

Mathew stared at the looping footage of the dying cultists for a long moment. When he finally looked back at Jin, the heavy exhaustion that usually stooped the Commander's shoulders seemed a fraction lighter.

"You said," Mathew asked.

From the foot of the table, Salvatore let out a low, gravelly grunt. The scarred Overlord crossed his massive arms, his silver eyes gleaming with approval.

"Long enough to start a real war," he rumbled.

“That was hope, People. Now, I want you to imagine twenty mage companies marching together. And remember this: not all of them will be equipped with defensive sorcery." Jin said softly.

He let the silence stretch out.

"We don't just hold the line anymore," Jin said. "This is how we march across this city. And we exterminate every last piece of them because we are in the endgame now.”
