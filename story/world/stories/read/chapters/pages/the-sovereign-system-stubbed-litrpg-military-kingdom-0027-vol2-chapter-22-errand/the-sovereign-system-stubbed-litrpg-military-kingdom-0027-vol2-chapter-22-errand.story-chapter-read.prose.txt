On the bustling streets of Ralis City, a lone figure walked with quiet confidence. Dressed in a pristine white-and-black robe, her sashay drew the eyes of many. There was a grace in her stride, a subtle allure that turned heads and silenced conversations.

But those who lingered too long under her gaze quickly regretted it. A sudden chill crept up their spines, as though dread itself had brushed against them. Instinctively, they averted their eyes, retreating from the inexplicable pressure she exuded.

Victoria clicked her tongue in irritation, resisting the impulse to lash out. She was no stranger to those kinds of looks, but today they grated on her nerves more than usual.

She didn’t know why, and frankly, she didn’t care. Pushing the thought aside, she refocused on the task at hand.

Reaching a busy intersection, she paused, her sharp brown eyes scanning the storefronts. Dozens of signs lined the road, but only one held her attention. Midway down the street, a weathered sign hung slightly askew. The word “Ceramics” was modestly etched into its surface.

A subtle smile played at her lips.

When Luke mentioned he needed elegant, expensive-looking vials, this was the first place that came to mind. She’d passed it before—on the night she slaughtered members of the Black Tiger Gang—and had always wanted to peek inside. After all, what woman didn’t enjoy beautiful things?

As she stepped inside, the chime above the door gave a soft ring.

“Good morning, young miss,” came a voice from within.

“Morning,” Victoria replied sweetly, her tone light and unrecognizable from moments before. It was as if she had become someone entirely different.

The shopkeeper was an elderly man with a pointed gray beard and a frail frame. Despite his age, his hands moved with surprising steadiness.

“What can I help you with today, young lady?” he asked. His voice was oddly high-pitched—noticeable, though not suspiciously so.

Victoria eyed him in silence for a moment. Then, her lips curled into a sly smile as her gaze dropped lower, pausing deliberately.

“What’s a eunuch doing running a shop on the main street?” she asked flatly. “Were you thrown out of the Inner Palace?”

Her earlier charm had vanished.

Victoria was nothing if not adaptable. She had originally intended to use her looks—perhaps charm him into a deal or pry out useful information. But the moment she recognized his condition, she shifted tactics. There was no use seducing a man with no interest in women.

The shopkeeper stiffened. “That’s none of your business, young miss,” he replied, the edge in his voice unmistakable.

If she noticed his displeasure, she gave no sign.

“You’re right. I was just making conversation,” she said breezily, turning to browse the shelves as if nothing had happened.

The old man watched her carefully, his mouth drawn into a thin line.

“So,” he said at last, “are you here to buy something, or just wasting my time?”

Victoria’s smile deepened. Still silent, she reached out and delicately picked up a small ceramic vial that had caught her eye. Her fingers ran along its polished surface as she studied it.

The vial was expertly crafted, its glossy surface catching the light with an elegant sheen. Though small, it was surprisingly heavy in her hand.

“How much for this?” Victoria asked, her eyes still fixed on the vial.

“Twelve silver,” the man replied curtly.

“Too expensive.”

“Excuse me?” His voice tightened. If he hadn’t been offended before, he clearly was now. “I make every piece in this shop by hand, miss. I’d appreciate it if you didn’t belittle my work.”

“I’m not belittling anything,” Victoria said calmly. “I have fourteen gold coins. I want one hundred and fifty of these, ready in ten days.”

She finally turned to face him, meeting his now deeply furrowed brow. But instead of pressing the point, she simply reached into her robe and retrieved a coin pouch. She set it gently on the counter. The heavy clink of coins inside said more than words could.

“The price isn’t the problem,” the old man murmured, eyeing the pouch. Her blunt approach—and the sound of real money—had clearly shifted his tone. “But ten days is far too short. I’ll need at least two weeks to fulfill such an order.”

Victoria smiled sweetly. “I’ll give you three weeks. But I want a few modifications. Do you have ink and parchment?”

The old man returned moments later with both. Victoria quickly jotted down a list of instructions before handing it over.

He scanned the page, eyebrows twitching as he read. “‘Jade Mist’? ‘Silken Petals’? Just what exactly are you planning to store in these vials?”

“I don’t think that’s any of your business, old man,” she said, flashing a grin.

The old man blinked, then chuckled as the realization hit him. She’d thrown his own words back at him.

“Well played, young miss. I apologize for prying,” he said, cupping his fist in a respectful bow. “I just hope you won’t be using my vials to store anything... illicit. I can’t afford to be caught up in such things.”

“Drugs?” Victoria’s brow arched.

The temperature in the room seemed to plummet. The light grew dim, as though something heavy pressed in on the walls themselves.

“You think I’m dealing in aphrodisiacs and narcotics?” she asked coldly.

The old man paled, bowing even lower than before. “F-Forgive me! Ralis City is plagued with such substances—I spoke without thinking. Please pardon my foolishness, dear customer.”

For a heartbeat longer, Victoria’s eyes glinted dangerously. Then, as suddenly as it had come, the pressure vanished. The room lightened, and the shopkeeper exhaled with visible relief.

“These drugs,” she said, voice still edged with steel, “are they being distributed by the Black Tiger Gang?”

“Yes,” the man replied, his voice lower now, almost a whisper. “Opium is everywhere in the capital thanks to them. They’ve got their claws in every major merchant group. If you don’t pay their toll, you won’t survive in Ralis. They've turned the entire trade district into their playground.”

Victoria’s expression remained unreadable. On the surface, she had only come to buy vials for perfume—but in truth, she had another goal in mind. She intended to make inroads with Ralis City’s merchant groups.

But now, that second objective felt impossible.

“I’ll return in three weeks to collect the merchandise,” Victoria said, turning to leave.

“Wait, young miss,” the shopkeeper called out. “A hundred and fifty vials will be quite a load. And if I toss them into a bag, they might get damaged. How about I have them delivered to your residence instead?”

Victoria paused, considering the offer. Then she nodded. “Fine. Make sure they’re properly secured—packaged so no one can see what’s inside. Send them to the Royal Academy, addressed to Viscount Luke Drakon.”

At the mention of both the Royal Academy and a Viscount, the old man visibly stiffened. He quickly bowed in acknowledgment.

With the transaction settled, Victoria stepped back into the sunlit street, intending to return to the Academy and update Luke. They would need a new plan for distributing the perfume—partnering with a merchant group was clearly out of the question, thanks to the Black Tiger Gang’s grip on the city.

“Hey, pretty lady. Took your time in there, didn’t you?”

The moment she emerged, three burly men were waiting for her just outside the store. Each wore a blade at his side and bore the rough, weathered look of men who’d seen their fair share of battles.

Victoria’s gaze slid over them lazily. “What can I do for you gentlemen?” she asked, her voice soft and innocent.

The men exchanged knowing smirks before the one in front stepped forward, rubbing his rounded stomach.

“We saw you walking down the street earlier,” he said, voice slick with amusement. “Thought we’d say hello. Get to know you better.”

“We’re with the Black Tiger Gang,” another chimed in, his grin wide and arrogant. “Maybe you’ve heard of us—the Wright brothers?”

Victoria’s eyes flicked between them, then toward the street corners. Dozens of pedestrians passed by, but none stopped. No one would intervene.

Her expression faltered for a split second. But just as quickly, she masked it with a look of helpless charm. Only her eyes betrayed the truth—the sudden, chilling cold behind them.

“What do you want from me?” she asked softly, shrinking back like a frightened girl. Moisture welled at the corners of her eyes, glinting in the sunlight. It made her appear even more delicate, more vulnerable.

The Wright brothers, enchanted, took the bait.

“Don’t be scared, sweetheart,” the largest one said, stepping closer. His towering frame loomed over her. “We just wanna talk. Might be a job opportunity in it for you.”

If not for the vile grin twisting his face, his words might’ve sounded almost genuine.

But Victoria knew exactly what sort of “job” they were referring to. This was how the Black Tiger Gang recruited courtesans for the Jade Silk Pavilion. Charm them. Corner them. Drag them in.

Unfortunately for the Wright brothers, they’d chosen the wrong woman.

“A job?” Victoria asked, voice trembling with false hope. “Would it pay well?”

The three men burst into laughter, nearly breaking character.

“Of course!” the leader said, reaching out and seizing her arm. “Let’s get your training started.”

For a brief moment, his body froze. A wave of primal instinct screamed at him—warning him that he was in danger. But it passed too quickly, like a shadow slipping away before he could grasp it.

He led Victoria through the winding streets of Ralis, eventually turning into a narrow alley some hundred feet from the ceramics store. Along the way, passersby cast her pitying glances—but no one stepped in. No one even slowed down.

It was clear: the Black Tiger Gang had their claws deeply embedded in the city’s flesh.

After several minutes of silent walking, they reached the alley’s dead end. A few homeless men sitting nearby caught sight of them, immediately scrambling to flee as if they already knew what was coming.

“W-What are you doing? Where’s the career opportunity?” Victoria asked, her voice trembling with feigned panic.

The eldest Wright brother let out a crude chuckle. “Heh. You didn’t think we’d recommend you to the Pavilion without a little inspection, did you? Gotta see if you’re qualified first. Wouldn’t want to send the wrong merchandise.”

Victoria backed away, her eyes wide, her posture timid. “W-What do you mean?” she stammered, her back pressing against the cold brick wall.

“Why don’t you strip off that robe?” he said, his voice turning hoarse with anticipation. “Let me take a good look... see if you’re worth cultivating.”

At that, Victoria’s expression flickered. The mask was slipping.

The two younger brothers watched with smirks on their faces as their elder brother moved in, oblivious to the tightening noose around them. A scream rang out in the alley—sharp, piercing—but it only seemed to excite them.

“I’m next after big brother!” one of them shouted, practically bouncing on his heels.

“No way, you went second last time! It’s my turn, idiot!”

“What? Are you brain-dead? I’m the second brother—I go second. That’s the rule!”

As the two argued like children at a dinner table, Victoria watched them silently. Like a predator in tall grass.

Their elder brother was already dead—collapsed in a heap on the ground, blood pooling beneath his throat where her blade had opened him.

But the other two hadn’t noticed.

She waited. Let them bicker. Let them feel safe.

Then, bored with the show, Victoria strolled up behind the second eldest. She placed a gentle hand on his shoulder.

“You said you wanted to go next, right?”

He blinked in confusion. “Huh? O-Oh, yeah…”

“Very well.”

In one swift motion, her dagger arced through the air, slicing clean across his throat. A fine mist of blood sprayed out, coating the alley walls and the stunned face of the youngest brother.

“B-Brother!” he screamed, frozen in place.

Victoria turned her gaze to him, spinning the dagger lazily in her fingers. Gone was the fragile, teary-eyed girl. In her place stood something cold and calm—a killer with dead eyes.

The youngest Wright brother let out a terrified shriek and stumbled backward until his spine hit the alley wall.

“P-Please! Spare me!”

Victoria tilted her head, her tone almost curious. “I have some questions about the Black Tiger Gang…”

She took a slow step forward, twirling the dagger once more.

“Cooperate—and I just might let you live.”
