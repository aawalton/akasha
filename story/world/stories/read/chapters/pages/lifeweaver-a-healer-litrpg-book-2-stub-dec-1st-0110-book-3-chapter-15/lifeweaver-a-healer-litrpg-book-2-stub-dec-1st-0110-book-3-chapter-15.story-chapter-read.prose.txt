"That'll be them," Curtis said, a weary slump to his shoulders. When a white Volkswagen bus turned a corner at top speed, he shook his head and turned to Luke. "Get the guild up and running, and I'll join you as long as you help me look into the Intruders."

"Had enough of Phoenix Phalanx already?"

"Let's just say my brother doesn't have the best personality."

Ray walked up to them, with Hannah trailing behind. "He's even more of a grump than you?"

The car stopped, and the side door flew open before a small group of people emerged, one of them the clear contender for the role of Curtis's brother. They looked like twins, almost, except this guy was the light and color to Curtis's somber tones and dark disposition.

"Curtis!" the guy shouted, showing a grin wider than anything Luke had ever seen on Curtis's face. He was the taller and more muscular brother, with long blond hair tied up in a messy ponytail at the back of his head. A white T-shirt made his tan skin look even more so, and the pink, tight shorts he wore didn't even reach down to the middle of his thighs.

"Nick," Curtis said.

Nick reached his arms out to the sides and showed an impressive reach before wrapping Curtis up in a bear hug. He spoke in a loud voice, his voice exuberant. "So, you saved the girl?"

"Yeah. The guildmaster of ISG is dead," Curtis said, voice strained under the pressure of the hug. "Luke here killed him."

Nick let Curtis go and reached out a hand for Luke to shake. "Luke! Curtis won't shut up about you. Good healers are hard to come by! Thanks for getting rid of that guild. They've been real assholes!"

Luke felt the bones in his hand coming near enough to breaking, but took the moment as yet another practice to ignore pain. "Hey, Nick. Nice to meet you. ISG is still around, though, even if their guildmaster isn't.”

"How does that work?" Nick asked. "Can you inherit a guild?" He shook his head, dismissing the thought. "Never mind that! Curtis, introduce me to the rest of your friends!"

Curtis pointed to Ray, then to Hannah. "Ray. Hannah."

Nick nodded to them. "Hey, guys! What are your classes?"

"Assassin," Ray said.

Hannah frowned. "Why?"

That set Nick off laughing, a booming sound, larger than life, that never seemed to end. Luke found himself growing a little irritated with Curtis's brother, and the two of them shared a look as Nick explained. "My brother never sticks around in one place for long. Once he inevitably leaves, I'll need to replace him with a support fighter. You look like you might fit the part!"

"Thanks, but I already have another guild lined up."

"Oh? What guild? I know most guilds in this area." He shrugged. "Mostly because there aren't more than a couple operating around here."

"Let's drop it, Nick," Curtis said, not looking all that enthused over his friends and family getting to know one another. "Shouldn't you introduce the others?"

"You're right, of course, Curtis. Where are my manners?" Nick stepped to the side and made a sweeping gesture toward the side of the van, where the rest of his party gathered. "These are my brothers and sisters in arms! Say hello!"

They sort of lined up, and each of them waited for their turn to say hello. First, a fifty-or-so-year-old who looked like he'd just stepped out of an office where he'd worked for thirty years. George waved as he introduced himself, making his pronounced stomach jiggle a little under a too-tight, short-sleeved white shirt with light blue vertical stripes. The man was balding, clean-shaven, and looked out of place with the others in the group. He was not someone Luke would've expected to see in such a small but renowned guild.

Next was Ariel. She was tall and thin, taller even than Luke, and wore a tight-fitted pink T-shirt that showed off her midriff, and too-short, baggy blue jeans, along with a pair of blood red sneakers that matched the color of her dyed hair. Her chipper but brief hello gave away a British accent.

Clementine, a woman in her late forties dressed in the traditional clothes of a nun, who looked a lot like a younger Maggie Smith, just gave her name without preamble before stepping back to give way to the fourth member.

"Hey, everyone, what's up? I'm Dirk, a Mirror Rogue, and I'm the one who makes this party work. Don't listen too much to what Nick, that doofus, says, and you should all be fine. We basically go from dungeon to dungeon and wipe everything out. You'd think we were living in that stupid van, but we have a shelter we bring with us, so it's all good."

Dirk resembled Nick quite a bit. Tall, muscular but not overly so, handsome, with short-cropped brown hair, and with a natural kind of charm undiminished by the word vomit he'd just unleashed.

"Same," Luke said. "Hey! I'm Luke. This is Ray and Hannah."

Introductions completed, Nick wrestled control of the conversation again. "Top level enters first! Curtis told me you're up there, Luke. A little strange for a healer, isn't it? What's your level?"

Luke saw no reason to lie. "Thirty-seven."

"Whoa! Not bad. How did that happen?"

"I've been busy."

"I bet you have," Nick said, voice loud enough to echo off the nearby buildings. "Guess you go in first! This is a one-off, though. Phoenix Phalanx operates alone, but since you're friends of Curtis, we'll make an exception. Aren't there more of you?"

"Not yet."

A thought struck Luke out of nowhere, and he couldn't help but blurt out the name. "Hiroki!"

"What about him?" Curtis asked.

Luke moved to point toward ISG headquarters but realized he didn't know where it was, and dropped his hand. "He was there. When we rescued Dot."

Hannah frowned. "He was?"

"Yeah," Luke confirmed. "He attacked me. Pretty sure it was Alan's fault."

"Another friend?" Nick asked.

Luke nodded. "Something like that."

Luke: "Hiroki. You there? Didn't hurt you too badly, did I?"

"He's not responding," Luke said after a moment had passed.

"Don't think he'd be strong enough to join us at this point, anyway," Curtis said.

Luke sighed. "Maybe not."

Both of them looked up the street at the same time, but Curtis was first to speak. "It's coming."

"Two Seekers, huh? That's awesome," Nick said, waving for the rest of his group to follow. "Let's get going!"

The portal opened without a sound as they approached, motes of light floating all around the dark blue oval.

"So the theory about color showing difficulty is bullshit?" Luke asked.

Ray nodded. "People have been testing and compiling results for the last couple of weeks, and yeah. Seems that way."

"We learn new things every day!" Nick shouted over their heads, his eyes fixed on the portal. "Yesterday, we found out that some monsters don't like it when you insult their mothers!"

Luke decided to ignore the comment. "What about the dungeon tester, or whatever it was called? How can it measure anything if the difficulty isn't decided until someone steps through?"

"There's a set level for monsters and general quests lined up in a portal beforehand."

Luke turned to look over his shoulder at Ariel, who'd been the one to answer. "What do you mean?"

"Don't get her started," Nick groaned.

"Shut up, Nick," Ariel said before turning back to Luke. "Every dungeon has a set starting level, let's call that X. If Integrated around that level go inside, it stays at X. But, if, let's say, you go inside instead, it changes to accommodate your higher level."

Nick let out a loud groan, which Ariel ignored as she continued her explanation. "You said you're thirty-seven. That means the dungeon level X rises to X plus two-thirds of thirty-seven, so around twenty-five. X plus twenty-five, up to a maximum of your level plus five."

"That means the dungeon tester measures the X?" he asked.

Ariel nodded. "And that means the maximum level for a dungeon for you, no matter what, is?"

"Forty-two."

Ray snapped his fingers and pointed double finger guns at Ariel. "The meaning of life!"

"That's the theory, anyway. So far, it's holding," Ariel said while she shot a quick glare Ray's way. "But there are a lot of other interesting tidbits. It's interesting stuff: dungeons and portals. Ever seen a pink portal?" Hannah and Luke exchanged a look, which she interpreted as a yes. "Oh, you have to tell me everything!"

"Luke!" Nick almost shouted, verbally pushing Ariel to the side as he gestured to the portal. "If you please?"

Giving Ariel a smile, Luke stepped away from the conversation and approached the portal. The glow made his skin take on a blueish tint as he stopped just before the threshold, hesitating.

"Something wrong?" Hannah asked from behind him.

Luke wasn't sure how to explain it. "No. There's just something. It's like I can see the thread."

"What's that?" Nick asked.

"Nothing," Luke said, shaking his head a little. He didn't feel like sharing every little secret about his class and its capabilities, and this wasn't the time to theorize about the interconnectedness of systems throughout The Greater System. Even so, he sensed it. A thread loped off and away from the portal, settling into the weave, or perhaps emerging from it. This was new. A byproduct of using Manipulate Thread, perhaps? It was a topic for further study. Not now, but later, when he was alone. What if he could touch the thread and move along it? Would that let him travel to the origin of these portals? Or back to the dungeons? So many possibilities. But, like so many times before, this was not the time for it.

"Let's go," Luke said, stepping through the portal.
