No one stopped him or interfered with his journey to the town center, and before long, Ike stood before the largest and most complicated series of bounty boards he’d ever seen. He hadn’t actually visited a lot of bounty boards, due to the aforementioned savagery, but the ones he’d seen were simple affairs: one board with bounties posted with pins, usually with a short description of what- or whoever they wanted killed. Usually, but not always, the post would include the poster’s best guess at what the monster’s tier was, and whatever details the poster thought might help someone decide if they could take them down or not. Sometimes, rarely, there were pictures, if the poster was artistically inclined or had a visual art-based skill.

This bounty board, on the other hand, was intense. An entire plaza was devoted to boards, which were lined up neatly throughout the space. The boards were organized by threat tier. The ones closest to the front of the plaza were for Rank 1 monsters. The deeper in, the higher rank the monsters became. The central board in each row was labeled with its specific rank. Everything behind that board, until the next labeled board, was a threat at that rank, with the more dangerous bounties organized toward the back of the segment.

Ike gazed at the forest of boards and crossed his arms. How unnecessarily complicated. Now he had to figure out what rank someone else thought Wisp and Mag were from outside… and also had to guess at how strong they actually were now. Wisp had gone up a rank toward the end of their campaign against Brightbriar and she simply hadn’t told him. Mag had ranked up during the final battle. It was very possible that they’d ranked up since.

That brings up a good point. He hadn’t thought about it, but he didn’t know how long it’d been since his last memory. He knew he was missing time, but he didn’t know how much. It could be ten seconds. It could be ten minutes. It could be ten years. He kind of doubted he’d lost that much time, but it was technically possible. He’d passed the rank at which mages stopped aging, or at least aged much more slowly, long ago. If a hundred years had passed, he would probably be able to tell, but if he only lost a decade, he wasn’t so sure he’d be able to tell at a glance.

He stared at the bounty board for another few seconds, then shrugged. He at least knew their lowest possible rank. It was a baseline he could use. He stepped forward.

Someone clipped his shoulder, hitting him with enough force to make him stumble. He turned in time to watch a man blast by, hurtling past the bounty boards.

“Stop him!” a guard shouted.

Ike looked at the fleeing man, then checked his hand. He still had his storage ring, and everything appeared to be in order. He stepped toward the bounty boards, ignoring the guard. This wasn’t his job. No one was paying him to stop running men. For that matter, he didn’t know why the guard was chasing the guy. For all he knew, he was on the guy’s side. Probably not, but he didn’t know.

Besides, the less he interacted with guards, the better. He wasn’t wanted by the law, but he was wanted, and the law had the ability to force him to remove his facial covering. He wasn’t going to get got because he randomly decided to punish a stranger he’d never met.

The guard cursed under his breath and ran faster, chasing past Ike. Ike watched him go, then proceeded into the boards.

The early boards were crammed full of requests, and likewise, full of mages looking for bounties. The further he went into the boards, the fewer mages and requests there were, until he finally reached the Rank 4 and 5 boards, the boards where he could realistically find hints about Wisp or Mag. Beyond Rank 5, the board simply read “6 and up.” The requests were slightly more populous than the Rank 5 board, but only because one board held all the requests above Rank 5.

He worked his way through the Rank 4 and 5 boards, then finished with the Rank 6 and up board. Rather than searching for Wisp and Mag on the final board, he scoured it for information that aligned with the visions he’d seen in the crystal. He pulled two requests that could plausibly describe Wisp, but found nothing for Mag. He was still wandering the final board when a man drew up alongside him.

“Looking for information on the divine skills?” the man asked.

Ike glanced at him. “Who’s asking?”

Ignoring him, the man continued, “You aren’t the first to think of this. The board’s already been stripped of anything that could be divine-skill-related. The vultures were down here a week ago, on day one.”

A week ago. That lined up with when he’d woken up in the desert with no memory. In other words, the whole divine skill thing had kicked off at the moment he’d awoken. What did it mean? Did it mean anything? He didn’t know, but he tucked it away for later just in case.

“You have a better idea?” Ike challenged him. He didn’t. He’d take suggestions. He said it aggressively, though. He didn’t want this guy sussing out that he had no idea what he was doing.

“Sure I do. We’ve got a line on one of the skills, but we need more people to take it down. If you’re back here, you’re either crazy or confident in your strength. We could use people like you.”

Ike narrowed his eyes, immediately suspicious. That sounded like a trick to get him into a nice quiet place where a bunch of people could jump him… or a way to get chaff onto a team so they could soak damage for the real team members. Putting aside Wisp and Mag, who barely counted as a team anyways, he’d only ever had bad luck with teams. Someone inviting him to one on circumstantial proof of his strength would’ve been suspicious to anyone, but it was especially suspicious to Ike. “You mean you could use a bunch of meatbags while you and the real team members take all the glory?”

The man laughed and raised his hands defensively. “Look, I’m not going to lie: You won’t get the skill. But you’ll be part of the team that gets the skill! And we pay well.”

“Pay random people you encounter in the bounty boards well?”

“There’s a test, of course, but sure, why not? You can’t come back here unless you at least stand a chance of fighting these monsters; there’s a barrier that prevents weak mages from progressing further into the bounty boards than they stand a chance of winning against. Precious few mages in this city can head this far back, and I don’t recognize your face. You’re an out-of-towner. You don’t have a team, or else you’d have brought them here. You’re strong and unaligned. That’s rare in this city.”

“What… really?” Ike asked. Where he’d come from, being aligned with a team or someone else at all was rare, let alone rare at this high a rank. He didn’t know that he knew of anyone with a team who was also at this rank. Maybe Those Who Oppose Llewyn, the useless bastards, but that was about it.

The man nodded. “Indeed. Things are different where you’re from, I take it?”

Ike nodded cautiously. He didn’t see how confirming that he wasn’t from here endangered him when he was already known to not belong to a team and wearing clothes that didn’t match anyone else’s, but he was sure there was something to the question.

“My name’s Cyan, by the way. And you are?”

“Ike.”

“Ike, great to meet you. Listen, you don’t have to come. But if you decide to show up…” Cyan reached into his robes and retrieved a card, handing it over. Ike took it, turning it over in his hands. It said something on it, but he couldn’t read the local language. “That should have all the details.”

Ike looked at Cyan. “I can’t read it.”

“That’s fine. Just do this and focus on it.” Cyan mimed holding the card up to his forehead. “It should transmit the information directly into your mind.”

Ike obliged. A location and loose information about the quest poured into his mind. The skill they were after, according to the card, was the large figure in the darkness. They were meeting just outside town tomorrow morning. Notably, no information about the monster’s location flowed to him. He frowned for a second, his paranoia triggered, then realized: if they told everyone where the monster was, no one would bother showing up for the meeting. They’d all go directly to the monster, to try to take the skill for themselves.

He had no intent to give anyone but himself any of the divine skills. He might make an exception for Wisp, and maybe Mag, but not for a bunch of strangers. And that was exactly why he was suspicious of this meeting, because that wasn’t a strange take for a mage. Especially the older, more powerful mages who’d grown up under the skill hunting system, which was exactly who the meeting was targeting. Everyone who showed up to this meeting would do it with the intent to double cross everyone else present and come home with the skill. In other words, even if it wasn’t Cyan’s trap, it was, without question, everyone else’s intent to turn this meeting into a trap for everyone else present. Ike was no exception.

Cyan smiled. “Hopefully, I’ll see you tomorrow. Think about it. In the worst case, at least you won’t die alone.”

“I don’t intend on dying.”

“No one does.” Cyan shot him a wink and wandered off deeper into the boards, likely to ambush the next person who arrived. Ike considered the card again, then tucked it away and headed off himself. He had some leads to follow before he made his mind up about that.

Aside from the natural trap that everyone attending this meeting was going to try and spring on everyone else, the invitation was an obvious trap. He wasn’t sure exactly what Cyan’s angle was, but he was sure it was a trap. Cyan probably hadn’t identified him as the divine skill holder, but if he had, then that was the catch. If he hadn’t, then it was probably just a basic newbie trap, like the one that had tried to kill him way back when with the Salamander, when he barely had anything more than the original unevolved Lightning Step. It was a hell of a newbie trap that targeted high-level mages, but on the other hand, wasn’t this exactly the time to target high-rank mages? Thinned out the divine-skill-hunting competition.

He didn’t mind a challenge. The only question was, why were they so confident? They’d invited him, and they could generally sense his power level. That meant they were sure they could kill him. And assuming they weren’t braindead, they knew every other mage they invited would also be of the same mindset of “fight the monster together, then immediately double cross everyone here to get the skill.” They were either supremely stupid, which Ike doubted, or supremely confident in their own strength. A higher-rank mage kind of confident in their own strength. Maybe even all the way to Rank 7.

One way to find out. If things went wrong, he could always run for it. He was proud of his speed. If he had to choose his greatest strength, he’d pick it. Now that he knew that Lightning Step, the basis of his divine skill, wasn’t just Unique but Divine, he wasn’t afraid to rely on his legs if he needed to. Sure, the skill had evolved into more than just a movement technique, but that was its heart. That was where it had begun. There might not be a faster movement technique in the whole world.

He didn’t know that, but he liked the idea. It certainly felt right, anyways. It wouldn’t matter if he was the fastest Rank 5 in the world if they had someone at high Rank 6 or 7; they’d still be able to out-speed him. As long as they were about the same rank, he should have the upper hand in terms of speed.

There was always the possibility that they were legitimately, naively looking for party members in the sketchiest way possible, and the only trap was the one the mages they’d invited were obviously going to spring. Ike doubted it, but it was technically possible. Either way, it was better to prepare for the worst and be surprised by the best; far superior to the other way around.

Which brought him to the two bounty notices he’d taken down. One was for a strange creature that had cloaked a valley in white fabric, while another was for a spider monster that lived in a tunnel. The spider monster was supposedly hairy and broad-legged, like a wolf spider, but who knew? Maybe they’d been mistaken. Either way, they were both worth checking out.

The meeting was tomorrow, which gave him enough time to get to the wolf spider. The two requests were relatively close, so he could pass by the strange-fabric-place on the way back. He wouldn’t have enough time to fight both, but if he checked out the second one, he’d at least know if it was Wisp or not.

No time like the present. Ike set off at a jog, quickly leaving the city behind.
