Don started making his way back towards the swamp. Before he left the cavern, he tried to look for more bats in the ceiling, both with his Echo Location and regular senses. There were none to be seen. If there had been any, they must have vanished when Don killed Snarlak. Dungeon bullshit.

That left Don with one realistic option. Morshoff. The terrible eldritch monster in the swamp waited in the darkness like an ancient, patient spider.

He wasn’t sure if he was ready for the real end boss of the dungeon, but if he was ever going to try, it was now. He had sonic bombs and fire. Combined with all of the strength he had acquired in the dungeon, he was feeling excited.

Sure, he didn’t know if he would make it. Sure it was scary. Sure he could die.

But that didn’t stop his confidence. He had earned this confidence. He had fought to death over and over, again and again.

Little by little he had built this. He had built a mentality worthy of a predator. Kill by kill he had earned his right to exist in this new reality. And nothing could take it away from him.

Sure, there were vastly stronger creatures than him in this new reality. Morshoff could be one of them.

Was it stupid to go fight the boss? Reckless? Foolhardy?

Sure.

But when he really weighed his options, he saw that there wasn’t much of a choice.

Don could either go forward and fight the boss, and claim a new height. If his confidence was strong now, it would be tempered into something unbreakable.

Or he could slink away and not test himself— and that would always be there. He could never escape that gnawing feeling in the back of his mind. He would always have to live with the fact that he bitched out. Whenever he would face an enemy, he would always know deep down inside of him that he bitched out. It would be inescapable.

Now, that’s a haunting feeling.

No… There would be no backing out. He wouldn’t trade self-respect for safety. Not when there was such glory on the line.

This new reality wasn’t just stat boosts. It was about adopting a new mentality. He could not afford his old weak mentality. Because what had that mentality from his past life earned him?Insipid mediocrity.

No. It was time to test this new Don. This new Don that he had been relentlessly building since this new reality had given him a chance. If he could pass this test, he would emerge as something profoundly different.

So as Don walked through the ice cavern, his body trembled with every step. Fear and excitement danced around the line.

That line that separated bravery from recklessness, and fools from geniuses. Losers and winners.

He wouldn’t know if he was a winner or a loser before he tried. But if he didn’t try… Well, that just meant he would default to being a loser.

With this Don wrestled as he backtracked the dungeon. On the way, he stopped by the Grimoid castle and looted the place. He found some rope and tied a bunch of spears into a bundle. He would have to McGyver some solution to accessing the spears in the fight. He would need a lot. He would probably have to go almost full ranged mode, utilizing his Telekinesis and Life Drain.

While bravery and foolishness often danced on the line, sometimes things were clear cut: If he went into the bog and tried to wrestle Morshoff down in a melee brawl, that would just end up with him dead.

Don collected the spears, checked back all the rooms of the upper floor of the castle. There were a few straggler Grimoid hobgoblins and an armory. He looted it and roped up a bunch of spears. He had three dozen in total in two bundles. That should be enough. In addition to that he filled his satchels with spearheads from splintered spears and even took a few wooden shields with him. Mostly to throw.

This would be a very different fight.

Don realized he would need another training session. If he was to face off against this overwhelming force, he needed every advantage he could eke out. That was what being a predator was.

It was ultimately about competition. This whole new reality. It was about competition. And you don’t compete without an edge. You just lose.

Don would have another training session and spend the Evolution points he had gained from the ice bat cavern.

I’ll have to upgrade my Psychic Sense. This fight will rely on my Telekinesis.

On some fundamental, intuitive level Don knew he wouldn’t just use a bit of fancy mind tricks and beat the boss. This fight would not rely on his Telekinesis. It would rely on everything he had learned so far. This was his tipping point. This was his trial by fire. Mom’s spaghetti and all that shit. And this moment was Don’s to capture.

If he could pass through this, he knew whatever Jacques had been doing was trivial. He would return back to the tutorial and absolutely dominate it. Take everything. Take Jacques’s System Points. Kill the monsters, control the areas.

It would all be his.

He had come so far. There was no way there were humans that were even worth his notice in the tutorial. He would have to plunder through it and search for strong foes.

But he was getting ahead of himself. He needed to focus on this moment. He needed to focus on what the plan was.

Don left the Grimoid castle, but stopped to pay respect to the Grimoid King. Due to whatever dungeon bullshit, the corpse was no longer there, but the wrecked room was as good a temple as any for their carnage and the lessons Don had learned.

Homass had been the first foe to really push him to the edge. After that he had hardened, and started to see things differently. And for that the bastard had earned Don’s respect.

After he was done, he left the castle and found himself back in the swamp.

It was still smelling of sulphur and smoke. Still as dim and moist as ever. The shanty shacks creaked around him. The road bridges swung idly in some wind Don couldn’t feel. And now that he was paying attention there was an ineffable dread in the air. Some kind of awful cosmic despair that seeped into the mind. Don concentrated on shielding his mind with his psychics. He pushed away whatever foreign tendrils of influence that were trying to squirm in. His mental shield broke the spell of dread. Morshoff had been influencing him. Don shuddered at the thought.

Suddenly his mind was clearer. He breathed in. His mind was still dancing between the line of excitement and fear. But now excitement was winning. The remnants of fear were pushing away. That was good. Fear and doubt would have no space in the coming fight. Only clarity. Only confidence. Only victory.

Don whistled. High and clear. A long piercing sound that he repeated. It bounced off the cavern walls, the shanty buildings, the wood of the platforms.

There were no Grimoids anywhere. But when he whistled and closed his eyes the echo pinged off the bog in the middle.

And Don got an image of the full body of Morshoff. It was monstrous. Beneath the water squirmed around hundreds of eel like tendrils. They were all connected to something of a bulbous turtle shell from which the head protruded. Beneath the turtle shell, between all of the tendrils was a long, really long thick stalk. It connected Morshoff to the bottom of the dark sludgy water pool like a root. The bog was deep. Don used Echo Location again and could feel Morshoff reacting to it. It was subtle, but it undulated on the stalk, waving side to side slowly.

Don understood something horrible.

If Morshoff thought it was going to lose the battle, it would use the stalk to submerge itself in the swamp. It would plummet back to the bottom to lick its wounds.

Which meant a very simple thing.

If Don wanted to kill it, he would have to plunge himself into the swamp, and rip Morshoff off the stalk.
