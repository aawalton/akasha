Chapter  LVIII (58)

The first thing to go were the years of muscles she’d built while training these last five years. Every year shaved off her body made her feel lighter and weaker. Thousands of hours of discipline disappeared in a matter of minutes. When her stature began to shrink, she accidentally stopped the spell as the loss of height created a weird sense of vertigo.

“Damn,” she muttered after blinking away the weird sensation. She raised a teenaged hand and stared at it. Minimally callused. Not yet hardened by her master’s sword regimen. Then she touched her hair.

“Shouldn’t my hair have retreated back into my head?” Mitsuko asked Sterling. “It feels even longer than it was minutes ago.”

The cat, who’d been watching her with interest, was quick to respond with an explanation. “Simply a matter of perception. Your body is now smaller, so your hair feels longer. As to why it didn’t retreat into your scalp; your hair isn’t quite you anymore. Yes, it’s attached to you. But, arguably, your toe nails are as much a part of you as your hair. You could target either of them as separate entities, or even expand your current Mend Self spell to include them. But if you target your body, they won’t be innately included. A lot of this comes down to your innate sense of self.” He paused. Then continued. “Similarly, the nutrients you defecate—”

“Got it!” Mitsuko said quickly. “Okay, teenaged me isn’t going to get me far enough. The guardian isn’t abducting teens. I need to go further if I want to blend in with the captured children.”

“You’re making a few assumptions about the current situation ahead of us,” Sterling said. “Chiefly, that those missing children are still among the living. It could be a powerful necromancer you face as the guardian. Younger souls are more malleable and a favorite target of soul mages.”

“But you don’t know who it is?” Mitsuko asked.

“No,” he admitted. “I have a few suspicions on potential historical figures. But nothing concrete.”

“Then I’m going to be optimistic and plan as if the children are still alive. Besides, being smaller could actually help me if I’m engaging in espionage and skulking about.”

“Your original stature already was not what I would describe as tall,” Sterling commented.

Mitsuko rolled her eyes, then resumed casting the spell.

She continued to shrink. Her body flattened and her clothes hung from her body as she entered a juvenile form.

Sterling cocked his head at her. “Oh, I suppose I should warn you about your teeth.”

By the time he said it, it was too late. Blood coated the inside of her mouth as several of her back teeth retreated into her gums. She lacked the ability to create objects with her Mend spell, so no baby teeth appeared to take their place.

“Ah, fuck you,” she said with squeaky, childish voice. She ended her spell and grabbed her jaw. There were several holes now in her mouth. Her two top canines had retreated all the way back and left behind only smooth gums. As had some of her molars. But at least her front teeth were still in place. She wouldn’t be talking with a lisp.

“I wish I had a mirror,” she said. “It would be nice to know what I look like. My family never had any paintings done when I was young.”

“You look like a child,” Sterling said. “If I had to guess an age, I would place you at eleven years old. Maybe ten.”

“It fits within the range of those taken by Verdant Island’s guardian. I don’t think I’ll need to de-age any further. This should be enough to fit in easily.”

“Not if you keep speaking in that cadence with your current vernacular.”

“What? My voice’s child-like now. Can’t you hear it? It sounds completely different.”

“Your word choice,” Sterling said. “Pick smaller words. Use run-on sentences rather than complex ones. This is a backwater village full of goblins. Stop sounding educated.”

Mitsuko nodded slowly. That made sense. The advice was strange coming from someone like Sterling though.

“Alright. I’ll dumb down my sentences.”

Sterling shrugged his feline shoulders and then returned to grooming himself.

Mitsuko’s next project was to figure out what the fuck she was going to do about her clothing. That was the missing piece in her plan. Her pants lay at her feet and her shirt, now oversized, acted like a dress, nearly reaching her knees. In the future, if she ever cast this spell again she would make certain to have some of Holly’s clothes on hand.

She picked up her pants and flicked her wrist. The action made her ring slide right off her hand. She scrambled on all fours and recovered the artifact from the tunnel’s rocky floor. Thankfully, the white metal glinted under the torch light. In her second attempt, the sword materialized in her hand. Completely off-balanced now. Her body wasn’t meant to hold this length of a weapon.

But she wasn’t planning to fight. Instead she used the blade to slice through the pant legs. Once cropped, she slipped them back on and tightened her belt. Baggy and unstylish. But functional. Only Sterling would be around to remember her outfit after this week. And he already had made it very clear to her he didn’t think highly of modern fashion in one of his many rambling rants. She doubted anything she wore would disappoint him further.

“Turn around,” she told the sage.

Sterling rolled his eyes but did as she asked.

She tossed aside her shirt, preparing to crop it like the pants. Then she paused.

The skin across her stomach was smooth and blemishless. She ran a thumb over it. Just…skin. The old gnarly scar was now missing. Her heart beat faster than it had in her last fight and her skin shivered under her touch. That old wound was now…gone.

Not that it wouldn’t return when the Prismatic Spiral reset. But she had the power to undo that damage permanently after they escaped the archipelago. At the cost of the last five years of her life. Was the loss of muscle worth it to reclaim her once perfect body? She still had the potential to rebuild her body again as well.

Her gaze shifted from the skin of her stomach, over to her sword. She wielded magic now. What use did she have for muscles and swords? Was it even worth rebuilding her body with the new power at her disposal?

This wasn’t a productive use of her thoughts right now. It was a decision to consider after she saved the archipelago.

A minute later, she tossed the cut up shirt on. She’d added a few additional slices in the fabric to help the outfit embrace the ragged appearance. Hopefully that made the already abysmal clothing look more natural. Like she was a street urchin.

Her boots sadly could not be cut down in size. She had to discard those and wrapped her feet up in some of the discarded strips of cloth from her shirt. Even if she stepped on nails though, she’d be able to Mend her feet back together. So there was minimal risk outside of momentary pain.

“You think my hair is too long?” she asked Sterling.

“Oh? Am I once again permitted access to my sight?” the sage asked in return. “Are my eyes no longer banished from the sight of you?”

“Stop being dramatic.”

Sterling turned to face her. His glowing green eyes scanned her top to bottom.

“You’ll be fine. It’s not an unnatural length of hair.”

“Good,” Mitsuko said. She’d always wanted to try out having this length.

“Now that you’ve reverted to your pubescent state, are you prepared to move onward?”

“Nothing holding me back now.” Mitsuko stepped forward and stumbled, falling on her face, her stride unnatural. This body was taking a bit of time to get used to. At least falling hurt less with her recent decrease in height.

The tunnel only required a single Mend more before Mitsuko reached the end of it. She found that while her new body lacked the strength she’d grown so used to over the last half a decade, it did come with a few advantages as well. Her small form slipped through gaps and holes easily and as she navigated through the tunnel’s rubble. Still…her stride length was small and she lacked endurance. Mitsuko had to refresh her body with Mend every ten minutes to keep herself jogging.

“I suppose I should warn you of a consequence that comes with removing years from your life,” Sterling commented as he padded alongside her.

“What’s that?” Mitsuko asked, once again refreshing her body so she could speak to him without gasping for air.

“When you take a jug of water and then dump it into a cup, what happens?”

“What?” Mitsuko asked, confused. “The cup fills with water?”

“Very good. You’ve switched out your jug of water with a cup.”

“Gross. Sterling, are you making a comment about my chest size?”

Sterling missed a step and fell on his face. “Of course not!” he spluttered upon his recovery. “I would never be so crass! And what point would such a comment serve? No! I am stating that your body no longer holds as much blood!”

“Oh.” Mitsuko slowed her jog to let Sterling catch back up. “Does that mean…casting spells will be slightly more taxing.”

“Precisely. Your power has been cut by half. Maybe a bit more. Don’t expect that body to perform the same feats of magic.”

“You might have mentioned this limitation before I transformed myself into a preteen.”

“I do not recall you consulting me for my opinion on the matter. You kept this all as a surprise for me.”

Mitsuko had to admit to herself that it was a fair defense. Still, she felt like arguing with Sterling so she opened her mouth. But the words died on her lips as something interrupted her.

“Do you hear that?” she asked the cat.

“I hear nothing,” Sterling said. “And let me remark that I currently have a body with superior hearing to your own. Are you perhaps suffering from an auditory hallucination? Perhaps you partook in food poisonous to humankind?"

“I haven’t eaten shit in days,” Mitsuko replied. “You know that.”

She frowned and listened. A distant melody echoed off of the tunnel’s walls. The notes were filled with wanderlust that pulled at her heart. She deeply wanted to embrace the source. To curl up next to it and let it carry her off into dreams.

“Something’s weird,” she said. “I hear music. We should investigate.”

“Music?” Sterling cocked his head. “What sort?”

“Maybe…a wooden flute?”

The thought of a flute punched her in the heart. She remembered seeing the bitelas boy falling from the clocktower’s stairway, pulled back by her spell. A flute tucked into his belt. Could it be him? Was he here? She couldn’t do it again. She couldn’t be expected to kill another child. Let alone that child. The one she’d already murdered once.

“Interesting,” Sterling muttered. “I hear nothing. It does narrow down my suspicions somewhat on who this might be.”

“The boy I killed?” Mitsuko asked, her heart sinking.

“What? Him? No. Only one guardian per island. Bupre is banished. I explained this before, didn’t I?”

“Bupre…” Mitsuko repeated the name.

“The son of the Bupre I once saw perform. Mentioned him back in one of our first loops together. Phenomenal musician. Same name as his son. Common bitelas tradition is to pass your name down to the next in line. It caused a great deal of political drama at the time since the boy was a bastard.”

Mitsuko mulled over that information. The good news was that it meant the music wasn’t coming from the boy. And…if Bupre was banished. Did that mean the boy wasn’t completely dead yet? She tried to recall what Sterling had said on the topic weeks ago when she asked about Rodrick.

“I’m going to see them again at the end of all of this, aren’t I?” she asked. “All of the guardians. They’re not completely out of the picture yet, are they?”

“Is this your question?”

“No.” Mitsuko felt confidence grow in her. She could do something. The boy might not need to die permanently. She just needed to find a solution.

“You can’t save him,” Sterling said. “I’ll tell you that for free. I don’t think any of the sages will complain about me parting with that bit of knowledge.”

“But—”

“Put thoughts of Bupre out of your head and focus on the task in front of you. The guardians all died long ago.”

Mitsuko didn’t argue. She simply let her thoughts simmer internally.

As they continued on their way, the music grew in volume. It continued to pull her forward, attempting to manipulate her mind. But the clearer it became, the more apparent it was that it wasn’t meant for her. For now, Mitsuko had the ability to beat back the music’s pull through willpower. Though she shuddered to think what might occur if its notes changed their trajectory to target her.

An old ladder and a metal hatch greeted them at the tunnel’s dead-end.
