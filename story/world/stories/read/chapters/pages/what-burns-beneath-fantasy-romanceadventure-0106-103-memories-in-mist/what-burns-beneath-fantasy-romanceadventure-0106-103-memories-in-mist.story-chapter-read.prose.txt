“What? You want to take a memory from me?” My voice quavered, sounding very small in the dimly lit space.

Colorless hair swayed. Then her entire body did, too, as the lethian sagged against one of the tables “N-no. Not take. Share.”

“Her kind feed on mortal memories.” Velidan sounded entirely unconcerned. “And when the human is calm, it’s a very gentle process.” He gave a dark chuckle, and I shuddered to think what it was like if the victim was not calm. “You may trade, if you feel inclined.”

Right. Probably shouldn’t think too much about that. And I should probably just be glad she doesn’t need something much worse than a memory, like a bucket of my blood. Salt, am I actually considering this?

A long look at the gaunt, shivering figure in front of me, a creature whose misery was apparently born from compassion towards my kind, settled the question.

“I will not take,” she whispered, pulling a pearly seashell bowl and a crystal decanter from one table. Whatever was inside the glass looked like smoke. “It will only h-hurt for a moment. P-please. Are you… w-willing?”

I looked at the little silver mirror, swallowed, and asked timidly, “What do you need me to do?”

She let out a little whimper and knelt, setting the bowl down. Trembling hands bid me kneel, then poured the crystal’s contents into the shell. It looked like bright water, with hazy edges that rose like fog. A little pond framed by morning mist.

Squishy dirt and cool grass cushioned my knees. I clutched the mirror in one hand and she grasped the other with icy fingers.

“Slowly.” The rumble made me jolt. He towered just behind her, casting a long shadow over both of us.

Rather than cower beneath the command, she only gave me an apologetic look. “It will only h-hurt for a moment,” she breathed again, lifting my wrist to her mouth.

Fascination and horror kept me still as the Fae pressed her frosty lips to the sensitive skin of my wrist. Then, after a concerned flick of those ruby eyes, she sank her teeth in. It happened so fast I barely had time to yelp at the flush of pain before she lowered my hand to soothing steam.

Cool fingers traced a round, silvery bite-mark on my wrist. Crimson swirls wound through the liquid, and it seemed to grow even brighter.

“Remember,” she murmured, touching my skin as gently as if it were shattered glass. “S-something good. A day. A m-moment. Joy, please, or h-hope, or… or love.”

I stared down, mouth open and head spinning, trying to do as she asked while transfixed by the trail of my own blood pooling below. My brow furrowed as I wracked my mind, thinking of Durst and Marion, of Fellbrook and the glint of sunshine on Royce’s hair, of days spent digging my fingers into garden soil or sprawled peacefully beneath a whispering witchwood.

Soothing heat slid up my wrist, wound its way through my veins and settled somewhere deep within me. A pleasant, blurry fog that crept into the corners of my vision and turned the pavilion to a dreamy haze.

Below me, the water changed.

Glimmers of color appeared. Night-black leaves, crisp green grass and the cool gray of our home’s stone walls. I gasped, leaning down as familiar faces winked into each ripple. Mist wound up, tinged red by blood, with voices echoing within.

And then it was the day after my eighteenth birthday.

I sat on sunlight grass, bare toes curled into squishy soil, with a bandaged rabbit cradled against my chest.

I could smell her. Distantly, some part of my mind insisted that it wasn’t real, that this was a dream or a figment in smoke and swirling water, but every sense told me otherwise. My arms were full of patchy, hay-scented fur and a tiny heartbeat thrummed against one wrist.

“Maybe we could find her a friend.” I giggled, nuzzling my nose to Rosie’s head. Then I set her down and reached for my little shears. “How do you feel about grandkids? Little fuzzy ones.”

Durst squatted nearby, broad hands scrubbing at clothes in a soapy wooden bin. He squinted at me. “Kid, I don’t say no to you very often.”

I bit down on a grin. “So it’s a yes?”

He grunted, sloshing water onto the grass as a shirt was lifted and hung from our laundry line. Water pattered onto the ground beneath it. “Or you could let her loose. That leg’s nearly healed.”

Two fingers cupped one delicate leg, lifted, and I set about carefully snipping away the red, peeled skin around angry-looking scabs between missing toes and patches of worn fur. The rabbit twitched a few times but, now-familiar with the process and with my touch, stayed calm.

“I will soon,” I sighed, then pressed a kiss to the soft space between her ears. “Once her fur’s mended.”

“Hm.” He squatted again, watching me before giving his head a little shake.

We worked in peace for some time. I made soothing noises and murmured sweet words to my pet while tending her wounded skin. Eventually the sounds of splashing and dripping cloth fell silent.

I glanced up to see Durst staring into the basin, mouth a tight line. Dark eyes flicked to mine. “Speaking of grandkids.”

I froze.

“Royce came by last night. While you were helping Clem.” He cleared his throat. “You probably know why.”

I carefully set the shears down and reached for a pot of salve. Trembling fingers dipped in, then reached for one of the rabbit’s more raw areas. “And, um… h-how many goats am I worth?”

He snorted a laugh.

I echoed it even as heat spread up my neck. “Or did you decide on cows?”

“Neither.”

“What?” I blinked. “Salt, you’re not going to make him do labor, are you? He’ll be miserable if you take him hunting every day!”

That earned an amused huff as Durst stood and strung one of my frocks. “Not just him. Kid doesn’t know what ‘quiet’ means. No, I told him to ask you about it.”

My nose scrunched. “What, about the… er, price? But I don’t know what-”

“Price, permission, timeline, all of it.” He waved one hand through the air. “Told him it’s not for me to decide.”

Songbirds chirped above us. Warm air ruffled my hair and brushed my skin. It all seemed to go on for a very long time.

Finally, in a voice that didn’t quite sound like mine, “I don’t understand.”

Broad shoulders lifted in a shrug. “It’s not my decision.”

“But… but it’s… supposed to be.” The words fell helplessly. Then, with a surge of something like anger, “You’re my guardian! I thought… ashes, even if we’re not blood-”

“Brin.” He pointed one soapy finger at me. Thick black brows lowered, drawing together with a deep crease between them. “You’re mine. ‘Course your mine. Blood or no. Just...” His deep tone softened. “You can be his, too. Or someone else’s. Or just yours. Won’t make any difference to me.”

I sat staring. Rosie crept close, whiskers tickling at my ankles and nose twitching against the breeze.

Durst tipped the basin, sending water and suds across the grass. Then, gruffly, “Won’t make you less mine.” He wiped his nose, then added with a huff, “An’ I don’t need any goats or cows. Your pet’s making me go gray, as is.”

I smiled, but it felt hollow. The lovely dream went still as I cast an uncertain glance around.

It… it happened just like that. Didn’t it? He left to start chopping firewood, and I sat there for a while and I… I thought about the future. What it would look like, and it was frightening but it was so exciting, too, and… it’s not like that now, is it?

Clouds drifted in front of the sun, casting shadows across the ground. A chill pricked across my neck, then crawled its way down my spine and into my belly.

“This is lovely, mortal.” The lethian’s voice drifted along the wind. She sounded… different. Her words didn’t quaver, they were as lush and smooth as the grass beneath my toes. “A sweet dream, indeed. Thank y-”

“It’s wrong,” I gasped, rising to my feet on a swell of dread. “I’m sorry, this isn’t… this was happy, but it’s different now. It’s gone. It… it…” I struggled to find the right word, heart thrumming, “broke.”

She murmured something in response, something in a very soothing tone, but the words died with the breeze.

It’s wrong. Isn’t it? My pulse hammered. Shaking fingers crept to my neck, then closed over a red jewel. This was good, it was, but of course it’s different now. I don’t want to watch myself laugh about marriage and children, I can’t have a family of my own, not when I’m-

I was walking on plush carpet, breath trapped in my throat and fingers iron-tight around the shard. A door of cracked glass and a snapped-off handle lay ahead, with blood-spattered ivory sweeping along outside. The other one was a good memory, once, full of promise and hope and a hundred futures, but now every step brought me closer to the cruel smile ahead and some hopes that just couldn’t-

“I’m sorry.” My cry echoed off of cold marble. “This isn’t… y-you wanted something h-happy, and I… n-not here.”

I fled from the vision as her voice rang out, calling me back. I’m supposed to give her something good! A happy memory, and of course I have so many of those! Something wet dripped down my cheeks.

I was fifteen, kneeling in fallen leaves atop a grave marked Fia Damelle. Hands outstretched beneath a pattering little rain shower that swirled above the stone.

“I did it earlier! I’ve been practicing and this time it came out perfect! I didn’t kill any tomatoes!” My chest swelled with pride. “Marion watched, too, and she laughed and had me use it on the whole garden and I only got dizzy a few times. It was amazing! I hope you saw. I bet you would have been really proud.”

The stone sat silent, patiently darkening beneath my summoned water. But it… it was just a rock. It was cold and hard, and then it grew too dark, twisting into something larger. A different kind of grave. One that I stared at from the shadow of a doorway while thunder rumbled outside the house. Durst’s bed with its heavy depression and the smooth, empty space beside it.

Then it was my bed, covered in shattered glass and Royce’s blood. I went to the window, screaming his name while he thrashed beneath shadows and teeth outside, calling for me to run. I gripped the sill and searing pain blossomed through my hands-

“Enough. End this.” The command came from just ahead, and somehow an entire world away.

“I’m trying, my lord. She’s… very upset.” The distant voice sounded strained. “I don’t want to hurt her.”

The sharp pain shot from my hands to my throat, then surged like fire through my face. Someone pressed steel to my neck and a gnarled hand was clamped over my mouth. Hard wood dug into my spine, every breath a frantic battle through nostrils clogged by blood. Thom was slumped beside me, wheezing and clutching his side with shaking hands.

Agnes Green straddled my waist, framed by the acrid smoke of burnt flesh. Her face twisted into a vicious leer as she hissed, “I’ll slit her throat. Drop your weapons.”

Panicked eyes lolled to Renner, who stood frozen with his sword and dagger in hand. Ward panted nearby, face ashen, and Josiah dripped blood as he sagged against a table. I moaned and twisted, begging Renner to run with muffled cries-

No, no! Stop! I’m here, I’m alive, I’m past this and I survived and I promised her something happy, I can find-

I was in the Snowswept Inn’s green bedroom, a long-cold washtub on one side. Hazy morning light streamed in through the window.

Renner drew close. Very slowly, as if he was afraid I’d bolt. Gloved fingers closed around my wrist. “The next time,” every word was low and measured, “someone has you pinned, you draw a weapon and fight back. Aim right here.”

His grip tightened, leading my hand up until the dagger’s edge was flush against his throat. Wicked steel pressed up, indenting the skin beneath his jaw.

“Saints, don’t-” I pulled down, but he held fast. The knife didn’t budge.

Copper eyes held mine. “Right here. Got it?”

My belly knotted, but that memory was good. And saints he made me feel safe, I wanted that, I wanted more-

I stood in a frosty pine forest, a half-dead witchwood at my feet and Renner standing ahead. Covered in mud and blood, eyes dulled by exhaustion and nose red from the cold.

I curled both arms around my middle, tears spilling down my cheeks. “I’m not there. I’m not with h-him. I’m here. I’m safe. I’m safe. I’m…” I looked up slowly, Renner’s face a watery blur. “A-am I? Right now?” I held my breath. “Safe?”

He grew very still, then gave a single, jerky nod.

I was, I am, I will be again, things will be better. Of course they will. I’m not alone.

“Lethian.” The word fell like a mortal threat.

“Come away, mortal.” Her voice was just at my ear. “Remember where you are.”

But I didn’t want to, because now I stood beneath a sky of midnight blue and a canopy of pine boughs. Wintry air nipped my nose as I stared in speechless wonder at Joselia.

“About twenty five years ago, I think it was,” she was saying, “I met a young woman on the road. She was about your age and just sitting there, like she’d been waiting for me.”

“It’s alright,” I assured the worried voice as Joselia continued, and my own words echoed through the forest. “Look, this was just earlier. It’s good, it’s… it’s wonderful. Watch.”

Joselia gave her head a little shake. “Truthfully, what happened next was the strangest moment of my life. She looked at me very gravely and said… ashes, I’ll never forget it. She said ‘by all the love a flower bears the dawn, I charge you.’ I don’t think I’ve ever been so rattled.”

I wrapped both arms around my middle, breathing deeply of the crisp forest air. The chill settled into that deep, fierce heat from just before I’d gone to bed.

She loved me. For almost her entire life, she loved me. Saints, I could hold onto that forever. It’s incredible. Someone loved me more than anything in the whole world. I… I wonder if I’ll ever-

I leaned into leather and safety on a moonlit cobblestone street. Darkness and cold air crept along the edges of my vision, but they didn’t press in. They softened everything; shadows shaped Renner’s face in gentle planes and the chill only made his grip on my waist feel all the warmer. My fingers curled against his chest as I gazed up.

Renner gave a slow, sly smile. “Careful, ladyship. I could get used to you looking at me like-”

“I said,” the snarl came alongside splashing water and a grip tight enough to snap bone, “enough.”
