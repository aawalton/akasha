The peasants were assaulting the door at the base of the tower with what looked like a battering ram. The shiny knight cheered them on. I made for the archway as lightning crackled through the sky, hitting the apex of the roof behind me and starting a small fire.

[Yes, go and look at the interior!] said the System, cheerily. [Inside you will find the tools of your trade [Which will be useful if you accept your class.]

We’ll see about that.

I made for the archway, pausing only to examine one of the gargoyles that guarded the way. The sides of the tower were positively bristling with as many gargoyles as a particularly gargoyle-y cathedral. This one’s ugly stone face was…well stony and blank. There was pigeon poop covering one of its eyes.

“Fine pair we make, me figuratively in the shit, and you, quite literally. But at least for one of us, there’s a simple solution.”

I wiped it clean with the hem of my stupid gown and darted into the tower.

The interior was dark and smelt bad. Like, really bad. Like rotting eggs and rotting meat. Overlaid with the sickly scent of incense. Ornate double doors flapped frantically in the stormy gusts, long white curtains streamed shroud-like into the night. A single guttering torch cast the circular room in a lurid orange glow.

[Here is your Enchanting workshop,] said the System. I felt like I was being sold a dodgy house by a very desperate estate agent. Okay, let’s see now. Where is the asbestos?

My bare foot landed on something warm and squishy and…damp.

With a wince I lifted it, looking down into the blank stare of a dead man who lay naked on the floor. He was spread-eagled in the centre of a diagram, his limbs aligning with the pentagram. A circle enclosed it. The first of many cracks in the foundation, as I suspected.

[Complete the ritual to summon a minor demon,] said the System, cheerily. [Of course you will have to accept the class first! With your outsized mana pool it will be easy! You will have mana left over for other activities!]

“Will this minor demon be able to defeat a horde of peasants?” I asked, trying to keep my stomach lining inside my stomach. The diagram’s lines flickered in my vision, so I looked away focusing instead on the…stab wounds in his chest and gut. At the gaping hole where his heart should have been. Ah there it was on the table. In a black bowl. Great. Wonderful. Absolutely bloody fantastic.

[It could probably take out a handful!]

“How do I complete the ritual?”

[Accept your class and eat the heart.]

Well. That escalated quickly.

“So, the Evil is not just an aesthetic then. And there is actual cannibalism involved. Just to be clear?”

[Have I mentioned it comes with great power? Great potential?]

“And a great view,” I muttered. “Quiet, kind, normal neighbours with excellent pitchforks. An inflatable pool.”

A long, pointy blade lay next to the dead man. A stiletto dagger. Elegant and vicious. I leant down and picked it up, as the thunder boomed outside. They are going to think I did this, aren’t they?

I turned and ran, sprinting frantically, leaving the body in my wake, taking the long thin blade with me. Clinging to the handle like a lifeline. Talking to the system was a waste of precious time. A back door, that was what I needed to find, then I could escape into the woods and find a nice cottage, start a new life.

Apprentice myself to… someone not evil. Apprentice myself to myself. Open a little shop. Why not? This time I wouldn’t let anyone get in the way. I certainly wouldn't be opening any joint bank accounts. No matter how much my family and friends told me that’s what ‘proper couples’ did.

The first door I tried led to an unlit room. Weirdly, I could see, despite the gloom. There was no colour, though, everything was shades of grey and black.

[A perk of the Evil Enchantress class! Tapetum lucidum! Excellent night vision. A gift to tease what is to come - comes standard with the body. So convenient. Though it will fade over time if you don’t confirm the class.]

“How come I can already see in the dark since I haven’t chosen a class yet?” There was an embarrassed pause.

[I might have jumped the gun on the body modifications. Also, it is a waste of mana and time if you die instantly, so let's just say this gives you a fighting chance. I suggest you don't waste it. ]

“Hmmph. Stay out of my head unless you have something helpful to say. Like telling me the location of a back door?”

Silence.

It was a bedroom, opulent, black and dark velvet, and contained a few more horrors. Stains on the rugs. Splatters on the wall. The floor was sticky. Wet. My bare feet cringed away. Body parts were scattered here and there, seemingly the result of some appalling act of murderous debauchery. My stomach heaved. My eyes skidded off the scene and landed on a dusty dresser. Absent-mindedly I wiped it clean with one of my sleeves.

Yup. Absolutely a set up.

This room had no exits. I reversed out and tried the second door. This one led to a spiral staircase, and I practically fell down it in my haste, tripping over the hem of the stupid, slinky black gown. Stopping briefly, I hacked the bottom off with the stiletto, which was gratifyingly sharp. That was better.

Down I tumbled, passing more doors built into recesses in the walls. Now I could hear noises echoing up the stairwell. Crashes and bangs and lots of shouting. Light bloomed below, warm petals of ochre and gold staining the walls. Steel rings on stone. More shouts. They were coming, they were inside the castle, and I hadn’t found the way out.

[Better hurry up and accept the class before they arrive.]

“So they can find a nice Evil Enchantress to butcher?” I hissed. The system did not reply, but the silence was huffy.

I turned back the way I had come, scrabbling up on all fours like some kind of possessed monster, and yanked open the next door I came to, darting inside. I would hide and then find my exit.

Inside was some kind of library. Whatever place or world I was in, it had books. All was not lost! Two closed windows were on one side, a fireplace on the other, chairs, a heavy table, bookshelves…a heavy unlit chandelier, rafters, and a tall ceiling. Nowhere to hide.

My heart sank.

I could hear them on the staircase. My time was up.

I raced to the windows, pulled open the shutters, and stuck my head out. Outside the storm raged still, raindrops splattering my face with splodgy fury. The window was just wide enough for me to squeeze out of. There was a dark ledge. It was narrow but… just enough room for me to stand? No one would look for me there.

I gulped as my eye was drawn down, down, down. I still couldn’t see the bottom. No it was too dangerous. Somewhere else? Where? The bookcase. The tramping was growing nearer, I needed to hurry.

I scaled the bookshelf, as fast as I could, shoving the dagger into my hair like a pin. My arms ached with the effort. The bookcases were tall, but I was still too visible. Even if I lay down. The rafters! Standing up to my full height, I jumped, arms swinging wildly over my head. One hand grazed the side of the great beam, sliding off with a contingent of splinters piercing my hand. Fuck.

[Another perk of accepting a class is increased strength,] said the System, [and the ability to increase your stamina!]

“Strong enough to fight them all off?” I said. “Or will I just be strong and dead?” My next leap saw me dangling from the beam, my hands scrabbling against the splintery wood. With a grunt I hauled myself up, grazing a knee. They were right outside now, I could hear voices. Crawling forwards, I laid myself flat, my body along the length.

The door banged open.

People and smoking torches spilled into the room. I ducked my head down, burying my face in my arms. It was dark and shadowy up here. The beam was thick. My hair was dark; my dress was black. I had a good chance of staying hidden. But if this was in fact a nightmare, now would be a good time to wake up. I blinked into the knotted wood and did not wake up.

I knew in my heart this was not a dream, but I hadn’t fully accepted it.

Straining my ears, I tried to make sense of the noises below, in between all the crashes and bangs as the peasants ripped the room apart, I was aware words were being spoken, but I could not make them out.

[Language integration! Another perk of having a class!]

Do you think I could talk my way out of this? I thought as angrily as I could. Somehow, I knew it could hear me. After they see the bodies upstairs?

The system did not respond.

Smoke from the torches drifted up to my hiding place, acrid and nasty and I nearly swallowed my tongue trying not to cough.

Eventually, after what felt like hours, they left, and I peeked down, catching a glimpse of a man in a pair of brown leggings and a tunic exiting the room. He was wearing the kind of outfit I would imagine in a medieval period movie, only dirtier. More worn. More real.

Books lay scattered across the floor. The chairs overturned, the curtains ripped from the windows.

If they left the stairs I could make a run for it. Or perhaps it would be better to just stay quiet? Perhaps I could find some clothes and blend in with them? Risky. This felt like the sort of place that would have wardrobes full of lacy nightgowns and scandalous ballgowns. Not that I have anything against scandalous ballgowns, they just aren’t what I need right now.

[Now would be an excellent time to accept your class!]

Two loud, clanking steps echoed in the stairwell. One, thump. Two.

A steel-encased, very shiny knight appeared, framed in the doorway. He was very tall and very broad, his shoulders barely making it through the door, so large were his shoulder-platey-things. He was even shinier close up. Very, very shiny, his armour reflective like a mirror. So shiny it was dazzling.

His visor was up. I could see his eyes: blue, bright, hard. Staring right at me.

“There you are,” he said in plain English, and lifted a crossbow.
