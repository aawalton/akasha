"No, you aren’t getting me… I’d use my body as a lure—"

"Stop making it sound even worse!" I cut her off. "And no, I’m not letting you use your body as bait!"

Mabel rolled her eyes.

"Ahhh," she groaned. "At least hear me out properly first," she said, already walking toward the turn.

Following her, we stepped back into the corridor. The goblin’s body was no longer recognizable—reduced to little more than a charred skeleton. The hobgoblins, however, were a different matter. Their corpses still had substance, the flesh seared but intact. Perhaps they could serve as bait, though I wasn’t sure whether the red-eyed bunnies would find roasted meat tempting.

The smell of burnt flesh still lingered, though I believed the blood mist had dispersed… or at least hoped it had. "Has the blood snow dispersed!?" I asked her with a sense of urgency.

She could be careless often, and I wasn’t interested in taking chances.

"Why’d I move in if it hadn’t!?" she snapped, then paused. "And don’t pretend to be relieved!" she added sharply.

I stopped mid-sigh. "Sure," I muttered. "Can we move on to the important topic now? What did you mean about being used as a lure, Mabel? Monsters aren’t really—"

"So…" Mabel interrupted loudly, deliberately silencing me. I was certain of it.

"The plan is simple. I’ll use multiple Quick-Step spells to move deeper into the fifth floor while you quietly sit on the stairs, waiting for my return."

"Being quiet is a requirement?" I asked more like interrupted.

"Be quiet!"

So it is important.

"So, as I was saying, I’d run around the floor, agitating bunnies with water bullets or maybe ice bullets… or I could just stomp on them—"

"Yeah, I get it, you’d agitate them…"

"Right," she nodded. "Then!" Her eyes lit up. "I’d come running back to you, use another Quick-Step to leap high at the perfect moment, and you’d launch a massive fireball, and badooom! All the bunnies die." She finished with a satisfied smile.

"Ahh," I sighed in relief. "So you aren’t actually going to use your body as bait, then."

"Yup. While I’d use it to lure them, my body isn’t the lure. That would be different if it were a vampire, though." Her voice grew somber. "But you know what?" she whispered.

"What?" I asked.

"Vampires don’t exist," she said with a click of her tongue, then turned her gaze aside and muttered, "I wish they did."

"You wish, huh…" I smiled as we passed the goblin commander’s corpse and neared another turn.

"You’re surely learning a thing or two from me," I murmured. Or maybe I picked it from her; who knows?

"And what’s that?" Mabel asked.

"Nothing," I decided not to admit that I was wishing for a succubus not so long ago.

"Also… if you burn me, I’ll kill you," Mabel threatened, though I was pretty sure she wouldn’t.

"Sure, the plan sounds good," I replied.

"If any bunny survives, I’ll take care of it!"

"Sure, the plan sounds—" I didn’t get to finish.

"Stop repeating the same thing."

A soft chuckle left my mouth. "Sure," I murmured. No idea why I was agitating her; it was just a fun endeavour for me, I think.

The search for the fifth floor was long and tedious. Mabel insisted I should trust her and leave the navigation to her. I nodded, knowing no better.

Still, I couldn’t help but notice her peculiar habit—tossing a coin at every turn. Surely, she wasn’t truly deciding our path that way.

"Left, yes." Mabel’s blue eyes gleamed with confidence. "There’s no doubt about it—left is the way to the exit!" she declared, slipping the coin into her skirt pocket.

With my eyes squinted, I gulped. The trust I had in this girl had faded by now. I glanced at the two options carefully, searching for any clue to the dungeon might give.

"You’re wasting our time," Mabel murmured with a pout, as if she weren’t the very reason I doubted her choice.

The gate was supposed to have a few torches, light spilling faintly from between the pillars. Still, I doubted it was enough to brighten the corridor—maybe just a little at the far end… an end I couldn’t even see. Why the hell was it so damn long?

My eyes flicked between the two paths, right and left. The right corridor was narrower, dimmer, and the dungeon grass thinned into sparse branches, which somehow made it feel more dangerous.

Like any ‘normal’ person, I muttered a soft "sure," cutting her off mid-sentence. I wasn’t even sure what she’d been saying, but with her arms folded like that, I figured she wanted me to know her choice was logical, not just the result of a coin toss.

"Good choice," Mabel murmured, nodding in approval before stepping in first. I quickly closed the gap to walk at her side.

The dungeon grass in the left corridor glowed faintly green, its light spilling over us. Brighter than the others, bright enough that I glimpsed two red-eyed bunnies gnawing at the stone floor—sharpening their teeth, perhaps?

I wasted no time. I didn’t want to give them a chance to attack. My left hand rose, ignoring the wand in my other hand; fire bullets needed little help.

"Fire Bullet!" I called, alerting Mabel to their presence while also giving away my position.

Mabel’s eyes widened—she had seen them too. And so had the bunnies; both charged at us.

My gaze narrowed. Two small balls of fire sparked to life on my thumb and forefinger, the heat licking at my skin before I fired.

[Squeal!] One hit dead-on, killing a bunny instantly. The other bolted, darting erratically down the corridor. I shifted my aim, tracking it with my finger.

Should I use a fireball instead? I wondered, unsure if my aim could follow its erratic movements.

Before I could act, an icy spear flashed past me, impaling the creature in one swoop and severing its head. Blood splattered forward. Mabel had killed it before I could take my second shot.

I glanced at her. She had used Lance, a basic spell that shaped magic into projections of all types—not true projection magic, of course, that falls in the Normal affinity.

"Huh… didn’t notice them," Mabel muttered, then patted my back, more like smacked it.

"Ahh," I groaned.

"Good work, Arnos! Your vision is definitely improving!" she shouted.

"Well, thanks, I guess…" I muttered.

Mabel studied me briefly, then turned her gaze down the corridor. "The gate should be there," she murmured, before whispering, "Quick-Step."

She vanished from my side in a gust of icy wind, deliberately leaving behind a sloppy trail of ice for me to follow.

I moved to the side, melting it with fire as I went—slipping and face-planting on the frozen floor was something I definitely wanted to avoid.

"Quick-step," I mumbled, following the trail of ice and the icy wind that only hit the left side of my body.

The long corridor ended within four bursts of the spell. I stopped, turned right, and there it was, the opening to the labyrinth. Mabel was already sitting at the gate’s entrance, waiting.

Quick-Step, I whispered in my mind. Not bothering to fine-tune the spell, I drew on dark affinity this time. A burst of dark magic erupted beneath my feet with a low, [Thum!].

The spell carried me to the gate in a single instant.

"You are late," Mabel looked up. She was crouching down, leaning on the rocky outer portion of the gate.

"Maybe you shouldn’t leave your teammate? I was half expecting you to be humming a tune as you descended, leaving me behind."

"I could never," Mabel lied. Standing from the ground, she dusted her skirt and the back of her top and then looked at me, with her other hand’s thumb pointing at the gate. "Shall we?"
