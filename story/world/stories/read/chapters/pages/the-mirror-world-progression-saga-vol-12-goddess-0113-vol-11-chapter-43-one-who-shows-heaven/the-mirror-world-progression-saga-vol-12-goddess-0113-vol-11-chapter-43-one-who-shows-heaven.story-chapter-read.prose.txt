From the early age of thirty-three — adolescence in the lifespan of the Alamadyrians, the giants of the Load Bearing Clouds — Venraiah knew her true name, long as it was: One Who Shows Heaven and Awakes the Creator Within. Within herself, in the language of her people, it made the fun phonetic name, Ashawein — with an ‘ah’ — if creatively formed from the acronym. This is who she identified as internally, secretly, and she knew later, the entire world of Alamacreias would call her, the secret made public once her true journey began.

For all time, before and after, and even between, she knew the Creator Within, whom she called Ein. As a small child, as an only child among an isolationist and insular people, she sang and spoke to her friend Ein, feeling her eyes, feeling her ears, though Ein never spoke back, never made her presence flagrantly known. To Ashawein, that was irrelevant because she knew it mattered, knew it was felt, and made her friend’s dreams happier.

She knew that these dreams birthed new worlds and possibilities.

In the early times, adults merely tolerated this minor eccentricity as normal for an only child, that is to have an ‘imaginary’ friend, odd that it was that the friend was only in her head. As she aged, and did not relent, her parents chastised her, and eventually brought her to priests to ‘fix.’ Sad that it made Ashawein, she learned to lie through this trauma, and hide the truth within her until the time was proper. She wondered if lying was what it meant to become an adult.

Her true mother, the sorceress Sorastes, believed her and supported her when confided in, however. Though none of her family liked the development, Ashawein was found to have the rare and extraordinary gift of spellcasting, among giants, a kind of ‘divine magic’ that did not draw directly from the gods. But, because of this, she had to inevitably become Sorastes’ apprentice, and once she did, she became much happier and productive around the youth of her first century.

Ashawein’s principal talents and passions were toward creation magic, art, materials, and architecture. Secondarily, she was interested in all things related to knowledge and civilization. These things she dedicated herself to with all her heart, soul, and body, and quickly became so skilled at them that her examples and demonstrations — particularly her sculptures — gained renown and prestige, both for her and her family, who’d gradually become more and more proud of her. Though Ashawein regarded this somewhat bitterly due to the long-time disapproval in childhood, it still felt good.

These were nice pluses, but it was not for any of these things that she obsessed over continual improvement, hour after hour and day after day. This dedication was for one overarching goal: to fashion a Heaven so perfect and beautiful, one weeped merely to see it.

It made her weep often, because she knew that even as she was fated to fashion it, it was as doomed as she was.

As a child, her oracular mind and senses were already tuned and well-honed, and though she hid the truth later, they only grew in power. They grew so fast and strong, they became binding chains, and she knew the coming struggle would never be hers to win. All that she did, she’d do for a goal even higher than the world she loved: it was for all of creation, in procession of time and Fate.

Even chained, she had her challenges and duties to conduct, and a role of utmost importance. Even within the shadow of destruction, she would taste love, happiness, adventure, and light. For this, she poured all that she had into her arts and aimed to make the most of a blessedly long life.

So many would be cursed with short ones.

Rumors of war inevitably came to the cloud-top temples that were her homeland, and Ashawein knew it was the sign that she had to start moving before it engulfed the world again. Already wealthy from her world-renowned works, there were many requests from the surface for her presence.

She took her work there, first by doing custom requests for sculpture pieces, which she could usually do in some months of time. In this way, she traveled much and learned of the smaller folks’ quicker ways of living. Deliberately, she absorbed and adapted to them, forging relationships. Most places held the giants in renown, and the people even still worshipped mutual gods that the Alamadyrians saw as their ancestors.

Once she understood the societies and plights of these peoples, she began to be inspired toward adventure, so she walked the path of a hero, adding to the repertoire and legend she knew she’d need in order to accomplish the high-order matters she aspired to. Finally, she took the name Ashawein officially, for these purposes.

She met Fennor, one of the god-blooded, a youth and adventurer favored by his great-grandfather, the God of Fire, Halcos. Saving his life from a wyvern attack, Ashawein gained not only a lifelong companion but the favor of a popular god and his cohorts. Fennor was even a prince of a powerful kingdom, Yarune, though merely fourth in line, and thus seeking adventure for his glory.

They’d both continue together on fortuitous adventures, growing to become relied-on agents of the popular pantheon of gods, and pulling more glory-seekers to them magnetically, their combined charisma and power like a good luck charm to the land. In that time, the awe-inspiring ‘Cloud Giantess,’ Ashawein, so unique and rare in her involvement with the smaller folk that she was practically worshiped, was setting the stage for the ascension that would come one day.

She was most known for her raw physical, martial prowess, performing devastating charges with her two-handed, semi-balanced warclub, and her unique brand of the Cloud Giant powers, specializing in creation/illusion magic, which she used for protection but also adapted in innumerable ways for ‘surprise’ offense. She often had fun with it, combining ‘traps’ with coup de grace melee charges.

But the days of slaying monsters, drinking in inns, and singing at campfires — which were to Ashawein, days of perpetual living nostalgia — came to an end, as war came to Yarune in violent and explosive fashion.

Many years passed as Ashawein was embroiled in the war along with the gods, and increasingly the whole world. Ashawein adapted her abilities for the battlefield, most iconically in wider and wider scale protection magic, including the renowned Wreath of the Clouds that forged clouds into mass ablative armor.

The bitter conflict between the rival kingdoms Yarune and Ulkaer ultimately ended with Ulkaer’s surrender, but not before most of the nobility of Yarune was slain in battle or assassinated. Fennor survived, his father and three brothers dead, and so he became king.

Despite this victory, won at terrible costs, the true battle was in another land, one that had stirred Ulkaer to fight with reinforcements of wealth, items, and magic. The sorcerers of Gerenna had been the corrupting whisperers in the ear of leaders, and not just in Yarune. Gerenna itself was expanding, as its original empire had once, under the Ebon Emperor, who even slew the giants and many lesser gods, which only an alliance of gods finally stopped.

His son after him had been strong, too, but ended the war and pursued peace that lasted for centuries, his interest in clandestine, arcane pursuits. A thousand towers were built, and the land became renowned for its magical knowledge. Something had happened within him over time, though. Supposedly unable to sire his own heir, he pursued darker arts to gain immortality. It changed him, and therein changed his land. It became known for necromancy, and its leader became known as the Pale Prince outside his borders.

Ashawein knew there was no stopping the Pale Prince. He would go on to do worse things than his father. Nonetheless, she would campaign against him her entire life, both for the principle and to do what she was truly called to do.

Tantamount to a demigod already, the hero, Ashawein, had been instrumental with her power and might during the war, and was well known to have protected the future king throughout it all. The hero worship was transforming into actual worship, and the gods blessed this development, desiring this bridge to the people in troubled times. Perhaps they even anticipated her ascension, her inevitable apotheosis.

She had other matters to conduct, however. Far enough from Gerenna not to be immediately threatened, and difficult to assail besides, were the wild forests of the Indigowood, named for the brilliant color of its leaves. But they were also known for their vastness and the many assorted civilizations within it. All paid homage to the greatest of them, though: the sapphire dragons, who coveted and hoarded jewels. The greatest and wisest of these, an elder dragon, was said to be like a goddess to the others.

Ashawein’s adventures took her here, ostensibly to secure reinforcement and support in the war from a land rich, but disparate, in power. The dangers were plentiful and fierce, the journey long and harrowing, but she finally found the palace of the Dragon Queen hidden in the thick wilderness, a jewel in itself. Within, she met a beautiful half-dragon, half-giant of the mountain provinces, called Othenia, who claimed herself the daughter of the queen, and one of the few allowed to lay eyes on the majesty of her mother. No foreigner was allowed, of course.

Through the princess, it was communicated Ashawein’s desire for a boon. What returned was a refusal, saying the Dragon Queen gave no boons to unworthy foreigners. Ashawein, though fearing the queen’s wrath, sent a final request, offering to prove herself so she may earn the boon.

The Dragon Queen sent back acceptance, and the first of twelve challenges she’d have to complete, to prove her worthiness. The first was slaying the Chimera of the North, which sojourned out of the mountains and plagued the gnoll tribes of the Indigowood’s border, glutting itself on their numbers directly and growing bigger by the year. It would be another long journey just to get there.

Ashawein, in resignation to this as her fate in life, set out. She was at least pleasantly surprised that Othenia opted to come with her, for ‘guidance.’
