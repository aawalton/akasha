Asher sprinted back through the second ring along the rooftops, occasionally glancing down at the throng of refugees filling the main road. People were crying as they tried to find friends or family, or simply leaning against buildings and staring lifelessly at the ground while they absorbed the fact that their homes were destroyed and their lives forever changed. Now that he was out of the slums and following the main path through the city, the crowd of people was dense and growing denser by the second. He spotted friendly faces doing their best to force their way through the crowd and offer healing, or trying to instruct the crowd to keep moving farther into the city to allow for others to join them from the first ring, but he didn’t have time to think about any of that.

No, he needed to prepare himself to ask for help from the woman he’d been deadset on killing only a few months ago.

Back before he’d made any real name for himself, when The Reaper was nothing more than a whisper on the lips of a few terrified gang members, Asher had drawn the attention of the Finger in charge of the city. One of the deadliest assassins in all of the Noala Kingdom, and a man who specialized in poison. Drip, or Eight, as Asher had come to know him by, had been almost impossible to track down. His communication with the rest of the Assassin’s Guild in Whikoga was done using mold-covered rats that ran through the sewers. His hits were always carried out through various concoctions of his, such as his fabled magadrine powder that was deadly enough to kill even someone with an evolved poison resistance in seconds. Asher’s very first death had been at the hand of Eight, which had left a lasting impression on him and officially began his war against the Assassin’s Guild as a whole.

It was only thanks to Crush and Thunder helping him capture a senior assassin alive, and then assistance from Bordon for letting him listen in on the man’s interrogation, that Asher had gotten his first lead. The ink Eight used to deliver his orders had been obtained from the Frent family estate; the noble family in charge of running Whikoga. It hadn’t been easy, but Asher had managed to sneak into the estate, in no small part thanks to a far-too-trusting young noble named Theorn. Once inside, he’d found the person responsible for making the ink. Only, they weren’t Eight.

Though they were capable of looking straight through his skills and spotting his Eternity element.

Leighann had taken her own life before Asher could kill her to keep his secret, and in order to try to stop her resurrection, he’d traveled all the way to Dormaul. Of course, once there, he’d had his first encounter with Gran, who ended up talking him out of the whole thing. Asher had known that she’d resurrected Leighann, but it wasn’t as if he’d ever bothered to swing back by the noble district in Whikoga and check to make sure the two of them were ‘cool’ by any means. Yet now here he was, in desperate need of someone with her exact skill set and not knowing where else to possibly turn to.

Oh, I hope she’s not the kind to hold a grudge… he thought as he threw a marked dagger and used Recall to bypass the wall for the third ring.

Asher clung onto the hope that the whole ‘killing herself to escape him’ wouldn’t really be a big deal for her. At this point, he was pretty confident Leighann might be the only person on the entire continent who had died and come back to life more times than he had. By her own admittance, she was the kind of alchemist who took risks nobody else dared to, which had led to her rather frequent demise over the years. She’d died and come back so many times she’d picked up a resistance to the negative effects that came with resurrection, which Asher hadn’t even known existed before she told him. The two of them were kindred spirits in that regard, and he prayed that she would see it the same way.

While he ran along the rooftops toward the fourth ring, he glanced at the groups of nobles running in the opposite direction. These ones were late to the party for whatever reason, but based on their shining armor and expensive-looking weapons, they were ready to assist wherever they’d be needed. Their classes and auras were no joke either, and Asher felt a little more confident about leaving the undead horde in the hands of others while he hunted for the lich responsible.

Before he knew it, he’d reached the wall separating the third and fourth rings. Normally, the wall was heavily guarded in order to keep the riffraff out of the noble district, but today seemed to be an exception. There wasn’t so much as a single guard patrolling the top that he could see, and Asher quickly leapt over the wall, making a beeline straight for the Frent family estate. Just to make sure he wouldn’t be stopped by anyone with an identification skill capable of seeing his class, he swapped over to awakened beast hunter again, recalling just how impressed Theorn had been when he’d seen it.

Hilariously, while he wasn’t wearing those weird, fancy clothes Draken had given him last time to try to blend in, Asher actually looked the part of the noble without even trying now. Most people would probably take one look at him and think he had on a pretty nice set of leather armor. The nobles, however, would no doubt immediately recognize the fact that it was sea serpent leather he was wearing, which was not cheap by any means, and one of the top picks when it came to leather. With his fancy class and expensive armor, he doubted anyone would have stopped him from wandering around in the fourth ring even if there still had been guards out and about.

Darting through the outer wall with Astral Dip, Asher ran across the well-maintained grounds of the Frent family estate, ignoring the curious glances he was getting from the few people still here. There was only one set of eyes he cared about, and they were almost certainly locked away in her workshop.

I really hope nobody is around to worry about this, Asher thought as he ran into the large building Theorn had led him into the last time he was here. According to Theorn, there was a magical array covering the entire building that would alert the family that someone was entering without permission if anyone tried to head inside without an appropriate signet ring. Asher didn’t have a ring to copy this time around, which meant he might only have a minute or two. Ideally all the people who would care about someone breaking into the building were busy stabbing zombies, but there was no way to know for sure.

Sprinting through the halls, he made it to Leighann’s workshop in record time. Not giving himself a chance to second guess how this was going to go, Asher threw open the door and stepped in.

Leighann’s workshop looked almost exactly as he remembered it. There were vials containing all sorts of reagents and half-finished potions sitting on shelves along the walls, vats of bubbling liquids taking up most of the floorspace, and racks of equipment that he couldn’t even begin to guess the name of. And standing directly in the center of all of it, was Leighann. The alchemist turned and gave him a wide-eyed look, clearly not having been expecting him to drop by. Her eyes were still the dark and unnatural twin voids in her head as they had been the first time they’d met, and Asher tried his best to ignore the sensation of those deep pits drawing him in and eating away at his many secrets.

Silence stretched between them for a moment. Asher stood unmoving in the doorway, and Leighann held a vial of sizzling liquid in one hand that she was stirring with some sort of feather in the other. Slowly, the feather dissolved fully into the liquid, and the liquid contained within shifted from a dull green to a bright red as it began sizzling more violently.

“Oh,” Leighann said, tearing her eyes from him as she stared blankly at the vial in her hands. “…That’s not good.”

Asher didn’t even hesitate. Reaching through compressed space, he yanked the vial out of her hands and hurled it down the empty hall, throwing up a Spatial Lock as well for good measure. Before it even hit the ground, the vial detonated, blasting a giant hole in the side of the building and sending sharp chunks of wood bouncing off his Spatial Lock, actually ticking the skill up to level 36. As soon as he’d confirmed the damage was done, he turned to stare pointedly at her.

“…What?" she demanded. "It’s not like I was planning to dissolve the entire feather.”

Asher’s Honest Ears skill kicked in, informing him that her statement was a lie, and his pointed look changed to one of exasperation. He’d forgotten he even had that skill on him at the moment, but he wasn’t surprised in the slightest to hear that Leighann had been about to blow herself up before he arrived.

“Look, I’m sure there’s a lot that needs to be said between the two of us, but we really just don’t have the time right now,” he said. “I’m sorry I was going to kill you before. I spoke with Gran, and I’m not planning to do so any more. And I appreciate you keeping my Eternity element to yourself.”

“I have to admit, I love the efficiency of this conversation,” Leighann grinned. Pulling off her scaled gloves, she tossed them onto her workbench. “Alright. I appreciate your apology, and I thank you for no longer trying to kill me. I suppose I should probably apologize for peering behind your concealment skill as well. My eyes aren’t so much one of my skills as simply a part of who I am now, due to my body adapting to my elements. I can’t really help what I see, but I suppose there wasn’t a need to tease you about it. I was just curious about your element I’d never seen before. Oh, nice job on taking down Eight, for the record. Though I’m a little annoyed with you about that, seeing as he was one of my favorite pen pals.”

“He was a mass murderer responsible for countless deaths!” Asher snapped, growling as he shook his head and tried to get back on track. “That’s not important right now. There’s an undead army pouring over the city walls. They’ve already destroyed almost the entire first ring, and they show no sign of stopping.”

“Yes, I did notice the fact that my sister was flying around up there and spitting flames down around the city,” Leighann drawled. “What exactly does that have to do with me? I’m not a combatant. I might be damn good at killing myself, but I don’t think I’d be all that helpful for killing zombies.”

“Remember how you freaked out about the Council of Death last we spoke?” Asher asked, nodding when the woman’s face paled. In fact, Leighann had been the person to inform him about the Council’s existence in the first place. “Yeah, the Council is leading a multi-pronged attack across half of the Noala Kingdom, right now. I just killed liches over in Poltar, Dormaul, and Furora. Now I’m going after the one leading this undead army here in Whikoga.”

“I’d heard about the coalition of course and what the High Prince was trying to do… but I figured we’d have more time,” Leighann admitted, frowning as she stared at her own hands. “I was working on an acid that only acted on decaying flesh… but it would seem I didn’t work fast enough. What do you need me for?”

“I can’t kill the lich leading the attack on the city if I can’t find it,” Asher explained. “I’m pretty certain the lich here is the leader of the entire Council, which means he’s probably the oldest and one of the most skilled when it comes to magic. I need someone capable of bypassing whatever concealment skills he’s hiding under. I need you to find him for me.”

“You want me to find one specific corpse in a zombie army, huh?” Leighann asked, laughing as she grinned at him. “Alright, sounds like a fun challenge. Though I don’t know about you, but I’m not exactly all that fast. We’re going to need transportation.”

As if waiting for that exact moment, the ground shook as something massive crashed into the courtyard outside of the building they were in. Asher flinched when the top of the building was ripped away effortlessly, and he stared in equal parts awe and horror at the giant, draconic head staring down at them with narrowed eyes. Smoke was drifting from its nostrils, and its mouth looked large enough to snatch him up and eat him in a single bite, if the dragon was so inclined. The head of the Frent family opened that very same mouth, but before she could incinerate him where he stood or bite him in half, Leighann clapped her hands together.

“Elnor, fantastic timing! Wait right there, I’ll get my saddle!”
