When they were done, Arthur nodded slowly, processing the new intelligence. The path ahead was clearer now, and infinitely more dangerous.

"One last thing," he said, his gaze shifting between them. "We cannot be seen leaving this room together. It would raise too many questions." He looked at Ysara, then Yudursh. "You must return to the ring."

There was no argument. Ysara gave a stiff nod of understanding. Yudursh, his face a mask of grim acceptance, simply closed his eyes. With a silent command, they vanished, leaving Arthur alone in the squalid room.

He let out a slow breath. The first part of his plan, getting his companions out of the room unseen, was complete. Now for the second part: creating a clean exit for himself. He focused his will, and the face of Kaelin melted away, replaced by the unremarkable features of Zane, the adventurer.

He reached into his ring and summoned the real Zane. The adventurer’s unconscious body materialized on the lumpy straw mattress with a soft thud. Arthur arranged him carefully, pulling a thin, stained blanket over his shoulders, making it look as though he were simply sleeping off a long night of ale and exhaustion. He then walked to the door, slid the iron bar back, and pulled it shut, leaving it unlatched. The real Zane would be found eventually, with a splitting headache and a missing day, but he would be found.

Wearing Zane's face, Arthur walked out of the boarding house. The proprietor, hunched over his morning accounts, didn't even look up. He was just another adventurer heading out for another day's hunt. No one noticed him. No one cared.

He found what he was looking for two streets over: a narrow, refuse-filled alley between two leaning tenement buildings. The air was thick with the stench of garbage and despair. It was perfect. He slipped into the shadows, consumed two monster cores to replenish the mana he'd spent on the transformations, and let the face of Zane dissolve. He then focused his will, pushing another stream of mana into the mask. The features of Kaelin formed, sharp and observant. He summoned Ysara and Yudursh. They appeared in the cramped alley, their expressions a mixture of disorientation and disgust.

Before they could speak, Arthur reached into his ring again. He produced two long, silver-hilted swords, their blades gleaming even in the dim light. They were Yudursh’s.

"You'll need these," Arthur said, his voice the quiet tone of Kaelin. He held them out to the warrior.

Yudursh stared at the blades, his expression unreadable. Taking back his own swords from the man wearing his dead friend's face was a surreal, almost profane act. But he was a protector, and a protector was nothing without his weapons. He took the swords, his knuckles white as he gripped the familiar hilts. He gave Arthur a stiff, formal nod of thanks, his jaw tight.

Arthur then handed them each a simple, hooded cloak he had looted from one of the ambushers. "Cover yourselves," he ordered. "We don't want to be recognized."

With their identities concealed, they stepped out of the alley into the chaotic maze of the outer district. The muddy streets were a confusing tangle, and for a moment, they hesitated. Ysara looked around, her expression unreadable beneath her hood. While she knew the Mayor's estate was in the inner district, navigating this sprawling slum was another matter entirely.

Yudursh, ever the pragmatist, stepped forward. He stopped a hurried-looking man whose clothes were worn but not ragged. "Pardon me," Yudursh said, his voice low and firm. "The way to the inner district?" The man, startled, pointed a grimy finger down a wider, slightly less muddy street. "Follow that road inward. Can't miss it." He then scurried away, eager to avoid any further interaction.

Following the man's direction, they began their journey. The outer district was a chaotic maze of muddy, unpaved streets. Dilapidated boarding houses leaned against each other for support, their timbers warped and stained with soot. The air was a thick soup of coal smoke, unwashed bodies, and the smell of cheap, frying meat from open-air market stalls. The faces they passed were weary and hollow-cheeked, the collared slaves a constant, grim reminder of the kingdom's foundation.

As they moved inward, the city transformed. The mud gave way to cobblestones, the crooked hovels replaced by sturdy, well-maintained buildings of timber and stone. The presence of Guardians became more prominent, their polished armor a stark contrast to the grime of the outer wards.

Finally, they reached the inner district. Here, the avenues were clean and wide, lined with ornate manors and shops displaying silks, jewels, and enchanted goods behind polished glass. The air was cleaner, scented with flowers from manicured gardens that spilled over high stone walls. The people here were well-dressed, their faces confident and untroubled, and the common slaves were replaced by liveried servants. Though they wore fine uniforms instead of rags, the polished metal collars around their necks marked them as property all the same, and they moved with a quiet, practiced deference. The city was not one entity, but two, a world of wealth built directly on top of a world of suffering.

The Mayor's estate was a small palace, a fortified manor set behind its own high walls and guarded by a contingent of soldiers in the city's livery. They were halted at the gate, their path blocked by two guards whose expressions were stern and suspicious. "State your names and your purpose," the lead guard commanded, his hand resting on the pommel of his sword.

Ysara stepped forward. Without a word, she pulled back her hood just enough to reveal her face and held out the small, silver sigil of House Valen.

The lead guard's eyes widened. His professional suspicion evaporated, replaced by a frantic urgency. "Your Highness!" he stammered, dropping into a clumsy bow. Straightening abruptly, he barked an order, and the other guards scrambled to open the heavy iron gates, ushering the trio inside with a deference that bordered on panic.

They were rushed into a private waiting room, the guards leaving them with nervous, backward glances. A few minutes later, the Mayor himself hurried in. He was a portly man in fine robes, his face slick with sweat.

"Princess Ysara," he said, bowing low. "We had heard... the King has already dispatched search parties. What happened?"

Ysara, her voice cool and regal, deflected the question. "I cannot speak of it now, Mayor. What I require is immediate and secure passage to the capital."

"Of course, Your Highness. At once," the Mayor said, his relief at not having to deal with the problem himself palpable. He led them through a set of ornate doors into a private, walled garden. With a series of complex gestures and a muttered incantation, he summoned a portal. The air shimmered, then tore open, revealing a swirling vortex of silver and blue light. Through it, they could see a guarded stone platform, and beyond it, the towering spires of a great city under a different sky. Valoria. The heart of the kingdom.
