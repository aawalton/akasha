Kalsin woke to the thin morning light sifting through the narrow opening above his bed.

He laid still, letting the world crystallize around him and slowly come into focus. Motes of dust swam in the dim half-light of the hut, the other two beds yawning empty along the walls. When he turned his head, he felt something soft tickle his chin.

A naked Ringtail laid on him, her head resting on his chest and one leg thrown over his hip. Her short hair fluttered softly as she breathed with an open mouth, looking more peaceful than he had ever seen her. Though she was tiny compared to him, her weight was curled around him enough to make it hard to move.

Kalsin let out a slow breath, too drained to do much of anything at first. He then gently slid her leg off of him, tucked her arm to her side, and slowly nudged her away by her shoulders. Ringtail stirred for a moment, mumbling something, but then rolled over to lie on her back and continue dreaming.

Kalsin propped himself up in bed, taking another bleary look around the room before his eyes returned to the woman sleeping next to him. She was pretty and, despite all the training they’d done, still softer around the edges than one would expect from a soldier. His eyes trailed down in appreciation, all the way to her belly.

Oh, hells.

Her softly rising and falling belly had a tight wrapping of cloth around it. A strip of fabric that looped around her midriff and created a barrier between him and her scar.

Kalsin watched her peaceful form in silence.

By any reasonable measure, he should have been panicking. He had completely forgotten about it last night. About scars and curses and ghosts, and all the worries that came with it. If there had been a single wrong move, if the knot around Ringtail’s chest had come loose on accident, he would be in a world of trouble right about now.

Kalsin knew all this, somewhere in a numbed part of his mind. He knew his decision last night was beyond reckless.

But—but looking down at Ringtail, all he could think about is that she did this for him.

She knew Kalsin had a phobia of scars. She didn’t want him to be afraid of touching her. Under all that brattiness and harsh words and competitive spirit… she cared.

Kalsin sharply looked away. The bed creaked quietly as he put his feet on the floor and stood. He found his boots in the far corner and his pants thrown across the other vacated bed. He paused there for a second, thinking of Shutter and how he would never again sleep here. He then shook himself out of his reverie, pulled the garment on, and went outside to greet the sunrise just appearing on the horizon.

Kalsin leaned against the doorframe of his hut, arms folded against his bare chest. It was a cloudy morning, with Warmlight painting the sky orange and purple. The row of huts stood silent and empty, and the training yard was silent in this distance. On any other morning, a few folk would be awake by now. Cyanfish by the pot. Wardoll doing her early stretches. Crawl…

Kalsin shut his eyes for a moment. The smell of smoke still lingered in the air from yesterday, but there was a hint of rain now contemplating the idea of washing it away. Kalsin squinted up at the sky again, wishing for a storm, before he reached into his pocket to find the folded piece of paper inside.

Jack’s letter was crumpled, but her handwriting was as clean as ever. The tiny script covered the pages on both sides, and something warm and tender glowed in Kalsin’s chest just looking at the thing. For someone with so few words, his Jack sure had a lot to say.

He smiled, smoothed out the letter, and started reading.

Kal,

I’m not sure how to start this, so I’ll just tell you right out of the gate: being Clenn sucks.

Well, correction—being Clenn in the army sucks. The regiment’s a joke in some ways and completely fucked in others. We’re sorted and trained separately based on tier, strength, and ‘aptitude for advancement’—which sounds like something a bloated recruiter came up with after choking on his own ego. It’s not like I can wish any harder to turn abyss. And sure, they showed us proper ways to meditate, but beyond that it would be nice if the drills actually worked on things you could, you know, improve, via good ol’ practice and repetition.

But they don’t. Not really. We have an hour’s worth of daily weapon training, and my shield-form’s gotten worse, I swear. These past few weeks I’ve felt more like a clumsy brick wall than anything useful.

Oh. I’m also a root now. So there’s at least something of a consolation prize.

I hope things are better over at the Scarrd division. I don’t know. Maybe it’s always been like this. Or maybe the system rotted sometime in the last few years and no one bothered to clean it all up. All I know is, if one more fella greets me by asking what kind of surge I’ve got, I might actually knock some teeth out.

Anyway. I didn’t start this letter just to complain, though you know I could fill a bloody tome with that alone. There's just been... time, lately. More of it than I know what to do with. Sitting alone in my tent, marching and staring at the sky like it’s going to spell out answers. Gives a person room to think. And when you think long enough, the past likes to crawl back up on you, digging up things you never said out loud.

So, here’s one of those things: I’m sorry.

I never meant to force my scar on you. Gods, I didn’t even understand what the hell I was looking at. When the bolts started to fly, my only thought was to get between you and those crossbowmen, and then you were convulsing on the ground beneath me and I thought you were hit, and then I took you in my hands and I saw the scar—my scar—materialize on your face, and then the guards came to drag us apart, and…

Everything just happened so fast.

Fuck. Even as I’m writing this, I have to reach up and cup my jaw to confirm that I’m not dreaming. Those long years of hunting for a cure… when all I ever wanted was right in front of my eyes the whole time.

And no—I’m not talking about anything mystical. I’m talking about you, Kal.

I love you.

Been the case for some time now. I love how perceptive you are, and how you see things that others don’t. I love how you always try to guess my dumb gestures without judgement, without showing that I inconvenience you… and even if your guess ain’t exactly what I meant, we just shrug and laugh and roll with the stupidity of it all.

Throughout these years, there was not a single time, not ONCE, where you turned around and said; ‘what the hell’s she miming about’. You looked at me. You saw me. Past the physical hurdles we both have and into the parts that truly matter.

Being with you is never—never—a reminder of what I’m lacking. And you’re the only person who, in the long run, ever made me feel that way.

I still remember when I made my first proposal. And you accepted, you damned fool! Despite knowing that scars make you sick, you gave us an attempt. We kept trying for weeks, we did. You puked. A lot.

I did not take it personally. I know I’m damned fucking ravishing, and I know you tried despite your body’s protests.

…

It was funny.

Sue me.

So this is my confession, now that I no longer have a scar. Now that you can kiss me without that ugly thing standing between us. The thing I could only dream of is finally possible: for us to be together as a man and a woman.

If… if you’ll have me.

Hah, look at me go. I’m getting all misty-eyed. Not the reaction you expected, eh? The thing is, I always knew you were special. Your gorgeous silver eyes, your strange way of looking at the world, and some… events happening around you that were hard to explain. There are some things that I’m purposefully not saying here. It’s not the time and not the place for that, I don’t think—it probably has to do something with your mysterious talent. You’ve already been through so much, and I don’t want to add another bucketload of trouble to all that. Later. There will be time for that later.

Hells. I can only imagine what it felt like—waking up with that fresh scar burned into your face. Crushing doesn’t even begin to cover it. You probably blamed yourself, didn’t you? Told yourself you should’ve seen it coming. Should’ve done something differently. And then you bottled it all up, like always, letting the guilt gnaw at you. Knowing that a more selfless fella—the kind they write stories about—would’ve spent his days healing others, even if he knew that there’s no light at the end of the tunnel for heroes.

I mean, the ability to cleanse Scarrd? To steal their scars onto your own body with a single touch? Oh, the spectacle that would follow. A new hope. A revolution. So many consequences that it’s practically impossible to even think about them all.

And yet… here we are.

You probably expect some sage wisdom from your bodyguard right about now. Do this in such a way, do that like so, weighing the pros and cons on the great majestic scale of morality.

On one side, there is foolish heroism. A spark in the dark that will spawn a bonfire underneath the Ember Republic. It will burn bright and sear itself into the pages of history books. A target on your back. A thousand scars you’ll drown in. Wives of your station and the admiration of millions. And likely, a quick and bloody ending for us both.

The other side is a cynical beast. It preserves the self over the will of the many. We could run away; away from the Republic, away from the Veiled, fleeing north past the Olna and Kartwist until we’re far enough away from the greedy arm of the Civil House to be able to find a quiet life for ourselves. We would bathe in the cold mountain rivers and tell stories until dawn, reminiscing about those busy days we’ve left behind.

These are the only two options we have. You’re a smart fella—smarter than me, that’s for certain, so you have probably figured this all out. And now, you’re waiting for me to give you that final push toward one of ‘em.

No.

I don’t have the answer for this one, and I won’t pretend that I do. What I want from you, Kalsin… is to choose.

Because anything is better than what we have right now.

Ask yourself: do I want to be in the same place I am right now five years from now? Ten? Letting that bitter silence eat your soul piece by piece, it’s not a way to live. Oh, I know all about the bitter silence. Anything is better than that.

I hate to imagine you in a limbo state. Kal. Kalsin. My Kalsin. You always shone the brightest when you picked a course, hashed out a plan—and acted. Doesn’t matter in which direction.

I want you to fly free, love.

Be fast, or be reckless, or be devious—hells, be scared, if that’s what it takes—but move. I’ll follow you wherever you go. I always have.

Come prepared to that hook-shaped rock between your barracks and the Clenn camp. We’re leaving this hellhole tomorrow at midnight.

I’ll be waiting for you.

Yours, no matter what comes next,

Jack.
