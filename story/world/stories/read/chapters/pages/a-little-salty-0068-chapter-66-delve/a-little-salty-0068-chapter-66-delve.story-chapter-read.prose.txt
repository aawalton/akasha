We make it to the lindwyrm’s den right on schedule. The area doesn’t look much different from the rest of the desert; I’d have walked right past if we didn’t have Álvaro insisting we’d reached our destination. The supposed den is among a cluster of boulders just ahead of us.

“Are you sure?” I ask Álvaro, eyeing the rocks skeptically. “That doesn’t seem big enough for a den.” Granted, they’re pretty big boulders, but I don’t see how more than one lindwyrm could hide behind them.

“It’s not,” Álvaro agrees. “The den is underground.”

“What?” I cry, rounding on him. “We have to go into a dragon-infested cave system?”

“Uh, yeah?” Álvaro cocks an eyebrow. “What did you think I meant when I said its den?”

“Well, I…” I hadn’t thought about it much, actually. Cyros doesn’t appear surprised by this revelation, either. I guess they both know more about lindwyrms than me. I probably should have done more research on them outside of just “how to kill.” I figured that would take care of “how to not get eaten by a lindwyrm” by extension.

Whatever. The plan hasn’t changed, now that I know we’re going to be venturing into a maze-like system of dark, dragon-infested caves. What could possibly go wrong?

Does thinking that sarcastically negate the jinx?

On the bright side, we’ve all got goggles with dark vision spells, which we’ve already been using to navigate the desert each night.

“Remember, we need an adult one,” Cyros tells us. “At least ten meters in length. So there’s no reason to kill any that don’t meet that requirement.”

“Killing them to keep from being eaten seems like a pretty good reason,” I say. Álvaro shrugs in agreement.

Cryos frowns. “I’d like to avoid any unnecessary deaths, if possible.”

Álvaro laughs. “Dude, aren’t you an assassin? What kind of assassin doesn’t want to kill?”

“Animals and people are different.” Cyros glares at Álvaro. “Some people are bastards; not all of them deserve to live. Animals are just acting in their nature—hunting for food, or defending their nest. They haven’t done anything wrong.”

“Sounds like you’ve got more respect for animals than people,” Álvaro says, amused.

“I can relate,” I mutter.

Cyros doesn’t address Álvaro’s comment. “I’ll decide which lindwyrm we’re going to hunt. Remember—part of the job requirement is to return with an intact hide. We’ll of course need to kill it, but nothing that would cause extensive damage.” He looks at me.

“Hey,” I object. “What did I do?”

“No acids,” he says.

Welp. There goes Plan A.

“And no lighting it on fire,” he adds.

And Plan B.

“And no—”

“I get it, I get it!” I sigh. “I’ll be careful.”

Cyros holds my gaze for a moment then nods. “Okay. Let’s take a break.”

“What?” Álvaro cries. “I was getting all pumped up! I hate taking breaks.”

“We’ve been walking most of the night,” Cyros says. “We’re not at our peak physical state. And lindwyrms are nocturnal, so if we go in there now, we’d be stepping into a den full of alert dragons. We’ll wait until sunrise, then sneak in while they’re sleeping.”

Álvaro nods emphatically. “Great plan. I love taking breaks.”

I’m impatient to get going, but Cyros is right to play it safe here. Besides, my entire build is based on preparation, and more time to prepare doesn’t hurt.

We set up camp on the shady side of the boulders and I take out my Apothecary to go over my prepared potions. I can comfortably fit five potions on the bandolier across my chest, and more on a belt around my waist. However, the more I wear, the more I give up maneuverability and stealth. Some of the potions glow, and I have to space all the clasps out so that the glass bottles won’t clink with each other no matter what sort of contorted gymnastic pose I might take.

“What are you thinking?” Cyros asks as he leans over my spread of potions.

I point to a bag of frost seeds. “Ice shouldn’t damage the hide, and they’ll be good for slowing the lindwyrms down.”

“You have ice magic?” Álvaro asks, peering down over my other shoulder.

“No, I have seeds that can do ice magic.” I fish one of the frost seeds out of the bag—I’ve got dozens to spare—and toss it into the middle of our camp. The second it hits the ground, ice explodes out from the impact, covering a large swath of ground.

“A.O.E.!” Álvaro cries, excitedly leaning down to run a hand over its surface.

“AOE?” Cyros repeats, baffled.

Álvaro flops onto the ice and spreads out his arms with a content sigh. “Where were you hiding this the whole trip? The heat’s been brutal!”

“They’re for fighting, not cooling down,” I say. “If I’d told you at the beginning of the trip, you’d be demanding we use them every day. And I don’t have the ingredients out here to make more.”

“Boooo, party pooper.” But Álvaro is grinning, sweeping his arms and legs apart like he’s trying to make snow angels. But without any snow.

I shake my head at him, trying not to smile myself. “Tell you what, if I have any left over after the lindwyrm, we can use some on the way back.”

“Wooo!” Álvaro pumps his fists into the air. “That’s what I’m talking about!”

Apart from the frostseeds, I grab invisibility potions, sleeping potions, health potions, and a smoke bomb.

“That will be more useful than one of your poisoned smoke bombs,” Cyros tells me. “Poisoned gas is just as likely to affect Álvaro and me as it is to affect a lindwyrm. But they have a sensitive sense of smell, so anything that can disrupt that will be useful.”

I add some poisonous orchid sap to my belt anyway; it would probably need to go straight into one of the dragon’s mouths to do much damage, given how big they are, but at least it’s unlikely to affect one of the boys.

As Cyros and Álvaro also prepare and rest, I move off to the side to play with my felis claws.

Wearing them on both hands will make it difficult to grab any potions without stabbing myself. And my left hand isn’t really good for grabbing stuff in the first place. I decide to leave my right hand claw-free to grab potions and knives as needed, while I attach the claws to my left hand.

With time still left to kill, I once again take out the refiner. The little black marble looks so innocuous. It’s hard to believe there’s some kind of entity in there. I’ve been feeding it my excess mana at the end of each night, when we’re preparing for bed, but so far I haven’t noticed much of a difference. I can still feel a hint of something when I press my finger against the crack, but I’m not sure it’s any stronger than it was before. I’m sure my naturally low mana capacity isn’t helping anything. Maybe if I have some leftover mana potions after the fight, I’ll try giving one to the remnant to see what happens.

Of course, I do have another way of gaining more mana, I just haven’t ever used it. In fact, I’ve so far made it a point not to use it. But Álvaro’s words keep rattling around in my head. Maybe using the Noxious Gauntlet doesn’t have to be some sort of defeat of my independence. Maybe I can make this weapon my own.

I imagine activating the gauntlet over the top of my felis claws, and the bracelet around my wrist stirs. I let go of the thought, and the bracelet settles back onto my arm. Instead, and somewhat impulsively, I slot the refiner on the back of my felis claws, then use some leather to strap it in place. I test my flexibility, and the claws feel as snug as ever. I try to knock the refiner out, but it stays fixed in place.

I’m not sure this is a good idea. Hell, I’m not sure it will even work. But I have a hunch.

#

Dawn comes soon enough. I wasn’t able to get much sleep in, too amped up over the upcoming mission, but I take a Stamina potion—or Glorified Energy Drink, as I like to call them—and am ready to go. I pass Stamina potions out to Cyros and Álvaro, too, then pass around some specialty potions I prepared for this trip.

The first is a variant of an Invisibility potion. Specifically, it makes our smell invisible. Lindwyrms have poor eyesight but a sharp sense of smell, so Cyros worked with me on developing a potion that will help obscure our scent. Like Invisibility potions, I’ve packaged it as a smoke bomb, since it needs to be applied to the outsides of our bodies. Álvaro squeezes his eyes tight—and mouth, when I tell him to stop smiling—and I pop the cork off the potion. A white cloud billows from the bottle, engulfing us in seconds.

[You have been subjected to a Scent Masking spell,] Echo reports. [Time remaining: 29 minutes.]

The fact that we’re all still visible makes me nervous, but Echo’s assurance that the potion is working helps. Nothing smells different to me, but humans don’t have a great sense of smell to begin with; I’ll just have to trust that it’s as effective as Echo claims.

The second potion sharpens our hearing. This is one I’ve used on a couple missions before now, so I’m used to the way every brush of cloth and scuff of a boot sounds magnified. Cyros drinks it without comment, while Álvaro winces after taking his.

Finally, I reluctantly pass around my Strength potion. It took me four weeks to collect all the ingredients for this one. I’d been saving it for a special occasion, and am loath to use it up now. But by the time any of us will need a boost in strength, it will probably be too late to call timeout and pause for a drink.

When we’re done, everything goes back into my Apothecary, and the Apothecary goes back into my Inventory. We look around at each other in silent understanding. Then we head into the dragons’ den.

Cyros scoped out the area a few hours ago, so he leads us straight to the entrance. Tucked between three giant boulders is a crevice in the ground. It might have been a natural cave at one point, but the entrance has been smoothed over time by countless scales sanding away all the coarse edges. A strong and bitter animal musk wafts from a hole that’s ten feet wide. I don’t like to think about what that means for the size of the creature we’re about to encounter.

I down my nightvision potion, and Cyros and Álvaro pull their artificed goggles down over their eyes. Together, we step into the dark.

It’s a steep but manageable slope down. I rely on Soft Step to avoid kicking pebbles and crunching gravel beneath my boots. Cyros is always quiet, and continues to be so here; it’s Álvaro’s stealth that surprises me, as I didn’t think he was capable of doing anything subtly. Then again, with a Role like Monster Hunter, it makes sense he’d have some assassin-adjacent skills.

Cyros abruptly stops, and Álvaro and I freeze behind him. I hold my breath, and my heartbeat pulses throughout my body. The dryad tilts his head to the side, waits, then signals for us to move once more. I let out my breath, slow and silent. We continue to descend.

As we walk, I nervously finger the Invisibility smokebomb on my belt. We didn’t use it as a group because it would prevent all of us from being able to see each other. On the surface that didn’t sound like a problem to me, but after Cyros pointed out how difficult it would be to fight alongside each other while invisible—without accidentally stabbing one another—I reluctantly agreed to remain visible. Even so, I left a spare on my belt, just in case, and it’s getting harder to resist using it by the minute.

The ground starts to level off around the same time the tunnel splits. We pause, considering our paths; I try to Check down each tunnel, but Echo isn’t able to pick up on anything, and neither look distinctly different to me. After a moment of consideration, Cyros signs, “Left.”

I pause to uncork a potion, and a bright, green light floods from the bottle. I squint against the light—doubly bright from the nightvision potion I took—and then tip it over, pouring some of the draught out on the ground. I trace a rough arrow pointing back the way we came, then cap the potion once more. It’s a simple potion; just a basic illusion spell that will last for a couple hours before fading out. But it will ensure we can find our way back out through the maze of tunnels when we need to leave. I nod to Cyros, and we start down the passage.

We’re barely a dozen paces in when something scrabbles against the rocks ahead of us. A blur of motion and a writhing shadow appears at the end of the passage. Cyros jumps back and Álvaro darts to the side. The twisting coils of a wyrm tumble down the path toward us, punctuated by growls and chirps. It’s heading our way fast—too fast. There won’t be enough time for us to get out of the way.

Then I see it: two sets of claws tangled in the snake-like coils, and the hint of an alligator-like snout. Wait, and a second snout—

I throw myself up against the wall, pressing as flat as I can manage. The wrestling lindwyrms tumble past me, a tail slapping against the wall only inches away and sending a cascade of rubble to the floor. The lindwyrms continue to grunt and snap at each other as they fight, oblivious to their surroundings. In a matter of seconds they’re down the curve of the tunnel and out of sight, only their echoing chirps and the scrape of claws on stone any indication that they’d been here it all.

Álvaro and Cyros are pressed up against the opposite wall, sharing bewildered looks. Then Álvaro cracks a grin, laughing silently as he mimes wiping sweat off his forehead. Really? Even this he finds funny? Does the guy take anything seriously?

The three of us hop down, drifting back toward the center of the tunnel. I gesture the way the lindwyrms went. “No see us?” I sign to Cyros. I haven’t been able to learn many signs in the time I’ve been in the Blackcloaks, but Echo can translate everything Cyros signs, so I’ve picked up a few words here and there.

He nods, replying in signs as well. “Didn’t smell us. Plus, they were young and playing. The next one might not be distracted.”

Those were young? That was a playfight? Granted, I only caught a glimpse, given how tangled up they were, but they at least looked big enough to swallow me whole if they’d really wanted to. And they’d been moving so fast!

I swallow down my nerves, mentally chastising myself. Come on, I knew what I was getting into here. And I’ve fought champions! I’ve faced gods. A nest of wingless dragons is nothing in comparison. Since when have I been such a scaredy cat?

In the silence of the tunnels, as we start moving once more, words Gugora once said drift back to me. “There’s nothing wrong with feeling scared,” he’d said after I begged to go on a hunting mission with him, declaring my fearlessness. “Feeling scared is a survival instinct. Don’t let it paralyze you, but also don’t ignore it. It might save your life.”

I’d rolled my eyes at the advice at the time. Now it just makes my chest squeeze in an unpleasant way. Weirdly, though, I think I’m starting to understand what he meant. Being in the Blackcloaks has taught me to pay closer attention to my senses; stay in tune with my body and listen to my instincts. Fear is my body trying to tell me something, and Cyros once told me the most common way assassins died was by discounting information that could have saved their life. Maybe it’s okay to be afraid. At least, just a little.

The air grows colder as we descend, and the tunnels split three more ways. The musky scent grows stronger, invading my nostrils and choking my throat. I try to breathe through my mouth instead to mute the smell, but then I can taste it, and that’s almost worse. From the way Cyros is grimacing and Álvaro is covering his mouth, I’m not alone. But where are all the lindwyrms? Besides those two young ones, we haven’t come across any—and why is the smell getting so bad when there haven’t been signs of anything else living down here?

Ahead of us, the passage vanishes into darkness. I squint and blink, wondering if my nightvision spell is glitching. Does the tunnel just end in a wall of black rock? We creep cautiously closer.

As we approach the darkness, my perspective shifts. It’s not a wall, it’s an opening: the entrance to a cave. As I edge up to the hole, the lindwyrm stench grows stronger. Shapes within the shadows begin to take form, and a quiet hush of movement and breathing rises from the dark.

The cavern is enormous. And coiled around the stalagmites that decorate the cave like rows of teeth is a nest of lindwyrms.

Hundreds of them.
