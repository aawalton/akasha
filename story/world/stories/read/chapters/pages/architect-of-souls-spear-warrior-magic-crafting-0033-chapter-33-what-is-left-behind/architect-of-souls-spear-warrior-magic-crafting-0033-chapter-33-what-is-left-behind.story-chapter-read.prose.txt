Morning came sooner than Ferris would’ve liked, but he managed to get himself up in time for breakfast. Sitting at the table, waiting for Mother Lily to bring out the food, Ferris decided to test Jade’s memory again. He should’ve tested it before bed, but he’d been too tired and forgotten.

Confusingly, Jade still remembered everything. Every number he tried, plus the names of fish and delvers, the slime recited perfectly. Even Ferris’ family name, which he hadn’t asked as often as he should have.

Ferris stopped testing Jade, leaving some numbers for later, but he wondered if this was the correct approach. He’d recorded these numbers almost an entire day ago, just a few hours after breakfast. That meant Jade remembered them longer than any of the previous times.

He didn’t understand, but wrote down the time and gave Jade a new set of numbers. For now, he would keep collecting data until a new clue appeared. He had about fifty written down when the food arrived, and Jade excitedly called to be fed.

Breakfast was fried bread piled high with a sweet fruity sauce. It was a bit hard to feed Jade, as the sauce topping was too sticky to touch, and the slime would’ve eaten any fork he used. In the end he pushed pieces off the side of the plate on top of Jade.

After eating, they set off for the third district. Hemon had given him directions, and a bit of information about what to expect. Spirit gem refinement was a spiritual process, so he would need to cultivate.

The third branch of Clan Hollowfield specialized in enchanting. They worked closely with the second branch next to them, which focused on physical crafting such as forging, woodworking, leatherworking, and more. A magical item often needed work done both physically and spiritually, but crafters usually specialized in one aspect.

Magical items were an expensive necessity. A bonded item would always be the core of a delvers’ toolkit, but it wasn’t enough. For example, Serena was bonded to a bow, but she likely carried a melee weapon to support it. Armor, consumables, and utility items were also common.

New delvers would often use mundane equipment, but replace it as soon as possible. Something made from magical materials, like the chain currently wrapped around Jade’s core, was the first step. Then, when they could afford it, delvers would purchase fully enchanted gear.

Ferris was looking forward to discovering how it all really worked, and not just hints from stories. It was a strange feeling, as back home he likely wouldn’t have had any opportunities to learn more for a long time…

Clan Bluereef’s martial tradition intentionally had a narrow focused, as anything more would spread themselves too thin. Or, so he’d been told. Individual delvers often pursued personal interests, but only in addition to the clan’s core teaching. The clan still needed magic items, but they primarily traded for them or relied on delvers that had branched out.

In contrast, Clan Hollowfield had cultivators and traditions that devoted themselves entirely to crafting. They bonded items best suited to their profession, such as a hammer for a smith. The form was important, as forging magical items required specific abilities.

The entire idea felt strange to Ferris. Why would they choose not to focus on delving? Did the clan force them into it? Maybe they were like me, he thought, desperate.

Ferris didn’t know. No one focused on crafting back home. At least, Ferris hadn’t heard of any new delvers doing it. At what point would a bonded spear give crafting abilities? He knew there were a few older cultivators that provided the clan with necessities, like the water breathing potion Ferris had used to leave home.

At least his uncle had given Ferris some knowledge before sending him off to Scalefort. Mostly about common mistakes to avoid, like investing too heavily in his gear and not himself.

While magical weapons, armor, and other items were necessary to delve… they were also temporary. Magical items required the same precious resources used in cultivation, but they didn’t grow stronger with the owner. In comparison, using those resources internally or for a bonded item would give permanent benefits and advancement.

Even worse, Uncle Winton had warned him that enchanted items with abilities require additional resources to make them usable by the standard cultivator. Bonded weapons inherently had a connection, while magical items need something to provide the same link. Jade’s chain didn’t require this, as it was only stronger than normal; there was no enchanted ability to connect to. If he wanted a weapon with an attack ability—and he did—it would require comparatively more resources than an equivalent bonded weapon ability.

The third district wasn’t far, as it was adjacent to Lily House’s location in the fourth. While the fourth district was built around the library, the third had no central focal building. Instead, it had large offices arranged neatly along the central walkway. The third and fourth districts were both part of Hollowfield, but there were clear aesthetic differences. The research branch had simply numbered each office, but the enchanting branch instead had a stone statue of a beast guarding each one. Hemon had instructed Ferris to go to the Ox building.

Ferris and Jade found the correct statue, after having passed the Pig, Rabbit, Monkey, and Tiger buildings. Each statue was dramatically posed, pridefully displayed, and clearly well cared for. With a bit of trepidation, he approached, curious what the job would entail.

Entering the Ox building, Ferris spoke to an attendant waiting just inside the entrance. After hearing his name and purpose, the man directed them to a room down the hall and promised to call someone to assist. Upon entering, the room was vacant, with a small table, two chairs, and cot.

Ferris settled down to wait at the table, set Jade on top of it, and tested the slime’s memory once more. Furthering his confusion, the slime had forgotten all the remaining numbers and names from yesterday. At least they still remembered his family name.

Ferris was trying to think of what else he could test with Jade’s memory, when a brief knock came from the door. Ferris opened it to find a short, balding man with a large mustache carrying a box.

“Ah, Adept Ferris Bluereef I presume?” The man asked.

Ferris nodded and stepped aside, motioning to welcome the man into the room.

“Yes, and this is my familiar, Jade,” Ferris gestured to the slime rolling around on the table.

“I am Adept Theodore White of Hollowfield, the manager for the spirit gem refinement program.” He set the box down on the table and sat down, “Please, take a seat and we can get started.”

Jade rolled over to investigate the box. Ferris had grown so accustomed to sending a continuous stream of visual information over the bond, he didn’t think about it anymore. He quickly told the slime to not eat the box, although he doubted they would have anyway. While Jade was a glutton, they had only once refused to listen: when Ferris had eaten the Core Tree’s fruit in the slimespace. If Ferris could just figure out the memory problem, his life would be a lot easier—and safer.

“It is a pleasure to meet you, Adept Theodore,” Ferris said, sitting down. The man had introduced himself as ‘Theodore White of Hollowfield.’ That meant he wasn’t technically a full member of the clan, but was more than a simple retainer. Likely, the man had made lifelong commitments to be a part of the clan, but hadn’t married in.

The family name of ‘White’ was about as generic as could be. Ferris didn’t want to be too judgmental, but it was a boring choice for a new couple to pick on their wedding day.

Regardless, Adept Theodore’s standing within the clan was above Ferris’, and he was here to do a job.

Theodore opened the box and removed a familiar disk. It looked remarkably like the jade slip reading device at the library, but the slot in the center had a different shape. The man then placed an iridescent cloudy yellow gem in the slot and clamped it down. The gem was uncut, and had a muddy haze to it; yet it also shone with an unnatural light. He pulled a small lever, and the clamp pressed down on the gem.

“Now, may I ask, do you have experience with spiritual gate devices?”

“I have used the jade slip reader at the library,” Ferris said, “is this similar?”

“Yes,” Theodore nodded, “This is known as a refinement device,” he continued, “it will grant access to any spirit gem secured within the slot.” With a finger, Theodore tapped on the yellow gem clamped in. “The device will also have tools within to aid the refinement process. Once finished, a new gem will be provided. Any questions?”

Ferris looked at the device, interested.

“The spirit gem refinement process, could you elaborate on it more?”

For the first time since entering the room, Theodore lost his stride. He had to take a moment to respond, glancing down at Jade, but then began speaking—albeit a bit slower than before. Ferris guessed that the man had expected to explain the refinement device, but assumed he knew refinement?

“Spirt gem refinement is a process of removing polluted material from the spirit of a magical gem. Clean out anything that is not crystal, without causing any damage. Some breakage is inevitable, but if the gem grade drops then you will be charged the value.”

“This… pollution,” Ferris asked, “what should I do with it?” He’d never had to dispose of anything in the soulspace.

“It is sufficient to move it away from the crystals.”

“What is it?”

“The gems are pulled from the corpses of monsters. The monster will have developed spiritually around the gem; that doesn’t just disappear when they die, although it does atrophy and wither.”

Ferris wasn’t sure what to think of that, glancing over at Jade. The slime had never cultivated before being bonded, yet the slimespace had somehow developed. Was gem refining just cleaning out a monster’s spiritual corpse?

“One bit of warning,” Theodore waited until Ferris looked at him, and had undivided attention, “there is a chance of an echo of resentment. This can create hostile constructs in the spirit, attacking while the refinement process is underway.”

Ferris smiled internally at this, although he tried not to show it. He had learned the hard way that nowhere was fully safe the day he’d lost his leg, but he didn’t want the man to think he was snubbing the advice.

“I will stay on guard.”

“Now,” Theodore continued, back on track, “this is technically a job assignment with a quota, but it isn’t like other positions that are paid on commission. As your manager, I set a required number of gems to refine each workday based on your ability. I understand this isn’t fair for those that can work faster, as I will simply raise the quota they must meet. Frankly, if you get to the point where this work is not challenging, we encourage you to move on to another job.”

Ferris nodded, a bit confused. It felt like they didn’t care how many spirit gems he refined each day, just that he did as many as he could. That was odd, as Hollowfield wasn’t a charity. If the work was less valuable, he’d expect them to pay less for it.

“May I ask why it is setup this way?” Maybe the man would just explain.

“No,” Theodore said, “if you’re curious, you will have to figure it out on your own.”

Ferris tried not to roll his eyes. Well, it hadn’t hurt to ask, but somehow he wasn’t surprised. At least he was fairly sure the man actually wanted Ferris to ‘figure it out on his own,’ and wasn’t just being a jerk. He had heard the line often enough to tell the difference.

“Now, I am starting your quota for a standard work shift at four grade D gems. After finishing, you are free to either refine more—for additional merit—or leave. If you are unable to fully refine all four within the allotted time, or one of your gems is found lacking, you will be allowed to return on a later day to complete the shift. Once a gem is refined, you may exchange it in the room down the hall for a new one.”

“How will I know when a gem is refined?”

“There are several techniques and methods for verifying the clarity of a gem—and an Adept will verify all gems each evening—but it is usually good enough to simply clear as much as possible. The gem’s clarity will also undergo a qualitative change.”

Ferris didn’t like the idea that he had no accurate way to tell when the job was complete. He may unknowingly spend longer than necessary. Perhaps once he has some practice, he could gradually do less work until the Adept rejects a gem, confirming the limit.

“Three shifts must be completed each week to maintain good standing. Any beyond that are at your discretion but will not count towards the future. It is encouraged to complete them early, to avoid mistakes.”

The last part was said with a pointed look. There were three days left this week, and three shifts to do. Ferris winced, but he didn’t feel too bad. He’d had his own problems, after all.

“Thank you, Adept Theodore,” is what he said, “I will endeavor to complete my shifts early, when possible.”

“Good,” the man nodded, “now, it is understood that cultivators will sometimes need to enter long periods of private meditation,” he continued. “While we make certain allowances for this, it is expected that every Adept at Hollowfield can manage their cultivation to also meet their obligations. For this job, that means that you need to speak to me beforehand, with at minimum three weeks’ notice. The two of us will discuss a plan so that you can complete your quota beforehand. If you don’t give me enough warning, or even worse you simply disappear without notice, we will still make a payback plan, but there will be harsh penalties.”

Ferris nodded back. Adepts were given a lot of special consideration to account for the struggles in their soul, but no one wanted to work with someone they couldn’t depend on. It likely mattered less for a job like this, but Ferris wouldn’t want to be on a delving team with people that often got stuck in meditation without warning.

“Last, I will periodically evaluate your progress, and update your quota. Please don’t try to hide your capability from me to keep your requirements low. It never works, and the conversations are embarrassing.”

Theodore stood and moved towards the door.

“Adept Ferris, I wish you the best in this role. Please inform me immediately of any problems.”

“Thank you, again, for the guidance, Adept Theodore.”

Ferris closed the door after Theodore left, then turned to look at the gate device. What does it really look like, inside the corpse of a monster’s spirit? He wondered. Is it like my soulspace, or as elaborate as Jade’s slimespace?

Well, it was time to get to work.
