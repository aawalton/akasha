“More statues,” I muttered, looking into the distance.

We had swum by a dozen so far, not counting all the potential insects that were swarming the underground. The monster using the Stoneweaver card was unquestionably skilled. Thankfully, it was also arrogant.

Splitting its focus among hundreds of stone puppets guaranteed that its attention was largely reduced. Ever since the unfortunate encounter with the initial guardian, we had done a good job not being noticed. That was one of the things that bothered me. Even an idiot could tell that we were using the canal, and yet nothing had been done about it. No traps, no creatures, not even anything that would set off a warning as we passed by.

“That’s the path to the temple,” Eleonor whispered back. “It means we’re close.”

After our recent chat, the goth had agreed to share all the information she could once we reached the seer. While somewhat appealing, that didn’t help in the short run. All that she claimed to know was that our target was locked up in the underground part of an ancient temple. Supposedly, there were only a handful of guards watching over her. If they were anything like the ones I had seen so far, I didn’t see us succeeding.

“There’s a gate that goes into the water.” I focused using my Eagle Eye. “No signs of corrosion. It must be made of magical metal.”

“Take out the guards,” she said.

At first, I thought the comment was sarcastic. One glance at Eleonor told me it wasn’t.

Only a rookie would come up with such an idea. In a way, it was flattering that she thought I could take two statues, though that had a lot to say about her ability to realistically assess the situation. The pair of giant guardians stood on both sides of the canal, ensuring that no one could pass through. With some effort I could chuck one of them into the water, though at the expense of the other raising the alarm.

“How long can you hold your breath?” I asked.

“A minute or two.”

That wasn’t enough. If I had the option to rip the breathing ring from my ribs and give it to her, I would have. Even if we were to assume that she could last two minutes, that wasn’t enough time for her to let us pass by the statues unnoticed.

“What deal did you make with the seer?” I changed my approach.

“I told—”

“Do you have to be there?”

Her eyes widened. I couldn’t tell whether it was fear emanating from them or surprise.

“If it’s really close, I can reach her unseen,” I quickly added. “You can’t.”

There was no way she’d like to hear this, but it was the truth. Without the backing of her deity, her powers were severely limited, and as it had just been proven, she wasn’t in a condition to handle the statues on her own.

“I’m not risking a fight,” I stressed. “You’re not strong enough. And neither am I.”

The last sentence managed to appease her a bit. As an oracle, she had to know I was telling the truth. Having everything seemingly handed to her, could she accept it, though?

“Just that we’d free her,” she relented. “The arrangement was that I’d set her free from her dungeon and she’d tell me who our target was.” There was a pause. “She also agreed to answer a few other questions as well.”

“Are the questions worth it?”

“No.” There was no sigh, no arguing, no passive-aggressive comments. “Can you really manage?”

“I stand a better chance alone.”

Hey! I’ll be with you! Serp argued in my head.

“Nothing’s guaranteed during a trial,” I added. “There’s always the chance that I die.”

I meant that to sound like a joke. It didn’t.

“Don’t die,” Eleonor said.

With a nod, I swam to the nearby canal wall, then cast a few fire circles. As a rule, water and fire didn’t mix. Creating a few circles, however, created an interesting effect. While no flames ever came into being, the circle heated up the water in that particular area.

“That should last for a while,” I told Eleonor. “Stay here. If you see anything approaching, hide.”

She nodded.

Just leave her, Serp said. We didn’t get any spheres out of this.

“I’ll be sure to keep that in mind,” I muttered beneath my breath as I submerged.

It didn’t take long for me to reach the gate. As I expected, it was rather sturdy, made of a material I didn’t recognize. I took out a divine blade and tried to scrape part of the bar off. No result. Whoever had made this made sure that the metal was on par with dark silver.

If that’s the way you want it. I cast a spell circle between the bars.

Among the few good things I had learned at school back before this whole thing started, it was that very little would withstand the force of nature. Ice had the scary property of breaking and bending almost any container, no matter how sturdy. These bars proved no exception. I strongly doubted ice barriers were meant to be used in such a fashion, but through enough persistence they got the job done. After half a minute, the distance between the bars was large enough to let me slip through.

I ended the spell, causing the chunk of ice to shatter into nothing.

One down, I thought.

As I moved forward, the canal changed, splitting into multiple smaller segments. Judging by the size, I could only imagine what the city above had been when it existed. No one built this amount of infrastructure unless they had to.

I took out the blades again. They were pulling me forward. Something strong was here. If I were lucky, it was just some Major Arcana guard and not the Stoneweaver.

Back in my previous run, Avon would often tell Pup about the importance of map-making. While the majority of the trials remained brainlessly simple, good maps were a means of finding hidden caches or alternative trial solutions. The kid had taken the conversations to heart, for at some point he had traded half the gathered cards in order to acquire the Mapmaker. At the time, I considered it a total waste. Now, I wished I had such a card.

You’ve been through here before, Serp said, annoyed.

I know. I’m just trying to get a general feel of the place.

Why?

It was scary how similar to me he was. Five years ago, when I had survived enough to become cocky, I thought exactly like him. In my young, arrogant mind, the distance between two points was always a straight line, and anything that didn’t fall on it was useless.

So, there aren’t any surprises.

Several “islands” of structures were scattered between the smaller canals. Here and there, hidden guardians stood, masquerading as pieces of decoration. The Stoneweaver had done a good job ordering his minions to blend in with their surroundings. An unsuspecting person could easily mistake them for statues. There was no fooling Harlequin’s Sight, though.

One glance and I knew exactly what I was facing.

After a while, I found the exact spot my divine blades were directing me to. Just as I suspected, it was a dungeon cell, though nothing like I had initially imagined.

A solid structure of black marble rose to the ceiling. Eight majestic columns filled the space, forming an octagonal shape with bars of glowing adamantium in-between. I had only seen the material twice in my previous run. It had composed the armor of a nasty creature called the hermit beetle. Dozens of participants had died encountering it, yet Avon had managed to convince enough of the group that the material could prove invaluable in future trials. He had been right, though getting it had cost another seven veterans. In the end, I hadn’t used any of it, choosing to trade my share for a card.

Any chance you could cut through that? I asked Serp.

No problem, he said in a tone that made me imagine him shrugging. After a few hundred spheres.

From what I could see, there were eight statues of red marble guarding the spot. Each was a few feet from a column. They weren’t my greatest concern. After a while of circling about, I finally saw what was drawing my blades.

Dead Sea Sentinel

Level: 20

Cards: Paladin

Specials: Swimming, Pain Endurance, Poison Blood

Weapon: Shark Saber

Aren’t you a long way from home? I wondered.

This wasn’t the sort of trial for mer-creatures. A mix between a siren and a triton, the entity was close to seven feet tall, with the general anatomy of a human. There were no webbed feet or gills on his neck, but he didn’t need them. The innate specials he was born with ensured that he’d have no trouble in water.

The Shark Saber concerned me. I wasn’t familiar with that sort of weapon.

Serp, think you can take him? I asked.

If it bleeds, I can kill it, he said with absolute confidence.

That was always good to know. And still, why would someone send a guard of flesh to guard a seer when statues would have done the job well enough? The creature wasn’t even a Grand Arcana holder.

I went through my options. I could try to throw Serp at him and hope that would do the trick. Chances were low. It would take me a lot of effort to jump out of the water, not to mention that he probably had all the Paladin skills.

Fighting on solid ground was also out of the question with that many statues around. It would take a lot more than restriction attacks to deal with a few of them, and by that time the rest, along with the sentinel, would have pummeled me out of existence.

Hold that thought. I put away my blades and swam down.

Carefully, I went up to the base of the prison. By rough estimates, twenty feet of wall separated me from the cell proper. Then again, such restrictions were only details when magic was concerned.

I placed both hands on the marble tiles. Just as I was about to cast a pair of magic circles, I felt the vibrations of a large splash. Not too far away, a guard statue had fallen into the water, sinking stoically to the canal floor.

Of course… I cast the spell.

Light covered the stone section as it moved to the side, forming the start of a tunnel. Ending the spell, I reached in and repeated the process. With whatever was going on above, I had no time to waste.

Bit by bit, I kept moving further and further. Every second I feared a stone hand would grab my foot, ending the attempt.

Looking briefly over my shoulder, I cast a mega spell circle. The entire tunnel floor flashed in light. A moment later, a mini-wall emerged. I was safe… at least for the moment.

Looking forward, I kept on drilling like a caterpillar through an apple. The material changed from standard marble to black. As the first spell circle covered the black surface, everything shook. My spell shattered, disrupted by the marble itself. Would have been too easy otherwise.

Really smooth, old man, Serp chuckled. You’ve sealed yourself in a corner.

Serp, I thought as I placed both palms on the sides of the tunnel. You really need to work on your metaphors.

All the stones above me erupted as another spell circle covered the whole tunnel. Like an active fissure, the ceiling broke up, tearing apart. I had done everything in order to avoid a fight, but now that I was here, I planned to win it.

Blanking my mind, I leaped up through the formed opening. Before I even got a look at my surroundings, I drew my divine blades, unleashing two preemptive thread slashes left and right.

One of the strikes split the air, striking the wall tens of feet away. The other landed straight onto the sentinel’s left shoulder.

You were waiting for me, weren’t you? I leaped to the side.

Barely had I done so when a glowing wave of light passed inches from me.

A divine strike. It was different from the ones I had seen before—a lot more power was put in it, allowing it to cut through stone.

For fractions of a second, I followed it slice through a stone statue, then continue, create a massive groove in the wall.

“Obey!” I triggered my Emperor’s card.

Green flames covered my divine blades. Lines of burning aether split the air as I combined every attack the cards allowed me—and a few they didn’t—targeting my opponent.

Before they could dig into his body, the threads flickered out like faulty firecrackers. Even while under the partial effects of the Emperor, the Paladin’s skills protected him from magic strikes. In order to win, I’d have to get up close.

How about this? I threw my blades at his leg.

The sentinel was likely expecting this, for he stepped back. Unfortunately for him, the Emperor had made his action just sluggish enough for the weapons to slice through. Ripping the trousers, they continued on like a pin through a plastic bottle, emerging on the other side.

You’re up! I summoned Serp and charged forward.

One of Swords! I performed a power thrust aimed at the creature’s chest.

The attack was easily deflected with his own sword. The blade was heavily serrated with teeth the size of my thumb.

Come to play, little snake? A new voice sounded in my head.

At first, I thought the entity was addressing me. When Serp trembled in my hand, I realized he was the target.

What the hell? I followed up with a circular attack.

A discussion between swords? That could only mean that the sentinel’s weapon was also an epic. Where was his card, though? From all I could see, the only card in his possession was the Paladin.

Isn’t that sweet? The shark blade laughed while deflecting all my attacks. You don’t even have teeth.

Several of the shark blade’s teeth curved, sinking into Serp’s like a can opener piercing through a can. For the first time in my life, I was witnessing one living weapon bite into the other. In theory, both were supposed to be indestructible. That clearly wasn’t true.

Shit! I unsummoned the saber.

There was no point in getting him devoured. Spell circles appeared on my hands as I tried desperately to think of an alternative. With Serp in no condition to fight, I was completely unarmed.

The sentinel realized that and his mouth twisted into a victorious smirk. The tip of his blade rose up. Then, suddenly, two more blades shot out from his chest.

Both of us froze, uncertain of what had just happened. Then, the monster burst into a cloud of yellow smoke.

“My, my, isn’t this a surprise,” a female voice said, sending chills down my spine, while a card and a red booster sphere fell to the floor. “I didn’t think you cared.”
