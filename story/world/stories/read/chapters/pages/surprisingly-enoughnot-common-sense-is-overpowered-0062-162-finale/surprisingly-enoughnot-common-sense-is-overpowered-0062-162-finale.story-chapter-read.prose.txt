"I still can't believe Xiao Fan won the whole thing," Zhang Feng said. "Qiu Han, Jin Shu, Zhou Sheng, Xiao Hong... he really went through all four of them."

"He was the biggest dark horse in the competition," Wei Zhusang said.

"Well, him and Ning," Zhang Feng added. "Nobody expected either of them to get this far."

Lang Rulang glanced at Wei Zhusang. "And now you're fighting the other one."

Wei Zhusang nodded. Both he and Ning had come through the previous round with only one loss apiece, Ning by beating a disciple whose name hardly anyone remembered, so being paired together wasn't particularly surprising. Qiu Han, who had already fought Ning and was left over in the pairings, had received a bye.

Zhang Feng grinned. "People outside seem to favor Ning too. You lost to Jin Shu, while Ning managed to beat her, so I can see why they'd think he has the advantage."

Wei Zhusang gave him a flat look. "That's not much of a comparison. Ning poisoned her contracted beast and ruined their coordination. Our fighting styles are completely different, and I've already prepared countermeasures for the methods he's shown so far."

"That's fair, but if someone only looked at the results, they'd probably still favor Ning."

Wei Zhusang sighed. He had known Zhang Feng for almost ten years, which was long enough to know there was no point in getting annoyed. The man usually spoke before considering whether what he was saying might irritate someone.

"Whose side are you on, anyway? I've known you for ten years, and you're already talking as if Ning has won."

"How could I choose between my two best friends? I'm completely neutral." Zhang Feng managed to maintain a serious expression for a few seconds before grinning. "Though Ning has actually been sparring with me recently while you've been avoiding me, so I suppose I'm leaning toward him a little."

"Then you're not neutral."

"I'm neutral enough. If you want my support back, start sparring with me again."

Lang Rulang gave a quiet scoff. "Pathetic."

Zhang Feng immediately turned toward her. "How did I become the pathetic one? He's the one refusing to spar with me."

Lang Rulang didn't bother answering him.

Wei Zhusang shook his head before looking back toward the arena. "I'm more surprised by Ning's cultivation. He was still at the Fifth Stage when the competition started, wasn't he?"

"He was when I fought him," Lang Rulang confirmed. "He only broke through afterward."

"And now he's already at the Sixth Stage. I only reached it shortly before the competition myself, so I wasn't expecting him to catch up this quickly."

"Oh, that." Zhang Feng seemed to remember something. "I actually know how he advanced so fast."

That finally got both of their attention.

"Ning got his hands on an intact Dragon Qi Grass a few years ago," Zhang Feng explained. "Roots, soil, everything. Somehow, he managed to use it to cultivate more."

"I see. That would explain it," Wei Zhusang said.

Dragon Qi Grass was one of the better-known first-grade spiritual plants for cultivators in the early and middle stages of Qi Condensation. Its medicinal properties could accelerate cultivation without damaging the foundation, making it extremely valuable despite only being a first-grade plant.

The difficulty lay in growing it. Seeds were already uncommon, and even with one, Dragon Qi Grass required soil from a place where another stalk had previously grown. Artificial cultivation demanded considerable skill as well, which kept the supply low despite the constant demand.

"He got lucky with the first stalk," Zhang Feng continued, "though apparently it became troublesome afterward. Once people found out he'd successfully grown more, several tried to buy them from him and wouldn't leave him alone."

"Why haven't I heard about this?"

"Probably because Ning didn't exactly advertise it. He refused most of them, and when some became too persistent, he started using the token Elder Shen gave him to make them back off."

Wei Zhusang's expression shifted slightly. "Elder Shen? You mean the elder who guards the Scripture Pavilion?"

"That's the one. Ning helped manage the pavilion for a while, so they got acquainted. Elder Shen even gave him permission to run that stall during the competition." Zhang Feng sighed. "I really need to learn how Ning does this. Somehow, he keeps getting acquainted with useful people wherever he goes."

"That is rather impressive. At least that explains where his recent cultivation progress came from."

Ning had been fairly open about the source of his recent progress, which was deliberate on his part. If people were going to notice how quickly he was advancing, giving them a perfectly reasonable explanation was much better than letting them imagine that he had stumbled across some mysterious inheritance.

Xiao Fan might be able to suddenly break through in the middle of a tournament without anyone questioning where his progress came from, but Ning would rather have his Dragon Qi Grass, farming records, and Elder Shen's connection ready to explain everything.

Lang Rulang had remained quiet for most of the conversation, but she finally spoke. "His cultivation isn't what you should be worried about. When I fought him, he already knew far too much about me."

Wei Zhusang turned toward her. "What exactly did he know?"

"My techniques, or at least enough about them to prepare several counters before the match even began."

Wei Zhusang's expression became more serious. He had watched that fight along with Ning's later matches, so he understood what she meant.

Ning rarely entered an exchange without several options available to him.

His archery allowed him to pressure an opponent from a distance, the mist interfered with their senses, his rain spell gave him another way to control the battlefield, and he could even alter the ground beneath someone's feet.

Anyone who managed to close the distance still had to deal with his body-refining technique, spear arts, movement art, and unusually fast spellcasting.

Then there were poison, traps, and whatever else Ning happened to prepare specifically for his opponent.

And apparently shouting Magical Spear before punching someone in the ribs.

Wei Zhusang rubbed his chin. "He's troublesome. Most of the things he uses aren't overwhelming on their own, but he keeps combining them until something gives him an opening, and then he takes advantage of it."

Zhang Feng laughed. "You make that sound like some terrible habit when you fight almost the same way. Neither of you likes leaving things to chance."

"Which is exactly why I know how annoying someone like that is to fight. If Ning prepared this much for Lang Rulang and Jin Shu, there's no reason to think he hasn't done the same for me."

"Then maybe you should be worried."

"I've been watching his matches too," Wei Zhusang replied, ignoring Zhang Feng's grin. "He's already revealed quite a lot during this competition, so I'm not going into the arena blind either."

Zhang Feng leaned back in his seat. "Well, you still sound confident. I suppose I can save the consolation speech until after the match."

"You can prepare whatever speech you want. I don't plan on needing it, and if I win, I'll make you give it to Ning instead."

Zhang Feng's expression immediately became serious. "Absolutely not. I was going to make it heartfelt, and I'm not wasting that much effort twice."

That earned a small laugh from Wei Zhusang before he returned his attention to the arena.

He had watched enough of Ning's matches by now to understand why Lang Rulang considered his preparation dangerous. Ning had revealed more and more of his arsenal with every fight, however, and all that information had given Wei Zhusang plenty of time to study him in return.

More importantly, there was one particular weakness he intended to exploit.

Ning had a flaw.

...

Ning sat cross-legged in his room, a palm-sized shard of pale-blue crystal resting in his hand.

Cold mist curled from its surface. This was a five-year-old Azure Flowing Ice Essence.

He exhaled slowly and placed it into his mouth.

The moment it dissolved, a biting chill spread down his throat and into his meridians. Frosty qi surged through his channels and his breath turned faintly white.

The cold tried to settle into his bones, but Ning guided it carefully through his meridians, allowing Jade Skin and Icy Meridians to gradually absorb the essence.

As the last traces of cold qi settled within his body, he could feel that the technique had grown stronger.

Ning slowly opened his eyes.

"That should do it."

He had made all the preparations he could. Now, it was time to see whether they would be enough.

...

When Ning stepped outside with his spear resting over his shoulder, someone called out to him before he reached the arena.

"Ji Ning."

He turned and found Qiao Mei waiting nearby. She was also a member of the Mutual Aid Association, though Ning hadn't spoken with her much. Usually quiet and reluctant to involve herself in trouble, she looked unusually nervous today.

"Qiao Mei? What's wrong?"

She clasped her hands together. "About your match with Senior Brother Wei... could you surrender?"

Ning stared at her. "What?"

"Senior Brother Wei needs a high ranking. The resources he receives from his clan depend partly on his performance, so this match is important to him." Qiao Mei hesitated before adding, "If you surrender, I'm sure he'll compensate you properly."

"Did Wei Zhusang ask you to do this?"

"No!" Qiao Mei answered immediately. "He doesn't even know I'm here. Senior Brother Wei would never ask someone to surrender for him."

That sounded more like the Wei Zhusang Ning knew.

Qiao Mei continued, "He helped you before too, didn't he? When you needed spirit stones, he lent them to you. I know you repaid everything, but he still helped you when you needed it, so I thought... maybe you could return the favor."

Ning looked at her nervous expression and finally understood.

Ah. She likes him.

Unfortunately for her, Ning had no intention of surrendering. He had come too far to give up his ranking and rewards, especially for something Wei Zhusang himself hadn't even asked for.

"Qiao Mei, if I surrendered because you asked me to, do you really think Wei Zhusang would be happy about it?"

She paused. "...Probably not."

"And match-fixing is against the rules."

"I didn't mean for you to fake the fight. I thought you could just surrender."

"That would be even more suspicious."

"...Oh."

Ning almost felt bad for her.

"If you want to help him, just cheer for him. Wei Zhusang made it this far on his own, so give him the chance to finish it that way."

Qiao Mei remained quiet for a moment before nodding. "You're right. I shouldn't have asked."

"It's fine. Just don't ask anyone else to throw a match. They might report you."

Her face immediately paled. "Would they really?"

"Probably." Ning smiled at her expression. "Relax. I'm not going to."

Qiao Mei lowered her head. "I'm sorry."

"Forget about it."

She stepped aside, and Ning started toward the arena before she called after him.

"Ji Ning."

"Hmm?" Ning looked back.

"Good luck."

Ning glanced at her. "...Shouldn't you be wishing that to Wei Zhusang?"

Her face immediately turned red.

Ning laughed and continued toward the arena.

...

"Next fight, Wei Zhusang versus Ji Ning."

The two stepped onto the stage.

After the main clash between Qiu Han and Xiao Fan, much of the crowd had already dispersed. It felt as though the true highlight of the competition had passed, which, to be fair, wasn't far from the truth.

That was why Ning was surprised when several cheers suddenly came from the remaining spectators.

"Ning, you've got this! Win it!"

"Show them the power of a spiritual farmer!"

"Huh?" Ning blinked, genuinely startled.

"You seem surprised," Wei Zhusang remarked.

"I am," Ning admitted with a nod. Despite his strong record, he had rarely been in the spotlight, no matter how small that spotlight might be.

"It's because you're a commoner," Wei Zhusang explained. "Most of the techniques you use aren't particularly rare either. Your spells can be acquired by ordinary disciples, and you're a spiritual farmer on top of that. Spiritual farmers aren't exactly known for their combat ability, so seeing one of them make it this far has naturally attracted some attention."

Wei Zhusang looked faintly amused at the rare hint of awkwardness on Ning's face.

"I never thought of it that way," Ning said.

Was the Make Spiritual Farmers Great Again agenda at it again?

"Their cheers won't change anything, though," Wei Zhusang continued. "I still intend to win."

"Me too."

Ning noticed that Wei Zhusang hadn't mentioned Qiao Mei at all. Either he had been overthinking the girl's infatuation, or Wei Zhusang already knew him well enough to realize that such a request would never work.

Either way, there was nothing more to consider. He had come here to fight, so that was what he would do.

The elder raised his hand.

"Begin."

Ning moved first, his fingers forming a seal without hesitation.

[Hidden Ice Mist Technique]

Cold qi burst outward in a rolling wave as thick frost vapor spread across the arena floor, swallowing the stone beneath their feet and rapidly obscuring both fighters from view.

By now, the technique had become so closely associated with Ning that the moment white mist began spreading across the platform, the crowd immediately knew what was coming.

Wei Zhusang moved at the same time, tightening his grip around the shaft of his heavy war hammer and lowering his shoulders before charging directly into the mist. The weapon dragged briefly across the frost-covered stone, scattering sparks as qi poured into the jade embedded within its head.

Ning's bow bent in a smooth arc, and three arrows flew from the mist in a single breath, each approaching from a slightly different trajectory.

All three were coated with poison.

Wei Zhusang's eyes narrowed as the first arrow emerged from the fog. As expected, Ning could still locate him inside the mist, but Wei Zhusang didn't slow.

Qi flowed into the disc-shaped shield mounted along his forearm, a low-grade artifact he had personally forged. The disc began spinning with a low metallic hum as earthen light spread across its surface, meeting the first arrow with a sharp clang and knocking it aside.

The second came from an oblique angle, only for the rotating disc to shift and intercept it as well. The third curved sharply near the end of its flight and slipped past the shield's outer rim, but instead of piercing flesh, it struck the reinforced armor hidden beneath Wei Zhusang's robes and bounced away.

Wei Zhusang continued advancing, uncaring for the arrows. He had prepared for it all.

Ning shifted through the mist using his movement art, changing his position as he fired arrow after arrow. Some came straight, others curved or approached from awkward angles, while several were deliberately aimed low at Wei Zhusang's legs and feet.

The disc shield intercepted most of them. Those that slipped through either glanced off the armor beneath Wei Zhusang's robes or struck the reinforced boots protecting his lower legs.

Wei Zhusang had studied Ning's previous matches carefully, and he had come prepared for exactly this kind of attack. Instead of allowing the constant arrows to slow his advance, he used the openings between volleys to steadily close the distance.

If Wei Zhusang entered the effective range of that hammer, Ning wouldn't be able to trade blows with him for long. The Earth-grade hammer art gave Wei Zhusang far too much raw power, and a clean strike would probably end the match immediately.

Ordinary arrows weren't going to stop him either.

Ning changed his approach as qi gathered along the shaft of his next arrow before spiraling tightly around its head.

[Spiral Arrow]

The arrow tore through the mist.

Wei Zhusang angled his disc shield to intercept it, but the moment the arrow struck, the concentrated rotational force drove into the artifact like a drill. The disc was knocked slightly aside, allowing the arrowhead to scrape past its edge and slip through a narrow seam between the overlapping armor plates near Wei Zhusang's shoulder.

The arrow embedded itself in flesh.

Wei Zhusang staggered half a step, and the rotating disc wobbled for the briefest moment.

Ning's Pure Eyes caught the opening immediately.

Two more arrows followed. Wei Zhusang managed to knock one aside, but the second grazed another gap near his waist before disappearing into the mist behind him.

The poison had entered his body.

Wei Zhusang's breathing changed almost immediately. His hammer lowered slightly, his knees bent, and faint veins began appearing across the exposed skin of his neck as his movements grew noticeably less stable.

The spectators noticed as well.

"He got through!"

"That's the same poison he used against Jin Shu's spirit beast. Is it already over?"

Within the mist, Wei Zhusang's silhouette swayed.

Ning stopped firing.

Attacking someone who had already lost the ability to defend himself was generally frowned upon, especially when the opponent had already been poisoned badly enough to collapse, and from the reaction of the crowd, most of them seemed to believe the match was finished.

However, Wei Zhusang had watched Ning's fight against Jin Shu. He knew Ning used poison and had been given plenty of time to prepare for it.

There was no reason for someone as cautious as Wei Zhusang to simply walk into the arena without a countermeasure.

The moment Ning came to that conclusion, qi erupted from within the mist.

The inscriptions carved into Wei Zhusang's boots flared with bright earthen light, and his supposedly weakened body suddenly accelerated forward in an explosive burst, covering the remaining distance before Ning could fire another arrow.

His hammer rose.

[Mountain-Quelling Hammer Art: Heaven-Sinking Strike]

Earth-attributed qi surged beneath Wei Zhusang's feet, anchoring his stance as the hammer came down toward the faint silhouette he had located through the mist.

Ning had already prepared his response.

[Fireball]

A condensed sphere of crimson flame formed before his palm and shot forward at point-blank range.

Hammer met flame.

BOOOOM!

The Heaven-Sinking Strike crushed downward at the same instant the Fireball detonated outward, and the resulting explosion tore through the surrounding mist in a violent blast of heat and shattered stone.

When the shockwave finally passed, the white fog covering the arena had been almost completely blown away.

Wei Zhusang stood near the center of a shallow crater, his hammer buried in fractured stone. The veins that had bulged beneath his skin were already fading, and the weakness he had displayed moments earlier was nowhere to be seen.

Several meters away, Ning slid backward before planting his spear into the ground to stop himself.

"I predicted that you'd use poison in your attacks," Wei Zhusang said as he pulled his hammer free. "That's why I deliberately let one of your arrows hit me. I thought you'd lower your guard once you saw the symptoms, but it seems you predicted my prediction."

"Well, I know you're a cautious man," Ning replied. "How could you not prepare for my poison?"

The moment Ning had used poison against Jin Shu and Hei Ya, he had already accepted that anyone who fought him afterward would prepare some kind of countermeasure. What was more surprising was the method Wei Zhusang had chosen.

Through his Pure Eyes, Ning could see the faint discoloration beneath Wei Zhusang's skin rapidly disappearing. Pills weren't allowed during the match, and although Wei Zhusang was permitted to bring additional artifacts because of his profession, he hadn't used anything to suppress the poison.

The resistance came from his body.

"Hundred Treasures Body?" Ning asked.

Wei Zhusang gave a faint nod. "As expected, you think quickly. I purchased Jade Pure Radiant Essence and refined it into my body."

"Jade Pure Radiant Essence... if I remember correctly, that costs around a thousand spirit stones."

The Hundred Treasures Body was a body-refining method that allowed the cultivator to gradually obtain various enhancements and resistances by refining different spiritual materials into the body.

Its potential was high, but so was the cost. As the name implied, cultivating the technique to completion required refining at least a hundred different spiritual materials.

Ning's gaze shifted toward Wei Zhusang's equipment.

There was the rotating disc shield, the reinforced armor beneath his robes, the qi-enhancing boots, and the heavy war hammer with its jade core. Competitors were normally allowed one primary weapon and one low-grade auxiliary artifact, while weapon refiners could bring three additional items they had personally crafted.

Most weapon refiners at their level struggled to produce even two decent low-grade artifacts.

Plus, with his Hundred Treasures Body, Wei Zhusang really showed why some people considered being rich a superpower.

The inscriptions beneath his boots flared again.

Wei Zhusang crossed the distance in another brutal burst, lifting his hammer as enormous amounts of qi gathered within its jade core.

[Mountain-Quelling Hammer Art: Heaven-Sinking Strike]

The earthen light surrounding the hammerhead grew increasingly dense as the weapon rose above Wei Zhusang's shoulder.

Ning didn't even consider blocking it.

His movement art carried him aside just before the hammer descended.

BOOM!

The platform cratered beneath the impact, throwing broken stone in every direction. Ning had avoided the hammer itself, but the pressure generated by the strike still slammed into him and sent him stumbling several steps across the fractured ground.

Wei Zhusang gave him no time to recover. The hammer rose again and swept horizontally, carrying enough force that loose pieces of stone were thrown aside before the weapon even reached him.

Ning ducked beneath the swing, but the edge of the pressure wave still clipped his shoulder and sent him tumbling across the platform.

Wei Zhusang immediately followed.

"You can't meet me directly," he said as he advanced. "That's the weakness I noticed in your previous fights. You have plenty of ways to keep people away from you, but once someone gets close enough to keep pressuring you, none of the techniques you've shown can match an Earth-grade martial art head-on."

"I know," Ning replied as he pushed himself upright.

There wasn't much point denying it. Against Wei Zhusang's raw power, Ning would lose almost immediately if he tried to exchange attacks directly.

Wei Zhusang stamped his foot.

[Wood Binding Art]

Qi flowed into the fractured ground, and thick wooden vines suddenly burst through the gaps in the stone before twisting around Ning's legs and waist.

Ning's pupils contracted.

This hadn't been in the intelligence reports.

Unlike Qiu Han, whose supernatural ability allowed him to form hand seals at abnormal speed, Wei Zhusang's casting speed came entirely from his own attainment. The vines had already tightened before Ning could completely evade them.

Wei Zhusang raised his hammer.

[Scorching Purge]

A thin stream of concentrated flame swept downward, blackening the vines before burning through them.

At the same time, Ning sent qi into the ground beneath his feet.

[Soil Refining Technique]

The earth softened just enough for his trapped foot to shift. Ning twisted free of the remaining vines and threw himself aside as the hammer crashed into the place where he had been standing.

BOOOOM!

The crater deepened, sending another wave of broken stone across the arena.

Wei Zhusang had already turned toward him.

The inscriptions beneath his boots flared once again, and he rushed forward before Ning could properly regain his footing. By now, repeated Heaven-Sinking Strikes had shattered the once-flat arena into craters, ridges, and loose chunks of stone, making Ning's movement art much harder to use at full speed.

Eventually, Wei Zhusang caught him.

[Fourfold Stability]

Ning brought his spear across his body and reinforced his stance, but the clash lasted less than a breath. The hammer smashed into the spear shaft with enough force to tear through his guard, sending a violent vibration through his arms and deep into his bones before throwing him backward.

He landed heavily, blood rising into his throat.

At this rate, he was going to lose.

Wei Zhusang clearly understood that as well, so instead of giving Ning room to recover, he continued pressing his advantage.

"You're good, Ning. You've prepared an answer for almost everything you've encountered so far, but there's still a limit to what preparation can overcome. You don't have anything that can match the raw power of an Earth-grade martial art."

As he spoke, Wei Zhusang reached into his robe and pulled out a length of dark metal chain covered in shallow binding inscriptions.

His final permitted artifact.

The chain swept low across the fractured arena before suddenly curving toward Ning's legs. The inscriptions carved into each link lit up one after another, causing the chain to stiffen as it approached the position Wei Zhusang expected Ning to move toward.

Ning's Pure Eyes and Extreme Space-Time Sensitivity worked together. The flow of qi through the individual links became clearer, revealing that the chain wasn't actually aimed at his current position.

Wei Zhusang had predicted that Ning would retreat half a step to the right and sent the chain there to intercept him.

Ning stepped forward instead.

The chain snapped shut behind him with a metallic crack, wrapping around a chunk of fractured stone.

Wei Zhusang's eyes flickered at the unexpected movement, but he didn't hesitate. Close range was exactly where he wanted Ning to be, so the instant the chain missed, Wei Zhusang surged forward and raised his hammer once more.

[Mountain-Quelling Hammer Art]

Ning was already within his reach.

[Overturning the Dragon]

The spearhead slammed into Wei Zhusang's ribs before the hammer could descend. His inner armor absorbed much of the impact, but the remaining force still sent him stumbling several steps sideways.

Wei Zhusang coughed and spat a mouthful of blood onto the ground.

Instead of becoming angry, his expression grew more focused.

Ning had deliberately stepped closer.

At this distance, there wasn't enough room for the hammer to build the same momentum, and Ning's spear was more flexible in close quarters. Even so, Wei Zhusang still held the advantage in raw power.

Ning steadied his breathing and adjusted his grip on the spear as cold qi began circulating through his body.

[Jade Skin and Icy Meridians]

A faint crystalline sheen appeared across Ning's exposed skin while cold qi flowed through his meridians and spread gradually through his limbs.

Then the frost covering the arena began to react.

Hidden Ice Mist had been active for most of the match. Even after the explosion dispersed the fog itself, the cold it had brought hadn't simply vanished. Frost still covered the edges of the craters, tiny fragments of ice remained scattered among the broken stone, and the air above the arena was noticeably colder than it had been before the fight began.

Cold qi began gathering toward Ning.

Wei Zhusang noticed the change and charged again, his boots flaring as the hammer swept toward Ning's side.

This time, Ning didn't try to escape completely.

He shifted just far enough to avoid receiving the hammer cleanly, angled his spear against its path, and allowed the weapon to slide along the shaft while turning his body with the impact. Most of the force passed beside him and crashed into the ground, though enough still tore through his arms to make his hands shake.

He remained close.

Wei Zhusang swung again, and Ning slipped around the second strike, allowing the edge of the hammer to scrape against his spear before redirecting it toward the ground.

It was almost an all-or-nothing style of fighting. One bad parry was all it took, and even then every exchange still sent pain through his arms.

But every exchange also brought their weapons into contact.

The temperature continued falling.

Wei Zhusang noticed frost beginning to spread across the shaft of his hammer.

His brows furrowed.

Ning thrust forward. Wei Zhusang knocked the spear aside with the hammerhead, only for another thin layer of frost to spread across the metal where the two weapons touched.

They exchanged several more blows, and with each collision, Ning guided another trace of cold qi through his spear and into the hammer.

The metal conducted the chill readily, while the inscriptions running through the weapon provided additional channels for the invading qi. Frost gradually spread across the hammerhead until even the jade core embedded within it began to dim beneath a thin coating of ice.

Wei Zhusang's expression finally changed.

He poured qi into the weapon, forcing the inscriptions to flare and breaking through part of the accumulated frost, but Ning was already moving again. His spear struck the side of the hammer, slid away before Wei Zhusang could catch it, then returned from another angle.

Wei Zhusang tried to create enough distance to swing properly.

Ning followed him.

For most of the fight, Wei Zhusang had been the one relentlessly forcing his way into close range. Now Ning refused to let him leave it.

The cold gathered faster with every exchange, drawn not only from Ning's body but also from the battlefield he had spent most of the match covering in frost. It seeped through the hammer whenever their weapons met, gradually numbing Wei Zhusang's fingers and slowing the circulation through his wrist.

Wei Zhusang swung horizontally.

Ning ducked inside the arc and struck the hammer shaft with his spear.

Cold qi surged through the point of contact.

Crack.

A layer of ice spread across Wei Zhusang's gauntlet and into the joints of his fingers.

His grip faltered slightly.

But that was enough.

Ning's Pure Eyes caught the opening, and his spear moved before Wei Zhusang could recover. The first strike knocked the hammer aside, the second struck the gap beneath Wei Zhusang's arm, and the third forced him backward before he could properly reset his stance.

Wei Zhusang tried to raise the hammer again, but his numbed fingers responded a fraction too slowly.

Ning stepped forward and stopped the spearhead at his throat.

For several seconds, neither of them moved.

The arena around them was almost unrecognizable. Craters covered the platform, broken stone lay scattered across the ground, and thin frost had begun creeping back over the areas where the earlier explosion had stripped it away.

Wei Zhusang lowered his gaze toward the ice covering his hammer, then toward the shallow wounds across his body before finally looking back at Ning.

"No wonder you kept forcing close combat," Wei Zhusang said slowly. "You never revealed that you'd advanced this far in your body-refining technique."

"I knew you were the type to prepare thoroughly," Ning said. "So the only way to overcome that preparation was to bring out something you hadn't prepared for."

At the same time, the elder announced,

"Winner: Ji Ning."
