“-up! Wake up!”

There were strong, warm hands on my shoulders. Renner’s face came into focus. His eyes were wide, and a bit wild. Pale sunlight cast his face in pink and yellow.

Sunlight? I sat up, glancing towards the window. The open window. Bitter air breathed against my clammy skin.

Renner straightened, pulling back- and as he did so, I caught sight of his hands and forearms. Which, I realized dimly, I’d never seen before. He always wore his long, dark gloves, but now… the bare skin of his lower arms was weathered and leathery, raised and almost twisted in places, all of it mottled white and red.

How awful! It looks like he was burned. What could have… how do his hands still work?

“Teela’s gone.” His voice was rough.

I snapped my gaze back up. “Teela’s… what?” Bare feet hit cool wooden floorboards. My gown, damp with sweat, clung to my legs.

“The door’s still locked.” He looked towards the window, jaw clenched.

I followed suit, rolling the words over and over with growing horror. Teela’s gone. The door’s locked. The window’s open. She… she was supposed to wake us up and why would she have opened the window and what does he mean she’s gone?!

Frosty air gusted my hair back as I rushed to the sill, peering out with wide eyes. As if she’d be standing just outside, red hair glinting beneath the dawn, waving up with a big grin…

There was nothing. No movement, save for a few swaying tree branches. No sounds, no people, no animals… just quiet homes and the faint, horrid scent of rot.

I raised one hand to cover my mouth… and froze. There was blood smeared along my palm. His blood. Shaking fingers twisted into my gown, hiding the damning crimson.

“Where did she… go?” It was a foolish question, but I couldn’t seem to form a better one.

Renner slashed one hand towards Teela’s things, unmoved from where she’d unceremoniously dropped them last night. “I woke her up a few hours before daybreak. Gave her the crossbow, told her to start yelling if anyone tried to come in.” His voice dropped to a near-murmur, hands dragging through already-tousled hair. “So either our Fae-touched friend played us, or…”

I followed his gaze to the open window. “But if someone came, if something happened… why wouldn’t she have screamed? Called out?”

“Good question.” He cast a dark glance about, as if the room could provide answers. “Check your pack, ladyship. If she decided to take off, she might’ve taken some of your gold. Maybe even that relic you think is so important.”

“Check my… what? You can’t think she-”

He rounded on me. “I think it’s a lot kinder than the alternative!”

“She wouldn’t have done that,” I whispered, flinching back from his tone. With sickening clarity, that frightened little whimper from the dream echoed through the back of my mind. Saints, no, no, that couldn’t have been her…

“Fine. I’ll do it.” Renner stomped over and rummaged through my things. Slowly at first, then with increasing fervor and viciously muttered oaths.

The mattress sank beneath my weight as I desperately tried to come up with some explanation. Some way that this could all be okay, because we were just overreacting and of course there was a plausible reason for it all…

Locked door. Open window. And, saints, that sound…

I lurched to my feet, unbolted the door, and ran down the hallway. My bare feet thudded against the steps. The common room lay empty, save for the innkeeper. He sat at a table, yawning and drinking a cup of tea. One massive hand massaged his temples.

“Master Lewen!” I rushed forwards, keeping my bloody hand hidden.

He jumped a bit, half-rising.

“Sir, please- have you seen Teela this morning? Or… or did you hear anything strange last night?”

He blinked at me, offering a wan smile. “Ah, Teela?”

“My friend from last night.”

Dense red brows scrunched together.

“The one with the hat.”

He wiped one hand over his face. “Ah… I’m sorry Miss, I must’ve… terribly unprofessional of me, I know, but I think I… that is, my head’s not the clearest at the moment. Bit too much festivities last night.” He gave a little chuckle, cheeks reddening.

I stared down. “Not the clearest? You… I didn’t see you drinking anything.”

“Not a habit, you have my word! Ah… you came in last night? With a friend?” He rubbed bleary eyes, looking friendly and thoroughly confused.

“I… yes. How can you not…” I glanced around, skin pricking with cold dread, searching for any sign that Teela had been down here. An empty breakfast plate, a second mug of tea… but, no. Nothing but tidied tables and a crackling fireplace.

Master Lewen heaved himself upright. “Can I fix you some breakfast, Miss? You said there’s two of you?”

What’s happening? It’s like some horrible dream. How can he not remember us coming in last night?

“There’s three of us,” I whispered through numb lips, backing away.

He blinked again, then nodded agreeably. “Ah, three plates, then?”

“No… no. My friend, the woman who was here last night, is missing! You haven’t seen someone this morning? She has red hair and she smiles a lot and…”

He just continued to look at me with that distant, pleasantly baffled expression.

I stared for a frozen moment, pulse pounding in my ears. Then I turned and fled back upstairs.

“He doesn’t remember her!” I cried as soon as the door was closed. Every breath was turning into a panicked gasp. “He barely seemed to remember me!”

Renner stood with both hands, gloved again, braced against the windowsill. His face was a storm. “Nothing’s missing,” he muttered, casting a baleful glare towards my pack.

I surged forward. “Of course nothing’s missing! She wouldn’t have robbed us and run off- certainly not without her things! She didn’t even take her boots!” My eyes burned at the thought that, wherever Teela was right now, she was wearing only a patched shift and had nothing to keep her feet warm.

Renner met my eyes for a moment, then turned away. Both fists were clenched.

I swallowed the tears. “Renner, what’s going on?”

He stared out the window, muscles in his jaw and neck working. “We’re on the second floor.”

“Okay. Okay, what does that mean?”

He gripped the sill. “I hoped maybe that lowlife from last night just… salt, ladyship, no human could have gotten the window open from outside. Not this high up. And definitely couldn’t have climbed in and dragged her off without waking the two of us up.”

I sank onto the rumpled bed, fighting down a sob. I can’t fall to pieces, not right now. Teela needs me. She needs me to stay calm and think and figure out how to help her.

“So a… a Fae. There was a monster last night, and it… what, climbed into our room?” The thought was wild. Utterly implausible. And yet… what other explanation was there? “W-why did it only take her?”

He didn’t answer. I wasn’t sure if it was because he didn’t know, or because he just didn’t want to add to my distress. I wasn’t brave enough to ask again.

I tried a different question. “Why didn’t she scream? Or call out, or try to shoot it?”

Renner paced the length of the room, jaw tight. “Magic? Maybe? I know there’s Fae that can mess with peoples’ heads, make them get dreamy. Complacent.”

“Magic. Alright. Maybe that’s why Master Lewen…” I trailed off, eyes going wide, then leapt to my feet. “Renner, the stablemaster!”

He offered no response, but the grim look in his eyes spoke volumes.

“You saw how he acted last night! He looked scared- no, terrified. Like he knew something was going to happen! We need to find him!” I rushed to the door. “Talk to him, try to figure out what’s-”

I had the door halfway unbolted when Renner’s hand closed over my forearm.

“Brin.”

His voice was almost gentle, and that scared me far more than anything else. For more than his shouting or pacing, than his oaths or frantic rifling through my pack. Because if Renner, callous and harsh and selfish Renner, was bothering to try and be tender…

My blood ran cold.

“She’s fine.” I met his eyes, daring him to say otherwise. Praying that he wouldn’t. I was shaking, and there were still tears threatening to spill out, but I managed to keep my voice steady. “We’re… we’re going to figure out what’s going on. And we’re going to find her. And she’s going to be just fine.”

He opened his mouth but, before he could say anything to the contrary- and he was going to, I could see it written across his face- I whispered, “Renner, she doesn’t have her boots.”

He went still. Stood in hesitant silence for a moment, then closed his eyes and sighed.

My heart grew just a little bit warmer when he turned, stalked towards the bed, and grabbed his belt.
