Right before the fourth wave began, the roof work was complete. There wasn’t enough manpower or materials to build a roof for the entire base, so it was strategically placed near the gates and barracks. The barracks in the middle was also wallless.

“As long as they don’t break the pillars, the roof should hold,” Andrew said.

Now that they were getting used to the dungeon, the new survivors were more proactive in fighting, and the fourth wave was cleared without any trouble.

[Congratulations, you have cleared the fourth wave. 0 casualties. The next wave is in 6 hours. You and the rest of the survivors have obtained 500 coins. You have unlocked the oasis.]

“Huh? It wasn’t the orchard?” James muttered.

“...Let’s check it out. Ah, we also need a team to cut down the forest for more logs,” Andrew said.

“I will handle things here at the base,” Bernard said. “Please find some food. I don’t want to eat crow or dog meat.”

“Understood.” Andrew nodded and headed for the lake while Sanders led a group toward the forest with James’s raptor.

The lake glimmered as fish swam about in its clear blue water. James waded in and caught one with his hand. He scoffed at how vulnerable the fish were—they weren’t afraid of humans. With a single scoop, the fish flew onto the shore. It flailed on the ground before James stabbed it with an arrow, killing it and roasting it over a fire from his firefur.

“Are you a bear?” Andrew snickered as he gathered several coconuts nearby. “Hm… we might need to build a cart for this. I wonder if the civilians can make one. Your raptor is no longer enough.”

James ignored Andrew’s remark and used his arrow like a skewer. The fish tasted tender, with a bitter taste of its organs. Thanks to his goblin stomach, he didn’t get sick, but one should have gutted the fish before eating it.

“Tastes like salmon,” James said. It was incredulous—a salmon in the middle of a desert. But James stopped thinking about it and kept eating.

“On one hand, this might be better than the orchard. It’s a rare source of water, meat, and fruit. These coconuts are priceless,” Andrew said as he hauled several coconuts back. James then pierced about a dozen fish with his steel wire and carried them back as well.

A few minutes before the timer reached zero, the purple moon made its appearance once more. Its light carved a violet hue across the brown desert. Just like before, monsters poured out from both portals in droves. The worst part of this battle was not the enemy, but the moon itself.

[The Curse of the Pale Moon has activated. Survivors are advised to take shelter. As long as survivors are in direct contact with the moonlight, a draining magic will activate, sapping their strength and eventually killing them.]

“Shit! These roofs have cracks in them! Arghh!!” one mercenary exclaimed as a hyena and a doghead rushed toward him. Thankfully, his teammates saved him.

“Incoming! Those birds crashed into the roof!” another mercenary shouted when the crows dived toward the planks like kamikaze bombers. Blood dripped from the birds as the mercenaries dispatched them with ease, but the chaos they caused was severe.

“Get those birds! All of you! I’ll handle this gate!” James enveloped himself in fire as he rampaged against the horde. Their teeth were useless against the might of his activated Firefur. A golden glow emanated from his sword as he slashed left and right.

“On it! Focus on the birds! Hurry up, men!” Bernard followed. He might not like how James ran things, but he had grasped how James behaved and trusted him. James was not like the usual survivor—he didn’t flee from the dread of the dungeon. Fear did not conquer him.

An hour later, the horde was vanquished.

[You have cleared the fifth wave. Casualties: 0. Injuries: 10. Congratulations, you have received 500 coins. A supply consisting of 500 logs, 500 nails, 50 meters of rope, 50 kilograms of Portland cement, and 500 blocks of stone will be provided. The next wave is in two hours.]

“Two hours?” James rubbed his eyes and checked the system notification, but it didn’t change. There were barely two hours before the next assault.

“Hurry up, men! Those who are injured, go to the barracks! Civilians, fix the broken roofs! I don’t care if you get hit by the moonlight—as long as you don’t die, you must fix the roof!” Andrew ran toward an injured soldier, dragged him away from the treacherous moonlight, and used a healing spell on him.

“Where is the saw?”

“What are we going to do about the leak on the roof?”

“Use coconut husks and fibers to cover them.”

“Help! He’s bleeding!”

“We need water! Is there any water mage here?”

Screams erupted from the encampment as soldiers rushed to drag the injured away and make space so civilians could work. The roofed pathway Andrew had built before the fifth wave proved invaluable for the repair job.

The timer ticked down to zero far faster than anyone hoped. Then another portal emerged beside the first one, fusing both portals into one massive gate. Three gigantic dog-headed warriors, each over three meters tall, came forth. They led three leashed jackals. Like rabid dogs, they growled and barked toward the encampment.

Unlike the previous horde, they were in no hurry to march toward the encampment, choosing instead to walk— A slow march toward the encampment, confidence oozing from their movements.

James took point at the gate, ready with his bow. If they were not rushing toward him, all the better; James could pelt them with his bow skills and magic. A golden meteor glided toward one jackal, only to divert and strike one of the monsters’ shields instead. A dull thud rang out as the arrow buried itself in the wood.

James blinked for a moment at the incomprehensible scene. It shouldn’t have been possible for such a thing to happen. He had used a Homing Arrow, a bow skill that chased after enemies. He didn’t know shield skills could divert the targeting system of his bow technique. James didn’t even try using other bow skills, knowing those hounds would simply dodge them.

“This changes some things,” James muttered. The team went silent as they saw the scene. If those shield skills could block James’s arrow, then they could do nothing but engage in melee. But James had not run out of cards to play. He still had his mind magic to rely on—the magic he had abandoned after all time, now proving to be more valuable than gold in this dungeon.

When they were at the 100-meter range, the dog handlers released their jackals, and they dashed toward the encampment, Their rabid bark sends shiver down to the mercs. The distance closed in almost an instant, but James had already prepared his magic.

His spell hit one dog-headed warrior right in the chest, and it stopped in its tracks.

“He didn’t attack his comrade?” James blinked in surprise while his parallel thought prepared another dose of mind magic for the jackals. These were elite soldiers, not a mindless breed like earlier. When one dog handler was hit by the mind magic and saw their target suddenly switch from inside the encampment to their allies, they knew something was wrong.

They stopped their actions, sometimes hitting their own heads to shake off the illusion.

It worked to a hateful degree, as the magic only lasted a few minutes. Without the chaos that the illusion provided, this magic was only as effective as Mind Flay or Sense Alteration. Fortunately, the rabid dogs they brought were not as clever. When James hit one with his magic, it turned and snapped toward its master, its rabid mouth occupied him.

“Sanders! Hit it with magic!”

“On it!”

“I’ll take one of them head-on!” James activated his Vorpal Lunge. His gut told him that this was no time to be a coward. He had to fight under the moonlight.

James lunged at a jackal, trying to shave off the extra. But a shield skill prevented him, forcing his attention toward one of the massive dog-heads. James didn’t execute the slash, preferring instead to slam his body into the shield.

A shield skill could deflect a weapon skill but not normal attacks. James knew this and capitalized on the chance. After slamming himself into the shield, he breathed fire toward the Anubis warrior. A lightning bolt shot out from his horn toward its face, taking out one of its eyes.

The beast cried out in pain and flailed its hand. Its strength was enough to send James flying from its shield. Another one of the Anubis warriors eyed James’s recklessness. James felt his strength draining away because of the moonlight.

He regretted his action; lunging forward like that had been reckless, and the enemy took full advantage. This was the worst outcome. They weren’t rushing. They knew that if they played defensively, they would prevail.

A golden meteor came from an unexpected direction. They were so concerned about James that they had forgotten Andrew, who threw his spear at the creature’s head, killing one of the Anubis warriors. They had thought two jackals were enough to distract the defenders. James took the chance to fire another bolt of mind magic at one of them.

The Anubis warrior knelt as pain assaulted its body. It was as if its flesh were being sliced by a hot knife. A weapon skill from its khopesh flung toward James, but this time, James was ready. His sword glowed golden, and as the skills clashed, James was teleported to the side, beheading the warrior that had challenged him. Its mistake was taking James head-on. The Lunar counter was such an invaluable skill for dueling.

The group had taken down the jackals that the Anubis warriors released upon them. Now, only one warrior remained. It had a strong shield skill, but that was useless once it was surrounded. As it charged toward Andrew, James beheaded it from the rear, finishing the wave with aplomb.

[You have cleared the sixth wave. Casualties: 0. Injuries: 6. Time elapsed: 31 hours. Congratulations, you have received 500 coins. You may now use the shop function. The next wave is in 12 hours.]

James jogged toward the barracks. As the glaring moonlight no longer covered his body, he could feel his strength returning. It had been a hard fight—James felt nauseous from how draining the moonlight was. The system notification served as a cruel reminder.

They hadn’t even made it to the second day yet. There was still a lot in store for them.

But for now, James had to rest. The rest could wait.
