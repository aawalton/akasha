Tristan’s expression flickered between disbelief and despair as he listened to Luke’s report. The mention of soul forms, the enemy’s movements, a traitor within the royal court—any one of these would have been enough to warrant alarm. But all three? It bordered on the impossible.

Yet not once did he interrupt. He stood there, silent and composed, waiting until Luke had finished.

“I… I don’t know what to say,” he muttered at last, his gaze distant, unfocused.

In that moment, Tristan looked decades older. His shoulders sagged under the weight of revelation, and for a fleeting second, Luke worried the man might collapse. If he hadn’t known Tristan’s strength, he might have rushed to steady him.

But Luke had no words to offer comfort. In fact, he had more news—worse news.

“I also have reason to believe the Crown Prince has fallen,” he said softly.

Tristan didn’t react. Whether the numbness had finally set in or the barrage of grim updates had eroded his capacity for shock, his face remained unreadable.

Luke hesitated. Perhaps he didn’t believe him? He began explaining his reasoning, careful to frame certain insights as innate abilities rather than expose the existence of the system.

But Tristan raised a hand, halting him.

“I believe you, young Luke,” he said. “If there truly is a traitor in the court backing the Second Prince, then I’m not surprised the Crown Prince was targeted.”

He paused, then added, “Before I transferred to the Royal Academy, I spent some time in the court. And while I don’t recall anyone openly supporting the Second Prince, that doesn’t mean such a person doesn’t exist. More likely, the traitor is connected to someone with substantial standing—someone who hides behind another’s shadow.”

Luke’s eyes brightened with curiosity. He hadn’t known Tristan once held a position in court.

“What are your thoughts on the Right Minister?” he asked.

“Alexander?” Tristan scoffed. “There is no one more loyal to the Marxx Kingdom than the Right Minister. Of all the potential suspects, he’s the last man I would expect to betray the king.”

The confidence in his tone gave Luke pause. He remembered his own uneasy encounter with the Right Minister. There had been no concrete reason for his suspicion—just instinct. Still, he was surprised to hear Tristan dismiss the idea so readily.

“Then who’s at the top of your list?” Luke pressed.

Tristan rubbed a hand along his damp goatee, lost in thought. After a long silence, he looked up and answered.

“The Minister of Intelligence. Heath Tinzel.”

Luke straightened, caught off guard by the name.

“He has more influence in the capital than nearly anyone—save for the king and the two other ministers,” Tristan continued. “That old bag of bones has been knocking on death’s door for the last twenty years. He must be over a hundred by now.”

He paused again, his voice dropping to a grim note.

“During King Julius’s rise to the throne, Tinzel was his greatest supporter. With his reach and power, he paved the way for Julius to claim the crown—over his own brother, the former Crown Prince. Even back then, the entire kingdom was stunned that the king emerged victorious.”

Luke was silent for a moment, his thoughts churning. He hadn’t met the Minister of Intelligence before, but the idea of anyone holding such sway within the kingdom—outside of the king himself—was concerning.

“What happened? Was the Crown Prince murdered?”

Tristan gave him a bitter look. “No, not exactly. It wasn’t that straightforward. In short... the Crown Prince took his own life, though the events leading up to it were anything but ordinary.”

He paused before continuing, his voice tinged with regret.

“He got a noblewoman pregnant. That stirred the hornet’s nest. I was still young at the time, but I remember the chaos it caused throughout the capital—especially what followed.”

Tristan scratched the back of his head, as if trying to rub the memory away.

“The noble family banished their daughter, disowning her publicly. The court branded her a harlot, a stain on noble decorum. And then... the king stepped in. He ordered the child terminated to preserve the sanctity of the royal bloodline—didn’t want a bastard interfering with succession.”

Luke’s jaw tightened. He already suspected this world held brutal traditions, but hearing it confirmed still made his stomach churn.

Tristan’s voice dropped lower. “The girl took her own life before they could take the baby from her.”

Luke looked down, lips pressed into a thin line. The cold in his bones had nothing to do with the weather now. He understood the kingdom was steeped in Confucian values—honor, family, bloodlines—but this felt needlessly cruel.

He wasn’t a scholar of political customs, but even he knew that bastards born out of wedlock weren’t unheard of. It would’ve brought dishonor, certainly—but death? For all involved?

He exhaled slowly. “And how did the Crown Prince respond?”

Tristan paused again, this time lowering his head.

“He followed shortly after.”

A heavy silence fell between them.

The whole affair was tragic. But it also reeked of orchestration. Luke’s mind swirled with questions. Had it all happened naturally, a tragic convergence of bad decisions and worse timing? Or had someone behind the scenes nudged the events along, escalating what might have been a scandal into a complete erasure?

Was the Minister of Intelligence involved, like Tristan suspected?

Luke kept those thoughts to himself—for now.

“Change of plans, we have to return to Ralis as soon as possible.” he said finally. “I’ll speak to the Second Prince. See if I can figure out who’s backing him before we decide on our next steps.”

Their conversation ended there. Tristan wandered off to relieve himself, while Luke made his way toward a nearby cookfire.

The soldiers were just beginning to boil water for the meal. Steam rose into the early morning air, curling like ghostly fingers above the pot. Luke stepped closer, letting the heat soak into his damp clothes.

He stared into the flames, his thoughts still unsettled.

If I chose to leave the kingdom… would Tristan follow me?

It was a bitter notion.

He had just been named a Viscount. His perfume had likely begun circulating among noble circles by now, opening doors to a lucrative business opportunity. He’d laid the groundwork for countless ideas—plans to modernize the city, strengthen its military, improve daily life.

All of it… threatened by a treacherous court and a war on all sides.

But is escape even possible anymore?

If I left, where would I go? And would the system even let me?

A cold shudder ran through him.

He remembered the penalties for failure—the quests that came with them, binding him tighter with every step. The Sovereign System offered no freedom. Not really.

It had chosen a path for him. And it wouldn’t let him stray.

What do you want from me?

Luke’s teeth sank into his lip, pain blooming from the raw skin as frustration surged within him. The question wasn't directed at anyone around him—but at the system itself. The Sovereign System. From the very beginning, it had been steering him down a specific path. And it didn’t take a genius to understand where that path led.

It had enrolled him into a military academy. It had given him skills tailored for command. It had driven him to become a general.

It wanted him to fight.To lead.To conquer.

But if that was the case… why here?

Why place him in the Marxx Kingdom? A fractured land, hemmed in by enemies, ruled by an ineffectual king, and riddled with traitors. It felt like a cruel joke. As though he’d been set up to fail from the start.

The uncertainty churned in his chest like a storm.

Then—A hand fell gently on his shoulder.

“Good morning, General,” came a soft, tired voice.

Luke turned. Prince Lyon stood beside him, wearing a faint smile. His face was still bruised, swollen in places, and there was a sluggishness to his movements—but he looked better than he had the night before. Less like a captive. More like a man.

“I’ve had better,” Luke muttered, attempting a smile of his own. His lip split further from the stretch, stinging sharply.

The prince chuckled. “You and I both. I can’t wait to sleep in my own bed again…”

Luke let the warmth of the fire wash over him for a moment before asking, “What are your plans once we return?”

It wasn’t the best question, not under the circumstances—especially since he hadn’t yet broken the news of the Crown Prince’s likely death. But he was curious. He wanted to see where the man stood.

Lyon’s smile faded. His gaze turned distant.

“I don’t know,” he admitted. “I’ve been arrogant. Wasteful. But I’m not stupid.”

He exhaled slowly, eyes flicking toward the dying embers.

“There’s something happening. Something deeper. Otherwise, how would the enemy know my identity? Why would they target me so precisely, from the very beginning?”

He shook his head. “Richard says we’re in Lhair territory now, which means they were waiting for us. Someone is feeding them information—someone in the capital.”

Luke’s eyes widened slightly. He hadn’t expected that level of awareness. The so-called ‘useless prince’ had connected the dots far faster than he’d anticipated.

Maybe there was more to him than the rumors let on.

“I’ve been meaning to speak with both you and Richard,” Luke said.

“Richard went to retrieve his greatsword,” Lyon replied. “He’ll be back soon.”

—

Some time later, the scent of soup drifted through the air, a comforting balm after the chaos of the previous night. Around the nearest cookfire, Luke, Tristan, Richard, and Prince Lyon sat apart from the main group, cradling small bowls of steaming broth.

The portions were meager—barely enough to satisfy, especially for Richard and Tristan, whose appetites were anything but small. Yet neither man uttered a word of complaint.

Richard had recovered his greatsword and now wore it strapped to his back as always. But there was something different in the way he carried himself. His usual light-hearted bravado had dulled, replaced by a quiet stillness.

Luke noticed the tension settling over the group like fog. The silence stretched.

No point dragging things out.

He set his bowl aside and glanced at the three men gathered around the fire.

He had questions. And the answers would shape everything that came next.

“Richard. Lyon.” Luke’s voice cut through the quiet. “I need to know the names of every supporter you have within the kingdom—regardless of their current standing or influence in court. I believe one of these backers has committed treason… with the intent of placing you on the throne in your brother’s stead.”

His eyes swept between the two men—one broad-shouldered and silent, the other slender and pensive.

Richard didn’t flinch. He set his bowl down with a sigh, his expression somber, already prepared for such a possibility.

Lyon, however, didn’t meet Luke’s gaze. He sat quietly, staring into the half-filled bowl in his hands as if hoping to find an answer there.

“I suspected as much,” Richard said at last. “When the enemy knew the prince’s identity so precisely, it could only mean someone from within was feeding them information.”

He leaned forward, resting his forearms on his knees.

“The problem is… succession in the kingdom is fought both in the open and from the shadows. Most nobles won’t declare their allegiance until the very end—when the outcome is all but certain. That way, if they back the wrong horse, they can claim neutrality and avoid being purged.”

Luke frowned, and Richard continued.

“You’ve probably heard of noble families being erased from history after their candidate lost the throne. It’s a bloody tradition here. One Marxx takes the crown, and a dozen noble houses die with the one who didn't.”

Luke gave a small nod and turned his attention to Tristan, who sat beside him, silent but attentive.

“Heath Tinzel,” Luke said. “Has he made his allegiance known?”

“The Minister of Intelligence?” Richard raised an eyebrow. “I’ve only seen him a handful of times. Looks like he’s got one foot in the grave already.”

Luke shifted his gaze toward Lyon, whose grip on his bowl had tightened.

“What about you, Prince Lyon? What’s your impression of him?”

The prince flinched, clearly having drifted from the conversation. His shoulders tensed as he straightened.

“I haven’t dealt with him much,” he admitted. “But… his grandson and I are close. He’s a little older than me, but back when everyone was fawning over my elder brother, he stayed by my side.”

There was a flicker of guilt behind his words—something raw and unresolved.

Lyon stirred the broth with his spoon absentmindedly. “I used to say some pretty awful things about Leo… maybe out of jealousy. I didn’t think much of it at the time.”

Luke’s ears perked up. He glanced at Tristan.

Their eyes met—and in that brief exchange, understanding passed between them.

A silent agreement.

Luke exhaled slowly, bracing himself for what had to be said.

“Lyon,” he began gently, placing a consoling hand on the young man’s shoulder. “I have reason to believe your elder brother is dead. The reason you were captured… was so they could install you as a puppet king.”

Lyon stiffened beneath his touch. His grip around the bowl trembled.

But it was his face—the emotions that passed through it—that hit Luke the hardest.

Disbelief.Regret.Sadness.All genuine. All gut-wrenching.

His eyes shimmered, and for a moment, he looked like a boy—lost and betrayed by the very world he belonged to.

“I’m sorry…” Luke murmured.

There was nothing else to say.
