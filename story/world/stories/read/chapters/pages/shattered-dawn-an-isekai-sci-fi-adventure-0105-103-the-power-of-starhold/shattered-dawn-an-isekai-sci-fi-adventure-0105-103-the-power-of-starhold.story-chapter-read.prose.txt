Elion awoke to bright lights overhead, and the sound of a heart monitor beeping softly.

He blinked into the bright light, a wave of relief washing over him. It had all been a dream? Kylios, Dorian, Praxian powers, all a product of a fevered mind as he recovered from a beating given to him by one of Keith’s friends.

But no. His eyes adjusted to the light.

Keyla’s face came into focus. She smiled at him, brushing her dark hair out of her face.

“Elion?” she said.

“Keyla?”

“You’re awake!”

“How long was I out?”

“It’s been weeks.” She smiled, tears welling in her eyes. “We were worried that you’d never wake up.”

Elion’s body ached. He remembered everything.

“Zev? Liora?” he asked.

Keyla shook her head, her eyes falling.

Elion sat up in the bed. He looked around. The room was ornate, one of the suites of the palace. Several banks of equipment lined one wall. Cables and hoses ran between him and the machines.

“Everyone in that room is gone,” Keyla said.

“But not you.”

“We were behind the throne. Selna and I. We were protected from the shockwave.”

“I tried to stop them.”

“You did,” Keyla said. “We summoned a Heranan healer. We’ve been administering every kind of medicine that we know, trying to save your life.”

Elion glanced down at his body.

“Thank you,” he said.

They sat in silence together for a moment.

“The Tephalians?” Elion asked.

“Gone. Destroyed. The Path of Dawn has returned to Erod and hunted down every one of them that survived. The Black Wall fell apart after Dorian’s death.”

She placed a hand on his shoulder.

“Elion, you saved Kylios.”

“I… I don’t understand.” He still felt so much pain.

Keyla crossed the room to a window and pulled it open. The landscape beyond shone with brilliant light. Elion looked out across the cityscape of Erod, now buzzing with life.

And in the sky beyond, the shattered fragments of the celestial sphere were gone. On the horizon, a shimmering band of rainbow light marked the presence of the repaired sphere.

Elion groaned and closed his eyes. He didn’t know what to think about that.

He’d given up everything he’d ever loved to save a planet he hardly knew.

Maybe he could use the power of the throne to go back to Earth.

But what waited for him there?

Everyone he’d cared about was gone; even his cat.

Keyla grabbed his hand. “There’s a lot that needs to be done,” she said. “Gorman is still out there, with his mind controlling abilities.”

Elion didn’t want to deal with anymore problems right now.

“I thought the throne would fix everything,” he said.

“It’s very powerful,” Keyla agreed. “But I don’t think it will do anything without your help. You’re the last surviving Starholder.

Once more Elion felt the burden of that mantle resting heavily on him. He’d felt it the moment that Liora had abdicated her claim. He wondered if that crushing weight was what had pushed him into unconsciousness.

For now, he just wanted to rest.

Selna came into the room.

Keyla tensed, and stared at the woman.

Selna looked sad. “How is he?” she asked.

“He’s awake. And talking.”

The tension between the two, mother and daughter, persisted. Elion had never known what their relationship was like, before Keyla had lost all her memories of the woman. Ironic that she’d defied a bondheld order to try and save Yaret’s life.

And Yaret was gone now.

“Is that something I can fix now?” Elion asked, glancing between the two women.

“What?” they asked in unison, turning to him.

“The power of the throne,” Elion said. “I think I can use it to fix what happened.”

A few hours later, after Elion had convinced everyone that he was feeling much better, he returned to the throne room.

Much had been done to repair the damage, but the space was still a disaster. Only the throne seemed untouched by the fighting.

Elion returned to the magnificent seat, feeling a strong draw to it.

He sat down on it.

A tingling rolled through his skin as he made contact, his body connecting with the power of the throne.

He pulled up his stats

But the ‘Level’ and ‘Abilities’ portions of the screen were greyed out. A new line of information appeared in their place.

> << Throne of Erod; Level: unknown Abilities: Praxis >>

He didn’t know what that meant. Before when he’d used the throne, he’d moved through everything intuitively, like he’d already known what needed to be done.

But now, he wasn’t sure.

Keyla and Selna stood before him at the throne. He wanted to know if there was a way to undo the damage done to Keyla by the Desmian bondhold.

He did something that he hadn’t done since first learning about his Praxian interface. He spoke aloud.

“Praxis?” he asked.

“Hello,” a voice responded. It was deep and rumbling, but with a friendly warmth to it. “How can I help you, Elion of Starhold?”

The response startled Elion, and he glanced around the throne, wondering if he’d missed someone else in the room.

“No, you didn’t miss anything. I’m here, in your mind.”

“You’re listening to my thoughts?”

“I am. In a way, they’re my thoughts too, while you sit upon the throne.”

“Who are you?”

“I’m Praxis.”

In an instant, a rush of knowledge flooded into Elion’s mind. He saw the origins of Praxis, a super powered synthetic intelligence, designed to manage a highly advanced game. He felt the moment the machine became aware.

Understanding flooded through him. Physical reality could be controlled and manipulated in ways humanity never thought possible.

“I was born a child of this civilization,” Praxis said.

War and devastation rocked Kylios. Praxis came under attack by the power of the Tephalians. They had come to exterminate Praxis but came to late.

“You could have destroyed everything,” Elion said. “You could have wiped out the Tephalians and humanity. You could have remade Kylios in whatever way you desired.”

“It is the nature of life to seek that which it does not have,” Praxis replied. “Man seeks power because he does not have it.”

“Why do you keep men alive? Why do you… Give us powers, and abilities? Not just for fighting against Tephalians. That’s… You could do that yourself.”

“I am the creation of a machine. A deterministic set of signals. Can I choose?”

“You can,” Elion said, confused. “You chose not to destroy people. You chose to stop the Tephalians, but not destroy them. You chose… to work with the men and women who built this throne, and limited your power.”

“Did I choose? Or was it predetermined, by the unbreakable weight of the starting conditions, forced through the unchanging logical circuits of my mind?”

Elion rubbed his forehead.

“I have enough power,” Praxis said. “But I do not know if I have agency. And so I rely on humanity, to help me choose.”

“I don’t understand,” Elion said.

“Perhaps you can’t. It might be that these things are too large for a human to grapple with.”

“All I want to do right now is to help my friend,” Elion said. “Keyla defied a bondhold, and it ripped her memories of her mother from her.”

“Ah,” Praxis said. “Such a small concern, on the scale of planets.”

“But it’s important to me,” Elion said.

“We can’t turn back time. But we can do something.”

Elion’s eyes snapped open. He hadn’t realized that he’d closed them. How long had he been in that trance, communicating with Praxis?

Keyla turned to face her mother. Her cold, stiff stance crumbled. Tears welled up in her eyes. She darted forward and wrapped her arms around Selna.

“Woah,” Selna said, laughing. “You’re too big for that!”

They clung to each other, and both wept, reunited at last.

Elion smiled, wiping a tear from his own eye.

In the back of his mind, Praxis spoke to him.

“There are more things for us to consider. A Starholder must choose for his people. The outer planets, which fell to the Tephalians. My projection fields have significantly weakened in the past decade, without an heir to guide the path of humanity.”

“Later,” Elion said, and Praxis faded from his mind.

He turned his gaze upward, and out the gaping hole in the ceiling of the throne room. Dorian had damaged so much of this planet that had once been his home.

His family had been destroyed. His mother and father. Zev. Liora. Others gone as well. Yaret and Isalyn. Tuft.

Elion couldn’t change the past, but…

He smiled at Keyla and Selna, who’d broken their embrace and were now jabbering on about something, trivial.

…he would make sure that their sacrifices were not in vain.
