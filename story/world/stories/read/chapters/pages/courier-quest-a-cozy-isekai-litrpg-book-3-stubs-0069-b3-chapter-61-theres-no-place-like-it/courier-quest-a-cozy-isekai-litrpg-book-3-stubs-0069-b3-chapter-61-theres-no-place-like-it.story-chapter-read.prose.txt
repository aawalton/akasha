Trevor and Adabelle rode Clay, in the form of a wracor, through the gates of Tosa. Their little walled town wasn’t the largest settlement they had been to, nor was it the greatest, but it was theirs. A palpable sense of belonging descended on all three of them the moment one of the citizens greeted them with a bow.

They had both agreed that dropping off the last of the goods for the quests they were given would come second. Completing them later would be fine so long as Trevor didn’t toss his cargo in the river. Instead, they were on a quest to get home for the first time in what felt like ages.

The mayor’s manor looked like a modern American house made of brick with plenty of windows and a chimney on either side of the building. Adabelle had been living there for almost two years now, and Trevor had moved his things in shortly before the wedding to make things official. He would miss his little home back at the Hero House, but that feeling was eclipsed by the sheer desire to live with his wife.

“There they are!” Rashie loudly announced from the front steps, jumping clean off of them to dash towards Clay. She leapt at the earth elemental’s face, hugging it tightly before scrambling up and over to tackle Trevor and Adabelle. “You’re finally home!”

“Hey there, shark,” the courier laughed as he held Rashie in his arms. He knew she’d scramble over him if she could, but he got to her first by virtue of being in front. “Were you waiting for us?”

She nodded. “Lora came and said you’d be home soon, so I wanted to wait for you, because we missed you both a lot.”

“We?” Adabelle asked, reaching forward to affectionately rub the girl’s head.

“Deputy dad’s in the house making sure it didn’t get too dusty or something,” she answered, leaning into the older woman’s touch. “You told him to take care of it, but I don’t think he visited much at all.”

“I’m sure it’ll be fine regardless. So long as that evil Bocoro didn’t get in there,” Trevor said ominously.

“Yeah, I’m not into that anymore,” Rashie sulked as they stopped in front of the house. She slid off first and started bouncing, waiting impatiently for the other Summoned to dismount. “Bocoro just turned out to be, like, some guy. He did his job and that was it. I can’t believe I got worked up over nothing like that.”

“Well, maybe I’ll find something in the files that shows he was an evil mastermind the whole time, and his machinations were simply something you couldn’t see,” Adabelle offered, only slightly teasing.

“Nah, I’m over it,” the shark girl said. She torpedoed into her aunt with enough force to make her take a step backwards and hugged her tightly. “Now I just want to spend time with my family.”

“Then it sounds like we should head inside, huh?” Trevor replied as he dismounted from Clay. The earth elemental shifted back into their usual stoat form, leaving behind five slabs of stone for their master to retrieve. They jumped onto Rashie’s shoulder afterwards, nuzzling the girl’s head enthusiastically.

“Oh! Are those new claws?” the shark asked as she peeled away from Adabelle. In response, Clay showed off the sharp obsidian hidden in their paws. “That’s so cool! You gotta tell me everything that happened!”

They entered the house to see Wayne waiting for them. He wore a black apron that suggested no one should kiss the cook. There was some dried sauce on it. “Welcome home. Y’all got perfect timing,” the Texan dwarf said with a grin. “Come on, I got grub waiting.”

“It’s good to see you, Wayne. How’d you know we’d be hungry?” Trevor asked as everyone took off their shoes at the door. They then followed him towards the dining room.

“I didn’t, but that doesn’t matter much when you got the ultimate food freeze Summoned in the house,” he chuckled.

“Yeah, got me there,” the courier agreed, nodding.

The table was filled with the kind of things Wayne liked to cook most, so it looked like an American backyard barbecue. Ribs slathered in the Texan’s homemade sauce, baked potatoes, beans, grilled vegetables, and cornbread. It smelled of home, and the newlyweds sat down, though not before Rashie commandeered the seat between them.

Any tension they felt from being gone so long melted away as they sat at their own table. There was being home, and then there was being home. They sat there for a moment, eyes closed as they basked in the feeling. Trevor and Adabelle reached behind the shark’s chair to take each other’s hand, doing what felt right even though they couldn’t see the other.

“What’s up with them?” Rashie whispered.

“They’re enjoying the moment, kid, just let them be,” Wayne chuckled. She went quiet, giving them some time. After a few seconds the newlyweds opened their eyes and started filling their plates with food. “Good to be home after a long trip, innit?”

“Always,” Trevor answered with a smile. “Traveling is fun. Meeting new people is absolutely great. Having new experiences with my wife is something that I’ll always treasure. But, home? There’s no place like it.”

“I’ll keep that in mind, Dorothy,” the dwarf teased.

“Who’s Dorothy?” Rashie asked.

“A girl with ruby slippers and a dog. If you clicked the heels together three times, magic happened,” Trevor answered as he and Adabelle finished making their plates. Clay, who was still on the shark girl’s shoulders, perked up. There was a question in their bond. “Yes, you could make some ruby slippers if you really want to. I’m sure Corrin would adore them.”

The earth elemental nodded, excited at the prospect, before glancing towards the door.

“Meow!” Mr. Tiggles announced as he ran into the dining room, the bell on his collar jingling. He stopped to sniff Adabelle’s legs, and then Trevor’s, before rubbing against them with his whole body. “Purr.”

Clay jumped down to greet the black cat, landing heavily on the wooden floor. They looked at each other for a moment before the earth elemental pounced. Mr. Tiggles ran at the first sign of trouble. There was no hissing or growling, though, and the Summoned listened to them chase each other through the house. Rashie was quick to join Wayne in grabbing food.

“I missed this,” Adabelle said as she retrieved a rib from the rack she had taken. “Just last night, at the top of the world, I was telling Trevor that I was ready to leave it all behind and get back on the road as an adventurer. Travel with him, Clay, and anyone else ready to join us.”

“So I was right!” Rashie exclaimed. “Trevor really is going to steal Addymanatine! I said it back when they came back from the festival of lights, and it’s finally coming true!”

“You thought that Bocoro was your real nemesis, but no, it was me!” the courier said, laughing evilly.

“I should have known that Uncle Trevor is a wily one. You’re no cinnamon bun roll at all, are you?” she asked, pointing a mostly clean rib bone at the courier in an accusatory manner.

“Not anymore,” he answered, thumping a fist against his chest. “I’ve fought ghosts. I’ve fought monsters. People have, against all odds, requested I raid more dungeons. I’m a verified adventurer now, so long as I have my overpowered party to back me up, that is.”

“So you’re planning on working your wife to the bone in the dungeons?” Rashie gasped before throwing the rib into her mouth. It only took her a few bites to grind it down and swallow. “Diabolical! Just say the word, Auntie Addy, and I’ll save you!”

“I’m not going anywhere for a long time, shark, and I’m certainly not being stolen.” Adabelle giggled, reaching across the girl to bat her husband’s shoulder. “I was going to say that, now that I’m back, I’m sick of the thought of leaving. There’s no way I’m going anywhere unless I absolutely have to.”

“I’ve been there before,” Wayne said with a nod.

“Me, too!” Rashie announced. “After Trevor and I came back from the Floating Isles, I didn’t want to leave Tosa for at least a week.”

“Speaking of, we actually got a chance to go there,” the courier said. “There was a meteor shower, and we got to visit the King of the Sea again.”

“Did you bring me anything?” the shark girl asked hopefully.

“Not from there,” he admitted, causing her to deflate. “But we have plenty of other goodies and souvenirs. That includes food from tropical Paladona, technologically advanced Aletander, and I also forgot to share the third bowl of ballabroody with Lora, so you and Wayne can split that. That gets you a meal fit for a king from the Isle of Horns.”

“It sounds like you’ve had quite the adventure,” the cowboy said before arching an eyebrow. “Just how technologically advanced are we talking?”

“Trevor drove me around in a car,” Adabelle answered with a smile. “We also went to an amusement park, complete with roller coasters, and even got to play a fighting game with characters you could move with a controller.”

“And laser tag inside of giant robots,” Trevor added.

“Oh, yes. That, too,” the demonkin confirmed, her tail swishing at the thought of it. “I have to admit that I never thought the gun training you put us through would come in handy, Wayne, but it was very useful when shooting down the Playground’s caretakers.”

The dwarf frowned as he looked between the newlyweds, who were both smiling. “If you don’t want to tell me, you don’t gotta, but you don’t have to lie about it.”

“Sammie the Gamer, man,” Trevor laughed. “You think Jackson had a big impact on our continent? You haven’t heard anything yet. They had battle bots, dude.”

“Wait, really?” Wayne asked incredulously. “Like actual robots? Or the little ones in the cage?”

“The little ones in the cage,” the courier answered excitedly. “Though, yes, actual robots as well.”

Adabelle cleared her throat, getting the other’s attention. “Let’s get through this meal, and we’ll adjourn to the sitting room where we can trade tales of heroism, adventuring guilds, and schoolwork. Sounds good?”

“Oh! Oh!” Rashie suddenly yelled, raising her hand. “I got the best marks in the class on my magical studies test after you left!”

“And I would love to hear all about it, shark,” the demonkin said warmly, patting her on the head. “If you’re okay with eating later, then feel free to tell us.”

The girl turned from Adabelle to the plate of food in front of her.

“Don’t you dare shovel it into your mouth just so you can get to gabbing faster,” Wayne warned. “I taught you better than that.”

“Yeah, unfortunately,” Rashie sighed. “Okay, food first, then couch, then we trade stories!”

“That’s the best plan I’ve heard in weeks,” the courier said with a smile.

Trevor shared a look with Adabelle. Just like her, there was no need for him to run out of the door for places unknown. The roads did not call him, and the horizon did not tempt him. He knew that wouldn’t last forever, and he was okay with that. Wanderlust was in his soul, and he was finally in a place where he could indulge himself.

For now, Trevor was simply content to be home.

It was, he knew, exactly where he belonged.
