Aric watched her face when he said the name. He wasn't subtle about it, either. His eyes stayed fixed on her after the word left his mouth. Lira understood, with a sinking feeling, that this had been deliberate, a test dressed as an offhand statement.

A test she had failed... she had reacted to the name... there was no taking that back now...

"What's wrong, Lira?" Aric asked, leaning forward slightly. "Why this reaction?"

Lira's mind raced through every option available to her in the space of a breath. She could outright lie and hope he didn't have some way of catching it. But if a knight like Henry could do it, surely a prince with better access to magic would, too.

No, she couldn't risk it.

That left only one path: a strategic deployment of partial truth.

"I was surprised you would trust him, considering what he has been up to recently."

"What do you mean?" asked Aric, his eyes focused on hers, unblinking.

"A few weeks ago, when I went to the bookkeeping tower and helped Cedric sort through some ledgers… you know that I worked in the bookkeeping tower for a short while, right?" she said, trying to project a layer of guilt at a level of 'caught with a hand in the cookie jar' rather than the 'I am planning to purge this kingdom.'

She paused, waiting for Aric to nod before continuing,

"While there, I came across some documents Harwin had compiled... on Lord Kenth Kelroth."

Aric frowned at that.

"The documents were out of the way, in a side room," she said. "After reading them, I realized that I might have found something I shouldn't have, Your Highness." She paused before starting to speak at a faster pace. "You know me. I like to read everything and anything, and I can read really fast."

Deren smiled at that. At least her acting was working on him. Aric remained like a statue, unmoving. Celene was watching the exchange, slightly concerned at the shift in Aric's mood.

"What was in those documents?" Aric finally asked.

"Property records, mostly. The Kelroth estate has been buying up gambling dens across the kingdom: taverns, night theaters, brothels, waystop inns, you know, that sort of thing. Most of it through proxies, at least according to Harwin." Lira put her hand behind her back and shifted slightly on her feet. "Something was off about them, though. The profits were doctored, I think. Every single property showed the exact same increase in revenue after the purchase, twenty percent, no more, no less. It was weird because businesses don't grow uniformly across several establishments."

"You think he's laundering money through them," Aric said.

"More than that." She shook her head. "I overheard Lord Friedric and his wife arguing in the library once. The man had lost all his fortune gambling, and his wife nearly took his head off because of it. I looked into it, and a lot of the nobles in the capital are suffering from gambling debt. I also found out that Lord Kelroth is lending money to people at a low interest so they can pay their gambling debt."

That got a reaction out of everyone.

"You are saying he is lending people money so they can pay him?" asked Deren, sounding dumbfounded.

"He is consolidating the debt from something owed to a random establishment into an official debt owed to a noble," frowned Celene.

"And how do you think that's connected to today?" asked Aric.

"I don't know, but he is obviously up to no good," Lira said. "He has his foot over the neck of the majority of the nobles. Handing him the crown's own treasury could spell the kingdom's doom, Your Highness." Her voice came out shaking, and Lira noticed that at that point she was no longer acting but talking from the heart.

Aric was quiet for a long moment. Long enough that Lira had to fight not to run.

"Why didn't you bring this to me immediately?" he finally asked.

"Because I'm a maid, Your Highness." She met his eyes head-on for this part. "A commoner who happened to notice things while snooping where she shouldn't. If word got back to the wrong person that I'd been reading Harwin's private files, I could be hanged for spying, and no one would think twice about it. Harwin is a trusted man, right? This is his information, not something I discovered myself. Surely he shared it with the crown, right?"

"He didn't," said Aric, his voice cold. "At least not with me, and I make sure to keep myself informed on those matters."

"Shit..."

"Shit indeed," said Deren.

Aric let out a breath through his nose and dragged a hand down over his face.

"Once again, you find something no one else caught. Last time it was the three missing maids and Hesta's involvement, and now it's Harwin's connection to Kelroth."

Lira went very still.

"You knew about that?" she asked. "That I was the one who found something about the missing maid."

She looked toward Celene, and the headmaid shook her head.

"No, Celene covered for you well," sighed Aric. "Baroness Valis told us about it, about what Kavard said to Luxia, and how you cleared your honor by outing Kavard's secret."

Damn it. I knew it would come back to bite me. I told her to keep it a secret, since it could get me in trouble... but why would she keep it? She owed me nothing. I was so used to her children's earnestness that I subconsciously trusted her.

Lira felt something cold settle low in her stomach. Was that why Aric had said Kelroth's name the way he had, testing her reaction? Had Henry's investigation about Lira Velora, whose father was killed by Kelroth, been the impetus? Did all the truth she shared now write enough to justify her initial reaction, or was there no shaking off suspicion when it had accumulated from too many angles?

"You are very good at this," Aric said. There was no accusation in the way he said it. There was a hint of admiration even. "Finding things from the slightest hint. Sharing them through a proxy or keeping them to yourself as you see fit."

"I don't know what you mean, Your Highness."

"I think you do." He held her gaze, and for once there was nothing playful in it. "I want to trust you, Lira. I have wanted to for some time now, if I'm honest. I could use someone with your mind working directly for me. Trained properly under Spymaster Miloud, you can help us make Halthar into a better place."

"I trust her implicitly," said Celene. "She helped me hide Maren when Kavard tried to assault her, then helped me clear up her name."

Aric raised his eyebrows at that. And Lira almost groaned.

What The Fuck, Celene? This is not the time for a heart-to-heart confession about my spy-like behavior.

"I trust her implicitly too," said Deren, winking at her. "She gave me the Sword Saint's manual that helped me defeat Salvek."

This one was better. It didn't implicate me in any crime.

"I want to trust her, but I can't." Aric shook his head. "If you want my trust, and the chance to join me to make this kingdom a better place for nobles and commoners alike, then we need to have one conversation where you lay everything on the table. No more careful half-truths carefully stated to not trip up a truth stone."

Lira stood very still and said nothing. Her throat had gone dry, and she didn't trust what would come out of it if she opened her mouth.

Aric watched her for a moment longer, then rose from the chair.

"Think about it," he said. "You have time. I will leave for the Holtary Mountains next month. You can tell me your answer when I am back." He paused at the door, one hand on the frame, and looked back at her one last time. "I hope you make the right choice, Lira."

Then he was gone, Deren giving her a small, apologetic nod before following him out.

The office was silent for a long moment after the door closed.

Celene finally exhaled and reached in a drawer for a bottle of wine and two glasses, and then started pouring.

"Well," she said, placing a glass next to Lira. "That's certainly one way to spend a morning."
