[Lamb POV]

Time is a funny concept.

I’d always thought of it as consistent—an immutable river that flowed in only one direction. Sure, there were rapids; sections where the river rushed past with impatience, each droplet carried along by its contemporaries, all desperate to reach their destination even if they had no idea where that might be. Times where life was chaotic and a million things happened in every heartbeat. But the river itself didn’t move any quicker. Quite the opposite in fact, at least in my experience. In a fight or a battle, a minute felt like a lifetime. It took only seconds for the world to change, so an hour was an eternity.

No, for me it was the slow times that slipped by quickly, as contradictory as it sounds. The times when the river was calm and its surface serene. Hours spent talking by the campfire just slipping away, days spent running through the world fading into obscurity. Just the river moving ever onwards, little droplets of memory carried away without notice.

I saw the crossbow pointed at my chest, and then I saw her eyes; clouded with pain, bright with anger. In that moment, my world stopped. Not the world, of course—that continued on apace—but my world. My life hovered on a precipice, and Varice’s finger tipped it over the edge.

The bolt flew with shocking speed.

A year of life and death battle had me reaching for my skills instinctively. Break-Step was there to greet me, the first to obey the command of my mana and intent. I was barely moving, a slight shift of momentum as I turned, but my feet were still rooted to the spot. It was movement I didn’t need, momentum I could sacrifice for the far more valuable resource of time.

They were two sides of the same spinning coin, after all.

The system had confirmed that truth, at least for me. Break-Step cut my momentum entirely, draining mana from my core instantaneously. I stopped, frozen in space, but in time I moved ever faster. Precious time, valuable time—I had brought myself a horde of treasure, but how could I spend it? To move I would need to trade the time I’d just brought. Were I to raise my shield or drop to the floor, it would cost all the time I had left.

But my mind still moved, my mana still obeyed my commands. And the bolt still flew. A Frozen Pyrre could manoeuvre my weapons into place, but my control was clumsy and my mind was working overtime and in sixteen different directions at once. My fang dagger started to rise from my belt, but the movement was slow and jerky. Besides, what was the point of deflecting a crossbow bolt from puncturing my lungs if my own dagger pierced me there instead?

Useless plan—discarded. I moved on. The Mountain’s Gate was immediate, supposedly. I had tested it in the mountains and found it to activate as soon as the mana entered the skill constellation, but my mana control was not instantaneous, and I had wasted precious time with A Frozen Pyrre already. Not to mention mana. Usually I could fight for minutes without exhaustion, but I’d been fleeing for a while, and my lack of fine control was causing me to waste precious mana as I shunted all I could towards the right skills to get me out of this mess.

No time to analyse, just instinct. The Mountain’s Gate was my most protective skill, and so I activated it. Mana surged through my channels, flowing from my core to the constellation in my soul and making it blaze bright in the void. Starlight; silver, blue, sterling and oh so beautiful filled the air before me.

And still the bolt flew onwards. It slid through the first layer of mana-forged starlight, barely slowed. My core drained at a precipitous rate, the speed and strength of the activation exhausting me between heartbeats. But the light cocooned me, solidifying into something more. The bolt was mere feet away now, and my skill was still forming.

A memory surfaced then; of the dream I’d become lost in beneath the Titan’s Crown, when I’d endured the agony of the Cursed Waters. I remembered the voice: “the mountains call to you brother. Will you answer?” I had answered, and now I called to them in turn. As the bolt came a hair’s breadth from my chest, the mountains answered me. I had spent my life in the mountains, shrouded in their embrace, and now they wrapped their protection around me with the force of my own mana.

My core guttered and my skills collapsed. My world met the world, our rivers colliding and mixing once more, and time resumed its relentless march forwards. The bolt fell to the floor, shattered into pieces, and starlight dissipated around me. I looked up in shock. It was the outcome I had expected—the one I had aimed for and begged for and wanted so desperately I’d spent all of my mana in a mere instant of time—but it still surprised me that it had worked at all.

Varice’s eyes met my own, and I laughed. She didn’t tough, just calmly reached behind her for another bolt. I frowned, tensed my legs and made to dash forwards, but her triumphant smirk stopped me. She didn’t even have the string cranked back—there was no way she’d load another bolt before I reached her.

Then the portal winked out of existence with a green fizzle. She was gone, the room behind her coming back into view. I turned on my heel slowly, eyes straining, ears straining. Every part of me straining for a hint at where she would next reappear. A flash at the corner of my vision had me diving to the floor. I rolled, bringing my shield up even as I turned to face the green glow. A crack as the bolt skittered off the carapace of my shield, sending a jolt up my arm that left my fingertips fizzing.

I’d found myself in a ranged battle with a woman that I couldn’t see and possessed the ability to teleport. I hoped my companions were in a better place than I.

***

“At least give me your name,” Varice called from the other side of the room.

I spun and threw Resolution with all of my might, hearing it sing as it cut through the air. It left a hole in one of the hanging tapestries and clattered to the floor with a tinkling sound that was strangely mismatched with its lethal purpose.

I was already moving, circling the edge of the room as it neared the spear. It had actually left a small scratch in the obsidian block, and given the Vault’s shocking solidity, it was a testament to my artifact’s power.

Varice let out a chuckle from somewhere behind me, and I slid to the ground, scooping the spear back up as I twisted to interpose my shield between us. I could have simply called Resolution back to me hand, using either the artifact link or A Frozen Pyrre, but my core was nearly empty and I couldn’t spare the mana.

“Would you like me to guess?” she called out again from a new position. Her tone was mocking, and while I knew she was attempting to get under my skin, that didn’t mean it wasn’t working.

“Call me Lamb,” I shouted back. “You deserve to know the name of your killer.”

We’d been playing the same game of cat and mouse for the last few minutes, with Varice popping into existence in new positions and attempting to shoot me full of bolts, while I tried to predict her movements and hurt her in turn. Neither of us had been particularly successful so far, but the fight—as frustrating as it was—was allowing me to regain some of my lost mana.

“You’re not much of a talker, are you, Lamb?” Varice asked, sending another bolt my way that I deflected by pure luck more than skill. “It’s a perennial problem with the young. You always sound as if you’re trying too hard. It comes off as stiff and unnatural.”

Her tone was casual, as if they were two friends having a dinner party, not trying their best to murder one another. It was frustrating, fighting like this, and I felt the familiar savagery of my early days threatening to take hold once more. No plans, no thinking; just run at the problem and fight until one of them died. But I was still in control, and the fact she was even attempting to rile me up surely meant something, right?

“You know,” I said, biting down a grunt of pain as my shoulder slammed into a wall as I evaded yet another bolt aimed at my chest, “if you want to just talk, we can do that.” I ducked beneath another swaying tapestry and reached the end of the room, pausing to pick up my trusty hatchet and slip it back into a loop at my belt. “I’m happy to put aside the fighting for a few minutes if you have something important to say.”

Another flicker of green light, another twang of a bowstring. Another evasion as I dodged death by a hair’s breadth once more. “Oh? Giving up so soon?” Varice replied.

“Why not?” I shot back. “I’ve got nothing but time. You’re the one that’s losing blood. You’re the one whose army is currently dying out there on the walls. You know what my purpose here is?” Varice didn’t say anything, but that silence was reply enough. “I’m supposed to tie you up. Waste your time. Prevent you from reinforcing the garrison until it’s too late.”

Another flash, this time far too close. I activated Break-Step, ducking and spinning even as I whipped my spear up to intercept the crossbow bolt aimed at my chest. My shield dipped, knocking aside the thing speeding towards my lead leg. It was a simple book, nothing magical about it, but I couldn’t take the risk and had ended up wasting some of my precious mana on the move. Better than the alternative though, and I was confident that Varice couldn’t keep this up forever either.

“Ah, so you were lying about it all?” Varice shouted from the other side of the room. Gods, she could move near instantaneously with that skill of hers. “Who are you then?”

“Back to asking for my name again,” I joked. I hadn’t been just blustering; I really did have nothing but time. She might not be in danger of death imminently given her enhanced constitution, but I’d dealt her a nasty wound. She could barely put weight on her leg, and while the Heart Of Winter might have actually helped her—freezing the wound and preventing major blood loss—it had still blown bloody chunks of muscle from her thigh.

“Who are you really, Lamb?”

I sighed. She hadn’t reappeared with another bolt yet. Either she’d run out or was simply biding her time. Either way, I wouldn’t look a gift horse in the mouth. “I am a God-Touched. Couldn’t have entered this Vault without that being the case. You know, it’s kind of ironic, this whole thing… If you hadn’t been hunting for me, Vera never would have found out about the duke’s plan, and we wouldn’t have come here and ruined it for you. But that’s just life, right? Mysterious and all that.”

“Vera?” Varice asked, and now her voice was different. Softer, colder—suddenly both more serious and less sure all at once.

“Oh, right! Yeah, you guys had no idea, did you? Fuck, it’s hard to keep track of all of this in the moment. No, Vera and Jorge picked me up way back in the Unclaimed Peaks. We’ve been hunting the Lions ever since, and we got our first major lead in Colchet. Francis is a talkative fellow, you know. He’s a noble, and all of you seem entirely too happy to wax poetic about your plans and your greatness. He actually gave me all the information we needed, and he’s the one who told us about this Vault. Finally convinced Vera to come back and here and correct the mistake she made all those years ago leaving the duke alive.”

Varice was silent once again, and I realised I’d been doing the very thing I’d just castigated Francis for. Fuck me, but I was monologuing! Still, there was a reason. Buying time, and all that.

“Is there anything else you’d like to know?” I asked to the empty room. “Or shall we get back to killing each other?”

Varice laughed. “No, you’ve given me everything I need.”

I smiled. “Let’s get to it then.”

Green light billowed out at the corner of my vision and I spun, shield already moving into place to block the incoming bolt. But there wasn’t one.

“Oh, Lamb,” Varice said sadly.

Something hit me in the side. I screamed, feeling my right arm crushed against my body. I flew through the air, crashing into a wall at speed. My shield was the only thing saving my arm from shattering, but my shoulder cried out in protest at the impact.

“For all your talk of silly nobles—and trust me, I share the sentiment—you’re not that different in the end.”

I looked up, vision blurring at the edges from the blow, and saw two purple chains hanging above me. One was even now returning to its position after having just slammed me into the wall. That explained the pounding in my head and my strangely wobbly neck. The chains were massive—the same ones she had ripped Estan asunder with, seemingly without much effort, and only now did I pause to wonder why she hadn’t used them in our fight since.

Now I knew. She’d been holding back, goading me into spilling my secrets and unveiling my master plan. The only silver lining here is that I didn’t have much of a master plan to begin with, and we were already far off-script, so hopefully my stupidity wouldn’t effect my companions too much. No, it was just myself that I needed to worry about now.

I shook my head clear. Instantly regretted it as I stumbled and fell to one side. When the world stopped swimming again, I clambered unsteadily to my feet, and saw Varice standing with one hand on her waist, hip cocked to one side. She was watching me with interest, her two massive purple chains hanging in the air above her. They were ghostly, dripping with dark mana and emerging from strange shimmers in the air—just like the portal she’d used to hide within earlier.

But she wasn’t hiding now. No, Varice walked languidly towards me with the confidence of an apex predator in her domain. “You let yourself grow a little too comfortable with your victory, Lamb. I can’t say I blame you, though. After all, I’m used to being underestimated.”

She muttered something—an incantation perhaps?—and lilac light bloomed within her palms. A rotating ball of constantly swirling sigils and glyphs that she drew back. I opened my mouth, ready to hit her with Axis-Shift just to try and do something, even if it wouldn’t work, but it was too late.

The light flew towards me, and then blackness enveloped me.
