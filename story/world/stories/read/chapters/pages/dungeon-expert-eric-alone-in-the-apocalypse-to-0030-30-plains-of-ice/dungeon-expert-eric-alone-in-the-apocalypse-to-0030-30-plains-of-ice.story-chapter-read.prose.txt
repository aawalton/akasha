Eric stepped into the dungeon, and out of fall and into winter. Snow crunched underfoot, and the trees spread skeletal limbs toward a cloaked sun. The sky and ground alike seemed to glow with diffuse light, the two almost blending into one another where the smoothly-gray-white-clouded sky met the soft snow-coated earth. Ice coated the snow so that each step punched through the upper layer of ice before plunging into soft powder beneath. Eric faintly noted that; it would make footing difficult in a fight. He (or his enemy) was liable to slip over the top ice, or else their steps would be slowed by kicking through it as they maneuvered through the powder beneath. He’d taken part in enough snowball fights-slash-snow wrestling-slash-no holds barred snow brawls with his sister to recognize the danger.

Wait a minute. This is guide material! I mean, it’s just basic snow advice, but it doesn’t hurt to get some practice in while I’m still in a no-threat situation. Eric’s eyes lit up. He could recognize it now! That was a step in the right direction. He cleared his throat. “You’re going to want to watch out for the footing in areas like this. It seems like it’s not that big of a deal, but look—” He rested his foot on the ice and skidded it across the top. “—it’s very easy to slip on. That, and once you punch through the ice, you have to remember it’s there.”

He stepped forward, punching through the ice again. “If I don’t lift my legs high, it’ll grab at my lower legs, like this. Not a big deal when you’re just walking around—well, it’ll waste some energy, but so will high stepping, so mox nix on that—but if you need to move quickly, it can trip you up when you least expect it.” He lunged. The ice caught at his ankles and slowed his legs a little, just enough that he stumbled a little. “Not a big deal out of a fight, but a problem in it where an inch can be the difference between life and death.”

He paused, looking at his imaginary camera. It usually floated about forty-five degrees to his right and slightly behind him. He didn’t know if there was such a thing as a camera, or what method the Game used to capture its participants, and there was certainly nothing there, just a patch of empty sky, but he had to have something to look at when he was doing his talk-directly-to-the-audience moments… which he’d apparently decided was forty-five degrees to his right and slightly up. “Well, those of you from the great frozen north are probably laughing at me. You’re masters of the ice. You’re hooking on your snow shoes and cross country skis and chuckling that anyone doesn’t know this stuff. This one goes out to the south, to everyone who’s seen ice once or twice in your life. Maybe even never. Remember, even the stuff that’s relatively easy to walk in can still trip you up in a fight.”

He showed his bare hands to the camera. “As you can see, I’m not carrying a weapon right now. The ground is too slick; I’m liable to fall down and gut myself if I carry anything bladed. I’d love to have that snake sword out right now, but I want to keep my guts on the inside more. As for my blunt weapons…” He pulled back his jacket, showing the Slugger on his belt. “I’ve got one right here in case I need it, but I really hope I can get through this with spells.”

Mentally reviewing the guide he’d just done, he shrugged a little. I don’t know if that actually deserves to make it into my guide, since it’s just snow walking, but the “be careful, you’re fighting monsters” version, but it’s good practice. He lifted his head and squinted across the snowy plains, desperately wishing he had some snow goggles or at least sunglasses. He was seriously risking snowblindness right now. Unfortunately, the little gift shop he was using as his base didn’t have any sunglasses for sale, let alone ski goggles, so he just had to do his best and squint a lot.

With no other features to head toward, he approached the forest. The snow continued uninterrupted in every other direction. Nothing prevented him from wandering off into the wilderness, but given the biting cold, he wanted to get in, figure out what was going on, and deal with it or get out before the elements got to him. The fluffy coat did keep him warm, but the wind pierced right through his layers of pants in the most uncomfortable way that made him unusually aware of his anatomy. I need to find some windproof pants. And then: I hope they have windproof pants. The closets hadn’t seemed very outdoorsy, which was unfortunate for him, but maybe they had extra storage he hadn’t found yet, somewhere they threw their winter gear in the summertime. He’d poked around everywhere he could think of to poke, but if they had attic or crawlspace storage, he hadn’t found the way in yet.

Something shrieked through the cold air. A gleam of light hurtled toward him. Eric dove to the side, and a arrow pierced the snow behind him. He turned back, staring at it. Did someone just shoot an arrow at me?

A dozen shrieks sounded, rapidly closing in on his location. He whipped back around. Arrows darkened the sky, momentarily suspended in the air overhead. Eric booked it, calling forth an Explosion at the same time. The arrows darkened the air overhead and spread out over a decent diameter, easily twenty feet of arrow-filled air. Instinctively, he tried to count them, but there were too many. A huge number of archers had made him their target, and they were determined to end his life by saturation fire.

The grouping of arrows was too wide, and he only had a few seconds. Eric sprinted as far as he could to the left, running toward the edge of the cluster where the arrows were less tightly grouped, then whipped around and threw an Explosion into the air over him.  It burst in a flash of heat, light, and force, and the arrows hurtling toward him were knocked aside, literally blown out of the air. They tumbled down, their momentum killed by the explosion and their streamlined bodies knocked sideways and all kinds of directions.

Eric breathed out in relief—and then the arrows he hadn’t knocked aside pierced down, riddling the snow where he’d been standing moments ago. For a second, he gazed at the hillock of snow that now more resembled a pincushion than a lump of snow, and then he charged for the forest again. If that had hit him, if he didn’t have Explosion, that would be him. He couldn’t stop. If he stood still, they could rain arrows on him until they ran out or he ran out of mana, and even with his expanded Mana Pool, he didn’t like his odds.

Another mass of arrows arced from the forest. Eric ran at a diagonal, proceeding forward and leftward at the same time. He’d noticed this clump of arrows a little earlier, and got out from under them before they rained down. The second they struck the snow, he turned sharply to the right, sprinting serpentine. The next set of arrows missed him by a mile, striking down generally where he would’ve been if he’d kept running left. He kicked off the ground again, meaning to race back to the left, only for the snow to go slick under his foot. Rather than kicking off the ground, his foot whiffed off the slick spot, and he landed on his face.

Instantly, the shriek of arrows sounded once more.

Shit! Eric rolled over, pulling his legs in to him to make himself as small a target as possible, then held an Explosion, pointing it directly over him. The arrows closed in. Three. Two. He loosed the Explosion, and it met the arrows on the way down, once more blasting them out of the sky.

Eric scrambled up. Moving a little more carefully this time, he sprinted toward the forest again. The forest was where the archers were, but it was also where the solution to his problem was. If he ran away, they’d just keep pelting them. There was only one way to deal with them permanently, and he was already very done with their behavior.

Luckily they aren’t good shots, he noted. Whoever was shooting at him, they had to go for big clusters of arrows to have a chance at hitting him. They didn’t have skilled snipers going for headshots, they had line soldiers doing their best to knock him down, spray-and-pray style.

He swallowed. I’m running toward a lot of trouble, aren’t I. It was too late for regrets; he was only about a hundred yards from the forest now. Close enough he could see the shadowy shapes of the archers on the ground and perched in the trees, bows drawn, their eyes faintly glowing under their hoods.

Eric ran on anyways, kicking his way through the ice. The archers sent one more volley his way, but he was ready with an Explosion—and a bag of flour from the general store portion of his current safe zone. He’d packed it in his inventory with the same attitude he’d packed in the I Love Truckin’ bag full of candy, but now, it came in more than a little handy. He pitched the bag of flour upward, still contained in its plastic wrapper, then hit it with the Explosion. The flour was dense enough at the moment of detonation that it didn’t explode, dust-explosion style, but instead filled the air with white powder. The powder drifted down on Eric, painting him white to match the snow, and hung in the air, clouding the archers’ line of sight. He darted to the side and ran on. The next volley screamed toward the cloud, nowhere near Eric.

Now’s the time! The second he heard the arrows’ telltale shriek, Eric charged toward the forest. He sprinted with all his strength and form he hadn’t used since high school track team. His newfound System strength and speed pushed him faster than he’d ever ran before. His hair streamed straight out behind him and he felt the wind tug at his face, pulling his cheeks back against his bones. The archers moved as fast as they could, but by the time they’d strung their next arrow, Eric was already on top of them, bare feet from the forest. He pointed his hand at the closest one, aiming for center-mass. “Firebolt.”

Fire leaped from his hand. The archer instinctively blocked, raising their bow between themselves and Eric. The fire alit on the bow, splashed past, and struck them in the chest. Eric reached them a second later. Running on pure adrenaline, he punched them hard in the nose. Bone crunched, blood splattered, and the mysterious shadowy figure went down.

Eric looked at the figure, then at his fist, then back at the figure. Damn! He was used to his punches being a joke, not a serious threat. Even with his low strength growth, he was still able to punch hard enough to kill now. Sure, most adults could kill with a lucky hit (office worker Eric no exception) but this felt… different. He’d changed. He was stronger now.

An arrow shrieked past his ear, almost cutting his face, and he jumped behind a tree. He was safer in the forest, but he wasn’t safe yet. There were still plenty of archers to take out. Plenty of… he glanced at the nearby corpse. He wasn’t sure what. The corpse had a humanoid frame, pale, bluish skin, white hair, glowing red eyes, and a pair of long, almost scoop-shaped ears, reminiscent of a cow’s ears. The dead creature was about four feet tall, with a slender, almost child-sized frame, but adult proportions. It wore black armor made of some kind of leather with shiny black plates mixed in. He nudged it with his foot, rolling it over to find a quiver full of arrows. Some spilled out, revealing standard arrows, but with a strange attachment on them not unlike the whistles he’d seen as a kid; probably the source of the shrieking, and possibly the source of their accuracy issue. What the heck is this thing?

A tool tip appeared over it. Noku, it read for a moment, then shifted. Ice Elf.

The Game sure has a loose definition of ‘elf,’ Eric thought. They were archers, so the shoe fit if he squinted enough, but he wasn’t sure he’d call whatever this was an elf. Then again, he didn’t have any context for whatever a Noku was, and Ice Elf at least fit the setting and the weaponry, so he couldn’t complain too much. Or is Noku their real name, and Ice Elf just a tag the Game added for my benefit? He knew it was ridiculous, but still; he didn’t want to humiliate this race any more than the Game already humiliated them. Goodness knew the Game didn’t need any help in that regard.

The body vanished, dissolving to black smoke at the same time Joe’s voice sounded in his head.

Don’t worry about it, kid; use their contextual name. I did all that research for you bald apes, you might as well make use of it.

“But still…”

Noku isn’t their real name, either. It’s something contextual from the previous Game they appeared in. Seriously. Don’t worry about it.

Eric’s concerns somewhat assuaged, he nodded. If he had no option, then there was nothing he could do. Another arrow whistled past the tree he was on, and he snapped back to the moment. He didn’t have time to feel bad for his enemies. Right now, he had to lock in and kill them.

Ironic. Using the wrong name for them gave me pause, but I feel nothing for killing them. It was the Game; this damn Game. It made everything so video-game-like that his brain stopped operating properly. Sometimes, it almost felt like he was playing a video game and commentating on the couch rather than actually present, in a kill-or-be-killed situation with an Ice Elf-infested forest full of archers who wanted him dead.

Eric took a deep breath and cracked his knuckles. Nothing he could do but keep moving. He drew the bat from his belt, then counted, waiting. Seven. Eight. Nine.

At ten, another arrow whistled past the tree. Eric whipped around it on the other side and charged, darting toward the next-closest archer.
