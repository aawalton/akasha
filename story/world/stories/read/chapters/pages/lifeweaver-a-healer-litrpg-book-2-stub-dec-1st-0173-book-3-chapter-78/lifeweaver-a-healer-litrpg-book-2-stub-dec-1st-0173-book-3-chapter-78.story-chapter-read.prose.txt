Dot's leap took Luke by surprise, but that didn't mean she got as much as a punch in. Calling her slow would have been a compliment, and even if she had managed to land a blow, she would have just hurt her hand. Cold anger spreading through him, Luke reached out with several threads of mana, holding her back.

"Tell me you're joking," he said.

She jabbed her finger at his face, then at the now-dead Lifeweaver on the floor. "Kill! Kill! Kill! That's your answer to anything."

He shook his head. "And here I was thinking you were starting to grow some balls. Look around you. Where do you think we are? What do you think we have become?"

"We're human. Same as him!"

"He's a dead human, you're not. Would you have preferred it the other way around?"

Dot quieted for a moment, but the fight didn't leave her eyes. When she next spoke, it was with a quiet intensity. "You could have stopped him without killing."

Exasperated, Luke turned to Luom, who looked like she'd gathered herself after that first moment of shock. At his look, her gaze twitched to Dot, then to the now-empty spot on the floor, and back to Luke. "Lifeweaver Dorothy is correct from an ethical perspective. B-"

"Thank you!" Dot shouted, raising her hands over her head as if to signal she couldn't believe how much of an idiot Luke was being.

"But!" Luom continued. "Sometimes we must make difficult decisions in life and death situations. In this case, I believe Luke made the right one."

"You told me you don't fight," Dot argued.

"I don't. That does not mean I don't see how it is sometimes necessary to do so."

"Let's just go," Luke said, not wanting to waste any more time. "We'll never see eye to eye with this, and I don't expect you to thank me."

"For killing a man."

Luke had already started walking and looked over his shoulder without stopping. "For saving your life."

"I'm not going."

That made him stop and turn back, and he raised an eyebrow with a chuckle of disbelief. "You're joking, right?"

"No."

"You won't survive."

The look of utter contempt on her face took him aback. Dot couldn’t even stand to look at him. Rather than feel more anger or frustration, Luke found he couldn't muster a single emotion in response.

"Fine," Luke said, turning again. He didn't look back.

A moment later, Luom caught up to him, panting a little from the run. She said nothing, and neither did he. He healed human Integrated as they made their way through the corridor, and she stopped a few times to heal Singers. Luke didn't help, but he didn't stop her from doing it either. More than anything, he was getting tired of this dungeon. It felt like something designed to stall for time so that he couldn't help his friends.

As they walked, they spotted another furry little Lifeweaver. Like Rem, it ran. More types appeared. Most of them ran. Those who didn't, attacked. Before they'd been separated from Dot for more than a couple of hours, Luke had been forced to kill some sort of screaming goat walking on its hind legs that kept and a reptilian monster with eyes all over its head, both of them Lifeweavers, of course. It was like being in a fucked-up fever dream he couldn't wake from.

When they found stairs, they descended. Once down, they walked until the next set of stairs. At one point, Dot's echo jumped far below them, then disappeared. Either she'd been able to get out, or she... hadn't. Luke set his jaw and pushed on. Whatever had happened was on her, not him.

"It's a maze," Luom said.

They hadn't spoken in a long while, and the sound of her voice made him jump a little. "The worst maze I've ever seen. Just endless corridors and stairs."

"Perhaps we're doing it wrong."

"What do you mean?" he asked.

Luom looked thoughtful. "What if it's a puzzle? There might not be an end to the floors."

"The quest says we need to make friends with everyone or kill them, or everything goes to shit."

"Do you believe it?" Luom asked.

"What do you mean?"

"If I fail the mission, do you think my system will cease to be?"

When Luke first read the quest description, he wasn't sure what to make of it. That hadn't changed, and he'd just opted not to think about it too hard. In doing so now, he shook his head. "That wouldn't make any sense. I don't know what's going on with allowing systems like yours to invade other systems, but there is no scenario where they, whoever 'they' are, would just exterminate an entire system."

"I agree," Luom said, nodding. "If we have a common enemy, bolstering our combined strength should be the ultimate goal."

"And all our systems hinging on the outcome from a single dungeon? I don't think so."

"Agreed," Luom began, but Luke interrupted her.

"So, what is the point of this dungeon? Pink dungeons are supposed to be special, but with the exception of the Relian replica and a whole lot of Lifeweavers running around all over the place, I fail to see the damn reason for it."

"Whatever it is, I doubt it has to do with healing humans or Singers."

Luke shook his head, grumbling. "Agreed. We've done enough of that. I'm not even getting echoes from them, so I can't use that."

"What was that?"

"Nothing."

Luom gave him a long look, then nodded, as if to say it wasn't any of her business. "The monsters then."

"What about them?"

"Perhaps they hold the key to this dungeon."

Luke watched a Relian monster clone come running down the corridor. There was nothing to distinguish it from the hundred others that had come before it, but it was worth a shot.

"Hey!" Luke said, stepping out in front of it.

Not-Relian stopped, but looked over Luke's shoulder with an expression of urgency. "Yes?"

"We need to leave. Where's the exit?"

"There's no exit," Not-Relian said with a chuckle. "Not yet."

"Then how do we leave?" Luom asked, coming up to stand shoulder-to-shoulder with Luke. "What is the purpose of this place?"

Not-Relian sighed. "We do not question. Those who arrive in our halls either live or don't. That's the only way out, through motes of light. A door must be constructed."

The fake doctor tried to push past, but Luke stopped him with a hand on his chest. "Constructed how?"

"Do I look like a carpenter to you?"

Luke's shoulders sagged, but then not-Relian spoke again. "I woke up with this. Just take it and let me through."

A key. Large, rusted, and crude, like something to open the door to a medieval dungeon.

"That's... something," Luom said once they'd let the monster go.

It was a mundane key, and inspecting it returned nothing. Luke turned it over, frowning. "What are we supposed to do with a key if there's no door?"

Luom gave him a helpless look. "Perhaps we are supposed to be the carpenters?"

That made him stop the examination of the key. Peering over at Luom, he slowly nodded. "I think maybe you're right."

"I am?"

Excited, he nodded. "Maybe. I think so. Let's find an empty room."

The closest patient room was empty except for a human Integrated in the bed. She wasn't far from death's door, but Luke healed her and sent her on her way, barricading the door with a cabinet and a table while waiting for the woman to turn into motes of light and float away.

"What are we doing?" Luom asked, giving the now-blocked door a look of doubt.

"Building a door," he said, getting up on the bed. "Keep watch. If someone tries to get in, let me know."

Luke didn’t hear her answer as he was well on the way into the submerged, soothing state of meditation. The weave of this dungeon was different from Earth's, with threads that didn't make much sense to him. Not only tough to follow, but they were also connected to each other in strange, unfamiliar ways. If pressed to explain it to someone unable to see, he wasn't sure how he would have done it without getting a lot of strange looks. Though perhaps the same could be said for Earth's weave.

Despite its strangeness, Luke found it comforting once he achieved the state of mind required to look beyond the mundane reality of the dungeon. It was a mess, but as a Lifeweaver, it was his mess. Luke searched for a suitable thread, then braced himself as he grabbed it. This time, rather than using weavestep, he held on. The word 'pain' was inadequate in describing the sensation of doing so, but his recent practice had made it bearable, if only just.

Forming what amounted to a live wire with electricity running through it into the beginning of a pattern he'd witnessed a few times before, Luke reached a thread out to another, pulling it in close. Once those two were in position, he drew in a deep breath and went back for a third. By the fourth, his nose was bleeding. By the fifth, so were his eyes. After six, it felt like his brain was on fire, and once the seventh and final thread was in position, the sensation was akin to someone taking a cheese grater to his skin. Even so, he persevered. Pain was nothing. It was unimportant.

Having formed a small part of the weave into the pattern he'd seen in a recent dungeon, and glimpsed before that, Luke held back, unsure how to continue the work. A spellweave is never still. Twisting and turning on itself, every spell he'd ever seen pulled into existence had moved in some way.

With an effort of will, he took ownership of the weave-based spell pattern and forced it into a swaying dance back and forth through the system weave. To bring it home, he needed a way to link himself to the spellweave, or it would stay in the metaphysical realm until it decayed, doing nothing at all.

Using a single thread of mana emerging straight from his own Meta-Heart, Luke reached out to his newest creation, his entire body trembling with anticipation.

A pink flash filled his vision, and a portal took form, building from a single speck in the weave as Luke pulled it into the physical world. He wanted to laugh, but doing so would shatter his concentration, so he settled on a strained smile as he pulled the opening portal toward himself. With another flash, it opened.

Luke's mana dropped lower than ten percent in the blink of an eye. The weave disappeared, pulling the portal with it, dragging it out of his hands, the thread connecting it to Luke disintegrating. If Luke didn't know better, he would have said he heard it snap, but it might have been the sound of his head hitting the floor after he flailed so much he rolled off the bed.

Panting, blood and sweat pouring off him, Luke pushed himself up, fell again, then struggled to his feet, clinging to the bed.

"What happened!?" Luom shouted, her voice full of worry.

"Mana," Luke gasped. "I need mana!"
