Charlemagne’s blast continued long after every single trace of the four-armed giant had been scoured from the throne room.  The solar radiance pouring in from above continued, forcing the rooster to continue to expel mana.  And, with the System lockout having been resolved, the potency of the mana was slowly returning to its original strength.  Unfortunately, even the rooster’s ridiculously potent physique wouldn’t be able to survive the blistering radiance much longer.

But in the short period of equilibrium between destruction and regeneration, Charlemagne had undergone rapid adaptation.  His Ignition Core had been polished like a precious stone by the sheer quantity of ultra-pure mana that had cycled through it, condensing decades’ worth of natural refinement into a few short minutes.  The Core had also grown more adaptable as well, allowing it to process the energy contained in high-energy photons more efficiently.

The outside world faded almost entirely away as the rooster turned his attention inward, examining the changes that his harrowing experience had wrought on his body.  He was surprised to find that his Core wasn’t the only part of his body that had improved.  His mana channels had taken a serious beating, but that turned out to be a blessing in disguise as I Lost the Blueprint took over, redesigning the damaged network to be not only more efficient but also more robust.

Charlemagne’s physique had also benefited from the experience.  His muscles, organs, and bones had marinated in highly compressed mana, granting them strength far beyond unenhanced flesh.  Similarly, the exposure to massive quantities of thermal energy had given Inferno’s Embrace a tune-up, a change that synergized well with Charlemagne’s well-tempered body.

The smell of burned feathers finally forced Charlemagne to abandon his inward examination and step out of the fiery furnace that had served both as the means to destroy the leader of the invaders and as a whetstone for his abilities.  The first thing that he noticed was Phatagin floating high above him, but something was off with the pangolin’s mana signature.  His Momentum Core, or whatever it had evolved into, was unstable.  For a prodigy like the rooster, this would merely have been an inconvenience, but the fluctuations in the Core’s signature were dangerous to the average creature.

The second thing that Charlemagne noticed was that Elidi had joined him.  Although the solar radiation was quickly mending the damage she had suffered from overexerting herself, her emotional state was another matter entirely.  She was letting out short, angry bursts of sound that took the rooster a moment to recognize as crying.

“Why?  Why?  Why wasn’t I stronger?  Why did you have to die instead of me!  My baby sister…little Phasima…why couldn’t I keep you safe?”

The Sun Queen looked up towards the ceiling before adding.

“And why didn’t you send reinforcements, System!  This could have been avoided if you had sent backup.  Even if you didn’t send him, there were plenty of other heroes that could have turned the tide.  We got lucky this time!  If this bird hadn’t been around, I’d be dead, and your precious Elite Systemic Games would have been for nothing!”

Elidi paused as if listening to a voice that only she could hear before continuing her complaints.

“I don’t care that he was busy when the lockout started and then couldn’t portal in!  What was so important, huh?”

She waited a few more moments before letting out a long sigh that turned into a sob.

“Fine…fine.  Just…just give me a few moments.  Yes, of course I want revenge.  But my Core is damaged, and I’m not sure how badly.  And I want to say goodbye properly.”

Charlemagne was confused: why was the Sun Queen’s Core damaged?  Hadn’t she integrated Phasima’s Core properly?  And given the fact that her level was so much higher than his own, why had she struggled to maintain her solar beam?  Curious, he tried to utilize his Monocle of Refinement to evaluate Elidi’s threat level, but, for some reason, the Squiggles failed to appear.

The unresolved questions on the gap between his and the Sun Queen’s power swirled around Charlemagne’s head until a pained grunt pulled him out of his introspection.  Phatagin was slowly descending.  The rooster noticed that the pangolin’s front claws were pressed against his chest, while his breathing was shallow and rapid: both signs that he was in pain.

“Bawk?” the rooster asked his Squire.

“I am quite worried that I have overexerted myself quite badly,” Phatagin panted as he pushed the Predator’s Beak up away from his face.  “Everything inside of me hurts.”

Charlemagne pulled on his Vertebrate Anatomy and Mana Surgery Skills, allowing instinct to guide his senses as they examined the pangolin.  For once, he found that Phatagin had not been exaggerating: his Squire was in rough shape.

“Bawk,” he explained.   “Bawwk bawak?”

“No, it does not bring me comfort knowing that I have several minutes before my entire endocrine system shuts down.   I hope that the System offers me a method of healing myself, perhaps a Boon Token? Oh, I have lots of Shop Points left!  I could buy one!”

The pangolin stared off into space for a moment as he gently landed next to Charlemagne and sank to the floor.

“Fungal ants!  The System rewards are not available yet.  Charlemagne, you simply must help me, or I shall perish!”

Josephine took the opportunity to call down from the balcony, “Ser Charlemagne!  Saving your Squire’s life will likely positively impact your overall Quest performance!”

The rooster turned his head and glared up at the annoying Attendant before taking a deep breath and reaching out with Mana Surgery.  It wasn’t difficult to use, per se, but he had gotten used to having the Skill assist him with the heavy lifting.  Without the Squiggle’s continual guidance, he had to do everything manually.  Calibrating the correct levels of each hormone that was supposed to be in the pangolin’s bloodstream and ensuring that the hypothalamus, pituitary, and adrenal glands were secreting the right amounts was both time and focus-consuming.  Still, it was easier than repairing lungs one alveoli at a time, and once he’d gotten the hormones situated, he was able to repair the damage to Phatagin’s organs without too much trouble.

“Thank you, everything hurts, and I’m exhausted, but I somehow feel better,” Phatagin groaned as he closed his eyes.  His breathing was deep and steady, indicating that the surgery had been a success.  Still, there was something wrong with the pangolin’s Core, but the rooster had no idea how to deal with that.

Turning his attention away from his Squire, Charlemagne sensed Josephine approaching.  She stood beside him without saying anything, and, after a moment, reached out her hand to pat him on the back of the neck.  He allowed it.

“Ouch!  Your feathers are like burning coals!” the Attendant complained.

“Bawk,” the rooster answered proudly.

“Yes, yes, you’re much hotter than you used to be,” Josephine responded.  “Not that you should consider it a good thing.”

Charlemagne disagreed but said nothing.

Elidi suddenly looked over at the rooster.

“Did you get an offer too?” she inquired.

“Bawk?” Charlemagne responded, confused.

“To take the fight to these terrorists, I mean.  There are always wars ongoing between the System and a number of anti-System groups.  The System rewards its soldiers well, which means that there’s no shortage of volunteers, but, well, the casualty rate is so high it’s practically a death sentence.  Apparently, the group that attacked us here is called the Severed: they represent universes that once had the System but were able to reverse the integration somehow.  Not content with that, they fight to remove the System from others, whether or not they agree.”

“Bawk, bawwk,” the rooster denied.

“Well, I’m going to take the System up on the offer.  If I succeed and become strong enough, it’s possible that I can find a way to separate Phasima’s Core from my own.  Maybe if I do that…just maybe,” Elidi broke off midsentence before speaking up again.

“But you, you’re from a newly integrated world, right?  You’re probably not likely to progress any further on your home planet for a long time, not until the ambient mana is dense enough to sustain high-level Dungeons.  Are there any other worlds that you could reach that have been integrated for some time?”

The rooster’s lack of response more than adequately answered the Sun Queen’s question.

“Well, then you have no real way to keep leveling up, not to mention the requirements needed to further evolve your Core and undergo Tempering.  Maybe if you flew close enough to your planet’s sun, you’d be able to get some gains, but that’s not a prospect I would bet on.”

The idea of challenging the sun’s full fury excited the rooster for a moment, but then he recalled that Phatagin had once told him that it would take years for him to reach it at top speed.  How the pangolin knew that, Charlemagne couldn’t say, but he certainly did not wish to make such a journey in any event, much less without the guarantee of a reward.

“Bawk?” he asked.

“I’m sure that the System would allow you to go there if you volunteered.  You might even be able to claim some rewards up front: after all, you just participated in the war effort, even if you did not know it.”

More rewards always sounded good to Charlemagne.  And, speaking of those…he asked the Squiggles if he could receive his Quest rewards.

The rooster squawked in annoyance at the delay, before turning his attention to Josephine, who was busy nursing her singed hand.

“Bawk?” he asked her.

“My contract requires me to be your Attendant in the Elite Systemic Games,” the woman responded, her tone brooking no argument.  “I have no intention of setting foot in a combat zone, nor should you, for that matter.  Take your rewards and go back to your farm, Ser Charlemagne.  Learn to enjoy your life before seeking to throw it away.”

Charlemagne had expected that the Attendant would be reticent, but he had not expected her to attempt to dissuade him.  Perhaps she really did care about his wellbeing.

“Bawk?” he directed the same question to Phatagin.  The pangolin slowly opened his eyes and sighed.

“I am simply not cut out for this level of danger, and my Patron needs me on Earth.  It has been, well, it has been a profitable if difficult journey, but we must part ways here, Charlemagne.  Perhaps we will meet again, once you have returned to Earth.”

A strange sensation welled up from deep within Charlemagne’s innermost being.  It was similar to, but not quite as intense as, the time that his hens and chicks had been slaughtered by the metal-clad human who had merged with the giant cat.  The feeling insisted that something important had been lost.

While the rooster was still grappling with his feelings, a flash of metal fused with flesh swept through his peripheral vision.  Charlemagne spun, thinking back to the battle he had fought at his farm in Parakou, and almost attacked before realizing that the being standing near him was not a threat.  It was Teem…and also Teem.

The strange human-like creature was more massive than before, standing almost a head taller and quite a bit broader.  His armor had changed as well, looking even more cobbled together than before but somehow thicker and stronger.  But the biggest change was that Teem’s head was now mounted on Teem’s left shoulder.  The bald skin and softened features made the extra cranium look like an overgrown deltoid.

“Teem will seek his revenge as well,” he announced as if discussing the weather.

“Uh, Squire Teem, are you quite all right?” Josephine asked.

“I am Ser Teem now.  Squire Teem is no more,” the two-headed man rebutted.

“Ohhhkay,” the Attendant said with a grimace before changing the conversation.

“You are aware that even Charlemagne is going to struggle to keep up on the front lines?  As far as I can tell, he is much stronger than you are.  Why are you throwing your life away too?”

“Teem is much stronger than Teem was,” the man responded, as if that settled the argument.

Elidi turned away and began to float higher in the air, basking in the power that poured into the throne room from above.

“If you are set on going, then please go now.  I wish to be alone.”

Understanding the link between solitude and death, Charlemagne was quick to grant the Sun Queen’s request.  After all, she had proven to be a solid ally, and the rooster would be dead had it not been for Phasima’s sacrifice.  The Sun Queen, he decided, would be an acceptable replacement for Phasima.  Likewise, perhaps Teem could, in time, fill in for Phatagin, although he had almost no idea of Teem’s abilities.

And so, Charlemagne asked the Squiggles for permission to join in the war against those who had invaded the castle, destroyed the Elite Systemic Games, and caused the death of his ally.  The Squiggles responded instantly.

A wave of excitement flooded through the rooster as he quickly made up his mind and told the Squiggles his request.  There was a long pause, so long that Charlemagne almost fell into despair, before a short response appeared.

And then, once again, Charlemagne found himself flung far beyond the boundaries of his current position in the universe, heading once again into a bloody unknown.
