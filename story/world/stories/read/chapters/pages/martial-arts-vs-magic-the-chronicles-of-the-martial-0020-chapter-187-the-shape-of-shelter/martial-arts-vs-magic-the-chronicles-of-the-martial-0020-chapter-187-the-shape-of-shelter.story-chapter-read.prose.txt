Ha-Yun had been a fool to almost draw her sword.

She realized this while sitting at her father's private meeting table, watching Iskandaar Romani eat a persimmon like he owned the building. He didn't. He owned the internationally wanted flying city, which was much worse.

The table was black lacquer, old enough that three dynasties had signed treaties on its surface. Tonight it held tea, a plate of dried fruit, and a persimmon that was now half-eaten.

To her left sat Monarch Jin Ha-Im, the Heavenly Mandate of Goryeo, an 8th Ascension ruler whose golden aura could make the palace stones hum when he was displeased. He was displeased now. Ha-Yun could tell because the stones were, in fact, humming.

To her right sat her younger brother, former Crown Prince Jin Ha-Young who’d given up his title so that his sister could become the Crown Princess. His spine was straight and his hands were folded in his lap with that careful stillness he used whenever a situation had grown beyond his ability to influence. He'd been like that since the moment they entered and found Iskandaar already seated, already served tea by a palace attendant who looked confused about how that had happened.

Behind her father stood Minister Zhou Byeong-Hak, a man of sixty-two years with the posture of a much younger bureaucrat and the political instincts of a much older one. He'd served three generations of the Jin family. He was also, Ha-Yun noted, the only person in this room who did not understand what was sitting across from them.

Which was why Minister Zhou spoke first.

"This is an insult to the sovereignty of Goryeo."

Zhou's voice carried the practiced authority of a man who had delivered formal protests to foreign dignitaries for decades. He stepped forward from behind the Monarch's chair. "A foreign wanted criminal enters the royal capital uninvited, breaches the palace wards without permission, and seats himself in the Princess's private quarters. Your Majesty, this man should be in chains. Why are we sitting here quietly?!"

Ha-Yun watched her father's jaw tighten.

The stones hummed a note louder.

"Minister Zhou," her father said.

"Yes? Should I summon the Royal Guard and have him escorted…?!"

"Minister Zhou!"

The Monarch's voice didn't rise. It didn't need to. Jin Ha-Im's golden aura pressed outward, just a fraction, and Minister Zhou's next word died in his throat.

Ha-Yun understood. Her father could feel it too.

The last time the Monarch had met Iskandaar Romani, the gap between them had been real. 8th Ascension against a mere 5th. The numbers alone told a story of distance that could not be closed by talent or trickery. And even now, on paper, the gap should have held. Her father was 8th Ascension. Iskandaar was 7th.

But numbers were paper, and what was in this room was not.

It was like standing beside a furnace that someone had left the door open on. The heat didn't move, didn't push, nor did it actively threaten anybody. It simply sat there, and everyone in the room had to breathe around it. Ha-Yun could feel it pressing against the edges of her fox-blessed senses, even though she was a 7th Ascension herself.

Her father, she knew, could feel it better than anyone here save for her blessed senses.

Iskandaar remained calm, smiling. He was sitting across the table in his black-and-gold robes, the lone-star sigil of the Heavenly Demon Divine Cult sitting over his chest and a ghostly white owl dozing on his shoulder. He watched Minister Zhou's outburst with the polite attention of someone watching a cat try to open a door.

"Minister Zhou," Ha-Yun's father said again, quieter now. "Let’s sit down."

Zhou looked between the Monarch and Iskandaar. Whatever he heard in her father’s voice, it was enough. He sat. Ha-Yun pressed her lips together, but a laugh slipped through her fingers anyway. She caught it with her hand, turning it into a cough.

Her father's eyes found her.

That look. Every child of a king knew that look. The I saw that and you will hear about it later look. How bothersome. Ha-Yun fixed her face and folded her hands in her lap, mirroring Ha-Young.

Internally, she thought about how absurd this was.

A globally wanted man, declared a fugitive by the Ethenian Empire, marked for death by the Church of Light, personally challenged by the Gold Dragon Arcane King, and fresh from a confrontation with the Maharani of Aryavarta – according to some sources.

And yet, she couldn't make herself afraid of him.

Part of it was because the same man was sitting in her father's private meeting room, eating a persimmon simply because he'd gotten bored waiting in her garden.

She'd tried thinking about it. When she first felt his presence in the yard, as her senses had screamed of an entity of at least 9th Ascension. When she'd drawn her sword and rounded the corner expecting an assassin or an Arcane King himself, she’d been afraid. She’d felt genuine fear, thinking Emperor Shengzong had appeared.

Then she'd seen him. Laughing at his owl, fidgeting with a volcanic stone on the table, looking up at her with those golden eyes like showing up uninvited to a princess's garden was something friends did.

The fear had died right there. For she was familiar with that notion.

He was still Iskandaar. The man who'd called Ao'kai, the Green Scale King, to save her life when Zhen Wei's lightning had stopped her heart. The man who'd killed the King of Xianli in a single night so that Goryeo's soldiers could stop dying.

A'okai had delivered the blow that mattered, certainly, but Ha-Yun knew who had asked him to come. She'd always known.

That knowledge was why she had accepted Lady Yueling's [True Blessing] despite knowing the risk, the cost. Demigods were not permitted to have Avatars in Vear'thia, because only the Twelve Gods and the strongest Devil Pillars held that privilege. What Lady Yueling had offered was technically a violation of divine law.

Ha-Yun had said yes anyway.

Because the world was moving, and she had decided she was not going to be the one left standing in the same place when it did.

Her father spoke.

"You've grown stronger, Iskandaar Romani."

Iskandaar set down the persimmon. His expression settled. The warmth in it didn't leave, but something behind it changed, the faint lean forward that meant he'd stopped playing the boy role. To be fair, he was a sovereign of his own right.

"Motivation tends to help. And I had a dozen reasons to be motivated."

"That is not a compliment. Strength gained this quickly invites scrutiny,” her father said. “It is demonic, some might assume. Although in your case, that assumption would be true.”

"I guess." A slight pause. "More than I'd have liked."

Her father studied him. Jin Ha-Im's golden aura pressed the room, and Ha-Yun watched what she'd never seen before. Her father pushed forward with it, centuries of the Jin dynasty behind the motion.

Iskandaar's presence didn't push back. It just… didn't move. The humming in the stones faded. Her father let it go. "Why are you in my capital?" Her father let a beat pass. "What do you want, Heavenly Demon?"

Iskandaar glanced at Ha-Yun. There it was, the look she'd seen in the garden, like none of this quite held the gravity everyone else in the room thought it did.

"Is this not what friends do?"

Ha-Young shifted in his seat, looking at his sister. Minister Zhou looked as if he were trying to swallow a live coal.

"You do," her father said, "but not when that friend is the Crown Princess of a nation that just ended a war, and you are a criminal."

Iskandaar tilted his head. "Fair enough. Alright, real reason,” he added. “I'm here because Emperor Shengzong is going to kill you."

The room changed. Minister Zhou's hand clenched on his knee. Ha-Young's careful stillness broke for just a moment as his eyes narrowed. Her father did not move.

“That is not a new hypothesis," the Monarch said. "My daughter feared the same a few months ago. I told her then that Goryeo's sovereignty..."

"Your Majesty, please.” Iskandaar leaned forward. The owl on his shoulder opened one eye, then closed it. "Do you know the kingdom of Jianghe?"

Silence.

"I've read the name in old records, yes," her father said slowly. "A Baolian vassal state. Dissolved two centuries ago."

"Hah, dissolved," Iskandaar repeated the word. "Jianghe was loyal to the Baolian Dominion for three hundred years. They paid every tribute and they sent more than enough soldiers when called. They delivered grain when demanded. And they never rebelled. They never even thought about rebelling."

He paused.

"What they did was produce a general. His name was Duan Feng. Ring a bell?"

Minister Zhou frowned. "The Crimson Halberd. A legendary man whose achievements earned him the role of commander in Baolian, despite being from a vassal state. He won the..."

"Eighteen campaigns. Yes." Iskandaar nodded. "Duan Feng fought all of them for the Emperor's glory. His soldiers loved him for it, because he was good. He was a legend, as you said. He was the kind of commander who actually brought people home."

"One winter, a group of soldiers in a border garrison made a bet. Nothing serious. It was a silly thing that men say when they're drunk and bored. They argued whether General Duan Feng's personal army could hold the Qinshan Pass against the Emperor's central forces. Not a rebellion, understand? A bar bet. An argument you'd have over dice."

Jin Ha-Yun listened to the story attentively.

"One of those soldiers happened to be the nephew of a court scribe. The scribe mentioned it in passing to a colleague. That colleague mentioned it at court."

The room was very quiet.

"Within a month, Emperor Shengzong issued an edict. He didn't arrest General Duan Feng. Someone as proud as him wouldn’t do that. Nor did he strip his command. No… He simply annihilated Jianghe."

Minister Zhou's face went white.

"The entire kingdom," Iskandaar continued. "Cities, farmland, population. The Crimson Halberd returned from campaign to find that every person he had grown up with, every family that had cheered his name, every building he had walked through as a child was ash. The Emperor's forces had been thorough. They'd salted the earth afterward. Duan Feng himself was left alive."

"Because the message," Iskandaar said, "was not do not rebel.” He scoffed. “Shengzong is a powerhouse beyond most. He didn't care about rebellion. The message was: do not become a name that stands beside mine in any sentence. As if he’s a God."

Nobody spoke.

Her father's eyes went to the table. To the lacquered surface, calmly like a man reading something written there. Ha-Yun knew that expression. It was the expression he used when he was checking arithmetic that he already suspected was finished.

When he looked up, his face was quiet in a way she hadn't seen before. Not composed but quiet.

"Goryeo's soldiers stopped dying," he said.

It wasn't a question. So Iskandaar waited.

"Under someone else's name." Her father's voice was careful and very level. "In the end, despite all our fears, we won. We won the war that we weren't supposed to be able to win, and the name those soldiers stopped dying under was not mine. It was yours."

He set his teacup down. The lacquer absorbed the sound.

"We made the same bet those soldiers made in that garrison. Over dice, drunk, as we argued about whether a different force could match the Emperor's own." He looked at Iskandaar. "We just made it with an army instead of words. And we were right."

He didn't say what being right had cost Jianghe. He didn't need to. Ha-Yun felt it move through the room. She watched her father's face and found something she hadn't expected, not fear exactly, but pure mathematics. Two plus two.

"And Shengzong," her father said quietly, "does not need us to have been disloyal."

"No," Iskandaar said. "He doesn't." He let it sit for a moment. "What happened with Xianli, A'okai, and the war as a whole has already given him the comparison he needs. You don't have to have done anything wrong. Jianghe didn't."

Minister Zhou's hands went flat on his knees. Ha-Young unclasped his hands in his lap. Her father did not move. Ha-Yun froze in place. So this peace, as she thought of it, had merely been a facade? And her father had been aware of it? Why hadn’t he told her?!

If the Baolian Emperor just so wished, he could come and wipe out Goryeo!

Ha-Yun didn’t know what to feel. Her thoughts were empty as she stared at Iskandaar, who remained calm. Then he reached into his robe and pulled out an orb.

It was small, about the size of a fist, with a surface of dark polished crystal that reflected nothing. It looked like a normal thing, but Ha-Yun had never seen anything like it, but she felt the weight of its construction, the density of mana compressed into it. Whoever had made this was operating on a level she couldn't identify.

Iskandaar poured Qi into the orb. Its surface rippled like disturbed water. Light gathered above the crystal, white and sharp, and then resolved into a figure. It was like a mirage!

Ha-Yun's heart stopped.

The projection was translucent, formed from refracted light, but the woman it depicted was unmistakable. It only captured her torso, which stood perhaps three feet tall in the projection, but that meant nothing because her presence filled the room like a wave filling a tide pool.

Dark hair in a low tail, with a few strands falling over her green eyes. An olive-fair complexion, stark in the projection's light, and above her brow rested a circlet of clear crystal shards that bent light and air around themselves, prismatic and impossible.

The [Crown of Emptiness].

Ha-Yun heard Minister Zhou make a sound like a man who had just swallowed his own tongue. Ha-Young's hands unclasped in his lap. Her father went completely still.

"It’s a little sudden, everyone, but please greet Her Majesty," Iskandaar said. "Maharani Indriyātītā, the Arcane Queen of Aryavarta. The Sage of Thousand Eyes."

The Maharani's projection looked down at the table. Her gaze moved from the Monarch, to Minister Zhou, to Ha-Young, and then lingered on Ha-Yun for a breath before returning to Ha-Yun's father.

"Monarch Jin Ha-Im," the Maharani said. Her voice came through the projection without distortion, clear as a bell struck in an empty room. "Iskandaar Romani has my support."

The Arcane King- or was it Queen? Since that was how Iskandaar introduced her? No, that didn’t matter. The Maharani hadn’t bothered to greet Ha-Yun’s father, and yet the man didn’t look offended. Monarch opened his mouth but didn't speak. Ha-Yun didn't blame him. What did you say to that?

The Maharani didn't wait.

"I understand your reluctance to associate your nation with a man the western powers consider dangerous. That is prudent. Especially when your continent’s sole Arcane King wants his head,” she added, “And yet, it is also true that your eastern border negotiations with the Xianli successor government have stalled because your treasury cannot afford the military garrison that General Park requested last month. Is it not?"

Ha-Yun watched her father's face drain of color.

Ha-Yun was scowling now. That information was classified. Not whispered in court type classified, but genuinely, it had been a sealed-chamber, three-people-in-the-room level of classified. General Park's garrison request had been discussed exactly once, in a private meeting between the Monarch, the Minister of Defense, and Ha-Yun.

Ha-Yun had been in that room. She'd sat at that table, and she'd set the privacy ward on the door herself, layering it twice because General Park's numbers were bad enough that she hadn't wanted them breathing through the walls. She knew which attendant had served the tea. That attendant had then been cleared four times for sensitive council access.

The Maharani had just read through all of it.

Ha-Yun’s eyes flew to the Arcane Crown on the woman’s head. That was the thing that let her see through all this, a crystal orb the size of a fist, from thousands of miles away.

She’d thought she understood what it meant to have sharp senses after becoming the Nine-Tailed Fox's Avatar. But this…

The Maharani had seen through a door Ha-Yun had warded herself and had pulled specific numbers from behind it without any visible effort. The gap between what Ha-Yun called awareness and what the Maharani apparently called a glance was something Ha-Yun was still working out how to measure.

The Maharani's expression held.

"To be clear, I am not making threats, Monarch. However, some words can naturally be perceived as threats, so I apologize if you feel threatened. I am merely demonstrating the quality of the ally being offered to you,” she smiled. It was a soft gesture. “My sight sees far and it misses little. If the Emperor of the Sun decides to appear in Goryeo, you will want eyes like mine watching his movements before his armies cross your borders. Or worse… himself.”

A breath passed.

"Consider it."

The light folded inward and the Maharani vanished. The orb went dark in Iskandaar's hand. Ha-Young's breath came out slowly. Her father hadn't moved.

Minister Zhou looked like a man who had been told the sky was a ceiling and someone had just shown him the bolts. His mouth moved. Ha-Yun watched him start to form the words Your Majesty, the habitual opener of a man who had managed difficult news for three generations of kings, and stop. There was nothing in forty years of diplomatic service that had a response category for this. He'd written formal protests to emperors. He'd negotiated border terms under drawn weapons. He had never, in his professional life, watched an Arcane Queen materialize in a dining room and voluntarily cite classified military intelligence as a courtesy.

He sat very still and looked at the place where the projection had been.

Ha-Young sat very still as well, but the calculation in his eyes had gone quiet for the first time in a long time that Ha-Yun could remember.

Her father was still looking at the place where the projection had been. His golden aura had gone quiet for the first time since Ha-Yun had entered the room.

Iskandaar grabbed the orb, and every eye tracked him. He paused, meeting their eyes. Then his lips curved up. He tossed it toward the Monarch, whose eyes snapped wide as he quickly caught it. “C-careful with it, Iskandaar!”

“You be careful with it, it's yours now,” he said, smirking. “Pour your Mana or Aura, and an Arcane Queen will pick your call. Use it wisely though, she might not look like it but she’s got a really bad temper.”

“....”

Everyone in the room was shocked. Such an artifact, he just gave it out like that?! How many people in the world had a direct way to contact one of the world’s true powerhouses? Incredible.

Then he looked at Ha-Yun.

His lips curved, just slightly. The warmth was there, the specific look she'd seen in the garden, and she understood it the same way she'd understood it then.

Not as reassurance, because he wasn't the reassuring type, but as acknowledgment.

Ha-Yun felt it. The flutter in her chest, sudden and warm, and for one breath she let herself have it. He’d done all this for… her. She hoped she wasn’t being delusional, because she couldn’t think of any other reason why he’d go this far. What other connection did he have to Goryeo, other than her?

So, Ha-Yun understood why butterflies fluttered.

Then she killed them all.

Jin Ha-Yun was the Crown Princess of Goryeo, the Spirit Sakura Syndicate, the Avatar of the Nine-Tailed Fox. The Heavenly Demon had just walked into her father's private room and displayed an Arcane King's backing like a card played at a gambling table. Every noble house in Goryeo would hear about tonight within a week. Every one of them would look at her and calculate.

What is the Princess's relationship with the Heavenly Demon? What does she owe him? What has she promised?

She thought about what it would mean. All of it. The protection and the price, as well as the shelter and the shadow.

She thought about the other women around him. Lilian Lunewolf, the Moon's Warden, granddaughter of a Legendary Hero. Nebula Carlstein, the Witch Crimson, who was the granddaughter of a mythical nightmare-class Vampire.

Solara Fenixia, the last of her kind. Amelia Duskleaf, the gold dragon princess who'd abandoned a father who himself was an Arcane King.

And by now, she was certain he’d filled his castle with a few more names. That was the problem, wasn’t it? Each of his lovers carried identities of such gravity… where would she fit?

More importantly, did she even want that?

She looked at Iskandaar Romani. He was still watching her with that faint smile. She did not smile back. But dammit, she wanted to.
