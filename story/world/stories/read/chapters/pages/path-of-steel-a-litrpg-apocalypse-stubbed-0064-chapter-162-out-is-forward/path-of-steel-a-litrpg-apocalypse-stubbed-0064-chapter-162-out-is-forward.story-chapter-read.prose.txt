Gary held his ragged breath. He instinctively tried not to make a peep, and the air built up inside him until it felt like his throat would explode. He exhaled sharply, then drew a loud, greedy breath.

It didn’t matter, anyway. They knew where he was.

Gary looked up, which required nothing more than to open his eyes. He lay flat on his back, half his bones broken while his regeneration worked overtime to fix them. Everything hurt. He could sense the cold soil under his back.

The only light came from the end of the hole he’d fallen through. It was a miniscule, diffused amount—not only was the hole thin like a well, but it was also cramped with hard vines which had speared through it like teeth.

He heard a loud bang. An aura burst washed over him, sticking his hair to the dirt. “Break it,” a voice ordered from above, and a chorus of impacts showered Gary. His ears hurt. The vines must have held, however, because he never heard them snapping.

Then came an even greater impact. The entire hole shook. Gary heard something giving way.

“You chose to seal yourself rather than let us capture you. A brave choice, but even this trap cannot withstand a level 40 sector boss. We’ll break through eventually. No matter how much time you earn, no one’s coming to save you.”

Asking for help was, indeed, Gary’s plan. A desperate attempt, of course, but anything was better than getting captured. He messaged the group chat.

Gary: Guys, I’m trapped in a hole in the orchard. They have the assassin brother, a level 40 sector boss, and a few field bosses. Can anybody save me?

ERROR: You are located in a chat-isolated zone. Messages cannot be sent or received during this time.

What!? His eyes widened. This had never happened before. Why would a random trap contain such a thing? Was it so insidious? He tried to message Quirk, but that also failed.

Gary needed to send a message. He’d never escape by himself. If the others could convince the boxing champion to come, or even Satan Jr.… The chances were low, but never zero. To even have that hope, though, he needed to escape this isolation zone.

Another massive impact rang above. Gary felt like he was in a drum that someone was violently beating.

“The isolator lasts for eight hours, Gary Steel. We’ll be through in four,” Jin’s voice came from above.

Gary gritted his teeth. “I’ll kill myself before you reach me!” he yelled.

“I don’t give a shit. Capturing you alive would be better, but your corpse will do.”

Gary pressed his eyes shut. Is there really no way? The trap earned me some time, but… What can I do?

Another great impact. Something broke. The light turned slightly brighter. “Come to daddy, little one…” the frog’s voice reached his ears.

Maybe I should just kill myself… Not yet, though. Until the last moment, I must endure. Maybe hope will appear. Maybe someone will come.

“Gary?” a whisper reached him.

“Goldie!” he whispered back. “Where are you?”

“I’m lying next to you. I’m scared…”

“We’ll make it, Goldie. You have to believe.”

“How?”

He didn’t have an answer for that. He fell silent.

“Gary?”

“I’m still here, Goldie.”

“I know that, silly. I can sense you. If you die, will you hate me?”

“Why would I hate you?”

“Because we didn’t make it…”

“Goldie, I would never hate you. You’re my best friend in the universe, remember? Even if we die, it’s not that bad. At least we tried.”

“Mm,” she said, perking up. “And we dodged the endless torture the puppeteer prepared for you.”

“Uh, yeah, that too.”

“Do you really think we can make it out?”

Gary pursed his lips. He wanted to say yes. “No,” he said. “We’re trapped, and there’s nothing we can do about it. You share my aura perception. You must feel it too—it is not random that I lie on my back, still alive. The trap was designed like this on purpose. It’s a coffin.”

Goldie gasped. Gary closed his eyes again, robbing himself of the little light. The more he healed, the more his aura field could spread, revealing exactly how fucked they were. Everywhere around him was densely-packed, magically enhanced dirt. It was so rich with aura he could taste it. He tried digging his fingers into it, but it felt like a mortal poking wood. He might have been able to dig through it if he could stand and wave his spear, but trapped in a lying position in this coffin-shaped hole, the best he could do was scratch the dirt with his fingernails. It achieved nothing.

He could even sense the dirt subtly pulling at him. It didn’t mean anything right now, but once he died and his body gradually lost its aura, the dirt would absorb him, strengthening itself and making space for the next unlucky climber. His clothes, his weapons, his aura stones, everything would be stripped of spirituality and absorbed until no hint of him remained. As if he never existed.

But Goldie wouldn’t be absorbed. She was eternal. She would forever lie down here, in this dark hole, with her only company being the unlucky climber who fell every once in a while. It made Gary glad that the horrors were coming for him. Goldie was precious—they’d take her along, and she would find a new wielder, eventually. She could live.

The colossal booms rang above as the frog tore into the vines. It was so loud Gary could barely think.

“We never stood a chance,” he said. “From the moment I enraged the puppeteer and we came to this war, our fate was sealed.”

“Don’t give up! There is always a way, that’s what my dad used to say.”

“Your dad meant the way to the nearest brothel, Goldie.”

“Hey! That was uncalled for!”

“Sorry, I’m just on edge. God fucking damn it. Just when I was starting to enjoy this.”

“You were? Oh! We can pray!”

“I don’t believe in God.”

“But you use His name all the time.”

“In expressions. It’s not the same.”

“You know God exists, right? He lives at the top of the tower.”

Gary’s eyes widened. “Really?”

“Yeah! Daddy told me!”

“Doesn’t the Emperor live at the top of the tower?”

“The Emperor lives on the 93rd floor. Nobody’s gotten higher. That’s the “top” toppers reach.”

“Really?” Gary asked, intrigued, before remembering he lay in a coffin of dirt under siege by overwhelmingly powerful enemies. “There has to be a way,” he muttered. With the initial shock over, and the pain of his regenerating body muted enough that he could think, he squeezed his brain for escape. He came up with lots of plans. Unfortunately, none worked. The frog kept smashing the vines above. The light coming from the top of the hole slowly but steadily grew brighter.

“Damn it. I wanted to experience more of this world, Goldie. Cultivation, enemies, friends, floors… I thought I was finally living, you know? I don’t want to leave so early.”

“Then, don’t give up! Keep thinking! You’re smart, you’ll find something!”

Gary gritted his teeth. Goldie’s right. I can’t just give up. I need to find something, anything…

His eyes brightened. Wait. There’s one thing I can try.

It wasn’t a real plan. It would probably fail, and even if it succeeded, it still wouldn’t save him. But it was something.

Gary told Goldie his idea. She materialized and tilted her head. “What’s the point?” she asked. “You’ll just suffer before you die.”

“I don’t care, Goldie. It’s not about survival. It’s about not giving up. Even if it will achieve nothing, me just lying here and waiting for the end is like surrendering. I won’t fucking do that. The Network, the Tower, whoever decided to play God... Even if they’ve already won, I won’t stop trying. I will resist to the end. I will never let my flame die. That is the only way to not betray Earth. Do you understand, Goldie?”

“No… But, if you want to try, I’m with you.” She wrapped around his arm. “Let’s do this, Gary. Together.”

Gary smiled. Death was far less scary if you went down swinging.

He had been level 25 for some time now, but he wasn’t ready to break through. He needed to increase his aura control by at least one more point. He needed to gather resources with aura-calming properties—aura stones weren’t going to cut it this time. He also needed the assistance of powerful cultivators to further compress his core.

If he tried to break through now, unqualified and unprepared, it would be much harder than normal. Even if he succeeded, his breakthrough would be weak, and even if it wasn’t weak, it would never be enough to defeat a level 40 sector boss. Even if all the stars aligned, it wouldn’t let him survive this.

But to stop trying meant surrender, and Gary would never surrender. Where there was life, there was hope.

His temper flared. His eyes shut, and he spread his awareness over his own body, completely withdrawing his aura field. Another bang came from above, snapping him out of focus. He focused aura on his ears and ruptured his own eardrums. It stung. A slight wetness reached his neck. The world went quiet—for the first time in his life, or so it seemed, he could hear nothing.

Speak to me in my mind if you need anything, he told Goldie. And let me know if they’re about to reach me.

He could feel the regeneration activating in his ears. He couldn’t control it, so he’d just need to rebreak the drums every time they healed. At least, his regeneration was slower because of all the broken bones before.

The bangs were now just tremors. They passed over him like waves over the sand. Gary was calm. He closed off the world. It was just him now, floating in an endless starry void.

Gary sensed his aura. It was rich, spread equally over his body, and most of it vibrating to a singular tune. That tune had long lost its name. It held something of the singing spear Kyle had taught him in the Floors of Trials—the singing spear he’d developed for himself. That core vibrational pattern was personalized by his black lightning vibration, his attainments in the spear, Goldie, and the Seed of Eternal Flesh in his soul. Even his fledgling body manual played a minor note in it.

All of those factors together formed a song, based on the Heaven Reaching clan’s singing spear but highly personalized to represent Gary. In the entire history of the tower, it was possible that no one had used this exact vibration before. He was carving his own path, and that made him proud.

He focused on his breakthrough.

Advancing from the Birth of Aura to the Core Formation realm was like adding an engine to a bicycle.

In Energy Harnessing, the cultivator filled their physical vessel with aura. In Birth of Aura, they vibrated that aura, using it much more effectively and also generating an aura field. However, in both those realms, the cultivator was limited by how much aura their body could hold.

The next step was forming an aura storage inside the body, from which the cultivator could draw at will. Their body remained filled with vibrating aura, but they could also store vast quantities of it in a compressed form which made it explode much more powerfully upon release. After transitioning to this stage, the aura of the body itself was more of a last resort, and the stored, compressed aura was what the cultivator mainly used.

But forming that core wasn’t easy.

Gary dove deeper and deeper into concentration. He forgot about his location and circumstances. He forgot he lay in a coffin. This lying position was just one he’d assumed to be more comfortable. The tremors that ran through him were a relaxing massage.

Every cultivator formed their core in a different place depending on their cultivation path. There were at least eleven possible locations, including the heart, the navel, between the eyes... Gary had already conferred with Master Colorful and decided which to use.

Gathering his willpower, he located the center of his abdomen and pulled. His aura resisted. It was so used to its current pattern that it staggered, unable to follow, and Gary realized this resistance was a product of his own mind. He was the one used to the patterns. He needed to break them.

With a tug of will, he upset the vibration of his body. The orderly patterns he was so used to broke, letting his aura rampage freely. He immediately felt how much of it he had—how fragile his body would be if he hadn’t learned to control it even in his sleep. Minor injuries appeared. A healer was usually nearby to heal the cultivator attempting the breakthrough, but Gary’s regeneration would be enough.

His aura yearned for balance. It yearned for order despite him cultivating chaos. It constantly tried to return to its familiar patterns, guided by everything he’d built so far, but he stopped it. To constantly reshape one’s path was one truth of cultivation. Instead of letting it be, he grabbed this wild aura and pulled it towards the core of his being, in the center of his abdomen. It wasn’t easy, but by this point, he’d developed great control over it. That 29 wasn’t for show.

Aura flowed in various streams, all ending at his core’s future position, where the aura simply gathered in search of a destination. More of it streamed in. It gradually grew crowded.

Gary’s temples pulsed. The more aura he managed to gather, the more it resisted. But, the more he managed to accumulate before forming his core, the more compressed it would be. The theoretical maximum was, well, all of it. Every drop. But that was impossible. Most cultivators gathered fifty to seventy percent of their aura, while geniuses aimed for over eighty. Anything below forty, and the core wouldn’t even form, causing the gathered aura to erupt outward in an explosion that riddled the cultivator with heavy injuries. It was salvageable with prompt healing. In Gary’s current condition, a failed attempt would kill him.

This was considered the last “easy” breakthrough on the path of cultivation.
