I’ve written several times now on the theory of the ideal form. Not a thing as it exists in Speranza, for all such things are imperfect for many reasons, if not just the compromise of circumstance and existence itself. I write of a thing as if it could be, if everything else essentially linked to it were to fall away.

A particular ideal form that I loved and yet hated was the Hero (with a capital H). Selfless and tireless, the kind of person who wins because they deserve to. I was raised to be that person. Euphridia made sure of it. Speranza expected it. And for a while, I even wanted it for myself.

And yet, running from that ideal form was the only rebellion I ever had.

But here’s what I had learned to this point: the Hero doesn’t win through virtue alone. The Hero wins by kicking dirt into an enemy’s face. By being sneaky with powers as they learn them, by accepting help when it matters. Every small act of cheating I’ve done, every compromise with that perfect, impossible form I held at arm’s length, had been a string of small but stacking victories all along.

So, as the Hero, what was the harm in leaning into cheating a little more?

Hadn't I worked hard enough?

“I’m ready to cheat for real.”

Even if it feels weird to say it like that.

As if maybe... it can't really work.

Jessamy raised an eyebrow. “Then why don’t you start by letting him help?”

“Who? Aurum?” I replied, jolting a bit in the saddle.

He turned his head to look at me, one ear cocked off to the side at the sound of his name.

“I’m not fighting him,” I objected, a bit confused. “I thought I was using his force in my attacks?”

Jessamy cracked her neck to one side, turning the movement into a look of vague disdain. “You’re not helping him help you. Why aren’t you extending your aura to him?”

“That’s... something I can do?”

Captain Armand gasped, but before he could speak, Jessamy threw up her hands. “Just who was in charge of your training?!”

“Captain Garvith,” I murmured. “Though I never learned to even properly ride a horse until after I left with Aleph and Relias—Damnit!”

Relias. Relias! It always ties back to him!

My pink aura crackled with rage as I smoldered, remembering I had been taught I wouldn’t have a horse in my final battle against the demon king.

That it could only be me alone.

Alban clapped his hands together sharply. “Focus on the present! All you need to do is let Aurum in.”

“In?”

“Your comrades have attested that you’re very good at reaching out to others, but how many have you let in?”

I hesitated.

“One… Maybe two.”

Thankfully, he didn’t ask for names. Olethros was someone I wouldn’t let go of, and Nora had insisted on coming in despite my walls.

Captain Armand straightened up with a knowing smile. “Then let the horse you ride be the third. He’s already with you. And what secrets do you have behind your mask that would matter to him?”

“None, I suppose, other than where I keep the rest of the apples.”

It helped to close my eyes. I still couldn’t see any of the holy knights’ auras, but this time, I wondered if it was because the Orders no longer existed, instead of my leaning away from Euphridia.

Or maybe the Orders no longer exist to them.

Or me?

But Aurum was there, pawing at the ground and snorting.

“I really wonder what you see in me. But I’ll try not to disappoint you, okay?”

Amity flared between us. And just for a moment, I think I completely understood him.

The ground churning under hooves at full gallop. New smells on new roads. Someone with him, rejoicing at what it felt like to run as fast and far as you can. Seeing him as a being and not just a mode of transportation.

“No, you’re definitely not a piece of equipment,” I agreed, opening my eyes. “Oh! Well, what do you know? Pastel pink looks good on you, too!”

Alban nodded solemnly as Aurum tensed up under me. “You should start with a walk first to make sure you—”

Aurum bounded off before I heard the rest, faster than he ever had before. Despite the knights’ shouts to return, the circuit lengthened on its straightaways to accommodate us. Eventually, however, Aurum was satisfied with our progress and returned for further instruction.

“Nora blessed his horseshoes,” Jessamy remarked. “He’s even faster than I thought he’d be, and in a pinch, he can even—”

“Become a weapon!” I finished. “I saw a horse stomp on a demon in the Wastelands, once—”

“Only in an emergency. Don’t give the General an opening to kill your horse! He’s not weak like his subordinates.”

I hugged Aurum's neck. “Right.”

“Pull out your saber,” Captain Armand commanded with a gesture.

Will’s hilt was very warm to touch.

“What’s this?” I asked, only for the sword to flicker darkly.

“It was pulsing during your ride,” Armand explained. “And so was your shield. If I didn’t know any better...”

“What, are you two jealous or something?” I asked with a bit of a chuckle. “Don’t worry, I didn’t forget about what you two can do as part of the team—”

Faith resounded in my mind, a cello repeating three distinct notes. Will joined in on the third round, with a deeper stringed timbre.

“I don’t understand what you’re trying to say—”

“Let us in.”

“Let us in!”

No longer the mere concerto of cellos, they said together: “As the collective Faith and Will of this world, let us in.”

“I thought you were the Faith and Will of Euphridia!” I objected out loud.

“You thought wrong,” Will snorted. “Let us in!”

“I…” I faltered. “Didn’t I already? I saw you two talking together earlier when you shared some skills, but—”

I realized only then that I had connected them to each other, and not myself.

Some Team Lead I am!

“Alright,” I relented, only to be subjected to their squawking onslaught.

“You tell us to work together, but you still do not embrace us!” Faith shouted shrilly.

Will grunted in disgust. “You limit us with your compartmentalized understanding. Do not constrain us with mortal labels.”

“I will shield. But I am not a shield,” Faith asserted.

“Reshape us as you see fit. Even as a saber, you think I am not long enough? What limits the length of a saber? Mortal misunderstandings! If I am reshaped longer, am I not still able to function as your weapon of choice?”

Taking the question to be rhetorical, I paused, becoming aware that the training yard around me had gone very quiet.

Armand’s eyes were wide and confused. Jessamy had stepped back, and Alban was spending equal amounts of time looking between us all.

“Uh, give me a few,” I said sheepishly before slamming my eyes shut once more. “Team huddle.”

“We are still waiting,” Will said pointedly.

Faith was gentler. “We have been waiting for someone to understand and let us in for quite some time.”

“I thought I did understand,” I replied. “But I guess I didn’t go far enough.”

“You did not want to need us,” Faith noted.

“Nothing personal, you understand. Or I suppose it is, isn’t it? You’re not just sentient weapons… I’m sorry that my understanding limits your form. Maybe I’ll try to—”

“You need us to be your sword and shield,” Will interjected. “Do not try to make us flesh.”

“O-okay. Sword and shield. Ideal forms of them, which depend on what I need, when I need them. All empowered by the collective Faith and Will of Speranza. Do I have it right now?”

There was a long moment of silence.

“Close enough,” Faith said with a soft chuckle.

Will sighed. “Any more and you’ll be overthinking it.”

Aurum started to stamp his feet lightly.

“Right. Time to practice more.”

Galenus appeared with a flash in the training yard, encircled in his customary floating runes. “This time, with the target visible. I wish to take measurements...”

Ragnerus flickered in on the other side of the yard, already charging as if he were the very embodiment of ball lightning.

Aurum and I didn’t wait for him to close the distance. Will was ready in my grip, a lengthy version of a saber, if you needed to give him a name. Faith was in buckler form, but even more determined than ever.

Look, I don't know how to describe how I could tell she was determined. Not a sound or a weight, but definitely more than a feeling.

Ragnerus’s greatsword came around in a sweep.

Clang!

Faith herself angled to meet before I could even issue the order to my arm, and Aurum’s momentum helped me redirect it.

Have you been pulling me along this whole time?

A flute trill of confirmation even as I struck with Will. I managed to hit, rewarded by a steely hiss but not enough pushback to signal a deep cut.

Aurum and I started to turn for our next pass. Even with a lean-in, the physics of the situation was less than cooperative.

It just took too long to pivot. By the time we righted ourselves towards the battlefield, Ragnerus had already repositioned himself. No longer having the disadvantage of the unknown, he surged forward as lightning once more. I willed my aura to solidify, asking for even more speed.

Will connected on our second strike as well, but Ragnerus turned to mist before any meaningful impact was made.

Aurum never hesitated. He veered away from the cloud, a decisive sidestep that let the mist pass harmlessly by our side. His subsequent turn back, however, was still too slow, and Ragnerus had already fully reformed before we could set our sights on him.

“A moment,” Galenus shouted just as Ragnerus dissipated entirely. “I wish to join your cheating team.”

Aurum slowed to a halt.

“You know, when we say it like that,” I started to object. “It makes me think that…”

I trailed off before I could finish the thought.

I really don’t deserve to win. And cheating heroes is an oxymoron.

Galenus floated up into the air to stare through his wire-rimmed glasses into my eyes.

“Miss Rachel, what is the goal of your encounter with Ragnerus?”

“You told me yourself that I should thrash him.”

Does he want to get in a swing or two, too?

Galenus nodded. “To what Purpose?”

“To get him to join us?”

“By showing how powerful we can be,” Galenus confirmed. “Should you not use every cheat you have access to? Cheating implies rules; rules we wish to sidestep. Why stop at some rules when we can cheat against them all and display our full abilities?”

“I don’t think I understand what you’re insinuating,” I replied, starting to feel a little squishy on the sindie.

Galenus settled onto the ground, holding his arms behind his back. “Physics has a very hard set of rules for beings of flesh. But my portals, combined with the strength of all in this bastion, sidestep them much better than your horse ever could.”

I blinked. “Portals? To move us around? Isn't that too much?”

Galenus opened a multitude of tiny wormholes. “I would even cheat with more if I had the means to do so, for I am more than equipped to bear the guilt as I witness his timely and overwhelming surrender.”

The other knights laughed a bit raucously at that.

I have to stop vacillating.

With a sigh, I accepted that I was not only able but willing to cheat on any magnitude, but that I could finally let the Hero go—ideal form and all.
