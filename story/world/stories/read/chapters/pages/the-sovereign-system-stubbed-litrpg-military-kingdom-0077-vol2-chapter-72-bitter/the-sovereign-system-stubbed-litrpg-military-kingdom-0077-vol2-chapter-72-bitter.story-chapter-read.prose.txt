Luke knelt on the ground, the weight of failure bearing down on him.

Every plan he had crafted—each one so carefully constructed—had collapsed like a house of cards. The intelligence and foresight he once took pride in had proven to be nothing more than an illusion, a veil that blinded rather than guided.

But now that the veil had lifted, the harsh truth lay bare before him.

This wasn’t supposed to happen…

Where did I go wrong?

He stared at his hands, as if expecting them to hold the answers. A crushing sense of failure pressed against his chest. In a single, catastrophic stroke, the entire imperial court of the Marxx Kingdom had fallen—including the King, and now the second prince.

From the south, a Lhair force marched steadily toward their destruction.

Victoria and Christian were gravely injured, though how they ended up in such a state remained a mystery to him. The memories of the last few hours were slipping away, swallowed by the fog of chaos that clouded his mind.

What do I do?

Where do I go?

A faint thrum pulsed within his chest—the quiet beating of his Dragon’s Heart. It offered no answers, but it did offer presence. A bastion of strength in the fog. A reminder that he was still alive, still breathing.

A presence moved in front of him. Familiar. Warm.

When Luke looked up, he saw Tristan kneeling before him, a tired yet resolute expression on his face.

“Remember what I told you?” Tristan said, placing a firm hand on Luke’s shoulder. “You don’t have to carry this alone. We’re here, Luke. No matter how heavy the burden—we’ll share it with you.”

Luke’s eyes widened. The words stirred something within him.

He remembered. Just a week ago, Tristan had said the same thing. Back then, Luke had promised not to shoulder everything himself… yet here he was, already breaking that promise.

A pang of guilt rose in his chest as he turned to see Riann and Richard nearby. Their expressions were grave, but their concern was unmistakable.

“He’s right, Luke,” Richard added, clearing his throat. His voice carried a note of sorrow, but also strength. “You can rely on us.”

Riann glanced at his nephew, seemingly surprised—but after a pause, he gave a solemn nod. He didn’t need words. The gesture alone reminded Luke of Master Boyd, Riann’s brother. A man of quiet strength.

Their solidarity sparked something deep within him. A warmth. A flicker of hope amid the darkness.

Luke inhaled deeply, steadying himself.

This isn’t the time to fall apart.

Tens of thousands of innocent lives were at stake. If he crumbled now, they would fall with him.

I already made a vow.

I said I would breathe life back into this broken kingdom. And I can't do that if I can’t even protect it.

He rose to his feet, jaw clenched, eyes clear.

The time for doubt had passed. Now was the time to act.

Despite the tragedy that had unfolded—and the crushing news of the second prince’s death—the three men standing around Luke exchanged glances of quiet approval. His change in demeanor was clear. He no longer looked defeated.

He looked ready.

“We need to mobilize the army,” Luke said, his eyes regaining their shine. “Riann, can I count on you to handle it?”

The large man nodded once, silent and resolute. Without a word, he turned on his heel and left, his heavy footsteps echoing with urgency. He understood the need for haste.

Luke watched him go before turning to Richard at his side. He placed a firm hand on the man’s broad shoulder—no words, just the weight of shared grief and unspoken resolve.

“Once we’ve taken stock of our forces, we’ll need to organize Ralis’s defenses,” Luke continued. “I need a full report on troop numbers, current provisions, and the status of our fortifications. If we’re not careful, we could be staring down the barrel of a prolonged siege.”

He said it grimly, his tone heavy with experience.

This would be the fourth siege Luke had faced since arriving in this world—and he hadn’t even been here for half a year.

“What about the crown?” Tristan asked, looking at him pointedly.

Luke shook his head. “There’s no time for coronation rites. The throne can wait. For now, I’ll act as General. You’ll serve as my Vice General.”

Tristan looked as if he were about to protest, but a sudden sound interrupted him—something striking the ground nearby.

Immediately, both Tristan and Richard stepped in front of Luke, weapons half-drawn, instincts honed from years of battle. But when they saw who it was, their tension dropped.

“Kayson?” Luke called out.

“Luke—” Kayson’s voice cracked, panicked. “I don’t know what’s wrong with her!”

He stumbled forward, cradling a lithe figure in his arms.

Luke didn’t hesitate. He ran to meet him, his eyes immediately falling on Victoria’s trembling form. She writhed weakly, her brown skin slick with sweat, her jaw clenched as she fought against whatever agony had gripped her.

His heart sank.

“Put her down—gently,” Luke ordered.

Kayson obeyed, kneeling and laying her on the cool stone floor before backing away to give him space.

Luke knelt beside her, placing a hand on her forehead. The heat radiating from her skin was intense—unnatural.

“She’s burning up…” he muttered, trying to suppress the panic rising in his chest.

He quickly began inspecting her for wounds. A fever like this usually meant an infection, and in a world without antibiotics or modern medicine, an untreated wound could easily turn fatal.

Please… no infection, he thought, beginning to pray without realizing it.

But after several tense moments, he frowned.

Nothing. No blood, no bruising, not even a scratch.

She looked untouched—yet her body screamed in pain.

Luke hesitated, considering for a moment whether he should remove her outer robes to check for hidden injuries. But just as he reached toward her, his eyes caught something—

A thick, black liquid spilled from Victoria’s lips, trailing down her cheek before splashing onto the stone floor. It oozed like tar and reeked of death—putrid and unmistakable.

Luke’s eyes widened in horror, then narrowed with sudden fury.

“Bishop…”

He spat the name like venom, his jaw clenched.

He knew this substance. He had seen it once before.

Soul Poison. The Bishop’s hidden weapon—corrupt, unnatural, and almost always fatal.

“What’s wrong with her?” Kayson asked, voice taut with fear.

But Luke didn’t answer. He couldn’t. His thoughts swirled in a storm of rage and dread. He forced himself to push the anger aside—it wouldn’t help her now.

He needed a solution.

A spark lit in his mind.

My Sovereign’s Blessing. It might work… it has to.

Luke summoned his system window, his vision filling with the familiar overlay. He navigated to his skills list, scrolling quickly until he found it:

Sovereign’s Blessing.

Just like before, the description was a mess of glitched characters, as if rendered on a dying 8-bit console—unreadable.

Still, he selected it and tried to activate its power.

Nothing.

His frown deepened. He tried again, this time placing both hands gently on Victoria’s body, urging the skill to work.

Her body arched in pain.

Victoria let out a strangled cry as more of the black fluid poured from her mouth, thick and chunky, like rotting filth forced from deep within. She began to choke, spasming violently.

“Shit—” Luke cursed, quickly rolling her to her side.

He tried to stay calm, but panic surged through him like wildfire.

She’s going to die.

There’s nothing I can do…

He could only watch, helpless, as the black liquid spilled onto the floor—an endless stream of her suffering. A familiar weight of despair crushed him.

This was his fault.

If he hadn’t sent her with the Crown Prince...

If he’d kept her safe in the capital, with Kayson and Sebastian...

She wouldn’t be like this.

Tears blurred his vision as he looked down at her writhing figure.

“Victoria…”

His voice cracked—raw with guilt and grief.

And then—her body convulsed violently.

Luke jolted back in shock.

Her eyes snapped open, locking onto him. But they weren’t her eyes. Gone was the warmth, the recognition. What stared back was cold, black—shrouded in a shadowy veil.

“Vic—”

Before he could finish, her hand shot up and gripped his throat, tight and unrelenting.

His breath hitched, eyes going wide.

She was choking him.

“STOP!” he gasped, raising one hand in desperation.

A blur of steel filled his vision.

A massive greatsword dropped from above—stopping just inches from Victoria’s outstretched arm.

Had the strike come a moment later, it would have severed her limb cleanly.

Luke blinked, stunned. The blade hovered like judgment itself—held back by the thinnest thread of hesitation.

“Luke! Why did you stop me?” Richard shouted, his voice unsteady, laced with confusion and fear.

Kayson stepped forward, reaching for Victoria’s outstretched arm, desperate to pry her off. But the instant Luke saw his hand extend, his heart clenched in alarm.

He swatted Kayson’s arm away with what little strength he had, barely managing a strangled command.

“Stay... back.” he growled through gritted teeth.

He didn’t know how the Bishop’s poison worked—not fully. But if it could be passed to others through contact, if this corruption was contagious…

He couldn’t let Kayson suffer the same fate.

He wouldn’t.

Luke glanced down at Victoria.

Her body had stilled. The spasms were gone. Her delicate hand remained wrapped around his throat, but her features were no longer contorted in pain. Instead, she wore a vacant expression—blank, robotic.

Cold.

Emotionless.

Her grip tightened. His throat burned.

Damn it! Why won’t you work?!

Luke’s system window flickered before his eyes, still hovered over the Sovereign’s Blessing skill. He slammed the command to activate it again and again, willing it to do something—anything.

But nothing changed.

The skill refused to respond.

His lungs burned. His thoughts turned sluggish as blood failed to reach his brain, the vice-like grip around his neck tightening slowly, methodically.

Like a python.

Like death itself.

Luke’s eyelids fluttered. His body screamed for oxygen.

Please…

Please, do something. Anything.

I don’t want to lose her. I don’t want to lose any more of my friends.

He bit down on his lower lip, hard. Blood filled his mouth, sharp and metallic, but the pain barely registered beneath the suffocating pressure.

Then something else surged through him.

Rage.

A wildfire tore through the numbness, burning away the helplessness that had shackled him.

You spout all this bullshit about the Sovereign’s pride…

“The Sovereign does not settle for weakness.”

“The Sovereign is dignified.”

Sovereign this. Sovereign that.

His thoughts twisted with venom, the dam of restraint obliterated by raw emotion.

You’ve held me to these impossible standards—but for what?

You’re the Sovereign System, right? Aren’t you supposed to make me into a Sovereign?

Then tell me—what kind of Sovereign lets his people die in front of him?

Don’t feed me that “a Sovereign does not need such weaknesses” crap.

His teeth clenched. The taste of blood spread across his tongue. His vision blurred at the edges, darkness creeping in like a rising tide.

He was out of time.

Victoria would die.

And he would follow.

Then—his eyes snapped open, bloodshot and brimming with purpose.

He looked at her.

Really looked.

Behind the corrupted expression, beneath the veil of darkness, he saw her. Not the seductress who toyed with others to keep them at bay. Not the confident, smiling mask she wore like armor.

He saw the real Victoria.

The woman who had been bruised by the world, who had learned not to trust anyone because the world had never given her a reason to.

She had never spoken those truths aloud.

But in that moment, he felt them.

The hurt. The betrayal.

But more than anything—

Her vulnerability.

Luke could hear her voice echo within his mind.

“Save me…”

His heart twisted at the sound.

He reached out, fingers trembling. He wanted to protect her—shelter her from the cruelty of the world. But he was powerless. Even now, his life was being squeezed from him, as if bound in the coils of a serpent.

As the final flickers of consciousness began to fade, a single tear slipped down his cheek—carrying all the bitterness, regret, and self-loathing that weighed on his soul.

And then—

Everything stopped.

The world froze.

Blood suddenly rushed to his head, sending a wave of dizziness through him. He tried to turn, to move, to look—but he couldn’t. His body was paralyzed, locked in place, as if time and space themselves had halted.

A new notification flashed across his vision.

[The Sovereign has heard your wishes]

Then came a voice—deep, resonant, and godlike. It didn’t just speak.

It rumbled through the stillness.

“My child… why do you cling to such a damaged woman? Is it the flesh that encases her soul? Is it so tantalizing that you would discard everything you've achieved just to save her?”

Luke frowned, wanting to scream in defiance. But in this frozen world, only his eyes could move.

“Worry not, Luke Drakon.”

“Your heart answers when your mouth cannot. After all, humans rarely speak the truth with their tongues.”

As if in agreement, the Dragon’s Heart within his chest gave a deep, resounding thrum.

A moment of silence passed.

Then—soft laughter.

“I see.”

Another chuckle followed, lighter, freer—yet still echoing with divine power.

“Very well, my child. I see how you view your subordinates…”

A pause.

“…or should I say—your friends.”

A warm golden light enveloped Victoria’s body in the next instant. It shimmered like sunlight breaking through a storm, casting gentle rays over her twisted form. Though time remained frozen, Luke could feel the relief washing through her.

Her expression softened.

The tension in her face melted.

The poison receded.

The voice grew distant, fading like a dream at the edge of waking.

“We will meet again, young Dragon.”

A final whisper.

“Do not disappoint me.”

Then—another chime.

[DING]

[The Sovereign’s Blessing is now applied to all subordinates]

Luke barely had time to read the notification before darkness overtook him. His body collapsed forward, unconscious, falling onto Victoria’s now-healed form beneath him.
