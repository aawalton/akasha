The wave of mana from [Knowledge is Power] left Melmarc in a rush. He could feel its reaches, knew where it intended to stop and where he intended to stop it as the man pointed a gun at him and the woman trailed Ark’s approach with the barrel of her gun.

The woman let out three shots in quick succession. It carried the quick burst that came with wielding an machine gun. It lit the ground in front of Ark, halting his approach. The ground carried three smoking holes in the asphalt.

Melmarc took note of how the weapon had been quiet. Silencers on weapons were never that quiet.

Crafted? he thought, wondering if the weapons were crafted by someone with the [Crafter] class.

“Don’t shoot at the kids,” the man snapped at her, but didn’t take his eyes of Melmarc.

The woman frowned but adjusted her weapon. For all the man’s warnings, she moved her weapon and aimed the barrel at Ark’s torso.

“Fuck my partner’s words,” she spat in what sounded like a tired voice. “I’ll put you down if you get too close.”

Ark grinned a little. He looked more than enthusiastic about rushing into the frey to face off against an enemy with a gun in her hand.

“You heard her,” he said, looking at Melmarc so that she ended up in his blindspot. “She’s going to kill me. Makes her free game, right?”

Melmarc almost cocked a brow at that. He could understand the therapist Jennifer, but now a guard that was clearly just doing their job that really had nothing to do with him? Was Ark developing some murderous tendencies?

You were more than alright with him killing her, though.

As much as his mind mocked him for it, Melmarc could note that there was a difference between him and his brother. Ark seemed to want to rush into the violence, he seemed to want the chaos, and Veebee was simply always hungry. As for him, however, he just didn’t care. Everything still seemed beneath him.

“No,” he said simply. “She’s not free game. You don’t get to kill her.”

The man’s face morphed a little. There was a touch of dismay while the wave of mana from [Knowledge Is Power] moved through the entrance reaching into the building.

“You seem to be the reasonable one,” the man with the gun said to him, some level of hope in his eyes. “May I ask that you kids turn around and return to your rooms? There is nothing here that will interest you.”

Melmarc wasn’t sure he could agree with that. When he returned from the Ruins of Caldath, he had been faced with a level of discomfort, like wearing a shirt that didn’t exactly fit right. Then he’d adjusted it and somehow the world had come to order. The world had fit right. Ever since, the world had continued to fit right.

It turned out that he had established his presence on earth, claiming it rightfully as his own. Here and now, he was feeling the same discomfort, but this was not the feeling of his shirt not fitting him properly. It was something else.

He looked at the building and its black and white design.

“Can I at least break something?” Ark asked. “She did shoot at me.”

“And next time I’ll shoot you,” the woman hissed.

Ark looked at her and his grin became a feral smile. “You know it’s not a good idea to hold such a deadly weapon in the presence of me and my good friend over there. Bad things can happen because of it.”

The woman spat while the wave of mana reached its conclusion. “Gifted children are always so full of themselves.”

Melmarc took note of her voice. It wasn’t condescension or hate that he sensed in it. It was some level of boredom, as if it was a bad trait Gifted children tended to have, even if not all of them.

“My partner will shoot if given the chance,” the man said. “I would rather not have to deal with a bleeding child. I doubt any of you, for all your size, are invulnerable to bullets.”

Ark looked at Melmarc, and Melmarc knew what his brother was waiting for. The distraction that came when the wave of mana passed through a person on the return. The question was if the distraction would be enough to affect them, to actually give them a chance of attack.

“My familiar’s missing,” Melmarc said simply. “It came this way. I’m only looking for it.”

The man shook his head. “No familiar came through this place.”

Melmarc tried to remember what he could from the memory he got from Veebee when Ark had shown him how to connect with it. At least he thought that was what Ark was trying to teach him.

Images flashed in his mind, the guards standing by the entrance looking bored as hell. Trying to simply appear inside and failing. Wondering if there was another way to—

Oh. Melmarc paused as how Veebee had entered into the building triggered in his mind. Veebee had simply existed between the two guards at a moment when they were both not paying attention to the spot between them and… fazed into the building? That didn’t seem right.

The wave of mana went through the female guard and Melmarc nodded. The woman frowned a little, her eyes darted to the side in what lasted for a fraction of a second and Ark moved. He darted to the side, fast and low. The man’s eyes widened in front of Melmarc, perhaps shocked at the fact that the boy was in a hurry to rush straight into a raised gun.

The woman moved immediately, adjusting to Ark’s positioning. Her gun moved, but Ark got to her before it did. Melmarc took note of that. Ark was faster than her.

“Don’t shoot!” the man barked as Ark grabbed the barrel of the gun and turned it aside. The woman frowned, it was the frown that came with strain. Then Melmarc felt the pain of the strain. Ark was faster than her, stronger too.

Then the wave of mana went through the man. The man frowned. Melmarc moved.

Unlike the woman, the man did not try to adjust his aim. He released his gun, allowing the weapon to dangle from the strap across his shoulder, then met Melmarc in head on combat.

Just to stop us from getting inside? Melmarc wondered as his interface popped up in front of him.

[Talk To Sephirot], Melmarc noted as he dodged a swinging elbow from  the soldier whose name now hovered over his head as. Oluwatosin Olarenwaju. It was very similar to Pelumi’s long name and he knew that he could not pronounce it properly even if he tried.

He would have to talk to Ark about [Talk To Sephirot] later on, he decided as he dodged the next swinging elbow that came at him. As far as he was concerned, Sephirot sounded like the name of a demon.

[You have chosen Krav Maga]

Melmarc expected knowledge to fill head, perhaps an upgrade on what he already knew about the fighting style. Instead, experience flooded his head. He ducked to the side, brought his elbow up and countered a falling arm aimed for his shoulder. It was an instinctive act, giving him leverage. Shock registered on Tosin’s face, as if he had not expected Melmarc to defend in that manner.

Melmarc leapt away from the man. “Ark,” he called out. “They aren’t Gifted.”

The woman scowled and tried to do something else. Ark jumped back and away from her, coming to a stop next to Melmarc.

He looked at Melmarc. “For real.” He looked back at the guards. “They aren’t Gifted.”

The woman’s frown deepened. “Kids these days.”

“Just go back.” Oluwatosin said. “Your familiar’s not here. You’re not even supposed to be here. Didn’t you kids see the sign?”

Ark sighed. They had, in fact, seen the sign. But they had been more than happy to ignore it. They’d seen it, taken one look at each other, and continued on. It was better than later finding out that Veebee had eaten someone.

“Are we going back?” Ark asked.

Melmarc blinked. “Me?”

“It is your familiar after all,” Ark pointed out. “If it was Spitfire, I’d be the one making the decision.”

“Fair point,” Melmarc conceded. “We are not going back.”

Ark nodded. “Got it.”

With his words said, the ambient mana around him darkened, then the same darkened behind the woman. Melmarc wasn’t entirely sure what was happening. But the two in front of them definitely didn’t know what was happening.

Then something erupted out of the darkness. It reached forward from behind the girl and wrapped a massive yet thin long arm around her. Only the top half of its torso settled into reality. The rest of it was hidden in nonexistence.

The woman stiffened up, panic registering in her widened eye. A scream settled on her lips as a massive wave of fear ran through Melmarc. Tosin turned, aimed at the creature, and Melmarc darted forward.

Tosin hesitated between pulling the trigger and turning to Melmarc. In the end, he settled for pulling the trigger. The weapon rang out, letting out more than three bullets before Melmarc got to him. In the blink of an eye, he grabbed the man, disarmed him, and brought him to the ground as gently as violently possible. He pinned him there, face down, then looked at Ark.

“Don’t kill her.”

Ark rolled his eyes. “I won’t.”

“And,” he looked at the creature above the woman, massive arms holding her in place with its goat head looking down at her with interest, “I’m guessing that’s sephirot.”

Ark paused. “You know about sephirot?” He paused, seemed to think about it. “Oh, your skill.”

“When did you use it that I didn’t notice?”

Ark shrugged. “I don’t really have to summon them into reality to use them.”

“Them?”

“All its limbs are different creatures,” Ark explained.

Melmarc couldn’t see it, but he wasn’t going to have an argument about it. Instead, he turned his attention to the man currently struggling against his hold.

“Let’s try this again,” he said to the man. “I’m looking for my familiar.”

“Looks kind of like a chibi,” Ark explained, completely ignoring the woman in sephirot’s grip. “Fun if you get to know it. Hates being called anything other than an it.”

Melmarc looked down at the man as Ark spoke and realized the man had no idea what they were talking about. Then again, Veebee had technically slipped through them undetected. And he still couldn’t sense it.

“I don’t think he knows,” Ark said.

Melmarc nodded. “I know. I was just hoping they’d heard something after Veebee rushed in and just didn’t know it was what I’m looking for.”

He sighed, suddenly tired.

Ark folded his arms. “I guess we have to get in there now.”

“We can’t let you do that,” Tosin said. “You can’t get in there.”

Melmarc sighed. “You must know that you can’t stop us,” he said, matter-of-factly. “Right?”

“If you go in there, you could be expelled. Do you want that?”

Melmarc looked at Ark.

Ark shrugged. “If he eats someone in there, we’ll be expelled either way. I say we risk it. To be honest, with everything we know, can you really call it a risk?”

Ark had a point, so Melmarc turned Tosin around a little roughly, and pinned his back against the ground. Tosin struggled against him but didn’t have the strength to hold escape Melmarc’s hold. It made Melmarc wonder why they would put two normal people in a school of Gifted to prevent the children from entering the building. They had to know that it would not work.

Then again, the specific building was entirely out of the way. A student could not naturally stumble upon it.

Melmarc banished the thought and Sephirot’s hand moved over the face of the woman in her hands. Melmarc watched the panic and felt the fear as the hand covered the woman’s face.

“You’ll be fine,” Ark told her. “Just don’t struggle. It will be over in a moment.”

With that, Melmarc placed a firm hand on Tosin’s neck. Tosin’s eyes widened in horror and Melmarc shook his head at him. “I’m not going to kill you. I’m not trying to.”

Understanding registered in the man’s eyes before he relaxed, simply resigning himself to his fate. Melmarc squeezed a little, then kept the hold.

At first, Tosin was calm, then as time went on, his body started fighting, struggling for survival, for air, for something.

Melmarc kept his hold, though. He did not release him but allowed him to fight.

Then the fight left the man and unconsciousness left him.

“Alright then.” He got up and looked at the door.

Ark walked up to him after lowering the woman gently to the ground. “You’re getting really good at that.” He nodded at Tosin’s unconscious body.

“At what, specifically?” Melmarc asked, unsure, but having an idea of what Ark was talking about.

“Choking people out without killing them,” Ark explained. “But you know that even though I’m going along with all these things I won’t hesitate to knock your teeth out the moment you start taking things too far, right?”

Melmarc nodded slowly. He knew, but Ark saying it made him really know. “I know.”

He wondered what counted as taking things too far as Ark walked up to the door and placed his hand on it.

“Now then.” Ark looked at Melmarc and pushed. The door opened inward. “Shall we?”
