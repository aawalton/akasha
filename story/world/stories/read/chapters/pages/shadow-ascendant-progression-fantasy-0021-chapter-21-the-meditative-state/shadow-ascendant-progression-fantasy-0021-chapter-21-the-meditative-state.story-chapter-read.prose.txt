Going back into the meditative state was comparatively easy today. It didn’t take me much time, perhaps just a second or two—and I was back in the world surrounded by darkness, with a faintly glowing body and a wand.

Though I got the wand today on the first try; I guess I was getting more accustomed to imagining things with my body now.

My eyelids pressed harder against each other as I tried to focus. Core… core, core. I murmured in my mind, something no one should do while meditating, though it worked for me.

In moments, I pictured the large, radiant ball of mana at the center of my chest. Half its surface glowed violet while the other half shone crimson, signifying both my affinities. The globe was barely half the size of my clenched hand.

As I moved outward from the core and focused on the channels, white glowing nodes of mana appeared at my wrists, ankles, and forehead.

With that done, I turned my attention to the wand in my hand. Cold and smooth, it lacked the rough, woody texture of the staff I’d used before—and it was much shorter, less than half a meter.

During my last training, I had barely extended my mana a few inches, so reaching the wand’s tip now would be a significant improvement, and I was still far from that goal.

My teeth clenched. Focus, Arnos, I reminded myself, forcing the doubts from my mind.

Similar to the staff, the mana from my body flowed freely into the wand with no conscious effort—the tiny mana stone embedded beneath the wood made sure of it.

I breathed steadily, watching the violet mana glow as it flowed from my hand to the wand, leaving my skin cold and prickly, though I was pretty sure some of it was because of the Icelure working at full capacity.

Removing my left hand from the wand, I glanced at it for a moment with my closed eyes, as I enveloped my index finger and thumb in a layer of violet mana—the mana of dark affinity.

With my fingers coated, I brought them close to the wand, nearer to the stream of mana, and pressed at half the length of the wand.

The mana from the three sources converged, twisting into a tangled globe at the center of the wand.

Move, I murmured inwardly, slowly parting my two fingers. The mana sphere split in two, dividing into two smaller orbs that drifted apart, each individually joined by a thin stream of violet energy.

With the flow divided just like before, half the problem was solved. Now all that remained was to maintain it—and nothing more.

Taking a deep breath, I swallowed. I should be able to do it, I murmured inwardly, stopping the flow of mana from my left hand.

The purple spheres of mana at my fingertips vanished, leaving only my faintly glowing finger and thumb.

I gulped, trying to keep the mana flow steady. Do not get excited, do not get excited, I told myself as calmly as I could as I let the mana flow with no mess-up.

And my efforts bore fruit—there was no ripple or instability in the flow. Yet the streams of mana began moving toward each other again, despite my best efforts.

Wait, am I even doing anything to maintain the new pathway? No… My eyes fluttered open, and the mana I had been seeing vanished—my imagination faltered.

I wasn’t doing anything but playing tricks. “If I have to learn Dark Pulse, I need to do better.” My lips tightened. “I have to,” I added.

With that new resolve in mind, I set aside the pillow and the wand on the bed and walked toward the study table.

Searching for my notebook, which I was certain I had kept beneath the Monster Biology one—it wasn’t there. Maybe I was sleepwalking? My eyes darted across the table, Monster Biology, dream notebook, rough book, history book—everything was in place, but my notebook was nowhere to be found.

Where did I put it? I wondered, my eyes turning to the bag. Deciding not to waste time on a useless endeavor, I grabbed my golden-black fountain pen and the rough notebook and walked back to the bed.

I tossed the notebook and pen to the top of the bed, climbed over it, and adjusted the pillow a bit before lying down on it. “Haah,” I sighed, my eyes turning to the furry blanket. I stretched my leg, grabbed it with my toes, and threw it over myself with a whimper—like a shivering dog.

Though unlike a shivering dog, I enjoyed feeling cold. Being under a blanket when the room was freezing? The feeling was freaking awesome!

With the blanket secured over my body, I looked to the notebook and pulled it in front of me. Opening a fresh page was a hassle—maybe it was time I bought a new rough notebook.

With the page open, I uncapped my fountain pen, feeling the cool metal against my fingers, and began sketching a diagram of the mana flowing through the wand.

In minutes, I stopped doodling, satisfied—or at least content, with the bad drawing I called a diagram. I made three of them, one with the wand, my hand, and the flow of mana; one where I removed my hand and showed mana flowing directly into the wand; and one where I removed both my hand and the wand, showing just the flow.

There wasn’t much reason to make three diagrams, and it’s certainly not something I’d recommend. I mean, the only reason I made three of them was cause, no ideas were popping into my brain.

Hmm… mana stone? How does its range work? I closed my eyes, turning around and lying on my back as I slid down from the pillow pressing on my chest.

“Sphere, yeah, pretty sure that’s how its range works. The stone’s range resembles its shape… and the one used in the wand? That was definitely a spherical one!”

I turned around, plopping onto the pillow again, as I drew an imaginary circle covering my hand and wand, with the tip of the wand as the center.

The attractive force produced by the wand was uniform, meaning whether there were mana particles near the center or at the edge of its range, the attraction they faced would be the same—and directed toward the mana stone, the core.

The force with which one expels mana was supposed to be uniform in ideal situations, similar to the attractive range of the mana core.

“Hahhh,” I sighed, rubbing my hair. Study was complicated; research even worse. “What I had to do, what I have to… hmm.” I paused. Closing my eyes, I fell over the rough notebook, my nose pressed into it, breathing in the smell of paper as my skin felt its cold surface.

“Why do I even have to do this training?” I cried out, rubbing my face across the notebook. I knew why I had to, but it was still frustrating.

Turning around, I lay on my back once again. “Luminous,” I murmured, turning the light off as I stared at the ceiling with no thoughts in my brain.

I kept looking at the ceiling—at the chandelier, when I noticed it moving slightly. It moved little, certainly not enough to make a sound, but it moved. My eyes turned to the Icelure placed on the wall. The cold air caused it to move. Caused it to move?

“Wait!” A revelation struck my mind. If instead of one Icelure I had two, or let’s say three, wouldn’t the chandelier move differently? I wondered.

“Luminous,” I murmured excitedly, my resolve hardening. Pushing the blanket off my body, I jumped to the floor and went back to my study table.

Where did I put it? I wondered, squinting slightly as I scanned the top of the table. My eyes finally stopped at the drawer.

I reached for the drawer; the metallic handle was cold. I pulled it open, and there it was—the small ball, just waiting to be thrown in a game of catch.

A smile spread across my lips. It should work; there’s no way it would move straight, I murmured inwardly, tightening my grip around the ball.

And no, I was totally not interested in playing catch right now.
