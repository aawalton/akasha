It was, I distantly thought with a dull roar in my ears, not as big as I would have expected.

That didn't mean that the fucking spaceship resting in the massive hangar beyond the room-spanning glass wall was small, of course. Merely that in my mind, I thought of starships as…larger than life. Enormous edifices to the ingenuity of mankind, a titanic construct of such complexity that vast resources needed to be poured into creating them to be even feasible.

If I had to guess…it was a little under a quarter of a mile in overall length. Barely under half a kilometer. Frankly, it just wasn’t big enough to be considered in lengths like miles. In total height, I’d put the thing at just a bit over a fourth of its overall width, with a little under that in length. So…what, maybe a hundred and fifty meters wide, and a hundred tall?

I had to restrain a hysterical giggle at the thought that the spaceship was a little fat around the middle.

Even though it wasn’t quite as huge as I had built up the idea of one in my mind, that didn’t make it small in absolute terms. This thing absolutely dwarfed my own Astray, hidden in a Velancian cove hundreds of miles south-easterly from here.

It made for an awe-inspiring sight, because contrary to any expectations I might have had…that was a surprisingly apt comparison.

This ship was surprisingly ship-shaped. As in, the overall shape of it was almost reminiscent of a seafaring vessel. That is, if you stripped the masts from two huge sea vessels and slapped them together, deck to deck. The result was a ship whose profile looked almost arrowhead-shaped. Wait.

No. That wasn’t quite right, was it?

It wasn’t arrowhead shaped. The overall shape was more like that of a spearhead. A fat, short, and broad one, yes. But the resemblance was there. And with that realization, I began to notice more details about it.

Another similarity I could see on it was that, in another resemblance to seafaring ships, I think it actually did have sails. On each of the four ‘bladed’ edges of the spearpoint, I could see what seemed to be long columns that had been folded back to rest against the hull. Just looking at the thing, I could see how they could fold out and deploy…what, I’m not quite sure. There certainly wasn’t anything like cloth or canvas anywhere down there. On the contrary, the entire vessel seemed to be machined out of the same white steel that I not only stood on now, but had stood on in the past.

In the Netherim bunker beneath Mt. Goryuen.

I found that entirely unsurprisingly, if for different reasons. I had never seriously considered the possibility that the Netherim had possessed the capability of space flight, and frankly, I wondered if this thing had belonged to them. Everything I had learned about them and how they had navigated through the universe pointed to their portal network. Multiple sources, some of them primary, had espoused the virtues of this network.

This was something entirely different.

Smooth steel, curved surfaces, masts of some mysterious design…this wasn’t anything like the shuttles I had seen on TV in my youth back on Terra. This was beyond that, in the same way a supercomputer was beyond a series of signal fires.

I simply stared out of the window, for a time, taking in the sight. “Where did it come from…” I whispered under my breath. I wasn’t really expecting an answer.

But I got one anyway.

“It is a gift from eons past,” A strong, self-assured, and yet oddly kind voice spoke up from behind me. “The hidden inheritance meant for the children of Vereden.”

I blinked, the voice knocking me out of the near worship I’d fallen into at the sight of the vessel. I turned in place to see who it was, and out of the corner of my eye, I noticed that Rafael remained still. He simply continued floating in place, staring out of the window at the object of our mutual obsession.

And I…met a pair of startlingly green eyes. A shade, in fact, that was identical not only to my own, but that of my Alveron’s and Aveline’s.

They were set into a face that had been completely flensed of all skin, the underlying muscle having been toughened into a wax, maroon surface, the individual fibers joined together to create a cohesive surface. There was an amiable smile on the person’s lips, the shade of which was nearly indistinguishable from the surrounding exposed muscle. Even with the lack of skin, I could recognize facial features, beyond the horror of its appearance.

He, because this was most definitely a he, was…decidedly average.

And completely in proportion and height, with a Human.

Not a Dwarf.

He dipped his head to me briefly, presenting a simple circlet of a familiar white steel upon his skinned brow, before smiling at me in obvious delight. “Welcome to the Aorta, as we call it…Nathaniel Hart. I’ve heard so much about you at this point, I feel as if we’ve known each other for years. It’s a pleasure to make your acquaintance, o’latest Precursor, Blade of the System.”

My lips parted, and full awareness of my surroundings snapped back into focus.

Behind me was both a spaceship and the missing Lich of my retinue, seemingly lost in his own internal world.

Before me was a fully Korvokian-skinned man who almost looked like a Human.

And behind him was the rest of my retinue, gathered in a defensive position. Over his shoulder, I met the alarmed sapphire eyes of Sylvia, and outright winced.

In my wonder at the sight of the genuine wonder beyond the glass, I had wandered deep enough into what was ostensibly enemy territory as to be foolish. I’d lost my tactical senses and forced my people to hurry after me, falling into a barely organized defensive position not far from us. But, oddly enough, they seemed to have stopped before they reached my previously enraptured form.

It was almost like they had been stopped from doing so. Perhaps, I thought, by the sudden appearance of someone blocking them from doing so. Someone who had moved so quickly as to reach me before they could.

I took a long, slow breath, and met the startlingly similar shade of eyes to my own. “Ul-Korveth, I assume?”

A wide, if not oddly sheepish smile appeared on the maroon cheeks of the Korvok. “Oh my, I’m still not used to hearing that!” He laughed, covering his mouth with one hand as he did. “Please, Nathaniel. You need not refer to me as my regnal name, if you prefer. For you, I shall answer to either Ul-Korveth or the name my mother blessed me with at birth. Up until recently, I was simply Dain.”

That…this was…

Not what I had been expecting.

Ul-Korveth, or Dain, apparently, was not what I had thought the leader of the Korvoks would be like. I’d assumed they would be some kind of hulking monster of a Dwarf, towering over me and spouting religious dogma with every breath.

But this guy…if anything, he seemed excited to meet me. Genuinely so.

Alright. I could work with this.

I nodded slowly and forced both a small, polite smile on my lips and my right hand forward before me. “Dain, then. You can call me Nathan. It’s a…pleasure to meet you.”

Dain lowered his hand, and I saw his wide grin still on his lips was still there. He enthusiastically took my right hand in his own, and I brutally repressed a shudder at the feeling of his in mine. It was almost plasticky in texture. “I assure you, Nathan, the pleasure is all mine. I cannot overstate just how excited we are to have you with us. Never once in a thousand, thousand years have Clan Korvok thought we’d be lucky enough to be graced by a Precursor. Please, follow me, and I would be happy to discuss matters with you in a more comfortable location. Refreshments and cleansers have been prepared in advance.”

“And my retinue?” I asked quickly, before the Korvokhan could take even a single step. “Are they welcome to follow us as well?”

Dain blinked a few times in confusion before twisting his head around to stare blankly at the sizable force at his back. It was like he was only then noticing their existence at all and was completely unconcerned at the sight of them. He laughed lightly as he turned back to me. “Why, of course! They may have to wait their turn with the cleansers, but we have more than enough fare for all with an appetite! The room itself isn’t terribly large, however. Most of your companions shall have to wait in the hall, I’m afraid.”

“That’s…” I said, my eyes drifting over my officers. “Acceptable. Lead on…Dain.”

“Then, if you’ll follow me?” Dain said with a bright smile, gesturing off to my right. In the distance, on a far wall, I could see what seemed to be a large, open, inviting doorway with a long, straight hall on the other side. “I’d be happy to tell you the history of our people on the way to our destination. I very much doubt the Skarned had much of anything to say at all.”

As Dain and I started walking toward the far hall, I subtly gestured for my officers and most powerful to join me at my side. It didn’t matter just how welcoming ‘Dain’ had turned out to be. This was still the leader of the entire culture we had been literally wading through blood to fight. I didn’t trust him in the slightest.

As they hurried to walk with me, I couldn’t help but notice something. Although Dain hadn’t even glanced once in his direction before setting off…

Korvakh the Steward was still trailing along in our wake, silent and unseen by his own leader.

………………………..

It was becoming increasingly clear to me that this entire area, the ‘Aorta’, as Dain had referred to it, was deliberately meant to be a copy of Netherim architecture. The way it was constructed was disturbingly similar to the halls of the bunker I had explored beneath Mt. Goryuen. It may have been quarried from smooth white stone, instead of ivory steel, but there was no mistaking the precise lines and placement of fixtures. I almost expected to see a fleet of the same cleaning AutoMats I had seen near the core bustle down the hallway. It was that similar.

I tried to put it out of my mind for now, and instead devoted all of my attention to Dain as he spoke, leading us leisurely down the corridor.

“Once upon a time,” Dain began wistfully, nodding to several curious Korvokian heads as they poked through doorways to gawk at us. “Clan Korvok had the honor of being the direct attendants of his Divinity, Yorgun, God of War. In those hallowed days, we were his most loyal, his devout warriors called to fight at his side. We were both his shield bearers, as well as those whom he trusted to act as his stewards in his absence. Alas, the annals of history tell that those halcyon days were not to last.”

He sighed heavily as we began to approach a threefold split in the hallway. I was disturbingly reminded of the same kind of split I’d seen back in the core of the bunker. One leg had led to the reactor itself, one had led further into the complex, and the other…

Had led into the open air of the mountain.

I couldn’t help but wonder if one of these splits led further to the ship I had seen earlier.

“The War in Heaven was what ended that age, of course,” Dain shook his head in seemingly genuine sorrow. “The perfidious Gods of Chaos rose against the rightful Gods of Order, and Mighty Yorgun was called to the battlefield to practice what he was best at. Of course, my ancestors followed, but in the end…we were not able to protect him from his fate. Lord Yorgun was ambushed by no less than four of the despicable Chaos Gods and was slain, his body defiled beyond recognition. Our forebears were only barely able to escape with their lives to tell the tale, but it was essential that they do so. Because, you see, our Clan had been entrusted with a most vital responsibility, to see to if our God fell in battle.”

“The Ship,” I said quietly, as we came to a halt in the middle of the split. There was an odd apparatus set into the doorway of the central leg, some kind of enchanted device that projected a filmy, translucent forcefield over it.

Dain gestured towards the field. “Please, feel free to step through it. It’s a cleansing device. As you can imagine, we’re quite used to blood getting into every nook and cranny when you venture beyond the Inner Heart. This will leave you as fresh and clean as a bright spring day if you step through. It’s quite safe, I assure you. We’ve had quite some time to refine the enchantment.” He smiled at me. “But, to answer your question…you’re correct. Our great responsibility is to the care and protection of the ship. It has a name, of course. Would you care to take a guess, Nathan?”

I hid a grimace as Sylvia boldly stepped through the forcefield without waiting and repressed a sigh of relief when she poked a newly cleaned head back through it to nod at everyone else. I immediately wanted to join the rest of my retinue as they nearly fought each other to use the cleaning enchantment. Instead, I focused back on Dain. “I haven’t the slightest idea, to be truthful.”

“Why…” Dain smiled more widely than I’d seen from him yet. “It has a most auspicious name indeed. That ship…”

“Is named the Vereden.”
