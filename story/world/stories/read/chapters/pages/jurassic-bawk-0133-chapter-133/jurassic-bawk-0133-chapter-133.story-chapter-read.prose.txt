Charlemagne turned all of his attention inward, tuning out the unnecessary and inevitable questions from the others.  His Ember Core had stopped leveling up long ago despite increasing in size.  Now, he could sense it was time to evolve it again.

The rooster had devoted much thought to his Core.  After all, it had saved his life at least twice: first against the mana-wielding plasmodium and then against the humans who had sought to slay him when he was vulnerable.  It remained the most powerful single Skill in his arsenal, allowing him to create the devastating mana attacks that he relied on to end most fights.  It had even subsumed other powerful Skills, such as Radiation Manipulation and Pyro.

The origins and effects of the Pyro Skill were still a mystery to the young rooster.  He had never done anything particularly fire-related before obtaining it, at least not to his recollection.  Sure, it was a welcome power, but it had influenced his evolution in a number of ways.  Not only had it served as the basis for his Mana Core to evolve into an Ember Core, but it had also influenced both his Physique and his newest Class selection.

Unfortunately, Pyro had been the last energy-related Skill he had gained, despite his attempts in the Battle Simulation to glean greater insights.  He felt like there had been a few moments when he had glimpsed possibilities, but the secrets that powered Phatagin’s Momentum Core still managed to elude him.  Even the battles against Phasima and Drago hadn’t afforded him the insight that he craved.

He had, however, benefitted greatly from absorbing the power of the latter’s sword: Phosphor.  The weapon’s core contained a portion of a star’s essence, and even though it contained only a sliver of the Item’s true power, Charlemagne could feel the potential flowing through his veins.  The only problem was that he had no idea at all how to harness it.  Every time he reached for a mote of the essence in order to examine it with his mystical senses, his efforts were completely rebuffed.

Leaving that mystery for another time, Charlemagne refocused on the changes that were going on inside him.  His engorged Ember Core was beating powerfully and steadily, the sheer heat emanating from it causing his internal temperature to soar.  His new Class had promised a bonus to “manipulating fire and combustion-related energies”, and the Squiggles had not been lying on that front.  He was able to retain the vast majority of the incredible heat that his body was generating internally, allowing only a little to slip out of his control and leak into the environment.  A blanket of steaming air formed around him, shielding him from the relative cold of the outside world as Charlemagne allowed his body to operate on autopilot and began to Brood.

He knew that he needed to evolve his Core, but what should it evolve into?  Should he lean even more heavily into his relatively new fire powers, or should he attempt to incorporate another aspect of his power set?  Could he even do that?  For instance, he was able to manipulate radiation and convert it into huge quantities of mana.  In fact, the incredible energy was by far the most efficient at increasing his mass.  For a few moments, the rooster regretted not choosing the Class Evolution that would have allowed him to turn into a giant.  But then he realized that it would have caused just as many, if not more, problems than his Inferno’s Embrace Skill when it came to the hens.

He needed something that gave him more control of his physiology, not less.

The rooster spent long minutes drawing a giant blank on what the next step was.  As his Core continued to throb, the barest hints of strain became evident, letting Charlemagne know that he was on a time limit.  Despite feeling completely stuck, he never considered attempting to halt the process…going forward was the only option.  And that would require him to do something that was rather distasteful, he realized.

Charlemagne opened his eyes, expecting to see the worried faces of his companions.  Instead, he found an inferno.  The luxurious box that had previously housed the remaining food was completely ablaze.  The pictures, furniture, carpet, and even the comestibles themselves had caught fire.  There was no sign of the others.

Bawk, the rooster called through the Party Chat, but no one responded.  Had Charlemagne been a human, he might have felt at least some despair at finding himself alone and possibly about to explode.  But despair was not a part of Charlemagne’s emotional range.  Instead, he got angry.

Molten Rage activated, sending his combat prowess skyrocketing.  For a moment, the rooster reveled in the feeling of strength, pure and simple, flooding his body.  Then his Ember Core thumped again more powerfully than ever, reminding him of his impending doom.  He could admire himself later.

As he attempted to calm himself, a stray memory bubbled up to the surface.  He, Ndiogou, and Sirius were sitting around a campfire as the sun hung low in the sky.  Meat from a bovine hung over the fire on a spit, hissing and popping as the Blind Hunter turned it slowly.  The human’s other hand was buried in the canine’s fur as they chatted while waiting for the meat to finish cooking.

“How do you stay so calm most of the time, when the people who killed your children are out there somewhere,” the canine asked.  “I wouldn’t have been able to delay my vengeance, if I were in your place.”

“I like to think of us all together in a better place, just having a nice meal on the terrace of a gorgeous hotel overlooking the ocean.  Everyone is smiling and having a nice time as we watch the sailboats slowly cruise around.  The air smells like salt water, and there is a wonderful breeze coming off the ocean that just carries all your cares away,” the human responded.

“Bawk, bawwk” Charlemagne added.

“No, the wind is only a problem if you have a small fire.  And I didn’t say that many swear words when I was trying to light it!”

“You really did,” Sirius barked.

The memory faded as another took its place.

Charlemagne was flying at a tremendous speed, forcing the air in front of him to part through sheer strength.  Eventually, he pushed through the point of greatest resistance, surpassing the speed of sound.  At the time, he hadn’t appreciated the sheer power of the wind, but looking back, it was something that he had struggled to overcome.  Air held a strength of its own.

That realization triggered yet another recollection.

In it, Charlemagne was flying high above the Earth, the atmosphere thinning with each passing moment.  But before he could start suffering from a lack of air, his Fop’s Raiment transformed to protect him, sealing the invisible but precious substance inside his helmet.  Without air, he would not have been able to withstand the desolate and cold void of outer space.

Connections snapped into place within the rooster’s mind as he recognized and appreciated the power of the air for the first time.  He had often taken his ability to soar for granted, but the Squiggles had reminded him of his heritage at just the right time.  His new Class held the answer: he was a Nova Avian Prime.

The air was his birthright.

Charlemagne took a deep breath, allowing superheated air to fill his lungs completely.  As a bird, nature had given him a respiratory system that was more efficient than that of a mammal, but the rooster realized that it could be even better.  Pumping mana into his air capillaries allowed the tiny tubes to gobble up more bits of air, supercharging his blood with its power.  And when that infused blood reached his Ember Core, the fiery mana within reacted violently.

Although the rooster had no way of knowing, the extra oxygen stored by his hemoglobin was exactly what his Core needed to jumpstart the evolution process.  Within seconds, the temperature within his Core had spiked dramatically before starting to fall again.  Not knowing what else to do, Charlemagne continued to flood his lungs with air and mana.  Despite hyperventilating, the excess oxygen was burning away as quickly as he could get it into his blood.  But his efficiency at absorbing oxygen was still climbing.  It quickly surpassed that of the high-flying bar-headed goose, a bird well adapted to the thin air of the Himalayan Mountains.  It broke 50 percent, then 55.

In response to the increasing flood of life-giving gas, Charlemagne’s Ember Core began beating faster.  The mild discomfort swelled into genuine pain as the Core’s rhythmic pulsing grew stronger as well, pressing against organ and bone with explosive force before clenching together with a powerful contraction.  Slowly, the Ember Core began to sync with the rooster’s breath, bringing him one step closer to the end of whatever it was that was happening.  He hoped.

Unfortunately, the process seemed to stall as Charlemagne’s lungs peaked just under 60 percent efficiency.  While this was far better than any pre-System animal, it was insufficient to fuel the incredible furnace that blazed inside him.  Throwing mana at the problem had worked up to a point but no further. The rooster needed actual comprehension of what was going on with his lungs in order to progress any further.

It took a few painful minutes before Charlemagne remembered that he had the Vertebrate Anatomy Skill.  Leaning into the Skill’s insights, he was able to notice that his lungs were pulling much more of whatever it was they were trapping from the air during the first moments of contact with his capillaries than later on.  He also sensed that his lungs were pushing things into the air at the same time they were taking something out of it.  With these two facts, the rooster came to a surprising discovery: he didn’t need all of the air, he just needed something that was being carried around in it.

Reinvigorated by the discovery, Charlemagne strained his internal senses to their utmost, trying to understand what it was that his lungs were taking in.  The individual bits of air were much too small to see, but that was okay.  The rooster could feel them as they passed through the thin walls of his capillaries.  And, with that knowledge, he could use his mana far more efficiently to trap them.

His lungs’ efficiency hopped up again, leaping straight past 60 percent to 62.  Before Charlemagne knew it, he was at 65 percent capacity.  His Ember Core was going wild now, each thump like a miniature atom bomb going off inside his chest.  His organs were bruised by the constant pressure, while his ribs had cracked several times under the strain.  The pain was constant, but Charlemagne had long learned to push through pain, especially when there was no other choice.

Reaching out with his mana manipulation, he pulled all the tiny bits that matched those entering his bloodstream from the surrounding area and concentrated them close to his body.  His breathing felt cleaner and easier after that, allowing his efficiency to once again climb.

68 percent.

69 percent.

70 percent.

As the amount of oxygen being pulled out of the air broke the seventy percent efficiency threshold, Charlemagne’s Ember Core suddenly ceased its incessant pounding.  The abrupt halt almost made the rooster lose his focus and rhythm, but he managed to keep pace.  The Core slowly began to expand as a mixture of mana, fire, and pure oxygen erupted constantly within, burning with a fury that Charlemagne had never before experienced.  Thankfully, since it was contained within his Core, the overwhelming heat did not vaporize the rooster where he stood.

The Ember Core continued to expand until Charlemagne could feel it press against every organ and bone in his upper body.  He wasn’t sure what had gone wrong, but applying his considerable skill at mana and fire manipulation wasn’t enough to keep his Core contained, and the expansion didn’t show any signs of stopping.

“Bawk,” he lamented out loud as he attempted to apply Inferno’s Embrace to his Core in the hope that his Physique Skill would set the Core to rights.  But that didn’t work either.

Charlemagne was about to try something extraordinarily foolish when he realized that he was still hyperventilating and feeding his Core excessive amounts of pure air.  Without hesitation, the rooster held his breath.  The Core stopped expanding almost immediately and began to return to its usual size.  But, as it did, it made a strange, strangled sound.  Then it began vibrating.

The rooster wasn’t sure if he had succeeded or not.  His Ember Core was running much hotter, it was true, but he didn’t feel the qualitative change in it that had accompanied its first evolution.

The Squiggles arrived with a message that partially allayed his fears while raising more questions.

So many questions.
