“This is surprisingly peaceful.”  The Pig’s Tail Descent was just a steady spiral downwards, long, easy loops of tunnel leading ever deeper underground.

“Noooo like, Bob.  Too deep.”

“It’s pretty spacious, though,” I replied to Bighorn.  I wasn’t entirely comfortable with the megatons of rock that were poised above me like an Acme anvil, but thinking about it didn’t help.  The tunnel was wide and well lit by the mage hands carrying lightorbs floating along above us.  I’d reverted to human form for the sake of convenience.  If I didn’t, the others would want to ride on me.  “No sign of the baddies, I really don’t see what all the fuss was about.”

“Too soon to judge,” Art said from where he perched on my shoulder.

“I was really hoping for a cuter pet sidekick.  Why couldn’t Bun-Bun have been less psychotic?” I grumbled.

“PET?  I’ve devoured the bones of ancients!  Marlepope the Burrower fell to these mandibles!”

I looked at the tiny albino mouthpieces chittering next to my cheek.  “X to doubt?  They definitely weren’t those mandibles anyway.  Who was Marlepope?”

It was Granvil who answered.  “A dwarven hero.  We never knew what happened to her.  We all just figured she’d gone too deep and either made a life in the Deep Places or had been eaten by something like that vermin.  You should kill it.”

“I’m not killing Art.”  The insectoid horror rubbed his carapace against my neck.  I couldn’t help but notice that the sharp mandibles seemed to pause for a moment next to my jugular.  “Yet.”

The millipede pulled away with an annoyed clicking sound that made me twitch and want to reach for a can of Raid.  I carefully resisted the urge to make a millipede smoothie on my shoulder.

“Damn straight.  This is the easy bit.  You don’t want to know what happens when you get past the halfway point.”

“We really, really do, you dwarf-eating insect,” Granvil snapped.  “Some of us can’t shit a new version of ourselves out of an eye.”

“I can’t do that again for another decade.  Thanks, Bob.”

I ignored the pointed snark and continued walking ever deeper underground.  The tunnel began to narrow slightly, but not enough to inconvenience the BB-209s who were leading the way.

“So what happens at the halfway point, bug?” I demanded.  There was a looming sense of doom building within me.  My mental menagerie was all huddled together, glancing around nervously.  Despite the absence of an obvious threat, my fingers kept trying to shapeshift into claws.

“Oh, nothing you need to worry about.”

I caught Bighorn's hoof as he attempted to splat Art against my shoulder.

“This is a new tunic, dude.  And I already wasted one today.  Art, you really do need to spill the beans, or I won’t block the next attempt to pulverise you.”

“Bet you wish you’d been nicer to me now, eh?”

“Don’t be smug, bug.  You’re bitesized now, remember?”

“You’ve got all of my old body in storage!  And all the broken machines you brought.  There’s no need to eat me.”

I twisted my head in order to treat Art to a level nine glare.  “Need isn’t the deciding factor, little guy.  Spill.”

“Funny you should choose that word.  How many spirals down are we now?” the insect asked.

“How the hell would I know?  After the first two, it all just blended into one for me,” I snapped.

“We’re six spirals down now.  There should be eighteen in total, if the old maps were true,” Granvil said.

“Thanks, short stuff.  So, even shorter stuff, what the hell happens?”

“We’re close to the Deep Places here.  Who knows what could happen?”

“What the giddy-fuckery is the Deep Places?  You guys keep talking about it like it’s some kind of hell, but I’ve chatted with demons, and most of them seem ok.  Mostly.”

“Demooons have rules.  In the Deep Places there is no rules.”

“It’s a libertarian's paradise?  Bring it on.  I wonder if they have pubs I can take over?”

“No pubs.  It’s just constant war between things that shouldn’t exist.  Most surface dwellers can’t survive five minutes in the Deep Places.”

“Good job we aren’t heading that far, eh?”  I slapped Granvil on the shoulder, earning an angry scowl.

“Oh, but you are.  You want the heart, you’re going to have to go in deep,” Art said sinisterly.

“Why do you make everything weird, dude?  So we need to reach the Deep Places and what, fight our way through hordes of pallid mutants?”

“Sure, but first you’ll need to survive the Descent.”

I looked around at the millipede on my shoulder and gave him a number five glare.  “So quit being cryptic.  I can just turn you into a stain on my tunic whenever I want,” I growled.

“You’d get fat.  Fatter.”

I checked the fit of my spare tunic around the waist.  It was tighter than usual, but I didn’t feel that taking half an hour to get an evolution that wouldn’t fuck me up too much while I upgraded was a good idea.

Half a dozen bunnyborgs pivoted and pointed glowing mana-cannons at my shoulder.  So Master of Puppets wasn’t always awesomely useful.

“You can’t use them either.”  Art squirmed down the back of my collar and aligned himself along my spine, his face sticking up above my clothes.  “Wash your damn hair sometime, mammal.”

“Get the hell out of my clothes,” I snarled.  I was more incensed at being slandered as a mammal than anything else.  His sharp legs bit into my skin just enough to hold on, and were kind of uncomfortable in a prickly sort of way.  The little monster couldn’t seriously hurt me, though.

“No way, you’re pointing bloody beam weapons at me, and I can’t regenerate for ten bloody years!”

“Can you not use the word bloody… quite so much when your toothpick legs are trying to turn my spine into one of those pineapple and cheese hedgehogs!”

“You’ve got god-tier armour stats!  Bloody babies!”

“Ow!  You did that deliberately!” I accused.

“Lies!”

I squirmed and tried to get a grip on the bastard, but he wiggled out of the way.  “You are the shittiest animal sidekick I’ve ever heard of.”

“I’m the one who’s going to save all your lives in a minute.”

Bighorn scratched one of his horns with the back of his axe blade.  “Not sure how?”

“I’m still mighty.  I might be a little… small at the moment.  I’m still an ancient being with thousands of years of life experience that you mayflies just can’t comprehend.”

I’d never heard an insect sniff before.  Not to clear a nasal passage they don’t possess, or to imply a sneer.  Hearing it from my hairline was a novel experience.

“If you want to last longer than a mayfly, I suggest you tell me what the hell is going to happen after we pass the halfway point.”

“You need to make it worth my while.”

“Granvil, dwarves have a weird subterranean diet, right?  It’s all mushrooms and moss and mooncows and stuff?”

“Mooncow?”  Bighorn asked worriedly.

I waggled my eyebrows at the minotaur, and he farted loudly in confusion.

“Oh, uh, yeah.  And insects.  We love a spitroast cockroach.”  Granvil did his best to sound convincing.

“You also like to eat them?” I asked pointedly.  Given the way Art’s mind seemed to work, I felt the need to clarify that we weren’t threatening anything sexual.  Just death, like normal people would.

“Oh yeah.  Protein is protein, after all.”

“I bet you like protein a lot, don’t you, dragon?” Art whisper-chittered into my ear.  I swatted at him, but he dodged back beneath my tunic.

“I’m not completely averse to ruining another set of clothes by transforming into my perfect form, insect.”

“I’ll hide between your scales if you do.”

I really, really, should have just let the borgs kill this asshole.

“What the hell is going to happen?” I snapped.

“You lot don’t have spiricales, do you?  It would be best if you didn’t.”

The tunnel was passing us by.  It was damp, but not as bad as the entrance to the Womb, and there was no sign of Dave the Deep One anywhere. With the floating lights above and the remains of my teams of minions, there was nothing here that could threaten us.

“How far down are we now?” I asked the dwarf.

“Eight loops of the spiral,” Granvil said nervously.  The dwarf glanced around, his beady eyes flicking over the mundane stone walls.

“So whatever is coming is coming soon.  Will you just tell us already?  I really am tempted to let the bunnies blast you.”

Art moved along my spine, his head peaking out to brush against the back of my neck.  The urge to snap my head back, trap him and then smack him into paste was almost overwhelming.

“That’s a weirdly appropriate choice of words.”

“What is going to happen, you little shit?”

“In about another hundred and fifty metres, the flyers will attack.  After that… the cleaning mechanism will kick in.” Glanced about at the conspicuously empty upper reaches of the tunnel.

“You’re pretty calm about this.”

“Of course I am, Bob.  I’m too small to be noticed, and I’ll get a fair few evolutions by consuming my way out of the problem.  Shame you say you’re so full up on biomass.  I’m hungry.”

“If we stop, do I have time to evolve?”

“Oh no.  Not at this point.  You should have done it in the Womb.  One of my surviving children might have attacked if you had, but at least you’d have had an empty belly now.”

Insects elicit a kind of natural revulsion in mammals.  Whether it was due to detritus feeders like woodlice or the hairy hideousness of the bird-eating tarantula type things that are the size of a dinner plate, I couldn’t say for sure.  But something about the insect was almost as instinctively repellent as the natural fear mammals have for snakes.

We had a cat when I was growing up.  Dad wouldn’t let us have a dog, but a cat was sufficiently low maintenance that he had concluded it wouldn’t interfere with family holidays or his day-to-day routine.  I’d discovered the malicious joy of sneaking up on Fluffytail while he was eating and leaving a cucumber on the tiles next to him.  He could leap a good four feet straight up from a standing start.

That is how much any sensible person hates snakes. And most sensible people feel something similar for shadow millipedes from before the dawn of time.

But I hadn’t killed him yet.  Art was being an obnoxious asshole, but my menagerie was relatively chilled out, and I didn’t really feel any impulse to kill him.

“You done fucked up, you idiot,” Art cackled.  I reassessed my position on letting him live.  As my hand reached around to try and snag and crush the bastard, I froze.

“You hear that?” I asked the rest of the group.

“Rumbling,” Bighorn added helpfully.

“No shit.  Sounds like a dam burst?” Granvil added.

I slowly turned to look back the way we had come as the rushing noise grew rapidly louder.  The air changed.  A sense of pressure washed over me, the air behind us was fleeing from…something?

“Oh dear god that had better be pus or milk! Bleurgh!”

A wall of creamy-white please-god-be-water swept around the tunnel to our rear and slammed into us.

“It isn’t!” chortled Art as his claws dug sharply into my spine.
