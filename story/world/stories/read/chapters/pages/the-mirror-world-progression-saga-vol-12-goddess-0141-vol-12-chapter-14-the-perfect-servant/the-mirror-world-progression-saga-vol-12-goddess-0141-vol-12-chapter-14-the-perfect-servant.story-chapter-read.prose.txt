For aiding another in the completion of a grand quest, you’ve gained 4 experience in a sub-class of your choice.

The golem didn’t immediately move or react to Lancelot’s call. Finally, he raised his head very slightly and said in the voice of a noble orator, “I know it, my compatriot, but this ritual act is no longer necessary. In faith, we have awoken to the end. In faith, I believe as she does.” His eyes finally rose to meet Samantha’s. “And so I ask that she who breaks all things is the one to name me.”

Next to her, Ana gasped. The golems were also stunned by this, their expressions especially frozen; stone-faced. The gold one even put a hand over his mouth.

Samantha nodded solemnly, though she couldn’t keep a ghost of a smile off her face. “You are King Arthur.”

The golem shook his head. “A king governs a land, and I am not of a land at all, only an order. By that order, I am a knight, like my brethren. Otherwise, I am merely a servant seeking perfect service; yea, to be a perfect servant, one cannot be a master. I am Sir Arthur, Your Majesty.”

The other golems seemed quite relieved, presumably by him technically naming himself through the correction.

“So be it, Sir Arthur,” Samantha declared, and gestured with her hand. “Rise. You’ve come at a late hour, but just in time. I take it you can summon your weapon.”

The imposing warrior rose and nodded down to her. “Indeed.” He rather dramatically looked up and held out his hand to the heavens, as energy coalesced in a grand flash, and a resplendent sword appeared in it.

It had a golden hilt and circular pommel, in a simple, iconic shape, but intricate designs were etched within the metal, showing dragons on either side with an unusual central black oval. Etched in the pommel at the end was the triskelion — three interlocking spirals. Runes ran down the blade, reading something like, ‘Take this up.’

Like the sword of Lancelot, it was otherworldly, but this one seemed to draw identity and power from afar rather than being an extension of Arthur. The energies mixed with Calrenazzod, forging an interstice with reality, partly warping it in an extensive radius. It was certainly as unique and individual as each of the golems themselves.

Arthur lowered the blade as a scabbard appeared in his other hand. “But it is not mine, Your Majesty.” He spun the blade around, showing the other side’s runic inscription, reading, ‘Cast it away.’ He sheathed the great blade smoothly. “Merely a loaner.” There was a playful note to his words.

Samantha was a bit speechless and in no small amount of awe. She felt heavy deja vu from the sword; felt as if she’d held it herself before, perhaps many times.

What he said was true — the sword could not be owned by anyone, just borrowed for a time from Fate itself. It was a shard made out of it, in some fashion.

“Excalibur,” Samantha muttered, breathless.

“A good a name as any,” Arthur replied with a hint of private amusement.

Collecting herself, Samantha swept her gaze over the greater assembly of the golems. “So we have the twelve Knights of the Round to enter the final conflict. You will fight, I take it?”

Arthur nodded and replied, “To the man — golem, pardon — we will, yes. And at your word, whenever you desire, we’ll unleash a powerful judgment on the battlefield.” He patted the pommel of his sword with his free hand. “However, it should be noted we must surround the target perfectly in a circle. It can be challenging to arrange, as judgment does not sneak, does not ambush, you see. Most will sense and not accept it, will run from and avoid it. As the judge must have enforcers to bring the guilty before them, so must the foe — or foes — be wrangled and submitted in some fashion.”

“Interesting. Is there a trial, then?”

“Of course. They’ll endure a trial to test them. Fail, and they are utterly destroyed.”

Samantha chuckled. “I think I get the picture.”

“I fear I must correct you on a certain thing, however, Your Majesty.”

“Oh?”

“There are not twelve Knights of the Round, but technically thirteen.”

“Fuck my li-” She cleared her throat. “Ahem. Excuse me.” Sighing, she asked, “What do we need to do to secure the other?”

“Nothing.”

“Pardon?”

Arthur smiled and pointed at her. “The monarch must by nature be one of us, and that is you. Though your title is far higher, you are also a Dame of our order.”

“As it is always true, yes,” Lancelot declared confidently. Despite his mithril body, he looked a little scorched for some reason. Odd.

“You held it from the beginning,” the gold one, Percival, said, “but welcome, nonetheless.”

Samantha smiled, feeling relieved. She touched a hand to her heart and inclined her head to Sir Arthur. “I am humbled and honored to be counted among you.” Wincing suddenly, she turned to the others. “Actually, sorry about being harsh with you all before. I was worried about Ana, but I got a bit carried away. I apologize.”

“No need, Your Majesty,” the iron golem, Lucan, called. “It’s nothing. We understand.”

“In fact,” the bronze golem added, “we deserved the scolding. It should not have gotten to that point.”

The silver golem touched both hands to his heart. “But oh, even for her abundance of power, see here! Marvel at the compassion and gentleness of our patron — how it touches the heart! For this, I will slay a thousand, I swear; for her beauty, two thousand!”

“Cease your boasting, Sir Tristan,” the lapis lazuli golem said in exasperation. “Though I have no doubt the enemy will fall like pollen in the spring, we’ll be coordinating together for the best results.”

Tristan snorted at him, dropping his hands. “As usual, the bumpkin knight holds no appreciation for poetic license, slaying the mood like no other.”

“Sir, I once again take exception to your preposterous and insulting characterization of-”

“Enough,” Arthur called firmly with a raised hand. Turning back to Samantha, he said, “We will otherwise submit to your authority and to those you’ve appointed as generals for the coming conflict.”

Samantha, still grinning at the banter, gave Arthur a thumbs-up. “Yes. Get with Jeeves and Dax, and please, if you have wisdom to share, do so. We’ve got minds closer to the specifics of this world, but I know you must be another strategic and tactical genius. Don’t hold back out of that politeness you lot seem to possess.”

Arthur rubbed his metallic beard in consideration. “I understand, Your Majesty. So it will be done.” He nodded in the direction of the steel golem. “Sir Lucan is arguably more brilliant than any in this, though he’ll deny it.”

Lucan seemed physically assaulted by this. He held up his hands. “With all due respect, Sir Arthur, I’m really not that-”

“Regardless of his vast humility, I will ensure his knowledge surfaces, one way or another.” He nodded with confident certainty. Meanwhile, Lucan appeared to be drowning in misery.

Ana spoke up at this point. “It should be pointed out that Galahad will need to go.”

“This is true,” the dour troll golem, Galahad, declared. “My production efforts will need to cease for a season so I may join my brothers in duty — if the Goddess grants it so.”

“Of course,” Samantha replied. “Ultimately, that production helped get us to here, the culmination of everything. I could hardly deny you being a part of it.” Slapping her hands together and floating upward, she addressed them all. “I leave you to your duties, knights, as I go to finish mine. Make your preparations count, because we’re leaving very soon.”

“Thank you, by the way, Mother,” Ana called, smiling upward in her general direction. “I’ve never felt less drained after one of these. Ironic, but true. I feel almost refreshed, in truth."

“I am always happy to help. Keep an eye on things while we’re gone, yes?”

Ana’s smile faltered a bit, but she did a cute and too-formal curtsy. “Of course, Mother.”

On Samantha’s tongue was the quick desire to say, ‘I’ll be back, I promise,’ but she just couldn’t make it spill forth, not to her daughter, not to an oracle.

Damn it.

Her senses reached out, taking hold of the whole tower, perceiving it, absorbing it, trying to understand intuitively if the place was her destiny ever again. Desperately, she wanted that assurance.

She didn’t get it. She just didn’t know.

I don’t know if I fear just losing, or winning as well. Director… have you felt this pain, too?

Unable to parse the discomfort and needing to hurry, Samantha zipped away, re-focusing deliberately on the many trials she had ahead. There was nothing else she could do.

Ana felt her spiritual mother fly away, the awkward energy in the air plenty thick enough to tell her that the goddess had swallowed a typical platitude she’d wanted to utter before leaving.

I hope I hid my sadness well enough, too.

The future was especially fluid lately, giving her only the barest glimpses. She did not know if her fears were prescient or the nerves of someone watching their loved ones go off to war. It was hardly an alien thing in the world right then. Perhaps a million people had felt the same thing in recent days.

The signs only pointed to an end, to the apocalypse. Everywhere she ‘looked,’ she saw it in her mind’s eye. It was nearly maddening, like the code of every spare definition to the world’s objects was ready to melt away. Unravel.

Signs had shown her various things, some to act on, some to simply know and confirm for the spirit of things. She’d seen the flexing proud statue in the mountains remain standing as purifying blue fire raged at its feet, and knew that Rivermount would be liberated and retained with strength to spare. She’d seen two men who were enemies shake hands under a golden heaven, and knew that one of the Dominion’s greatest champions would convert to their side. Fate had surprises, such as Palthennius not dueling Canmore in the pivotal moment, but instead Azure, but the sign still came true.

Anything she’d done to help things along had been minor. Tiny nudges, suggestions here or there. That was her role — a little something to contribute in the proper timing.

One way or another, all of the signs came true.

But it had tapered off more and more as she went along, eventually telling her next to nothing, and so she was very grateful for the significant task of the golems on her plate. She’d been driven like never before to see it through, and she had. Now, her life seemed… finished. Full stop.

Only emptiness was left, like a void.

It was an odd and terrifying feeling, especially for an oracle.

She made her way to her room and sat down alone in the starkness of the quiet darkness. Perhaps she was lucky in that — to taste what was waiting for nearly everyone in the end, if oblivion was their final home. Being blind, she’d had to reconcile herself with it already.

Head turning upward, she muttered a prayer, and to whom or what, she didn’t know.

“Please… please let me see them all again…”
