Callie ran with all her might, but she couldn't keep up with her brother. For a moment, she had this horrible feeling that her brother would leave her behind. Not just at this moment, but forever. That he would become like everyone else in the village who didn't believe a word that she said.

But then Callen glanced back at her. Seeing her fall behind, Callen slowed down and grabbed her hand. "It'll be alright. I'm sure it's just a minor injury."

Callie could feel some of the tension within her wash away. Her brother hadn't turned his back on her. She would have to explain things properly so that Callen could truly understand the situation. But for now, they had to focus on the crisis at hand.

When they reached the healer's hut, hand in hand, Callen threw open the door, and they both saw that Dad was already standing by one of the beds. Mom was lying on it, her eyes squeezed shut in a pained expression.

"MOM!" Callen and Callie both yelled and ran to their mother's side.

"Settle down, kids. Yelling isn't going to help," Granny Ethel chastised.

Callie was barely paying attention to her words. Instead, she was methodically looking over Mom. She saw a few bruises on her face and hands. There were a few holes in her leather armor, some of which had light stains of blood surrounding them. But none of the injuries seemed deep. Merely scratches.

Then she noticed a nasty gash on her arm. While it was a bit deeper than the other scratches, this one stood out for a different reason. The skin around it was pulsing bright red, and she could see a bit of pus forming in the injury.

"Granny Ethel, is this wound infected?" Callie asked nervously, pointing at the wound in question.

This was bad. Infections were no joke, especially those received from either animals or arcane beasts. Granny Ethel had taught Callie the importance of hygiene and keeping things clean around wounds. But claws weren't exactly clean; bits of dirt, grime, and even meat residue could easily get into the wound and infect it.

Then another horrible realization hit Callie. Mom had been tracking an arcane beast. What if this one had a similar ability to Wiggles? Like making its claws acidic or poisonous?

Granny Ethel sighed. "Yes, but I'm afraid that's not all. Maeve was tracking a glauxis. Am I right?"

Dad nodded. "Presumably. She went to confirm whether it was a glauxis or just a lynx."

Callie had never seen a glauxis before. She knew it was a creature similar to a lynx, except larger, but that was all she knew. It was uncommon to see large arcane beasts around these parts.

"Then the wound is not just infected but also poisoned. Glauxis can secrete venom in their claws. It's why they are such fearsome hunters. Just one scratch is all that they need to secure their prey."

Callie gasped in shock. No, this couldn't be. Her Mom was… poisoned. By an arcane beast no less. She didn't know what to think of this. It was horrifying to think that her Mom was poisoned. Wh-What if she died? Would it be the glauxis's fault that she died? Or was it Mom's fault for hunting it?

Her emotions were in turmoil, fear and doubt filling her.

"You can create an antidote, can't you?" Dad asked desperately.

"I can, but there are several paths we can take in this situation. I can assure you that she will not die," Granny Ethel said.

Callie, Callen, and Dad all sighed with relief. That was the most important part. As long as Mom survived, nothing else mattered.

"However, if I can't make an antidote in two days, we'll have to cut her arm at the shoulder to stop the spread of the venom," Granny Ethel said with a hardened expression.

Callie's stomach lurched. Her hands turned clammy, and cold sweat covered her whole body. Cut off… Mom's arm?

"N-No…" Callie whimpered. "You can't cut off her arm!"

Dad put a strong hand on Callie's shoulder. "If it must be done for Maeve to survive, then do it."

"No, no, no! Mom can't lose an arm… Mom can't…" Callie's eyes filled with tears. Something so horrible couldn't happen to her Mom. "Granny Ethel, you must prepare the antidote."

"I would do it right away, but I am missing a few key ingredients," the elderly woman said, her eyes filled with regret.

"What do you need?! I'll find it!" Callie said with determination.

"That's it. If there's a way to save her arm, we'll do whatever is in our power!" Callen added, and Dad nodded.

"Two things. One is a herb growing deep in the forest. Its blooming season is yet to begin, but there is hope that a few early buds might have sprouted in some sunnier places. Callie must already know of it. Dandilynx," Granny Ethel said.

"It's the bright yellow one with many tiny petals," Callie said, although it was still too early for the first dandilynx to bloom. They usually appeared in late summer. It was when Granny Ethel usually sent Callie and Tristan to collect them. "How much do you need?"

"Not much. A single blooming flower should do, or around three still blossoming buds," Granny Ethel said. "But it's the other ingredient that got me more worried."

"What is it?" Callen asked.

"It's… mireling slime," Granny Ethel said, giving Callie a significant look.

Callie's eyes widened. Under normal circumstances, finding mireling slime was a game of luck. Nobody could predict where mirelings went. Predicting where they would leave some slime residue was even harder. But for Callie, this wasn't an issue.

"I'll go get it right away!" Callie said, sprinting towards the door.

"Grab a jar from the storage first," Granny Ethel called after her.

Callie whirled around, ran into the storage, grabbed the satchel and one of the empty jars, and ran back home before anyone could say anything. The villagers gave her strange looks, and some even asked questions as she rushed past them, but Callie ignored them.

She could save Mom. All she had to do was find Wiggles. Would the mireling still be near the well where he had hidden? She desperately hoped so.

The moment Callie got to their backyard, she called, "Wiggles! Wiggles! Are you here?"

Initially, nobody responded, and she feared that the mireling had returned to the forest. But after a minute, the mireling appeared from behind the chicken coop.

Friend?

"Wiggles!" Callie called in relief. She also realized that she had never even introduced herself to him. While now hardly seemed like the time for it, Callie figured it would be rude. After all, she had named him, yet not given him her own name. "I'm sorry."

Sorry?

The little mireling slid closer to her.

"I didn't introduce myself, despite us meeting so many times. My name is Callie," Callie said, crouching down by the mireling.

Wiggles extended its tendril, and she allowed him to touch her palm. As if in a handshake.

Callie. Friend. Me. Wiggles.

Callie chuckled. She took the tendril gently and squeezed it gently. "Nice to meet you, Wiggles."

For a moment, they enjoyed the moment quietly. Callie also wondered how best to bring up the topic. It somehow felt wrong to ask Wiggles for his slime. It was part of him after all. But then she got an idea.

"Wiggles, can you wait for me here for a moment?" Callie asked.

Wait.

She ran inside, and after rummaging around the cooking area for a bit, she finally found what she was looking for. A real treasure. Something saved up for special occasions. But this was a special occasion. With this, Callie will be one step closer to saving Mom.

Coming outside, she held her offering gingerly in her closed palm, making sure not to squeeze it too hard.

"Wiggles, could I ask you something?" Callie said, crouching down in front of the mireling.

Yes.

Callie reached out her palm towards the mireling and opened it, revealing a sugar cube. They only had a small amount of the precious sweet treats. Sugar was very expensive, and it was only ever used when Dad made pies for someone's birthday. But Callie felt it was a fair price.

What that?

Mireling extended a tendril towards the sugar cube and touched it very carefully. A gentle prod first. Then a slightly stronger one. Callie saw a little bit of the sugar dissolve in the slime.

Oooo! Tasty!

Wiggles surrounded the sugar cube with his slime, and it dissolved very, very slowly. As if he was savouring the flavor. The mireling wobbled with delight, all the while chanting.

Tasty. Tasty. Tasty!

"Wiggles, could you help me with something? You see, my mom is very sick," Callie began.

Mom?

Callie pondered the question for a moment. "Mom is the person who created me. She cares for me and protects me, even if we argue sometimes. Mom is very precious to me."

Despite lacking any facial features, Callie could tell that Wiggles was listening to her words intently.

Mom. Precious. Kin?

"Yes, Mom is my kin. And to help her, I need medicine made from your slime. C-Could you please give me some?" Callie asked, pulling the glass jar out of her satchel. She honestly felt rather bad for asking. Slime must be important to a mireling.

Slime?

"If it's possible. It is needed to make an antidote for Mom," Callie said. She dearly hoped that getting some slime wouldn't be a painful process for Wiggles.

Sure. Sure. Callie gave the tasty. Wiggles give slime.

The mireling extended a tendril into Callie's jar, and she watched as the mireling filled it halfway up with its slime.

"Does it hurt?" Callie asked nervously.

No. No pain. Need eat more. Make slime.

"Should I give you food?" Callie asked, although she wasn't sure what exactly she could offer him. It wasn't like they had loads of food lying around. Perhaps something from the garden? Dad would be quite mad if he found out, though…

Wiggles eat tasty thin potatoes.

"Thin potatoes?" Callie cocked her head quizzically.

Wiggles slithered away, behind the chicken coop, and Callie followed him. She then saw that the mireling was eating the potato peels from yesterday's dinner.

Tasty thin potatoes.

Callie chuckled. The mireling looked very content, munching on the peels.

"I'll leave some more for you later," she promised.

Yay! Thanks, Callie.

"Thank you! I'm going to take this slime to the healer. Thank you so much, Wiggles!" Callie said and gingerly placed the jar in the satchel.

She walked to the healer's hut at a much slower pace, afraid that she might trip and break the jar of slime. That would be disastrous. When she returned, Dad and Callen were still talking with Granny Ethel.

"I got the mireling slime!" Callie announced, pulling the jar out of the satchel and showing it to everyone.

"What?! How did you get it so quickly?" Dad exclaimed in shock.

"This looks so pure!" Granny Ethel took the jar, admiring the greenish slime within it.

Callen smiled. "That means we just need to find one more ingredient. Right?"

"Yes, but…" Granny Ethel looked at Callie in surprise.

"I'll explain where I got the slime later. I think it's important that we search for the dandilynx flower right away," Callie said.

"She's right," Dad confirmed. "Maeve's health comes first. Callie, you know where to find the flowers, right?"

"I do," Callie confirmed.

"Lead us there. We're all going to search together," Dad said.

"Head into the forest beyond your house. I'll send Tristan to search the flower meadow and the forest beyond it," Granny Ethel said.

Callie nodded and ran off. Dad and Callen ran after her. They didn't speak; instead, their minds were focused on one thing only—finding the flower to save Mom. Callie wouldn't allow her to lose her arm. No! Something so horrible couldn't happen to Mom.

They searched the forest for a long time. Even Dad, who should have gone to work, continued to search throughout the day. But all they found were young dandilynx leaves. Nothing even resembling a bud, much less a grown flower. But they didn't give up. They kept searching, all three of them.

"Any luck, Callie?" Callen asked.

"No… It's just too early for them to bloom," Callie whimpered, feeling tears welling up in her eyes. If they couldn't find the flowers, Mom's arm…

"We'll find it! We just have to keep searching!" Dad said stubbornly, not lifting his eyes off the ground.

Dad couldn't even tell the herbs apart very well, but he nonetheless kept searching, often asking Callie to confirm whether he was looking for the right plant. Initially, most of what he brought was wrong. But by the end of the day, Dad had memorized the typical sharp edges of dandilynx leaves.

Not that leaves would help them. It was the flower that contained the necessary properties for the antidote. Frustrated and filled with fear, Callie stared at one of the dandilynx plants.

"Why haven't you bloomed?" She muttered at it angrily.

At that moment, she felt something stir within her. The hot sparkling energy swelled in her chest. Could she maybe use it to make the flower grow?
