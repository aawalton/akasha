Her mother was busy with doing some kind of prep work when Beth walked in. Neither of her sisters were around, likely still in their rooms.

"I see you're back," her mother said, glancing over at her. "You should have let me know you were going out there. Be more careful."

"Well, dad knew. Isn't that okay?" Beth replied.

"Don't conflate your father and me. I would appreciate being informed when you run off to fight monsters," her mother frowned. "Anyway, have you eaten?"

"I had some stuff at the meeting hall," Beth replied with a frown of her own. "But I am still hungry."

Her mother pulled out a container from the refrigerator and set it on the island with some dressing. She pulled out a second container and stuck it in the microwave. Beth started in on the first container, a large salad which she coated in Ranch Dressing. Her mother set a glass of water in front of her and took the dressing.

"Easy on the dressing, Beth," she said, putting it back in the fridge.

"Hm, hmph, smph," Beth replied.

"Just eat, Beth."

Beth tore through the salad like a buzz saw through, well, lettuce. Just as she was finishing, her mother set a bowl of pasta in a light sauce in front of her and took the salad bowl. She rinsed the first bowl and left it in the sink and returned to the prep work she had been doing when Beth walked in.

"Thanks for the food," Beth said, getting up.

"Wash your hands and face before you leave," her mother admonished.

Beth did so and then returned to the living room where Zack and her father were still going over information. They had finished going over the neighborhood safety information, including contacting some people via their cell phones to check their safety, and were now on to information about monsters and levels. Beth reclined on the couch and waited, looking at the TV, until Zack brought her into the conversation.

"So, Beth. I'd like to try some things. Would you be willing to take your father and I to the woodlot to try to take out a few ants?" he asked.

"Uh, sure. It's not really that hard, let's go," she responded, hopping up again.

"Give us a moment to talk to your mother. Go out to the backyard and wait," her father responded.

Zack and Thomas moved into the kitchen to speak with Rachel briefly while Beth walked out through the mudroom into the backyard. After just a minute, Zack and her father joined her. Beth simply motioned for them to follow and walked through the yard towards the woodlot.

As they entered the wood, Beth looked around while proceeding slowly. She could take out the ants easily with her ability, but they could still hurt her with their mandibles. She would really like to not be surprised and have bandages on both legs before the end of the day.

"There," she said, pointing off the left.

"I see it. Let me try," Zack replied and walked toward the ant. As he approached, he drew a large, double-bladed knife from a sheath attached to his belt and assumed a fighting stance, bringing his hands up to guard.

As he got close, the ant performed the same as each one Beth had killed so far. From antennae wiggling frantically, to turning towards the source of disturbance, to charging at the source headlong. Zack stopped as the ant charged and observed its approach. When it got to within a few feet, he stepped forward at an angle much like Beth would and slashed with the knife. Whether from being blessed with low intelligence or poor reflexes, the ant barely even started to react as the knife sliced entirely through its left eye.

The ant immediately hissed and thrashed, but Zack was an experienced old soldier and had already taken a step back in preparation for a retaliation from the ant. Its flailing about didn't hit him, and as it seemed to refocus and turn toward him, he stepped forward on the ant's left side and drove the point of the knife deep into the already ruined left eye. The ant made a slight hissing sound and collapsed, dead as a doornail.

"Well, these little bastards are certainly tougher than you might expect. A good steel knife still gets the job done, though," he commented before getting a far-off look.

"You can look through the class menu. I'll make sure nothing runs up in the meantime," Beth told him.

After a minute or so he turned to Beth. "I have a couple questions kid. First, I only get to pick one of these things, right?" he said while pointing at empty air in front of himself.

"Yeah, for now at least. In some games you can get more classes later or evolve your class, but right now it looks like you just get one," she responded.

"Second, what would you recommend? I have Young Warrior, Young Mage, Man-at-Arms, and Marine," he questioned.

"Well, I would say it is either between Man-at-Arms or Marine. It seems like almost everybody on Earth gets the first two. Anything besides them is bonus for either your stats or your accomplishments in life or something," she explained, again tapping her chin.

"I don't really know the difference. I would pick Marine as my first choice because, well, Semper Fi. But is it better than Man-at-Arms?" he asked.

"At this level you're just looking for stats. I think it might also affect what happens later, if the class evolves. Like a Man-at-Arms might become a knight or champion of some kind. A Marine might become like a fleet commander or a general or something. I don't really know enough about how the Path works."

"I see, so you're saying both stats and future classes weigh in on this? Even an old Marine like me can do this simple math, they both get six points total every level. I think I'll stick with what worked for me for twenty-two years," Zack pondered, then made a gesture in the air while again saying, "Semper Fi."

Beth chuckled, "You don't have to touch it, it's even better than a touch screen, you just think of the option you want, and it picks it."

"Right, girl, let's see about getting Thomas one of these classes," he ignored her laughter and got ready to continue on.

"Hold on. You should assign your points first if you didn't. You get two free ones for getting to level one, and you can put free points in any stat. Depending on what your class gives you per level, it might be a good idea to shore up what it doesn't give you a little."

"And what do you mean by that, sweetie?" Beth's father asked as both men attended to her explanations.

"I don't know how important each stat is; there’s so much I don’t know about this. But, based on what I know from games and such, you don’t want to totally neglect any stats. I would try to get everything to at least ten at first, and maybe we’ll learn more about how stats work as we go. I would just say to be aware that every stat has a use. For example, Intelligence usually affects how much damage or how big a spell or special ability is; I don’t know how that applies to us, but you might need a few points in Intelligence to get your skills to do any actual damage," she explained, holding her chin-tapping finger up as she lectured.

"I see. Well, I get a Wisdom every level but not an Intelligence, though my Intelligence is at 7. I guess I should catch my Dexterity up, it's only at 6," he said before his eyes got the slightly far-away look of somebody interacting with their screens. After another moment he re-focused and said, "Let's get your dad a kill now."

They started heading north again, and Zack flipped the knife in his hand to grab the blade and held it out to her father. Her father took the blade with a nod, and they continued for about half a minute before Beth stopped and pointed.

"Another one ahead and just to the right," she said.

Her father approached the ant carefully and proceeded to use much the same technique, stepping forward at an angle. He did not, however, go for an eye slash like Zack, but with careful aim, stabbed down right behind the ant's head and twisted. The head of the ant practically popped off and the body collapsed immediately, the legs twitching slightly.

"Good kill," commented Zack.

"Check your classes, we'll keep watch," Beth commented, giving her father a thumbs up.

Her father checked through everything while nodding. He pulled out a small pocket notebook like Mr. Scott had been using and made a few notes. He continued for a minute before giving a firm nod and turning to the two of them. "All done. I would like to go back to the house and swap out for Rachel so she can get level one as well."

"What did you get?" Beth asked eagerly.

"Other than the standard classes, I had several other options. In the end, I picked Apprentice Spellblade. I liked the description, and it has rather good stats."

"Oh, a Spellblade. That's pretty nice," Beth nodded.

"What the hell is a Spellblade?" asked Zack.

"They are usually swordsmen of some kind that either cast magic on their sword or fight with a sword while also casting spells. They aren't necessarily the best swordfighters or the best mages, but the combination of both physical and magical attacks makes them really strong," Beth explained again, tapping at her chin.

"Sounds pretty tough. Well good, let's rinse and repeat," Zack said, and led the group back through the woodlot and to the Bell's house. Beth's father handed the knife to Zack and headed inside and, a minute later, Beth's mother joined them.

"I guess I'm killing an ant?" she asked.

Zack simply nodded and handed her the knife.

They repeated the same process, walking into the woodlot a way until Beth pointed out an ant again.

"So, I just stab it, huh?" her mother asked.

"Let me help, it doesn't matter for the first kill," Beth replied.
