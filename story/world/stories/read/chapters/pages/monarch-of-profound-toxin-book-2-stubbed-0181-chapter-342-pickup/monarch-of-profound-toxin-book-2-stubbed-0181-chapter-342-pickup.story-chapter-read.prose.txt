Within seconds, the sluggish flow of miasma from the mouth of the entranced spawn of the Lord of the Moon grew to a steady stream of pitch.

For another eight to ten seconds, the miasma continued to stream swiftly. Building up more and more mass, it enveloped the effigy and appeared to thicken and condense around the main body like a cream sauce being reduced in a pan but was quickly absorbed to disappear without a trace.

“Yes!” Eik exclaimed, throwing his hands into the air.

Forgetting his own strength, the spawn he had been holding went skyward as well, spinning through the air. A tendril of Profound Toxin snatched it out of the air and brought it back down to the ground.

Eik tossed it over toward the two others that had already been deemed duds.

“The effigy is swallowing up the miasma…” the lead analyst breathed. “But why only that one? Why not those other two before it?”

Mn’Toakh shrugged. “Maybe they have already spent their miasma recently. If it functions like some of the bigger abilities of common Awakened, it’s possible that they simply can’t do it endlessly.”

The scientist nodded to himself as his fingers absentmindedly scratched through his unkempt stubble. “That makes sense. But with enough miasma in this world to completely permeate the entire atmosphere of the planet, there is no way that this is the only one of its kind. There has to be more of them.”

“They’re tough to locate,” Chop piped up, speaking unprompted for the first time since they had gotten there. He had finally returned the new, glittering blade of azure crystal to the scabbard. Everybody listened attentively as he spoke. “Even Eik and I could only barely sense them in the miasma. Their aura signatures are so similar that its as if they’re not there.”

“So we get rid of the miasma first,” one of the geologist suggested. “What’s the big deal? You’ve done it easily on other worlds, have you not?”

Eik bit his lower lip and sighed. “The skill I used to do that obliterates everything, including entire forests, mountains, and ocean, so a fragile little thing like this would simply end up as unseen collateral, unfortunately,” he said, gesturing at the semi intact effigy. “And anything subtle enough to let us recognize one of these before it is destroyed would take more time than I have.”

“Before we worry about that, how about we check how the last two beasts do, just to see?” Mn’Toakh urged.

The fourth spawn managed to deliver a minuscule and barely noticeable amount of miasma before becoming spent, while the fifth and final one released the greatest amount yet, the almost liquid blackness pouring out like a jet.

Discussions broke out among the various experts, Eik growing quiet for a few moments as he became lost in thought. He cleared his throat and spoke to Andihar and Mn’Toakh. “Maybe we don’t have to get rid of the miasma after all,” he said as an idea began to take hold in his mind. Senseless destruction was certainly a great answer to many problems in the Unified Mass, but that didn’t mean it was always the best.

“Go on,” Andihar pushed.

“If it takes too long to locate more effigies on my own, then I just need to recruit an army.”

“An army?” Mn’Toakh repeated. “You mean that group of yours, Eik’s Great Group of Guys? You’d be sending them to their deaths out here.”

“No, not them, of course;” Eik said with a grin and held up a hand, fingers spread apart. On the tip of each finger a tiny serpent manifested in a swirl of brilliant Profound Toxin. “I’ve got just the thing. Or actually, I’ve got more than a million of just the thing.”

The spawn of the Lord of the Moon moved deftly through the thick miasma, its senses, whether eyes or something completely different, allowing it to traverse and avoid obstacles as if it was a clear and sunny day.

If one had possessed an ability capable of looking into the mind of another being, a deep dive into the spawn’s head would have been a disappointingly uneventful trip indeed.

At present, there was really only room for a single desire inside the pathetic little walnut of tissue that was supposed to be its brain—completing the task granted to it by its master.

And what a privilege it was. What an honor.

Birthed by the will of a single, supreme existence, what else could it be but pure bliss to dedicate its life to the very purpose of its creation—service to the Lord of the Moon?

Somewhere inside that simple head was a sliver of pity. Pity for all other creatures in the universe that did not have the opportunity to experience this absolute joy of servitude.

It put a smile on its humanoid features. No, that wasn’t right. A smile didn’t do the expression justice. It was a grin. Wide and toothy, it took up a good third of the real estate of its pale face.

Ah, there it was!

Finally, the goal was nearing. Just up ahead.

Gradually, the spawn slowed down to a speed that resembled that of an A-ranked Awakened. Eventually it turned into a skidding stop and then a slow jog. It wouldn’t do to damage the object by accident. It deserved to be treated with all possible care.

This wasn’t the first time the spawn had been sent on a mission like this. They had different purposes and specialties, and this was what it did best. It was a point of pride that the master deemed it especially skilled at this.

Impossibly, the grin widened even further.

The effigy was exactly where the spawn knew it would be, its base buried in the ground so the main body could stand freely and surrounded by the miasmic flows. Cautiously, it knelt in the soil.

Under one arm, the beast carried a second black figurine. It knew not what the object was nor why it this exchange had to be done. But none of that mattered. All that mattered was unquestioning obedience and certain success.

Carefully, it laid the effigy it had brought on the ground before grasping the base of the buried one with two of its four hands, the other two lightly supporting the main body. The grin faded momentarily as concentration on the job at hand took over. With slow and deliberate movements, the effigy was loosened from the soil until it came free completely.

Perfect execution!

Lips widening immediately, the grin returned in full force.

It was with reverence that the spawn placed the filled effigy on the gray soil and picked up the new one. With a hand, it pierced the ground deeply, up to the elbow. It was a perfect fit for the new effigy.

The dirt briefly whirled up into a small cloud as the monster scooped it into the small spaces in the hole and packed it tightly to make sure the construct would stay standing upright even in tougher weather, but with the weight of the miasma, it was quickly pushed back down to the ground again.

With everything over and done with, the spawn stood back up, the filled effigy held gingerly against its chest. Satisfied, it turned and began to walk back the way it had come, this time advancing slowly.

It didn’t make it five paces before… something passed by behind its back.

Now on high alert and nearly dropping the filled effigy, it whirled around to confront whatever it was. There was nothing at all.

But there had been something… right? It had been brief and it had been silent, but it had been there. A snarl escaped its throat as it took a few steps toward the spot where it thought the movement had been.

With no windup, the spawn abruptly lunged forward, one of its hands releasing the soft grasp on the effigy to rake the ground. The miasma was dispersed violently by the resulting blast that carried multiple tons of broken debris into the air as the ground exploded.

Eleven more vicious attacks shattered the area around it in all directions. If it hadn’t been able to see through the miasma before, it would be able to now that it had been scattered into the wind, and its eyes flitted all around, scouting for even the slightest hint of movement or ability use.

Still nothing.

Eyes narrowed, it lurked around the area as the miasma slowly returned and covered it entirely again.

Had it been one of its brethren, it would have known. But no other monsters had been left alive on the world so that was also out of the question.

Not a single indication of a foreign presence remained.

For a good hour, the spawn of the Lord of the Moon continued its search for an enemy that it couldn’t even know with certainty had ever been there. But it was cautious by nature and its master would appreciate a thorough investigation before returning.

But with no remnants of skill use, no physical trail, and no other sign of a presence other than its own to speak of at all, the concern in its stomach gradually died down until, finally, it became convinced that it had been nothing after all. It stood up straight, folded all four arms over the effigy again and turned around to return from where it had originally come.

This time, nothing popped up, and it walked back undisturbed.

However, if it had had the ability to look just a little bit deeper—if it had been just a little bit more perceptive—perhaps the spawn of the Lord of the Moon would have been able to realize what had happened.

Everything about its facial features, and indeed its whole head, was almost entirely like that of a human, and that included its ears. And deep within the canal of that ear, a brand new resident was in the process of establishing its new home.

Quietly and with a presence erased to completion, the Living Manifestation settled in to bide its time.
