The next morning started from Elynara waking up and immediately going about realizing a new part of her grand Plan!

And she was even having some success with it, until a sudden slap landed square into her right cheek, waking her from the dream with the precision of a creature that had the move completely practiced out.

Groaning, she flicked a hand upward without looking, and felt the soft bounce of a certain glowy puff retreat just enough to avoid a second round.

Starpuff chirped above her like it hadn’t done anything wrong, which was technically true and completely false.

She cracked one eye open.

Warm ceiling beams. Faint breeze through the window. The unmistakable scent of spiced flour baking into something probably dangerous to her self-control.

“Rina’s at it again,” she mumbled, voice still coated in sleep.

The air shifted near her pillow, and Starpuff drifted downward in a slow spiral, bumping into the blanket with just enough force to announce itself again. She swatted half-heartedly at the puffball with a bit of mana for it to gobble up and sat up.

Last night’s thoughts didn’t rush in, but they were still present. Even her dream was about them.

Not that she remembered much of it already, as dreams are wont to be.

But what mattered were her thoughts from the last night.

That quiet tension, and potential, coiled and stable, yet ready to be released at a moment’s peril. And then her thoughts turned to the other realizations, and stopped.

She still couldn’t really do anything about them. But they gave her a clarity of mind she didn’t know she needed.

A grin tugged at the edge of her mouth.

For now… She decided there was no reason to tarry.

Her legs swung over the side of the bed. The floor held a mild chill, enough to wake her properly. Her staff waited nearby, leaned neatly against the wall. She took it with her as she settled into the open space beside the bed and crossed her legs in practiced ease.

The morning cycles waited.

Starpuff circled once, then perched silently on the windowsill, glow reduced to a low pulse, watching her with its own sort of curiosity.

She let the rhythm take her.

Inhale. Draw. Hold and push.

Exhale. Release. Pause and restart.

Her core responded quickly, the flows of mana didn’t resist, meeting her halfway.

She noted as her awareness expanded through the familiar routes, brushing against the reserves she had stretched and trained so many times before. She eased into the tension. Held it, and let it flex.

The hundred-point line had long since faded behind her. One hundred and ten came smooth. Clean.

She didn’t press past it, quite happy with the slow progress.

There was no need to rush, not when she had plans to train… and the Plan to get to.

She opened her eyes.

The light had changed, and it was morning proper now. With a clear sky and the sound of birds making noise about it.

Behind her, Mina shifted under the blanket, made a noise that sounded like resistance and regret tangled into one word, and promptly flopped back over. Pufflet didn’t move, but a soft snort confirmed he was still present and unconcerned.

Elynara stood slowly. Her limbs moved without the morning drag, the meditation helping ease her muscles in ways she didn’t expect, but very much appreciated. The quiet ache in her shoulders from days past had faded into memory too. She stretched both arms up, cracked her back once, and smiled at the sound it made.

Movement outside caught her eye. Someone passed by the window with a basket. It was probably Finnie, given the speed and lack of directional consistency. Rina’s voice followed not far behind, sharp and cheerful, already halfway into a scolding.

The smell of food had grown more aggressive. She smelled cinnamon, and something else she couldn’t place. Definitely something fried and nice.

Elynara’s stomach made a dramatic case for urgency.

Starpuff trilled a single note and zipped once around her head before darting toward the door.

“Traitor,” she muttered, grabbing her outer tunic and slipping it over her head as she followed. “You don’t even eat.”

It chirped again. Smug.

She didn’t mind.

The Plan could wait a little, all progress needing time and effort.

And if she moved fast, she might just make it to the kitchen before the best slices disappeared!

And judging by Mina’s shuffling behind her, the girl had much the same idea.

Moving out, the kitchen had already tipped into a manageable disaster.

Finnie shot past the stove with a handful of herbs, spun on one foot, and nearly collided with a flour sack.

Theren didn’t flinch. He kept slicing roots in neat, even pieces, expression fixed somewhere between serenity and denial. Aren sat near the window with a mug cupped in both hands, eyes tracking nothing in particular. Possibly meditating, or just enjoying the relative quiet. While it lasts.

Elynara slipped inside behind Mina, ducking under a thrown dishrag that was absolutely not being guided by anything magical, just good aim and tons of practice.

Probably.

Starpuff zipped once over the table, knocked itself over a wooden spoon, then vanished under the rafters, not even acknowledging its failure.

“Morning,” Elynara called, already reaching for a cutting board.

Rina waved without looking up.

“Grab a knife. And don’t touch the red pot, it’s piping hot.”

“Noted.”

She took her place near the edge of the counter and got to work. The motions came easy to her now. All the practice at the church, helping out here and there, and even dismantling meant she had little trouble cleanly slicing, half-turning, and stacking on repeat. Her eyes didn’t need to stay on the knife, technically, but the habit was hard to break.

Which was good, because the moment she reached for her third root, it happened.

“So!” Rina declared at the poor Mina, loud enough to shake the rafters. “Now that your spark’s flickering, how about carving some spirit charms today, little missy?”

Mina froze mid-bite.

Elynara looked up just in time to catch the full-body pause—the fork stopped halfway, eyes wide, shoulders locked.

A slow blink followed. Then another. Like the brain was rebooting.

Across the table, Rina beamed and plopped a half-shaped block of wood directly in front of her.

“You’ve got fingers, don’t you? Use ‘em.”

Mina turned.

Her expression clearly said: help.

Elynara bit the inside of her cheek to keep the grin from getting smug.

“Rina is rather blunt, but I think the practice could do you some good.” she offered, stepping around the table.

Still silence.

She leaned in and gave Mina a light, brief hug around the shoulders.

“You’ve got the sense for the mana sense now. That’s the hardest part. This is just practice.”

Mina glanced down at the wood. Her fingers curled slightly.

“And I’ll be heading out to check more shrines,” Elynara added, voice low enough that only she could hear it. “But I’ll be back by lunch. Don’t worry, you won’t be alone.”

“You promise?”

“Of course.”

That seemed to help, and the tension around her eyes eased. Even her grip on the fork relaxed, though she caught herself before it fell.

Rina, apparently deciding this counted as agreement, launched into a full explanation of carving tools, safe grain patterns, and what not to do if you liked having thumbs. Somewhere in the middle of it, a tray of honeybread was lifted out of the oven with theatrical flourish.

Mina gave Elynara a look.

Elynara shrugged.

“I didn’t plan that, not exactly.”

“You knew.”

“Possibly.”

Starpuff drifted down from its hiding spot just long enough to tap the top of Mina’s head and then vanished again in a smug sparkle.

The carving knife clinked softly against the table. Mina had picked it up.

Elynara watched the girl with growing pride.

“Don’t let Rina overwhelm you,” she said, gathering her things. “If it gets too much, just throw a bit of flour in the air. Confuses the local fauna.”

“I’m the fauna, aren’t I.”

“Welcome to the kitchen.”

They smiled, finished their meal, and soon everybody was getting ready for the day ahead.

Elynara slipped her staff onto her back and gave a short wave, careful not to knock over the drying ladle rack. Mina lifted the knife slightly in response.

Then she stepped out into the morning sun, heart lifted.

Her Plan was still rough around the edges, with plenty of steps missing.

But what it had was growing in the right direction!

She hummed happily while walking ahead, Starpuff in tow.

The wind tugged softly at the edge of her sleeve, but carried no scent.

Aren walked ahead, as usual, his pace even and unbothered. He hadn’t spoken since they left the last fence line, but that wasn’t strange. He seemed to think with his boots.

Probably thought steps spoke louder than small talk.

Elynara followed without complaint, staff balanced across her shoulders, arms hooked loosely over the familiar weight.

She entertained herself watching Starpuff do its thing with all the random things it found fascinating.

Which meant a collection of weird looking plants, but also a fence post it took issue with and bonked into for some reason.

Truly a magnificent and mysterious creature.

They reached the next set of farms by midmorning, Aren once again introducing her to all the places she would need to look over today, before leaving to do his own thing.

It was all routine now, and any hesitation she might have had on day one completely disappeared by now. The next few hours simply a repeat of going to greet the farmers, talking for a bit, being directed to where the shrines and other ‘weird spots’ might be, and then just getting there.

‘Just getting there’ took the most time. The farms were ridiculously huge, and her legs oh-so-short.

Not that she would admit that last bit to anyone.

And so her day went, with the first shrine coming in empty.

She stepped back and marked it on her map.

It was not the first, nor would it be the last. Just another dot with a hollow circle and nothing to show.

Still, Elynara knew others were out there, just waiting to be discovered!

And so she would continue, and keep on going.

The second shrine had a small and faint sprite around. Barely a flicker, but happy to gobble her mana up.

She liked to think it perked up a little after that.

The third held no spirits again, and the fourth had collapsed in on itself, half-swallowed by bramble.

The seventh spot was missing the shrine entirely.

By the time she reached the eighth, her pack had grown lighter. Not because she carried less, but because the hope tucked beside her supplies had worn a little thin.

The shrine was still there.

Cracked at the top. A groove etched through the middle like someone once tried to carve it deeper and gave up. Moss spread up one side in quiet defiance of time.

But once again what greeted her was only rock.

And silence.

She stared at it long enough for her knees to ache.

These places had been built by people who knew what they were doing. Who came with purpose. With offerings. With faith. Maybe not the sort wrapped in hymns or rites, but sincere regardless. It had become tradition, like love which passed hand to hand across seasons.

And still, even that hadn’t been enough. The spirits were gone, dead or…

“Spirited away,” she muttered dryly.

The words left her mouth and hung there like dust in still air.

Then she snorted, the corner of her mouth twitching in reluctant self-awareness.

“Really. That’s the pun you go with.”

A sharp chirp pierced the quiet, and Starpuff divebombed under her nose with precise glee.

The sneeze hit her before she could prepare. Loud, abrupt, and utterly humiliating.

“Stars above,” she hissed, scrubbing at her face with her sleeve while the little glow-fiend spiraled up like it had just solved world hunger through pestering.

“You little traitor.”

She didn’t chase after it, she had enough dignity left not to. Simply straightened slowly, dusted off her knees, and drew a breath through her teeth.

The silence returned quickly.

But this time, it didn’t press quite so hard.

She marked the shrine.

Then turned and walked. Step by step, past another missing shrine, another spirit gone. Her staff clicked lightly against her shoulder with each movement. The road stretched ahead, clean and dry.

There were still more spirits to find.

And she wasn’t done looking.

The path curved gently around the edge of a dry field, a quiet slope of scattered straw and patchy green.

Elynara walked it with the sun warm at her back and her staff balanced lightly across her shoulders. Her legs had settled into a steady, comfortable rhythm a while ago, with just the whisper of grass brushing against her boots.

No one else was in sight.

Aren had peeled off a good while back, though he hadn’t offered much in terms of company really, and she hadn’t needed it, in truth.

Solitude was good for thinking. And she had a lot to think about.

Starpuff zipped ahead, doing its absolute best to carry a leaf uphill using what appeared to be a mix of chaotic enthusiasm and pure delusion. It buzzed around the poor thing, flickering around it fast, then drifted beneath it with an exaggerated push.

The leaf barely budged.

Starpuff tried again, undeterred. It’s energy endless. As if the process itself was the point.

Elynara watched the display with a soft smile, then adjusted the strap on her pack and kept going.

The last shrine had been another empty one. It was a husk of stone, long since overtaken by time. She hadn’t lingered. Not because it hurt less now, but because her thoughts were already elsewhere.

Magic.

And the way it had shifted.

Last night, when she’d sat on the floor with her mana pressed into shape, the spark had come instantly. Like the flame was a door she could now open on command. The practice had paid off. But something about the ease of it nagged at her.

It didn’t feel like power. It felt like understanding.

Her thoughts turned back towards the conclusions from the day before. They had to do with the way she perceived magic. Mana. And perhaps everything around her.

She didn’t really need to ‘rub mana together’ to ignite anything now. It would just happen, if she willed it so. And puff, fire. Or boom, explosion!

So not only was it clearly not a factor, she had another piece of evidence - in the form of the invisible creatures she was now mapping out - come to mind.

They changed the mana somehow. Shaped it perhaps? And made it palatable to plants. Helped them grow. She doubted there was a ‘plant’ element. And doubted her floaty friends could even conceptualize one as such.

They just wanted to help the plants grow… and they did.

Sure, they did it to snack on them later, probably, but still!

It made what she needed to do quite obvious indeed.

If she knew what she wanted, and wanted it hard enough… it should work. The mana might just respond.

It would definitely not be that simple, otherwise she would have already done it. And it might require lots of practice. But it was a direction she simply had to follow now.

One she was happy to pursue.

And perhaps… perhaps she would be able to help the plants grow herself at some point?

She slowed her pace and shifted her staff down into her hands, fingers curling around the grip. Her pulse remained steady, but her thoughts raced.

Was that how it really worked? Not the structure of what was cast, but the clarity behind it? The truth of the request?

Her shoulders relaxed, and she took a deep breath. There was no point in overthinking it now.

It was time to find out.

But first she had to find a spot to practice for that. She was already done with her survey for the day, after all.

The grove behind the third farm wasn’t much, it was just a shallow dip in the trees, with a wide stretch of scattered stones, and a patch of moss that looked like it had claimed the place out of sheer stubbornness.

But it was quiet, and seemed like as good a spot to practice as any. Cool shade spilled from the trees in angled lines, and the stones held onto the sun’s warmth.

Elynara sat cross-legged near the edge, staff balanced across her lap, and set her fingers to rest just above the ground.

Starpuff made one lazy spin, looped a branch for no reason at all, then plopped onto a rock nearby and stared at her with what she generously interpreted as encouragement.

It was time to test her idea.

There was no point trying something complex yet, seeing if her theory works at all took greater priority.

And if it did… she would have much to practice.

A shudder ran through her, the giddiness at the very thought of possibilities spreading shivers down her spine.

But no, she had to focus!

And just try things out. Not from plant growth magic though, that seemed a little too advanced. Or more like something she barely even knew where to begin with.

She thought back to her first night in this world. Of how she thought of the fire, and of the moons…

There should be a book on them in the library, she would have to ask…

And chose to focus on the second best thing. She didn’t know much about earth, didn’t know how to imagine the wind, or lightning.

But water? That she could do. The flowing and ebbing currents. How it felt on her hands. How it seemed to cleanse all things and feel so… wet.

She shaped a blob of mana to test. Focused on it. There was no rubbing needed here, she knew. And perhaps no action with mana needed either.

Her mind pulled back to water.

Water that moved. Water that lingered in silence and carved through stone with time. The weight of it. The patience of it.

She thought of rain clinging to rooftops. Of dew caught on leaves in the early morning. Of cupped hands dipped into streambeds, cold enough to sting but clear enough to reflect.

That. That was what she wanted.

She pushed it into the mana.

Every single thing she could think of about water. And tried.

Again. And again.

Yet… Nothing.

There was a piece missing somewhere, she knew.

Her understanding of the flame, made her lose the need for rubbing the mana to start. But she only understood it so well after dozens and hundreds of casts. Was that something she needed after all?

To find that trigger first?

But… No.

The point wasn’t the trigger. It was direction. Or the lack of one. What she wanted, that day, was to ignite a flame, to get warmth, flame, explosion.

What did she need… from water?

A ball, she surmised. A ball of water to wash away the thirst, held in the air before her.

The first try - failed. And so did the second, her mana dispersing uselessly. It wasn’t until the third when something had changed.

The mana flowed, seemingly ready to do her bidding.

And then failed too. But that was enough. She knew what to do now.

It took her a dozen more tries before she got that part down. Her mana was flowing into something now. And yet the result was… a little mist? Starpuff happily messed that mist around, but she wasn’t satisfied.

She tried again, coaxing the pulse forward. Trying to make it hold.

The next shape twisted, then faded.

She didn’t stop.

Again, and again.

On yet another attempt, the spell shimmered lightly, just a thin curl of light running along the edge. Like the air around it had thickened.

That was new.

She adjusted. Focused less on the sensation of touch and more on the idea of motion. Water didn’t fight its container. It became it. It flowed where space allowed.

The mana shifted. Curled. Cooled.

A soft mist formed around her fingertips.

Starpuff immediately launched into it, flailing joyfully as if she’d conjured a spirit playground. She batted it gently away before it inhaled half her progress.

At least the mist was thicker this time.

She decided to push harder. Poured more mana in. Double. Then triple. The mist grew, but it was still not enough.

There was no point in holding back now, and Elynara poured as much mana as she could into the working.

Nearly bottoming out her growing reserves. The spell now rivaling one of the explosions she used against the horned bear. The bigger of the two at that.

Her heart beat fast, Elynara let it form and flow.

And there. It used a lot of mana. But as she quickly cupped her hands under it - there was enough water in the air now to fill a small cup, flowing down into her waiting hands.

She testily drank a little bit.

It really was water. Fresh, cool, wet.

A grin spread on her face, tension leaving her body.

There was no system message yet, but she knew she was on the right track now.

Starpuff chirped wildly and did a spin so enthusiastic it bonked into a branch and bounced off with a tiny thud.

Elynara didn’t laugh, but her joy was unmistakable. She just sat there, palms damp, sleeves clinging slightly from moisture, and let the moment of wonder flow through her.

Her hands really were wet.

The mana had listened.

Mina would have surely made fun of the manic grin she must have sported now.

But the only one to remember it would be Starpuff now.

And the little sprite would not tell the world if it tried.

She rose up, at last, after a good minute of just sitting there, enjoying the moment.

There was a promise she had to keep however, and a long path back to walk still.

It would burn through all the giddiness she felt for sure.

Yet her great mood would remain.

The front path glowed with soft light by the time Elynara stepped past the last turn in the fence. Golden beams spilled over the grass in long stretches, catching on stray dust and the edges of old brick. She slowed for a heartbeat, taking in the warmth of it, and then picked up her pace, boots brushing quietly over the stone as she rounded toward the side door.

The house smelled like flour and wood shavings. A good sign.

She peeked into the workroom.

Mina was mid-laugh, one hand holding a half-eaten cookie, the other gesturing with a carving knife while Rina waved dramatically beside her, mid-story. Something about a goat, judging by the pantomime. Possibly also a bucket.

Pufflet had melted under the worktable in a heap of fuzz and crumbs, snoring softly.

Elynara leaned into the doorway.

“I leave for a few hours and suddenly you’ve joined a carving cult.”

Rina turned with a smirk.

“We accept all converts. Entry fee’s a little whittling and a decent sense of humor.”

Mina beamed, eyes lighting up when she saw her.

“You’re back already!”

“Fast walking,” Elynara said, stepping in. “And possibly bribery.”

Before she could sit, Rina tossed a chunk of softwood her way.

“No escape now. You’re in it.”

Elynara caught it and sank into the open stool beside Mina. The younger girl shuffled over instinctively, nudging her leg against hers without looking.

The table was scattered with half-finished charms. Some rough, some better than expected. A few had faint mana threads still clinging to their edges, drifting like lazy steam in the afternoon air. Elynara reached for one and turned it gently in her fingers. The work was uneven, but the core was solid.

“You felt it?”

Mina gave a proud little nod.

“Once. I think. Rina says it counts.”

“It does.”

“Even if the charm kind of looks like a turtle.”

Elynara tilted her head.

“A very spiritual turtle.”

“Exactly!”

They grinned at each other while Rina muttered about ungrateful apprentices and went to go check the oven.

The work passed quietly. She carved while Mina narrated her whole afternoon in excited bursts. About how she had figured out which piece of wood had more mana, how the second charm had cracked because she got too ambitious, how Rina had let her test a few old finished ones for reference.

Elynara didn’t need to ask if she had been happy. It was written in every gesture.

And when Theren came in from fieldwork and shouted for them to help with stew prep, they rolled up their sleeves without hesitation.

Dinner was loud, full of too much seasoning and laughter. Finnie tried to sneak food to Pufflet. Aren pretended not to notice. Starpuff tried to steal a potato wedge and immediately regretted it.

By the time the dishes were cleared and the last cup drained, night had taken the windows.

Elynara found Rina leaning on the back porch rail, quiet for once.

“Thank you,” she said, voice low.

Rina gave her a look, then tugged her into a hug and slapped a hand over her hair.

“Told you not to be sappy.”

Mina wandered over next and was immediately absorbed into the embrace. She squeaked, but didn’t pull away.

The house dimmed. Candles flickered out one by one.

Back in the guest room, the blankets were too warm and the air too full of sleep. Pufflet curled between their knees like an oversized sock. Starpuff took watch above them, pulsing gently with all the subtlety of a blinking rainbow.

Elynara had just started to drift when a hand tapped her sleeve.

“…Thank you. For bringing me here.”

The words were quiet and soft.

Elynara reached over and pulled Mina into a hug, one arm around her shoulders, no words needed.

Mina stayed there.

And in the hush that followed, wrapped in warmth and the steady breath of someone who trusted her, sleep seemed to come to Elynara easily.

Starpuff joined them mere moments later.
