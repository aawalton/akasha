The very instant I got within speaking range of the Elven guard, they started in on their typical spiel. It was, as expected, the exact same thing they had told me every other time I had tried to enter their ‘borders’.

“No enter,” The Elf in an owl mask said stolidly, his shoulders tense. His companion in a wolf mask didn’t say a word, though I noted that the grip he had on his spear had tightened.

There was, after all, a reason for that. Every other time I’d been by trying to see Alveron, I had been alone. I had been polite, but I had been by myself.

I wasn’t anymore. This time, I was accompanied by an entire squad of higher-level, experienced Classers. Sylvia, Maria, Renauld, Bella, Walter, and Fade were among them, as well as half a dozen of my more hardened expedition members. The kind of people who either wore platemail at all hours of the day, or cloaked themselves in blackened leather armor with full face concealing hoods.

Some of my most trusted and most capable.

Not only that, but I had people accompanying me who could conceivably have a measure of authority over them. The Elves were, after all, essentially squatting on borrowed land.

To my side, Bleddyn crossed his arms over his bare chest, eyeing the guards with a distinctly displeased air. To his back, the massive bear-like man I had met briefly after my initial coma, who I had since learned was named Theodorus, eyed the Elves with a distinctly evil gaze. The four other members of the Unchained behind the monolith of a man all had similar expressions on their faces too.

Hmm. You know, it had just occurred to me. I sincerely doubt that Walter and I were the only people who had been captured as slaves by Elven raiders, and nearly every true member of the Unchained was a former slave. It was entirely possible that either the Unchained were very used to slapping down Elves, to the point they didn’t distinguish between Elves under Fynneas’s control and those who weren’t…

Or they all might have been captured by Elves in the past themselves.

I smiled slightly, the attention of the owl mask snapping back to me instantly. “I am not asking,” I said calmly. “This is not a request. This is a prediction of the future. My companions and I are going to walk through those gates, and there is nothing you or your people can do to stop us. I have grown quite sick of the Elves' refusal to communicate with anyone. If I have to go through the both of you to find someone with authority to speak to, I will. I won’t be happy about it, but I’ll do it.”

While the owl mask tensed at the open threat, the wolf mask lowered his spear, switching to a ready position. The reaction he got from me was a bored glance, and a snort from Bleddyn.

This guy…he wasn’t a threat to anyone here.

I continued. “I’m going to give you a chance to reconsider matters. I understand you’re only following orders, but that doesn’t matter to me. Not anymore. You have until I finish counting to ten, and then, if you stand in our way, we’re going through you.”

Owl mask took a step back, while wolf mask lowered his stance. I could read the tension in every line of their bodies, but it didn’t matter.

I wasn’t bluffing. I really would go through them if they didn’t step aside. I alone would be enough to deal with these two, but I wouldn’t kill them. Just…smack them around a bit.

“Ten.”

Over the guard’s head, I could see that the commotion was drawing attention from within the Elven encampment.

“Nine.”

People were stopping in the middle of their errands, between the natural lanes that had sprouted up between their mobile homes.

“Eight.”

Even though every one of the Elves wore a mask, I could see that they were a bit frightened, just from their posture. Oddly…

“Seven.”

I’m not actually sure if they were scared of us.

“Six.”

Something about the atmosphere within the Elven section of the settlement seemed tense.

“Five.”

If I were being honest…

“Four.”

It almost reminded me of how the afflicted Dwarves had looked, in their little ghetto that had gathered around Olgar.

“Three…”

But still…

“Two…”

If it was needed…

“One…”

I was perfectly fine being the boot that would kick them in their ass if they were too scared to move.

It was time for the Elves to contribute to the defense of Vereden.

They were part of this world.

In the ensuing silence after my countdown, Bleddyn slowly unfolded his arms, and our contingent shifted in place.

I did the same, and slowly, I cracked my neck back and forth, before taking a single step forward…

“Wait!” A frantic, female, familiar voice cried out, cutting through the tension. Someone sprinted through the gathering crowd within the Elven encampment, shoving their way through until they skidded to a stop between the guards and me.

I have a say, I was actually a bit relieved. I had been perfectly willing to following through on my threat, but it was looking like I might not have to. It would have made things awkward in the short term if I had needed to slap around these two.

Because the person who had stepped in to stop the impending ass-kicking was none other than Kierla.

I hadn’t personally spoken to the young Elf we had rescued out in the middle of Velancia in weeks now. The last I had seen of her, she had been cozying up to Walter instead. The two of them seemed to have gotten much closer in the weeks and months since I had assigned my young squire to look after her, back before we had linked up with the Elven column under Alveron’s leadership. She had cleaned up in the time since, no longer looking like a cross between a dirty, penniless orphan and a wild spirit of the forest. Clean clothes, mended weapons, and a safe place to let down her guard that wasn’t stranded in the middle of nowhere had been good for her.

Strangely, she was still the only Elf I had ever met who didn’t wear a mask. That let me easily see the frightened defiance on her elfin features, as she interposed herself between our groups.

“This is not needed!” Kierla shouted. “Please, wait a moment!”

I felt his presence more than saw him, as Walter stepped up to my left side. “Kierla, what…?” I heard him say in a concerned tone.

Said Elf shot him a strained smile of recognition, before immediately rounding on the two guards who had stood in our way. What followed was a rapid string of incomprehensible Elvish and wild hand gestures exchanged between both parties. I couldn’t help but raise an eyebrow at just how heated the conversation seemed to be getting. The wolf mask even stepped forward aggressively in an obvious attempt to intimidate the young girl, only for her to bodily shove him away.

I leaned in closer to Walter and whispered. “You wouldn’t have happened to have picked up any Elvish with all the time you’ve been spending with your little girlfriend, have you?”

“A little bit, but they’re speaking too fast-” He paused, before turning bright red, shooting me a wide-eyed look. “My what?”

I just smirked back at him. My attention was stolen, though, when both the guards backed off and seemingly returned to their posts.

Notably, on either side of the entrance into the Elven quarter. It was always hard to tell emotions with Elve’s, considering their ever-present masks, but based on their body language, the wolf seemed pissed off, while the owl just seemed resigned.

Kierla turned back to face me with a strained look of her own. “I have spoken to them. You may come in. I will take you to the Chief.”

Interesting, for a few reasons. The first, most obvious, was that her language skills seemed to have improved. She used to speak like someone very obviously struggling with Herztalian Common parlance, sounding more like a toddler than anything else, really. Maybe she had improved with all that time spent around Walter.

The other was that she was going to take me to the Chief.

Not the Elder, as she had referred to Alveron in the past.

Still, this suited me just fine. I nodded easily at her and gestured forward. “Lead on, then. I’m more than happy to settle this matter peacefully.”

Kierla nodded sharply at me, and we fell into step as I finally managed to set foot among the Elves again after weeks of trying.

Almost immediately, I realized I had been right about something.

The atmosphere among the Elves was different from what it used to be. In the past, after we had initially met up with Alveron’s faction of Elve’s free from Fynneas’s influence, they had been…a bit standoffish, to admit. But not unwelcoming. They had appreciated being under the umbrella of a larger martial force when their actual number of combatants was low. In fact, they’d had the lowest total number of warriors to contribute to the defense of the original expedition.

Most, after all, were civilians. Refugees, really, if not voluntary ones.

Alveron had emptied all of Haven when he brought his people north. Something I…somewhat disapproved of, to be frank.

I had never told him to his face, but I flat out thought it had been wildly irresponsible to bring so many non-combatants and children into the hostile lands of Velancia.

Especially now, when it seemed almost like all of them were somehow in mourning. The atmosphere in the camp was, as I had suspected, very subdued. The firepits were packed with people sitting shoulder to shoulder, and those shoulders were noticeably lowered. Heads were bowed, and conversation, if it did exist, was quiet and personal. The children of the Elves were strangely apprehensive, as well, in that uncomprehending way that children acted when they were too young to understand why something bad had happened…

Just that something had.

Altogether, it painted a picture that threatened to put a scowl on my face.

Please, for the love of God, don’t tell me that Alveron was actually dead. I feel like that was something I should have been immediately notified about. I…know it sounded callous of me, to refer to the possible death of a genuine piece of Veredenese history.

But Goddamnit, we were in a struggle for our very lives right now. I needed to know if one of our most theoretically powerful combatants was lost to us.

However…I’m not sure that was the case. Rafael had clearly sensed something about Alveron, which had driven him away from accompanying us. The Lich seemed to believe that our erstwhile Paragon was still among the living, and I was inclined to believe him.

What else could be driving this atmosphere, then? It’s not like the Elves had suffered a great many casualties during the battle of Rhoscara. The expedition and the Unchained had practically bent over backwards, to protect them from the marauding forces of Anguis and the Savoy. They had been shuffled into the Long Road tunnels beneath the city with startling haste and efficiency.

I guess the only way to find out was to ask this apparent Chief of theirs.

Good thing it looked like Kierla had finished leading us to them.

The young Elf stopped in front of one of the only actual structures here in the Elven quarter. It was a decently sized animal hide tent with a flap over the entrance. It was large enough that I think you could have fit two of the oddly spherical wagons of the Elves inside. As I examined it, Kierla turned to me with a pleading look, directing her gaze towards my procession.

I got the gist. I turned to face my people. “Maria, with me. Sylvia, Walter, keep an eye on things out here. Bleddyn?”

My friend nodded and cocked a look at his own squad. “Theo, you’re comin’ with. The rest of you, stay here.”

Our chosen seconds nodded, and as joint leaders, Bleddyn and I brushed aside the entrance into the tent with them at our backs. Inside was…

About what I had expected. A whole gaggle of Elves of all types, with various kinds of masks, were standing around a central table. It was…oddly detailed, actually, making me think it had been a home furnishing from Haven that had been broken down and transported north, only to be reassembled as a command structure. That was interesting, sure.

But my eyes were on a few different things.

The first was who could only be the Elf Kierla had referred to as the ‘Chief’. He was, to put it bluntly, the single largest Elf I had ever seen in my life. Not fat, oh no.

This guy was even more heavily muscled than Bleddyn and had nearly foot on the werewolf at that. A true bruiser, he wasn’t dressed much differently than any other Elf I’d ever met. They were a cloak-loving people, I’d found, all the better to move through the trees quietly. The little I could see under the canvas of his earth brown cloak looked to be darkened leather armor, also relatively standard for his people.

But that was where the similarities ended.

The first was his choice of weapon, because this guy was very obviously a warrior. Most Elves I’d seen favored the spear, in a manner that I honestly tended to do as well. If not that, then the bow at the very least. Not so for the Chief.

Instead, he had what seemed to be an absolutely enormous greatsword leaning up against the edge of the table within arm's reach. The handle alone seemed to be as long as my forearms, and the blade itself…honestly, I think it might have been taller than I was. Single-edged with a thick flat, blunt surface on the other, I was sure that thing could slice through armor like a hot knife through butter.

The other difference…was his mask.

He wasn’t wearing one, just like Kierla wasn’t.

His bald head was on full display, easily showing off his long, knife-like ears. Like other elves, he had the same emerald green eyes of a shade only slightly different than my own, set beneath a heavy brow. His features were harsh and square, almost literally so, with a broad, boxy jaw. His default state appeared to be almost glowering, leading him to have a nearly sullen look.

Which was directed right at me, of course. His eyes only momentarily rested on Kierla, who stuck her jaw out almost defiantly at his regard, before coming to consider both Bleddyn and I.

I had paused in the doorframe, partly to size him up as he was me, and partly to be polite.

Speaking of…

I smiled slightly, unintimidated by the large Elf on the other side of the table. “Excuse us. I am Marshal Nathaniel Hart, and this is Bleddyn of the Unchained. I believe it was high time we spoke, and I’m afraid matters can no longer wait.”

The Elf grunted, standing up from his leaning position over the table. “Perhaps.”

So he spoke Common, at least. It wasn’t exactly a ‘common’ skill among the Elves, and yet I could barely detect an accent in his gruff voice.

Silence, for a moment, before Bleddyn broke it. “The old man not here?” He grunted.

A slight scowl crossed the Elf’s face, then. But I don’t think it was directed at anyone in particular. More on the situation in general. “So it is true. You do not know.”

Without another word, he stepped away from the table and veritably stalked over to a particular corner of the tent. To my dread, it was covered in a broad sheet, completely blocking it from view. With one meaty fist, the Elf drew it back.

Revealing the supine form of Alveron lying on a cot. He…didn’t look like he was sleeping, to me.

I don’t think he was even breathing.
