My plans had to change. I wanted Nate's method to control some of the monsters, but now that was off the table unless I found Maggie. That wasn't really an option either because I sure as hell wasn't going to run into the cornfields where she could easily set a trap for me.

Damn. Damn it. I should've been more patient. Maybe knocked the older woman out and tied up Nate myself.

Nothing I could do about it now besides improvise and keep moving forward. Always forward.

I stepped over the two corpses, back into the tent and beelined towards the room with the flagpole. I got to work lowering the red triangle, but once it was lowered, I found myself at a loss. There were two other flags, one black and one green. I knew red wasn't something that would get the scientist's attention, so I just decided to hook both the green and black in and hope their messages weren't diametrically opposed.

Maybe I'd get lucky, and the scientist would come visit all alone, and I could bash their brains in. Fat chance. Maggie was doubtless heading in that direction now and would intercept the man before he made it anywhere close, and even so, I didn't doubt he carried a group of the plant monsters with him.

I needed a path forward. While I'd love to recycle the trapdoor spider plan and slowly kill all the trapped Visitors, I wouldn't just be going after mindless creatures anymore, but also the man directing them. He'd adapt to me, and I'd have to adapt to him, and with my bleeding foot, he had the overwhelming advantage in that regard. While I could technically leave the Instance anytime I wanted by dying, that wasn't my goal. A good last resort, but only that.

Such a pain.

Maggie and the scientist would undoubtedly be looking for me. By this point I've become too much of a threat to ignore. They'd either kill me, taking the meager reward of me being gone from the Instance, and wipe their hands clean or go all in on capturing me. They'd go for the latter. This whole plan of theirs – trapping Visitors in their Instance through a technicality – was incredibly greedy, and greedy people didn't change their nature so easily. I should know. I am a very greedy creature by nature as well.

So it was a game of cat and mouse. They needed to find me, and I needed to turn on the power. Really, nothing about the Instance's initial win conditions had changed.

I walked over to the map the two Traitor Visitors had meticulously assembled. All the major landmarks were pointed out. The barn, the Greenhouse, a house, the central road, and a few scattered sheds. The barn or the house would be the two locations the generator could be and was also in the direction Maggie seemed to run in. The phone was in the barn, and if I were to design an Instance, I wouldn't put the generator needed to win right next to the phone. My bet was on the house.

The problem became obvious, if I just ran after Maggie the chances I ran into an ambush was too high. Luckily, the map solved that problem as well. When I first entered the Instance, I wondered if the cornfield went on forever or if it looped in on itself, and now, with a bird's eye view, it was apparent it was a contained loop. Instead of going south, the most direct and shortest path to the house. I could go north. Maybe I could make it and find whatever generator before they realized what was going on. I crumpled up the map, shoving it into one of the bags along with the canteens of Nate's blood, and left the Greenhouse.

I ran recklessly, limping through the field with abandon, wincing with every step as the punctured muscles in my foot screamed at me. I didn't have time to hesitate here. Hesitation meant I'd lose, and I'd be damned if I let the Instance Holder of this place have their way.

The fact that the System allowed this to happen at all pissed me off. I mean, seriously, so many rules and restrictions on me, but the Instance was free to hold people here indefinitely because, technically, it didn't kill them.

After a while, the wind ebbed and revealed that I wasn't the only rustling in the field. I stopped and greedily sucked down air. My bloodied foot must have attracted it. I strained my ears to try to gauge a direction and– East. It was coming from the east.

My half-sprint half-hobble resumed and I hissed pain through grit teeth. The worst part wasn't even the pain. It was the feeling the muscles gave, the feeling of wrongness, the voice telling me I needed to stop running or I'd hurt myself. The way my lungs felt heavy with phlegm, and something in my nose and tonsils smelled of iron as pain and numbness danced across my leg in equal measures. At least when I was moving the feeling was constant, predictable, controllable. Going from a stop to a start … I could feel the betrayal my body felt.

I changed course slightly, I had seen on the map there was a shed a little northwest of here. It'd extend my route by a few minutes, but I'd have the cover needed to fight. I'd make it. I was sure of it. But the noises still gained on me, it caressed my spine with promises of an agonizing death like Nate.

I arrived at the shed, slamming against its door with my shoulder, only to be met with a dangerous creak of warped wood as I realized it was a pull, not a push. I quickly corrected my mistake, slamming the door behind me, and was met with a tool shed much like the one in the cemetery. Something smelt putrid. At the back of the shed was a tarp, but it sagged and bowed, allowing me to catch a glimpse of a compost trough. From the brown-red rotting sludge, a leafy, malformed head with closed eyes grew. The top of the growth was heavy enough that the stem folded under the force, causing the head to lull back into the dirt it sprouted.

I didn't know what it was. I didn't care. It was probably a threat.

I took steps forward to the tarp and carefully drew it up to cover me. A few more steps forward and I was over the trough. I leaned down, hands cupped as I searched for the head through the barrier. It was easy to find, and with one hand on the stem and the other gripping the head, I pulled, and it came away easily, like ripping the top of a sunflower. It woke up right before the end, sending a weak vibration through my hand before it went quiet.

A second later, an impact slammed against the door. Out of time.

I ran back over to the creaking door. It wouldn't hold for long. Lifting the tarp high into the air, I pressed it against the rim of the doorway.

Another impact.

Crack.

The center of the door shattered inwards.

I swung Peeler at the tarp with all the force I could muster. A cheer almost escaped my lips as the strange spatial static ability activated, pinning the tarp against the wood. Sight wasn't an issue anymore, but I needed a weapon. My eyes flung themselves from side to side. Sheers? No. Would rip the tarp. I needed blunt and weighty. The glint of a shovel in the bright moonlight caught my eye. It would do. I tore the impromptu club from a rack of tools, sending the rest clattering to the ground.

Reaching past the tarp, I turned the handle and, with all my strength, kicked the door open. I felt my body slow to a crawl as my leg stuck out of the now-open threshold. It felt like moving through molasses or running in a dream as I threw my body back to recede fully into the shed.

I stumbled and fell, my back impacting with the hardened points of a fallen rake. I hissed in pain, scrambling to my hands and then feet as I whipped around, swinging the shovel more like a sledgehammer than a bat straight at the tarp.

The timing worked out. The flat impacted the tarp and the invisible monster behind it with a dull TING, and I could feel the creature reel back. I heard the soft impact of the plant creature on the dirt. I saw my chance. Using the wall to gain extra height I grabbed onto Peeler and tore it from its frozen position. I collapsed to the ground in a heap, my body tangled in the now loose tarp. I just barely managed to stand to my feet when I was thrown back onto the ground.

I couldn't tell if the creature recovered fast or if there was another one, and between the air getting knocked out of my lungs and the thrashing of myself and the plant, I didn't really consider either option. Instead, I just struggled. I pushed against the creature, trying to lift it off of me. These monsters were light, and if they had been sitting still, it would've worked, but unfortunately, neither of us wanted to die. Spikes pierced the thin plastic. One found my shoulder. It steadily grew as it tasted my blood. I could feel it wriggling against through veins as it pushed down my arm.

A guttural noise, half choke, half snarl, escaped my lips. My other hand reached across and grabbed at the spike. I was pinned and couldn't pull it out, especially not with it traveling so far. So I leaned my head over and bit. Hard.

It was like chewing a rose's stem, tearing at my gums and cheeks. Getting in between my teeth and breaking my soft enamel. I flinched back, loosening my bite. The creature began moving back. I couldn't allow that. I bit down again, the taste of iron and the tightness of my chest the only thing I could focus on.

I tore at the spike, and it split. Stringy tendril-like veins writhed in the shallow connections between broken halves, trying to reconnect. They tried to latch into my mouth. I bit the rest of the way through and spat to the side. It was more of a sputter as I felt disembodied tendrils vying for space against my teeth.

The creature ended up pulling me on top of it as it vibrated harshly in what I assumed was pain. The tarp stayed between the two of us. I could feel the monster writhing underneath, but the creatures were, in truth, physically weak. They couldn't beat me without space to draw their limbs back for their whip-like attacks. I braced my knee on the largest area of mass I could and for a moment I forgot about killing it.

My hands went to my mouth, I could feel the things wriggling, refusing to die. I grabbed onto one end of the tendril, and pulled. I thought of one of those videos of a spoon worm being pulled out of sand on a beach. Except for the sand was my gums and the worm was trying to burrow into me. It was hard to focus when the monster underneath me was bucking wildly. I continued to pull and the thing just kept being extracted, more length over more time as I felt the void like channel it left behind in the roof of my mouth. Finally, it gave way with a tearing sensation somewhere in my mouth and I threw it to the side.

I let out a yell, my adrenaline spiking as my body shivered at the sensations. It was hard to think, hard to stop the tears from welling in my eyes, so I just didn't. I tried my best to stop thinking about it. I reached for my crowbar raised it up high, and brought it down. It slid off the thing's head. Once, twice more to the same result. I I didn't have time for this.

Dropping one hand from the crowbar, I began slamming my palm to where I thought the neck was, waving it back and forth until I caught its throat.

"Not so slippery now huh? Was it worth it? Was it!"

Finally, holding its head in place, I got five good swings in before it went limp. I gave it another five and a stomp to be safe.

Panting heavily, I took a small break to drink some water before quickly folding up the tarp. I wanted to rest. I wanted to lie down or bite my own tongue and drown. I wanted to stop, but if I stopped now I wouldn't get back up, so even though I didn't want to fold the damn tarp or even bring it with me because it was a fucking waste of time I still did because I needed to think and if I stopped moving to think I'd die.

If I couldn't do this much, if I couldn't win against the first Instance how the hell was I going to win against any of the others.

I heard more rustling, this time coming from the north. I closed my eyes and listened. There was no panic to rise from my chest, all of it was already at the surface, so instead it just roiled. I couldn't deal with it now. Stop thinking. Just breathe. Come up with a plan. They're attracted to blood. I have canteens full of it. That was my plan anyways to use them as a lure.

I unscrewed one of the canteens full of Nate's blood and painfully slowly. So slow, but so careful. I poured it over the invisible corpse of the monster. I stood as far back as possible, not wanting to get any on me.

Rescrewing the cap onto the canteen would be pointless, so I simply dropped it before exiting the shed and sprinting south. I'd have to do a longer loop around, but it could work. I just hoped Nate's blood would overwhelm the noise I was making.

I continued running, my face bit into by the sharp stalks, my feet tired, and my lungs gasping for air, but in the distance, I could see the silhouette of a roof, and even further off, I could barely make out the top of the barn.

Good.

I dropped from a sprint into a jog into a crawling walk. I kept close to the ground, edging closer and closer to the house. I closed my eyes, focusing entirely on my hearing. It's not like I could see the monsters coming after all.

So I knelt at the edge of the clearing in the middle of a cornfield leading up to an isolated house, wondering what nasty surprise would be in store for me next.

The thought of using more blood or coating an area around here to catch any lingering threats crossed my mind, but I ultimately decided not to. If there were truly no monsters in the area, drawing them to me would just fuck up my plans.

I stood up to step out of the clearing and froze. Almost literally. I could barely move my body. I was pushing through quicksand, but my muscles still listened to my previous commands, making me taller and taller. Slower and slower as I stood.

My eyes darted to and fro, getting a better lay of the house, and I scowled. The windows. There were monsters in the windows looking out. That was the only reason I wouldn't be getting swarmed right now. Fuck. I was in their line of sight. In a few more seconds, I'd be completely frozen in place, unable to move and having literally delivered myself to the mad scientist's doorstep.

I felt weak. Helpless. There was nothing I could do as I watched my body respond asynchronously with the commands of my mind. I thought about blinking and my eyes didn't close until I no longer wanted them to. I wanted to yell out of frustration.

I wracked my brain for a solution. Screamed at my muscles to stop. I may not die. I might just get rescued when my timer hit zero. At the very least, my Stalker effect would activate, and they'd probably find and kill me, but that would be a loss. I didn't want to lose to these cheaters.

Finally, my throat produced the yell I wanted from before, which only exasperated my panic and anger. There had to be a way out. There was always a way out.

Inspiration hit. My body would slow until it froze, but it wouldn't freeze me in space just stop me from moving. I just needed to ensure I fell to the ground. I stopped fighting against my previous movements, instead using already straining muscles to tilt back. My knees bent, my weight rocked back to the balls of my feet, and the numb, icy, fiery pain got its revenge for being woken back up.

It was enough. As I felt my body seize up and freeze completely, I fell backward, and as I hit the dirt, I felt some semblance of control pierce back into me. I was still slow, but not nearly as much as before.

I began slowly pushing the entirety of myself back into the cover of the corn fields, breathing a heavy sigh of relief before scowling at the house.

How many times was I going to make the mistake of not being careful enough. Of course, the scientist would booby-trap his house. Of course, he'd leave guards there at all times. It'd be stupid if he didn't, and as much as I didn't like the guy at this point, I had to admit that as vile as he may be, he wasn't an idiot.

I smiled. I wanted to kill him, but we were still playing our game of cat and mouse. The smile turned bitter as I reached into my bag and pulled out the tarp.

Whatever, I wouldn't be the mouse for long and the ending is all that mattered.
