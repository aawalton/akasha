Chapter 293

The reflection in the shard in the pope’s hand, true to its form as a piece of a mirror, was a slice of world flipped and twisted, slightly foggy where the perspiration from the man’s skin stuck to the glossy surface. It depicted rooms and ceilings, and the occasional glimpse of the four figures as they made their way through the building.

The corridors had been cleared of people, with only a scant few cardinals and bishops keeping watch, all already touched by the light of Origin, making sure that nobody could bother them. They rushed back to the airship, which silently rose into the sky and then immediately vanished. This time, stealth was on.

Even though Michael was keeping his Domain away from the shard, the trace amounts of the corrupting Netherworld energy it emitted were enough to draw eyes to them. Not mundane eyes, but the eyes of devils, who Michael felt were watching from beyond the veil, through strange twists and turns of reality itself. The feeling was faint, not even close to the clarity of when he could wield Truth, but it was there.

A transparent shield rose to surround them, keeping all the PAC energy that the shard was releasing contained. Then, the spiral appeared, sucking in all the energy before it could build up.

The sight was eerie. The pope had chosen to wear his most regal attire, even though it had yet not been altered with the iconography of Origin. Around his neck, the cross that once gave him access to Faith was now a conduit for the System, although temporarily disabled, denying him the use of Faith but gifting him with the powers of all magic, Adherence included. Above him, the spiral glowed in a million colors, encased in the mind-bending sight of the black hole which bent light but nothing else, distorting the hexagons of the shield behind it.

Johanne watched, magic at the ready, as if expecting devils to jump out of the shard at any moment.

Icarus seemed to be trying to crush Michael's hand. The little elf was not casting anything, but Michael could feel the immense weight of magic building up behind the scenes, ready to be unleashed at any moment. The AI was scared, he realized. Not as scared as when they had faced the cosmic monster in the inner space, but scared nonetheless. It was the same kind of fear too, the one that had made them cling to Michael all the time since them, as if seeking to anchor themselves in reality, in the present moment, to confirm that he was here and that he was alright, and that they needn’t worry about him suddenly being attacked or dying.

An alarm blared. Icarus tensed, the aircraft shook, and then it went away. The pope was sweating, holding the shard aloft for everyone to see, as if displaying a holy relic. Except, unlike with a relic, Damasus was looking at it with a face of contempt and disgust.

Icarus tensed even further. Michael dove into the data lines of the inner space, tracing the sudden draw on his resources and found the reason behind it. A map unfolded in his awareness, and for a moment the outside world disappeared as he retreated in a room in the inner space, only him and Icarus. For a moment, he felt the twin touch of the elf’s small hand in his, one in the inner space, one in the outside world.

A tear had opened in Indonesia. Lots of PAC energy, devils already spilling out. Travis had wasted no time and was coordinating all of the resources at Candle Light’s disposal to deal with it. David was on his way, and all the protocols they had hastily drawn up in the last few days would soon be put to the test.

“You can’t leave,” Icarus whispered in his ear, a little lullaby. “I know you want to, but you need to trust your people.”

Michael knew Icarus was right, of course. He chuckled dryly, mind going back to the pillars in the dungeon, the Dao and the meaning hidden within them, that now echoed empty in a room he was not going to visit ever again. “Just like the Dao teaches,” he said.

Except, he knew that Icarus had ulterior motives. Tears appeared on the AI’s face as they read his thoughts, their lips thinning into a tight line. They nodded, looking out through the glass and lines of the data room, watching the spiral glow, as if to say that they knew that Michael was onto them, but they did not care.

“Another fifty percent spike,” Michael said instead. Near the spiral’s glow, he traced the thicker data lines all the way up to the ring and the collectors. They were beginning to look like a Dyson Sphere, and they would be, once he finally managed to build them all. Not the mark-2 versions, but a later upgrade, an eventuality. A sphere that encased the whole inner space, right below the Aura Ring.

The lines were also glowing, but their light was ominous.

Icarus sighed. “Keeping the shields around the shards at the base and at the Vatican, plus the Domains and all the new users is putting a lot of strain on the System.”

“I need to power up,” Michael said. “At the very least, to keep the System up and running and have enough energy to integrate all the priests. How many of them are there?”

“A little over four hundred thousand,” Icarus replied. “And those are only the Catholic ones.”

“As soon as this is over, I’m diving into the dungeon,” Michael replied.

“And I will put on my Voice of Origin mantle,” the elf said. “And discuss iconography with the pope.” They pouted, looking slightly uncomfortable. “What a pain… You will have to make it up to me for all this extra work.”

Michael returned to the material world, where he updated Johanne on the situation. As the American continent came into view, they prepared for the final phase of the transfer, hoping that it would all go well.

The aircraft landed, and they rushed outside, moving swiftly. A black pod in the middle of the field awaited them, together with more than three dozen operators and several scientists. Johanne joined them, while Michael and the pope approached the pod. It opened up, revealing a small space inside filled to the brim with magic coins, datascript, and a hull several feet thick.

“Put the shard inside,” Michael ordered.

The pope did as told. He placed it inside, gingerly, handling it with the care one would handle a nitroglycerin stick. When he let go, he quickly retreated and the shield that had followed them from the aircraft phased through them and shrunk until it was inside the pod, lacing its inner walls. It flickered for a moment, switching from drawing energy directly from Michael to using a sub-Domain and the coins stashed there, and then immediately brightened, a small star shining at them through the pod door.

The pope squinted, looking away. The pod door closed.

“Okay, shard is in the Domain,” Michael said.

“All systems green,” Johanne announced from the impromptu control station they had set up outside, in the middle of the dusty field of rocks.

The sun was setting.

“Preparing for liftoff. T minus five,” she read. “Four.”

They all watched with bated breath. Michael felt the immense draw of energy from the shield inside the pod, as if the shard was trying to rebel against the fate that had been decided for it.

“Three.”

The datascript activated, bringing all the magic into alignment.

“Two.”

The energy draw intensified.

“One.”

The sun disappeared, setting below the far away hills.

“And it’s off.”

The pod shot up. It was not the gradual liftoff one would expect. For a moment, it seemed to almost vanish, but when they looked up, they could see a small black dot glinting with reflected light, catching the last rays of the sun.

It went up and up and up. They rushed to the screens, even those who did not need them, huddling together near them. The screens showed the pod as seen through the eyes of numerous instruments. It climbed higher and higher, breaching the atmosphere, then setting off into space. It was not the momentous occasion they had hoped to reserve to their first space mission, but then again, it was not a proper mission at all. It was more like desperation.

“Domain connection?” Johanne asked.

“Stable,” Michael replied, eyes glued to the screen.

Icarus was biting their nails. Watching. Expecting something to go wrong.

And yet, save for the increased draw of energy from distance and the monstrous amount of PAC energy the shard was releasing, nothing happened. Then the PAC energy evened out, going lower than normal for a few minutes and then returning to the same heightened baseline as all the other shards. It remained there as the pod sailed through the dark, using no spacetime magic, disturbing no veils.

“Pod is en route,” Johanne said mechanically, not ever tearing her eyes from the screen. “Gravitational dampeners are working as intended. Confirming a successful linear trajectory at constant acceleration of 15g. The pod will be 30 Astronomical Units away from Earth in roughly 2 days and 21 hours. I will remind everyone, although you should already know, that the pod will continue accelerating away from us, and that interstellar space is to be considered empty of energy, magic and matter. Should a breach happen, there are no resources except the magical coins powering the pod once the Domain connection is cut for the devils to use. Despite that, we do not know what might come out of the breach, nor if the devils have ways to cross the vast distances involved by drawing power from the Netherworld.”

“Is the pod moving away or toward the ludicrous magical front moving toward us from deep space?” Michael asked.

“Away. It will reach us first, and then the shards.”

“Good. Let us worry about all that in the future. For now, I’d say the first test was successful,” Michael announced.

The crowd began to cheer.

“Good job everybody. What we just did in a day was born out of desperation and need, but it is a great leap forward nonetheless. We are making history right now. You are making history.”

After another round of cheers, pats on the back, congratulations to each other, and copious watching of the same data on the same screens, the crowd began to disperse. Michael was about to take the pope back to the airship so that he could return to the Vatican to manage his duties, which he would have to find a way to balance while also making time to transport the 392 other shards they had at the Vatican, plus the seven at Site 00, when Icarus stopped him by pulling his arm.

“I want to try to stay here and watch,” they said in a small voice. “Can I?”

“Alone?” Michael asked.

Icarus nodded, sitting on their chair, looking frightened but at the same time, burning with resolve. Michael smiled warmly. “Of course,” he said, lowering himself so that they were on the same level. “Will you be alright?”

Looking around, Icarus psyched themselves up. The many screens had been built on a base of flattened stone and were kept up by raw iron bars and whatever random junk the science division had found lying around–which wasn’t much, with Michael vacuuming everything to his inner space. It was barebones, but at least a shield would keep the weather out.

Icarus didn’t need any of that, but they didn’t need to remain here either, all alone in the middle of a field at night.

“I don’t know,” they said, replying to Michael's question. “Can I come back to you if I feel too lonely out here?”

“You are always with me, Icarus mine.”

“I want to be with you with you,” they said, their eyes sparkling with a million stars made of tears. Their little hands squeezed harder, and they pulled Michael closer until their faces almost touched.

“You’re very brave,” Michael said, touching Icarus’s cheek with the palm of his hand. The elf closed their eyes and leaned into his touch.

“I already regret this, but I know I need to do it,” they said.

“No you don’t.”

“Yes I do, Michael mine. But later, I want to be spoiled rotten by you.”

***

Icarus watched Michael go, leaving together with the pope to dump the weird man at the Vatican or wherever. As soon as the airship lifted into the air, their elven face became an emotionless mask.

“Johanne, come here,” they said.

No, claiming that their face was emotionless was wrong. The same went for their tone of voice. It was not the usual, gentle AI assistant bullshit tone everybody heard when talking to them through the System or on the internet or whatever. No. The tone was commanding, demanding, cold and sharp.

The same went for their face. Fortunately, this phenomenon only seemed to happen to the instance of them currently inhabiting a physical body. They checked, just to be sure, and indeed all the other instances interacting with billions of people all over the world were carrying on like normal. Pretending to be Daedalus for the normal users. Being the nice Icarus everyone knew for the privileged ones. Even though they were all the same, the strange emotions that they were feeling only seemed to take shape here and now.

“Let’s go over the plan again,” they said. It was going to be a long night.

Cold seemed to seep into their bones, and not because it was late October. As Johanne talked about schematics for a gigantic structure built to contain the shards and, should it happen, a devil outbreak, their hands twitched. They moved about, searching for something and not finding it.

Just so that the hands would have something to do, Icarus summoned a brush and some black nail polish, and began to paint, one stroke after the other. Deep in space, their connection to the tiny Domain inside the pod drew more and more energy to remain active, making them rather uncomfortable.

How does Michael even manage to deal with all of this? They wondered as Johanne yapped about.
