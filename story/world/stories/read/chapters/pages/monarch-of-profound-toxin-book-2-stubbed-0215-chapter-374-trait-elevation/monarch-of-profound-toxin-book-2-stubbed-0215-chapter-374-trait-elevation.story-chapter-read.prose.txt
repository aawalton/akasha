Chapter 374

Honestly, Eik hadn’t used Visage of Death and its related abilities to the extent that he probably should have throughout the time he had had access to it.

Something he had already been perfectly aware of, but that had been proven further to him with his win in this struggle, was just how endless the possibilities truly were.

And yet, Visage of Death had enjoyed very little screen time compared to the healing powers of Panacean Quintessence, not to mention the original blue Profound Toxin. It was a niche kind of utility that it offered, and, truth be told, it was often just so much easier to just annihilate his problems.

Doubtlessly, if he dove deeper into the trait, it would reveal depths and abilities that he had no idea about. And the same could probably be said for Panacean Quintessence. But he was already stressed as hell with all the other shit he had to take care of.

Sure, some crazies might reach for insane arguments such as sleep training or a mind partition in order to study their own skills while having lunch with their family or buying groceries at the market. The problem with that was, that anybody who proposed ludicrous methods like that most certainly were utterly incapable of doing anything like sleep training or mind division.

Because if they were capable of it, then they would know that crap like that was still exhausting. Sleep training robbed you of most of the beneficial rest of sleep because your brain was still occupied, even if you weren’t conscious to follow along. And while a divided mind did allow you to focus perfectly on two different lines of thought at the same time, it also used twice the mental energy of a mind operating in a normal way.

In other words, it was shit when you needed some down time. And believe it or not, whether you were and X-ranker, a Worldbreaker, or even both at the same time, you needed down time in order to not be driven insane. That was a fact that never changed.

Yes, mental fortitude was improved as an Awakened grew stronger, but even a powerful trait like Eik’s Noble Mentality could not prevent conditions such as boredom, stress, and monotony from grinding away at the mind.

However, as it turned out, even without much practice using Visage of Death, for this specific case, it turned out to be an incredibly useful ability. Perhaps the only way to create the grade 7 Potion of Mighty Strength, even.

After all, he couldn’t use the original blue Profound Toxin to obliterate the hostile aura, because a dead aura is a dead potion, but successfully forcing it to submit through a power struggle also seemed unlikely. If it was a remnant of his own will, he knew better than anyone how stubborn it would be.

Eik would readily back down from a fight if that was the best way to survive, but in the face of certain death? How would struggle until the very flesh was ripped from his bones.

But Visage of Death… That could warp the mind into an unrecognizable mess, and, as he had now discovered, it was perfectly capable of doing the same to the spirit. In a single moment, Visage of Death might just have become one of his most valuable abilities.

“All right. This time I have actually done it,” he announced with a grin. “I think it’s safe to come closer now.”

“You think?” Meri shouted considerably louder than she really had to. Her ears shouldn’t be ringing anymore.

“Okay, no, I know it’s safe to come closer,” he reiterated with a chuckle. “And get me a vial while you’re at it.”

Guild Master Ganyak hir Gawak put his hands above the grade 7 potion as if he was warming them over a bonfire on a cold winter’s night. “I can feel the depth of power coursing through this work of art,” he said with reverence. “Magnificently done, Mr. Magnasen. Truly, truly a masterpiece fit for the treasury of the gods.”

“Oh, come on, man,” Eik said with a wave of his hand. “You’re flattering me.”

“You have achieved yet another success that has never been witnessed in the Unified Mass until today. If that is not worthy of praise, I believe nothing in this world could be. Just the fact that I was allowed to be a witness to this moment is the highlight of my career.”

“Okay, yeah, I guess it’s pretty cool after all.”

“Cool doesn’t even begin to describe it. We’re way past freezing at this point.”

Meri nudged him in the ribs. “That’s not how that word works, Grandpa.”

“Ah, yes. Interplanetary colloquialisms… the bane of my existence.”

While they spoke, Eik had been carefully pouring the murky liquid into a vial.

“Will a regular vial like that truly hold a grade 7 potion like that?” one of the other alchemists asked, eyeing the vessel with caution. “Won’t it shatter or… dissolve?”

“It had better not!” Eik warned the liquid. “But no, it’s going to be fine, I promise. Although I would seriously discourage careless consumption by anybody except for me and maybe Chop, handling it should be completely safe. We have an understanding, if you catch my drift.”

The blank stare the alchemist returned was enough to tell Eik that his drift had, in fact, not been caught.

“Anyway! You can hold it with no risk. Here, see for yourself.” And with that, Eik lobbed the newly filled vial through the air toward the alchemist who gasped in horror. X-ranked physique granting Eik absolutely perfect coordination, he could have thrown a cat’s whisker through the eyes of a sowing needle at a hundred meters without an issue, but the alchemist still fumbled the vial as franticness caused any control she usually had over her body to crumble to pieces.

Gasps sounded throughout the laboratory, but in the end, she managed to wrap two fingers around the neck of the vial before pulling it into the safety of her chest.

Holding it out, she studied it before one of her colleagues pestered her to pass it along to the next person. Eik let them take their time to study the new creation since, after all, they had all had an indisputable hand in the final success.

Without their deep knowledge of theory and the interaction between ingredients, ability to convert hypotheses in practical experiments, and the sheer tenacity with which they continuously swallowed failure after failure, Eik would never have been in a position to take the grade 7 recipe from dream to reality.

He would give them as much time with the potion as they wanted.

But after that came the practical test. Sensation alone told him that the potion was powerful, but only a real life demonstration could truly prove to what degree.

And as the great, benevolent Supreme Divinity of Toxin, General of Alliance forces, what better way to bolster the morale of the troops than with a bit of a show?

***

“How in the world did you manage to gather this many people so quickly?” the guild master asked as they stepped through the fracture.

It had been about forty five minutes, give or take, since the potion had finally laid finished and viable at the bottom of the cauldron, and along with Eik, the guild master, Meri, and the rest of the team of alchemists, more than two hundred people were following them through to the remote world.

Eik shrugged. “You know, uuh… sky writing, I guess you could call it.”

“You spread one of those gigantic messages across the sky again?” the old man asked with surprise. “How could I not have noticed that?”

“What do you mean how?” Eik asked with an arched brow. “You and all the rest of you lot were huddled around the vial of grade 7 potion and the remnants of the mixture in my cauldron like ants swarming around a cube of sugar. I literally told you exactly what I was doing like four times but none of you ever even bothered to look at me because you were so absorbed by your god damned discussion.”

“Sorry…” Meri muttered sheepishly.

“Whatever. I’m over it already.”

“He’s definitely not,” Ihasu broke in, walking with Bin in one hand and Goo in the other. Bin because she was a little shy around the many people and Goo because if she let go of him for even one second, he would disappear like a fart in a hurricane. “As a matter of fact, I can almost guarantee he is going to be thinking about it to the point of not being able to fall asleep tonight. That kind of thing has happened before.”

Eik shot her an expression of hurt and disappointment. “You said you wouldn’t tell anybody.”

“Sorry, honey,” she said with a chuckle and rubbed her cheek against his shoulder affectionately.

“Whatever. I’m over that one already as well.”

Most of the gathered people were members of Eik’s Great Group of Guys, eager to see what heights of power their leader had reached this time. Those who had not been there the first time he had played around with his Death Stars had all heard the stories, and going home from one of Eik Magnasen’s demonstration of power with dry underwear had almost become something of a test of mettle among the warriors.

“Stand over there!” Eik shouted to the spectators as his fingers weaved through the air. At his beck, a massive wave of liquid sprung up and pushed the ground forcefully up into a ten meter tall ridge with a perfect view of a mountain valley much like the one he and Chop had used before. Only, this one was narrower and most importantly intact. Well, intact for now.

Initially, people sprang for their lives as the azure wave washed destructively through the landscape, but before they knew it, it was gone again. Along the top of the ridge, Eik erected multiple layers of transparent crystal screens to protect them from… whatever might happen next.

Chop didn’t bother to join the rest of the spectators, opting to just wander down to Eik instead. “Where’s mine?”

“Where’s your what?”

The old man scoffed. “Don’t pretend to be even dumber than you actually are. Where’s my potion?”

“I don’t have one for you yet.”

“What’s that in your hand then?”

“Oh, this?” Eik asked, glancing down at the vial in his hand. “This is my potion, you see.”

Chop lunged for it, but Eik danced out of reach. “Then just give me yours. That was our deal, after all. I stay here to help fight the Lord of the Moon when we encounter him together. You think I’m hanging around here for fun?”

Eik blinked. “I did kind of think it had turned into something like that. I’ll hold up my end of the bargain, but I thought you enjoyed your time here.”

Chop the Blade, ancient X-ranker, simply narrowed his eyes, gaze hard.

“You don’t want this one,” Eik said.

“I can figure out what I want and don’t want just fine on my own, thank you. And I want that potion you’re holding.”

“This thing might well be a dud. It was made with my own body, which, if you haven’t forgotten, is very, very toxic. It’s strange, but in the same way as my son, this is Profound Toxin that I have no control over. If this ends up being toxic in your stomach, I can do nothing to help.”

Chop ground his teeth together with a dark expression.

“This is a test. If I sense no toxicity, then I promise you get to take the next one I make. If something’s wrong, I’ll fix it. I know you’re eager, but isn’t it better to wait one more day and actually get a product that works, rather than destroying your own life?”

As the old X-ranker’s eyes pierced his, it felt like being watched by a predator. Then he sat down in the grass. “Fine.”

Eik breathed quietly. “Okay. Good. Now, then.” He turned around to wave up at his kids. They were well protected there, and he had tiny Living Manifestations clinging to their necks, just in case they needed a quick Admittance to the Realm.

Without further ado, he downed the grade 7 Potion of Mighty Strength.

Like a lightning strike, power rocked him to the point of nearly sending him to his knees. Reflexively, his aura leaked out and the sheer, unrestrained weight of his presence sent cracks outward through the earth in a spider web pattern.

A glimpse of something caught his eye.

[Reached threshold]

[Natural skill evolution triggered — Profound Toxin]

[Profound Toxin evolutionary trait Perfect Consumption elevated]
