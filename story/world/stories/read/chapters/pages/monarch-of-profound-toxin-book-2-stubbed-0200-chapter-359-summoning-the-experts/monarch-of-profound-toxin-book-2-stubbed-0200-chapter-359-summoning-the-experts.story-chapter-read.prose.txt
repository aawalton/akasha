“Wha— H-Honey…?” Ganyak hir Gawak was about as confused as a person could possibly be. Even with his eyes fully open and looking, his brain just didn’t seem to be capable of translating the information provided by his sight into anything that even made remote sense.

And why would it have? The teleportation that he had just been subjected to was nothing like traveling through a fracture. At first thought, one might believe that they’re similar, and in some ways they certainly are, but as far as what it feels like to do it, they could barely be compared.

Albeit exceedingly strange and unsettling at first, there was actually a kind of smooth transition when walking through a fracture. There was original location, then the first side of the fracture, then the wobbly bobbly, ethereal, layer of haze in the middle, followed by the other side of the fracture, and then finally the target destination.

It might feel pretty awful for first time travelers, but it was still a five-stage transition. Teleportation consisted of original location and then an utterly abrupt shift to the target destination. Physically, it was not terribly different, but mentally, it just did something to your brain that made you lose it for a little bit.

Luckily, just like a fracture, with a few rides under your belt, you got used to it. Eik knew, because he had tested it extensively on two other old men.

Guild Master Ganyak hir Gawak was dressed… lightly. A thin, knee-length robes that appeared to be made of a soft material resembling silk was slung around his body without anything to hold the front closed. As a matter of fact, it was pure luck that it had landed in such a way that it covered up all of his nobler parts when he landed in the couch.

Crap. It was probably a good idea to make sure not to interrupt anything sensitive next time he plopped down one of his minions in his living room. Eik was tempted to blame it on his own grogginess, but it was difficult to claim that he wouldn’t have done the same in a normal state of mind.

“Uuh, well, I’m… Sorry, guild master,” he said sheepishly, trying to appear casual as Olivia threw a blanket over the old man. “But, hey, compliments and congratulations on the vigor.”

“M-Mr. Magnasen?” he stammered as his eyes went wide. He looked at the X-ranker as if he was only just now seeing him. “Would you mind leaving my bedroom?”

“I’m not in your bedroom, man,” Eik assured him without standing up from his seat. “You’re in my living room, though. Don’t you remember that I told you I could summon you to my side now that you’re in my Court of Toxin?”

For a few seconds, the old man just sat, confused, and stared into the ceiling. It was impossible to tell if his silence was due to too many thoughts at once or too few. “Vaguely,” he finally admitted.

“Well, that’s what this is.”

“I must say, I expected a little bit of… time to prepare myself.”

“Yeah, sorry, dude. I didn’t mean to interrupt…”

“No, yeah, of cour— Yeah, I… Can I go again now, or?”

“Uuh, no, actually, it would be great if you stayed. I have some pretty dire news to share.”

“Oh,” the guild master breathed, the words seeming to hit him like a sobering shower. “I understand. Can I borrow some clothes?”

With a twirl of his fingers, Eik wrapped Ganyak hir Gawak in an opaque suit of liquid Profound Toxin, allowing him to remove the blanket Olivia had provided.

“Speaking of,” Eik added. “I think we should call Gul, Andihar, Mn’Toakh, and Pölse as well. Better that they know as well. I guess I can just—” he said but stopped himself before the mental command could come to fruition, recalling the unfortunate state of Guild Master Ganyak hir Gawak. “Actually, they can come here themselves.”

Four Living Manifestation exploded out of the window and leaped across roofs at extreme speed, searching for the recipients of their messages.

“Are you seriously okay, Eik?” Rasmus asked again.

Eik shot him a beaming smile. “Of course, I am. I’ve never felt better!”

A few minutes after Eik had sent out the Profound Toxic beasts, Chop wandered into through the front door on his own.

He managed to get his shoes off, wander into the kitchen for a glass of water, lean against the counter as he drained the glass in a few sips with a sigh of contentment accompanying each mouthful, and walk over to sit down on the couch before he even noticed that all was not as it had been when he had left earlier.

It took him a double take to realize that what he was sitting on was in fact only one half of the original sofa. “What’s this?” he mumbled under his breath before looking up to find more than half a dozen people staring down at him. Puzzled, his eyes found Eik, now awake. “Oh, good.”

It was actually kind of insulting how little enthusiasm those words seemed to contain.

“Aren’t you supposed to be an X-ranker with senses keen enough to notice a mosquito land on a cow’s ass from two towns over?” Olivia asked him incredulously as her eyes flitted to Eik. “Do you all just share the ability to turn off your brains or something?”

“Hey, why am I getting shot at here as well?” I didn’t do anything!” Eik protested.

She shot him a look. “You’ve always done something. It’s just that you sometimes don’t remember.”

“Yeah, okay, that’s fair enough,” he admitted with a sigh.

Just then, Chop seemed to see the still confused guild master for the first time, his glittering suit of pure, planet-destroying toxin stinging the eyes. He nodded to himself, as if taking a mental note for later. “Nice shirt.”

Mn’Toakh was next to arrive.

She had apparently already been in Forest to take care of something or the other, so the Living Manifestation had been able to locate her swiftly. She was relieved to see Eik awake as well, although she was one of the ones who had never doubted that he would have a quick recovery.

As recent events had developed, she seemed to have found an almost reverent trust in his strength. Considering what he had just gone through, she probably had considerably more faith in his abilities than he did himself at this very moment.

As a matter of fact, he was experiencing a profound loss of self-confidence, and it seemed to be getting worse with every passing moment.

Gul, Andihar, and Pölse’s arrival pulled his mind away from that specific pit, however, and the conversation began to flow as Eik told them about what he had happened during his encounter with the Lord of the Moon.

When he had finished, leaving no relevant detail out, he paused and looked around the room, taking in a wide variety of reactions.

The generals were stoic and resolute, refusing to show on their faces the fear they must certainly have been feeling. Rasmus and Ihasu didn’t bother to hide it, the fracture specialist taking Eik’s father’s hand and squeezing it tightly. The kids had cleverly been sent outside to play.

Chop was… eating, and grumbling about a missed chance to fight the ancient Worldbreaker.

Guild Master Ganyak hir Gawak simply slumped back into the cushions, blood draining from his face as he stared at the floor. “So… he really is resurrected… the Lord of the Moon. We’re all done f—”

With a flex of his aura, Eik seized the room completely before the guild master could continue his gloomy train of thought. He could feel that it was affecting him. He couldn’t afford to become even more scared than he already was.

“No more of that,” he commanded. “None of us are done for. We already knew he was real. Nothing’s changed and nothing’s lost.”

Inside him, however, Profound Toxin raged. It didn’t happen often, but sometimes they clashed hard on something. This was one such time.

When you more or less shared a soul, it was an exceedingly tumultuous experience to practically fight to have your emotions prioritized within your own body. All Profound Toxin wanted right now was to rush back and murder the living crap out of the six-armed sack of shitty genes.

But even if they wanted to, none of Eik’s Living Manifestations had remained on that world, and he had had neither the presence of mind nor the time to leave another one behind before yoinking himself out of there. Not that it would have been able to escape the Lord of the Moon’s notice.

Eik, on the other hand, shameful as he might be about it, felt only relief that he didn’t have to go back now. While he had gotten rid of the parasitic swarm eating into his skin, a seed of fear was festering in his heart.

And the only way he could think of to quash it was to grow stronger.

Eik’s front door flew off the hinges with a wooden crack as Ganyak, hir Gawak’s aide, Jarice, crashed through, Meri following closely behind him. Red-faced and near collapse, Robert made up the tail, stumbling over the threshold and falling on his face.

“Sir!” Jarice shouted as he caught sight of the old man. “Sir, what happened to you? Are you unhurt?”

“I’m fine, Jarice. Or rather, I’m far from fine, but not for the reason you think. I’m safe.”

“The lady came running out of your chambers, screaming that you had vanished out of thin air. She was all sweaty from worry—”

“Okay, I get it, Jarice,” the guild master said with a raised hand. His eyes flitted briefly to his granddaughter’s grimace, but he tried not to meet her eyes. “As I said, I’m fine. Mr. Magnasen needed me urgently and summoned me here to save time. Thank you for worrying about me, but you may leave.”

“I will not, sir. I remain at your side at all times.”

The old man sighed and glanced at Eik, who only shrugged. “Fine. You can stay. So, since we’re all here, how are we going to get through this, Mr. Magnasen?”

With a thought, a large, smooth table of crystal appeared in the middle of the room. It was more than big enough for all of them to gather around it. From his belt pouch of holding, he retrieved multiple piles of disgusting offal, bloodied, pale skin, intestines, muscles, and bones. And finally came the crowning piece.

A warped, ugly as all hell head of an X-ranked spawn of the Lord of the Moon.

“I need that potion more than ever,” Eik told Meri and the guild master. “Let’s get started.”
