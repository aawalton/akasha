The letter was a summons.

Eola and her fellow apprentices joined the throng of students slowly working their way through the dormitory’s halls and out into the cold evening air. She’d bundled up, but the Winterstalk’s bite marks ached, a phantom chill that rested all around her knee even though the third-year overseeing her healing had pronounced her fully recovered.

“An unscheduled assembly? Why?” Colin asked as the students filed into the Grand Lecture Hall. The third of a circle of granite sat cold even as fires roared in the long, looming hearths across the side; the evening’s cool had seeped in through the windows, and there clearly hadn’t been enough time to warm the room. “Varin’s Academy only has two assemblies all term.”

She shrugged and found four seats next to each other, about halfway up the rows of desks that towered over the stage below. The blue and silver banners hung, shimmering as heat slowly rose up to the cavernous ceiling.

Then she saw the assembled professors. Madame Reyanna, staring stolidly forward. Headmaster Tomlish, looking over a few notes. Clearance, Fringe, and Yarrowbloom, and a dozen other professors besides them. And Instructor Tarik. His eyes roamed the assembled students, then stopped on Eola’s. He mouthed something.

“What’d he say?” Colin asked.

Eola could only shrug a second time. She’d missed it, her eyes locked on the other figures standing near the Grand Lecture Hall’s gigantic demonstration circle. There were almost a dozen of them as well, and they wore the mask and robes of the Cult of Y’aer.

Madame Reyanna had warned her. Instructor Tarik had mentioned something, too, if she remembered correctly. But to see them here.

“Welcome, aspiring mages, and thank you for coming so quickly. This should be almost everyone—if you know someone not in attendance, please fill them in as quickly as possible,” Headmaster Tomlish said. The lecture hall gradually quieted down, and he continued. “As you can see, we have a few new guests at Varin’s Academy, and I’ll introduce them shortly. However, before that happens, a few new notes to mention.”

“First, incidents in Varin’s Town have increased, especially between visiting mages and students. The town was built to support visiting mages as well as you, and you would do well to remember that. I assure you that your coin, while valued, would not be missed. Be on your best behavior, especially when encountering any visitors to the town and academy.”

He shuffled his notes. “Second, and at Professors Tarik, Reyanna, and Yarrowbloom’s combined request, we will be introducing a new program at our school. Many of our first-year students are in need of support, and many of our older students are ready for additional responsibility. Therefore, we have decided to open sign-ups for mentorships.

“These mentorships will be in addition to your current class schedule, and…” Eola wasn’t paying attention. Instructor Tarik had caught her eye again, and the professor mouthed whatever he’d said at her again. She still hadn’t picked most of it up, but what she’d gotten had been enough to refocus her.

She lowered her voice and leaned over to Colin, lips inches from his ear. “He says to keep our heads down. Can you tell Roth? I’ll let Patrice know.”

“Got it,” Colin replied. “About what?”

“I have no Y’aer-cursed idea.”

“So, if you are interested in mentoring the next generation of Varin’s Academy’s best, be prepared to sign up,” Instructor Tomlish finished.

Then he paused. One of the cultists, whose mask and robe both seemed a little more intricate than the others’, shifted slightly. Their face turned to look at him, and he nodded slowly.

“And, finally, the Cult of Y’aer has asked for a new, strictly advisory role here at Varin’s Academy. As you know, the cult has the support of both the Governing Houses and the people of Alemia, and given High Cultist Calliene Andrese’s request, I—“

Roth’s voice cut off the headmaster as he stood. “Wait. Who?”

And, as Eola looked around, she realized he wasn’t the only one. Patrice was also on her feet, jaw dropped.

And so, two rows back, was Catrine, a horrified look on her face.

“Center yourselves,” Instructor Tomlish said, voice dripping with stern disapproval. Colin grabbed Roth’s arm and pulled him back to his seat, while Patrice managed to settle herself. But Catrine…Catrine’s friends tried and failed to calm her down, and a moment later, she fled the room, Garreth Skagget right behind her. Eola smiled smugly—it was good to see Latrine Catrine on the back foot.

“Yes, High Cultist Calliene Andrese will be overseeing the Cult of Y’aer’s advisers. This is primarily in response to a growing trend in attunements, not only here at Varin’s Academy but across Alemia, that are incompatible with magic.”

“What does that mean?” Colin asked. Eola reached over and touched his knee, and his hand overlapped hers, fingers meshing. She had no idea what it meant, but it definitely had something to do with her and her friends—and whatever it was, it wouldn’t be good.

She glanced at the professors, and Tarik caught her eye. He didn’t mouth anything this time, but his eyes screamed a single message.

He’d talk about it later.

High Cultist Calliene Andrese, until relatively recently Lady Andrese of the same house, looked a lot like her daughter.

The perfect blonde hair. The tall, slim frame—though in her case, the Cult of Y’aer’s robes hid most of it, and age had taken its toll. The disgusting sense that she felt like she was better than everyone else in the room. As the High Cultist took her mask off and walked to the podium, Eola focused in on every word she said. Maybe Instructor Tarik would have some insights, or maybe her friends would.

And if that failed…her hand drifted to her pocket, where a small, warm carving of a cat hid away. Maybe Atta would know something.

The woman’s voice reminded Eola of Catrine, too. “Thank you. Y’aer, unlike the demanding base gods we’ve all cast aside, does not ask for our worship, or for our love. Nevertheless, we persist in that worship. Let us begin.

“The gift of magic. The curse of monstrosities. Those are Y’aer’s legacies. And yet, they gave us both—and not only them, but the three Gifts. However, the traditional teachings don’t tell of their fourth Gift—that of attunement.

“Attunement is Y’aer’s most recent gift, handed down by the Great Mage Frelanne. A mage’s attunement defines them, gives them focus, and fits them into the world around them. It is that gift which my fellow Revisionists and I believe to be the most important one in all of the Saint of Curses and Blessings’s many they bestowed upon us, for it defines how we behave as mages.”

She paused. Her mask sat on the podium, eyes hauntingly like those of her daughter. “They also fail to recognize the saint’s Last Gift, handed down by Varin himself. Ordered Magic.”

Patrice made a confused sound next to her.

Eola couldn’t help but notice that several of the professors nodded along, while others looked uncomfortable with the school’s namesake being invoked as a holy figure. Tarik and Tomlish in particular shot looks at the back of the High Cultist’s head. Not that Eola was pleased about it, either. She’d be hearing about this from Atta, she was sure.

And worse, this didn’t line up with what she’d known—what she’d been told by Patrice and Reyanna and even Instructor Tarik—about the Revisionists. What had happened in the Cult that’d caused such a new belief to become ascendant?

“However, the Fourth and Final Gifts are just those—gifts, to be used and cherished.” The cultist’s eyes washed across the room, lingering right next to Eola. Patrice shivered and tried to shrink into her chair, and Eola reached over with her free hand—Colin still had one in a death grip. “And there are some students and professors here who may not understand that.”

Patrice shivered. Eola sniffed. What was that scent in the air? It hadn’t been there a moment earlier. Sage? Juniper? She couldn’t place it.

“The Cult of Y’aer will be monitoring Varin’s Academy, especially those students with odd attunements, to help them find their place in the world,” the High Cultist finished, then stepped back from the podium.

“Thank you, High Cultist Andrese,” Headmaster Tomlish said quietly. His eyes flashed, and for a moment, Eola wondered if they’d see the Great Mage Tomlish come out. He’d been a mighty mage in his prime, and Eola doubted that High Cultist—

In the blink of an eye, the scent in the Great Lecture Hall shifted. Where it’d been herbal, it took on a sickly sweet, cloying odor, then went completely odorless. A few professors stared at the Third Order spell hanging in front of the High Cultist, and she smiled sweetly as she put her mask back on.

“Thank you, High Cultist,” the headmaster said again. “Students, please treat the Cult of Y’aer as if they’re not present in your classes unless they address you directly. Other than that, go about your business as if nothing has changed. You are dismissed.”

By the time Eola and her friends fought their way through the crush of students milling about in the dormitory’s hallways, shoved their suite door open, and started shouting, the tension had grown so thick she couldn’t have cut through it with her sword.

“Well, that’s that,” Patrice said. Her voice was far too loud, and Eola pointed her father’s wand at the door and sketched a chain. Silent Stand was a First Order-equivalent spell, and one she’d borrowed from Roth. She dropped her layer of silence all across the door as Patrice continued. “My mother and father are Revisionists as well, but they hadn’t told me about anything like this. Whatever’s going on, though, it’ll be the end of my quest for magic.”

“No, it won’t,” Eola started to say, but Roth’s voice ran over hers.

“Lady Calliene Andrese. Lady Calliene Andrese is here.” His fists had balled tightly, and his eyes were closed. He stood, staring at the door, body so tense Eola could practically see him coiled even through his robe, like he was about to storm out and go after the archmage here and now.

“I know—“

“You don’t know. You can’t possibly know,” he snapped.

“Yes, she can,” Colin said.

Roth whirled to face Colin, who sat down on the couch and pulled Eola next to him. His hand hadn’t left hers since the High Cultist had finished speaking, and she hadn’t tried to escape, either. Roth didn’t even seem to notice. “How in Y’aer’s cursed name could she possibly know?”

Colin swallowed and glanced at Eola.

This was it. The moment she decided whether she could trust Roth and Patrice as much as she trusted Colin. She swallowed painfully. “I can know, Roth. I do know. Colin, can you…?”’

Colin nodded and squeezed her hand. “Four years ago, Eola’s parents were both trapped in Lord Card of Greenarbor’s dungeon. She’s been trying to find a way to get them out. He shut the door and sealed it behind them.”

Silence. It hung in the air for a few moments while Eola blushed and tried to bury her face in Colin’s shoulder.

Then Roth coughed. “Curse Y’aer and all their gifts. Fine. You know, then. You know.”

“Know what?” Patrice asked.

“Not now. But later. I promise I’ll catch you up later.” Roth’s glare shifted to her. “So, what do we do about Lady Andrese?”

“We’re not doing anything about her.”

“You’re saying that because your parents are Revisionists.”

“No, I’m saying that because Eola could barely beat Latrine Catrine last term, and she was barely even a Journeymage. Lady…sorry, High Cultist Andrese isn’t a problem we can solve by fighting or by doing magic. Not even by doing very high-quality, cutting-edge magical research. Yes, I’m talking about you, Eola,” Patrice said. “What’d Tarik say to you, Eola?”

“He told me to—“

“I told you to keep your heads down,” Instructor Tarik said as he opened their suite door and stepped inside, then shut it. “Well done on that spell, Miss Lemiene, but you’ll want a ritual—probably Second Order—to properly block off sound. This muffles it, but it makes the sound strange. I could tell you were using a spell easily enough.”

Before Eola could extract her hand from Colin’s, the professor rounded on Roth. “Mr. Gerr, you are thinking about making a devastatingly bad mistake.”

“And?” Roth’s glare could have killed.

“And if you continue, I’ll have no choice but to report you to the High Cultist, if only to protect your fellow apprentices and the ones I intend for you four to mentor after Y’aer’s Day.”

“But sir,” Eola said, “High Cultist Andrese said specifically that she was out to monitor students with broken attunements. That means us.”

“Does it? I have it on good authority—and have witnesses, including the Great Mage Tomlish—that three of you can do magic just fine. The Cult of Y’aer has no legal authority to do anything, including trying to act against what they consider to be heretical.” Instructor Tarik sighed and rubbed his temple with a single finger. “And as for you, Miss Clerk…you’re running low on time, but your name should protect you for a while longer. Miss Lemiene, what have you uncovered?”

“Well, I was about to use Inner Vision to take a look at Patrice’s, um, Mana lines and soul. I had a conversation with Madame Reyanna earlier, and she explained more of the theory behind Mana lines, souls, and conduits, as well as gave me a warning about the Cult of Y’aer,” Eola said.

“Good woman. Stubborn, arrogant, and off-base about almost everything, but a good woman. What else?”

Eola’s brow wrinkled. “The third floor. We need to go back to the third floor—back to exactly where we went to find the Ensorcellment manuals. The Phantasm was obsessed with you, Patrice, and I want to know why.”

“You’re serious. You really think that…thing…is a possible solution?”

“Yes, I do.” Eola stared down the tall redhead. “And after we use Inner Vision—Colin, you can cast it on me, right?”

Colin nodded.

“Great. After we compare our soul structures, then we can start putting together a unified theory of why Patrice’s magic doesn’t work right.”

Instructor Tarik nodded and turned to Colin. “Mr. Tremory, I’ll need to talk with you privately. The rest of you, we’re still proceeding as normal with all of my plans.”

“You never tell us any of your plans,” Roth said.

“Quite right. You’ll continue with your classes, we’ll continue working with your unique attunements during your apprenticeship time, and you will each do everything in your power to avoid antagonizing High Cultist Andrese. Is that clear?”

Eola nodded. After a moment, so did Patrice. Colin made an affirming sound as he stood.

“Is that clear, Mr. Gerr?”

“Yes,” Roth ground out through clenched teeth. He stalked to his room, slammed the door, and started playing one of his instruments. Loudly.

“Ah, and one more thing. Miss Lemiene, do not use the Cutting mark unless you absolutely must. Mr. Tremory, with me.” Instructor Tarik bowed slightly, then left as quickly as he’d come.

Colin looked back helplessly as he followed the professor out into the hall, and then Eola was alone with Patrice—and with a lot to think about. But none of it would stick in her mind.

“Well,” she said after an awkward minute or two of silence. “Well, I know how to cast Inner Vision. We may as well get started.”
