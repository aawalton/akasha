“So, keep in touch.”

With that, Ning saw Xiao Fan off. The protagonist departed with his white fox at his side, though she still seemed annoyed with him over some comment he'd made along the way.

The two of them made quite a pair.

Thinking about it, Ning had never intended to get close to someone like Xiao Fan. Protagonists were unpredictable variables who attracted trouble the way spiritual herbs attracted beasts, naturally and with alarming consistency. Anyone who stayed around them long enough was bound to get dragged into something eventually.

If this were a novel, I'd probably count as a named character by now. The kind who appears often enough to have a personality, but not often enough to have a guaranteed survival clause.

Depending on the story, he could end up as comic relief, a rival, a stepping stone, or perhaps that convenient ally who appeared whenever the plot needed an extra pair of hands.

Well, he'd already accepted that possibility. Whatever trouble came from associating with Xiao Fan could be left for his future self to deal with, especially since avoiding someone entirely over what might happen seemed like a waste.

Besides, Xiao Fan was useful. For all the chaos that followed him around, he delivered whenever Ning actually asked him for something, and as far as collaborators went, Ning had certainly worked with worse.

Before separating, they'd also exchanged some useful information.

Ning had asked about the Heaven Shattering Fist, specifically whether Xiao Fan could provide him with a genuine, intact copy. Unfortunately, the answer was roughly what he'd expected.

The sect had prohibited Xiao Fan from distributing it. Although he'd successfully repaired the first level of the technique, there was no guarantee that hidden flaws wouldn't cause problems later in cultivation. Until the elders confirmed that the restored version was safe, Xiao Fan wasn't permitted to pass it on to anyone else.

More interestingly, the person who'd delivered that decision was Elder Zhou Chen, apparently with barely concealed smugness.

Xiao Fan suspected the elder had played some part in getting the restriction imposed, and considering Zhou Chen had already gone so far as to interfere with anyone trying to take Xiao Fan as a disciple, Ning couldn't exactly dismiss the possibility.

That elder really seems to have far too much free time.

Still, there were plenty of other techniques in the world. If the Heaven Shattering Fist wasn't available now, Ning could always look for something else.

With that settled, he turned his attention to his new companion.

The White Flame Raven was perched on a low branch nearby, its pure white feathers standing out clearly among the leaves. It had been watching Ning ever since Xiao Fan left, seemingly content to wait and see what its new master intended to do.

It looked, objectively speaking, very cool.

“Come,” Ning said. “Let's get you registered.”

The raven continued watching him for a few seconds, almost as if deliberately making him wait, before spreading its wings and gliding down onto his shoulder.

Ning immediately noticed the additional weight.

“Hm. Heavy.”

The raven turned its head toward him.

“I'm just noting it.”

It continued staring.

“…It was a compliment. Dense muscle mass indicates good physical cultivation.”

The raven seemingly didn't consider that explanation worth responding to, because it simply turned away.

Ning was about to start walking when another thought occurred to him. He'd been referring to the raven as he in his head ever since Xiao Fan brought it over, despite having absolutely no idea whether it was actually male or female.

Well, that was somewhat embarrassing.

“So,” Ning asked, glancing toward the bird on his shoulder, “are you male or female?”

The raven ignored him, probably still annoyed about the weight comment.

Ning decided to ask someone at the Beast Taming Pavilion instead. A proper beast tamer would probably be able to tell at a glance, while his own fairly extensive knowledge did not, unfortunately, include identifying the sex of ravens by sight.

There would be plenty of time to learn more about his new companion later, so Ning set off toward the pavilion with the White Flame Raven still perched on his shoulder.

...

The Pure Qi Sect was arranged in concentric rings, with the outermost areas housing outer sect disciples and lower-ranked cultivators, while the inner sect occupied the regions farther in. Both were further divided according to specialization, with entire sections dedicated to alchemy, spiritual farming, beast taming, and the sect's other professions.

Ning's Courtyard 69 was located in the Farmland Region, where most of his neighbors were spiritual farmers or cultivators with earth and wood attributes. It was a place where people woke early, worked most of the day, and generally kept to themselves, which suited him perfectly.

The Beast Taming Pavilion was located in another part of the inner sect, close to a natural treeline with plenty of open space. That made sense considering some contracted beasts could grow larger than houses, and keeping all of them near the residential courtyards sounded like a disaster waiting to happen.

As they walked, Ning decided it would be better to establish a few things with his new companion early. The raven was intelligent enough to understand him, and even if it couldn't speak, it could certainly form its own opinions.

“I don't have a strict system for contracted beasts,” Ning said. “You're not a tool, and you're not a pet either. Think of it as a partnership. You benefit, I benefit, and we both try not to get killed.”

The raven tilted its head slightly as it listened.

“I'll provide pills, resources, and whatever else you need for cultivation. In return, I'd like to borrow your white flame when I need it for alchemy. Beyond that, you can mostly do what you want.”

The raven remained quiet, though Ning noticed that some of the tension in its posture had disappeared.

Good enough for a start.

By the time they reached the Beast Taming Pavilion, Ning had already passed more spiritual beasts than he'd seen during most of his time in the outer sect. The pavilion itself was a broad, two-story building whose ground floor had large open entrances, presumably so that bigger beasts could actually fit through them.

It was Ning's first time visiting this region of the sect. Back when he'd been a weaker outer sect disciple, wandering through an area filled with freely roaming spiritual beasts hadn't seemed like an intelligent thing to do.

Most contracted beasts were disciplined enough not to attack people without reason, but accidents still happened, especially when weaker disciples got too close to something temperamental.

Now that he'd leveled up a few times, places he'd once avoided were gradually becoming ordinary destinations. It was just like accessing the previous restricted area in an RPG.

Ning approached the registration counter, where a female disciple with neatly pinned hair was sorting through several documents.

“Yes? How can I help you?”

“I'd like to register my spiritual beast.”

She looked toward the White Flame Raven on his shoulder, and her attention immediately sharpened.

“A White Flame Raven? That's quite rare.” She studied the bird for another moment before smiling. “And a handsome one too.”

Ning noted the important part.

Male.

As expected of someone who worked with spiritual beasts for a living. Ning had spent half the walk wondering about it, and she'd answered the question with a single glance.

“White Flame Ravens have a good bloodline and can naturally reach Foundation Establishment if raised properly. Their white flame is also quite valuable among yang-attributed beast flames.” Her gaze returned to Ning. “Is he your first contracted beast?”

“Yes.”

“Then I'll explain the process.”

She reached beneath the counter and brought out a folded document. “At minimum, you'll need a blood contract. It establishes the bond between you and allows you to sense some of his intentions, which makes communication much easier.”

She unfolded the document in front of him.

“The pavilion also offers a formal Beast Partnership Contract. It includes the basic blood bond, but it also registers the beast with the sect and establishes your responsibilities toward each other. Once he's registered, other disciples will know he isn't a wild beast. The contract costs five hundred spirit stones.”

“I'll take it.”

Ning answered quickly enough that she gave him an approving nod.

“Good choice. You can find cheaper contracts outside the sect, but ours also come with protection under sect law. If someone deliberately harms your contracted beast without cause, you can report the matter directly to the pavilion.”

Ning was already aware of that, which was precisely why he'd come here instead of trying to save a few spirit stones elsewhere. He liked saving money, but there were some things worth paying full price for.

The senior sister laid the contract across the counter and went through its contents with him. The wording was dense, though the terms themselves were straightforward: Ning would be responsible for any serious damage caused by his contracted beast, abuse of the contract was prohibited, and disputes involving either party could be handled through the Beast Taming Pavilion.

Nothing seemed unreasonable.

“Then we can establish the blood bond.”

Ning pressed his thumb against the designated seal, activating a small formation within the document. A single drop of blood was drawn from his thumb and spread through the inscription as a thin red line.

“Now it's your turn,” the senior sister said to the raven.

The White Flame Raven examined the contract before extending one talon. The second formation activated at its touch, drawing another drop of blood and connecting it to Ning's through the inscriptions.

A faint warmth traveled up Ning's arm as the contract took effect. A moment later, he became aware of something new at the edge of his perception. It was so faint that he might have overlooked it if he hadn't been paying attention.

So this is what a beast contract feels like.

He couldn't hear words or directly read the raven's thoughts, but there was now a vague connection between them, enough for him to sense simple impressions that hadn't been there before.

The raven seemed to notice the change as well. It ruffled its feathers once before returning to its usual composed posture.

With the bond established, the senior sister stamped the completed contract, recorded the information on a jade slip, and handed Ning a small identification token.

“This identifies him as your contracted beast. Keep it with you when traveling outside the sect, especially if you're entering a city or passing through a checkpoint. Anyone authorized can use it to confirm the registration.”

Ning placed the token into his storage pouch. “Thank you.”

She was about to put away the remaining documents when she remembered something.

“One last thing. I need a name for the registration.” She looked toward the raven again. “What are you calling this handsome fellow?”

Ning had already decided.

“Fenghuang.”

She recorded the name before giving a small nod. “That's a good name. You clearly put some thought into it.”

Considering some of the names she probably had to enter into official sect records, Ning suspected the standard wasn't that high.

Once they left the pavilion, Ning looked at the raven on his shoulder.

“Fenghuang,” he repeated, resting a hand against his chin. “You know, there's actually a reason I chose that name. In some ancient legends, there was a divine bird associated with flame and rebirth. It was considered an auspicious creature, one that stood at the very peak among birds.”

He glanced at the white raven perched on his shoulder.

“There were many stories about it, but what I remember most is its flame. No matter how much time passed or what hardships it endured, that flame would always return.”

Ning gently ran a hand over the raven's white feathers.

“A flame that refuses to fade. I think it suits you.” He smiled. “And since we're going to cultivate together, perhaps one day we'll reach those heights ourselves.”

Fenghuang tilted his head and quietly observed him. After a moment, Ning felt a faint sense of approval through their newly formed contract, followed by a short caw.

Ning took that as agreement.

“Good. Fenghuang it is.” He smiled and added, “It's quite a distinguished name, you know. You should feel honored.”

The faint approval disappeared almost immediately.

Ning nodded to himself. The contract was working perfectly.

...

Their next stop was the Spiritual Farming Hall, a familiar, low, wide building surrounded by neatly organized spirit fields. Ning had visited often enough during his outer sect days that he barely paid attention to the surroundings anymore and headed straight for the counter.

“Hello, Senior Sister Hu Yue.”

“Oh, if it isn't Junior Brother Ning.” Hu Yue looked up from behind the counter and smiled. “It's been quite a while.”

Thinking about it, almost everyone Ning knew who worked behind a counter seemed to be a woman.

It was quite a coincidence.

“I'm here for the Spring Breeze Seedling Technique,” Ning said.

It was the next spiritual farming technique he'd decided to practice. The first level of his Crimson Flame Control was already approaching completion, giving him some room to focus on something new, and the Spring Breeze Seedling Technique seemed especially useful. By supplying spiritual qi during important stages of a plant's development, it could accelerate growth without harming the plant itself.

Hu Yue was surprised. “You can already learn that?”

Then she seemed to remember who she was talking to.

“Actually, why am I surprised? You've already passed the Spiritual Farmer certification.” She nodded to herself with satisfaction. “It seems I really do have a discerning eye. I spotted your talent early.”

“Of course. I couldn't have done it without Senior Sister's guidance.”

Hu Yue looked quite pleased with that answer.

Ning wasn't as familiar with her as he was with Si Sihua, but he'd visited the Spiritual Farming Hall frequently during his outer sect days, so the two knew each other fairly well.

“Since you have the certificate, you can learn the technique for free. One of the better benefits of being certified.” Hu Yue reached for the appropriate jade slip, then paused. “Right, what level is your Withering and Flourishing Technique?”

“Great Accomplishment.”

“Already?” Hu Yue gave him another look before shaking her head. “Then you're fine. The Spring Breeze Seedling Technique accelerates growth, so you need enough control over a plant's vitality to avoid damaging it. Great Accomplishment is the minimum requirement.”

Ning nodded. “Understood.”

Hu Yue handed him the jade slip, and Ning had just put it away when someone called out from nearby.

“Excuse me.”

He turned toward the side corridor, where a broad-shouldered, middle-aged man was approaching.

“You must be Ji Ning from Courtyard 69.” The man stopped in front of him and smiled. “I'm Sui Feng, Courtyard 68. It seems we're neighbors.”

“I see. I've been meaning to introduce myself, but there was never anyone there.”

“I was away on a mission. I only got back recently and heard someone new had moved in.” Sui Feng sensed Ning's cultivation and gave an understanding nod. “Sixth Stage... You must be one of the winners from this year's outer sect competition.”

“Yes, that's right.”

“Hah! Then I got lucky with my new neighbor.” Sui Feng laughed. “Have you met Old Fang yet? He's in Courtyard 67. I left him to keep an eye on my fields while I was gone, though I'm starting to wonder whether that was a mistake. That old man is lazy enough that my radishes might have learned to water themselves by now.”

“No, I haven't met him yet. I've been busy settling in over the past month.”

Ning had intended to introduce himself to the surrounding disciples eventually. Maintaining good relations with neighboring spiritual farmers was useful, especially since mandatory sect missions could take someone away from their fields for days or even weeks. Having someone nearby who could water a field or deal with an unexpected problem made things much easier, much like the arrangement he'd once shared with Old Zhou.

“That's fine. You're still new.” Sui Feng waved it off before apparently remembering something. “Actually, you caught me at a good time. A few of us from the surrounding courtyards are getting together today. Nothing formal, just some food and conversation. I should've introduced you earlier, but I was away.”

“I understand.”

“Come along, then. It'll save you from knocking on everyone's doors later.”

Ning needed about a second to decide. Meeting the people living around him was something he'd planned to do anyway, and networking was rarely a wasted effort.

“Alright. Please lead the way.”

...

The gathering was being held in Courtyard 67, which belonged to the aforementioned Old Fang. His courtyard was slightly larger than Ning's, with several fields surrounding a stone-paved area where a wide table had been covered with food. There was spiritual grain rice, braised spirit vegetables, several dishes Ning didn't recognize, and a jar of fermented plum wine whose aroma reached him before he even sat down.

Several people were already gathered around the table. One older man was sharpening a hoe while talking to the person beside him, another disciple was making notes in what appeared to be a planting record, and someone farther down the table had decided that the gathering was an excellent opportunity for a nap.

Sui Feng introduced them one by one.

The older man sharpening the hoe was Old Fang, who was every bit as lazy as Sui Feng had claimed, despite being one of the most experienced spiritual farmers in the area. Senior Sister Chu specialized in water-attributed spirit herbs and had learned several rain-type spells specifically for farming, while the others each had their own fields and specialties.

Ning committed their names to memory.

Fenghuang also attracted a few curious looks from his perch on Ning's shoulder, though no one made much of it. Contracted beasts weren't unusual among spiritual farmers, since many could help with fieldwork or protect crops from pests. Spirit bulls, chickens, and similar beasts were far more common than a White Flame Raven, however.

Ning accepted a cup of plum wine and spent most of the gathering listening. He was new to the group, and there was little reason to force himself into every conversation when simply paying attention was more useful.

The topics were quite interesting, ranging from who was growing what to which spiritual farmers had recently partnered with alchemists, along with the occasional bit of gossip about their fellow disciples.

Ning listened with genuine interest, picking up a few useful pieces of information while enjoying the surprisingly good plum wine.

The food was good, his new neighbors seemed friendly enough, and everything was going smoothly.

A perfectly normal gathering, just as he'd hoped.

Unfortunately, it seemed the heavens had other plans.

“Wait! Why is he here?”

Someone suddenly pointed at Ning.

“He doesn't belong here!”

Don't tell me this is another one of those unavoidable xianxia encounters.
