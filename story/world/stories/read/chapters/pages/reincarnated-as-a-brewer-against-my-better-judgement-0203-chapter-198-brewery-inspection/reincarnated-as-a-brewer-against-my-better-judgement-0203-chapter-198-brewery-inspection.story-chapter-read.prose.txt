Alric walked with his guard contingent, but they slowed as they approached the brewery road, as it was now called.

The guards had officially closed the road; looking past them at the entrance, he could see more guards farther in. Calling it a brewery at this point felt like an understatement; it was more like the brewery complex.

As they stepped onto the road, the brewery guards opened the gate for him with a smile. To his immediate right was the barracks; it had previously been a warehouse, but the previous owners had been evicted. The soldiers the king had assigned were all training; the warehouse courtyard now served as their training yard.

One of the soldiers nearby gave Alric a big smile and approached. Alric looked back at the men drilling in the training yard.

“Be seeing you, Mister Alric.” He said with the same grin. Alric glanced at his uniform; he had some marks on his shoulders that probably meant something to someone else. Alric’s personal guard made room for him to approach.

“Good to see you too…” Alric was about to say his rank but realized he had no idea; calling him a soldier might be offensive. He decided to try to blend in. “The boys training hard?” Alric gestured to the training yard.

The soldier nodded. “Night watch been saying things, Mister Alric. About the boys and their drinking.” He glanced over at the training. “Said if they can fit in their armor, they should walk the walls some.” His grin grew.

Alric’s eyes narrowed; he glanced at the soldiers again. One of them was sparring and clearly taking it seriously. “Captain Rien say something like that?” Alric asked, looking back at the soldier.

The soldier's grin widened with glee. “Wouldn’t know about that. The captain is a clever bugger.”

Alric and his nearby guards scoffed. “That he is.” Alric said with a nod. The soldier kept his grin and headed back to his men.

Leaving the soldiers to their training, Alric headed further in. The nearest building was known as the cask building. They sent returned casks there first. Mostly for cleaning and steaming. The brewery buildings formed a U shape: the cask building and the fermentation building formed the arms, and the brewery formed the back. This meant that nobody needed to approach the main building directly.

Not much had changed on the outside of the buildings; only when he entered the cask building did he notice the first change. A small kiln sat at the back of the building. Alric approached it immediately.

Alric sniffed the air and noticed the faint grain smell. A worker hurried to catch up, attempting to bow to Alric as he approached the kiln.

“Uh. Mister Alric, we are happy to have it.” The worker said, having bowed for the third time.

“None of that. When did we get this?” Alric asked, pointing at the kiln.

The man glanced at it, then back at Alric. “For the grain, Mister Alric. Dryin’ it, and we can toast here too.” He looked at Alric’s expression. “We got permission, Mister Alric? You said it had to be here and not the fermentation building?”

Alric continued studying it before the memory surfaced. It had been suggested in a meeting, and he insisted it had to be here because they kept grain in the fermentation building. It also had to use a heating stone. Alric glanced inside and saw the recesses for it.

“I’m not complaining, I just hadn’t seen it yet,” Alric said with a shrug. The idea immediately made him consider doing this for a bakery again, although he knew he didn’t have the mages to support it. He glanced around the rest of the building. The steamers that used to work inside the brewery were now here, steaming casks. Otherwise, the building was full of shelves and racks of empty casks. Staff quarters existed, but he had no intention of inspecting them.

Overall, it looked neat and orderly. He talked with the worker briefly. When casks were returned, they were cleaned in the main brewery yard first, then brought here to be steamed, then joined a queue that would move toward the brewery. Before filling them with wort, they would be steamed again.

“That all makes sense, thank you,” Alric said. The worker smiled and stepped away. Alric couldn’t help but notice the man seemed relieved.

The main building struck him with the familiar warmth and dampness. The air felt wet despite the open roof, and Alric could see moisture trails running down the inside walls. Mara approached him just after he was across the threshold. “Good to see you, Mister Alric.”

Alric didn’t like her calling him Mister, but with staff around, he couldn't help it. “Good to see you too, Mara,” Alric said, but he was barely paying attention. Instead, he looked at the changes in the germination section and pointed them out. Along the wall were many quern stations. He expected those because he had signed off on them, but it was one thing to approve something and another to see it.

Mara grinned at his expression and gestured for him to approach. “You haven’t seen them yet?”

Alric shook his head and approached the nearest one. These were somewhat larger than the old one they had worked very hard. “These easier to use?” Alric asked, tilting his head.

The querns were simple; two circular pieces of stone were stacked on each other like short cylinders.  The top stone had an upright wooden handle and a hole in the center. They put grain in the center hole, then turned the stone with the handle to crush the malt. The stones sat in a wooden basin which had its own spout.

“Yes, Mister Alric, although grain can be stubborn and they are a pain to clean,” Mara explained, pointing at it.

Alric nodded; querns were querns in his mind, and there wasn’t much more to say. Someone deeper in called for Mara; she glanced. Alric waved for her to go. She nodded and headed to whoever had called.

Alric watched the operation for a while longer. The boilers worked the same way, although all six were in use today. A seventh had been installed in the final boiler section but wasn’t in use. Hal clearly had that in hand; he nodded when he made eye contact with Alric before he shouted at someone to slow their tip.

Pretty much their brewery now.

The rest of checking the brewery was quick. The cellar was filled with wine that would become brandy as well as lager casks. Tarrow walked with someone who was taking notes.

“Oh, Mister Alric!” Tarrow said with a grin; he waved Alric over while holding up a small cup.

“Good to see you, Tarrow,” Alric said, still looking at the casks. They had small charcoal scribbles in the heads. Alric was about to ask about it when Tarrow asked him a question.

“Glad you came through; I can't decide about some of these,” Tarrow explained; he tapped on a nearby cask. “Tastes different, but same batch.”

Alric took a small taste from different casks. The first had a distinctly strong fruity scent. “This one has been resting too warm; you know this one from the Ale?” Alric asked as he sipped.

Tarrow shrugged. “Wasn’t sure it was the same with the winter beer. The staff do love those, and sometimes I worry they do it on purpose.” He gestured to the cask.

Alric grinned; if it didn’t happen too often, he didn’t mind. It was easier to let little things like this slide than to try to police the staff in his mind. They’d step in if it got out of hand. “It’s not terrible; I think we should just send this one to headquarters. My guards will like it.” Alric said casually.

Both Tarrow and Alric watched his note-taker out of the corner of their eyes, his shoulders slumped. That got a laugh from Alric and Tarrow.

The rest had simple problems; one didn’t have yeast. Since Alric had started using powdered yeast, they had to pitch it when it wasn’t too hot. Another needed more time. One had gone horribly bad, and Alric just blamed the cask. Many of the samples tasted different; Alric chalked most of that down to temperature shifts.

“This kind of beer is going to be awkward, Tarrow. Not a lot you can do.” Alric explained.

Tarrow, however, frowned at one of the nearby casks. “It’s good beer, I know, but it's so finicky.”

Alric frowned before he recognized that Tarrow was trying to get it perfect. “It doesn’t work like that, Tarrow. If winter decides to punish us more, the beer will change. Some batches will be great, some won't. We have to adapt. Simple as that.”

Tarrow let out a long breath through his nose, still frowning at the nearby cask. “You going to the ferment building?” Tarrow asked, to which Alric nodded. “You’ll need to suffer my company a bit more then.” He added with a grin.

“And terrible company that is,” Alric said with an equal grin as they left.

Alric had seen what he needed to see; he moved to where he thought he could contribute: the fermentation building.

Walking into the building, the first problem became obvious. Alric glanced around the many racks and shelves containing casks till he spotted her. She had seen him earlier and was approaching. Alric waited for her with Tarrow.

“Good to see you, Hesta. I hate to tell you this, but it's too warm.” Alric said, gesturing around.

“Good morning, Mister Alric.” Hesta said, somehow in a way that was both correcting his speech and greeting, “We’re going to have to make a little less. We can’t keep up.”

Alric wondered what that had to do with it being too warm. “We need to cut back?”

Hesta pointed to the nearest heating stone. “We are draining the station keeping the building from freezing. When the day is done, we are mostly moving casks.”

Alric asked her to explain further, and the problem quickly became apparent. She was trying to keep the temperatures consistent by moving the casks from warm to cold daily. They walked through the building; Alric put his hands on a few casks, trying to guess.

“Hesta, why don’t you move the stones around rather than the casks?” Alric asked, with a frown.

“Because the stones are in the breezier parts of the building, Mister Alric. If I were to move them, the cold would get too cold.” Hesta explained, pointing to one of the nearby closed shutters with a huff.

“Ahh.” Was all Alric said as he looked around. “Slowing is something we could do, but Hesta, you really shouldn’t be moving them more than once a week.”

Hesta let out a deep breath and threw a small glare at the nearest cask. “I’m working blind here, Mister Alric. Tarrow helps but…”

Alric could see why she was doing it; there was only so much cold going around, and she was trying to get the casks through it rather than cooling the entire building. The yeast, however, was getting different temperatures every time.

Alric took a deep breath. “They might be better off a little warmer if they stay that way. Warming and cooling them all the time won't really help. Show me which are furthest along.”

As they walked, Alric tried to remember the rhyme someone had taught him. Start at ten degrees, warm to eighteen, then drop it by two a day, leave at two degrees for a month.

They tasted those; they had a clove flavor. To him, this was an off flavor, but who knew, to the city at large.

Tarrow spoke up before he did, “That’s the thing that. That taste. I don’t know it, don’t know what to do with it.” Tarrow said, pointing to the sample cup.

“These need to sit colder and for longer.” Alric explained, pointing to it, “I know you're working blind, but I’d drink this happily, Hesta. I would say these needed to be colder for maybe a month.”

Hesta’s shoulders drooped; both she and Tarrow glanced at each other. Tarrow delivered the news first: “All of this batch has it.”

Alric just shrugged. “We may have started this too soon. You’ll get it, Hesta. It takes time to learn what you’re looking for. As I said, I’ll drink this.” He drained what was left of his cup.

Hesta blinked up at him, but Alric saw the light return to her eyes when she smiled.

Alric gave a small smile. “Now, Hesta, I have another question. Whose kids are those?”

Hesta and Tarrow both frowned at him; they glanced where he was indicating. A small group of eight kids was laughing. They even had a bench and some toys scattered nearby.

“The staff's Mister Alric. You said they could bring them?” She gave Alric a slightly suspicious look.

Alric tried to remember that. “It was just after the scare, Mister Alric.” Tarrow pointed out.

No memory surfaced of that; he decided it didn’t bother him anyway, and he would have allowed something like it. “Don’t really remember, but I don’t think it's an issue.”

Hesta smiled. “We did talk about it. The little goblins keep me busy most mornings.”

Alric chuckled; the memory finally surfaced. They had talked about it when he was thinking about what they would do when Mara’s baby arrived. He hadn’t considered how many of the brewery workers already needed it.

“Are you teaching them how to read?” Alric asked her with a wide grin. Hesta frowned and shook her head. “I’ll see to it that you get some paper. I don’t want you getting bored.”

Hesta opened her mouth, probably to defend herself, but nothing came out because Tarrow was laughing out too loudly at that comment.
