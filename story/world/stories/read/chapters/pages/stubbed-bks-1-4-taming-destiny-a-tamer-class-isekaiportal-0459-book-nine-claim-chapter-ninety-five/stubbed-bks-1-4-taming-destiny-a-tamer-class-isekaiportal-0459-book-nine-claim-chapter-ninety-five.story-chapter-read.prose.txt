After healing my companions, I quickly rise into the air again. Despite the explosion that killed a number of surrounding Tier twos and even more Tier ones, the battle rages on. However, without the Tier threes supporting the weaker beasts, the advantage is decidedly on my allies’ side.

I hesitate briefly, torn between two priorities. The Tier threes are still infected, even if they’ve been neutralised for now, but Valence is surrounded by enemies, the beasts having closed ranks completely during the fight with the crocodilian Tier three.

Valence, do you need me to come and help? I ask through the Alliance Bond.

We’re managing for now, but we’re running low on resources, he admits. We’ll soon consume the last of our mana and stamina potions – when that happens, we’ll start struggling.

We will aid you, Elarion answers, his tone cool and efficient as always. As long as Layton and Josiah’s forces act as defences for my mages and archers, we can concentrate on thinning the beast numbers and creating barriers to direct the beasts around you.

Our Warriors will protect your retinue, Layton confirms.

Alright, seems like that’s settled, I answer with a hint of amusement. I’ll clear the parasites out of the Tier threes and remove them entirely from the battlefield. Then I can come and help if needed.

The others acknowledge my words and then we all focus on our tasks.

Clearing the parasites out of the elk, bull, and mole is challenging – they seem to have been infected longer than the okapi and lion-like beasts had been, and the parasite only digs in deeper the longer it’s in place. But every time I surface from an indepth healing session, I see fewer and fewer beasts attacking our troops – my allies have it under control.

Once I’ve dealt with those three parasites, the next step is to deal with the larnatis male Bastet brought down. Unfortunately, since I’m already at capacity with unwilling Tier three Bonds, I need to get rid of at least one before working on him.

Speaking with each separately, I quickly discover that the mole-like Tier three just wants to go. She’s actually quite a shy creature, specialising in finding treasures underground – or stealing those of other beasts.

With the Bond in place, it’s easy for me to determine that she doesn’t have any thoughts of revenge, making it a safe Bond to release, as much as part of me doesn’t want to. Her skills would make her very useful as a combatant. However, even if I did have the capacity to hold everyone, I’d feel guilty forcing her when I can sense she only attacked us because of the parasite controlling her actions.

I direct her to move right to the other edge of the clearing first, though, just in case.

I’m going to release you now, I tell her, feeling her sudden surge of hope in response. If you ignore us from now on, we will do the same to you. However, If you would ever like to become part of us in truth, you would be welcome.

I understand, the mole-like beast replies, and I feel a curl of thoughtfulness flicker through her. She doesn’t say anything more, so I break the Bond, doing my best to ignore the now-familiar dull stab of pain that follows. As soon as she feels her freedom, the mole dives into the ground.

Reaching out with my Earth-Shaping, I sense her figure disappearing into the distance with impressive speed.

With that freeing up enough space, I quickly move to deal with the larnatis.

He puts up a hell of a fight in the Battle of Wills space, but I finally manage to force the Bond on him. Holding the Bond in place with sheer force of will, I quickly deal with his parasitic infection.

The moment I pull out of his body, I realise that the flow of battle has changed. It takes me a moment to process exactly how.

No longer are the still-infected beasts charging my allies’ forces without care; now, they seem to be hell-bent on making it back to the underground caverns.

Stop them! I call in both Alliance and Bond networks while leaping into the air and soaring quickly over the heads of the beasts. Clearly the Tier four has realised it’s lost its ‘generals’ and so now is calling for a retreat. The issue is that if we don’t deal with them out here, we’ll most likely have to face them again in far more confined conditions.

Landing on the hill just above the cavern entrance, I activate my armour’s shield at the same time as resummoning my wings as stone ones to keep me safe. Then, closing my eyes, I sink as much mana as I can into the earth, pulling up a thick wall to cover the entrance to the cavern.

The effort exhausts me, and I almost lose the bull-like Tier three’s Bond as my will briefly weakens at the feeling of an empty mana-pool. I manage to hold onto it by the skin of my teeth.

Damn it. I’d better release some Bonds. I’m more reluctant to release the bull or elk – their bloodlust makes them more likely to turn on us – but if I let one of the Bonds slip it will be worse than if I release them willingly. And we still have to fight the Tier four.

The moment I can, I retransform my wings back into the best version for flying and take to the air again. From above, I see how Valence’s group has managed to work its way forwards and is now guarding the blocked entrance to the cavern while the others are targeting the beasts massing around them.

Nodding in satisfaction at their actions, I decide they’ve got that bit well in hand.

I feel a sudden swoop in my stomach as I see that the larnatis male is no longer tied up – Artio has obviously set him free. Yet he’s not trying to escape or attack anyone either. He’s not even fighting the Bond as hard as he did initially. The reason for all this is very obvious – he’s currently sidling up to Bastet, his intentions easy to read in the Bond.

Well, hello, I hear him project seductively to Bastet as soon as he gets close enough, his plumed tail high in the air, his ears pricked in interest.

Bastet isn’t visibly impressed, giving him a side-eye and then focusing her attention back on the infected beasts she’s slaughtering. However, our Bond is deep enough that I can feel it’s largely a front she’s putting up.

I see him sidle closer, sniffing at her tail. She whirls around, clobbers him with a taloned paw, then snarls in his face. A moment later, she twists back and leaps into the beasts, attacking them with obvious aggression.

The male doesn’t seem to be disheartened, though. If anything, the way he lifts his tail indicates he’s even more interested. Perhaps larnatis males appreciate powerful females. Well, good luck to him!

Rolling my eyes in amusement, I turn my attention back to the other two Tier threes.

With the larnatis clearly intending on sticking around for Bastet – at least for now – I’ll keep the Bond on him. The other two are another question.

Despite his bloodlust, the elk is willing to sincerely promise mutual non-interference and disappears into the forest without any last-minute betrayal. The bull is another question – he is stubborn and refuses to believe what I’m telling him, despite the copious evidence around.

I decide to maintain his Bond for now – I can’t be sure that he won’t just turn around and attack us if I let him go. With two Tier three Bonds gone, hopefully holding onto him won’t prevent me from fighting at my best with the Tier four.

As promised, I go and help my allies now I’ve rationalised my Tier three Bonds.

By this time, the mass of beasts has been significantly reduced, and I reduce it more by throwing fireballs and then shooting poisoned arrows when I run out of mana. Perhaps realising the tactic isn’t working, the Tier four sends out new instructions. No more are the beasts attempting to enter the cave system; now they are once more selling their lives dearly, fighting against my companions and allies claw and fang.

But their numbers are much smaller – they don’t have the tactics we do, and few have healing abilities that work on each other. We’ve even managed to convert two of the Tier threes to our side – the larnatis has obviously decided that slaughtering its once-allies is the best way to impress Bastet, and I order the bull to help. The beasts don’t stand a chance.

I do feel sorry for them – like the Tier threes they haven’t chosen to attack us; it’s the parasite controlling them that is throwing them at us. Yet for all my pity, I’m not going to try to spare them. I can’t Bind and heal them all, and trying to capture them without harm to them risks wasting resources my people need, and puts us in danger. All I can do for the beasts is to try to kill them as painlessly as I can – and then ensure that this doesn’t happen again.

And at the same time, their deaths are not completely wasted. I see many fighters stained in black ichor and stinking like a sewer, having levelled up at least once during the battle. From the amount of it coating some people, I suspect they levelled up twice or more. And the moment the last beast falls, I will call for everyone with butcher skills – or Skills – to move in to process the carcases. Our people will eat well tonight.

However, the battle is not yet finished. Even though the remnants of the beast’s army are of little threat, the Tier four lives.

Landing on the ground near the back lines, I don’t even need to signal for the other three lords there to come.

“We need to deal with the Tier four,” Elarion tells me briskly the moment he enters vocal range.

“I know,” I agree. “The question is exactly who should enter the caverns.”

“I’m not sure I would be a good choice,” Josiah admits, joining the conversation. “Many of my Skills are far less effective underground than out in the open.”

“Then you should remain out here to help deal with the remaining beasts,” I agree, then look back at Elarion. “What about you – how are your Skills underground?”

He looks uncharacteristically uncertain, glancing at the massive tree he grew of wood and ice intermingled.

“Some of my Skills are effective, however I am reluctant to leave the Forestheart alone until the battle is over. I cannot grow another one for a while.”

“That’s fine,” I tell him, “We need people to control and guard the exit – for all we know, the Tier four has powerful reinforcements somewhere else who might attack our backs.” I glance at Layton. “I know that you’re still powerful underground.” He shrugs in response. “This is what we’ll do. I’ll take Layton and Valence with me, along with several of my companions. Meanwhile, Josiah, you and Elarion guard our exit with the majority of our forces.” They nod in acknowledgment.

“Has anyone heard from the Earth Mages sent to close any other exits?” Layton asks with a frown furrowing his face.

I glance around at the others, seeing shaking heads.

“No,” I admit. “I’ll send a few of my companions to see if they can make contact.” True to my word, I reach out to Sirocco, Noir, and Ivor and ask them to fly and try to find the Earth Mages and scouts. Hopefully they’ll actually manage to spot the little parties – they were supposed to do their best to avoid detection so it’s not guaranteed that they’ll be visible.

“In the absence of contact, we’ll have to assume that they haven’t been successful and proceed with caution,” I decide. “I haven’t sensed the Tier four’s location yet, but I’ll do my best to find it as we enter the system itself. Any questions or concerns?”

Apparently not.

“Alright, then Layton, let’s go and collect Valence so we can finish this.”
