Chapter II.XXII (2.22)

She had no idea how long it had taken her to burrow into the sea monster and kill it from the inside. An hour at least. Half a day at most. It had all passed in a blur. But, regardless, it had been more than enough time for her ship to flee and leave her for dead. She supposed it was only fair. Regardless of if they knew what she had planned, she had been gambling with their lives asking them to take the direct route. She wasn’t going to begrudge them leaving her behind. Not when she knew firsthand how devastating the leviathan could be.

Mitsuko used her ice swords to create a makeshift ladder up the side of the monster’s corpse. The smaller eyes acted as decent footholds as she climbed her way up. Twice, she fell. But Mend made that risk obsolete as she simply set herself back in place.

It didn’t take long until Mitsuko crested the beast’s girth and stood on top of its head. While it stank marginally less outside of her burrow of flesh, the tradeoff was that it was more slippery on the leathery skin. She stabbed a sword straight down and sat with an arm wrapped around it to stabilize her.

She shifted as there was a bulge under her. Then repositioned. A glance revealed she had sat down on one of the many exposed eyes. Gross.

Time passed and she simply took in the sea as the corpse drifted on the currents. She Mended her body to keep weariness at bay. She wasn’t keen on dozing off and falling into the sea.

“You actually survived, remarkable.”

Mitsuko glanced over her shoulder. Several birds had accumulated on the beast’s corpse and were hard at work pecking out the many eyes and gobbling down the slop within. One bird in particular hopped over to her, beak open as it spoke.

“Juniper, you found me,” Mitsuko remarked. “I suppose you must have noticed the loop never ended.”

“You are quite resilient,” Juniper commented. “As the minutes passed I admit I expected your life to end down in the depths. I flew over several times in hopes of spotting you, but saw nothing.”

“It took a while to die.”

“I see that. You know, not many have the courage to face down a leviathan. Let alone the strength to kill one.”

Mitsuko glanced at the monster beneath her. A massive whale with a thousand eyes. “You know, before I came to the Prismatic Archipelago, I always imagined leviathans looking different.”

“Leviathans all appear utterly unique. No two look the same. This is known as an ocular leviathan. For obvious reasons.”

“Hm. Interesting. Sterling mentioned that the last time I fought it.” Mitsuko leaned against her sword, the cold rubbed against her cheek, a cool contrast to the sun beating down overhead.

“Do you have a plan from here? Or do you wish to continue drifting along aimlessly for days on end?”

“Drifting seems fun,” Mitsuko commented. “I guess I could use a break.”

They said nothing for a while, Mitsuko continued to stare out at the placid sea. She thought about the different islands far on the horizon.

“What makes Porcelain Island so cold?” she asked suddenly.

“Is that a question? Or are you just speaking out loud?” Juniper asked.

“One of those special questions to tap into all your hoarded information.”

Juniper tapped a talon on the sea monster’s flesh as she seemed to consider the question. “The treasure at the center of the island. It emits a unique magical signature that has frozen the surrounding area. While powerful, it cannot match the sun itself in overwhelming force. It usually takes every night to heal the sun’s damage.”

“You speak of the artifact as if it has a mind,” Mitsuko observed.

“A debatable topic. It’s not quite the same as an artifact as you think of. Though it is tangentially related. Suffice it to say, there are some scholars who believe the island’s heart has a will of its own. ”

“And your opinion?” Mitsuko pressed.

“I’m… uncertain. I’ve never explored the island in depth. There is something truly special about this specific artifact, though if it is life and will, remains a mystery to me.”

“Where did it come from?”

“That would cost you another question,” Juniper said. “That is, if I had any information. The origin, likewise, remains a mystery to me. Better to direct that question at a more knowledgeable sage than I.”

Mitsuko leaned against her sword. The sage wasn’t particularly helpful. She missed Sterling. Though Mitsuko supposed that information likely wouldn’t help her all that much anyway.

“Do you know who the guardian is and his abilities?” Mitsuko asked. If she was going to expend a few questions, might as well do it now while there wasn’t anything else to distract her.

“His?”

“Oh, he spoke to me in the last loop while I was down in the ice tunnels.”

“An interesting hint to the guardian’s identity. However, without more information, I cannot help you. I am as ignorant to who it is as you are.”

“He seemed pretty dismissive of my existence, if that’s any help. Dismissive of all people, to be honest.”

Juniper clacked her beak in what might be a laugh. “That sadly does not help. Many sculpts throughout history have a deep dislike for humanity and other species.”

Mitsuko sat there and listened to the other gulls squawk while they fought one another for the remaining eyes. She was trying to think of a question or a way out of her current circumstances, but the shrill cries of the birds kept rattling her brain. In a brief moment of irritation, Mitsuko considered casting Deteriorate and rotting all the eyeballs on the leviathan. But, not only did she doubt that would deter the gulls, she also suspected it would result in a far smellier situation.

“Could you go fetch Holly for me?” Mitsuko asked the bird.

“How should I communicate with her?”

“Write a message?”

“I am not familiar with the modern scripts your generation uses,” Juniper said, dashing Mitsuko’s idea. But then she opened her wings and flapped them once, as if testing them. “However, I will go and see what I can do. Several days on a rotting leviathan does neither of us much good. We can hardly expect to make progress like this.”

With those final words, Juniper took flight. Mitsuko noted that the sage lacked the supreme grace of other gulls. To her, flight seemed to be a means to the end, rather than a passion or a part of who she was.

“Maybe I’m looking too far into it,” Mitsuko muttered to herself. “I doubt gulls think much of flying or are particularly passionate about the subject.”

Hours passed. The leviathan continued to drift along with a slight sea current. For the first time in a long time, Mitsuk felt… bored. She took to studying the birds, since there was nothing else to readily occupy her time. Back on Mauve Island, Fuku had commented that Juniper’s species was not native to the island. The coloring was likely a dead giveaway of that fact. But these gulls lacked the distinct colors prevalent on any of the islands nearby. They just looked drab and ordinary. Mitsuko supposed that must mean they came over with chartered ships. If that could happen with birds, did that mean other species also snuck out onto these islands? Were there mundane mice? Or invisible creatures? Humans existed, the same color scheme as always, on Mauve. And Fuku had also mentioned going off to find a succubus in the city. How many invasive species existed out on this random corner of the planet?

“Ho, there!” a voice called from below, shattering Mitsuko’s musings. “My eyes deceive me? Or is there someone alive up there?

She scrambled onto her feet and nearly slipped on the slimy skin as she crossed over to the edge of the leviathan.

An old man with a long white beard on a yellow skiff stared up at her, his hand shielded the sun over his eyes. One hand gripped his tall walking stick, an odd accessory on a boat.

“Who are you?” Mitsuko called down to the man. “What are you doing out here?”

“Oh! Someone truly slayed a leviathan and lived to tell the tale? Fascinating!” The old man beamed up at her. “My name is Zeaqu. I am but a lowly wizard of Amber Island sent to sea in an effort to better understand the barrier that surrounds us.”

“Um. Nice to meet you.” A pause “Any leads?” Mitsuko asked, mostly because she thought that would be the question expected from her.

“It’s utterly unique!” The man pulled his beard and beamed up at her, a massive smile split between his mustache and beard. “In all my studies, I have never come across anything quite like it! The way it blends both space and time, an absolute marvel of a creation. Oh, how I long to meet with the creator. Though I doubt any mortal is capable of such a feat. It would require all the mortal blood in a modest sized nation to power! And I saw no signs of deterioration either. An absolute enigma of a design!”

“You’re not concerned about it?”

The wizard took a step back, as if struck. “Concerned? Do you not comprehend the beauty of our situation? How could I allow concern to mar this historical blessing! I have no time nor space in my heart for it!”

Mitsuko laughed at the audacity of the man. She decided she liked the wizard.

“Hey, do you have room on your skiff for another person? I’ve always wanted to visit Amber Island.”

Zeaqu bobbed his head. “Normally you need an invitation to set foot on our home. However, due to our rather auspicious meeting, I believe I can extend a unique invitation. I am certain more than a few of my peers will be interested in hearing how you bested a leviathan. It looks as though you used a spell to bore a hole in the beast. Did it sink your ship while you fought?”

“No. They all fled. It took a few hours to take it down. Probably best that they got out of the way. I assume they all think me dead now.” Mitsuko flicked her wrist to create a new sword. Planting it in the dead leviathan, she cast Deteriorate to soften the skin with rot and slid down the beast. Her sword tore through the softened skin, allowing her a smooth descent.

The wizard’s skiff extended out and a spatial barrier caught her feet. She stepped onto the wooden skiff and felt the boat sway under the new addition of weight.

The skiff was sparsely furnished. A few barrels and crates with a tarp over them formed a sleeping nook that was stuffed with plush purple and yellow pillows and blankets.

“They left you for dead?” Zeaqu asked. “Rather unsupportive. Did they not have faith in your capabilities? You seem to have left the scuffle unscathed so you are doubtlessly no stranger to battle.”

“They just don’t know me well.” As Mitsuko said the words, she felt a pang in her heart. It was true. How well did Holly truly know her now? It had only been a few months trapped in the timeloops, but already Mitsuko felt she’d changed into someone unrecognizable. The Mitsuko before the Prismatic Spiral would never have taken on a risk as stupid as burrowing into a leviathan like a parasite. Let alone survived the attempt.

Zeaqu pulled a lever on his skiff and it puttered around the leviathan corpse and started off in the direction of a distant yellow cloud on the horizon.

“While we travel, I don’t suppose you have any interest in card games? I recently acquired a deck imported from mainland Tross.”

Two chairs flipped out of the deck followed by a table with a deck of cards.

Cards from Tross. It took only a glance for her to confirm her suspicions. She chuckled softly at the sight. “You know, some old friends of mine actually designed this game.”

“Truly? So I take it you’re familiar with the rules?”

“Yeah. I’ll play, but I call dibs on the summoner deck.”
