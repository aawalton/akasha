Far northwest of Vitian, a vast structure, its size rivaling that of a small city, could be found. Four towering spires, each representing a different aspect of magic, rose from its center. Formed from smooth, white marble-like stone, each stood rigid and immaculate, connected by a network of wide, elevated walkways. Low walls encircled the entire complex, with evenly spaced towers lining the perimeter.

A magical barrier enveloped the structure, strong enough to withstand several direct blows from Ninth-Tier mages. It was a fortress in every sense of the word. One that rivaled even the kingdom’s capital.

It was a place of renown. A place of prestige. A place of research. For any aspiring mage, there were only two destinations that mattered.

The first was Valeborn Academy in Annesheim. The second—was the Mage Association.

In the southeastern quadrant stood the Arcanum Spire, a tower dedicated to theory and understanding. Within it, a group of mages had gathered around a long table, each holding an identical set of notes. Among them were senior mages, junior assistants, and academic researchers.

The discussion had been ongoing for nearly twenty minutes.

Some proposed interpretations. Others were dismissing them outright. Much of the time was spent conversing, writing potential outcomes versus actual practical testing.

“Are we being serious right now?” one senior mage said. “Think about where these notes came from. A complete nobody. A woman who isn’t even registered with the association.”

“Does that really matter?” a woman shot back.

“What did you say?”

She sighed. “Just because someone isn’t registered with us doesn’t mean we should dismiss them. Deena was a transfer from Aat’mar. She wasn’t registered here either, and she still provided valuable insight to solving the residual mana instability problem in collapsed constructs.”

The man rolled his eyes. “Yes, but she was recognized in Aat’mar. She had credentials. Recommendations from the professors there.” He raised the notes in his hand, waving them slightly. “The woman who wrote these? Not registered anywhere.”

“Are you sure about that?” another mage interjected. “According to the report, she may be dark elven. There’s a chance she’s from the Elven Forest.”

He paused.

“In fact… I’d say that’s very likely.”

“Are you serious?” the first man scoffed. “Elven magic is fundamentally different from ours. It’s nature-based. Mystical in nature. They don’t follow Paths the way we do. Their spell structures don’t even align with Vitian systems or practices.”

“All the more reason to examine it,” the other replied. “A different perspective could offer insights we’ve overlooked.”

The door to the room clicked open. “Alright. That’s enough.”

Everyone fell silent once they heard his voice. The words came from a man in his early thirties.

Mages in the association were all distinguished by their robes: juniors in gray, seniors in purple, and assistants in blue.

Unlike the others, he wore black robes—the mark of a representative mage. Each tower maintained three such individuals, tasked with overseeing research, managing personnel, and guiding the direction of study within their domain.

“Stop arguing over whether the theory is legitimate,” he said. “Discussion alone won’t get us anywhere. Do your jobs as mages and verify the claim.”

“Yes, Sir Lucian,” the room echoed.

Lucian stood with a straightened posture, presence small, but commanding once acknowledged. His dark hair was neatly kept, falling just past his ears. His sharp features gave him a naturally stern expression, combined with the cool and steady look of his eyes. A thin, silver clasp was fastened to his robe at the collar, bearing the Arcanum Spire insignia.

He turned to face the man in purple robes who had been arguing.

“And Drake,” he added. “This woman may not be registered with us, but Hugh confirmed she follows the Path of Ruin and Tempests. He verified it personally. That alone means she has a working understanding of magic.”

His eyes narrowed slightly. “For now, set aside your assumptions. Test the notes. Then report the results.”

“Yes… Sir Lucian.”

“Good. There are other materials Hugh sent over that I’ll also need to review myself. I’ll be in my office in the meantime. Once you’ve compiled your report, bring it to me.”

“Yes, Sir Lucian,” the room echoed.

With that, he turned and left. Quietly, the murmur of debate was already rising again behind him.

Out in the hall, Lucian exhaled quietly as the arguments resumed.

“When will they ever learn…” he muttered.

He walked along the wide, white corridors. A few junior apprentices passed by, offering quick bows as he went. Lucian acknowledged them with a slight nod, his thoughts already elsewhere.

Before long, he reached his office. But then he noticed someone was already inside.

A man sat behind his desk, feet propped up on the table, reading a book resting lazily in his hands.

“Voss?” Lucian said.

The man tilted his head slightly, lowering the book just enough to glance over its edge.

He looked to be around the same age as Lucian, though the resemblance ended there. His dark hair was loose and unkempt, falling messily around his face, and his posture lacked any sense of discipline. A faint smirk lingered on his lips, as if he found amusement in everything around him. Even his black robes were worn casually, the collar slightly undone, the sleeves pushed back just enough to suggest a complete disregard for formality.

“Oh, hey,” Voss said. “Didn’t see you there.”

Lucian’s gaze flattened. “This is my office.”

“Oh, don’t be like that,” Voss replied, waving a hand dismissively. “We’re all part of the association. What’s yours is mine. And what’s mine is mine.”

“Don’t you mean what’s yours is also mine?”

“Don’t be greedy now.”

Lucian rolled his eyes.

Voss was one of the other representative mages of the Arcanum Spire. Where Lucian focused on structure: spell formulas, refinement, and theoretical consistency—Voss specialized in application. New casting methods, chantless techniques, and experimental spell matrices.

“What are you doing here?” Lucian asked, stepping inside and closing the door behind him.

“Waiting for you,” Voss said. He snapped the book shut—The Chantless Arts, Vol. 6—and flicked his finger. The book shot through the air and slotted neatly back onto a shelf along the wall.

Lucian walked around the desk, lifted a hand, and twisted his wrist.

The chair—along with Voss—tilted sharply to the side, dumping him onto the floor.

Lucian took his seat without pause.

Voss meanwhile, landed cleanly on his feet.

Rather than reacting, he stepped forward with renewed interest, planting both hands on the desk and leaning in.

“I heard you got an interesting submission.”

Lucian raised a brow. “Word spreads quickly.” His expression dulled. “Who leaked it?”

“Trish.”

Lucian clicked his tongue. “She can’t keep quiet for more than a minute…”

Voss leaned back, chuckling. “Go easy on her. She’s just excited to be here.”

“I’ll worry about my own mages, thank you.”

“Suit yourself,” Voss replied with a shrug. “But go on. What’s the news?”

Lucian hesitated, pressing his lips to the side.

“…A mage from Ronswick,” Lucian said at last. “A woman Hugh personally recommended. She submitted a set of theory notes.”

“Ronswick, huh?” Voss echoed. “What kind of theory?”

“Spell compression.”

“Compression?”

Lucian nodded. “A focused application. She outlines a method to compress the Tier-2 spell Guiding Mote into a Tier-1 framework.”

Voss’s eyes widened. “You’re serious?”

“I am.”

He leaned forward slightly, the earlier laziness gone. “Spell compression theory is barely touched anymore. Not after variable-shifting became the standard. Most researchers abandoned it entirely.”

“I’m aware,” Lucian said. “Which is exactly why it caught my attention. That—and Hugh was unusually insistent that we review it.”

He glanced down, opening a drawer and retrieving a single sheet of paper.

“What’s that?”

Lucian hummed, hesitant to answer. “It’s… another matter. From the same person. However, it’s a bit extreme. I’d rather wait for the compression theory report before I acknowledge this one.”

Before Voss could press further, a knock sounded at the door.

Both men turned.

“Come in,” Lucian said.

The door opened, and a young mage in gray robes stepped inside.

“Rowan?” Lucian said. “What is it? Are they arguing again?”

Rowan shook his head quickly. “N-no, sir.”

“Then why are you here? You should be assisting with verification and preparing the report.”

“About that, sir…” Rowan said, struggling to contain himself. “Elin told me to rush over here and inform you—the theory works.”

Lucian’s expression sharpened. “What? So quickly?”

He nodded. “Yes, Sir Lucian. Once everyone settled down, they began reconstructing the spell as instructed. There were some initial difficulties, but Elin managed to compress the structure and cast it within a Tier-1 framework.” He swallowed. “After him, Eleanor succeeded. Then… Drake.”

Lucian’s brows knit. “The efficiency—surely the notes exaggerated—”

“The efficiency was true as stated,” Rowan said impatiently, cutting him off. “Elin and Eleanor both reached roughly ninety percent conversion. Drake achieved ninety-two.”

Silence.

“…On their first attempts?” Lucian asked.

“Yes, sir.”

Voss let out a short laugh. “Drake? That stubborn fool managed it on his first try?”

He turned toward Lucian—but the man was already moving. Without a word, Lucian strode past them and out of the office.

“Huh? Hey!” Voss called, stepping after him. Rowan followed, both of them watching as Lucian moved quickly down the corridor.

However, it wasn’t the direction they expected.

“That’s… not the way back to the experiment halls,” Rowan murmured.

Voss scratched the back of his head. “Where in the blazes is he going to be that in a rush?”

Already out of sight, Lucian was speeding up the stairs. In his hands was the same paper he had pulled out of his drawer. The additional note Hugh had sent over.

An ingredient list to make a very specific concoction. A supposed ‘cure.’ He assumed it was rubbish. But he dared not throw it away—not until that mage’s other work was verified.

But now… three mages had successfully compressed a Tier-2 spell into a Tier-1 framework. A feat that had never been achieved before. Not with such a level of efficiency. Rowan was one of his star pupils; he was not the type to lie. Of course, none of that meant the cure would be legitimate, too. However, the theory itself earned it a try. Because if the absurd theory were real—

Then the rest of her claims might be as well.

Lucian pushed open the doors to the Tower Head’s office, located on the second-highest level of the spire, just beneath its apex.

Inside, an elderly man with a long white beard and gray hair tied neatly into a bun looked up from his papers. His eyes creased as he set his pen aside. “Lucian? What is the meaning of this? Why are you—”

“Tower Head Thorne,” Lucian cut in, his tone controlled but urgent. “I have a document that needs to be delivered to Archmage Ballamus immediately.”

Thorne’s brows furrowed. “What?” He leaned back slightly. “What document?”

Lucian straightened, his gaze sharpening. “I believe… a mage from the Ronswick branch may have discovered a cure for the nether-grasping disease.”

Silence followed his words.

“On what grounds do you make such a claim?” Thorne asked, his voice turning firm. “For years, the finest physicians, Blooming mages, and even Elven shamans have failed to treat it.”

Lucian inclined his head slightly. “I’m not claiming it will cure the disease,” he said. “But I believe it is worth testing.”

“And why?”

“Earlier today, we received a submission tied to a registration request from that same mage.”

“Yes,” Thorne said. “The one from Ronswick. I’m aware.”

Lucian nodded. “That mage also provided a set of theoretical notes on spell compression. A method of restructuring a spell matrix—compressing a Tier-2 construct into a Tier-1 framework.”

“And?”

Lucian didn’t hesitate. “In under five minutes, three of my mages successfully replicated it,” he said. “Two achieved a ninety percent conversion rate. Drake reached ninety-two.”

Thorne’s eyes widened, his composure slipping for a brief moment.

“That… level of conversion?” he murmured. “Our most advanced compression models barely reach thirty percent—and even then, the casting time nearly doubles.”

“I was skeptical at first, too. But on my first read-through, the logic seemed sound. With three mages being actually able to test the theory, the rest of the report from Hugh seems to hold much more merit. That’s why I believe this,” Lucian said, raising the sheet in his hand, “may hold some truth as well.”

Thorne’s gaze shifted to the paper. “Is that…?”

“It was provided alongside the theory,” Lucian continued. “A complete ingredient list with instructions. No ambiguity. Clear, with no caveats. Just… a direct guarantee that it will work.”

“What is this mage’s name? And what do they want in return?”

“Her name is Veronica Everwells,” Lucian said. “As for what she wants—she wants to be registered as an association mage. And that we also provide protection for a creature in their possession.”

“A creature?”

Lucian hesitated for only a fraction of a second. “A gemling terrorbeast.”

Thorne froze for a heartbeat. Then he leaned back heavily into his chair. “…Nine hells,” he muttered. “A gemling terrorbeast… You are sure about this?”

“Yes. Hugh and Tower Master Rarran assured me that they were speaking truthfully. Whether or not the mage was telling the truth, they couldn’t confirm. But they were willing to stake any reputation they had for it, due to the nature of her notes. Apparently, this mage may have many more theories to share.”

Thorne exhaled slowly, his expression tightening as he leaned forward again. “How does someone like that even exist…” For a moment, he said nothing. Then his gaze hardened, staring back at Lucian’s eyes. “You may proceed,” he said. “Go to the upper spire and meet with Archmage Ballamus.”

Lucian nodded.

“But,” Thorne added sharply, “you will not guarantee anything. Do you understand?”

Lucian stilled, staring at him.

“Do not promise a cure. Do not imply certainty. Not in front of him.”

“…Yes, sir,” Lucian said.

“Good. Then go.”

Lucian turned and left without another word, already moving toward the stairwell that led higher into the tower.

The door shut behind him.

Thorne leaned back in his chair, letting out a long breath.

“An unknown mage…” he murmured. “A theory that surpasses anything we have… and a gemling terrorbeast.”

He turned slightly, gazing out through the tower window at the sprawling grounds below, where mages moved through the commons in earnest.

He'd been hearing rumors of the Ronswick branch. Rarely did they ever reach out. But now... how convenient for something akin to a miracle to fall right into their laps. A crafted cure for Ballamus' sister wouldn't just mean the mage would gain an archmage's favor or protection. No—it meant gaining the protection of the entire spire.

“Veronica Everwells…” he said quietly. “Who... exactly are you? To appear out of nowhere with a theory like this… and to travel with a creature of legend…”

His voice lowered further.

“I can’t tell if this is an omen… or the beginning of something far greater.”
