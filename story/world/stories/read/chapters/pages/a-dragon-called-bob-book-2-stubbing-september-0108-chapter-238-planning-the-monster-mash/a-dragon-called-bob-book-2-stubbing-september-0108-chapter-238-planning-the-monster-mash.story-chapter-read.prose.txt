I had refined my technique for introducing myself to any new armies that appeared. If they didn’t immediately hail the Champion of Bulb, as evidenced by the constant rain of golden sparks around me, they were bad guys. I kept the portal-thudding-impact method. I liked the vibe, and it kept Vanity appeased.

My army of undead was now spread out in a vast ring around the hill where I’d made my camp. This kind of recruitment was hardly moral, but I wasn’t worried. The battle would be a cakewalk. I had the orlic, dwarves, and my human forces prepared to deploy via portal, as well as just shy of five thousand bunnyborgs and BB-209s.

My toothy grin glinted in the morning light as I surveyed the site of tomorrow's battlefield. A stream ran along the edge of a thick forest on the eastern flank; on the west side, two miles away from it, a broad river created the other environmental constraint that dictated where we would fight.

Word travelled fast, unfortunately, and the various nobles were now grouping up miles away and arriving en masse, so the easy pickings were long gone. I was confident that I’d swung the pendulum heavily in favour of the good guys, though.

“It isn’t like modern warfare back home,” Duke Armand Smith said coolly. The man was sitting on a simple three-legged stool in his unadorned armour. It was still respectably shiny, and having seen the man in action at the last battle, I knew to mind my manners with him.

“It’s like they don’t want to win.”

“They want to win the right way, Bob. There is a code of honour. The glory to be gained… it is only as great as the challenge. They think you’ve made a laughingstock of them.”

While the soldiers of the other light-side players were ecstatic to see a squadron of dragons orbiting overhead, their commanders were not so pleased. I’d received six missives of disapproval, over a dozen snotty emissaries that I had carefully refrained from eating, and been snubbed by almost all the nobles on my side, except for the Outremonde Armand.

“You see those tents and banners?” he asked.

My supposed allies' camps were, on the whole, neatly arranged. Blocks of tents for cover, hundreds of cook fires per camp, and the mobile glints of steelshod men and women going about their business. They were set up off to one side, halfway between my hill and the town. Keeping their distance from the undead as much as possible, they moved amongst themselves, but almost never came to see me.

A few were less carefully organised. The wilder lords from the east and their rough and tumble soldiers didn’t bother with disciplined tent placement, and they wore more fur than metal, making them blend in with the mud a lot more effectively.

Our opposition had camped several miles to the south and hastily erected anti-air defences. They’d been expecting me to be here, and had come loaded for dragon. The Armaments Guild had supplied them with copious ballistae, lighter bolt throwers, and more of those damn golems I’d run into at Baginton.

Mages were also thick on the ground. Pete had gotten a bit ahead of himself yesterday and tried to steamroll them in the night. He was currently recovering after some magical healing had dealt with the worst of the damage. Still, he’d looked like a badly burned draconic pincushion when he flew back into camp, mewling like an injured kitten.

“I see them.”

“It’s all about the pomp. For a bunch of those young idiots, tomorrow is going to be the best day of their lives. For a lot of them, it will be their last day as well. But this battle will be recorded in the annals. The Emperor himself will be on hand to bear witness.” He sipped from a cup of tea, pinky finger outstretched.

“It’s stupid, Armand. Why risk our own people? The surprise of the rest of my troops, plus, you know, bloody dragons, means we’re all set. We could take them right now.”

“It’s not about who wins. The Umbrati and the Illuminants have always been at odds, always simmering just below an outbreak of serious violence. This is just a flare-up of an ancient feud. Bob… neither side will risk harming the Empire. Your planned deployment behind their lines, which I approve of, is also seen as cheating. Your condition has made it so you never really had to learn how to fit in with what passes for human culture on this world.”

“Mammals,” I growled.

“Yes. You’re bigger than the last time I saw you.” Another precise sip of tea. I wished I knew what the hell this dangerous man was thinking.

“I got a few more evolutions since the last time I saw you.”

“It’s impressive. What are your goals for tomorrow?”

“I need Hateskale’s head.”

“It’s an odd hobby. I myself collect antique astrolabes.”

“To bring peace between the Empire and the Skuldrak, the furries from the Brokebone Isles. They wanted the heads of the men in charge of the raiders who kidnapped their girls.”

“Reasonable enough. It wasn’t only Hateskale, you know? A number of lords were rumoured to be involved in that trade.”

“They wanted three of them. Two are sorted or will be soon, and one is still swanning around in that pile of shit over there.” My snout swung in the direction of the Umbrati encampment.

“Dangerous. Of the biggest players in that sick business, some ought to be untouchable. The head of the small council, for instan—” He snorted tea as I produced Dimpleworn’s head from storage for a moment.

“Way ahead of you, buddy.”

“I had been wondering where the bulk of their forces were.”

“Down there.” I nodded at the untidy grey blob that ringed my temporary roost. “I turned them, or what was left of them after the orlics were finished.”

“A thrifty dragon.” Armand nodded thoughtfully.

“There is no other sort,” I chuckled. “Which again is why we should be putting them down today. A third of their troops won’t arrive until this evening. We could take them out piecemeal.”

“I’ve had the argument before. The natives won’t go for it. You have to work within the system as it stands. Trying to overturn the status quo is a good way to get dead on Helstat.”

He stood, his teacup vanishing into storage and pointed a hand out at our allies.

“They aren’t your friends, Bob.”

“No shit. But they’re on my side, and there’s no point in more of them dying than they have to.”

“Have to. That’s the right phrase. You’ll figure it out. I’ll see you at the talks tomorrow morning?” He turned blank eyes up at my face.

“Sure.”

“Good day, Bob.”

He stalked away, each footstep mechanical in its uncanny precision. No movement was wasted in anything he did. Armand terrified me on a fundamental level. Puny mammal though he might have been, I would not be happy to end up on his shit list.

Even the undead understood the threat the man represented, parting like the Red Sea as he approached and waiting as he passed through them.

I stretched in the sunlight and ruminated. I ruminated hard. Gah, everything was tainted by the mammals! Some of them were fine. Esme was perfect, for instance. But so much of my life was complicated by their silly rules.

If I were subject to said rules, I might take them more seriously, but I wasn’t. As I thunk my deepest thinking thoughts, I turned my gaze to the enemy. Vulnerable. Especially from above, despite their attempts to counter us. Acidic fire from half a mile up wouldn’t care how long it took to fall.

Was indiscriminate bombing a bad thing? Jace would certainly disagree with the idea. He was still sulking because I called him off after a bunch of the “good guys” turned up and started complaining.

Some of my minions considered war crimes to be de rigeur. The orlics and the dwarves wouldn’t even blink at the thought of killing a dude when he was asleep, or wrapped in the arms of a loving partner. They were no less civilised, as a species, compared to humans, and they had an attitude to warfare that felt far more natural to my draconic instincts.

Kill ‘em all and fuck their feelings didn’t sound like a karmically positive attitude. However, it was undeniably effective. I had… about twenty thousand undead milling about doing bugger all that I’d been planning to use as arrow fodder in the morning, but perhaps they could be useful in advance?

Taking flight from a high place is a big advantage. I strongly recommend that if you think you can fly, you try it from the ground the first time, though. A few wingbeats sent me down to land at the edge of the sea of zombies. I foolishly flicked out my tongue to taste the air; it was the force of habit, and the reek of death filled my mouth.

“Garn?” I bellowed. As the boss, I wasn’t going to waste time sorting through them to find the one I was looking for.

A minute later, the former spearman staggered to a stop in front of me.

“What the hell happened to you?”

“You haven’t fed us.” He was missing the flesh on his arms, down to the bone in places.

“You’ll all be off to… heaven soon? Wherever the gods think you ought to go. Or maybe reincarnation, if that’s on the cards for you. I need a quarter of you for a special mission.”

“I volunteer!” all of them chorused at the same time. Wincing, I glanced across at my allies, and sure enough, a lot of heads had spun round to look at us.

“Keep it down! How many of you can dance?”

“Dance?” “I learned how to skank a while back.” “Moshpits for the win!”

Stupid zombies.

“I’ll get you some music and a singer. I need to know if you can jam and sing all night long, deny the fuckers sleep so they’re all cranky and tired in the morning.”

“Why not send us all?” asked Garn, scratching a chin that was already green.

“They might sally and try to take you out. I don’t really want to lose you before the fight. And yes! Once the battle is done, you can all die, again.”

“We’d appreciate it, Bob.”

“You needed me? Hee hee!”

A portal snapped closed behind MJ bunny as it moonwalked over to me and spun, grabbing its metal crotch as it went up on tiptoes.

“Think you can teach this lot the dance moves to Thriller before the sun goes down?”

“Sure, Bob. I love dancing. You will release these poor souls afterwards, though?”

“Yep.”

“Shamone! Alright, you lot… can you get me Alicya? I feel like we need a werewolf.”

“I’m not dragging her all the way over so you can get the vibe a bit closer to the video. You’ve got the zombies!”

“Pfft. Fine. I can do this without her. HOOOOOOOO! We need to break them down into groups. Garn, four groups of a thousand, if you wouldn’t mind? Hee hee!”

“I’ll leave you to it, then.”

“Shamone!”

Flying uphill was a bit more challenging than just gliding down, but I didn’t bother to land. I found a thermal and rode it up into the sky. In every direction, soldiers were marching. It was like watching a colony of ants converge on something sugary.

Tomorrow, hundreds of thousands of warriors would fight, and a bunch of them would die. The whole system, not you IMPS, was fucking stupid.
