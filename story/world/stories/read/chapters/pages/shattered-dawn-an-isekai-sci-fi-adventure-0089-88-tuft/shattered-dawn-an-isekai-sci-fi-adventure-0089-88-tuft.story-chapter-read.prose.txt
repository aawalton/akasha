Elion activated Save a Friend through his Praxian menu. Aurelian light surged powerfully within him. But it fizzled as it reached his fingertips, dribbling weakly into Selna. A meager handful of golden threads wove through the air around Selna, settling into her skin. The action calmed her somewhat, but she still shook and quivered on the floor.

A wave of exhaustion swept over Elion. He knelt, doubled over. His arms shook as he placed them on the floor, breathing deeply, fighting to remain conscious. He was too drained. His powers were almost entirely exhausted.

“You’re a healer?” Elion asked. “Can you heal him?” He pointed to Tharnen’s form lying in the door way.

The man rose arduously, and shuffled over to Tharnen. He placed a hand on the man’s chest, and listed at his mouth.

“This man is dead,” he said. “Even if he wasn’t, Gorman’s enhancements would have prevented me from doing much.”

“We need to get out of here,” Elion said. “Can we move her?”

“She’ll only get worse,” the man said. “If we can’t do something for her. You mentioned an Altar," the old man said, his voice steady and assertive.

“When did I mention an Altar?” Elion asked.

“An Aurelian Altar will provide protection from outside controls. It might help us break her free." The old man ignored his question.

“I think we just need to get out of here,” Keyla said. “She’ll just slow us down.”

“The broken bond,” Yaret muttered.

“What’s wrong with her?”

"Elion," Yaret asked. "Is there an Altar we can bring her to?"

"Yes," Elion nodded, the room spinning around him. "Prator has it. It’s not far. He moved it into his mansion. We can go there."

Elion struggled to his feet. Keyla slung her laseram over her shoulder. Yaret scooped Keyla’s shaking mother into her arms, his eyes bright with sadness.

Elion recovered Tharnen’s rifle from where it had fallen beside the man.

He looked down at the man he had hardly known. They had fought alongside one another. The man had saved Elion’s life. He cursed his own inability. He had so much power, and yet had been unable to save the man. A pang of sorrow struck him, and his knees trembled.

*Pull it together,* he thought. *You’re fine.*

"Elion," Yaret prompted, urgency in his voice. He surged down the hall toward the front of the building. The old man caught up the lantern. He shuffled forward on bare feet, slowly moving down the hall after Yaret, seemingly struggling to walk.

Keyla followed, her lack of concern for Selna grating and unnatural. Even if Keyla truly had never met the woman, she wouldn’t have been this cold to her.

Elion emerged into building’s front lobby. Keyla stood at the window, looking out at the street. Her eyes reflected teal streaks of light, laser fire against the night sky. She watched the patterns in the fighting.

Keyla's lantern illuminated the old man’s face, twinkling in his eyes. Behind the bushy beard, the man smirked. He recognized this man, but couldn’t quite place him.

“Keyla,” Elion said, grabbing the girl by her shoulder. She looked at him.

“This is your mother,” he said, pointing to the woman in Yaret’s arms. “You really don’t remember anything about her?”

Keyla cast a disdainful glance toward Selna, then looked away.

“Something’s wrong,” Elion said. “You’re not like this. She’s sick, and you don’t care.”

“Why should I care about a random woman?” Keyla growled.

“She’s your mother!” Elion protested. “I know that the bondhold did something to your mind, but don’t you believe me?”

Keyla shook her head and shrugged hopelessly.

“She can’t,” Tuft said. “The broken bond is inhibiting her ability to feel anything about Selna.”

“So… can it be removed?” Elion asked. “Can she be healed?”

“She would suffer.” Tuft said, his voice grave. “The numbing effect prevents the pain of loss from killing her.”

"Stop," Elion said, his voice tight. "Tell me who you are. Are you a Heranan?"

The man smiled. "I am. I’m a Cultivator, Elion."

"How do you know my name? How did you know there was an Altar nearby? Have you been spying on us?"

The man's smile faded, replaced by a teasing expression that made Elion feel even more like he knew this man from somewhere.

"My name is Tuft. And I have been watching your journey with much interest."

"But who are you?" Elion asked again, his suspicion rising.

"It doesn't matter," Keyla said, pulling his attention back to the fighting outside. "If he's going to help us."

“Elion come on,” Yaret said, leaning against the wall as he tried to control Selna’s seizure.

Elion reluctantly pulled away from the man, a sinking feeling settling in his gut. He opened the door, peering out at the dark street. Laser fire cut through the night, blindingly white against the dark sky.

Orange flashes from projectile weapons lit up several windows on the second floor of Prator's mansion, directed at a taller building a few doors down from the low prison where they now sheltered. Prator's mansion wasn’t directly across the street, making their dash across the street a longer route. The only cover available were the pillars of the mansion’s portico.

"Do we just run for it?" Keyla asked, her voice tense as Elion scanned the street.

Elion cursed. The street provided no cover. He felt the Ascendancy Stones he had taken from Boff in his pocket. As soon as he reached the Altar, he’d use them, and select a protection skill that would allow him to shield others.

*Unfortunately that won’t help right now.*

"I don't think we have any other choice," Elion said. "We'll be sitting ducks. Let’s hope their attention is focused elsewhere."

“We have to try,” Keyla said. Selna shuttered, a low moan escaping her lips.

"On my mark," Elion said. "We'll wait until another one of those big rockets explodes and hope that it draws everyone's attention away from us."

He pulled the door open wide, stepping a few feet back to avoid drawing attention. Yaret stood just behind him, Selna in his arms. Keyla raised her weapon, taking up the rear.

"Let me carry her," Elion offered.

"No," Yaret spat. “I’ll run, you fight.”

"You can still run with her?" Elion asked.

Yaret nodded, clearly struggling to hold the woman.

Tuft shuffled up to the open door. “I’m ready.”

“This is madness. We can’t run out in the open like that,” Elion said. “You can barely walk, old man.”

“I’ll be fine,” Tuft said.

Just then, a loud explosion shook the building, bright light flashing outside, illuminating the street as though it were midday. Keyla ran, pushing past the others and sprinting out into the street.

“Crap,” Elion muttered, chasing after her.

Elion sprinted across the street, running in front of Keyla in hopes that he would draw any incoming fire. Elion ran at an angle toward the building. The high pitched whizz of projectiles ricocheting past his head energized him. He dashed for the cover of the pillars protecting the front entrance.

Elion reached the relative safety of the pillars and found four of Prator's men lying dead on the front steps. He heard shouts from inside the building. Laser fire smashed stone nearby as he ducked behind a pillar. Keyla climbed the steps, taking them three at a time.

Just behind him, Yaret slipped, stumbling up the last few, falling as the weight of Selna threw him off balance.

Diving from cover, Elion reached out, catching Selna in his arms as Yaret fell. He held the woman against his chest as she convulsed. Her frail body was astonishingly light, her arms weak as he restrained her movements.

Yaret struck his chin on the sharp edge of the top step. He scrambled to his feet and dipped behind a nearby pillar. More laser fire sent shards of chipped stone cutting through the air around them.

Elion scanned the street for Tuft, expecting to see the man’s crumpled body lying on the ground.

"Where's Tuft?" Elion yelled.

"I'm here," the man said, waving from the darkness behind a pillar on the side.

Elion marveled at the man’s speed. *How did he get here so quickly? The man can barely walk.*

Sheltering behind a pillar, Elion held Selna, waiting for the laser fire to stop. Their next goal, the door into Prator's mansion, stood closed. Elion watched it, waiting for Prator’s men to burst through and attack them.

Nobody came, and the laser fire died down. Elion ducked into the deep entryway, hoping the stone archway would shield him from incoming fire.

He listened at the door. Guards shouted from the upper levels, but the entry on the other side of the door was silent.
