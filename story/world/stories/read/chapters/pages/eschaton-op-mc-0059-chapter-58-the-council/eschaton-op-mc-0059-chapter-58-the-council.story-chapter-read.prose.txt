The first Council of the Ascended is two days away from when General Guan Yu hands me the letter. We spoke on it after I closed a Fracture in a Chinese nuclear powerplant. The time passes quickly. I’m nervous about it, but Morgan seems to be excited about the prospect of meeting people who can be called our peers.

She spends most of it preparing small gifts, little Opuses capable of fulfilling small annoying tasks. The one she makes for Guan Yu is a small metal tablet capable of cleaning armor with just a quick pass and a tiny amount of channeled Potentia.

“I don’t think they need those things, you know.” I mention to her as she floats from workstation to workstation.

“You’re right, I’m sure.”

“Then why make them?” I ask.

“It’s not about the gift being useful or necessary. It’s about the gift itself. It’s business relations 101. It’s something to show you’re sincere about the meeting and continuing a relationship.” She tilts her head. “Or least give the impression of that, anyway.”

“I… guess. Between the two of us, you’re definitely the socialite. I never even went to a college party.”

“Why am I not surprised?” Morgan says, laughing.

“I was busy all the time! The engineering trenches were not fun!” I say defensively.

She pats me on the arm. “Don’t worry, I shall teach you the way of the party. Now, go get me that sheet of aluminum over there.”

Finally the time to meet comes, and Morgan lays out a portal ritual. She actually uses the quick-dry spray paint from Ambassador Chen for it. She looks at the can appreciatively.

“Say what you will about China, but their labs do not disappoint. There’s something to be said for the sheer number of researchers. I’ll have to reverse engineer this.”

The can vanishes into an impossibly deep pocket, and in its place she has the portal anchor given to us by General Guan Yu. She sets it in the center, and activates the ritual. The runes light up orange, and burn away. The portal anchor vaporizes. With a crack, a hole in space opens in front of us. I step through first, like I always do.

Leading the way.

As soon as I step through, I can feel the weight of many other Ascended nearby, about a dozen. Our Auras clash for dominance, though none win. The world seems to warp and bend under our raw, metaphysical strength. But my attention is on something far more mundane.

The Northern Lights are beautiful, made even more so by my expanded visual range. A million different colors across the infrared, visual, and even the ultraviolet spectrum in a joyful dance high up. I laugh in awe.

The North Pole is frigid, and windy. The sun, just barely above the horizon, casts long shadows. Negative 33 degrees Celsius and the light wind tugging at my cloak would kill an unprepared Typical in under an hour. Though the view would make a prepared expedition a worthwhile risk, I think.

Morgan gasps as she exits the portal that closes behind her with a pop.

“Oh, wow. That’s beautiful.” She turns to look at me. “I can’t imagine what it looks like to you.”

“Magnificent!” I assure her. “Beyond words.”

Guan Yu, already here, chuckles at our reactions.

“Yes, it is awe-inspiring. The immensity and power of nature is truly jaw dropping, is it not?” He then looks around at the small group of us—about half of the Ascended. Already we’re grouping up into factions, and I can’t help but mourn that. Predictably, it’s based around nations.

There are two Indian Ascended here standing next to each other, and one of them is wearing a pilot suit. The man clearly taking charge between them two of the wears long flowing robes, and I recognize him from his fight against a Kraken. He goes by Singularity, and commands gravity to a terrifying degree. His compatriot is probably the fastest man alive, and goes by Adrenaline.

Three others stand behind Guan Yu. One young man wears a PLA’s dress uniform and a second wears a formal suit with a cape. The final member of his entourage, a young woman, wears a black funeral dress. A veil hides her face, though I can tell her skin looks pallid and unhealthy. Deep bags are under her eyes.

She looks like she’s in mourning.

Their unique abilities are less publically known. The Chinese government has done a good job keeping them under wraps, and I haven’t felt the need to hack into the government to find out more. They go by Warden, Feng Wei, and Sĭwáng respectively.

Several other Ascended stand around in large circle. A huge German woman with long blonde braids standing across from me impatiently taps her armored arms. Galahad, nearly as tall as I am, beats out a staccato rhythm of impatience on her vambrace.

She casts a stark contrast to the Japanese man, Hashimoto Hiroto, next to her. He stands with his arms clasped behind his back, a calm look on his face. His white lab coat writhes in the wind, the fabric crackling as ice forms and quickly breaks. Ice and snow ignores the rest of him as it does most of us. I’m the exception, and layers of ice are quickly building up on me.

A elderly woman, a unique trait among our group, has a friendly smile on her face. She leans on a cane, and her long hair whips in the howling wind. Her white dress, embroidered with bright blue loops, whirls in the wind. The dress is clearly something much more suited to a summer beach day rather than the North Pole. Only an Ascended could ignore the hideous weather so easily.

“I had hoped more of our peers would realize the importance of this meeting. Especially from nations that only have one. Well, no matter—”

A bright speck appears on the horizon, a glowing bolt of energy and light. I reach out with radar systems to get a bead on it. It’s flying at nearly ten times the speed of sound.

“Something’s coming,” I say, pointing at the high-speed object. “Or maybe someone? They’re moving real fast, I don’t think there’s a mundane explanation for it.”

Radar returns are scattered and indistinct, I’m barely able to get the speed as is. Whatever it is is putting out large amounts of radiation of its own and is surrounded by a ball of plasma. Soon, it becomes obvious what—who—the bolt is.

Estrella, the Brazilian Ascended hovers high above us. She looks like a human woman made out of fire. The light she puts out pushes back the eternal early dawn and the cold, though none of us were bothered by it to begin with.

She slowly drops towards the ground, though still hangs above our heads. Her light dims a little, and the fires settle down, but they don’t disappear entirely.

“Sorry, I’ll have to stay up here,” she says, floating above us. “Any lower and I’ll start melting the ice. I can only pull back so much.”

“Understood,” Guan says. “I suppose it is time for us to begin. Welcome, all, to the first Ascended Council. Let us introduce ourselves. All of you already know me, though just for formality’s sake, I am General Guan Yu, of the People’s Liberation Army.”

“And for what purpose have you brought us here, General? Your invitation was unhelpfully vague,” Singularity asks.

The light warps around him, bending in strange ways. His eyes are pitch black with a thin orange ring. Even the snow seems to dance strangely when near him, reaching towards him before drifting away on the Arctic wind.

“I brought us together to foster diplomacy,” Guan Yu says. “Given the power and influence we wield—knowingly or otherwise—being able to discuss matters of great importance on short notice is imperative.”

Warden, standing behind Guan Yu, agrees. “We cannot afford to fight amongst each other. Not with so much at stake. Not to mention the damage we could cause.”

His gaze lingers on me for a moment, and the world feels thin. Like creaking ice barely holding up my weight. The feeling passes when he looks away.

“No one knows the kind of collateral damage we could cause if we come to open blows. We already know what one of us can do unopposed. Khalid ibn al-Walid of the Empire scooped out a city with a single strike. Does anyone want to find out if that’s his limit?”

Galahad half snarls in a combination of rage and disgust.

“I agree we should not fight amongst ourselves. But we cannot let that monster Berhane—” she spits the name out in disgust— “And his lackeys get away with the crimes they’ve committed. And continue to commit!”

“I agree.” I say. “The Party of Heroes are fighting against them right now! We are already fighting amongst each other.”

Guan Yu shakes his head. “The Party of Heroes aren’t fighting Emperor Berhane—”

Galahad cuts him off. “Do not give him that title. He does not deserve it. He is a monster and should be treated as such.”

“Whether we like it or not, he has taken that title. To ignore it is to ignore him and to incite conflict. Conflict we cannot afford. In private company without his presence, we can drop the title if you so wish.” General Guan replies coolly.

“Unacceptable,” Galahad growls out. “Evil cannot and should not be negotiated with. It should be destroyed. The Party of Heroes has the right idea.”

“That was the point I was about to make, Galahad. They aren’t fighting Berhane. They attack his Typical and Empowered troops. They defend cities and destroy strongholds. They stay away from Berhane and Berhane stays away from them. Berhane may be a reckless imperialist but even he is not stupid enough to open the can of worms that would be open warfare between Ascended.”

He looks out at our assembled group.

“That is the point of this group, this ‘Council’ for lack of a better word. Conflict between us must be avoided at all costs. It is an unacceptable risk to those we all wish to protect.” He pointedly looks at both me and Galahad. “None of us want to see the atrocities that Berhane is committing. But none of us want to make it worse.”

“And what about us, General?” Adrenaline asks. “Our nations have not had the best of relationships,” he says wryly. “I’m sure you know much about that.”

“Bah!” Pythia scoffs. “The past must stay in the past. The future is what matters.” She shuffles forward with a tray of pastries suddenly in one hand. She offers it to everyone except me. Even Estrella briefly lowers for a piece.

She looks at me sadly. “Sorry, dearie. I didn’t bring any snacks for you.”

I wave her off. “It’s alright.”

Morgan eats the piece of baklava in two big bites. “Holy moly that was good.”

“Family recipe!” Pythia beams.

“I brought gifts, too,” Morgan says, brushing crumbs away.

She quickly hands them out, and the small boxes disappear into backpacks and coats with a flurry of thanks. After everything has been handed out, Guan Yu speaks up again.

“Pythia is correct,” he says to Adrenaline. “We live in a new world. The past must be left in the past and we must chart a new way forward. That is something we can do. We have power and influence, whether we like it or not. We are living weapons of mass destruction with only a handful of equals. I’m sure every single one of you has received offers of all kinds in exchange for your loyalty. Or at the very least, your talents.”

He looks at all of us, and like all of us, his gaze has weight. From both his words and his existence as an Ascended. It’s something we all share, this weight. We bend the world with every step. Our very existence is simply… more. It makes me uncomfortable. The world is unequal enough as it is. We don’t need the very fabric of reality to accept that, too.

“The powers that be have yet to understand how the world has changed. They view monsters as natural disasters that can be dealt with like hurricanes or tornados. They view Empowered and us as new weapons or resources to be hoarded. They will not change. Whether that is because of denial or incompetence, the fact is the same. They will try to play the same games even though the table has been flipped. It is up to us to guide them as best we can.”

“I am not a king, General,” I protest. “I will not become Berhane. I will not become Yangsan. I will not carve up America or the world and rule over it!”

Most of us look deeply uncomfortable with Guan’s words. Pythia clears her throat.

“Dearies, the General is not talking about that, I’m sure. Merely that we use our influence to help people make good decisions.”

“Who are we to decide what is good?” Galahad says. “We’re still fallible. We still make mistakes.”

“Who else understands the threats the new world faces better than us, Galahad?” the General retorts. “We are some of those threats. I think if anyone is to advise the nations of the world on what we are capable of and what the Fractures do it would be us.”

He gestures to me, then Morgan.

“Machina here has dived more Fractures than anyone and can see more in them than any human could ever hope to. Morgan le Fay is on the cutting edge of magic in all fields. No one understands the theory better than her. We are qualified to advise on many matters. The point of this Council is to help bring that experience and qualification together so everyone may benefit.”

“Fine,” Galahad grumpily agrees. “As long as we do not attempt to rule, I will accept that we can and should advise. But no further.” Most of us agree as well, though to differing amounts.

“This is all well and good,” Estrella says from high up. “But we haven’t decided on what to do about Berhane. We must do something!”

General Guan Yu sagely nods. “Yes, you are correct, we must do something. But we have few good options. Open conflict between us must be a last resort. I have a few ideas, but casualties among the Typicals and low-Starred Empowered are a guarantee.”

If I still had a stomach, it’d be dropping, and given by Galahad’s expression, she agrees with my sentiment. Neither of us interrupt Guan Yu’s suggestions despite our personal qualms.

“We can either act as the Party of Heroes does. They attack Typical and Empowered troops and destroy strongholds. They rescue people who have been enslaved and assist in defending refugees as well. This would obviously require our direct action, and is the riskiest option of them all. We’d be more liable to provoke a serious response from Berhane and his vassals, as well as unnerve nations across the globe.”

“I assume you have another option?” Hashimoto Hiroto, the Ascended from Japan, says. “As much as I wish to stop Berhane, I am not a fighter. I am a scientist, not a warrior.”

“Yes. We push nations to fund proxy forces inside the empire. Freedom fighters, resistance movements, guerilla forces, whatever you wish to call them. Wars of conquest create plenty of enemies, and with how fast the Empire is expanding, they are making more of them than they could possibly handle at once. With the right weapons and training, we can force the Empire to at the very least slow down.”

“That would be… bloody.” I say.

General Guan Yu shrugs. “Less so than open conflict. I understand that you are looking for the best option. You want a single glorious battle that will break Berhane and his Empire in one fell swoop without civilian casualties. I am sorry to tell you, Machina, but there isn’t one. There are only shades of gray, options on a sliding scale of terrible. Such is the nature of war and conflict. As much as we would all like it to be otherwise, those who start a war rarely feel its impacts until it is long over. It is always the foot soldier, the average person on the street who bears the brunt of it. You cannot shoulder that burden, as much as you would like to.”

I deflate, and Morgan pats my arm with mournful expression.

“Resistances rarely win, General,” Sĭwáng, another Chinese Ascended says.

“True. But they rarely truly lose. The harder a nation tries to stop them, the more enemies they create. A tight grip cannot hold sand, and a nation like the Empire cannot afford to loosen theirs.”

The Arctic wind tugs at her black dress, and the smell of forest in the fall fills the air. Incense wafts with drifts of snow for a moment before vanishing into the gale.

“If you say so, General.”

“Well?” Guan Yu asks. “Do we agree? If anyone else has any ideas, please speak them.”

The world slows to a crawl as I try to come up with something—anything. How do you break a nation like that? You break the leaders, but how do we do that without catching a continent in the crossfire? Berhane has never left Africa and given he snubbed this meeting, I can’t imagine he’d leave for anything. Nothing, nothing, nothing. The Empire of the Sun has no critical digital infrastructure that if destroyed would impact their military in any serious fashion. If anything, it’d help them. It’d make it easier for Berhane and his troops to hide their crimes.

I say nothing. I have nothing to offer. All my processing speed is ultimately meaningless in the face of incompetence on the field of battle.

“It seems we are in agreement. I will push for my government to begin this plan of action, but it will take time. That is all I have for now. Unless anyone else has anything else they would like to discuss as a group?”

The circle of us quickly fragments into small groups as others talk to each other, and General Guan Yu approaches me. He clasps my arm with a smile.

“I am glad you came, Machina. It is you who inspired this, after all.”

“What? How?”

“Your boundless optimism of course,” he chuckles. “You speak of unity with such conviction I can only assume it is a form of magic. It has not fallen on deaf ears, I promise you.” His smile falters. “I do wish more shared your sentiment. Not even all of my countrymen showed, to my shame. The Ascended are as varied as the rest of humanity I suppose. Now, I’m afraid I have to go. I’m sure you understand how busy the life of an Ascended can be.”

He steps away, and bares his guandao, similar to glaive. He slashes the air, and a portal appears. He steps through it with the three members of his entourage in tow. It closes behind Sĭwáng as the last one through . As soon as the portal closes, Galahad walks over to place a hand on my shoulder and lean in. She’s easily the tallest person I have ever seen, the top of her head coming up to my chin. She must be at least a foot taller than Morgan.

“It seems we are of similar minds, Machina.” She says. “We both value what is right and what should be over what is easy and what is.” She nods towards where Guan Yu just left from. “You cannot trust a man like him. Those who look at the dead and call them ‘casualties’ and a number on a spreadsheet who do not see them for what they really are.”

“A tragedy,” I say. She nods.

“Yes, yes, you get it. No loss can be considered ‘acceptable’. No path with a story cut short should be considered a ‘good’ option.” Her breath fogs in the air, though it’s quickly swept away by the wind.

“You cannot trust him. He is a snake. A man who sees the world as a number to balance will cut you loose if he thinks you tip the scales. Watch yourself around him.”

She squeezes my shoulder again, gives Morgan a nod, and walks off. She reaches forward, and her fingers disappear into nothing. With a flex and a grunt of effort, she rips a hole into the air and steps through.

“Does everyone have the ability to portal but me?” I ask Morgan quietly, and she laughs.
