A heavy silence settled over the room. The kind that clung to the corners and crept into the folds of one’s breath. No one moved. The soft ticking of a distant clock broke the stillness in even, hollow beats.

Oswald shifted slightly in his seat. Across the table, Selene’s gaze remained locked on the Duke. Her hands rested still in her lap, the curve of her fingers unmoving, but the tension behind her eyes said more than words could. “I will do what I can to help. But I need to know, do you have any leads?"

Edward sat back in his chair. The weariness in his features deepened. “I wish I could offer you something solid, but we’ve found very little. I’ve scoured Brightmarch top to bottom. My men have done the same. However, the information we have is unreliable, even in the best of times. Anything useful slips away like smoke.”

“But have you sent anyone into the Old March? I don’t mean to sound unkind, but that region has a rather persistent history of… complications.” Selene said.

“I have, on multiple occasions. But they’ve returned empty-handed each time. The people there do not welcome outsiders. At best, my men were met with silence. As for those we’ve tried to recruit from within, they’re discovered almost immediately."

"That certainly complicates things."

“Forgive the question, Selene, but do you truly believe the Old March holds the answers we’re after? The root of the Maiden's Bane and the spread of Siren’s Tears?”

“Perhaps. Perhaps not. But if every other trail has run cold, then I believe the Old March may still offer us something of value. If not in regard to the sickness, then certainly concerning the Siren’s Tears.”

Oswald tilted his head. That’s too organized for a bunch of people barely getting by. Either someone’s keeping ‘em scared… or keeping ‘em paid.

Oswald glanced sideways at Selene, curiosity tugging at his brow. “Hey... what exactly is Old March?”

Selene didn’t look at him right away. Her gaze lingered ahead, thoughtful, before she spoke in her usual calm cadence. “Old March was the heart of Brightmarch, its original district. The first settlers built their homes there, carved the streets by hand. But as the city expanded and wealth shifted elsewhere, the district was neglected. Bit by bit, it fell into disrepair.”

“Now it’s a slum. A place the rest of the city would rather pretend doesn’t exist.”

Oswald shifted forward, elbows resting on his knees. “That so? Then I could go to Old March and take a look."

Both heads turned to him. With the Duke especially raising an eyebrow.

“I’m serious. Yeah, I’m an outsider, but I grew up in places like that. The slums in Loria weren’t exactly friendly either. I know how to move in streets like those.”

Duke Blackridge raised a brow, but not in mockery, just careful consideration. His gaze lingered on Oswald for a few seconds more than comfort allowed, then finally nodded once. “Nothing I’ve tried has worked, so I don’t see why not.”

Not exactly a ringing endorsement, but I’ll take it. Oswald thought to himself.

Selene looked at Oswald. “You’ll need to be cautious. If something truly dangerous has taken root there, your life will be at risk."

“Don’t worry. I’ve been dodging worse than knives since I could walk.” Oswald said.

Selene turned her gaze back to the Duke. “While he investigates Old March, I’ll make inquiries elsewhere. Between the two of us, we might be able to find a thread worth pulling.”

Oswald followed the maid down a narrow corridor lined with tall, gold-framed portraits. He adjusted the strap of his cloak, still half-dusted from the courtyard, and glanced sideways at the young woman leading the way.

“Is this where you’ve got Vivi?” he asked.

The maid nodded, hands folded neatly before her. “Yes, sir. We’ve provided your companion with the finest care. She’s been kept comfortable.”

Oswald huffed a quiet chuckle. “She’s a cat. She sleeps on roofs and knocks over mugs for fun. She don’t need anything fancy.”

Still, he was curious. The maid stopped before a wooden door. She reached for the handle and opened it with a soft click, stepping aside to let him through.

Oswald stepped in and nearly choked on his own breath. What?

The room was bright and airy, filled with the scent of lavender and fresh linens. Sunlight poured in from a tall window framed with sheer curtains, and beneath that light, on an embroidered cushion larger than most beds, was Vivi.

Three servants hovered nearby. One gently brushed the cat’s fur with a fine ivory comb, another placed a crystal dish filled with tiny cuts of fish beside her, and the third stood fanning the air with a delicate feathered fan, a fan, for a cat.

Vivi stretched luxuriously, one paw extended forward while her golden eyes blinked half-lidded in sheer contentment. Her tail curled into a perfect spiral, flicking with idle satisfaction.

Oswald stood frozen just inside the door, staring. Fuck me, I wish I got this kind of treatment.

Vivi turned her head toward him with a soft mrrp, eyes gleaming with smug recognition before she returned to her pampering without so much as lifting her chin.

Oswald ran a hand down his face, biting back another laugh. “Looks like you end up livin’ better than me.”

Oswald stepped deeper into the room, still half-convinced his eyes were lying to him. Vivi lifted her head just slightly, though not enough to interrupt whatever heavenly trance the servants had lulled her into.

“Alright, princess, you’ve had your fun.” Oswald said.

He walked over to the cushioned throne she lounged on and reached down, sliding one hand beneath her belly. She chirped softly in protest but didn’t resist, letting him scoop her up with a lazy stretch. One of her paws batted lazily at his collar as she nestled herself against his chest, utterly unbothered.

Oswald turned toward the servants still hovering nearby. “Uh… thanks for the help. She’s... definitely not gonna forget this.”

One of the women gave a modest bow. “If you require anything else, sir, don’t hesitate to ask.”

“Actually, is there a room I can rest in for a bit?”

“Of course. Right this way.”

He followed the servant through the hall, Vivi purring quietly against him with every step. They passed through more stone archways, past tall windows where the light had shifted to late afternoon gold. Dust motes floated lazily in the air, and far in the distance, he could just barely hear the rhythm of horse hooves echoing through the streets outside the estate.

The room he was shown to wasn’t large by noble standards, but it still held more warmth than anywhere he’d slept in weeks. A canopied bed sat in the center, its quilt tucked neatly and lined with subtle embroidery. The walls were paneled with dark wood, and the windows were half-draped to let in a soft amber glow.

“Thank you,” Oswald said, already stepping inside.

The door closed quietly behind him. He sat down on the edge of the bed, the mattress dipping under his weight. Vivi curled immediately into his lap, tucking her head against his stomach with a low, contented hum.

Oswald’s hand rested lightly on her back, his fingertips moving in slow, absent strokes through her fur. Vivi’s coat felt warm beneath his palm, each strand soft and silky, rising and falling with the rhythm of her breathing.

His thoughts drifted. Didn’t think I’d make it this far. Feels like just yesterday I was scrappin’ for coin in the alleys back in Loria. Barely enough to eat, dodgin’ trouble just to stay breathing. Now I’m neck-deep in vampires, Animus spirits, and Progenitors.

His thumb traced a slow circle over Vivi’s side. She gave a faint sigh through her nose, content and unmoving.

Then, he heard a few knocks on the door. “Oswald? May I come in?”

Oswald glanced down. Vivi flicked one ear, but didn’t bother to lift her head. Her eyes remained half-lidded, unconcerned. He looked back toward the door. “Yeah, you can.”

The door opened with a soft creak, and Selene stepped into the room. She paused just inside, her eyes sweeping over the space before settling on Oswald and the curled shape in his lap.

“There you are, I had a feeling I’d find you here, but I didn’t expect the princess to be included.”

Oswald chuckled, brushing a hand over Vivi’s back one last time. “Yeah, she’s been real humble about it, too. Might start expectin’ servants to follow her around after this.”

He scooped the cat into his arms and rose, then gently placed her on a nearby plush chair. Vivi flopped onto her side, utterly content, tail twitching like she was waving him off. “Look at her. Ten minutes away from orderin’ wine.”

Selene’s smile widened, her arms folding loosely. “Careful. Cats have long memories. You may find your boots suspiciously full of surprises should she feel underappreciated.”

Oswald smirked and leaned back against the edge of the bed, arms crossed now. “At least then I’d know she still likes me.”

The flicker of mirth in Selene’s eyes gave way to something quieter. A beat passed before she spoke.“You’re truly going to do this aren't you? Heading to Old March."

Oswald leaned forward, resting his forearms on his knees. “Figured we need answers, right? And if no one’s talkin’, then I gotta find someone who will.”

Selene stood a few paces away, her arms loosely crossed, the light from the flames catching the edge of her white cloak. She looked toward the window before speaking. “Old March is not just a poor district. It was once the cradle of Brightmarch. It held the city’s first stones, its founding families, its heart. Now it’s forgotten by most, except those who have nowhere else to go.”

So it used to mean something. Funny how fast a place can rot when the gold dries up.

“I been in slums most of my life, I’ll be alright.” Oswald said.

Selene’s gaze shifted sharply back to him. “Don’t take it lightly, Oswald. Many of the men who live there now were once mercenaries, blades-for-hire who came to Brightmarch hoping for a place in the Duke’s ranks. When they were turned away, they didn’t leave."

Oswald scratched his jaw, expression thoughtful. “Appreciate the heads-up. Is there anyone in particular I should be watchin’ out for? Big names?”

“The Red Dragoons, they’re the largest gang in Brightmarch. They’ve claimed Old March as their territory, and they hold it with blood. I wouldn't be surprised if the proliferation of Siren's Tears is at least in part, due to their work."

Of course there’s a gang with a name like that. Sounds like the kind that brands their own knives.

“Who runs ’em?”

“A man called Aldric Marr, former warlord from the western front. He's… done a lot of things that I won't get into now. All that you need to know, is that he is dangerous."

“Right. Marr. Got it. I’ll keep my head down.”

“There’s one more thing, while you’re in Old March... do not use your Animus. Not under any circumstances.”

“Didn’t exactly plan on whippin’ it out for fun. I’m goin’ in quiet.”

“No. hear me out Oswald. If a Progenitor is involved... and there’s even the smallest chance that they’re watching the city, then using your Animus would be the same as firing a signal flare into a moonless sky. We have no idea how far their reach stretches. No idea how many eyes might already be in place.”

Oswald gave a short nod. “I’ll be careful and keep my head low.”

Selene studied him for a moment, the weight of her gaze pressing into his skin like frostbite. "Good."

“Anyway, are you gonna be alright? Last time we talked, you had that cough. Sounded rough.”

“That? It was nothing. Animus Hosts don’t fall ill. Not in any traditional sense. What you heard was likely the result of fatigue. It happens, occasionally, when I push too far for too long. But I’m fine now.”

Oswald sighed through his nose and nodded. “Alright. Just… don’t push too far again, yeah? I get it, you’re strong. Doesn’t mean you gotta carry everything on your back.”

Selene tilted her head ever so slightly, a strand of pale hair slipping loose from behind her ear. “Just focus on your own task, Oswald.”

But then, she reached into the folds of her cloak and drew out a folded piece of parchment, sealed with a faint wax mark: small, discreet, and colorless. She stepped forward and held it out to him.

“This contains what you need to know about Old March. Names. Places. Affiliations. Most of it’s drawn from contacts I trust.”

Oswald took it carefully. The seal cracked with a soft snap when he pressed his thumb to it.

“There’s a map sketched into the back, and a list of the Red Dragoons’ known operations. I’ve included notes on the key figures you may encounter. Use it wisely, and don’t trust everything you hear down there.” Selene said.

Oswald slipped the folded note into his inner pocket, pressing it flat against his chest. Guess this is where the real work starts.

He gave her a firm nod. “Got it. I’ll keep my eyes open.”
