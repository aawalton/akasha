Despite the somber atmosphere of the rescued villagers, there was a celebration in Trisken when the legion returned. Aside from Kelia and Caleb, dozens of young men and women had signed up with the legion including the three initialized Gavin, Doren and Revin. While the village celebrated the return of their people, they mourned the loss their neighbors had endured and the celebration was more a funeral for the lost than a celebration for the reurned.

Word of Caleb and Kelia’s ranks had gotten out in their absence and they returned to receive offers of lodging from the wealthiest members of the community. They refused them all, deciding instead to return to the mill for their duration of their time back.

Grisim—who had also had a class evolution to rare—took every offer he was given. Every offer.

Kelia’s father had returned in their absence and gotten word to Kavil that he would remain in the outskirts of town. While they may have overlooked Kelia’s heritage now that she was returning the rare initialized wife of one of the village’s favored sons, they could not forget his association with the Frigidi.

Before they entered the village, Kelia had taken off her snow cloth clothing and dressed instead in a neat scarlet legion officer uniform. Caleb had dressed to match, but had to borrow one of her constitution ingots to manage the cold without his accustomed furs and leathers.

During the first night of mournful celebration, Kelia snuck out to see her father after sending Avis out to find him.

That night when she returned, she filled Caleb in on what they discussed.

Caleb could tell she’d been crying, and he let her share the tale at her own pace.

“My mother was a Frigidi,” she opened with. “Dad found her injured in the forest. He bandaged her wounds, set a broken leg and made her a pair of crutches and she limped off back to her people. After she healed, she came back to visit and thank him. The visits became regular and eventually I was born. She hid my existence from her people, hiding the pregnancy she said she was going on a pilgrimage from the top to the bowels of the volcano but instead she lived with dad. After I was born she left me with him, visiting when she could until one day she never returned.

“Dad never new what happened, but after the attack he went to demand answers.”

She paused, tears welling up again.

“The chief had demanded my mother become his wife. She tried to run away but was captured. He couldn’t more answers out of them, but she didn’t live long past that.”

Caleb pulled her into and embrace and they sat in silence for some time, until she worked up the courage to say the next part.

“The basilisk you killed and the stairwell you collapsed… that’s why they attacked. It refined their Frost Steel. They traded with the troglodytes.”

“I know,” Caleb said.

He’d always suspected as much. There was no other explanation for the timing. He regretted his choices, but even now looking back saw no alternatives.

“The Frigidi are not doing well,” She said. “There’s no chance they will attack again soon, and a good chance they might not survive the summer.”

Summer was a loose term for the time of year where it was just slightly less cold and it snowed less.

“They are going to send someone to negotiate. Try to open trade.”

“I think its unlikely the village will agree,” Caleb said.

“I know,” she said. “I don’t know why dad wants to help them, but he asked I talk to Canus. They’d might offer members to the legion in exchange for their continued existence.”

Caleb thought of how Fridigi could be integrated into the legions. The rest of the empire and the regions they sought to conquer were less cold than the north mountains around the ice volcanoes, but they still had winters. Frigidi scouts could prove deadly.

“Are you going to do it?” Caleb asked.

Kelia chewed her lip, considering.

“I don’t know.”

The routine from the field continued despite their return to Caleb’s home. The Legionnaires were put to work building better fortifications for the village while the equites were left to their own devices. The scouts ranged far and wide as to not be surprised by another invasion be it beasts or Frigidi. In his scouting, Grisim killed two more wyverns, this time doing most the work himself while Gavin, Doren and Revin—who had been assigned to his scouting unit—supported him.

After the first scouting mission, Grisim asked Caleb if he could fashion a shaft for his gladius so he could wield it as a glaive instead of a sword. Grisim was certain his spear path would still apply to such a weapon while it was only marginally useful on the sword.  The further weapon was from the weapon path a initialized walked, the less efficient the stamina boost and reinforcements were. Grisim could strengthen a sword and hone its edge, but it the effects were done at a high cost to little effect.

While Caleb could do the work, he suggested his brother go ask Master Dimel. His former master had far more experience mounting weapons and since this magical sword couldn’t be modified, Caleb’s skills would have little use.

Dimel did the work happily, eager for the chance to study the weapon as he worked on it.

Caleb spend his time continuing to study Cassandra’s armor, glad that the Legate had been pulled away so he could examine it further.

On returning to his home forge, he’d forced himself to divide his time, not spending all of it on the study. With Mettius’ help, Caleb was able to get samples of gear with agility, willpower, acuity and perception enhancing enchantments. He tried using Mettius pauldrons, which allowed him to redistribute skill points however he choose, but the enchantment was far too complicated for Caleb to follow.

“If I have known I could just buy little metal bars with stats, I would have just done that instead,” Mettius had complained when he’d learned of the ingots.

With those samples on hand he was able to craft a whole array of ingots for himself, Kelia, and Grisim to allow them to tailor their stats for the situation. Caleb had grown accustomed to the temperature resistance the added constitution gave him and he constantly walked around with a plus 9 to the stat. Kelia split her available 7 points between agility and constitution so she could better train her mana control

Grism kept his split amongst the three physical stats as each served him well for different purposes. Mettius had no need for such ingots with his pauldrons

For Bog, Caleb finally got around to doing something he’d been meaning to since he first realized Bog could copy the metal he ate. He was going to forge the dragon new teeth and scales. Bog had done his best to utilize the metals he’d eaten and integrate their designs into his body, but there was only so much that could be done to make a dagger tip look like a tooth.

Caleb, using the beta meta steel the legion could spare, crafted bog a set of teeth, quenching them using the same process he had used to craft his hammer. Despite now knowing the steel powder he’d taken from the Fridigi was called Frost Steel, Caleb was reluctant to call it that and continued to refer to it as quench steel.

He did the same with the scales, only he didn’t make enough to cover the whole dragon. Instead he made a few of varying sizes and trusted in Bog to learn to replicate him should he eat enough meta steel.

Bog swallowed the work whole, and in a few days time he had a fresh set of watered steel teeth. The scales started to slowly replace his old ones, only these didn’t show the water pattern. Caleb had left the forge scale on the scales to keep Bog’s distinctive sooty black coloring. Caleb was certain now that Bog wasn’t and iron dragon, but he didn’t know what he was. He thought it best if they kept the ruse up as long as possible lest others learn Caleb had a full metal affinity.

After a week of study, Caleb finally felt ready to begin crafting something to mimic. The mana absorption effect of Cassandra’s armor was within Caleb’s ability to comprehend—even though it took weeks of study, but its means of converting the mana into enhanced stats  was beyond his understanding as it stood.

Caleb had gotten Mettius to let him study the gauntlets he wore which did something similar in pulling in ambient mana and mana from the bearer’s mana pool and converting it to mana of the space, time, and force affinities.

Together, Caleb had what he needed to forge an item that absorbed mana and turned it into another type. Or at least the thought he did.

He tried forging the enchantment into a gauntlet for himself as a practice piece, but the meta steel he used couldn’t hold the enchantment. It was like trying to build a ship in a bottle only the bottle was smaller than the ship he was trying to build. He tried every variety of steel Dimel had and what he could wring out of the legion quartermaster but nothing worked. Nothing until he asked Kelia for her ring back.

The mage steel ring was like a bottomless pick for enchanting. He easily built the enchantment into the item. As he worked, he stopped following the example exactly as it had been laid out, and let his intuition guide him as he formed the mana in the air above the piece and hammered it on. He spend an entire day at the forge working at the task, relying heavily on his boosted constitution to avoid the need for a break, and int the end, he had it.

Greater Mana Steel Ring of Frost

Aspect Mana (minor)

Drastically accelerates the rate at which you draw mana from your environment and increases the capacity of your mana pool. Your mana pool will absorb a portion of the magic from enemy attacks, storing the metal within the ring, rapidly converting it to cold mana.

He’d been unable to improve the aspect of the metal, but the fact he’d been able to add an enchantment without lowering its aspect was proof of his increasing skill.

When he gave the ring back, Kelia was ecstatic, and immediately demanded that Mettius shoot her with a Force Bolt. The prince obliged, though he only shot it through he mana pool and not at her directly. The bolt of force entered the pool and diminished as it paste through, shrinking to half its originally size as it exited the other end.

After seeing this, Mettius strong armed the quarter master in way Caleb had failed to do and secured enough Mage Steel  for Caleb to make him a ring as well. Without the fleck of Frost Steel, Caleb was unable to add the mana converting effect to the ring for Mettius, but tapping into the innate properties of the mage steel he was able to make it so the ring stripped the aspect of the mana it absorbed, leaving it unaspected and allowing Mettius’ gauntlets to more quickly convert it.

And, once that was done, Caleb finally got to embark on the task he’d long planned on starting. Making himself a full set of armor.
