Luke sat on the edge of his bed and let out a long, contented sigh. It had been a few days since Victoria had left the academy, determined to find a merchant group willing to distribute their new product.

If he was honest with himself, those days had been blissful. Only in her absence did he realize just how much stress she brought into his life.

Maybe she’s better suited to working from the shadows… That way I can finally get some peace, he mused, stroking his chin.

A knock at the door shattered his moment of tranquility.

“Young master, I have returned.”

The door swung open, and Victoria stepped inside—draped in a striking red robe that shimmered subtly beneath the lamplight. At a glance, she looked no different from the noblewomen who graced the courts: wives, mistresses, and consorts of powerful men. But there was something different—something more dangerous—about her presence.

For a moment, Luke forgot how to breathe. He’d seen her in maid uniforms, battle gear, and even drenched in blood, but this—this elegant, sensual version of her—was by far the most disarming.

The robe clung to her hourglass figure in all the right places, its scarlet folds highlighting her olive skin and accentuating the sway of her hips. Her raven-black hair cascaded freely down her back, while her warm, brown eyes gleamed with mischief.

Luke sat frozen. By the time his mind caught up, Victoria was already before him, flashing a playful smile.

“Oho? Did I catch you off guard, young master?” she teased, her voice soft as velvet.

A shudder ran involuntarily down Luke’s spine. The sudden jolt helped him regain control of his scattered thoughts.

Clearing his throat, he forced his gaze away and said stiffly, “Welcome back, Victoria. How was your mission? Did you learn anything useful?”

Instead of answering, Victoria giggled. She gave him a brief once-over before casually turning around.

“Help me out of this first,” she said. “I feel suffocated in these robes.”

She swept her hair aside, revealing the graceful curve of her neck and the smooth expanse of her back. Luke hesitated, swallowing his protests as he reluctantly stood to help.

The robe slipped from her shoulders and pooled silently at her feet, exposing her bare skin beneath. Luke spun around on instinct, jaw clenched and face flushed.

When will this stop? he thought bitterly.

Whenever Victoria was around, it felt like he wasn’t in control of his own body. His plan to use her presence as some kind of exposure therapy was proving far more difficult than anticipated.

Some time later, with her modest servant attire once again in place, Victoria joined him on the bed. Her tone turned serious, the flirtatious edge gone from her demeanor.

“First, the Black Tiger Gang has a deeper grip on Ralis City than we initially thought,” she began. “They’ve got the merchant networks in a chokehold, and more disturbingly—they have connections to the royal family.”

She brushed a loose strand of hair behind her ear, her expression unreadable.

“The second prince—Prince Lyon—is infatuated with the daughter of the Black Tiger Gang’s leader. He thinks their support will secure him the throne, even over the crown prince.”

“From what I’ve gathered, Ming Hu has no real intention of supporting the second prince,” Victoria said, her voice laced with conviction. “He’s likely playing both sides of the succession to extract the maximum benefit, no matter who wins.”

Luke listened silently, but his thoughts were far from still.

As I thought… women really are the downfall of powerful men, he mused inwardly.

Victoria frowned. Her gaze sharpened, cutting through the air as she turned to glare at him.

“Hm? What’s the matter?” Luke asked, feigning innocence.

“I don’t know. I just got the sudden urge to punch you,” she said, flatly.

Luke stiffened, then gave a quick cough to cover his unease. “Ahem… Right. Anyway, so Ming Hu is an opportunist,” he said, steering the conversation back. “But he sounds too greedy to have reached his current position without being reined in. There must be something we’re missing.”

Perhaps the Black Tiger Gang has another tie to the royal family… someone powerful enough to give them the confidence to act so brazenly, Luke thought, eyes narrowing.

The first figure that came to mind was the Right Minister—those cold green eyes and sharply defined brows etched into his memory. A man like that, if serving as their patron, would allow Ming Hu and his gang to move through the capital without fear of reprisal.

Of course, it was just a theory. Luke had no concrete proof linking them. And even if the Right Minister were involved, would someone so powerful truly allow himself to be bought?

“Shall I continue my report?” Victoria asked, her tone composed and respectful.

“Mmm,” Luke hummed in assent.

“Qin Hu—Ming Hu’s daughter—is known as the ‘Fairy of Ralis City,’” she said. “Not only is she proficient with various instruments, but her beauty and voice are so enchanting that many noble families have expressed interest. That said, even without these qualities, her background alone would make her a highly sought-after match.”

Luke’s brow lifted slightly. Hearing Victoria praise another woman’s beauty was... odd. Just how stunning was this ‘Fairy of Ralis’ for someone like Victoria to compliment her without sarcasm?

“With the second prince openly expressing his affection for her,” Victoria continued, “none of the other noble houses have made any public attempts to court her. They wouldn’t dare challenge a royal suitor.”

Luke exhaled sharply and shook his head. The second prince already sounded far less capable than his older brother, Prince Leo. By staking an open claim on Qin Hu, he had likely alienated all the influential scions who were also eyeing her.

In a battle for succession, such political missteps were fatal. A prince needed the support of the noble houses to secure the throne, and Prince Lyon’s actions seemed more impulsive than calculated.

“Then it’s clear,” Luke said with a sigh. “The second prince will likely fail in the succession struggle. I doubt anyone would support him, even if they intended to use him as a puppet.”

After all, who would place such a liability on the throne willingly?

But then, a thought struck him.

His spine tingled.

What if that’s the point?

His brow furrowed, and a slow chill crept through his veins.

Luke had already suspected there might be a Lhair sympathizer embedded somewhere high in the Marxx Kingdom’s political structure. After all, how else could Lhair’s forces have infiltrated the kingdom’s northern cities with such ease?

Not to mention—they had known exactly when reinforcements were being dispatched to Xiu Fortress, timing their attack on Clayton City with ruthless precision.

If it hadn’t been for Luke’s deduction back then, it wasn’t an exaggeration to say that the Marxx Kingdom might have fallen soon after.

Or at least, that’s what the system claimed.

If I were secretly allied with Lhair, placing a young, foolish prince on the throne would be ideal. The more stubborn and inept the ruler, the easier the conquest… he reasoned, thoughts swirling darkly.

“Luke?” Victoria’s voice cut through the haze, soft with concern.

“Ah—sorry,” he said, shaking himself free of his musings. “Please, continue.”

“You were right,” she said, picking up where she’d left off. “From what I observed, it seems that very few support the second prince. Even the noble clans I visited showed open disdain when his name came up.”

Luke blinked, his surprise visible. “Wait—you visited the noble clans?”

Victoria giggled, a teasing lilt in her voice. “Did you not notice the robe I wore when I arrived? Where did you think I had been, playing dress-up in the academy halls?”

It clicked.

The luxurious attire. The noblewoman’s poise. She hadn’t just been acting the part—she’d become the part.

Still, it wasn’t easy to accept. As a commoner, Victoria shouldn’t have been able to gain access to those circles, not without serving one of the households directly. And yet, she had infiltrated the upper echelons of Ralis City’s elite without raising suspicion.

“What else did you find out?” he asked, the edge of admiration creeping into his tone.

Victoria’s grin turned mischievous. “I found out many things, young master. But I suppose you only want what’s relevant.” She leaned back slightly, her eyes sparkling. “Those noble ladies do little but gossip all day, but between all the idle chatter, I managed to plant some seeds…”

She paused for effect.

“I introduced our new product. Nearly every woman I spoke to expressed interest. If we give out a few free samples, I’m confident it won’t be long before perfume becomes the trend in Ralis City.”

Luke nodded slowly, impressed despite himself. “That’s good progress. But it doesn’t solve our real problem—distribution.”

He stood, pacing slowly as he spoke.

“Without going through an established merchant group, we risk exposing our identities. And after the gunpowder incident, I really can’t afford more eyes on me.”

He rubbed the back of his neck, brow furrowed with worry.

“What we need,” he muttered, “is a way to bypass the Black Tiger Gang entirely…”

Victoria laughed—a crisp, melodic sound that rang with amusement.

“Oh, young master,” she said, eyes dancing with mischief. “Why bypass the Black Tiger Gang… when we can use them?”

Her smirk was wicked, confident.

“No other merchant group in the city has their reach or influence.”

Use the very people who tried to kill me? What kind of twisted idea is that? Luke thought, gritting his teeth.

Still, he could tell from her expression that she wasn’t joking—she had a plan.

So he simply nodded, gesturing for her to go on.

“Qin Hu is the most coveted woman in Ralis City, as you know,” Victoria began, casually tucking a stray strand of hair behind her ear. “Despite not being born into nobility, she’s often invited to noble gatherings. The women I spoke to are practically itching for a chance to outshine her.”

She leaned forward slightly, her voice tinged with amusement.

“If every noblewoman begins wearing perfume and Qin Hu doesn’t… she’ll become the odd one out. The center of whispered ridicule. Eventually, her pride—or her jealousy—will lead her straight to us.”

The expression on Victoria’s face was a strange mix of beauty and cunning. There was a glint of satisfaction in her eyes, like a general delighted by the chaos she had sown.

Luke chose to overlook the clear defects in her personality—at least for now. He focused instead on the merits of her plan. It wasn’t hard to believe that the so-called Fairy of Ralis City had long been a target of envy. And if Victoria’s scheme worked, the pressure from the other noblewomen might be enough to draw Qin Hu in without any direct approach on their part.

Still, doubts lingered.

Would someone like Qin Hu—exalted, desired, and already in the limelight—truly care enough about a new fragrance to seek them out?

Even if she did, how would they contact her without exposing themselves?

The Black Tiger Gang already knew his face. Who was to say they hadn’t continued hunting him in the background?

Luke voiced his concerns openly. There was no point in holding back now—Victoria had already laid the foundation for this plan, and if anyone had thought through the potential risks, it would be her.

As he finished, Victoria's lips parted into a brilliant smile, her perfect white teeth catching the light.

“Your butler’s wife, Hilda... She’s now the personal guard of the Black Tiger Gang’s young mistress.”

Luke’s eyes widened.

It clicked immediately.

Just moments ago, he had been racking his brain for a trustworthy intermediary. Someone with access to Qin Hu, someone capable of protecting their secrets.

And here it was—hand-delivered by fate.

“The heavens are on my side,” Luke muttered with a relieved sigh.

“You’ve done exceptionally well, Victoria,” he said sincerely. “In just three days, you’ve infiltrated both the Black Tiger Gang and the noble circle of Ralis City. You’ve given me information and influence—and possibly a foothold into the upper tiers of power.”

At that moment, he realized something else.

Victoria was more than just useful—she was irreplaceable.

A ghost in high society, a knife in the shadows, and devastatingly beautiful to boot.

She was, in every sense, his Black Widow.

“I have a gift for you,” Luke said with a smile.

He had initially set aside a small sample of their first perfume batch as a token of appreciation—but now, that felt paltry in comparison to her accomplishments.

Victoria didn’t look surprised. In fact, her expression was one of calm anticipation, like someone patiently waiting for what was already hers.

Luke led her to the corner of the room, where several ceramic vials sat. The perfume had been curing there for three days. He carefully selected one of the finer vials that had arrived just the day before and filled it with the golden liquid, sealing it with a polished cork.

When he handed it to her, Victoria didn’t hesitate.

She raised it to her nose and inhaled.

Her eyes fluttered closed, and a soft, breathless sigh escaped her lips.

“This is… amazing,” she whispered, her voice dazed with wonder.

Luke couldn’t help but smile, but he wasn’t finished.

He crossed the room again and retrieved a pale white brick, smooth and softly gleaming. At first glance, it looked like an elegant candle—save for the absence of a wick.

Victoria tilted her head. “What is it?”

“It’s called scented soap,” Luke replied. “You use it in the bath to cleanse your skin. It’s infused with fragrance.”

She brought it to her nose and took a deep breath.

Her eyes widened in disbelief.

“Is this… heaven?” she murmured.
