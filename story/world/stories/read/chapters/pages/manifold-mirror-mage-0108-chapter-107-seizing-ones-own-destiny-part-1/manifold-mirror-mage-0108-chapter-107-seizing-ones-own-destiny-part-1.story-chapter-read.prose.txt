Despite his frayed nerves, Gio managed to get a decent night’s rest. He sat across from Chandrika in the dining hall, grasping a cup of tea and unenthusiastically picking at the carcass of what was once an omelette. His breakfast companion seemed to be far less anxious than he was, which was admittedly helping soothe his mind away from the treacherous daydreams he had been subjecting himself to. Images of red threads and superweapon necklaces floated around his head as he tried to keep his mind on the task at hand.

“Well? We’ve got twenty minutes. Want to start heading over so we can grab seats?” Chandrika asked.

Gio’s reflection caught his eye from the surface of his coffee, miming the action of checking a wristwatch.

“Sure.” Gio plainly answered.

Chandrika tilted her head, looking at Gio and noticing that his eyes had glazed over as he dissociated.

“You’re way too far in your own head,” Chandrika said. “You’re going to pass.”

“I know! I know. I’m just going to be very happy when this is over.” Gio said, snapping back to reality.

After allowing the enchantments on the table to spirit away the remnants of their meal, Gio and Chandrika made their way over to a row of mirrors.

“See you there,” Chandrika said, disappearing into the twilit passage generated by the scrying plane.

Gio placed a hand upon the glassy surface and was greeted by a message in white letters above the rest of his usual interface.

[Welcome, Gio. Would you like to go to the examination hall? You have 17 minutes until the midterm examination commences.]

“Yes, please,” Gio said.

The manse had been a bit more familiar feeling ever since Saint Baphelus had taken him to his Phylactery. Gio didn’t really have enough mental capacity to consider the full implications of the change to his daily interactions with the school’s network.

Stepping into the dark, a door appeared in front of him. It was grand and shining, looking very similar to the front doors of the grand reception hall, two rectangular lattices of crystalline facets that glowed with an inner light. Gio allowed the doors to part for him, taking a deep breath in as he surveyed the examination hall in earnest.

A nebulous fog of pink and green mist obscured the rafters high above his head, from which long gilded tapestries in heraldic colors draped down, forming the impression that the entire space was contained in a bohemian tent bigger than any athletic arena that Gio had ever seen. On the far wall, the tapestries parted, revealing a display of stone carvings that looked like a menagerie of different animals. At the base of each statue, their habitats blended to form a patchwork vista of natural scenes. Gio spotted a familiar-looking Owl amongst the court of statues. On top of this owl, a curious statue sat cross-legged, a detailed carving of an older man made from some kind of darkened stone. Smaller polished marble-like pebbles seemed to orbit this statue, which drew Gio’s eye. Among this vista of statues, a curious door bedecked in vines stood, guarded by a small fan-like formation of Inquisitors, many of whom wore pitch-black robes and polished, featureless masks.

I wonder what’s behind the door? Maybe that’s where the Proctor will arrive from?

A half-shell stadium of seats was slowly filling with hundreds of students wrapped around an oval-shaped stone dais, upon which sat a stately extended desk. At this desk, four individuals sat, chatting amongst themselves.

Professor Alexander Anaximenes had his hair down, allowing his mane of white to drape over glossy red armor that looked like blood that had been shaped into glass. At his sides, two skeletal figures floated. His psychopomps had bird-like skulls, white-flame-wreathed wings, and long brass staves that shone with swirls of white and red mana. He could certainly cut an imposing figure when he wanted to.

To his left, Gio recognized the teacher of Papyrus’s class, more from his public image than from interacting with him at the school. Harold Steamcrest towered above his ‘seat’, which was really more like an elevated pillow, because Professor Steamcrest was a centaur. Bedecked in copper-colored armor that hissed gouts of pressurized steam periodically, the hulking half-man looked to be the spitting image of the modern legend that people spoke about. Having earned his surname for his world-renowned mastery of steam magic, it was perhaps unsurprising that the centaur professor was perpetually surrounded by a slight haze of hot vapor. Strapped to his back, Professor Steamcrest carried a massive pillar of sharpened metal, his steam-powered spear, an object nearly as famous as he that allegedly weighed several tons.

The third professor at the table was the subject of many rumors among the student body. Rumored to be an outcast Haruspex from the Great Grassland, it was said that the school was incredibly excited to have her as a member of the staff, being that the grassland peoples were usually tight-lipped about their ceremonial magics. Seeing her, Gio felt that he understood why some students found her unnerving. Margulis Fast-Bloodsheen rested upon a sort of portable nest-like construct set upon wooden legs that ambled about to carry her from place-to-place. She was swaddled from the neck down in a wrap of some kind of bat-wing-like material that sat upon a bed of dried grass and raven feathers. Her hands were clasped over her chest, covered in bangles and rings of polished bone that had been carved to resemble totems of screaming faces, each alit with a rainbow of different colors of mana. Perhaps most unsettlingly, her eyes had been sewn shut with thick, coarse black threads. She wore an easy-going smile as she spoke to the last occupant of the table.

Gio didn’t know anything about him besides his name. Nerod Cazlitine was a member of the Gold-Ring nobility, which one could obviously tell because he was dripping in gilded accoutrements. He had golden threads woven into his complicated braids, gold plates sewn into his robes as a sort of ceremonial armor around the shoulder blades. He had gold jewelry, several golden teeth, and even suspiciously golden irises, likely the result of cosmetic enchantment. Other than his gilded facade, he appeared to be excessively muscular. His upper body was so overdeveloped that he seemed to be bursting out of his outfit, revealing much of his pectoral muscles in a way that Gio found to be somewhat distracting.

As Gio and Chandrika managed to find seats, Professor Cazlitine stood up to address the crowd with a booming voice, causing all students in the stands to go silent.

“Greetings, First-year students of the Crystal Ring Academy. I am Professor Cazlitine, and I am honored to be one of your examiners for today’s event. We shall begin in just a few moments, as the last of your classmates filter in. We are only waiting on the arrival of our esteemed Proctor, Saint-”

A harsh laugh echoed from above, interrupting Professor Cazlitine, whose formerly welcoming smile instantly turned to a sneering frown at the sound. Gio craned his neck upward through the many-colored tapestries, surprised to see a balcony peeking out over the testing platform. Loose rows of hooded figures sat in chairs that could pass for thrones. Standing at the crest of this balcony was a bold figure of a man.

Ah, that must be the ‘peanut gallery of useless academics’, as the Headmaster described them.

The first thing that Gio noticed was the cloak that the mystery figure was wearing. The fabric of his cloak seemed to be a fabric reminiscent of translucent insect wings, but instead of showing the Professor’s body, the scenes visible through the diaphanous material were panoramas of sunrises and sunsets, which gleamed out from him to create an admittedly beautiful cascade of twilight. The man’s imperious features were bent into a dismissive scoff as he addressed the professors on the platform.

“If you had been paying attention instead of socializing, Professor Cazlitine… you may have noticed that our esteemed proctor is already here. Another petty display of power to surprise and delight the students at the expense of the faculty, perhaps? Or is it just another sign that nobody at this school respects the time and schedules of those of us who actually have things to do?” The cloaked professor scowled.

“Excuse me?” Professor Cazlitine scoffed. “You dare-”

“You need not rise to his provocation, Nerod. And I thank you, professor, for that inspiring introduction,” interrupted a smooth, almost mechanical voice from somewhere in the room. Gio looked around, seeing nothing that would indicate where it was coming from.

“As always, your need to discredit our institution in front of the children is as predictable as it is unnecessary. I was merely catching up with an old friend, and wished to give the two students who have yet to arrive-”

As if on cue, two panicked-looking students erupted out of the large rectangular mirror at the mouth of the room. Under the eyes of all present, they tried their best to melt into the crowd of the stands.

“The benefit of the doubt.” The voice finished.

The statue of a man that was sitting cross-legged on top of the Owl statue began to move. A halo of purple light bloomed outward from his stone body as the proctor levitated forward. A few gasps of recognition echoed out as students around Gio and Chandrika seemed to recognize this individual. Jean, who had quietly slipped into a seat to Gio’s left at some point in the last few moments, was one of the gasps. Their proctor was, seemingly, a statue of a man carved out of some kind of slick dark gemstone that had inclusions of some kind of marbled metal. He was surrounded by a constellation of pretty little stones that seemed to thrum in time to the resonant hum of his magic.

The cloaked professor scoffed. He flipped some of his wind-swept gray hair out of his face, taking his seat and leaning against his armrest with a bored expression.

“Greetings, student body. My name is Vaught. You may know me as ‘Saint Vaught of the Brink’, but I find that to be a stuffy title that people only use when they want something from me. I won’t bore you with my qualifications, but know that I consider myself to be a staunch defender of the art of learning in all of its forms. I am truly honored to be here today as your proctor.” The Saint said.

Saint Vaught. The first person to explore the infinite orrery, and a master of astrological magic.

Even though his mentor was also a Saint of magic, Gio had a hard time not being starstruck in the presence of such a legendary figure. Jean, it seemed, was perhaps even more starstruck than Gio.

“He’s… wow,” Jean uttered.

I suppose that makes sense. Jean’s family uses star magic, and Saint Vaught is probably the most famous space magic user of all time.

“Now that we’re all present, we can take care of some last bits of business before we begin. Firstly… I must take an oath. I swear, in front of the spirits of this school, to uphold the values of our union. I shall speak with conviction and act with impunity as a representative of the standards set in stone here. This place, this legend, shall be upheld. This I swear.”

From the tapestries that flowed down from the ceiling, lines of metallic magic in shades of gold, silver, and copper flowed out of the embroidery to wrap around Saint Vaught, seeming to charge him with authority over the hall. The statues on the far side of the hall moved in unison, bowing to the Saint briefly before raising their heads to resume their vigil.

The mysterious door rattled as the vines that encircled it peeled away. Carvings of beasts roared silently, as an odd light seemed to shine from beyond the seams where the doors touched the frame. The wood of the door creaked as it swelled slightly, almost as if it were breathing.

What’s going on over there?

“Yes, yes.” The Saint said, seeming to be speaking to something audible only to him. “That door exists as an alternate challenge for those who do not wish to partake in today’s examination. At any time, you may choose to enter it. Know that if you do so, you will no longer be considered a student of this institution and will lose access to the Manse.”

The stands erupted into hushed conversations.

“What?” Gio gasped. “There was another path this whole time?”

A flush entered his cheeks. Had he been played like a fool?

So… there was no point in going through all that trouble. All of that work, just to find out that the rules are made up, and there was another path-

“Well? Are you going to tell them? Nobody?” Asked the twilight-cloaked professor from the balcony, looking down with judgment at the four professors and the proctor. “None of you wish to elucidate the students you claim to love about the nature of that door?”

Professor Anaximenes’s gaze could have melted steel.

“Be careful with your words, professor.” Harold Steamcrest spat.

“Pathetic.” The rogue professor replied. “It once again falls to me to correct the deficiencies of the seriously failing standards of this school.”

The cloaked man stood up, leaning over the balcony to address the students below.

“You should all know that the spirits want you to go through that door.” He said.

There was a charge in the air. Gio felt pressure slam into his ears, like he’d been sent straight to the bottom of the ocean. There was a feeling like the instant when a fragile piece of glass was suspended in midair, and the only thing that anybody could do was wait for it to drop and shatter.

“Silence. Speak no more!” Saint Vaught commanded.

But then, the cloaked professor waved a hand.

The pressure disappeared. It was just… gone. Chandrika and Gio both let out gasps of air as they felt the sensation vanish, to the confused glances of other, less perceptive students.

“So beholden to the whims of the spirits. I know what I can and cannot say. I respect your power, Saint Vaught… and I’ll not make you act upon your oath. But I am the authority on law in this room. I should think that you would know that.”

“You tread on dangerous territory.” Saint Vaught replied. “Say what you will, and let us move on.”

“With your blessing, then.” The professor said. He returned his gaze to the student body.

“That door is old. Older than the Saints.” He spoke. His voice was crystal clear as it washed over the stands. “It is a remnant of the way things once were. You are free to open that door… but you cannot return here once you do so. Think about the plans that you had this morning, and ask yourself how this new information factors into those plans. This is all I can say.”

It clicked for Gio.

This. This is what the Headmaster wanted to talk about, but couldn’t. That door is dangerous.

Gio looked at the door in a new light. From the beasts carved into the wood of that door, under the odd light that leaked from its seams… was something else. Some sort of appetite, like a mouth waiting to be fed.

“... Thank you, professor.” Saint Vaught curiously said. “It’s always such a dramatic time when I get the chance to come do these examinations. In any case, it is time to begin.”
