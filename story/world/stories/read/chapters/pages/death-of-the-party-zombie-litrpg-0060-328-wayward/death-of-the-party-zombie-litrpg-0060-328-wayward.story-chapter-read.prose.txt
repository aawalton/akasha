Max pushed through the doorway into his cottage, closing it behind him. Other than now being in Sanctuary, the small house was a near replica of the one they had lived in on his previous world. Cozy and rustic, and out deep in the woods. His purple eyes scanned the quiet living room before he placed his hat down on the table.

“Are you home, dear?” he asked out loud.

“Taking a bath.”

With a smile on his face, he walked around the furniture and stepped through into the bathroom. Steam filled the space as the elf lay soaking in hot water. She gave him a tired glare, but her usual frown had mostly dissolved due to the relaxing bath.

“I’ve always said you looked divine, but now we’re actually demi-gods.” Max grinned as he perched on the side of the wooden counter.

“Save it.” Ren rolled her eyes. “It might feel more real once we actually have those powers, rather than being stuck in limbo.”

“I’ve spoken to the higher-ups. Until Theo returns, nothing can change.” The magician tilted his head in thought. “Apparently he has the habit of occasionally traveling through random points in space and time.”

Ren sank lower into the bath and exhaled. “Sounds rather reckless for the Aspect of Death to be so fickle. Letting us get the kill allowed us to keep the Guardian powers for ourselves… but feels shortsighted.”

“Perhaps that’s why you’re the Aspect of Order.”

“That’s also bullshit.” The elf scowled. “I’m not that much of a stick in the mud, am I? Certainly not more recently, where I’ve become a magician in my own right.”

Max raised an eyebrow in thought before shrugging. “Replicating some of my abilities could be considered going along in an orderly sense. Out of our party, you were the most level-headed, despite some of our… activities.”

“Especially in Hell. Dark times.”

“I certainly feel some weight off my shoulders now that I’m not the Demon King on this world. While the new Hell here is similar to our old one, there’s something different about it. It’s free of the burden we once endured.”

“We have enough responsibilities coming up without that tying you here.”

The magician paused in thought for a moment. “You mean our lives beyond protecting Sanctuary?”

Ren closed her eyes. “There are five Aspects now, you know what that means. If the Radochs are defeated, we’ll not sit idle, I’m sure.”

The universe known as the Sea was full of problem worlds, not to mention unknown threats. An elite fighting force of adventurers who couldn’t help but involve themselves in heroics would find no shortage of work as more of this reality was discovered. Perhaps he knew that was their purpose, even if he hadn’t directly addressed that truth.

“It’ll be a shame to leave Wolf and the others behind.”

Ren opened her eyes again, a rare softness in her expression. “You’ve seen how happy he is here. Being a Guardian of the forest suits him well. Plus, I’m expecting a wedding invite from Tanya and Quinn any day now.”

“With the end of the world upon us, why not?” Max pulled a face. “I was just in a short meeting with Sally and the rest, and our future performances might be at more of a disadvantage than we first thought.”

“Speak plainly, trickster.”

“Our immortality is being replaced with traditional resurrection means. For us Aspects, I doubt we’ll be short of resources, but it’s something to consider. It’s the cost of fixing the Sea. Bringing order to every world, one by one.”

“Don’t start that.” Rent shook her head and sighed. “But, what’s a little stakes between friends? If we or anyone else can be brought back, even if the method is more of a struggle, that is at least better than definite oblivion.”

“Either way, we’ve got quite the future ahead of us. Greater power, larger audiences, and a universe-spawning tour.”

“Very… chaotic.”

“Good thing I have someone to keep me in order, then.” Max stood up from the counter. “Room in there for one more?”

Theo was multiple. Theo was one. Theo wasn’t.

He spent time as a bowl of three marbles, one of them chipped. For eternity, he was a ghost wrapped in static. Darkness blinded him, even as the light tore him atom from atom. Purpose fulfilled, death itself stretched him across not only the Sea, but every available universe.

The lull of something beyond eternity, abstract from every dimension, drew him closer to finality. He knew everything, everywhere, and everyone. Now seeped through reality, he would either end everything, or end only himself.

Then, something both abrupt and foreseen prodded at his waning consciousness.

A probing string, impossibly thin and just as long as it needed to be. His bare particles clung to the lifeline, spiraling around the thread over either seconds or millennia.

Violence jerked him away, stripping all of the infinite knowledge away. Splitting him back to his barest form. He never moved, but the velocity of his descent tore through the abyss like a burning star.

Through all of this, he could only think of one word: Sally.

The zombie paused, halfway through organizing her Inventory. Her stomach lurched at a threat unseen. Before she could move, an urgent message pinged through from Dent. Her presence was requested at the tower.

This could only be one of two things, but her rolling insides pointed to the more hopeful of the options.

A flash of blue light illuminated her as she teleported directly to the tower’s upper reception. The doors to the round meeting room were already open, and nobody was at the waiting desk. She ran through to the door at the back that led to the control room and manual transport pods.

Heart beating in her chest, she flung the doors open.

Half of the room was painted in bright crimson, blood covering every surface. Amid the dripping red stood the flickering form of Chuck, a very stressed and bloodied Dent, and a third figure. This last one was easy for her to recognize, even though he was covered with gore.

“Theo!” She hopped forward, pausing briefly once she saw the look in his eyes.

Rather than the usual red eyes, his sockets were pure darkness. Beyond empty - it was as if they held small black holes.

“He didn’t just appear like normal,” Chuck said, strain clear in his voice. “He exploded into existence.”

The machine behind the vampire looked torn open, metal struts bent and charred. Steam ran from both the electronics and Theo himself.

“Has he said anything?” she asked. Her eyes narrowed as she tried to determine if there was anything she could fix with her powers.

Dent shook his head. “Other than giving us heart attacks, he took two steps forward and then froze in place. I messaged you immediately.”

The zombie crossed her arms. This wasn’t the first time her husband took a little stroll through existence. Despite the harrowing experience, he usually snapped out of it in short order. For as much of a goober as he could be, his mental fortitude was second to none.

“Theo Middlename Danger.” She tapped her foot on the ground, on a puddle of blood. “How dare you keep your lady waiting for so long?”

The room fell into silence as the vampire continued his dead-eyed stare at the opposite wall. Other than slowly breathing, he otherwise had no reaction or signs of life.

“Plan B is that I put my dagger through his skull.” Sally clicked her tongue. “See if turning him off and on again fixes the problem.”

Chuck wavered and pulled a face. “Given his explosive return, I am hesitant to see if that gets worse on his next respawn.”

With a frown across her brow, Sally crouched down and stuck a finger in the blood. After observing it more closely she gave it a taste. “Hmm. It’s definitely his own. Doesn’t taste corrupted or anything. Any malady is probably mental rather than physical. Call Lucius in.”

Dent nodded and looked away to access his STAR.

“Fangs being catatonic should only be a temporary issue.” The zombie stood again and gave the vampire a quizzical look. “Given his spates of insanity before, it’s a good thing that he isn’t delirious and active with how powerful he is.”

“Well, don’t count your chickens just yet.” Chuck rubbed at his temples. “Although I’m happy to see him back, this is something of a distraction. If you could take him somewhere safe for recovery, I can work toward making this final System a real thing.”

The weight of the word final gave Sally slight pause. Their world had known many changes in the rules governing their lives over the years. She herself had experienced at least a dozen more from traveling to other planets. Getting one last ultimate, unchangeable System felt… well, what if it wasn’t perfect, or was worse in some way?

“There’s a little den out in the mountains,” she said. “I’ll send Lucy the coordinates. It’s out of the way of everyone, just in case.”

“I trust you.” The Architect nodded. With a sweep of his hand, the blood covering the room vanished away. “Give me the word and I’ll send a projection out to you.”

Sally stepped over to the vampire and grasped onto his arm. This close to him, she could definitely tell something was wrong. More than just absently vacant, she could feel the hollow coldness of the Sea radiating from his body. His physical body was here, but Theo wasn’t.

“C’mon, goober.” She sighed and jostled him. “We have a world to save.”

With a flash of blue, the pair vanished from the tower.

News of the failed Radoch scouting group eventually made it back to the main invading fleet. Preliminary scanning determined that the world no longer existed. Other than mocking the deceased as incompetent fools, no one mourned the loss of life.

The fleet had grown in size over the last couple of months it had spent slowly grinding through the intangible Sea. Sanctuary was close, and those seeking the golden goose of immortality salivated at the ripe meal lying right before them.

Numbering over a million, this was the largest force the Sea or any System had ever seen.

Only one planet stood in their way of having control over the entire universe.

They would win at any cost.
