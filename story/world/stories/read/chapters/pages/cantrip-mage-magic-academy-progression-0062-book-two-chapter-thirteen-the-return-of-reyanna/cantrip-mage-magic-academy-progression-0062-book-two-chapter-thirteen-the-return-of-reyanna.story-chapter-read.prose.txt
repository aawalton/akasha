Eola spent the morning watching as the rest of her Spellsword Tactics class practiced distance-closing and flanking maneuvers against a battle mage. One student in each group of four acted as the battle mage, using Bright Ball to simulate their powerful magic and a long pole to act as their heavy weapon. The other three used whatever tactics they chose, whether swordplay or Ordered Magic, to close the gap and ‘kill’ the battle mage. After thirty minutes, a team finally managed to kill their target without ‘losing’ a spellsword—which seemed in line with Maestro Yarrowbloom’s expectations, based on his expression.

As the class applauded, booed, and heckled each other’s performances, Eola couldn’t help but notice how badly her classmates planned their assaults. Maybe it was the three spellswords trying to work together, or maybe it was just that most didn’t have experience with battle mages, but they kept making the same mistakes over and over.

But for the most part, her mind wasn’t on the fighting. She stroked Atta’s fur between the cat’s shoulder blades and thought.

There was the notebook, of course. She hadn’t made as much progress as the cat in her lap wanted her to, and she knew it. Atta had made that clear once Colin and Patrice had left. Something—some key to understanding magic—was inside of that notebook, and her familiar insisted that she had to know it. So far, Atta hadn’t led her too far astray. Eola doubted this would be any different. Messier, maybe, but not different.

Then there was Patrice. Eola had spent most of the class with her spellbook open, the page turned to a sketch she barely remembered making—that of Inner Vision. The spell wasn’t overwhelmingly complex. At First Order, the four modifying marks should have been easy to track down. And, indeed, she’d found the Personal and Peacebonded marks, but nothing else. Her sketch, it seemed, had been hasty. She remembered that day—being used as an example in front of the entire class by Madame Reyanna.

The scarlet-colored gnome had the answers she needed. But Y’aer’s curse, it’d be hard to go talk to her about them.

But while the spellbook was open for most of Spellsword Tactics, Eola’s mind was elsewhere. She had a lot of thinking to do, and most of it was about Colin. He’d kissed her, and she had to decide how she felt about that. On the one hand, they’d been courting for quite a few weeks now, and they’d spent a lot of time together. And she did like him. But on the other hand…

Was there another hand?

Y’aer…her friends had been pushing for this for a while. This was something couples did. And they’d slept together already, crammed in that tent—though Roth had been there and Eola had been hurting and cold. She was pretty sure that wasn’t how it was supposed to be.

Eola thought about it for a long time, and eventually, she reached three conclusions.

First, it was time to finish the notebook at any cost, save her progress on the ensorcelled wand.

Second, she’d very much like to get kissed by Colin again sometime—preferably when she could return it instead of standing there looking like a fish breathing air.

And third, if she wanted even a chance of seeing what was wrong with Patrice, she’d have to swallow her pride, gather every speck of humility she possessed, and visit the one person at Varin’s Academy who could not only help her with Inner Vision, but offer some advice on her friend’s problem.

It was just a shame that the gnome hated her so much.

Considering how similar Madame Reyanna’s office was to the last time Eola had visited it, it had changed a lot.

The last time she’d been here, Eola had been in serious trouble. She’d also been framed. Catrine had told Madame Reyana a story about their highly volatile relationship, and the gnome had swallowed it wholeheartedly. It had been just one more bump in an almost equally rocky relationship between Eola and the professor.

Fortunately, she was here alone today. The thick blanket over the door held back most of the incense in the air, and the overwhelming scent of cinnamon was mixed with a sweeter one, one so cloying it all but overwhelmed Eola. She found herself happy she’d left her thick cloak hanging in the hall, with her notebook in its pocket, and only brought her spellbook and robes into the sweltering office.

But the professor’s desk…that was very different. It was covered in papers and almost as messy as Instructor Tarik’s usually was.

“Come in, come in,” Madame Reyanna said from her quarters. “I’ve got a tea on, and I’d be happy to pour you…a…oh, it’s you.”

“Yes, it’s me,” Eola said.

The room was all but silent, save for the crackling of the fire in the hearth.

“Can I come in?” Eola asked from the door after a few awkward seconds. “I have questions about a spell you taught last term in Mana Studies.”

“I have far too much to do to spend time on a student who’s neither one of my apprentices nor deigned to enroll in any of my courses.” Madame Reyanna sniffed and climbed the steps up to her chair behind the large oak desk.

“It’s about Inner Vision.”

“Mana lines? You’re finally coming around, then? Sit down.” The gnome sketched a spell, and a teakettle and a pair of porcelain teacups appeared. She waved at Eola. “Pour those. I have reading to do.”

As Eola obediently poured the tea—a spiced mix with a cinnamon and clove scent to it—the gnome held up one of the papers. “Do you know about the mess with the Cult of Y’aer?”

“Not at all,” Eola replied. The letter bore the Cult’s seal at its end, stamped in wax. So did many of the others, though they all had different handwriting.

“Y’aer save me from the ignorant. I’ll educate you on that, too, since you’ve managed to stay uninformed. Now, what’s this about Inner Vision?”

“Right.” Eola gathered herself and launched into her explanation.

When she finished, Madame Reyanna nodded briskly. “And you want me to sketch the spell for you again so you can try to learn it in your own bizarre way? Then cast it on another student unsupervised?”

“Exactly.”

Eola didn’t have a great deal of hope, so she was surprised when the gnome did, in fact, sketch the spell, then slid the parchment across the desk. “Make sure you get it right. Who knows what strange errors your magical methods would introduce?”

“And…about Patrice?”

“Lady Clerk? Are you going to cast this on her? Good. That girl skipped the lesson on Inner Vision, and I was very curious what her Mana lines looked like, as well as the state of her soul,” Madame Reyanna said. Eola very wisely did not remind the professor that, as far as she was concerned, Mana lines were highly theoretical and didn’t exist except in a metaphysical sense. “If you’d kindly report back to me with what you see, I’d appreciate it. She’s such an interesting case, isn’t she?”

“Interesting? How so?” Eola asked. Then she shook her head. “Actually, I was wondering if you had any idea what to look for when I cast it on her.”

“Ha! If I had any clue, I’d have offered to help her more. House Clerk isn’t a major player, but nobility is nobility, and Catrine has some…well, it’s not my place to say much about other students.” Madame Reyanna nodded solemnly, then proceeded to do just that. “Now, Lady Clerk…she’s an interesting one. If I had to hazard a guess based on the notes Lan’s shared with me, I’d estimate she has the second or third most potential for soul growth out of any student in Varin’s Academy.”

“Who’s first?”

“Don’t make me say it. Unfortunately, she has no interest or ability to work on her soul, and I’m only guessing, as I said. Do you know how magic moves through the body?”

Eola shrugged. She’d never learned Mana lines as a child—her mom and dad hadn’t seen any reason to teach her that stepping stone to casting, and they wouldn’t have helped with her broken attunement, anyway.

“Right. Lesson time.” The gnome produced a chalkboard from nowhere and began drawing a rough stick figure—or at least, so it appeared at first. As Eola watched, the gnome added more and more details, tracing curving lines that all flowed out from the figure’s stomach, just below where its navel would be. Every line extended through the body, but almost all of them eventually terminated at either the hands or feet.

“Mana flows through a mage’s body. This is true no matter what your parents taught you. Mana lines are real, whether they exist or not. All flow starts with focusing on Mana lines. Exhaustion both builds your soul’s capacity for Mana and taxes your ability to move Mana through your conduit and into—“

“I’m sorry. Your conduit?” Eola interrupted.

Madame Reyanna sighed dramatically. “Yes, you silly young woman, your conduit. Every soul has a conduit. It’s critical for Mana development.”

“Is this another one of those theoretical parts?”

Madame Reyanna’s eyes narrowed dramatically. “Miss Lemiene.”

“Sorry, Professor. It’s associated with Mana lines, then?”

The gnome looked slightly mollified, and she nodded. “Yes. The conduit is connected directly to the soul, and all Mana lines emerge from there. You’d know that if you’d stayed in class during that lecture. From the conduit, Mana follows the line of least resistance—which, for most mages, is the line associated with the attunement the mage uses.”

“How does that square with the research on…you know what, never mind. Go on.”

Eola listened intently as the gnome continued talking on and on about how Mana lines worked, jotting notes on a sheet of scrap parchment and kicking herself for not bringing her notebook. Her mouth dried out from the room’s sheer heat and parched air, but she kept listening until, after nearly fifteen minutes of uninterrupted lecture, Madame Reyanna finally stopped.

The gnome’s eyebrow raised, and she shot an appraising eye at Eola. “You know, I never disliked you that much.”

“M’hmmm,” Eola said noncommittally.

“It’s true,” the professor lied. She tapped a finger on one of the letters still covering her desk. “You should know that the Revisionists recently took over the Cult of Y’aer. You’d be very wise to know about Y’aer’s Triad and the Fourth Gift, and how that faction of the Cult integrates them into their beliefs. Under their current High Cultists, they’ve gotten a lot more aggressive about their prothletization of Ordered Magic as Y’aer’s most recent gift from their Chest, and you, Miss Lemiene, are—“

“Not using Ordered Magic,” Eola said softly. “Who are their High Cultists?”

“No idea,” Madame Reyanna replied promptly. “But you’d best be careful. Now, get out of here and let me enjoy my tea in peace.”

With her painful meeting with Madame Reyanna over—Eola couldn’t believe how ignorant the woman could be about a theory that did not align with practice at any but the most basic of levels—she retreated to her dorm room and began dissecting Inner Vision.

The spell was eye-shaped, the Old Alemic forming the entire structure while the four modifying marks, including the two she’d missed. Echoing—and Stilled, to her surprise—were in Forsooth Script just as she’d expected. Unlike Personal and Peacebonded, however, those two had been concealed almost perfectly within the place where iris met eyelid, and without Madame Reyanna’s sketch, she wouldn’t have seen them at all.

The way Personal interacted with Echoing was critical to the spell’s function. Normally, a Personal modifying mark bound the spell to the caster only. But for some reason Eola couldn’t figure out, the combination of Echoing, Personal, and the structure of the Child’s Magic itself allowed it to affect a different target—almost like the Personal mark wasn’t there at all.

Once she’d isolated the Old Alemic cantrip—it was Bright Ball, because of course it was, it was always Bright Ball—Eola got to work designing the spell-chain for efficiency. She wouldn’t be able to cast it until Patrice made an appearance, but she could at least be ready for when her friend did show up. Stilled would have to be last, of course, and Personal first, but it didn’t turn out to matter which order she chained the other two marks in.

Halfway through, Atta hopped up onto the desk. “You know the expression ‘An hour in the library can save two weeks of experiments?’”

“What’s your point? And are you about to call me a stupid girl again?” Eola asked.

“No. You’re working hard to help your friend, and that friend is one way to learn what you need to know. But—“ A hacking fit interrupted Atta, though she smiled smugly even as she choked on her own words. “Anyway, and not related, you should continue—“

“Working on the notebook, right?” Eola asked. “I’m making progress. Tonight, I’ll be translating the Forsooth sections as quickly as I can. Once I have those, I’ll focus on the Southron and Orantic. They’re functional languages with rules they generally follow.”

“And the Gnomic and Elvish Cursive?” Atta asked.

Eola shuddered theatrically, and Atta laughed and licked her paw. “I understand, but—“ Another fit cut the cat off. “Curse Y’aer for their gifts, anyway. I can’t explain why, but you’ll need a complete translation.”

“You know, sometimes I wonder just what you are.”

“I know. I can’t answer that, and you know it.”

As she stood and closed her spellbook, then tucked it into her cloak pocket, Eola stopped. “I know, but maybe you can explain this one thing. Why does Inner Vision work on other people? It should be governed by the Personal mark.”

“Ah, you noticed, then.” Atta rubbed up against her hand, begging for scratches. “It’s integral to the spell, and its use in this kind of case is consistent across Ordered Magic, but I can’t say more than that. I’ve done enough coughing for today.”

“Fine. Keep your secrets,” Eola said.

“Speaking of secrets, you haven’t told me about the boy yet.”

Y’aer’s curse.

“What are you going to do about him?” Atta asked.

“I’m going to—“

“Because he’s a distraction from what matters.”

“Are you going to—“

“You should get rid of him.”

Eola clamped her hand over Atta’s mouth—earning a vicious set of claw marks for it—and said, “Yes, I know that’s what you think. I’m not going to listen to you, though. Colin’s good for me. I like spending time with him.”

“And kissing him.” Atta’s voice dripped with disgust.

Eola glared. “Yes, I think I will enjoy that, too. Now, let me get some translation done, or help me translate it.”

Atta didn’t say a word, but she shook her head as she hopped off the desk, then climbed up the ladder to Eola’s loft. Eola shrugged, trying not to feel too angry, and started working on the Forsooth Script sections of Varin’s journal.

She was still working on that two hours later, when someone knocked on her suite’s door. When she went to check on it, no one was there, but a professor was halfway down the hallway, kneeling to put something in front of another door. Eola looked down at the four identical letters sitting there, each with Varin’s Academy’s seal coupled with a second one.

The Cult of Y’aer.
