Borevin had seen a lot of things in her life as a priestess—many of them extraordinary, and a few of them even downright abominable. But she had never seen anything like this.

For a man to bond with a reanimated corpse and gain such immense power from it—breaking through not one but two spirit locks at once. Defying the laws of the system itself!

It should have been impossible. A spirit lock was not something that could be broken without fracturing the soul itself. Yet, the boy remained alive and well, radiating an eerie air of power and despair. His irises—once a vivid purple highlighted by crimson—now had a ghostly band of gray and crimson ringing them.

He hadn’t gained any other physical manifestation aside from the change in eye color, but one glance at him was enough to know that something was deeply wrong with his make-up—not in an evil way, but in a way that was just undeniable.

Borevin shuddered in aversion. As unwilling as the merge had seemed, the empath certainly didn’t seem too sad about it. Had she been the target of this unfortunate event, tainting her soul with necromancy, she wouldn’t have had the courage to face her enemies, let alone her family.

How would her soul ever find rest in the great beyond if she ever died? How would Tihamun ever embrace her tainted soul and bring it into his bosom?

Her aversion spiked, and she shuddered again, noting with no small comfort that she wasn’t the only one ill at ease with the latest development. However, even as disgust simmered within her at the boy’s excited chatter, a part of her bubbled with an endless series of questions—curiosity, the bane of all truths, raising its ugly head.

The Skalk—whatever it truly was—had just shattered a core rule of advancement, breaking a lock never before broken. Even in her aversion, Borevin couldn’t help but wonder how such a thing was possible. Was it replicable? If she got herself a Skalk, could she save her dying cousin from his spirit lock?

Each question raised another, and slowly, very slowly, Borevin found herself inching forward, eyes narrowed in curiosity. But she wasn’t the only one. Aldric’s evolution had been witnessed by everyone present—the Runes, the telepaths, Artemis, Warp, and dozens of others who were at the same time entertaining the same questions she was.

Damien Rune reached him first, a thin smile on his face as he spoke. “What a marvelous achievement you’ve unlocked. Can you explain in detail just how you managed it?”

“I truly don’t understand; I just connected with it.” Aldric smiled cluelessly, an action and expression that irritated Borevin to no small extent, as she too was very interested in that response.

However, before anyone else could grill the boy further, Daruk masterfully directed the interrogation with a question of his own.

“Can you manifest it?” He asked with a smile. “If the Skalk has become your familiar, then you should be able to manifest it like Aodhán can.”

Borevin paused, watching intently as Aodhán began whispering inaudibly to the boy. More people gathered, their earlier disgust now replaced by something new. Fascination. Anticipation.

It hummed through the air, tangible enough that even she could feel it, or perhaps, it was brimming within her. Borevin refused to acknowledge the vile emotion, her eyes narrowing even as she stepped closer, just enough to grasp Aodhán’s words.

…there, that tight knot nestled within your core. Can you sense it?”

Aldric didn’t respond. Instead, he closed his eyes tightly, eyebrows scrunched in concentration. Borevin felt her heart tighten as she waited for something to happen. Anything at all.

And then Aldric smiled—a smile so wide and joyous—Borevin almost forgot to remind herself to be disgusted. He opened his eyes a moment later, joy radiating from them as he whispered. “I can feel it. And it’s the most beautiful thing I’ve ever seen.”

Borevin wasn’t quite sure when her mouth opened and she asked. “Please, Aldric, can we see it too?”

Aldric felt the knot pulse within him, faintly luminous, like a heart struggling to remember how to beat. It was heavy, oppressive, and yet… familiar. Despite its original make-up, the knot wasn’t just a ball of despair. It was sorrow, devotion, hunger, and a strange, lingering tenderness that almost felt… warm.

At Borevin’s request, he reached toward it—not mentally, but emotionally—and the knot moved, a guttural yet soft sound emanating from it like a breath. The sound wasn’t entirely internal. It resonated through every fragment of his being—wind and death undulating through his spirit in a never-ending dance of frost and decay, but also peace.

It was such a strange feeling to have something alive and breathing inside him, and with a smile of absolute joy, Aldric willed the Skalk to reveal itself.

The air stilled immediately. Even the wind held its breath.

And then, faintly, like a shimmer, wisps of gray essence rose from his shoulders in a slow, deliberate motion. The motes gathered and condensed, taking form, bone by bone, flicker by flicker, until a skeletal frame wrapped in translucent mist appeared, twin pools of purple and crimson gleaming from its eyes.

It was the Skalk. But it had changed drastically.

Gone was its aura of vengeance and rage. Gone were its ghastly contours and spindly limbs. Its presence—once eerie and suffocating—now felt fragile and new. The once frightening creature was gone, replaced by a ghostly specter that almost felt human.

Its skeletal arms had been draped with what seemed like cloaks, and its face was hidden completely behind a cowl. What little of its skeletal body that was visible underneath the ‘cloaks’ glimmered faintly with golden veins very similar to the Lichtenberg scars on Aodhán’s skin, and a faint aura of peace radiated from it—like the air above a battle with only one man standing.

However, the most obvious change in the Skalk was the text floating above its head.

[Deviant Skalk—Tier 1]

“Stars above?” Aodhán breathed, unable to believe what his eyes were seeing. Aldric himself couldn’t believe it.

It made sense now where all that spiritual energy had come from and why it had seemed so overwhelming. Only a few minutes ago, the Skalk had stood at the 100th tier of advancement, radiating an immense aura of vengeance and despair, and now that aura was completely gone, reduced to a faint, unthreatening spark.

The Skalk had funneled all its essence and spiritual energy from the 100th tier into his spirit, paying him back for keeping it alive. Paying him back with all it’d had.

The thought sparked a wave of happiness within him, and Aldric raised a hand to his chest almost as if he could touch it. Emotion surged out of him, unseen, rippling across everyone gathered in a synchronization so massive and undetectable it was practically mind control. His joy became their joy, spreading like wildfire through the crowd without a single person realizing they'd been touched.

It was his Tyrant of Boundless Empathy skill, but scaled up to such an extent that even Mythics couldn’t escape from it. With Aldric now at the advanced class, and the emotional resonance of the Skalk undulating with his spirit, Aldric had unknowingly become a walking emotional disaster.

His emotions infected all those around him like a virus, forcing them all to feel the same thing he was feeling. And unfortunately, for the priestess of Tihamun, she was the first to break.

“Oh, what the heck!” Borevin cursed as her eyes watered from the intense emotion. “We are free. We should be celebrating and praising Tihamun for life.” She grabbed Magda and hugged her tightly. “We made it, Magdalene. Glory to Tihamun.”

What followed was a massive shift in countenance as people broke down with tears of happiness, hugging each other and crying as emotions surged uncontrollably through them. Aodhán, Daruk, and Aldric hugged again, tears flowing in genuine but amplified happiness. Cyrus and Mirith hugged tightly. The telepaths hugged. Artemis and Warp hugged.

Everyone hugged and cried in celebration of their freedom. For many, it was genuine, and for those whom it wasn’t, well, thanks to Aldric, it had just become genuine.

And for someone like Aodhán, who had almost given up hope on living to see a new day, it was almost too much to bear.

He hugged Aldric and Daruk tightly, unwilling to ever let them go.

So much had happened in the last few weeks since their capture, within Abyssos and out, but none compared to the joy he felt in this moment. He was free. Jethro was dead, and he could finally see his family again.

It couldn’t have been more perfect.

And then Tyrus joined, and Aodhán realized he’d been terribly wrong.

The fire awakened added a cozy warmth to their tight hug that practically made Aodhán melt. A lump rose in his throat, and the tears he’d been trying to hold back fell.

“Oh, how I missed you all.” He sobbed, hugging them tighter. “I thought I’d never see you all again. Thank you. Thank you for finding us.”

Daruk nodded, eyes bright with tears. “Me too. I love you, Aodhán. And I love you too, Aldric. I don’t quite love you, Tyrus, but I like you very, very much.”

Tyrus wiped away tears. “I like you too very, very much.”

Rather than speak, Aldric drew them tighter, feeling a huge lump in his throat. Tears pooled in his eyes, and he let them fall freely. Not only had he found his brothers during this insane mission, but he’d also gained a breakthrough like never before.

His brothers may have gained their freedom, but Aldric knew he gained something even bigger.

Despite prolonging the hug, his brothers let him have the moment, patting his back gently and simply basking in the presence of each other. When they eventually pulled away, Aodhán turned to hug Destra, who latched onto him like a lifeline. “Thank you, Aodhán. Thank you for everything.”

“You don’t have to thank me—” Aodhán began, but Destra cut him off.

“No, I do. I wouldn’t have survived the second level without you. None of us would have. We all owe you and Varéc our lives.”

“Yes, we do.” Sebastian spoke from behind them, and Aodhán looked back to find the Unorian noble staring at him in gratitude. “Thank you, Aodhán. I am in your debt—”

“No,” Aodhán shook his head. “I am in your debt. You had the idea about the abyssal pins. Without you, we would all have perished.”

“But you had the dragon idea,” Mirith spoke, coming closer. “If you hadn’t bargained a deal with Serythion, we wouldn’t even have survived the blood phoenix, so yes, thank you, Aodhán. I owe you one, and no, I do not say this lightly.”

Aodhán stared at them all and finally nodded. “Thank you all too for being my rock when I needed it. For helping me when I was down, and for carrying me when I was weak.” A memory of Brennon and Baxter flashed across his mind, and his voice cracked. “Thank you all for everything, and for making it this far. Brennon and Baxter would be proud.”

His words brought fresh tears to drying eyes, and another round of hugging ensued. An aura of gratefulness hung thickly in the air, and in response to the atmosphere, Aodhán pulled out a circular object from his spatial space and handed it to Destra.

It was the Aurorafrost Gem he’d found buried in the ice.

He’d wanted to give it to Daruk earlier, but after all he’s learned about Destra in the last few days, he felt she needed it more.

When Destra saw the gem, her eyes widened. “You said you wanted to give this to your brother.”

“I changed my mind,” Aodhán shrugged. “I think you deserve it more.”

Tears welled in her eyes once again, and in a sudden motion, she threw herself at him and hugged him tight. “By winter gods, you don’t know how much this means to me.”

“You’re welcome,” Aodhán chuckled, and when she pulled away, he smirked. “I still don’t see how you and Daruk are fated, though. Have you even spoken to him?”

“I spoke to him during your battle for the grand abyssal pins.” Destra frowned. “But as helpful as our conversation was, I think I misinterpreted my visions. Perhaps I wasn’t fated to meet Daruk at all, but to meet his brother—you—who would then give me this treasure, forever changing the trajectory of my life.”

Aodhán blinked. “That’s… oddly specific.”

Destra nodded solemnly. “Prophecies usually are, right before they make no sense.”

Aodhán squinted at her. “Shouldn’t the vision have just shown you me, then?”

Destra shrugged, entirely serious. “Visions work in mysterious ways.”

“Or,” Aodhán countered, “maybe the vision just didn’t want to admit it was wrong.”

Destra gasped in mock offense. “Are you calling my vision prideful?”

“I’m calling it vague and dramatic,” Aodhán said. “You two would get along nicely.”

Destra smacked his shoulder, pretending to glare. “Says the man who made a deal with a dragon.”

“That’s different,” Aodhán said, fighting a grin. “At least I read the fine print.”

Destra scoffed. “You? I doubt you’d ever read anything that didn’t explode afterwards.”

Aodhán couldn’t help it—he laughed, deep and free, and Destra joined in, her laughter bubbling bright and unrestrained. Something sparked between them in that moment—subtle, warm, electric, and dangerously close to longing. Their eyes met as laughter faded, and they realized just how close they were to each other.

Destra’s breath hitched, and her voice turned soft. “I’ll miss you, Aodhán. I know we don’t know much about each other, but I will miss you.”

“And I, you.” Aodhán nodded slowly, his voice a whisper as he felt a flutter in his belly. He knew what this was.

It was attraction.

Now that he wasn’t fighting for his life, he could finally appreciate how beautiful Destra was—and it seemed she too was doing the same.

The space between them suddenly felt fragile, like a breath held. Her hands brushed against his, and the faint warmth of her touch sent a shiver racing up his spine. For a heartbeat, neither of them moved, both caught in that quiet, impossible pull that followed survival and relief.

And then Destra leaned in, eyes closed, snow-white hair blowing softly around her. Aodhán didn’t hesitate. He leaned in, a quiet smile tugging at his lips as the noise of their friends faded to the background.

However, what should have been an intimate moment between them was abruptly ruined when Aldric suddenly appeared beside them, a wide smile on his face as he said. “Boy, don’t you two look cozy.”

Aodhán let out a frustrated sigh and turned to glare at his brother. “I love you, Aldric, but this isn’t exactly what I would call right timing.”

Aldric grinned. “Oh, it’s perfect timing. I texted Mum and Dad already, and they're ecstatic. Mum wants to throw a party to celebrate your freedom. Destra here could come. Everyone could come.”

Destra's eyes widened in excitement, and she squealed. “Oh, that’s amazing. A party for us all before we return to our various kingdoms. This is great news.”

And yes, it should have been great news, but the mention of Unrid and Synové sparked a dread that Aodhán had nursed right from the moment of his capture.

His smile faltered. The warmth drained from his face, and the weight of all that had happened in the past few hours suddenly crashed down on him like a mountain. In all the excitement, he had forgotten what waited for him back in Ragnarok.

Too many people already knew about his status as a transmigrant. And after the sheer scale of his icon, there was no more denying it. Going back to Ragnarok was a risk that would only end in his death. He had thought extensively about it during his time in Abyssos, and the only solution he saw was leaving.

A fresh start in a new place where no one knew him. Perhaps some backwater sector of Calodan where he could hide in plain sight. A place where he could assume a new identity.

Leaving Lutia altogether would have been more preferable, but he would need a Calamity for that, and he didn’t have one at his beck and call.

The point, however, was that he couldn’t go home. Not if he intended to keep his life. His eyes watered as he realized that even though he had gained his freedom, he wasn’t quite free yet. The life he had known with Unrid and Synové was gone. If he went back now, it would be the end.

His vision clouded with tears, and he whispered. “I can’t go home, Aldric.”

“What do you mean you can’t go home?” Aldric asked softly, his expression a mask of dread as if he too had seen this coming. When Aodhán didn’t respond, his eyes watered and he nodded. “It’s true, isn’t it? The rumors?”

“What rumors?” Destra began, but she never quite finished the statement before her attention was suddenly diverted elsewhere, a quick burst of joy sending her toward Seojun, who was tearfully hugging his sisters.

Aodhán nodded. “It is. I’m sorry, Aldric.”

“Why didn’t you tell me?” Aldric’s voice broke as his expression grew even more strained. “Why didn’t you tell us?”

Aodhán shook his head. “I told Daruk, but Aldric, that’s not the point here. I can’t stay. I need to leave, and soon.” He glanced back at the war wrapping up in the distance. “If the agents of the awakened council find me here, my life is over.”

“They will not find you.” Aldric growled protectively, more than ready to fight the world on Aodhán’s behalf. “I just found you, and I will not let anyone take you away again.”

“I’m afraid that is a circumstance beyond your control, Aldric.” A voice echoed from behind them, and they turned around immediately, eyes wide and energy brimming in preparation for battle. All of that vanished the moment Aldric laid his eyes on the man standing before them.

Zarxan Al’ruhn. The Calamity who had ripped three other Calamities apart in seconds.

Fear flooded through him, but before he could speak or react or do anything at all, the world around them froze, and the man spoke. “I’m not against you, Aodhán. In fact, I came to Lutia to find and save you. Aldric can testify to that.”

Aodhán glanced at Aldric, and he nodded. “He was the one who ripped the console thing apart to save you.”

Zarxan nodded and fixed his runic gaze on Aodhán, a subtle pressure emanating from him. “You do not know me, Aodhán, but I know you. I came across your will imprint in the origin plane, and I recognized it very distinctly as similar to that of my master, Az’marthon Ranok.”

The mention of Az’marthon’s name caused Aodhán’s eyes to widen. “You… you knew Az’marthon?”

“I didn’t just know him, Aodhán. I was his apprentice, back when that was still a thing.” Zarxan smiled. “I left Lutia to Gordon after his ascension many years ago, so when I sensed his signature in your will imprint, I just had to come see you for myself.”

Aodhán stuttered in amazement. He couldn’t believe that anyone who knew Az’marthon could still be alive, but then again, the watchers had lived for nearly a millennium.

He found his voice a moment later and bowed. “Thank you, Calamity, for saving me and my friends. But I fear it’s only a case of frying pan to fire for me.”

Zarxan nodded knowingly. “You will never be safe in Lutia. No matter where you hide, they will hunt you down in minutes. They did find you here after all, despite all the measures the children of the watch took to keep this place hidden.”

“What are you proposing then?” Aldric asked, his expression tight.

Zarxan smiled sadly. “I think you already know what I’m proposing. Gordon is very different from Lutia. We have our flaws, but it doesn’t include the killing of transmigrants. You will be safe there.”

Aodhán wanted to latch onto the opportunity, but if there was one thing he had learned from all that had happened in the last few months, it was that he couldn’t trust anyone. How could he know that this Calamity was saying the truth—

“He is,” Aldric whispered, voice trembling. “I can’t feel his emotions per se, but I can feel the truth in his words.”

Zarxan's expression sobered. “I understand your hesitance, Aodhán. Even commend it. But as it stands, you only have two options. Run and be hunted down, or come with me and be free.”

As simple as those options sounded, they weighed heavily on Aodhán. The first offered no guarantee of survival, and the second meant a complete upheaval of his life. If he left Lutia, he would never see his friends and family again—not Aldric, not Daruk, not Yurin, not Andrew, not Yuna, not Synové, not Unrid.

He would be starting out in an entirely new continent, one he knew next to nothing about, with only this Calamity for a guide. But as such situations usually went, Aodhán never got enough time to think about it.

The Calamity glanced at the horizon and grimaced. “It seems we are out of time, Aodhán.”

He released his hold on the flow of time, and reality snapped back into motion. Almost instantly, a portal tore open above them. Several members of the alliance poured out from it—soldiers, champions, the four ambassadors, and hundreds of awakened council agents whose gazes honed in on him immediately.

Aodhán felt his heart shudder at the sight, but before any one of them could move, a commanding voice echoed. “No one move an inch until I say so.”

The voice belonged to General Lucas, whose expression flickered between displeasure and distaste as he walked to the front of the line. He didn’t want to do this, and the moment his gaze landed on Zarxan, he nearly let his relief show.

“Greetings, Calamity,” he bowed. “We cannot thank you enough for helping us with the watchers.”

“I did not do it for you.” Zarxan replied curtly.

The tension of the moment spiked, and the happiness that had loomed over everyone was sapped away instantly.

General Lucas hesitated a moment before continuing. “Be that as it may, you still deserve our thanks. But can we ask why you’re here?”

Zarxan smiled then—a smile so sharp and protective it caused many to flinch. With slow steps, he walked forward to stand just beside Aodhán. “I think you know why I am here.”

From the other side of the gathering, Daruk’s expression shifted to dread, and he immediately rushed over. He’d barely reached Aodhán, however, when General Lucas replied with a statement that took the current tension and dialed it up to a hundred.

“I’m sorry, Calamity, but if you intend to take the boy, I fear I cannot allow you to do that.”

But Zarxan’s smile only widened. “I would love to see you all try and stop me.” He glanced at Aodhán. “This is not a kidnap. If Aodhán doesn’t want to go with me, then he doesn’t have to. Perhaps, you should try convincing him.”

All eyes came to rest on Aodhán in that moment, but Aodhán had already made his decision. Or perhaps fate had made it for him. He couldn't run now, not with hundreds of agents and soldiers out to get him. The only way he could leave this place alive was with Zarxan.

That realization, however bitter, sharpened his resolve, and with a shuddering breath, Aodhán spoke. “I will go with the Calamity.”

“That is a mistake.” An agent stepped out angrily. “This Calamity has trespassed on Lutian soil without notification. He is a criminal, and if you follow him, you become one too.”

“Ignore him.” Zarxan’s aura flared in warning and pulled Aodhán behind him. “You’ve made the right decision, Aodhán. You may not know it now, but you will soon. Now I fear it is time for you to say your goodbyes. Our friends here will wait quietly while you do so.”

Tension rippled through the alliance ranks as they watched Aodhán pull Aldric and Daruk into another hug. Many of the other harvests watched cluelessly, except a few like Mirith, Cyrus, and Serenya, who seemed to have gleaned an insight into what was going on.

No one moved though, not even the agents, lest they trigger a Calamity to violence.

Behind him, Aodhán, Aldric, and Daruk sobbed as they said their last goodbyes. No words came until Aodhán finally pulled away, his tear-stained face a mask of grief and pain. “Tell Mother and Father I love them.”

“We will,” Daruk cried, wishing he could follow. “We will tell them.”

“And Yuna too,” Aodhán continued, fishing into his spatial storage to pull out the voltrite ore Artemis had given him. “Give her this. She’ll need it.”

Aldric accepted the ore without words, trying to speak yet failing. He had found Aodhán, but now he would have to go home to tell his mother that Aodhán wouldn’t be coming home. His fists tightened around the ore, and with a strained expression, he pulled Aodhán into another hug.

“We love you, Aodhán,” he finally choked out. “We will miss you. If you can ever come back, you’ll have a place with us. Never forget it.”

“Never.” Aodhán shook his head. “I’ll never forget it.”

He pulled away a moment later and turned to glance at the Calamity. “I’m ready.”

Zarxan nodded. “Then it’s time.” He glanced at Aodhán. “You will be able to return one day. Once you reach the Mythic tier, no one can take your mark any longer. You’ll be of no use to them any longer. You’ll be free.”

“Tier 100,” Aodhán breathed, and Zarxan nodded.

“Tier 100. For you, it could be a few months or a few years. But what’s important for now is that you live to see it.”

Aodhán nodded, his face hardening with resolve. Tier 100. With dedication, he could do it in a few years. And then he would return. He would see his family again. He would see Daruk and Aldric again. He would see Unrid and Synové.

He wouldn’t have to run or hide again. He would be truly free.

He glanced back at Aldric and Daruk, his jaw tight with determination. “I will come back, brothers. As soon as I reach the Mythic class, I will return. Tell Mum I’ll come back.”

Aldric smiled through his pain. “We’ll tell her. Now go.”

Aodhán nodded, his gaze lingering on his brothers for another moment, memorizing their faces, every contour, every line, every strand of hair. The memory burned itself into his mind like a scar that promised never to heal, and when he was done, he whispered again. “I’m ready.”

Zarxan nodded and, with a flick of his fingers, tore a swirling portal of liquid plasma into existence. Lightning arced around it like sparks against the frame of reality, and from within it thunder boomed.

Aodhán stared at the portal for a long moment, glanced back at the waiting alliance, and then—

“Aodhán!” Destra’s voice cut through the silence, clear and unwavering. Their eyes met across the crowd, and Aodhán saw that she was crying. “Be safe.”

That was all she said, but those two words speared through his heart like a knife. His throat tightened. He couldn’t speak, so he nodded once. Lightning crackled. Thunder called. And without further hesitation, Aodhán stepped into the portal.

A new chapter of his life had just opened.

And yet, it felt like an ending.
