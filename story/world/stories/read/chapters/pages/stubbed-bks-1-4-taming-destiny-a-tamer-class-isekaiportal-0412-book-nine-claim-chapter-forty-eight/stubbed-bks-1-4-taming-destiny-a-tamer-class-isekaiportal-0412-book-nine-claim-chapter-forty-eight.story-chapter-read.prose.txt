When I do open my eyes, I’m surprised and a little perturbed to see just how many bleeding and dying creatures lie around us. From crab-like crustaceans to odd lung-fish-like beings that seem to have flopped more than walked onto the beach, from seal-like creatures to strange spiny-shelled beast, it seems as though the ocean has vomited its most annoyed denizens onto the land – to quickly meet their ends at the blades of our guards.

I vaguely remember, during the course of the ritual, feeling the disturbed creatures when we were reorganizing the seabed. This must be the result: instead of running or swimming away, these creatures decided to attack the source of their sudden discomfort. Or perhaps these are simply the ones who stayed and chose aggression, while many more fled.

For our sake, I’m very glad for my forethought in organising a large party of fighters to protect us – I strongly suspected that we would be vulnerable during the ritual. The warriors are grim-faced and clearly tired from the intense fights, their weapons covered in different shades of blood and ichor.

Still, I also regret that we started our stay on this continent with a massacre. They attacked us, sure, but we’re the ones who came over and started destroying their homes. My eyes linger on a crab-like creature that’s still twitching weakly. A passing Warrior clearly sees the same thing and buries his ichor-stained blade in its carapace. It stills.

I swallow and turn my eyes away. This is the way things are in this world. Beasts kill humans to progress, but also each other. So do humans. The Warriors who have fought to defend us while we were conducting the ritual may even level up from their efforts, making them better at defending us later.

Anyway, even if we’d found a different way of bringing the ships in closer to shore, who can say whether the beasts would have left us be. It’s my responsibility to keep my people safe – if my negligence meant some of them were ambushed by one of these creatures later, that would have been on me. That’s why we decided to create a proper harbour area even though we’re not certain this is where we will settle – it’s far more defensible and having the ships in closer to land lessens the risk of being attacked from something below while we’re unloading goods.

We decided it was worth the effort, though I’m not sure the original denizens of the area would agree with our conclusion.

Several of the mages exclaim loudly as they rejoin the real world and see the consequences our magic has had on the surrounding area. But the detritus of battle holds their attention only briefly, and it soon drifts further out to sea to look at the results of our ritual.

As I expected from what I’d felt within the magic, the piers look almost man-made, which, in a way, they are, though not in the traditional sense. Already, Josiah’s ship has entered the harbour and is sailing towards a berth. The others are close behind on its tail.

Thank goodness for the low-power direction enchantments, which allow ships to manoeuvre themselves with a delicacy that few Earth ships could match. As big as they are, I don’t fear that they will come to harm against the stone walls.

While I wait for the ships to fully berth, I pull a few chairs from my Inventory and set them out on the sandy beach. The other mages take the invitation gratefully, though I see several sending uneasy glances at the blood-soaked sand that lies between us and the sea. A ritual like that isn’t physically exhausting, but it does take something out of me – I doubt I could do another in a day. Looking around, I suspect I’m not alone in that.

In contrast, Kalanthia seems to be energised. She demands some scratches as her due, but then, even before the ships have all docked, she stands up, almost dancing on her paws.

I’m going to explore, she announces. I don’t make the mistake of thinking she’s asking for permission.

Enjoy yourself, I reply, allowing only a hint of concern to cross the Bond – an expression of my desire that she takes care rather than an implication that she won’t. She receives it in the spirit it’s meant, then takes off up the beach. Several of the fighters make unhappy noises when they lose sight of her after only a few steps – as always, I’m impressed at how quickly an elephant-sized leopard can disappear.

A few minutes later, I feel rested enough to become twitchy myself – there’s so much to do now we’ve arrived.

Pulling a table out of my Inventory, I pull out the maps that we’ve been annotating with every update from a scout. Then, contacting my group of companions, I start adding more details from them as well as updating them on what I felt during the ritual – particularly about the sense of warning I got from the dense collection of trees to the southwest.

I’m glad to hear that they’re being careful and cautious – especially because they’re seeing evidence of Tier threes. A lot of them. Far more, from their reports, than we were used to near the samurans’ village, which itself had more of them nearby than Moriaxans are used to.

I suppose it makes sense – the Energy density here is almost double what it was on Moriax, and significantly more than it was on the sea. Good for progress – but also dangerous.

They also have indicated that the forest is old – very, very old. Artio reports that the trees are more sapient than any he’s ever felt before and warns that his combat ability, and that of any other Plant Mage, will depend on whether they are capable of either winning the allegiance of the plants around them or dominating their will. Interestingly, his words remind me of my own class and the contrast between Tame and Dominate. It makes me wonder whether that’s the reason I never managed to learn Plant-Shaping – because I was expecting it to be more like my elemental Shaping Skills rather than my Taming Skills….

But I push that thought to one side for now. I have enough on my plate at the moment. Plant-Taming will have to wait.

Soon, the ships have docked and everything becomes a flurry of activity as gangways are lowered and people pour off the ship.

Though I know everyone is eager to be off what has, even to me, started to feel like a floating prison, at the moment we’re being strict about who’s allowed off the ships. Only those who are actively involved in helping to determine where we should place our settlement and those to defend them are currently leaving. Because the thing is that we could be attacked at any moment – and probably will. Staying on the ships means it’s easier for people to leave a dangerous situation.

Similarly, we’re not doing much unloading of actual goods. Just like with the non-combatants, we’ll wait until we’ve decided on a permanent settlement. Magic will at least make that easier and quicker when we’ve decided on the location. For now, we’ll continue sleeping on the ships, though we do need to quickly establish a food supply.

But I have a feeling that we’ll stay in this area. We’ve already sent our scouts up and down the coastline a bit, and there’s nowhere that’s much better. What I felt in the ritual just confirms what the scout reports had indicated. We have water not far away, the forest floor feels fertile, and the trees themselves will be excellent material for our production purposes.

“Impressive work,” Layton greets me as he crunches through the sand to stand near the table, looking over my shoulder at the annotations I’ve made to the map.

I send him a wry look.

“Don’t give me false praise – artist was never a career path for me.”

“Oh,” Layton responds, covering a smile with a hand. “I was speaking of the harbour.”

I chuckle in response.

“Then thank you, though I didn’t do it alone.”

“That is the point of having allies, is it not?” he points out. I incline my head to him, granting the point.

I start explaining some of the annotations, then have to repeat them as Josiah and Elarion join too. Valence is the last to the group, understandable since his ship was the last in.

“We’ve already discussed various scenarios in detail on the ship, so I don’t feel that I need to go into depth right now – does anyone disagree?” I ask after I’ve brought everyone up to speed – and added a few more annotations that one of the groups of human scouts has sent in via a mechanical messenger, courtesy of Lady Keshaka. Since neither she nor Tiria are allies, they aren’t part of strategy meetings, but I remind myself to get their thoughts later – they could raise some important considerations.

No one asks for details so I just nod and summarise.

“Good. Then, Elarion – you’ll be investigating the forest with a mixed group of farmers and fighters.” With his family’s odd Ice Druid Class, he’ll be able to give a unique perspective on how to transform the natural wilderness into fertile farmland – sustainably.

Elarion nods curtly.

“Valence, you’ll be in charge of organising the hunting parties. So far, the scouts have indicated that there are tracks of large numbers of Tier two beasts and far more Tier threes than we might expect, including the herbivores, so please make sure that whoever you choose is capable of facing those. We don’t want to lose people at this point.”

“Noted, my lord,” he agrees.

“Josiah, you’re in charge of fishing parties and investigating the rest of this bay as well as setting up.” My lips twist wryly. “We need to know about any Tier threes around – preferably before they start attacking our ships.”

“Again,” Josiah adds.

“Again,” I agree, both of us sharing long-suffering looks. Overall, Josiah’s ship has probably come off worst in the fleet, though mine is a close second. His ship’s enchantments were never as strong as the rest of ours, and the damage my ship suffered at the beginning of the journey left it weak for much of the rest.

“Finally, Layton–”

Abruptly, I’m interrupted by a bellow from the forest.

All of our heads snap towards the source of the noise. A crashing sound heralds the creature’s arrival – a massive bull-like creature, rather similar to one of Nicholas’s Tier Three bonded beasts. But where his is brown and stands as tall as Kalanthia, this one is only half the height and bright green, with plants writhing around it like a living cloak.

It’s big, it’s angry, and it’s a Tier three.

For a moment, we all freeze. And then everyone reacts with the efficiency drilled into them from months of training and from pitched battles at sea. There’s only a slight hesitation due to this being a different setting, but I’m pleased to see the teamwork everyone has learned holds even when the battle is on land rather than water.

The Tier three bull charges straight towards the quickly-assembling line of fighters even while its vines wriggle even faster through the ground and strikes at those behind.

Yet, even as its vines begin causing havoc, our people adapt to the surprise of it entirely bypassing the first line of fighters. Attacks arrow forwards and land, making it bellow again – this time in pain. Though at first successful in making fighters stagger backwards or drop their weapons as the sharpened roots slam into their armour, the vines soon start being deflected by shields.

Quickly regrouping, the back-line fighters press closer to those wielding axes or similar weapons. Trying to attack those groups, the living roots find themselves chopped through before they can land another strike.

And when the bull actually crashes into the line, it finds that they’re not so easily destroyed. Warriors specialising in defensive Skills create a bulwark that stops the bull in its tracks, opening it up for the counterattack of more offence-oriented fighters.

Mages strike, their attacks honed on sea serpents, massive fish, flying jellyfish, and air-wielding birds, each magic-fueled strike making its mark.

The Tier three, impressively, manages to push the Warriors back two steps, but no further. Its vines writhe and attempt to strike at the Warriors’ backs, but those fighting with them easily fend off the attempt to stab them in the back. Three seconds…ten.

And then, perhaps realising that it’s bleeding far too much to be healthy, the bull swings its head from side to side, its unexpected attack goring two unlucky Warriors, Apparently satisfied with the blood its spilled and unwilling to lose more of it, the bull starts stepping backwards, retreating.

But it has left it too late. It hadn’t realised that the weapons used on it have been copiously laced with poison that kills without ruining the meat. Three steps, four steps, and then it stumbles.

Another step.

It falls.

Everyone piles in. Within seconds, the bull has gone limp and its vines have stopped twitching.

It’s almost anticlimactic.

I cast a stern eye over the group of fighters, nodding in satisfaction when I see that they’re already organising the injured. A flash of white catches my eye and I turn my head to spot several healers already hurrying over to help. Thessalanor himself heads to the two Warriors who were grievously injured in the bull’s parting blow.

Maxen, any dead? I ask my commander who was, of course, right in the thick of things. Ever since accepting the Warrior Class, he’s been determined to level it up the hard way and has turned his already-impressive sword skills into something incredibly deadly.

Not as long as those two pull through, sir, he reports briskly. However there have been a good few broken bones and serious flesh wounds.

I’ll be with you shortly, I tell him, then cut the connection.

“Well, at least we know we aren’t helpless against the creatures here,” I say wryly to the other lords.

Valence and Layton look troubled.

“A Tier three already,” Layton murmurs.

I shrug.

“We did create a massive magical disturbance here,” I point out. That was partly on purpose. We decided it would be a good way to check whether we’re in the territory of something we can’t handle while we can still get away easily enough. It might have meant that those off the boat would have to scatter and be picked up later, but considering almost everyone off the ships right now are Classers or fighters, they’re the most likely to survive something like that.

“True,” Layton agrees.

“Which brings me back to your job – though you are probably already aware. Organising patrols – if another Tier three, or, gods forbid, a Tier four come this way, we need to know about it before it attacks,” I tell him seriously.

“Of course,” he agrees.

I nod.

“As agreed, I’ll act as the communications hub, and keep you all updated with anything relevant. Any questions?” No one asks anything. “Then this is it, gentlemen. Everyone is counting on us so…let’s try not to mess it up, shall we?”
