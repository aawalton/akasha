Our next destination looked like an open-air theater. Rows of stone benches formed a wide semicircle around a raised stage, climbing higher and higher until they disappeared into the distance. The room could probably seat several thousand dreamers if every bench were filled, although only a few hundred had gathered tonight. Small groups chatted quietly while waiting for the discussion to continue, giving the place the atmosphere of a university lecture rather than a performance.

Nina and I appeared already seated around the sixth row, slightly left of center.

Above the stage floated glowing letters.

Hano Room Five: Highlighted Freelancer.

"So this is where people discuss famous freelancers?" I whispered.

"Mostly," Nina whispered back. "The rooms can change depending on what is happening in the city."

As I looked toward the stage to see if I recognized anybody, my stomach sank.

Eight large painted portraits stood on wooden easels arranged in a wide V formation across the front of the stage.

Raik's portrait stood proudly at the center.

Kan's portrait rested to his left while Vena's stood to his right.

My portrait stood to the right of Vena's, with Calr's to the right of mine, while Ja'a's rested beside Kan's, with Katar and Shingo completing the formation farther to the left.

"...Oh no."

The speaker wasn't discussing some famous team.

He was discussing my team.

I immediately shrank lower into my seat.

Before anyone could notice me, I spent a small thread of Inspiration to change my clothes. A baseball cap appeared over my head, and my hair shifted into a ponytail that threaded through the back opening. Dark sunglasses covered my eyes while a plain cloth mask wrapped around the lower half of my face.

The disguise looked completely ridiculous.

Then again, maybe not. When I looked around, I noticed that half the audience had come wearing equally absurd disguises.

Some dreamers looked impossible. I knew there were diverse people in the Seven Realms who didn't look human, but I bet half, or at least a quarter, of the people here weren't in their actual bodies. One woman appeared entirely made of shifting golden light, while another had replaced his head with a floating lantern. Several people looked more like anthropomorphic animals than regular beastkin.

Dreamers clearly enjoyed experimenting with appearances. I narrowed my eyes as I saw a red, squat, spiky abomination, the meme I had started, appear to be alive.

"That concludes the available information we have on the Cleric."

My attention snapped back toward the stage.

The speaker then added, "She is currently the only Holy Cleric registered with the Freelancer Guild. Sir Jaime occasionally travels with the Twenty Man Army, but he is not a guild member."

The speaker gestured toward Vena's portrait.

"Does anyone have additional information regarding Miss Vena?"

"It looks like we arrived halfway through the presentation," Nina whispered. "I think we missed Raik's part."

"Why are they doing an exposé on my... them?" I whispered back, stopping myself from saying my team.

The man sitting beside me answered before Nina could.

"Because Sergeant Raik was officially promoted yesterday."

I looked toward him in surprise. He looked like a Valkyren man, except his muscles were exaggerated like a Baki character. He clearly didn't understand anatomy very well.

"He completed Elder Okar's challenge," he continued. "Everyone in Hano wants to learn about them, and any bard who can deliver new information will drink for free."

Well... that explained the timing.

I noticed several wooden paddles rising throughout the audience. Each carried a glowing number. I looked down at my seat and noticed that I had one too, with the number 423.

The moderator pointed toward one of them.

"Number Twenty-five."

A tall man stood.

He had pale blue scales running across his neck and cheeks, feathered wing-arm appendages ending in hands, and visible gills beneath his jaw.

"Miss Vena is not merely an orphan," he said in a refined voice. "She is Princess Vena Moonbearer, fifteenth in line to the throne of the Veil Holy Kingdom."

A ripple spread through the audience.

"Can you verify your source?" the moderator asked, leaning forward.

"I have contacts within the Mythic Realm," he said calmly. "Her full name is Vena Moonbearer, daughter of Paladin Sofia Moonbearer and Knight Vergil Moonbearer."

He paused.

"Both died stopping a goblinoid migration from the Wilds."

Several people quietly wrote notes.

"The child displayed extraordinary talent from birth," he continued, "and the current king, her uncle, quietly arranged for her removal from court so she wouldn't overshadow his own children."

He sat back down with a satisfied look on his face.

Almost immediately, another paddle shot upward, shaking eagerly.

"Number One Hundred Fourteen."

A young man stood up.

"The Veil throne can only be inherited by someone who has ascended to Cleric." He pointed toward Vena's portrait. "So if what Mr. Evan said is true, her Ascension at only sixteen years old makes her an actual contender for the throne."

He sat back down.

"This is surprisingly organized," I said.

"It won't stay that way," Nina whispered.

"Why not?"

"We haven't reached the controversial part yet."

The moderator looked around the audience.

"Any additional information regarding Miss Moonbearer?"

Several more paddles rose.

One person described Vena volunteering throughout Hano's slums and helping in the temple-sponsored soup kitchen.

Another mentioned that she had been in the Pikar Steppe, where she helped deliver a baby.

Someone else listed villages where she'd supposedly healed dozens of people. All of them were places we had traveled through during Raik's challenge.

The moderator waited until the room settled again.

"If there are no further reports..."

When no more paddles rose, he turned and looked at another portrait.

"Let's move on to the third notable member of the team." He deliberately paused, allowing the room to settle. "I would like to remind everyone to remain civil."

He pointed toward the next portrait.

"The third member is Kan Karda, daughter of Yanka Karda, the Adamantine Warrior, the man remembered for leading the populist uprising against the former council before his execution for colluding with the Old Realm Cult."

"Karda was a hero!" someone shouted.

"Traitor!" another voice answered immediately.

The room exploded.

People leaped to their feet as arguments erupted across half the theater. Dreamers pointed fingers at one another while trying to shout over the growing noise.

"He fought corruption!"

"He betrayed the city!"

"He stood against the nobles!"

"He worked with the cult!"

I stared in disbelief as two men climbed over the benches and started shoving each other.

"Order!" the moderator shouted, his voice booming across the entire amphitheater. "I said order!"

The instant the words left his mouth, every sound vanished.

The theater became unnaturally silent.

The two men who had been fighting disappeared entirely, as though they had never existed.

I blinked.

"What..."

"They got kicked out," Nina whispered, sounding completely unsurprised.

The moderator calmly adjusted his notes before speaking again.

"I would like to remind everyone that we are here to discuss Kan Karda, not her father." His voice remained perfectly calm despite what had just happened. "If you wish to argue about Yanka Karda, Hano Room Forty-three: Internal Politics is hosting a historical debate on the former council. Please direct all political discussions there."

I saw, though I couldn't hear, a few people chuckling.

The moderator waited another few seconds before continuing.

"Kan Karda has been a registered Freelancer for approximately two years." He pointed toward her portrait. "Most of that time, she worked independently or accepted occasional contracts alongside Sergeant Yon's Misfits."

Several people nodded in confirmation, as Yon's Misfits had probably been discussed extensively only recently after he dismantled the group.

"Her work primarily consisted of agricultural monster control and pest removal throughout the surrounding fields."

Another gesture, and the portrait shifted to how she looked when I first met her, wearing the tight blue silk bodysuit.

"She previously used a whip and short sword in combat," he said.

Then, with another gesture of his hand, the image shifted once more to Kan's current equipment, with mail armor and weighted chains dangling from her arms.

"Earlier this year, she acquired a Chain Manipulation Soulbook and has since transitioned into fighting primarily with weighted chains."

He flipped another page in his notebook.

"Before joining this team, she most recently participated in the battle against Todor during the Pikar Steppe incident."

He looked around the audience, making certain everyone remained quiet before making another small gesture.

The sound returned, and the room immediately filled once more with the quiet murmur of conversation.

"That concludes the currently verified information." He looked across the audience. "If anyone has additional reports, please raise your paddle."

Wooden signs immediately rose across the theater.

"Number Thirty-three."

A middle-aged woman stood.

"Kan was one of the people who uncovered the Old Realm Cult activity beneath Hano." She glanced toward Kan's portrait. "Alongside Calr and Alice."

My shoulders instinctively tensed.

"This current party appears to be the merger of two smaller groups." She pointed toward Raik's display. "Raik, Ja'a, and Katar were already a team."

Then she pointed toward me.

"So were Kan, Alice, and Calr."

She continued confidently.

"Later, they added Shingo and Cleric Vena to complete the party with an additional frontline fighter and a healer."

The moderator nodded thoughtfully.

"Do you have evidence that Kan knew Alice before the sewer investigation?"

"No." The woman shook her head. She hesitated before adding, "Any information regarding Alice from earlier than a few months ago is... unreliable."

Several people murmured in agreement, and the moderator made a small note.

"Very well," he said. "We will discuss Miss Alice shortly." He tapped Kan's portrait. "For now, let us remain focused on Kan Karda."

I shrank a little lower into my seat.

Next to me, Nina noticed immediately, letting out a small laugh.

"So famous," she whispered.

"I hate this."

Another paddle rose.

"Number Two Hundred Seventy-three."

A young woman stood.

"I'm from Ectamal." She looked slightly nervous speaking before so many people. "I served Kan dinner when she passed through our town."

"She added pieces of iron to her meal before eating it," she finally said after a hesitant pause.

A wave of confused murmurs swept through the audience, and almost immediately another paddle shot into the air.

"Number Thirteen."

An elderly Valkyren slowly stood. His once-black feathers had faded almost completely gray with age.

"Yanka Karda also consumed metal."

More loud whispers spread across the room.

"The ability was never publicly explained." He folded his hands behind his back. "But you have to remember that Kan is his youngest child, born after her father's final Evolution."

"It is entirely possible she inherited a portion of his abilities." He nodded toward her portrait. "As well as the unusually high talent ceiling commonly observed among the children of exceptionally powerful individuals."

Several people quietly wrote notes while others raised their paddles.

One recalled seeing her practicing alone long after the other children had gone home.

Someone else mentioned that she had been bullied because of her family name.

Another woman added that Kan had spent years moving between relatives until her older sister, Ensign Sashka Karda, became old enough to take legal responsibility for her.

Finally, someone suggested that Kan might be dating Raik, and the room broke out into chaos again.

The moderator carefully reestablished order once more before making a declaration.

"Remember, these accounts are only as trustworthy as the people presenting them. Try to independently verify everything."

"That becomes even more relevant considering the next person we're about to discuss."

The moderator turned toward my portrait.

The moment he did, I felt my stomach sink, despite technically not having one, being inside a dream and all. My entire body tensed beneath my ridiculous disguise as hundreds of dreamers shifted their attention toward the painting standing proudly at the front of the stage. I had expected people to know my name after the Pikar Steppe incident, perhaps even recognize me in the streets every now and then, but watching several hundred complete strangers gather specifically to analyze my life was an entirely different experience. It felt deeply invasive, like accidentally walking into a room where everyone had been reading my diary aloud.

The portrait certainly wasn't helping.

Whoever had painted it had somehow managed to make me look far more confident than I had ever actually been. I stood with my bident spear planted beside me, shoulders straight, looking every bit like the seasoned adventurer I most certainly wasn't.

"Alice Hecate," the moderator began, "registered with the Freelancer Guild only a few months ago. According to Guild records, she is from the Mythic Realm."

"Shortly after arriving in the city, she acquired a San title by depositing one hundred gold coins in the Bank of Hano. Around the same time, she also subscribed to a Lightning Soulbook before undergoing formal combat instruction under Lieutenant Garo Agame. During the same period, she received additional spear training from Sergeant Edmund, Knight of Hano, and the bannermen of the Twenty Man Army."

"Her early Guild career consisted primarily of routine contracts," he added. "She participated in the sewer hunt that led to the investigation beneath Hano and assisted with silk collection alongside Sergeant Yon's Misfits before coming to public attention during the Pikar Steppe incident."

The illusion shifted again, showcasing an open field of tall grass with long shadows stretching across it as though it were near dusk.

"It was during that battle that she publicly demonstrated a teleportation ability previously unknown to the public, locating missing civilians before transporting Commander Kitchi Agame directly onto the battlefield during the engagement against Todor."

Another illustration appeared beside it, showing our team traveling in a cart, with Katar driving while Shingo slept in the back. I sat between Vena and Ja'a.

"More recently, she accompanied Sergeant Raik Agame during Elder Okar's challenge, where independent witnesses reported repeated tactical use of long-range teleportation throughout Ectamal, Hano, and Loumpa's new settlement."

The moderator closed his notebook with a quiet snap.

"That concludes the information currently verified by multiple independent sources."

My shoulders slowly relaxed. If this were all the information they had on me, I could live with that. After all, it could have been so much worse.

They didn't seem to question whether I actually came from the Mythic Realm. They didn't know about my Sky affinity or the connection between my teleportation and the stars.

Most importantly, they hadn't mentioned the Sunless Reach, shooting stars, or wishes.

As long as those secrets remained hidden, I could live with people knowing the public version of my adventures.

Then the moderator looked back at the audience.

"If anyone has additional information regarding Miss Alice, please raise your paddles."

Nearly every wooden paddle in the theater shot into the air.

"Oh no…"

I stared in horror.

Beside me, Nina quietly buried her face in her hands to hide her laughter.

"I think," she whispered between suppressed giggles, "you're much more popular than you realize."

"I think," I muttered back, watching what looked like half the audience volunteering to dissect my life, "I'm about to vomit."

The moderator calmly surveyed the sea of raised paddles before pointing toward the front rows.

"Number Two."

A middle-aged woman stood, carrying a notebook thick enough to rival one of my research journals.

"The earliest confirmed sighting of Alice Hecate occurred approximately half a year ago, shortly after her arrival in Hano. Several witnesses observed her entering the Holy Temple, where some people reported hearing her speak a foreign language, after which she immediately received the Common Translation Soulbook, the same one normally reserved for visiting Holy Messengers."

She paused just long enough for several people to write that down before continuing.

"According to the same witnesses, this was also the day she first met young Vena." The woman adjusted her glasses. "Interestingly, Cleric Vena experienced her Ascension later that very same day."

A ripple spread through the audience.

"I am not suggesting those events are connected," she clarified. "I merely find the timing... unusual."

I slowly covered my face with both hands.

Bloody hell... They had managed to trace me all the way back to my first day in Hano.

It also raised a deeply uncomfortable question.

Exactly how many people had been collecting and trading information about me without my knowledge?

The moderator glanced across the forest of raised paddles before lowering his hand toward another volunteer.

"Number Six."

A broad-shouldered man rose from the middle rows, carrying several folded pages covered in tightly packed handwriting. I finally understood why so many people wrote things down if they couldn't take them back to the real world. It was because they used them here in the Great Dream to further spread gossip.

"Alice Hecate officially registered with the Freelancer Guild shortly after arriving in Hano, declaring herself a traveler from the Mythic Realm. By the time she reached the Guild, she had already acquired a Lightning Soulbook."

"Guild members reported that she established friendly relations with receptionist Nada almost immediately. Sergeant Nakera also appears to have taken an interest in her unusually quickly, though whether they had previous contact or whether it was simple attraction from Nakera, who is known to engage in casual sexual relationships with women, remains unconfirmed."

I blinked.

Did he just out Nakera's sexual orientation in front of hundreds of people?

The audience didn't look surprised, so maybe the matter was already public. Still, it felt like a major invasion of privacy. Besides, I didn't like the insinuation that Nakera had only been friendly toward me because she wanted sex. That was a very toxic way of viewing homosexual women.

"On the very same day, she entered the Perfect State during training and suffered the obvious adverse effects. Over the following month, she received regular instruction from Lieutenant Garo Agame in Lightning magic and Sergeant Edmund in spear combat."

A faint smile crossed his face.

"According to multiple servants in the Agame compound, Lieutenant Garo Agame was so impressed with her that he recommended her as a bride for his nephew, Lord Takur Agame, the Dragon Slayer."

The man lowered his papers, and a mixture of gasps and laughter rippled through the audience.

Damn it, Garo. You decrepit old geezer!

My frown deepened as I made a mental note to be considerably more careful about what I said inside Guild headquarters. Half of that information had clearly come from an official source.

The moderator waited for the audience to quiet before pointing toward another paddle.

"Number Twenty-five."

The blue-scaled man with feathered wing-arms stood once again.

"As I established earlier," he began in the same calm, scholarly voice, "I maintain professional contacts within the Mythic Realm."

He folded his wing-hands behind his back.

"Following reports of a mythic teleporting Freelancer arriving in Hano, I conducted a fairly extensive investigation."

The room noticeably quieted.

"I began with the obvious. I attempted to verify the existence of an Alice Hecate, but sadly, I found nothing. She certainly wasn't a famous person before arriving in Hano."

"I was likewise unable to locate any family bearing the surname Hecate." He slowly shook his head. "I did find families named Hayseed and Hacker, but no Hecate."

He let the silence linger before continuing.

"After all those setbacks, I decided to broaden my investigation to divine blessings associated with teleportation. Historical records suggest that long-range teleportation blessings became extraordinarily rare following the death of the God of Travel during the Undead Calamity three thousand years ago. Numerous godlings attempted to inherit that domain, but none succeeded."

"So, unless I am missing something..." He glanced toward my portrait again. "Miss Hecate's abilities appear to be historically unusual."

He calmly returned to his seat.

The room immediately dissolved into dozens of whispered conversations.

Holy shit. Holy shit.

These people weren't pulling any punches. This was the fantasy equivalent of Reddit trying to find the Boston Bomber. Armchair dreamers sharing information left and right without any concern for the consequences to the people involved. I might have found it fascinating how similar it was to Earth's forums if I weren't currently the subject of their scrutiny.

The moderator patiently waited until the conversations died down before acknowledging another raised paddle.

"Number Sixty-one."

A young woman wearing a cloak that looked like oversized owl feathers draped over her shoulders stood up.

"I interviewed Rell Ashhand. A young Freelancer who trained alongside Alice under Sergeant Edmund."

She unfolded a small notebook.

"When I asked about her, he gave the following statement." She cleared her throat. "I quote, 'She's definitely got Kindred blood somewhere. She's stronger than the average Mythic girl.' End quote."

Damn it. I closed my eyes.

Rell... I am kicking your ass the next time I see you.

"The statement is obviously speculative," the woman continued, "but considering she could keep up with a team led by Raik Agame, I thought it was worth mentioning."

She sat back down, and the moderator scribbled a brief note before selecting the next speaker.

"Number Twenty-one."

A thin foxman with three tails stood up.

"The money that afforded her the San title came from a trade agreement with Lady Tan Je'e." His tails flickered behind him as he spoke. "She appears to have remained in contact with the woman, having visited her villa at least once."

He sat down, and more paddles rose.

"Number Seven."

The man who stood possessed immaculate white Valkyren wings, each feather almost glowing beneath the dreamlike light of the theater. Suspended above his head floated a slowly rotating golden septagon, giving him the appearance of some overly dramatic angel.

Unlike every previous speaker, he carried no notes whatsoever.

He simply folded his hands together and surveyed the audience.

"I believe," he began evenly, "that there is a Phoenix-bright point that cannot reasonably be ignored any longer. I speak, of course, of the recent Holy Saint rumors."

A collective groan rolled across the amphitheater. To my surprise, I wasn't the only one groaning. Dozens of people rolled their eyes while several slumped dramatically against the benches.

Apparently, even the regulars were already tired of hearing that particular discussion.

The white-winged man smiled knowingly.

"Yes," he said. "I see many of you share my frustration. Unfortunately, regardless of how tired we may be of the subject, it has become widespread enough that it deserves to be examined."

He looked toward the moderator.

"I therefore request permission to summarize the evidence both supporting and contradicting the claim, so we can hopefully put at least some of the more ridiculous rumors to rest."

The moderator nodded once.

"Proceed, but please distinguish clearly between verified facts and speculation."

"Naturally," the white-winged man replied. "That is precisely why I raised my paddle."

Instead of speaking from where he stood, he flew down and stood next to the moderator.

"Back in Hano Room Seventy-seven: Holy Faith, we conducted a fairly extensive investigation into the Saint rumor. While there are currently several individuals being discussed as possible candidates, including Miss Alice, Lady Vena, and Lady Sana, our investigation reached a rather unexpected conclusion."

He glanced around the amphitheater, making sure he had everyone's attention.

"We believe the Saint myth did, in fact, originate with Miss Alice."

He paused, letting the statement linger in the air for a few seconds.

"We managed to trace the rumor back to two independent inception points."

"The first originated with the Doomcaller Cult." He raised one finger. "As some of you know, the Doomcaller Cult predicted that a devastating plague would eventually spread across the Mythic Realm, claiming approximately one in every ten lives. More importantly, they also foretold that the Holy Temple already possessed the means to prevent the calamity."

Several members of the audience nodded.

"The Doomcaller prophecies have, historically speaking, proven remarkably reliable."

"They're never wrong," someone called from the audience.

"But they can be prevented," someone else answered.

The speaker nodded in acknowledgment of both interruptions.

"Back in the Holy Lands, this prophecy caused a great deal of confusion within the Temple itself." He slowly paced across the stage. "The Holy of Holies himself had no knowledge whatsoever of any revolutionary treatment for plagues. As many of you know, the Temple maintains an unusually transparent policy regarding medicine and healing. Whenever they discover something new, they distribute that knowledge freely throughout the Seven Realms."

Several people nodded again.

"There was no secret cure." He paused. "Or so everyone in the Mythic Realm believed."

"As it turns out, there actually was something new. It simply wasn't discovered in the Holy Land." His gaze settled briefly on my portrait. "It happened here, in Hano."

I wanted to crawl beneath the bench.

I hadn't expected this information to leak. I wanted the Temple to receive all the credit for the vaccine.

"Miss Alice shared previously unknown medical knowledge with several members of the Temple. That information was investigated and verified by Sir Gray, Lady Sana, and Lady Camille before being forwarded through official Temple channels."

Several dreamers immediately began writing furiously.

"The exact nature of that medical knowledge remains unknown, but it has been reported that Captain Lucy, the Freelancer Guild's highest-ranked healer, was seen meeting with the local Temple leadership. There truly is a new advancement in healing."

The moderator quietly nodded, confirming the statement without interrupting.

"The second inception point proved to be considerably less dignified."

He chuckled before continuing.

"It originated from an offhand comment made by Sir Jaime following the transmission of this new healing knowledge to the Mythic Realm via the Holy Messenger, Prince Marrek."

He cleared his throat before continuing.

"Sir Jaime said, and I quote: 'That girl Alice... I swear if she were one of the Holy Faithful, she would be the next Saint.' End quote."

The room burst into laughter.

I slowly lowered my face into my hands.

I swear I am going to murder Jaime... slowly... painfully.

"He did not, to the best of our knowledge," the speaker continued once the laughter settled, "intend the statement to be taken literally. It is a well-known fact that Miss Alice isn't a believer in the Holy Faith."

"Unfortunately..." He sighed. "Prince Marrek later repeated that remark to his niece. His niece repeated it among several of her noble friends within the Holy Land. Those nobles then repeated it to others."

"From that point onward," the white-winged man continued, "we enter the realm of educated speculation." He gestured with one hand, "as our reliable source was an interview with Lady Juliana, the Holy Messenger's own niece."

"Our reconstruction suggests that Sir Jaime's joke eventually became entangled with the original Doomcaller prophecy. As the story passed from person to person, the details warped and assumptions accumulated."

He gave an almost apologetic shrug.

"Eventually, the rumor returned to Hano through the Great Dream, but no longer as a joke. It returned as its own independent prophecy."

"The version currently circulating throughout parts of the Mythic Realm claims that a new Holy Saint named Alice will emerge from Hano to save countless lives from a plague. Some other versions omit the name, which has led to Lady Vena and Lady Sana also being, to a lesser degree, suspected of being the next Saint."

He spread both hands.

"But our investigation therefore concludes that, to the best of our knowledge..." He inclined his head toward the moderator. "The Saint myth is almost certainly nothing more than a misunderstanding."

"Ordinarily..." His smile slowly disappeared. "That would have been the end of the matter. But this concerns the Mythic Realm."

"And belief itself has power in that Realm."

I felt a knot form in my chest.

"The moment enough people sincerely began believing that a future Saint existed, the rumor ceased being merely a rumor and started taking on a life of its own."

My heart began pounding with panic.

"The belief has already reached the point where it is beginning to reinforce itself. There are already traveling bards composing songs about the unnamed Saint of Hano. The faithful are openly discussing the prophecy. Several donations have already been offered, asking for the future Saint's blessing."

"And perhaps most concerning..." He looked directly at my portrait. "Every public act performed by Miss Alice is now interpreted through the lens of that existing belief."

He sighed.

"When she rescued civilians during the Pikar Steppe incident, that story reinforced the myth."

"She also helped two members of the Holy Faith Ascend, or three if we count Vena."

He looked back toward the audience.

"The myth is growing because every genuine act of kindness performed by Miss Alice is now being absorbed into an already established narrative."
