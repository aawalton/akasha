The next two days, Thalion spent inside his mansion constantly working on his armor and creating one wild theory after another on how he could fuse the amulet with the elemental core.

His streak of ideas was interrupted by Pikar storming into the mansion looking as though he hadn't slept in days. His hair was disheveled and his clothes looked like they had flown through multiple treetops judging by the small sticks and leaves hanging from his robe.

"Thalion, what have you done?" he exclaimed in a high-pitched voice.

"I assume you mean my little show of power?" Thalion guessed. Pikar couldn't possibly know about the massive improvements he had made to his crafting during the last few days. If he continued at this pace, he might finish the base of the armor within a few more days and then only the tempering process and giant crystals would remain.

"Little?! What in the bloody hell was little about that? They took a few resources and you brutally killed one of them in front of everyone. Just what were you thinking? Now Ricarda has to work day and night calming the elves down. Did you know they were already planning an assassination attempt?"

Pikar's voice rose with every sentence.

"Interesting. And here she told me the elves would be absolutely loyal." Thalion wondered out loud.

"Yes, they were until you murdered one of them. Elves are delicate and you had nothing better to do than kill one over basically nothing." Pikar continued shouting at him, which Thalion found rather annoying since the man was missing the point by quite a large margin.

"I don't know what you've been told," Thalion hissed. "But the situation was far from simple. The elves were treating the humans almost like slaves again. Furthermore, I don't have the luxury of simply ignoring them openly mocking me when I confronted them. To them we are nothing and you should use your brain a bit more. Tell me, what do you think would happen if I didn't threaten them or simply left? How comfortable would you feel being part of a faction ruled by elves as a human and former slave?"

"Still, killing one will only create more pressure, which isn't good for this base—"

He got no further as Thalion interrupted him.

"Pikar, I don't have time to discuss this. Also, I find it troubling that you completely ignore how the elves taking all the humans' loot doesn't create pressure. Besides that, did you find the teleportation circle?"

Thalion was really getting tired of being lectured.

What had happened to the supposedly intelligent man and all his clever theories? Had the second system event broken him or was it something else?

"Argh, fine. Another day then. Ricarda is currently calming them down anyway, so it shouldn't become too much of a problem." Pikar sighed before beginning another rant. "Do you have any idea how dangerous it was getting to that teleportation circle? I had to travel through a forest filled with spiders and ants. Both races resisted my taming skill completely. Furthermore, they chased me down and even tried to enslave me in return. I barely escaped with my life only to find that the teleportation formation has already been claimed by an incursion that's currently building a massive base exactly like we planned. I even spotted slave collars."

The slaves weren't surprising at all.

What troubled Thalion far more was the growing base.

On the positive side, he currently had a large number of stubborn elves on hand and this seemed like exactly the right occupation for the coming days. They could risk their lives dealing with the incursion while he focused on his armor.

The spiders and ants sounded more like a future farming opportunity than an actual problem.

"Thanks for scouting, Pikar. I'll take it from here. There is a lot of organization that needs to be done. You'll handle it and make sure the new elves from the divine domain behave. Otherwise, I'll step in."

Thalion spoke while deep in thought.

Hadn't Pikar said that Ricarda was currently talking with the other elves?

Wouldn't it be best to simply assign the task to them directly or send a messenger?

And once again, in only a matter of days, Pikar found himself in the wrong place at the wrong time.

"You want me to tell the elves they need to deal with the incursion on their own? Right now? After everything that happened?" Pikar exclaimed.

"Yep."

"Oh man, this really isn't my lucky day. They'll probably be thrilled to see another human telling them what to do. How generous of you to keep bestowing one glorious assignment after another upon me."

Pikar continued complaining as he left the mansion.

Thalion had to hold back a grin for a moment before his expression turned serious again.

The elves' attitude was still a problem and it needed to be resolved sooner rather than later.

Killing one of them had bought him time, but it hadn't solved the underlying issue.

The biggest problem, in Thalion's view, was that the elves simply didn't want to change. Accepting that their race wasn't naturally superior to all others was probably difficult and admitting it would make one look like quite the loser among their own kind.

Maybe he needed to start his own belief system.

Or perhaps infect them all with his blood curse.

That would certainly give them a different perspective on the world.

On the negative side, he would then have competition when it came to blood and that wasn't something he particularly wanted either.

More scheming wasn't going to help much anymore and so Thalion fully immersed himself in crafting once again.

After so many failures, he finally began making real progress.

In fact, the only parts still missing were the shoes, helmet, and gloves.

Well, he also needed the crystals and countless rounds of tempering, but when it came to the actual shape of the armor those were the only pieces still unfinished.

Afterward he would have to spend a great deal of time strengthening the materials, but Thalion was now confident that the armor would turn out exceptionally well.

Once the design was complete and he had obtained the crystals, it would merely be a matter of time until the tempering process was finished and he could finally wear it.

That last step would take the longest, but thankfully the blood pond would handle most of the work for him.

Of course, he still intended to personally infuse more of his essence into the materials, just like Saranya's father had supposedly done, but that would have to fit between hunting trips.

Four more days passed before he was interrupted again.

This time it was ten elves and when he opened the door every single one of them looked as though they would rather be somewhere else entirely.

"Ehm... We were sent here to construct another communication circle in one of your rooms so that you can commune with the gods in private instead of always using the plaza." One of the elves muttered while carefully avoiding eye contact.

That actually wasn't a bad idea.

Thalion showed them one of the rooms on the first floor.

All of his crafting took place in the enormous cellar, so they shouldn't have any idea what he had been working on even if the metallic scent of blood still lingered faintly in the air.

He watched the elves construct the circle.

The formation was far too complicated for him to understand, but he wanted to make sure they weren't adding anything extra that would allow them to listen in on his conversations with the gods.

The construction took several hours and Thalion used the time to think about his next move.

His armor was almost complete and taking a dive into the ocean to retrieve the massive crystals didn't seem like a bad idea.

Shrinking and corrupting those crystals so they could be integrated into the armor would take a considerable amount of time as well.

So the following day Thalion gradually strolled toward the harbor, commandeered two ships to accompany him, and set off toward new adventures.

He also hoped that by the time he returned the gods would finally have sent a few beast corpses through the portal so that he could begin experimenting with new forms.

What Thalion didn't know was that the high gods of Integrated Space were facing quite a few problems of their own.
