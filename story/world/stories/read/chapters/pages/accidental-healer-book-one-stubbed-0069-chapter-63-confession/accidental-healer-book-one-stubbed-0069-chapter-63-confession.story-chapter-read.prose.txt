Martin waited just outside the door to his home. It was late in the night. After a long day dedicated to helping others, Claire would be waiting for him inside.

He rested a hand on the door. How was he supposed to tell his wife that he’d been lying to his faction, that he’d chosen his son over them? That he’d allowed them to work every day with a false hope, knowing all the while the grim truth.

His fingers wrapped around the wooden latch. It felt heavy, but he forced himself to draw it back. The door cracked open and he stepped into the dimly lit room. Claire was waiting for him, the soft light of a system interface exposing the tired eyes on her beautiful face.

The door clicked shut, and she looked up and smiled at him the same smile that he fell in love with so many years ago. The smile he’d come to rely on when the pressures of life threatened to overwhelm him. He swore that smile had magical powers even before mana existed.

Looking back on his life, so many of those moments, where he didn’t know what to do, all just seemed so trivial now. Maybe if he knew the world was going to enter an apocalypse, he wouldn’t have worried so much about how he was going to juggle paying his mortgage and Layton’s college tuition on a teacher’s salary.

“You’re home late.” Her tone was gentle, but he knew it carried with it questions.

He grunted and sat in the chair opposite her.

“We must be getting close. How much did they get done today?”

Martin rubbed his eyes.

“Martin?”

She reached out and pulled a hand away from his face, cupping it in hers.

“There’s…” His voice came out husky. He cleared his throat. “There’s something I need to tell you.”

He felt her hands tighten around his.

“A couple weeks ago I got a system message…” He paused and looked up into those eyes he loved so much.

“Layton is alive.”

Claire closed her eyes and pulled his hand to her cheek.

“I wanted to tell you for so long, I just…” She pulled his hand tighter, kissing it, bathing it in her tears.

“Where is he?”

He couldn’t stand to look at her. “Martin, where is my little boy?”

“He’s just on the other side of the teleporter.” His voice was even lower than a whisper.

Claire gasped.

Martin’s head sagged, and he began to retell the story of his experience with Kevin, and what Boretek planned. He told her about the system message. The timer and his impossible choice. He confessed how he’d made the choice of saving their son, of choosing to ensure that he was protected from the dangers on their side of the teleporter.

He explained it all and his wife listened. Once he’d told her everything, he leaned over the table and covered his head in his hands.

“So he’s safe?”

Martin looked up through bloodshot eyes. “He is.”

Wood creaked. Claire knelt on the wood floor before her husband and wrapped her arms around his waist. He leaned into her, pulling her head into his chest.

“He is safe.”

***

Boretek’s patience was finally paying off.

New Boise sat on gently rolling hills, buildings peeking over wooden walls, with a prominent stone building rising proudly at its center. The people here took their sweet time to complete that building. He figured it was probably intentional.

Kevin would’ve told them everything. Not like it changed anything—besides prolonging the inevitable.

That boy.

Boretek wasn’t sure why he liked him. Maybe it was his willingness to do whatever it took to survive. There was no way he’d ever trust someone like that. But in a strange way he was more predictable, than anyone.

Boretek liked “predictable.”

He hoped the boy was still alive somewhere in the city. It would be interesting to see what betraying his entire faction would do to his class.

According to his other sources watching the city, he wouldn’t need to wait much longer to find out.

New Boise would cease to exist—today.

***

A few hours later Martin made his way to the teleporter with the other leaders in their faction.

He watched as the polished shard was lowered delicately onto the bronze stand. He closed his eyes and offered a silent prayer. The portal buzzed to life.

He opened his eyes and pulled up his system interface. Hands shook as they toggled through the screens to find a new tab titled…

Interfaction Travel

His finger pressed against the words and he held his breath. The screen opened.

Faction Management scrolled across the top of the page. His eyes searched the page and breathed a sigh of relief.

The page was empty.

Layton received his message.

“Where are the other factions? Why is the page empty?” Bob asked, voice laced with concern.

“There are no other factions…” Martin whispered, feeling relief flood through his body.

“No other…” Bob shoved passed him and tried toggling back and forth between screens as if trying to refresh the page. “All our work… we’re doomed… I need to find my family…”

Bob pushed his way through a crowd of gawking faces.

Martin refused to meet their eyes. He could never tell them, never let them know he’d chosen his son over them.

He’d confessed his sins to his wife. She understood, and that was enough for him. That didn’t mean he was done here. He wasn’t about to lie down and die without a fight.

“This doesn’t change anything. We still have a city to defend,” he said gruffly. The people in the room didn’t look convinced. Everywhere he looked all he saw were pale faces and shifting glances. Who could blame them?

A notification lit up his screen.

Congratulations! You have completed the construction of a great building.

Factionwide reward.

Reward: Your entire faction has been awarded a level. All builders and crafters who participated have been awarded an additional 2 levels.

Martin read the announcement and used it to his advantage.

“Gather everyone who can fight and get them to the walls. Personally, I’m not just going to lay down and die.”

***

Martin wasn’t exactly sure how Boretek was aware they’d completed the teleporter.

At this point, did it really matter?

He was here, standing outside their walls, and that was all Martin needed to know.

Boretek looked different then the last time Martin saw him. Larger, more filled out. He’d also traded in his Greco-Roman-themed robes for an armor chest plate that looked like it was made from gold, and a plumed helmet that matched, with studded leather pants.

Martin felt hollow.

He was level 33 now. He knew Boretek was at least level 50. It didn’t take a genius to figure out the math.

His city invested in barriers, but all those would do was prolong the inevitable. Once those were overwhelmed…

Martin clenched his jaw.

Enough.

He was here.

He’d made his choice. Whether it was right or wrong, it was over with. He wasn’t going to die wallowing in self-pity. He adjusted the straps of his shield and felt the weight of his mace fall in his hand.

They might be outmatched, but they outnumbered the Gree-Cek three to one. He had twenty thousand men and women ready to fight or die standing on his city walls.

Greg, his second-in-command, nocked an arrow. He drew back on his bow and the arrow ignited in bright orange flames.

“Let’s get it started.”

The arrow flew like a comet, arching over the flowing grass. The shot was aimed perfectly, and would’ve struck Boretek directly in his chest had it not been stopped by an invisible barrier first. An explosion of flames engulfed the four-armed monster, signaling to the rest of New Boise that the fight had begun.

Arrows and spells filled the sky.

Boretek stepped from the burning flames, unbothered, while several mages wreathed in purple and gold robes followed close behind. It was hard to tell at this distance, but it appeared as though the leader of the Gree-Cek were laughing.

With a casual lift of his spear, his army returned fire.

Golden spheres of power, fire, stones, and many other powerful projectiles filled his vision.

Each impact sent shivers through the barrier.

The wall under his feet trembled. He scowled.

Their barrier wasn’t meant to handle so much abuse. It did just fine against low-level mana spawns and weaker factions, but this?

The Gree-Cek’s attacks made his own faction’s look like child’s play. Martin flinched.

Another volley dashed against the city’s barrier. Large ripples washed over the nearly transparent wall.

It wouldn’t be long now.

Martin wasn’t going to wait for it.

His feet landed in the charred, ruined grass outside of the city walls. He didn’t have a plan, but that didn’t stop him.

As he drew near, he was surprised to see Boretek lower his spear. Boretek cocked his head. He waved his mages away and strolled out to meet him.

“You must be Martin.” His voice was deep, his tone playful.

“And you’re Boretek.”

Up close he was even more impressive. His height would rival the tallest NBA player, and each of his four arms bulged with rippling muscle. His long hair and square jaw made Martin feel like he was looking at one of the Greek gods from myths.

“Bold of you to face the Gree-Cek alone.” At his height he was able to easily look over Martin.

Martin’s hands shook.

He knew that there were other options, maybe even ways some of his faction might’ve been saved. But he wouldn’t change it.

He didn’t regret his choice.

But the choice was made alone, and now he had to face it the same way.

Planting his foot, he used a skill that had served him well in the new world. Boretek wasn’t fazed.

He reached out a hand to swat the attack away. Just before his hand connected with the block, the skill activated.

The mace accelerated, slipping past Boretek’s guard. Before his mace could even scratch the untarnished gold armor, it careened off an invisible force, slamming into the dirt.

Martin knew it was a long shot. He pulled his mace up and lifted his shield.

Boretek wasn’t even looking at him.

He was staring at his hand. Martin set his feet and squared up.

When Boretek finally looked up, there was fire in his eyes.

In a move so fast he struggled to follow it, his shield was ripped from his grip. He felt bones crack.

He needed to move. He tried to move, but something held him in place. That was when he finally noticed the spear sticking directly through his stomach, and that he was looking down at the much taller Boretek.

“Return to your city and die with the rest of them.”

Gravity disappeared.

As Martin soared through the air, he closed his eyes and felt a profound sense of peace. Boretek was more powerful than he could’ve imagined. Today he’d been able to protect his son one last time.

When the impact finally came, it wasn’t as intense as he thought it would be. His body was in shock. Maybe he’d die before the pain came in full force.

He probed his body and was surprised to find he didn’t feel any pain at all.

“You should open your eyes.”

Martin’s heart stopped.
