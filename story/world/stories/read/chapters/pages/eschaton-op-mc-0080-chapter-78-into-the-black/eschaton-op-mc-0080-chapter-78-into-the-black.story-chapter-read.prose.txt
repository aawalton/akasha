Two days, a blank-check shopping spree funded by Morgan, and a crash course on a multitude of scientific devices later Tiffany and her two compatriots meet with Machina in the same meeting room. The long sandstone table is a single massive stone slab carved from a Fracture at some point. Likely by the Guildmaster herself, given her penchant for interior design.

“Well, did you all get what you need? I know you requested a few pieces from Morgan, and I talked to her about it. She has a few ideas, but would need more materials from Fracture to pull them off.”

“A running theme these days,” John says with a sigh. Machina rumbles in agreement.

“What about the salvage process?” Tiffany asks. “Did you manage to get more of the floaties?”

The floaties are huge crates capable of being brought down and then sent back up to the surface. They have balloons filled with a chemical powder that when catalyzed, rapidly degrades and turns into a gas. Each one is capable of lifting several hundred pounds straight to the surface of the ocean. Once on the surface, they’re scooped out of the water by patrolling ships. Being so useful for Fracture harvesting means they are in high demand, expensive, and difficult to source. Most teams might only have one or two that they refurbish after each dive.

“Yes, I managed to get about two dozen. Will that be enough?”

“Uh. Yes. You do know those are hard to get, right?”

“Oh.”

Tiffany internally sighs. This guy.

“Well, what’s the plan? Who’s going to help with the recovery? We’re going to hopefully be pulling a lot of stuff, and the normal contractors are going to charge an arm and a leg to fish it all out of the water for us.”

“I called in a few friends to help with that,” Machina says. He looks towards the door, and opens it. A blonde woman, about the same height as Tiffany and wearing a National Guard uniform has her fist raised as if to knock.

“Are you a precog now, sir?” She says a bit flippantly.

“Just very observant. It’s good to see you, Lieutenant. Where’s Captain Rains?”

“He’s off coordinating with the Coast Guard in person. I’m here to brief you and your team on the recovery plan,” she says eyeing the Cerulean Guard warily.

“How’s Second Squad doing?”

“Well. They’ve gotten another handful of medals for the Fracture in Denver, you know. It makes them some of the most decorated National Guardsmen. Sandra got a second Purple Heart, you know. A broken nose counts apparently. Who knew?”

Machina steps away from the head of the table, and the Lieutenant takes his place.

“Right. I’m Lieutenant McKinely, of the United States National Guard,” she says briskly. “I work with Machina on coordinating his responses and engagements with Fractures. In response to his decision to take on the Fractures that currently reside in the deepest parts of the Monterey Canyon, also known as The Deep, we have mobilized the Coast Guard.”

“What now?” Danny jerks.

“We called up the George Cobb, a Keeper-class cutter. It’s not armed, but it’s not like a gun would be helpful. It’s perfectly suited to pull heavy cargo out of the water. Which includes hauling Machina out to the drop point and out of the water once he’s done.”

“You… can just do that? Call up the Coast Guard and say ‘Hey, we need a ship tomorrow’ and they just say yes?” Danny says faintly. “What am I saying, of course you can.”

“Coastal Buoy tenders aren’t exactly in high demand right now,” Lieutenant McKinely says matter-of-factly. “And the Commandant of the Coast Guard agrees with Machina; this is a great opportunity to make a big difference in the fight.”

“How much cargo space does the cutter have?” Tiffany asks. “A lot of the contractors Empowered hire for this aren’t exactly cargo ships. Most of them are retrofitted fishing yachts at best.”

“The short answer is yes. The long answer is that the crew of the George Cobb will find the space to store whatever you send up. Don’t worry too much about the weight. If the Deep Water Recovery Crates can lift it, we can store it. We assembled as many as we had at the time, and I’ve been told that more are being stored away on board as we speak. Any more questions?”

Danny and John both shake their heads. “None from us, Lieutenant,” Tiffany confirms. “We’ll go gear up.”

“Good. See you on board.”

Myself, Lieutenant McKinely, and the Cerulean Guard in their eclectic mix of equipment meet the commanding officer of the George Cobbs at its dock at the Alameda Coast Guard Island.

“Sirs, ma’ams,” he says with a crisp salute. “I’m Chief Warrant Officer Perry of the George Cobbs. It’s an honor to have you on board.”

“The honor is all mine,” I assure him. “You and your crew are brave to be going out there.”

“I won’t lie, sir, we’re all nervous as hell, but clearing even one Fracture before it breaks will save a lot of lives. It’s a tough job, but someone’s gotta do it. If not us, then who?”

“My thoughts exactly. And Chief Perry?”

“Sir?”

“No courage without fear.”

“No courage without fear,” he repeats. “Yeah, you’re right. No courage without fear.” He stands up a little straighter than before. “Alright, let’s get underway, we’re ready to go if you are.”

All of us board the George Cobb, and McKinely splits off from the rest of us to join Chief Perry on the bridge. The ship roars to life, and lurches away from the dock before smoothing out.

“It’ll take us about an hour to reach The Deep,” Chief Perry tells us over the radio.

“*Understood, we’ll be ready.”

I look over the Cerulean Guard and their mix of gear. All of them wear SCUBA wetsuits, though they’ve forgone the normal equipment that goes with that. None of them have oxygen masks or tanks. They don’t even have goggles. They do have flippers, the one concession to conventional equipment.

All three of them are wearing armor, though Danny has the heaviest. That being said, all of them are relatively lightly armored: Danny’s heavy armor is a simple chestplate with vambraces and thigh plates. All of it is unenchanted mundane materials infused with magic—the best we could get on such short notice. Enchanted armor is made on demand and fitted to the commissioner. Both John and Tiffany are wearing lighter, stiffened scalemail—literally, in this case. Their armor is made up of cured and stiffened monster scales, likely from a fish of some kind.

A big one, by the size of those scales.

Danny wields a rusty steel harpoon even taller than he is for a weapon. The tip is blackened, jagged, and wicked looking. It oozes malice and a desire to inflict pain. Just looking at it fills me with a feeling of deep revulsion.

Why would they have a weapon like that?

John has a long trident, though it’s much less cruel than Danny’s trident—not to mention smaller. It’s made of gray metal that looks like it was salvaged from a destroyer. It even might have been, I’ve personally been in Fractures full of shipwrecks.

Tiffany doesn’t even wield a weapon at all, evidently choosing instead to focus on using her magic. Beyond Danny’s harpoon, all of their equipment is… rough, if I’m being honest. Their choice as a team to focus on lower category Fractures has cost them: good equipment is expensive, and the lower category Fractures don’t pay well, at least relative to the costs of being an Empowered. The belt of half a dozen elixirs each one of them has, bought directly from Alchemist Doxiadis, was two million dollars. Each!

Fortunately Morgan was willing to throw some money at the problem in the meantime. Once we’re able to recover enough resources from the Fractures, either her or one of her apprentices will be able to custom make a few pieces of equipment. That should give them some serious firepower, though I doubt it’d equal the fire in them.

They’re strong, they just don’t know it yet.

I don’t know how I know that. It’s just a feeling I get when I look at them. There’s a fire in them, one I don’t see in everyone. Second Squad have it too. A hunger for more, a chance to be more, to do more. I place that thread of thought on pause.

“Alright guys, let’s go over the plan again,” Tiffany orders. Her tone brooks no argument.

“John, no need to focus on healing Machina for obvious reasons, but you’re gonna have to be on the ball with Danny. That harpoon still hurts him and he’s gonna be using it more than ever. I’ll keep the monsters knocked around, and I’ll try to push them to you Danny, and you as well, Machina,” she says, nodding in my direction.

“What’s wrong with the harpoon?” I ask.

“It’s cursed,” Danny says. “It hurts the wielder. It’s actually one of the few things we’ve been able to recover from a Cat-3 Fracture.”

“It wasn’t made?”

“No, we found it. It was stuck in a Moby Whale, and we managed to pull it out in one piece.”

I scan the harpoon with every sensor I have.

“No, you can’t have it,” he says dryly. “I know it’s interesting, but it’s really powerful. Easily one of the most powerful artifacts in the Bay Area.”

I lean away. “Sorry, I was just curious.”

“We don’t bring it out often,” Tiffany says, shooting her teammate a commiserative glance. “When we’re punching down on Cat-1s, we don’t really need the firepower and it’s really not pleasant.”

Danny gives me a grin I see right through. “It’s not that bad. Pain is just weakness leaving the body, right? S’what my dad always said.”

“John’s a good enough healer to keep him in good shape, but still. It sucks.”

“What does it do to you?” I ask Danny, and he shrugs.

“It’s hard to say. Just feels like I’m wasting away. Like a bad flu. Full body aches, can barely move, the works. Minus the sniffles, at least. Not going to ask me to put it down?”

“No.”

I want to tell him that he doesn’t have to do this. But that’s not true, is it? I don’t have to push myself as hard as I do, yet I do anyway. Because I have to. I won’t belittle his sacrifice and ask that he give it up. If he wants to carry this burden, then the best thing for me to do is to trust that he can carry it.

“Why? These two do, constantly.” He sounds surprised.

“We all have things we have to do. And I trust you. If you say you can do this, you can do this. Can you do this?”

“Yes. Absolutely,” he declares.

“Then you can do it.”

He gives me a vicious grin and slams the butt of the harpoon against the metal deck. “Yeah, you’re goddamned right I can.”

“We’re here,” Chief Perry says after an hour.

I look over the edge of the ship into the dark waters. Even with my advanced sensor suite, I can only see about a hundred meters down. Once I get in the water and can use sonar I should have a much greater range.

“Let’s move quickly,” Tiffany says. “Being out here is dangerous.”

The hook of the crane hits the water, and the two mile cable slowly starts to unspool. I leap into the water, and the ship rocks. Three more splashes in rapid succession follow me into the cold ocean. I grab hold of the cable, and start to sink. Soon we’re swallowed by the darkness, the hungry ocean having consumed the light.

Down, down, down we go.
