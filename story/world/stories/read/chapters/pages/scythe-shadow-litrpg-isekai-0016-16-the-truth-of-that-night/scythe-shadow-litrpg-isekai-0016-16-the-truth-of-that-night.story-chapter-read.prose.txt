Beep…

Beep..

Beep.

The familiar sound of medical machines woke me from slumber.

I was still in the hospital. Even beneath the sheets, I could tell one of those stupid low-thread-count hospital gowns had been draped over me like an oversized dress. My bed was tilted not quite to a 45-degree angle, making it easy to see my surroundings.

The first time I’d been conscious here, I’d still been in shock from everything—I thought Penumbra had been a dream. Now, I could actually assess my situation.

Outside the hospital room window, the sun’s rays were breaking over top the dozens of skyscrapers blocking the horizon. Ah, to see the sun again!

The shades were pulled shut, but they were a cheap semi-translucent material and did little to obscure the view.

Derek no longer rested in the chair in front of the window. He must have left at some point in the night.

Avery, on the other hand, still lay sprawled half across my legs at the end of my bed, half on a chair. Asleep. A lock of auburn hair fell across her face. The drool had been wiped away, either by a subconscious swipe of her sleeve or the attentiveness of a kind nurse. Sunlight reflected brilliantly off the diamond on her engagement ring.

She looked peaceful, despite the awkward angle of her back and the poor cushioning of the hospital chair. I didn’t want to disturb her, so I remained quiet as I tried to determine how badly injured my Vitrial Plane body was.

Strange, to already be distinguishing between my physical forms here and in the Umbral Plane.

I had no idea how long I’d been asleep, aside from my brief visit courtesy of Calliope. It could have been hours. Days… Nothing I’d learned about the Umbral Plane so far indicated that time there was like time here. In fact, it wouldn’t shock me at all if there were some weird relativistic time dilations going on that would have even Einstein pulling his hair out in confusion.

I wiggled my toes, being careful not to wake Avery. They responded normally. My right forearm, where the skull beings had cut a lengthy slit—which I now realized housed my retracted scythe and a bizarre inventory system—was unblemished. Not a scratch in sight. A dull ache throbbed in my skull, and my vision seemed slightly off. Some sort of bandage was wrapped around my head. Pain seared across my stomach, but not as much as I would have expected from the attack.

An attack, which I honestly should not have survived. The beast had literally been eating my insides. I was no medical genius, but I was fairly confident no human could survive without intestines. And if I was in such a rough state, surely, there would be more devices hooked up to me than a single IV and an EKG.

Something doesn't add up here.

I untied the gown from the back of my neck and pulled it down past my abdomen. Aside from a decent size square bandage, the only injury I saw was a large black and blue bruise across my left rib cage. Certainly not the wide open hole I’d been expecting.

The door handle to my hospital room clicked and turned. I quickly pulled the scant clothing back up and tied it in place. The door opened slowly, allowing some light and the distant mumbles of conversation and rapid footsteps of the hallway to enter.

Avery sniffed, but remained asleep.

A hand carrying a tray with two steaming styrofoam cups appeared in the doorway. My nose perked up at the scent of coffee. It smelled burnt, but I didn't care. It felt like years since I'd had caffeine.

The backlit form of my brother followed the cups through the door.

Derek’s eyes widened at seeing me awake, but I put a finger to my lips, silencing him. He nodded, seeing Avery still asleep, and set the drinks on a nearby table. Hurriedly, he repositioned a second chair by the bed next to my head. His expression wasn’t that of sorrowful sympathy or even concern.

“Hey, Miles,” Derek said, keeping his voice low.

He held a fist out and I bumped it with my own in our usual fashion, as if nothing strange was going on. As if we weren’t in a hospital. As if we were simply catching up after time apart.

As if I wasn’t a Penumbral monster reaper.

“What the hell is going on here, Derek?” I asked.

He grinned, revealing his signature perfect teeth—never even touched by the middle school curse of braces. “We may have drunk a little too much. And by we… I mean you.”

I stared at him, nonplussed, waiting for more than the minimalist explanation he provided.

“I dunno, man,” he said. “At some point, you ended up out by the pond. Must have slipped and hit your head, fractured a rib or two, got stuck by a sharp stick.”

“Hit my head…? A stick? That’s… that’s it?”

“I mean… I guess you hit it pretty hard. Doctor said there’s a crack and everything back there, like fucking humpty dumpty.”

"How long was I out?"

"I dunno. Like twenty-seven hours or some shit?"

That long?! Or… Only that long? I didn't know which way to feel. So much had happened in that time. But then again…

No beast attack. No devoured insides. I wanted to ask—needed to ask—if I’d been attacked by something. I’d seen my friends running toward me. I’d felt the monster’s teeth and claws in my body. But there were no signs whatsoever. A bumped head and a broken rib wouldn’t result from an animal attack. I’d seem insane if I brought it up now.

Maybe I was insane…

Was that a more realistic possibility than there being an entire alternate plane of reality that housed humanity's sins and those who fought against them?

I put a hand to my forehead, remembering what I’d seen drawn there when I woke up that night.

Derek chuckled quietly. “Don’t worry. The nurses cleaned it off before Avery saw you. They didn’t find it nearly as funny as we did. You’re dick-free. At least your face is. I assume the… uhh… other one is still in-tact.”

I sat back, relieved. So, at least one memory was right. But I still had no idea what else from that night actually happened.

“Did you or anyone else see a l—" the words cut themselves off, just like they had when I tried to say my real name in Penumbra—even though there was no System here to stop me. Right? Could I get around it? Maybe an open-ended question? "Nothing else… weird happened?”

Derek laughed louder this time, unable to control himself. “Man, a lot of weird shit went down that night. Your friends are whack.”

Avery stirred again by my feet. She rubbed her eyes and yawned lazily. Then she shot upright.

Her eyes locked on mine.

“Miles!” Avery was at my side in an instant, holding my hand, practically shoving Derek out of the way.

My brother held his arms up and backed away, then sat in the now vacant chair by my feet.

Avery grabbed my face. Kissed my cheek and lips with the fire of someone who’d been away from her love for months instead of days. She was wearing the sweet cherry lip balm that always made my lips tingle.

I tried to reciprocate her enthusiasm, but between the pain, my mind trying to reconcile this life with everything that happened in Penumbra—and the idiotic fucking grin on my brother’s face—I was in no state for passion.

I intentionally winced, pretending my ribs hurt more than they did. I mean… They did hurt, so it wasn't a total lie.

She pulled away quickly, apologizing, and placed a hand on my arm as she sat where Derek had been.

Guilt stung in my heart. I couldn’t imagine what horrors had been running through her head as I lay here unconscious for over a day. She must have been feeling so many emotions to finally see me awake. And here I was, pushing her away.

Avery stared directly into my eyes, unspeaking. There was a longing, a fear, a desperation there. I wasn't sure why she was so concerned. This is nothing compared to the condition I expected to be in. And so contrasted against Derek's initial reaction to seeing me awake.

Of course, Derek was the calmest bro in the world, so it wasn’t necessarily surprising. The entire universe could be burning around him, and Derek would simply stroll along, swinging a fire extinguisher by his side, squirting it whenever flames inched too close. Probably whistling all the while.

Yeah, he'd thrive as a Reaper.

“I’m okay,” I finally said.

The tension in Avery’s shoulders visibly relaxed.

“I’m just a bit shaken up trying to figure out how I ended up here.”

“Luckily, he only remembers the good parts.” Derek winked at me.

Avery slapped his arm. “How’d you let him get so drunk? You’re supposed to look after him!”

“Hey! He's the adopted brother. It’s practically his job to misbehave. And he's been slacking. Gotta make up for lost time eventually.”

He wasn't wrong. I'd never misbehaved. No drunken high school parties. No staying out past curfew. Good grades. Only that one detention from Ms. Frontbutt who hated everyone.

Yet still my adoptive parents kept me at arms length. There was always something between us. They never said anything, but they must've known my history before agreeing to adopt me. All my history.

That's why Derek accepted me. He didn't know. He never would.

But his parents. It wasn't that they didn't want to accept me. It's that they couldn't

Because they were afraid of me.
