I sat upon a throne I did not remember claiming.

Gold, ivory, and a fabric softer than dream-silk cradled me, cushions breathing beneath my weight like some sleeping beast. The arms curved like serpents. The back rose high and regal, carved with sigils I half-recognized—shapes that felt older than crowns, older than bloodlines, older than the concept of ruling at all.

Before me stretched a long black table, polished to a dark mirror sheen, stretching outward until darkness swallowed its length. It was not merely long—it felt endless, as though the void itself was attending the meeting.

To my left, another throne. Same shape, same presence, but inverted in color—night where mine was sun, obsidian where mine shone like bone-gold. To my right, a third throne, its palette neither opposite nor identical, but… balanced. I felt it as much as saw it. A trinity of selves.

On the throne to my left sat a version of me I had never seen before—not even in the Mirror Trial’s cruel reflections. He wore simple clothes, soft and plain, his sleeves rolled to the forearms. Ink stains spotted his fingertips. A stack of bound parchment rested on his lap, and he read in silence, turning pages with a calm, practiced motion, as though this place were merely a quiet afternoon and not the root of our soul.

A bookbinder. A quiet heart. A life that could have been, though never was.

On the throne to my right sat another me. His robes were ancient in cut, extravagant in a way my world no longer remembered, layered with fabrics dyed in long-forgotten techniques. His hair was trimmed like portraits from dusty histories. Runes glimmered faintly beneath his skin, his fingertips inked not with pigment but with living mana. He did not read, nor drift in peace—he simply watched, chin lifted, a scholar-magus judging both world and self with equal weight.

Both turned to look at me at the same time.

And I, instinctively, turned to look at them in return.

We locked eyes. Or rather—I locked eyes with myself twice over. Thought mirrored thought. Confusion met confusion. Something in the air hummed with tension. We opened our mouths—

“Who are you?” We spoke in perfect unison.

It echoed through the space like a decree. Or a mistake repeated across three throats.

A beat passed. Then another. Silence, heavy and awkward as childhood shame.

We sighed. The same sigh, layered thrice, weary and resigned.

“Right,” we all muttered together. “So you are me, and I am you.”

Another synchronized sigh. As though rehearsed across lifetimes.

The bookbinder-me closed his tome with deliberate gentleness, sliding a ribbon between the pages to mark his place. His gaze lifted, soft but sharp as a stitcher's needle.

“Then the question is not who,” the scholar murmured, his voice soft as vellum turning beneath careful fingers. “The question is which.”

Gold-leaf rings clinked against the arm of his throne as he tightened his grip. Light shimmered within his eyes, ancient script rippling like some luminous language only we could ever read. His robes whispered against stone — relics of ages lost or never lived.

“And more importantly,” he said, gaze heavy with centuries that had never happened but somehow still weighed on his bones, “which of us is the rightful heir to this seat?”

We all shook our heads. Our movements were not synchronized — they were inevitable, as though one thought echoed in three skulls.

“Nay,” I answered, and we answered, the words folding into each other. “We are not fragments. We were not divided.” A breath — shared. “Remember why we were brought here. To become what we already are.”

As though some unseen hand turned us like pieces on a board, each of us lifted a fist and set a cheek against it, elbows resting upon the endless table. Our free hands tapped thoughtfully — parchment, wood, bone, or imagination — it did not matter. The rhythm was one heartbeat cut into thirds.

“We are cowards,” I confessed. No one disagreed. “We are scholars.” A nod from the robed one. “We are investigators.” The binder lifted his eyes from his unread page long enough to acknowledge it. “We are so much more than any single sum.”

A unified sigh followed, the sound carrying into the void like a tired god exhaling creation.

“We are fools.” This time we smiled — each of us with the same crooked, stubborn curve, the same weary acceptance.

“Aye,” I breathed, feeling something tighten in my chest and then release — like a knot remembering it had never been tied to begin with. “What we already are.”

The silence hung like velvet in a cathedral, soft but suffocating. A space between breaths. Between identities.

The scholar folded his hands, eyes luminous with knowledge not yet earned. The binder placed a ribbon between pages that were never written. And I, the version here-and-now, drew in a slow breath. If this place had air, it tasted like parchment dust and candle flame and the press of destiny waiting to be acknowledged.

“We know why we're here,” I said quietly.

The binder looked up at me, brows furrowing with mild annoyance. “Do we? Because I was in the middle of a rather gripping chapter before suddenly becoming involved in existential self-analysis.”

“You were binding the chapter,” the scholar corrected. “Not reading it.”

“Same thing. One reads what one binds.”

“One could bind what one refuses to read.”

“You would know about refusing things. Like joy. Or unstructured thought.”

The scholar looked offended, which felt surreal. Could parts of me insult parts of me? Apparently yes. The binder sniffed and smoothed his apron, the soft canvas stained by years of ink and leather. The scholar lifted his chin, polished and poised like a marble statue of a sage.

I rubbed at my forehead. “Focus.”

Three voices. One heartbeat.

And beneath it all, a whisper familiar enough to make my blood cold: a hunger. An echo of that which waits. That which watches. Danatallion’s Halls do not forget. Power does not sleep. Contracts do not dissolve just because one wishes them to.

“We stand before a throne,” I began. “Three versions of the same soul. The question was which of us is the true heir.”

The scholar’s lip twitched. “Was. Past tense.”

“Because we already know the answer,” I said.

The binder leaned back, arms folding. “And yet we're still sitting in this cavernous void arguing with ourselves. So perhaps we do not know so much as we pretend.”

A pause. The echo of truth.

He wasn’t wrong.

“We have always been too fond of thinking we are above our fears,” the scholar mused. “As though intellect alone shields us.”

“Intellect helps,” the binder replied. “Stories help. They anchor us, remind us of humanity.”

“And yet,” the scholar murmured, “stories are often about kings who believed they could resist corruption.”

The word echoed like a stone striking the ocean’s floor.

Corruption.

A taste rose in the back of my throat. Metallic. Real. Memory or prophecy, I could not say.

“Power reveals,” the scholar continued. “It does not create hunger. It uncovers it.”

“And that,” the binder said, “is precisely what we fear.”

I swallowed. A knot formed in my chest.

“I do not fear gaining power,” I whispered. “I fear losing myself in it.”

We all knew it to be true. A tremor passed through the room, as if the throne itself acknowledged it.

Power is a mirror. And I had seen reflections before. The Dominus. The Walkers. Spirits. Machina. Contracts written in unseen ink. I had seen what power does when it is not questioned. When it calcifies into arrogance. When it seduces.

When it whispers:

Take. Become. Ascend.

The scholar answered first. “We fear not tyranny itself but the subtle path that leads to it. The steps are quiet. Innocent. Justified.”

“And once the crown rests upon the head,” the binder added, tapping the closed book in his lap, “it is so very easy to forget what life felt like before.”

“We are not afraid of power,” I said again. “We are afraid of gluttony.”

The binder gave a small nod. “Of feasting on everything until nothing remains. Until the boy who loved pages becomes a god who devours stories rather than reads them.”

“And history remembers gods poorly,” the scholar murmured. “Every myth of ascension ends either in ruin or worship. Both kill the self.”

A soft hush settled around us, heavy and contemplative.

“What do you fear losing most?” the binder asked gently, voice like warm cloth and quiet afternoons.

It was not a question aimed at either of them. It was aimed at me.

My first instinct was to say Fractal. To say Fallias. To say family. To say humanity. But those were pieces. Fragments. The question was deeper.

“What I fear losing most,” I said slowly, “is choice.”

The scholar leaned forward.

“Elaborate.”

“If power becomes instinct, if desire becomes hunger, if destiny becomes gravity… then the self becomes performance. And I become something inevitable rather than someone choosing.”

“Freedom,” the binder whispered softly. “You fear prison disguised as power.”

I nodded. The words sat in my chest like a truth carved in bone.

“Yes. I fear the crown that claims it grants freedom while chaining every thought. I fear becoming so powerful that I no longer consider whether I should act. Only that I can.”

The scholar smiled, but it was sad.

“You have the mind of a philosopher. The heart of a creator. And the shadow of a king.”

I flinched. The word king echoed too close to other memories. Other spirits. Other chains and thrones and celestial authority.

“In every version of us,” the binder said, “there is the seed of rule. Not because we seek subjects, but because we crave order. Understanding. Purpose.”

“And in purpose,” the scholar added, “lies danger. For purpose can demand sacrifice.”

We fell silent again.

The throne waited.

Once, I imagined power as something distant. Now I felt it breathing behind my spine. Watching. Waiting like a patient predator, offering a leash made of gold.

“Is fear a weakness?” I asked.

The scholar shook his head. “Fear is knowledge.”

The binder nodded. “Fear is memory. Fear is story.”

“And fear,” I said, “is a warning.”

We stared at one another. Not in hostility, nor suspicion, but with the weight of mutual knowing. We are one mind, split only long enough to question itself.

“If I take this seat,” I murmured, “I must be more than I am. But if I refuse it—”

“You will never become what you already are,” the scholar finished.

“And something else will fill the vacuum,” the binder added. “A worse version. Or worse: nothing at all.”

I closed my eyes briefly. The throne felt colder now. Or perhaps warmer. Realer.

“What if I do not deserve power?” I whispered.

The scholar chuckled. “Those who ask that question are the only ones who ever do.”

“And what if I cannot restrain myself?”

The binder smiled gently. “Then we will restrain you.”

A beat.

“We,” I repeated.

“Yes,” the binder said. “For you are not alone. Never alone.”

The scholar nodded. “We are not three. We are one. Debate is not division. Debate is integrity.”

I exhaled slowly. Something inside my chest softened and sharpened at once.

“What if power corrupts absolutely?” I asked quietly.

“It does,” the scholar said.

“It can,” the binder countered.

“And yet,” they both finished together, “we are not children.”

I blinked. They stared at me.

“We are not reckless,” the scholar said calmly. “We plan. We calculate. We observe.”

“We love,” the binder added softly. “Books. People. Peace. Warmth. We know what matters.”

“And those who know love,” the scholar murmured, “are harder to break.”

“Unless love becomes weakness,” I whispered.

The binder raised a brow. “Or unless power convinces you it is.”

A hum rippled through the endless hall. Like something approving. Or amused.

“So what then?” I asked. “What is our path?”

The scholar looked to the binder. The binder looked to me. And then we spoke in unison.

“To grow.”

“To hunger without devouring.”

“To rise without consuming.”

“To become without losing.”

“To carry power, not be carried by it.”

“To choose. Always choose.”

The words thrummed. Not spell. Not oath. Not yet. But truth acknowledged.

The binder stood first. His throne creaked softly. He pressed the closed book to his chest.

“I fear being forgotten,” he admitted. “I fear becoming myth rather than man. I fear losing touch with the ordinary.”

The scholar rose next. His robe swept across stone like passing wind.

“I fear arrogance,” he said. “Knowledge without humility. Wisdom without empathy. Logic without compassion.”

Then they both looked at me.

“And you?” they asked.

I rose slowly. The throne behind me felt less like a seat and more like a promise, gilded and patient.

“I fear becoming inevitable.”

Silence again.

The binder breathed out. “Then do not.”

The scholar nodded. “Power does not erase choice. It only amplifies consequence.”

“And if I fail?”

“Then you rise again,” the binder said simply.

“And again,” the scholar added.

“Until we get it right.”

The trembling in my chest stilled.

“So,” I whispered. “Who takes the throne?”

The binder looked thoughtful. “That is the wrong question.”

The scholar smiled faintly. “The throne is not meant to be taken.”

I frowned. “Then what is it meant to be?”

They answered as one.

“Balanced.”

Realization hit me like light through stained glass.

Not one. Not two. All three.

The throne shimmered. Its shape softened, then multiplied, dividing into three equal seats of gold and ivory and fabric I still could not name.

The binder placed his hand on his seat.

The scholar touched his with a reverence that might once have been worship.

I faced mine. My pulse steadied. Not racing. Not fearful. Steady. Determined.

The binder smiled at me, warm as sun on leather.

“Power is a tool. Not a hunger.”

The scholar bowed his head slightly, respect layered with warning.

“Knowledge guards against temptation. Reflection guards against tyranny.”

“And choice,” I breathed, “guards the soul.”

I sat.

The throne accepted me. Not as ruler. As piece of a whole.

Something vast stirred. Not outside. Inside. A resonance. A joining. Like three voices tuning into harmony, no longer echoing but merging into one true sound.

We did not crown ourselves. We did not declare dominion. We aligned.

And the darkness around us cracked like an eggshell struck from within.

Light bled through the world. Gentle. Dawn breaking in reverse, from the center outward. The thrones dissolved into gold dust, carried away on wind that did not exist moments ago.

We remained.

Self. Unified. Alert. Whole.

“What now?” I asked quietly.

The scholar faded first, his last words soft as falling sand. “Remember fear. It keeps you awake.”

The binder followed, voice warm as a hearth at dusk. “Remember love. It keeps you human.”

They were gone.

I was alone. And not alone.

I looked at my hand. It did not tremble. Power coiled not around me but in me. Waiting. Patient. Willing to obey.

I had not conquered it. I had not surrendered to it.

We had agreed.

A soft laugh rose in my throat. Quiet. Honest.

“I do not fear power,” I whispered to the empty room.

“I fear forgetting why I sought it.”

And now, I would not.
