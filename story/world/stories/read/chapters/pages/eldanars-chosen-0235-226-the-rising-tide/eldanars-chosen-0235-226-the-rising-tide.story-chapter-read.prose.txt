The ringing silence of the cleared walkway did not last.

"Well, look on the bright side, lad," Bhel wheezed, leaning heavily against the wall as he wiped a thick smear of dark, foul-smelling sludge from his beard. He offered Josh a weak, incredibly strained grin, though his dark eyes were tight with the agony of his fresh bleeding chest. "At least the wind is blowing the stench away from us. If we were downwind, I reckon I’d have fainted by now."

Josh let out a short, ragged breath, his shoulders dropping slightly as the tight, coiled tension from the fight finally dissolved. He looked at the dwarven fighter, a faint, weary trace of amusement trying and failing to reach his storm-grey eyes.

"I don't think dwarves faint, Bhel," Josh murmured, his voice incredibly rough. "I think you just fall over like a dropped anvil."

"Aye, and we make twice as much noise doing it," Bhel chuckled, though the laughter instantly cut off into a sharp, breathy hiss of pain as he clutched his heavily bandaged ribs.

Josh stepped forward, ready to reach out and steady his friend. But before he could take a single pace, a soft, wet slithering sound echoed from the blood-slicked floorboards behind them.

The sound was tiny, completely masked by the distant, rhythmic thudding of the horde below the walls, but to Josh's heightened, battle-honed senses, it sounded like a thunderclap.

He spun around, raising his dark steel heater shield by sheer instinct.

Lying on his back near the edge of the parapet, the young boy in the oversized boiled leather tunic began to twitch.

The movement was completely unnatural, a series of rapid, violent spasms that made the boy's limbs jerk like a puppet being yanked by tangled strings. His shattered, ruined throat, which had been completely torn open by the bloated zombie's jaws, was no longer bleeding bright crimson. The blood pooling on the wooden walkway had turned a thick, stagnant shade of dark purple.

Then, the boy's milky, lifeless eyes snapped open.

They did not hold the terrified, innocent light of the seventeen-year-old stable hand who had bravely held his spear just minutes ago. They burned with a dull, sickening green soul-fire.

"Light preserve us," a nearby militia member screamed, dropping his iron spear as he scrambled backward in sheer, unadulterated panic. "He's turning! They're turning!"

All across the blood-stained sector of the walkway, the five fallen militia members began to push themselves up from the floorboards. Their bones popped and cracked loudly as the dark, ambient necrotic energy of the siege flooded their dead vessels, forcefully animating the fresh meat.

The psychological impact was instantaneous and devastating. The civilians, who had just barely reclaimed their courage after Josh and Bhel's heroic charge, erupted into a chaotic scream of terror. They stumbled over one another, backing away from the newly risen horrors as if the wall itself were collapsing.

"Kill them!" Captain Aris roared, his voice ringing with absolute, cold pragmatism as he charged down the walkway, leading a squad of heavily armoured spearmen. "Do not hesitate! They are no longer your brothers! Strike them down before they find their footing!"

A seasoned city guardsman did not wait for the order to settle. He stepped forward with a grim, hollow expression and drove his heavy iron spearhead directly through the forehead of the rising boy.

The strike was clean and merciful. The sickly green soul-fire in the boy's eyes instantly flickered and died, and his body collapsed back onto the walkway as a heavy, permanent corpse. The remaining four reanimated militia members were systematically destroyed in a matter of seconds, pinned to the floorboards by multiple spears and decapitated before they could take a single step towards the terrified civilians.

Josh stood perfectly still, his longsword lowered, watching the guardsmen pull their bloody spears free from the fresh dead.

The horrific reality of the magic they were facing crashed over him like a physical, suffocating wave. They were not just fighting dungeon beasts born of ambient mana. They were fighting the world itself. Every single defender who fell upon these timber walls would instantly join the ranks of the enemy, turned into a weapon to be used against their own friends.

And looking down at the squall of rotting, purple flesh pressing against the base of the thirty-foot wall, Josh finally understood the true, tragic origin of the horde.

Those thousands of shambling zombies, those rotting villagers wearing tattered linen shirts and simple woollen trousers, they were not monsters from a deep barrow. They were the common folk of the outlying northern settlements. They were farmers, weavers, and children who had been caught in the initial, rapid spread of the dark corruption, raised and leashed to the Bone General's will.

He was standing on a wall, brutally hacking apart the very people he had sworn to protect.

A heavy, suffocating hand of pure guilt squeezed his chest. He turned his head away, unable to look at the boy's ruined face any longer, and began the long, heavy-footed march back to the central gatehouse, Bhel trailing quietly beside him.

As the morning wore on, the fighting below them settled into a slow, grinding, and exhausting rhythm.

The Bone General was playing a calculated game of attrition. The heavily armoured skeletal knights and the towering Goliaths remained perfectly static in the distance, waiting patiently just outside of bow range, whilst the endless ocean of lesser zombies was continuously funnelled into the meat grinder.

The tactics of the defence became entirely mechanical. Every ten or fifteen minutes, a new, writhing pile of rotting bodies would successfully scale the timber wall, scrambling over their own dead to crest the parapet. A guard captain would scream an order, a high-tier adventurer would sprint down the walkway to shatter the breach, whilst a mage would destroy the foundation and the pile would collapse back into the smoking alchemical pitch below.

Josh and Bhel reached the central platform, where Brett and Carcan were waiting.

The elven healer did not offer Bhel a sweet smile or a word of gentle relief. The moment her silver eyes locked onto the fresh, bright crimson spots blooming across the pristine white bandages wrapping his chest, her features set into a mask of pure, terrifying stone.

"Sit down," Carcan commanded, her melodic voice dropping into a chilling, quiet octave that left absolutely no room for debate.

"I am perfectly capable of—" Bhel began, raising his thick hands defensively.

"Sit. Down," Carcan repeated, stepping forward and pressing her small, delicate hand firmly against his shoulder.

The dwarf physically shrank, his booming bravado vanishing instantly. He dropped heavily onto a wooden supply crate, muttering dark, colourful dwarven curses into his beard as he looked away.

Carcan did not waste a second. She knelt in the dirt and blood of the platform, her amber staff pulsing with a concentrated, warm golden light. She pressed her palms directly over the bleeding bandages, her eyes closing as she wove a delicate, complex thread of healing magic directly into his torn chest cavity, forcefully sealing the ruptured blood vessels and repairing the torn stitches.

"You are an absolute child, Bhel," Carcan scolded softly, her voice trembling slightly with a mixture of anger and deep, protective fear. "If you open these wounds again, I will personally bind your arms to your sides with iron chains."

"Aye, and who's going to swing the axes then?" Bhel mumbled, though he remained perfectly still, letting the soothing holy magic clear the grey exhaustion from his pale face.

Josh stood near the railing, resting his heavy heater shield against his leg. He looked over at Brett.

The elemental mage looked completely hollowed out. His pale face was covered in a thick layer of black soot and cold sweat, and his long, elegant fingers were trembling visibly. The Ember-silk cloak wrapped around his shoulders was still humming with absorbed heat, but Brett's personal mana core was running dangerously dry.

"We are just trimming the hedges, Josh," Brett whispered, his voice incredibly dry from the heat. He pointed a long, smoking finger towards the right flank of the defensive line, roughly eighty yards away. "Look there."

Josh followed his gaze.

A massive, terrifying structure was rapidly forming against the timber wall. The zombies were no longer building a simple, triangular ramp. Driven by some dark, coordinated directive, they were actively constructing a hollow, vertical tower made entirely of their own interlocking, writhing bodies.

Hundreds of ghouls and skeletal beasts were scrambling upward, their claws digging into the ribcages and limbs of their comrades, creating a solid, moving elevator of rotting flesh that was rising at an unchecked pace. It was already twenty-five feet high, mere seconds away from spilling over the upper parapet and completely overwhelming a terrified, shivering squad of civilian militia.

The archers were firing frantically, but their arrows were simply being absorbed into the mass of meat without slowing the ascent.

"I don't have the mana to melt that base, Josh," Brett admitted quietly, his eyes dark with worry. "And if that tower reaches the top, the entire right flank will buckle."

Just as the highest point of the writhing undead tower crested the thirty-foot mark, the dark, smoky air surrounding the section warped violently.

Kaelen, the broad-shouldered dwarven Stoneweaver they had joined forces with in the forest, emerged from a nearby stone watchtower. Her earth-brown robes were heavily splattered with mud, and she carried her massive granite staff in both hands.

Kaelen did not shout. She simply slammed the heavy base of her staff directly onto the wooden walkway.

A deep, deafening subterranean roar echoed from the earth far below the timber walls. The ground outside the gatehouse violently fractured. A massive, jagged fissure split the freezing mud directly beneath the foundational centre of the rising undead tower.

The earth did not just open; it actively swallowed.

A powerful terrestrial vortex dragged the lowest layers of the interlocking zombies deep into the stone bedrock. The sudden, violent displacement of gravity caused the massive, thirty-foot tower to tilt sharply to the left. With its structural foundation forcefully removed, the entire column of rotting meat sheared away from the wall, tipping sideways in a slow-motion avalanche of flailing limbs and shattering bone.

They crashed heavily onto the plains, sending a massive spray of freezing mud and pulverised bone into the sky.

A ragged, deafening cheer erupted from the terrified militia members who had been mere seconds away from being slaughtered. They waved their spears, their tear-stained faces bright with a sudden, desperate surge of hope.

"We have geomancers," Josh noted, a small, genuine spark of confidence returning to his chest.

"We have a few," Brett corrected quietly, his tone offering absolutely no comfort as he looked down the line of defenders.

The elemental mage turned his pale, analytical eyes back to the platform, looking at the dozens of lower-tier casters and civilian archers slumped against the wooden barricades.

The reality of the defensive grid was horrific. It had been barely two hours since the first zombie threw itself into the pitch trench, yet the lower-tier defenders were already completely spent. Their quivers were entirely empty, their faces were drained of all colour from severe mana sickness, and their arms were trembling so violently they could barely lift their water skins.

They had held the wall against the first wave, but the victory was an illusion.

"We are not managing our resources, Josh," Brett warned, his voice tight with a cold, logical dread. "The General has only sent the trash. He is using the cheap, expendable villagers to force us to empty our quivers and burn our cores. And look at them. They have absolutely nothing left to give."

Brett pointed a long, trembling hand towards the distant northern hill, where the pale morning light was fully illuminating the battlefield.

The thousands of heavily armoured skeletal knights and the towering Bone Goliaths had not taken a single step forward. They stood in perfect, silent order, a massive, unyielding wall of dark steel and bone, waiting patiently for the magic of Ashenfall to burn itself to ash.

"If they charge now," Brett finished softly, "we will not have the power to stop them. We are running out of time."
