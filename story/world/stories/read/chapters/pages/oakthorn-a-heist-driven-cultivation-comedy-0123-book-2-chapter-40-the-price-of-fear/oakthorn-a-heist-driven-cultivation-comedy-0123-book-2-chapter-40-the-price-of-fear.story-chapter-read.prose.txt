The corrupted paladin’s greatsword missed Nora’s throat by a finger’s width, sending a rush of displaced air across her face. Nora spun with the momentum, bringing her own blade around in a defensive arc that scattered light across the cathedral’s broken interior.

“Four hells, you’re fast.” Nora planted her feet, sword raised. “Nora Lancaster. And you are?”

The corrupted woman’s laugh was dry as bone. “You stand before Elizabeth Conroy, last true servant of Honor.” Her eyes—swirling with emerald malice—flicked dismissively across Nora’s plain armor. “What? No sigils? No markings?” She scoffed, the sound surprisingly wet. “You deserve my blade, deserter.”

They circled each other, blades catching the strange light of Bernard’s ritual. The corrupted paladin’s armor still bore Honor’s sunburst sigil, though it pulsed with the same sickly green energy that coursed beneath her skin.

“Deserter? There are plenty of wandering knights in this land. How do you know I’m not one of them?” Nora replied, feinting left before withdrawing. Testing. “Besides, paladin orders are overrated. Those oaths they swear are just pretty words to ugly ideals.”

Elizabeth’s face contorted with rage. “JUST. WORDS?”

She lunged forward, her strike so swift that Nora barely managed to parry. The impact sent shockwaves up her arms.

Gods, she’s strong.

“Bernard’s debrief on you was quite thorough, you know. You cannot fool me with your wordplay,” Elizabeth snarled, pressing her advantage with a flurry of strikes that forced Nora back across broken tiles. “His network is... extensive. We knew everything about your little crew before we even left the peerless Honor’s Light.”

The corrupted paladin made a gesture of respect at the mention of her deity’s capital.

Nora deflected another blow, pivoting on her back heel to create some distance. Around them, the battle raged in chaotic violence. The origami soldiers—hundreds of them—swarmed from the Pandemonium Box, their paper bodies somehow slicing through corrupted flesh with terrifying efficiency.

The moment she’d opened the cube, a powerful yet unstable telepathic link had seared into her mind. It gave her limited control over the legions pouring out of the box. But it was exhausting to constantly keep a part of her mind occupied in reminding the textile soldiers to not kill her allies.

“Spearman, go attack those flaming skeletons. Aim for their eyesockets!” she commanded quickly before returning her attention to the varicose-veined paladin.

“Oh, you know about us? Is gossiping part of Honor’s code now?” Nora taunted, directing another portion of the paper soldiers toward a section of wall where cracks were spreading. The little constructs barely responded, their chaotic energy resisting her control. “Or did you get excommunicated for being too chatty?”

Elizabeth’s eyes flashed. “Morana sends her regards.”

Nora’s blood went cold. “What did you just say?”

“Morana. Scorn’s siren was oh-so-willing to chat to our informants when it came to you and that upstart over there.” Elizabeth’s smile was razor-thin. “She had quite the tale to tell about your... departure from the Order. How you abandoned your knights to fight each other for your old position. How you betrayed your oaths at the first sign of difficulty. And how you now use illegal magicks to trick those who would bring you to justice. Well, you’ll find I’m not so easily vexed.”

The memory struck like a physical blow—Morana’s face, twisted and screaming as Evie hung from chains in a golden cage. Her paladins rushing to fight her in the middle of the night. The blood on the temple steps.

“You don’t know shit about what happened,” Nora growled.

Elizabeth laughed. “I know enough. I know you’re an oath-breaker. A traitor. Worse than scum.”

She used Nora’s momentary distraction to lunge forward, her corrupted blade slicing through Nora’s leather pauldron and drawing first blood.

“Shit!” Nora hissed, warm wetness spreading across her shoulder.

“First blood to Honor,” Elizabeth proclaimed with vicious satisfaction, her eyes gleaming with fanatical triumph. “Your impurity weakens you. If you’d kept your vows—”

“Vows,” Nora spat, finding her center again. “You want to talk vows? Look around you! This cathedral—this entire marsh—is Honor’s domain. Your god built this place to contain something ancient. And now what? You’re helping Bernard unleash it?”

Elizabeth’s strike faltered, something like uncertainty flickering behind the corruption. “I serve Honor’s true purpose. The Light promised I would always serve, even when they cast me out.”

“And Bernard promised you what? That defiling a sacred site is service?” Nora pressed, seeing the crack in Elizabeth’s zealotry. “That helping this... thing inside him is Honor’s will?”

“SILENCE!” Elizabeth roared, her blade a silver blur as she charged.

Nora forced herself to laugh. “Poor Elizabeth. So desperate for purpose she’ll believe anything that feeds her obsession. Tell me, ex-Arbiter, how does it feel to know deep down that Honor broke his word to you. Honor, the oathbreaker. Has a ring to it, doesn’t it?”

That pushed Elizabeth over the edge. Her eyes blazed with emerald fire as corruption flooded her system. Her next attack came with inhuman strength, nearly tearing Nora’s sword from her grip.

“Some things,” Nora grunted, struggling to maintain her defense, “matter more than oaths.”

“NOTHING matters more than an oath!” Elizabeth screamed, each word punctuated by a devastating strike. “Without honor, we are nothing! Without our word, we are NOTHING!”

The wall behind them shattered. Skeletal abominations poured into the cathedral—the same monsters they’d fought in the depths of the crypt, now drawn to the surface by Bernard’s command. The origami soldiers met them in a surreal clash of paper against bone.

She’s burning her life force for power, Nora realized, noticing the ashen pallor spreading beneath the corruption on Elizabeth’s skin. Every strike was fueled by both zealotry and the unnatural strength granted by Bernard’s corruption. The combination made her lethal.

“You know what your problem is, Liz?” Nora said, ducking under a vicious swing. “You never asked why Honor kicked you out in the first place.”

“They feared my devotion!” Elizabeth snarled, her blade crashing into a stone column as Nora dodged. “They feared what true service meant!”

“They feared you’d become exactly what you are now,” Nora countered, her voice steady despite her racing pulse. “A weapon pointed at the wrong target.”

For half a heartbeat, something like clarity flickered in Elizabeth’s corrupted gaze. Then Bernard’s voice cut through the chaos.

“Enough talk! Kill her, Elizabeth. Prove your worth.”

The corrupted paladin’s shoulders straightened, whatever doubt Nora had planted instantly crushed beneath renewed fanaticism. She attacked with renewed fury, each blow threatening to shatter Nora’s guard.

There was only one option. The option Nora hated.

Fear magic.

The darkness stirred within her, eager and hungry. Cold power flooded her veins, turning her vision sharp and predatory. The tendrils of fear magic swirled around her blade like black smoke, eager to feed on Elizabeth’s deepest terrors.

When their swords met again, Nora’s blade bit deeper, leaving traces of shadow that clung to Elizabeth’s armor. The corrupted paladin faltered, her eyes widening as the fear magic began to take hold.

“What—what is this?” Elizabeth gasped, suddenly defensive. “What foul magic—”

“You wanted to know why I left the Fateweavers?” Nora asked, her voice dropping to a dangerous whisper. “This is why. Because some things you can’t pray away. Some things can’t be contained by pretty oaths and shiny armor.”

Elizabeth’s next attack was clumsy, her focus clearly disrupted by whatever nightmare the fear magic had conjured from her subconscious. Nora pressed her advantage, each strike more precise than the last.

“Destiny used me just like Honor’s using you,” Nora continued, shadows dancing along her blade. “I was a tool. A weapon. Nothing more.”

Behind them, another wave of skeletal abominations poured from the widening cracks. The origami soldiers fought valiantly, but they were beginning to falter, their paper forms tearing under relentless assault.

Liz’s sword hissed through the air in a vertical arc descending for Nora’s skull. She twisted her grip, parrying the strike off of the flat of her blade. She left the corrupted paladin’s blade use her own as a guide, and with her incredible strength, the Elizabeth’s sword dug deep into the cathedral’s stone floor. Nora headbutted the woman, green ichor exploding from Conroy’s nose from the blow. The Dreadcaller followed up with a devastating blow to her sternum with the pommel of her greatsword.

She was about to finish the woman off, but an explosion of blue light drew her attention. Her vision flicked to the cathedral entrance. Through the chaos of moving limbs of paper and bone, she witnessed Guildford dissipate into nothingness as Augustus disemboweled the horned poltergeist.

Evie was cornered.

“Elena!” Nora called out, parrying Elizabeth’s increasingly desperate attacks. “Evie needs backup! Augustus is pushing her toward the marshes!”

From the corner of her eye, she saw Elena disengage from her opponent, sprinting across the cathedral floor with preternatural speed. “On it!”

The moment of distraction cost Nora. Elizabeth’s blade slipped past her guard, opening a gash across her ribs that instantly soaked her shirt with crimson. Pain lanced through her, hot and immediate.

“Looks like we’re even,” Nora acknowledged through gritted teeth. “Shall we go for the tiebreaker?”

Elizabeth’s answer was a horizontal slash that Nora barely ducked beneath. As she rose, Nora channeled more fear magic into her counterattack, sending shadowy tendrils snaking toward Elizabeth’s face.

The excommunicated Arbiter screamed, clawing at her helmet as if trying to tear away invisible spiders. Her sword swings became wild, desperate.

I hate this, Nora thought as she methodically exploited each opening. Gods, I hate this.

With each erratic arc from Elizabeth, Nora patiently snaked her sword in to cut straps, shins, and wrists—any weak point in the paladin’s thick steel plate. After nearly a minute of this, the woman before her was riddled with over a dozen bleeding wounds.

“You will burn for this, heretic!” Liz screeched, green ichor spilling out of her in thick rivulets.

Nora responded with an explosion of darkness in the woman’s face.

She screamed.

Using fear magic always left her feeling tainted. It wasn’t just the connection to Dread—though that was certainly part of it. It was what the magic revealed about her: her willingness to inflict psychological agony for tactical advantage. It was this cold calculation that allowed her to turn someone’s deepest fears against them that terrified her.

Because a small part of her was unsettlingly okay with this. And she hated that most of all.

Elizabeth, despite her earlier boasting, was incapable of rebuffing Nora’s magicks. But something abnormal was happening with the residual energy of Nora’s attacks. With each blow, Nora felt dark energy pooling at her feet instead of dissipating. She followed its trail, and the oddity turned into horror as she saw that it wasn’t simply pooling at her feet. It was moving.

The fear magic was drawing toward the ritual circle like water circling a drain.

And with each pulse of fear magic, Nora felt something else: a presence. Ancient. Familiar. Hungry.

“Oh shit, oh shit, oh shit,” Nora whispered as she ducked underneath a vicious horizontal slice.

She stepped into Elizabeth’s guard and sent a gauntleted fist into the woman’s jaw. Her head whipped back, but her supernaturally toughened muscles kept what would’ve been a knockout blow merely painful.

Her magic continued to flow into the fissure conjured by Bernard’s ritual.

She knew what was closing in—what yanked on her dominion’s energy like a man climbing a rope up a cliff.

Dread.

He was close—closer than he’d been in years. The boundary between worlds was thinning, and her magic was like a beacon guiding him toward that weakness.

Way to go, Nora. You’re basically rolling out the welcome mat for the god of nightmares.

A flash of movement caught her attention. In her brief distraction Elizabeth had sprinted away. She followed the corrupted paladin’s trajectory, and her heart slammed against her chestplate.

She was angling toward where Evie fought against Augustus at the cathedral’s entrance.

Time slowed to a crawl.

“EVIE!” Nora screamed, but her voice seemed distant, underwater.

Elizabeth lunged toward the siren, blade extended for a killing blow. Evie, locked in combat with Augustus, wouldn’t see it coming.

Something snapped inside Nora—a final restraint, a last moral anchor. She stabbed her greatsword into the stone and raised both hands. Fear magic exploded from her palms in a concussive wave, blacker than midnight and cold as the void. Her spell ignored the monsters that crawled along the ground and columns. It had one target. And with a splash of silver-gold sparks, it collided with its target.

Fear engulfed Elizabeth completely, freezing her mid-stride.

The corrupted paladin’s scream died in her throat as she was subjected to every terror she had ever known, all at once. Her body contorted, muscles locking as her mind broke under the onslaught. Nora poured everything she had into waking nightmares.

Nora stalked forward, her eyes bleeding darkness. Her sword remained sheathed in the stones behind her. This wasn’t tactical anymore. This wasn’t calculated. This was raw, maternal fury channeled through the prism of her darkest power.

“You coward. You don’t get to touch her,” Nora growled, her voice barely human. “You don’t even look at her.”

With a final, brutal stroke, she clenched her fists and twisted. Shadowy cords, already wrapped around the corrupted paladin’s neck and head, severed Elizabeth’s head from her shoulders. The corrupted body collapsed, twitching slightly before going still. Green ichor leaked from the wound and into the hungry soil of the marshes.

The moment Elizabeth fell, Nora felt the shift. The temperature in the cathedral plummeted. The cracks in the floor widened, revealing not just more skeletal abominations, but something deeper—a glimpse into a shadowy realm that should have remained sealed.

A familiar laugh echoed from that darkness, sending ice through Nora’s veins.

“Daughter,” the voice crooned. “How kind of you to open the door.”

Dread was coming through.

The god of fear materialized partially through the widening fissure—a towering figure of shadow and bone, his antlered crown scraping the cathedral ceiling. Only his upper body had emerged, but that was enough to fill the space with suffocating dread.

“I’ve missed our little talks,” he said, eyes like twin pits of endless darkness fixing on Nora. “The nightmares you’ve caused in my absence have been... adequate. But nothing compares to the real thing, now does it?”

Nora raised her fists, though she knew it would be useless against a god. “Get back in your hole, Dread. You’re not welcome here.”

The god’s laughter shook dust from the ceiling. “But I was invited! By you, little nightmare. By your hatred. By your fear.”

He extended a skeletal hand, fingers longer than Nora was tall. “Let me show you what I’ve learned in my exile. Let me show you true fear.”

The blast hit Nora like a physical blow, knocking her back several paces. But the real impact was within—memories long suppressed suddenly erupting into vivid life.

Six years old, hiding in the cellar as her father’s footsteps creaked overhead, searching. The terror of being found. The worse terror of what would happen when he did.

Twelve years old, holding a knife to her sister’s left arm because Father commanded it to punish them both for talking back to him. Seeing the trust in Seryn’s eyes even as the blade drew blood.

Fifteen, standing over her father’s sleeping form, Seryn beside her, both of them trembling as they prepared to end his life.

Nora fell to her knees, overwhelmed by the cascade of memories. Each heartbeat brought a new horror, each breath a fresh terror. Distantly, she was aware of the battle continuing around her—Cade working frantically at the ritual circle, the origami soldiers being overwhelmed by skeletal monsters, Evie and Elena fighting desperately against Augustus.

They needed her. But she couldn’t move, couldn’t break free from Dread’s mental assault.

“This is your true nature,” Dread whispered in her mind. “My perfect little instrument of fear. Why fight it? Embrace what you are.”

Through the haze of remembered terror, a different voice reached her—small, desperate, familiar.

“Nora! NORA!”

Evie. Calling for her.

Nora forced her eyes open, fighting against the weight of Dread’s influence. Across the cathedral, Evie was pinned by Augustus, his massive hand around her throat.

“Let her die,” Dread suggested. “Then you’ll truly be mine again. I can make you forget all of this pain. All of this uncertainty. Give me your fear, child.”

“No.” The word escaped Nora’s lips as a barely audible whisper. Then, stronger: “NO.”

Nora Lancaster reached deep within herself, past the fear, past the shame, to something fundamental—the core of who she was. She was not just a Dreadcaller. Not just an ex-paladin.

She was a protector. A woman who had clawed her way out of darkness once before and would do it again.

With trembling limbs, Nora forced herself to stand. Each movement was agony, pushing against the tide of terror that Dread poured into her. But she moved anyway, one step after another. The god’s magical tendrils snapped and bit into her flesh, forming long lacerations down each and every one of her appendages. But she stepped again.

And again.

And again.

With shaking fingers, she wrapped a hand around the hilt of her sword.

Blood dripped from her brows and into her eyes and mouth. But she blinked through the agony and met fear in the eyes.

“Impressive,” Dread admitted, seemingly amused by her resistance. “But futile.”

Another blast of fear magic struck her, stronger than before. This time, the memory was crystal clear—the night she and Seryn were meant to kill their father.

They stood over his bed, both teenagers shaking with a mixture of terror and resolve. Their father slept soundly, unaware of their presence. Unaware that his years of cruelty had led to this moment.

“I can’t,” her older sister whispered, her knife trembling in her hand. “Nora, I can’t do it.”

Nora tightened her grip on her own blade. “Then I will. For both of us.”

She raised the knife, the metal catching moonlight from the window. One thrust. That’s all it would take. One moment of courage to end years of torment.

Her father’s eyes opened.

“My daughters,” he said calmly, as if he’d expected to find them looming over him with blades. “Come to kill the monster at last?”

Nora’s arm froze mid-descent.

“Do it,” he urged, his voice oddly gentle. “You’ve wanted to for so long. Both of you have. I’ve seen it in your eyes. The hatred. The fear.”

He sat up slowly, making no move to defend himself. “But you won’t. Because deep down, you know the truth. You’re just like me.”

Nora felt something cold and terrible settle in her chest. Because part of her—the darkest, most honest part—knew he was right.
