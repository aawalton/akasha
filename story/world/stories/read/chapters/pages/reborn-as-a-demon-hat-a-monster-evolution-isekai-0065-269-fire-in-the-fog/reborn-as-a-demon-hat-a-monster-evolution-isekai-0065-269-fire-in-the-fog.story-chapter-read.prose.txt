A firebolt went trailing through the mist-coated skies of Argwyll’s Southernmost Mountain range.

“Contact!”

Within the dense coverage, one Hybrid called out to her comrades. She then bent low, rolled, and just managed to avoid the nasty spell through sheer dexterity alone. The firebolt slammed into a mossy wall behind her, turning its surface to pitch.

And then came two more, headed straight for her.

She whistled, impressed by the tenacity of her opponent. But, tracing the trajectory of his two spells, she could now pinpoint his location with near-complete accuracy.

“Twenty-five meters, Northeast!”

She didn’t need to stop and wait for her comrades to reply. Instead, she drew her daggers and sprinted forward, jumping one firebolt and then slicing the next one apart – her enhanced Onixian blades cleaving through the energized flame like cutting a well-done cake. Her opponent, cloaked in shadow and mist, must have realized her intent, and quickly flapped his hands in an attempt to cast a spell of binding.

But as soon as the thought occurred to him, he found that his stone-hands simply wouldn’t obey his command.

He [Appraised] himself, seeing that spectral chains coiled round his arms, seeing that the spell that had been cast on him must have been cast from the hands of an extremely potent magus – perhaps an [Archmage] or [Master Warlock].

These days, you just couldn’t be sure of what class – or class combos – you were dealing with.

That fact was made abundantly clear as the dagger-wielding hybrid sprang forward and sank her both her blades into the neck of the Golem mage, cleaving through his stone-skin and leaving him to crumble into the dirt from which he was spawned.

The Hybrid kept her grip on her daggers as her opponent died, however. Because in this place, no one was truly alone.

The canyon around her grew dark, and cold. Wisps of magically-dominated fog lapped at her ankles, and the ground rumbled with a familiar, deafening pitch.

And in this place that meant one thing, and one thing alone…

Her nimble paws moved of their own accord, avoiding the grasping grey claws that burst from the ground beneath her. She somersaulted and sent one Onixian throwing knife right into the palm of one claw, shattering it and its owner to pieces before the rest of its brethren emerged from their hiding places.

She counted fourteen of them, surrounding her on all sides.

And she smirked.

That made it a pretty even fight.

She readied her blades, hissed, and waited for the bulky golems to make the first move.

Instead, she felt another shockwave ripple through the ground, and felt the impact of something far heavier than a golem strike the earth before her.

“Woah – woa-!”

She almost fell of balance as the creature who had just jumped from the mountain above and fallen right in front of her took a mere two seconds to regain his balance. His large, muscular form was visible through even the dark leathers he wore that matched her own.

And he let out a battlecry that tore through the foggy heavens as he punched the ground, once, in front of his feet.

Instantly – a feeling of pressure building beneath the earth rocketed up each golems grey limbs. Their bodies shook, sensing the powerful seismic vibrations, and then rushed the second Hybrid with silent fury.

…only to find that their legs had already crumbled beneath them, the shockwave having ruptured through their gangly limbs and destroyed them before the golems even knew what the larger Hybrid had done.

With a series of frenzied kicks to each one of their abdomens, the new Hybrid finished off the entire brood in less than ten seconds, each one of his strikes hitting a pressure point that his eyes told him would result in an immediate kill.

When the last golem fell, the feline Hybrid shook her head, incredulous, but barely even surprised.

“You really had to swoop in and be my little rescuer, didn’t you?”

The newly arrived Hybrid removed his hood, revealing his fine combed, hairy Lycae features.

And he immediately made a beeline for his comrade, so much so that she didn’t know whether to hold up a dagger or her arms for him.

He approached her and, without warning, held a massive paw to her stomach.

“Did they hurt you?” he asked, panting not through exertion, but anxiety. “Did – did any of them –“

“Oh, by the Archon!” she laughed, pushing his hand away and removing her own hood. “Can you give the whole concerned father thing a rest already?”

He wasn’t for listening. He never was, these days.

“We talked about this, Tara.”

“Yeah, we did! And you promised me you wouldn’t cause a scene during a fight!”

“Well – I lied. Ok?”

Both of them launched into an argument that had lasted at least for the past year, not noticing how the fallen remnants of the golems began to shift and move around their feet.

“Klax,” Tara sighed. “Listen, we have to –“

“On your right!”

Klax’s movements were swift – he’d lunged to try and interpose himself between his mate and the giant boulder that came crashing from the mountains down towards her. But Tara was faster. Her enhanced feline senses allowed her to deftly track the trajectory of the object before it had even been hurled. She’d gracefully rolled out of the way before Klax’s warning had left his mouth, leaving the Lycae to smash the stone to pieces with a hardy headbutt.

“That’s using your brains, doggo!”

Tara’s quip came from behind him, where she was watching the stones of their fallen enemies shudder and shake on the ground, before coalescing together like a pack of ants. The fragments of the destroyed golems weaved themselves a giant torso and legs within just a few seconds, and from this headless torso burst four stone-limbs that were already poised to strike at the two Hybrids staring blindly up at them.

“How about it?” Tara asked her companion as she drew her shortbow. “We do this one together?”

Klax licked his lips, smiling in spite of the situation.

“Together.”

The limbs came down, smashing through the ground and sending tremors throughout the entire canyon section. Tara’s bow made its retort – firing four arrows at once that latched on to each limb and stayed there, impaled on the grey matter of the construct. With a roar the creature spun to crush her. It would have caught her were it not for Klax’s charge at its feet – an attack that knocked it off balance successfully and pushed it well back into the base of the nearest canyon.

The hulking brute let out a roar from its mouthless torso, sending all of its limbs arcing down at the grinning Lycae beneath it.

And then – with a snap of her fingers – Tara’s arrows detonated.

All four of the giant golem’s limbs exploded in a hail of fire and stone – sending pebbles and boulders spraying across the canyon floor like blood from eviscerated arteries. Klax was at Tara’s side, blocking the hailstorm, within a split second.

She looked up at him weathering the pain of the creature’s destruction while she slung her bow again. And though she rolled her eyes in his direction, she couldn’t help but flash a smile at his chivalrous display.

“You’re honestly such a dogbrain…” she chuckled.

Before Klax could reply, however, the hailstorm retracted. Every piece of flaming rock zipped right through the air to reconnect with its fallen host. And this host had, at this point, added even more of the environment to its already significantly armored hide.

“You gotta be kiddin’ me…”

Tara was speaking for both of them – they watched as the golem heaved the entire base of the canyon it had crashed into out – attaching it to its body and sending the rest of the edifice toppling down right on top of them all.

When it came to these stone men, it seemed that they’d do anything to protect the secrets buried in this place.

Tara and Klax braced, ready to sprint for their lives. But as their assailant charged forward, renewed strength surging through its frame, they heard a voice penetrate their skulls.

It was a quiet, reserved voice. And yet the word it spoke was said with the intensity of lightning cutting through a rainstorm:

Down.

They obeyed without question, throwing themselves prone (Klax cupping his hands round Tara’s small belly) and felt the warped geometry of a powerful [Arcane Shield] wrap itself around them.

Together, they waited for the inevitable hellstorm that they knew was about to be unleashed on their enemy.

And they weren’t disappointed.

A spark ignited in the sky – something far brighter, and far more potent, than the fire spells commanded by the golem magus in these mountains. The pair glanced up to see the sky on fire, streaks of molten energy cascading from the fog-covered sky above and pouring over the hulking golem as it tried to strike the two of them. The creature attempted to shield itself from the rain of fire – in vain. As soon as it raised its limbs a bolt of lightning wreathed in cinders sliced right through the middle of its torso, ending its life – again – with one fell blow that rocked the entire Southern hemisphere. The fiery rain then melted the rocky limbs of the creature before it could even think about regenerating again.

The entire spectacle had taken more minutes. And just as soon as the tornado of fire and lightning had arrived, it was gone, and the fog parted above to reveal the single mage who had just cast ruin upon the world.

This mage looked down on her incredulous colleagues and made a slight twitch of her right hand, undoing the shield spell that had protected them.

They were revealed to be holding each other tight, huddled together like they’d just been lazily lying on some sun-drenched beach resort.

“Show-off…” Tara muttered, smiling up at their sister.

The mage pulled down her hood and brushed her floppy ears back in response.

“Sorry to interrupt,” Fauna said. “But we have a job to do, remember?”
