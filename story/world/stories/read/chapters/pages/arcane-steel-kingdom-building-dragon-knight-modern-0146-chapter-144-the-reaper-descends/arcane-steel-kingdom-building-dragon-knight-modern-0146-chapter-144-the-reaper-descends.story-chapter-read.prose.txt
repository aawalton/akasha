I woke up the same way I had for the last two days in my quarters in the MCIC. Pinned.

A dragon was curled up on my chest. I usually joked about him gaining weight, but the restricted breathing was starting to make me realize those jokes might have some merit. Lyra and Ela feeding him a constant stream of pastries was definitely packing on the mass.

Either that, or I was just the luckiest person in the world to get a dragon who expressed affection via asphyxiation.

Tes, I thought, fighting for a shallow breath. Is he actually getting heavier, or am I just weak?

[Master, it has more to do with his environment. A dragon who utilizes the space element is well known to shrink their size. The size and mass depend on how comfortable they are and how safe they feel. Being in an active battlefield is making him unconsciously increase his mass to project a higher threat profile.]

I managed to worm my left hand free and gently rubbed his back. The suffocating weight immediately lessened to a manageable level as he relaxed his density.

Well, it works perfectly fine as a draconic alarm clock.

Time to return the favor.

I began poking his scaly cheeks. Kaelus didn't open his eyes. He just kept trying to swat my finger away with the tip of his tail, an annoyed flick that slapped against my collarbone. I kept poking. Finally, he cracked open one starlit eyelid.

Brother, this better be about breakfast, his mental voice was groggy and thick with sleep. Or else I am going back to sleep.

I patted his head. "Did you forget Cinderfall is attacking us in about three hours? We need to prep."

He grumbled, rolling off my chest and stretching his back legs like a house cat waking from a long nap. Brother, can I eat one of the birds? Aquarius told me it tasted quite good. Apparently, the meat is spicy.

I paused, halfway out of bed. Tes, is that okay to do? Won't he get sick?

[Water and Ice dragons are the natural predators of Phoenixes. As Kaelus is the heir apparent to the Blue Dragon throne, he shares similar traits to the Water/Ice dragons and Storm dragons, possessing a mastery of lightning and wind magic. Furthermore, there are the rare Platinum dragons. Dragons are categorized by their color: a deep blue dragon is an ice dragon; a lighter blue is water; sky blue signifies a mastery of wind; and a bluish-grey signifies the lightning lineage. The final, rarest form—the bluish-platinum color—denotes a dragon’s mastery of space magic. Aquarius leans heavily toward a water dragon but possesses some ice dragon skills, hence he could easily hunt phoenixes. Kaelus is a hybrid dragon with mastery over space, electric, water, and ice elements. Therefore, it is perfectly safe for his digestive tract. However, it is recommended he not consume too many in one sitting, as the concentrated thermal energy can induce an upset stomach. Also, Kaelus has been heavily neglecting his air magic training.]

The long, detailed report flowed into my mind seamlessly, but the very last bit didn't escape Kaelus’s notice.

HEY LADY, ARE YOU TRYING TO RAT ME OUT?! Kaelus snapped, his head whipping around to glare at empty air.

I smirked. It seemed Tes had made her reply intentionally long just to bury that little performance review at the end. She was definitely getting cheekier.

I started pulling on the under-layer of my armor. "Well, you shouldn't ignore any element," I scolded him lightly. "The more elements you master, the more tools you have to deal with your enemies."

Kaelus huffed, a puff of ozone escaping his nostrils. But Brother, it is so boring! The only good thing you can do is produce a breeze or cut soft things. Or reduce the air pressure so I can fly faster. No fun there.

He clearly needed a visual demonstration. It was time to show my ignorant little brother the power of a tornado.

Tes, display videos of Category 5 tornadoes from Earth. Show him the structural damage.

The footage played directly into my neural link, which Kaelus shared. He watched houses splinter, massive trees ripped from the earth by the roots, and heavy trucks tossed like toys in the roaring, dark funnels of wind.

Kaelus immediately sat up straight, his cosmic scales shivering with excitement. He had an innate inclination for space magic, but he had mostly learned water and ice just because he spent time with Aquarius. It was a casual "I can do that too" kind of effort. He had mastered lightning magic purely to impress Lyra and her insatiable love for huge explosions. Hopefully, the sheer, chaotic violence of a tornado would motivate him to master wind.

We headed to the mess hall to grab a light breakfast, grabbing some MREs on the way out.

The walk from the back lines to the front was a stark transition. All the elves and human personnel had been evacuated from the forward trenches. The only living thing waiting for us at the absolute front line was Aquarius. The massive water dragon was laying lazily in the mud, while Kaelus was jumping around him excitedly, trying to show off a miniature, sputtering tornado he was conjuring between his front paws.

Bob stood nearby, already fully encased in his specialized Mark II-G armor. He offered a crisp salute as I approached.

"Young Master," Bob rumbled, his voice deep through the helmet's speakers. "My duty as your personal knight has not changed. I took an oath to protect you, hence my first priority will always be your safety."

I had almost forgotten the exact phrasing of the oath he had sworn to me when I was just a toddler, right after the assassination attempt. "I'll be counting on you, then."

...

Three miles across the ashen valley, the Cinderfall lines began to organize.

Through the feed of a high-altitude X-9 Specter, I watched the vanguard form up. The five Tier 8 knights took the absolute forefront.

Their armor was distinct. It was heavily reminiscent of Roman legionaries, but thicker, darker, and more Gothic in design. They all held massive tower shields with a small, semi-circular arch cutout on the right side. A long spear rested in that cutout, and a heavy broadsword hung at their belts.

This was the standard-issue heavy infantry armor for Cinderfall Phoenix Knights. The metal was specially treated and enchanted to withstand absurd amounts of heat. The shields and weapons were designed to act as conduits, channeling huge amounts of fire magic from the knight and the phoenix and redirecting it outward.

They began a synchronized charge.

Shields locked tight, spears leveled from the notches. They weren't just marching; they were a moving wall of absolute destruction. They channeled an obscene amount of heat magic directly in front of them, creating a rolling, solid wall of golden fire that acted as a battering ram. The heat distortion blurred the air around them, turning the grey ash of the valley floor into patches of bubbling, red-hot slag.

The core of the charge, made up of the Tier 8 and Tier 7 knights, was virtually impenetrable. Not even the heavy, localized fire from the Fortress Breaker cannons could crack the center of that burning dome. The plasma bolts simply splashed against the concentrated heat, dissipating before they could touch the armor of the knights within.

The flanks and outskirts of their formation, manned by the lower-tier knights, were slightly weaker. I directed the automated defense grid to focus fire entirely on the edges. The rapid-fire suppression turrets chewed away at the perimeter, tearing through the thinner shields and taking down the lesser knights. But it wasn't enough to stop the core advance.

They were closing the distance fast. The heat radiating from them was palpable even from the safety of our trenches, drying the mud to cracked clay.

I ordered the Mark-M MECH units to launch synchronized, heavy plasma volleys. The goal wasn't to kill the high-tier knights; the goal was to arrest their momentum and create a massive distraction.

A hundred of the twenty-meter-tall titans locked their articulated, spiked feet into the frozen mud. The twin Fortress Breaker cannons on their shoulders began charging, groaning with the intake of power, glowing with a fierce, blinding blue light.

They fired.

A fifty massive plasma slugs slammed into the golden fire wall simultaneously. The impacts didn't breach the shield, but the sheer kinetic force caused the charging knights to stagger. The physical pressure of the barrage forced them to halt for a fraction of a second, digging their boots into the ash to maintain their footing against the explosive recoil.

I looked at Bob. He gave a slow, heavy nod.

I ran the final diagnostic checks on the Reaper. This was the first time the suit was entering active, large-scale combat. The internal core hummed, a captive star eager to be unleashed.

At the same time, I projected a quick warning to Kaelus. Be careful. Stick close to Aquarius.

The rolling, golden wall of fire eventually reached the edge of the first trench lines. The intense heat instantly vaporized the razor wire and began to melt the steel reinforcements of the barricades.

My Mark-M MECHs abandoned their stationary firing positions. They ignited their back thrusters, throwing their twenty-meter frames forward, initiating a brutal, ramming charge right back at the knights.

The collision between the Mark-Ms' full-power kinetic shields and the Cinderfall flaming charge was catastrophic.

The impact shattered the air, a deafening crunch of metal against magical force that sent a shockwave echoing for miles. The sheer mass of the MECHs grinding against the immovable wall of the elite knights created a massive flurry of debris. Pulverized ash, shattered armor plating, and liquid fire sprayed in all directions, turning the center of the battlefield into a chaotic, blinding maelstrom of destruction.

Using the chaos of the collision, Kaelus and Aquarius materialized from the thick, oily smoke. They hovered just above the warring lines and launched a full-power, combined dragon breath directly into the center of the staggered knight formation.

A beam of concentrated starlight and a torrent of freezing, high-pressure water slammed into the golden fire wall. The collision of absolute cold and extreme heat created a massive, secondary explosion of superheated steam. It blanketed the front lines in an impenetrable white fog, completely obscuring the combatants from view.

Through the thick mist, brilliant flashes of golden light flared up, one after another. The resurrection cycles had been triggered. The dragons' breath had vaporized several of the knights holding the center line.

Bob and I began walking toward the fog bank.

From the dense mist, a flock of massive, fiery birds ascended, tearing toward the sky, their shrieks cutting through the roar of battle. It was a clear, unmistakable challenge to the dragons. Kaelus and Aquarius obliged, their massive wings beating the ash into a frenzy as they ascended into the cloud layer. They had a bone to pick with those burning birds.

I reached into my spatial storage.

The iconic VWOOM sound of the plasma blades igniting rang out in perfect synchronization. Eleven black-hilted katanas materialized in the air around me, hovering in a tight, protective halo of azure light, their micro-thrusters humming.

Bob drew his heavy, silver broadsword, the blade catching the harsh glare of the floodlights.

We broke into a dead sprint toward the smoke.

Around us, the Cinderfall infantry had bypassed the initial MECH collision and were actively attacking the rapid-fire turrets in the trenches. Thousands upon thousands of my automata surged forward from the rear lines to meet them, a black, mechanical tide crashing against a sea of crimson armor.

I didn't look up at the raging battle in the clouds. I kept my eyes fixed on the thick wall of steam, my thrusters whining as I accelerated, my hovering blades humming with lethal intent.
