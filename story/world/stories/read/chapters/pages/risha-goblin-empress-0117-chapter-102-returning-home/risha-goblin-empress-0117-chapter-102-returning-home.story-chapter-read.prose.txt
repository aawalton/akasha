Risha returned from the council and opened her eyes to see the three scaled apprentices watching over her.

She’d been too slow.

The Echo had nearly gotten to another one of the Mothers. Even if it was her, Risha didn’t want to let the Echo get more power.

A call echoed out in front of them, and Risha looked up from the cart to see movements in the dark.

She stood tall, staring into the darkness as her people slowed.

Movement over one of the tunnels caught her eye as something with six legs crawled over.

Risha grabbed the chain swords at her waist and slowly unwrapped their chains.

Klaz’zks leapt forward, the other spiders joining her as screeches echoed through the dark.

Large six limbed insects streamed out of the holes around them, only to be met by webs, arrows and fangs.

The battle was over moments after it began.

Kliks shifted in Risha’s hair, moving to the top of her head as Risha surveyed the twenty or so insects that had attacked them.

“Wrap them and eat as we move.” Risha’s voice was hard from the stress of the tunnels. She was ready to see the sun again.

The spiders followed her orders, wrapping the creatures in webs, then continuing on into the darkness. The cart rumbled as Risha sat back down.

Damien hadn’t caught up to them.

He’d won his first fight against the Echo, but Risha didn’t know how strong it had become with the power of a Mother to aid it, and she couldn’t stop the anxious beating of her heart.

Her eyes moved to Siyah, who held the objects that had been gifted to her reverently. A necklace and a sword. There was something special about the items’ spirits, but when Risha tried to look, they hid themselves from her gaze.

And so they continued through the darkness, pushing themselves to a hard pace.

***

A gate with a small contingent of Under Goblins outside of it was a welcome sight. They straightened as Risha’s party emerged from the darkness.

The gate was quickly opened, and Risha let out a breath of relief as the white fires of Koliekan The Chained filled the halls.

Risha jumped off of her cart, and looked around as everyone moved swiftly. Several items came from the carts, rare trade goods her people had acquired during their short stay.

Risha looked over them, then let out a breath and closed her eyes.

Tension left her like a stream, and she wavered where she stood.

She hadn’t slept well during the four days of travel. No one had.

“Mother?”

Risha opened her eyes to find a nervous Under Goblin looking up at her.

“Ola wants to see you.”

Risha took a deep breath, steadying herself, then walked through the tunnels. The Under Goblin walked beside her until they emerged into a cavern with a large crystal heart at its center.

“Risha!” A small figure covered in vines ran at Risha, and jumped into her arms.

Risha spun with the small Lin child, stuffing her exhaustion down so that the child could not see it. “Hello, little one. How have your days been?”

Lin looked up at Risha with a wide smile, still held in her arms. She’d grown bigger since Risha first found her. “Amazing! Koko has taught me more than that stupid ugly Sapling ever could.”

Risha smiled with pride. She was already picking up Goblin insults. Risha looked at Koliekan’s heart, where Ola slowly stood. “Koko?”

“The child honors me with the nickname.”

Risha laughed, then set the child down. Leaf-Watcher and Talo stood nearby. The Shade’s expression was hidden behind his cloth, but Leaf-Watcher had a toothy smile to match Risha’s. Strange plants covered the floor around the Spark, some flowering, others bearing fruit, and others reaching high to the ceiling of the large cavern.

Risha held Aliya’s hand as she made her way to the circle of chairs that surrounded the spark.

“How was your trip, Mother Risha?” The spark’s words echoed, only audible to Ola and Risha thanks to her skills.

“There are many things to discuss, Koko. But they can wait.” Risha looked at Ola, who bowed low to the Mother, “You wanted to see me, Ola.”

“Ah, not quite, Mother. It was I who requested to see you.”

Risha tilted her head, then looked to the Spark questioningly.

“I will be direct. The Saplings cannot be trusted. If they had kept to their ways, it is possible that your daughter would’ve been trapped, chained to that teacher’s will, bound in ways I can not express through words.”

Risha stiffened, then her teeth exposed themselves. Igni and Nyx reacted to her fury as it filled the room. Waves of heat radiated from her, her shadows extending through the room and muting even the white fire of the Spark. Leaf-Watcher and Talo took a step back, only Aliya seeming to be unaffected as her grip tightened around Risha’s hand. “What?”

“It is not certain. But the Sapling wished to complete a ritual with her that is sacred, and can only be done by one you trust with your entire being. He did not warn her of the risks, nor were his preparations as complete as they should’ve been.”

Risha closed her eyes, and calmed her anger. Despite her desire to crush the sapling where he stood in her city above, her forces were spread thin, and they would need to prepare for possible attacks from below with the turning of the Dwarven Mother. She was not certain she could sustain another war against the saplings in the forest.

“Are you certain?” Risha’s voice came with a growl, her blood rushing through her despite her attempts to calm herself.

“It could’ve been ignorance, nearly as dangerous as avarice. These techniques are ancient, and known to few. But… from what the child has told me, I do not believe this sapling to have kind intentions.”

Risha bowed her head to the Spark, “Thank you for telling me.”

She paused, then turned to the child at her side who stared up at her with wide eyes. She let out a breath, then knelt down to be eye level. She placed her forehead against Aliya’s. “What do you think of your Sapling teacher, little one?”

“He’s mean.” Aliya’s eyes were serious in her answer.

Risha’s smile was sharp. “I see.”

Risha stood tall. “Leaf-Watcher, ensure that the Sapling teacher is captured alive, and held in a building of stone so that he cannot warn his people.”

The White Wolf bowed low to her Alpha’s orders.

“It is time to renegotiate our peace with the forest.” Risha looked at Koliekan’s heart, still covered in ancient chains. “My offer remains open to you, Koliekan.”

“It is not yet time, Mother.”

Risha gave a small nod, then looked at Talo and Ola. “You should continue your lesson. Risha needs rest… it has been a long trip.”

The two adults bowed their heads, and Risha gave Aliya one last hug, before turning to find a spare bed in her Undercity.

***

A knock on Risha’s door woke her.

Kliks stirred, and crawled over to Risha’s chest where the little spiderling snuggled closer.

Risha smiled at the fuzzy creature in her arms.

Another knock woke her fully, and she slowly pushed herself up, the furs she’d been using as a blanket falling to the floor.

She grabbed her sword belt, and tied it to her waist before making her way to the door, and opening it.

Klu stood there, and bowed his head. “Risha, something requires your attention at the gates.”

Risha blinked, clearing the fogginess from her brain, then nodded. She stepped forward, only for Klu’s hand to gently stop her with a handful of clothes folded over each other.

“You may want to change.”

Risha looked down at the simple underthings she slept in, having grabbed them after a quick wash. She nodded with a small yawn, grabbed the clothes, and closed the door. The clothes were fresh silks and furs that felt quite comfortable. Her old panther cloak had been brought down as well it seems.

After getting fully dressed, she emerged to find the tired looking orc waiting. She wondered if he’d slept since they got back. He’d been just as exhausted as she was by their journey home.

She followed him through the tunnels, where she met Siyah and Sun-Chaser waiting for her.

Risha smiled, and ran up to Sun-Chaser, giving the beautiful fox a big hug.

Sun-Chaser returned the hug with a tight grip for several seconds before letting Risha go.

Risha turned to them, then stood tall, “What’s happening?”

“I think it’s better if you see.” Sun-Chaser motioned towards the closed gates.

Risha nodded, then followed the three of them as the gates were opened.

A familiar Demon stood there with a trickster’s smile, his hood lowered. Behind him, demons of all shapes and sizes talked and laughed with each other in a large camp. Fires had been set up, cooking food while laughter echoed through the darkness. Hundreds of demons were gathered here.

Damien himself looked exhausted behind his smile, circles under his eyes, but otherwise was fine.

As Risha and her companions stepped out from behind the gates, the conversation slowed to a stop, eyes turning to her, and the Demoness with broken horns beside her.

Damien walked up to them, then bowed low. “I’m happy to see you both in good health.”

“Risha’s glad you’re alright.” Risha said, caught off-guard by the village waiting outside her gates.

Siyah took her wide eyes away from the gathered demons, and smiled at the Old Brother, “It’s good to see you Damien.”

Damien made a wide motion towards the gathered demons who all watched with baited breath, “Well, since everyone’s here, I might as well start. Risha, Mother Of Monsters, Empress of your land, and Siyah, personal advisor, accomplished mage, and my little sister. I, Brother Damien, General Of The First Order formally request that the both of you grant asylum and protection to my people.” He bowed low, his voice echoing over the gathered demons with power that was tinged with magic.

Risha looked them over, lifting her head as she took on the imperious look of Mother Shyanne. She looked over to Siyah, who bowed her head in a silent request.

Risha turned to Damien. “Brother Damien, with the power of a Mother, I accept all your people as my children, and offer you the protection of my empire.” She smirked, then turned to Siyah, and bowed her head for the Demoness to speak.

Siyah blinked in surprise as she stared at Risha, then lifted her chin as confidence filled her. She turned to the Demons who watched her with baited breath. “You are my people, and you are all welcome in my home.”

A cheer went through the crowd.

Risha turned to Sun-Chaser who’s face had gone pale.

Risha smiled wide as she made her way back inside, “I’ll trust you to find homes and jobs for them all Sun-Chaser!”

With that line, Risha strolled back behind the gates.
