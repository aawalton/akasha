Duration: 60 Hours

Resource Gathering mission: 48 hours

Time left till deadline: 28 hours 42 minutes.

Aodhán paced in tight circles, his entire body tense as he waited for news from Varéc—anything at all that might signal he was still alive.

The last he had sensed from Varéc was a mighty pull of willpower from his core about ten minutes ago, and since then, nothing. No images, no emotion. Nothing.

He had tried to connect with Varéc, even forcing himself into a meditative position to clear his mind, but the interference was too much. Which meant he was operating blind. He had no idea how Varéc's mission was going—if he was safe or if he'd been locked up in a void cage by the dragon and its spawns.

"It's been twenty minutes." He whispered to himself. "What the hell is going on down there?"

"I'm sure he's fine," Destra said uncertainly, reaching out to stop him from pacing. "Let's give it a few more minutes."

But Aodhán bypassed her arms without noticing, his eyes closed in tight focus.

Another minute passed without anything, and Aodhán's expression tightened with determination. He glanced at the ground and frowned. "I'm going down there if he doesn't come out in the next five minutes."

"You will do no such thing!" Baxter scowled at him from where he sat, his gaze reprimanding. "If Varéc is dead, he'll reappear in your spirit space. The fact that he hasn't means he's still alive. Going down there might ruin our chances."

"Death isn't the only danger he faces down there!" Aodhán snapped, regretting this stupid idea in the first place. "He could have been captured or imprisoned."

"Those are both the same thing," Brennon quipped, and when Aodhán turned to glare at him, he raised both hands in a placating gesture. "Not the time, I get it. But surely, that was worth pointing out?"

Magda sighed. "I think we need to weigh the advantages and disadvantages of you going down, Aodhán. Would you even survive an encounter with the dragon? What do you aim to accomplish?"

Aodhán stuttered, and she smiled kindly.

"I can imagine how you feel, Aodhán. You and Varéc are linked in a way no one here understands. It's only natural that you're worried. But you can't let it cloud your judgment."

Aodhán closed his eyes and sighed deeply. "You're right. I just worry. This was a stupid idea."

"You seem to have a lot of those." A voice scoffed, tone thick with religious superiority and judgment.

Aodhán scowled at her, anger simmering within him, but Borevin either didn't care or didn't notice because she simply continued. "This is why it's important to serve Tihamun. He will prevent you from making foolish decisions."

"Borevin!" Baxter scolded, then glanced pleadingly at Aodhán. "Calm down. Ignore her."

Borevin glanced up at him, and Aodhán felt a vein throb in his forehead as he forced himself to retain his composure. His lips tightened into a scowl as he prepared to utter something terribly scathing—then he felt another draw of willpower, this one far more powerful than the last.

His anger drained like water through a sieve, replaced suddenly by concern as his gaze snapped to the earth.

A second of silence. Two.

And then, Aodhán felt it—a bundle of lightning essence racing upward toward them.

He smiled, his body shuddering in relief as Varéc burst out of the earth, black scales rippling as he deactivated the Lightning Body skill. Aodhán didn't even bother asking questions before launching himself at Varéc, hands encircling his sinuous neck.

Behind him, everyone sighed in relief, while Borevin scoffed, prayer beads clinking as she continued her devotions.

Baxter exhaled, glad they'd managed to avoid a conflict. "Welcome back, Varéc. You had us worried sick."

Varéc growled in amusement as Aodhán's arms tightened around him. But then he remembered Serythion's oath, and he nudged Aodhán urgently, tail swishing.

"What is it?" Aodhán pulled back, running his hands along Varéc's body as if expecting wounds. "Are you hurt?"

Another growl, deeper this time—impatient. A flicker of pride surged across the bond, followed by the impression of ancient eyes and a voice that was not his own.

Aodhán's breath caught. "Wait… he agreed?"

Varéc dipped his massive head once, and Aodhán's eyes widened, shocked that his crazy plan had actually worked. He glanced back at the others who were watching him with varying degrees of anticipation and let a smile bloom on his lips.

Destra gasped. "Is that a yes? Did the dragon agree?"

Aodhán nodded enthusiastically, and the whole group practically leapt into the air with joy and relief as the tension of the last few minutes bled away.

"Oh, I'm so glad," Destra squealed, hugging Brennon's arm tightly. "We're not going to die after all."

"Bless Tihamun," Magda smiled widely, wiping tears of relief from her eyes, while Borevin raised her prayer beads to the sky in thanks, muttering a recitation under her breath.

However, their happiness didn't last long because Varéc growled again, his tone guttural and foreboding.

Everyone froze, and Aodhán swallowed nervously as images and sensations flashed across his mind. The dragon had made an oath but had tied it to a demand.

A demand that could either make things worse—much worse—or better.

Aodhán grimaced and shook his head. "Twenty-four hours is too little time. We don't even know where to start."

"Start what?" Destra asked, the panic in her tone evident. "What did Varéc say?"

Aodhán's grimace deepened into a frown. "The dragon made a demand."

"What demand?" Baxter asked, his gaze darting between Aodhán and Varéc impatiently. "What does the dragon want?"

Aodhán's gaze met the wary eyes of his comrades, and he ran a hand across his face. "The dragon wants us to free it from its bondage in the next twenty-four hours, or its oath is void."

Varéc growled again, and Aodhán scowled. "That was five minutes ago."

"Fuck!" Baxter cursed loudly, echoing the thoughts of every single individual in the group, except Brennon, who was apparently focused on the time frame.

"How can we do that in just twenty-three hours and fifty-five minutes?" he said worriedly, waving to the empty distance around them. "Does it know just how large this ascendant-forsaken realm is?"

"The time frame doesn't matter, Brennon!" Magda snapped. "This is a dragon we're talking about. We cannot even begin to imagine its true intentions."

Aodhán shook his head in disagreement. "Whatever its intentions may be, it made an oath to Varéc. It is bound by it."

"That's not enough." Baxter disagreed. "Releasing this creature earlier than scheduled—if we even can—means giving it time to regenerate and create more spawns before the earth splits open and it's finally unleashed against us. What if the cultists find a way to render its oath ineffective?"

"Then we'll be facing a dragon far more powerful than it should be, with more spawns than it should have." Magda reasoned fearfully.

"Exactly!" Baxter nodded. "Thereby reducing our chances of survival even further. And they're already abysmal!"

But as right as they were, Aodhán realized it was fear talking.

More likely than not, the key to releasing the dragon was the abyssal pins scattered within its section. In the images Varéc had sent, the dragon had been held up by three chains instead of four—one having dissolved recently, which meant their actions had dissolved it.

The question then was how? How had the fourth chain dissolved?

And then it clicked.

There were sixteen grand abyssal pins.

Four for each section.

That meant—he looked up, realizing that whether they released the dragon now or not, it would eventually be freed as people found more grand abyssal pins. Already five pins had been uprooted, which meant only eleven more had to be found before calamity fell on them.

For the dragon, only three grand pins needed to be located.

It would be difficult, considering the size of the shivering plain, but those abyssal pins should be within the dragon's section. That is, within a range of about ten miles in width and—

"Aodhán!" Baxter's voice cut into his thoughts like a knife, stern and sharp. "You can't seriously be considering this?"

Aodhán glanced up to find them all staring at him—Brennon with hope, the others with a mixture of fear and wariness.

They were expecting an answer from him. A solution. But Aodhán doubted they would like what he had to say.

Bracing himself for another lecture from Baxter, he began. "Our chances of survival if we decide not to heed the dragon's demand are less than thirty percent. Thirty percent of—"He consulted his harvest screen to check the number of people still alive—"eighty-seven is twenty-six."

Destra flinched, and Magda burst into tears. Even Borevin stopped praying to glance at him, her expression somber.

If only twenty-six people survived the coming calamity, the chances that the six of them gathered here made it into that number were incredibly slim. Practically nonexistent.

"And what are our chances if we do heed its demand?" Destra asked, her tone reluctant, as if she dreaded the answer.

Aodhán hated that it wasn't more hopeful.

Inhaling deeply, he replied. "I don't know. The dragon is a variable we can't be one hundred percent sure of. But if it does keep its oath and fights for us, then roughly, I'd say our chances lie in the high fifties, if not sixties."

Brennon's eyes widened in hope. "That's more promising, isn't it?"

"Yes, it is." Borevin replied soberly. "But only if the dragon keeps its oath. If it doesn't, we risk lowering our already dismal chances even further."

"We also risk increasing them." Aodhán argued. "If the dragon fights by our side, that eliminates two of the creatures. Meaning, rather than face four, we'll only have to face two." He met each person's gaze, letting them see his own fear before proceeding. "I say we put it to a vote. Risk survival by unleashing the dragon and bringing it to our side, or take our chances at the twin-peaked mountain. What do you all say?"

His question weighed heavily on the group, and for a few seconds, there was silence.

Until Destra broke it, her eyes shining with fear. "And what will you do, Aodhán, if we all decide to take our chances at the twin-peaked mountain?"

Aodhán grimaced.

Unlike the others, he only had one option here. Danger awaited him at the twin-peaked mountain, what with all the resurrecteds and sleeper agents that would be gathering at its base—not to mention the telepath who would most likely proclaim his status as a transmigrant to the whole world the first chance she got.

No, he couldn't risk it. Unleashing the dragon was the less risky option by comparison.

But he couldn't tell Destra that, so he shrugged. "This is a vote, Destra. You don't need to know my intentions to cast yours."

"But that's just the thing," Destra swallowed, her expression hardening in determination. "I want to go where you go. And if you're voting to release the dragon, then I shall stand by your side."

"Me too," Brennon voiced his support. "My chances in a legendary clash of humans and monsters are lower than abysmal. I'd rather take my chances piggybacking on a dragon."

Aodhán smiled. "That makes three of us then."

He glanced at Magda. "What do you say?"

"We are with you, dust-licker." Borevin replied on behalf of both of them, and Magda nodded. "Tihamun has placed an opportunity in our laps. We would be wise not to waste it."

"That makes five of us," Aodhán said soberly and turned to Baxter. "You're the only one left. What do you say?"

Baxter scowled at him. "There's no point stating my vote any longer. I've been outvoted. But if you think for one moment that I'll let you out of my sight, Aodhán, then you're sorely mistaken."

Aodhán smiled. "Then it's settled. We're all in agreement to release the dragon, and we have twenty-three hours and forty-eight minutes left to do so."

"Great," Baxter replied sarcastically. "Please tell me you have a plan?"

Aodhán shook his head. "Sorry to disappoint you, Baxter, but I do not have a plan this time. I do, however, have the beginnings of one. First, we need to figure out just how large the dragon section is. We know it's roughly ten miles in width. But what about length? The sooner we find that out, the sooner we can begin searching for the three grand abyssal pins left in the section."

Varéc growled behind him, and he smiled. "I think we have to go on another portal-hopping adventure."

Portal hopping turned out to be quite fun, and after nearly an hour of phasing and teleporting, Aodhán and his comrades were finally able to get a rough idea of the size and shape of the dragon section.

They gathered at the base of an ice mountain, the only structure around for miles, and began putting their calculations on paper—or in this instance, transmuted snow, courtesy of Borevin.

Wielding an ink quill—also transmuted snow—Aodhán and Baxter began adding up the miles they had crossed lengthwise and breadthwise. Their calculations came out very irregular, and Brennon suggested that a drawing might help them get a better visual.

Nodding in agreement, Aodhán sketched out a rough map of the dragon section in the snow, and they all gathered around it to try and make sense of the shape.

The drawing, however, turned out to be more irregular than the calculations suggested, and after a few seconds, Baxter muttered. "Is that even a shape?"

Aodhán frowned. "I'm not quite sure, but when Varéc and I phased through the dragon's section, this was the shape we came up with."

Varéc growled in agreement from beside him, and they all turned to study the shape once more. It wasn't a square, or a rectangle, or even a circle, as Aodhán had suspected it might be. Instead, the dragon section seemed to resemble—

"Is that a triangle?" Destra asked, taking the words right out of his mouth.

Borevin raised her prayer beads as if to ward against the shape, shaking her head. "Triangles… vile, crooked heretics among shapes."

"Shapes can't be heretic." Aodhán scoffed, rolling his eyes at the overly religious priestess. "They're just shapes."

Borevin snorted at him as if he were a mere child with no knowledge of the world beyond the physical. Aodhán ignored her, bending his head slightly to get a better perspective on the sketch.

But no matter how he turned his head, the triangle was the closest shape the drawing resembled. It, however, had a circular base, making it look more like a pizza slice. But why was it so…

"Jagged?" Magda suggested, responding to the question on his face, and Aodhán chuckled.

"I was going to say misshapen, but jagged works too." And then he frowned. “It does seem a bit anticlimactic, though. They divided the realm into four equal sections and gave each creature a slice of pie?”

Brennon bent his back slightly to see the shape better and grimaced. "I don't know how you guys can make any sense of this, but I guess it vaguely resembles a slice of pie. What does it mean for us, though?"

"It means the size of the dragon sector isn't something we can accurately calculate." Aodhán replied. "The length is pretty straightforward," he glanced at the calculations they had done earlier, "just twenty-two miles. But the width varies from the apex to the bottom."

"The mid-region is about ten to eleven miles," Baxter added. "The base is around fifteen, while the apex is about five to seven miles."

Brennon whistled. "That's a lot of space for a dragon that isn't even that big."

Varéc growled, shooting Brennon an annoyed look, and Aodhán sighed. Somehow, in their twenty-minute conversation regarding the life of humans, Varéc and this dragon had become friends. And every wrong word about this Serythion character was a slight to Varéc himself.

Aodhán sighed again. "That gives us an area of about one hundred and fifty square miles to cover. That's too much for us to search, moving as one. If we're to make it on time, we'll have to split up."

Borevin grimaced. "Then we better get on with it. Pair me up with Magda. She's the only one here I see myself working with."

Aodhán grimaced. "Right. As long as Magda is amenable to that."

"Of course she is," Borevin scowled at him. "She's the granddaughter of a priestess, and I am a priestess myself. We're peas in a pod."

Magda grimaced but nodded anyway. "I'll be fine with Priestess Borevin."

"Okay then," Aodhán nodded. "Magda, you and Borevin will take the top of the triangle." He glanced at Destra. "You and I will take the center."

Destra nodded.

And then he glanced at Baxter, who was already scowling at him. He smiled. "Baxter, you and Brennon will take the bottom of the triangle. Please don't kill him."

Baxter glared at Brennon. "Don't make me kill you."

Brennon chuckled and walked to stand beside Baxter. "Come on, brother. We'll be the best team."

Baxter glowered at his partner and muttered something under his breath before turning to address the whole group. "Aodhán and I have come up with an idea to keep track of each other using the leaderboards."

Aodhán nodded. "Each grand abyssal pin is worth fifty points, so I suggest we don't gather any other pins while we search. That way we'll be able to monitor the number of pins we've gained to know when we've gathered all three. Does that make sense?"

They all nodded, and Aodhán continued. "I'm on the leaderboard, so I'll be representing my team. Baxter is also on the leaderboard." He pulled out a dozen abyssal pins from his spatial storage and handed them over to Magda. "Magda, you'll be representing your team."

"If any one of us three gains points after this, then that means we've found a grand abyssal pin. Do we all understand?"

Brennon shook his head. "What if someone finds two grand abyssal pins?"

"Then they'll gain a hundred points." Baxter scowled at his partner. "Try to keep up, Brennon."

Aodhán smiled. "Okay then, let's get to work."

Baxter opened a portal for Magda and Borevin to the first stratum of the sector before yanking Brennon by the neck and teleporting them to the bottom stratum, leaving only Aodhán, Varéc, and Destra standing at the base of the mountain.

Destra glanced at the empty stretch of snow before them and asked. "Are you sure about any of this?"

Aodhán shook his head. "I'm not sure of anything, but it's too late to go back now. We've already come too far."

"By winter's tits!" Destra cursed as Aodhán levitated on a storm platform and invited her to join him. She glared at him for a moment before grasping his hand to join him on the platform. "I hate those two statements so much."
