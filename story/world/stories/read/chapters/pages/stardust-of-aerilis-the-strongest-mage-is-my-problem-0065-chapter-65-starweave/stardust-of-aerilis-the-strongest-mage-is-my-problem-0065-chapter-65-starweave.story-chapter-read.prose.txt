The white marble office of the Obsidian Spire felt unusually quiet after Pelka and Vane exited. The only sound was the low, rhythmic thrum of the building’s mana-stabilizers and the faint whistle of the high-altitude wind against the reinforced glass.

Cyria Amberfang remained leaning against her desk, her tail giving a single, thoughtful flick. She watched Aiven as he finished his recount of the meeting in the Fold. She didn't interrupt, nor did her playful smile falter, but her golden eyes sharpened with every word he spoke about the "bottom of the world" and Lucien’s plan to lower the islands.

Virelle hovered just behind Aiven’s shoulder, her arms crossed and her eyes fixed on Cyria like a hawk watching a burrowing prey. Her prismatic orb hummed with a low, protective frequency.

"So," Cyria finally said, her voice a smooth, dangerous purr. "He gave you the grand tour of the apocalypse, did he? The 'descendant of God' routine. He’s always had a flair for the dramatic, that one."

"Then it's true?" Aiven asked, his hand tightening on the strap of his backpack. "The dungeons are eating the world? The islands are sinking?"

Cyria sighed, walking toward the floor-to-ceiling window to look out over her city. "The physics are true, yes. The core readings don't lie, and Vulpine’s own sensors have detected the structural decay Lucien mentioned. The world is breaking. And Lucien truly believes he is the only one with the right to stitch it back together."

She turned back, her expression shifting from corporate mask to a rare, chilling sincerity. "But here’s what he didn't tell you, Aiven. Lucien is a purist. He wants to save the world, but he couldn't care less about the people currently living on it. To him, the billions of lives in Aerilis are just variables in an ancient equation that went wrong. If destroying the dungeon means half the population dies in the transition or the ensuing wars for land... he views that as a necessary rounding error."

Virelle let out a sharp, mocking sniff. "A man who views lives as ink on a ledger. I think that’s quite similar to you, Fox. It seems the only difference between you and the delusional god is the price of your suits."

Cyria let out a short, genuine laugh. "A fair point. But there is one major difference. I like this world. I like the commerce, the innovation, the gold-flecked buns, and the way people strive for more. I don't want to see it erased and replaced with some prehistoric balance."

Cyria paused, her tail giving a slow, deliberate flick as she leaned back. She watched Aiven with a knowing, predatory intensity. "But let’s be honest, Aiven. A man like that doesn't just invite someone to his private dimension for a cup of tea and a lecture. Did he offer you anything... enticing? A promise of power, perhaps? Or a way to keep you and Virelle safe?”

Aiven took a breath and looked Cyria in the eye. "He offered me a choice. He said he thinks I’m special, and even offered to teach me how to control my power using the Loom-Breaker."

Cyria’s ears flicked, and a glint of sharp satisfaction crossed her eyes. "So my hunch was right," she murmured, a thin, knowing smile touching her lips. "You really are another anomaly."

Aiven frowned, his heart giving an anxious thud. "Another anomaly? What does that even mean?"

"In simple terms? Humans that possess traits, capacities, or mana-signatures that no ordinary human should ever be able to manifest," Cyria explained, her tail swaying thoughtfully. "You aren't just a man who got lucky with a summon, Aiven. You're a fundamental deviation from the biological norm. But," she added, catching his growing look of concern, "that is a lecture for another time. We have more pressing matters."

She leaned in slightly, her gaze locking onto his. "Elaborate for me, Aiven. What exactly was the nature of this offer Lucien proposed?"

Aiven hesitated, glancing back at Virelle before continuing. "He said that if I joined him, he’d abandon his plan to use Virelle as a weapon. He promised us a lavish home, absolute safety from the government, and a life where we wouldn't have to be fugitives anymore. He called it a partnership." His voice dropped into a somber, hollow tone. "But he also told me that if I ever refused to help him once I joined, he'd have to get rid of me. He won't allow a power like mine to exist outside his equation."

Cyria’s golden eyes shimmered with a calculative warmth. "So... what do you think of Lucien’s proposition?”

Aiven hesitated, "I still haven't made up my mind," he admitted. "But if I'm going to decide whose side I'm on, I need the full picture. It’s my turn to ask you a question, Cyria."

Cyria arched a brow, her ears flicking. "Proceed."

"At the Sunken Fane, I met Sylphaine," Aiven said, watching for a reaction. "She knew you. She told me you were planning to betray Lucien with the Loom-Breaker. What did she mean by that?"

The Fox CEO let out a long, weary sigh and leaned back into her marble chair, her posture relaxing into something more genuine and tired. "Fine," she murmured. "I suppose transparency is the only currency I have left with you. I couldn't hide it for much longer anyway."

She gestured toward the holographic display. "Lucien has a team. I’m sure you’ve met them before. Sylphaine is one of his key field agents."

Aiven’s mind immediately flashed back to the hotel, to the white-haired vampire and her brother. "Valerion," he whispered, his obsidian arm whirring in memory of the limb it replaced.

Cyria nodded. "That team is tasked with obtaining artifacts from all over Aerilis—Legendary-class relics, forgotten technology, anything with high mana-output. Their goal is to prevent the government from getting their hands on them, so that Lucien’s 'grand mission' can be accomplished without bureaucratic interference. They are the scalpel he uses to carve out the old world's foundations."

"And were you tasked with getting the Loom-Breaker?" Aiven asked.

Cyria shook her head, a sharp, knowing grin playing on her lips. "No. If that were the case, Sylphaine wouldn't be crying 'betrayal.' The Loom-Breaker was my own independent initiative. It wasn't on Lucien's shopping list—partly because even he didn't realize it was within reach."

She leaned forward, her golden slit-pupils locking onto Aiven. "With the Loom-Breaker, I gain the ability to foil one of the most critical instruments in Lucien's entire design."

"What instrument?" Aiven asked, his heart beginning to hammer against his ribs.

Cyria didn't answer with words. Instead, her gaze drifted slowly to the right, landing squarely on Virelle, who was floating beside Aiven.

Aiven noticed the look. His mind raced back to the Fold—to Lucien’s strange, cryptic hints about Virelle’s origin and how she was the "Starweave."

"You couldn't mean..." Aiven started, his voice failing him.

Cyria looked Aiven in the eye, her expression becoming hauntingly solemn. "Yes, Aiven. Virelle—or the Starweave, as we called her—is an entity that is meant to be sealed away in her original form. She isn't an elf, and she isn't just a powerful summon."

Various possibilities raced through Aiven’s mind.

Cyria took a breath, the weight of the secret finally out. "She was originally a sentient Dungeon Core. The very thing Lucien wants to erase from existence to save the world, but he needed to borrow its power."

Aiven nearly blurted out that it was impossible, his mind reeling as he looked at the girl who had shared honey bread with him, wondering if the elf he loved was actually the very monster that was supposedly eating the world.

"What do you mean by that?" Aiven demanded, his voice cracking. "How could a dungeon core manifest into... this? Into a person?"

"The events leading to this were quite complicated," Cyria said, her voice dropping into a professional cadence. "To put it simply, Lucien has used the Loom-Breaker once before. He used it on a legendary-class dungeon that was even tougher than the Sunken Fane. He managed to tame the dungeon core and used the Loom-Breaker to free the power within it—the literal lifeblood of the dungeon. However, even Lucien cannot absorb that much raw power. So, using other legendary artifacts, he created a vessel. A living body to house that energy."

Cyria gestured toward the mage. "With that, Virelle was born."

BAM!

Virelle slammed her hand onto the marble desk, the impact causing a localized shockwave that rattled the tea sets. "That is preposterous!" she shrieked, her eyes burning with a manic lavender fire. "I am a master of the celestial weave! I could never be a mere plaything or a 'vessel' for that delusional man!"

Cyria didn't flinch. She watched the trembling mage with a look of clinical pity. "I can understand your frustration, Virelle. But that’s the truth. I know because I was there to see it happen."

Virelle looked like she was about to explode. "You lie! You have fabricated this story just like the pale leeches! You and them are on the same team—you are telling these fairytales to sway us so that my Master and I will join your pathetic cause!"

She raised her hand, mana gathering in a lethal, jagged whistle. But before the spark could fly, Aiven reached out. He grabbed Virelle’s wrist, his grip gentle but firm.

"Virelle, wait," Aiven said, his muted gray-brown eyes searching hers. "Please. Just... listen to her side of the story first. We need the full picture before we decide what's a lie."

Virelle looked at Aiven’s concerned expression, her chest heaving. Slowly, she looked away, her silver hair hiding her face as she drifted back a few inches and fell silent, though her fingers were still twitching.

Cyria continued, her voice steady. "Lucien needed a power that could speed up his plan, and he found a dungeon that could provide it. The dungeon was called the Starweave. With Virelle's creation, he possessed a power capable of wiping entire islands off the map. She was his ultimate weapon."

She paused, her ears flicking back. "However, after she was created, the Loom-Breaker disappeared. The sheer magnitude of power required to free the Starweave from its dungeon confinement caused the artifact to vanish into the ley lines. Artifacts of that class do not break; they simply respawn in another high-mana dungeon."

"And that’s the Sunken Fane," Aiven interjected, the pieces clicking into place. "You managed to pinpoint the spawn point of the Loom-Breaker before Lucien could find it."

Cyria nodded. "He’s been searching for it for a while. I just happened to have better data analysts."

Aiven thought for a while, a new confusion surfacing. "But if what you're saying is true... I summoned Virelle inside a low-level burrow. A trash-duty dungeon near Lowhaven. How could she arrive there if she was supposedly under Lucien’s control in some high-level facility?"

Cyria’s mischievous smile returned, but it was tempered by a shadow of something darker. "That... is a long story. One that involves a very messy internal schism and a series of events you might be better off not knowing."

She leaned forward, her eyes shimmering with a new offer. "Nevertheless, if you truly wish to know the circumstances of her escape and how she ended up somewhere else... I can tell you what I know. Provided we make another deal in exchange."
