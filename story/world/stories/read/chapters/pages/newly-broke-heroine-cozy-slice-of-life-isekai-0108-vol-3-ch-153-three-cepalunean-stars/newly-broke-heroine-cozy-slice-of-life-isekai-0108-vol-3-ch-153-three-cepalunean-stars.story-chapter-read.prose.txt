This arrogant, petty, spiteful dragon sitting on enough explosives to put Fiefdala into orbit. When did my life become a Saturday morning cartoon complete with exaggerated villains?! I should have just let Jake handle this whole thing.

She ruminated on whether they should grab Karlin now. Dragon or not, he was still no match for her, if his brother's fighting capacity was any indication.

The thought made her wince. Doug actually could put up a good fight as a kobold, surprisingly. She hoped dragons never picked up on stealth as more than a suggestion.

Hopefully, they’d signal when they were in position. Fiona hadn’t seen the front door to this place, and wondered if the teleport was the only means of egress. That sounded like a really bad idea, especially being underground.

Bonnie jabbed a finger at her tool. “Oh, it’s here, alright. We’re on a clock, and if nothing else, we are saving this stupid dragon’s life from an unforced error of comic proportions.”

“Alright, one for you, one for you…” she delicately ignited the beverages so that the small treat on top caramelized, with a few small claps around the table. “Can I help anyone else?”

“Oh? Dazzle me, then,” he said, steepling his claws together and looking endeared. Internally, she wondered at what point was he going to drop a giant ‘gotcha’ moment and say he knew it was her the whole time. Then again, so long as she kept up appearances, he shouldn’t.

Right. Keep up appearances. Her illusion spell was one stiff breeze away from failing any kind of scrutiny, and the quick voice modulation spell Bonnie used made her feel out-of-sorts. This dragon had already taken an interest in her.

She pushed all those worries away and listed out a few selections of wine  Doug had recommended. At least she had one inside source–and she threw in a few obvious bad choices to draw him to the one he wanted.

He picked the best of them: A ruby red wine from the Arkantines, fermented from a strange grape-like plant that thrived in the dark.

It tasted like a lifesaver candy, when she’d attempted to try it. Like cherries, almost. She produced the bottle and gave it a good pour for him, and Missy–but, she stopped her from pouring. “Uh…something else?” Fiona proposed.

“I don’t suppose you have something a little spicier? I’d like a fireball,” Missy said with a grin.

Karlin grunted. “I can provide those on demand, you know.”

She let out a titter of laughter. “Oh, no. I meant the drink.”

Wait. Is that a coincidence? They had a drink mix called a Fireball back on Earth. Fiona nodded and went to get the drink. And, an opportunity.

What if Missy were from Earth? She came from Vale. That choker collar on her looked like–

She knew there was some kind of split between her and Karlin. She had to figure out what was eating at her. She just needed to get her talking for five minutes and away from the crowd. It was a backup plan that didn’t involve having to bash the security door down.

She prepared the drink, and then went to the table, to the enthusiasm of Missy. After sipping it, she let out a gasp. “Oh my goodness. How do you know what a fireball is?”

“Oh, grab a seat, I had questions! I still haven’t gotten an answer on who made the sweet rolls this morning!” Missy practically jostled Fiona into the seat. She wore an edged smile; this was not the way she planned this, and she wondered where this woman was going with this.

“I’m not normally supposed to–”

“Suzie?” Darla asked as she prepared meals for the table, smiling wickedly, “You’re the hostess. Dazzle and entertain. She had some interesting questions for me. I was wondering if you might lend some insight?”

Holy hells, is Darla jealous I made a sweet roll almost as good as her? No way. Also, rotten timing, my favorite prickly barista! They had a plan, and for once, Fiona wasn’t doing the sleuthing. All she needed to do was draw attention.

She was pretty good in that department.

Missy leaned in, while Karlin looked intrigued. “Someone told me they were underselling some accomplishments.”

Fiona glanced out of the corner of her eye toward the kitchen as Nick guzzled a potion while Bonnie gave a subtle flick of her wand, mimicking his movements at the cooktop, just out of range of where the others could see into the room. This was what they had practiced; while she didn’t like being the center of attention, she wasn’t going to let this moment pass.

Nick’s body went translucent, then disappeared altogether. Within a second, Bonnie had a magical simulacrum mimicking his motions, set on a loop. The giant corvid would hopefully find some shiny alchemical cases and eradicate them. Bonnie would otherwise keep up the deception, for as long as possible.

Fiona smiled politely, and began with genuine modesty. “Okay, I made the sweet rolls. But, it was under Miss Brightstone’s tutelage. I had some background doing baking, and I have a rather problematic sweet tooth. So I brought them in one day, you know, making them like I always do.”

“Do go on. What exactly do you do to make them special? I can’t for the life of me figure it out!”

Fiona took a more relaxed pose while Darla twirled and whirled like a darkling in the kitchen. “Okay, see, the thing is? Sweet rolls are just that, too sweet, when you get them at your run-of-the-mill bakery. What you need is actually…something a little unconventional.”

Missy frowned. “Wait. Is that why I taste spice? And the fruit glaze tasted tart?” she asked. “You balanced the sweets and added spice to accentuate them, like the cinnamon.”

Most meals are about balance. It’s about putting two opposed things on a plate or a singular dish, and making them work together, rather than against each other.”

They were then added to the cooked meat of a cavern crawfish, and tossed into the small oven. “Honestly, you know what I find? I’m very comfortable doing creature habit food for others. For high-end clientele, it’s entertainment. For people who come by my travelling cafe, they want an experience of culture.”

What?

His current mood hovered near imminent homicide, judging by his wearing a razor-edged ‘I will kill you so hard if you say another word’ smile on his face, aimed at his compatriot. Quick, think of a distraction! This must be important! “Uh…I hate to ask, but since they’re um…”

The voices were stronger through here, on the opposite wall. Hope there aren’t any alarms in here, I have no intention of taking his stuff.

The Godshism. She thought back to Doug’s mentions of the braziers at his mother’s sanctuary, and recognized some of them. Others, she didn’t.

Hang on. Doug’s version of this was that gold fell from the heavens. The aurelium is gold carrying divine essence from Feo’Thari. So what’s this giant spire? It looks like it erupted from the ground, not the other way around.
