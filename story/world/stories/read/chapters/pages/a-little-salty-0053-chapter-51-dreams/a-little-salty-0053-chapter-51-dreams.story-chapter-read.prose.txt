I’m lucid dreaming.

At least, I think this is what lucid dreaming is like. I never had them back on Earth, but recently, it’s been happening more and more often. Like I’m watching a movie through the eyes of the main character.

Only, it’s not seeing, exactly—it’s more abstract than that. Like I know what’s around me and what I’m doing and feeling, even though I don’t see it happen.

Dreams are weird.

“I heard it was an earthquake,” a classmate says. “Or a tidal wave.”

“What’s going on?” I ask, pushing into the crowd.

“That can’t be right,” another student says. “It was inland. Do they even have earthquakes there? I thought it was all ice.”

“Doesn’t matter now. They said the whole city’s gone. All of them.”

“Who’s gone?” I ask again. “Which city?” But my classmates ignore me.

The mess hall is buzzing with low, urgent voices. I notice Master Nilla at the head of the room with some other professors, and I make my way toward him. I catch snippets of conversations as I pass other groups of students.

“...too powerful, I heard…”

“...the will of the gods…”

A shiver runs down my spine.

“Master Nilla,” I say as I draw close. “What happened?”

“Troubling news,” the old elf says, turning toward me. “Troubling. Don’t believe everything you hear—there is still much we don’t know, and rumors are bound to take root. Remember, always—

“—practice skepticism,” I finish. “Yes, I know. Now will you please tell me what’s going on, or must I go dig it out of someone else?”

A thunderclap cracks through the room. I clap my hands to my ringing ears as several people let out shrieks of surprise. Then an unsettling quiet falls over us. Warmth bathes me in pulsing waves. I turn to its source, and it warms my skin like the sun.

It’s coming from a woman.

A woman who’s hovering ten feet off the ground.

She regards us with an expressionless face. Her robes and flowing tassels likewise are hovering with her, as if she’s floating underwater rather than in the air. Several of my classmates shield their eyes as they look up at her.

“Lorata.”

It’s that first whisper that kicks off a hushed repetition of her name, like a breeze sweeping through the room. We fall to our knees in reverence, but I don’t bow my head. I bask in the warmth of her presence, unable to fully believe it. Is this truly happening? Can it really be her?

“Congratulations,” Lorata says. Though she doesn’t speak the word loud, it sounds as if she’s standing right before me, close enough to touch. Her voice is like nothing I’ve ever heard. Musical. Ethereal. Beautiful. I want to hear it for the rest of my life.

“Your esteemed academy has proven itself worthy of hosting a God’s Tournament,” Lorata says.

Thrill flutters through me at her words. A God’s Tournament? Here? No, this can’t be real.

“As you know, I value foresight. Integrity. Wit,” she continues. “Those who wish to compete will undergo a gauntlet of tests, of which the first one begins today. In one month’s time I will return to deliver the second assessment to those who are still in the running. If any are left after this, they shall undergo my final trial. Whomever succeeds first, if any at all, will have the honor of serving as my champion. I expect nothing less than perfection.”

More murmurs susurrate through the room. Lorata raises a hand, and scraps of paper abruptly swirl through the air. A faint wind rustles my hair as a page moves past, but much like Lorata’s clothes, the papers are drifting in a controlled manner, rather than spinning on a breeze.

“The first test is writ on these pages. Take one to take the first step.”

And then she’s gone. The warmth vanishes. The suspended papers abruptly go limp, like the strings suspending them have been cut, and flutter to the ground. Silence prevails for a moment.

Then everyone scrambles to grab one of the papers. I take one, too, slipping it out from under the reach of a nearby classmate.

He scoffs. “You heard Lorata. She wants the best of the best. You really think someone like you could compete?”

“No.” I turn to him with a grin. “I think someone like me can win.”

I wake up with a grin on my face, the feelings of competitive determination still lingering as I sit up and shake the last vestiges of sleep from my mind.

Ew. Excited to participate in a God’s Tournament? Less of a dream and more of a nightmare. At least it hadn’t been Widengra’s Tournament. A familiar rage bubbles up within me at the thought of the war god. One day I’ll be strong enough to fight him. One day I’ll be able to avenge those who died on my behalf.

Terimus. Rena. Layf. Talia.

The last one hurts the most. If I hadn’t frozen up, if I’d reacted faster, she might still be alive.

As I get dressed and start to prep some breakfast, details of the dream float back to me.

Lorata; she’s the head of the pantheon. It’s weird that I had a dream about her. I’ve hardly spared her two thoughts the last few months, what with Shirasil and Widengra taking up my complete “worry about the gods” bandwidth. Apart from being the head god, she’s supposed to be the god of light. And wisdom, I think? I snort. Can’t be that wise if she lets gods like Widengra go around slaughtering people.

I finish making breakfast, and my Role Requirement doesn’t chime in to let me know I’ve satisfied my daily requirement, since I completed it last night. The lack of her remark is something I notice more than if she’d actually spoken up; I’ve made meal prep such a strict part of my routine these days that it’s basically become autopilot. I won’t ever be blindsided by an unfulfilled Role Requirement again.

As I step out into the streets, the city is moving with life—though not as much as one would expect. Moonfall is often called The City that Never Sleeps, because over half of the city’s inhabitants are nocturnal. In fact, nighttime is when the city is at its busiest, but some members of species without dark vision, like humans and harpies, still operate during the day. At night, arachnoids, dhampyrs, and felis crowd the streets. Because of this there’s not really one best time for Blackcloaks to operate, so I adapt to whatever schedule best fits my current job.

I glare at the pebbles on the road as I stalk back toward the guild. I hadn’t picked up everything I needed the night before, too distracted by Shirasil’s annoying appearance, so now I have to return and hope I don’t run into Cyros again while I’m there.

Along the way, I swing by the district of the previous night’s hit. Smoke is no longer billowing out of the window—it would have stopped a minute or two after I left—but there is a cluster of City Guards on the street outside the house. Ugh, great. It’s going to be a pain to get to the target now that they’re tipped off that someone is trying to have them killed.

I sigh as I step back into the guild, a refreshing cool washing over me as I leave the daytime desert air behind. Instead of heading straight to the herbalist, however, I swing by the Job chamber. I’m running low on money—the hit was supposed to earn me a very comfortable sack of coins—and it can’t hurt to check if there are any quick and easy F Tier jobs I can snag to cover the cost of my potions.

When I step into the chamber, however, a familiar form is waiting for me.

Nieve, Cyros’s master, is a felis with black fur and startling blue eyes. They’re standing at the job wall, their cat-like tail swishing thoughtfully behind them. Nieve’s back is to me, so it’s not too late to sneak out.

Not that I’m intimidated by them; I just don’t want to admit why I’m here.

As if able to read my mind, (I did one time suspect this was the case, but Echo assured me they don’t have an Affinity for mind magic,) they turn and catch sight of me. My heart sinks, and I trudge over to their side.

“Ah, Sal,” Nieve says, their voice a warm and quiet purr. “It’s good to see you again. It seems like congratulations are in order.”

I blink. “Congratulations?”

Nieve has been away for a month on a mission of their own, and while they’re not directly my master, they often offer tips and advice. I wonder if they came here looking for Cyros but found me instead.

They gesture to the job wall, where a list of symbols and ranks are arrayed. It’s too dangerous to list the names of every member, so instead we all have symbols to represent our names, and these tokens are sorted from Tier F to S. My symbol is a butcher knife.

And now it’s in Tier C.

My stomach leaps up my throat. Is that real? I went up a tier? But how? I totally failed my mission.

“A bit messy from what I heard,” Nieve remarks. “A guard was also killed by the poisonous smoke. You’ll lose half your earnings for that. But the target was neutralized, and no evidence was left at the scene. Good work.”

It worked? Holy crap. That smoke bomb must have been more potent than I thought. I’ve developed such a high resistance to my own poisons that they only affect me in extreme concentrations.

I check my stats to be sure.

[Name: Sal]

[Title: Champion]

[Class: Culinary Rogue]

[Level: 29]

[Attack: 72]

[Agility: 36]

[HP: 99/99]

[Affinities: Poison]

[Role: Chef]

And I’m only a sliver away from leveling up to thirty! About time. I won’t get a new class at that point, but Echo has said I’ll get access to some new skills or spells. Alright! I can probably grind the rest of the experience points out today, if I spend the afternoon in the weapons hall, or head out into the dunes to find something to fight.

Nieve chuckles. “You seem surprised.”

“What?” I snap my mouth shut. “No. I’m just excited to get better jobs now!”

Nieve regards me with a faint smile. “You’re progressing quickly. But careful about pushing yourself too far too fast; that’s how promising new recruits end up dead. The road to B Tier jobs will be longer than your path to Tier C, and A and S Tiers will be even further beyond that.”

“I can handle it,” I assure them. I’ll have to be able to if I want any chance of confronting Widengra.

But every day I can feel myself growing stronger. Faster. And recently, my HP has even been increasing. I didn’t think that was possible without changing your body—but I’ve never been as toned as I am now. Maybe I can break 100 HP before the next Tier.

“Any idea what job you’d like next?” Nieve asks.

My eyes skim over the listings, but I don’t even read them. I’m too excited to hit my next level up and see what new skills I might uncover.

“Maybe later,” I say. “I’m going to go collect the bounty for this last one and then go train for a bit.”

Nieve raises a perplexed eyebrow. “I thought you were eager to advance to the next Tier.”

“I am,” I assure them. “Definitely. I’ll check back this evening to see if there’s anything new. See you later, Master Nieve.”

Nieve nods with a faint, curious smile as I step away to collect on my (surprisingly) completed job. That felis is one tough nut to crack. They don’t ever seem bothered by anything. Even the few times I’ve seen them upset or annoyed, it’s a quiet, restrained kind of anger, which is somehow even scarier.

I turn in my job slip and accept my payment—significantly diminished, as Nieve had guessed, since it wasn’t a clean kill. But money is money, and I can pick up some lower Tier jobs to cover the difference if I need to.

As I stuff the sack of coins in a pocket, secure enough until I can add it to my Apothecary later, words whisper through the back of my mind. I killed someone.

I wrinkle my nose and shake my head, as if that could dislodge the intrusive thought. Of course I killed someone. That was the point. That was why I took that job. So I could prove to myself that I could. So I could get stronger. And hey, it worked—I’m almost level thirty now.

And that’s what I should be focusing on. I stuff the queasy feeling down and turn my thoughts back to the topic at hand: Let’s force this level up.

I leave the guild and start to make my way down the city. I could have found someone to spar with in the combat room, but I’m not in any mood for play fights. I want some real action. Besides, real fights result in more experience points than fake ones. And there’s always something to fight out in the dunes.

Because of some sort of life arcana source nearby, the desert grows oversized animals. They’re almost always great for potion supplies, and whatever meat and pelt is leftover can be traded at the marketplace. Hunting down some monsters sounds like a great way to level up. And take my mind off things. But that’s beside the point.

I reach the ground level of the city, which is still a couple stories higher than the floor of the desert. The mesa that Moonfall is carved out of is an impressive fortress, and the height helps its inhabitants avoid the surrounding wildlife. It won’t do much to stop me, however.

Bypassing the standard passage out of the city that winds down through the bottom of the mesa, I instead jump down the last few stories. My stomach flips for the few seconds of freefall, an exhilarated laugh pulled from my throat, then the sand slams into my boots. The impact jolts up my legs.

[1 point of Fall damage sustained,] Echo reports.

Man, still 1 point? I’ve been building up my Fall resistance for over a month. What is it going to take for me to be able to make these jumps without taking any damage?

Straightening up, I pull the hood of my suncloak over my head to shade against the brutal sunlight. I touch a hand to my hip, checking to make sure my sheathed knives are still there. A few potions are strapped across my chest, and my night goggles are resting on my collar bone; all is in order. Squinting against the wavering heat, I step out into the desert, already on the hunt.
