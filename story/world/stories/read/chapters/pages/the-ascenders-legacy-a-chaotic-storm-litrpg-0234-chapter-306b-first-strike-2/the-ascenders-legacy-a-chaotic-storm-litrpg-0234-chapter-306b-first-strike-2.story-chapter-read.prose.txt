The next morning came with silence that wasn't silence at all.

The rain had stopped, yet the air hummed, charged with leftover electricity that prickled over Aodhán's skin as he stirred. A cold, gray light streamed into the room from nowhere, painting it in shades of storm from the lightest gray all the way to black.

The storm sect didn't wake to birdsong or the smell of flowers. Rather, it woke to distant thunder—low, restrained, rumbling—and full of possibilities.

Varéc lifted his head first, nostrils flaring as the charge in the air prickled his scales—like a low current coursing through him. His scales crackled at the sensation, drinking in the energy saturating the morning air.

He shifted, and Aodhán practically leaped to his feet, weapons blazing. A blade of pure, condensed lightning crackled in his right hand, and a spear in his left.

It took him a few seconds to realize he was no longer in Abyssos. And the moment he did, the weapons fizzled out immediately, transforming into tiny motes of light and energy that dispersed into the air.

"Calm down, Aodhán." He whispered to himself, shaking the feel of static off his shoulders. "You're safe here. For now."

Varéc growled behind him, and Aodhán grimaced in embarrassment. "I guess I was dreaming."

He glanced around the room then, taking in the large space in the light of day. But nothing had changed. The room looked exactly the same as it had last night.

Aodhán wasn't sure whether to be relieved or disappointed that someone hadn’t tried to attack him. Maybe he was traumatized after all.

Exhaling, he sat onto the bed and closed his eyes. His gaze strayed to the clock at the edge of his vision, and he frowned.

It was nine am, almost noon. He must have slept for eight hours.

"Ugh." He groaned, opening one eye to glance at Varéc. "Zarxan said the initiation will take place tomorrow, which means I have the whole of today to do some sightseeing." And some digging. He thought to himself.

Varéc growled in excitement, and Aodhán chuckled. "I'm not sure what the rules for familiars and overhead flight are here in the outer sect, but if it's allowed, sure, we could take a spin around the sect."

Varéc leaped to his feet, and Aodhán laughed. He was just as eager as Varéc to fly around the sect, but before that, there was something he needed to do.

His status screen appeared before him in a burst of red, black, and gold, and Aodhán realized he had a small list of pending notifications.

Congratulations! You have gained a new title: Continental Adventurer [See full details below].

Congratulations! You have gained a new title: Leech [See full details below].

Congratulations! You have gained a new skill: Tidal Rupture. [See full details below].

Aodhán read through his status screen with a small smile. Even at first glance, the changes in his screen were obvious.

The first change was his class.

Rather than Advanced Storm Awakened, it had changed to Advanced Storm Cultivator. It was a very small change, considering they both meant the same thing, but it showed just how much attention the system paid each person. It had noted the change in his location and promptly adjusted his status screen to fit the appropriate cultural terms.

That was extensive detailing, in Aodhán's opinion, and with a slight frown, he wondered if there was ever a time the system wasn't watching.

With that frown still in place, he glanced at the second change.

Absorb Energy >>> Leech Energy.

This particular change, Aodhán disliked.

As much as the tag lessened the restrictions on the ability, giving him a wider range of attack, the word 'leech' sounded more like an insult rather than an improvement.

Varéc growled amusedly at his thoughts, and Aodhán scowled at him. "I am not a leech. That's a friggin' parasite. And I do not leech off people."

An array of mental images rushed into his mind in that moment, courtesy of Varéc, and when Aodhán caught a glimpse of lilac hair, his scowl dissolved into an unamused frown.

“Sebastian was a one-time thing, Varéc. I shouldn’t be getting a tag like this for something I did once even though I want to do it again.”

Varéc snorted, and Aodhán grimaced. “Okay, I used it on Orin too, and that life awakened cannibal. But I shouldn’t be getting such an insulting tag for something I only did thrice.”

Varéc growled amusingly, suggesting that thrice was more than enough, and Aodhán turned to glare at him. He didn’t respond, though, because he knew the system was right. Having to touch someone to absorb their energy was incredibly restricting. But with that restriction gone, the skill had just become much more dangerous.

And far more exciting.

Smiling, he glanced down at his palms, stretched one toward Varéc, and activated {Leech Energy}. The skill activated with a burst of willpower, but the rush of energy and power Aodhán had expected never came.

Varéc glared at him amusedly. Almost pompously.

Aodhán's smile morphed into a playful scowl. "We really need that size-altering perk. If you were the size of a pea, you couldn't possibly look down on me."

Varéc growled, chest heaving and wings flaring with riotous laughter. It was an infectious emotion, and before long Aodhán found himself laughing too. He spent a few more minutes scanning his screen for more changes, he and Varéc teasing each other, and when he was done, he pushed himself up from the bed and made his way to the bathroom for his first shower in Gordon.

Varéc’s teasing and laughter followed him into the bathroom, and when Aodhán closed the door behind him, his smile was wide and genuine. Almost carefree.

For the first time since he'd arrived in Gordon, he felt happy. The emotion bathed the world in a golden glow, and ten minutes later, when he stepped out of the bathroom, wet and refreshed, the smile was still firmly in place.

After another ten minutes, he was finally ready to step out. Dressed in a black robe and pants, Aodhán looked no different from the average outer-sect disciple save for his bloodline manifestation, which would no doubt stick out like a sore thumb.

He contemplated his look in the mirror for a few seconds before deciding to conceal both his hair and face with a cowl. The last thing he needed right now was a scene or some pompous disciple challenging him to a duel.

Satisfied with his final look, Aodhán turned to glance at his towering familiar. "Back in my spirit you go, you big brute."

Varéc bared his fangs in amusement before vanishing into Aodhán's spirit, purring at the dense amount of chaos in the space. He sent a stream of images to Aodhán's mind, and Aodhán chuckled. “Infuriating, lazy brute.”

Still chuckling, he walked toward the entrance, pushed the door open, and stepped right into chaos.

Standing only a few feet away from his room were four boys, three of whom had the fourth backed against the wall, the bronze lapel on their robes identifying them as outer sect disciples. The fourth boy, however, had no lapel and was dressed in a simple, yet expensive-looking robe. His feet dangled a few inches off the ground, utterly still despite the arm pressed violently against his neck.

"Even now, he's looking down on us, Gump." One of the disciples scowled at the dangling boy, a hint of bloodlust leaking into his tone. "I want to gouge those pompous eyes out with my bare hands and feed them to dogs."

“Easy, Kevar. We can’t feed Crixis’s eyes to dogs,” the one called Gump muttered, standing behind the other two boys. He was obviously the leader of this little gang, but when he spoke next, it was obvious he hated the dangling boy just as much as Kevar did.

“That would be an insult to dogs. We should feed them to gremlins and ogres. Wouldn’t that be better, Crixis?”

The boy's nostrils flared, but he said nothing. Neither did the superior expression on his face change. He might be pressed against the wall, yet he didn't seem to feel threatened at all.

Aodhán had no idea what was going on, and perhaps the old him would have stepped in, but he didn't even feel the inclination to do so this time. Instead, he leaned gently against his doorframe to simply observe, his core sense expanding to encapsulate the entire hallway.

A grimace touched his lips as the cores of the disciples appeared in his mind's eye—burning balls of storm essence that seemed too mediocre for cultivators at the seventeenth tier. Crixis’s core, however, blazed brightly, overshadowing the others with its intensity despite being three tiers weaker.

“Are you scared, Crixis?” Gump asked again, his taunting smile widening. “Are you about to cry for your mother?”

“Or his father,” the third disciple chuckled. “Sadly, they are not here to save you.”

The boy called Crixis stared at his three bullies for a moment before finally speaking, his tone bored. Resigned. “What do you want, Gump? Whatever issues we had in the shelter, I would have thought them squashed by now."

"Shut up!" Gump sneered up at him, his face reddening as if the sound of Crixis’s voice had sent him into a spiraling rage. "We are your seniors now, Crixis. Until and even after your initiation, you're to refer to us as Senior Brothers."

"Yes!" The third disciple spoke again, a tendril of lightning curling around his fists. "You might have been able to defeat us back at the shelter, Crixis. But here we are, your seniors, and even a perceived disrespect will earn you a beating you'll never forget."

Crixis didn't respond, but something in his aura changed in that moment, becoming more hostile. The disciples must have felt it because they all sneered, lightning sparking all around them in fury.

"You little freak!" Kevar sneered as he pressed harder against Crixis's neck. "Who do you think you are? This is no longer the shelter. Your father and mother are not here to protect you. You cannot look down on us!”

"I say he be taught the hard way!" Gump spat, robes rippling as he drew closer to Crixis. "We challenge you to a duel, Crixis. One-on-one against all three of us. Let us show you the power we've gained away from the shelter. By the time we beat you to a pulp, I bet you won't have the strength to even keep your eyes open, let alone look down on us!"

A beat of silence passed as the disciples flexed their power to intimidate Crixis, lightning rippling all around them. But Crixis didn't seem intimidated at all. If anything, he looked down on them even more, and after another moment of silence, he sighed.

"Fine. If it's a duel you want, then a duel you'll get. Can you let go of my neck now? I think it's starting to bruise."

The disciples stuttered, shocked by his utterance, but that shock was quickly replaced by a rage so overwhelming it was almost tangible. Gump gritted his teeth in fury and slammed a fist into the wall beside Crixis's head, a venomous snarl appearing on his face.

"This… this is why I hate you, Crixis. You think you're so untouchable because your parents are Mythics. But here in the sect, you'll soon come to realize that no one, not even the elders themselves, is untouchable. And I cannot wait to touch you, Crixis. I cannot wait to smash that smug face of yours back into your skull and leave you permanently damaged for all eternity."

He pulled back slightly, his snarl tightening into something even more brutal—a sweet smile.

"You and me, Crixis. Today. The arena. It's time we settled our dispute, don't you think?"

Crixis didn't respond even as Kevar pulled his arms away from his neck and stepped back with a snarl. His feet hit the ground with a loud thud a moment later, and he carelessly dusted the sleeves of his robes. “I think you’re right, Gump. It's time we settled our dispute in a less verbal manner. Noon, I will be there. Until then, storm bless you."

The disciples sneered at him and simply stalked away without response, their walk confident and boisterous. But Aodhán barely spared them more than a glance before turning his gaze back to Crixis, watching curiously as the boy leaned his head against the wall with an exhale.

Aodhán stepped out then, locking the door behind himself. The sound drew Crixis’s attention, and he raised a finger to his head in greeting. "Storm bless you, neighbor."

Aodhán mimicked the gesture. "Storm bless you. My name is Aodhán."

Crixis nodded, dusting off wood splinters from his robe. "Nice to meet you, Aodhán. I take it you're here for your initiation too?"

Aodhán frowned. "I didn't realize I wasn't the only one getting initiated."

Crixis smiled at that. "You're not alone, Aodhán, although we are not a multitude either. I've been here two days now, and you're the fourth initiate I've met."

"Is that a good or bad thing?"

Crixis chuckled. "Depends on who you ask. My friends who just left would tell you it's a terrible thing."

Aodhán’s frown deepened. "They didn’t seem very friendly to me.” His gaze hardened a bit. "Why didn't you fight back? Why didn't you decline the challenge?"

Crixis frowned at him, and Aodhán wondered if it was because he couldn’t see his face or because he couldn't believe the words he was hearing. His next words proved it was the latter.

"Are you joking? Or are you completely ignorant of the socio-political ways of the sect?"

Aodhán shrugged. “I’m new here. I’m a bit ignorant.”

“No kidding,” Crixis chuckled. “You can’t ever decline a duel in the outer sect. The loss of face will ruin you, and for people like us who haven’t even been initiated, we’ll be labeled a coward before we ever have a chance to prove ourselves.”

“So, it’s better to lose?”

"Certainly." Crixis nodded. "Better to be beaten to a bloody pulp than to be labeled a coward. Quick advice, Aodhán—do not piss anyone off. And in the event that you do, the worst thing you can possibly do to yourself is decline a challenge to a duel."

“That sounds messed up,” Aodhán noted, unable to believe his ears.

Crixis shrugged. “It sure is. But the outer sect is a boiling pot of fragile egos and denied dreams. You could be challenged to a duel simply for breathing too loudly.”

Aodhán couldn’t help himself. He laughed at the absurdity of it all and muttered. “Well, I’ll try not to breathe too loudly around anyone today. Thanks for the advice, Crixis.”

Crixis smiled. "You're welcome, Aodhán. And have a good day."

"I'll try my best. I might even come watch your duel in the arena."

Crixis grimaced. "Then you must be prepared to take me to a pub to help me drown my sorrows."

Aodhán laughed, enjoying the banter. "I'll bring bandages."

Crixis sighed. "Oh, my poor bones."

They both laughed, and a few seconds later, they said their goodbyes. Aodhán shook his head in amusement as he walked down the hallway, a wide smile still on his face. It seemed he had an acquaintance already.

Perhaps his stay here wouldn't be all doom and gloom after all.

He exited the visitor's quarters a minute later, his core sense spreading out to create a mental map in his mind. Now that the rain wasn't falling, the sect looked much different than it had the night before. Gray clouds covered the sky, completely obstructing the sun save for a few streaks of gold that broke through. The air was fresh and alive, charged with residual electricity, and with a deep breath, Aodhán began walking.

Disciples moved to and fro all around him, either carrying out one duty or the other, their dark gray robes rippling around them as if they had a life of their own. Aodhán walked calmly between them, his destination yet unknown.

He wondered if he and Varéc could just take to the air, but when he saw no sign of another familiar even after several minutes of walking, he decided it would be better to just walk.

It didn't take him long to reach the market district, and there he found a bustling crowd of storm cultivators—craftsmen, alchemists, merchants, dungeon divers, and a swarm of eager disciples.

The market wasn't just busy; it was alive with the cries of eager merchants, the back-and-forth of money talk, and the clatter of steel against steel as blacksmiths and runesmiths worked away.

Rows of stalls stretched out like arteries through the district's heart, dividing it into several sections—one for crafters, another for alchemists, and others for mundanities such as food, clothes, soap, and the like.

Aodhán walked into the first section, inhaling deeply as the scent of heated metal filled the nostrils—sharp and pungent, threaded not with flames but with the unmistakable scent of lightning itself.

Disciples filled the area, some buying weapons, others carrying bags of metal ores they’d looted from their dungeon or rift delves. One particular shop was more crowded than the others, though, and out of curiosity, Aodhán walked toward it.

As he drew closer, he realized that the shop was not a shop at all but a forge, easily two dozen meters in length and half that in breadth. Several blacksmiths worked within, the clang of metal against metal growing louder with each step he took toward it.

And when he reached the entrance, a small banner confirmed his suspicions.

Algrim's Battle-Ready Rune Swords and Repairs.

Buy the best rune weapons here, or work with a rune-smith to create a weapon for yourself.

Motto: If It Breaks, You Bring It Back

Aodhán read the content of the banner with a small smirk. Battle-ready swords and repairs were great services, considering the number of duels that were sure to be happening within the sect daily. But the chance to work with a runesmith to create your own weapon was a fantastic idea.

No wonder the shop was so crowded.

However, Aodhán was still a bit surprised by the demand. Metallic weapons were great until one had to fight against a metal awakened, and then those weapons simply became one's greatest enemies. Besides, it had long been proven that constructs were just as, if not more, efficient than metal weapons, considering how easily they could be manipulated.

So, to see so many people here was a bit of a surprise. That was until he entered the shop and found the huge array of runic weapons displayed for sale. They covered the walls of the entire shop, blades gleaming with a hundred different hues unlike that of any metal he had ever seen before—almost crystalline. However, what caught his attention the most were the runes etched onto their surface, the least of which were advanced runes—severely boosting the speed, strength, agility, and power of their wielders.

This wasn't something a normal construct could achieve. Even with a skill like Daruk's forge construct, Aodhán doubted it was possible.

Curious, Aodhán moved closer to inspect the blades, hands itching to take one—not to wield, but to study. Crafting weapons was a profession Aodhán had always found interesting, and perhaps, one day, he would finally get the chance to venture into it, creating weapons just like he and Professor Caldwell had done back at the academy.

Smiling at the memory, he walked deeper into the shop, studying each weapon closely to identify its runes and base materials. His inspection, however, was soon cut short as laughter suddenly boomed from the other side of the shop, drawing everyone's attention to a lanky young disciple wielding a beautiful, crimson greatsword.

He was at the 32nd tier of advancement, and his core blazed brightly as he swung the sword around, testing its balance and weight, a wide smile on his face.

"Oh, this is marvelous!" He laughed again, his smile widening. "I could climb ten steps up the rankings with this. Are these runes all grand runes?"

The staff member attending to him nodded proudly, stating the other qualities of the weapon.

Aodhán smiled at the man's infectious excitement from where he stood. He understood now why so many people were gathered here. The swords he had seen so far had been great, epic ranked at least, but the crimson sword the man was wielding was an epic+ ranked item, and those didn’t come by easily.

It seemed this shop had a very advanced runesmith to create a sword of such ranking, and considering the average tier of outer sect members, no construct could give them the sort of boost these blades would.

Impressed, Aodhán was about to turn away to see if he would find another Epic+ blade when suddenly the man shouted. “I need to test it out.” His gaze scanned the hall and then landed on Aodhán. "Hey, newbie, you will help me test the strength of my new sword."

Aodhán barely had time to process the odd request before the man was flying at him, weapon aimed.

Only a day into the sect, and it seemed he was about to make his first enemy.
