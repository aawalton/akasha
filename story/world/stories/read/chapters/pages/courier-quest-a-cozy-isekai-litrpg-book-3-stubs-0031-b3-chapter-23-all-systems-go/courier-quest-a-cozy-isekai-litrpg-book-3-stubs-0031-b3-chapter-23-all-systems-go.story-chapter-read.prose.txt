Trevor and Adabelle were holding onto Croal’s antler-like horns in preparation for their flight. The courier, following his creed, had enough rope to create a makeshift harness so he wouldn’t fall. His wife, however, trusted her balance and strength and stood on the blue dragon’s head with a single arm around his horn.

Clay had opted to stay in the backpack, which felt safe.

“Final checks,” Trevor announced as he tugged at the rope. Satisfied, he reached for his wife. She touched his hand in solidarity, their fingers barely grazing each other from how far apart they had to stand. “All systems go. Ready for flight.”

Trevor placed both of his hands on the smooth horn as tightly as he could. This was something of a dream come true for him. Flying with an actual dragon. He wasn’t sure if anything was going to top this.

“I already told you that was unnecessary, but I’m glad you’re feeling safe,” Croal said. “Adabelle?”

“Ready whenever you are,” she responded simply.

“Then off we go!” he announced before dipping slightly back into the water.

Which they then shot out of like a rocket. Trevor stumbled slightly, though he was able to keep his grip on the horn, while Adabelle leaned backwards slightly from the force. She was absolutely fine and even shared the big smile her husband had as they started gaining altitude.

As the noblewoman had sneakily requested on her way up, Croal passed over the Hello Sea. She easily picked out Lora and her guest, and gave them a polite wave. Trevor, seeing this, copied her gesture though he didn’t see why until it was almost too late.

Surtla was there, jaw hanging open as he saw the two Summoned he had tried to scam rise into the air on Paladona’s biggest celebrity. The look on his face was absolutely priceless, and Adabelle couldn’t help but laugh at the sight of it. Lora said something, which caused him to grimace and walk away. Then she jumped up and down and waved with obvious glee.

“Thank you for playing along, Croal,” Adabelle said. “My husband might be too polite to rub something like this in someone’s face, but I am most certainly not.”

“Wait, did you request this?” Trevor asked, confused as the dragon laughed. “I didn’t hear you say anything.”

“That’s because your ears aren’t nearly as good as ours, honey, but that’s okay,” she replied, offering him a dazzling smile. “What I’d like to know is how come there’s not any wind and you can hear me so easily.”

“Hey, yeah, it is pretty quiet,” the courier remarked.

“More magic,” Croal explained as he banked to the right, taking them towards Paladona. “The same kind I use to still the water works on the air. That way, it’s a nice and smooth ride for you where you can talk, call things out, whatever you’d like.”

“Wow, that’s awesome, Croal,” Trevor praised.

“Aww, it’s nothing. Just a little magic to make life a little easier,” he said humbly. “Enjoy the ride, alright?”

They soared over the streets of Paladona where it seemed like almost everyone underneath was waving, pointing, or shouting up at them. The view of the city from above was marvelous, and they continued to climb to the point where the people were not much bigger than ants.

Trevor remembered the view of Eskretet from one of the castle’s tall spires and the Floating Isles from Alder’s Crab Shack, but they were nothing like this. It was breathtaking. Croal swooped this way and that, diving towards some places seemingly at random, though he probably had a plan. He flew so close to the Basilica Draconian’s spire that Adabelle could almost reach out and touch it.

Clay, sensing their master’s wonder, was hanging out of his backpack. They watched the ground with delight, pointing at various buildings whenever something caught their eye. From this high up, they could see something that most people never did.

The city had been built like an ouroboros, a snake coiled into a circle that was eating its own tail. Or, more likely, a dragon.

It wasn’t immediately visible, but it was there. Paladona had grown a lot since the days of its founding and the pattern had been broken in a few places where buildings had been reconstructed. Trevor pointed out that there was a triangular section that reminded him of a mouth near the beach.

Adabelle nodded when she saw it, and pointed out where it looked like the settlers had started building outwards. Homes, shops, museums, even the cathedral was outside of the inner circle, showing that it had been a later addition to the city.

After flying over a few times, Croal banked softly towards the ocean. “Alright, hope you two don’t mind some speed!”

“Bring your worst,” Adabelle replied with a cackle.

“Yeah, sure, let’s do that then,” the dragon said, accepting the challenge.

“I’m glad I put on a—Whoa!” Trevor yelled out as Croal suddenly accelerated.

The ride so far had been smooth as butter as they flew through the sky, but now he tore through it. Trevor braced himself, incredibly glad that he had the foresight to make a harness. Especially since his wife was standing there unperturbed. If it weren’t for the big smile and the jubilant shout, he’d have guessed she was simply waiting for the bus.

They passed over Mount Cruon and continued towards the horizon before Croal started dipping towards the water. “Hold on!” he announced before letting out a mighty whoop.

Trevor closed his eyes and braced himself for impact as the dragon’s snout disappeared into the ocean. He was fully prepared to take some damage, because there was no way that slamming into the water this fast wasn’t going to hurt.

Nothing happened. Clay patted him on the back, even more wonder passing through their bond.

The courier opened his eyes to see that Croal’s horns and spine had started glowing, illuminating the world under the waves. An air bubble surrounding the dragon’s whole head kept them dry and safe as he slowed down to a more sedate pace. They stared at the bottom, watching as fish, crabs, and monstrous beasts lived their lives without concern for the sudden appearance of the top of the food chain.

Taking the long way home, Croal gently turned back towards the island while the Summoned took in all that they could see. For them, this was a domain usually reserved for Rashie. There would be no replicating anything like this back at home.

There was a large underwater cave on the side of Mount Cruon, and the dragon turned into it. It was more than large enough for him and his passengers to fit. Bioluminescent algae lined the walls, guiding them deeper under the island until it took a hard turn upwards, which they followed.

Croal emerged into the caldera of the dormant volcano. The outer walls had several tracks around it just like the Basilica Draconian did, indicating that this was where he came to rest, and he began to spiral so that he could lay down.

“Behold,” he said. “My collection!”

The center of the caldera was taken up by the hole they had just come from while the outer ring was all tracks. Between the two was a cacophony of colors that swept all the way around the rest of the space.

At first, it looked like random items. A statue here, a sign there. Chairs. Looms. Glass. Rocks. The items were random, but the way that they were arranged was not.

As Croal came to a rest, Trevor noted that it started with black, then followed the colors of the rainbow until it made a full circle back to white. Every object was color coordinated to be near something that was similar, but slightly different. Some stretches were longer than others, like the greens compared to the purple, but there was no mistaking that the dragon had created a color wheel with random things.

“This is a very large collection,” Adabelle stated as she let go of Croal’s horn. There was a platform on the other side, and she started walking across his head to get to it. “How are you feeling, son of Ander?”

Trevor sent the harness back to his inventory without having to take it apart and followed her. “Like I’ve just gotten off of a roller coaster,” he laughed. “I gotta say, man, you got me when you started diving towards the water. The speed I could handle, thanks to the harness, but that surprised me.”

“Yeah, that was a little cheeky of me,” Croal agreed with a toothy grin. He raised his head to look at them better now that they were no longer on him. “But it was memorable, right?”

“It sure was,” Trevor agreed.

“Yes, thank you very much for taking us along,” Adabelle said as her tail returned to her husband. She smiled at the dragon. “Now, I assume you can tell us what this little chat is about?”

“Now? Yes. You don’t want to look at my colors first, though?” he asked, turning his head to his collection.

“Your colors?” she asked curiously.

“Oh, I totally got it right!” Trevor exclaimed, and she tilted her head towards him. With a smug grin, he pointed at the large assortment of things. “It’s a color wheel. Starts with black, goes through red, orange, yellow, and so on all the way back to white. That was the first thing I thought of when I saw it! Dude’s got a really nice setup.”

“Thanks,” the dragon said, perking up at the praise. “I’d say that it’s all my doing, but the innermost stuff was mum’s. She started the collection. They really only made things in a limited style back then, though, so I’ve added a ton of stuff to it. It’s really coming along nicely.”

“That’s awesome, Croal,” Trevor said. “We’ve got a dragon who lives nearby Tosa, too. She came to our wedding. Collects clocks.”

At the courier’s words, Croal turned his full attention back towards the Summoned. His eyes focused on the man intensely, and he took a deep breath.

“That’s what I wanted to talk to you about,” the dragon admitted. “I can smell another dragon on you. Which is weird, because the only other one I’ve ever met is mum. At first it was strange and I thought my nose was all mixed up, especially because it was so faint, but then you were talking about her, right? Glirfezeth?”

“Oh, no, not like that. Glyrphizith,” Trevor said.

Croal frowned. “Glyrphezith.”

The courier chuckled. “Don’t worry, I had trouble at first, too. It’s Glyr-phi-zith.”

“Glyrphizith,” he tried again.

“Yeah, you got it,” Trevor said proudly. “Your sense of smell and hearing must both be something else if you caught all that while you were doing your thing though.”

“Dragons have excellent senses, and they’re enhanced further by magic so long as they haven’t expended themselves,” Adabelle explained. “That’s how I was able to tell Croal to get close to the Hello Sea with nothing more than a whisper. Again, thank you.”

“No worries. I can’t say I’ve ever been scammed before, for obvious reasons, but it sounds awful,” he replied with an earth-shaking shrug of his shoulders several yards away. “But, yeah. I’ve never met a dragon other than my mother, and she told me not to go seeking out the nearest one. Apparently he’s some nasty red guy over in Kartathoon, wherever that is.”

“Glyrphizith is a dear friend and nasty is the furthest word I’d use for her,” Trevor stated confidently. “She’s green, like emeralds. In fact…”

He moved to the side and looked down at Croal’s collection of colors. The greens were the largest section by far, and finding the right shade was fairly easy. He did take out his binoculars to make sure, though. Something told him that Croal took this kind of thing seriously.

“Alright. You see that space between the evergreen you planted, the statue covered in moss, and that sign that has a… chicken-shaped hole in it?” Trevor asked, unsure if that’s what it was. He shook his head a moment later. “Anyway, if you look at that glass there, that’s a close approximation of what she’s like.”

“Oh,” Croal said, his eyes focusing on the bottle. “That’s a pretty green.”

Adabelle arched an eyebrow. “Out of curiosity, and don’t feel pressured to answer if you don’t want to, but how old are you?”

“Just shy of three hundred,” he answered, not missing a beat.

Trevor frowned in thought. “Glyrphizith’s, like, seven hundred and something, right? I never really got an answer earlier.”

“Just so you know, this isn’t some romantic thing or anything,” the dragon said as he turned to face them again. “I just want to meet another of my kind, you know? It’s been kind of lonely since mum died. I’ve got all of Paladona, and High Priest Worth is a good guy even if he’s a little pushy sometimes, but it’s not the same. I just want to make a new friend with someone who’s like me. Dragons are pretty rare, apparently.”

“That is true,” Adabelle confirmed. “Glyrphizith doesn’t like to travel much these days, so you would more than likely have to go and see her. Not saying she won’t come down, just not to expect it.”

“That’s fine by me,” Croal said with another shrug. “I don’t get away often, but really the only thing that keeps me here is Fustavele and mum's legacy. Everybody's really got their stuff together now. Going away for a little bit won’t be a bother, especially if I can travel there by ocean. You think I could fly fast? You should see me swim.”

“We’ll have to introduce you to Rashie, then,” Trevor chuckled, imagining a race between the two. “She’s a little shark girl, amphibious, and can go really fast.”

Adabelle cleared her throat, and they both looked at her. “If you’d like, we can deliver a message for you. It will be a couple of weeks before we’re home again, but Trevor can take it to her once we’re back from our honeymoon. I’m sure it would be no trouble for Lora, the Summoned with the Power of Teleportation, to bring news the next time she comes to Paladona.”

“You’d be willing to do that for me?” he asked hopefully, his eyes shining in the light. “Should I write a letter? Or send a gift? Would that be too much?”

“A gift might be, but a letter would certainly be fine. Perhaps even a scale, if you want to personalize it further,” Adabelle offered. “She would likely be able to glean a lot about you from it, which would help her decide whether or not to meet you. Just don’t be too upset if it comes back negative, okay? I don’t think it’ll happen, but there’s always a chance.”

Trevor glanced at Croal’s scales. Despite his size, they were rather fine. Most of them were as big around as his fist, so they would be very easy to store in his inventory if that was the route he wanted to take.

“Yeah, we’re all pretty solitary,” the dragon said with an understanding, though excited, nod. “Still, even if a meeting is out of the question, maybe we could still write. That would be great.”

“Then you’ll need to have something worked up by morning,” Adabelle replied with a smile. “We’ll be leaving early to keep with our schedule. If I remember right, High Priest Worth mentioned something about working until sunrise?”

“Ah, yeah. Actually, I should be getting you two back to the mainland,” Croal said, looking back in that direction.

“We’re staying by the beach, so how about you drop us off there and then come back in the morning,” Trevor suggested. “Have one of the priests write it for you so it’s small enough for me to take, and we’ll get it to her as soon as we can.”

“There’s no need to rush it. We dragons are patient,” he bragged before smiling softly. “You two are great people, helping me out like this.”

“We aim to please, and you can trust Trevor Anderson with any delivery,” Adabelle said, bragging a little herself.

“Yep. You can count on me.”

Croal’s smile grew into a grin. “Okay, we really need to get going. Oh, I’m so excited! Hop back on. I’ll take you back, and then return for the rest of the ceremony. To the skies!”
