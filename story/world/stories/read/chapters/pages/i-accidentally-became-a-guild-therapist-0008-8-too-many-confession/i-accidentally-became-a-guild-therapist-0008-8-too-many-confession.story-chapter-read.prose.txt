ARC I: THE ACADEMIC EXILE

SYNOPSIS: Livia Marcelline Quinn, now Mental Supporter Lv. 3, is drowning in the guild’s endless sea of neuroses. Rapid back-to-back therapy sessions, combined with her new Empathic Insight skill, push her to a critical breaking point. Facing total collapse, Livia is forced to choose a defining Awakening path: protect her sanity, or fully embrace her role as the guild’s emotional conduit.

Livia Marcelline Quinn, freshly promoted to Mental Supporter Lv. 3, was drowning in her own Therapist’s Log.

The pages had stopped being organized days ago. Now they were layers—notes on top of notes, margin scribbles arguing with main text, question marks next to entries she’d been too tired to follow up on:

Bron: Existential dread. Provisional resolution. Watch for relapses. (Note: relapse confirmed. New outlet: philosophical blacksmithing. Monitoring.)

Nyx: Dark phobia. Progress. Ten seconds. (Note: do not think about the smile. Ongoing.)

Phina: Slime romance. Active. Glimmer glowed. Jiggle chart ongoing. (Note: Glimmer gave me a wooden heart. What does this mean. What does any of this mean.)

Alaric: Gambling. Unresolved. Currently betting on his own recovery. (10:1 against. He told me this unprompted.)

Guild general: Emotional black hole. I am the only thing between this guild and total psychological collapse. This is not a hyperbole.

The guild’s base camp was a fever dream of glowing runes, scattered enchanted gear, and a sentient slime that had graduated from cartwheels to synchronized swimming in a mana puddle. The mana puddle was not supposed to be there. Nobody had explained it.

Livia slumped on her crate, silver-trimmed robe a small mercy, and stared at her notepad.

I am their shrink, their mascot, and a subreddit star with 75k subscribers.

I flunked psych. I am the emotional janitor for a guild of disasters.

I am also, apparently, 1,200 EXP away from the next level, which means I am leveling up by doing this, which means this world considers this a viable career path.

This is fine.

[Achievement Unlocked: Overworked Empath. +10 Stress Points (Overflowing)]

“Accurate,” Livia muttered. Her progress bar read Mental Supporter Lv. 3: 400/3000 EXP. Her new Empathic Insight Lv. 1 was, in theory, a gift—detecting subtle emotions beneath the surface, seeing what patients couldn’t say. In practice, it meant the guild’s collective neurosis was louder than ever.

I am one sob story from a breakdown, she thought, and wrote in her notepad: Issue: I’m losing it. Approach: Unknown. Coffee?

Her crate had become, without her consent, Grand Central Station for guild complaints. The line moved fast. Too fast.

Bron arrived first, his Livia plushie tucked under one arm and a piece of scrap metal under the other.

“Buffer!” He set the metal down on a nearby rock with the care of someone presenting important work. “Immortality’s pointless without meaning. I’ve been thinking. I need a craft. Something that endures. Something that says—” he paused, searching, —“I was here.”

[Patient: Bron. Surface: Enthusiasm. Beneath: Dread of being forgotten. Severity: High.]

Oh. That’s the real one. Not boredom. Fear of erasure.

“That’s actually a meaningful instinct,” she said carefully. “Making something that outlasts you.”

Bron’s face lit up. “Philosophical blacksmithing! I’m going to hammer the existential into metal!”

“Maybe—” Livia started.

Too late. Bron, demonstrating his new craft, slammed the scrap metal onto the rock. The resulting spark was not a normal blacksmith spark. It was cartoonishly large, bright yellow, and traveled with suspicious purpose directly toward the nearest sign, which read: “Welcome to Bloodbath & Beyond: Where Safety is Optional.”

The sign caught immediately.

Livia reached for the water bucket that she had, through hard-won experience, started keeping beside her crate. “Philosophical basket-weaving,” she said. “Five minutes. Away from the tents.”

Bron considered this. “Can I weave existential dread into the pattern?”

“You can weave whatever you want into the pattern.”

He nodded, appeased, and wandered off muttering about “temporal entropy and yarn.”

[You eased an immortal’s fear of erasure and averted minor property damage. +300 EXP]

Fear of erasure, Livia wrote in the margin. Not boredom. The blacksmithing is about leaving a mark. Follow up.

Before the smoke from the sign had fully cleared, Nyx materialized at her shoulder.

His flashlight was on—always on—but his grip on it was tighter than usual, and he was standing slightly closer to her than he normally did, which was already closer than most people would consider a comfortable distance for someone who professionally moved through shadows.

“Livia.” His voice was very quiet. “The dark’s back. Worse.”

[Patient: Nyx. Phobia returning. Romantic tension: elevated. Stress: high.]

I know, Livia thought. I can feel both of those things and I am only going to address one of them right now.

“Okay,” she said. “Picture the dark as a quiet friend. We did this. You did ten seconds.”

Nyx nodded, but his Ghost Rogue Stealth Cloak chose this moment to express its feelings about his elevated stress levels by glitching. For approximately three full seconds, he flickered in and out of visibility, each flicker leaving a neon purple silhouette that lingered half a second too long with the words ROGUE DETECTED hovering above it in block letters.

Several nearby players turned to look.

Nyx flickered back into full visibility, hood up, face somewhere between mortified and resigned.

Livia reached over and tapped his arm once, steadying. The glitch settled. “Flashlight on, for now. You’ve got this.”

He managed a shaky exhale. His gaze lingered on her hand, then on her face, then on the ground.

I noticed. I am not writing that down.

[You calmed a rogue’s fears and resolved an involuntary visibility incident. +200 EXP]

Phina arrived next, Glimmer on her shoulder, her expression caught between delight and poetic anguish.

“Empath Eternal,” she began.

“Please don’t—”

“My bond with Glimmer falters! It jiggles… distantly.” Phina’s hands clasped. “As though our souls, once synchronized in cosmic frequency, have—”

Glimmer, at this moment, noticed Livia.

It extended a small pseudopod.

From somewhere inside itself—Livia chose not to examine where—it produced a miniature wooden heart, roughly the size of a bottle cap, clearly hand-carved by something without hands, and placed it with great care on the edge of Livia’s notepad.

It jiggled.

Expectantly.

Livia looked at the wooden heart.

She looked at Glimmer.

Is this a love triangle, she thought. Am I in a love triangle with a slime. Is that what’s happening.

“Phina,” she said carefully, “I think Glimmer may be expressing affection through gift-giving. To… multiple recipients. This could be a positive sign of its emotional capacity rather than distance.”

Phina gasped. Her expression cycled through several emotions before landing on inspired. “Gelatinous independence! Complex courtship rituals! The cosmos is vast enough for many loves!” She swept Glimmer up, already composing an ode, and glided away with the energy of someone who had just resolved a philosophical crisis and found it thrilling.

The wooden heart was still on Livia’s notepad.

She moved it to the corner.

She was not going to think about this.

[You soothed a slime romance crisis and received questionable affection from a second party. +400 EXP]

[System Note: Livia Quinn is now the subject of devotion from: 1 Ghost Rogue, 1 sentient slime, 75,491 subreddit members. The system observes this without comment.]

Alaric arrived smelling of ale and looking like a man whose morning had started badly and declined from there. His holy aura was doing its best. It was not having a good day either.

“Buffer!” He held up one hand in what might have been a greeting or a surrender. “I bet my holy shield on a troll race. Lost. Again. Fix me.”

Livia opened her mouth to deploy the standard redirection.

Then her Empathic Insight activated.

[Patient: Alaric. Surface: Sheepish. Performing casualness. Beneath: Fear. Cold, specific, deep-seated. Not about the bet. Not about the gold.]

She closed her mouth.

She looked at him properly.

Alaric, under her gaze, shifted. His flask hand lowered slightly. His holy aura flickered—not dramatically, not like a power surge, but the way a candle flickers when a door opens. Small. Uncertain.

“You don’t care about the shield,” Livia said.

Alaric’s performance faltered. “What?”

“You don’t care about the gold, or the shield, or any of it.” She kept her voice even. “You bet because when you’re waiting to find out if you’ve won, your holy aura doesn’t flicker. The risk makes you feel chosen. Like the light’s still in you.”

Silence.

Alaric’s flask dropped from his hand. He didn’t seem to notice. His eyes had gone very wide, the practiced drunk’s haze clearing the way things clear when something lands precisely where it was aimed.

“My holy aura…” he started.

“Is flickering,” Livia said, gently. “Has been for a while, I think. And you’re scared.”

He stood there for a long moment. Then he bent, very slowly, picked up his flask, and turned away.

“I have to go confess to the High Paladin,” he said, mostly to himself.

He walked off, steadier than he’d arrived.

[You triggered a major existential breakthrough in a paladin. +500 EXP]

[Mental Supporter Lv. 3: 1800/3000 EXP]

Fear that the divine chose wrong, Livia wrote. That the light is leaving. Bets are a test: if luck is with me, maybe faith is too. Follow up.

Her head throbbed.

The guild’s chaos had been escalating in the background throughout all of this, the way weather escalates—gradually, then suddenly. Bron’s philosophical basket-weaving had somehow produced a structure. Not a basket. A wicker man. Approximately four feet tall, structurally questionable, with a small sign attached that read “TEMPORAL ENTROPY” in Bron’s handwriting.

Nyx had retreated to a crate. His neon purple silhouette was visible through the slats.

Phina’s new ode had caused Glimmer to bounce with sufficient enthusiasm that it had knocked over two tents. It was currently attempting to return them to upright. It was not succeeding.

Alaric had reappeared, slightly more sober, and was attempting to bet his boots on the wicker man’s structural integrity. Three players had already taken him up on it.

And Livia’s Empathic Insight was still running.

All of it. All of them. Bron’s fear of erasure, Nyx’s layered anxiety, Phina’s aching sincerity, Alaric’s quiet terror that the divine light was leaving him. Four people. Four sets of things they couldn’t say out loud. All of it filtering through her, all at once, because she had a skill that made her better at her job and her job was this.

Her head didn’t just throb. It rang.

She slammed her notepad shut.

“I can’t do this!” The words came out louder than she intended, loud enough that Bron looked up from his weaving and Nyx’s silhouette went still. “I’m not cut out for this! I flunked psych, I’m a meme, I have a wooden heart from a slime on my notepad, and I am currently the only thing standing between this guild and total psychological—”

Her HUD glitched.

Static crackled across it.

Then a voice—not the system’s usual flat tone, something different, something that felt like it came from further in:

THERAPIST AWAKENING INITIATED.

EMOTIONAL OVERLOAD DETECTED.

CHOOSE PATH.

A holographic menu materialized in front of her, clean and still amid the chaos:

EMPATH SURGE: Amplify emotional connection. +20% breakthrough chance. -10% mental stamina per session.

EMOTIONAL BARRIER: Reduce emotional overload. +10% mental stamina. -5% empathy depth.

Livia stared at the two options.

The camp noise faded, or she stopped hearing it, one or the other.

Emotional Barrier.

She read it again. Reduce emotional overload. +10% mental stamina.

It was, objectively, the sensible choice. Professional distance was not a flaw in therapy—it was a requirement. You couldn’t help someone if you were drowning alongside them. Every textbook she’d half-read said the same thing: boundaries, sustainability, self-preservation. You put on your own oxygen mask first.

Her hand moved toward it.

It would be easier.

Safer.

She’d still help them. Just from a distance. Professional distance. The way an actual therapist would.

The way she was supposed to be, before all of this.

She almost touched it.

And then, without deciding to, she remembered.

Bron’s tears on the bench, the first day, falling like he’d been waiting years for someone to give him permission to cry. What’s the point of living forever if I’m just existing? And her answer—half-asleep psych training, shaky voice—and him breathing. Actually breathing.

Nyx in the dim corner, white-knuckled on a Hello Kitty flashlight, counting to ten. Not because the dark had stopped being terrifying. Because she had said I’m right here, and he had believed her.

Phina kneeling on the camp floor, eighteen months of color-coded charts in her sleeve, saying I see you. I choose to be near you. Every day, on purpose.

Alaric’s flask hitting the ground. The haze clearing. I have to go confess to the High Paladin. Walking away steadier than he’d arrived.

Those moments were not the product of distance.

They were the product of being close enough to feel what they couldn’t say.

If she put up the Barrier, she would still help them.

But would it feel like that?

Would Bron’s relief land the same way if she was processing it from behind a wall?

Would Nyx have counted to ten for someone who wasn’t really there?

She thought about her old life. The girl who studied other people’s emotions from a safe academic distance because her own were too complicated. The girl who was, in her mother’s words, too sensitive for this, in the tone that meant too sensitive, full stop, as though sensitivity were a design flaw.

She had spent years trying to fix that about herself.

And she had ended up here.

In a world that gave her EXP for it.

Maybe, she thought, and it was a strange thought, unfamiliar, the sensitivity was never the problem.

Maybe what had ruined her life wasn’t the sensitivity at all. Maybe it was believing the sensitivity had to be switched off before it could be worth anything.

She had spent years trying to become less affected. Less porous. Less of a person who cried at the wrong things and noticed too much and cared before she’d decided whether it was safe to.

Aethelgard was not asking her to become less affected.

It was asking her to become more deliberate instead.

Her hand moved.

Not toward Barrier.

She pressed Empath Surge.

The effect was immediate.

Her HUD flared. Her robe glowed, the silver trim brightening, new faint blue runes tracing themselves along the hem. And then—the guild’s emotions hit her like a wave.

Bron’s fear of erasure, sharper and more specific than before.

Nyx’s layered anxiety, and underneath it something quieter and more stubborn that she recognized as hope.

Phina’s aching, radiant sincerity.

Alaric’s terror, and beneath that, the ember of faith that hadn’t gone out yet.

And Miles, across the camp, not looking at her—his back turned, arms crossed, facing the camp perimeter—something locked and heavy that her new skill brushed against and could not quite read.

It hurt.

All of it. The weight of five people’s unsaid things, amplified.

It hurt, and she did not remove her hand from the selection.

[EMPATH SURGE SELECTED]

[+1500 EXP. Achievement Unlocked: Burnout Breakthrough]

[Mental Supporter Lv. 3: 3300/3000 EXP — OVERFLOW]

[Warning: Mental Stamina Drain Rate increased 20%. You chose this. The system respects your decision.]

Livia exhaled.

“Fine,” she muttered, to herself, to the guild, to the system, to whoever was running this particular cosmic joke. “I’ll keep fixing your messes.” She picked up her notepad. “But I’m getting a coffee skill next.”

She wrote: Issue: Burnout. Choice: Empath Surge. Regret: TBD. Reason: They counted to ten because I was there. I intend to keep being there.

Miles materialized beside her with the timing she was beginning to recognize as his: present for the aftermath, never quite present for the moment itself.

“Knew you wouldn’t quit,” he said. His smirk was back, but something underneath it was… not quite composed. Her Empathic Insight was still running hot from the Surge.

[Patient: Miles. Surface: Satisfied. Beneath: Something unresolved. He was watching you choose. He wanted you to choose Surge. He is not going to tell you that.]

Livia looked at him.

He looked back, smirk holding.

“You’re stuck with us, Buffer.”

“I know,” she said.

Not as a complaint. Just… as a fact.

She was stuck with them.

She had chosen to be.

[Achievement Unlocked: Emotional Sponge]

[System Note: You chose Surge. You chose all of them. That was not the easy choice. The system notes this for the record.]

The wicker man, behind them, finally collapsed.

Alaric collected his winnings.

The camp roared on.

Livia opened her notepad to a fresh page.

I’m either a saint or a masochist, she wrote.

Possibly both.

The system is not going to tell me which.

[END OF CHAPTER 8]
