Ferris carried the unconscious Jade back to their room at Lily House and lay down to meditate. He wanted to quickly get back to the slimespace to continue watching the acid drain out of the dome, yet nothing was quick when it came to meditation.

Despite the deluge of random thoughts and ideas, he was able to eventually enter the soulspace. Ferris immediately went to check on the slimespace, but there wasn’t much change. The acid flooding inside the dome had drained a bit, but it was far from gone.

Ferris sat on the edge of the Heartfire tower, watching the slimespace. He now knew how the flooding worked when Jade was injured, so it was time to make a plan to protect his abilities from the acid waves.

He wondered how Janice and Gumly had done it. The journal never mentioned anything specific about their soulspaces. Janice had also bonded a slime without any martial tradition to guide her; she must have walked a similar path of discovery he was on.

He thought of how she died, attempting to break through to the second tier. Unfortunately, it wasn’t an uncommon story. Cultivation could be tremendously rewarding, but it carried an equal amount of risk.

It also made him wonder about others. Familiars were much less popular than weapon bonds, but slimes were widespread. Surely other groups had tried bonding them.

Typically, people wanted to follow proven paths. Martial paths preferred weapons with a good starting ability to help delve. Were there groups out there somewhere, bonded to slimes? Usually, it took someone growing strong to start a new tradition.

Long-term, Ferris wondered if others back home would want to follow in his footsteps? Could he find a path worth sharing? It was the reason he kept as many details as possible about Jade’s slimespace secret, rather than sell them to Hollowfield. In the grand scheme of things, merit was cheap compared to his goals. Simply a carrot Hollowfield was holding over his head, with plenty of other ways to earn it.

Ferris sighed, looking out at the lake of acid. If he found something truly valuable here, he wanted it to help Bluereef.

Of course, first he’d had to put in the work to uncover this place’s secrets himself. His eyes were drawn to the dome’s damaged area. The locations of the holes had a pattern, and it had stayed the same both times Jade was hurt. That meant Ferris could plant the Grow seed either between the holes, or high up above them.

Frankly, Ferris felt that higher on the dome was safer. If he could get the Grow seed planted above the height of the towers, then it was probably worth it. There was still a risk of an unknown problem or the pattern changing, but he had to take some risks to move forward. The Core Tree survived by being high up, so it felt safe enough.

Although if Ferris found a way to plant the seed up there, he would also need a way to get qi to it every day…

The Jump seed had a similar, obvious, yet more problematic solution. The two mid-sized towers showed a working example: just plant the Jump seed higher up than the waves of acid could reach. Unfortunately, Ferris would need to construct something both tall enough and immune to the acid.

After a while of watching the acid drain, Ferris decided to focus on something he could do: building a fence.

Back in the soulspace, Ferris looked around his Heartfire. The area felt a bit like a home base combined with a campsite, with tools and corpses scattered around. The area around the Heartfire had always been safer than out in the darkness, but obviously not safe enough to protect the oath statue.

Speaking of the statue, Ferris had given it some of his qi that morning, so he walked over to check on it. There was no visible difference in the cracks, but the white chains hanging down were longer than usual after just a day. Hopefully, it only needed more time to repair, and he wasn’t just making the chains stronger.

Grabbing his cooking pot, Ferris considered how he would make the fence. The experiments with melting demon corpses showed that he could create building material without qi, but Ferris was hesitant to try that here. Uncle Winton had focused on the intent of the fence, and anything in the soulspace that had relied on his intention so far worked better with qi.

He decided to add half demon and half qi, using the same method he had for weapons. Hopefully it would be studier than pure qi, yet still hold the intent. After cooking the mixture and letting it cool, Ferris placed a hand in the pot and imagined what he wanted.

Pulling out his hand, he now held a long, thin fence post. It ended in a point like a spear on one side, and had a hole on the other. He planned to place these posts along the edge of his base, and then string a demon-reinforced qi chain between them. It was pretty flimsy as far as fences go, more of a roped-off area, to be honest. He hoped it would carry the intent.

Taking the post to the edge of his camp, Ferris tried to drive it down into the ground, but had some trouble. He ended up going back to the Qi Pool to craft a demon corpse reinforced hammer. Unfortunately, the loop at the top of the post was crushed when he hammered it into the ground. He made a new version of the fence post, moving the hole to the side of a flat top.

With this, he was able to place eight posts in a loose circle around his camp. It wasn’t large enough to contain his Qi Pool, but he got his Heartfire, the oath statue, the core pedestal, his collection of equipment, and stored demon corpses inside.

Next, Ferris wanted to string qi chains between them. He went with the same recipe as for the chains he used to fish up qi chunks from the Digest River, with a slight change in intent when qi forming. Instead of imagining resistance to acid, he focused on designating a forbidden area.

Thankfully, Ferris had plenty of demon corpses stored at his base. The pile had only grown when he was making daily trips to the chain fort. Thankfully, they didn’t appear to ever rot.

Once he had the chain formed, he walked around the circle of fence posts, focusing on his intent. At each post, he would string the thin chain through the loop and imagine the idea of designating the area off limits to demons. When he finished, he didn’t feel anything special. Ferris wasn’t sure if it worked or not. There wasn’t a great way to test it, other than waiting to see if anything was stolen.

The thought reminded him of the cloaked demons stealing the qi in his Air Passage. Maybe he could confirm if the idea worked there, although that was a much larger area to wrap with chains. It would cost him much more qi to build than he would save from their thievery in the short term, but it was an idea to keep in mind.

Ferris checked on the slimespace, but it was still draining, and Jade was still silent over the bond. He guessed that the acid level had dropped by about three-quarters of the way to empty. Ferris decided to stay and watch the acid finish draining. Perhaps something would happen when Jade woke up.

Soon after he perched high on a spire on top of the Heartfire tower, Ferris saw some parts where the ground was becoming visible. Ferris had never noticed it before, but the area where the river drained out was at a low point. There were small hills and valleys inside the dome, but everything sloped downward towards the river. The draining acid made it obvious from his view up on the tower.

What would happen if he dug a hole, or built a dam? Would the acid stay pooled up, or would the ants fix it somehow?

Everywhere the acid receded, ants emerged to resume their normal patrols. As he watched, Jade started to send weak thoughts over the bond.

Ferris’ current working theory was that the grey ants were a spiritual representation of Jade’s thoughts. He had proven Jade could remember for three days straight by keeping the ants alive. If he was right, then what did it mean when Jade was unconscious? Ferris knew there were still ants in the slimespace, they were just underground.

The first day Ferris had bonded Jade, the slime’s mental body had died to a silver fish in the Digest River. Thinking back, Ferris had been in the slimespace when it happened. The fish had darted through Jade’s body and destroyed the core.

The slimespace had rumbled, but there hadn’t been any waves of acid. Ferris knew some abilities would work differently in the soul than in the real world, but Jade had shown their mental body’s healing and eating worked. Anything Jade ate in the soul would appear above the Consume Pool, so why didn’t the damage at that time flood the slimespace?

A particular memory stood out to Ferris: the ants had all retreated underground when Jade died. If they were thoughts, was there meaning to them being above ground? The ants had always swarmed everywhere in the slimespace, but Ferris always thought they were on patrol looking for enemies. Maybe there was a larger purpose to their movements… When the slimespace was flooded, they had to stay underground. Was that related to Jade losing consciousness? He wasn’t sure if he had any way to test it yet.

The acid quickly drained down now that it was reaching the bottom. More and more green dirt was revealed, and the ants continued to pour out to cover it.

Watching the speed of drainage, Ferris felt he was missing something. When Jade ate enough to flood the Digest River, it took hours for the acid level to return to normal levels. Now, the entire slimespace had emptied in just a little longer. There was no way it had all drained through the Digest River. There must be other ways for it to flow out.

The yellow spires, he thought, many of them have entrances to tunnels down low. Is that it?

Ferris remembered that the ants had emerged from the top of the yellow spires, despite the fact that the lower entrances were submerged. There must be separate paths down there.

The flow of acid had slowed down, although the Digest River and Consume Pool were still flooded as high as he had ever seen them. Jade was fully awake, so Ferris left meditation to greet them in the real world.

Jade had been groggy, but as usual, returned to full awareness very quickly. By the time Ferris woke up, there was no sign that anything had been wrong, other than Jade’s smaller size.

He pulled the journal out and checked the slime’s memory. Jade wasn’t able to recall a single number or other word Ferris had given them. It had been less than four hours since he had started the test, and his familiar had forgotten everything not specifically protected. Even the rating game and memories of the magical food Uncle Winton had fed them were gone.

They headed down to the basement, where Jade ate enough straw to return to normal size. Ferris also let the slime finish off the carrots and the axe. Privately, he felt a sense of satisfaction seeing the blade slowly dissolve.

It had hurt his familiar. But in the end, Jade was still here, and it was not.

By then, it was time for dinner, so he took Jade to the common room to eat. They hadn’t been present at the evening meal for the last three days, and they were greeted with more enthusiasm than expected.

“Ferris! Jade!” Brix said as she saw them, a large grin on her face. “Guess what?”

“What? Did something happen?” Ferris asked, a bit taken aback.

Still smiling, she struck a dramatic pose.

“I passed! You are now looking at a fully qualified delver.”
