“I am he who bears the sin of the Tribulation,” Anguis said sorrowfully, bowing his head and putting a broad palm over his heart. “That which you call the Skyfall. Alas, but in our haste to cure Fynneas of his ills, we lost our grip upon his Mantle of Divinity, once it had been torn from him. Truly, what fools us mortals be, that we believe we can so easily trespass into the realm of the divine.”

“An accident?” I asked incredulously, trying to shrug off the grip of Elysael’s goons. I failed, and grit my teeth. “It was all an accident? You dare to say that the corruption of the world around us, the very air we breathe, and the murder of thousands upon thousands of innocent lives was an accident?! That’s not good enough!”

Seemingly unbowed by my shouting, Anguis raised eyes that shone with unshed tears. “It is the greatest sin of my life, Marshal, I do not deny it,” He sighed heavily, shaking his head. “I shall bear it for all of my days. Alas for the innocent dead. Alas for their souls. However…” Anguis looked directly at me then, and I was chilled by the animation in his gaze. “We have succeeded. It was not all for nothing, I promise you that.”

“Yes it was!” I screamed at Anguis at the top of my lungs. “It’s getting worse, you fool! Every day that we waste in this pointless fighting the Skyfall grows stronger! We only have a few goddamned months before it overwhelms wards! All wards, everywhere! And then everyone on Vereden dies!”

Utter.

Silence.

The only thing that could be heard was my own heavy breathing, as I stood panting. Gradually, I became aware of the depths of the quiet. Looking around, I couldn’t help but wince.

Every single eye, from the hundreds who had gathered to watch this meeting, was now on me. The looks in all of them were deeply, deeply horrified.

This…wasn’t exactly information I had shared with many other people. Alveron had told me this fact when we had recruited his people, and I had...made the decision to keep it close to my chest. I hadn’t told the rest of the Captains, and I hadn’t told the rest of the expedition. Sylvia was the only person I had trusted enough to share it with, and she had agreed with me.

I sure as hell hadn’t told any of the Dwarves.

Including Elysael.

“Nathan…” I heard a hoarse voice say from behind me. “How…how do you…”

Fuck.

Why had I said that?! It wasn’t like me to just blurt out sensitive information like this! I was trained better than that! I had been a spy goddamnit! I even had a fucking mental Skill that helped to control my emotional state. My Middle Lattice was just as confused, because he had been monitoring my emotions. But still…

How…why…how had I grown so angry to just…announce that in front of everyone?

I made to turn around and face the music from Elysael, before I was stopped.

By the sound of Anguis tutting from his side of the table. Gritting my teeth, I turned to face the Prince of Vittolia, standing right back in front of the scroll. There was an almost disappointed look on his face.

“Oh, Marshal,” Anguis sighed, shaking his head. “Who told you that? Was it him?” He said, flicking a dismissive hand over at the downcast Alveron. The Paragon had yet to even move after the lambasting he’d received from Anguis.

Something…something about that struck me as strange, but I couldn’t figure out what.

“It’s not true, Marshal,” Anguis said patiently. “There are no wards that are in danger of falling. The ‘corruption’, as you call it, is not getting worse. Rather…it’s getting better. Because, you see, in spite of my misstep…we’re still succeeding.”

Anguis graced the world with a nearly benevolent smile. It made my back crawl to see it, but I was one of the few. The world was, in turn, hanging on his only word.

“There is power building up within Fynneas’s mantle, yes,” He announced proudly. “But it is not the cursed energy of the Chaos gods who drove him mad. It is not as Alveron says. Instead, the proper Divinity of the God of Freedom is growing stronger. It is shaking off the chains that bind it, and soon, very soon, it will have been cleansed. It is serendipitous indeed that our accident shall soon result in a reborn god, something that our home of Vereden so deeply needs. The natural energies of our world are cleansing the Mantle of madness, and soon, we shall all enter into a true Golden Age, standing side by side with the only god left who can aid us. Truly…we are blessed.”

Murmuring broke out once more. To my dismay, there was an element of excitement to it.

Desperate, I started looking around wildly, and my fear only grew when I found closed-off faces greeting me. Elysael looked colder than I had ever seen her, and even my Captains…

Even my Captains looked like they were furious with me.

“What does that matter?” I spit out. “Even if you manage to cure Fynneas, it will never make up for the thousands of lives you’ve taken by inflicting this on us.” Copying him, I flung out a dismissive hand to point at the sky, and the bars of magenta lightning that streaked across it. “How many families have you broken with your gambit, Anguis?”

“A true tragedy, Marshal, I cannot argue with that,” The Lord of House Savoy agreed sorrowfully, sighing. But then…

His eyes sharpened, as did his smile.

“But a temporary one, at best.”

At first, I didn’t understand what he meant. How could mass-death be temporary?

And then I understood.

“You can’t possibly expect us to believe…” I said with a shaky laugh. “That you can bring them all back, can you? We’re not that stupid.”

I expected at least a little bit of support from the audience at that, because Anguis’s claim was outright farcical.

Except…

I received the opposite.

Someone from the audience, one of the Dwarven soldiers, from the sound of his voice, called out from the battlements. “Do you speak true, Lord Savoy?” He said, his raised voice filled with hope. “Can the Mad God truly resurrect the dead?”

A handful of similar questions joined the first one, until I raised my voice to speak over them

“Of course he can’t!” I shouted through the noise, though it didn’t seem to stop. People were getting too excited at just the idea of true resurrection. “It’s not possible!”

“On the contrary, Marshal!” Anguis bellowed, his voice actually cutting through the noise. The audience quieted down, but seemed to hang on his every word with bated breath. “He can! Every soul that has died to what the good Marshal has termed ‘corruption’ now resides within the Mantle itself! Even now, every soul taken by this accident awaits us above! Safe and sound! Fynneas has personally promised me that when his Mantle has been truly cleansed, he will exert his full will and re-embody them once more! My friends…I promise you.” He paused, looking around at all of the people gathered to watch. To my alarm, more than one of them were starting to sob in place at the promise I was positive was false. “You will be reunited with your loved ones, and soon. Only a matter of months.”

I shook my head slowly, stunned that people were starting to fall for this. Lost, I twisted my head around to stare at Elysael. “Ely, please, say something. These are your people, they might actually listen to you.”

To my shock, there was a grimace on the female Dwarf’s face. “I…” She started, and then stopped. “I’m not…positive Lord Savoy is lying. There are…sporadic reports, in the historical record, about what the old gods were truly capable of. There…might have been a few references to particularly favored warriors being brought back from beyond the veil. I cannot say with absolute accuracy that Fynneas could not do this thing.”

I started breathing heavily and spun in place to look at someone else. If Elysael couldn’t help me, maybe he could. “Alveron!” I reached out to grasp his shoulders, shaking him. “You were there! You knew the gods, you knew Fynneas! You would know that this isn’t possible! You have to say something!”

The tired, haggard-looking old Elf raised his watery eyes to stare at me for a moment before lowering them. “I cannot,” He whispered brokenly. “Only the gods knew the full breadth of their powers. The Florens is right. There were…whispers, once upon a time, that they could grant new life to the fallen. I never witnessed this feat for myself, but…”

I staggered back as Anguis laughed joyfully. “You see, my good people!” He crowed, to a disturbing chorus of cheers from both his own people, as well as the watchers from the walls. “Even Gréim Coill himself cannot deny the power of his grandsire! The possibility of him!”

I clenched my fists and thought wildly for a moment. “How…” I croaked out weakly. “How can we even know you’re telling the truth!” I said, my voice growing stronger with every word. I stabbed a finger towards Anguis in accusation. “This could all be a trick! A ruse, a speech you’ve prepared to lay doubts before battle!”

The response I got from Anguis caused my stomach to sink.

A sympathetic smile crossed his face. “Truly, Marshal, I understand,” He said kindly. “There is a great deal of antipathy between us, largely created because of my monstrous son. I understand. The only thing that could possibly convince you is proof. Luckily…I’ve brought some with me.” Anguis turned away from the table and its scroll, and turned to face the closest ship, floating just off of it in the river. “Leonard, it’s time. Please, bring the crown so the Prince and the Marshal can inspect it.”

What? Leonard?

But the only Leonard I knew of…it couldn’t be…

Right?

A figure emerged onto the deck of the ship, and my heart sank in my chest.

I recognized that staff. Not only that, but the style and material it was made from gave me a sense of vertigo, from how obvious it all seemed.

A tall, slender spire of smoky crystal was being carried in one hand by the human-sized figure, three splines extending from the top of it, clad in a black, hooded robe form head to toe. Around the man’s shoulders hung an intricate, embroidered cloak of green and gold, signifying his allegiance to House Savoy.

In his free, hand, however…

He carried a wooden box absolutely covered in runes. The carved sigils littered the surface of the enclosure to the point tthat here wasn’t an inch of it that wasn’t free of them. But they only seemed partially effective at best.

Even from where I stood, I could feel the power emanating from the box. Whatever was in there…it was strong. Strong enough to raise the hairs on the back of my neck.

It was…it was almost like a mantle, honestly. Only instead of an actual expression that could be perceived, it was just an aura of pure power.

I had a bad feeling about this. It only got worse when the man jumped the distance of the river from his place on the boat to alight onto the docks. He didn’t even make a noise as he touched down.

The entire port was silent as the man slowly approached Anguis, carrying the ominous box in one hand like it was a bomb about to go off. As he drew closer, the wan light of the Skyfall lit on his face just enough that I could see underneath his hood.

Pale, crystalline blue eyes met mine as the man gingerly set the box upon the negotiation table. As a stray lock of long, platinum blonde hair slipped from underneath the hood, the man let a single, smug smirk of recognition cross his aristocratic lips. I drew a short, shuddering breath as my worst fears were realized.

So this was where he had gone.

Leonard Ashran, the once former warden of Caer Drarrow. And judging by the look he’d shot me, he still remembered me for my part in toppling him from his position.

Fuck.

Anguis didn’t miss the interaction, but other than a lightning-quick smirk, he didn’t comment on it. Instead, he merely picked up the box himself and approached the wooden figure of Alveron with it held in both hands, as if he was presenting an award.

It felt like the world was holding its breath as the Prince of Savoy stopped in front of the Prince of Lasgach. “Well, oh Heir of Fynneas? You are the only person here who can confirm if what I hold in my hands is legitimate. If it’s real…then every word I’ve spoken is the truth. Because this could not be separated from the God of Freedom with anything less than his explicit blessing. Behold!” Anguis cried, cracking the lid of the box.

A fell violet light began to leak from within the box, and with it came a sensation.

“The Crown of Coillmhòr! The very crown granted to him, upon his ascension as the first King of Lasgach so long ago!”

I…could barely even describe it, much less think. It was an oppressive feeling, not unlike as if I were an ant being beheld by a giant. I was nothing before the scope of this power. For all of my strange, System granted might as a Precursor, it was like comparing a match to a forest fire. I'd only ever felt a fraction of this might before...

And that had been when I had been taunted by an arrogant 'goddess' between worlds, right after I'd slain Rhazal.

The angle was wrong for me to actually be able to see the contents, but just right for Alveron to. Fighting through the feeling of the supposed crown’s aura, I watched as Alveron studied whatever was in the box. The entire world was still and silent as his tired, defeated eyes examined it in detail.

Finally, Alveron…

Bowed his head, and we all heard his whispered verdict. “It’s real…”

I felt the world fall out from under me, as plated Dwarven hands fell upon my shoulders once again. I didn’t even react as I heard a cold-voiced Elysael speak from behind me.

“Guards, please see Marshal Hart and Prince Alveron to the Palace. A number of things must be re-examined, while Prince Anguis and I continue our…dialogue.”

Neither Alveron nor I fought back as the squad of Corvid Knights began to lead us away from the docks. The revelations of the last hour may have overwhelmed me, but I think they had shattered him. Nobody spoke in our defense as we were marched up and away, to await our fate in the Palace of Rhoscara.

The last thing I saw before I disappeared into the streets was the alarmed, sapphire gaze of Sylvia watching me go.

And then I looked away.
