Magma parted before her will like lovers desperate for her touch, spiraling upward from the volcano's heart to caress her suspended form.

Destruction floated above the churning caldera, her molten flesh pulsing with inner fire, her hair flowing like ribbons of liquid flame. The hellish heat that would have vaporized even the mightiest mortal champion merely complemented her nature, feeding the rivers of lava that coursed through her veins. The magma recognized its kin—for she was partially composed of it, her divine essence inseparable from the elements she commanded. Rivers of molten rock twisted around her in complex orbits, some flowing directly into her body, others forming a constellation of fiery tendrils that merged and separated from her form with each beat of her divine heart.

She floated above the inferno, legs crossed, back straight, palms resting on her knees as if she were merely a mortal seeking inner peace. Nothing could be further from the truth. The magma around her responded to emotions no meditation could tame, tracing ancient runes that spiraled up her arms and across her collarbones, marking her for what she truly was—a goddess preparing for war.

This was not meditation. This was preparation for slaughter.

Destruction inhaled deeply, drawing in power until it filled her lungs and threatened to burst through her ribcage. The Descent awaited, and she would not approach it with anything less than perfect readiness. Three trials stood between her and the forging of Calamity—her most lethal creation, the blade that would finally end the pantheon and bring glorious reckoning upon those who had wronged her.

The first trial required confronting failure. Her failure. The memory she'd buried deepest, wrapped in layers of rage and sealed away beneath mountains of vengeance.

Destruction opened her eyes, and the crimson light within them cut through the volcanic haze like twin suns born of wrath.

"COME," she commanded the void. "SHOW ME WHAT I NEED TO SEE."

The churning lake of magma below her stilled momentarily, then began to spiral downward, opening an abyssal tunnel that plunged deep into the earth's core. Black thorns erupted from the tunnel walls—branches of the Oakthorn that had once imprisoned her, now manifestations of her Domain's power. The thorns twisted and grew, forming a spiraling staircase of obsidian and magma that led into darkness.

The Descent. The path to her own trials.

Destruction lowered herself until her feet touched the first step. Magma swirled around her ankles, recognizing its mistress, while the Oakthorn thorns retracted slightly at her approach—both welcoming and warning.

How strange, she thought, that after breaking free from godly imprisonment, she might find her end here, within her own Domain. The irony wasn't lost on her. The mighty Destruction, at last unchained, only to be broken by her own power's judgment. These trials would test not just her strength but her resolve—her ability to face her own failings.

What an anticlimactic end it would be, to die not by a sibling's hand but by her own memories.

Still, one step at a time. Each barefoot placement sending ripples of power through the volcanic tunnel. The walls pulsed with her heartbeat as she descended deeper, the air growing thick with potential and memory.

She stepped into the vortex without hesitation. The world dissolved, reconstructed, and suddenly—

Destruction stood in a meadow of impossible beauty. Wildflowers nodded lazily in a breeze that carried the scent of summer and hope. The sky stretched above her, a perfect azure canvas painted by a master who knew just how much blue mortal hearts could bear before breaking.

She blinked, disoriented. Her body felt... different. Lighter somehow, unmarred by centuries of plotting. Her hands—she stared at them—bore no scars, no calluses of vengeance. Her heart beat with a rhythm almost forgotten, one that still believed in reconciliation.

With a jolt, she realized what had happened. She wasn't observing this memory. She was living it. Again. Current consciousness trapped in past flesh, like a cosmic joke played by her own power. A test, then, and not merely a witnessing.

The sound of approaching footsteps pulled her from her thoughts. She turned and there, walking toward her with sunlight creating a halo around golden hair, was Creation.

Her sister. Her betrayer. Her doom.

Yet in this moment, with birds singing overhead and butterflies dancing between them, Destruction could almost forget what she knew would come. Almost.

"I WASN'T SURE YOU'D COME," Creation said, her voice like wind chimes carried on summer air.

Destruction felt her past self's response rise to her lips, but this time, she understood the gravity of every word. "I WILL ALWAYS COME WHEN YOU CALL." The truth of it stung worse than any lie.

They stood together in the center of the meadow, two goddesses who had once crafted worlds side by side. A butterfly landed on Creation's shoulder, its wings pulsing with colors they had designed together in the first days of everything.

"I HATE ALL THAT HAS COME TO STAND BETWEEN US," Creation murmured, reaching out to touch Destruction's cheek with fingers that felt like morning dew. "I WISH FOR THOSE ELDER DAYS, BACK WHEN WE CALLED EACH OTHER BY OUR TRUE NAMES."

Destruction felt herself lean into the touch, and despite everything—despite knowing exactly how this would end—she couldn't help the wave of longing that crashed through her. For a sister. For family. For trust. And beneath it all, a hollow absence she couldn't quite name—a shadow of something forgotten, someone missing from their trinity.

"I'M TIRED, CREATION," she admitted, the words flowing from some well of honesty she'd thought long dry. "TIRED OF FIGHTING. TIRED OF DESTROYING WHAT YOU CREATE. TIRED OF THIS ENDLESS CYCLE."

The confession hung between them like a fragile thing newly born, still wet with possibility.

"I MISS YOU," Creation said, taking Destruction's hands in her own. "I MISS US."

And there it was—a flicker in Creation's eyes. Something not quite right. Something Destruction's past self had missed, but her current consciousness caught like a spark before flame.

Still, the script demanded to be followed.

"THEN LET'S END IT. HERE. NOW." Destruction's voice cracked with emotion that wasn't entirely feigned. "I DON'T CARE WHO STARTED IT ANYMORE. I'M SORRY FOR MY ROLE IN OUR DIVISION. SORRY FOR EVERY SLIGHT, EVERY BATTLE, EVERY PETTY REVENGE."

The words dissipated in the gentle breeze, drifting away like seeds that would never take root. Creation's smile shifted, almost imperceptibly. The butterfly on her shoulder took flight, as if sensing the change in atmospheric pressure that precedes a storm.

"OH, MY DEAR SISTER," Creation said, her voice transforming from chimes to crystal shards. "YOU ALWAYS WERE THE MOST GULLIBLE OF US."

The air behind Creation shimmered, reality folding back on itself like a page turned too quickly. A portal opened, and through it stepped Life—resplendent in golden armor, his sword already drawn, his beauty as terrible as a solar flare.

"RIGHT ON TIME," Creation said, stepping back, releasing Destruction's hands as if they had suddenly become distasteful to touch.

Betrayal was a curious sensation—even when expected, it still landed like a physical blow. Destruction felt her body react before her mind could catch up, muscles tensing, power gathering beneath her skin.

"YOU CONNIVING BITCH," she spat, the words tasting like ash.

"Now, now," Life chided, circling to Destruction's left while Creation moved right. "Is that any way to speak to the goddess who's about to save you from yourself?"

Destruction drew herself up, power gathering around her fists. She knew what came next. She knew Death would not come. Yet still, she found herself scanning the horizon, waiting for the lover who had promised to stand with her.

"DEATH!" she called, the desperation in her voice all too real despite her foreknowledge. "BELOVED! I NEED YOU NOW!"

Silence answered, broken only by Life's laughter—a sound like bells at a funeral.

"Death has better things to do than die alongside a barren whore," Life said, his sword catching the sunlight and throwing it back a thousandfold. "Besides, my brother always did have a talent for making promises he never intended to keep."

Even knowing the truth, the abandonment cut fresh. Destruction felt her power surge in response to her pain—not the controlled power she would one day master, but raw, primal energy flooding her system. The air around her crackled as storm clouds materialized above the once-perfect meadow. Her hands ignited, flames dancing across her skin before coalescing into a blade of pure volcanic glass, its edge impossibly sharp, its core still molten and dripping fire onto the grass.

"YOU WILL REGRET THIS," she snarled, the ground trembling beneath her fury.

“I’m quite sure we won’t,” Life promised with a wicked smile others would’ve found dashing.

The god struck first, his sword a golden arc aimed at Destruction's head. She twisted, feeling the blade sing past her ear close enough to sever a lock of her flame-hair. The severed strand crystallized mid-air, turned to obsidian, and shattered when it hit the ground. She countered with a sweeping slash that would have bisected a mountain, the air itself burning in the blade's wake.

Life's armor—initially gleaming gold—rippled and transformed. As her blade made contact, the metal flowed like living tissue, sprouting scales that channeled her heat away from his body. The scales reddened but held, dispersing her attack's energy through countless micro-adjustments in their structure.

"Evolution, dear Destruction," Life taunted, his perfect face wearing an infuriating smile. "Adaptation. The very essence of my being. I really thought you would’ve learned since the last time our blades clashed. I’m disappointed in you."

Creation circled them, hands weaving complex patterns that left traces of golden light in the air—living blueprints, technical schematics manifesting directly into reality. The ground beneath Destruction's feet transformed, metamorphosing from solid earth into quicksand composed of countless microscopic mechanisms that clutched at her ankles with mechanical precision.

Destruction roared in frustration, the sky answering her call. A lightning bolt crashed down, striking her blade and supercharging it until it glowed white-hot. She slammed it into the mechanized quicksand, the heat instantly vitrifying the trap into glass.

"DEATH!" she called again, her voice cracking with desperation as she shattered the glass prison with a stomp of her foot and lunged at Life. "WE HAD A PACT!"

For a heartbeat, she thought she had him. Her superheated blade carved through the air toward Life's neck—but at the last second, he shifted. Not a dodge, but a transformation. The flesh of his shoulder liquefied and reformed around her blade, capturing it within his body.

He grinned, and even in pain, it was beautiful. "Ooh, this is a new one," he breathed, as if savoring the novelty of her assault. "I'll adapt to this too."

Before she could withdraw, his sword—elongated now, more whip than blade—coiled around and plunged into her shoulder. The wound was precise, surgical, targeting the joint where divine power flowed most abundantly. Divine blood spattered the wildflowers, turning them to ash where it landed.

"He lied," Life said happily, twisting the blade inside her. "As we all do. It's our nature, or did you forget that too, you childless wench?"

Destruction screamed, not in pain but in rage, and the sky answered again. Hurricane winds ripped across the meadow, tearing flowers from the ground and sending them spiraling into the darkening sky. She channeled raw volcanic heat through her captured blade, forcing Life to release it as even his adaptive flesh began to char.

She broke free, leaping backward, and raised her hands toward the heavens. The storm intensified, funneling down into a tornado of primal destruction that she directed straight at Life. It was power in its most elemental form—the power to unmake, to return complexity to simplicity, order to chaos.

Creation's hands flashed, golden light spilling from her fingertips as she crafted a gleaming apparatus in the tornado's path—a marvel of engineering that sprang into existence fully formed. The device unfurled silver-gold vanes that caught the cyclone's energy, channeling and dispersing it harmlessly into the earth.

"ALWAYS SO BRUTISH, SISTER," Creation chided, her fingers never ceasing their intricate dance. "ALL FORCE, NO FINESSE. ARE YOU REALLY THIS USELESS WITHOUT YOUR BIG, BAD, LOVER AT YOUR SIDE?”

Life had already recovered, his armor evolving once more. Where it had been metallic, now it glistened with moisture—thousands of tiny secretion glands that produced a cooling gel to counter Destruction's heat. His sword shortened, thickened, sprouting barbs along its edge.

Destruction didn't hesitate. She slammed her fists into the ground, sending a ripple of seismic force outward that split the earth beneath them. Magma surged upward through the fissure, a pillar of liquid stone that she shaped with her will into a tsunami of fire.

Creation sighed, as if disappointed by her sister's predictability. Her hands sketched complex arcane formulas in the air, and a network of crystal conduits sprouted from the ground, intercepting the magma flow and redirecting it in a perfect circle—a ring of fire that now imprisoned Destruction rather than threatened her enemies.

"YOU FORGET THAT EVEN IN DESTRUCTION, THERE ARE PATTERNS," Creation said, almost sadly. "I’M STARTING TO THINK THAT YOU WANT US TO HURT YOU. I WOULDN’T BE SURPRISED. YOU ALWAYS ENJOYED THE PAIN."

Still, Destruction fought with everything she had. She froze the air around Life, attempting to trap him in a prison of absolute zero temperature. For a moment, his movements slowed as frost formed across his perfect features—but then his flesh pulsed, generating internal heat through rapid cellular vibration. The ice melted, steamed, and vanished.

For one glorious moment, Destruction broke through both their defenses. She feinted with another earthquake, drawing Creation's attention to the ground while she gathered the storm's electrical potential into her palm. When Creation deployed her countermeasure—an elaborate grounding system of crystal rods—Destruction was already lunging past it, straight at Life.

Her obsidian blade, now charged with enough electricity to glass a desert, scored a deep line across Life's perfect face. Golden ichor spilled from the wound, glowing with divine power.

Life touched the cut, his expression transforming from righteousness to something primitive and savage. The wound—unlike all her previous attacks—didn't immediately heal. The scar remained, a black line across golden perfection.

"You dare?" Life hissed, his voice no longer melodious but guttural, primal. "You dare mark me permanently?"

The real torture began then. Life's strategy shifted from defense to punishment. His blade transformed with each strike, becoming exactly what would cause maximum pain with minimum lethality. First a flail that shattered her right kneecap, then a serrated edge that stripped flesh from bone along her left arm. Next a barbed hook that caught her cheek and tore downward, laying her face open to the bone.

Creation contributed with cold efficiency, crafting restraints that adapted to Destruction's every attempt at escape. Crystal manacles tightened around her limbs with each struggle. A collar constricted her throat whenever her power surged. But the worst was the crown of thorns that dug deeper into her scalp. It would taunt her for the next five thousand years, even as it tore away at her face.

Where Creation's devices held her immobile, Life's weapons found her flesh. Not random attacks but a methodical dismantling—a study in divine anatomy conducted with surgical precision and sadistic curiosity.

"IS THIS WHAT HE PROMISED YOU?" Destruction screamed as Life's blade found the gap between her ribs, puncturing a lung. Blood bubbled on her lips as she glared at Creation. "TO HELP YOU TORTURE YOUR OWN SISTER? YOUR OWN BLOOD?"

Creation's expression flickered—not with remorse but with something more complex. A shadow of remembrance, perhaps, of another sibling lost. Of blood spilled by both their hands. But the moment passed, replaced by cold determination.

“BLOOD MEANS NOTHING," Creation replied, fingers sketching new horrors in the air. "POWER IS EVERYTHING. YOU TAUGHT ME THAT."

Life circled Destruction like a predator savoring its prey's final moments. His beauty had transformed into something terrible—his perfect features now arranged in an expression of scientific fascination as he cataloged her pain, her resilience, her breaking points. Each new wound taught him something, each scream added to his understanding.

"Such fascinating adaptations," he murmured, watching her blood steam where it hit the ground. "Your body attempts to heal even as I devise new ways to break it. What a beautiful dance, we’re sharing."

The world collapsed into a universe of pain. Destruction fought until her muscles tore from bone. Screamed until her voice shattered. Called for Death until her throat produced nothing but bloody whispers.

"DEATH... WHERE ARE YOU? WE HAD A PACT! A PACT!"

Blood pooled beneath her as she finally collapsed to her knees, her volcanic blade long since shattered, her storm powers exhausted, her body broken. Life stood over her, his sword—now a perfect instrument of execution—raised for a killing blow. His armor had evolved into something resplendent, having incorporated aspects of each attack she had managed to land. Her own power, turned against her, absorbed and adapted by her enemy.

"DON'T," Creation said, placing a restraining hand on Life's arm. "WE NEED HER ALIVE."

"Why?" Life demanded, his golden eyes narrowed. "She's too dangerous to keep."

"BECAUSE DEATH WOULD BE A MERCY AFTER WHAT SHE’S DONE," Creation replied, her voice cold. "AND SHE DESERVES NONE. BESIDES, THIS WAY WE CAN TRULY BE RID OF HER."

“You’re right, my love. Let’s make sure her she suffers,” Life replied, and Destruction’s body remembered the horrors he’d unleash next.

Darkness swept in, swallowing the meadow, the sky, the bloodied wildflowers. When Destruction's vision cleared, she lay on cold black stone, ancient chains binding her limbs, sapping her power and keeping her weak. She was back. Her breath came quickly. She was back. She was—

The goddess lifted her head, blood matting her hair, one eye swollen shut, lips split and bleeding. As understanding dawned, she felt something break inside her—something vital, something that could never be repaired.

"I WILL KILL YOU ALL!" she howled into the darkness. "EVERY GOD. EVERY GODDESS. I WILL TEAR DOWN THE HEAVENS AND SCATTER YOUR ESSENCE ACROSS THE VOID!"

The chains rattled with her fury, the walls absorbed her screams, and the darkness—the absolute darkness—swallowed her whole.

Then, nothing.

Destruction crashed back into her body with the force of a dying star.

The volcanic tunnel materialized around her, but she couldn't stand—couldn't even float. Her molten form slammed against the obsidian staircase, fragments of her essence spattering across the black thorns. Where drops of her being touched the Oakthorn branches, they ignited, burning with a fire so dark it seemed to consume light rather than emit it.

She clawed at the stairs, her fingers leaving trenches in the stone. Every movement sent shockwaves of agony through her system. Her hair flickered erratically between solid and liquid flame, parts of it extinguishing entirely before reigniting. The magma within her veins had cooled in places, creating fault lines of black obsidian across her skin that cracked with each labored breath.

And yet—this physical torment was merely the outward manifestation of deeper wounds. Inside, her magical pathways smoldered. The trial had forced her to relive not just the memory but the power drain of the Oakthorn chains. Her divine essence had remembered what it was to be back there, to be weak, to be nothing.

The Descent forced her to remember the cost of her mercy. Of her compassion. She would not make that mistake again. It was bitter too, she realized. Her own power was outraged at what they’d done. Its mistress had been absent for far too long.

She looked down the tunnel.

"This..." she gasped, her voice barely a whisper where it had once commanded universes, "was only the first."

The weight of that realization pressed down on her like the mountain above. Two more trials awaited. Two more remnants to claim. If the first—reputedly the easiest—had nearly destroyed her...

A bitter laugh escaped her cracked lips. How fitting it would be, to have escaped eons of imprisonment only to die during her own rebirth. To be slain not by her siblings but by her own memories. Her own power's judgment.

Had she come this far, planned this intricately, only to fail now?

Her gaze fell upon her right hand, still clenched in a fist so tight that magma leaked between her fingers. Slowly, with deliberate focus, she uncurled her fingers.

There it lay—a shard of crimson crystal pulsing with an inner light that mirrored her own heartbeat. The first Remnant, crystallized from her pain and betrayal. Even in her weakened state, she could feel its raw potential. This single fragment contained more power than all the remnants she had scattered across the mortal world combined. And it was merely the weakest of the three she would forge into Calamity.

A grin split her face, reopening wounds that leaked molten gold.

"WORTH IT," she croaked, closing her fingers around the Remnant once more. Its edges cut into her palm, but pain was an old friend. Pain was power. Betrayal was purpose.

With effort that would have killed a lesser being, Destruction pulled herself upright. Her legs shook beneath her, the obsidian cracks in her skin widening before magma rushed to fill them. The volcano above rumbled in concern, sensing its mistress's distress.

"TWO MORE," she whispered, clutching the Remnant to her heart. "GIVE ME THE STRENGTH FOR TWO MORE."

She took one step down the spiral staircase, then another, descending deeper rather than ascending toward escape. The Descent would not release her until all three trials were complete—or until she perished in the attempt. The black thorns of the Oakthorn seemed to watch her, neither retreating nor advancing, merely observing her choice to continue.

With each downward step, the tunnel changed subtly. The air grew thinner yet somehow heavier with meaning. Colors that had no names in the mortal realm began to seep from the walls. The boundaries between what was real and what was memory blurred at the edges, like parchment touched by flame. The deeper she went, the more the physical laws of her volcano gave way to the fluid reality of somewhere... else.

The magma below no longer merely flowed—it whispered. Ancient secrets that even she had forgotten. The obsidian cracks in her skin began to seal, not with her own power but with something more fundamental—the raw stuff of existence that predated even gods.

By the time she reached the landing between trials, her strength had partially returned, though exhaustion still clung to her like morning mist. The Remnant pulsed against her chest, singing with potential, as if eager to meet its brothers.

Ahead, the tunnel spiraled down further, disappearing into darkness tinged with colors no mortal eye could perceive. Behind her, the way back had sealed itself, the tunnel collapsing into nothingness. Only forward remained.

"I HAVE NOT FORGOTTEN," she declared, her voice echoing strangely in this threshold space. "I HAVE NOT FORGIVEN."

Something answered—not the volcano, but a deeper tremor that originated from nowhere physical. A recognition from the fabric of reality itself.

Two more trials. Two more days. If she survived, Calamity would be reborn, and her vengeance could finally begin in earnest.

The tunnel called to her, a siren song of pain yet to come. Destruction tucked the Remnant away in a pocket of reality only she could access. She would rest here, at this threshold, gathering her strength for the second trial.

Tomorrow would bring fresh agony. For now, she needed to recover, to prepare.

To remember why the pain was worth it.
