Jack got up a bit earlier so as to see a certain someone before he left and have breakfast with them.

“Wish I could go with you,” Screamer said as she chewed on some eggs slowly and studied a piece of round sausage speared on her fork, twirling it slowly. “Increased freedoms in this joint pale in comparison to the thought of being out there again.”

They were in a small cafeteria area very much emblematic of the psyche ward that it was. It was functional, slightly pleasant, and the tables were bolted down. A handful of patients were around, and an equal number of orderlies were ready for anything. A few seemed a tad off, but it was quite calm at the moment.

Screamer perhaps looked a bit out of place now. She looked tidy, well-kept, and gave all the signs of a young woman looking after herself impeccably, as if she’d reapplied military and/or general feminine discipline to a greater degree with each day of her confinement. If she wasn’t rather slight and appearing too youthful, she’d look more like an orderly than a patient.

Jack nodded in sympathy as he stuffed his face with rather generic, yet absolutely divine breakfast food. They even had Infused syrup for the pancakes. He glanced around and said, “I’m glad you’re at least around people again. The semblance of ‘the public,’ as it were. I hope you’re making friends.”

Screamer snorted as she took a nibble out of the disk-like sausage. “Yeah, I’ve got so many I can relate to here.” Looking to the side, she shrugged and replied, “Wide-eyes Jerry ain’t so bad, I guess. He nods a lot more than he talks, but he’s a good listener.”

Jack winced, feeling guilty he’d not be able to visit her all over again, though she’d claimed a couple others had visited her recently. “That’s good.” He covered his awkwardness by eating more.

Screamer glanced at him and smiled faintly. “I’ll be fine, Jack.” She drummed her knuckles. “Stable as this table. Thanks for caring, though. Really.”

Jack shook his head, slightly exasperated. “You don’t have to keep thanking me for that; that’s just what-”

“What friends do, I know. I’m doing it anyway, probably forever, and you can’t stop me.”

Jack frowned at her, and she just grinned smugly and bit into more of her sausage. Jack scoffed. “Tch! Brats, I tell ya, I’m surrounded by brats…”

“Mmn. That’s how Momma makes us. I hope one of them became your girlfriend by now. That’s the only way to get a return on bratiness. You active service boys always have them, anyway. Heck, by your age, most have bratty wives.”

Jack allowed a chuckle at that. He almost segued into mentioning ‘Ira’ or some vague facsimile, but the reality of things was a bit classified by extension, so he hesitated. Then he had a sudden thought that made him hesitate for a whole other reason.

Wait, shit, did she mention that because-? She doesn’t have feelings for me, does she?

Jack’s brain scrambled for a response at that point, and just a bit too late. He cleared his throat and said, “Ah, right, yeah. Maybe I should just get hitched before I leave, just to be safe.”

Screamer nodded slowly, gesturing with a fork with raised eyebrows. “Lindsay, Jack. Perfect target for a proposal, eh?” The dark sarcasm was obvious. “Top tier brat, too.”

That elicited a wince and disapproving groan from Jack. It was just a bit too dark to think about, after what had happened to Lindsay and her fiancé. Screamer evidently knew about it.

“Sorry,” Screamer said quickly, embarrassed. “Should’ve kept that intrusive thought inside.”

“Probably so, yeah. But it does give me the excuse to change the subject, so it's not all bad.”

Screamer saluted with her milk carton and said, “Fair,” before drinking.

Naaah. She doesn’t have feelings; she just blurts out everything randomly, still. Our energy isn’t like that. Just in case it's in the back of her mind or something, though, I’m definitely not mentioning a ‘girlfriend’ before leaving her here for who knows how long…

Jack’s delay in offering a new subject made Screamer break the silence instead. “Being cooped up in here just makes it impossible to get normal. I need” — and she gestured with a wave of a hand — “out. Amongst my kind, and even Joe Shmoe public a little. Anything but this, or I’ll just stay crazy and off.”

“Well, being a little crazy and off is okay,” Jack replied. “Everyone is.”

Screamer sniffed derisively as she chewed on a small bite of eggs. “You’re not at all. Total Normie McNormalson.”

Jack gave her a deadpan glare. “Now you’re just insulting me.”

She grinned and shrugged. “The truth is the truth.”

“Well, Normie McNormalson will continue to advise you to be patient, listen, and cooperate with the doctors while he’s away. That’s the best chance to get what you want. There is no other way through the process but through it. Keep your eye on the prize and think before you speak or act. Make a habit of breathing deeply before answering questions. Demonstrate sober-mindedness through thoughtfulness. Don’t just say what they want to hear; meditate on what they want you to be and speak as that evolving, learning person. That includes asking questions, especially ones that feel like follow-ups to steps you’ve already taken. Swallow your animosity and engage with their precious process. Leave any mockers of the process in the dust. They’re not going anywhere. You are.”

Screamer studied him as she listened, and nodded slightly at different points. “I think that might actually be some good advice.”

“I guess that’s what mere normies do, huh?”

“Alright, Jack, get over it — I wasn’t serious. You big baby…”

Jack grinned as he crammed in the food rapidly disappearing from his plate. “Am not! You are…”

Without any ceremony, the entirety of the team bound for the borderlands filed into smaller transports from the tower, bound for a flying fortress and then quickly transferred to larger transports. The first leg would be ultra-fast by supersonic ‘zipjets’ almost entirely focused on hyper-accelerating direct levitation.

They didn’t look much different from sleek passenger jets, but anyone with a trained eye would note their models were older than the modern ones. There was technically no need to update levitation vessels, so they were usually slightly behind the times when it came to their ‘backup’ systems.

They’d cross something just under half of Memorial territory at around a thousand kilometers in a relative supersonic blink, making for another aerial platform at the edge of their operational distances. From there, they’d transfer to slower, conventional, ultra-reliable tilt-rotor vessels for the rest of the trip to the coastline.

The levitational jetliner experience was all easy-peasy and fairly comfortable, at least for Jack. Various others looked nervous. More nervous than anyone was Neex — or Ira, as she was sticking to the disguise for much of the trip before they were descending down to her people’s territory under the sea.

Naturally, she was seated next to him, and hugged his arm tightly at the first motions of the liftoff, even though they were as gentle as could be.

Jack patted her arm and leaned over to say, “You can trust this vessel, babe. It’s all Memorial processes in this case.”

Ira nodded quickly, not convinced and not letting go of his arm. She then blinked suddenly. “Babe?”

“Oh, sorry — a term of endearment, but I-”

“I know!” She leaned her head on his shoulder and sighed. “It’s quite fine. More than. Babe. You will continue to use this often, and me, too. Babe.” She giggled.

Jack grinned. “You got it, babe…”

Bo was right across from him over the central aisle, and he felt her gaze, so he had to look over. She mouthed ‘obnoxious’ silently. And then ‘dorks.’

Jack merely shrugged shamelessly.

Going so fast and so far, Jack quite literally felt the connection to Memoria alter along the way. By no means did his sense of his powers and so forth disappear, but at a certain point, it definitely began to fade in strength; as if to reach him, it had to stretch out more and more.

Of course, according to the way his powers worked, all he had to do was actually summon a bit of memorite. He did so, just to verify, and instantly felt a strengthening of his Allotment, as if that tangle of many prosthetic limbs suddenly had a new and improved distant joint to operate from as a source, instead of needing to stretch across the distance.

Baby spiders ready to do Momma’s will far from the central web. Ugh. I wish I hadn’t thought up that analogy, now.

Across from him, Highfive met his eyes over Bo’s head and smiled, giving a thumbs-up. “Yeeeeah! Big Iron’s giving us a better connection, everyone!”

“Yay,” Bo said without enthusiasm, and began a sarcastic clap.

A mixed, sarcastic and non-sarcastic clap took hold. Jack waved with his hand and smiled around. “You’re so welcome, everyone.”

Solvent whistled to make it die down. “Alright, that’ll be enough, Agentus. Agent Highfive, there’s no danger worth worrying about yet, but we’re already getting close to ‘abroad’ — let’s not talk openly about what gives us this, or who, and if you have to, refer to the ‘Queen’ rather than the individual directly. Said Queen should limit use to necessity until we’re ready, too. Roger that, Agentus? Sound off.”

Everyone shouted their acknowledgements, including Jack. Afterward, Jack commented, “Guess that also means that if the Queen gets a verbal or radio order, they should just execute, and not sound off like this, thereby admitting who the source is.”

“Good point, Ferro,” Solvent called. “Most vets will tell you paranoia has demonstrably paid off on several occasions out here, and the lack thereof has, in contrast, demonstrably gotten people killed.”

Silence persisted on the plane for a while after that statement.

Transfer to the tiltrotors from the levitation platform relay base was immediate, but there were significantly more of them to transport everyone, and there were far more protocols involved. Everyone needed emergency parachutes, and in general, the trip wouldn’t be quite so comfortable, as the vessels needed to cram people in as much as possible. Soldiers were told to stay put and make their own comfort in their heads for the longer trips. Deep and easy sleepers were the envied. On the plus side, their trip wasn’t too long, after all: claimed at two hours to cross about 600 kilometers at cruising speed, to make it to the coast at Fort Sledge.

Jack didn’t particularly like that assessment from his own knowledge, and when he checked with the pilot, was told that was ‘granny measurin’ and he’d get them there the same as any other ‘flyer worth his salt’ would, which was an hour and a half with change. Furthermore, he ‘didn’t know any grannies’ among the other pilots, so the same would be true of the whole group.

The passengers were all placed at the sides, facing inward, and otherwise packed pretty close. Once again, Neex/Ira was sat next to Jack, no doubt to keep her more comfortable with the unusual situation. She clearly didn’t like the ‘bumpier’ flight inside the passenger compartment, but remained a relative trooper about it.

He also got a pleasant surprise when Ira was digging in her pack at one point, and she suddenly smiled indulgently upon sight of something within. When Jack met her eyes curiously, she flashed something out of the pack briefly: the steel canteen he’d got her as a gift way back when, with a custom paint job of a sky, and golden letters that read, ‘Neex, Honorary Skyhuman.’

Through the mental bond, Jack exclaimed, “Awesome! You can show it off when you’re home.”

That got a warm acknowledgement from her. “I certainly will. It is very special to have an object declare a title you’ve earned. And you were ignorant of that, correct?”

Jack blinked. He’d almost forgotten about Neex’s — and apparently Deucalian — love for custom titles. “Uh, yeah. Is that good or bad?”

“Very good! It just shows I authentically earned it, you see. Mmmph.” She closed her eyes, smiled, and hugged her pack, obviously thinking of the canteen inside. “I will have the most special title of any Deucalian, in a way.”

Jack only partially got it, and knew he did, because the alien feeling of joy he was getting from Neex was not entirely fathomable. “I see. Well, good! I’m really glad it makes you so happy, that’s for sure.” He had to think back pretty hard to remember any names he’d thought of for her. But he really wanted to drop one on her right then. Like a blessing from the goddess herself, it finally came to him. “Cheer Giver.”

Ira cast a gaze of absolute adoration at him, her carefully cultivated human eyes melting into octogirl chaos. Her head kinda flopped back as she sighed. “More like Cheer Getter.”

Jack smiled. “If you insist? Throw it on the pile with the rest.”

“That would mean we are both Cheer Giver and Cheer Getter!” She made a snorting sound as she smiled, obviously holding in laughter. “That is very silly.”

Jack shrugged. “I mean, we are, after all. Silly.”

Ira continued smiling adoringly at him without saying anything.

Jack was aware of Bo across from him, head turned upward, muttering something silently to the heavens with her eyes squeezed shut.

Heh, she can’t handle it, yet she can’t look away.

Unfortunately, Ira saw it, too, and became alarmed. “Bo?! Are you okay?!”

Bo’s head came down, and she shrugged, clearly still miserable. “Nothing. Just need, ah… t-to pee. Yes.”

“Oh no! Perhaps they could stop?”

A gaggle of sarcastic snickers and chuckles around the vessel answered that question.

Ira looked around at the reaction, deflating. “I take it that’s a no, then… oh. Um, I guess you’ll have to hold it, Bo.”

“Oh trust me,” Bo said, eyes flickering at Jack, “I am. I’ll suffer through it.”

Their arrival at Fort Sledge was not very scenic, as the vessel had dropped into a protective hangar with the rest. They saw only a bit of night sky above, peeking beyond closing hangar doors, as they filed out of the vessel’s exit, removed their extra gear, and stretched.

Everyone seemed to have both a strange pep and energy, and a contrasting discomfort, with more than a few sour or confused faces. Jack felt some of it, too, though it was mostly positive for him, having been through the experience multiple times. He found himself breathing more than necessary, just because it felt so good.

Altitude. We’re waaaay further down than we’re used to at this point.

Ira seemed far livelier, all smiles and practically bouncing. She was breathing in like she smelled fresh bread in the air.

Jack sniffed and quickly detected what it was: the sea.

Bah! Should’ve known that one. Damn, I could go for some fresh bread, right now…

“Oh!” Ira exclaimed, and nearly tackled Bo as she grabbed her hand. “You need to find a bathroom to urinate immediately! It’s unhealthy for hu- for us humans not to when they feel pressure! I’ll help find one, but we have to hurry, because they’ll expect me to head to the coast soon!” She moved, tugging Bo by the arm.

“R-right,” Bo muttered as she was hurried along in Ira’s energized wake.

Solvent showed up in the general center of the stewing-together greater group — that or everyone was just naturally drawn to center around him. He raised his hand and whistled once. “Alright, soldiers, listen up! Take a little break, but interface with your comms, in your ear and on your hip. Minimum fifteen and no promises after, so handle your business pronto if you got any. Helmets optional inside, mandatory outside. We’re not engaging the Queen protocol yet. Transport time is modular but tight on response, so don’t miss comms or I’ll have your ass instead of excuses. Everyone got it?!”

“Got it, sir, Agent Nonpareil!” came a somewhat synchronized response from the greater group.

Before Jack took two steps, it was like Solvent had teleported nearby, as his hand clasped Jack’s shoulder. “Agent Ferro,” he said, nodding. “Stick with our VIP like glue. The same goes for her bathroom buddy, but she’ll be even stickier.” He nodded over at Lattice, who stood nearby. “And she makes the quatro. Works for you?”

“Yes, sir, Agent Nonpareil,” Jack replied.
