Awakened Music, as seen by an Adult Awakened.

Music is one of those things that exists everywhere. Throughout the WidePond, within the Shallows, everywhere. Each culture, each family, each group has some sort of music, something they listen to, sing along to, and dance to.

The biggest change I've seen in how we appreciate music is how we dance. Most people I grew up around weren't overly athletic, weren't coordinated, weren't great dancers. What happens when everyone is faster, stronger, able to react to a misstep before gravity takes its toll? You get people who dance decently, even well.

Then you look at Combat Pathers. Their lives rely on their bodies. Even the healers, the Energysts who fight from a distance are still physically fit in ways that defy Unawakened understanding. They move, preternaturally.

They crave music that moves, that creates beats, rhythms. Music that shows off their talents, that turns the dance floor into a battlefield of extravagance and brilliance. They want music they can feel, not just hear.

~ * ~

Fay could tell Briana was nervous. She didn't have a convenient bond to feel the underlying emotions, they didn't spend their time in band that would let emotions bleed through despite best efforts, she simply knew.

Briana wouldn't tell her where they were going for dinner, just that it would only be the four of them, a double date, a family meal. It would be somewhere new, somewhere she'd found out about and was certain would be perfect. Then she'd smile with a clenched jaw and find a way to change the topic.

Fay trusted her, she knew Briana wouldn't do something to hurt her. Why was her birthday doing this? She just wanted to spend time with them, do something small, something calm. She didn't want stress for any of them, not on her account.

And now here they were, a block away from the restaurant in a strange city, a foreign Pond she'd never heard of. she couldn't see their destination, Briana had insisted they stop out of sight.

Her girlfriend was as beautiful as she was flustered, all but shaking with anticipation even as she smoothed her dress. Seb and Anya were as they always were, a lovely couple who were there for their sister. They had no more idea than she did, but they weren't the one about to be led blindfolded around the corner and up the block.

"Really Bri? A blindfold?"

"I want it to be a surprise."

"I won't let you trip or fall. I promise," Anya added.

Fay turned to glare helplessly at her. That wasn't helping. Seb simply gave a small nod, he wasn't going to tease her or let Anya do something sisterly.

"Not too tight," Fay said.

The blindfold, or rather the light scarf serving as one, settled over her eyes before being cinched. For a second it was almost too tight, she was ready to complain when it was loosened, adjusted so it wouldn't slip.

Her fingers tightened on Briana's arm, barely managing to keep from squeezing too hard. She took a deep breath, then another, her fingers letting go one by one. It was just a block, just a few steps and then she'd be able to walk through these strange streets with her eyes open.

She heard Briana take the lead, felt as much as heard Seb and Anya step to either side of her, letting her hold their hands as they made their way around the corner. It wasn't so bad, it would be nearly impossible for her to bump into anything without Briana running into it first, and she was between the others, no one would get close without warning.

Step by step they made their way down the block. Step by step she found herself relaxing, not enjoying being blind, but growing more confident. Then she heard something, the faintest of notes drifting through the air, growing louder with each step. Familiar notes, familiar instruments, ones she hadn't heard in an eternity.

Next she found herself gasping, inhaling scents she knew almost as well as she knew her reflection.

"Bri..?"

"A few more steps love."

She blushed but forced herself to keep walking, to connect her senses to her memories. Where were they? They hadn't gone through any Portals, they hadn't left the metropolis Briana had led them to. Why did she feel like she was about to step into her family compound, sit at the foot of the table-

She tore the blindfold off, her one hand jerking out of Anya's and the other pulling away from where it rested against Seb's arm. She had to see, she had to be sure.

~ * ~

Seb put his hand on Anya's shoulder as she winced and shook out her hand. Fay had pulled away too quickly, too abruptly. She'd taken a step away from them and frozen, her body caught mid-motion in a pose that couldn't be comfortable.

Hand?

I'll be better in a moment, she yanked away in a straight line, Anya sent.

"Bri..?!"

The restaurant was one they might have walked past without noticing, set back just slightly from the rest with next to no indication it was there. There was the music, but there were other sounds that drowned it out, there were the smells of food, but he'd only noticed them when Fay first tensed and he tried to understand.

"Surprise..?" Briana said.

"How, where, what?"

Fay had finished taking her steps forward, had nearly crashed into her girlfriend as they stood looking at the door. Some of the tension had faded, whatever had scared her wasn't there. She still looked ready to run away, to vanish if something, anything, startled her further.

"You remember I found someone selling Kaf?" Briana said. "I asked them about food, about a restaurant."

"Food? Restaurant?" Fay said, clearly echoing the words she was hearing.

"They said this was the best that would accept out-Clan diners?"

"Out-Clan… yes… that makes sense. They wouldn't send you…"

"Fay?" Seb said.

She spun to face him, eyes widening before she seemed to recognize him, before she managed to take a breath that shifted her entire posture.

"Sorry, that wasn't- I wasn't expecting."

"Are you okay?" Anya asked.

"Yes," she answered. "Yes. I'm better. We have a reservation?"

Briana nodded then turned towards the door, only to have Fay stop her with a light touch.

"Thank you." Fay said.

~ * ~

Seb sank onto the couch, Anya both on him and next to him as she settled with him. Dinner had been excellent. Dishes he hadn't known the name of, that he still didn't know how he would explain them to someone who hadn't been there, seasonings that tasted unlike anything he recognized, and, of course, Kaf.

"Sit down Bri," Fay said.

"I was going to make some coff-"

"Sit! You are not making coffee tonight. You had Kaf. Kaf!"

Fay was halfway out of her chair as she spoke, apparently ready to drag Briana back to the living room if she didn't listen.

"Yes, we had your precious Kaf. Now I know what you're expecting me to make."

"It was good, right?"

Seb felt Anya tense a little before answering.

"It was a little strong," Anya said

"A bit sweet," Seb admitted. "But tasted great otherwise."

"Bri?" Fay asked.

"It wasn't coffee."

"I keep saying coffee isn't Kaf, so we agree."

Briana finally came to join them, Fay standing just long enough to make room before sitting back down in her lap.

"I over-reacted," she said after a moment.

"You already apologized," Anya said. "My hand stopped hurting before food made it to the table."

"I shouldn't have had to apologize. I shouldn't have pulled so hard, moved so sharply."

"I surprised you. I didn't let you know, I didn't prepare you," Briana said.

"If you'd told me I would have said no. I would have refused," Fay answered. "I'm glad I didn't have the chance."

"They seemed to be happy to have you there," Seb said.

"You noticed?"

He blinked, staring at her past Anya's hair and let Fay think about what she'd asked. Had he noticed? Anya had noticed, Briana had noticed, the handful of other diners had noticed. The waitress had given Fay a long look as they sat, she hadn't asked any questions, hadn't said anything, simply let her eyes linger.

When Fay started ordering with only the briefest of glances, rattling off names of dishes without consulting the rest of them, even ordering their drinks for them when water was first brought out, the waitress had given a smile then all but run away. Not in fear, she hadn't acted like there was any problem, but rather as though she'd found something.

Every single employee, even the chefs, had come out at one point or another to bring them dishes. None of them had lingered, none had spoken to them other than their waitress, but they had all taken a moment to look at Fay, even if it was only one look.

"They didn't expect you," Anya said. "They didn't expect someone who wasn't… out-Clan?"

"No," Fay agreed. "They didn't. Clan doesn't, didn't, wouldn't? Out-Clan dining is for visitors, for people who recognize the food but that's it."

"Surely they have Clan diners," Seb said.

"Of course. They eat there, others eat there. But not as guests of Out-Clan. They expected all of you. They didn't expect me."

"Why?" Briana asked.

Seb watched as Fay settled more deeply into Briana's embrace before answering, until she looked as though she was going to fall asleep rather than answer.

"It isn't done. They won't judge. They won't gossip, much. They were surprised."

"They weren't upset?"

"No Bri," Fay said. "They simply didn't recognize me. They didn't ask, they left it up to me to say anything."

"You didn't," Seb said.

"No. Not tonight," Fay agreed. "Tonight was my birthday. Tonight was my family, my future. Not my past."

Not tonight, he sent.

I guess we're going back at some point, Anya said.

The food was good.

Their choices of desserts weren't great.

Is that all that matters to you love? Dessert?

She didn't answer. They were still with family, this was still Fay's time.
