Dominik walked through the alien jungle. He carried his sniper over his shoulder. Keeping to the foliage to avoid being seen, he searched for more game. More things to kill. More ways to screw over the aliens. He had not chosen to be here.

With every kill, he grew more confident. They underestimated his abilities. Taken out from his home in the mountains, living a life of solitude, he had never thought there would be more life beyond the stars. Taken against his will. The only way to show his defiance was not just to survive but to kill anything and everything that looked down on him.

The face of the man, only a few years younger than him, flashed in his mind. His disgusting behavior in siding with that silvery demon. Chasing after alien tail, Dominik chuckled at the thought. To have given up his pride as a human and to leave himself at the mercy of aliens. Dominik thought that death was the only mercy for that man.

As he stepped further into the jungle, his satchel rustled, filled with roughly sixty eyeballs. He had only killed several of the beasts. He refused to play by the rules of the Arbiter, choosing to instead kill the other participants of the event, stealing their loot one after another.

It wasn’t enough.

Not until he cleansed the jungle of all other sentient life forms.

The roar of a beast echoed further ahead, from down below. Smiling, Dominik followed the sound, creeping slowly, masking his footsteps, using the sounds of the insects as cover and the shadows of the jungle as a cloak.

Pushing several vines away, Dominik crouched low, his sniper still mounted on his shoulder, waiting to be fired. His breath felt shallow, the humid air pressing against his tight gray suit. Sweat beaded along his brow, but his hands were steady on the stock of his rifle. While old and rusted slightly, Dominik was lucky to have found his favored weapon in the rubbled alien city, in an abandoned building.

The gun had yet to fail him. Once more, it would be fired.

A corpse lay ahead. Just as Dominik had predicted, another Krovalith had fallen. The Krovalith’s bulk sprawled across the churned earth, its muscle-bound flesh torn open. Dominik’s sharp eyes tracked the jagged wounds along its flanks—clean cuts. This was good. No sign of a ranged weapon.

His last encounter with an alien had him scrambling. The green-haired men, feigning to be human, held huge destructive power with those rifles. Yet he had still killed one of them, sending the rest of them running. He didn’t need such a weapon.

He spotted his targets through the broken foliage. Their blue skin made them an easy target to spot. They were tall and slender, their limbs long, their movements fluid. Their heads were sleek, crested with faint ridges that caught the light, and their eyes were more like yellow orbs that glowed faintly.

He counted two targets.

Easy.

Dominik’s fingers tightened on the rifle. Imagining the blue creature’s eyes in his hand, holding it in his hand like an ornament. He shifted slightly, lining up his shot. He took a deep breath, slowing his heartbeat, calming every minute muscle in his body. Settling the crosshairs on the closet target. Right on its neck. He squeezed the trigger. The rifle kicked hard against his shoulder. The alien flickered for only a second, then it disappeared. The bullet shot through the air, carving a hole into a distant tree.

He scrambled, attempting to load another round. Before he could close the magazine, the figure he shot at re appeared only several feet away from him. Its yellow eyes locked onto him.

Dominik clenched his fist. It showed no fear—only curiosity. It blinked out of sight before he could fire. He snapped his vision towards the other alien, but it simply stood watching them, choosing not to engage.

I’m being played with.

He pushed back into the jungle, moving along the tree line, his boots crunching against the damp leaves. It was fast, too fast, but he had distance and he knew he was smarter. He could never lose.

He used the reflection of his scope to see behind him; he swiveled his rifle, firing blindly. Smoke trailed from the nuzzle, but again he hit nothing. Faint whispers permeated through the air. It rushed past, just out of sight.

He dropped to one knee, leveling the rifle towards its friends that stood by the Krovalith corpse. A shallow pool of Krovalith blood shimmered under the light. He focused, letting the world shrink down to the narrow tunnel of his scope.

He caught movement. Just at the edge of his vision. The creature flickered into view, crouching low among some ferns. Dominik lined up another shot, waiting for it to move again. Sweat slid down his brow, his fingers trembling on the trigger.

The creature blinked, reappearing again only a few meters away from him. Dominik fired on instinct, the bullet grazing the creature’s shoulder, drawing blue blood. The creature hissed, high pitched and echoey. I dived forward, becoming a blur of motion.

Dominik barely had time to roll before the creature’s feet punched into the ground where he’d kneeled, sending dirt and splinters into the air. He scrambled back, loading another round. The creature’s friend still only watching from the shadow—entertained. It waited for the hunt to end.

His back hit the trunk of a tree with no more room to run. The wounded alien cocked its head in amusement. Its chest flared slightly, the veins running along its spine pulsating in rhythm with its shallow breaths.

Aiming for the head, Dominik tried once again. The creature vanished.

He tried to step away, but it was already beside him. A blow struck him in the ribs, sending him crashing to the ground. Pain exploded through his side. His rifle sliding away from him into a bush.

Dominik coughed, spitting blood onto the dirt. His vision blurred. The blue alien loomed over him.

I refuse to die to this thing.

Life or Death Scenario Detected Temporary Boost To All Stats

New found strength flowed within him. Strength he didn’t know he had. His muscles tightened, no they grew. Everything moved in slow motion, his eyes trained on only one thing. His prey.

The blue lifeform, sensing Dominik’s rejuvenation of strength, seemed to smile. It allowed Dominik to stand up. Watching him with keen interest. It craned its neck, inspecting Dominik more closely.

Dominik slowly went for a punch, but the entity simply stepped back. What it didn’t account for was that it was a feint.

Throwing his arms out, he threw himself at the alien. The blue humanoid didn’t expect the cannon ball of force. Its yellow eyes wide with surprise.

It couldn’t make a noise. Dominik clamped his arms around its head. His chest pressed against its face. At first, it simply tried pulling him off. Realizing his intense grip, he flailed, trying anything to get him off.

It staggered back and wobbled. It was losing its breath. But that’s not what killed him.

Crunch.

The force of his grip was so strong, the humanoid’s head popped. Completely flattened. Dominik felt the warm blood erupt. It’s green brain completely drenching the top of of his head.

The blue alien’s body went limp, falling to the ground. Domino fell with it, his grip still on the non-existent head.

With blood and brains melting together, dripping down his face. Dominik’s eyes met with the other alien left.

Caught between terrified and angry, the creature didn’t know what to do. Dominik would decide for him.

He got up and walked over to where it stood.

It tried to turn and run. But Dominik was too fast. He reached the alien in a split second. Dominik threw a punch right in its chest, sending it flying back.

It had lost all its fight.

Dominik smiled. This was the way of the jungle.

The alien lifted its torso, attempting to get up.

But by then it was too late.

It looked down towards its chest. Dominik’s entire arm impaled within. Its eyes shook. Dominik gleamed with joy. Finding the organ he desired, he gripped at its heart. Yanking it out.

The alien screamed in pain, but without a heart, it slowly wilted into nothing. Its eyes rolling back, once full of light and interest, now completely dead.

Dominik stepped back, its heart still in his hand. The body fell to the ground pitifully. He stomped his boot against its head, planting the body deeper into the soil.

Dominik crushed the heart under his fist.

He alone stood at the top of the food chain.

Dominik felt with each passing second as his fight went on, his strength waning. Just as he was about to pass out, a message arrived.

You Have Leveled Up! You’re Now Lvl 5

His strength returned to him. He looked towards his hand, drenched in blood and the crushed tissue of an alien heart. Tilting his hand, he let the heart’s remnants fall to the ground.

Dominik looked at the sky. It wasn’t as otherworldly as the last planet. This could’ve been Earth for all he knew. The swarm of insects and the Krovalith were the only sign of another world.

Yet he didn’t miss his planet. The rush of power, just as he felt his life slipping, felt so addictive—intoxicating. If only he could harness that power. He could do anything.

The man…who had started all of this. He called himself Arius Steiger. His words echoed in Dominik’s mind.

“The Omniplex could turn you into a God.”

The power, the system. It made Dominik wonder what the Omniplex was. And why were they given it?

He sighed, realizing it wasn’t important. Not right now. It was best now to lie low and wait for the event to end. He had done plenty. Meeting plenty of objectives.

He presumed he’d be brought back to the first planet. The city of rubble. If that area held all the humans, he needed to find them. At first, Dominik thought this was a simple case of killing everyone and being the last one standing.

But the Exofront. It was more than just a battle to the death. Alliances and outside interference, all of it suggested a deeper battle. The rest of the events might need teamwork. He needed to prepare.

Just can’t have fire boy join, he thought as he walked back towards the bush that his rifle had slid into. Dropping to his knees.

His knees hit the ground, and he heard something faint in the air. At first Dominik pushed it aside, focusing on withdrawing his gun, thinking it was the buzzing of insects.

Then it grew louder.

An odd twisted laughter that made Dominik want to close his ears. It sounded like nails scraping against a blackboard.

“Oh, I’ve found you. If it isn’t the hunter.” The voice surrounded him. The trees speakers, acting as his agents.

Scrambling, Dominik grabbed his rifle. Slick with dirt, he quickly wiped it with his sleeve. His eyes never leaving the surrounding trees.

“Who’s out there?” he shouted.

“You don’t remember?” the voice mocked, high pitched and shrilly.

Dominik didn’t feel fear or whatever this hidden man intended for him to feel. He lifted his gun and circled around the trees. “Why don’t you come out, so you can jog my memory?”

“No can do. Your strength is far too great. I myself hide in the shadows, for your mere presence is overwhelming.”

A vein popped out under Dominik’s skin. “You dare try to mock me, alien!”

“Alien no. I’m just like you.”

Some sense of unease lifted from his shoulders. Dominik signed, “if you’re a human, come on out.” He lifted his rifle in the air. “I’m armed, you know.”

“What a human? You and me? No.” The voice scoffed.

With each word the man spoke, Dominik tracked the origin, searching for his location. Found it. He lifted his rifle and fired into a large bush.

He heard nothing. Not the sound of a bullet hitting flesh. Nor screams of pain. He trudged over to the bush, pushing aside the greenery. Nothing was there.

He clicked his tongue. Where is he—

“What are you looking for?”

Dominik jumped. His heart felt like it would explode. The figure behind him disappeared within a split second. So fast he didn’t catch a glimpse of his face.

“Who are you!?”

The voice cackled once more. “I already said. I’m like you. I’m a hunter.”

Shaking his head in frustration, Dominik cried, “you don’t know me!”

“Oh…”

Suddenly, the air went cold. Dominik’s surroundings disappeared. He looked towards his hands. They were still there. He could roughly see about a meter of radius. Everything else was black. A ring of darkness surrounded him. It felt like a nightmare given form. He walked, and with every step, the small ring followed him. His shoulders brushed against the trees. His other senses were there. But his sight was gone. His weapon useless.

“Where are you?” A feeling of discomfort spread over his body. This wasn’t like his other battles. He felt small. Insignificant.

“I can tell you everything about you. You probably grew up somewhere rural. A place with harsh land. You had to hunt to live.”

“Great, you think you’re some fortune teller.” Having had enough of chatter, Dominik fired his rifle in the direction he had thought he heard the voice.

Nothing but air.

Dominik raised an eyebrow. “Lucky guess,” he muttered.

“Oh, that’s not because of some tragic backstory. No. Most likely your mother abandoned you and your father. She didn’t like the rural life. She was promised big dreams and it all but ended when you were born.”“What!?” His eyes grew wide with anger. “Don’t you dare bring her up.”

What was it exactly?” The voice grew louder than went quieter. At some point Dominik felt as though he was only an arm away from him.

“And so every waking day, you’d devote yourself to the hunt. Whether it was a bear or an elk. It did not matter, did it?”“No, it didn’t.” Dominik responded quietly. “And you’ll die like all the animals I’ve killed.” His threat felt empty, but it hung in the air for a while.

“Yes. You’ve killed plenty. That’s what started your belief, isn’t it? The belief that you alone stood at the top of the food chain.”

I’ll kill you. I’ll wring your neck like I did with those blue monsters from earlier.”“You live a contradiction. You remind me of someone. That rifle you use. You take pride in it. When those aliens use their technology, you use what you were taught. But deep down, you know the truth. That weapon is as alien as the other weapons on this planet. From the material to the mags. To the feel of the grip. It wasn’t made on Earth. You chose to ignore it.”
