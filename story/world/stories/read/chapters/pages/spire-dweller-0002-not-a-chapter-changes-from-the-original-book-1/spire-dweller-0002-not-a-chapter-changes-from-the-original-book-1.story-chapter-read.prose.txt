Plot:

- Samantha has new motivations for wanting to become a cultivator, so her relationship with her family changed

- Her uncle and aunt (Thomas's parents) were senselessly killed by a random cultivator during a family outing. Samantha's goal after receiving the quest was to become a cultivator to act as a shield for mortals she loved.

- Because relationships between mortals and cultivators are so poor, her parents do not initially support her decision to pursue this quest. Samantha also feels a level of bitterness at the many things she's required to give up in pursuit of a cultivator's title/power, such as all the important relationships in her life will likely changing for the worse.

-Failing to save Aiden and receiving the hidden quest puts Samantha on a short spiral of hopelessness because she failed to protect a single mortal--her goal in becoming a cultivator in the first place. She despairs at how staying on the floor will probably leave her too weakened to ever achieve that goal in life, but leaving the floor makes the many things she's suffered and sacrificed feel pointless.

- After she kills the Rat King and his mortal minions and visits home, her father takes issue with her newfound callousness towards human life. A rift begins to form when her mother disagrees, saying the situation is nothing like what happened with Aldon's brother and his brother's wife. - Later, Aldon completely disowns Samantha and warns her if she returns to the shop he will report her to the authorities and force her to face the ascension/justice she's seeking to avoid. This deepens her emotional spiral, because now even staying on the floor crippled, she cannot be with her family like before.- The decision to ascend starts with the Braxand duel, but solidifies when threatened by Lord Braxand. Samantha feels she is only causing more trouble for everyone the longer she stays on the floor.- Aldon only begins to soften towards Samantha after he learns she's ascending (accepting the action as Samantha taking responsibility for her wrongdoings). Unfortunately, ascension is less than a week away at that point, so there is no way to perfectly mend their damaged relationship.- The family does make up/come together as they are leaving Yivesh, as best as circumstances allow. Though, hurt is still present.

- Thomas is more an aloof character than a womanizer now, having withdrawn after his parents' murder. And their relationship through the book is more "uncertain". Not in the "I don't know if I like you" way, but the "I haven't had a proper conversation with you in years and don't know how to act" way. - He is upset with Samantha for uprooting his life in Yivesh, but, not wanting to leave things between them strained with her departure from the floor, suggests the "traditional Cray family remedy". This involves giving Samantha a truly horrendous haircut, which she is forced to travel to the ascension obelisk with. She swears a cultivators oath not to hide/fix the awful hairdo until after she is inside the real Registrar's Hall, upon which Thomas wholeheartedly forgives her, per tradition.
