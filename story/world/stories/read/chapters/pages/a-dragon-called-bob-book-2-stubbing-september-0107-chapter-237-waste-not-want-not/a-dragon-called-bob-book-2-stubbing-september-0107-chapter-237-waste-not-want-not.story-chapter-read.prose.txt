A flight of dragons is beautiful.  Stunning to behold and terrifying to the puny mammals below.  Gallow’s Garnish was a nice little town when viewed from half a mile up.  I wouldn’t like to comment on the living conditions for the locals, but they were certainly better off than the poor bastards in Dampcut.

Except that there was about to be a big ass battle nearby.  The roads were packed in both directions.  The residents seemed to be heading away, while merchants and the traditional assortment of camp followers streamed into the town.  It was like watching people queue up to be first in line on Black Friday, except they’d be looting dead bodies instead of grabbing a bargain.

“Can we bomb them?”  Jace asked.

“No, we can’t bomb them.”

“What about them?”

“Still no.”

For two long hours, I’d had an endless barrage of requests to bomb people, buildings, herds of cattle and rocks.

“I’m going to kick so much ass!  I’m gonna be like BOOOM!  Ate you, ate you, and you as well!”  Bargleblaster was far too pleased with my plans, such as they were.

“Can we all just focus on the mission, please?”

“Now you want discipline?  You’ve left them alone for too long.”  I could hear Chi sniff at me despite the wind rushing past my ears.

“I’ve been busy, Chi.  I do have an empire to run.”

“Petty theft from the mammals, herding them into your shops to buy trinkets and be taxed for the pleasure.”

“That’s capitalism for you.”  I shrugged, always a mistake while in flight.  Once I’d stabilised and resumed my position at the flying V of scaly ducks, I snorted.  “Right, we’ve got armies in three directions that I can see.  Anyone got any more?”  We banked around, the rest of the TOTS holding almost perfect formation behind me.

“I can see six, can I bomb th—”

“No Jace, no, you bloody can’t.  Not yet anyway.”

“We’ve found them. What is your most wise plan now?” Chi asked sweetly.

I did have an idea for how to proceed, and honestly, I kind of liked it.

“We head over to the nearest one, and I pop down to see which side they’re on.  If they’re brown, we flush ‘em down.”

“Not a great plan.”

“Thanks, Chi.  How about you leave the war to me, seeing as you never fight, and I’ll leave the passive-aggressive grandmothering to you?  Back in a minute.”

I shifted back into my human form, and gravity reached out to do what it does best.  A portal opened below me, and I vanished into it, thudding to the ground right in front of the first ranks of the nearest army.

As the dust cleared, I looked up and rose from my superhero landing, black silk boxer shorts fluttering in the wind.

“You there!  Which lord do you serve?”  I demanded imperiously.

Half the soldiers had tried to step back, but the sheer weight of warriors and armour behind them drove them towards me anyway.  A few spread out and tried to move past, keeping their spears and hastily drawn swords aimed in my direction.

“L-Lord Cravenspire… sir?” said the footman I’d pointed at.  His armour was clean and polished, the plates well-fitted over his mail.  I did not know which side Cravenspire was on.  Their tabards and banners were all a silver grey, with what looked like a crooked tower rising from a cliff embroidered into them with black thread.  Looked kind of Umbra-loving to me, but I needed to be sure.

“How do you feel about the Lord of Light?”  They had formed a circle three men deep around me now, but I politely ignored the points of the weapons they aimed at my heart.

“He’s ok?  I mean, a lot of his followers are a bit weird?”

The private wasn’t being helpful.

“I noticed that.  I meant, is he like a good guy or a bad guy for you?”

“Well, his core philosophy is one of illumination.  Making things visible, ya know?”  This second private reached up to remove his helmet and scratched at his bald spot.

“Yeah.  And the whole being linked to the sun thing is pretty useful?  Need the sun for the crops to grow,” added a third.

I had a feeling I would be adding several guards to the menu in short order.

“I don’t mean his relevance to the operation of basic astronomy—”

“But his followers are really weird, though.  Like Garn said, they’re into some kinky shit.  Now I’m not a man to judge.”  Menu selection number four raised a steelclad hand to the sky.  “I don’t care what consenting adults get up to in private, and they do provide a lot of support to the whip and paddle-making industries.”

“My ole man makes paddles.  They’re his best customers.  No idea how they wear them out so fast,” added a side order.

“I like street lights.”  Guard number one offered.  “Makes the towns look pretty from the hills.”

“They do that.  Makes spotting the richer parts easier as well, so we can prioritise looting.  No point wasting time on the peasants, a nice big glowing sign that says ‘rich fuckers be here’ is a welcome sight after a long march.”

“Did none of you notice my sudden appearance and landing?” I asked, hoping to derail the discussion back onto the track I wanted.

“Very impressive, I thought,” Guard number one, who was possibly called Garn, said supportively.  “You made a crater when you landed and everything.”

“Cheers.  I was pretty pleased with it.”

“Now what you really need to do is get those sparkles under control.  If they only went off when you wanted them to, just as you hit the dirt, it would have looked great.  As it was… I’d give it a seven out of ten.  Still good, but it lacked that pazzazz to make it really special.”

I glared at guard number two.  It was a solid seven out of ten glare, and he flinched.  The soldiers streaming around our little meeting bumped him back into position.

“You don’t think it was impressive enough?”

He sucked air through his teeth and steadied the spear so that the tip, which was a foot from my face, stabilised.  “It was impressive, don’t get me wrong.  But it lacked that little something special.”

“Like sparkles?” I asked flatly.

“Or some kind of flame effect?”  offered Garn.

“Maybe even some kind of sound?  It was all very whoosh!  But maybe some trumpets or something?  A little brass always gets me fired up.”

A long, deep breath calmed me somewhat and helped settle Wrath down a bit.  Not much, but a bit.

“Are you on the side of the light or the shadow in the civil war?” I enunciated slowly and clearly.

“I like them both!”

“Yeah, me too.  The higher-ups worry about that shit, I’m just a grunt and know my place.”

Another slower and deeper breath.

“Which side is your lord on?”

“Oh, he’s an Umbrati through and through.  He craps shadows and eats candles.  But I don’t think it’s fair to judge us based on what he believes.  I mean, my preferred deity would have to be Floof.  She’s just so warm and fuzzy.”

“Picking a god is a tricky thing, for sure,”  Garn agreed.  “What do you value most?  Comfort and happiness, or do you want to avoid cruelty and pain?  Lots of folks flock to the badder gods to try and keep themselves safe from the malign influence of darker powers.  It’s not like people worship Gonorritis because they want the clap.”

“Please be advised that this next question is very, very important.  You are absolutely sure that your boss is fighting for the shadow in the civil war?”

A chorus of yeps, yes’s, and um-hmms confirmed it.

“You’ve all been very helpful.  I’d like to suggest you take cover and run away if you aren’t that bothered about who wins the war.”

“Oh, can’t do that.”

“No, be shameful.  I’m a Cravenspire man to my marrow.”

“Would you turn your colours on your lord because some wazzock in his undies told you to?”

Sometimes, you’ve just got to let people make their own mistakes.  I shrugged and opened another portal below my feet, vanishing from the surface and reappearing above the circling delta of dragons.  I shifted back into my true form immediately and glided into position above the others.

“You’re good to go.  Their shadow lovers.  Although the men aren’t true believers, they’re apparently overly inclined to religious debate.  So killing them is probably for the best.  Jace: bombs away.”

No further prompting was needed.  Bargleblaster folded his wings and dropped like a green stone.  Pete followed closely behind, his growl dopplering like a dive bomber.  Lille was a streak of white behind them, already bristling with spines in anticipation.

“May the lord have mercy on me for what I have set in motion.”

“No lord to forgive you, Bob.  Why bother?  You’ll only do something else in a minute,” Chi snapped.

“Look, Chi.  I really feel like we’ve gotten off on the wrong foot.  Maybe if you gave me a chance, you’d see—”

Jace belched a stream of red orbs down at the few thousand mammals below us.  He avoided any risk of hitting his colleagues; friends was perhaps too strong a word to use,  but he produced a neat pattern around the outside edges of the formation.

The fiery eggs exploded on impact, and rivers of red flame snaked out from each hit.  Even so high above them, I could hear the soldiers screaming as the fire engulfed them.

Then Pete, Bargleblaster and Lille hit them.  The white dragon flew low and with every wingbeat or flick of her tail, Lille sent waves of spines down at the soldiers below.  She didn’t know Dragon Fu, as far as I was aware, but she effortlessly dodged the anti-air fire that she drew.

Pete just became a silver blur of teeth and claws, tumbling across the surface and hoovering up humans like they were lint and he was a Roomba.  His tail speared anyone nearby and supplied the meat to his teeth, alive or dead.

When Bargleblaster hit the dirt, he went through it, and a ripple spread out across the ground around his point of entry.  The land healed up in his wake as though he hadn’t been solid, and the soldiers nearby looked around nervously once they picked themselves back up.  Then his snout began rising and falling like the soil was water and he was a hungry shark.

“See what you’ve done?  You’re a bad example.”

“Look Chi, I’m doing the best I can.”

A massive explosion rang out below me, and I snapped my eyes away from the grumpy old dragon flying by my left wing.  Pete had been knocked back, tumbling across the ground, but Lille recovered midair and launched her spines to defend him while he sorted himself out.

“Best no good!  Do better!  Be better.  They look up to you.”

“Like hell they do.”

“Bargleblaster and Pete in particular, but all of them to some extent.  You’re the one who is living the dream.  The hoard, the life, and respect from the humans.  None of us has that.”

“Chi… I’ll think about it.  But right now, I need to recycle that army.”

Swooping low, I bellowed “Missa Vivica!” over and over again and turned the once mighty army of Lord Cravenspire into my undead minions.

“Shut it!” I ordered as I landed.  “Oh hey Garn.  You can be in charge of this lot.  We’re going to set up camp on the hill overlooking Gallow’s Garnish.  Your lot can just wait patiently for now, and stay the hell away from the town.  We’ll be adding to your numbers soon enough.”

“I knew I should have gone into paddle-making, but no, had to be clever and join the bloody army,” the newly undead Garn muttered.  “You lot, pick yourselves and your pieces up and let's move out!”
