The sun was setting behind Gary as he sat cross-legged on the grass by the waterfall. The night wind made him feel alive. His eyes snapped open, and he got to his feet, positioning the Golden Bolt to thrust. He activated his aura field. Then, he changed it up. It was like using his body as the instrument to craft a symphony.

He vibrated the aura in his chest harder, softer on his arm, vigorously at his lower waist. The way the aura ripples merged to create the aura field seemed arbitrary, but after experimenting, he’d discovered which body parts to vibrate and how to achieve the effect he seeked.

He visualized the trajectory of his thrust as a tunnel through his aura field. At first, it seemed the same as every other part. Gradually, though, the energy there expanded. The aura Gary poured into his chest and arm and lower waist was transmitted to that particular part of the aura field, turning it intense. His eyes snapped open.

“First Song: Mud!” he yelled.

His spear flew forward. It drilled into the tunnel of enhanced aura he’d created. There was resistance, far more than other times, but Gary’s muscles bulged as he pushed through. The aura was pushed along with the spear, gathering around its body. By the time his spear finished crossing, all those layers of aura had converged as one, shooting off a mighty aura thrust. It sailed over the pond to strike a tree on the other side—its trunk exploded, flakes of wood spreading everywhere. A piece flew past Gary’s head. He grinned.

“Finally…” he muttered, then pumped his fist. “I did it!”

“That felt good! Can we do it again?” Goldie said, sticking her head out of his sleeve.

“We must do it again! How else would I get it down?”

“Yay! But, you know…”

“What?”

“This attack is meant to be slow, right? It didn’t feel that slow.”

“Slow doesn’t mean slow,” Gary replied. “This strike took me an instant longer than usual. The difference is tiny, but it matters. An instant is important against a skilled opponent. A slightly slower strike, if done improperly, can give them time to seize the advantage and turn around the entire battle.”

“When your master demonstrated it, his strike moved like a turtle.”

“He did that on purpose so we could see. This is the actual speed. It’s like the difference between a front-hand thrust and a back-hand thrust. Tiny, but gigantic.”

“How do you know?”

Gary looked out at the tree he’d exploded. “I could feel it,” he said. “Everything was harmonious. The aura piled on, then shot out as one. This strike is all I’ve been practicing for days. I know. Plus, it was brown.”

“Okay. I believe you! That was awesome, Gary!”

He grinned. “Thanks. Let’s do it a hundred times more?”

“Yes!”

By the time they left the waterfall, the moon had risen a third of the way to its peak. This was normally the time when Gary would go to bed. Today, he made a quick visit to Master Colorful to learn the second song. After that, he walked down the mountain, then turned towards the clan’s exit and started running.

The Gate Horse sleeping by the mountain foot opened an eye to watch him but said nothing.

Gary leaped off the cliff, sensing the familiar exhilaration that accompanied any excursion, then landed on the forest floor. His steps took him through the forest, onto a gravel road, into the city’s northern gate.

Nothing and no one on this floor could threaten the Shores of Glory. Therefore, its gates remained open at night. Gary flashed his medallion and underwent the usual inspection to go through. Once inside, he looked around to orient himself, then took off in a certain direction.

Before long, he arrived at a part of the city he’d never visited before. The buildings were large, rectangular, and dilapidated. Most were abandoned warehouses from a time when trade flourished. He hid in a corner and opened his chat.

Gary: Where are you?

The reply came instantly. Gary dove through the alleys, reaching a warehouse’s back entrance, then opened the small iron door to slide through. The inside was dark. He paused. “Hello?” he said.

A torch blazed to life, illuminating a broad, dark-skinned face. Its position indicated that the person was significantly taller than Gary. “Welcome,” it said.

Gary squinted. “Torga?”

“In the flesh.”

“What are you doing here?”

“Chloe and Asim hired me as chief of security.”

“I see. Well, it’s been a while,” Gary said. The torch got clasped to a handle in the wall, revealing the tall, wide, and shirtless black man. Dense musculature covered every visible part of his body, while his fists were hidden behind knuckles of wicked brass. The man’s aura was as intense as Gary remembered.

Torga Ba was a climber from Earth, one of the strongest in their drafting. Gary had originally met him just before the Floor of Trials ended, and the two had fought a close duel. That had been Gary’s first real defeat in this world. Since then, they’d only met in passing in the Bad Babes once.

Gary was certain he’d worked as hard as possible and run into many opportunities in his time in the Clan Floor. If asked, he’d claim himself as the strongest drafter of his generation, or at least close. Torga’s aura, however, felt powerful. Gary suddenly itched for a rematch.

“You’ve grown stronger,” Torga said.

“Same to you. Have you joined a clan?”

“I prefer to be alone.”

“I understand. I wanted to apologize about that time in the tavern. I was distracted by my own problems and said something stupid.”

“Don’t worry about it,” Torga replied, stepping aside to reveal another person. “This is Bob.”

“I’ve heard a lot about you,” Gary said.

“Likewise,” Bob replied.

This wasn’t just any Bob. He was Bob the Cultist, the dealer Chloe had recruited and who supplied many of Gary’s clansmen with Muscle Sap. He wore dark robes with the hood pulled back, revealing a completely non-descript face of short dark hair. He had an easygoing aura about him, found in his casual smile and relaxed eyes. Unlike what Gary expected, he did not smell of weed—not the tiniest bit.

“I’ve heard you’re in an interesting cult, Bob. Or, sorry—is that the wrong word?”

“Cult is right. We’re the Cult of the Broken Compass. The surfers of destiny. The hand of the true tower.”

“Right,” Gary said.

“Chloe and Asim are waiting at the meeting point. I will go to my location shortly,” Torga said. “If anything happens, Gary, I expect you to barge in there immediately. I will fall from the ceiling one second after you. Bob will run.”

“Then why is he here?” Gary asked.

“I felt the vibes,” Bob replied. When faced with Gary’s raised brow, he laughed. “Relax, my friend. Destiny knows where it takes me. I’m here for a reason, I just don’t know it yet.”

“Thanks anyway, Bob. You’re already taking a risk in helping us spread the Muscle Sap.”

“Speak only with code words,” Torga said.

“Don’t mention it, my friend. It’s a pretty path. An askew one, admittedly, but that’s always where a broken compass leads.”

“I’m going. We’ll be in touch,” Torga said. He slid the door open a crack and disappeared in the darkness. Gary couldn’t hear anything, but he knew the big man was scaling the walls to reach the warehouse’s roof, from where he’d scout the approaching parties. Chloe and Asim were already waiting in the main space of the warehouse, where the meeting would take place.

There was still an hour, though. Torga had insisted they arrived first. Gary waited in awkward silence with Bob the Cultist, who seemed content to twiddle his thumbs. At some point, he fished for a small booklet from his robes and started reading in the torchlight.

It was titled, Rebellion from Below.

“Isn’t that mine?” Gary asked.

“Is it? Chloe gave it to me from a friend. How wonderful that our fates keep converging.”

“I gave it to her because she said the doodles were similar to your cult’s. Come to think of it, I never heard back. Did you discover anything?”

Bob brandished the booklet. “This is great work,” he said. “The doodles are instinctive, yet clear. Whoever wrote it knew what they were doing. Where did you say you found it?”

“On a corpse.”

“I see.”

“A corpse I created, to be precise. But the man was an asshole and tried to kill me first. I hope that’s not a problem. He didn’t seem like you.”

“The right path is to die sometimes. As long as we leave ourselves in the hands of destiny, all will be as it should.”

“Okay,” Gary said.

“The person who doodled these vibes signed as Zink. Was that his name?” Bob asked, pointing to a set of four simple doodles on the last page, which Gary couldn’t decipher.

“I never got his name. I just call him ball guy,” he said.

“Well, I believe that’s him. Look here, and here, and here.” He flipped through the booklet, pointing to what seemed like random doodles on random pages. “Our holy writing is hard to decipher, but the little I’ve researched so far shows me that whoever wrote these understood they were part of destiny’s plans. They enjoyed their part, but they suspected they would die soon. In fact, they claim their death would be their real contribution to the tower’s destiny, kickstarting a series of inevitable events. Did killing this person change your course?”

“Tremendously,” Gary admitted. If he hadn’t killed the ball guy, he wouldn’t have earned advanced System access, he wouldn’t have gotten all the boss balls they used to run their Muscle Sap operation, and he wouldn’t have won the boss ball quest. The rewards of that quest had included the Temper Serum, without which he would have failed the third floor’s inheritance trial, if he even made it there.

“Hmm,” Bob said, flipping to another set of random pages in the booklet. He pretended to read the doodles while occasionally glancing at Gary. “Hmm.”

“What do you read in my fate?” Gary asked jokingly. Bob took it seriously.

“A great deal of things. You carry destiny, my friend, though how far it reaches is beyond me. One thing is for sure: you are meant to bring change. I suspect you already have. Your actions have great ripples.”

“What’s that supposed to mean?” Gary asked, but their conversation was interrupted by an urgent message.

Torga: Abort. They have Core Formation people. Abort.

Before Gary could react, the first message was quickly followed by others.

Torga: Negative. Do not abort. They have eyes on the area. We need to carry through.

Torga: Fuck. It’s the brothers. Chloe set us a meeting with the fucking brothers. Chloe, Asim, get out of there immediately. Run to the side room with Gary.

Gary’s aura roiled.

“Who are the brothers?” Bob asked.

“Remember that guy I mentioned? These are his friends,” Gary replied.

“Let’s hope they don’t figure out who killed their friend!”

“They already know, Bob, and they’re pretty fucking pissed. They know all of us. Shit.”

Another door barged open to let in Chloe and Asim, both wearing black masks that covered their face. Neither wore their clan colors. They barged in and closed the door behind them. “Shit,” Asim said, retreating to a corner.

“That’s what I said,” Gary said.

“I’m really sorry. I had no idea who they were,” Chloe said.

“How could you not realize the gang you were talking with were the brothers?” Gary asked.

“I’m sorry, okay? We only spoke through dead drops. I couldn’t know. Shit, I could have suspected it. We knew they had boss balls. It makes sense they’d look to sell them.”

“What do we do?” Asim asked.

“In a way, I feel this is fate. You have history with these people, and here you meet again. How fitting,” Bob said, drawing the glares of everyone present.

Gary cursed and quickly made a group chat with everyone. He hadn’t intended to reveal his advanced System access here, but now wasn’t the time to hold back.

Gary: Torga, speak to us. Can you see them?

Torga: They’re walking to the warehouse. One of my look-outs disappeared. I don’t see the assassin, but he is definitely here, and we can’t sneak out from under his gaze. They’ll find us. We need to hold the meeting.

Chloe: They know we’re close to Gary. They’ll recognize and kill us. You have to do it.

Torga: I’m a drafter, too. They will suspect our relationship and won’t let me go. Everyone, ideas please.

Shit, they know all of us. Shit, Gary thought.

Suddenly, everyone had the same idea at the same time. They turned their gazes to Bob.

“How wise is destiny,” he said, nodding slowly. “Then again, if it set up the problem and the solution, is it wise, or is it fair?”

Asim pushed a big bag into his hands. “Go!” he hissed.

“I don’t know what to say.”

“We’ll instruct you in the chat. Get out of here!”

Bob pulled up his hood, hiding his face in shadows, then walked out of the side room and into the warehouse proper. Through the open door, Gary spied stacks of crates and rotting furniture scattered in a mostly empty floor. Then, the door closed, and Chloe started setting up a complex device on the floor. When she was done, Asim extinguished the torch, sinking them into darkness.

Gary sent one final message.

Gary: Hey Master. I’m sorry to message you this late, but I’m trapped in the city, and my Core Formation enemies are just outside… Any chance you can come here?

It was a long shot. The messages didn’t wake people up, so if Colorful was asleep, he’d only read it in the morning.

Gary got no reply. Everyone was asleep this deep in the night. He sighed and settled down to wait. Could Bob the Cultist really outsmart the brothers?
