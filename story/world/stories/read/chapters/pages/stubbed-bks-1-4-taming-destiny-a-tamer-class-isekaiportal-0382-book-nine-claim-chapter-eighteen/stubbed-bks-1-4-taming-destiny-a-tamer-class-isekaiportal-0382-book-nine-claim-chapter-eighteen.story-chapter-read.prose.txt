As far as I can see, the youngest female raptorcat is engaged in an intense training session, using the balistas, cannons, and piles of rope as obstacles and sources of cover. She leaps, hides, and ambushes imaginary foes, swiping and biting at the air. As Captain Garous indicated, the sailors, whether free or collared, are avoiding that area with assiduity.

Tapping into the Bond between us, I feel a sense of intense concentration and frustration emanating from her. Though I don’t agree with the captain’s reasoning for bringing me over, I’m glad he did.

“I’ll speak to her,” I promise, nodding at the captain. He bows deeply in response without replying verbally.

I head down the stairs towards Ninja’s impromptu training area. Crossing some invisible boundary, I’m surprised when I have to dodge claws coming towards me. Maybe the sailors’ nervousness wasn’t entirely unwarranted.

“Ninja,” I call calmly tapping on the Bond between us and dodging another swipe and bite combo. Though, oddly, I notice that her attacks aren’t coming anywhere close. I would think that they were accidental misses if not for the intent expression in Ninja’s eyes. “Can you stop so we can talk, please?”

Reluctance pulses across the Bond, but the raptorcat pauses, returning all four paws to the deck.

“What’s the matter?” I ask gently instead of challenging her on why she’s chosen this spot as a training ground when training goes on every morning on my ship.

Reluctance once more emanates from Ninja and she looks away, her wings curled forwards protectively.

“If you tell me, then perhaps I can help,” I suggest, lowering myself to one knee in front of her and holding out my hand in invitation.

The raptorcat eyes my hand for a long moment and I wonder whether she’s going to take me up on it or not. And then she takes a small step forwards and leans her head against my hand.

Taking that as permission, I start scratching her gently behind her ears.

We stay in silence for a while and I continue to stroke her. Over time, I manage to convince her to lie down with her head in my lap – sitting on the deck isn’t the most comfortable thing, but it’s better than kneeling. Leaning against a box improves the situation further.

Around us, the sailors start approaching the area once more, retrieving objects that they’ve been barred from for who knows how long. Ninja ignores them, her emotions settling little by little as she relaxes into my touch.

I just wait patiently for her to open the conversation. I’ve made the offer and, like when stretching out my hands, I will let her choose whether to accept it or not.

Finally, I feel her side of the Bond stir from the settled state it had become. Not happy – I haven’t felt Ninja be happy since we lost Storm – but not as filled with frustration as it was when I arrived.

She sends a complicated package of emotions and pictures that takes me a moment to parse.

“You feel useless?” I ask, a frown carving a line between my brows. “Ninja, you’re far from useless.”

The response to my words is strong. Ninja practically shoves emotions and pictures at me. Since most of the pictures present a grey space I don’t recognise, it again takes me some time to decode them.

Finally, I work it out – the grey space I’ve never seen before is actually within my spatial tattoo. That’s interesting enough that my focus is almost derailed – I knew that my companions were able to interact with each other in the tattoo, but I never imagined it would look like this: almost a replica of Kalanthia’s old cave in the other world, only made of indistinct grey walls instead of brown rock.

But Ninja shifting impatiently brings me back to the situation at hand. Now I know the grey space is the tattoo, I’m able to put the pieces together.

“You were unable to help in the most recent battle, and you fear that that will be repeated going forward,” I summarise.

Ninja sends me reluctant agreement, and then contrasts her claws with the attacks that she saw go flying between the ships in the moments before I called her along with almost everyone else into the tattoo.

“You’re concerned that you have no ranged attacks?” I guess and Ninja confirms a moment later. “But Ninja, that’s just where you are right now. That doesn’t make you useless. Maybe you’ll develop a ranged attack when you Evolve. Or maybe not. And either way, it’s fine. Your strengths are in close combat – and there you shine. If we’d got to the point where Torrent’s crew boarded us, we’d have definitely valued your aid. And when we arrive on the Lost Continent, you’ll be more than useful again.”

Ninja accepts my words, but I feel that they’re not enough for her. Ever since Storm died, Ninja has been changing. And by this point, the differences are stark. She’s no longer the shy, nervous cub who preferred to hide in the shadows – and ambush others from them. Instead, she practically throws herself into battles – never recklessly, but with a grim determination that sometimes worries me. And not just me – Trouble and Nicholas’ Bonded, Malaya, separately expressed concerns about how hard she pushed herself during the siege on Zlona.

That she was sent to Zlona instead of being able to come with me probably was part of that, but I suspect it’s not the whole. It’s like she’s aiming for an impossible standard – and gets frustrated with herself when she’s unable to reach it.

Ninja sends me another collection of emotions and pictures, this time centred around her actions this morning.

“That’s why you’re here?” I ask with my eyebrows raised slightly. “Because you think that if you practice enough, you’ll be able to develop a ranged claw and bite attack?” My thoughts return to how her attacks had missed me. I was right in that it was purposeful.

Ninja agrees, a sense of shame at her failure creeping along the Bond between us.

I pull my hand away from where it has been stroking her gently, meeting her eyes when she turns her head to glare up at me.

“You’re smarter than that,” I reprimand her and see her put her ears back, her lips curling back slightly from her teeth. “You haven’t failed, especially if you’ve only been practising this for a few days. If anything, I would have been more surprised if you’d succeeded – I don’t even know if it’s possible for beasts to develop abilities without Evolving.”

I’m not trying to discourage her – just because I don’t know if it’s possible doesn’t mean it’s impossible. But at the same time, I don’t want her being so hard on herself. I’ve defied what others believe to be impossible multiple times, but I didn’t get there by berating myself.

“And whether it is or isn’t there are other ways you could help in a battle even now,” I point out.

‘There are?’ Ninja almost manages to say, the tentative hope in her communication heart-breaking.

“Sure, there are,” I emphasise. “As we’ve said, you’re excellent in close-range. You can fight better with your claws and teeth than most non-Classers can fight with their weapons. If you can’t use ranged attacks, then the most important factor in a battle for you becomes mobility.”

Ninja cocks her head to one side, sending confusion along our Bond.

“Mobility is your ability to move, simply speaking,” I explain. “And with magic, it doesn’t just come down to your speed in running.” I consider what I’m going to say carefully. Mobility doesn’t just affect Ninja and I have several options now that can help my Bonded to be more mobile.

“For example, you could ask Noir or Ivor to give you a lift, taking you out of one place and putting you in another where you can do more damage. Or I can come and you can jump into the tattoo and then jump out again later, where appropriate. And it’s not like we’re going to be at sea forever.”

Ninja seems to be considering that thoughtfully.

“Actually…maybe you can help me,” I continue slowly. Ninja cocks her head to one side, listening impatiently. “I’ve got a new Skill – Bond Teleport. I’m hoping I can use it to pull my Bonded out of dangerous situations. It might help you – and others – if I can figure out how it works.”

That is met with enthusiasm – as I thought it might be. I think the idea that she will be instrumental to helping me test something that might save other pack-mates is a bigger boost than any of my words have been.

Ninja agrees to return to our normal ship and we begin testing Bond Teleport. At least the deck isn’t too busy as the morning training has just finished.

It quickly becomes apparent that Bond Teleport is a bit of a mana hog. Not so much when Ninja is almost next to me, but the further the distance, the more the mana cost ticks up. By the time I’m pulling her from the opposite end of the ship, the demand is becoming noticeable, perhaps a tenth of my mana pool used in one instant. The length of time it takes Ninja to be teleported to my side also lengthens the further away she is, though the difference is only in seconds – at these distances, anyway.

Ninja doesn't experience any sort of delay or in-between state. Instead, for her, it’s like she blinks and everything around her changes. She found that disorientating at first, but soon learns to adapt.

In terms of its utility for battle, I’d say that it’s more of an emergency use than something I should build into my strategies. I have to put a surprising amount of concentration into summoning Ninja – it only works when I vividly visualise her appearing at my side and dedicate my mana to making that happen. And that would make me vulnerable for however long it takes depending on the distances involved. Plus, since I usually fight more with magic than anything else, it would reduce what I could contribute to a battle.

Unless, of course, I take on more of a support role and dedicate myself to leading from the back. That’s something I’m still not too keen on, for all that I know it’s strategically better at times and I know several of my Skills support it.

We’ve just got Noir and Ivor to join in, testing out strategies that involve them taking Ninja somewhere and then her signalling when she needs to be pulled out when I feel a sudden surge of excitement come through the Bond.

A moment later, Loran appears at the top of the stairs that lead below decks. His face is a picture of trepidation mixed with excitement.

I immediately stride towards him, sending a quick message to Ninja that she’ll have to wait a moment before I summon her again.

“What is it?” I ask urgently.

“I…I think I’ve developed an internal matrix?” he half-states, half-questions.

“You have?” I ask, surprised despite myself. Though I’d theorised it might work, I’m still taken aback that it has.

“You have?” repeats a higher-pitched voice, filled with even more excitement than Loran’s own. A clatter draws our attention and we turn to see Tiria hurrying towards us. In her wake is a yoyo that’s been knocked to the floor and an annoyed Keshaka whose spell was evidently disrupted by Tiria’s rapid abandonment.

Tiria reaches us within moments and she peers eagerly at Loran, as if the development of an internal matrix might be patterned on his face. “Can I cast some spells on you?” she asks, even as she lifts a hand that’s shining with white light.

Loran sends me a helpless look even as he takes a step backwards.
