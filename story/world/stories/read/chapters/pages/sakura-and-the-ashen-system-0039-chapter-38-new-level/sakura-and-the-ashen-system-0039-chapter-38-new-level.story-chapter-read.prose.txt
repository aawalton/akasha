The obsidian creatures sensed Sakura before she had even started down the slope. They were both already looking in her direction.

From the top of the hill, that felt especially unpleasant. Two black figures had frozen below in the swampy muck, looking as if they had been waiting for exactly this. They were small, but no less disgusting for it. Definitely shorter and smaller than Pampli, but still angular, with strange broken backs and sharp limbs. A dull crimson glow showed through inside their black bodies.

Sakura studied them more carefully from a slightly closer distance and realized she could not afford to hesitate. If she froze, started overthinking, or gave them the initiative, everything would spiral into disaster fast. And somewhere up on the hill, Gilbert and Pampli were watching. She could practically feel their gazes between her shoulder blades. She could not see them, but she was sure Gilbert looked cold and focused like a strict teacher, while Pampli was still offended that he had not been allowed into the fight. And she absolutely did not want to fall flat on her face in the mud in front of them in her first real solo training fight.

Sakura tightened her grip on the scroll case, then sharply put it away into her Inventory and raised her hand.

“All right,” she breathed. “Let’s do this.”

The first creature moved toward her at once. Sakura did not wait for both of them to rush her at the same time. She picked the closer one, focused, and immediately tried to activate [Devourer].

The black creature dropped low, as if it felt something very bad was about to happen. Sakura almost felt the skill latch onto it, but at the last instant the obsidian thing darted aside so fast that the lock broke.

“Damn!”

The creature lunged again immediately. It shot through the muck in short, violent bursts. Sakura had time to think that she had not expected that kind of agility from such a warped body. She barely managed to jump back before a sharp black edge cut through the air where her leg had been a second earlier. Dark water and mud splashed in every direction.

Easy. Stay calm. One more time.

She slid back, nearly slipping on the thick, wet bank, turned, and thrust her hand forward again. This time without the extra panic, not trying to force all the skill’s energy into one desperate last-second strike, but pressing it steadily into the target.

“Come on...”

[Devourer] latched on.

The creature jerked as if it had slammed full speed into an invisible wall. The crimson glow inside its body flared brighter. Sakura felt the familiar disgusting feedback, as if something alien and foully alive had started breaking right under her skin.

The obsidian creature crackled. Fractures spread across its shell, and crimson light burst out through them. Then it was simply gone. The black mass tore away toward Sakura in an ugly ash-dark vortex, and pain slammed into her arm with it. It was so deep and brutal that Sakura wanted to double over and scream. But she did not allow herself that.

Sakura squeezed her eyes shut for the space of a heartbeat, feeling the burning flare up again under the skin above her left wrist, where the dark petals of her strange mark already spread. Another one opened beside the others, digging into her flesh with hot, blunt pain.

But Sakura could not afford to dwell on it, because the second creature was already coming for her.

She opened her eyes just in time to see an obsidian maw almost in her face. She instinctively threw herself backward, and the clawed strike ripped through her sleeve instead of her throat.

“Oh, come on!”

This time there was no time to think. Sakura knew [Devourer] would not let itself be used several times in a row. The skill had a clear limit. So she activated [Inventory] at once. The scroll she had already set aside earlier appeared in her hand. Sakura barely had time to process it before she had already unrolled it and hurled the magic forward.

[FREEZE]

The frost hit instantly, locking around the obsidian creature’s legs first, then the body. Pale frost raced over the black surface, and a second later the thing was frozen mid-step in a grotesque, twisted pose.

Sakura sucked in a sharp breath.

“Yes...”

But it was too early to celebrate. Out of the corner of her eye she had already caught a short line on the scroll itself: on living targets, the freeze would hold for no more than thirty seconds. And even that, Sakura was sure, would not apply equally to every cursed thing in Veyr. Experience had already taught her that the magic in scrolls was never all-powerful. They gave you a chance, not a solution. Even that gardening scroll had only helped the tree recover for a while.

Thirty seconds.

“All right. All right. Enough.”

She pulled out the second scroll almost immediately, more confidently now, without the trembling that had been there before. Her fingers spread the paper open, and with almost angry relief Sakura drove the magic into the target before the ice could begin to weaken.

[DAGGER SWARM]

The daggers flew out in a dense, vicious swarm and crashed into the frozen obsidian creature together with the ice, shattering it into pieces. There was ringing and cracking and several other terrible sounds all blending into one unbearable burst, and then black fragments of the creature scattered together with the muck and the bloody glow.

After that, everything went still. Sakura remained standing alone in the marsh stink and obsidian shards. She did not come back to herself right away. She did not fully understand at first what she had just done.

“I... won.”

It sounded almost stupid. Her heart was pounding as if she had just run across half the world. Her palms were damp, and the arm with the new mark was still aching.

But she really had done it.

By herself.

A system window flashed before her eyes at once.

LEVEL UP.CURRENT LEVEL: 4.

PHYSICAL ATTRIBUTES UPDATED:Strength: 6Endurance: 8Agility: 8Constitution: 3Perception: 8

Chance of survival: 16.4%

Sakura froze. Then, unable to stop herself, she smiled wide.

“Yes.”

The System did not disappear. A new line appeared under the first one, and something eager stirred inside Sakura.

NEW SKILL UNLOCKED.[Suffocating Petals]

Sakura reread it in surprise several times.

“Suffocating... petals?”

The name was so strange that she did not even know how to feel about it right away. It sounded beautiful, horrifying, and very much in line with her new life.

Then another line appeared below it, much less poetic.

TYPE: ACTIVE SKILL.EFFECT: TARGET RESTRAINT AND COMPRESSION.

That was not entirely clear, but it was definitely interesting. At least there were no broken empty System windows this time.

Someone up on the hill snorted loudly. From the animal tone of it, she knew it was Pampli. Sakura turned back toward them. A little farther away stood Gilbert, as calm as ever, but she knew him well enough by now to catch the approval on his face.

She exhaled, looked back at the dead creatures, measured the scale of her training fight, then glanced once more at the patterns of the mark on her arm, the one she always had to hide under her sleeve, always risking that any search or random accident might expose her. What she felt now was not just relief, but a real, stubborn, living surge of strength.

She had gained a new level. And for the first time, she really felt how the stats lived their own life inside her body, how her muscles had grown firmer and more flexible, how more energy had settled into her.

And this, she thought, was only the beginning.

But Sakura barely had time to be happy.

Something splashed sharply in the swamp muck to her right, as if something small and very angry had just launched itself at full speed. She jerked her head toward the sound and barely caught sight of a new creature.

It appeared out of nowhere.

Smaller than the others, below her knee, with a stretched body and long thin limbs. A dimmer, more nervous crimson glow pulsed inside it, like a flickering spark. It reminded Sakura of a Tasmanian devil in both size and aggression. It did not charge head-on the way the others had. It attacked in short sharp bursts, changing direction mid-movement.

“Where the hell did you come from?!”

Sakura jumped sideways. The creature shot past so close that one sharp edge scraped the fabric of her pants. A little closer and it would have buried itself in her leg.

She spun around, trying to catch the darting, spring-loaded thing with her eyes.

Stay calm. Don’t panic. It’s smaller. That means weaker. But faster. Hell, a lot faster.

She focused on it as it tore through the swamp in short jagged bursts. A pale line of text flashed before her eyes for a moment.

OBSIDIAN SKITTERLevel: 4

Level four. Sakura exhaled through her teeth. So not a fatal disaster. At least not supposed to be. But the level said nothing about how nasty this thing was at jumping, latching onto legs, and ricocheting off surfaces like an expert parkour runner.

Then Sakura remembered. The new skill. There was no time to doubt. If the skill really worked through restraint and compression, she only needed one thing: enough time to aim.

And that was the problem.

The creature did not stop moving for even a second. It was already racing around her in an arc, coming in from the side again. Sakura glanced around fast and saw a cluster of large stones jutting from the muck a little farther off, like a low dark island.

She needed the height and the distance to buy herself even a couple of extra seconds.

Sakura bolted for them. Something splashed behind her again. The creature noticed the movement and rushed after her. Once, her foot nearly slipped on the slick edge, but she still hauled herself up onto the broadest stone. From above, it was a little easier to track where the black blur was moving.

“Come on,” she breathed, turning to face the swamp.

The creature sprang for the stone immediately. Sakura raised both hands without thinking. Something inside the new mark on her arm answered with painful but familiar heat. A dull crimson glow spread from her palms.

[Suffocating Petals]

With that red light, petals poured outward. Dozens of them, dark crimson and translucent, as if woven from blood and light. They burst from her hands in a wide swarm, spun in the air, and in the next second plastered themselves all over the obsidian creature from every direction.

The thing jerked violently, trying to tear itself free, but the petals were already clinging to its body, forcing themselves into the gaps between its plates, settling over its limbs, spine, and narrow glowing core. The swarm tightened and tightened until it was almost a cocoon.

The creature kept fighting, still trying to thrash free. Its clawed feet scraped desperately across the stone, but it did not help. The petals had fastened themselves to the obsidian body and were pressing in harder and harder, trying either to suffocate it or crush it.

Sakura felt it almost in her own fingers, as if she herself were drawing invisible cords tight. The obsidian creature began cracking louder and louder. The crimson light inside it fluttered wildly, then started to dim like the fading pulse of a heart.

The petals kept squeezing until the creature finally went still. One more second, and the black body gave out. Cracks spread through it, and then it burst into small shards that fell into the muck.

Sakura remained standing on the stone, breathing hard and staring down.

“No way,” she whispered.

She definitely liked the new skill.
