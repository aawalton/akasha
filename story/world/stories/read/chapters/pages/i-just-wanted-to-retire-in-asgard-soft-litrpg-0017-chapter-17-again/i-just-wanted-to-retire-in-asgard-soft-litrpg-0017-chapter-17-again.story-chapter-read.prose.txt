Barry stood his ground in the middle of the wrecked workshop and watched Thor's face change.

The silence dragged. For weeks Thor had been sizing him up, testing him, waiting to learn whether this outsider was any different from the mortals who ran the odds and folded once things got real. Now Barry could watch that hope die.

"You can say no," Thor said. His voice stayed low, almost gentle, with something sharp underneath. "You can stand there and say no while I am dying?"

"Yes," Barry said. "I can."

"Then you are a fool. Or just a simple coward."

"No, your god perspective doesn't matter Thor, and I am neither." He didn't shift his weight or cross his arms. His hands hung loose at his sides, the portal gun still holstered.

Thor kept closing towards the vortex, but his gaze still toward Barry. "You...you egotistical shit!" His voice finally broke out. "I tried to help you, I tried to defend you! When you enslaved a whole planet. You answer my loyalty like this? With your human pettiness?"

"You want a yes. You want me to help. But to do that I'd have to trust the people asking, and your mother cursed me the second we met. Your father watched everything I did in Jotunheim and decided it didn't count. So no. Not to you, Thor, but to your family, which hasn't done one thing to earn it."

Thor's jaw tightened. His hand drifted toward Mjolnir's haft and stopped just short.

"Careful what you say about my mother."

"Don't," Barry said, almost bored. "You're running on fumes already. Split your focus now and—"

The vortex cut him off.

It gave no warning. No rising howl, no pressure in the ears. One moment Thor was planted on the stone, and the next his boots left the floor and dragged him backward like a hook through the ribs. Loki, half-conscious against the far wall, saw him and lunged and caught his wrist as Thor's heel hung an inch from the ragged lip of the tear.

"What the hell is happening?" she said, looking at the vortex. "Stormbreaker," Loki said, her voice tight, her eyes open now. "Call it. Now!"

Thor gritted his teeth as he let Mjolnir fall on the ground, the tattoo on his wrist glowing.

"Stormbreaker! Come to me!" Thor roared and called the axe.

Barry saw the angle half a second early and almost smiled, knowing all would be solved now.

Stormbreaker came in fast, faster than anticipated and clipped Loki's hand on its way through.

"Ow!" Her grip broke. Thor lurched sideways, and his momentum carried him toward the void. For one long heartbeat the three of them hung there: Thor half over the edge, Loki's fingers bleeding, the axe spinning in midair as if it hadn't decided whose it was.

"Fuck, you gods really can't do anything right..." Barry didn't run the numbers. He raised the portal gun and fired into the heart of the vortex. "Look down!" he warned.

The blue jam of the portal melded with the vortex, pulling it within, and then...

DOOM!

Splash of white exploded all around the room. Blinding all three and the pull died like someone had flipped a switch.

Thor and Loki crashed to the floor in a heap of limbs and armor. Stressed wood creaked as it settled, and a faint hum lingered in the air. Barry lowered the gun and looked around.

The crystals were gone. So were the books, the divine alloy samples, and the dragon-shell plating he'd spent three weeks stabilizing. The vortex had swallowed most of it before he shut the thing down. His workbench lay on its side, tools strewn across the stone, and half the ceiling had been stripped to bare rafters since dust drifted in slow spirals, still looking for somewhere to land.

"Aaa..." Thor grunted as he pushed himself up and met his eyes. He looked at Barry and Barry looked at him. Neither of them spoke. There was nothing left worth saying.

"Is that normal?" Loki asked. She was staring at the far wall.

Where the vortex had been, a black spot had formed. Small and dense. Barry saw it as well, and immediately knew it.

"What the—"

He grabbed Loki and Thor both by the shoulders and hit the air-blast before either could finish the thought. Thor and Loki tumbled through the doorway. Barry, still inside, hauled the reinforced door shut and threw the secondary lock he almost never used. The bolts drove home with a sound like snapping bones.

The black spot didn't stay small.

It swelled fast, swallowing splinters, metal shavings, and broken glass, then the overturned bench, the tools, the air itself. Barry felt the pressure crush against his back for half a second. Then weightlessness. Then nothing.

"What! What happened? Why did he do that?" Thor took the door off its hinges with one swing of Stormbreaker. Wood and metal blew inward.

"Barry!" Thor called, his blue eyes looking for him, but the workshop was empty, and the black spot was gone. Nothing remained worth examining except scoured stone and the sharp, clean smell of burnt ozone. The walls looked sandblasted. The floor had been polished smooth in a perfect circle where the tear had been.

"Barry..." Thor called again. "BARRY!"

Far down the corridor, through the open doors of the throne room, Odin watched, his all-seeing eye watching everything in detail. He said nothing until the black spot finished its work. Then he smiled and murmured a single word to the raven on his shoulder, too quietly for anyone else in the empty hall to hear.

"Finally."

***

Barry woke face-down on something that wasn't stone and definitely wasn't soil. It gave under his weight like packed ash mixed with bone dust.

He coughed, tasted iron and his own blood, and pushed himself upright. Everything hurt, everything, in a particular way that meant several systems had gone offline.

Strength enhancement: dead.

Reflex overlay: dead.

Even the basic pain dampeners had gone dark. The hem of his jacket was still smoldering, so he stripped it off, flung it aside, and watched the last of the fabric curl into black flakes.

Then he breathed in, and stopped. The scent of dragon-shell. Divine alloy.

He was breathing his own work. Whatever the vortex had taken was here, ground down to particulate and drifting into his lungs with every inhale.

"So this is what I get for being a smartass savior," he said as he crouched in the ash.

He pulled off the goggles. Both lenses were cracked through, and he let them fall. The portal gun's casing had split along one side, and the charge indicator was dark. He squeezed the trigger. Nothing. He squeezed it again, because he was that kind of person. Still nothing.

By the last reading he'd gotten before the workshop came apart, there had been one charge left, and even that was gone now, burned off in transit or in the minutes he'd spent unconscious.

Dead weight. He holstered it anyway.

The mist was thick, very thick, and it left a film on his skin that smelled like wet graves. Skulls lay underfoot, some whole, some crushed to white gravel.

"Where the... where in the hell am I? Seriously, what is this place?"

Shapes moved through the mist that weren't skulls. They came without sound, no breathing, no footfalls, just the soft drag of bone on bone.

The first one stepped into view, and Barry went still.

It was wearing his jacket. His blue jacket. The same burn marks on the left shoulder from Jotunheim, the same scorched hem, the same broken zipper he'd never gotten around to fixing. He had just watched his own burn to nothing. The fabric of this one had half melted into the thing's ribcage and fused there, and the sleeves dragged on the ground because whatever wore it was shorter than him. Compressed. Hollowed out.

The second had a portal gun casing embedded in its chest, cracked along one side.

The third opened its mouth.

"Probability of survival: zero point zero..." The voice was almost his. Almost. It sounded like a recording played through shitty speakers, glitching at the end of every phrase, the System's flat cadence stripped of everything useful and left as an empty shape. "Zero point zero zero..."

Barry looked at them for a second. Just a second.

"Okay, fuck it." Then he started swinging.

One of them reached for him, and he kicked it back hard enough to fold its ribcage inward with a dry crack. Another came in from the left. He hit it and felt his knuckles split against the cold bone of its skull. The strength was gone. The enhancement layer that normally turned his punches into something close to a battering ram had vanished with everything else.

He kept swinging anyway. There were too many, and they wouldn't stay down. The ones he broke just pulled themselves back together and came again, jaws working, empty sockets locked on him with the patience of things that had already watched him die.

"What is this? Is this supposed to scare me? Huh? Whoever is doing this, show yourself! Be a man!" he said, while he put three down in ten seconds and still lost ground. A fourth caught his ankle and yanked. He went over hard, shoulder first, and teeth scraped across the leather of his boot.

"Shit!" he muttered as he drove his heel into the thing's face until the jaw tore loose, and it still didn't let go. More hands found him: wrists, collar, the back of his neck. The mist closed in. Above him the sky had no stars, only a flat, dirty gray that pressed down like a lid.

The one with the glitching voice leaned in close enough that he could make out the portal gun casing fused into its chest. The crack ran the same direction as the one in his. Same split. Same dead indicator.

"Zero point zero zero zero..."

"Yeah," Barry said, and hit it again. "I heard you the first time."

Then something fell out of that sky.

Not a meteor but a man. Golden, bright enough to look off in a place this dark. He hit the ground between Barry and the dead hard enough to crater the packed ash, and he started clearing them with an efficiency Barry, even half-concussed, recognized as trained.

No wasted motion, no flourishes. Elbow, knee, the edge of a hand, each strike finding a joint or a weak point in the bone and unmaking it. The dead surged at him in a wave, and he cut through them like wet paper. When the last one folded and stayed down, he turned.

He looked at Barry's face, and something shifted in his expression. It definitely wasn't recognition. His eyes ran over Barry once, sizing him up.

"You don't smell dead," he said, his tone neutral and poised.

"Duh, do you see me breathing?" Barry looked at the mist, the skulls, the starless sky. His lungs burned just from taking in the misty air.

"Am I in Helheim?" Barry guessed.

The golden man nodded once.

Barry closed his eyes, pressing hard, drew a breath that tasted of ash and divine alloy and three weeks of work. He spat to one side, wiped his split knuckles on his pant leg, and stared up at the empty gray.

"Fuck you! Odin!!!"
