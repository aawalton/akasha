Lina closed her eyes, expecting the agonizing torrent of a Rank 4 catalyst, but she didn’t get it. Instead, Hayden smoothly injected the purest catalytic pattern she had ever experienced into her core. The sensation was…

Sensual.

That was a terrible way to put it, but there was no other word to describe it. It felt like someone was drawing their nails on her bare back, smooth and sensitive, but not ticklish.

She enjoyed the feeling and took control, pulling in more energy with her core to increase the speed and pressure it was cutting her gate with.

Who knew how long she cut her gate, but it ended far too soon. She whined when it was over.

“That’s it?” Lina pouted.

“That’s it?” Kayline cried. “You just cut a Rank 4 catalyst! Or… whatever it was.”

“Yeah, I know, but it felt good.”

Hayden turned to Kayline. “Don’t worry. You’ll feel it soon enough. Please sit in front of Lina.”

Lina panicked at the implication, but she complied, following Hayden’s instructions to put her hands on Kayline’s back.

“To transfer a catalytic pattern, you’ll feed cora through your gate. It’ll create that pattern, and then you’ll release it. You currently lack the attraction skill to cut one correctly, but this little guy will do that for you.” Hayden patted Gluttony. “Now pull cora through your gate.”

Lina activated her core, drawing cora into her wind gate. It created a spiral that left her core and passed through her hands.

Kayline panicked when she felt the cora enter her body. She dreaded that Hayden gave the task to someone who didn’t know how their core worked twenty minutes ago. Yet to her shock, the sensation felt good. It was like scratching a light itch, but without the increased itching afterward. It increased in power gradually, and soon, it was over, leaving her feeling unfulfilled.

“Right?” Lina asked. “I wish it would’ve lasted forever.”

“Yeah…” Kayline said. “Does it feel good to use it?”

“Not really. It’s like giving a massage rather than getting one. It sucks.”

“I can’t believe we’re complaining at all. Didn’t we just burn like… a Rank 4 catalyst?”

The two giggled tiredly. Hayden could say whatever he wanted, but they owed him—a lot.

“That should be enough for today.” Hayden turned to Kayline. “Tomorrow, we’ll work on your core. So I want you both to be here at five.”

“Five?” Lina cried. “Can’t you do nine? No, eight. I’ll do eight.”

“No,” he said.

“Cruel.”

Hayden smiled, but he was unyielding. The next morning, he worked them to the bone in a private gym. He gave Lina exercises to strengthen her channels, and he taught Kayline how to spot the Halva particle and control it. Seeing it was easy, but controlling it was very difficult—

And dangerous.

Hayden brought in a plasma barrier to ensure that Kayline didn’t destroy the room as she worked on her attacks. Thankfully, she used a water spirit, so her attacks weren’t inherently destructive. That said, it was still dangerous. She usually made a water ball the size of a bouncing ball because it would balloon into a normal-sized attack, but Hayden had her make huge spheres to keep her focused. If she lost focus, her spirit’s attack would grow out of control and submerge the entire room in water.

It was a disaster.

One day, Kayline felt risqué and mischievous and wore a white undershirt. When her water ball broke above her, it soaked her white shirt and exposed the very top of her bra. She wanted to know what Hayden would do.

She imagined he’d just glance and look away boredly. What happened instead was worse.

Hayden touched the shirt with a clear line, and all the water in the shirt splashed out in a waterfall, leaving it dry, but the rest of her clothing soaked.

“Keep focused,” Hayden said. He said it emotionlessly, but she could tell that he was chastising her.

Kayline replied with a nod and a self-deprecating smile. It was a shameful ploy, but at least she understood that she was too deep in the friend zone to ever get out. No, in the teacher zone, or something. He might as well have been her dad. It sucked.

She vowed never to wear white around him again.

A week passed, and her control improved dramatically. Just as she was approaching a breakthrough, Hayden said—

“I’ll be missing practice tomorrow. So continue without me.”

—and left without discussion.

Never before had Kayline felt so much unexpected anxiety. It wasn’t just him leaving—it was his face. He had grown increasingly serious as time moved on, and both of them noticed.

“It feels like something big’s going on,” Lina remarked. “Doesn’t it?”

“Yeah, he’s been acting strange… You think the rift’s opening?”

“Probably.”

“You think we can actually help him?”

Lina sat down tiredly. “Probably not. Hayden can just hire A-ranks right now if he wants them. That said… I don’t think we’ll hinder him.”

“Unless the Sickles come after him,” Kayline said.

“Yeah, but the Sickles won’t touch him. The whole universe is watching him; there’s no way anyone will touch him.”

“True… but….” Kayline didn’t know why, but she felt deeply unsettled. In her mind, there was no way anyone would let Hayden get what he wanted. Any sensible government would obtain it first and use it as leverage against him. So, they would hire guilds to find it.

Things appeared calm on the surface—but it would doubtlessly get messy.

“Prepare for the worst,” she concluded. “Everyone’ll be after what he’s looking for. So we need to help him find it, and give him everything he needs before then. Alright?”

Lina nodded. “Alright.”

As the Iska sisters speculated on the rift, Jeela waited for Hayden in a restaurant to discuss the same topic. Unsurprisingly, he entered the room with the same wooden expression as usual. She expected it, but given the circumstances, it was particularly aggravating.

“You would think you’d be at least the slightest bit nervous,” she remarked.

“Never show your emotions or feelings,” Hayden said as he sat at the table. “What is the status of my resources?”

Jeela’s cheek twitched. “Ready for deployment,” she said. “If…”

“If what?”

“You tell me the real date the rift opens.”

“The day after the expo.”

Jeela slapped the table and stood, rattling the porcelain dishes and cutlery. “Are you serious? You’re bringing galactic leaders to a red planet when the rift opens?”

“Correct.”
