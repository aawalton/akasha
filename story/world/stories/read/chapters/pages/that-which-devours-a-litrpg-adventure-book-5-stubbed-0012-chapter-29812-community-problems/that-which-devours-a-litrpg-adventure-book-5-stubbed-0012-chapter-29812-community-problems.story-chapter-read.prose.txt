Someone laughing loudly near Doc stole my attention away from Abby, which was a good thing. Admitting to oneself that you were willing to fight someone, potentially to the death, to take their territory, should cause some kind of emotional response.

Yet, it kind of didn’t.

No concern. No worry.

It was just another fight on my path to growing stronger, and moving up the divinity ladder. It didn’t matter, somehow. Whoever it was would be a Rank Three, and I’d get it done.

Abby shifted in her seat, her head twisting to look at Doc.

That laughter rose again, cutting through the noise.

“Not again,” muttered Abby, getting to her feet.

Doc frowned at a lady who had leaned across the counter closer to him. He shook his head at whatever she’d said, and crossed his arms.

[Kelly, Drunk, Woodworker, Level 48, Prey, Unknown.]

I stood and focused on her.

Suddenly her spine straightened, and she turned to look in our direction. Her brown eyes met mine, and her mouth dropped.

I shook my head.

Her face went pale, and she stumbled away from the counter. Someone nearby called her name, and she twisted in that direction.

Doc nodded at me with a tight smile.

I held up my glass in his direction, and he waved at me to come back to the counter.

Abby glanced back at me, then at Kelly, who stumbled away with the help of her friends. She joined me as I headed back to Doc.

“She’s going to be a problem,” said Doc, as he refilled my mug. “Believe me.”

“That’s saying a lot,” I said, to rib him just a little. I remembered him wanting my bottle of moonshine more than anything.

He nodded again.

“I need to figure out what to do with her,” mumbled Abby.

“How often do you have parties like this?” I asked, thinking about the booze.

“Once a week,” said Doc, motioning to the bottles. “That’s it. It takes a long time to make these.”

“We do community dinners more than that,” added Abby. “We rotate those who cook, and those who harvest from the farm.”

“You aren’t cooking all the time?”

She shook her head. “I don’t get as much of a boost from it since my profession changed, and I’m focusing on my class more anyway.”

The golden shields came to mind.

“I bet you and Dad make a killer team together,” I muttered, thinking about the synergy between their classes.

“We haven’t had much of a chance to work together,” she said, before snapping her mouth shut and turning a bit red. “Time for me to cut myself off.”

Doc laughed, as I just shook my head.

“So, how are you gonna solve the problem of us outgrowing this area?” asked Doc.

Abby glared at him.

“What? It’s a problem that all of us are thinking about.” He took her mug and tossed it into a bin. “Well, those of us who are paying attention and not sticking our heads in the sand. I mean, isn’t that why the prefab housing hasn’t all gone up?”

“No, that's because people are too scared to leave the cavern,” she answered.

“Scared?” I asked, interrupting the two in what seemed to be a common argument.

“Of the storms, other beings, anything that might bite at them,” said Doc, with a frown. “It’s crazy we have been given a second chance at everything, and people are hiding.”

“Again, that’s a little rich, coming from you,” I said, thinking of the compound.

Abby muttered under her breath about how not everyone wanted to fight, but I still heard it.

“That’s what I mean,” said Doc. He leaned closer to me. “I know I’ve fucked up in the past, but I’ve started over, and now I want the world. I want people and cities, maybe even a little bar.”

“Kabi’s world has a small marketplace in the dungeon, which is pretty interesting. You might be able to set up shop there for a bit.” Others had mentioned megacities, but I hadn’t been to one yet. “There is an entire universe out there.”

“Now, that’s what I mean,” he smiled, the expression lighting up his face.

Abby shook her head. “I’m going to get some sleep. Alex, do you want me to show you to a unit that’s free? It’s the same one as before.”

“I remember where it is, thanks.” I said. I sipped more of the cherry drink with glee.

Doc started packing up stuff, and wiping down the counter.

I wandered away from his counter and several people waved, including Mary and Hawk.

Yet, I couldn’t bring myself to head in that direction. Instead, I wandered through the pathways between the houses until I was out of sight. Then, I leaped up onto the almost-flat roof of the building I was next to. I slowly returned to the plaza, but stayed hidden.

Listening to the voices, but not needing to interact with any of them, felt right.

I just didn’t have anything I wanted to say. Instead, my thoughts went to Strange. The connection between us stretched out toward the portal to the north. Hopefully, I could find a way to get him back, even if he was having fun.

He still had a dang quest involving me.

The stars sparkled overhead, and I wondered how Lenna and Asceto were doing with the jungle folk. She had a ton to deal with as well. Hopefully, she wasn’t as antisocial as I felt at the moment.

Each breath reminded me that I didn’t belong on this world, no matter how much I tried to ignore it. It was like a small itch I couldn’t reach.

My pocket in my pants lit up, and I pulled the long-forgotten communication stone out. Lenna’s voice spoke. “Hey Alex, can you get a ride from John to my village?”

“Sure.” I sat up, before asking, “With the shuttle?”

“Yeah, I’m joining you on your adventure to get Strange.”

I blinked, then just chuckled to myself. “I’ll ask. When do you need me there?”

“Tomorrow, if that works,” she replied.

“I’ll check.” I quickly jumped off the roof as carefully as I could, and used my radar to find my brother. Given that there was someone in the workshop, I went there first.

John stood next to a table with a sled-like object sitting on top of it. “Come on! You should work,” he said to the inanimate object.

“John,” I said, interrupting him.

He jerked up and hit his hand on the handlebars. “Alex. What’s up?”

Before I could answer, he leaned back down with a tool in one hand.

I stepped closer, careful to not touch anything. The air was heavier here. Switching to my second sight, I saw that more mana drifted through the air here then outside. I asked the question I’d come to ask. “Can I get a lift to Lenna’s village tomorrow morning?”

“Sure, it’d be early, though. I want to do a pass to the far east. I’m slowly mapping out that area, and need to recharge my drones that are working in that quadrant.” He glared at something in front of him.

Mana gathered around his hand, then covered part of the machine.

“There it goes…” he muttered, before glancing back up at me. “Anything else?”

“Not really,” I said with a smile. “Just watching. I love what you’ve done with your class.”

He wiped a hand across his face, leaving a smear of something. “I wouldn’t have tried it if you hadn’t kicked me in my ass. Metaphorically, of course.”

I snorted. “I mean, I could do it literally, if you wanted. It might hurt.”

“No thanks, I’d rather not have my sister accidentally kill me while goofing off.” He patted the machine on the seat. “These bad boys are my newest changes. The hovercraft taught me so much, you have no idea. They use so much less energy now, and other people can ride them.”

“Okay, what do I need to do to ride one?” I asked, leaning closer.

John shrugged. “Tomorrow we can take them north instead of the shuttle, if you want.”

“Yes, please!” I said. Now, that would be a blast.

“Alright. I need to finish this up, then, and get to bed,” he said, as he turned back to the hover bike.

Smirking, I headed back into the darkness. Light still flickered from the dying fire in the center of the plaza, but there were way less people milling about. Doc’s station was gone, but Hawk remained at a table by himself, watching the stragglers.

I joined him, walking silently across the area.

No one else noticed me, but his eyes flickered in my direction.

“Good to see you back,” he said, paying attention to the two tables with people still sitting around them.

One of them had Jaxon and Mars, along with a few people I didn’t know. The other was full of unknown folks.

“I see you’re still dealing with drama from before,” I said, as I sat down.

“Not exactly,” he muttered.

Jaxon shook his head and slammed his mug on the table. “I shouldn’t have joined the ship.”

All three of the others at the table went silent.

“Why couldn’t I have died like my parents?” his words came out almost as a sob.

My stomach clenched as Hawk stood, but my hand touched his shoulder, settling him before I walked over to the next table.

“Luck and chance,” I said, before I could stop myself. I wanted to help, even knowing he didn’t deserve anything from me.

Everyone at the table froze as I sat.

“And now, you need to decide what you want to do about it.” Walking the line between being harsh and being motivating was difficult, but I was trying.

“Alex…” his wild eyes stared at me, and he let go of the mug, spilling it across the table. The alcohol lingered in the air.

“Jaxon,” I replied in an even tone. “There is so much more out there, you have no idea.”

“I don’t want this,” he said. Tears gathered in the corners of his eyes, and his hands clenched and released multiple times.

“Then change it.”

“But my class is broken,” he replied, and his voice cracked again.

“That’s an excuse,” I replied. This time, my voice came out a little colder. “If it's broken, fix it.”

His mouth gaped like a fish.

“John changed his class into something usable. Learn skills that are a better fit, go to the academy and listen to what the professors tell you. It is doable.” I softened my voice. “Everyone can change their class or profession. It takes hard work and time, but it is doable. Anyone who says otherwise is trying to mess with you. That is our advantage. We work hard to accomplish what we want, and if we work hard, we can accomplish anything.”

Jaxon stared, then he nodded. “What is out there?”

I smiled, and his eyes went a little wide. “Anything you can imagine. More worlds with different environments and creatures than in your wildest dreams. Megacities bigger than anything from Earth.”

“Really?” asked Mars.

“Yes, really.” Glancing around the table didn’t give me much. I turned back to Jaxon. “You can do this. I believe in you.”

“I’ll do it.” He swallowed hard and stood, but wobbled, definitely still drunk. “Go to the academy.”

“In the morning, dude,” said Mars, with a nod in my direction.

I left the table, but didn’t head back to Hawk. He gave me a sharp nod before I vanished around the corner.

Jaxon hadn’t earned that mercy, but the power difference between the two of us was such that I could afford to be kind. It wasn’t like he could hurt me anymore, I was so far beyond his petty taunts and physical threats that it was laughable.

Hopefully, he could get the help he needed, and not just with his class. Survivors' guilt lingered inside most of those from the ship, and it was hard. I had some experience of that, too.

I slowly headed to the housing unit that I’d used last time. Taking a hot shower was at the top of my list, followed by a comfortable bed.

Footsteps followed behind me, and I paused in the shadows, waiting for them to catch up.
