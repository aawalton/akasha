Alright.

Okay.

Enough moping about, Hart. This wasn’t the end of the world. You may currently be medically paralyzed because several of your bones were outright ripped from your chest, but you weren’t down and out. You had options, and they didn’t include just lying about while the world moved on without you.

I had just practiced one of them earlier today. It was, admittedly, going to suck a bit.

But whatever. I’d deal.

As much as I was able to, I breathed out and concentrated. With a mental trigger, I activated Umbra Gemina Exactoris, and one of my Sprites popped into existence, floating in place to the left of my bedridden self. Fade twitched at the sudden appearance of the tiny, fae-like being, but I wasn’t done.

I, as the Outer, apologized to Middle and Core for what I was about to do, but they understood. I always understood.

In the same way I had transported my mind to the Sprite I had left with Aveline earlier, I used the evolved aspect of The Lattice of Heave and Earth to transfer my Outer Lattice to the Sprite. Unlike that time, I still had the strength to transform the Sprite…

Into a full-sized clone of myself. An uninjured one.

That was a small quirk I had noticed about my clone creation Skill. A quirk that I was currently very thankful for. No matter how injured I was at the moment of creation, there was a baseline state that my clones seemed to pull from. My current working theory was that I actually did have an underlying blueprint to what a healthy state looked like for me. That was how the Preceptors regrew limbs and lost extremities for most people. But the theory of the Gyreite Church was that the template that allowed one’s Status to accelerate the baseline healing rate afforded to Awakened individuals was corrupted in some way with Precursors. That was why they had not been able to apply the Rite of Regeneration to my missing arm last year.

But I thought it was still working fine. I still healed up just fine from minor injuries on my own, given time. No, I think my template still existed…it just wasn’t accessible by anything but the System itself.

In other words, Zheng Wei’s Rite of Regeneration didn’t have the admin privileges that an actual, System given Skill did.

And so, when I transformed my Sprite into an outright Clone…I was in perfect health.

If not at half-strength.

Every single clone I created took away half of my level, after all. I was only barely able to use Mana at all, right now. I was likely only as strong as I had been right after I had arrived at Hinaga, in the aftermath of slaying Rhazal.

Not only that, but I was strangely alone in my own mind. Neither Middle or Core could come with me into this clone. We had experimented in the past and discovered that I couldn’t fit more than one Lattice into a clone at once. There just wasn’t enough space within the Aetherial construct that was one of my clones’ minds.

Besides, they needed to stay with the main body anyway. They needed to maintain it while I was injured. It would be a bad idea to leave my flesh sack behind with no controlling mind left within it. That was something I had never been brave enough to test.

It felt too much like death to even consider.

I breathed in deeply with the false construct lungs of my clone, and couldn’t tell the difference from real ones. Now that I was up and about, I took a look around at the tent, and quickly found my affects lying discarded in the corner. This clone was in nothing more than a thin white tunic and black trousers, but it would have to do.

My actual clothes, including my enchanted, armored coat, were soaked through with blood. My blood.

I…did see the shattered remains of my destroyed extendable spear, lying on the stone next to it all, though.

I stopped, briefly, just to consider it.

This was…probably a wake-up call for me. Daffyd had swatted aside the last of what I had considered my signature weapon like it was nothing more than an irritant. It didn’t matter that the blade and mechanisms themselves were forged from Oninite. I was moving up into levels of strength and power where even a small amount of structural weakness, brought about from complexity, was too much. My fighting style needed to adapt to those circumstances, and that meant…

I needed to retire the design. I’d…come up with something else when I had the time.

For now, I removed what I privately called my utility belt from the pile and strapped it onto my waist, adjusting it. Terractus on my right hip, and the goddamned laser gun you didn’t use on the left hip.

I huffed an irritated sigh at myself.

There was that arrogance again. It had been overconfidence that had nearly gotten me killed, as much as it had been the level difference.

Shaking my head, I slipped on my boots, picked up my staff, and nodded my head.

Time to go find out what was going on.

Turning away from my discarded jacket, I found Fade considering me from next to my body. He briefly shook his single antlered head. “These things always creep me out.” He admitted to me, falling into step as I opened the canvas flap of the tent. “It’s like they’re you, but not you.”

“Better get used to it, bud,” I muttered to him, eyes scanning the world outside my makeshift hospital room. “I’m going to be like this for a while. Now, c’mon. Time to go find the others.”

Stepping into the dim light of the Long Road, I was enveloped in the chaos happening in the camp around me.

And it was chaos.

Like I’d thought, this looked like an encampment of the Unchained. We appeared to be in a large underground cavern of some kind, but not a natural one. This had been carefully excavated and then reinforced. This place was easily as large as one of the city blocks of Rhoscara, likely just as big as the tannery district that the expedition had been camping within. It stretched far enough into the distance that I could easily make out the rows upon rows of thick stone pillars holding up the roof, hundreds of feet above me.

And nearly every inch of it was packed.

Tents, and carts, and wagons were stuffed in here so densely that there was only a small amount of room to slip between them. To make matters worse, there were so many people down here that not all of them had a place to stay. They loitered in the spaces between, looking frightened and overwhelmed from the circumstances they had found themselves.

Those were the people of Rhoscara, of course. The ones that could be saved, that is. There must be thousands of them down here, perhaps even tens of thousands.

That was…only a fraction of the population of the city, though. I didn’t need to imagine just what might have happened to the rest of them.

Anguis had told me his plans, and I had seen the results.

But there was far more than just refugees among the Dwarves of Rhoscara. There were the warriors.

Mainly Human and Elven, from what I could see, with a number of grim looking Dwarfs thrown in for good measure. From where I stood, I thought most of them, aside from the Elves, were members of the Unchained. Former slaves, or ideologically motivated people from Herztal who had heard the cry of freedom for the bonded, and chose to stand in the face of tyranny. It didn’t matter where they came from.

What mattered is that they did.

These were my people. Just as much as I did with the Order of the Polaris Reach, I felt an immediate kinship with the hard-edged, almost raw sense of the soldiers of the Unchained. If but for a few decisions made, I would have been right there in the middle of things with them. I wouldn’t have even minded taking orders from Bleddyn.

It…comforted me to finally be in the midst of them. It let me realize that while I might have lost the support of Rhoscara, in the battles to come…

I wouldn’t be disappointed to fight alongside the Unchained.

But I had other responsibilities than just standing here and basking in the kinship I felt with my fellow former slaves.

I had my own people to see to.

And I was guessing I would find out what had become of them if I found where the leaders were. Both mine and others.

I stepped out into the teeming mass of soldiers and refugees, and did my best to flag down one of the soldiers scurrying past, his burly arms full occupied with an overstuffed crate larger than he was.

“Excuse me, do you know where-”

“Ain’t got no time, ask someone else,” The man grunted, barely even glancing at me before he pushed his way through the crowd.

Alright…he was busy, it was understandable.

I tried again.

“Pardon me, but-”

“Piss off, mage boy,” The woman loitering in the open doorway of her own tent eyed the amber crystal of my staff evilly. She took a long, slow drag of her pipe as she shifted her eyes my way, and I got the distinct impression I was inches away from being stabbed.

I decided to leave her be. It was…understandable, I suppose, for former slaves to be wary of Magi. The slave bond was a product of Mana, after all.

I…received a similar reception from the next few people I tried to ask, as well. People seemed to be on edge, and I suppose I didn’t blame them.

That left me with a problem, though.

I didn’t see anyone from the expedition in the chaos around me, and I didn’t think I was likely to get help from these people.

I sighed, the sound lost in the noise about me, and looked down at Fade below me. “I don’t suppose you know where everyone is, do you?”

I didn’t even bother using my actual voice to ask the question. He probably would have heard it, but I wouldn’t have.

“Sure do,” Fade said nonchalantly. There was a slight mischievous slant to his wolven lips that I couldn’t help but notice.

I scowled down at him, aware of just how ridiculous we must look.

This was Sylvia’s fault, I just knew it. That woman loved her little mischiefs, and she was a terrible influence when it came to them.

“And why didn’t you say anything?”

Fade just shrugged his furry shoulders at me and then nodded his head off to the right. “Just felt like it. C’mon, follow me. They have this command center further in, and all the important people are always hanging out. At least, they have been for a while.”

Privately, I thought he had delayed me for...other reasons. Fade wasn't a child anymore, nor was he naive. In his own way, I think he had wanted to me to see that...well.

Things weren't over. As badly as the battle for Rhoscara had been going-

(Gone?)

-we hadn't been broken. Bent, battered, and deboned, perhaps.

But not beaten. He'd let me wander around the game purely to see that we were still alive. That thousands of people had survived the chaos, and we...we had helped that to happen.

Let him have his little machinations, though. I didn't begrudge it.

I followed after Fade as he started weaving his way through the crowd, people almost instantly making space for the large, frankly intimidating-looking wolf. Fade was up to my waist at this point, and that was when he was on all fours.

Any indignation I felt faded as I followed him. I understood why he’d actually played his little trick. In the end, all I could do was tell myself that it was always darkest before dawn…but pithy little sayings wouldn’t bring back the thousands and thousands of people Anguis had slaughtered.

I sighed, a single thought floating through my mind.

Some of the things Fade had told me had made me suspicious.

I’m…not sure, just how long I had been in a coma. I suspected it was longer than I might think. Everything I saw around me was very much a temporary emplacement, if it was even stable in the first place. The kind of thing that could be packed up in a matter of minutes if push came to shove.

All these refugees, my expedition among them…might have just stopped on the move. I certainly didn’t see any entrance up to the surface, anywhere on the walls, far from where I stood in the midst of everything.

And the Long Road itself stretched out, seemingly never-ending, in two opposite directions.

I…think I had missed quite a bit while I was out.
