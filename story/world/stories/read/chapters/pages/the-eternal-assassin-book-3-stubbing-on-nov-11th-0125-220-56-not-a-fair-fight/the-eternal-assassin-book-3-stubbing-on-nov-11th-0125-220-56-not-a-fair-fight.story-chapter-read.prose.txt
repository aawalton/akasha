The world flipped upside down without warning, and Asher yet again reflexively tried to slip into the astral and escape whatever grabbed him before his brain caught up. Cursing, he used Distortion to elongate his mithral dagger, slashing out and severing the root before it could drag him who knew where.

Expecting to land in the water with a splash, Asher nearly stumbled and tripped when he discovered the knee-deep water was now only up to his ankles. Shaking his head at the annoyance of fighting in a realm where even the terrain shifted around him, he tracked the root back to its source, eyeing up the tree that had definitely been a regular-old tree just a few moments ago.

Now, the tree in question was in the middle of tearing its roots out of the ground, dragging them out of the water and pointing them directly at Asher with clear intention as to what it was planning. He half expected to see some sort of demonic face appear on the tree and for it to start laughing maniacally at him, but nothing of the sort happened. Instead, once all its roots were free, it attacked.

Root after root was thrust directly at him, and even though their points didn’t look all that sharp, there was enough force behind every attack that Asher was all but certain a single hit would mean broken bones at the very least. Rather than let that happen, he focused on Distortion, using the skill in short bursts to nimbly compress space and dodge out of the way of the different attacks. Each time he narrowly avoided having a root slam into him, he slashed down with his daggers, slicing the tips off before focusing on the next.

I’ve never fought a tree before… he thought as he continued evading and pruning his silent assailant. Sap bled from the many wounds he was inflicting, mixing with the shallow water and driving the shimmering fish that were suddenly a lot bigger into a feeding frenzy. Despite the increasing number of root chunks falling all around him, he had no way of knowing how effective any of his attacks really were. He didn’t even know if Sever the Thread functionally did anything at all in this realm, either, as creatures seemed capable of changing at the drop of a hat.

Just as he was nearing the end of the roots, the wild realm surprised him again as he felt something latch onto his ankle. Glancing down after narrowly dodging another thrust from the tree, he frowned at the sight of the glistening fish. After eating the sap from the tree, each of the fish was now about the size of his fist, and had teeth like that of a piranha. They were big enough that they struggled to even swim through the shallow water, but it was clear that the entire school had his flesh in their sights as they flopped toward him.

“This realm is ridiculous!” he complained to no one in particular as he kicked off the fish latched to his ankle and took a giant step back with Distortion.

Walking right off the cliff that hadn’t existed moments ago.

Screaming, Asher spun around, his eyes going wide as they took in the few hundred feet between him and the ground. Reaching into his rift, he yanked out his potion of Feather he’d only just purchased from Polly, downing it in an instant and letting out a sigh of relief as his fall immediately slowed to non-lethal speeds. Taking advantage of his now-leisurely fall, he began to think.

I can’t fight anything in the wild realm on my own… The moment I take my eyes off my surroundings, the dangers change. No wonder rule number one was never enter the wild realm alone, I need people just to stand there and keep an eye on things!

I either need to come up with a way of keeping things stable around me, or I need to focus purely on running away from any threats that appear.

The first thought that came to mind was the artifact he’d picked up from Talon’s corpse back in Vildelph. He had a hunch the ability to declare an area of land around him as his domain just might prevent the realm from twisting it into something else, as he’d technically be monitoring the whole domain at all times.

Unfortunately, he’d just given that artifact to Samantha a few hours ago.

His next idea was to try using Spatial Lock to basically the same effect. In fact, that would probably work even better, as it would be him using his own skill rather than a borrowed one. The only downside was that that plan ran into the same issue as hiding within his Personal Rift. Keeping Spatial Lock up and active took energy, and if anything attacked its walls, he might suddenly find himself completely drained and helpless in the face of a powerful monster.

He supposed he could try-

Something slashed across his back, snapping him out of his thoughts as he focused on the here and now. Thankfully, his serpent leathers were tough enough to keep the surprise attack from doing any actual damage, and Asher blinked as he realized what it was that had tried to cut open his spine.

A falcon with the face of a snake, hissing at him as it banked around for another strike. Despite the fact that the bird-snake didn’t have any talons, and in fact appeared to lack legs of any kind, the wings had a metallic sheen, similar to how Crush’s body looked when he used his Iron Body skill. Seeing that, Asher shook his head at the ridiculousness of the situation.

Here he was, fighting a one-on-one with a half-metal snake-bird while slowly falling off a cliff that the world itself had surprised him with.

The wild realm truly was wild indeed.

Unable to really dodge with Distortion while falling, Asher instead elongated his mithral dagger, waiting for the bird to finish banking and come at him. As soon as it started its dive, he compressed space between the two of them, timing his slash perfectly and cutting the freaky creature perfectly in half. Before he could celebrate, however, a flock of identical snake-birds flew out of a nearby cloud, hissing in unison as they dove at him all at once.

Asher desperately tried to slash and dodge his way out of the maelstrom of sharp wings and pointed fangs, but he’d never been all that great at taking on large groups of enemies all at once. And with the bird-snakes attacking him from all angles, even coming straight down at his head and flying up from underneath, he simply wasn’t able to get away unscathed.

Focusing on protecting his head, Asher hissed right back at the snake-birds as he felt countless fangs punch straight through his serpent leathers, before being ripped right back out as the monsters’ momentum kept them flying past. At the very least, his leathers kept his skin and muscles from getting torn to shreds from the repeated wing strikes.

Lopping off a wing here and a head there, Asher took care of his foes one by one. By the time he landed, panting and covered in blood from both himself and the snake-birds, he was exhausted.

He’d had to burn Complete Resilience’s second tier effect twice during the battle, purging the venom injected from the snake half of the birds whenever he felt it starting to build up too much.

Taking in his new surroundings, he let out a sigh of relief at the realization that he was now in a flower-filled meadow. There were a few creatures that looked like extra-fuzzy deer curled up here and there, but they didn’t spare him so much as a passing glance, content to simply lay there and lazily munch on the surrounding flowers. Thankful for the break in all the chaos, Asher turned and kicked one of the many bird chunks now lying all around him.

“These things don’t even drop shards!” he complained, freezing as he was suddenly hit by a new idea.

The key to surviving the wild realm was keeping one’s surroundings from changing. Seeing as he was alone, he couldn’t look at everything at once. And he’d already ruled out using his own skills.

But what about using a skill he’d borrowed from someone else?

I really hope this works… he thought, focusing on the massive number of shards within his soul and manifesting a small sliver of them before him. Though rather than manifest an orb with whatever number he wanted, he tried something he’d never done before.

He manifested a single shard. And then another. And another.

Asher quickly manifested a large handful of individual shards, slightly annoyed at the fact that he wasn’t able to bring them all out at once like this. If he wanted to manifest twenty shards, his soul would always spit out a tiny, crooked orb of twenty shards, even if he wanted them to be a big cluster of individual shards. It was annoying, and tedious to continue to manifest one at a time, but for his plan to work, it was what was required. As soon as he finally had two large handfuls of single, tiny violet shards, he spun around in a circle, hurling them in all directions with all his might.

The shards scattered in every which way, not moving like an ordinary object would and seeming to lose all their momentum within a few dozen feet as they slowly drifted to the ground. That was quite alright, however, as it was his immediate surroundings he was most worried about. Nodding at the scattered shards, Asher focused on the second skill he currently had his Permanence locked on.

Shard Detection. A tier 2 skill that allowed him to sense any and all shards in close proximity to him.

Just as he’d hoped, his awareness stretched out across the scattered shards, keeping the location of each and every one of them highlighted in the back of his mind. He waited a couple of seconds to see if the shards located behind him in his blind spot would move or vanish, and he let out a quiet cheer as they remained in place, not being whisked away by some brand-new terrain popping in directly behind him.

“Thank God,” Asher muttered, letting out a sigh of relief as he kept his focus on the flower-field before him. Even if his immediate surroundings were finally locked down, that didn’t mean he wanted the calm, pleasant field before him to suddenly transform into an ocean of rolling lava filled with skeleton pirates, or whatever other insane things the wild realm was capable of. There had to be some sort of rhyme or reason as to what the wild realm was conjuring, but according to everything he’d read, nobody had ever managed to figure it out. There were certainly plenty of theories, like that the wild realm was the scattered remains of the old material realm destroyed in a battle like none other, or it was the domain of an ancient, powerful wizard who’d tried to create their own world. But as far as he could tell, nobody truly knew anything.

Asher tried to come up with his own idea as he rested, watching the gentle deer with far too much fur munch on flowers from their cozy nooks. One of the thickest ones relatively close by eventually ran out of flowers, letting out an angry bleat like a sheep as it rotated its head around, confirming it couldn’t reach any more without standing up. Bleating again, the fuzzy deer slowly got to its feet, revealing that the creatures had short, squat legs that looked incredibly out of place.

As well as the fact that it had been lying on top of three large, equally fuzzy eggs.

Asher stared at the fuzzy eggs in shock as the deer stepped a few feet to the side, continuing to munch on a new patch of flowers. His eyes bounced from the eggs to the deer, then back to the eggs. Eventually, he sighed.

I swear to God if this was all just to make a magical omelet, I’m going to kill Richard no matter what Donvath says.
