Oswald stayed still, pressed tight against the cold, wet stone. Water trickled down the rocks beside him, threading through the cracks and dripping onto his shoulder in slow, steady intervals. They know I’m here. Just waitin’ to strike.

Suddenly, the figure shot forward like a thunderclap, too fast for its size. Oswald immediately shoved off the wall and rolled out into the open just as a flash of steel cleaved through the space he'd just occupied.

A deafening crack split the air. Stone shattered behind him. Oswald turned mid-motion, skidding against the slick floor as he twisted to look. The rock he’d been hiding behind was now cleaved clean down the center. Fragments of the rock clattered across the ground, some bouncing past his feet. Would’ve taken my damn head off if I wasn't careful.

Oswald drew his sword and shifted instinctively, knees bent, weight forward, blade raised. His eyes locked onto the figure standing just beyond the ruined stone.

They were tall. Broader than anyone Oswald had run into in a while. Even in the pitch dark of the cavern, his vision cut clean through the black, and what he saw wasn’t comforting.

Up close, the figure looked like it had been dredged up from the bottom of some long-forgotten battlefield. Blackened metal clung to its frame, warped and battered. Dents peppered the chestplate, each one telling its own story. Scratches cut across the surface in jagged lines, some so deep they gouged through to the underlayers.

The armor was far from uniform. Plates had been strapped together with strips of old cloth: frayed, filthy, and barely holding on. The fabric draped in hanging shreds, stirring slightly with the figure’s every breath.

The bastard was huge, broad in the chest, with wide shoulders. Oswald felt his muscles tighten on instinct, not fear exactly, but the kind of tension that came from knowing what he was looking at could break bones without trying. Big fucker… built like a damn wall.

In his hands, he held a massive sword which was so chipped it looked like it had been dragged through a dozen battlefields and never cleaned. It hung low in the knight’s grip, the tip trailing across the stone floor with a low scrape that echoed through the cavern. That sword looks as big as a person.

The knight took a step and turned his head toward Oswald. The motion was slow, mechanical, like bone grinding against rusted metal. Then he spoke.

The voice that followed wasn't natural. It echoed, warped by the hollow of his helm. "You bear the mark of the Wretched Host... and by that curse, your fate is sealed. You will die here."

Oswald didn’t answer. His eyes stayed locked on the figure, posture loose but wired beneath the surface. The fuck's a Wretched Host anyway? The name meant nothing to him. But it probably wasn't good news.

Soon, the knight lunged, his entire body blurred forward in a burst of surprising speed, it was as if the heavy armor didn't impede him at all.. His greatsword swept low and wide, dragging sparks from the stone as it carved through the ground.

Oswald hurled himself sideways just in time. His boots scraped against wet rock, slipping slightly before he hit the ground and rolled. A second later, the sword struck.

Stone exploded beneath the impact, sending shards and dust spiraling into the air. A thunderous crack tore through the cavern as a jagged trench split open where Oswald had just been standing. Bits of shattered rock peppered his back as he came up on one knee. Close one, that would’ve split me in two if it hit.

Oswald shot to his feet, crunching against the fractured debris beneath him. But he didn’t strike just yet.

He watched the knight's every movement. The armored figure gripped the massive sword and wrenched it from the floor, the blade dragging stone with it. Chunks clung to the edge like broken teeth before crumbling away in a slow cascade.

The knight turned to face him once more. Then, without warning, the sword cut through the air again in a wide, sweeping arc.

Oswald dropped low as the knight's blade passed just above him, the wind of it trailing sharp against his cheek, pulling at the edge of his cloak. The force alone felt like it could’ve knocked a lesser fighter clean off their feet.

And then the cave screamed. The strike slammed into a nearby stone column with a thunderous crack.

Dust and debris exploded outward in a violent burst, fragments of stone tearing through the air like shrapnel. Cracks tore up the cavern wall, spreading in wild patterns. Massive slabs of rock broke free, crashing down in a chain reaction that shook the area.

Oswald weaved through the chaos, slipping across loose gravel, brushing past a falling boulder that smashed into the floor with a quake that rattled his spine. Shit, that was close.

Dust thickened the air, coating his tongue. The ground groaned beneath the weight of collapsing stone, yet still, the knight moved relentlessly in the dark. If he tags me once, I’m done.

He’s faster than I gave him credit for… but I’ve still got the edge on speed. Long as I stay sharp and keep moving, I’ll have my chance eventually.

The knight lunged, sword raised overhead and swung down. The blow crashed into the ground with a sound like breaking bones, sending a shockwave through the stone. Dust billowed in all directions, and a jagged crater now marked the spot.

Oswald slipped behind the knight and slashed across his side. The steel of his blade scraped against the armor, catching on a joint between plates. No real damage, but the metal had flexed slightly beneath the hit. Not bad, there has to be weaker spots. .

The knight turned, backhanding with the flat of the sword. Oswald ducked, causing the edge to catch a stalagmite nearby, cleaving straight through it and sending the top half tumbling.

Oswald slid to the side. When he saw another opening, he struck again, this time at the back of the knee where the plates looked uneven. His blade hit the gap but didn’t break through. Not there.

The knight let out a low, guttural roar as his voice rang through the cavern. “Coward! Get back here and face me!”

Oswald’s lip curled into a grin. Sorry, I ain’t here to prove nothin’. I’m here to win.

He launched forward again, sword at the ready. The cavern echoed with the harsh clang of steel meeting armor. Sparks burst as his blade glanced off pauldrons, skated across gauntlets, and tore through seams where plates didn’t quite meet. The knight tried his best to punish Oswald's movements, but the boy was never caught even once.

Once, Oswald slipped in close, shadowing the knight’s massive frame like a wraith. He moved low and fast, ducking beneath the arc of the greatsword. His blade came up and carved downward in a clean slash, right across the back of the knight’s leg, precisely where the armor dipped.

This time, it hit deep. Steel shrieked as it pierced the gap, the impact singing up Oswald’s arm with a jolt that rattled his bones. The metal gave way, and a hiss followed as thick, dark blood oozed from the wound. It splattered across the cavern floor in heavy, viscous droplets, clinging to the rock like oil.

At the same time, Oswald's sword pulsed. Heat bloomed beneath his fingers, rising through the hilt in a slow, steady burn. A faint crimson glow bled along the blade’s edge, flickering in rhythm with his heartbeat. It wasn’t fire, but the air around it shimmered like heat coming off a forge. The glow grew stronger with each breath, like the weapon itself was waking up. Ahh, this again.

Oswald didn’t question it. He moved forward, pressing the advantage.

The knight had staggered, just enough to show the strike had landed hard. Oswald raised the blade, lining up a follow-up strike toward the chestplate, ready to dig in deeper and keep the pressure on.

But the knight roared. It wasn’t the bellow of a wounded man, it was powerful. Dust shook loose from the ceiling. Oswald halted his swing, forced back by sheer instinct as the knight slammed one armored foot into the stone and dragged himself backward, opening space between them.

Shadows burst forth from the knight's sword, tendrils of black energy that consumed the blade whole. Within seconds, the entire weapon had vanished beneath the veil, its true form hidden beneath layers of shadow. What remained looked like a sword, but forged from absence and malice.

Oswald felt the shift in the air. A strong feeling of cold rolled over him in waves, cutting through his damp clothes and settling into the marrow of his bones. That ain’t good.

The knight charged again, and this time, he was faster. When the sword came down in a black arc, Oswald barely had time to leap aside.

The blade struck the stone floor, and the ground ruptured. A spiderweb of cracks exploded outward, shards of rock flung into the air like shrapnel. One chunk clipped Oswald’s shoulder, spinning him into a roll. He caught himself and came up just in time to parry the next swing.

Oswald’s blade groaned under the pressure. The force behind the knight’s strike rattled up his arms, and his knees nearly buckled. He’s stronger now, way stronger.

He dodged again, slipping past a brutal horizontal slash that tore a gash through the cavern wall. Chunks of stone rained down, crashing into the floor and stirring clouds of dust.

The boy tried to retaliate, striking toward the knight’s ribs, but the dark blade was already parrying with unnatural speed. Another slash came for his head. Oswald ducked, twisted, and backed off, but he was panting now. His arms ached from each block. Every dodge took more out of him. Come on… just one more clean hit—

Yet, the knight didn’t let him. With a surge, the armored figure rushed in and brought the sword down in a wide arc. Oswald raised his blade in reflex to block—

CRACK.

Steel split. Oswald's sword shattered in two with a ringing crack that drowned out everything. The upper half of the blade spun away into the dark, clattering uselessly against the ground.

Oswald staggered back, the broken hilt still in his hand. Uh oh.

Without a weapon, Oswald was at a significant disadvantage. It was an opportunity that the knight was keen to exploit.

Oswald sprinted through the cavern. The air stung his lungs, but he didn’t stop.

The knight’s shadow loomed behind him, sword tearing through the dark. Each swing slammed into the cave walls, blasting stone into the air. The ground shook with every blow.

A black arc swept past Oswald’s head, close enough to rattle his teeth. He ducked, rolled, and kept moving. Think, dammit. Gotta be a way to fight back.

Then he saw his golden opportunity. A jagged stalagmite was cracked near a collapsed column, like the broken fang of some ancient beast.

Oswald slid to a stop beside it, dropped low, and gripped the stone with both hands. With a grunt, he snapped off a hefty chunk, about the length of his forearm and roughly the same weight as a sword.

The knight roared behind him. But Oswald was already running.

He circled wide across the cavern’s edge, using the shattered terrain to weave through. The knight swung with wild fury, his blade hissing through the air. Wherever it landed, stone erupted, walls split, and dust choked the space between them.

Keep movin’. Stay low. Don’t give him the angle.

With a burst of speed, Oswald darted behind the knight, dodging a crushing downward slash that splintered a boulder nearby. The second the knight’s back was exposed, Oswald reared back and hurled the chunk of stalagmite with everything he had.

The rock struck the back of the knight’s head with a ringing thud. The impact knocked the brute forward a step, and a sharp dent bloomed across the rear of the helmet.

Oswald’s eyes widened. It worked.

Now he had a plan. A very questionable plan, but a plan regardless.

He scrambled across the cavern floor, scooping up broken stones, fractured stalagmites, anything with weight and an edge. Every time the knight turned, Oswald was already moving—ducking behind stone, slipping through shadows, flanking wide. Then, he started throwing.

Thud!

Clang!

Crack!

Each successive rock throw connected, hammering the back of the knight’s helmet again and again. The brute staggered once, but didn’t fall. He roared in frustration, thrashing through the dark. But Oswald always managed to stay ahead.

The knight bellowed, voice cracking with rage. “Face me, worm! Where is your honor?”

Oswald said nothing, he was focused solely on winning. Honor don’t mean a damn thing if you’re dead anyway.

He kept his breath steady, broke off another chunk of rock, and threw it as hard as hard as he could. It struck the same spot, hard.

The dented helmet crumpled inward with a brutal, crunching clang, the metal folding under the force of the impact. The knight dropped to one knee like a collapsing pillar. His sword scraped along the stone, dragging a line in the floor as it followed him down.

He swayed once. Then, with effort, he tried to push just barely finding his feet.

Oswald, of course, had no plans on letting him off easy. He found another heavy rock, and threw it with everything he had left.

The rock crashed against the side of the already-ruined helm with a sickening crack. The knight’s head jerked violently to the side. His footing gave out.

The towering figure of blackened steel folded in on itself and collapsed. Armor crashed to the ground in a twisted heap, the sound echoing through the cavern.

The knight’s sword skidded free, sliding across the stone with a hollow, metallic scrape before coming to a stop. Dust rose in a slow, curling drift, twisting through the air.

Oswald waited. Stay down.

The knight didn't move for a long time. But Oswald knew that the battle couldn't possibly be over yet. Whenever something actually died, the system let him know by showing him how many souls he'd gained. But there was nothing this time.

The knight moved, just a bit. His hand scraped slowly across the ground, dragging claw-like grooves into the stone. The sword was still inches from the knight’s hand, dark energy flickering faintly along its edge like embers refusing to die. Nope. Not lettin’ you grab that again.
