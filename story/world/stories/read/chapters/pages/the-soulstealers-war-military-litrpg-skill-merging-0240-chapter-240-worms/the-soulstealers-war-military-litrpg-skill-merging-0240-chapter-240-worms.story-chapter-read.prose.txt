There wasn’t much left of the trebuchet tower for me to perch on. I stood on a spire of stone and looked down over the battlefield, surveying the fields of orcs. I couldn’t make a good guess of how many of them were out there, and when I used my Perception Skill one last time to check, both looking for the Hangman and curiously wondering if it would help me sense how many orcs were left.

There were too many, though, and I wasn’t used to using my Perception Skill on such a massive scale. Even with most of the orcs being Irons, an overwhelming pressure built in the front of my mind. A faint golden gloss ran over the crowd as my senses outlined the beasts for me.

Instead, I blinked, waiting for the Skill to time out, then searched for physical signs of the Hangman.

I’d seen it once, so it should be easy enough to find again. At least I knew what it looked like.

Of course, I hadn’t exactly done anything to make myself less of a target. As my gaze swept over the crowd, arrows whistled toward me. I caught some of them on my shield, and let my armour and durability-enhanced skin take the rest of the blows. Nothing pierced through, but I was pretty sure it would leave bruises all across my body.

That was a problem for tomorrow me, though.

Today’s problems were the shamans. A few of them had seen me and were taking aim, and if I didn’t find Lord Ceonwin and the Hangman soon, they’d blast holes in my armour and destroy my shield.

Near the edge of the battlefield, up on the hills at the edge of the valley, dirt exploded from the ground. A whip of dark, rotten energy cracked against an invisible shield, which crackled in the air with Lord Ceonwin’s signature invisible Arts.

Saeglaf and his apprentice darted around from behind. While Saeglaf’s Arts were almost indistinguishable from Lord Ceonwin’s, his apprentice was a druid. The boy helped mitigate the rot, regrowing the ground as the Hangman’s feet rotted it away, then sending vines up to catch him.

The pale orc stood in the middle of all of it, making quick but brutish swipes of his sword to deflect incoming attacks.

Yesman’s squad stood closer down the hill, all armed. They fanned out and approached slowly, trying to push the Hangman away from Hyrdford and lead the battle into the hills, where it would do less damage.

I couldn’t wait any longer. Springing off my perch, I raced toward the fight. I still had just under a hundred points of Presence left. Though I probably would’ve lost track of how much Presence I had used if I’d just been my regular self, my Focus helped.

Ninety-three points left, to be precise.

I used three more points to launch myself through the air toward the Hangman. I soared, and my stomach churned, but I knew I could handle the landing. I didn’t make it all the way to the fight between Path-walkers, though. I fell about halfway to my target, landing among the orcs. Though I didn’t pull myself down, my impact still created an explosion of dirt and threw aside the nearest of the orcs.

Then I launched myself again. I accidentally caught a few orcs in my swell of gravity, and I flung them along with me as I launched myself the rest of the distance to the Hangman.

Eighty-one Presence left.

With my Art now at the expert level, it cost six Presence to make a single well, and twelve Presence to make a double well.

As I soared, I waited for my opening. Lord Ceonwin backed away from a swipe of the beast’s sword, and Yesman covered him with a lunge and a swipe of his axe. It worked for a second, but the Hangman’s tentacles swished back toward the sappers. They grew off the side of his body, and they wrapped around Yesman’s forearm.

His armour rusted on contact, and it would need repairs, but it held for long enough that his squad’s tank could slash the tentacle off.

They were all reeling. The Hangman kicked the ground, sending a swell of rot-filled dirt flying back into Saeglaf and his apprentice. Both of them skidded back.

Before the Hangman could make a move toward Hyrdford in the opening he’d created for himself, I slammed myself down toward him with an angled Art. At the same time, I activated my spear resonance Skill, dragging myself down atop the Hangman and aiming for his chest. It was the biggest, most open target.

When I landed, I drove my spear in as hard as I could. It pierced through the orc’s bare upper chest, and the impact made him stagger back a few paces.

At least he wasn’t leaving, but I’d hoped for something a little better than that.

I twisted, using as much force as I could to try to wedge my spear up and out of the Hangman’s body. If I could create a bigger rip, that was for the best. But the wood splintered as I tried to lever the spear up.

I needed a spear with a stronger shaft if I wanted to pull that off. As the Hangman thrust his left arm out toward me, sending the tentacles whirling toward me, I kicked off backward, and pulled my spear straight out—I couldn’t afford to try anything fancy, now. The Hangman’s sword clipped my shield when he swung, sending me tumbling off to the side where Lord Ceonwin stood.

I rolled to a halt. When I stood up, the top half of my shield crumbled.

Seventy-four Presence.

“Glad you could make it,” Lord Ceonwin said, rushing in front of me. He whirled his Great Warbrand, cutting through the tentacles and dispersing them before they could reach me. The sappers circled from the west, continuing to push the Hangman up the valley slope. Saeglaf and his apprentice funnelled from the other side, stopping the Hangman from darting out and trying to get back to the battle that way.

Instead, the only way he could go was back.

“What kept you?” Lord Ceonwin asked.

“Holding the wall,” I replied, jumping up to my feet. Without waiting for orders, I moved in to protect Lord Ceonwin’s flank and further cut off the Hangman. “How are we doing?”

“We suspect he only has a quarter of his Presence left.”

The Hangman backed up slowly, pointing his sword between us as if he couldn’t decide who he wanted to attack.

From here, the Hangman was taller than he looked at a distance. Broader, too. He had to be the most muscular orc I’d ever seen. The wound I’d put in his chest barely seemed to make a difference. Fungal growths spread across it, sealing up the wound. Sure, his black blood may have seeped around the edge, but it kept him alive longer.

Maybe I should’ve gone for the head, but I’d had no idea whether I could pierce his skull. No sense in taking risks.

Worse, the rest of his body was covered in wounds, sealed over by fungal growths. At least half of his pale hue had to be due to scars and wounds sealed by his Arts.

But that had to cost Presence. We could eventually wear him down to where he couldn’t heal anymore.

His yellow eyes glowed bright, and when his gaze reached me, they made a shiver run down my spine.

Finally, he snarled, “Witless worms. My master will send more. You kill me, and the Queens will attack. You may win on this front, but even once your barren lands here are cleared…the northern cities will fall.”

I kept my spear level. Now that we were all here, we would attack at once. Naturally, everyone’s gaze shifted to Lord Ceonwin, waiting for his signal.

“There is no victory,” the Hangman continued. “Give up.”

“Sounds like what someone who’s losing would say,” I said. “Let’s get this over with.”

Lord Ceonwin dipped his chin, and we all encircled at once.

I leapt from the side, driving my spear toward the beast’s neck. A single hit would help. Even cutting him drained his Presence.

He deflected my spear with an easy swish of his sword. Maybe I should’ve been honoured, but at the moment, I devoted all my concentration just to winning. I tracked my openings and used my Art to pull on my spear, driving it in harder with the help of my resonance Skill. I waited until I had a clear shot before committing to an attack, and if I couldn’t commit, then I hammered him with a double-well Skill to give myself an opening, or to slow his tentacles and help the rest of us survive.

I landed a stab in the orc’s ribs, and a slash across its throat. Both, it healed using its fungal arts, instantly packing the wounds with mushroom stem.

I was down to sixty Presence, then forty eight, then forty one. The drain tugged on my brain and made my eyelids droop, but I was used to pushing through presence loss. I could weather it, so I did.

The harder part was making sure I survived the battle. The Hangman retaliated with broad swipes of his sword. All of us approached, hammering him harder and faster. I had to dodge Saeglaff and Ceonwin’s attacks just as often as I had to dodge the Hangman’s. When I had an opportunity, I spun around and joined up with Yesman’s squad.

As our circle grew tighter, my hope swelled. Was this going to be it? Would we just encircle the Hangman and put an end to his attacks?

That couldn’t be it, right?

The Hangman slowed as we chipped away at him. His arms drooped, and his sword swished lower and lower, making desperate plays for our ankles—at best. Yesman hit him with an axe in the upper shoulder, and he only half-healed the wound.

I was down to fifteen points of Presence. We were getting close.

How much more could the Hangman take?

Finally, Lord Ceonwin brought his blade down on the back of the Hangman’s head. Even with its enhanced sharpness and Ceonwin’s Arts, it didn’t split the Hangman’s skull entirely. The beast leaned forward.

Finally, with a screech, it tilted its head back. Its eyes flared brighter than ever before, and it screeched a high-pitch.

More fungal growths erupted out of every wound, and tentacles flashed toward us. One wrapped around my neck, and another caught Yesman’s tank—also by the neck. They dragged us in, and the Hangman swung his sword in a flash.

My neck burned. I didn’t even want to think about what was happening. My Vitality resisted it, but the stench of my own throat trying to rot away was nearly unbearable.

Before I even registered what was happening, the Hangman’s blade flashed. It lopped off the head of Yesman’s tank, and the Dupe crumpled.

The blade was racing toward me next, and I couldn’t dodge it.

I only had enough Presence for one more Art, and I made it count. With my Focus, the world slowed. I created a double-well Art, with two wells both pulling in toward the beast’s face. They dragged my spear in, and my resonance Skill triggered, sharpening my spearhead and pulling it through the Hangman’s mouth.

The Hangman died before his sword reached me. The tentacle fell loose, and I stumbled forward, clutching my throat. I gasped, then shook my hand when I felt my own slimy skin. My Vitality was doing its job for now, though. A healer could wait—while I was still alive, we had a siege to win.

I hadn’t realized how long it had been, but when I looked to the east, the sky was lightening in a faint band.
