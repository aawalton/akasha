[Panel]

[Name: Ji Ning

Spiritual Root: Mid-grade Spiritual Root

Attributes: Five Elemental Attributes

Talent: Extreme Space-Time Sensitivity

Realm: 2nd Stage of Qi Condensation (21/200)

Cultivation Method: Pure Qi Sutra (1st Floor: 87/100)

Martial Arts: Bone Forging Fist (Minor Accomplishment: 19/200), Shadow Steps (Minor Accomplishment: 11/200)

Spells: Small Cloud Rain Technique (Minor Accomplishment: 13/200), Soil Refining Technique (Starting: 99/100)]

Glancing over the panel, Ning nodded in satisfaction.

It had been four months since he'd arrived in this world. Compared to the bare-bones collection of skills he'd started with, his panel was finally beginning to look respectable.

Most importantly, after nearly two months of steady practice, his Soil Refining Technique was finally on the verge of reaching Minor Accomplishment.

It had taken roughly the same amount of time as the Small Cloud Rain Technique, although comprehending it had been much more difficult. Ning had only managed to maintain a similar pace because his cultivation had improved in the meantime, allowing him to cast the technique more frequently.

After all, learning a spell involved more than memorizing an incantation and throwing spiritual energy at it.

From Ning's experience, there were five main components to mastering a spell: precise chanting, accurate hand seals, stable spiritual energy control, an understanding of the underlying principle, and a clear mental visualization of what the spell was supposed to accomplish.

The first three could be improved through repetition. Chant enough times and the words became instinctive. Practice the hand seals until the movements became familiar, and controlling the flow of spiritual energy would eventually become second nature as well.

The final two, however, demanded genuine comprehension.

With the Small Cloud Rain Technique, Ning had possessed something of an unfair advantage.

His understanding of rain came from his previous world's science. Water evaporated into the atmosphere, condensed into clouds as it cooled, and eventually returned to the earth as rain. Once Ning understood how the spell imitated this process through spiritual energy, visualizing it became much easier.

Old Zhou understood the same phenomenon in an entirely different way.

According to him, the Small Cloud Rain Technique worked by guiding the water qi within heaven and earth, drawing upon the Yin essence gathered within clouds and harmonizing it with the Yang energy provided by the cultivator. When Yin and Yang reached the proper balance, water qi would gather and descend as nourishing spiritual rain.

That was how cultivators generally understood the technique.

It fit the saying that different roads could lead to the same Dao.

The Soil Refining Technique was another matter.

Rain followed a cycle Ning already understood, while Soil Refining dealt with something more abstract: transformation.

Old Zhou described the technique as "nourishing the veins of the earth" and "awakening the latent spirit sleeping within the soil."

It sounded very profound.

Ning had absolutely no idea what an earth vein was supposed to feel like, though. So, as usual, he translated the explanation into something his own mind could work with.

To Ning, soil was an enormous living system containing minerals, moisture, and microorganisms, along with countless other components that influenced its condition. Healthy soil retained nutrients and water while allowing air to circulate through it, whereas poor soil could become compacted or depleted.

With that understanding, he began approaching the Soil Refining Technique through concepts he was already familiar with.

He visualized his spiritual energy loosening the compacted earth and dispersing accumulated impurities, much like the processes involved in restoring depleted farmland. Fresh spiritual energy would then circulate through the soil, gradually improving its condition.

Once he'd settled on a principle he could understand, the visualization became much easier.

Of course, imagining strange supernatural phenomena had never been much of a problem for Ning. After a lifetime of cultural baptism through movies, games, anime, and all the other entertainment his previous world had to offer, his imagination had received plenty of training.

After recovering enough spiritual energy, Ning placed his palm against the earth and cast the Soil Refining Technique once more.

Spiritual energy spread beneath his hand, sinking through the soil in gentle waves. Ning maintained the circulation, adjusting the flow whenever he sensed resistance until the compacted earth gradually loosened and a faint trace of vitality spread through it.

A familiar notification appeared before his eyes.

[Soil Refining Technique (Starting: 99/100) → Minor Accomplishment (1/200)]

"Finally! That's two spells at Minor Accomplishment."

Ning grinned as he examined the notification.

The Soil Refining Technique was especially valuable to him because prolonged use could gradually improve the sub-first-grade soil assigned to peripheral disciples until it reached true first-grade quality.

First-grade soil could support higher-tier spiritual crops, allowing him to grow more valuable plants and earn greater profits. Better yet, improving the quality of his assigned farmland counted as a contribution to the sect, meaning he could earn merit while working on something that already benefited him.

Those merits could then be exchanged for resources that weren't available through ordinary spirit stones.

A beautiful arrangement, as far as Ning was concerned.

Speaking of resources, Ning reached into his robes and took out one of his remaining spirit stones.

The translucent blue stone rested innocently in his palm, with pure spiritual energy faintly swirling within.

Looking at it, Ning almost felt his inner Gollum emerging.

My precious.

He quickly suppressed the urge.

Still, spirit stones really were precious. Practically every step along the path of immortality required resources, and these little rocks could be used to purchase almost anything a cultivator needed.

The problem was how quickly they disappeared.

The hundred spirit stones Wei Zhusang had originally lent him, along with his earnings from spiritual farming, had already been spent almost entirely on cultivation resources.

Thankfully, Wei Zhusang had introduced him to a few more potential investors.

Zhang Feng had lent him eighty spirit stones, while Lang Rulang, much to Ning's surprise, had handed over a full hundred.

Given her usual personality, Ning hadn't expected her to agree so readily, much less match Wei Zhusang's investment. If her expression hadn't remained completely unchanged while handing him the spirit stones, he might have suspected she had some ulterior motive.

Old Zhou had also lent him some spirit stones, and convincing him had been easier than expected.

As a spiritual farmer himself, Old Zhou understood what Ning's progress in the Small Cloud Rain and Soil Refining Techniques represented. They'd also grown familiar with each other over the past few months, so Ning hadn't needed to do much convincing.

After admiring the spirit stone for another moment, Ning reluctantly put it away and looked across his farmland.

"Now that Soil Refining has reached Minor Accomplishment, I should be able to handle another acre."

With more land, he could grow more crops and earn more spirit stones. Improving the soil would increase the value of his harvests even further.

Most importantly, those additional earnings could be invested straight back into his cultivation.

Ning nodded to himself, already imagining the possibilities.

Yes. The future was looking bright.

...

"Junior Brother Ning."

Ning turned at the call and saw a tall young man approaching through the fields. His robes were neatly embroidered with thin golden lines, and even while walking through farmland, he somehow maintained an air of superiority.

It was Su Fan.

"Greetings, Senior Brother Su." Ning cupped his hands and stood up from where he'd been waging his eternal war against weeds.

"Hm." Su Fan acknowledged him with a slight nod before surveying the surrounding fields.

Ning waited for him to explain why he'd come, but Su Fan seemed perfectly content to continue inspecting the scenery.

After several awkward seconds, Ning decided to take the initiative before they ended up standing there until sunset. "If Senior Brother has something to discuss, why don't we talk inside?"

Su Fan inclined his head, looking as though that had been his intention all along. "Very well."

Once inside the small hut, Ning prepared a cup of warm milk tea and placed it before his guest.

"Here, Senior Brother."

Su Fan accepted the cup with his usual composed expression. Only after taking a sip did his eyebrows rise slightly. "Not bad."

Ning smiled at the compliment. Dealing with someone like Su Fan required a little initiative. The man wasn't difficult to talk to, but if Ning waited for him to carry the conversation, they might both die of old age first.

"So, what brings Senior Brother here today?"

Su Fan took another sip of milk tea before asking, "How is your training as a spiritual farmer progressing?"

Ning decided to overlook the blatant avoidance of his question.

"Both my Small Cloud Rain Technique and Soil Refining Technique have reached Minor Accomplishment."

Su Fan paused with the cup halfway to his lips.

Ning had deliberately chosen to reveal his progress.

Ever since deciding to establish himself as someone talented in spells, he'd seen little reason to conceal every achievement. Demonstrating his abilities would improve his standing within the sect and help him gain access to opportunities that might otherwise remain out of reach.

Of course, he'd considered the possibility of attracting unwanted attention, but Old Zhou had already reassured him on that front.

His rate of comprehension was impressive, though it wasn't unprecedented. Among the hundreds of disciples recruited every five years, there were bound to be a few with exceptional talent for spells. Old Zhou had even known someone similar during his own generation.

That disciple had possessed only a low-grade spiritual root, yet his talent for spells had allowed him to accumulate enough resources to obtain a barrier-breaking pill and attempt to enter the late stage of Qi Condensation.

He'd ultimately failed, but reaching that point with a low-grade spiritual root was already an achievement.

So, compared to the monsters born with top-grade spiritual roots, being unusually good at learning farming spells was pretty tame.

Su Fan eventually set down his cup. "It seems Wei Zhusang is a little behind on his information. He only told me you'd reached Minor Accomplishment in the Small Cloud Rain Technique."

"I only reached that level with Soil Refining recently," Ning explained. Wei Zhusang had helped him a great deal, so there was no reason to let the man take the blame for something so trivial. "Most people don't know about it yet."

"I see."

Su Fan made no attempt to verify his claim. Ning couldn't tell whether that was because he trusted him or simply considered the matter too trivial to investigate.

"That's even better. I've come today with a proposition," Su Fan continued, leaning back slightly.

Ning grew interested. "What kind of proposition?"

"I'm an alchemist's apprentice. The sect provides materials for practice, but good ingredients are always in short supply. Any alchemist who intends to progress eventually needs reliable sources of high-quality spiritual herbs."

Ning nodded in understanding.

From everything he'd heard, alchemy was essentially the cultivation world's most respectable method of setting money on fire. Learning the craft consumed enormous quantities of medicinal ingredients, and failed batches were disgustingly common.

It made sense that alchemists would establish relationships with capable spiritual farmers.

"Wouldn't Senior Brother be better off working with more experienced spiritual farmers?"

Spiritual farming was a common profession within the sect. Its entry requirements were relatively low, so plenty of disciples without strong backgrounds eventually ended up pursuing it.

"I already do," Su Fan replied. "But experienced farmers have existing arrangements, and the best among them know exactly what they're worth."

Ning understood immediately.

Expensive.

Su Fan might be wealthy, but plenty of other alchemy apprentices were wealthy too. Competition for experienced spiritual farmers could easily drive up prices, especially when several apprentices wanted to secure the same supplier.

"What I need is someone capable of eventually producing higher-grade medicinal herbs," Su Fan continued. "You're inexperienced now, but your comprehension is excellent. At your current rate, obtaining a Spiritual Farmer Certificate within a few years isn't unrealistic."

For the first time since entering the hut, a faint smirk appeared on Su Fan's face.

"Besides, I don't mind investing some resources in someone with promise."

Ning looked at him for a moment.

Damn.

The expensive robes and casual arrogance were already impressive, but the way Su Fan spoke about investing resources in promising juniors really completed the picture.

This bastard is aura farming right in front of me.

The worst part was that it was working.

Ning returned his attention to the offer.

Obtaining a Spiritual Farmer Certificate required reaching Great Accomplishment in at least three recognized spiritual farming techniques, with the Small Cloud Rain Technique being compulsory.

It was a difficult requirement, but the benefits were substantial.

Ordinary spiritual farmers had to surrender a percentage of their harvest to the sect. Certified farmers only needed to provide a fixed amount, allowing them to keep everything they produced beyond that requirement.

For someone capable of producing high-quality harvests, the difference could amount to a small fortune.

Ning considered the offer before nodding. "Since Senior Brother has gone out of his way to explain everything, it would be rude of me to refuse."

"Good." Su Fan seemed to have expected that answer. "But I have one condition. Within two years, I want your Small Cloud Rain Technique to reach Great Accomplishment. If you manage that, I don't mind sharing a portion of the profits from any medicinal herbs you grow for me."

Two years.

Ning quickly calculated whether he could meet the requirement.

Comprehension wasn't his biggest obstacle. The real problem was spiritual energy. Reaching Great Accomplishment required extensive practice, and he'd need to reach the middle stages of Qi Condensation before he could repeatedly cast the complete technique without exhausting himself.

Could he reach the middle stage within two years?

With his current progress and access to cultivation resources, it seemed entirely possible.

"Alright, I accept."

"Good."

Su Fan reached into his sleeve and produced a jade bottle barely larger than Ning's thumb. A faint medicinal fragrance escaped from within as he casually tossed it across the table.

"Take it. Consider it an advance and some encouragement."

Ning caught the bottle and removed the stopper. The familiar fragrance immediately told him what was inside.

Red Bud Pills.

Twelve of them, to be exact.

However, the scent was noticeably purer than the pills he'd used before, and each pill carried a faint marking across its surface.

"Senior Brother, these seem different from ordinary Red Bud Pills."

A hint of pride entered Su Fan's voice.

"Those are Star-Marked Red Bud Pills. Medicinal pills are generally divided into three grades: defective, genuine, and star-marked. Star-marked pills contain almost no impurities and possess nearly twice the medicinal efficacy of ordinary genuine pills."

Su Fan took another leisurely sip of milk tea before adding, "I don't need them anymore, so you can have them."

Ning examined the pills with renewed interest.

He wasn't surprised that medicinal pills had different grades. This was a cultivation world, after all. If something existed, cultivators would inevitably find a way to divide it into more expensive tiers.

What surprised him was how casually Su Fan had handed over twelve Star-Marked Red Bud Pills.

Suddenly, Ning understood something.

So this is why arrogant rich young masters always have so many lackeys.

The employee benefits were fucking incredible.

"Then I won't pretend to refuse." Ning cupped his hands in gratitude. "Thank you, Senior Brother."

Su Fan gave an approving nod, seemingly pleased that Ning hadn't insisted on going through the traditional three rounds of I couldn't possibly accept this before accepting the gift anyway.

"Do your best."

With that, Su Fan rose and left with the same effortless flair with which he'd arrived.

Ning stood at the doorway and watched the figure disappear between the fields.

Su Fan was certainly arrogant, but Ning didn't find him unpleasant.

His arrogance was different from Xiao Hong's. Xiao Hong seemed to demand that everyone around him acknowledge his superiority, while Su Fan behaved like someone who had long since accepted his own elevated status and saw no reason to discuss it.

Ning supposed upbringing had a great deal to do with it.

This wasn't Earth, where people were taught from childhood that everyone deserved equal treatment. In this world, strength and lineage determined one's position from an early age. Someone raised among powerful cultivators, surrounded by praise and accustomed to seeing others defer to his family, would have little reason to question his own importance.

Of course, Xiao Hong's mindless arrogance might simply be a terminal case of proximity to the protagonist.

Ning considered psychoanalyzing the matter further, then decided he had more important things to worry about.

He looked down at the jade bottle in his hand, his expression brightening at the sight of the twelve Star-Marked Red Bud Pills.

Whatever personality flaws Su Fan possessed could be analyzed another day.

For now, he had pills. Loads of them.
