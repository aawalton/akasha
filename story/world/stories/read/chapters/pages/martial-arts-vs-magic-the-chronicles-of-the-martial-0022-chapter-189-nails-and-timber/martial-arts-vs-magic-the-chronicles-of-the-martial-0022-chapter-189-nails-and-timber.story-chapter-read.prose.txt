I found it funny that the first branch of the Heavenly Demon Divine Cult had rotten floorboards.

Goryeo had treated me well so far. Most of the old floorboards had been replaced by the time I arrived that morning, but three still sagged near the rear wall. I marked those with chalk, then stepped over a bucket catching rainwater from yesterday, climbing the ladder to the second floor.

The building sat below Hwangcheon's eastern market slope, in a district where rainwater gathered and respectable merchants did not. Apparently it was the manor of a banished noble, and then snatched by the local gangs. Ha-Yun had acquired it through people who knew people who knew better than to put her name on the paperwork. Officially, it would become a foreign cultural office and martial clinic.

Calling it a cult hall would have made several ministers ill.

Earth mages had already stabilized the foundation, while local carpenters replaced most of the frame. The roof was new, the walls were nearly finished, and Stratos could have sent drones through the Bifrost and completed the remaining work before lunch if I asked.

Instead, I stood shirtless beneath the morning sun with a hammer in my hand.

The original Heavenly Demon had built his first hall himself.

I remembered, both from the game and his memories that lived rent-free in my head, how his hands hammered away at the walls. His hands had been even younger than mine and covered in blisters because he had refused to reinforce them. His master had watched from beneath a tree, offering no help while correcting every third nail.

At the time, the young Lee Cheon-Ma had assumed this was another cruel lesson. But decades later, after palaces had been raised at his command, he finally understood why his master had made him work.

People behaved differently inside a place they had built.

There had been no grand scripture behind the lesson. A man hesitated before breaking a door he had fitted himself, and a disciple swept a floor more carefully after spending a week laying it.

I had enough inherited memories to know how badly the Heavenly Demon had failed to follow that lesson near the end of his life. Speaking, I still didn’t know how this old monster died. Or if he died at all…

So I used the hammer.

It also helped that the workers found my bare hands unsettling. I had driven one nail in with my thumb yesterday, and the carpenter beside me had quietly put down his tea and gone home early.

"You missed one."

I glanced down.

Lilian sat on a stack of lumber in the shade, with one knee raised and a meat skewer in her hand. Her silver-white hair was tied back, while a strip of cloth hid her ears more as a style than to fool anyone.

"No, I didn't."

"Oh yeah, but you were about to."

I stared at her. "Were you watching the nail?"

Her red eyes finally moved slowly up from my stomach. "Eventually."

I shook my head and set the next nail. Lilian laughed to herself, then looked toward the street again. Three children had been circling the yard for the better part of an hour, pretending to play with a wooden hoop. An old woman watched us through hanging laundry across the road. Two men in scholar robes had passed the entrance twice.

Lilian bit another piece of meat from the skewer.

"The one on the left has a knife in his sleeve."

"No way, how did you notice?”

She ignored my dry sarcasm and added, "The other smells like palace ink."

Goryeo's palace scribes actually used ink mixed with pine resin. The old me wouldn’t have noticed it from this distance, but Lilian's nose was better than most detection spells. Although… To be fair, as a Transcendent, both my ordinary and supernatural senses far outweighed hers but I let her keep talking.

"Seems like your princess sent someone to watch you," she said.

"Her father, probably."

"Why, you think Ha-Yun trusts you with all her heart when her father doesn’t?” she asked, raising an eyebrow. I didn’t answer. “Should I scare them?"

"Let them do their jobs while you do yours."

Lilian looked disappointed, though not for long. She shifted on the lumber and kept watching the road.

By noon, the heat had slowed the workers.

I fixed the final brace and then flew downwards. A few of the children watching paused, and then I saw their eyes glitter with excitement. Lilian held out a towel and a wooden cup before my feet touched the ground.

"Thanks."

“You could have climbed down like a normal person.”

The water was cold and faintly sweet. I drank most of it before wiping the sweat from my face. "You're getting almost good at this wife thing."

She looked at me over the tip of the skewer. "You want me to start practicing in public?"

"I thought this was practice."

"No, this is like the maid duty that I thought I stopped doing. Turns out I still have the skill for it."

That sent me down a spiral of nostalgia. It’d merely been two years since I’d come to this world, and yet the memories… There were so many memories. I’d tasted so many different shades of life. I, who’d awakened as the son of a noble, was now the leader of an international criminal organization building my second base…

Laughing to myself, I glanced toward the workers. The closest carpenter had become deeply interested in sorting nails.

Lilian moved aside when I sat beside her. Our shoulders touched, but neither of us mentioned it. It was normal for us, compared to the first time we met.

Not everything was sunshine and rainbows, though.

"How bad was it last night?" I asked.

Her chewing slowed.

"I woke up cold once."

"Just once? That’s better."

"Well… technically, it's three times." She finished the skewer and tossed the stick into a waste bucket. "The third time, I got food."

That explained the empty kitchen shelf this morning. "Turn around."

She raised an eyebrow. "Here?"

Why was she making it weird?

"It's fine since the workers already think we're strange."

"They think you're strange." She pulled her hair over one shoulder. "They like me."

One of the workers glanced at the claw marks she had left in a support post yesterday, then quickly looked away.

"I’m sure they do."

Her work shirt had a loose back seam for transformations. She pulled it down far enough to expose her spine, revealing the black-red lines beneath her skin.

The marks began below her neck and branched along her ribs. They looked like scars until they moved with her heartbeat. I stared at it, holding myself back from glaring at that hateful thing.

I placed two fingers against the fourth line.

Lilian's shoulders tightened.

"Does it feel cold?"

"Yeah, your fingers."

"I meant the shackle."

"Then ask properly, young bastard."

Clearly, it wasn’t just cold if her mood was suddenly sour. I opened the Demonic Sphere, amplified by the Third Flower, before answering something unwise.

The Sphere had changed after the Third Flower. Before, it showed me an outline of bodies, energy, distance, and movement. Now, if I narrowed my focus, it showed the small things a body did to survive itself.

Sometimes I still felt unsure how this woman was alive. Valeria’s sacrifice really was powerful, it seems.

The Eight Shackles of Mortal Flesh removed the limits that kept muscle from tearing and bones from breaking under their own strength, and over time slowly transcend into Immortality. Over time, across centuries, if not millennia. A cultivator was supposed to open one shackle, spend years teaching the body to endure it, then move to the next.

Lilian had forced seven open over a handful of days because a future version of me had left the technique burning inside her head. What was that stupid fucker thinking?

The first three had settled because her body was already far beyond human. The fourth and fifth remained half-open because I’d trained her in the best Body Arts. The problem started from the sixth, that was what stole heat from her every night, while the seventh had buried itself so deeply into her bloodline that I could barely tell where the damned technique ended and the werewolf began.

"Am I dying?"

She said it lightly, but her back had gone still under my hand.

That was when I couldn’t help but laugh. It was a serious situation, I knew, but I couldn’t control it. She shot me a glare as I managed to calm myself.

"Why are you laughing? You checked for a long time so I was asking."

“Lilian, I want you to know something,” I said. “True death is the death of body and soul, rather than simply your soul leaving your body. I want you to remember that… as long as I live, true death will not dare come near any of you.”

She stared up at me, eyes still as she understood the weight of the promise behind my words. I leaned closer to see her face. "Why are you quiet now?"

"That was a good line, but don't look pleased with yourself."

Chuckling, I pressed Stellar Qi into the fourth line. Her claws came out and sank into the lumber with a crack.

The shackle fought like a knot pulled tight beneath wet cloth. Forcing it closed would tear whatever had grown around it, which would be terrible. So I fed a thin strand of qi along its edges and waited for her body to loosen its grip.

Lilian breathed through her teeth.

"You can curse me if you want."

"I'm thinking of one bad enough."

"Take your time."

"Found it."

She used a word from the Moon Wolf language that I’d never heard of. The fourth line shrank beneath my fingers. I held it there until her body accepted the new position, then moved to the fifth.

This one resisted sooner.

Lilian reached back without looking and gripped my thigh. Her claws stayed retracted this time, though barely. "Still with me, Lilian?"

"Unfortunately. G-give me a warning next time!"

I stopped before the line could tighten again. Half-closed was enough for one session.

“Sure thing,” I said. The sixth shackle had formed a loop through the muscles around her spine, taking warmth and returning violent bursts of strength whenever her body felt threatened. Useful during battle. Less useful while sleeping. Why did she even feel threatened during sleep, was the question.

I caught the loop with two threads of Stellar Qi and changed its angle.

Lilian's grip tightened. Her forehead lowered until it nearly touched her knee, and a rough breath left her. The heat drain slowed.

I kept my hand against her back for another few seconds, making sure it stayed that way.

"Better?"

She considered it. "Well it feels warmer, at least."

"Mhm, that’s because the drain has slowed. Congrats."

She slowly turned to meet my eyes, “You don’t sound very happy."

"I should have fixed this earlier."

"It's okay you were busy."

Was I really? Busy enough to leave her to endure such pain when dealing with it didn’t even need much time? No, she had simply been waking up cold, eating whatever she could find, and waiting for me to notice. Very reasonable behavior.

I touched the seventh line.

She looked nervous, “More?”

I nodded. Silver moved beneath the black-red mark.

The shackle tightened at once, drawing on her lunar bloodline and something deeper left by Vargathrian's heroic blessings. It had fused during the Maharani's attack. An Arcane King’s attack was more than physical force, I should keep that in mind. Anyways.

Perhaps that fusion of bloodline and blessing was why the Seventh Shackle got so entangled with her body. It might also be why forcing it closed could damage her permanently.

I removed my hand.

"That one needs time."

Lilian straightened and pulled her shirt back into place. "Okay that’s good.”

"Don’t be happy. I don't know what closing it would do to you yet that’s why I’m taking more time."

"Oh,” she blinked. “So I'm stuck being your homework."

"For a while."

She gave me a flat look. "You've always been slow."

I didn’t comment on that, and she suddenly stood up and caught me by the back of my neck since I didn’t have a collar to grab onto. Her mouth pressed against mine before I could say anything else. She tasted like poorly roasted meat and the sweet water she'd handed me. Sweat made her palm slide slightly against my chest, so she tightened her grip and bit my lower lip when I pulled her closer.

There was sawdust on my arms. Some of it ended up on her shirt. Not that either of us cared.

Her back hit the stacked lumber with a dull knock. She drew me in between her knees, breathing harder now for a much better reason than the correction session.

A cluster of shadows fluttered over the yard.

Lilian stopped and growled against my mouth.

Small black bats folded in on themselves near the entrance until Nebula stepped out of them. She wore a dark navy Goryeo overcoat with silver trim along the lapels and cuffs, a white inner collar visible at the throat, and fur lining soft at the shoulders. A single red hairpin held her white updo in place, and that red was the only color on her that she’d chosen intentionally. The coat fell to mid-calf and moved like it had been made for exactly this kind of entrance.

It fit perfectly, and she looked like she knew it.

Her icy eyes moved from Lilian's loosened shirt to my mouth. She didn’t show any reaction, even as her brows tightened just a little. She said, "I have news."

Lilian rested her forehead against mine for one last second, then let me go. Then she turned toward her rival, "It better be something interesting."

"Baolian is preparing a diplomatic delegation to Goryeo."

Huh.

Interesting that sure was.

Lilian fixed her shirt while I stood. The workers nearby had gone quiet without anyone telling them to. "How certain is the news?" I asked.

"Certain enough that I came here instead of using the telepathic link." Nebula glanced at the workers. Lilian understood first. She stood and clapped once.

"We're done for today. You'll still be paid."

Nobody argued. Tools disappeared into boxes, and the workers filed into the street with the hurried dignity of people determined not to look hurried.

Nebula waited until the last man left.

"My thralls in Xianli heard the movement order. Another asset near the western grain office saw supplies being reassigned under diplomatic seals. Lailah found matching reports through Leviathan merchants in Qingluo and Dazheng."

"You have thralls in the imperial armies?" Lilian asked, surprised. “Leech.”

Her expression turned faintly offended. "Did you think I stopped at Xianli?"

"No. Why would I think about your thralls?"

"That is somehow worse."

I let the girls continue their banter while processing the important part. A diplomatic delegation by international laws required Goryeo's reception. Refusing would insult Baolian and give Shengzong a grievance he could shape however he liked.

Accepting meant letting his people enter Hwangcheon under guest rights, where every attempt to limit them could become another insult.

An army would have been easier to answer.

"Do you know who leads it?"

"No name yet."

That left too much open. A bureaucrat would measure Goryeo's willingness to bend. But in the same place, a general would make sure the court understood what happened if it did not. Shengzong could also send someone whose presence made those distinctions irrelevant.

"Alright. Any previous annexations?" I asked.

Nebula nodded. "The same pattern appeared before three of them. A goodwill delegation arrived first. Legal disputes followed once Baolian officials had learned where to press."

Lilian leaned against a support post. "Why are you guys suddenly tense? I don’t see what the issue is if we simply don't let them walk around."

"They'll ask politely," Nebula said. "When Goryeo refuses, they will record the refusal politely. Then they will stop their pretend play, and by then it’ll be too late."

"I still don’t see the problem.”

Of course the stubborn Lilian, ready to fight it out, didn’t see the problem. Funnily enough… I agreed with her for once.

So what if they were playing games? Under all the hazy mist, their goal is simple – a big fight. As long as we’re not scared of a fight, which we aren’t, then what is the issue even if they’re playing mind games?

Their goal remained unchanged.

This was a somewhat basic war tactic. I had never been much of a reader before, but as a sovereign, martial arts couldn’t be my only weapon so I’d read a lot of books ever since I started ruling Nevaramis.

When the enemy creates complexity, find the simple thing they are trying to make inevitable. In this case, it was submission or war.

Analyzing their endgame made that objective clear. So what was there to fear? Perhaps Nebula had noticed my lack of reaction too as she walked beneath the unfinished roof and studied the building. Her fingers brushed one of the new beams.

"Iskandaar… Are you sure about this? I mean, are you really?"

"The building?"

"Goryeo."

Her voice had lost its usual edge. Lilian noticed too so she didn’t answer it for me. She too looked curious. Nebula looked toward the neighboring houses. "You are putting Shengzong's attention here because you would rather have him looking at you. I understand the strategy."

She turned back to me.

"Have you decided what happens to these people if he refuses to look at only you?"

I had thought about that question since leaving Ha-Yun's palace. Thinking had not produced a better answer.

"That is not the strategy," I said. “Emperor Shengzong will attack Goryeo anyhow, it’s merely a matter of time. That is not a lie. The reason I am putting a branch here… is to have the right to protect this land with the excuse that my sect exists here.”

Nebula waited. When she saw that I did not add anything else, she sighed. “Then I wonder if Jin Ha-Yun is worth it, to risk your and our lives over it.”

That made me recall a memory. During Merasca, when the entire world had surrounded me. When I’d truly thought, for a moment, that it was the end for me. Back then, very few in the crowd trusted me. Even Amelia had misunderstood me.

But in that crowd, I remembered Princess Ha-Yun, who’d pushed through the people to find me surrounded. Her lips had moved softly as she’d uttered, “Iskandaar…” with a tone of worry, while wearing a frown toward the crowd, showing that she was offended that people were surrounding me.

Of course, she didn’t grab her sword and stand before me. But I couldn’t blame her for that. Her voice simply wasn’t strong enough to matter there, after all.

But my voice mattered now.

So what kind of friend would I be if I didn’t use my powerful voice to protect a friend who was powerless to protect me?

By evening, Solara had arrived with food and the workers had returned to collect two tools somebody left behind. The four of us sat beneath the open frame while the sunlight faded over Hwangcheon.

Lilian ate from a steaming bowl beside me and Solara warmed rice cakes over a thread of phoenix flame, much to the delight of the children watching from the alley. In the meantime, Nebula stood near the open wall in her uncomfortable robe, simply staring toward the palace.

I was halfway through an apple when Princess Jin Ha-Yun entered the yard.

She wore semi-formal blue and white, deep navy with gold embroidery along the hem, white silk beneath catching the lantern light. Her hair was down and slightly wavy with a silver ornament at her brow. Pink petals drifted around her as she walked but not blown by any wind, simply following.

Her eyes passed over the building, Nebula, Solara, and Lilian sitting against my shoulder. She greeted everyone correctly before looking at me.

"A letter has arrived, Iskandaar."

I set down the apple. "Officially?"

"An hour ago." She stepped beneath the frame. "Baolian wishes to send a goodwill envoy to strengthen neighborly relations with Goryeo… They will arrive within two days."

Nebula's attention sharpened.

"Your father accepted?" I said.

She smiled, "Could he refuse if he wanted to?"

Ha-Yun sounded composed, though she had folded one hand tightly over the other.

"No, he couldn’t,” I admitted. “Any news about who leads them?"

"Lord Fang Yizhen."

Nebula went still.

Ha-Yun noticed. "You know him?"

"By reputation, yes. I was reading a file about him in the Leviathan’s reports. He administered two Baolian annexations."

"General Park said nearly the same thing." Ha-Yun looked at me. "Then he asked my father to double the palace guard."

Lilian set down her bowl. Solara's flame went out beneath the rice cakes.

"Any confirmed Level?" I asked.

"He’s 9th Ascension, I don’t know Level."

It would not. If Fang Yizhen was weak, the delegation would bring someone who was not. Emperor Shengzong wanted Goryeo to accept the visit before learning exactly what it had welcomed through the gate.

Ha-Yun looked tired. She had hidden it well enough that I might have missed it before our time in Xianli. So I asked, "How did your father look when he read the letter?"

Her eyes lowered to the apple beside me. For a moment, she was back in whatever room she had just left.

"Like a man reading his own death sentence."
