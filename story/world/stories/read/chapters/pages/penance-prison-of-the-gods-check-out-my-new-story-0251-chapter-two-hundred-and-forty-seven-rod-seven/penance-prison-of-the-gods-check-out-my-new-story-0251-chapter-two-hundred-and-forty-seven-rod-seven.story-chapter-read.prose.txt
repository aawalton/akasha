I staggered back, raising the staff, but not quickly enough as the beast lunged for my throat. Syla tackled me from the side, knocking us both to the floor just as the warg’s jaws snapped shut an inch from my face. Its breath hit like rotting firewood. The creature skidded past, paws clawing for purchase and leaving deep gouges in the mirror floor. I rolled and scrambled upright, staff in hand, ribs screaming.

Syla was already standing again. “It’s not alone,” she said quietly.

She was right.

The mirror across from us rippled again, subtly, like wind on water, and a second beast emerged. A third flickered into being, the sound soft, too soft for things that big—a sound of displacement rather than snarls or stomps.

I turned in a circle, heart hammering as every reflection showed something different. Some showed beasts circling us, some stalking. Some didn’t match reality at all, displaying mirrored delays or lies or echoes. One even showed a fourth warg licking blood from its claws, but nothing stood there in the real world. I couldn’t breathe. Couldn’t track them. Couldn’t fight what I couldn’t see.

Syla didn’t hesitate. She flicked one dagger out and carved a long line across the nearest mirror-wall. “Only trust what bleeds,” she muttered.

I nodded, gripping my staff tighter. The real world was now marked; everything else was just noise. I needed space and time to think, but instead, I felt that pulse in my ribs. The boiling tension of summoned instinct rose like bile in my throat, a familiar thrum resonating through the wood of my staff as I prepared the incantation.

“Summon,” I growled, driving the staff against the mirrored floor. “Phantom Wolf!”

The air warped. A crescent ripple spiraled outward, and my wolf materialized: a silver-gray beast of bone-smoke and hunger, eyes glowing with spectral heat. It hit the closest warg head-on in a blur of motion, the two beasts crashing together like boulders in a landslide. Fangs met tusks. Claws met bone. The wolf didn’t howl; it tore. Its spectral jaw clamped onto the warg’s throat, driving it backward into one of the false mirrors. The wall didn’t shatter, but the reflection did, splinters of false light cracking across the surface like ice under boot.

That one, at least, was real. I staggered after them, hand outstretched, determined to end this.

I turned as one of the wargs leaped, aiming for Syla, not me. It dropped from above, claws outstretched, tongue lolling between jagged teeth. For a heartbeat, I thought it had her, but she didn’t flinch. She stepped into its shadow, her form vanishing like a bad frame in a broken film, and the beast hit nothing but mirrored tile. It howled, disoriented, as its momentum carried it past where she’d been.

Syla reappeared behind it, her daggers already in motion. Two clean slashes across its back legs snapped the tendons, and the warg’s hindquarters gave out, crashing to the ground in a mess of muscle and momentum. It howled again. She drove a blade clean into the base of its skull, a stunning blow although not deep enough to kill.

“I’ll finish it,” I called.

“Don’t bother,” she replied, already vanishing into the fog of reflections again.

I turned, pulse ragged, to see my Phantom Wolf locked in combat with a second warg. It had latched onto the beast’s foreleg, biting down with ghostly jaws. The warg twisted in pain, crashing against a column of mirrored glass.

This time, the mirror imploded. The creature was real.

I charged, staff raised, and slammed it down toward the warg’s skull as it stumbled from the broken glass and fell to its knees. My strike landed with a sharp crack as the core of its skull dented inward. It slumped, apparently dead.

Except it wasn’t.

Because the real one struck from the side.

Its reflection had played me. A crushing blow hit my ribs—its shoulder, I think—and I flew sideways, bouncing hard off the mirrored wall. Pain exploded behind my eyes. My staff skittered from my hands. I heard Syla yell something, but I couldn’t make it out through the ringing.

My summon turned to defend me. The Phantom Wolf lunged for the attacker, but its claws hit a mirror, striking its reflection instead of the beast.

The beast had already moved.

The moment the wolf hit the wall, the glass responded. A ripple flared across it like a water droplet on a still surface, and for a second, it felt like the mirror itself tried to bite back. A dozen mirrored wolves erupted from the impact: false copies of my summon, each twisting into jagged, angular beasts made of glass and hate. They turned on the real one instantly, and my Phantom Wolf howled in pain as the copies began to tear into it. I scrambled to my knees.

“Dismiss! Phantom Wolf, DISMISS!” I shouted, my voice desperate.

The summon evaporated mid-snarl. The copies collapsed like broken statues, scattering into dust and glint. The mirror pulsed again, satisfied.

This place was learning.

I crawled for my staff, still dizzy, still bleeding. My hand found the shaft just as a warm palm grabbed my forearm. Syla. She yanked me up in one motion, tucking herself beside me in a half-crouch. Her breathing was sharp, but focused. “They’re using the reflections to flank us,” she said. “We don’t fight them in the open.”

I nodded, coughing blood into the back of my hand. “So where do we fight them?”

She tilted her head toward the far wall. “That mirror cracked when you cast the summon. It weakened the illusion field. We use that.”

“We?” I asked, already bracing myself.

She smirked under the hood. “You’re bait. I’m the knife.”

As I exhaled, I stood. Behind us, one of the wargs began to prowl again, its claws clicking against tile. Round two was coming, and this time, we were ready. The warg came fast, snarling and slavering, bounding over shards of broken glass with terrifying speed.

Syla moved first. She darted left, dragging a dagger across the mirror surface as she passed. The reflection cracked, a jagged fault line cutting through my double’s face. That was the key. Every mark we made weakened their veil. I followed, staff braced low, using the line she’d cut as a guide. The warg lunged toward her, but she pivoted as I stepped in.

“Now!” she hissed.

I slammed the butt of the staff into the cracked mirror. The glass disintegrated, and with it, the illusion behind the warg faltered. It staggered, blind for a heartbeat.

That was all I needed.

A faint warmth spread from my core as I channeled the energy. “Summon: Blaze Ants.” Heat rolled up from my boots as the air shimmered. From a rift in the floor, a swarm of searing crimson ants burst forth, legs flickering with embers. They climbed the warg like fire made flesh.

It screamed.

The warg flailed, trying to shake the swarm off, but the Blaze Ants had already burrowed into its fur, their touch smoldering through flesh. Syla was a blur beside it. She slashed once across its snout, then rolled beneath its crashing paw as it reared back to crush her. She vanished, shadowstepping to its blind spot.

I pointed my staff.

“Detonate.”

The Blaze Ants exploded. A gout of fire engulfed the warg’s torso, the air cracking with pressure as shards of armor-like bone sprayed out. The smell was awful: scorched meat, singed fur, and melting glass. It dropped to its knees, and Syla finished it with a single stab to the soft flesh under its jaw.

She didn’t even look at me. She whispered, “Next.”

Another beast tried the same mirrored trick, pacing us through a false reflection. Syla marked it. I copied her pattern. Together, we carved a spiral around the field, dismantling the chamber’s illusion bit by bit until the fog lifted. The real walls appeared, revealing scorched steel and fractured tile instead of polished glass, pockmarked with battle scars and blood.

The next warg stepped forward, but its reflection didn’t move.

A flawed target.

I grinned. “Got you.”

The staff flared, a familiar prickling on my skin accompanying the cast. “Lightning Sparrow.” The crackle was instant. It dove from above in a snap of ozone, wings slicing the air. The warg turned as the bolt struck its core.

The mimicry didn’t save it.

Then the mirrors shifted again, all of them, even the broken ones reforming. The room pulsed like a living lung exhaling. A new shape emerged from a mirror that hadn’t existed a heartbeat ago, something that was clearly neither a warg nor any other beast we had encountered. It was taller and slouched, with hunched shoulders and twisted arms. One arm ended in three-jointed fingers that drummed idly against its thigh with a wet, clicking sound. Its skin was the color of old bruises. Its grin was too wide.

A Troll Trickster.

It clapped slowly, mockingly, as if applauding the show. It bent low and stepped through a reflection of us, choosing Syla’s image instead of mine. The troll’s form shifted as it moved through her reflection. Its clothes darkened. Blades grew from its sleeves. It hunched lower, mask-like bone reshaping across its face until it resembled Syla, but a corrupted, twitching, marred version with blank eyes. It darted forward with her speed, her stance.

“Syla, it’s copying you. Don’t let it touch your shadow!” I shouted, but she was already reacting, throwing her dagger straight at its throat.

It caught it and mirrored the throw. Her own blade spun back toward her, and she was forced to dodge her own technique.

“Don’t let it touch you!” I reiterated, a new urgency in my voice.

The thing was learning. We couldn’t win like this, not with illusions still half-active and a doppelgänger stalking the real Syla. She grunted, backing toward me. “This thing’s absorbing my attacks. I can’t land a hit unless I break the pattern.”

I scanned the room. “We need to change the stage.”

I grabbed one of the cracked mirror slabs and slammed it down onto the floor. The reflective plane splintered, revealing a narrow maintenance corridor behind it.

“Go!” I barked.

Syla dove through. I followed. Behind us, the troll let out a low, gurgling laugh and stepped back into the mirrors. The next round would be its, but now we’d have room to fight dirty.
