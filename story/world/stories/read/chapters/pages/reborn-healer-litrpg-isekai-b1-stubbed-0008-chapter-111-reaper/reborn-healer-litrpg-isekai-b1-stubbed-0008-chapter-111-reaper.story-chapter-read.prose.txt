Valencia

The northern wastes of Tarn were not the ideal location for a soul mage, but most of the better options had come with different complications. Carving a place out in any of the human great kingdoms entailed attracting far too much attention. Each of them carried at least one force that she didn’t want to anger before she could develop her position further.

The Saint-tier Halcyon king, the Grandmaster legion of the Final Church in Leyeril and their Sky Saint, the various Saints on both sides of Selvek and Torra, the Sovereign-tier human who held dominion over the Nameless, and many more… not to mention the Heavenly Emperor himself. Though he was listed in textbooks and official communications as a mere Sovereign-tier, the Soul Saint knew the truth.

The continent of Tarn was ruled over by a Divine-tier, the closest that man could reach to divinity without an ascension far truer than the one that brought them to the final three stages. He may have been Sovereign once, but parasites from the other continents had started looking to Tarn as a potential area to encroach on. The demon islands had been a deterrent for some time, but if the Heavenly Emperor had not reached Divine, Valencia knew that the continent would have been taken some time ago.

Hells, an organization from beyond the seas already existed on Tarn. The Revenants primarily dealt with Grandmasters who tried to upset the balance of things too much, but they were more than willing to fight Saints if the problem got too great.

Valencia knew this quite well, naturally, being one of them herself. Times were changing, and with other Revenants starting to break their oaths of non-interference, she had started her own, but there were limits.

All this was to say that she was very aware that despite being at the Saint tier, she was still yet to reach the stage where she could brazenly do as she wished. That had landed her in the northern wastes, where no kingdom ruled and the only rule was that of power.

She had planted seeds of her power here before, of course. Valencia had long been aware that there was a possibility her Order of the Seeker would disintegrate, whether it was because one of her idiot pawns would screw something up or because she was forced to burn them when her fellow Revenants came checking in on her at an inopportune time. She hadn’t quite been prepared for how it had been taken from her, but the act of its destruction itself, including total annihilation or not, was something she had been prepared for.

Now, though she lacked the same manpower she’d gotten operating in the dungeon underneath the Halcyon kingdom, she had regained a number of people. Three of them were lost souls, too, and alliances had even been managed without melting and reshaping their minds.

Aster, an orphan with a pacifying aura who had been raised by snow golems instead of people. Ernest, a noble mage from a fallen house who had been exiled to beyond the borders of the northernmost kingdoms, sent into the snowy, barren lands to die. Falin, a changeling summoned in a ritual by the Red Cloak assassin enclave and trained to kill with exceptional prejudice.

Despite being between fourteen and seventeen years of age, all three had already reached the Master tier. The northern wastes were packed full of adverse conditions and hostile monsters as well as tribes who would not hesitate to kill or enslave, and it had sharpened these three young prospects into very promising stock.

All three of them had died in the span of two seconds, their souls annihilated so quickly that even a Saint who had spent centuries devoted to the subject couldn’t keep them from flying away.

It had been late afternoon, but the sky was black now. Death, shadow, and strangely enough, restorative mana intertwined through the air, hungrily devouring every sign of life in the area.

Valencia’s defenses were failing quickly, but she was still alive, enough to know what this effect was. She hadn’t observed it directly, but she had seen memory capsules of it before. Every Revenant had.

Sovereign-tier spell, Endless Night. It was unique in its combination of two types of magic—healing and the Nightmare, the same combination that lost soul in Liaren had possessed.

This was the doing of the being formerly known as the Reaper Sovereign. It had been the twenty-fourth Cataclysm, had never been put down or even properly defeated, its absurd ability to heal itself outdoing everything anyone short of the Heavenly Emperor could throw at it. It had been almost a century since the former human’s last emergence in Tarn.

Unlike many of the other Cataclysms, this one had not grown all that much larger when it had shed its last fragments of humanity and fallen into the primordial magics. Valencia’s soulsight penetrated the darkness, letting her see the twelve-foot-tall creature at the center of this magical effect, some two miles away. It could almost be mistaken for a woman, if one excluded the fact that it no longer had a face and its internal organs were composed more of shadow than of flesh.

This was not a fight she could win, but it was not one she could flee entirely. Letting a Cataclysm roam free was a recipe for disaster continent-wide, and at least stalling it would possibly lead to its return to the World Dungeon or whichever alternate plane it usually existed in. Not doing so would result in the destruction of far too much, and even someone as selfish as Valencia knew she had a duty to stop this. The Heavenly Emperor would eventually step in if the Cataclysm ate a kingdom, but by then, the signs of weakness would be too great.

As much as she had quibbles with Tarn, the other continents were far, far worse. She would keep this continent from falling.

Right now, Valencia was likely the only Saint in a hundred mile radius. That also  meant she was likely one of the only people still alive in the area. Far, far away, she could just barely sense the hints of others coming. The monasteries had a couple of Grandmasters, at least. It wouldn’t be enough to defeat a Cataclysm, but maybe they would hold out for some time.

Another one of Valencia’s shield sets broke, and she cursed, redoing the Grandmaster-tier spells on herself. They hadn’t broken since coming into contact with a False Divinity, and not for years before that.

If I survive this, she told herself, I’ll find Ren Kane again. Just as a treat.

“I walk the narrows between heaven and hell,” she incanted in the old arcane, the language of gods. “Reap not that which cannot die. Rest not in death. Behold!”

[Valencia III, the Soul Saint] has triggered an Aspect.

Praying that this would be enough to survive, she shouted, “THE EIGHTH HELL.”

Sebastian

He was in Leyeril when the Cataclysm’s re-emergence made itself known.

Sebastian shuddered at the sudden, complete disappearance of a wide swathe of the threads of fate he was always watching.

The former commander of the Federation’s Liaren branch had grown used to seeing probabilistic holes. Ren Kane, it seemed, was not the only blind spot in his vision. There were others, and as Sebastian had taken to the shadows, operating in a confidence only shared by other high-level members of the Federation, he had started appreciating the fact that the age truly had changed. There were extraordinary people everywhere, many of whom were even in his guild. Of those, there were a handful whom he simply could not read.

This was different from that sensation, though. The Federation had extended outposts into the northern wastes—of course they had, for they had already established strong presence in all seven great kingdoms, and the only ways to expand were towards the demons or towards the unknown wastes. Having been forced to accept the mastermind role and give away his ambitions of taking Liaren for himself, Sebastian had grown into the position of ensuring that the dice always rolled well for his guild.

The Cataclysm extinguished almost the entire swathe of threads related to the wastes in an instant.

Sebastian swore to himself. He had barely been conscious for the last Cataclysm to hit the continent, and that time he had felt nothing more than a shiver. Being as aware as he was now came with its curses. It physically pained him feeling futures die, but there was nothing that could be done.

His role was not on the battlefield. There was nothing he could do against a Cataclysm in any case. Trying to do so was like attempting to fistfight a hurricane.

How many people died just now? There was a pertinent problem to focus on. There were nearly two thousand Federation members in the north, and this had wiped out a majority of them.

Casualty reports, next steps, reframing goals, logistics. Sebastian immersed himself in matters he was better suited towards, trying to ignore the chill down his back.

After he had drafted up the basics of what to do and transmitted them to the leadership in the northern kingdoms, he turned his attention back to Leyeril.

The Final Church was making moves with the assumption that the elven kingdom would assist them, but that was unlikely. Though the futures Sebastian could see were fewer and fewer now that there were more problems interfering with it, he could at least tell that there were far too many where the elves attacked Leyeril instead for it to be safe to assume their alliance would hold.

Liaren had ousted the Federation, and in the process caused Sebastian to lose a number of promising prospects for his guild. One of them had carried that aforementioned ability to simply eliminate divination spells.

Ren Kane did seem to have a particular tendency to get himself involved in disasters, as well as one to wreck the most carefully laid plans. Sebastian wanted that power on his side, but he was well aware that he wasn’t going to be the one to get it.

As he planned for the Federation’s response to the incoming kingdom-level conflict, he decided he would allow himself a brief aside to see if he could get Ren again. Ren would probably prove more useful than the other probabilistic voids, given their respective track records. Sebastian doubted Ren would be the most important piece, but it would be nice to have him again.

There were a couple favors he could call in. One in particular seemed to already have a connection to the boy, so that would be easy enough to pull on.

Sebastian rifled through his dossier for a living image of a man in his preferred dress of a white mask and a black suit. A Revenant, though he had never given his name.

At the very least, that would result in something entertaining.

Ren

Today, I learned how much devastation a Cataclysm could wreak.

Unlike the other times I’d watched one of these death replays, I didn’t see what happened after any of them. Before, I’d usually gotten anywhere from a couple of seconds to nearly half a minute to watch the resulting chaos or aftermath of whichever poor soul had perished, but this time, every vision was invariably the same.

Eleven times, I stood next to someone as they went about their day. That varied from training to eating to fighting back monsters to, in one case, having intimate relations. Eleven times, their lives were interrupted by an overwhelming wave of darkness.

I did actually have a point of comparison for this pressure. When I’d jumped on a plague bomb at the Grancrest guild village to save the Lord Prince, I had been tossed directly into the Nightmare, and my soul had been battered to bits by the divine being. This felt almost exactly the same—the pattern of fury from a god that shouldn’t have been able to feel.

Then, invariably, less than a second after the darkness ended, I would be shunted to the next person. That was it. People had been scattered in a pretty hefty range, as was clearly visible from the different landscapes in each one, and they had all died in the same instant.

Other people from Earth. My people, in some sense. All of us probably with some kind of bullshit like Rivia and I had, all dying just like that.

It made me acutely aware of just how far I had to go, and just how quickly it could be over. Eventually, the grim replay of death after death came to an end—well, almost.

The twelfth location I appeared in was high in the air. This time, there was no association with any dead body. In fact, I could see a sea of darkness spreading out underneath me, presumably from what had killed everyone else.

My stomach sank as I realized who had managed to get me to see them. I had hoped to never see this face again—soul, I supposed. The face wasn’t the same as before, but I could feel who this was.

The woman that the system had identified as Valencia III, the Soul Saint, turned to look at me in the throes of her Aspect.

“Ren Kane,” she said, amused, a hint of exhaustion threading through her voice. “Fancy seeing you here.”

The good news was that she hadn’t thrown me into a soul cage this time. As far as I could tell, she had just managed to snatch my soul while it had been thrown from death scene to death scene using what I recognized as her ultimate weapon—her Aspect, which I’d seen activated once under Liaren.

“Don’t worry,” she said, clearly strained by the Cataclysm’s power beneath her. “Though it pains me to say so, I won’t be able to keep you this time.”

I didn’t want to risk manifesting myself, so I didn’t respond.

“But to think, an Adept-tier who can soul project this far? To do what, view the dying moments of other lost souls?” Valencia threw her head back and laughed, even as she was clearly being pressured by the terrifying presence beneath her. “I’d like to meet you again, Ren, properly this time. If you ever see another Revenant, save your poor departed mother, tell them Valencia yue eien yakta.”

A flurry of thoughts spilled through my mind. Valencia was one of the best soul mages on the planet, so it only made sense that she would understand what was going on with my soul here, but her inviting me to speak didn’t jibe with what I’d seen of her so far. It was probably a trap, though it was impossible to tell with Whispers From Within totally unusable under these circumstances, but that last part actually seemed like goodwill.

“I will see you again, Ren Kane,” she said, the hold on my soul weakening sharply as she returned her focus to the fight at hand. “Try not to die before then, please.”

The second her Aspect stopped holding onto my soul, I was immediately ripped away back into my body.

I gasped awake, holding a hand to my chest. I hadn’t felt so powerless during my Soulwalks in quite some time now.

“Ren?” Mizuki asked, shaking my shoulder. “Ren, are you okay?”

“I’m fine, I think,” I said, following one of the deep breathing exercises Iryn had taught me to calm down. “Are you?”

“Yes. A Cataclysm just manifested on the continent, from what I’ve heard. I assume that’s what got you in that state?”

I nodded, trying to clear my head. “It was. How badly did it affect everyone else?”

“Varying degrees, but none as bad as you,” Mizuki told me, supporting me as I got to my feet. “I got a nasty sense of dread, but I think there are some people who were hit hard enough by it that they passed out. Most active magic got canceled, but it was only for a moment.”

“It happened up in the northern wastes,” I said. “The effect is that powerful down here?”

Mizuki, for once, looked entirely lost. “I don’t know. This is the first time I’ve been alive to experience one, and even the memories of the bloodline all blank out every time there’s been one. I… didn’t think it would affect us this much. The northern wastes, you said? You soulwalked that far?”

“Special circumstances,” I said. “I hope this doesn’t affect Gerald’s problem.”

The world was large, and as important as the issues we were dealing with were, they belonged to the microcosm of a single city in a single kingdom on a single continent. I didn’t actually know if there was more world beyond the edges of the continent, but the recent stark reminders that there were so many more conflicts elsewhere at any given time were a powerful motivation to get stronger.

To do the most good I could in this world, I would need to expand past Liaren. I would need to gain power from the sources my mother had listed to me. For that to work out, though, I needed to first ensure that Liaren didn’t get destroyed. Up until about five minutes ago, I had been pretty sure that I had the pieces to do what I needed to.

With an event occurring thousands of miles away carrying enough weight to stop a city in its tracks, I wasn’t so sure anymore.

Hopefully, the plans I’d set into motion wouldn’t be steered too far off track.

Iryn

She felt the Cataclysm through the Nightmare. It was impossible not to for anyone who’d formed a connection on the level that Iryn had. Any of the other remaining members of the sect, if there were any even alive and active right now with Aria gone, would have felt it too. She wondered whether Ren had sensed the same.

There was no system notification for the arrival of a Cataclysm. There would be for some, Iryn knew—in her time active, she had learned about those who were in closer touch with the divinities who had collaboratively assembled the system and thus had more detail on the happenings of the world. The Nightmare was not one of those entities.

This, however, was the being once known as the Reaper Sovereign. For quite some time, it had been regarded as a lesser deity in the sect, one of the secondary aspirants that a devout follower of the Nightmare could strive for.

Its arrival was a powerful influence on the entirety of the Nightmare, though that was less because it could control the plane itself and more because it was simply drawing on so much power. The Reaper Cataclysm’s existence was like a boulder dropped into an otherwise still pond. In comparison, Iryn might have called herself a smooth pebble skipping across the surface. It was inevitable that the ripples it caused would throw her off path.

Fortunately for her and Ren, Iryn had grown very skilled at navigating the Nightmare. She had thrown herself out of it for the time being, but she knew it was going to take some time before she would be able to access it again. Cataclysms could operate for days at a time, and her access was going to be shaky until then.

Even escaping had been a narrow thing, and it had left her at least a dozen miles off course. Iryn would be able to make up some of the distance to the elven kingdom on foot, since she was still quite fast on her own, but it was going to take at least an additional day.

Ren had found her with the Soulwalk skill that he’d grown significantly more skilled with at a frankly terrifying pace and told her to execute on their plan, and so she had. There were limits to how much she was permitted to interfere in this kind of conflict, but this was well within those bounds.

Irritatingly, she had no way to tell Ren that it was going to take more time. Vigil allowed her to leave landmarks for herself that she could detect and return to, and the ruined shrine she had appeared in was one of those. To use that, though, she had to travel through paths in the Nightmare, which the Reaper Cataclysm had disrupted. Ren’s abilities would likely be disturbed in the same way, though she had no way to confirm or deny that.

With a deep sigh, she set out on foot towards the elven empire herself, expanding her senses to make sure she didn’t kept snuck up on.

Iryn paused before she could even make it that far from the shrine. There were signs of someone else in this area, which there shouldn’t have been by any means. The shrine had been enchanted with runes meant specifically to avoid prying eyes and turn away travelers, but someone had taken shelter nearby.

It didn’t take long for her to find him, and the reason became clear once she found his small camp.

“Locke,” she said in disbelief. “Why are you here?”

Every instinct in her body told her to kill the abomination before her, to take the lifeline stored in the air before her and erase this demon from the world, but she quelled them forcibly. Aria had spoken to her about this creature so many times, and in the end, she’d entrusted Iryn with his continued existence.

Aiken.

She’d lied to Ren when he’d asked her about what her trials in the Nightmare had been, knowing that he was going to have to work with these beings in the future. It had been no Cataclysm that devastated her hometown, but these demons. The worst of the worst, the beings of pure hatred and violence who could only live if others died. They had killed her mother, father, and sister before her eyes, then tried to kill her while wearing their skins.

Iryn forced herself to relax, breathing deep. Locke had not slain her family, nor had he committed any crime beyond exist. Yet.

The Aiken looked older than he had the last time she’d seen him, though he still didn’t look much older than Ren. He must have exhausted life force at some point, using the magic only demons could—the power of the void.

“I was hoping I would be able to contact either Aria or Ren via a Nightmare shrine,” he said. “When it turned out to be infeasible, I decided to stay and regain my strength before committing to a final approach. It’s dangerous to travel alone in these parts now, after all.”

“You’re returning to Liaren?” Iryn tilted her head. “You were dealing with approaching demons, were you not?”

“I was, but there are others doing the same job,” Locke said. “I judged my options and felt that rejoining Ren would be appropriate.”

Iryn tensed. That would already be suspicious from anyone, but from an Aiken? From one of the demons who swapped bodies like she did clothes?

Locke noticed her unease, mana crackling gently by his hands. It died when he saw her looking.

“Aria is dead,” Iryn said. “A Revenant killed her. You were never going to reach her here.”

“I’m sorry to hear that,” Locke said flatly. “I will wait for her next life, then.”

“What do you want with Ren?” Iryn asked.

“He is interesting,” Locke replied. "And I would rather he not die before he is able to bloom into full development.”

He was intentionally lowering his guard so that Iryn would see that he was honest about that, she noticed. It was an effective problem solving method, she had to admit.

“Acceptable,” she allowed.

“Where are you going?” Locke asked.

“South, to the elves,” Iryn replied. “I have similar motivations as you, I would say, though I would also like for my city to not be overrun by elven attack.”

“I can accompany you, in that case,” the Aiken said. “It will be faster that way.”

Iryn weighed her options for a second, then nodded. Time was of the essence.
