“Lord Markus, may I ask you to come and observe something for a moment, please?" Thessalonor’s tone is polite, but there’s an edge to it that makes me look up sharply.

"Just give me a moment, please," I reply. I’m currently treating the wound of a carpenter’s apprentice whose knife slipped at the wrong moment.

Following the council meeting, I’ve taken to volunteering in the healers’ rooms, treating the common folk – even without Bonds to most of the people I’m treating, their inability to resist my magic means it’s not too taxing. And, unlike potions, my magic regenerates with time and meditation.

It’s completely consumed the little spare time I had managed to carve out between helping with the research on the Energy absorption treatment, managing the various teething problems of a new settlement, and acting as a hub of communication that almost rivals Sarran.

Unfortunately, that means no more walks in the forest or anywhere else with Tiria, something she’s not the only one disappointed about. I’m grateful that she understands, at least, and has reacted to the situation by throwing herself even more into her own side of the research.

"Of course, my lord," Thessalonor replies, waiting patiently.

I finish pressing magic into the wound, knitting it closed.

"There you go," I say with a small smile to the carpenter’s apprentice. "Take more care next time, you hear me?"

"Yes, my lord," the teenage lad replies, looking abashed. No doubt he already received an earful from his master when he earned the injury in the first place – he’d been loitering just outside the healers’ compound for a few minutes before I saw the blood starting to drip and called him in. He was even more nervous when he saw exactly who was calling him, but I wouldn’t take no for an answer.

Now, as he stretches out his arm and finds not even a trace of the deep slash that had opened it minutes ago, his expression shifts into wonder.

I love magic, I think, quietly enjoying the simple act of helping someone. Even if this wasn’t the best use of my time, I’d still want to be here – I can’t deny how satisfying it is. It’s always felt like one of the best aspects of magic I possess. Almost better even than flying.

Then, remembering Thessalonor and his request, I dismiss the lad and stand.

"I’m with you," I tell the healer, my mind starting to race as I consider what might be the reasons for him calling me in.

A slightly unwell cook doesn’t fit any of the worst-case scenarios I’d been considering, which is, in some ways, reassuring. On the other hand, that he’s called me in at all has me on edge.

I look at Thessalonor, a question in my eyes.

"Please, would you examine this patient, Lord Markus?" he asks. I’m assuming that his vagueness is intentional so, after giving him a long look, I focus on the person.

“You’re Kera Pless, are you not?” I ask, having a strong suspicion I know who this is.

“Yes m’lord,” she mumbles, dropping her eyes and twisting her hands together in her lap. I nod, not that she sees it. I’d thought so – this is Rory’s sweetheart. I’m glad to see their relationship is still going strong – stronger, I suspect, since Rory came to join her in the kitchens.

Though he’d been assigned to help the tanner on the ship due to his previous experience as a tanner’s apprentice, Rory had taken every moment he could to sneak off to the kitchens. While I’d assumed that was to see Kera Pless – and it probably largely was – he managed to pick up several tricks and tips in the course of his visits.

He also discovered a love of food preparation, perhaps stemming from knowing what hunger feels like all too well. He was nervous when he asked me if he could be reassigned, but after checking with the galley chef whether he would be more of a help or a hindrance, I acquiesced.

By this point, he’s actually been offered an apprenticeship from a Chef and is likely to earn a Class before the year is out, if he continues working as he has been. Not bad for a mugger sentenced to slavery. I suspect that the lady in front of me right now is a large part of his motivations and I hope that nothing is seriously wrong with her – for his sake.

I don’t say any of that, of course. Instead, pushing all thoughts of Rory to one side, I put on a professional smile and crouch down next to her.

"Well, Kera Pless, would you mind if I examine you?"

"Oh, my lord, of course– Of course not," she stammers, seeming surprised that I even asked.

My professional smile doesn't waver. She’s not the only one to be confused – most of my patients look at me strangely when I ask for permission before examining or treating them. But I’m not going to stop being considerate just because most people think it’s strange.

Placing my hand onto her shoulder, I feed magic into her system, searching for whatever it is that has Thessalonor concerned.

By the time I withdraw, I’m frowning.

"Excuse me a moment," I say politely to Kera Pless, then stand and draw Thessalonor to one side. I raise Seal to keep our discussion from listening ears.

"There must be a reason why you called for me," I begin once Seal is in place, "but for the life of me, I can’t work out what it is."

"That’s exactly why I called for you," he says solemnly. "Because neither can I."

I frown and gesture for him to continue.

"Kera Pless came to me almost a tenday ago – a day after our last council meeting – with persistent headaches, slight fever, chills, occasional vomiting, and joint pain. I examined her but could find no source for her symptoms. I gave her one of our remaining low-strength healing potions – if it had been a minor illness, that should have sufficed – and told her to return if the symptoms did not improve. As you see, she has returned. I still cannot find any cause, but I am wary of giving her another healing potion when the first had no effect."

My eyes narrow in thought.

"I hate to cast doubt on the patient, but is it possible the symptoms are psychosomatic?"

Thessalanor looks at me in confusion. "What is this?"

He attempts to repeat the word, but the way he says it makes it clear the translation magic has failed to get the message across.

"Psychosomatic," I repeat. "It means symptoms caused by issues in the mind, such as trauma or grief, rather than the body. Could that be the case here? We’re all under pressure – could that be causing her symptoms? Or has she lost someone?"

"I did not ask those sorts of questions," Thessalonor admits. "I am not a Mind Healer." He looks troubled. "Although, if that is indeed the source of her symptoms, then she will have little recourse for aid, as none of the Healers here specialise in mind healing either."

"Well, perhaps we should ask and see whether we should even be considering this as an avenue," I suggest, lowering the Seal.

However, after questioning Kera Pless further, we are still puzzled. She is feeling stressed, certainly – as are we all – but not unduly so. Careful questioning reveals that she is not high enough in the kitchen hierarchy to know the true state of our food supplies. She simply knows that we must use them wisely, which is little different from normal – certainly not enough to alarm her.

Nor has she lost anyone close. She knows people who have died, of course – despite our efforts, deaths are inevitable in a place like this – but she has no family or close friends among the patrols or hunting parties, which are the most dangerous roles.

I examine her again, but even a second attempt reveals nothing. In the end, I have to give up.

"Please return daily," I tell her. "It’s possible that whatever this is will reveal itself in time."

Unfortunately, before the end of the day, two more cases present with similar symptoms and the same lack of any obvious cause. The following day, five more arrive.

We begin keeping detailed records – what they have eaten, where they have been, where they live, who they live with – in case those factors reveal a pattern.

By the afternoon of the third day after I first examined Kera Pless, Thessalonor and I set some time aside to sit down with eleven case studies, trying desperately to identify any common thread in this mysterious malady. It’s far from an epidemic, but that it seems to be spreading without obvious cause is more than a little concerning.

I wish our researchers were available; this is far beyond my expertise. I can devise treatments with my magic well enough, but the kind of detective mindset required to identify the source of an unknown malady is another matter entirely.

“These four all worked in the farmer’s fields three days ago,” Thessalanor offers.

I flick through the case studies nearest me, then shake my head.

“But these seven were otherwise occupied three days ago, and four of them haven’t been anywhere near the farmers’ fields since we moved in.”

Thessalanor sighs and we both bend our heads over the files again.

“I’ve found a commonality in these files,” I say slowly a good half an hour and several more scratched theories later. “They’re all living in District Five.”

Thessalanor lifts his head and narrows his eyes. "The one near the entrance?"

"That’s right," I reply. We still haven’t settled on names for the districts, so for now we’re simply using numbers. There have been plenty of suggestions, but none have stuck yet. I tap the files spread before us. "All of these seem to live in that area."

Thessalanor hums and studies the records. My hope rises the longer he takes to answer.

Then he shakes his head again, and my hopes plummet.

"This one," he says, tapping a file, "lives near the command building."

That’s District One – not even adjacent to District Five. So much for that idea. I exhale heavily and turn back to the files, frustration pulsing through me.

Only a few minutes later, though, Thessalanor speaks again.

"But..."

I lift my head and look at him sharply, something in his tone immediately catching my attention.

"He lives near the command building," Thessalanor says slowly, "but he works in District Five, at the wood processing plant."

I frown, considering the information and suddenly doubting my own theory.

“But many people work in District Five – and even more pass through it. Shouldn’t this be more wide-spread if that were the link?”

“Perhaps it only takes effect if they spend prolonged periods of time in the district," Thessalanor suggests. “Besides, I hate to say this, Lord Markus, but there’s no guarantee that we won’t start seeing more wide-spread cases soon. Indeed, based on the last three days, it seems increasingly likely,” he continues heavily, shadows in his eyes.

“Then what could it possibly be resulting from? Some air-borne miasma coming in from outside?”

Thessalanor hesitates, then shakes his head.

"I wouldn’t think so. There would be more cases among the outside workers if so." He considers the matter thoughtfully. "Perhaps it’s the food that’s available in that section?"

This time it’s my turn to shake my head.

"We ruled that out, remember? Kera Pless only eats food from the kitchen. By the time she returns home in the evenings, she does nothing but drink water and go to bed."

We subside with almost identical sighs of frustration. After a few moments of silence, Thessalanor pulls files over to himself and starts looking through them again. For my own part, I get up and start pacing – I practically know those files by heart by now; there’s little point in reading them over again.

We must be missing something.

A commonality of large periods of time spent, but no shared food or single space otherwise. No recent contact between most of the patients. A mixture of males and females and different ages, though no children as of yet. Similar but not identical symptoms – could this actually be multiple illnesses that have coincidentally appeared?

That seems unlikely.

I continue pacing, then feeling that my mouth is dry, I pull a waterskin out of my Inventory and drink heavily.

Something strikes me as I’m drinking, and when I pull the waterskin away, I gaze at it thoughtfully.

“Where do you think they’re getting their water?”

Thessalanor looks up, a flicker of confusion passing over his face.

“My lord?”

“The patients,” I clarify. “Where do you think they get water?”

Thessalanor frowns.

“Those living in District Five should be getting it from the well,” he replies slowly.

I nod.

“And what about someone who works there?”

Comprehension dawns.

“Surely at some point they would need to drink water – which is most likely drawn from the District Five well.”

I smile grimly.

“We ruled the water source out from the start thanks to the purifying enchantments on the wells, but what if something has gone wrong?”

Thessalanor looks disturbed.

“If the well is the source of this, it’s highly unlikely that these eleven are the only ones to have drunk from it.”

“Precisely,” I agree heavily. “We need to investigate it immediately.”

I turn towards the door, eager to get started, but the moment I do, I’m interrupted by a mental call that comes through the Alliance Bonds I share with the other lords.

It takes me a moment to realise it’s Josiah.

Markus, we need help, he sends, sounding on the edge of panic.

What is it? I ask sharply.

A Tier three from the depths. A powerful one. We don’t have enough fighters on our ships to fend it off. You’re the fastest and most individually powerful – you’ll reach us first. Please come and bring help – quickly!

I glare at the wall, torn. The well could be spreading disease, but if the Tier three overwhelms Josiah, we might lose ships from the fleet. That would be a disaster for our already-precarious food supply.

“Investigate the well,” I order Thessalanor, the stress making me sharper than I intended. “Take whoever you need with you. Limit its use – quietly. We don’t want to provoke panic. And set someone to track everyone who does drink from it.”

“Are you not coming with me, my lord?” Thessalanor asks, clearly surprised.

“I’m needed elsewhere,” I inform him grimly, growing my wings. I’m coming, I tell Josiah.
