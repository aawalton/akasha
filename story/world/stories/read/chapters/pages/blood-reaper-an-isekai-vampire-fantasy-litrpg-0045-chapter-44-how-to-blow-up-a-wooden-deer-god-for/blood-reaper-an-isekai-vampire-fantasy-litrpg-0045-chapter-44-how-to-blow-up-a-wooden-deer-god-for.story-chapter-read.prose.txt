The former merc moved with the fluid confidence of a predator, each movement calculated to avoid being crushed by the Behemoth’s fists. This time, Victor was bait—and Ace felt a sick sense of satisfaction at the thought.

He needed the perfect shot—only then, would he strike.

This time, something in its stance changed—a subtle shift in its balance.

A tell.

It was preparing to charge.

Victor, however, didn’t seem to notice. The mercenary continued his circular pattern, apparently oblivious to the pending danger. The Behemoth's green nodes pulsed faster now, its massive form coiling like a spring.

At that moment, Ace saw the perfect opportunity.

Still kneeling at the edge of the forest, Ace reached for the darkness within himself. He went inward, toward that cold void he'd felt growing with each kill, with each drop of blood consumed, with each fragment of his humanity surrendered to the System's cruel game. It had been there since the first beast he had executed, a whisper at the edge of his consciousness, offering power wrapped in shadows. He had resisted it, uncertain of what embracing that darkness might mean—and what it might transform him into.

But Tara's blood still lingered on his tongue. Olivia had yet to get up. Rachel was gone. Marcus could barely walk.

Doubt was a luxury he could no longer afford.

Ace closed his eyes, his fingers spread wide as though reaching for something only he could perceive. He found the thread of darkness coiled in his core and tugged on it with all his willpower.

As he accessed Shadow Realm Immersion, the world fractured.

Reality peeled away like an onion skin, colors inverting and draining entirely as the temperature plummeted. Victor hurled a rock at the Behemoth, but the beast ducked. The stone sailed right toward Ace’s head. Though the rock would’ve been big enough to take him out, it passed through the space where Ace had been—through him—with no resistance. The stone became suddenly translucent, and it soared into the trees in grotesque slow motion. Sound compressed to distant, underwater echoes, the monster's roar reduced to a hollow vibration that Ace felt rather than heard.

Cold.

It was so fucking cold.

Not the physical chill of winter, either, but something deeper—a primeval absence of heat that laid frost to the bone, to blood, to whatever passed for his soul. His breath crystalized in front of him, hanging suspended in the non-air of a dimension that existed alongside reality.

The Shadow Realm.

Darkness stretched in all directions, not as an absence of light but as the presence of something else—a substance that clung to his skin like oil and smoke. The physical world remained visible but altered, as though he were watching it through smoked glass. Victor raced toward the monster in surreal slow-motion, his face contorted with predatory focus.

Olivia groaned nearby and pushed herself off the ground, one hand to her head as she winced—alive, thankfully, but clearly still injured.

The System was gone, now, but something else moved in the darkness.

Not his teammates. Not the Behemoth. Not even Victor. Something native to this place, something that existed in the shadowed margins of reality.

Vague silhouettes shifted in his periphery, dissipating when he tried to focus directly on them. Whispers tickled his consciousness—not words but impressions of what had perhaps once been people. The darkness itself seemed to pulse with awareness, acknowledging his presence as an intruder.

A visitor.

A potential meal.

Ten seconds. That's all the Skill description had promised him. Ten seconds of Shadow Realm immersion before he would be forcibly ejected back to reality. Anything more than that would come with a SM cost he didn’t want to risk paying right now.

Eight seconds remaining, actually, according to his internal counter.

Ace bolted forward, unhindered by the shadows around him as he phased through dimensions. The Behemoth lowered its head and tried to skewer Victor with its horn, but it missed by a fraction of an inch. Ace positioned himself behind the creature, planning his attack for the moment his Immersion ended.

In this world of darkness and half-light, the monster’s nodes burned brilliantly green, like perfect targets hovering in the void.

Seven seconds.

The whispers grew more insistent, circling closer. One shadow detached from the others, its form coalescing into something almost humanoid but wrong—its proportions stretched, its limbs too numerous, its features suggested rather than defined.

Once again, Darkmoor appeared in a flash of smoke amongst the others—watching. Waiting.

Six seconds.

Blinding veins of green light coursed along the Behemoth’s body, clustering in those nodes he had been targeting thus far. One, however, glowed brighter than the others. At the base of its neck, a cluster of light burned like a mini emerald sun.

Four seconds.

Shadow coalesced in his palm—darkness given physical form through soul energy. The blade materialized with a sound like reality tearing at the seams. The dark steel of its blade existed as both weapon and void, its edges shifting like fog rolling off the sea.

The Duskblade hummed against his palm, hungry for blood. It wasn’t just a weapon. It was an extension of whatever the hell he was becoming. Each time he summoned one, the blades seemed more eager, more alive.

As if they recognized him.

Two seconds.

Out of pure instinct more than anything else, he raised one palm toward the glowing node. A vibration rocked his body, and a circle of black smoke painted the node like a target.

Shadow Mark.

One second.

In another flash of smoke, the figure in his periphery appeared in front of him. Ace's eyebrows shot up his head in surprise, his focus on the monster briefly broken as a haunting face hovered before him.

The figure’s features rippled like ink in water, the suggestion of a face constantly forming and dissolving. Where its eyes should have been, two pinpricks of cold blue light floated in bottomless sockets. It tilted its head in curiosity as it studied him. The creature's body billowed like smoke caught in a gentle current, edges fraying into wisps that rejoined its mass in hypnotic patterns.

Before Ace could react, the shadow's pseudo-fingers—elongated appendages that tapered into claws—pierced his chest. They plunged into his heart, not breaking skin but passing through it, bringing a bone-deep cold that spread through his veins like frost.

But it didn’t touch his body.

No, it touched something deeper—the part of him that the darkness recognized as kin.

“Found you.”

The whole ordeal had lasted for less than a second, and then he was kicked out of the shadows.

Reality crashed back with physical force. Sound returned in a deafening wave—the Behemoth's roar, Olivia’s scream of pain, the crack of Victor's whip. Color flooded back, nearly blinding him with how bright it had become, over-saturated and painful after the monochrome void. Ace materialized directly behind the Behemoth, his body solid once more and his senses reeling from the transition.

The creature hadn't even registered him. Its full focus remained on Victor, whom it was desperately trying to rip in half.

Still high from his time in the Shadow Realm, instinct burned through Ace rather than conscious thought. His fingers tightened around the Duskblade’s hilt, and his glare focused entirely on the Shadow Marked node at the base of the Thornhide Behemoth’s neck. Before the beast could turn around, Ace hurled the Duskblade with all his might. It flew perfectly straight, guided by Shadow Mark, and embedded deep in its target.

Shadow energy crawled through the monster's body like fast-growing cancer. Bark plates shattered as the creature's wooden skeleton contorted, its joints bending in directions nature never intended. It stumbled, and now Ace could see its contorted face. Its massive head reared back, those emerald eyes dimming as the corruption spread.

The roar it released wasn't pain. It was existential terror—the sound of something recognizing its own death.

It lashed out wildly, desperate for vengeance, but everyone kept a safe distance. The monster had become a tactical unknown, its death throes unpredictable and lethally random. Olivia retreated to higher ground, limping alongside Marcus, her blood-streaked face set in a grim frown.

Victor circled with predatory caution, his shadow-whip at the ready but unwilling to approach the dying behemoth. He no doubt understood that getting in close now would be a suicide mission.

Too bad. At this point, Victor wouldn’t be missed.

The Behemoth convulsed, its fifteen-foot frame suddenly rigid as every muscle locked simultaneously. Shadow energy erupted from its eyes, its antlers, from every joint where its bark plates connected.

"Everybody down!" Ace shouted. He dove behind a nearby boulder, his body moving before his conscious mind could process the threat.

Not with fire or shrapnel, but with shadow—a concussive wave of smoke that expanded outward with supersonic speed. A few trees at the edge of the forest were caught in the blast radius. They didn't burn, but rather just ceased to exist. It didn’t touch the stretch of forest where he’d left Tara, thankfully, and he counted his lucky stars for that. A blackened circle appeared in a sudden rush in a perfect thirty-foot radius from the beast’s feet.

With his back to the boulder, Ace braced himself. The ground rumbled, and the whistle of something shooting through the air followed.

Something headed right for him.

Before he could move, the whistling projectile hit the stone, and a deafening crack shot through the boulder. A split second later, one of the creature’s thorns broke clean through the rock and impaled his bicep.

Not even his armor was enough to stop it.

He gritted his teeth to stifle an agonized yell. Miserable waves of white-hot pain shot through him as he fought to keep himself from blacking out. He glared down at the barb protruding from his arm and clenched his hand into a fist.

With a furious tug, he pulled himself free of it—and did his best not to scream as the pain ripped through him, worse than ever.

Then, stillness.

His blood stained the boulder. Thick crimson rivers poured down his forearm and pooled in his hand, dripping from the gaps between his fingers in a steady rush. He stumbled, trying to regain his balance, but his natural vampire healing wasn’t enough to close it.

When Ace raised his head, the Highlands and treeline had been transformed. The Behemoth's carcass lay at the center of a perfect circle of devastation, its fifteen-foot frame collapsed into a third of its original volume, and its bark plates fused into a charred husk. Ancient trees stood truncated at precise angles where shadow energy had passed through half of them, creating a surreal landscape of perfect destruction.

"What was that explosion?" Victor's voice carried across the unnatural silence, and genuine shock disrupted his perpetual sneer.

Instead of feeling depleted, Ace felt a rush of energy that could only mean his Soul Meter was replenishing.

Wait. That didn't make sense. He'd spent resources on Shadow Realm Immersion, Shadow Mark, and Duskblade Manifestation. His meter should be lower, not higher.
