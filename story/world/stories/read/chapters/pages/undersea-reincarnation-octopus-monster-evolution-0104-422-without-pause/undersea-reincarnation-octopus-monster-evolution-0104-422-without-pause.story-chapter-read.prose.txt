Say what you will about A-ranks. They were tough bastards. And now I’m one of them, Henry mused as he kept his eyes peeled for any movement. On the other side of the cave, Sel’Veth was still being hammered by Toxic Rays, though by now, she’d encased herself in a bubble and seemed to be up to something. Mana was being funneled toward her, for some reason, and that meant he was on a timer.

“[No notification,]” tersely warned Maurice, and Henry was well aware. He could feel the outrage-filled aura wafting off the cloud of blood and dust, which was why he pulled two manifested Octominds to him and had Maurice latch on to one of them.

“[I know, but I’ll finish him off. In the meantime, if things look dicey, one of’em will store you in Maw. Do something about her. I don’t like how she cocooned herself like that.]”

“[Got it!]” Maurice sent back, then with an escort of the remaining four Octominds, he swam away. As for the creatures Golden Mask had been commanding, they were gone. Pretty much as soon as the imploding bubble had detonated, they had swum into the murk, looking for their master, or maybe they’d been recalled. Whatever the explanation was, they were out of sight, but not out of mind. Henry had left his manifested Octominds to Maurice, but there were nearly twenty invisible Avatars waiting alongside him to see what came out of the dust. Whatever it would be, Henry was ready. By now, the pulsing magic being funneled and concentrated in his core was making small arcs of red lightning dance dangerously down his arms, and Henry was still funneling mana into the nexus.

He was fully intending on ending the Merfolk on the next exchange. And so it happened that it was the Merfolk that initiated it. Like a bullet out of the fog, the Merfolk shot out, surrounded by a corona of ghostly whites, trailing blood in his wake and Henry almost, almost, winced in sympathy at the missing shoulder and bare, bleeding ribs. But the shark-like teeth and the maddened gaze made that really hard.

That and the willingness of the man to make a puppet out of Henry.

Golden Mask howled, his red, glowing fist pulled back. There were no spears anymore, but that didn’t mean there was no danger. Henry could sense a terrifying amount of Aspects in that cocked fist of his, but so too had the skill Henry had been nursing for the last few seconds and right before the impact, Henry recalled how threatening the parasite A-rank had felt a couple weeks back. How his senses screamed at him that he couldn’t allow himself to be hit. But even though that parasite and this one were nearly at the same level, Henry didn’t think he was in that much danger.

Well. While he wasn’t risking his life, he was definitely going to bleed a little. But nothing he couldn't heal up.

Henry met the Merfolk’s fist with two folded arms and immediately lost all feeling in them as his own blood burst out from the implosion. But while he’d likely lost two arms, one of them had wrapped itself around Gar’Dak. Henry sensed the panic and the attempt at extricating himself, but three more arms whipped out and really secured Henry’s grip on his target.

The Hug of Death was primed, and Henry let it rip.

The cave lit up red, as if the thunder of a demonic god had suddenly come down to smite the Merfolk. The water boiled and frothed as violent bubbles of superheated steam surged around Henry and the Merfolk. A thunderclap rang out, creating multiple shockwaves while lightning arced and crackled, reaching for anything else to ground itself in. A handful of Henry’s clones ended up being collateral victims though thankfully, the lightning didn’t chain too far, and even though Henry was much more resilient than the last time he’d used this attack against the seahorse, this one was still orders of magnitude more powerful. But while his body was burning and cooking as well, his soul was just fine.

The backlash was significant, but it wouldn’t put him out of the fight.

An explosive, wild second later, the intense red mellowed as most of the power was discharged, and as the colors of the cave came back, the purple and black remains of the A-rank came into view, the exposed ribs glowing red. For a moment, Henry thought he’d sensed the Merfolk’s aura stutter and tremble, but then… it winked out. Like a blown candle, his the life of the Merfolk was snuffed out, and with it, the notification came through.

1x [Master of the Sunken Menagerie (A) - Lvl 35] greater core fragment was collected.

Henry stared at the corpse for a beat, feeling… not quite cold, as the remnants of the Lightning Discharge were still zapping down his own damaged limbs, but a little disconnected. But only for a beat before he stuffed the body in storage and turned his attention back to his surroundings and as he did, he wondered whether his lack of anguish was due to the Merfolk’s attitude and how he wanted to enslave them, or because he had become more kraken than man. It was worth thinking about. But not right now.

“[Henry! I can’t stop her. I think… she’s doing something teleport-y. But neither my Domain nor my Bubble is getting through her barrier. Also, I got a greater core fragment! Yay!]”

Henry stole a look toward Maurice and the barrage of Toxic Rays being sent at the spherical barrier, which, now that Henry was paying attention, was most certainly using Aspects. Also, there were still three A-rank monsters around, and now that their master was gone, the three seemed much less intent on fighting.

Well. There was that fact that their leader was dead, but there was also the fact that all three seemed to be barely awake at this point. Only minutes ago, all three had seemed dangerous and at the top of their game, but now, all three, the ghostly shark, the anglerfish, and the mantis shrimp seemed to be lethargic. Almost drooping.

Had the Merfolk taken something from them before he’d died? Maybe he’d borrowed some of their vitality when he’d tanked all of that damage, just like he had borrowed their abilities.

Maybe Maurice’s attack had done more damage than it had seemed, he thought. But considering they weren’t attacking, Henry sent an Octomind at them to see if they wanted to be pulled out of this cave, while he collected Maurice and approached the Merfolk with the pearlescent mask, and as he did, he sensed her a little better.

She was afraid. Which… for some reason, pissed him off a little more. She was looking up at him as if he were some aggressive monster that went out of his way to hurt them. When it was the exact opposite of what had taken place.

“I told you to leave us alone,” he told her, not without some heat while he wrapped one of his uninjured arms around Maurice, then, as he healed his still mangled two front arms, he began prepping for a retreat.

He wasn’t sure what Pearlescent Mask was up to, but he didn’t feel like killing her, no matter how much her woe-is-me attitude got on his nerves, so he was ready to head out. Especially considering that the recently freed beasts were already in his Maw, having realized that as soon as the Merfolk left, they’d be left to suffocate in this dark, dank cave. Obviously, the mantis shrimp had given him some attitude as his Octomind explained that bit to them, but while none of them seemed to be at the level where they could have a discussion, they understood the concept of being stranded just fine.

Now, Henry could leave. His path up to the surface was already drawn, as Find the Path was surprisingly good at finding them an exit. But he still was curious, so as he pulled his Avatars to him and stored most of them away, he watched, considering how he might potentially salvage this relationship if the Merfolk were eventually needed for the war with the otherworldly invaders.

He didn’t need to watch for long.

The mana stopped flowing toward the A-rank, while in front of her, a mirror-like orb had suddenly appeared, a few paces out of the barrier that protected her and the other injured A-rank she had brought with her.

Henry looked at the orb, unsure of what his senses were telling him, but then, before he could make sense of it, the orb stretched and flattened in a way that almost hurt his head, but soon he understood what it was, as the more it stretched, the more its foggy, oddly colored surface cleared, until Henry found himself looking into an office lit up with blue lights, and behind the desk, a Merfolk with pale, yellow scales looked up toward him.

Then the aura billowed out, and Henry’s eyes widened, while Maurice began tapping him urgently on the arm.

“[Uh… Henry?]”

Henry was already activating the teleport to run up his chain of relays, only for a wall of light to hit him. Cursing, he transitioned immediately into a Lightning Dash toward the closest exit. He didn’t even pause to cast Identify.

That aura felt nearly as powerful as Zerathstra’s.
