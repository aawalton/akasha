Jianyi woke up in the middle of the night. He had been hunted through the wilderness like a deer by a pack of wolves, and wherever he ran, purple eyes were following him.

***

Group Lapis stood on the training field, and Ruolan’s fellow inner disciple, whose name was Wen Tao, demonstrated Piercing Fang.

To Jianyi, his execution seemed flawless, nevertheless he raised his hand to ask a question once Wen Tao was done.

“Yes?”

“Senior Brother Wen, thank you for the demonstration. I was wondering whether we will go back to the movements at a later stage?”

From their empty faces, it was clear that several of the youths around him did not know what he meant, but Tao responded. “It is considered old-fashioned to start with the movements. Faster results can be achieved by teaching the forms directly.”

Jianyi bowed his head in thanks for the response, but Ruolan interjected herself. “Do you think you know better?”

He was tired of the needless hostility but was not interested in another confrontation. “Not at all, I was just curious as I was taught differently.”

“If you had been taught properly, you might have performed better at the assessment.”

He briefly considered whether he needed to defend Minghao’s and Elder Sun’s honor, but he was sure both would just chuckle at the thought, so he just shrugged and ignored Ruolan’s attempts to rile him up.

It was not like going through the individual movements, like Rise or Advance, was terribly exciting, but learning the forms with Minghao had felt considerably easier knowing which movements to combine.

He certainly wouldn’t argue with the inner disciples, but he resolved to spend more time on the movements in his private practice sessions to complement the study of forms in the classes.

For the rest of the time, they practiced the form individually with the inner disciples providing pointers on the correct forms.

Fortunately, Ruolan largely stayed away from him.

***

A week passed. Jianyi was busy but happy. Every day, he learned something new, and while the spear training under Ruolan and Tao felt slightly uninspired, it was still hours and hours of time dedicated to perfecting his forms.

Despite all the classes and chores, Jianyi found the time to train his qi control, meditate to unlock more of his will, and sneak in a few training sessions with Minghao or even on his own.

He had eagerly looked forward to this morning as it was finally time to open his dantians. He knocked on Elder Sun’s door and entered.

“Good morning, Jianyi.”

“Teacher. Thanks for making time for me.”

“You must be excited about taking this step?”

“I am! I should finally be able to cultivate and use my cards. It’s what I dreamed about for a very long time,” he said, blushing slightly for admitting his childhood fantasy.

The elder chuckled. “I remember. It’s been a very long time, but I still remember that feeling. Now, I don’t want to torture you with an old man’s stories.”

He produced a small translucent pill bottle that held just a single, green pill, then placed it on the desk in front of him, so that Jianyi could grasp it, but would have to lean forward.

“This is a Nourishing Open Sea Pill. You can take it now, and within one hour from now, your dantians will be open, and you are well on your path to becoming a powerful cultivator.” The elder smiled.

Jianyi squinted. He was young, but he was not a fool. The elder hadn’t even tried to hide the ‘but’ in his statement.

He was still tempted to just take the pill and be done with this.

To that day, he hadn’t fully recovered from his failed attempt to open his meridians. He felt like he had regularly taken the harder path to unlock his full potential. It would be nice if things could be simple for once.

He suppressed a sigh, but given his teacher’s amused expression, he hadn’t succeeded in keeping his emotions hidden.

“Is there a drawback to using this pill?”

“Most would say no, or rather that they are negligible. This is the way that almost everybody opens their dantians these days.”

The elder’s smile looked almost predatory.

“You disagree, though?”

“I am glad you ask, my young friend. In my experience, it almost always pays to question these commonly held beliefs, in particular if it affects your cultivation path.

“To answer your question, yes, I disagree. It certainly isn’t wrong to use the pill, and the side effects are limited, but why would we aim for anything but perfection, I ask you?”

Jianyi nodded slowly. It really wasn’t that he didn’t agree, but he could already sense that he wouldn’t be walking out of this room with his dantians open.

“But let me be transparent. Besides this pill, there is another way to open the dantian, which is, if you will, a ‘manual’ approach in which you use the qi in your lower dantian and your qi control to apply pressure to the dantian walls until they are fully formed.”

The elder pulled a scroll from his storage ring and placed it on the desk beside the pill bottle. “The Spiritual Sea Forging Technique guides the path. I am sure you are wondering why you would want to go through that effort if a pill can give you a much quicker result, and I have to admit it is much quicker. If you follow the manual approach, it will probably take you two weeks to complete the task.”

Two weeks! Jianyi wondered how many of his fellow disciples had already taken the pill and opened their dantians.

“First of all, almost every pill you take leaves alchemical residue in your body, very similar to the impurities that you just removed successfully from your dantians. Individually, that is not a problem, but you will have to take hundreds, maybe thousands of pills over your lifetime, and it all adds up. Collectively, they can diminish the efficacy of pills and potions you take, which means you will need to spend time and effort to remove the impurities. I admit, though, that that alone would not be a reason not to take the pill.

“Another small downside of this approach is that if you rely on the pill, you will later have to learn a simple technique that will allow you to view your own dantians and the cards therein. Again, this is a small price to pay, and I am sure you would learn it quickly.”

The elder seemed to have fun. So far, he had always left every decision up to Jianyi and not interfered much, but now he worked on selling the manual approach like a street merchant hawking overripe pears minutes before the market closed.

“The main reason, though, to use the manual approach is that it provides the superior long-term growth path. When you apply this technique and manually expand your dantians, they remain more pliable, more flexible, which eases qi circulation and makes expansions of the dantians during ascension across the realms easier. This is something that will pay off once you reach Core Formation and beyond, which I know feels like a long time, but as a cultivator, it is important to build foundations that will support you through the centuries.”

The elder leaned back and smiled at him. “Obviously, my young student, the choice is yours.”

Jianyi almost snorted. He didn’t think the elder would punish him if he chose the pill, but it was also ‘obvious’ that the elder had a very clear preference, and why would you not follow the advice of somebody who had infinitely more experience when you had none? But more importantly, he did see the point. Why indeed strive for anything but perfection?

“Thank you very much, teacher. After careful deliberation, I have come to the conclusion that the Spiritual Sea Forging Technique is the best path forward.”

Elder Sun laughed. “I knew I had a smart student. Let me know how you progress with the opening.”

Jianyi took the technique, bowed, and left the room. Another hurdle, but nothing that couldn’t be overcome with a bit of discipline.

***

Sitting in his cultivation chamber, Jianyi unrolled the scroll with the technique Elder Sun had gifted him.

And it had been a gift, something that Jianyi had overlooked in the moment and since then thanked his teacher for. He was pretty sure that the technique would have eaten his hard-earned contribution points should he have had to get it from the library. It was important to him not to make this a habit, but given the fun that the elder had had with getting him to agree to the manual approach, he thought it was acceptable.

He started to read the scroll and quickly grimaced. It was unusual for a technique to include a warning that it would be painful, and the two weeks that Elder Sun had indicated would require him to basically cut the little sleep he got in half, spending hour after hour working on his breakthrough. He sighed and carried on.

The starting position was clear; his lower dantian had been opened when he had received his core card during the heavenly trial, it now needed to be expanded and stabilized, while his middle and upper dantians were fully closed.

The Spiritual Sea Forging Technique outlined a method of cycling qi through his pathways, where whenever he would pass through his lower dantian, he needed to brush along the sides as if he were handling a palette knife and extend it. For the other two dantians, it was essentially the same, just with the caveat that it initially required an opening procedure similar to the opening of his meridian gates.

Actually, the technique didn’t provide just one method, but multiple, depending on the number of threads of qi one could control simultaneously. Obviously, the more threads, the quicker the method worked.

Jianyi paused for a moment. He had been able to control two threads for quite some time now, otherwise he wouldn’t have been able to use the Martial Meridian Art after all. Despite his disciplined exercises, he still struggled to control three threads, though. It turned out that the complexity grew exponentially.

He was sure that handling three threads was in reach, but, after taking a deep breath, he admitted that, outside of his own desire, there was no real urgency for the dantian opening. At least not to the degree that he needed to risk messing things up with handling more threads than he could comfortably do right now.

It had been different with the Imperial Gate Opening Technique—he could not have opened twelve meridians with that technique, so a risk was warranted. Opening the dantians a few days earlier or later, however, was not critical.

That decision made, he could focus on the method with two threads, and that, indeed, looked like something that was doable in the two weeks that his teacher had talked about.

Beyond the cycling technique, one other key element of the scroll was outlining the definition of when one was done, and the rather simple comment was that a larger dantian in general was better than a smaller, and that one should continue until one couldn’t take the pain anymore.

Jianyi shook his head. The cultivation world seemed insane sometimes, and he wondered what was wrong with him that he was not worried at all.
