The next few days passed in a blur of activity. From group meditation to playful duels, from group delves to training sessions with Crixis.

Yet even amidst the relentless schedule, Aodhán’s newly reignited love for runes never waned.

As the hours turned into days and the days into weeks, he even managed to rope Crixis and Sophran into it, teaching them the first twelve runes of the runic alphabet while he wrestled with the final three.

RAVOIR—the flexibility rune.

SKALDIR—the barrier rune.

And finally, THRYGAR—the lightning rune.

Unlike the others, these three runes were so complex and convoluted that they almost seemed like advanced runes, their structural lattices making even the word complicated feel like an understatement.

But Aodhán wasn’t fazed by their complexity.

Instead, he attacked each one with dogged determination, spending every spare moment sketching circuits, tracing pathways, and memorizing layered structures until their entire matrices were practically burned into his soul.

All that hard work paid off. And finally, as the second week drew to a close, he perfected them.

The breakthrough came an hour past midnight near the end of the second week while the rest of the sect slept.

Aodhán was in his room with Crixis and Varéc, as had become their tradition over the past few days. Varéc lay curled in the corner of the room, a crackling sphere of black lightning hovering lazily before him, while Crixis, despite his newfound passion, had passed out atop a stack of runic texts, a half-formed ISA rune abandoned on his practice sheet.

Aodhán sat at his worktable, wide awake, grinding away at THRYGAR, the last rune with single minded focus.

Scraps of wrinkled and torn parchment lay scattered all around him, the evidence of all his failures. Yet, despite having been at it for more than five hours now, Aodhán’s hands were as still as stone as he inscribed the last ring into the runic matrix, looped it across the spinal anchor before seamlessly joining it to the activation circle.

So lost was he in the process that it took him a heartbeat to realize he was done.

A heartbeat where nothing happened.

And then—

The rune glowed, golden light spilling across the room as it shone with quiet perfection.

Aodhán grinned, excitement surging through him at the achievement. He wanted to shout. To scream and jump for joy.

But he restrained himself to a whispered scream and a series of silent fist pumps that drew Varéc’s attention, causing the familiar to shuffle excitedly as he shared Aodhán’s triumph.

After letting himself celebrate for a few moments, Aodhán glanced back at the glowing rune, a look of genuine satisfaction settling over his face as he studied it.

He had done it. Finally.

After more than a year of practice.

In all honesty, he had probably spent only a quarter of the last year truly practicing—what with all the wars and kidnappings—but today still marked a year and three months since he had drawn his first rune.

He could still remember that day clearly. The runic charts lining the walls. The lab suits Professor Cladwell had forced them to wear. Yurin sleeping through class. And most importantly, the mess of whorls, rings, and curves he had called FEHU.

He even remembered the joke Professor Cladwell had made, teasing him that his version of the rune could be considered a long-lost cousin of the real thing.

He chuckled at the memory, shaking his head.

He had come very far since those early days. If someone had told him back then that, somewhere along the way, his passion for runes would wane, he never would have believed it.

Yet somewhere amidst the wars... the killing... the deaths… it had. Until exactly a week and five days ago, when he had watched Elder Gumi casually create a Mythic item and toss it as though it were nothing more than a common trinket.

Until he’d watched her inscribe elder runes far more easily than he would an initiate rune yet do it with such artistry and mastery that it was practically otherworldly.

The sight had struck something deep within him, reigniting his passion for the ancient language in a way he had never experienced before.

Even now, as he stared at the glowing rune, he could feel that hunger urging him onward.

He grinned; any tiredness he had felt earlier vanishing at the prospect of attempting his first advanced rune.

But before that...

He glanced at the notification blinking in the corner of his vision.

Congratulations! You have completed the first step to truly becoming a Runic Engineer.

Aodhán stared at the message for a moment, then frowned, brows furrowing as he searched for the rest of it. But there was nothing else.

He hadn’t gained a title for this.

He grimaced and shook his head.

It seemed he would have to do something greater than simply complete initiate runes to evolve his merged title into something better.

His gaze drifted back to the glowing rune, and his grimace softened into a grin, determination shining in his eyes as he picked up his pen and set it against the parchment.

Unlike initiate runes, advanced runes had no fixed number or arrangement. They were simply the combination of two or three initiate runes entwined together—not in a circuit—but in an interaction that created a single rune instead of three linked together.

Simply put, if the initiate runes were letters, the advanced runes were words—simple ones, but words, nonetheless.

They were vastly harder to create than initiate runes, requiring a high degree of resonance or overlap before they could truly entwine.

A good example was the heat, force, and direction runes entwined together to create the Flame Jet rune.

Aodhán had read extensively on the topic beforehand, and the concept itself was simple. As difficult as advanced runes were, the challenge wasn't their complexity but understanding exactly how to entwine two or three different runes into a single one.

And that was what he truly needed to learn. Fortunately, he still had Geneva’s practice note to see him through.

Chuckling to himself, he pulled the note out of his storage space and was just about to open it when Crixis stirred awake, blinking as he took in the golden glow of the room.

“You finished it?” he asked, eyes wide as he scrambled to his feet.

Aodhán chuckled. “I did. Just a few minutes ago.”

“Wow,” Crixis whispered. “Congratulations, senior brother, this is amazing.”

"Thank you." Aodhán grinned, a slight sense of giddiness washing over him at the praise. "I've looked forward to this moment for so long, but it feels even better than I imagined." He glanced back at the glowing rune. "I might still have to practice it a little more to get my speed up to scratch, but right now, I'm certain I could draw it in my sleep."

“Oh, now you’re just bragging,” Crixis groaned and Aodhan chuckled again..

"Don't I deserve to? You know you don't have to pop my head the moment it starts swelling."

"Oh no." Crixis shook his head vigorously. "That's basically my sole job." He raised his own rune book, where a hideous ISA rune had been etched. "Besides, I'm not in the best of moods right now."

"You slept off," Aodhán said, pressing his lips together to keep from laughing, but Crixis didn't make it easy.

"My brain was turning to mush," he hissed. "My eyes were looping."

Aodhán laughed, unable to help himself as he remembered all the times Yurin had made the exact same complaints. On the other hand, Crixis had only taken up the hobby a week and a half ago and had already perfected the first four runes.

That was more progress than Yurin had made in a month. Perhaps even more than Aodhán himself had managed in two.

So Crixis’s case wasn’t quite so hopeless.

"Come," he said, still chuckling as he took Crixis's notebook and pointed out his mistakes. "You're meant to do it this way..."

Time passed in that manner, and before long, the first rays of dawn broke through the room, soft white light spilling across the wooden floor.

By then, after more than two dozen failed attempts, Crixis was on his final try, his expression taut with concentration as he added the last branch to the sharp-edged matrix, twisted the circuit, and connected it to the activation rune.

The rune blazed to life immediately, and Crixis nearly collapsed with relief as golden perfection lit up his face.

"I did it!" he squealed, his grin stretching from ear to ear. "I swear I was ready to give up. If this hadn't worked, I was ready to quit."

Aodhán chuckled. "Sore loser. You can't quit at the first sign of failure." He glanced at Crixis's notebook and grinned. "Or the two-dozenth."

Crixis glared at him, shaking his head. "I'm currently fighting the urge to bury my Anglewalkers in your smug face right now."

Aodhán's grin widened as he glanced at the gift he'd given Crixis. "I'm not sure they'll fare so well against my face. I mean, I've heard I'm quite striking."

Crixis stared at him for a moment before exhaling. “I’m going to sleep.”

Aodhán watched him go, chuckling to himself as the door clicked shut behind him. Then he turned his attention back to Geneva's notes. Rubbing at his eyes, he flipped them open to the advanced rune section without conscious thought, having done so a dozen times over the past few weeks.

This time, however, as he turned past the last of Geneva's initiate runes, the pages glowed faintly, and hidden text appeared.

Congratulations, Aodhán. If you're seeing this, it means you've perfected the twenty-four initiate runes. I couldn't be prouder. And so, I've left you a gift to encourage you to continue.

It is a technique passed down to me by my father and grandfather before him to aid the activation of runes. Use it wisely and grow. Looking forward to seeing how powerful you become.

Love, Geneva.

Instinctively, Aodhán read the message in her voice, and the sound of it stirred something deep within him. It felt like ages since he'd seen her—since he'd seen any of the champions—and he missed them all.

But mostly her.

Because she had been more than just a champion. She was a friend—one so close she might as well have been an elder sister. She had been there for Aldric's first breakthrough with his Miracle Rain. She had been of great help during Aldric's trial.

And now this.

A pang of guilt gnawed at him as he realized he hadn't spoken to her once since leaving Lutia. While it was impossible to communicate with the others, he still had the holographic ritual circle she had given him.

And although it still wasn't entirely safe for him to contact anyone from Lutia—not until he reached the hundredth tier—his earlier fears and distrust no longer held much weight. He was firmly entrenched within the Storm Sect now. Nothing from Lutia could ever reach him here.

Perhaps it was finally time to check in and see how things were going...

But before that, he flipped the page, and—

Light burst forth as the technique came alive, elder runes peeling themselves from the parchment in streams of golden light that shot into his mind before he could even blink.

His body seized immediately, every muscle locking rigid as the runes forced their way into his consciousness, each one flooding him with knowledge of quintessence and energy manipulation as they embedded themselves directly into his mind.

He spasmed, his legs giving way beneath him as his consciousness struggled against the onslaught.

But this was no attack. Despite the pain.

It was an inheritance.

Rather than resisting it, his consciousness absorbed the essence flowing from the runes like dry foam soaking up water.

It lasted only a few seconds.

Then, just as abruptly as the pain had begun, it vanished, dissipating like smoke within his mind.

Aodhán gasped, his chest heaving as his thoughts slowly knitted themselves back together. When he opened his eyes, he found Varéc staring at him worriedly.

Varéc growled, slitted eyes gleaming in confusion, and Aodhán shook his head.

"I'm fine. I..." He shook his head again, his entire body still trembling from the intensity of what he had just endured. "Sorry for worrying you. I—"

Before he could continue, a line of text crawled across his vision.

Congratulations! You have inherited Lesser Rhyntharion Runic Technique of Energy Transformation.

Convert pure energy into affinitied essence to activate runes.

Aodhán stared at the message, first in confusion, then in dawning shock as he read it again.

He had always assumed Geneva's ability to shift and transform energy into any affinity she chose was a skill tied directly to her affinity or bloodline. But it seemed he’d been wrong.

It was a technique.

One she had just handed to him on a platter of gold.

Aodhán stilled. His eyes widened, and without hesitation, he focused on the technique, drawing energy from his core. Yet even as the energy rushed toward it, he could already tell it wouldn't work.

A moment later, the energy dissipated uselessly within his spirit.

He glanced at the description once more, and his gaze caught on a single word.

Pure energy.

What the hell was pure energy?

While Aodhán puzzled over the inheritance Geneva had left him, unaware of the storm brewing beyond the Storm Sect's borders, events were already unfolding elsewhere.

Back in Lutia, Geneva Rhyntharion stood atop the northern wall of Ironfel—a frontier city in Sector Two—gazing out at the approaching beast tide.

It had been a long time since a beast tide this large had attacked an inhabited city in Ragnarok. Longer still since they'd needed a Champion to stop one. But as she gazed at the towering cloud of dust rising on the horizon, Geneva understood why she had been called.

This tide was no mere gathering of beasts.

It stretched across the entire horizon as far as the eye could see, filled with creatures ranging from Tier 50 to Tier 90.

Perhaps it was a migration that had spiraled out of control. Or perhaps it was the lingering aftermath of Sunstone's attack all those months ago.

She couldn't be sure.

What she was certain of, however, was that this city's lord had been negligent in his duties.

How could a beast tide this large gather in his own backyard without him noticing? How could it have supposedly appeared overnight?

No... She frowned, her eyes narrowing. Something was off.

"Ch-Champion Rhyntharion," the city's captain stuttered, his eyes wide as he stared at the horizon. "Can you handle it?"

Geneva glanced at him, noting the hope in his eyes. She wanted to ask how he had been so negligent in his patrols that he hadn't noticed this weeks ago. How a city far outside any known beast migration route now stood at the center of a horde this large.

Instead, she nodded, studying him carefully. “We’ll take care of it, Captain. Don’t worry.”

The man sighed, relief evident on his face.

She watched him and his men for another moment before turning her gaze to Aldric Brystion, who stood to her right, his violet-black irises fixed on the tide as though he wasn't hyperaware of everything around him.

The eldest Brystion brother had grown tremendously in the seven months since Abyssos. Countless rumors surrounded what had happened to him there—how he had somehow broken the spirit lock enforced by the system and evolved all the way into the advanced class.

Aldric had neither confirmed nor denied any, simply allowing the rumors to unfurl until it became impossible to sift truth from exaggeration any longer. No doubt, some important personalities understood what had truly happened. Geneva, however, had never truly asked.

One thing, however, was certain.

Aldric Brystion had become a monster on any battlefield.

Armed with the ability to subdue others beneath his will using his Tyrant skill, he was a walking disaster once he gathered momentum. And then there was his familiar—the skeletal creature of despair wrapped in ghostly mist that he called a Skalk.

The creature floated behind him now, exuding a quiet pressure that seemed to weigh upon the world itself.

At only Tier 42, Aldric should have been far too weak for a battle like this, but Geneva knew him well. And this was a chance to know him even better.

Lowering her voice slightly, she asked, "What do you think, Aldric?"

He glanced at her, a faint smile tugging at his lips. “You’re not being paranoid. Something is indeed off.”

Her eyes brightened, and she nodded. “I thought as much. Does the captain know anything?”

"No." He frowned slightly before subtly glancing down the line of terrified soldiers atop the wall. "None of them do. They're practically saints.”

Geneva raised a skeptical eyebrow, and he chuckled.

"I know, right? Soldiers are rarely righteous. But that's exactly what makes it so suspicious." He looked back at the approaching horde. "Someone knew I'd be here."

Geneva paused before giving a slow nod.

"We'll deal with that later." She smiled. "Right now... how would you like to stretch your powers?"

Aldric smirked. “Now you’re speaking my language.”

He stepped off the fifty-meter wall without so much as bracing for impact, drawing startled gasps from the city guards. Geneva merely smiled. Before his feet could strike the ground, however, his familiar transformed, dissolving into a revolving crown of gray mist above his head.

Despair.

The pressure radiating from him quintupled instantly. The earth groaned beneath him. Then an instant later, Geneva felt—rather than saw—countless tendrils of boundless empathy burst from him. Tens. Dozens. Hundreds. They raced toward the approaching horde in an invisible wave.

They sank into their targets, and the next instant, dozens of creatures suddenly turned on their comrades, biting, clawing, and tearing them to shreds.

Blood pooled.

Monsters shrieked.

And with every death, the revolving crown spun faster. The Skalk was absorbing the essence of death and despair and feeding it directly into Aldric.

Within seconds, a few dozen thralls became a hundred. Within a minute, they became two hundred.

Awed gasps and hushed whispers echoed across the wall as the soldiers watched Aldric slowly dismantle a horde they had once deemed impossible to stop.

He swung no sword.

He held no spear.

And yet, hundreds of monsters fell every second.

Geneva watched him battle for a moment longer before turning to her right, where her newest partner stood as still as a statue, as cold as an icicle.

He was dressed in what seemed to be semi-liquid ice fashioned into clothing. Wisps of snow-white hair framed his face, and a biting chill radiated from him.

The boy met her gaze impassively, his expression so neutral that even her heightened perception barely caught the slightest shift in his features.

She smiled faintly “Will this satisfy your thirst for adventure, Daruk?”

Daruk regarded her for a moment before turning his gaze back to his brother. "No, it won't. But I could stretch a few muscles."

Without another word, he stepped from the wall, landing far more gracefully than his brother.

Geneva watched him go, an uneasy smile tugging at her lips. After winning his Sigma at the Grand Tournament, Daruk's request had sent ripples across the entire continent. Many hadn't understood it.

But she had.

And as she watched the semi-liquid ice suddenly harden into armor around him, she understood it all over again.

His feet touched the ground an instant later, and willpower burst out of him in torrents, far more than even she would have dared to pour into a single skill.

The temperature plummeted instantly. Ice raced over the earth. Then, in the blink of an eye, thousands of jagged ice spikes erupted from the ground, impaling monsters dozens of tiers above him and killing them where they stood.

On the wall, the city guards fell into stunned silence, their eyes wide at the overwhelming display of power.

Geneva chuckled softly, a proud smile spreading across her face.

Ever since Aodhán, she had somehow ended up collecting Brystions like collectibles. Not because she'd wanted to, but because they simply kept gravitating toward her. At this point, she was like an elder sister to them all.

And she couldn't have been prouder.

But even as she smiled, a question surfaced in the back of her mind.

If Daruk and Aldric had become this powerful, how much more terrifying had Aodhán become? How much more…

Ruinous.
