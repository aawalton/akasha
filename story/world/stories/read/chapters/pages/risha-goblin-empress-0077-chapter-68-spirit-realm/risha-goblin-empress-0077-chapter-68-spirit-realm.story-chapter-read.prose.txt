“I like to name them, the stars. That one is Leo, that one is Elly.” The Ancient Shaman pointed at two different stars as he continued to walk, the grass path appearing under the two of them. “It helps me remember where I’m going and where I’ve been.”

“What are they?” Risha asked, then paused as a large purple nebula moved past them. It twisted towards them, only for the Shaman to shoo it with his hand.

“Doorways. Paths. Some are even older than I am, leading to places I don’t dare tread.” He gently pulled on her arm, leading her away from the strange purple fog. “Rule one, don’t travel the void on your own. If you must, you can contract a spirit to guide you.”

“Contract?”

“Ask their name, then bind them to their word with it.” He gave her a grin, “You’ll know if it works.”

The two of them stopped at the edge of one of the stars, its light muted now that they were closer. “Rule number two, don’t call to the void. You don’t know what will answer the call. Rule three, uh… don’t… hm… three should be, oh, yeah, follow your instincts. Us goblins are closer to the spirits than any that walk our world. Trust that part of you.”

He offered his hand to Risha.

She placed her hand in his, and they entered the star.

The world twisted around them, then settled into a hill of flowers with a simple path leading up to a small cottage. Risha looked around at the flowers, caught by their beauty as they glowed with bioluminescence.

“Do you remember the name of this star?” The Shaman ran his hand through the flowers, searching for something.

Risha nodded, “You called it Elly.”

He smiled, and plucked a flower. He gently tucked it over Risha’s ear, then examined her with mock seriousness. With a nod, he turned towards the cabin and started walking, “Yep. It’s the name of an old friend. This was once her home. If you ever need to find me, you can leave a message here.”

Risha watched the goblin’s back, wondering what was going on in his mind as they walked towards the cabin.

“What happened to your friend?”

“I don’t know. I was… gone, for a very long time. When I came back, I couldn’t find her.”

“Where were you?”

He didn’t answer right away, instead quietly opening the door to the cabin. Inside was a simple space, with a bed, a fireplace, a trunk, and a desk. The fireplace was lit, crackling away. “I failed. I wandered through the stars, losing myself so that I’d never have to face that failure. It’s funny how things end up.”

Risha opened her mouth to ask more questions, but he shook his head and looked at the fire.

“Igni, wake up.”

The fire blinked, then eyes looked from the flames. They flickered in and out, watching Risha with curiosity.

“Risha, this is the fire spirit, Igni. I want you to make a contract with him.”

Risha bowed her head, “Hello Igni.”

The fire cackled, and [Brood Mother] translated the strange noise for her. “Hi! Hello! You sparkle and shine! Can I taste? No, no, Elly wouldn’t like that.”

Risha looked at the shaman, “How do I make a contract?”

He smiled, “You’ve been calling to the spirits, haven’t you? Asking for their help is fine, they’ll accept since your spirit tastes so unique. A contract, however, is different. It is a binding of partnership or servitude.”

The Shaman held out his hand and a staff of dark wood materialized, a single golden flower growing from its end. He tapped the staff against the ground, and shapes of glowing gold filled the air, covering the floor and lighting the room.

“This, is my contract. It binds my spirits to me, it sets the rules, so that they will not fail me, and so that I will not fail them.” He lifted his staff, and the shapes grew smaller, circling through the air around Risha. “I can teach you to make something similar, but for now, you can use my bindings. All you need ask, is for Igni to serve you.” The shaman paused, “He can choose to refuse, but I don’t believe he will.”

Risha watched the shapes, studying their intricacies in amazement. She wasn’t sure how long it would take to learn them and replicate them, but the thought excited her.

She took her gaze away from the magic and looked at the fire spirit, “It binds me too?”

“Yes. It prevents you from causing them harm, and promises them your energy as payment for their service. If you wish to wait to make your own-”

“No.” Risha stepped forward and kneeled in front of the spirit, “Igni, will you serve me?”

The fire roared, filling the fireplace as a snoot of flame stepped towards her, “The Family calls, and a loyal spirit answers.” The fire rushed forward, into Risha, then disappeared.

The Ancient Shaman cheered, and smiled wide with his pointy teeth. “I knew you had it in you! I’ll see you soon, Risha.”

He lowered his staff, and placed it against Risha’s chest, then she was thrown back, through the void, and into a restless night of dreams.

Risha woke with fire on her breath, it pooled through her body, ready for her to call upon it.

“Igni?” Risha asked.

Risha opened and closed her hand, enjoying the warmth.

Commotion from outside her tent made her focus. She threw on her black cloak, and stepped through the flap of her tent.

She looked over the moving army until she found Glo. He stood speaking with a group of scouts, a frown on his face.

Risha recognized him. It was the messenger they’d sent offering surrender to the enemy.

Risha bared her teeth, Igni heating within her as all she heard was her own heartbeat.

To treat one of their own like this… all to send a message? Worse than a refusal, this was evil.
