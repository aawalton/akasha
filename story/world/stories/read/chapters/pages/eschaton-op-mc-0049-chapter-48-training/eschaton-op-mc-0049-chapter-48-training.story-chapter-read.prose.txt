Morgan hums to herself as she double checks the carved ritual circle. It’s more than forty feet across, and a massive block of titanium sits in the middle. I understand absolutely nothing about any of it.

She brushes non-existent dust off her hands—Morgan is a bit of a clean freak when it comes to her personal gallery. The government might have kicked her out from the lab they offered her, but she’s the current holder of the Buchanan fortune. A relatively remote ranch outside Denver and a couple tons of metal is well within her capabilities.

“Well, it’s ready. Let’s get this show on the road!”

I hand her a Shard from a Cat-3 Fracture. She looks it over.

“Amplitude 3.4. This’ll do.”

Not all Fractal Shards are alike. Higher Category Fractures not only form more Shards, the ones they form are far more powerful. As a rule of thumb, each Category forms Shards with ten times the power of the tier below. There’s room for variance within each Category, and so it’s the same with the Shards themselves.

In case, a 3.4 Amp Shard is 25x the power of Category-2 Shard, which would start at 2 Amp. A single Shard of any strength contains a truly immense amount of energy. Morgan used one of the first Shards ever recovered to overcharge a simple spell.

It turned a tiny laser beam into a solar flare. The relatively short lived energy storm changed the weather over the entirety of Long Island for a day. That Shard was one of two we recovered, and the second one was rated to be a 2 Amp shard. Something with more than ten times the energy of that is not only mind boggling, but terrifying.

We’re lucky that Morgan is so far the only person who can use Shards in that manner. Others have experimented with them in rituals, like what Morgan is about to do. Despite being a more metered release of energy, plenty of aspiring ritualists with more excitement than sense have vaporized themselves—and entire city blocks.

There’s a reason we’ve come to such a remote location. There’s not much outside the walls of Denver many people care about anymore, and them massive compound was considered remote while highways were considered safe. Only a few others besides myself and Morgan are here, and not a single one of them is below 2-Star.

Fellow researchers and aspiring mages make up the bulk of them. Morgan’s put her family’s considerable resources towards understanding the new fundamental force that’s overwritten reality. This tests, however, is taking place in her own personal gallery. With so much energy being released, it was decided for safety’s sake that everyone not virtually indestructible should be far away as possible.

She places the shimmering rainbow crystal in a receptacle Electrum, made from metals recovered from a Fracture. Morgan calls it a Fractal Core, but everyone else just calls it a Mana-Engine. The Electrum acts as a magical superconductor, and the Mana-Engine is designed to absorb and disperse the immense energy of a Fractal Shard.

Theoretically, of course.

The one here is rated for Shards up to 3.9 Amp, which is why it’s the size of a 16-Wheeler’s engine. It’s nearly as large as I am, which is saying something.

The Mana-Engine comes to life, and the air becomes thick with the smell of ozone. Morgan’s hair puffs with the static charge.

“I guess I’ll need to rework the shielding. Again.”

“At least it didn’t melt down. This time.”

The first attempt at a Mana-Engine ended with the Opus reduced to a pool of superheated metal. Morgan barely prevented a more explosive end to the first iteration, just managing to rip enough Potentia out of it. She wasn’t able to save the Mana-Engine, but a minor loss in the grand scheme of things.

Despite the incredible efficiency of the current design of Mana-Engines, even a tiny loss is an ocean of energy. Still, the vast, vast majority of the Potentia locked away inside the crystal is forced into the ritual circle. The channels, carved into floor’s white marble and then filled with electrum, glow with energy. A hum fills the air.

The titanium block glows in tune, and its color slowly shifts from a shiny silver to a glimmering white. The Shard inside the Mana-Engine slowly vanishes, leaving not even a residue behind. The smell of ozone vanishes, though Morgan’s long, fiery red hair stays frizzy.

I step over the now discharged ritual circle, and tap on the transformed metal.

“Yep. Mithril.”

Morgan wildly grins.

“All the way through?”

“Appears so. All sensor readings point to that, anyway.”

I carefully slice the cube of metal in half using my sword. The metal is the same glimmering white on the inside as the outside.

Morgan launches herself into the air with a shout of victory.

“Yeah! Alright! We did it! The very first time Mithril’s been synthesized in industrial quantities!”

Mithril makes amazing armor, even better than the best ceramic plates or mundane alloys. Magically durable, it can allow lower-Starred Empowered a chance against monsters that would normally walk over them. Even a typical wearing Mithril full plate could take on a few lower Category monsters. It’s not heavy, either. A full set of Mithril plate weighs half as much as one made from steel.

Up until now though, it’s only ever been created in small quantities, nowhere near enough to actually be helpful on a grand scale. Rituals powered by people just don’t have the power to pull it off. The few who probably are strong enough are far too busy. As for Morgan, well, she has the Shards. And the power, of course. But that’s not the point of the experiments.

Morgan’s all over the Mithril cube, analyzing it with a variety of magical gadgets. She hums to herself in thought.

“I think we could have gotten away with a weaker Shard. Probably why there was so much waste in the air…”

I pick up one half, and bring it to the warehouse built outside for this purpose. It joins an eclectic group of Mana-Engines of varying sizes, chunks of metal, mundane or magical, and piles of scrap. The core I ripped out of the Crane Golem weeks ago is here, still softly glowing orange.

The remains of dozens of different magical monsters retrieved from Fractures are stored in rows of industrial freezers. Everything from Dread-Bears to Mega-Crabs have representatives. Even the monsters made from more artificial materials, like the Tentacables or Traffic-Mantids are preserved.

As I exit the warehouse, I’m met by Matt Teller, a professional sword fighter. He originally worked at Renaissance festivals as a dueling knight. Normally as a 2-Star Empowered, he’d be patrolling what is rapidly becoming Denver’s walls, but Morgan hired him to help teach me.

During the last Fracture we cleared, a Category-4 in Amiens, France, I was forced to fight a copy of a 4-Star swordfighter by the name of Duelliste. I couldn’t even touch him. Despite my strength and speed, Duelliste’s stolen skill was more than enough to run circles around me. Matt is here to make sure it never happens again.

“Machina, there you are. Are you ready to continue your training?”

“Let me check in with Morgan first, and then we can.”

“Of course, sir. I’ll be in the gym preparing for today’s session.”

Morgan is still going over the newly made Mithril. She sliced off a tiny amount, and has the sliver under a few large pieces of equipment.

“Morgan, do you want me to move the rest of it?”

She’s so focused on her newest achievement she doesn’t even notice me. Her excitement at her newest breakthrough in alchemy and enchantment is literally palpable. Her Aura fills the air with the joy of discovery and the wonder of magic.

I chuckle and leave for the gym. She’ll be there for hours.

“Remember what we talked about? You have many advantages, but the two most important ones are your durability and reach.”

Matt is swinging a greatsword in a whirling circle, the tip whizzing over his head before barely skirting the gym’s floor.

“Greatswords live and die on positioning and momentum. Let the enemy get too close, and you die. Get your blade stuck in something, and you die. Well, not you. But you get my point, yes?”

I match his movements, my blade of stars moving in slow arcs.

“Yes.”

“Good. You need to keep the blade moving. In normal greatsword techniques, you use the momentum of the sword, not the weight. A conventional steel greatsword isn’t all that heavy, really. Around the eight pound mark is pretty common. The heaviest I’ve heard of was fifteen pounds. I can’t say how heavy yours is though.”

“I don’t know either,” I admit.

“…Right. Well, the point still stands. The advantage of a greatsword isn’t its weight: it’s the momentum you get from such a long weapon. They’re wielded more like polearms rather than swords.”

He snaps out mid swing with a quick thrust before continuing the slow motions.

“Keep the sword moving, and quick thrusts if you need to.”

He follows up with a wide slash.

“And when you’re surrounded by multiple enemies—the purpose of a great sword is to fight multiple enemies, by the way—wide slashes.”

My sword zips through the air.

“Yeah, just like that. Then back to keeping it up and moving. You spend momentum to attack, and you want to build it up again.”

“What about parrying?”

“Do you need to? I’ve heard you’re indestructible, so why stop attacking when you don’t have to? Take the strike on the armor, and keep going. That’s how even Typicals fight in full plate. That’s the whole point of armor. If you stay on the attack, and never have to block, that means every strike is opening. Now, let’s try half-swording.”

He grips his blade with his bare hand.

“Right. Pinch the blade between your palm and your fingers. A normal person would have to worry about cutting themselves. I’ll be alright, this is just a training blade. With a real sword I’d be a lot more careful and wearing a gauntlet. You’ll have to shift your grip on the pommel as well.”

I grip my sword about half way to the tip, and just above the cross guard.

“Like this?”

“Yeah. Now you don’t have a sword, you have a spear. You treat it like that.”

He snaps out a series of rapid fire thrusts, all aimed at an imaginary opponent’s weak points.

“Not sure how useful it’ll be for fighting monsters. Half-swording was for sneaking in strikes through the gaps in heavy armor. Visors, joints, damaged plates, you get the idea. People are a whole lot squishier than magical monsters.”

I’m reminded of Duelliste running rings around me.

“I’d still like to know how to fight more conventional opponents. Really fast ones. Just in case.”

Matt nods.

“Yeah, all right. So in a one-on-one duel, you don’t really need to do the wide momentum swings. You have the reach on pretty much… everything I think. Except maybe some serious pikes? Anyway, use that. Keep the enemy just outside the tip of the blade. Far enough away to keep them from getting inside a strike, but close enough to get them with a quick thrust. Here, let’s try it. Be careful, please.”

I switch my true sword for a training one. It’s the same size, which means it’s still plenty dangerous, even being made of foam. I could probably hit hard enough with it to break bones through plate. At a certain point, speed at contact is more important than durability or weight.

I follow Matt’s instructions, keeping the tip pointed as his chest. Every time he tries to step into my reach, I keep him back with a warding thrust. He tries to push my sword out of the way, but I pull it up and around, leaving us both where we started.

“Good, good. In a one-on-one situation, reach is incredibly important. Probably the most important thing, really. There’s a reason people made polearms.”

He tries to sneak under my sword, and I quickly switch to a half-sword grip before forcing him back.

“Good! Well done. As soon as I got inside, you shortened the reach with the half-sword grip. Not necessarily what that technique is for, but there’s a reason sword fighting is an art, yes? Well done.”

He backs away, and we end our short duel.

“I think we should try out the illusionary opponents Sorceress le Fay enchanted the gym with. What do you think? Feel ready for a spar?”

One of the first achievements in enchanting Morgan came up with since Amiens was the construction of a training gym. It has a few enchantments designed to protect the people inside. The most impressive one though, is the ability to create multiple illusionary opponents. Both monsters and more humanoid enemies can be created, and they can fight at varying degrees of difficulty.

“Absolutely. Let’s give it a shot.”
