“There are a few men discussing your murder,” the note read. “I am not certain who the others involved are, but the one they are trying to hire to do the job is the Assassin King.”

James remembered the man Zhang Chāo’s note was referring to. He was one of the many fellow Rulers that James had encountered in the last hour or so since giving his speech. It had been a very brief encounter, almost bumping into each other, but the Assassin King had been memorable in part for that very casualness. He hadn’t been one of those seeking to curry favor with James. In fact, it had looked as if he was rushing somewhere else.

Roscuro, shapeshift into the form of a mirror, James ordered telepathically. Try not to be noticed by others in the room besides Zhang Chāo.

The Soul Eater Orb, in its usual form on James’s wrist as a bracelet, unclasped itself, seemingly turned semi-liquid, and then slithered, snakelike, into James’s hand. Then it began to morph slowly and subtly into a flat compact mirror. It retained its usual coloration, but with the polish and reflectiveness of an actual mirror.

As Roscuro completed the transformation, Zhang Chāo watched with undisguised fascination. It was regrettable that James had to show his Ego Weapon off now, but he would have to hope that observing it once wasn’t enough to nullify the edge the Soul Eater Orb could give him in combat.

They continued to speak quietly about something governance-related that James wouldn’t be able to recall talking about later. James raised the black mirror slightly and turned it to scan the area behind him until he saw the Assassin King, an ambiguously brown middle-aged man dressed in an all black cloak with a hood.

James quickly memorized the faces of the three men—and one woman—who had gathered around the Assassin King.

Then James’s entourage stepped in all around him and Zhang Chāo.

“How goes the networking, Your Majesty?” Rotter asked.

“It’s gone well, thank you,” James said. “But tell me about your progress.”

He had lowered his hand holding Roscuro as soon as the others’ shadows appeared in his field of view. Now James sent a silent order, and Roscuro turned back into the Soul Eater Orb’s normal bracelet form.

James saw Mina and Zora had both noticed it, but he couldn’t tell if anyone else besides, of course, Zhang Chāo, had seen it.

“I think I made some progress in my tasks,” Rotter said carefully. “Could I have a moment alone with you, sir?”

“Sure.” James spoke casually, but his heart was pounding. What else was coming?

Rotter’s assignments had been twofold. He had some deals to negotiate, and along with everyone else on the Council, he was supposed to be trying to figure out who it was that had nullified everyone’s Skills. If he could gather any other intelligence, that was just a bonus.

But the fact that he wanted to speak with James alone in the middle of the Summit certainly couldn’t be about the deals that Rotter was negotiating, which were frankly of little interest to James except in the sense that he knew they needed to get done. Trade agreements, non-aggression pacts, the construction of information networks with friendly peoples from around the world. Statecraft.

The two men walked some distance away from the others before Rotter began to speak in a quiet voice.

“I think I know who is responsible for everyone’s Skills not working for the last hour or so,” Rotter said.

“You think you know, or you know?” James asked.

“It’s an educated guess.”

“Tell me.”

“Your friend, Zhang Chāo.”

James raised an eyebrow. “You should know he can probably hear us.”

“Nothing to be done about that, sir,” Rotter said. “I assume half of this place has incredible eavesdropping power. Probably even more if the big guy who came with Zhang Chāo wasn’t nullifying their Skills.”

“What’s the basis for you saying that?”

“I asked around. A lot of people from his side of the world know that Han Jianguo has this Skill. They were eager to point the finger at him.”

“Sound like you were talking to Zhang Chāo’s enemies.”

“Even people who hate Pú Tiě Shān have repeated the same story, sir.” Rotter spoke with a firmness that was uncommon for him. “This Skill is extremely rare, but Han Jianguo is known to use it. At least it’s known to those who have spies within Taiwan.”

“So this is like a national security secret from their point of view?” James asked.

“I think so.”

“Still, there’s no excuse for Zhang Chāo hiding that from me in these circumstances.” James’s eyes bored into Rotter’s. “What exactly did you know about Zhang Chāo and his people before the Summit? You seemed before now as if you felt comfortable with them. Trusted them a bit.”

“I wanted us to work with them, Your Imperial Majesty,” Rotter said. “That’s not the same as trusting them.”

When Zhang Chāo said it, I sounded cool, James thought. From you, it just feels obsequious…

“All right,” James said evenly. “Why did you want us to work with them, then? Tell me the abbreviated version of what you wrote up for me to read about them that I probably didn’t actually read.”

“They’re strong. They are considered to be reliable and good-natured partners by their neighbors. They’re not poor. They’ve basically rebuilt industrialism on a smaller scale, mixed with magic. They badly need strong and dependable allies. That was the significance of you making that speech, I’m sure you realized. Zhang Chāo’s Taiwan still has the same backer old Taiwan had. An American superpower that can directly counter the strength of mainland China. It’s not all that different from the United States’ old relationship with Taiwan from before the System, frankly.”

I think Zhang Chāo is actually quite a bit more ambitious than that…

But James didn’t try to discuss the point further. He knew what he needed to know. He nodded and turned away, looking back toward where the group was gathered. “Thank you.”

“Wait, what are you going to do, sir?”

“The only thing to do is to confront Zhang Chāo directly.”

“Is that wise, sir? I know you mentioned he might have overheard us, but this is a delicate matter…”

“I don’t care if it seems wise or not. It’s absolutely necessary. If I don’t, this nonsense will be hanging over us for the rest of the Summit.”

He strode back over to Zhang Chāo while Rotter walked much more slowly, keeping his distance.

Mina and Zhang Chāo were in the middle of talking about something—it sounded like Mina was asking him about hair products, but that must have been a metaphor or analogy for something much more serious. In any case, James interrupted.

He took Zhang Chāo gently but firmly by the elbow.

“I think to borrow the Heavenly King for a minute, sunshine,” he said.

“You boys play nice,” Mina replied. Her voice was chipper, but James saw a hint of concern in her eyes.

Zhang Chāo walked away with James until they were far enough from the group and other Rulers to have a semi-private conversation.

“I never lied to you, James,” Zhang Chāo began. “About Han Jianguo.”

At least he’s not denying it.

“It was as good as lying,” James replied, not letting Zhang Chāo finish whatever he had prepared to say. “Especially when you were doing it in the middle of asking for a favor. And I had to get on the largest stage in the world to do that favor. I could have embarrassed myself, made enemies I didn’t need to make. Maybe that speech is why someone wants me dead. Sabotaging it doesn’t seem like the act of a friend. We don’t have to be friendly to work together, of course. This is international relations, after all. But some level of trust is a requirement for me. I took you for a man of honor, and now I just don’t know.”

Zhang Chāo blanched at the last sentence.

“Please let me explain,” he said quietly.

James nodded.

“I had heard things about you before the Summit. Mostly good things. And you’ve lived up to your reputation. You are known internationally as a powerful leader who has gathered a diverse coalition of various Races around you by some unknown means—which according to you was essentially just carefully applied violence.” He chuckled. “But you are not known for being one of those Rulers who can bewitch an entire room with your voice or command the allegiances of men with rhetoric alone. I believe all Rulers have Skills to enable them to speak in public effectively. Given that yours were not known to be exceptionally honed, I took the risk of ordering Han Jianguo to deploy his Skill throughout the time we were in the Summit. It would put you or anyone who spoke on our behalf on an even playing field with Pú Tiě Shān, who is known to be a dominant speaker. If you will recall, I said directly at the time that the absence of Skills leveled the playing field and that it was a good thing.”

“You could have just used it to disable his speaking Skills, right? Or rather, you could have asked Han Jianguo to deactivate his power when I got up to speak?”

“Only if we wanted to tip our hand. A room full of Rulers would undoubtedly notice the special treatment. Even if they didn’t feel that you were using speaking Skills while our opposition was not, some of them would notice that their own Skills were available while you were speaking but not while Pú Tiě Shān was. They would piece together the truth. The easiest thing was for Han Jianguo to keep his power active the entire time. I apologize for doing that without giving you any hint. Verbal discussion would have been picked up by someone with high Perception, but of course, I could have slipped you a note. Next time something like that happens, I will trust you with the full facts.”

“All right,” James said.

“Really?” Zhang Chāo said.

“I accept your apology, with the understanding that in the future, we will be open and honest between ourselves about matters that affect both of us. Men of greatness have nothing to hide from each other.”

James would not normally have been so forgiving, but nothing Zhang Chāo said had set off the Ring of Lies. The ring was a gift from a god and had been functional throughout the last hour despite Han Jianguo’s Skill. James had been lied to multiple times as he went around the room talking to people, from little white lies told in order to flatter up to lies that made the ring feel rather uncomfortable.

So Zhang Chāo had apparently come clean, and given his motives, this was not such a significant breach of trust that James intended to hold a grudge over it.

Although, actually, Zhang Chāo technically never lied in the first place, James realized. The Ring of Lies would have told me. He must have said things like ‘someone is doing this,’ ‘a Ruler ordered it done,’ and ‘the Skill is active right now,’ playing games with pronouns and passive voice to disguise the fact that he was talking about himself. Not technically lies. Does he have some way of knowing that I can detect lies, or is he just that careful…? Either way, he’s a dangerous person to make an enemy. Better to keep as close an eye on him as I can. Maybe I should ship Rotter to Taiwan to have them watch each other. He managed to refrain from smiling at the thought, but only just barely.

“Of course,” Zhang Chāo said, letting out a sigh. The Ring of Lies again did nothing.

You don’t have some kind of divine-tier item that lets you fool my ring, right?

“Then we’re good,” James said aloud.

“Still allies?”

I guess we did say we were forming something of an alliance earlier. Keep your friends close and your enemies closer… and I’ll figure out which one he is later.

“And the threat against my life is real?” James asked.

For the first time, Zhang Chāo looked slightly offended. “I wouldn’t lie about something so grave.”

“Fair enough. I do have one other question, and I hope you won’t take offense at my being a bit blunt. What conflict are you trying to get me involved in? I gave a speech opposing Pú Tiě Shān, and now someone is trying to have me killed. It feels like there’s something deeper between you and him that’s suddenly endangering my life.”

“I’m not trying to force you into any fight,” Zhang Chāo said. “But it’s no secret that I want to take over mainland China, to liberate it from the likes of Pú Tiě Shān. Part of why I was eager to get you to speak was that your word, as the probable strongest Ruler from North America, will carry weight with those who might be on the fence about allying with me. You need not do anything. The shadow you cast is enough. As for Pú Tiě Shān, I wouldn’t put anything past him, but it would surprise me if he was willing to do something so overt as plot an assassination while you were in the same room with him.”

James stared at Zhang Chāo for a long moment. “That’s all reasonable. But I feel that you’re still not telling me something. You’re about as powerful as I am, I think. Aren’t you?”

He couldn’t sense the quality of Zhang Chāo’s aura, with both of their Skills deactivated, but James had a hunch that it would not be much different from his own.

Zhang Chāo simply nodded.

“Then why do you need to borrow my reputation?”

“Because despite what strength I may possess, I have never demonstrated it on a large scale, the way you have. I have only consolidated and defended my island. I don’t regret that conservative approach, it has kept my people relatively safe, but it does not help one develop a reputation as a great warrior.”

James nodded. That explanation made enough sense, and it hadn’t set off the Ring of Lies.

“Allies, then?” Zhang Chāo asked once more.

The air was tense for a moment.

Then James extended his hand. “Allies. Still.”

They shook.

“We should go and figure out who it is who actually wants to kill you,” Zhang Chāo said.
