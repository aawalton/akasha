“You’re destined for greatness, Max… If you survive long enough! Your father would see you dead soon… if he remembers you exist.”

Max shook his head, still remembering the mocking laughter his mother had given him on her death bed. He had been nothing but seven then, and now six years later, he was thirteen and a half.

He stood at the gate of the Little Hearts Mended Orphanage and stared out into the busy streets of the border city. It was busy, busier than usual, with the arrival of so many townsfolk and villagers from the surrounding farm lands, hills, river bends, and mountains, for the annual ceremony to wake their Patron Inheritances.

Everyone wore thick clothing and hoods over their heads. It was snowing lightly.

Kids his age huddled closer to adults. Parents fussed over their children and made sure they were nice and warm. Elders led entire village populaces.

Max had neither a cloak to cover his head or thick enough clothes to efficiently fight off the cold, much less everything else they had. He swallowed the sudden pang of envy that threatened to make him puke before he did anything stupid.

It was pointless anyways, wanting what others had never changed anything nor did it help him.

He took another deep breath–

“Make sure you come home early, Maximilian,” a familiar voice shouted from the inside of the orphanage.

Caretaker Isabella was a kind soul that did nothing but smile and care for them.

He turned to look back through the open doorway and caught her attention.

Max cursed under his breath.

Instantly, her golden eyes, the same color of her hair, narrowed. “I know that look, Max.”

“What look?” he immediately fought off the irrational urge to rub the scar running through his eyebrow. A bad habit he was working on eliminating. “I don’t know what you mean.”

Isabella walked up to him briskly and leaned forward, almost bowing to bring her face to his. She studied him for a long moment.

“You got that look again. Same one as last month when you opened the stable’s gate and caused Mr. Hampton a week long headache. He was not happy about all of the paperwork you made him go through just to get his horses back.”

“He deserved it–”

“Doesn’t matter. You got that look and you intend trouble. Don’t–”

Max frowned.

Trouble tended to find him wherever he went. Mr. Hampton had been stealing coin from the stablehands, threatening them with loss of their livelihoods if they didn’t hand over half the coin they got as tips and gifts from the customers they helped.

Trouble would find him today out of necessity to escape his fate. He refused otherwise.

“–they’ll catch up to you eventually. All it takes is once and you’ll find yourself in the jail house. You hear me? Max? Max?”

“Yes, Ma’am. I understand.”

“It doesn’t sound like you do…” Isabella sighed and kneeled fully on the ground.”Listen… I know you’ve had a tough life so far a-and I’m not your mother–”

Max’s eyes narrowed and fists balled up. That was always a touchy subject for him, enough that he had gotten into multiple fights with the older children. His mother had not been perfect. No. She had been flawed and a horrible mother, in the grand scheme of things, but she was his.

She was–

“–but I worry for you. You have so much pent up anger. One day it will explode out of you and you will end up doing something you regret–”

Max blinked as the reason for his impossible mission, and the look he apparently had, appeared in his vision.

Please use a Skill Shard to unlock your Patron Inheritance.

That was the crux of the matter.

Somehow, someway, he had gotten this notification a few days ago.

He wondered why his current skills couldn’t unlock it but a Shard could. Why did his Patron Inheritance need one? Why did he unlock it now and not before or after? Was there a specific reason or was it random? Is it possible to rechoose and hope for a better three options next time forward? Why did he get one that was nothing but random symbols and letters that made no sense to him–

That had Max start on a frenzied hunger to learn about Patron Inheritances and Skill Shards.

He knew the basics, of course, everyone unlocked them at the age of eight to get the best possible outcomes.

Those that were poor unlocked them later on in life and with much worse options.

Max had woken up to this message five years late, and with only three options to boot. Each one worse than the one before it. Having a timer the second he woke up didn’t help him make a better decision either.

Bastard system only gave me thirty-two seconds!

He still remembered the ridiculous options as clear as day.

Tier E- Farming Patron

Tier F+ Hoe Smithing Patron

Tier ? Patron T̶̠͆h̵͙̓r̵̬͂ö̶̜́ň̷͎e̶̫̍V̷̗͌o̵̜͠i̵̥͌d̴̖͘

Each one worse than the previous one.

Tier E- Farming Patron

A very low tier Farming Patron Inheritance that provides a marginal boost to normal farming practices.

+ 0.7% boost to crops

+ 1% to crop life

Tier F+ Hoe Smithing Patron

Unlock a natural affinity to making farming tools, primarily hoes.

3% boost to consistently create chipped and weak hoes and farming tools

55% negative to creating anything of higher quality

Neither one was an option he could use.

Worse yet, the third one was equally as hard to select simply because he hadn’t known what the hell it was. It had given him nothing but question marks.

Tier ? Patron T̶̠͆h̵͙̓r̵̬͂ö̶̜́ň̷͎e̶̫̍V̷̗͌o̵̜͠i̵̥͌d̴̖͘

The Skill (The Ledger) is unlocked…

???

???

Max had no clue what the ledger was nor did anyone he asked–

Isabella flicked his forehead.

“Ow!”

“You aren’t listening! I’m worried for you! We are worried for you, all of us,” Isabella stopped and let out a sigh. “Just… promise me to be careful. Okay?”

Max rubbed his forehead with the right and hid his left hand behind his back, fingers crossed. “I promise.”

“Good.”

She got up and left to separate another pair of boys that had gotten into a scuffle. The majority of the other adults had taken all the eight year-olds to Merchant Polendius’s stall. He had promised to sponsor this year's batch of Patron Inheritance unlocking, a group of five total, for his own benefit. The man had been caught price gouging and needed a foolproof way to get the public opinion on himself to change.

What easier way than to fake mercy and magnanimity than to help orphans unlock something they would have never had a chance to do at their ages?

None, that’s what.

Max shook his head and walked away before Isabella could catch him again.

He recalled all the information he had gathered recently.

The main thing he had learned from his extensive research of eavesdropping on village elders lecturing their groups, parents giving their children lessons, and even sneaking into the library from a window entrance to read up on the matter.

Another oddity he had noticed.

Max knew how to read and write because his mother had spent significant coin, enough to keep them destitute and filthy poor, to get scribes to teach him.

Enough that he had unlocked both skills at the age of six and had been increasing them both ever since.

Reading (Common Tongue) - Level 4

Writing (Common Tongue) - Level 3

No other orphan had either skill.

He had two other skills that did not quite fit, either stuck at level 1 or no level at all. One he had since he was a child and the other he had gotten from selecting his Patron Inheritance.

The Ledger (Patron Inheritance Skill) - Level 0

System Inventory (Unlocked) - Level 1

His System Inventory had been unsealed the second he selected his inheritance as though it had been waiting for that moment.

System Inventory (Unlocked) - Level 1

Six cubic inches of a Spacial Pocket impossible to access except by the owner

Max slowed his walking pace and looked around one more time. His eyes found the legionaries, wearing their grey lamellar armor and regalia to denote they served the master of this city, Marquis Eisen, and standing guard in pairs around the city. Their eyes always found him whenever he paused.

He needed to be extra careful when entering the alleyways so he made sure he didn’t draw any attention.

Today he was going to do the unthinkable.

Max was going to steal a Skill Shard.

That was the only way someone like him would ever get his hands on one.

He had made the trip to the merchant quarters the same day he had chosen the strange Patron Inheritance and gotten that notification. The prices he had seen were the things he could hardly dream of. The cheapest one had been some jumping ability, Max hadn’t had the mental strength to read the description after seeing the price, which was priced at roughly eighty-nine gold and thirty-three silver.

Max was lucky to carry ten copper at any given moment.

The prices of the actual useful ones were far, far more than a random jumping ability.

It took some time until he finally found a pair of legionaries that were more focused on apprehending someone for vandalism of Noble Property than him sneaking into the alleyways that were connected to where he had hidden his dirty cloak, another acquisition he was currently borrowing, and was directly connected to the merchant stall that housed the Skill Shards for sale.

He entered without looking back, turning in a series of alleyways, pausing to hide from thugs and gang members, loose dogs, curious housewives that were looking for anything to distract them, or the old men that sat in groups that would talk his ears off if he gave them a chance.

Max smiled, happy with the job he had done slinking by. He had reached his cloak, put it on over his mop of black hair, and was currently resting his back on the wall nearest to his target.

I should’ve gotten a Stealth skill like the books call it. Nobody noticed me the entire time.

“–and Class Shards as well! Best prices in the entire province! No! The entire Empire! None dare say they can match my wares!”

Max perked up at the merchant hawking his merchandise.

Unlocking a class was a distant thing he hadn’t even considered if he was being honest. He would have to reach level ten if the books were right, which he assumed they were. He hadn’t snuck into the library just to be fed false information. He hoped not at least. Getting a Class Shard of any kind would accelerate his growth and give him years, if not an entire decade, of a head start compared to even the average person with a family.

He couldn’t help but lick his lips.

Max was already here and committed to the crime. Taking two items instead of one made no difference to him.

First, grab a Skill Shard with my System Inventory and hope it's something good and then loop around. If no one noticed then grab the Class Shard.

He had been testing this all night as well. Unfortunately, he couldn’t just look at something and make it vanish into his System Inventory. He had to be in possession of it, meaning he needed to hold it cleanly without it slipping or being contested by others. He rubbed his jaw where he had been punched by one of the older kids. He had tried to test it out on a roll of rock solid bread. The older boy grabbed the bread before Max could make it vanish and the ensuing brawl for it had left him with an aching jaw and most definitely no bread.

Alright time to go–

“What are we stealing this time?”

Max yelped and went airborne. He felt his heart nearly burst into a thousand pieces in fright.

A young voice laughed behind him.

Max turned his head and found a short, gap toothed smile and a crown of red hair staring at him with wide, innocent eyes. “Illias? The hell are you doing here?”

“Saw you sneaking around trying to get into the alleys,” the boy’s smile grew wider. “You know you don’t have to do that, right? The Marquis’s legionaries don’t care what we do as long as we don’t break their rules. Old Goat said they call it the law. But guessing you did go all sneaky… you’re planning to steal something big! I just know it! Let me help! I can be the best distraction ever! I’ll cry and beg and jump and dance and scream and spit and fight and–”

“I get it. I get it. You’re good at distractions–”

“Mhm!”

“–but no. You are too young to do anything like that. Old Goat will kick my hide if he learns that you helped me–”

“No one will ever know, I won’t say a word to anyone! Cross my heart a dozen times, twirl, jump on one leg, and break the other leg if I ever tell a soul! You don’t even have to give me anything!” Illias dropped to his knees and held his hands together before him. Eyes closed in perfect begging form. “Please! Just let me help! Please, oh, please let me help! Please. Please. Please. Please.”

Fuck… Illias will tell the world twice over if I don’t… How the hell did he even follow me through the alleyways? Did anyone else sneak behind me?

“You don’t have to worry. No one else followed. I checked!”

Max cursed under his breath, he really needed to work on his expressions. “Okay. But you do what I tell you and then you run. If you stick for a second longer I won’t help you escape the legionaries or the merchant’s guards. You hear me, Illias?”

Illias did a fist pump and face brightened multiple times as his smile grew wider.

This is going to be such a headache…

Max could have done it the day after, but he was committed now. There was no going back. More importantly, he had no idea if there would be any Class Shards tomorrow. Or maybe the merchant would choose to leave in the morning and was happily dumping his merchandise with lower prices.

That thought caused him to shiver.

Eighty gold pieces… as a nearly throw away price just to get rid of the Skill Shards…

Max didn’t doubt the price for the Class Shards were much higher too.

“Alright, listen up. This is the plan…”
