I ACCIDENTALLY BECAME A GUILD THERAPIST

Chapter 22 – The Therapist Salary. Wait... What Salary?.

ARC III: THE WORLD REMEMBERS

SYNOPSIS: Livia discovers she has never been paid. The guild discovers this simultaneously. Alaric had a notepad ready. He did not make it today.

The guild’s quarterly financial report was on the main hall holographic panel when Livia walked past it.

She stopped.

Walked back.

Read the member compensation table.

Twenty-two rows. Names, roles, monthly disbursements.

Her name was not in it.

I have been here nine months.

My name is not in this table.

I am classified as: Event-Based Buff NPC.

I have done three hundred and twelve sessions.

[System Note: Entry search: Livia Marcelline Quinn. Result: No entry found. Classification: Event-Based Buff NPC. The system notes: the system is embarrassed on behalf of the guild. The system notes: the guild should also be embarrassed. The system notes: go find Miles.]

She found Miles in the strategy room and put the report on his table.

He looked at it. Looked at her. Looked at it again.

“Nine months,” she said. “Three hundred and twelve sessions. Basement shrine. Wedding officiation. Server kidnapping. Two dungeon collapses. One man who hacked reality for my ASMR.”

Miles closed his eyes for exactly two seconds.

“Full guild meeting. Today.”

“That’s it? That’s your response?”

“I can also say I’m sorry.”

“Say both.”

“Full guild meeting, today, and I’m sorry.”

He said both.

Filed.

Moving on before I process this properly.

Fifty-two members, main hall, chairs in rows.

Bron arrived with his shield. Nyx arrived last and stood near the exit. Alaric arrived with a flask and a notepad.

The notepad was not new.

The pages had tabs.

He did not make this today.

He brought it.

Miles stood at the front. “Administrative matter. Livia has been our Mental Supporter for nine months. She has never been paid. My error. We’re correcting it.”

Silence.

Then Bron, slowly: “We haven’t been paying Buffer.”

“Correct.”

“For nine months.”

“Yes.”

Bron looked at Livia with the expression of a man doing math he did not order.

“I cried in front of her at least forty times,” he said.

“Forty-three,” Livia said. “I have documentation.”

Bron sat down. The chair made a sound.

From the third row, Phina raised her hand. “I brought charts to seventeen sessions. About a slime. That I was in love with. And she—”

“Officiated the wedding,” Livia said.

Phina covered her face. Glimmer pulsed guilty pink.

Alaric opened the notepad.

“I have a proposal,” he said.

“We are not at proposals yet,” Miles said.

“I’m ahead of schedule,” Alaric said.

“The meeting started four minutes ago.”

“I prepared in advance.”

Miles looked at the notepad. It had tabs. Colour-coded tabs.

“Go ahead,” Miles said, in the tone of a man watching a door he cannot close.

Alaric stood up. He flipped to Tab One.

“I Called this: "Emotional Capitalization Framework". Compensation unit: the tear,” he announced. “Not per session. Per tear. Individual, singular, countable unit of emotional output. One bronze each.”

Livia stared at him.

“You want to pay me per tear.”

“Professional tears only,” Alaric said. “Ambient crying doesn’t count. If someone cries walking past you in the corridor, that’s free.”

“How,” Livia said, “would you count individual tears.”

“Volume estimation,” Alaric said. “I have cried in front of you many times. I have a strong sense of my own output. I could establish a personal baseline and extrapolate.”

Total silence.

He said: volume estimation.

He said: personal baseline.

He said: extrapolate.

These are words he has been saving for a room.

“I support per-tear,” Bron said. “I cry a lot. I want my contribution accurately reflected.”

“Thank you, Bron,” Alaric said, pointing at him.

“I’ve also cried at the plushie,” Bron added. “Does that count?”

“The plushie,” Miles said, “is not Livia.”

“It has her face.”

“Bron.”

“It has her face, Miles.”

“We are not monetizing the plushie.”

[System Note: Current time: 14:09. Expected meeting duration: 20 minutes. The system notes: it is already minute seven. The system notes: Tab One has not finished yet. The system notes: there are four more tabs.]

Phina raised her hand. “I object to per-tear. My sessions had minimal crying and maximum emotional complexity. Crying is not the unit of value. I find it reductive.”

“Noted,” Alaric said, flipping to Tab Two. “Which is why I have a modifier system.”

Tab Two had sub-tabs.

“Standard acknowledgment: base rate,” Alaric read. “Emotional breakthrough: double. High-Lore Sobbing: triple.”

“Define High-Lore Sobbing,” Miles said.

“Crying with narrative context,” Alaric said. “When the grief has backstory. When the tears are load-bearing.”

“When the tears are—” Miles stopped. “When the tears are what.”

“Load-bearing.”

A pause in which everyone in the room individually processed the phrase load-bearing tears.

“My immortality crisis,” Bron said slowly. “The tears were load-bearing.”

“Absolutely,” Alaric said.

“My slime confession,” Phina said. “Three-act structure. Eighteen months of emotional buildup. If anything qualifies for triple—”

“I’m adding a fourth tier,” Alaric said.

“Do not add a fourth tier,” Miles said.

“I’m calling it Ultra-Lore.”

“Alaric—”

“For sessions of exceptional narrative density. Phina qualifies retroactively.”

“Nothing is retroactive—”

“The work was already done,” Phina said. “The narrative density already existed. Not acknowledging it now would be revisionist.”

Miles looked at Livia.

Livia was writing in her notepad.

“Are you taking notes on this?” Miles said.

“I’m documenting it for my own mental health,” Livia said.

It got worse at minute forty-one.

Alaric had opened Tab Three, which covered what he called "the Serialized Content Premium" for multi-session ongoing arcs, and Bron had immediately requested the premium be applied to his twenty-session existential crisis, and Phina had seconded this and proposed her own slime romance be classified as a limited series with a feature-length finale, and someone from the back row had asked whether the echo dungeon counted as a crossover event with bonus compensation, and the holographic display had run out of room for numbers and opened a second panel automatically, and then a third panel, and at that point the system intervened.

[SYSTEM ALERT: Financial Calculation Overflow. Three display panels active. Tier count: 7. Sub-tier count: 12. Retroactive multiplier chains: 4. Current back pay estimate: [ERROR: DISPLAY BUFFER EXCEEDED]. The system notes: the system has processed guild financial data for six years. The system notes: this has never happened. The system notes: the system requires a moment.]

The three holographic panels flickered.

Numbers cascaded across all of them simultaneously.

One panel showed only the word RECALCULATING in large red letters.

Another showed what appeared to be a pie chart that was mostly one colour.

The third showed Alaric’s face reflected back at him as he leaned too close to the display surface.

Nobody spoke for four seconds.

Then Miles said, very quietly: “We are not monetizing emotional breakdowns.”

A pause.

“At least,” he said, “not retroactively.”

He said at least.

He said not retroactively.

Miles Vexley just implied that going forward, monetizing emotional breakdowns is potentially on the table.

Nobody is going to point this out.

Alaric is writing it down.

[System Note: The system notes: Miles said “at least not retroactively.” The system notes: the system is choosing to interpret this as a moment of tactical flexibility rather than a breakdown in his position. The system notes: the system is being generous. The system notes: the system would like it on record that it noticed.]

The meeting was saved, as meetings in this guild sometimes were, by the person near the exit saying one thing.

Nyx raised his hand. The room went quiet.

“What about sessions where nobody cries,” he said. “But something changes.”

Alaric’s pen stopped.

Everyone looked at him. He looked at his notepad. Back at Nyx.

“That’s a gap,” Alaric said.

Nobody said anything. Because Alaric admitting a gap in his framework was the kind of event that required a moment to sit with.

Then Osric, from the back—a mid-tier member who had attended two sessions and said nothing at either—raised his hand.

“When she doesn’t say much,” Osric said, “but you still leave and something is— different. Like something shifted while you weren’t watching it. Is that a tier.”

Silence.

Osric.

Who I thought wasn’t paying attention.

Described it exactly.

Alaric wrote for ten seconds without looking up.

“It’s called the Quiet Shift Rate,” he said. “No formula. Assessed by the therapist. Paid at cost.”

Miles looked at the notepad.

Then at Alaric.

“That one stays,” Miles said.

“I know,” Alaric said.

The final calculation required all three panels and one emergency fourth that the system generated without being asked.

The number appeared.

Livia looked at it for a long time.

In my previous life I once found twelve dollars in an old jacket and cried.

That number is not twelve dollars.

That number is aggressively not twelve dollars.

“The lore multipliers inflated this,” she said.

“Appropriately,” Phina said.

“The Ongoing Arc Premium is included,” Bron said.

“The arc is ongoing,” Miles said. He said it without inflection. The flat delivery of a man who had chosen his battles and this was not one of them.

He slid the transfer confirmation across the table. Guild seal. His signature.

“Back pay. Going forward: the framework, monthly, Qualitative Impact assessed by me.”

“I want to be consulted on the QIA process,” Alaric said.

“You will be informed of the outcome.”

Alaric wrote something in the notepad. Livia was almost certain it said QIA REFORM: PHASE ONE but she had made a decision not to look directly at the notepad for the rest of the day.

[Achievement Unlocked: First Paycheck. +0 EXP.]

[The system notes: the Emotional Capitalism framework is currently fourteen pages. The system notes: Alaric added three pages after the meeting ended. The system notes: page eleven is exclusively about the plushie situation, which Miles has ruled non-compensable, which Alaric has flagged as “pending appeal.” The system notes: the Quiet Shift Rate has no formula. It just says: assessed by the therapist. Paid at cost. The system notes: it is the best line in the document. It is the best line Alaric has ever written. The system notes: Alaric does not know this yet.]

The meeting disbanded.

Nyx walked past Livia on his way to the exit. He did not stop. But mid-stride:

“The Quiet Shift Rate.” One beat. “That one was for you too. Not just for billing.”

Gone before she could respond.

He named something that was hard to name.

And told me it was mine.

Filed in the section that has no label yet.

That evening Miles found her in the main hall, staring at the transfer confirmation like it was a document in a language she had not learned yet.

“My first paycheck,” he said, sitting across from her, “went entirely on weapon upgrades. Because equipment made sense to me. Everything else felt like guessing.”

“What would you spend it on now?”

A pause. Longer than most.

“Different things,” he said.

He has said this twice now.

Different things.

Never says what the things are.

Filing it in the section marked: pending.

“Goldmere’s good,” he said. “If you want somewhere to go. Walk around until something catches.”

He left. She sat with the number. Then wrote in the margin of the report:

What do I want? Not what do I need. What do I want.

I have never been in a position to answer this before.

Tomorrow: Goldmere.

[Mental Supporter Lv. 7: 6,900 / 7,000 EXP.]

Alaric has submitted an appeal of the written declination. The system notes: go to Goldmere. The system notes: bring someone. The system notes: the system has a candidate in mind. The system is not saying who.]

[END OF CHAPTER 22]
