More shouts from the upper levels, sound coming through the broken windows around the side of the building. Keyla and Yaret sheltered behind pillars on the porch. The sound of laser fire faded. Either the people shooting at them before had moved on, or they were waiting for someone to break cover.

Elion motioned for Yaret.

He slipped into the sheltered space beside him, taking Selna back into his arms. Blood dripped from his chin, and he leaned against the archway, steadying himself.

Elion summoned his knife, his legs weak from and the stress of using his ability for too long. He plunged the knife into the seam of the double doors, cutting down where he recalled the lock and bar to be.

For several minutes he sawed at the door seam. He kept working his blade up and down, pushing it deeper and deeper into the gap as he cut away at the bar. Occasionally he stopped, listening for any indication that they’d been detected. Finally, the door loosened, and with one last push, it swung open.

The entry hall sat exactly as Elion had last seen it. Nobody was inside. No alarm was raised. Quietly, Elion ran across the room. Yaret followed, and Keyla swept the room with her laserarm. They moved into the shadowy hall beyond. This hall would lead them to the courtyard where Prator had moved the Altar. Elion checked behind him, peering around Keyla, surprised to see that Tuft had managed to keep up with them. The man didn’t even seem winded.

“Just go,” Tuft said, waving his hand. “Don’t worry about me, I’ll keep up.”

They crept along the hall. Shouts rang out from the building’s peripheries. Gunfire cracked, and explosions rumbled in the distance. All eyes turned outward, and nobody remained to notice the group creeping through the heart of the house.

They entered the courtyard, the statue of Aurelia standing in its the center. A tarp stretched overhead to obscure its presence from any skyskimmers passing by. Elion immediately felt the sensation of coming home, safety, and security that he now associated with Aurelian altars. He breathed a sigh of relief.

He stopped abruptly, and Keyla stumbled into him.

They were not alone.

Prator knelt at the altar, an array of Ascendancy Stones spread out on the stone before him. He glowed as golden armor coalesced around him. The air cracked with the sharp noise of a whip. A multicolored flash consumed the stones on the altar. Prator gasped, inhaling strongly as his armor finished forming around him.

A gigantic sword, made of twisted golden light, solidified in his hands. It was comically large, intricately ornamented, and wickedly thin. Prator stood, swinging the sword as though it weighed no more than a feather. He turned and saw Elion standing there, watching him. Prator’s eyes shone like polished gold.

He smiled broadly.

"Welcome back," he said.

“Hurry,” Tuft said, pulling Yaret forward to the Altar.

Keyla leveled her rifle, but Elion stopped her.

“Is this the girl?” Prator asked, gazing at Keyla. “The one Venya was supposed to deal with? I assume you got to her before Venya did. Since she’s still alive.”

“Prator,” Elion said, “We need to use the Altar. This woman is sick and we’re trying to heal her.”

“I know who that is,” Prator said, making no move to stop Tuft and Yaret. “She’s the one who tried to kill me. Tried to shoot me in the head from across the street. What is your interest in her?”

Yaret laid Keyla’s mother down at the Altar, and Tuft began working, mumbling words under his breath as delicate blades of ethereal emerald grass sprouted up around her.

Elion crossed the courtyard, approaching Prator.

*He seems genuinely interested in what we’re doing here,* Elion thought. *Maybe the truth will convince him to leave us alone.*

“She is Keyla’s mother,” Elion said. "Her mind was controlled by the power of a false Sentinel. We’re curing her.”

“You’re full of surprises,” Prator said, flexing his forearms and giving his sword a few test swings through the air. “Where did you find the Cultivator?”

“He found us,” Elion said with a shrug. He watched Prator’s movements cautiously. If the man wanted to fight him, he wouldn’t stand a chance. “I’m ready to go with you, to find Zev.”

Prator raised his eyebrows. “You’re accepting my offer? That’s unfortunate. Too little, too late.”

He produced Elion’s starholder pendant—the Bondhold—from his shirt.

“You’re coming with me. I was foolish to think that Zev offered any kind of future for us. I was caught up in excitement, in the frenzy of the moment. I’m turning you over to Venya. She’s called in reinforcements. But first, you and I are going to go kill all those invaders.”

Elion glanced at Yaret and Tuft. They both knelt beside the woman. Yaret clasped one of Selna’s hands in his own, pressing it to his face. Tuft rested his hands on his legs. He tilted his head back and closed his eyes, breathing deeply as healing Heranan power swirled around Selna’s head and chest. Actual grass sprouted from the ground around him, lush and green.

Keyla growled threateningly, advancing with her rifle.

“Stay back,” Elion muttered. “You can’t hurt him like this.”

Prator followed Elion’s gaze, looking toward Selna. “You’re right. I suppose we have to deal with them first. I was curious to know who of my guard betrayed me, but I’d rather not let this woman go free. Stand aside Elion.”

The man took two big steps toward Elion, closing the distance between them.

Elion felt the pressure of the man’s order. The world around him flashed, and he thought he saw a red chain, extending from his chest to the pendant.

To disobey Prator would be to abandon Liora. Elion knew the thought was not his own, but the feeling gripped him, overwhelming his reason.

To obey Prator would be to abandon Keyla.

Elion did not move, caught on the horns of the dilemma.

“Elion, I order you to stand aside,” Prator said, hefting his sword.

Elion saw another chain. A binding, extending into another dimension, attaching him to his sword.

*It’s not a sword. It’s a stupid butter knife.*

But it was a sword. Not in size or shape, but in purpose, and in heart. The weapon believed itself to be a sword.

Elion shook, the inner battle overcoming him. He had been in his Ascended state for too long. Sickness settled into his gut.

Prator looked curiously at the Bondhold in his hand. “Are you trying to disobey? I don’t recommend it. It will break your mind.”

Flicking his eyes to Keyla, Prator smiled. “Oh,” he said. “I see. She has already made that sacrifice for you. She doesn’t remember her own mother.” He addressed Keyla directly. “Dear girl, what have you given up for this boy? He asks you to throw away your own family, but he cannot bear to do it himself.”

“You lie,” Elion hissed.

“Ah, Elion,” Prator preened. “For you, truth is whatever I say it is. Now go kill that woman.”

A voice whispered in the back of Elion’s mind. “*See the truth.*”

Elion saw the red chain connecting him to the Bondhold again. He saw his connection to his sword. Thousands of other connections poured out of him, stretching through time and space, binding him to people, places, and things.

Something was different about his connection to the Bondhold. The chain was unnatural, welded in over the top of the original.

Elion saw a weak link. He pulled on the chain connected to his sword. The blade came through space and time, reaching him in a flash. He snatched the sword out of extra-dimensional space, and attacked the false chain binding him to the Bondhold.

“What are you doing?” Prator asked, staring bemused.

*He can’t see it. He doesn’t see our connection.*

He sliced through the false chain, severing it completely.

Desmian power exploded around him, bathing the courtyard in blood red light. He dropped to his knees, bindings pulsing and flailing through the air. Elion reached out and siezed his natural connection to the starholder pendant and pulled.

The pendant flew from Prator’s grasp and landed in Elion’s outstretched hand.

"How?" Prator asked, confusion evident in his voice.

Elion collapsed to the ground, nearly overwhelmed from the exertion of his Aurelian powers. He muttered the command to disarm. His armor and sword faded away in a puff of golden mist. He clutched the pendant tightly to his chest for a brief moment.

He struggled back to his feet, and, in the same movment, unslung his laser rifle from his shoulder. Taking careful aim, he fired.

The laser briefly connected Elion and Prator, the turquoise beam striking Prator directly between the eyes. Golden light flared and flashed. A wave of heat washed over Elion, and Prator staggered backward. But he seemed unharmed.

Keyla stepped up, standing over Elion’s kneeling form, her laser rifle aimed at Prator. She fired, and Prator staggered backward again. This time, Prator brought his sword around in one hand and lifted his other. A golden shield formed on his arm, tall and rectangular, protecting his body from the ground to his eye level.

Keyla fired again, but Prator deflected the shot with his shield. He laughed. "A shame that you choose to fight me, Elion," he said. "We Aurelians need to stick together."

Elion scoffed, picking himself up off the ground. "You're the one that chose to fight me, Prator. You're the one that's choosing to side with Dorian. Give it up."

Elion heard a rustle of noise behind him. Selna Arden stepped up between him and Keyla.

"Give me that rifle," she said, taking the gun from Elion's hands and aiming it at Prator. "You can't be trusted, Prator"

"You really think you can hurt me with your flimsy little toys," Prator scoffed. "In my ascended state, nothing can touch me."

Selna grinned. "You want to know why we tried to kill you? You want to know who among your guard betrayed you, who let me into your bedroom, gave me the opportunity to strangle you in your sleep?"

Prator tilted his head. His eyes burned with fury, but his curiosity held him back.

"Of course you do," Selna said. "That's all you've asked me, day in and day out, ever since you caught me. You can't stand the thought that someone in your guard might be disloyal, might have betrayed your honor."

"Who was it?" Prator demanded, lifting his sword and pointing at the woman. He strode closer. "Tell me, or I'll kill you."

"Stay back," Elion said as the three of them shuffled away from Prator, cowering against the Aurelian altar statue.

"You wouldn't dare shed innocent blood at the foot of an Aurelian altar, would you?" Selna asked.

Prator grimaced. "Of course not," he said, "but these two fired first. They attacked me."

"And by your own admission, are incapable of harming you," Selna said. "You would kill us, kill them, and thereby violate the sanctity of the altar's protection?"

Prator scowled again. "I'm not interested in killing you. I'm not interested in anything you do after this," he said to Selna. "Tell me who betrayed me among my guard, and I'll let you go free. But Elion has to come with me."

“I loved you once, Prator,” Selna said. “I believed in your lies.”

“I never lied to you,” Prator snapped, his face burning.

“You coat your words with honey, speak in half truths and with misdirection. You tiptoe around the truth, never willing to address it. Your rationalizations and justifications sooth your conscience, but they cannot make you what you are not. You are a liar and a bully.”

Prator’s face darkened, his mouth flapping. “Shut up,” he growled.

Selna laughed. "I’ll tell you what you want to know! You haven’t figured out which of your guards betrayed you? Who let me into your mansion and led me to your bedroom? Think about it, Prator. How could I have done all of that with the assistance of merely one traitor among your ranks?"

"The truth is, all of them were in on it," Selna smiled. "Every single one of them is terrified of you. They don't respect you. They don't honor you. They are afraid of you. You are no better than Dorian," Selna said, "beating people into submission, binding them, enslaving them with the help of your friend, Bortim. If you were a true Aurelian, you would not have to use such tricks, and men would freely choose to follow you of their own volition."

As Selna spoke, Prator's veins bulged, his face beet-red. Fury grotesqued his features. He charged forward, slicing through Selna's laser rifle, disabling it, and grabbing the woman by the throat. He lifted her off her feet, hardly seeming to notice the weight.

"You lie!" he screamed. "My men are loyal to me. You lie. Who is the real traitor?"

Selna dangled in the air, unable to breathe. But she smiled, then spat in Prator's face. He dropped her, wiping the spit from his eyes.

His legs buckled, and he landed on his knees, strength going from him. The power of his ascended state faded. “All the strength of an Aurelian, and yet I cannot bring myself to do what I must,” he muttered. “I am still blinded by love. Tortured by a woman who has twice tried to kill me.”

The air whipped and cracked. Ribbons of purple Zelian power spiraled in a vortex just outside the protective dome of the Aurelian statue. With a thunderclap, Venya stepped out of the portal. Prator sprang to his feet.

"Well, well, well," she said, striding across the courtyard. "What have we here? Is that Elion? Excellent work, Prator. You have done very well this day."
