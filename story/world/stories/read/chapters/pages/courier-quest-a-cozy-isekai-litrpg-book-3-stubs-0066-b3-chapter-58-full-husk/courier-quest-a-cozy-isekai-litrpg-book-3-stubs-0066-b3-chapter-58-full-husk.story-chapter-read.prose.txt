Hans was surprisingly patient. He had apologized once again to the trio for his actions, and it seemed like he meant it. The earth elemental had tried to cow the yeti by making their displeasure at their master’s kidnapping known, but stopped when they realized the Summoned just thought they were playing.

It didn’t take long for Adabelle to don her armor. Clay, too, had requested and received another slab of their husk. Their form hadn’t changed much, keeping to the visage of a monstrous dog, though they had become noticeably bigger.

They stood outside of the Froslan Peak dungeon’s gates. The palace cut an intimidating figure on the top of the world. What was inside remained a mystery since the newlyweds hadn’t had time to prepare, but Hans had a few words to say when asked about it.

“Carved it out,” he said. “Now, only bottom floor. All monsters there. Make hunt easy. Fast. Fun. No search, just bash.”

“And it hasn’t fixed itself?” Adabelle asked curiously as she pursed her lips.

“Tries. Hans break again,” the yeti said proudly, beating his chest with his fist. “Good at breaking. Adbelle, Trev, Clay see. Will win.”

“We’ll see about that,” she returned. “Just remember not to break any monsters that aren’t moving. Those are Trevor’s, remember?”

Hans nodded solemnly. “Yes. If don’t move, not good hunt. Those yours. ‘Membered.”

“Alright, then good luck in there, big guy,” Trevor said, patting the yeti on the arm. He got a grin in return, then held up his hand. “Alright, we start in three, two, one… go!”

Adabelle and Hans took off like greased lightning as he lowered his hand. She had boots to handle the ice, just like Trevor, while the yeti simply jumped over it. That seemed to be his preferred mode of transportation, and the courier did have to admit that it looked fun.

Clay stayed with their master as they ran, though they were slower than the other two. The familiar fixed this by lowering their body and running underneath Trevor before straightening up again. He flailed for a moment before a handle appeared between the earth elemental’s shoulder blades. He sent his mace back into his inventory and held on as tightly as he could.

Which was good because Clay proved both faster and more agile than the courier. Their four clawed paws dug into the icy floor with every step, propelling them forward with ease. The stone underneath Trevor slowly morphed to become something comfortable for him. His familiar had reached a good form by the time they entered the palace.

“Oh, man,” Trevor muttered to himself.

The inside of the dungeon looked like a frozen, hollowed out beehive. He had an inkling of what Hans had meant when he said he had carved it out, and it made sense. There were rooms along the edges of the outside walls, their floors broken so thoroughly that he would have been hard pressed to find space to stand on the ledges that remained. There was no way any monsters would be able to hide up there.

Which meant they were all on the ground floor. Trevor looked past Clay’s head to see that the room was packed with ice golems in formation. Only a few were as tall as the monster that had gone outside, thankfully, and each one wielded a melee weapon. Spears and swords were the most common, but he saw hammers, flails, and clubs as well.

Adabelle and Hans had already made contact with very different results. The demonkin was testing their strength against her own, getting used to the monster’s movements. So far she had managed to break the weapons of two of them. They were forced to use their hands, but she was as swift as the yeti was brutish.

And he was very brutish. Hans barreled through the ice golem’s ranks with an enthusiastic battle cry that rumbled through the hollow ice palace like thunder. Every time he swung one of his massive hands, at least three of the monsters flew backwards, cracked but not quite broken. It was clear that he was having the time of his life.

Trevor patted Clay on his shoulder. “Alright. I’m counting on you, little buddy.”

The earth elemental nodded before rushing towards Adabelle’s side of the room. They were quick and smart, darting into the ice golem’s blind spots while they were busy with the demonkin. Trevor reached out and tapped two of them in quick succession with an outstretched finger. Their bodies disappeared into his inventory.

He only had the reach to grab one of the freezing hearts as it fell, though. The other hit the ground, but did not shatter. It seemed as though the ice cradled it somehow, which didn’t go unnoticed by Adabelle.

“Don’t leave the hearts,” she commanded as she redirected a golem’s overhead swing. A plume of ice appeared where it struck the ground. “I’d rather not see what happens. I’ll try to disarm them to make it easier.”

“Got it!” Trevor replied as Clay performed a hard stop. He reached out and touched another monster, sucking its shell into his inventory before grabbing the heart before it hit the floor.

Clay jumped backwards, evading two spear strikes in the process. It brought their master close to the first heart, though it was starting to sink into the icy ground. He touched it before it could happen.

Trevor winced as his finger became darker with every touch. He had been keeping the surface area minimal and his new Hazard Protection skill was helping, but there was no doubt that it was hurting him and it was only going to get worse. If he could reduce the number of times he had to touch the ice golems, then he would be more effective.

The problem was that he didn’t know what the dungeon was doing.

“Hans!” Trevor yelled. “What happens if the hearts disappear underground?”

“Bad stuff!” the yeti called from the other side of the room. “Big big come, with big big heart! Crush hearts fast!”

The courier breathed out. Taking the shells and leaving the hearts behind wasn’t going to work. That was a quick way to fill up his inventory, anyway. The ice golems were similar enough that Stacks was working, but they were too big to store easily.

If he only went after the hearts, though, then that would change the game entirely.

“We’re going centaur mode, Clay, full husk!” Trevor announced. His familiar immediately backed away from the fight. Some of the monsters chased them, but Adabelle was quick to fill in the gap.

The rest of Clay’s slabs fell from the courier’s hand, striking the ice with a loud crash. With glee, the earth elemental descended on them. The three remaining pieces were absorbed into their main form, sliding fluidly into place before the familiar reshaped itself.

Once again, Trevor took a moment to appreciate his little buddy. No matter how they incorporated their husk, they certainly lived up to their namesake.

The process only took a few seconds before the earth elemental adopted their new form. They were, once again, headless, but had a thick torso with four arms. Their front legs were also much wider to compensate for the distribution of weight. Clay’s fingers flexed and their claws scraped at the icy ground, which cracked beneath them from the weight as they waited for orders.

There was only one thing missing. Retrieving a certain pirate hat from his inventory, the courier set it atop his familiar's shoulders. They grew a head in a moment specifically to hold it in place, and he could sense their bubbling enthusiasm.

“We’re going after the hearts directly,” Trevor said, patting Clay on the back. “I’ll protect our sides and I trust Adabelle to watch our back, you just need to get their weapons out of my face. Got it?”

Understanding filled the bond between them and Clay lowered their body before charging. They were fast even with the added weight, though Trevor knew it would come at the cost of stamina. His familiar wouldn’t be able to keep this up for long.

They slammed into the first golem, which was threatening Adabelle’s flank. Their clawed hands went through the ice, lifting the monster up to where Trevor could reach them. He had been unsure if this would work but was willing to try, and the courier tapped the part of the heart visible in the enemy’s chest window.

It disappeared into his inventory, and the ice golem stopped in its tracks. There was no twitching, no last burst of power. At one point it was animated, and now it wasn’t. Clay used the shell to bash the monster behind it before grabbing another.

Adabelle had clearly heard her husband’s instructions and seemed to agree with them because she moved behind the stone centaur to protect their backs. It took a minute for the team to find their groove. Clay was the first as they were able to sense everyone’s movements through the ground, but the demonkin wasn’t far behind. She was used to fighting as part of a team and had the skills to keep up.

Trevor was the weakest link, but he was trying his best not to be. With Clay’s new form, there was a blind spot on their flanks that he was intent on keeping safe. Whenever he thought an attack would strike him or his familiar, he placed one of his bulwarks in the way.

There had been enough Floatstone that he could make six of the barricades, and he used five of them to great effect. The last one was, of course, set to his new Conditional Withdrawal. He took great care not to deploy his only contingency against getting blindsided.

Hans’ crashing continued from the other side of the room, but they didn’t need the noise to know he was still okay. The yeti had been laughing the whole time. This was obviously a source of great joy for him.

Which was more than Trevor could say. He trusted his wife and familiar, but the situation was still stressful.

The ice golems surrounded them from all sides, and their attacks were relentless. Frozen weapons struck Clay only to get stuck inside of him, and they battered the bulwarks in an attempt to get them to move. The Floatstone gave way an inch at a time, which was as good as nothing. Adabelle continued to parry, block, and redirect. She even lashed out and broke legs when possible, leaving the monsters to crawl.

But their numbers were dwindling. Every time Clay caught an ice golem, Trevor tapped the heart and their shell was discarded. Sometimes it was backwards, over Adabelle’s head. Other times it was into the crowd. On occasion, the body was used as a club.

The ice golems swarmed like insects, climbing over their fallen friends in order to reach the annoying enemies that took their hearts with but a touch. It seemed like there was no end to them until, suddenly, there was.

Trevor’s hand was almost completely black from frostbite as the last monster was tossed away. Adrenaline kept the pain dull as he looked around for the next one. There were a few on the ground, advancing without legs thanks to Adabelle’s efforts, but no more charged haphazardly towards them in a rush to end their invasion.

Clay reacted to something unseen, grabbing Adabelle and gently placing her with Trevor as they ran back to the entrance. There was a feeling of trepidation in their bond, as if something was coming. Hans apparently felt it, too, because he landed beside the fleeing earth elemental.

“Big big come,” the yeti grunted.

“But we didn’t let any hearts fall,” Trevor complained, frowning as he downed another health potion to fix his hand.

“Sometimes, don’t matter. Come anyway. Best fight outside. Room to jump. Big.”

“How big, Hans?” Adabelle asked.

He gestured vaguely at the inside of the dungeon. “Big big.”

“Good,” she said with a smile. “Clay, stop at the entrance. I’ll handle this one.”

Even though the familiar didn’t seem to agree with this, they did as instructed. Hans stopped a few feet away, watching curiously as Adabelle and Trevor dismounted from the centaur. He stared harder when Clay turned back into that monstrous dog form, this time with two saddles on their back.

The ice broke loudly, sending cracks up the walls that reached all the way to the ceiling. The adventurers’ gazes were forced back down to the floor as a humongous hand forced its way out from below. That alone was as large as the yeti.

Adabelle watched with a satisfied smile as a giant amongst ice golems made its way up from the basement, its head emerging before a pair of broad shoulders. The monster’s mouth was open in a silent scream, and its sight was set on them.

Its chest, the size of a small building, housed a truly massive heart of ice. At this size, they could make out small symbols etched on the outside as if there were words all around the magical power source. Trevor shook his head at the sight of it; there was no way that was going into his inventory. Not with everything else.

“Do you remember what Klarkartar said about the hearts of ice back in the Grandmother Tree, Trevor?” Adabelle asked.

He glanced at her out of the corner of his eye, not wanting to take any attention off of the monstrosity coming for them. “Kinda. Weren’t you drunk for that?”

“That’s not important,” she pouted, reaching out to tap his shoulder as if to chastise him. “He said the rumors go that a Froslan Peak ice golem’s heart is so cold that it could last in a desert for a hundred years.”

“Okay, yeah, I do vaguely remember that,” Trevor confirmed as the massive ice golem continued to pull itself out of the ground.

His wife flashed him a devious smile. “Shall we test that?”

He stood up a little straighter and took her hand. “Are you going to use your Power?”

“I should use it to protect the ones I love, shouldn’t I?” Adabelle asked in return. She was steadfastly ignoring the fact that their foe was now standing up on the broken ground. Its head almost reached the top of the palace several floors up. “And, perhaps, it’s okay to abuse it to end our honeymoon with a bang.”

“I am absolutely okay with this. Go for it, darling,” Trevor urged.

“As you wish,” she replied before leaning in to kiss him. It was deep, passionate, and over far too quickly for her liking. However, she had a job to do.

Maneuvering her husband behind her, Adabelle waved her hand in an almost casual manner. At first, nothing happened. The wind blew, but that was nothing new.

Then, heat.

Blisteringly hot air forced everyone except for the demonkin back a step. Clay moved to protect Trevor, but he had already put up a bulwark to divert the wind. Even with something solid to stand behind, the heat continued to rise.

The courier caught sight of a pillar fire that spun around the room, melting whatever ice it went near. It wasn’t subtle, and the golem was quick to try and strike it. The monster’s arm went through, cutting the stream in half. Its effort amounted to nothing though that didn’t stop it from trying.

Adabelle took a deep breath and then swung her hand again. The blaze grew to epic proportions, completely filling the Froslan Peak dungeon. Hans was already retreating from the heat, and Clay pushed Trevor back. He fought against his familiar, worried about his wife who was standing mere feet away from the disaster she had summoned.

“Are you okay!?” the courier called over the roar of the flames.

“When you’re by my side? Always,” she answered, looking back with a brilliant smile. “This will only take a moment.”

The firestorm raged. Any water the melting ice made evaporated before it could put out the flames. They could see and hear the ice golem inside fighting against the magic it had been subjected to. It lashed out against the walls, ceiling, and floor. The sound of ice scraping against stone somehow overtook that of the fire as it tried to push forward, unable to do so.

Slowly, bit by bit, the shadow inside of the firestorm became smaller. The magic holding the ice golem together was whittled down by Adabelle’s Power as it fought desperately to do something, anything, to protect its dungeon.

Even though he knew it was an unthinking, unfeeling creature due to being a golem, Trevor couldn’t help but feel a little bad for it.

That sympathy was dashed by how incredible he thought his wife was. That was not new information, though.

The blaze slowly diminished to a paltry ember, which floated across the breeze before it was snuffed out from the cold. This wasn’t difficult because there wasn’t much of an indoors left; she had melted the vast majority of the dungeon’s palace.

The only thing left was a charred heart of ice. Damaged, but surprisingly intact. It would be a great boon for the village.

With a flourish, Adabelle turned around and took a bow. Trevor was the first to clap and whistle, cheering for his wife. He didn’t approach her, because he could still feel the lingering heat.

“Adbelle scary,” Hans murmured, scratching his chin with wonder.

“Yes,” Trevor agreed with a grin on his face. “Yes, she is.”
