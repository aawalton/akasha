It was late by the time we returned to the Bastion, although if you stopped me and asked what else was discussed at Cybill’s mansion, I wouldn’t have been able to tell you with any coherence. Sure, there was a plethora of advice about who and what to watch out for at the ball, how to get to the dungeons, and what topics to avoid in polite conversation, but it all went over my head.

I was forgetting something—no, many somethings.

My journal lay on my bookshelf in front of me, its cover a bit dusty. I hadn’t been keeping up with it, let alone using it to list out my priorities as I did back in…

Chairo.

The name brought up all sorts of memories I wasn’t equipped to deal with. Most of them involved Relias, and how I had let him handle me for far too long.

Then there were the short snippets of conversation with the heroes of the past, their dénouement culminating in the idea that they had moved on, yet their final rest could still be so unsettled. Were they back in the hero queue inside Naught? Frozen somewhere in Paradise’s garden as data overflow? Or did they no longer exist?

Was Relias using them, somehow, like he was using Epiales?

“I’m too tired to even think straight…”

I was about three seconds away from landing in bed when I heard yelling.

It wasn’t the kind of shouts that you could, in good conscience, ignore. And Olethros was not someone who raised his voice without reason. When he did, it had a particular timber that called up every small fear hiding beneath the skin, regardless of whether you were the target.

Why now… And how come I can hear it all the way in here?

I followed the voice out of my bedroom, through a glowing corridor, and into a flashing portal. Olethros’s dark and crackling form was facing away from me, his attention entirely fixed on Amos.

Who was wearing my face?

Oh. This was definitely one of those “somethings” I had forgotten.

“—And what if she saw you undertaking such a contemptuous act without consent—”

“He has it,” I said from behind as the demon king flinched. “It was my idea, actually.”

He turned slowly, eyes squinted tightly as black mist pooled around his boots.

I took a deep breath through my nose and straightened, clasping my hands behind my back. “I should have informed you. I’m sorry, Your Majesty.”

The oppressive silence that followed made my distancing inflection even more apparent.

“I am not Your Majesty,” he eventually replied, “any more than you are my subordinate.” He turned back to Amos. “I withdraw my rebuke. Leave us.”

“Please,” I added, finding his apology lacking. “I’ll explain it.”

Amos bowed, holding his head down just long enough for me to catch a rather rebellious glint in his eye.

He set me up on purpose!

Amos disappeared just as the thought crossed my mind.

“Well? I’m waiting.”

I dropped my hands to my sides, fists forming out of frustration. “You said I’m not your subordinate. You’re not my majesty. So, what does that make us, then?”

He blinked several times, and I realized he truly believed he was going to get my explanation about Amos first.

I guess he really can’t read my mind anymore.

“Partners,” he replied, the declaration surprisingly strong. “You are the one I trust most.”

“Oh?” I trembled with a hollow laugh, the words I had hoped to hear landing poorly on my ears. “But you didn’t trust me enough to tell me what those public headpats truly meant.”

He glitched, his face paling on solidification. “Who told you—”

“That’s not the point! The point is—” I stopped, finding my jaw so tight my teeth were unable to let the words through. A sniffle broke the hold. “Did you mean it? Or was it just another game? A way to keep other demons in check? I think I deserve to know!”

He stood silent as I steeled myself for rejection.

“It was no ruse,” he finally whispered.

Relief flared through my extremities, and rage soon settled in its place.

“Then why didn’t you tell me?!”

“It wasn’t the right time.”

“The hell?! That’s not an explanation!”

He shrank a bit, his face flushing crimson. “I simply wished to state my feelings for the record. Without forcing yours! You—everyone. Everyone you interact with—every last one of them. They have manipulated you for as long as I have known you. I’ve watched it happen again and again.” He threw his arms wide, his cloak fluttering. “I do not want to be another one forcing your hand!” Then his own went to cover his face. “Even if it’s toward something I want.”

A cold sweat prickled my skin. “Olethros…”

“I know you defer yourself. What you need. What you want. You toil, trying to mend what was broken long before you ever arrived, and I do not wish to take advantage of that.”

“I don’t think I do that anymore—”

He held out his hand to stop me. “The sack of apples.”

“…What?”

He drew a long breath. With emerald eyes glittering, he said, “You promised Aurum a sack of apples when you leave, so that he would understand your leaving wasn’t about him. Even when you make the right choice for yourself, you let guilt claw at you. Then you try to compensate. I will not be something you have to compensate for! When you make a choice—whatever it is and when—I don’t want to be your resulting regret.”

My arms slid around him before thought caught up, my face pressing into his chest without his consent. A whimper rattled free. "I'm sorry..."

His arms hovered, then settled around me.

“Don't apologize,” he murmured against my hair, holding me tight. “Rachel. I’d do anything you’d ask of me. You know that, right?”

“Yeah,” I replied, more to his neck than anywhere else, where the scent of citrus and sage was strongest.

“I’m waiting for you to tell me what you need—”

“Can you escort me to a ball?” I blurted.

His grip loosened a little. “When do we go?”

I looked up, my raised eyebrows preceding my teary and exhausted gaze. “You’re not going to ask why?”

His face was still a little red, the blush doing a terrible job of hiding his confusion. “I’m sure you have your reasons.”

I tried explaining. I got partway through the coronation timeline and Prince Mito’s dungeon situation before I ran out of words and stopped.

“I’m overwhelmed,” I admitted, putting my head on his shoulder again. “I keep thinking about everything that has to get done, but I’m losing the threads holding it all together.”

“Sleep first. We’ll talk about other things later.”

“Yeah…” I replied, though I didn’t budge.

His cheek brushed against mine, his breath warm. “A warning. If I must put you to bed, I’m not leaving.”

My lips twitched. I would win this game of Chicken if it were the last thing I did. “Well, good luck with Chester then. He likes to take his half out of the middle.”

***

I woke up to the sound of a page turning.

True to his word, Olethros was beside me in bed, reading. The volume in his hands was slim and without any spine text, its illustrations imperfectly reconstructed from my, or perhaps Chester’s, memory. Words were blurred or missing entirely in places, but there were some pictures intact where one of us had apparently retained what mattered.

“What is the plot of this?” he asked, tracing the long edge of the graphic novel.

“Mmm…. Oh. That one’s a modern office romance with a really long and convoluted title... Both leads are rival department heads who have feelings they refuse to admit for about, uh, two hundred and seventy-five chapters, give or take.”

He turned a few pages, his lip curling a bit contemptuously. “And then?”

“Mm… They’re still figuring it out, I guess. Oh. It’s right to left by the way, not left to right,” I instructed, pointing to the cover on the back. “Derivative plot, but the pictures are nice.”

He flipped back a page and considered one of the more intricate illustrations depicting the female lead dominating her beau with a perfectly executed kabe-don behind her office door.

“Agreed.”

Then, he closed the book and set it aside with a smug expression I chose not to examine further.

***

Cybill’s house was considerably more chaotic at fitting time.

Most of the troupe had arranged themselves in the far corners of the room in a configuration that suggested their collective, wordless agreement was to remain as far from Olethros as the stone walls would permit. He appeared entirely unbothered, studying the updated castle map while a silent, stern Cybill added some finishing touches to the hem of my gown.

It was by no means meant to steal the show, as the soft blue satin was modest by all standards. Its neckline was demure, and the skirt was full enough to move in without being dramatic. It suited a minor noblewoman who hadn’t shown her face in high society for a decade just fine.

“I’m informed there have been no witnessed angels in the city,” Oltheros stated at one point, his tail twitching. “Is this intel accurate?”

Alaric, who had played the original demon king, gripped the chair he had placed in front of him as a shield. “O-our fair city houses no demons, so there is no need for angels to come here—”

Olethros turned to give him a withering look, then glanced at a servant pouring a pot of tea. “Do you wish to tell him, or should I?”

The teapot smashed on contact with the floor. “Dread lord!”

Olethros sighed as he stared at the broken bits of ceramic. “Neither of us deserved such insults. My father was the dread lord, not me.”

“F-forgive me!” the servant squealed, poofing out of existence.

Even Cybill froze after stabbing herself with her needle. “Stella?”

“I don’t know if that bit about angels will hold true through tonight,” I murmured, handing Cybill a piece of cloth to staunch the small bleed. “This is the trappiest trap that ever… trapped.”

Look. I was nervous and in a dress. Not all quips can be gold.

“Indeed,” Olethros agreed. “Vernie was allowed to come this far. He’s expecting you to do something this eve… but I don’t believe Relias thinks it will go quite like this.”

“Of course I’d rescue Prince Mito!” I thundered. “He’s my friend!”

The demon king grinned. “In a ball gown and high heels? Everyone knows how much you hate such ceremony. The old you would launch an attack outside the castle before resorting to dancing your way in.”

Even chaos had a place in battle. If it could scramble my thoughts, then it could certainly stupefy your enemy.

“Only the gown. No high heels," I advised, flicking my gaze at Cybill. "Flats that hide under its hem. As for the others.... Let’s hope Amos-as-me and Nora fool them…”

Tim, Tetora’s huge but still human counterpart, slid down the far back wall, his knees finally giving out from all his trembling. “Emmy… Emmy will make sure of it.”

Two fake heroes. One fake to be the faker of the two fakes.

With the rest of the party posing with a squad of demons.

It was so ridiculous, it might work.

For a few minutes, anyway.

“I’ll handle the timing with Nora,” Olethros replied, fixing his serpent’s mark on my gown like a brooch. “Press here to summon your weapons.”

“You should have told me you came with portal inventory,” I mumbled. “Think of all the accessorizing I could have done!”

Olethros shook his head. “It is faster to draw your weapons than summon them. And it takes a bit of my concentration not to let my animus interfere with their amity. I need no extra strain.”

“It’s the same everywhere,” I said with a whimsical smile. “You just don’t want to carry my purse.”

“Pockets are my preference,” he replied, adding the disguise pin to my collar. “That should hide your hair and eye color.”

“What about you?” I asked. “What are you going to wear?”

He shook out his hair, then pulled it into a ponytail. “This.”

“You look the same.”

“Not at all!” Brittany, Vernie’s younger twin, said with a start. “He reminds me of Gerald.”

Cybill squinted. “Gerald? No. He looks a bit like Johnathan. Especially around the jawline.”

“Who—” I stopped. “Never mind the names, but maybe explain what’s going on?”

Olethros shrugged. “I look like someone vaguely familiar to them. I still lack the capacity to mimic realistic human eyes, so my illusion is more mental than visual.” He pulled at a pair of gloves that had formed over his hands. “I won’t be able to fool any decent amity users at close range, however, so I may need to disappear. That doesn’t mean I’ll be gone, however.”

I stepped in front of him and held out my hand, knowing the carriage would soon arrive. “Fair warning. I’m probably a terrible dancer.”

“You just haven’t had the right dance partner,” he said as he reached for it. “Until now.”
