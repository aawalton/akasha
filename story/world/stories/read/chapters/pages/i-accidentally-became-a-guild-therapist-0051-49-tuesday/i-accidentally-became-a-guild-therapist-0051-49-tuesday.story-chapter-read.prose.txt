SYNOPSIS: Tuesday came. Three people, one frog, one direction: east toward the Cradle of Aethon.

Tuesday arrived the way significant days sometimes did: looking exactly like any other day until it wasn’t.

Livia was at the guild hall entrance at 08:00 with her notepad, her mug, and Lumen drifting at her shoulder. She had told Miles the evening before. Not in detail — just: there’s a location east of the rendered zones, I’m going to check it, I’ll be back by evening. "Take Nyx," he said.

"The guild needs everyone right now."

"You don’t have a combat stats."

"I know. I’m not going alone."

"Who—"

And then Yuki had walked through the guild hall door, staff on her back, already live — her stream overlay visible in the corner of her display, chat already running. She had nodded at Miles once. The kind of nod that communicated: I know what I’m doing and I don’t need you to approve it.

Miles had looked at her. At Livia. At Yuki again. His expression made a small adjustment that was not quite relief but was adjacent to it.

"Fine," he said.

The core cast had gathered without being formally summoned — that was how it worked with B&B, things were sensed rather than announced. Bron was in the yard with Boris, but he had positioned himself so he could see the entrance. Phina was on the upper walkway, Glimmer at her shoulder. Nyx was somewhere that was not immediately visible but definitely present. Alaric had his tablet and was very obviously not looking at them, which meant he was absolutely looking at them.

Then Blorbo arrived.

He came from the direction of the Tessarae, moving with the unhurried confidence of something that had decided its own pace was the correct pace. He was carrying a daisy — a fresh one, not wilted — and wearing his pixelated glasses at a slightly less askew angle than usual, which was the closest thing to formal he apparently had.

He stopped in front of the group. Looked at Yuki. Presented the daisy.

The guild hall went quiet.

Not the quiet of alarm. The quiet of collective processing.

Bron stared at Blorbo with the open expression of someone whose mental image of a situation had just been replaced with something significantly different from what he had imagined.

"That’s… Blorbo?" he said. "The voting one?"

"Ninety-eight thousand and seven votes," Blorbo confirmed.

Bron looked at Alaric. Alaric looked at his tablet. He had the expression of a man whose model had not accounted for this and who was updating it in real time with visible reluctance.

"I thought he’d be…" Bron started. He stopped. He looked at Blorbo again, at the glasses and the daisy and the iridescent patches and the absolute self-possession of something that had 98,007 votes and knew it. "…taller," he finished.

Phina, from the walkway, made a sound that was either a cough or a suppressed laugh. Glimmer pulsed pink.

Nyx materialized from wherever he had been. He looked at Blorbo for a long moment. Then he looked at Livia with the expression of someone who had a question but had decided not to ask it.

Approximately the size of a doorway standing alone — two uprights and a lintel, made of something that was not stone but had the quality of stone, that had been here long enough to have become part of the space rather than a thing placed in it. Inside the frame: nothing visible. Just the same quiet light, and the sense of something on the other side of it that the eye couldn’t quite follow.

Lumen drifted past Livia’s shoulder and moved toward the center. Its lightning was very slow.

"This is it," Blorbo said. He had stopped at the edge of the arrangements. He was not moving closer, and his voice had lost its usual composure — not much, but enough. "I told you it was expectant."

"You were right," Livia said.

"The arrangements," Yuki said. She was scanning the space with the focused attention she used when she was reading a dungeon layout — methodical, no wasted motion. "They’re not random. There’s a pattern." She moved along the edge, not entering, staff held at low guard. "Somebody put these here. On purpose. A long time ago."

Margo had gone very still. She was standing slightly behind Livia, looking at the structure with the alertness of someone whose body knew something their brain hadn’t caught up with yet.

"Is it… safe?" she said.

"I don’t know," Livia said. Honestly. "But it’s not hostile. It would have said so by now."

She walked toward the structure. Lumen moved with her. The sound of the space changed as she got closer — not louder, but more present, like the difference between hearing something and having it directed at you.

The arrangements around the structure were clearer up close. She crouched to look at one — a set of objects placed in careful relation to each other, each one distinct, none of them recognizable as any item she knew from the server’s registry. They had the quality of things that predated the registry.

Yuki moved methodically through the outer ring, staff still raised, noting exits and angles. Nothing moved. Nothing responded.

"Clear," she confirmed.

Livia stood and looked at the structure. At the frame. At the non-absence inside it.

Then Blorbo said, from behind her: "Oh."

She turned.

Blorbo was looking at something on the ground, approximately three meters to the left of the structure. Something he had apparently just noticed, or just understood. His head was tilted at the angle of genuine discovery.

"This one is different," he said.

"Different how?" Yuki said, already moving toward him.

"The others are… context," Blorbo said. "This one is…" he looked at it, "…itself."

It was an object. Small, like the others. But where the others lay flat or rested against each other, this one was slightly raised — suspended at an angle that physics in the normal server would not have permitted. It had a quality of density that its size did not account for. The surface did not reflect light the way surfaces did — it reflected light from the wrong direction, as though lit by a source that existed somewhere other than the space they were standing in. The edges of it were slightly uncertain, not glitching but not committed, like something that had not yet decided its final form. Looking at it was like looking at something that existed more than the space around it.

Blorbo reached out and touched it.

The space exhaled.

That was the only word for it. Not an explosion, not a flash. A long, slow exhale, like something that had been holding its breath for a very long time and had just been given permission to stop. The ambient sound deepened. The light shifted. The structure in the center pulsed once, warm and clear.

Everyone went still.

Nobody moved. Nobody spoke. The exhale moved through the space and settled into something that was not silence but was the quality of after — after something long-held had finally been released.

Lumen’s lightning slowed to almost nothing. It hovered near the center of the space and pulsed once.

Blorbo pulled his hand back slowly. He looked at his foreleg. At the object, which was now very slightly brighter than it had been. "I was not expecting that," he said. His voice was different — quieter, with something in it that was not usually there.

"Are you okay?" Livia said.

"Yes. It didn’t hurt. It was more…" he looked at the object, "…like being recognized. By something very old. That was very surprised to see me."

Yuki was already crouching in front of it. "It’s not registering in any item category I have access to," she said. "But it’s there. The system sees it. It just doesn’t have a name for it." She looked at Livia. "This is what we came for."

"I think so," Livia said.

She reached out. Her hand stopped an inch from the object.

The warmth was there — she could feel it from here. The same warmth as the anchor point. The same warmth as Lumen’s lightning. The same warmth as the moment the server had said, without words: you are not a top-layer event.

She knew, before she tried, that she wasn’t going to be able to pick it up.

She tried anyway. Her hand moved through the space where the object was as though the object existed in a different layer of the same location. Occupying the same coordinates but not the same existence.

Yuki tried. Same result.

Margo, who had moved closer without fully deciding to, reached out. Same result. She pulled her hand back and looked at her gauntlet. "Is it… supposed to do that?"

"No," Livia said. "But it’s doing it anyway."

Blorbo looked at his foreleg again. "I touched it," he said.

"You triggered it," Yuki said. "You didn’t pick it up."

"Is there a difference?"

"Yes," Livia said. "A significant one."

She crouched in front of the object and looked at it. Bright now, steadily, with the quality of something that had been woken up and was not going back to sleep.

Waiting. Still waiting, but differently than before. Not patient waiting. Alert waiting.

She wrote in her notepad:

Object — unregistered, no item category. Triggered by Blorbo. Cannot be picked up by any of us. It’s waiting for something specific. Or someone specific.

She looked at the structure. At Lumen, which had moved very close to the object and was pulsing in a rhythm that matched the object’s glow. At Margo. At Yuki.

"We can’t take it today," Livia said.

"No," Yuki agreed. "But now we know it’s here."

"And it knows we’re here," Livia said.

The object pulsed once, as if in confirmation.
