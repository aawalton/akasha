“What terrible bad luck!” Satan cried out. “Well, it was an accident, so it can’t be helped. I’m fortunate to have a fortified nascent soul, which I’m not allowed to use on this floor, but I can’t help it if there’s a soul explosion. What a shame, what a shame. And here you’d almost won. I commend your skills in battle, but you were just so unlucky. I’ll torture that devil for eternity to make amends.”

He shook his head in mock camaraderie. On the front lines, the battle had stopped. Everyone held their breath as they watched Satan Jr.’s innocent monologue.

“Wait, that’s it?” someone said. “We won?”

“We lost!?” someone on the puppeteer’s side said.

“That cheating asshole struck the puppeteer with a soul attack! Of course it can’t defend before developing a nascent soul! This is completely unfair! Someone should lodge a System complaint!” someone yelled.

“Idiots. Did you really expect Satan Jr. to lose to a normal monster?” a witch said with a snort.

Gary didn’t know what to think.

“Um, did we just win the war?” Goldie said.

“I think so,” he replied. He put off his relief until the screen came. It took a suspiciously long time. Satan looked around questioningly.

“Not enough? I guess I should break her physical body, too?” he said, reaching towards the puppeteer. Suddenly, he halted. A green and gray light rose from the ground under the puppeteer, enveloping her body. There was a jolt as if reality snapped to a different balance point. The light turned into flames, burning around the puppeteer, finally focusing on her forehead.

The light winked out. There was silence. Then, a tentacle shifted, and the puppeteer’s wide open eyes regained their color. “What an unlucky accident that was, Mr. Satan Jr.,” she said in her real voice—that of a dark, ominous woman. “Thankfully, I survived.”

“That’s impossible! You can’t have soul protections in the Core Formation realm,” Satan said angrily. “You’re cheating! That’s unfair! I’m the one supposed to be cheating!”

“It’s all a big coincidence,” she replied sweetly. Then, her voice became a pulse, a beacon of attention which swept through the battlefield like a gale of wind. “Kill everyone.”

The tentacle monsters revved to life. They charged ahead recklessly, throwing themselves at climber weapons, tearing out their own tentacles to throw at the gymnasium army’s backline. The tentacles themselves came alive—they were weak, but there were many. When they captured someone, they stuffed themselves down their throat, then the person bloated and transformed into a monster. It was the stuff of nightmares. Horror spread through the army.

“Hold the lines!” the commanders yelled, sending people to take care of the tentacles, but it was useless. Only the legion kept their formation—everyone else escaped wildly in all directions, running for where the gymnasium’s anti-deserter enforcers waited. The tentacle monsters and enemy climbers chased them. They fell on their backs. It was slaughter.

Gary rushed to his friends. “Follow me!” he said, cleaning all tentacles in the surrounding area. They ran towards the edge of the battlefield. Torga didn’t disagree this time. Gary covered their backs.

Satan Jr. had grabbed the puppeteer’s woman form from the chest with one massive hand and was smashing her head in with the other. “You’re spreading so much of your power,” he said. “Die, die, die!”

She only laughed. Everything he struck, the green and gray light repaired. It streamed endlessly from the ground. “You’re knocking on the wrong door, Mr. Satan Jr. My ascent is inevitable.”

“Who are your backers? I demand to know your backers! They won’t get away after messing with me!” Satan said.

“Oh, trust me. They will.”

“Who would act against Hell right now?”

“This isn’t about you or Hell, Mr. Satan Jr. The fourth floor is just one step. I am going to conquer all of the lower floors.”

“Why?” Satan roared, smashing her face which kept regenerating. She didn’t seem to feel pain.

“I fucking hate this,” Torga growled. “They’re messing with us. It’s all a big joke. I didn’t enter the tower to be played like this.”

“Tell me about it,” Gary replied, staring at the puppeteer as they retreated.

“Man, fuck cheaters. Rules are there for a reason!” Cog said.

“I hate to break it to you, Cog, but it’s very clear that the rules don’t work,” Gary said.

“Well, duh! I could understand the door, but this is blatant cheating! She can’t have soul defense in Core Formation, or that kind of regeneration! I mean, I get it isn’t really cheating, but it is!”

“What do you mean it isn’t really cheating?” Alicia asked.

“They couldn’t sneak in real Nascent Soul protections during this event, as there’s a System barrier around the entire floor. They’re creating it through an advanced formation and permitted ingredients, like Satan did with his soul explosion. Or like the teleportation before. It’s smart, okay, but it’s unfair! We should have won this war!” Cog said.

“Okay, wait. Everyone slow down,” Gary said, turning his attention fully to Cog. He’d already beaten back their chasers. “You’re saying that the puppeteer’s invulnerability is provided by a formation?”

“Well, duh. Isn’t it obvious?”

Gary looked around, seeing the same incomprehension in everyone’s eyes. Marla shrugged. “I thought it was just magic,” she said.

“Nothing is just magic,” Cog replied.

“What formation, Cog?” Gary said.

“Well, uh, it’s a Penta-Soul Formation. The System would stop soul-attuned materials from entering the floor, so whoever is doing this must have used climbers to sneak in a set of unattuned materials which can combine into attuned ones. Then, it’s just a matter of setting up a formation which performs the fusion outside its controlled radius, so it doesn’t trip any System alarms, and pushes it into the ground. Ah, they must have added the puppeteer’s essence to the mix, too, so the energy travels to her. That level of regeneration is practically invulnerability against people who can’t use soul attacks—which is literally everyone on this level.”

“Back up. You’re saying there’s a formation somewhere healing the puppeteer? Can’t we break it?” Torga asked.

“I guess,” Cog replied.

“Cog! Why didn’t you say it sooner?”

“Hey, we’re at war! I didn’t think about it. thought everyone knew.”

“We all thought there was a topper present!”

“Woah there, they can’t cheat that much. A topper’s energy would get detected immediately and they’d be auto-expelled. Even if they affected us from the void, it would still get detected. There can’t be a topper. It’s a formation utilizing a System loophole.”

Gary and the others looked at each other. “Where’s that formation?”

“In the puppeteer’s base!” Marla said. “That’s why they brought the entire base here. It was already set up inside!”

“Duh,” Cog said.

Gary threw a fiery glare at Cog, then glanced at the base. “Don’t even think about it,” Asim said.

“I know. I can’t get there anyway. Let’s wait out Satan.”

The massive devil was already under siege by the puppeteer, the brothers, and Liu Dong. Their combined power was extremely formidable. He was steadily chipped away. He still pummeled at the puppeteer, but the green and gray energy healed her perfectly.

They reached the edge of the battlefield. The gymnasium enforcers against deserters tried to stop them, but Gary made short work of them—they were surprisingly powerful.

Why didn’t they participate in the battle? he wondered.

The group was about to escape into the forest when an aura burst came from behind Gary. He turned to find Jin standing just a hundred feet away—within instant reach of his speed.

Gary lifted his spear.

“There are very few people I’d kill for free, Gary Steel,” Jin said. “You are one of them, but I’m also getting paid. Handsomely. Let the people behind you go and come to face me.”

Gary didn’t look away from the assassin. “We’ll help,” a voice came from behind him—Torga’s.

“You can’t,” Gary replied. “You haven’t broken through yet. Just run.”

“Can you beat him?” Asim asked.

“Go!” Gary said.

He heard muffled whispers and sliding feet. Before long, they left the range of his aura field and disappeared. Jin smiled. Under his leather armor, his gears whirred, and two daggers with glowing, neon blue blades appeared in his hands.

“Twice you have escaped me, Gary Steel. I told you, there will be no third time.”

“I’m stronger now. What makes you so certain you can take me?” Gary replied, levying his spear at his opponent.

“Oh, I’m certain,” Jin said, assuming a battle position.

“Why didn’t you sneak attack me? Aren’t you an assassin?”

Jin laughed. “I enjoy the hunt. But you can stop delaying me, Gary Steel. You are important to us and the puppeteer. Satan Jr. won’t fall before you.”

Gary frowned deeply. That had been his plan indeed. Thankfully, he could see the other three brothers and Liu Dong still fighting Satan alongside the puppeteer. Jin had come alone. What made him so confident?

The assassin must have read his thoughts. “Zink was my biological brother,” he said. “We didn’t always get along, but I loved him. I asked for this honor.”

“Zink, the ball guy?”

“Yeah.”

“He died like a bitch. So will you.”

A machine revved inside Jin. He crossed his daggers before himself and launched forward, reaching Gary in an instant. He blocked and got flung back. As he righted himself, Jin was already there, a blue line zipping around him and pelting him with strikes. It was the first time he used two daggers. Gary took surface wounds. His spear was far too slow. He tried to make some distance, but he couldn’t even make it six feet away before Jin was on him again.

A hundred strikes fell in the blink of an eye. Gary blocked and parried some. He took the rest. There was no deep wound, or one in a vital spot, and they healed quickly, but the damage accumulated. He felt glad for his great Vitality. Suddenly, he realized he’d moved away from the forest without realizing it, stranded in the middle of a near-empty battlefield with only enemies watching from the distance. Chimeras were going around, raising the dead as a new army—one meant to fight in the fifth floor.

Gary had no time to watch all that. They were only flashes of awareness through this high-speed battle. He couldn’t even see Jin, only his bright blue daggers. He thunderstepped continuously, backing up, while the assassin was everywhere. “Fan the Spear!” Gary yelled, but Jin’s attacks were not so weak as to be considered projectiles. He took a deep gash on the thigh. Blood flowed thickly from a major artery, but his regeneration patched it up. That wound was quickly followed up by one in the arm and one on the side of the chest, accurately drawn between two ribs.

He tried to dance away, but it was impossible. Finally, he got an idea.

“Song of Rain!” he said, splashing out a wide field of aura drops. It wasn’t one of his stronger techniques, but it specialized against targets he couldn’t catch. Jin paused in his tracks, falling back and around the attack instead of taking it head-on, and Gary took that time to retreat a long way. By the time Jin caught up again, he was ready.

“Song of Rain!” he yelled.

He kept the assassin at bay while retreating at top speed. He had no illusions of beating him in an empty field. He just couldn’t keep up. He needed a cramped battlefield—a corridor.

Gary wasn’t retreating towards the forest, which was too sparse. Or towards Satan Gym, whose entrances were barred. He was retreating towards the only other place with corridors.

The puppeteer’s base.
