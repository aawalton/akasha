From over 1,000 people, only 27 died. The newspaper reported how brave and efficient the UN was when dealing with the red advanced dungeon. They also listed several heroes who performed above expectations.

Naturally, James’ name was included, along with other people such as Anderson, Silas, Andrew, Kouki, and Sanders. Anderson, Silas, and Sanders were particularly highlighted. Several pieces of information from the survivors soon followed—how they should survive, how advanced dungeons can teleport people outside of their boundary, and so on.

“What the fuck is with this news? None of it resembles reality! Are the editors and journalists there fiction writers?” James scrunched his face. He couldn’t stomach how the newspaper praising the UN for sending 1,000 elite personnel against dungeons of unprecedented difficulty and coming out basically unscathed. “What’s Silas’ name doing in here?”

James had always wondered why the dungeon spat them out without giving him his reward. Other dungeons he had dived into gave him rewards—coins and weapon boxes. But when he read the newspaper, he figured it out. It was because that advanced dungeon was red. They didn’t enter the dungeon. That’s why he didn’t get a reward. That also explained why the system was silent.

Andrew smiled wryly. Silas was a UN lapdog and was technically the leader of this operation, so the UN really pushed its limits by crediting Silas in their report. They had been staying at the motel for the past two days, resting. James had already booked a ticket home, but it was scheduled for three days later.

“Well, the UN said they will give us 100 million dollars split among the three of us. They also asked us for support in the future.”

“Support? Hell no. I am not babysitting another deadweight!”

“Of course, we will not send people to the dungeons nor were you going to fight red dungeons. That’s a separate commission. Support here means keeping silent and praising our government.” Silas came into the dining room. “Red dungeons mean monster invasions, so the UN is doing everything they can to keep order. This is a chance for the government to justify military spending. I hope for your cooperation.”

“Tch!” James spat. Not only did this guy get out scot-free, he was even lauded as a hero. That really put him in a bad mood. This guy was the reason he had failed to upgrade his weapons! Those rusted weapons that could be repaired were now lost forever in that dungeon!

“By the way, time passed normally inside the red dungeon. So, all in all, we have spent a month inside the dungeon. In that time, three more advanced dungeons had gone red. Several medium and dozens of novice dungeons had popped,” Andrew said. “The local survivor guild had dealt with it along with the military, but there will always be a standing commission for experienced survivors such as you, James. Will you defend them?”

“Dunno… I am not good at working with others. I would prefer to dive into dungeons…” He sighed. His drive—his passion for exploring dungeons—had evaporated. It wasn’t because of fear of dying. It was like when his neighbor had better wares than he did.

The world no longer needed survivors to raid dungeons. The better solution was to wait outside until the dungeon turned red and bombard it with conventional weapons.

James was no longer the miserable salaryman he had been before. He now had millions of dollars to his name, and with Ryan, he had become the boss of a supermarket. Not a gigantic supermarket like Walmart, but still a respectable one. He could live the rest of his life with ease.

“Are you going to help them?” James asked, wondering whether he should just retire.

“Not really. I would if they asked. But just like you, I am thinking of opening a business. I want to lie low for a bit, maybe opening a farm or something. Then I can sell to your supermarket,” Andrew sighed. The stress had gotten to him. After being betrayed by Silas and the government, he wanted a breather.

“That would be wonderful. Though I must say, you wasted your talent. You have life magic, right? There would be a position for you as an assistant surgeon. Scientists have figured out that healing magic is invaluable during surgery,” Silas said. “As for other magic, such as your ice magic, they are valued in the logistics.“

“You are not welcome here, Silas.” James scrunched. He then snickered, “UN hero.”

“Listen, this might sound odd to you, but I am not trying to become a hero either, alright? Those UN presidents were the ones who appointed me. Unlike you free-spirited people, I have a job—a binding contract, alright?” Silas said. His eyes were different this time, but James didn’t notice. Nor did he care.

“You didn’t even level up inside the dungeon, tch. Did you even pick up a weapon?”

“I guess you didn’t like me very much, huh? I wonder why. Didn’t I always behave cordially toward you, James?” Silas said, tilting his head.

“Nah. I just hate government guys,” James said. Actually, he didn’t dislike Silas because he was a government guy. It was exactly because Silas acted so politely and obsequiously that James hated him. It felt like he was not speaking from the heart—like he was faking it. James had trust issues after his firing; that was why he instinctively avoided him.

“I see.”

“Maybe James didn’t like how you behaved, Silas. I mean, you literally backstabed me and the guild,” Andrew sighed.

“I must clarify that it was not me who betrayed you. It was the UN who didn’t accept your proposals,” Silas said.

“I see. Well, I don’t think we will ever meet each other again,” James said. He then gave Silas a glass of wine. “Here, for you.”

“Thank you,” Silas said. “Don’t be so sure yet. They might request help from you. We experienced some difficulty when dealing with a medium dungeon, and we will definitely need help when dealing with an advanced dungeon, James.”

“There are no more dungeons that need attention, right? It’s not like they took out a nuclear reactor. You can just leave them alone. For medium dungeons, you just need proper weapons—like ice and fire.”

“Does that mean you are not willing to help?”

“Call me. I will come when I am not diving in a dungeon,” James said. He then began munching his sandwich. The words flowed out of his mouth. He was surprised by how smooth that sounded.

“Huh? You will still dive into dungeons, James?”

“Probably the novice ones. Or maybe, after I get a few level ups, I will try an advanced dungeon. I wonder what the real one would look like.”

“That was not the real one?”

“The one we raided several days ago was red. You don’t remember? I don’t know the difficulty inside that dungeon, but there were some things missing. Real dungeons often give you rewards for clearing them. That’s the most obvious part.”

“Ah, I see. Message me too, James. Let’s try doing one together in the future. I will call others, and we could probably assemble a team,” Andrew said.

“No. I am done babysitting greenhorns,” James nodded.

“Am I still a greenhorn to you?” Andrew asked.

James looked over at him. Andrew had helped him numerous times during the previous dungeon. His skill was solid.

“…No. You were helpful.” As much as James hated to admit, Andrew was useful.

“I am glad. Well, I will be sure to gather only elites. It’s not like a civilian will join us after knowing we can clear a dungeon just by sitting outside, anyway.”

“Is that so?”

“The survivor guild has been officially disbanded. All matters relating to dungeons have been delegated to the army,” Silas said while nodding.

“I don’t care. I will still dive. See you. I am hitting the sack.”

James left. As he exited the motel, he saw the plain stretching out before the nuclear complex. His eyes swept over the city behind him, then toward the wasteland where the dungeon once stood. He was financially secure now, but the city and its riches no longer interested him. His gaze was drawn to the unknown.

James resolved himself. Even if he had to risk his life, he would continue exploring dungeons. He promised himself that. He wanted to explore every dungeon. He would not rest on his laurels.

Hiro now understands. Dungeon raiding was his passion. This was his life’s purpose.

He won’t stop.
