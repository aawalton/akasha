The traffic jam lasted most of the night, and Varin’s Academy’s dormitory building was filled with thumps, thuds, and scraping sounds as the next wave of first-year students moved in. Eola stayed up, studying the class guide and Varin’s notebook; with the chaos outside, any semblance of quiet hours had been set aside. Somehow, Patrice managed to sleep, but Roth played melodies on his harp until far later than necessary, and Colin ended up lying on Eola’s bed as she went over her class list one more time and dawn started to break over the circular granite wall around Varin’s Academy and the town that shared its name.

It was…nice to have him around again. Colin had been her first real friend at the academy—even if it had taken her longer than she’d be willing to admit to treat him like a real one. And now…well, things had changed, hadn’t they? As she tinkered with her list, he talked about his trip south. The people he’d befriended. The walls of Highstone. The battle the carriage’s escort mages had fought against a real, honest-to-Y’aer wyvern. Eola let him talk. It sure was nice to have him around.

Twenty minutes after the last crashing sound from the dormitory hall, Colin yawned. “I know it’s almost breakfast time, but I’m exhausted. I’m going to sleep.”

“I should, too,” Eola muttered, standing. “I’ll see you tomorrow. Or today, I guess.”

A quick hug and a kiss on the hand that didn’t linger, even though she sort of wanted it to, and they stood awkwardly by her room’s door. Colin was blushing, too, so at least she wasn’t alone. “G’night,” he said after a minute.

“Good night.”

She waited until he disappeared, then scratched her head and composed herself as Atta snored from her spot on the desk. Then, after a quiet few seconds, she padded through the suite’s common room and opened the hallway door.

The traffic jam was not, in fact, solved.

A full wardrobe had—somehow—gotten jammed into the hall tightly enough that traffic had to route either up or down a floor to get around it, sealing off their hall temporarily. That was the cause of the relative quiet, but the detritus of moving-in day was plain to see outside of the neighboring doors. Eola hadn’t had anywhere near this much stuff when she’d moved in. Her mother’s armor and smallsword, her father’s wand, her spellbook and notebook, a few changes of clothes for under the school’s robes, and the portrait on the wall—everything else had been unnecessary.

There was one other thing in the hall. A letter with her name on it. No, not a letter. A note.

Miss Lemiene,

I expect you in my office at ten bells to discuss your second term’s course selection. Come prepared.

Instructor Tarik

That was all it said. Eola flipped it over, just in case there was more written on the back, but no. The apprentices’ master was much more verbose in person, but his notes were often relatively terse. She tucked it into her robe pocket and turned to go back inside. There was time for her to finish organizing Varin’s apprenticehood notebook, or to go over the class brochure one more time.

Instead, she ran into her cat.

Atta’s yellow eyes followed her, and she coughed once. Eola braced herself; the last time she’d had a proper hacking fit, she’d spat up the entirety of Varin’s apprenticehood notebook. But no—after a few spine-wracking coughs, the cat turned and pranced back to Eola’s bedroom.

Eola followed, curious. Just entering the common room was a big change for her blue-furred familiar, and clearly, Atta had something to say. She shut the door behind her, lifted Atta onto the desk, and sat down. “Alright, what is it?”

“Heresy. Interesting stuff those two said last night. I was hoping you’d get rid of the boy. He’s more trouble than you can afford right now, silly girl. We could have had this conversation last night, while it was still fresh.”

“You know something.” It wasn’t a question. Atta obviously knew something.

“Yes. I don’t know how much I can say, but the short version is that the Cult of Y’aer is probably looking for you to make a mistake. You, specifically, and your friends more generally. It’s got to do with—“ More coughing interrupted the cat.

“Y’aer curse it, Atta!” Eola blurted out. Her frustration—and questions—boiled over. “You’re insanely powerful for a familiar. None of the others talk. Not even Tarik’s, or Headmaster Tomlish’s snake. How long have you been alive?”

“Too long.”

“You’re not going to tell me that, either, huh? I’ve been letting you off the hook because of your conveniently timed coughing fits, but we should have had this conversation a long time ago, and we’re having it right now. You eat and cough up books. You did something to save my life when I fell out of the third floor—something no familiar should be able to do with magic. That collar’s clearly binding you, but you won’t let me take it off. Clearly, you’ve got your own goals, and you’ve already said you’re not my familiar, at least not originally. So…what are you, Atta?”

The cat stared at Eola, and she stared back. Atta broke first. Her eyes shut, and she licked her paw. “Fine. I’ll do my best to answer.”

“That’s all I can ask, apparently.”

“Stop that, silly girl. The collar’s binding me—and no, it can’t come off. I am…was…the familiar for a very powerful mage. That gives me an insight into magic your friends’ pets don’t have. It also gave me a long time to…grow, shall we say. The stone figurine, the storage space in my stomach, all of that is…”

“Is?” Eola pressed.

“I’m trying my best, Eola!” Atta yowled. “This is not as easy as it looks, even for me. When I ate you—

“You ate me!?”

“Stop being so horrified, girl. It was more of a swallowing, and I spat you up just fine, thank Y’aer. When I ate you, it…all of that is…they’re tools, given to me by my master to…”

Silence. Eola leaned in, eager to catch every word.

Atta hacked and coughed in her face.

“Fine! You won’t say anything? Y’aer take you!” Eola stood, wiped spittle off her cheek, and put her hand on the doorknob.

“Wait.”

She froze.

Atta coughed one more time, then tapped a paw on the stack of yellowed, saliva-stiffened papers on Eola’s desk. “Instead of worrying about me and what I can do, worry about this task. That’s what you need to do. Finish the notebook, keep learning your magic, and keep your head down.”

Eola hesitated. Then she nodded, opened the door, and set off for Instructor Tarik’s office. As she stepped into the hall, a familiar weight dropped into her pocket, and she rubbed Atta’s head with her thumb while she crossed Varin’s grounds.

But by the time tenth bell rang across the campus and Eola navigated the choked, furniture-filled dormitory halls and crossed the courtyard to Instructor Tarik’s office, all she’d managed to do was make herself nervous about her choice of classes. It didn’t help that Atta insisted on changing into her cat form again. She was draped over her shoulders like a heavy, blue scarf. “I want to make sure you make good choices,” she’d said.

So, as Eola arrived at the cluttered, messy office and—after moving a stack of ink-sketched runes off the rickety oak chair—sat down, she braced herself to do battle with the professor across from her.

Instructor Tarik’s brown beard had grown out in the weeks between the end of the term and now. His skin had tanned, too. Clearly, he’d been getting some time outdoors. His brow wrinkled as he pored over a book. Eola read the title: Magic and Power in Pearl Bay: A History of Post-Ordered Magic Politics at the Spine Mountains’ Terminus.

“That looks thrilling, sir,” she said after nearly two minutes of silence.

“It does, doesn’t it?” The book shut, and Instructor Tarik looked up for the first time. His rabbit familiar hadn’t looked away from Atta since Eola arrived, the poor, nervous thing. “I assume you have your class list, as well as several potential changes, correct?”

Eola slid her list across the desk. Then she waited as the professor read it.

Advanced Mana Studies - Madame ReyannaSpellsword Tactics - Maestro Yarrowbloom and Lieutenant-Librarian FringeCombat Cantrips: Professor D’LeonidePatrol Duty - Lieutenant-Librarian FringeApprenticeship - Instructor Tarik

Extinct Languages - Instructor TarikAdvanced Ideograms - Instructor TarikIntroductory Ensorcellment - Lannith

When he reached the end, to her surprise, he laughed.

“What?” Eola asked.

“You’re bolder than any of your fellow apprentices. I doubt any of them expect to drop three of the standard classes, much less in favor of electives. And remember, you’re on the combat track. Dropping combat courses for generals is frowned upon for good reason, Miss Lemiene.”

“I can justify all three of my choices.”

“Then do so,” Tarik said. He steepled his fingers and waited.

“I know there are good reasons to require Combat Cantrips. Reading up on the course, I discovered that Professor D’Leonide is considered to be one of the best minds in Alemia at using Child’s Magic in combat. He’s creative, skillful, and quick. That should be perfect for me, in theory. But I think my style of combat is going to look a little different than his in practice. He sounds like he’s more aggressive and probing. I’m more reactive. I’d rather take Advanced Ideograms.”

“Because of your Cutting mark?”

Eola nodded. “Exactly. Cutting does nothing against Child’s Magic. It’s too fast for me to react, and cutting a single modifying mark away doesn’t do enough to change the spell. Dropping a First Order spell from four marks to three, though…that’s significant. I need Advanced Ideograms to keep pushing my knowledge of runes.”

“Ideograms,” Instructor Tarik said reflexively. “They’re ideograms, not runes. That’s a justification for Combat Cantrips. How about for Advanced Mana Studies?”

“Madame Reyanna hates me.”

Tarik snorted before he could stop himself.

“And, more than that, she’s almost certainly not going to teach any skills that apply to my attunement. Everything except Exhaustion relied on an attunement, or at least only really worked with one. I can practice Exhaustion training myself, but what I really need is a different approach to my soul’s efficiency. Spell-chains are too expensive, and if I try to make them more efficient, they eventually stop being spell-chains and start being Ordered Magic—and then I can’t cast them at all.”

She waited. Would Instructor Tarik see it?

He did. “What do you know about Lannith?”

“Nothing.”

“Good. It’s more fun that way. You intend to use ensorcellment to create a tool for casting more efficiently?”

“I don’t know.” Eola shrugged. “I intend to take the Ensorcellment course with the hope that Lannith will know more about it than I do. She—“

“It.”

“I’m sorry?” Eola asked.

“It. Lannith isn’t a man or woman. The correct pronoun for what Lannith is is ‘it,’” Instructor Tarik said. “It’s good that you’re looking at Ensorcellment with an open mind. You’ll need it. And finally, Extinct Languages in place of Patrol Duty? A bold move. Why? What’s your reason for taking Extinct Languages?”

Eola swallowed. “I don’t have one. I just thought it sounded interesting.”

For a long time, Instructor Tarik just stared. Eola swallowed a second time, then a third. It wasn’t a very good reason, but she couldn’t exactly tell him that she’d found a book written by Varin himself and was working on translating it…could she?

After a minute or two had passed, Instructor Tarik nodded. “Patrol Duty is mandatory. Pick one of my two classes. You can take it in place of Combat Cantrips, and Ensorcellment in place of Advanced Mana Studies.”

“Extinct Languages, then,” Eola said quickly.

Tarik’s eyebrow raised, and he stared at her for another few seconds. Then he shook his head. “That’s what I thought you’d say. Very well. Keep your secrets. Now, before you go, Miss Lemiene, a word about your magic.”

“Sir?”

Instructor Tarik didn’t say anything. Instead, he drew a rune in the air—a Third Order rune. Eola barely recognized the core elements. It was a ward. The Old Alemic and Forsooth Script made too much of a tangle too quickly for her to learn more than that. But when he finished it after nearly thirty seconds of casting, Eola’s ears popped painfully, and she winced. So did the professor. “I’m sorry. That should keep most eavesdroppers away.

“I’ve been discussing your magic in depth with Headmaster Tomlish over the term break, Miss Lemiene. Specifically, your description of what the Cutting mark does has serious implications for Ordered Magic. It’s made it vulnerable in a way that most mages believe impossible.”

“Countermagic,” Eola murmured.

“There’s no need to be quiet. This ward is unbreakable except by a Great Mage, of which Tomlish is currently the only one nearby. But yes. Countermagic. That destabilization, even more than your unique way of casting spell-chains instead of ideograms, will cause problems for the Cult of Y’aer and the Governing Houses.”

“I’ve been keeping it quiet. The only people I’ve told have been my friends, you, and the headmaster,” Eola said. She squirmed in her chair. A confrontation with one of the Governing Houses had always been inevitable; as long as her parents were trapped in Lord Card’s dungeon, she would have to face him in battle or across a desk eventually. But she’d hoped to do so as a Truemage or Archmage, not as a student who hadn’t even reached Journeymage.

“Good. Keep it that way. I’d caution against using it in your classes, but the way Yarrowbloom and Fringe run Spellsword Tactics, you’ll be forced to by the end of the first week. With that said, would you show it to me?”

Eola hesitated. The Cutting mark was hers. She’d learned it through months of work, and giving it to someone felt…wrong. But this was Instructor Tarik. If she couldn’t trust the one professor who’d bet on her—even if she was, on some level, an experiment—she couldn’t trust anyone with it. After some thought, she drew it out. “You pick a modifying mark and draw it on top of the mark. It cancels the mark out, but if either you or the other mage doesn’t finish the spell…”

Actually, what would happen if she let the spell fade, and if the other mage did the same thing? Eola had never tried it. Something to experiment with, she supposed. If the spell simply stopped, that was an even more effective counter-spell than what she’d been doing. But if not…she had to know. It felt vitally important. Something for the Cells, with Patrice, maybe?

“You don’t know, do you?” Tarik asked. The black rabbit perked its head up to stare at her, and she shook her own head. “I thought not. Is there anything else?”

“Yes. Patrice. Her attunement doesn’t make any sense. The rest of us have made progress with ours, but hers keeps being unmanageable.”

Instructor Tarik nodded as he carefully folded her Cutting sketch and tucked it into his jacket’s breast pocket. “It doesn’t make any sense, does it? I’ve been grappling with that as well. Fate Unwoven should mean that here’s unlimited potential, but it seems like her attunement’s gotten the better of her. It’s getting the better of me, too. If you come up with anything, I’d love to hear it.”

“I’ll let you know, sir,” Eola said.

“So.” Instructor Tarik stood and walked to the door. “Your final class list. Ensorcellment, Extinct Languages, Spellsword Tactics, and Patrol Duty. Those, plus your apprenticeship, should keep you busy enough. Do report back to me on that as well, once you’re through the first day or two.”

Eola stood, gathered her notes, and slipped out the door. Just before Instructor Tarik shut it, he cleared his throat. When she looked back, he smiled widely. “I’m curious how you’ll survive Lannith.”

For the time being, neither would affect his work, but it was only a matter of time, and unfortunately for him, he’d be one of the first professors at Varin’s Academy to draw the Cult’s eye.
