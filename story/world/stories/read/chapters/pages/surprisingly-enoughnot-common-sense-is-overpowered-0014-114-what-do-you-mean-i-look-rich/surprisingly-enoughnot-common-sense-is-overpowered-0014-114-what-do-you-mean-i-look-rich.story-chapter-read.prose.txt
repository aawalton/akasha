"The opportunity to reach the fourth stage within a year has finally been revealed. It's a Cleansing Pill," Wei Zhusang announced, looking around the room.

The members of the Mutual Aid Association had gathered at their usual teahouse for another meeting, with disciples from different backgrounds and divisions of the sect seated around the table.

"What's a Cleansing Pill, Brother Wei?" Qiao Mei asked sweetly, her eyes shining as she looked at him.

The scent of hormones is strong in this one, Ning thought, watching the lovestruck girl flutter her lashes.

At fifteen, developing a crush was hardly surprising, especially when Wei Zhusang was quite charismatic in his own right.

Ning, of course, had little interest in such things. As the saying went, "Love only slows the hand that draws the sword."

He had more important things to worry about, such as cultivation. Besides, with jade beauties being as common as grass in this world, why would he willingly walk into such a cliché?

"The Cleansing Pill is a top-tier first-grade pill," Wei Zhusang explained. "It purifies the marrow, improves one's complexion, and removes impurities accumulated within the body."

"It's especially valuable because it can clear away the residual impurities left behind by excessive pill consumption," Zhang Feng added. "Refining one also requires far more skill than most first-grade pills."

Several members gasped upon hearing this.

Even with careful cultivation, it was difficult to eliminate every trace of pill residue from the body. Over time, those impurities could accumulate in the meridians and slow one's progress, making a pill capable of removing them extremely valuable.

A top-tier first-grade pill could also be used by late-stage Qi Condensation disciples. Ordinarily, low-grade first-grade pills were intended for early-stage cultivators, mid-grade pills for mid-stage cultivators, and so on.

Even someone who had no intention of consuming it could make a fortune by selling one.

"That's great. If only we could get one," Chen Mu from the Beast Taming Hall sighed. "But among us, only Senior Brother Wei, Brother Zhang, Sister Lang Rulang, and Brother Su Fan can even attempt that level."

Those four had already reached the second stage of Qi Condensation, while the rest were still somewhere in the first stage.

Ning sighed along with them. Despite his diligence and the pills he'd consumed, he was only approaching the peak of the first stage.

"You get what you spend," Lang Rulang remarked.

Unlike the genial Wei Zhusang or the carefree Zhang Feng, Lang Rulang was sharp-tongued and blunt.

Alas, even reincarnation is a kind of skill, Ning thought, glancing at her.

Those born under good fortune always had a higher starting point. Ordinary people could toil for years and still struggle to reach heights that the blessed attained with ease.

After spending a month in the sect, Ning had already gained a fairly good understanding of how wealthy disciples cultivated.

Pills accelerated their progress, while Spirit Gathering Arrays allowed them to absorb spiritual energy from spirit stones. The energy contained within spirit stones was too violent for direct absorption, but these arrays could extract and refine it into a gentler form, similar to the spiritual energy found in nature.

Although some energy was lost during conversion, the resulting increase in cultivation speed more than compensated for it.

There were also medicinal baths that strengthened the body and allowed longer cultivation sessions without excessive strain. Add to that special incense, guidance from experienced elders, and all sorts of cultivation aids, and the advantages of having wealth became painfully obvious.

Of course, none of these conveniences came cheap.

Even a basic Spirit Gathering Array cost a hundred spirit stones, and that was before accounting for its activation and operating expenses. For someone like Ning, the total might as well have been astronomical.

Listening to the murmurs around him, Wei Zhusang continued, "The most fortunate are those with top-grade spiritual roots. The sect fully nurtures them with the best pills, arrays, and incense. With proper support, they can reach Foundation Building in just five or six years."

Envy spread through the room. In a few years, most of them would probably still be struggling somewhere in mid-stage Qi Condensation, while those talented disciples would already be advancing into the next realm.

Ning sighed inwardly before brushing the thought aside. Reality had slapped him plenty of times already, and he doubted this would be the last.

With the somewhat depressing topic concluded, the group moved on to sharing news and rumors from around the sect.

This was usually the more lighthearted part of their meetings. The association gathered every fifteen days, which some members considered too frequent, but the short interval helped everyone become familiar with one another. They planned to space the meetings farther apart as their cultivation advanced and their responsibilities increased.

Attendance wasn't compulsory, either. Those who were busy could skip a session, as Su Fan frequently did.

Most of the news was ordinary, though one piece of information from the Bee Husbandry division caught everyone's attention.

Wong Zhi leaned forward. "Xiao Fan discovered a rare three-striped bee that produces tri-colored honey. The manager tried to claim it for himself, but a Foundation Building elder intervened and sided with Xiao Fan. He was even rewarded for his discovery!"

He paused before adding excitedly, "He's already reached the second stage of Qi Condensation, making him the fastest among the disciples with low-grade spiritual roots!"

"So lucky!" someone exclaimed.

Ning narrowed his eyes. Whenever Xiao Fan pulled off something like this, it became increasingly difficult to deny that the fellow had the protagonist's halo.

Alas, I really need to speed up my cultivation.

With Xiao Fan around, Ning had a feeling that the sect wouldn't remain peaceful for very long.

Even this sudden appearance of a great opportunity related to Foundation Building, shortly after Xiao Fan joined the sect, seemed suspicious.

Coincidence? I think not.

If cultivation novels had taught Ning anything, it was that protagonists had a remarkable talent for attracting trouble. Sooner or later, some enormous upheaval would come knocking, and anyone unfortunate enough to be nearby could easily get dragged into it.

If Ning wanted to avoid being swept away, he'd have to become strong enough to weather whatever came.

With that thought in mind, he continued listening until the meeting came to an end and everyone dispersed.

...

Whenever Ning went outside, he made a habit of staying alert. It was simply common sense in a cultivation world, and so far, the habit had served him well.

He'd managed to avoid random conflicts, arrogant young masters, mysterious grudges, and most other forms of trouble that seemed determined to find cultivators the moment they stepped outside.

Today, however, trouble decided to stand directly in his path.

Ning was returning from the association meeting when two large figures stepped onto the narrow stone path ahead of him. Both were broad-shouldered and thick-necked, the sort of men who had probably relied on physical intimidation.

One cracked his knuckles while the other folded his arms.

"You seem pretty rich," the taller one said. "How about lending us some money?"

"Me?" Ning blinked, momentarily speechless as an almost offensively classic cliché unfolded before his eyes.

"Yes, you." The shorter man grinned. "Don't worry. We'll safeguard your spirit stones for you."

Ning gave them a careful once-over. Their green robes marked them as peripheral disciples; their hands were heavily calloused, and the slight irregularity in their breathing suggested they still weren't accustomed to circulating qi while moving.

Newbies.

That explained a lot.

Anyone who had spent more than a few months in the sect should have known better. Extorting fellow disciples was explicitly punishable, at least when the person doing the extorting lacked enough connections to make everyone conveniently forget the rules.

Judging by these two, Ning doubted they had that sort of backing.

He clasped his hands behind his back and answered calmly, "I'm poor."

The two men stared at him.

"...What?"

"I'm poor," Ning repeated.

"Stop joking." The shorter man frowned. They'd watched Ning for a while before approaching him, and despite wearing the same green robes as every other peripheral disciple, he carried himself far too confidently to look penniless. "You clearly have money."

"I'm not joking. In fact, if you manage to find even one spare spirit stone on me, I'll sincerely thank you."

Ning spread his arms. "Go ahead. Search me."

The two exchanged suspicious glances before the taller disciple muttered, "He's bluffing."

"Then search him," his companion replied.

Ning patiently endured the indignity as they checked his sleeves, waist, boots, and even the inner lining of his robes. A short while later, both men stepped back with confused expressions.

There was nothing. Not a single spirit stone, not even a mortal coin.

The taller disciple frowned. "Where are they?"

"Where are what?"

"Your spirit stones!"

"I told you, I don't have any." Ning sighed. "If you're looking for some, I'd actually be interested in joining you."

That was completely true.

Ning had developed the remarkably effective anti-robbery strategy of having nothing worth robbing. Whenever spirit stones entered his hands, they rarely survived for long before being converted into pills or some other useful resource.

Why let perfectly good money sit around doing nothing when it could be turned into cultivation progress?

The shorter disciple looked genuinely offended. "Then why do you look like you have money?"

Ning considered the question seriously before shrugging. "Innate temperament, I suppose."

Walking around as though he had money even when his pockets were empty might be a talent in itself. Ning had developed that skill in his previous life, and it seemed to have transmigrated along with him.

Seeing that the two still seemed unwilling to accept reality, Ning decided to offer some friendly advice.

"Also, if you're planning to make a career out of collecting protection fees, you should probably read the sect rules first."

Their expressions changed slightly.

"Extorting fellow peripheral disciples is punishable, especially during your first year. If someone reports you and the Discipline Committee decides to investigate, this little business venture of yours is going to have a very short lifespan."

The shorter man's confidence visibly weakened, though his companion stubbornly frowned.

"We're not afraid."

"You should at least be informed," Ning replied. "Anyone who spends two weeks here and actually reads the rules would know this."

The two fell silent. They were certainly large and intimidating, but it was clear they weren't putting their big brains to good use.

"Besides," Ning continued, "if you're determined to rob someone, at least choose your targets properly. Look for disciples carrying storage pouches."

"Storage pouches?" the shorter one repeated.

"Yes. Even the cheapest ones cost thousands of spirit stones." Ning couldn't entirely keep the envy from his voice. A portable storage space was still very high on his list of things he desperately wanted.

"Thousands?" The taller disciple's eyes widened.

"That's a lot..." his companion muttered.

While the two contemplated this newly discovered avenue of financial ruin, Ning calmly stepped around them and continued down the path. He was fairly confident he could handle the pair if necessary, but there was no reason to start a fight over literally nothing.

He'd only made it a short distance when raised voices sounded behind him again.

Ning glanced back and saw the same two disciples blocking someone else.

Someone familiar.

Xiao Fan.

Ning stopped for a moment before immediately understanding what was about to happen.

It seemed his advice had already inspired them to seek a new victim.

They'd somehow gone from attempting to rob the safest possible target to attempting to rob the protagonist.

Ning turned around and continued walking.

It seems I've contributed to maintaining the peace and stability of the sect once again.

With his good deed for the day complete, Ning quietly departed, hiding his fame and merit.
