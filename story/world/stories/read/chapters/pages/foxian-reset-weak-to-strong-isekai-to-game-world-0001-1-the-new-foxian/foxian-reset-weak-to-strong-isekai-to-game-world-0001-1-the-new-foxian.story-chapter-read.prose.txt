The lone player gazed at their computer screen, idly watching and waiting for something interesting to happen.

It was a lazy Tuesday afternoon, and Guild Wars had started. Usually, it was 50v50, player versus player. Players went against one another in a vicious and chaotic team death match. In a way, it was like the game: Capture the Flag. Raid the enemy base, kill their members, destroy their crystal, and place the Guild’s own flag where theirs used to be.

But something was different about this team compared to all the others in the world of Keostar Online.

Ray Belmont, a lone player, sat atop the world of his castle like a falcon surveying his kingdom. The vast land of multiple fortresses stood before him like carvings in the mountain. Yet despite his being the largest among them all, there was no one to share this vast wealth with.

Ray brushed his hand through his dark hair. He shifted his monk coat, idling, waiting for some semblance of excitement to filter through his chest.

“Hm, I remember when there were so many people playing with me. Everyone is gone now…”

The lone man sat by his Guild’s crystal, a tad bored by it all. With crow-dark hair, a scar on the left side of his cheek aged from battle, and a sharp, dark-blue Monk-like uniform, he looked every bit the hardened veteran of war.

Being the last one in the Guild still playing, it wasn’t as fun as it used to be. Over time, his fellow members had all left the game they once said they loved.

But love can be fickle at times, and once it’s lost, it can be hard to gain that passion back. Ray confessed a stirring feeling that’s been in his heart for the past year.

“After this last match, I think I’ll quit too…”

For once, he put it into words.

“Hurry up, everyone!”

Celsius, the second-strongest guild in the game, broke in, charging toward the isolated base. The High Monk was too far gone, lost in thought about everything that had passed over the years. Guild Wars, one of the most sought-after challenges of Keostar Online, was a forethought, like a forgotten meal in the back of the freezer.

“This should be easy! Hurry up, everyone!”

The Celsius clan cheered, destroying Ray’s defenses as they made their way up the winding road to the throne. The High Monk was still lost in thought until a message appeared at the bottom of his screen.

In a slight daze, Ray stood up.

“Here we go.”

He sighed enthusiastically behind the screen of the game. His eyes lacked the glow that should have brought excitement to his fingertips.

“This… and this…”

Like a machine, Belmont organized his arsenal without thinking much about it. Only motions to deck out the best way to deal with the flies coming his way.

All 50 players were demolishing the pathway to the lone High Monk. It was only a matter of time before they reached him, ready to brutally murder him and destroy his guild’s crystal, earning absolute victory.

The first five fastest players of Celsius caught sight of the High Monk, three Archons of Elements, and two Shadow Reapers.

[Hurricane Kick]

With a single tap of a key, Ray Belmont executed a skill. He spun in a kick, swiftly hitting and wiping away the five high-ranked players, demolishing them with countless 999,999+ damage numbers flooding the screen.

The system couldn’t keep up as they blasted away. The game started to lag as the numbers filled the display like pop-ups on a virus-ridden website.

“What the heck is he?!”

The Guild Leader, Jayden Stiffelman, screamed in the chat.

Paladins, Avatars, Eternal Rangers, Archon of Elements... nothing was spared from Ray’s over-trained skills. Each arrived only to be instantly erased before the High Monk. Celsius was wiped out, and their respawn time would be too long to reach the crystal before the end of Guild Wars.

Standing there at the top of the world, Ray finally said the words he never wanted to speak from behind the screen.

“Alright, I’m done with this game. It’s not fun anymore.”

He admitted, accepting reality for the first time... in a long time.

Ray stretched with his character a simple taunt that used to bring a smile to his lips. But it had been ten years of Keostar Online, and all that passion had faded into normality. Ray felt as though he had seen everything. Done everything and was left with...

Nothing.

Most of the old players had left long ago, but this ghost of the past lingered around and grinded to the point that Guild Wars, one of the most sought-after PVP events, had become... boring.

Despite how much Ray wished for that old flame to return so he could be excited to wake up and play this game, there was nothing left to play anymore. But before he could click out of that screen, exiting the game for the last time...

Something caught his attention.

“Hm, there’s still one more player here?”

At the top of the screen, it showed that one more player was still in the battle area.

1/50.

So, not wanting to leave a job undone, Ray, the ever-diligent player, decided it would be best to give them a proper welcome and goodbye.

“Alright, one more kill and then I’ll turn the game off and get a job...”

He mused on the thought. Maybe the world outside of Keostar has changed. Just maybe...

Ray Belmont, along with plenty of other aliases, had been addicted to this world for the last ten years. In that time, avoiding getting a job had become his job. But since his yearning for the game was satisfied and he had effectively “won” Keostar Online, it was time to set the game down for good.

“Use this, add this, this goes first, and that should maximize my damage with this…”

Ray calculated like a supercomputer, combining the best options to maximize his damage on his next and last victim. He used up all his ATK buff items. Since there wasn’t technically a max stat in this game, these were the highest numbers he had seen in a while.

When the last player finally reached the top of his tower, he was surprised to see that it was just a lone regular Priest. No headgear, basic weapon in hand. As if she’d never seen a real guild war in her life.

She looked to be low-level, too. The innocence in their eyes didn’t stop the next attack from coming, however.

[Hurricane Crusher]

With a flash of the High Monk’s skill, Ray appeared and punched the Priest in a flash.

-2,451,486,060 damage obliterated the poor novice. Not the highest damage he had ever seen, but it was still satisfying, nonetheless.

“Hm?”

Ray’s screen shifted; the area around them changed to that of a holy realm. White pillars and golden clouds basked over the angelic world. A new map Ray had never seen before.

“What the heck?”

It all happened so suddenly that he nearly stood up from his seat. He marveled at the breathtaking sight. Something new in the oasis he had walked in for all these years.

“What a magnificent specimen you are, Ray Belmont.”

Above him, the golden clouds opened up. A stairway of golden light flowed down to his feet from above. From the magical gate, a woman with ash white hair down to her feet, a holy white robe that covered her shapely body, as she walked down from the heavenly realm above.

She was like the picture of perfectness, a goddess from mythical origins. Her beauty was unmatched by mortal means.

“You are...”

Ray swallowed as his heart pounded. A sensation that he hasn’t felt in what could have been a lifetime by this point. Her apple-red eyes gazed at her new prey. A cunning smile basking on her lips like a lion ready to eat.

She strummed her golden harp that cradled in her arms. The same color of the crown sat neatly on her head. She was the queen, no, the goddess of the heavens.

“Ray Belmont, an abuser of my world for far too long.”

She reached the bottom, leveling out with her fresh new prey. The angel strummed her harp; the gentleness of her instrument calmed Ray’s trembling heart. Each string was magnificent, unworldly, and exhilarating.

“You’re Rexia, the Goddess of Keostar.”

Ray shakily spoke.

“I am.”

She answered, and Ray’s heart started thumping out of his chest. Someone within this game, outside of other players... answered him?

“Are you bored? Do you want some excitement from my world?”

The skies opened up with holy light, and mythical spears hovered from portals above. Ray’s instincts kicked it. Like bullets, the weapons fired at the high monk were like bullets raining down. But he dodged them with ease.

“This is new...”

Ray whispered, warm labored breaths escaping his partially opened mouth. The veteran's fingers moved fast, his new target, the Goddess, before him. Channeling his strongest skills, he charged.

[Hurricane strike]

“Oh, please.”

She blocked his punch with a barrier appearing around her. All the power in Ray’s strike reverberated into a fierce counter.

“Tsk. Anti-physical.”

Ray flew back. The Goddess brought out a bright red blade from the lore and legends. It was double in size. The holy world around them fell into ruin, turning into a demonic realm. Her blade rippled with demonic spikes. The harsh red thunder sparking from the blade was infecting the game world.

The Goddess beauty rose in the air, far above Ray, with a smile mirroring Lucifer, the devil.

“Silly little fool. This is my world, my logic... So, I’m not bound by the same rules as you when I make them.”

Ray gulped...

The dryness of his throat...

“Not bound by rules?”

Those words brought new life into their soul. Before they knew it, Ray Belmont disappeared into the wave of power so brilliant...

A new sensation he wanted to chase.

“Huh?”

The lights flickered in their room. Before they could react, the game shut off suddenly, and their room went pitch black. A breath touched their ear in the darkness of the cluttered room.

“You killed my favorite Priest so badly, she vanished from the world.”

A voice groaned from the heavens, echoing into the ears of the lone player. The same voice of Rexia, the Goddess who destroyed them in seconds, but filled their dull world with color.

Confused, the player looked around as a whisper invaded their ears once again.

“Alright, you’ve abused this game far too long. How about you try to do the same thing with only one chance to live?”

The isolated player blinked and opened their eyes. The wind danced across her face as strands of blond hair washed over her vision. A new sensation of silk could be felt along her back. But that didn’t hold a candle to the strange, fluffy sensation that wagged back and forth, bouncing around on her slender backside.

“Fluffy tail?”

Her ears twitched as she looked up at the collection of trees above. As if a wave of nostalgia took hold of her, her lush green eyes were captivated by such a simple sight. Out of her thin lips, she whispered the name of this strange new place she found herself in.

“Is this really Onyx Forest?”

This was the intro stage of Keostar Online, a place where all the novices would learn the rules of the game. After the hours-long setup and downloading, players were greeted with this somber forest.

The echoes of animals and critters in the distance. Rivers running alongside the trees, and the gentle wind blowing leaves, all encapsulated the nostalgia of a new player.

A slight grin raised the corner of her lips.

“T-There’s no way this is real, right?”

The nervousness seeped out with a hint of eagerness.

As her tail swished back and forth, she rubbed the collar of her short purple and white dress. Cautiously, she looked around at the three-dimensional world and only then decided to touch her face. It was smooth and soft, very feminine. From there, she felt her silky hair and the pointy, fluffy things on top. Pointy ears that felt nice to pat.

In slight irritation, the realization finally came.

“I’m a Foxian, aren’t I?!”

She looked down at her body, confirming that she was the fox race of Keostar Online. With that fluffy tail, there was no mistake. Basically, a human with large fox ears and big tail...

“Ahhhh!”

Her feminine voice rang out in the forest air, and a message box appeared above her head before fading away a few seconds later.

“I must be going insane! There’s no way…”

Again, the same thing happened. Her excitement only grew as she moved about in a circle, feeling the crunch of grass under her shoes.

“Why are you yelling?”

The Goddess, Rexia, appeared above her. Holding a golden harp, the Goddess elegantly hovered over the Foxian. A Goddess who swore to protect this world and bless those who entered with the power to control or destroy the very world they were now a part of.

“W-What’s going on?”

She demanded answers. Probably due to a mixture of elation and anxiousness, her tail wouldn’t stop moving.

“I’ve watched you for ten years exploit everything in the world of Keostar! Do you know how annoying it is seeing someone so proud win at this game?”

The Goddess pointed her tongue out at the Foxian.

“It really pissed me off!”

The fox crossed her arms and slanted her eyes, feeling slighted by the Goddess.

“Proud? I’m not proud!”

The Foxian stated firmly.

“I’m just a good player. It’s a fact.”

With one hand on her hip, she defended herself.

“Not good enough to kill me, though. How fast I wiped out your Ray Belmont character. Such child's play. I expected more~.”

The fox bit her lip. How fast that all happened, like a fever dream she wished to go back to and try again. But it was too late. The Goddess floated around her, the victor of that sudden but intoxicating match.

“Then show me how good a player you are! Here are the new rules, Foxian...”

Rexia smiled devilishly, an expression the veteran player had never seen in any promotional videos, let alone the game world itself. The Rexia she "knew" was humble, loving, caring... not this bratty, devilish imp in front of her.

Too many new sides to Keostar Online in less than an hour...

“If you die, you will die permanently. There are no redoes like the game allows for all other players. You don't get my blessings.”

The Goddess giggled as she strummed her harp gently.

“And in order to escape this world, you’ll have to find where I reside in the heavenly realm and beat me!”

Rexia declared, laughing, her chest bouncing with vigor. She kicked her bare feet, jovially in the air as she floated around. Annoyed, the Foxian touched her ears again. They were super fluffy, but that wasn’t the problem here.

“Wait, I’m a Foxian! This is the worst race in the game!”

She grabbed her tail, showing the Goddess before her. But Rexia waved off her concerns.

“What of it, Fox?”

Rexia snickered, her red eyes glowing devilishly like she was playing a sick joke on the veteran.

Every guidebook for Keostar Online warned against playing the Foxian race. They had inherently low stats starting off. Although they made up for it by gaining more skill points, the problem was that their skills weren’t very good in the long run. Nothing about them was considered great in the grand scheme of the game.

And this Foxian knew this like the back of her tail!

“You know this race is garbage despite how cute they are!”

The Foxian protested.

“At least let me choose what race I want to be, Rexia!”

But Rexia crossed her arms and lifted her nose proudly.

“Figure it out! Don’t die to the slimes in the tutorial stage! I want to watch you struggle for your life!”

In a bratty, know-it-all tone, the Goddess barked back.

“Suffer for me, Kae Loredom!”

The Goddess kicked her feet about and vanished in the wind. Her laughter lingered longer than she did, sending a chill down the Foxian’s back. Kae rubbed the back of her head, feeling that silky blonde hair of hers.

“A Foxian, huh? I never played this race, so at least it will be a different experience...”

Her tail wagged back and forth at the thought of the new horizon before her. The dark Onyx Forest looked inviting as if she were a brand new player trying this game world for the first time...

"Here I go."
