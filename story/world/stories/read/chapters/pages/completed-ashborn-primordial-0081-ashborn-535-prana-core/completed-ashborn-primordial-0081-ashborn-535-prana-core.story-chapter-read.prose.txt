The airship moved slower than it otherwise would have thanks to the navigator being extra careful to chart a conservative course across terrain that no map—Imperium or newer—had ever recorded.

The last thing they wanted, after all, was to run headlong into some mountain obscured by clouds.

The slower pace suited its passengers just fine, as Balagra, Malik, and Cirayus were huddled together discussing the vast amount of work that had been heaped into their laps, while Greesha stood by the railing, gazing down at the strange new lands below.

Vir didn’t join them. Instead, he sat cross-legged on the open upper deck with his back against the forward mast and his eyes closed, Siya sitting quietly beside him.

He wasn’t sleeping, however. How he wished he could afford such luxury.

No, he spent his time deep inside his Crown Chakra mindscape, standing in a space that was once again empty and devoid of life, stripped of the machine’s augmentation. Though now, it showed one unified realm rather than the three of before.

The prana core sat at the center of it all, and what it was doing made no sense to Vir.

He’d assumed it was simply trying to expand outward, pressing against the boundaries of his soul. That was manageable, if painful. He could cycle his prana to keep the pressure from building past a critical threshold, discharging it in the form of Ultimate Arts when necessary and repairing any damage to his soul via the Heart Chakra.

But that wasn’t what was happening.

The core was growing. Generating new prana that seemed to appear out of thin air.

Worse, it was multiplying at a rate that ought to have been impossible. It defied everything Vir knew about the mechanics of prana—indeed, about the rules upon which the whole world functioned.

Prana could be absorbed from the environment or converted into energy. It did not spontaneously multiply like some virus.

And yet, as Vir cycled as rapidly as he could, pushing the excess through every minor Chakra point in his body and back through his core, he realized it wasn’t even close sufficient.

He activated Balancer of Scales, focusing it on a point directly in front of him. Not for any useful purpose, but simply to bleed off excess prana. The Ultimate Arts were, among other things, ravenous for prana.

Normally a crippling limitation for demons, that aspect might very well be his salvation now.

The pressure eased, but it was a momentary thing. Within minutes, it once again overwhelmed Balancer of Scales and kept climbing.

At a loss, Vir opened his eyes to find Siya sitting opposite him, staring into his chest.

“It is getting worse, isn’t it?” she said.

“It’s growing, somehow,” Vir said, figuring there was no point hiding the truth from her eyes. “The prana inside me is generating new prana from what appears to be nothing at all.”

Siya reached out and pressed her hand against his chest, closing her eyes.

“You need a sink,” she said a moment later. “Something to receive and dissipate prana continuously. Do you have your Imperium Chakrams?”

Vir reached to his back, retrieving two of the discs.

“As I suspected,” Siya said, inspecting them. “These were built to hold enormous quantities of prana. Charge them now.”

Vir obliged, releasing the flow from the core and directing it into both Chakrams simultaneously. The pressure in his chest eased considerable, but then the core replaced what he’d transferred in moments, and kept generating, the pressure climbing again.

“It helps,” Vir said. “But it’s not enough.”

Siya looked at the Chakrams, then to the open sky around them.

“You need to purge it, or your body will rupture,” she said.

“I know.” He looked at the deck around him and the Asura milling around. “But not here.”

He found Cirayus near the helm with Balagra, studying the navigation charts they’d created over the past several years, though Vir suspected they would do them little good now.

As much as he had attempted to maintain the old geography of the realms, the border had to be altered by virtue of the Ash Boundary’s absence. The new unified realm wasn’t quite the same as the one that had existed during Janak’s time.

Which was why creating new maps was of paramount importance.

Vir kept his report brief, stating that he wished to see the new realm for himself and that he would catch up later.

The last thing he needed was Cirayus and the others panicking over their leader’s health. Not when Vir fully intended to find a workable solution.

“I’d send Asuras,” Cirayus said, stroking his beard. “But we both know they couldn’t keep up. Just please stay out of trouble?”

“Have you forgotten, Cirayus?” Vir said with a smile. “The new realm has no Ash Beasts. And even if it did, precious few could stand against me as I am now.”

Especially with unlimited prana at my disposal, Vir mused. Who would have thought the dream of every mejai in history would prove to be such a terrible curse?

He went back to the railing to find Siya standing on her tiptoes, trying and failing to peer over it.

“You’re not coming,” Vir said, understanding her intent in an instant.

“Yes, I am!” she shot back.

Vir was about to dismiss her statement as childish petulance, but held his tongue.

“Can… you truly help?”

She nodded. “I can. So you need to take me along.”

With a sigh, and despite every instinct in his body telling him it was a bad idea, Vir scooped her up and jumped before Greesha could reprimand him.

Balancer of the Scales slowed their descent, and knowing what to expect this time, Siya was silent the whole way. As they fell, Vir looked out at the land below. At what the Ashen Realm had been until this morning.

It looked like everywhere else now, though it was one thing to see it from within Janak’s machine, and another entirely to fly through it himself.

He still couldn’t believe it was all real. That they’d accomplished the impossible, thwarting their seemingly all-powerful enemy.

Scrubland stretched in every direction, and not a soul was in sight. No Ash falling from a lightning-scorched sky. No Ash Tears marring reality.

Just empty land and prana beasts roaming under a late afternoon sun.

Vir set Siya down when they landed, pointing to a rocky outcropping fifty meters away. “I want you to hide back there, alright? I’ll attempt to direct the bulk of the discharge away, but I don’t want you in any danger.”

He’d fully expected an argument, but instead, Siya just nodded, walking to it without a single comment before peeking over the edge.

Just barely suppressing a contented smile, Vir turned to face the open expanse and unleashed his prana.

Even directed outward, even with the Balancer of the Scales, Clarity, and Aspect of the Demon God sapping as much prana as was possible, the torrent of energy that left his body was unlike anything he had ever seen.

The prana was so dense it became opaque, rushing from his body with such force that it formed a beam. A beam of magic so dense it was nearly solid.

It fired off into the distance, continuing unabated until it slammed into a nearby hill.

Vir expected that would be the end of it.

How wrong he was.

The slightest twitch of his arm moved the beam across the hill, splitting the rock and decapitating the mound before finally dispersing into the air beyond.

The roiling mass of prana proved far greater than anything even Vir’s substantially fortified blood could handle, and by the time it finished, his right arm was nothing more than a bloodied stump. The near-invulnerability Aspect of the Demon God was supposed to give him had been completely overwhelmed by just a couple of seconds of sheer raw power.

Coughing up blood, Vir pushed through pain on a level that would have had most mortals fainting.

Despite the agony, he forced his legs to turn, searching for Siya.

She was still there, and Prana Vision assured him she’d emerged unscathed. He’d contained the blast well enough, it would seem.

After that, it was a simple matter of flopping onto the ground and enduring the pain until Yuma’s Embrace, feeding off the infinite energy, regrew his limb before his eyes in mere seconds. His armor reformed an instant later, and it was as though his injury had never been.

He sat up and took a deep breath. Siya had been correct. The pressure in his chest had abated significantly.

“I’ll need to do this every hour,” he said. “More, if the prana continues to increase as it has.”

Siya emerged from behind the rock, frowning in thought. She stood in front of him in silence for a moment, her eyes on the distant hill.

“I have no explanation for why it’s multiplying,” she said. “The knowledge bestowed upon me through the Imperium’s Vidya network gives me nothing comparable. Whatever you’re carrying, it is self-sustaining in a way that shouldn’t be possible.” She paused. “But… I still believe I can help.”

Before Vir could get a word in, she plopped herself down, crossing her legs in the dirt and closing her eyes before going still.

This time, Vir did panic, as the prana in her body suddenly shifted and all her bodily functions slowed.

Not for the first time, he forced himself to remember that she was not a mere child, no matter how similar her mannerisms might be.

Forcing the panic down, Vir sat across from her, monitoring her vitals. Though her body might have entered something resembling a hibernation state, her mind lit with prana like the sun.

And yet, nothing appeared to happen for almost a full minute.

Then, somehow, prana began to leave her body and coalesce in front of her. Not coursing through an inscription the way a mejai or a Warrior would, but seemingly arranging itself into a structure of extraordinary intricacy all on its own.

Inscriptions wove themselves by the hundreds before shrinking down and latching onto yet more inscriptions, each layer building upon the last.

The structure then finally collapsed and became solid, forming a pure white outer shell.

When she was done, a small sphere sat in the dirt before her, roughly the size of a large marble, its surface dull grey and unremarkable. It resembled any C-grade orb, though Vir knew it was anything but.

Soaked in sweat from the effort, Siya finally opened her eyes.

“I… forgot how exhausting it was to do that,” she muttered, swaying unsteadily as she reached up to wipe her forehead.

Vir was already Blinking, catching her before she reached the ground. Her eyes slid shut, and her breaths were slow and even.

Simply asleep. Exhausted after the miracle she had just performed.

Vir held her sleeping form against his chest and reached for the orb she created.

The instant he did, prana gushed out of him at a rate that would have killed any other demon.

And somehow, against all explanation, it pulled against the core directly, bypassing his blood and the prana circulating through his body.

Which meant his body suffered none of the prana poisoning effects it had earlier.

Except this was no mere prana battery. Though impressive in its capacity, Saunak had created batteries in the past.

No, the impossibly intricate inscriptions on Siya’s orb were not there simply to store, but to diffuse the prana outward into the surrounding air in a continuous stream clearly visible through Prana Vision.

The pressure in his chest dropped to a level he hadn’t felt since before he entered the machine… and to his shock, it stabilized there. The orb dispersed new prana at a rate roughly equal to how quickly his core generated it.

Vir tried to examine the inscriptions inside the sphere before realizing he couldn’t even see most of them with how tiny they were. Of those he could see, he understood maybe a tenth. The rest operated on principles he had never encountered. Nor, he suspected, had any living being. Saunak included.

And Siya designed this from nothing in under two minutes.

Vir looked down at her sleeping face. He’d known she was a being from the Age of Gods. He’d known she was Janak’s daughter, but he saw her in an entirely new light now.

He gently moved the sleeping Siya into a comfortable position against his chest and tucked the orb into his armor where it could press directly against him.

Then he began bounding east toward Samar Patag with a clear mind. Which, if not free, was protected against the prana core’s torment, at least for the moment.
