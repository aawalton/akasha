The sky was overcast today. Gray clouds blotted out the sun. Not the kind that bore rain—the kind that was dark, and broody, and carried only the threat of rain without every following through. They created tension in the sky, like the world itself was on the verge of erupting.

Gary felt that in his bones. He awoke just before dawn, in his bed at the cabin, with Cog in the opposite bed. The first thing he did was gaze out the window, knowing that today might be the last time he ever saw the sun. The clouds felt like a slap of fate. Not even the sky would give him mercy today. The last time seeing the sun had already passed without him knowing.

“Good morning,” Cog said hesitantly.

Gary nodded. He grabbed his spear, comforted by its familiar weight. “Let’s go,” he replied.

Cog followed him out of the cabin. More men exited the surrounding ones, giving Gary a wide berth. Their eyes held both respect and pity. A gladiator walking the world of the living.

Gary gave his bed a final look, then shut the door and turned to the path. Alicia waited for them outside the cabin area. The three of them ran down the mountain, over the rocky area, then leaped down the cliff. The freefall filled Gary with nostalgia. This, too, might be experienced for the last time.

By the time they reached the city, Gary had become aware that everything he passed was left behind forever. His world was closing. He’d never see the clan again, never see the mountain, or the forest, or the city gate, or the streets of hawkers and crowds. Never see a glyan-drawn carriage almost run over pedestrians.

They entered the tunnel leading to the arena’s spectator stands, and the natural light disappeared. Gary stopped. He looked back, at the line where the dim sunlight was replaced by darkness, and the colorful world beyond. If he lost, he’d never see the outside of this building ever again. His world had the width of a building. Everything else was gone forever.

If Gary hadn’t tried his absolute hardest so far, he’d be riddled with regret. As it was, he felt only sadness. His chances of victory weren’t great. The bright world bade him goodbye.

“Gary?” Alicia said softly.

“It is done,” he replied.

“What’s done?”

“Everything else.”

He turned and led them deeper, his straight back looming large. The din reached them first. Light filled the end of the tunnel. Gary walked into it and emerged in a bright arena, completely packed, so much that even walking to his seat would be trouble. The stairs were full of people. He had no idea how they entered. Even the VIP seats were packed today.

“Hey, it’s the Baldinator!” someone said, and the crowd turned to Gary.

“Baldinator!”

“Take his hair off!”

“We believe in you!”

Gary tried to smile at them. He really tried. In the end, he simply raised a hand, which was enough to procure another round of cheers.

“And the Baldinator enters the arena!” Rollo Frollo announced from his position above the bracket board. A spotlight focused on Gary, guiding every eye. Behind him, Cog and Alicia recoiled, but Gary kept his head up and faced the crowd.

“Thank you for coming, everyone. Today, I will win,” he said.

The crowd erupted. “What a bold statement from the underdog!” Rollo Frollo yelled. “Isn’t this exciting? Isn’t this electric? One of two great men will lay down their lives today, and it will happen right in front of our eyes!”

Gary felt separated from the deafening cheers. Like he lived in a different world. How could they not see his fear? How could they laugh at someone else’s death? Contempt rose hard in his soul, aimed at everyone and everything around him, at the thousands of ignorant people cheering. They disgusted him. The dark fire in his heart burned, growing higher, potent, violent.

I hate them, he realized, walking to his seat. And they deserve it.

“And here comes the other dueler! Sui Duang, the clan’s number two junior disciple, has just entered the arena! Neither chickened out! They’ll duke it out, a duel to the death in the middle of our crowd!”

Gary locked eyes with the man who’d just walked in. The rest of the world fell away. Only the two of them existed.

For the first time, Gary saw emotion in Sui Duang’s gaze. There was fear, anxiety, regret. Of course, they were overshadowed by arrogance and sickening confidence, but Gary felt relief at realizing his opponent was human, too. He had a soul.

A soul he could break.

The gazes of the two gladiators crossed, exchanging their fears and hopes. Then, Gary broke away, calmly reaching his spot and sitting down. The spear remained in his hand. He was never going to let go again.

“Don’t fret, Gary! You’ll win for sure!” Goldie said. She tried to sound cheerful but couldn’t hide her worry.

“Thank you, Goldie,” Gary replied, petting her head through his sleeve.

“Do you want me to tell Sui Duang what you did to his mother? That will surely break his morale!”

“I’m fine, Goldie, but thanks for the thought.”

Even more people flitted in, until the stairs were intraversible. Good luck to anyone wanting to use the bathroom. Gary searched for Asim and Chloe in the crowd but didn’t find them. It was hard to hide his sadness. Cog and Alicia stayed by his side, though, as did Caramel, Pop Ear, and Helen. Most other disciples avoided his gaze.

A massive crowd sat around Sui Duang, chatting like he wasn’t going to die today. Gary hated them, too.

Even the VIP seats were filled. There was Vice Head Colorful, Elder Sui, Elder Rat, the Branch Head, as well as Elders and powerful figures from various clans in the city. Very few seats were empty. One of them was the throne reserved for the Shoresmaster.

“Welcome, everyone, to the Heaven Reaching tournament’s semi-finals!” Rollo Frollo announced. The crowd cheered. “We have illustrious figures in the audience today! This is the fifth tournament I have the honor to present, yet I’ve never seen such VIP attendance, even in the finals! This year is something special!”

He made a theatrical bow towards the VIP seats. People laughed. Some bowed as well.

“We have two very special matches today,” Rollo Frollo continued. “One of them needs no introduction. For the first semi-final, I present to you, the Battle of the Geniuses! Lin Xin entered the clan only fourth months ago, breezing through the notoriously challenging clan selection. Her low heaven talent is the greatest we’ve seen in years, her comprehension is nothing to scoff at, and she wields the legendary spear, Snow White!”

Lin Xin leapt on stage under thunderous clapping. She raised her white spear—cheers erupted.

“Lin Xin! Lin Xin! Lin Xin!”

“With beauty like that, it’s no wonder the audience goes crazy!” Rollo Frollo said. “However, today might be the day we see the ice queen toppled! Because standing in her way is the number one disciple: the invincible, outstanding Feng Shen!”

Even fiercer cheers thundered through the arena as Feng Shen lightly stepped on stage. The number one fluttered on his back, embroidered in golden silk over cyan robes. His ponytail fluttered over it. Feng Shen cupped his hands, and the crowd screamed his name.

“Feng Shen! Feng Shen!”

Rollo Frollo’s voice rose over the crowd’s. “The two most talented disciples clash today, but only one can advance to the finals! Will it be the invincible Feng Shen, or the beautiful Lin Xin? Let’s find out! Disciples—fight!”

As the cheers reached a crescendo, Lin Xin brandished her spear. A ring of ice spread on the sand around her. The air turned chilly. Below her elegant brows, fierce eyes watched Feng Shen. She held her ground.

“Lin Xin is challenging Feng Shen! She’s inviting him to attack!” Rollo Frollo said.

Feng Shen drew his spear. It was simple, white with a black stripe coiled around its length. This certainly didn’t seem like a top-quality spear, but for Feng Shen to be wielding it, it had to be.

“Why isn’t Lin Xin attacking? She always attacked before,” Cog said.

“Her aura is different today,” Gary said.

“Really?”

“Either she never went all-out before, or she just learned a new technique. In any case, she’s not underestimating Feng Shen. She believes a defensive battle is her best shot at victory.”

“But she can’t win,” Alicia said.

“She thinks she can,” Gary said.

Feng Shen charged. He didn’t use any aura techniques, but his form was impeccable. He reached her almost instantly. His spear performed a basic thrust—yet, when Lin Xin met it, her ice was shattered, and she was blown back. Feng Shen had already retrieved his spear and was ready for the next attack.

“There it is! Feng Shen’s greatest strength—his rock-solid fundamentals! As the number one disciple, not only does he possess amazing comprehension and high talent, but he also works harder than anyone. No wonder he’s at the top!” Rollo Frollo yelled while the battle below unfolded.

Feng Shen tore into Lin Xin. Her spearplay was excellent, her aura tightly controlled, and she used her ice vibration efficiently. Her defense seemed like a steel wall. Yet, when Feng Shen reached that wall, he made it seem full of holes. His spear struck hard and fast. The moves bled into each other, anticipating her own, cracking her ice and slowly pushing her back.

“What vibration is he using?” Cog asked, craning his neck to see better.

“I think it’s precision, or a pure vibration of the spear!” Caramel explained.

“Fools. He’s definitely using that black-and-white vibration, we just can’t see it,” Pop Ear exclaimed.

Gary shook his head. “Those are all wrong.”

“Wrong?” Alicia asked.

“Right now, he isn’t using any vibrations at all. That’s pure spearplay,” Gary said.

“No way!” everyone exclaimed at once.

“He can’t fight without a vibration!” Caramel insisted.

“And yet, he’s doing it. Look closer. He possesses excellent technique and high stats. His aura control is almost perfect. You can’t sense a hint of his aura before he strikes, and the moment he does, he releases a burst precisely as hard and as long as he needs it. Lin Xin is good, but not that good. He’s a level above her in everything. That’s why he can push her back without even using his vibration.”

His voice hadn’t been low. Silence fell among the nearby disciples.

“The Lin Xin who defeated everyone…is treated like that?” someone muttered.

“What kind of monster is Feng Shen…” another said.

Rollo Frollo was announcing a similar thing as Gary to the whole stadium.

“He isn’t even using a vibration! This is truly the number one disciple. How can Lin Xin turn this around?”

Feng Shen hounded Lin Xin on the sands below. Besides not using his vibration, he wasn’t holding back at all. His strikes came hard and fast, keeping her off balance. She was fully on the defensive now. She held on, but the longer this went on, the farther she was pushed from the arena center.

“She isn’t using any techniques out of pride, but if this goes on, she’ll just lose!” Rollo Frollo said.

Lin Xin must have arrived at the same conclusion. Her eyes held rage. “Hail Storm!” she yelled, jumping back and unleashing a series of rapid thrusts. Where her spear cut the air, it froze, sending forth a slash of frozen aura. Feng Shen couldn’t reach her at this distance, but she could.

His spear blurred. He shot every blade out of the sky, smashing them to the sand or shattering them outright.

“Snowstorm!” Lin Xin yelled. A layer of ice aura was pushed out of her skin, forming a storm around her,  which she pushed with her spear towards Feng Shen. It grew as it traveled. Feng Shen tried to jump away but had to stop short. The ice blades he’d blocked before may have shattered, but the aura inside them hadn’t dissipated. It filled his surroundings with little clouds of frost. Moving around them would slow him down—breaking them would do the same. The storm still hurtled at him.

“Lin Xin was paying attention! She takes a page out of Snake Boy’s book and fixes the field with her element, cornering Feng Shen! Is he in trouble?”

Feng Shen’s eyes glowed. He span his spear over his head. A black and white ring spread around him, seeming like tentacles of alternating colors, annihilating the pockets of frost and delaying the storm itself. As it entered the vicinity of the spinning tendrils, the storm grew smaller, its energy rapidly consumed.

“There it is! The top disciple’s famous vibration, Yin Yang! That’s a high-grade concept which even Core Formation cultivators struggle to grasp—but he got it at the middle Birth of Aura! It’s what originally made him number one! Right now, who knows what heights he’s reached!”

The crowd cheered as the rest of Lin Xin’s attack dissipated. Rather than upset, she seemed happy.

“I made you use your vibration,” she said.

“You’re an excellent cultivator,” he replied. His spear bit forth—a rapid thrust seemingly too far to reach her. The world was sliced apart, one half black, the other white. The halves converged. Lin Xin was sent flying back, but she slid to her feet. Her spear was held in a blocking position, and a point in its shaft smoked.

“The Convergent Strike! It was blocked for the first time this tournament! Go go, Lin Xin!” Rollo Frollo exclaimed.

“Go go, Lin Xin!” the crowd chanted.

Feng Shen pursed her through the sands. His spear kept thrusting. The world continuously turned black and white, then converged on her, then resplit into colors. Lin Xin was tossed around like a ragdoll. She somehow blocked everything, but how long could she keep this up?

Gary kept his eyes peeled. This was the strike he’d failed to see through before, but his awareness and aura control were much higher now, and the strike was performed many times in succession. It lasted but an instant. It felt like he missed it, always blinking at the wrong time, but he knew that was an illusion caused by Feng Shen’s vibration. Gary stopped blinking altogether. He looked at the strike. He really looked, deep and hard, focusing all his senses on that point of seemingly instant convergence.

And finally, he saw it. “Woah,” he said, leaning back.

Feng Shen’s spear shot an aura thrust that somehow split the world in two. It moved some property of the ambient aura from one side to the other, making one side white and the other black. One empty, the other full. When the thrust reached its target, it stopped pushing that mysterious property, which instantly sprang back to its default state. A stream was formed. Gary was unsure whether the property moved from black to white or the opposite, but whatever the case, that stream of moving aura was razor-thin and cut like a knife. The thrust’s movement pushed the stream in its direction, forcing that razor-sharp edge against whatever it had hit. The stream erupted outwards. It lasted but an instant, but the destructive power it held was air-splitting.

“It’s not just a thrust,” he muttered. “The spear thrusts, but the vibration slashes! It’s an instant, double attack!”

“What? You can see it? What is it?” Cog asked, but Gary ignored him. He focused fully on the fight.

Now that he knew how the attack worked, he could see it. Every thrust produced a slash, cutting down on Lin Xin’s spear with terrible force. That’s why it smoked. A lower-quality weapon might have been slashed in half already. Lin Xin was blown back because she wasn’t just facing Feng Shen’s brute strength, but also the strength of the world itself!

And yet, against this amazing attack, Lin Xin resisted. She was thrown around, and her robes were torn in places, but she remained uninjured. Her gaze was focused—as if waiting for something.

Feng Shen jumped high, unleashing a barrage at her. The world split and converged with every strike. His thrusts created slashes, and his slashes also created slashes. Every hit struck double. Lin Xin was nailed into the sand, which flew around her wildly. The grains were sliced in half. The stone was sundered beneath her feet. Anything her aura didn’t protect turned into cubes, which were sliced into smaller cubes. Her skin was covered in ice now, but through it, thin red lines were carved into her arms. Her torn robes revealed slender legs and tender skin.

Still, she held.

“What are we watching? Is this really a fight between Birth of Aura cultivators!?” Rollo Frollo yelled.

“He’s used that move so many times, there’s no way he’s not tired,” Gary said.

Finally, Feng Shen raised his spear high. The world split into black and white and converged on the tip of his spear, then again, then again. A sphere of overlapping black and white hovered right there. Finally, he thrust it down, straight towards Lin Xin. The world went still. Only that ball moved.

“Final Convergence!” Feng Shen yelled, announcing his move for the first time.

However, the time he’d taken to charge it had allowed Lin Xin to regroup. Facing the coming attack, she didn’t shy away. Gary suspected it was somehow undodgeable despite its seemingly slow speed. Instead, she drew her spear in a thrusting position. “Nine-Layered Ice!” she cried out.

All the ice aura she’s scattered around the arena flew at her, converging on her spear. It was encased in ice over and over. Nine layers covered her spear. She thrust it. The ice flew forth while the spear remained in her hand, a majestic, white projectile that was both aura and physical. It headed straight for Feng Shen’s ball. They clashed.

The world became a kaleidoscope. White and black rays flew everywhere. The arena’s protective walls activated, containing all harmful aura, but Feng Shen and Lin Xin were in the middle of it. The ice spear resisted. It shed its layers one by one, each holding massive power. To a weaker cultivator, each layer could be its own finisher.

Ball and ice both unraveled until the ice spear was gone. Feng Shen’s ball resumed its movement, falling towards the exhausted Lin Xin. It exploded before it reached her—a black and white calamity that sundered the air itself. Sand flew higher than the stands, contained by the energy walls, and the blast was deafening and blinding.

Finally, the sand rested. Sound and light disappeared. A calm arena was revealed, with Lin Xin standing in the center of a ten-foot-wide circle that had no sand, only stone. Feng Shen stood opposite her. The two stared at each other for what seemed like a whole minute.

Lin Xin cupped her hands. “Thank you for your guidance,” she said.

Feng Shen smiled. “That was a great battle. I also got some insights. Thank you,” he replied.

“And the winner is Feng Shen!” Rollo Frollo announced, and the so-far numb audience exploded into wild cheers. People hollered their names. The arena shook by the stomping and yelling—maybe the entire Empyrean Steps did.

Gary realized he’d been holding his breath and sucked in greedy mouthfuls of air. “That was a great battle…” he said. The joy was immediately overshadowed by the realization that he was next. Momentarily, he was surprised by the worry of presenting an inferior spectacle, then quietly chuckled to himself.

“That was a great battle! Everyone, let’s clap for our two young geniuses! Regardless of who won and lost, I’m sure that both of them will reach great heights, and their names will echo in the Cosmic Tower for centuries to come!” Rollo Frollo announced. Feng Shen and Lin Xin returned to their seats amid thunderous clapping. Four deacons jumped on stage and used their aura to level the sand.

“Was that a lot for your heart? Because there’s more!” Rollo Frollo yelled. “Stand up, ladies and gentlemen, and let’s pay these next two duelists the respect they deserve. Their lives are on the line. We’re about to witness…a Duel of Honor!”
