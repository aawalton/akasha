For eliminating enemies while using your talent, you have gained one free point!

Reid sighed, irritated. He was glad he’d gained another point, of course, but every point felt… pointless.

“What am I doing here?” he thought for what must’ve been the thousandth time.

It was everything he could do not to just leave in the night. He’d almost done so more than once. If, for one minute he didn’t think he was actually doing good down here, he would’ve left without a second thought.

While they were still retaking the land lost by Neogon, he could believe it. He was actively preventing more Rift breaks, and keeping Mera and his Mom and Dad safe. Now that the Rift was secure, though, the two-year draft terms were beginning to itch.

“Oy, Hunter! How many is that now?” asked Veckit.

“Too damn many, Veck,” he replied as the man approached carrying a mug of something hot. Steam was coming off it and Reid’s mouth watered at the smell.

“Here ya go,” the man said, handing him the hot cup.

Reid accepted gratefully, feeling guilty even as he did. He knew how much food the army had taken from his hometown. He was eating while they were tightening their belts.

He’d gained well over sixty points since joining, and had earned quite a few naturally as well. Most of them he’d put into his Archery skill, but he hadn’t completely ignored the advice of the trainers. A healthy amount added to the core three had felt only prudent.

He tipped the bowl into his mouth and savored the food. It wasn’t anything great, but it was hot. He ate slowly, careful not to spill even a drop. There wouldn’t be more till morning.

Lowering the bowl, he grabbed his bow again as the surface of the rift began to ripple. A war troll wandered through, looking enraged. It was just about to start running when an arrow pierced its skull.

“Still don’t know how the hell you do that,” Veckit said in awe. “Damn arrows bounce right off when I shoot ‘em. That or they get stuck in the skin.”

Reid shrugged nonchalantly. “I’ve been hunting my entire life. Once the Archery skill gets high enough, your arrows can pierce straight through solid stone.”

‘That and I have a bow that could turn a rank amateur into a savant,’ he thought. He’d never say it aloud. Mera had told him her talent was Elite. She was a bad liar, but when she’d first told him, he’d been surprisingly impressed. Anyone who hadn’t known her their whole damn life might’ve believed her.

Almost anyone.

Masterful, or — more likely — Legendary.

With her father's word that she was safe, Reid had finally felt content to serve out his time in the military and make sure this damn rift stayed close. That had felt like real progress. Working to protect his family, his town, and the girl he hoped might marry him one day.

How the hell Mera’s mom had teleported away with her, he’d never guess… but then the woman had always been a bit of a mystery. He’d mostly put it out of his mind, content with the knowledge that she was alright enough to send letters to the front.

Keeping the rift closed had made him quickly realize why it had been called the Everchore. Mera would've fucking hated it here, so far from her lab, and her experiments. It was for the best that she'd managed to return home. After all, how much trouble could she possibly get into so far away from the front lines?

Telling himself that helped. It made this tedious monotony a little easier, knowing that every troll he killed was one less that might ever have a chance to attack her.

Another sip of soup. Another dead Troll. Sometimes he forgot to even use his talent. It was so unnecessary that it barely mattered.

“Gods gifted you at birth, they did,” Veckit said again.

Veckit was a bit of a kiss-ass. Everyone liked him, but hardly anyone respected him. Still, he brought hot soup, and he’d fought just as fiercely as Reid had when they were taking back the rift front in truth.

Their numbers were still thin. Currently, they were manning a small outpost about thirteen miles from the Eastern War Camp. They had about fifty soldiers. At normal times, this post would’ve had five times that. The entire rift front was staffed that way though, and they couldn’t complain.

“If you think this was the gift they gave me, you should see the girl I have back home,” Reid joked.

Veckit grinned, and Reid wished he hadn’t brought her up. The men here just wanted to know about how she looked. Veckit proceeded to surprise him, though.

“Yeah? I got a girl too, but… well. Was sort of hoping to find her down here. She was with Neogon’s Army. Joined before all this shit, while I stayed home. I was never the military sort.”

Reid blinked and turned back to the shorter man. “Really?”

“Well, don’t look so damn surprised,” he said. “She… well. We were never what you’d call traditional. She was… tougher than me. Sounds silly, don’t it?”

He thought back to when they’d really pushed to take back the Eastern War Camp just a few short weeks ago. The man beside him hadn’t been the most deft with a weapon in hand, but then again, neither had he. He was plenty strong, and his axe had found itself buried in more than one troll skull that day. He was a simpering fop outside of the battlefield, but on it? Reid wouldn’t want to be on the other side of him.

A walking pile of contradictions.

“You seemed plenty tough to me when we took back the Eastern Camp,” Reid said.

The man grinned weakly. “Yeah. Can’t wait to show her… if… well. If she–!”

“She’s alright,” Reid insisted, putting a hand on his shoulder. “I’m sure of it.”

“Yeah. Yeah, Neogon’s still holdin’ off the fuckers in the North. She’s fine,” he said.

They lapsed into a silence that might’ve been awkward once, but had begun to feel natural. Little reassurances that none of them could verify were commonplace here.

When he thought about it from Veckit's perspective, Reid felt ecstatic that Mera wasn’t here. Sure, life might be tough at home now, but she wasn’t near any armies, trolls, or gods forbid, a kerlunka. At least this way, he knew where she was. Veckit didn't have that comfort at all.

Another ripple. It was a goblin this time. Another headshot. He remembered to use his talent this time. It was almost like shooting fish in a barrel.

In the same way one might look at the sky and find objects in the clouds, he picked out a word in the line of corpses before the rift. He shuddered and shook himself, willing the illusion to go away.

He might’ve been out here a bit too long.

He wasn’t alone on his watch. There were about fifteen little towers built at this particular outpost, each manned by archers or mages, but he HAD earned enough trust to be left alone in his tower if he felt like it.

Most of the soldiers in the towers to either side didn’t even bother lifting their bows anymore. He killed the monsters that spawned before they got the chance, most of the time.

There were a lot of Hunters in the army. They were all called by their first name. Reid was the only one they simply called “Hunter.” None of them had ever seen him miss.

He’d almost forgotten what it felt like to miss a target.

“Gods, damn them! Damn them! Damn you all!” Came a sudden scream that pierced the frosty morning air.

Oddly, it came from above.

Reid pivoted his neck just in time to catch sight of a woman dressed in green. The hairs on the back of his neck rose. He drew back an arrow but didn’t bother to release. The woman was too high even for his skill. A small speck in the sky, shouting with words that were louder than they should be.

The clouds were high in the sky as the sun rose, and he’d never have spotted her if she weren’t so damn loud. She continued cursing, heedless of the war camps below, as she flew in a straight line.

“What in the hells?” Veckit asked.

“The woman who attacked Maulander. It’s the Wind Witch,” Reid said, licking dry lips. He felt terrified. He’d only barely survived her last attack thanks to the numerous healers the Army had on hand.

Fortunately, she didn’t seem interested in them at all today.

Instead, the crazy woman flew straight into the rift.

“What… in the hells?” Reid repeated, equally baffled. “She flew into the rift! You saw that right? Tell me I’m not seeing things.”

“No, I saw it,” Veckit confirmed.

“We’ve got to report this,” Reid said. “Do you want to go, or should I?”

“Uhm…” Veckit’s eyes widened as he pointed at the rift.

Reid turned back towards the barrier. The ripples had picked back up, and they weren't slowing down. His stomach dropped.

“Incursion!” He shouted. “Incursion imminent!”

He took aim and let an arrow loose before the first creature even fully emerged. It didn’t matter. More of them were coming by the moment. The other towers all began to open fire, and Veckit also picked up a bow as bells began to ring along the front.

“Gods, I hope Mera’s dad is assigned somewhere near here,” he murmured.

He wasn’t going to have enough arrows.
