It Hurts

It feels like a universal fact of life, magnified a hundred times over.

It Hurts.

Geng He screams and screams and screams because the agony is so blinding it almost starts moving past the idea of pain. He hurts more than the human system is designed to hurt, and reaches a level past that, and then keeps hurting.

The organs in him are in agony. The changes made to him feel like bright, vivid scars painted on reality, carving away his sanity.

His hands are around a throat.

He’s not sure whose throat it is. He’s not sure if he’s not sure, he’s not sure if it’s even happening, but he squeezes and blood oozes out from between his fingers as flesh compresses and tears.

It Hurts

He’s already moved to another victim.

Sometimes he catches glimpses. His mind processes in stuttering microseconds what’s happening or not, driving him to respond in spasmodic jerks of movement, but now and again the pieces of him get put back together somehow.

He feels his veins screaming and every part of him suffering and somehow, in spite of everything, he is not allowed to go insane. Somehow, something holds him together.

He cuts. He carves. He uses the exoskeleton he’s been implanted with to slash and bludgeon and rip and tear. He moves through applications of force, his mind spasmodically throwing out every element of his complex, muddled cultivation, until he glows and more bones grow from the world around him and–

And sometimes he’s dreaming.

He doesn’t know of what or why.

Using his cultivation like this should kill him. Wielding Qi inside one’s own body with such blind, manic, feral violence should have had him explode hours ago.

And yet, something keeps him together.

He keeps fighting and screaming and crying.

He doesn’t die.

Something won’t let him.

The city sounds like a vision of the Hells. Some realm atop the bubbling sea of nothing beneath reality, for surely nothing else could fit such suffering.

Dai Yuhan cries as her hair turns to blood and her waters run red and black with the suffering all around her.

The mortals are holding each other close. Whole communities are coming apart in violent fits of agony, families struggling not to tear each other apart, but still they hold tight and take comfort in each other. Pain overwhelming seeps through the land itself, barely held back by the sect’s failing defenses, and still, overhead, the world is ending.

If it were possible, she would tear out her Qi senses whole. They cannot go blind or deaf as an eye or an ear could, when forced to undergo things far beyond its ken, but she is unable to stop her very being from understanding flashes of what’s above.

The Peaks have descended, and the war they wage is one of Warriors against Beasts capable of matching them. Their clashes make the ground shake constantly, make the sky turn a million different hues, until everything feels chaotic and hard to parse and it all hurts.

And she cannot stop seeing it.

She turns her attention away, and still the powers around her and their scale are simply too loud not to perceive.

She feels coils of living, whip-like flesh holding her tight, anchoring her in place, even though the figure they are usually connected to is on the ground, curled tight.

She sees someone holding up a falling wall, letting people escape from beneath it, only succumbing to the weight and deflating to a human size after they have cleared away.

She sees so many colors of so many sects and so many people, all of them struggling not to kill each other or themselves, all of them together.

She finds herself being held by someone. She’s not sure who. She embraces them back, uncaring about her group of acolytes or what sect or anything at all. Maybe they’re even mortal.

Her hair, long ago turned liquid by her bloodline, continues to run red as she hugs someone close and screams.

They may even be mortal. She’s not sure.

There are people all around her, suffering, and all around her, the blood that flows from her fills the streets and flows down stairs and into buildings.

She can feel them all.

They’re afraid.

So is she.

It hurts a little less, not being alone.

The target continues to evade capture.

The thing that he holds in his hands (that holds him) is screaming, and the sound it makes is an un-sound, quieter than silence.

Underneath the skin of the world, the shape of it gets vaster.

It should not be, this thing he holds. Conventional magics would say it is impossible.

But his Soul was taken from him. It left behind an absence. It broke him.

But an absence is a presence.

Nothing is something.

He learned early, beneath sweeping trees, under a sky clearer than any other:

“To move is to kill. Every step taken in one direction kills the step that might have been taken in the other. Every shape that is eliminates every other shape it could have been. Every act of creation is an act of destruction.”

All things are killing. All things are destruction. All that is also is not.

He was made to be not. Centuries of refinement, millenia of service, and that which defined him, a definitionless man, was stolen away by the one he failed to kill in truth.

He is nowhere.

He is no one.

He is nothing.

All for the Family.

And then, his Soul was all those things too. No longer his. No longer real. No longer anything. A tool to kill anyone, capable of shaping itself to match any target, become nothing but a void.

A knife is a shape that kills every other it could have been. All shapes are.

A void is a shape.

He holds it in his hand.

Beneath the skin of what is, its tendrils grow for miles. Its faces scream in ecstasy. Its flesh is not-flesh and its not-flesh is flesh, and it is utterly, perfectly sane.

It is big enough to encompass the entire battlefield and more, and it is in him and his.

He swings it (it allows him to swing it) and carves away another part of the world. Like matching a shadow to its object, only to turn that object to shadow in turn. What it mirrors, it becomes, and what it becomes then becomes-not in the world.

And beneath the world, the shape of it grows larger.

Some things are, conventionally, impossible.

The man who has spent his entire life as a tool of unmaking was supposed to be nearing that threshold. A Soul that changes? That shifts to match another’s? Not a mirror, but a true inverse, born of calculated pruning and shaping, done over decades or longer? Such a thing should destroy the ego of anyone who suffers it, nevermind their cultivation.

But it was demanded of him. So it was done.

But still, the madness of his being now stands so much deeper beneath what is and what-should-be.

No one should be able to remain in the Nascent Soul Realm with a Soul that is not there. It is contradictory.

So he is not in that Realm anymore. But he has not fallen lower.

No one should be able to call a nothingness a Soul.

So it is not a nothingness. Or it is not a Soul.

No one can survive touching a Daemon without defenses.

But he is no one.

So he swings what used to be his Soul, and carves away another mountain of flesh, and feels that thing underneath reality continue to stir.

It’s not awake yet. Not fully. Its inversion is far too much so. But to all things, a threshold. No one can control what is not there, and no one can comprehend it, no one can know how it will act when it wakes.

But the man who Is Not There has been No One for almost all his life.

I Am Not There.

I Am What I Cannot Be.

I Am No One.

So he continues to allow it to use itself, pretending to be a man who wields a knife.

And it keeps unmaking and becoming.

And the man who is No One builds the weapon of oblivion closer and closer to the shadow of the monster he was told to unmake.

And he prepares to follow that command more thoroughly than has ever been possible.

A herd can be so unruly. Untended to, allowed to run wild, even the most necessary of stampedes ends with the calves crushed and the land broken.

The Beast that is Lord Aurick, Zou Lingxin, Du Guang, Broken Walls Beneath Hooves, and many other names besides, though one above them all, Blessed Saint Of Many Touches, stares out at the mess happening with an expression of tired annoyance and bemusement.

It’s not the worst Beast Tide he’s seen. He’s earned a good number of his names in previous ones. It is said that one should fear the old that endure when most die young, and he is not yet so old that the saying has lost its meaning for him. This one is actually relatively manageable, thanks to the way the earth is screaming.

Rivers of red and strange Qi flows throughout the hidden land of the harvesters make songs of agony, and they are expansive enough to cover most of this little world. Beasts too weak to endure the agony are driven mad, and the ones weaker still are driven to unconsciousness, but even those strong enough to push through Pain are still left slowed. The effect extends up into air and water as well, and the centaur can see the way that the descending Peaks in all their majesty are slowed, uneven. Like shooting stars coming to earth, slamming into thousands of points of interest and of conflict, but not as coordinated as they could be, not quite as efficient.

And the chaos below doesn’t help.

Cultivators from the western edge of the hidden world they call “Morae” have arrived. A coalition of factions, acting differently than the Peaks, which have clearly designated targets and domains for each of themselves and their subsidiary sects. These newcomers move all as one, carving through the Beast Tide as a singular, organized army, pinching this smaller tide between itself, the sects it’s already sieging, and the falling Peaks.

It might still take years for this to resolve, as it stands. Beasts will pervade “Morae” until whole biomes are changed. Geniuses will be lost amongst the cultivators, resources will be lost, new alliances or wars will be drawn up between factions. All this chaos will not fade away so easily.

For now, though, the chaos is in the form of a moving herd of flesh, one that he is a part of, and even if it is doomed or slated to end and transform, his Truth cares not for the future. It, like he, is a beast of the now.

I Am One With Those Who Travel With Me. A fact of reality, one longer and more complex than most, refined over a few millenia. Shorter Truths have more versatility, more room for interpretation, but those that are clearer are also stronger, steady in their own definitions.

So long as the Beast Tide endures, a representative of Many-Mouths and the other Apex of the Pack shall endure with it.

He rises from where he rests in the air, taking a step and then a gallop.

The shifting bodies below / beside / With him shift in accordance.

They travel with him, not the other way around.

He might not be at the level of one of the greater Divine Beasts, but he has never needed to be. That is not his role. He is a manager, a guide, a saint to which beasts may pray. It is his name, which is beyond language and found only in Intent. Where he touches down, blessings follow, either in the form of the power of the last great Heart of the world, or in the ways he shapes the world in favor of those who follow him.

But even blessed, the Beast Tide will not endure past a decade. Rather short for one, in his experience, but impactful, especially against so well-entrenched a foe as the Republic of Morae.

And in the meantime, there’s more still to do.

His original goal has been met, of course. He followed the trail of ruin and death of one who might have been an Apex, as so commanded by his god, and found what remained of her. Instead of a corpse still wriggling or a true rebirth of the one known as Raika, there is instead a mess.

In fairness, perhaps it would be stranger were that not the case.

But now, he is at a bit of a loss. So, he gallops, guiding Beasts to sites of rest and strife, towards natural resources and to victims and to shelter as he deems fit, and he meditates.

The land screams.

The sky is torn open by attacks of endless magnitude.

An explosive shockwave of sword-Cuts blasts across the world, carving Aurick apart into pieces too small to call organic– and then is dissolved across millions on millions of other Beasts, leaving him with a slight shave. A beam of light wider than a river blasts in wobbling arcs after a fleeing figure, everywhere it touches liquifying under its heat and then exploding into spikes that impale the air itself. Moonlight falls from above, forming auroras over whole landscapes, and those deemed targets beneath it find themselves dissolving to ashen mists.

War between great powers. Day and night invert in places. Trees and plants skyrocket in growth to match the mountains. Fires that burn on miserable secrets cut them down, turning the charcoal they become into hateful ghosts that claw at the minds of those who see them. A canyon opens up and swallows whole a mountain range and all the millions that lived upon it, then closes, only to be torn back open by a red-gold many-armed Qi construct shaped like a sage.

When Raika was slain, when the pieces of her left in the Overgrowth began to grow wild, the Pack celebrated. The resources within were delicious, unique, and the sheer amount of Qi pushed out of them was enough to let many environments mutate to higher forms.

But Many-Mouths, wisest of the Apex, master and mistress to the Blessed Saint Of Many Touches, grew concerned and furious in turn.

It took Aurick months, even with Many-Mouth’s help, to create the Beast Tide to breach the walls of the Jiang’Hu. It will take years for the consequences to even begin to settle.

And yet, rather than a useful corpse or a recovering Apex, he found this.

Bubbling maggots, fattened on corpse-flesh. A swarm without queen or hive, attempting to build a thousand more in confused mitosis.

Things have come from what was Raika, but they are far less stable, less powerful and more. No longer an Apex, but a plague, an infestation, growing out of control and with self-perpetuating violence.

If asked, Aurick would admit that he does not know how one might kill such a thing. Its strength and complexity is among the smallest of the great powers in the field, but the amount of Qi it carries, and how decentralized it is, make it nearly immortal.

Though that one evergreen absence seems to have a good argument otherwise. Yet another faction, one far more specific and yet indicative of far greater dangers than this little place.

Aurick chuckles as he gallops across space, leading his herd through the worst of the madness. To call Morae a little place… even with his incredibly specific, powerfully specialized being, it would take many days to cross the breadth of the land. Truly, the world is vast.

And at its center, a golden tumor grows vaster still.

Ah well. One thing at a time, hmm?

As if summoned by such wisdom, another thing appears.

The sky of Morae changes color.

Between the sun and moons and the earth below, between the heights of the Jiang’Hu’s isolationist bubble and the true heavens beyond, something writhes and coils.

Larger than mountains. Larger than mountain ranges, and long enough that it cannot be measured in the ways of physiks.

The battle slows for a moment.

And then the god in the sky roars.

Aurick sighs.

He much prefers dealing with his god. The others are so much more… messy.
