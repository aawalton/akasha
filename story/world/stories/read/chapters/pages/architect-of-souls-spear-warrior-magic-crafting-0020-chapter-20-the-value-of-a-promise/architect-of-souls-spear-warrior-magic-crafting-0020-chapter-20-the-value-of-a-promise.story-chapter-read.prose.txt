Ferris looked around again, to make sure. The oath statue was completely gone, nowhere in sight.

The magical statue binding him to Clan Hollowfield, that he feared was controlling his thoughts, was missing. If it was destroyed, Elder Harper would know and kick him out—or worse. If it wasn’t destroyed, it would dig deep into his soul in a place he hadn’t intended or protected. This was a problem.

He searched around the Heartfire—which should have protected everything around it from thieves, but clearly hadn’t. Nothing else was missing. Everything was as he left it last night… except the life-size heavy-as-a-boulder stone statue that had been literally chained to the ground. But was now gone.

Around where the statue used to be, there were clear signs of it being moved. Thankfully, the thing was heavy, and left an imprint in the ground. A long drag mark was visible, giving Ferris a clear trail to follow.

The trail went off into the darkness, and Ferris took a couple steps after it before stopping. Taking a deep breath, he returned to the Heartfire, sat down, and considered his options. He needed a plan.

He had to follow it… but needed to be smart. While he was up against a clock, he did have some time.

Ferris looked closer at the trail. It was stolen, he thought, not destroyed. Either the thieves have some use for it, or they weren’t able to break it here.

The statue was tough. As a mental construct representing the binding contract, it wouldn’t crumble like simple stone. Breaking it would represent breaking the contract, so it took a large amount of force. If whatever took it was trying to destroy it, it would probably take a while. If they weren’t trying to destroy it, he would have until it had put down enough chains that his axe could no longer uproot it.

Both of those may take a while, but he had no idea how long. It could be a day. A week. Longer?

Likely not longer. He wasn’t that lucky.

If he followed the trail and died, it would take him at least two days to reform his mental body. He needed to make sure he had the best chance possible of winning the fight the first time.

Mentally, Ferris reviewed every resource he had to get stronger within a day or two.

Jade’s mental body might be reformed tomorrow, but they might not. Having the slime with him would increase his combat ability, but he wasn’t sure by how much. The fish had shown how vulnerable the slime’s core was.

Neither the Grow nor the Jump ability seeds would help, they were long term investments. The unknown fruit still maturing on the tree wouldn’t be available in time, even if it were mature today. Abilities took time to grow.

He had his qi construct spear and axe, both reinforced with the corpse of a blue frog demon. There was a bit less than a bathtub’s worth of liquid qi in the Qi Pool, so he could likely craft something. He didn’t have any more corpses, but those were… acquirable.

Outside of the soulspace, he had nine merit points, and access to the library and merit store. Those… were worth exploring. He could likely find both knowledge and resources that would help him fight in the soulspace—although he might not be able to afford them. Gilbert at the library mentioned that First Steps of the Soul was provided for free, so he could check to see if it had anything helpful.

Thinking of the library, Ferris remembered his idea to throw glass vials of Jade’s acid at enemies. It may give him a ranged attack option… if it worked.

Waiting provided one guaranteed advantage—Jade—and several other potential benefits. Now, what was the risk or not following immediately?

He knew he was on a timer, but he had no idea how long. If the thieves were trying to break the statue, his best guess was at least two days. That was loosely based on his uncle’s description of the binding contract. They had talked about how to break it intentionally, if everything with Hollowfield turned horribly wrong. That discussion had come with strong warnings of the consequences, which now he might actually face.

Ferris took a deep breath. For now, it was best to focus on how to fix this.

The trail might grow cold. It could either disappear or move through hard terrain. Ferris had been hesitant to explore his soulspace, so he had no idea what was out there. The first time he’d tried, red imp demons had swarmed and killed him, after all. He had no idea if the trail would stay or not. He’d never thought to test if changes like that persisted… why hadn’t he tested it?

If he followed now, and died, he might have time for another attempt. The second attempt would have more information, and he would be better prepared. If he waited a day or two, then he would likely only get one shot.

All the downsides to waiting were unconfirmed, but two of them could be critical mistakes: waiting too long that the statue broke, or he lost the trail. There was a serious risk of Ferris losing the trail. No amount of advantage would help him if he couldn’t find the statue.

Resolved, Ferris decided he had to risk following immediately. If possible, he hoped to avoid a fight and just track the statue. That would allow him to learn where it was taken, yet still give him time to prepare. It risked getting caught in a fight, but he decided the danger of losing the trail completely was worth it. Ideally, this plan would also give more information to prepare with.

With his path decided, he considered if there was anything that could give him an immediate advantage. He grabbed his spear and created a new lightstone, but couldn’t think of anything else. Any new weapon would be less familiar. If he knew a way to get stronger, he would already be using it. He considered not taking a light, as it would increase the chance he would attract demons, but he wouldn’t be able to follow the tracks in the dark.

Setting out, Ferris moved at a quick pace, not even trying to sneak. He figured he would never have the element of surprise while shining like a beacon in the dark. The thieves could’ve taken the statue anytime since the previous night, but it was heavy enough they would likely be slow—he hoped. He didn’t run, as he didn’t want to tire himself when a battle could happen at any moment.

Physical exertion was strange in the soulspace. His mental body would get tired and slow down just like a real body, but he could recover much faster.

Fifteen minutes later, Ferris was further out than he had ever gone. The flat wasteland was giving way to hills, although it was the same barren, lifeless dirt. A cultivator’s soulspace was vast, although exactly how large varied greatly and grew with advancement. The lightstone doesn’t shine very far, he thought, there could be anything beyond the edge of darkness.

He heard the demons before he saw them. Rattling of metal clanked ahead, out of sight. Nothing he’d seen so far in the soulspace sounded like that.

Ferris stopped moving, then adopted a ready stance. Relaxing his body, he breathed deliberately, settling his nerves, and rested while he could. If he could hear them, then they could see him. There was no need to chase after enemies that would throw themselves on your spear.

Three squat, fat demons covered in rattling chains staggered into view, the shimmer of the lightstone reflecting off the metal links. They had slow, lurching movements as they pulled lengths of chains behind. While they were only about half as tall as Ferris, they were much larger than the other demons he had seen. Their horns were silver, the same color as the chains that draped all over the demons.

He braced himself as they advanced with a shambling, stilted pace. The smaller demons had all been quick and nimble, but these chain demons had more bulk. Beyond their size, they appeared to have some trouble controlling their bodies.

Ferris watched them carefully, then started to circle around to the left. The first encounter with a new type of enemy was the most dangerous. He had an advantage in speed, and he wanted to use it to engage one while positioning the other two behind it.

Once he had the three chain demons lined up, he charged in. The closest demon let out a scream and started flailing sporadically. The chains it had been dragging behind were flung about, as if possessed with a life of their own.

One of the chains swung at Ferris. He batted at it with his spear, but it twisted around the shaft, moving unnaturally to wind its way around like a snake. Ferris tried to pull back, but it had a solid hold of his spear, wrapped around several times. He tried to slide the spear out, but the chain had it locked tight.

Ferris had to either drop the spear, or charge in and attempt to overpower the demon. Only a moment to decide.

He let go of his spear.

The decision hurt, but he quickly moved away before any chains could entangle his body.

Backing up again, unarmed, Ferris watched the demon’s chains pull his spear in. They definitely moved independently, magically controlled instead of just swinging around.

He probably could’ve won a one-on-one fight. The chains would’ve wrapped around him, but he liked his chances of killing the demon in a grapple. Unfortunately, there were two more enemies behind it. Even if he could quickly kill one, the others would attack while he was still tied up in the chains.

Without a weapon, he had no chance against the three demons… so he ran away. Thankfully, the demons were slow and quickly left behind. His chances of recovering the statue were now nonexistent, but he still needed information.

Exploring unarmed could quickly get him killed, especially if he encountered some of the faster, smaller demons. He didn’t see much choice, but in the future, he wanted to carry a backup weapon. Even a small knife would have been better than this. Previously, he had decided that the qi wasn’t worth it. If he lost his spear, he was likely dead anyway, but now he was changing his mind.

He continued to follow the trail, but at a much quicker pace. Twice more he ran into chain demons, in groups of two and three, but both times he circled around and continued the trail.

The first sign he was near the end was a smell. It was a pungent smoke, like someone was burning something rotten.

The rolling hills began to get steeper, until he crested one of them to see a light ahead.

Atop a hill taller than any around it, a small, fortified stone structure stood. Ferris would’ve called it a castle if it’d been larger. It had an elevated tower at each of the four corners, surrounded with walls about twice his height. Each tower held a blazing bonfire, lighting up the entire area. It was the first light he’d found in his soulspace, other than the slight glow of qi chunks.

The foul smokey smell was stronger, likely coming from those four bonfires.

Strangely, the entire fort was draped with chains. While it was striking to look at, it felt like an odd choice to run chains up and down the walls. He knew heart demons weren’t really ‘smart,’ instead more personified metaphors for his emotions. Still, someone could just climb up the chains and over the wall…

He spotted several demons walking around the fort. At the center, sticking up like a hangman’s gallows, was a frame of wood. Suspended from this frame was Ferris’ oath statue, a large hook holding it up.

The statue looked a little beaten up. Its white chains hung beneath it, unable to reach anything. Every so often, something would smash into it from below, slamming the statue around on its hook. The stone was tough, magically stronger than it would be in real life, but it wouldn’t be able to take this abuse forever. There weren’t any cracks yet, but it was only a matter of time. The persistent intention to break it would win out eventually.

Ferris circled around the fort, examining it from all sides. The walls were made of stone blocks, tall enough he couldn’t see inside. Heavy metal chains draped all around the fort and hung between the towers. He was wary of trusting them, as they looked a little too easy to climb.

Not that the gate even had a door. He could just walk in the entrance instead.

Each wall had a pathway on top, and a chain demon would patrol along them at random. Ferris couldn’t see any pattern to their movements.

Many groups of demons patrolled around outside the fort. They would enter or exit through the open gate, although on closer inspection it looked narrow and well-guarded.

After making a wide circle around the fort, Ferris decided it was time to leave. He had what he came for, and every second he risked encountering a demon he couldn’t outrun.

Luckily, making his way back was easy. He always had an intuitive sense of his Heartfire, no matter where he was. This gave Ferris the confidence to cover up the lightstone as he walked, reducing the chance of more enemies finding him.

As he stumbled back to his Heartfire, Ferris’ mind was racing. He tried to take a few slow breaths, but his hands refused to stop shaking.

He closed his eyes in his soulspace, then opened them to the real world. The sun had gone down, and Jade was rolling around back and forth. The slime pushed against the limits of the chain, concern seeping over the bond.

Out of immediate danger, he curled up into a ball on the bed. Why did this happen? Tears welled up, and soon he was quietly sobbing. It isn’t supposed to be like this. I always thought… everything would be perfect after I unlocked my soul. Why can’t I just go home?

Jade stopped rolling and started bubbling. The slime tried to move towards him—but was stopped at the end of the chain. Ferris didn’t see it, but he somehow felt it, and in that second both the slime and the human hated the chain cage together.

He got off the bed, freed Jade, and pulled them into his lap.

“Ferris in pain?” Jade asked, still swirling.

Ferris just shook his head. He had little ability and no desire to translate his problems.

“Eat, then pain stop.” Jade decided and shifted towards the door, trying to both stay in his arms yet pull them both forward.

A bit in a daze, he lamely followed the slime’s direction. He briefly thought about all the times he had told Jade what to do, and the slime had unquestionably followed orders.

Ferris found two plates of food in the meal box outside his door; he must have missed dinner. Thankful for the small mercy of avoiding social interaction, he grabbed them and took them back into his room.

Eating… did help.

He ate slowly, savoring the food. Mother Lily had breaded and fried an assortment of vegetables: sweet potatoes in odd shapes, circles of onion, a few varieties of sweet peppers, and more. Plus more bird meat, of course. Can’t have a meal without that. There was a rich brown sauce for dipping, and a selection of pickles on the side to complement the meal. It was cold, yet still delicious.

Jade also enjoyed the meal, to no surprise. Over the bond, the slime had a self-satisfied happy mood, as if enjoying the rewards of a job well done.

Finished with the meal, Ferris was tired. He needed to plan, hadn’t gathered qi, and needed to hunt a demon to make a new weapon… but he also needed to wait until tomorrow. Instead of anything else, he took Jade and crawled back into bed.

The meal—and crying—had made him feel better. He decided to just talk with his familiar. Over the bond, he would imagine a picture of a person from his life and tell Jade a bit about them. His family, his favorite instructor, a cute girl he’d once had a crush on, and others. It was a little bittersweet to think that Jade would probably forget, but it made him feel less lonely. Less like the world was falling apart.

At some point in his stories, he drifted off to sleep.
