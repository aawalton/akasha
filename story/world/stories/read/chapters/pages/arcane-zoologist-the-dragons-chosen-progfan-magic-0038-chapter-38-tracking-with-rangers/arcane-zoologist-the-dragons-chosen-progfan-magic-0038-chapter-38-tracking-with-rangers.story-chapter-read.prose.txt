"Wait, Callie!" Mom called, hurriedly grabbing Callie by the shoulder.

She looked around, seeing that she had run off on her own, rangers following some distance behind, albeit reluctantly. The ranger man especially looked as if thunderclouds were forming above his head.

"Kid, do you have any sense of danger? Who just runs off like that?" He chastised.

"He's right, Callie! You can't just go off running like that!" Mom added.

Callie frankly hadn't even noticed that she was running, so focused she was on her magic. Their interruption nearly made her lose her hold on it, the feeling of the thunderfox's location barely an echo in the distance.

"I'm sorry, but I think I got a clue of where the fox is," Callie said.

The ranger man didn't seem to believe her at all, while Captain Voss looked thoughtful. Mom seemed to believe her, but her expression was also tinged with worry. Callie could see her sneaking glances at her vambrace as if to make sure that the glow from her magic wasn't leaking through.

"What clue?" The ranger man asked, narrowing his eyes.

"Shh, Lysander. Let her search. Go on, little one. We'll follow, but be sure to stay close," Captain Voss said.

Callie threw a glance at Mom, silently asking whether it was alright to follow the magic. Mom looked torn for a moment, thinking deeply about it, but finally nodded. "Go on."

Mom did move to stand to move on Callie's right, sticking almost uncomfortably close. But Callie concluded that she would keep her right arm blocked from the ranger's view should her magic glow somehow leak through the vambrace.

Temper your heart.

Callie took a steadying breath to keep calm, but also listened to her strong desire to find the thunderfox mother. It was hard to latch onto the bare echo of her presence. There were many distant voices—other arcane beasts.

It took all of Callie's control not to let her heart swell with excitement. She would have loved to see them all, but it wasn't the time for that. The thunderfox mother had to be found.

Callie decided to focus on voices that seemed fearful or worried. It was likely what the arcane beast felt, separated from her kits. She did feel some distant, fearful whisper, but it was so, so faint. Callie's magic also wasn't the most cooperative. It wanted to latch onto other arcane beasts, onto the stronger voices that would be easier to follow.

"Callie, are you alright?" Mom whispered, gently brushing her forehead.

Only then did Callie notice that she was sweating from strain. Why was magic this hard?

"I-I'm just a bit tired…" Callie said.

"Could we stop to rest for a moment?" Mom asked.

Callie looked up and saw that the forest was getting darker and darker. They wouldn't be able to keep up the search for long. Not to mention that Callie didn't dare to release the hold on the magic. She didn't know if she could establish that connection again.

"No! Let's keep going," Callie protested.

"Kid, you look tired…" Captain Voss noted.

However, Lysander, having gone some distance ahead of them, said, "Captain, there are tracks here."

"Thunderfox?" She asked.

"No, likely human. The odd thing is that they are covered. Footprints have been erased; however, their hair seems to have been caught on a branch without noticing," Lysander said, pointing to a small broken branch and a brown, not overly long hair stuck there.

Captain Voss took the hair, examining it from all sides. "Too early to say it was someone deliberately messing with the foxes. It might be some eager forager, searching for mushrooms."

"This far from the town and village?" Mom pointed out.

"Unlikely, but not impossible," the captain shrugged.

"Let's keep going, please!" Callie begged. "I can do it!"

"Are you sure you're alright?" Mom asked.

"Yes," Callie confirmed.

She tried to listen to the fearful voice again, heading in the direction where Lysander had found the hair. Previously, she only had a general sense of the area, but thanks to his find, she could see that they were getting closer.

However, as they got closer, Callie realized that the voice wasn't just afraid and worried. It was incredibly weak. It was like the thunderfox mother was barely holding onto her consciousness. This also made it difficult for Callie's magic to find her.

She also felt like the energy within her was steadily running out. Callie picked up her pace—she knew she wouldn't be able to lead the rangers right to the thunderfox. It was just too far. But if she could narrow their search area, then that would be enough.

"Another one, Captain," Lysander announced, pointing at a piece of moss.

Callie at first didn't understand what was so wrong with it, but then Lysander lifted it, showing that it had been torn out of place. Underneath it, there was a part that was undoubtedly a bootprint.

This felt like definite proof that somebody had harmed the thunderfox. But why? For what purpose did this horrible person do these things? Callie clenched her fists in anger and then felt her magic connection break.

"Ah…" She exclaimed.

"What's wrong?" Mom asked.

Callie tried to reach out to her magic again. To temper her heart and resume the search. But it was pointless. She didn't have enough energy to continue.

"I… I don't think I can keep going…" Callie admittedly. She felt so useless. Would the kits be doomed now, just because she couldn't control her frustration again?

"That's alright, kiddo," Captain Voss said, a slight smile on her lips. "You can keep going from here, can't you, Lysander?"

The man nodded. "I believe so. Let's continue in this direction. Watch for anything out of place, even if it seems minor. It seems we're dealing with someone quite skilled."

"Shall we head back to the wagon and wait there?" Mom suggested.

"I-I want to see this through until the end…" Callie whimpered. Maybe she could notice some tracks too if she looked hard enough.

"Very well, get on my back then," Mom said.

"I can keep walking," Callie insisted, taking a step forward, only to realize that her legs felt unstable. Had the magic really exhausted her so much?

"Don't be stubborn! Get on!" Mom insisted.

In the end, Callie had no choice but to get on Mom's back. It was humiliating, but she couldn't help but admit that her back felt warm and safe.

"My dear child, you're so light. I can barely feel you on my back," Mom joked.

"How old are you, Calluna?" Captain Voss asked, although her eyes were still focused on searching the forest for tracks.

"I'm nine," Callie said, also keeping her eyes focused.

"Nine? You're quite bold for a nine-year-old. Sneaking into wagons, heading into forests to search for arcane beasts," Captain Voss commented.

Callie shrank, pressing herself closer to Mom's back.

"Don't get me started on it. Sneaking around searching for glauxis eggs," Mom said, a clear hint of disapproval in her voice.

"Glauxis egg?" Captain Voss and Lysandre suddenly stopped to look at Callie. She pressed herself even closer to Mom.

"Wasn't that supposed to be a secret?" Callie muttered.

"The whole village knows. I'd hardly call it a secret," Mom shot back.

"Wait, hold on just a moment!" Captain Voss stepped closer to Mom and Callie, her expression darkening. "You stole a glauxis egg?"

"NO! I didn't steal it! I returned it to the glauxis," Callie objected.

"Yes, that is true. It saved our village, but I wish you had shared it with your parents at least," Mom said.

"I promise not to hide anything anymore…" Callie muttered.

"Returned it? So someone had stolen the egg?" Lysandre noted bitterly. Callie was surprised that despite the deep creases on his forehead, he didn't look any less beautiful. Was this magic of some sort? Usually, people looked scary when angry.

"Yes. Sadly, we never found out the culprit," Mom said.

"It must be the same person who did something to the thunderfox mother," Callie said.

"What makes you say that? We're a good way away from Greyfen," Mom said.

"I… I don't know. It's just this gut feeling. I feel like it's the same person."

Captain Voss and Lysandre shared a look, but didn't comment any further.

"Let's focus on finding the thunderfox for now. It's our priority," Captain Voss commanded, and they continued on their way.

The forest was quickly getting dark, making it harder and harder to spot any tracks. However, working together, they managed to find them. Mom spotted some crushed berries, barely visible underneath the fern that was set to cover them.

Callie noticed a disturbed ant's nest. The culprit had tried to cover them, but the ant eggs that were out of the nest and the nervous scuttling of ants made it obvious that somebody had stepped on it. She felt bad for the little ants who now had to work hard to recover their eggs.

However, Lysander noticed by far most of the signs. Not that there were many. The person had been very careful in covering their tracks. At one point, it felt like their search would come to an end, as they couldn't find anything. The dark certainly wasn't helping. They had to light the torch and consider setting up camp for the night.

But then they heard something. A quiet whimper that might have easily been missed should anyone have said something.

"Captain," Lysander looked at the tough woman.

"I heard it. Let's keep going! Sorry, Calluna, you're going to have to endure it for a bit longer," Captain Voss said.

"I wouldn't have it any other way," Callie said. In fact, having rested on Mom's back, she was now able to walk on her own, so she, along with the adults, went towards the source of the whimper.

They all moved quietly, listening. Another whimper, much closer. It was hard to tell where exactly it was coming from. It almost seemed to come from underground. They searched and listened, but whimpers came very rarely, so it was difficult.

Come on! Help me find the thunderfox!

Callie tried to reach for her magic again, focusing on the thunderfox while trying to retain control of her emotions, not letting the urgency and fear overtake her. The connection lasted for a bare second—it was as much as she could manage with her energy depleted.

But it was strong. Callie could instantly tell where the thunderfox was.

"Here!" Callie ran to the place where her magic had pointed her to, dragging Mom along with her.

At first glance, it seemed like any other part of the forest. Just like it had been with the egg. But Callie knew to look underground. She dropped on all fours and searched the ground for a hole or some place that felt loose and unstable. Before long, she was rewarded.

She came upon something like a small wooden platform. It was covered with a layer of dirt and perfectly layered moss and even growing fern, so it would look no different from the rest of the forest.

"Dear heavens…" Mom exclaimed in surprise.

Lysandre and Captain Voss lifted the platform, and they all peered inside. A thunderfox, much larger than the kits, was in there, tangled up in a snare wire.

Callie gasped. In some places, the wire had painfully dug into the thunderfox's skin, and she was so thoroughly tangled that she couldn't even move properly.

"How can someone be so cruel?" Callie gasped.

Captain Voss's and Lysander's expressions hardened.

"It's a miracle she's alive," Lysander said, jumping down into the hole.

The thunderfox barely registered his presence. She let out a sad whimper, not even strong enough to snarl or even have a coherent enough thought for Callie to interpret.

"There's a hole in the side wide enough for the thunderfox to crawl through. She was likely lured in with food, but she likely already ate it. She's likely been here for a long time… We need to treat her as soon as possible," Lysandre observed, and with a knife, hurriedly cut off the snare.

To everyone's surprise, the thunderfox bit his arm the moment the snare had been removed. But Lysandre smiled. Callie felt her heart stop, and even Mom gasped. His smile was so lovely that he could easily be mistaken for an angel.

"It's alright, momma. We're here to help! We'll heal you up and bring you to your babies," he said in the gentlest of tones.

The thunderfox looked at him for a while, then released his arm. Blood could be seen dripping down, but he didn't appear bothered at all, crawling out of the hole while gingerly holding the thunderfox. The heavenly smile disappeared as soon as he had stepped out.

"She's severely weakened. Not even strong enough to generate electricity," Lysander noted. "We cannot wait until morning. She might not make it."

Captain Voss turned to face Callie. "I'm afraid you're going to have to keep going for a while longer. We're heading back to the wagon."

Callie nodded firmly. "We have to do everything we can to save her!"

Mom hoisted Callie on her back, and they set off at a brisk pace. Callie looked at the barely breathing thunderfox, praying with all her might that she might make it until the morning.

A darker thought crossed her mind, seeing the poor suffering creature. This couldn't continue. This was undoubtedly the work of the same man who had stolen the glauxis egg. The fact that he once again resorted to burying things in the forest was a telltale sign.

There was also a new clue. The single brown hair. Was it the hair of the culprit or some unfortunate passerby? Even if it was the culprit, it hardly narrowed things. After all, most of the people in Greyfen had brown hair, including her own mom and brother. Still, Callie had a feeling that she shouldn't just dismiss it.
