[Time]: 1980

As the weeks of her apprenticeship accumulated, Irene arrived at a cold, empirical truth: Ovelia behaved infinitely more like a Lantern Cat than the apex of Witch civilization. An omnipotent, infuriating, completely unaccountable Lantern Cat.

Her lessons arrived on no schedule and followed no syllabus, materializing entirely on whim, unbound by time, location, or the twelve-variable alchemical synthesis Irene happened to be stabilizing at that exact moment. A spatial rift would tear open above her workbench at three in the morning, dropping a violently complex spatial equation directly onto her patent schematics, followed by a disembodied voice demanding an immediate practical demonstration. Ovelia dismantled the fundamental laws of the universe with the detached, breezy carelessness of a cat batting a crystal glass off the edge of a table, just to watch it fall.

It was infuriating, and completely, magnetically irresistible.

The summons had been explicit: Arrive at the perimeter at dawn. Irene arrived fifteen minutes early, breath pluming into the freezing pine-scented air, coat tailored, posture immaculate.

By the first hour, she had erected a windbreak-and-thermal ward perimeter around her position, conjured a high-backed chair from her spatial inventory, set a small floating golden luminary against the grey morning air, and was sitting in the frost-free clearing she had created, methodically reverse-engineering the theoretical framework from Ovelia's previous lecture.

By the fifth hour, the sun had climbed to its zenith and she had closed her third book. She had replayed every single syllable Ovelia had ever directed at her, cross-referencing the material for hidden variables she might have missed. Finding none, she extracted a full prepared meal from her spatial ring, arranged it with precision on the small conjured table beside her chair, and ate alone in the pine-scented quiet.

By the eleventh hour, the sky bruised into a heavy, punishing twilight. With a quiet, measured sigh, she reached into her spatial ring, abandoned the snowbank, and deployed a heavily reinforced expedition pavilion with overlapping thermal wards that quarantined the interior from the sub-zero temperature outside. She conjured a plush reading sofa, brewed a fresh pot of premium tea, and continued her vigil in climate-controlled comfort. Her communication crystal chimed with requests from three faction leaders. Irene stared at the glowing stone, considered the possibility that Ovelia was late because she was managing a continental crisis, and shut the device down. If I leave, she reasoned, the calculation hard and cold behind her eyes, and she arrives to find an empty camp, the resulting response could level a district.

A full day passed.

Irene spent the night drafting the schematics for a new patent while a blizzard raged ineffectually against the reinforced canvas outside. She knew, with the sinking clarity that came from being too intelligent to lie to herself, that she was being stood up. Her pride told her to pack up the tent and leave. But the calculus kept updating: she had already invested twenty-four hours into this coordinate. What if Ovelia arrived at hour twenty-five?

She remained.

Thirty-six hours after the designated meeting time, the fabric of reality a few feet from the pavilion entrance shivered and split.

Ovelia stepped through the tear. She was wearing a loose silk nightgown that stopped at mid-calf, her black hair serenely, artfully mussed, her tail swaying behind her in the freezing air as if the sub-zero temperature were merely a suggestion.

She had woken from a magnificent dream, stretched her arms high above her head in a long, languid yawn that ended with a small, contented hum, and drifted toward a secondary sleep cycle, when in the final moment before unconsciousness reclaimed her: a stray thought surfaced like a bubble breaking the surface of still water.

Oh no. Cat forgot to pick up the kid.

It was a day and a half past the designated meeting time. Panic spiked, and was immediately routed by the shameless survival cunning inherent to her species. If I go look now, Ovelia had reasoned, her tail swishing as she drafted her contingency, and she's gone, I can simply claim it was a sincerity test. If she couldn't wait a single day, she was never serious enough to begin with.

Ovelia had stepped through the rift fully prepared to deliver a disappointed lecture to an empty snowbank.

The pavilion flap opened, and Irene stepped out, her coat unwrinkled, her bearing exactly as it had been thirty-six hours ago, her golden eyes completely, terrifyingly still.

Ovelia's pupils expanded into wide, mortified circles. The silence stretched.

"Why," Irene asked, her voice carrying the measured, pressurized calm of a glacier reaching the edge of a cliff, "are you so remarkably late?"

Ovelia's carefully constructed script collapsed on contact. She threw her palms up and opted for the only defense available: unvarnished honesty.

"Cat overslept."

Irene smiled.

It started as a breathtakingly beautiful curve of her lips, the kind of smile that had made the academy's entire faculty feel they were being personally distinguished. Then, the warmth evacuated from it. The smile froze into the blinding, absolute stillness of a solar flare in the microsecond before it incinerates everything in its path.

"Ovelia," Irene said softly. "You are truly complete scum."

Ovelia's tail gave a single, sharp, guilty twitch. Then, as if it registered the audacity of being held accountable, it snapped rigidly, perfectly upright into the freezing air.

Ovelia's eyes widened. "H-how can you speak to your master like that?!" Her voice pitched upward into aggressive, manufactured outrage. "You have no concept of respect at all! Cat overslept! Couldn't you have just gone home? You don't know how to walk?"

Irene closed her eyes. "If I had left," she said, her tone hollowed out to a precise, flat instrument, "you would be standing here right now claiming it was my fault for leaving first."

Ovelia's tail swung around and swatted Irene squarely in the waist: a fluffy, irritable thump of the sort a cat administered to express displeasure, which had absolutely no business hurting as much as it did.

Irene pressed one gloved hand to her side without changing her expression, her golden eyes locked onto Ovelia without flinching. "Then please," she said, biting down on the final syllable with surgical precision, "fulfill your responsibilities as a master."

Ovelia snorted, and the environment sheared away. The freezing wind and the snowdrifts vanished, replaced by the dense, heavily warded warmth of an inner sanctum. With a lazy sweep of her wrist, Ovelia sliced a fingernail through the air, the space parting like soft fabric, and reached into the void, withdrawing a raw, unpolished gemstone roughly the size of a pigeon's egg.

"Gems," Ovelia lectured, her tone shifting seamlessly from defensive child to undisputed sovereign. "The most conductive medium for drawing and housing ambient mana. Mastering gem enchantment is a mandatory foundation."

She rotated the stone between her fingers and shot Irene a look of profound, personal aesthetic disdain. "Cat truly cannot understand why you insist on obsessing over gold."

Irene lifted her chin. "Because I can achieve the same results with gold as anyone else can achieve with a gemstone."

Ovelia let out a low, melodic exhale saturated with condescension. "Arrogance is not your most terrifying enemy," she said softly. "Ignorance is."

A flame bloomed between them. It was an impossible flame: dim, contained, and utterly matte. Irene stepped closer, and her sensory array registered a perfect, terrifying anomaly, zero thermal radiation, the temperature in the sanctum unmoved by a single fraction of a degree.

The dark flame licked the surface of the gemstone. The hard mineral lattice collapsed instantly, liquefying into a floating sphere of luminous, fluid crystal. Ovelia tapped the sphere with one finger and pulled. The liquid gem stretched into impossibly fine, iridescent threads, finer than spider silk, radiating a mana density that made the air ache.

"Your cloak," Ovelia said.

The threads shot forward on their own, driving themselves into the fabric of Irene's ordinary winter cloak suspended in the air between them. Ovelia guided the weave with her right hand, threading a Legendary-tier defensive matrix, elemental resistance, spatial anchoring, kinetic dispersion, directly into the molecular structure of the mundane wool in real-time, as if rearranging the laws of physics were a minor administrative task.

And Ovelia wasn't looking at it.

Irene's attention snagged on Ovelia's left hand, which was occupied with something else entirely: a small, pebble-sized gemstone, pinched casually between her thumb and forefinger. A needle-thin filament of raw mana extended from her fingertip, dancing across the stone's surface with blinding, erratic speed. She was treating a Legendary enchantment as a background process while she doodled on a rock, with the focused absorption of someone waiting for water to boil.

In under three minutes, the dark flame extinguished itself. The final thread settled seamlessly into the wool. The cloak drifted into Irene's arms, humming with a density that made the wool feel like armor.

Ovelia crossed her arms and let out a deeply, comprehensively smug hum. "Well?" she purred. "What do you think of that, stupid disciple?"

Irene gripped the enchanted cloak. The mathematical reality of the skill gap pressed against her with the impersonal weight of a geological event. She met Ovelia's gaze, her expression perfectly even.

"I can do the same with gold."

She could not replicate fluid-gem manipulation to that microscopic tolerance yet. But she possessed a certainty that ran deeper than confidence, an absolute knowledge that she would eventually close that distance. She would find a way. She always had.

Ovelia's smug expression curved into something playful and predatory. "Oh?" she said. "And what about this?"

Her wrist flicked, and the pebble-sized gemstone arced through the air. Irene caught it. She channeled mana into her eyes, magnifying her vision to inspect the surface of the stone.

The surface was covered in text.

Etched across the curved face of the gemstone, in microscopic, flawlessly proportioned script that required a magnifying matrix just to perceive, was a book. The entirety of the Complete Book of Runes, all three hundred and sixty-seven pages, complete with cross-references and diagrams, carved by hand, simultaneously, while performing a separate Legendary enchantment, in under three minutes.

Irene stood in the sanctum, staring at the gemstone in her palm.

Ovelia gave a cheerful, dismissive wave of her hand, dissolved into the ambient mana, and vanished, leaving Irene entirely alone, holding a legend in one hand and an impossibility in the other.
