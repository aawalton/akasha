Non-Combat proficient elite Divers.

Elite Divers are capable of, at the very least, holding their own against Depthsborn at the level they dive. Actual Combat Divers are capable of defeating multiples at the same time, of wading their way through the cavern, especially when supported by the rest of their group.

Certain members however are there purely for their additional skills. Coordinators ensure the entire battle is managed, they are force multipliers that improve the performance of all involved. Healers are also often seen as not needing to do more than hold their own, their role is to help with recovery, or with mid-combat wound patching.

This view is ultimately short-sighted. You cannot guarantee that they will always be safe from combat, or that they do not end up needing to protect someone else momentarily against odds they are ill-equipped to face. Beyond that are other Awakened, should a duel or combat ever occur, these roles are seen as higher priority, they need to be competent, even if never quite so much as those who are dedicated purely to combat.

Cara wished she was anywhere else. Not that she was going to go anywhere, not without Gwen, and unfortunately Gwen was just as trapped as Cara felt. Some battles weren't worth fighting, some situations were beyond her control. Bern was both.

The first half of the day had been pleasant, even enjoyable. Knowing that Lord Percivus would be their mentor, that they would once again be learning from him, being able to put effort into demonstrating that they were worthy of his time, was as thrilling as it was terrifying. His refusal to agree with Bern about Anya had been oddly satisfying, especially when she'd thought he had been on the verge of agreeing.

Nearly every pairing in their dueling class had been a predictable and slightly boring exercise. The so-called nobles had fallen quickly, barely enough of a challenge to work up a sweat. Bern's group had been a recipe for disaster, Gwen had made it clear they were to put in enough effort to show they were trying but not an iota more. Keldren had trouble with restraint, and the years of practice meant everyone knew that they'd recover quickly enough even if he wasn't gentle.

Anya and Seb on the other hand were a hopeful sign of future sparring. They hadn't used Bladesong, hadn't taken advantage of that singular gift, instead they had turned things into a cooperative dance. Neither side had said anything, had hinted they wanted to find a shared pattern, they had simply fallen into step.

Each volley slightly more aggressive, testing reflexes and ability to take a blow, each clash halted the moment one of them so much as stumbled. It had felt more like the private experiments between her and Gwen than any matchup she'd ever been in against Bern.

"-ridiculous. Who does he think he is to-"

Cara held in a sigh. Bern refused to accept the loss. They'd told him Seb used Bladesong. Their old teacher, likely one of the finest fae Bladesingers alive or dead, was here specifically to teach the art. The teachers knew of Seb's ability, she'd felt it, and yet Bern had somehow still believed they had been wrong.

To hear Morgan tell it they'd trounced the pair in their first match, exactly as would be expected. He'd admitted Seb had gotten the best of him both times, once through some degree of technique, the second through a surprise that Morgan clearly had thoughts on. That Seb had managed to close on Bern, to take him on and then end the battle with a punch? That was apparently only the second most grievous affront.

The first, of course, was that Keldren had not only been toppled but disarmed. That somehow that woman, that most-definitely-not-family impostor, had managed to outfight his guardian. Keldren had said nothing about the match, not that she'd expected him to. She wasn't even sure if he was disappointed, upset, or thrilled at having met someone who could challenge him. It didn't matter, Bern had enough complaints for everyone.

"Bern," Gwen said when her twin finally found himself repeating complaints. "You lost. They are talented. You knew they had to be if they made it as a pair. You knew we might meet groups that could match us."

"That isn't the point," he answered.

"It is," Morgan said. "They found a path to victory. They did so fairly and without any tricks."

"Bladesong-"

"-is not a trick," Gwen snapped. "Or are you going to claim Lord Percivus is renowned for trickery?"

"No, of course no. But he is who he is. Those two are-"

"-our peers," Gwen finished for him. "I'm done. I want dinner and I will involve both our teacher and the couple in question if you bring it up again."

Cara stood from where she'd been sitting against the wall, having tried to keep herself out of the argument as best she could. She was opening the door to lead Gwen out before anyone thought to answer. She didn't turn to look, she did her best to act the proper Lifesworn who had no involvement in the affairs of her Mistress, at least in those affairs that were familial matters.

"I wish we'd been able to see it."

Cara didn't answer, there wasn't anything to be said, not when they were arguably in public.

"Cara," Gwen said. "Can you imagine what Bern's face would have looked like if he'd actually been punched?"

"Vividly," she chirped before she had a chance to consider her words. "I mean-"

She felt Gwen crash against her back, her weight hanging onto her shoulders. She nearly panicked, had someone- Gwen was laughing. Not just smiling politely or chuckling lightly, she had lost her balance in amusement.

"Never let him hear you say that," Gwen finally managed between breaths. "Never let him realize you've even thought of it. Please."

It seems Valene had been right. Well it seemed he was probably right. Jean hadn't provided any evidence or confirmation, simply assuming Anya was what she appeared to be. And because there was a full quintet of them there? That was all but unheard of.

He didn't believe in coincidences, or rather he refused to accept that these sorts of coincidences didn't have additional reasons behind them. There was clearly some sort of generational timing at play, especially if two of the five were…

That was an angle he hadn't even bothered thinking about he realized. He'd made assumptions due to her appearance, wondered why records were missing from both sides to the point where only an excision remained, yet he'd gone looking for disasters, for events that would have been covered up because of public impact.

She matched royal family line markers. Whoever her father had been would have been of note, even if only in secret. The absence of records pointed to unrest within that family, unrest that was deemed too significant to allow anyone to hear about.

He started drafting messages, his eyes flitting between his feeds as he composed both simultaneously. He needed actual answers, it didn't actually matter what the answer was, as long as it gave him details he could work with. He had enough to push the Keepers, pretending everything was fine only went so far when their goddaughter was likely face to face with relatives.

Phelann had to know as well. He couldn't tell her much, certainly not enough for her to actually act on the knowledge, but having her show up for the first visit without any warning might be even more disastrous. He'd send hers later, he might be able to add more to it, but he'd make sure it went out a month before she had her first encounter with the Doorkeeper. Long enough to prepare, not long enough to dwell on answers she could not find.

He wouldn't warn her about the Doorkeeper though. That was an experience everyone ought to face at least once, if only as a guest and visitor. And the Doorkeeper deserved what he suspected would happen as well.
