[I am... so sorry, Master. I don’t know why, but... I failed to teleport him out of the chamber.]

“It’s... alright, Celeste. Not your fault.”

Viktor let out a deep sigh. “Teleportation is forbidden in this room,” the Apostle had said, and that was exactly what had happened. But how the hell did it work?

Nephra once mentioned that she could block teleportation into and out of Iskora whenever she pleased, so perhaps this was something similar in principle. Nevertheless, this dungeon belonged to Celeste. It was her domain, the space over which she had absolute control. For some outsider to just stroll in and casually strip her of her rights, it was beyond absurd.

And more importantly, where in the world did the bastard even pull that trick from? Everything he had done up until this point, however outlandish, could be boiled down to body manipulation. They were all the same thing dressed up in different clothes. Yet, this one was different. It was something else entirely. Was there even any limit to what that man could do? If he could just keep reaching into some bottomless bag and bringing out one new miracle after another whenever the mood struck him, how could anyone possibly deal with him?

Calm down, Viktor.

He took a deep breath, forcing himself to recall and analyze what had unfolded during the engagement.

He had lost Khenemhotep. Not permanently, of course, but for all practical purposes, the undead priest was out of the fight for the time being. Which meant that power, the beam of green light that could chew through the Apostle’s flesh, had been removed from the equation.

A great loss, indeed. But it was not like he had walked away from this exchange completely empty-handed.

First of all, he still believed the flesh the gray-haired man had within him was not infinite.

After he devoured that swarm of insects, his outward appearance didn’t change in the slightest, meaning whatever he ingested had been compressed inside his body. Before he entered the dungeon, he must have crammed in the flesh of a hundred men, or even a thousand, who could say. That would explain the raw strength, the endless regeneration, everything: it was because he had a vast and terrible reservoir. Still, even deep reservoirs ran dry if you drew from them continuously.

And the fight against Khenemhotep had cost him dearly on that front. Every time one of those green beams found its mark, his reservoir bled out a little more of its stored flesh, drop by drop, wound by wound. That one sweep of the staff in particular, that alone could have taken away one-third of his stockpile. Small wonder he didn’t risk getting close to the priest again.

In other words, the Apostle had emerged from the fight considerably weaker than he had been at the start. Thus, the next round would be somewhat fairer. Not fair, of course—Viktor wasn’t foolish enough to hope for that—just fairer a bit. Well, the guy had also eaten Khenemhotep in the bargain, but in terms of flesh and bone, the old priest’s desiccated body probably offered him very little in return, certainly nowhere near enough to offset what he had lost.

Second, and this stung more than Viktor wanted to admit, the Apostle had won the battle by outthinking him.

He had laid out three possible courses of action in his mind when the engagement began, but somewhere along the way he had second-guessed himself, all because of that man’s behavior. Now he could see it clearly. His opponent had acted that way on purpose in order to steer him toward the one door he wanted him to walk through.

He had decided against joining the fight himself. That decision stemmed from his own cautious nature, yes, but the Apostle had stoked it further by doing many suspicious things. He had also ruled out sending Galatea to help the ancient priest, since he needed her fully committed to maintaining the trap laid out for Xander. Which meant there were only two options remaining on the table: order Khenemhotep to retreat or let the priest stand his ground.

And then, he was made to believe this was a golden opportunity to kill his greatest foe right then and there, all the while assuming Celeste’s teleportation would always be available as a way out for the priest should things go wrong. Hence, he had chosen to let Khenemhotep fight on his own, completely oblivious to the Apostle’s secret power.

“So I’ve been playing right into his hands, huh?” Viktor murmured.

Still, bitter as it was, it had told him something. If the Apostle had needed to resort to deception to secure his victory, then there was a good chance he couldn’t win through raw strength alone. In other words, he was not quite the unstoppable force he wanted people to believe.

“Can you teleport things now, Celeste? In and out of that chamber?”

[Yes, Master. My power has returned to normal.]

Perhaps the blocking effect only lasted for a few seconds. Makes sense, I guess. If the man could keep it up indefinitely, he would have shut her down from the very start rather than saving it like a trump card to be used right before the finishing blow.

So there was a limit to what that man could do, after all.

Of course, it was still possible that the Apostle had simply been toying with them the whole time, and he was indeed unstoppable. But if that were the case, everything would be moot anyway, so there was no point in dwelling on that thought.

Closing his eyes, Viktor sent his vision to the third floor. After defeating Khenemhotep, the Apostle had wasted no time. He immediately made for the door behind the golden throne, smashing his way through those unfortunate enough to stand between him and his goal. Soon, he would arrive here.

And there he was, right on cue—that gray-haired bastard, still every bit as stark naked as he had been since the moment he stepped into the Chamber of the Dead.

The man came to a stop, sweeping his gaze across the vast, glimmering expanse of water that defined this realm. When his eyes finally settled on the entrance to what had once been the old Core Room, now repurposed to house the staircase leading down to the fourth floor, he leaned forward. From his back, a pair of enormous wings tore free and unfurled. The wings spread wide and began to flap, sending the man soaring up into the open sky above the water.

“Of course he has fucking wings,” Viktor muttered. That would allow the bastard to bypass the entire water realm.

[What should we do, Master? Should I order the Acolytes of the Deep to summon a suitably gargantuan tentacle to intercept him?]

“I doubt that would be enough. No, leave him be. We fight him at the cathedral.”

Besides, Cedric’s party should have reached this floor by now. According to the script, once they arrived, they would be attacked by some Acolytes. That would serve two purposes. First, if they made it through the entire dungeon without having a single skirmish along the way, people would undoubtedly grow suspicious. And second, once the battle was over, they could hack off a few pieces of tentacle to carry back with them as proof that they had indeed made it this far.

Obviously, Viktor was not going to fight the Apostle in full view of the Daelin team. Also, he had already laid down preparations for an engagement on the fourth floor, just in case the trap set in the Great Hallway failed to do its job. Now all that remained was to make some adjustments based on how things had actually unfolded.

He told Celeste what needed to be done, and while she set about implementing it, he sent his vision back to the Great Hallway to see how the situation there had developed in his absence.

Xander was now locked in melee with Galatea, blinking from one spot to another, his flaming sword lashing out toward whatever blind spot he could find in her guard, while she met him swing for swing with her glowing hammer. Around them, the soldiers pressed in, exploiting every opening they could find to aid their commander.

All going to plan, it seemed. Viktor believed that Galatea would eventually grind down the bastard and emerge victorious, so he would just leave her to the task and turn his attention elsewhere. A shame, though. He would love to put that mad dog down himself, but a man couldn’t have everything, and there was a bigger fire to deal with at the moment.

The other battle, on the other hand, was over. Valeska-Rowan-Cynthia slumped down, their merged body carved up in a dozen places, their weapons long since knocked from their grip. Even Cynthia’s staff had been shattered, along with the arm fused with it. Broken bodies littered the ground around the fallen creature, Templars and tomb guards alike, testament to the combined might of their martial skill and magic. Yet, it seemed this was as far as they could go. The Apostle had forged the three of them into this grotesque shape to buy himself some time, and they had done exactly that. Now, their end had finally come.

Sebekton loomed over the wreckage of them. “You fought well,” he said.

Valeska let out a long, exhausted sigh. “Make it quick,” she whispered. A Templar stepped forward and brought his blade down in a single clean arc. The warrior woman’s head left her shoulders, tumbling and rolling down the length of the ramp.

Rowan said nothing at all, his eyes staring blankly ahead, the way a soldier did when there was nothing left to say. Another blade swung, and his head rolled away as well.

Cynthia, unlike the other two, wept. “I... I don’t want to die...” she said, tears welling in the corners of her eyes. The blade came down all the same, sending her to join her companions at the foot of the ramp.

Well, the moment they set foot in this dungeon, their fate had been sealed. And, given the state they were in now, this could even be considered a mercy.

Viktor opened his eyes. “Teleport Sebekton to the fourth floor,” he told Celeste. “Then teleport me there as well.”

Rising from his chair, he walked to the nearby table and began gathering the items laid out across its surface. He closed his fingers around the familiar grip of his rapier. Yes, he was going to use it this time.

Celeste began her work, and the world shifted around Viktor. In an instant, he found himself greeted by a sudden, overwhelming flood of radiance that seemed to pour in from every direction at the same time. The Domain of Light, indeed. The realm of people immune to blindness, who took delight in blinding others.

As his eyes adjusted, he realized he had been brought to the broad staircase leading to the great doors of the cathedral. Sebekton was already here, standing by his side.

“Master,” the Crocodilian said.

“Good job,” Viktor said. “Putting that abomination out of its misery.”

Sebekton nodded. “It was a good fight.”

“Are you going to eat it?” Viktor asked. After all, the Crocodilians had a tradition of feasting upon the flesh of their defeated foes.

Sebektion gave him a troubled glance. “I think I’ll pass this time.”

Figured.

“I’ll keep the heads as trophies,” the Crocodilian continued. “As for the rest, I’d burn if I were you.”

“I’ll think about it,” Viktor said. Actually, he was planning to have Khenemhotep pick the corpse apart, the same treatment he had given that mutated Lyndorian spy some time back.

“I heard the High Priest has fallen.”

“He has, but not permanently. He’ll be back with us soon enough.”

“Good to hear.” Sebekton lifted his gaze to the platform suspended in the sky. “A tough opponent is coming, I take it?”

“Yes.”

“Master,” came another voice, from off to his right and a little above, dripping with the particular smugness only one creature in this whole dungeon seemed to manage.

Viktor turned to give his favorite bird-man a look. “You’re also here, huh?”

“Of course I am,” Haku said. “Wasn’t it your own order, Master? To have all of my kind gather here?”

Well, yes. The Tengu were going to join the fight against the Apostle. While sending flesh and blood against that man might not seem the best idea, if he kept every living thing away from the enemy, he would soon find himself having nothing to fight with. Besides, these fellows were agile and highly skilled in close-quarters combat, and they had been warned about the flesh-absorbing ability, so the risk was manageable. He had also briefly considered bringing along the Ettins or the Cyclopes as additional muscle, but ultimately decided against it, since both were considerably slower than the bird-men.

And so, with his trusty Guardian standing beside him, twelve Tengu overhead, and scores of Templars behind his back, he was going to make his last stand here.
