Chapter 70

Between Heaven and Earth

In October, the Stepnye Volki, Raven and Unitas squads and several other elite small units continued pushing toward the center of the distortion in Vanavara, accompanied by more than two hundred other units and full armies on the terrain.

It had been a long time since Mai had seen so many people gathered together, and at the same time so many deaths due to the missions.

The aeons overseeing the advance estimated that over five million souls were marching toward the heart of the distortion. Humans, feys, aeons, recently deployed combat mechs, drones, vehicles, tanks, and ships armed to the teeth—everything that could be thrown into what was projected to be the closest approach anyone had achieved since the conflict began. Considering that new estimates put the death toll at almost two billion lives that had been lost since the rise of the fractus, an army of five million felt almost small… if it meant ending the threat once and for all.

As they pressed forward, the fractus grew stronger, adapting to every tactic used against them and shifting their routes. Just as ants did when their path was cut off, it was not difficult for them to create another one, considering that they could move through an additional dimension. It was a miracle that they did not use that ability for something else. If only a small group of fractus had had a higher learning factor, it would have been a disaster for operations.

In Barnaul, a city in southern Siberia, the situation became even more complicated when a new portal opened—but they couldn’t turn back. The distortion in the region had expanded, creating a warped space that made communication and maneuvering difficult.

The three squads, with their units, were dispatched to Barnaul to reinforce the forces there. If everything went well, the operation shouldn’t take more than two days before they rejoined the front at Vanavara.

The mission was twofold: secure the passage through the city and protect a fractium-energy facility essential for the forces fighting nearby. However, the fractus—specifically the Xe-res—began forming packs moving with alarming speed and synchronization. Their strategy was changing, they weren’t merely reorganizing anymore—they were evolving into more efficient hunters of entire squads.

By the time they reached Barnaul, the reinforcement ships nearly crashed, as Shrikes dived from the sky to hunt them mid-air. They hadn’t even touched the ground when the ship’s weapons had to come alive. The air already carried that metallic taste and the smell of ozone that had become a second atmosphere to everyone—something Mai now associated with days where the line between victory and extinction thinned into a nanofilament thread.

There were no arguments this time, no more lingering frictions from previous missions. The regional forces were on the brink of collapse, and the three squads—Stepnye Volki, Unitas, and Raven—understood that the moment they saw the horizon, and later, the landing site littered with fallen Shrikes.

Barnaul was no longer a city, it had become a series of concentric trenches protected by hybrid fractium armor, eight three-hundred-meter-tall pulsing energy towers fitted with railguns, and hundreds of exhausted soldiers defending every meter with a resigned ferocity. When Stepnye Volki arrived, the local command barely looked up to greet them: the fractus offensive had opened a new portal to the northwest, and every minute without reinforcement meant another meter lost.

The Xe-res were advancing from the edge of the distortion they were carving into the terrain, moving in perfectly synchronized packs like ants in formation. It wasn’t instinct or chance—this was strategy, primitive, but still strategy. They formed columns that unfolded like fans, cutting off retreat routes and surrounding critical positions before defenders could react. And behind them, like shadows hunting flesh, the Shrikes fell from impossible angles in the sky.

This time, the squads didn’t have to negotiate tactics. Barnaul’s commanders had already prepared a visual map of fractus movement, Stepnye Volki simply took the least stable northwest flank—the one no one wanted—where the line had collapsed the night before.

The commanders marked positions with surgical speed: Stepnya Volk with the 3rd Local Brigade. Raven, cut fractus communications at the marked points—to scatter those shitty particles to disorient them. Unitas, open a hot corridor toward the fractus and hold it so the towers can sweep them. They won’t survive another charge without support, and those towers were the best defense to sweep.

The first wave of that day hit before they even finished deploying.

The Xe-res burst from the portal like arrows shot without a bow. Barnaul’s units fell back a step, and Mai understood them. Even for veterans, watching the Xe-res multiply mid-movement felt like witnessing a living equation devouring the world.

Stepnye Volki’s railguns thundered in unison. Each shot produced a blue pulse that made the atmosphere shiver, destabilizing the Xe-res’ warped tridimensional layers. The fractus burst inward, imploding in a flicker of black light that left behind only their fractium cores—purple, dense, vibrating like mineral hearts.

To the right, Barnaul’s soldiers seized the opening: they hurled compact electric charges that detonated low, slowing the swarm’s attempt to reorganize.

From the rear, Soo-ah with Raven team handled the drones as if she had wired them to her own pulse. A swarm of Shrikes dove toward them, but each Raven drones generated a fractium interference dome that disoriented the creatures just long enough for Unitas’ division supporting the towers to blast them down with hot plasma, leaving crimson trails across the snow.

Coordination was as clean as one could hope for in that hell.

“Mai, your flank,” warned Nikolai from farther ahead, his voice laced with an inhuman growl.

She turned just in time to see a greater Xe-res splitting into a spiral of three simultaneous bodies.

She fired and unit followed those shot with almost choreographed precision.

The Xe-res folded inward, bursting with a flash of purple light.

The advance continued like that for hours: compact groups, shifting lines, minimal retreats. Every recovered stretch was won with electricity, sweat, and shattered fractium. The local forces—exhausted but still standing—fought shoulder to shoulder with them, following every instruction with a discipline Mai deeply respected.

At a critical moment, a cluster of Shrikes broke through the eastern defenses, heading straight for the fractium facility the locals were tasked with keeping operational. If it fell, the entire western line would collapse—and with it the city’s defense—because there was no other place to produce the fractium weapons and specialized cores for the towers. Reinforcements were out of the question with most forces locked around the Vanavara encirclement.

“Volki, with me,” ordered Sasha, not raising his voice even through the comms.

Mai and the others regrouped instantly as Unitas and Raven focused on collapsing the portal, channeling a concentrated discharge into it.

The counterattack was direct, leaving no room for hesitation. Mai pierced a Shrike mid-air, Nikolai brought another down with an impossible leap, driving his electric claws into its nodal core. Natalya and Soo-ah’s team struck the air with an overload pulse that left three creatures frozen, like glass sculptures on the verge of shattering.

When the noise finally died and the towers’ discharge sealed the portal, Barnaul was still standing.

The local forces looked at them as if they had arrived from another world—but Mai knew better.

They were made of the same exhaustion, the same fear, the same stubborn need to keep breathing.

That night, there were no frictions. Only fatigue and shared silence. Just in case some surprise could appear.

Reinforcements arrived from the east in the morning, bringing several ships and extra weapons to maintain the line of defense.

There was a long road ahead for the three teams. And they had to return to Vanavara soon.

***

The sunlight was slowly dying over the horizon, staining the Siberian sky red as the last echoes of explosions rumbled in the distance. The squad watched, along with countless others, as the massive detonations of the atomic charges flared in the air like newborn suns. Each one was like a slap across the face of the Earth— like a desperate attempt to erase the distortion that had corrupted both the land and the minds of those fighting it.

The atmosphere tensed under the booming sound of destruction, though as the explosions faded into the distance, the silence that followed was even more deafening. The distortion—that cursed sphere devouring everything it touched—remained, unbothered in the sky floating. And like a wounded creature refusing to die, it held firm, almost laughing at the attempts to erase it.

The squads stationed several hundred kilometers away, Stepnye Volki among them, had witnessed it all—but the result was the same: the distortion didn’t move an inch.

Beside her, Sasha watched in silence, his expression grave. Ludovic’s face reflected the same frustration, tempered by a fearful acceptance of what was coming next.

Nikolai, wounded during the previous battles, had been evacuated to the rear lines. The deep gash in his leg wasn’t something that healed in days—a Shrike fragment had lodged into him and, because of the shard's fractal structure, he required a specialized surgery. His regeneration would take months, maybe longer, as happened with the most severe injuries.

A heavy sigh escaped Mai’s lips.

The accumulated tension of the past months, the constant pressure, the unending fight… it all seemed to have stagnated her hopes. The atomic-charge mission had been a failure. The distortions remained, spilling over the landscape, feeding on despair.

Mai clenched her teeth.

Every passing day, the distortion around the sphere seemed like was swallowing more territory, and the spheres kept spawning from its core like rotten eggs, releasing Xe-res and Shrikes into the world to multiply. Nothing seemed capable of stopping it.

But then, only days after that failed attempt, command made the decision to deploy a kind of magical strike that had been in development for years: a concentration of plasma combined with a torrent of magic channeled through the local ley lines. The strategic-rank mages, who had stayed out of reach during the initial waves of battle, were deployed to carry out a massive attack.

It wasn’t explosives this time, but something far subtler—and far more dangerous: the fusion of pure earth magic with energy, as if the very fabric of the universe could be torn open by the combined power of the mages.

It was something that had been studied for decades by certain occult circles obsessed with immortality, and other part that involved the mages specialized in elemental magic, but only now bore fruit in an entirely different way.

This was because most of the elementals generated by magic were pure but raw forms that sprang from the Earth and that mages could eventually tame. Now those secret studies would be put to a different use.

The Vanavara Circle would become a gigantic invocation array, drawing on two types of energy to force the portal into collapse. ZAIEN’s tectonic spires were being used as magical catalysts.

The vast majority of the world’s most powerful mages had been sent for this, though the project had been kept secret until the very last moment.

For security reasons, nothing had been revealed to the public—there was fear that some kind of fractus might access the information, causing the forces being vomited from the portal-sphere to increase.

Apparently, something similar had happened last year, but it was just secret rumors, and the full details weren't known.

In the preceding days, airships carrying mages and feys had studied the weather conditions and seeded the skies with fractium particles. Drones positioned like needles above the clouds, and seals acting as electrical catalysts, all worked to concentrate the energy—mirroring what was being done on the ground. For this purpose, special alloys were used, and nearly every orbiter-type fractus core on the planet had been collected—they were, after all, the ones that chased storms and fed on atmospheric electricity.

It was similar to cloud seeding to either produce storms or dissipate them—but required far greater precision, because they needed to create the conditions of an electrical storm in the middle of winter, which meant generating both high- and low-pressure systems.

The operation took place several days later, at dusk.

“If this ends up being a definitive solution, it might give us an advantage,” Sasha had said before the attack, though his voice carried a tension that betrayed the weight of the moment. It wasn’t hope… it was the echo of it, a fragile and stubborn shadow.

Mai remembered those words as she stood with the rest of Stepnye Volki, several kilometers from the epicenter, on a devastated plain where the freezing wind felt like a prelude to whatever was coming. From there, they could see everything through the transmission: the immense machinery of a ritual no human—nor fey, nor aeon—had ever attempted.

That day, the sky didn’t darken.

It was consumed.

The five million combatants deployed around the sphere—in a circle spanning more than a hundred kilometers—formed a pattern impossible to comprehend from the ground. Drones, mechas, armored units, and elite squads held the perimeter, maintaining a mobile defense against the fractus coming from the sphere. Behind them aligned like the vertebrae of some colossal creature, stood the strategic-rank mages.

Thousands.

Tens of thousands.

The best that remained after years of war.

And farther below, buried deep in the rock, the ZAIEN tectonic spires vibrated. Monoliths of a material never used for anything like this—for they’d originally been designed to release tectonic energy to prevent earthquakes—now stretched into the depths like the roots of a slumbering god about to wake.

Mai held her breath without noticing it. Even from that distance, the magical pressure made her fractium exoskeleton hum, as if the metal wanted to howl.

Then it began.

First came the silence—not the absence of sound, but its murder. An unnatural pause that even made the drones hover without a single rotor twitching.

Mages uttered words or used amulets to aid their concentration, sorcerers channeled their powers, while wizards used their invocations or familiars. Others remained completely silent, needing to utter nothing to use their magic.

Then the ley lines awoke.

The ground shuddered. Snow split and melted into luminous filaments that slithered like electric serpents toward each mage, wrapping them in halos of white, blue, violet.

A million points of light connecting.

A million breaths syncing.

The air warped.

And the sky… the sky shattered like an ancient mirror.

The ritual rose in a spiral, forming a vortex of magical plasma that seemed ready to rip the clouds away and fling them into orbit.

But that wasn’t what happened.

In that moment, the upper section of the sky activated, linking itself with the Earth’s energy. The storm clouds churned like a hurricane, discharging lightning that collided with the spiraling ley energy seven kilometers above the ground. The glow was so intense that every person cast two, three overlapping shadows on the snow. The freezing temperature in the sky disapeared as the air heated to levels never seen in the region—not counting the accident almost two hundred years earlier in Tunguska, farther north of Vanavara, where the sphere now stood.

The energy from the sky had two effects. At a very basic level, it might seem to combine the two types to create a distortion that would cause the sphere to collapse. But it was actually much more technical than that. The discharge from the sky had another effect: it returned to the ground the magic that was escaping into the air. That mid-air encounter could create the effect of overloading the region—not only with electricity but also with magic—by using earth and air as catalysts. This allowed them to maintain the desired direction without the dispersion caused by the tundra wind itself ultimately destabilizing the pressure centers that the earth and air elementalist mages were trying to maintain in order to achieve the appropriate temperature and conditions. They were even using ancient storm-control techniques combined with magic now.

Mai felt a tremor climb her spine.

It was fear.

And it was awe too.

It was the certainty that they were attempting something forbidden even by the laws that had shaped the world before history existed. Or perhaps not? If what Nevermore and other institutions had uncovered was true, then every civilization on the planet had been wiped out by unknown causes.

Perhaps each of them had witnessed something like this before vanishing beneath millions of years of soil, waiting for a new civilization to rise from zero.

A deep roar emerged from the earth.

The tectonic spires answered.

Hundreds of kilometers of rock and magma resonated in unison beneath them, as if all of Siberia had become a colossal bell.

The sky and its hurricane-born clouds began to turn a saturated, liquid blue.

An inverted ocean.

The mages’ columns of energy converged slowly, like rivers being pulled toward a central abyss. The sphere— that dimensional tumor over ten kilometers wide— reacted at once: its dark surface rippled, warping the light like a bubble of black oil.

It swallowed light.

It devoured sound.

It twisted the horizon.

And so... magic and electricity struck it.

The blast was silent through the screens, yet powerful enough that Mai felt her breath seize and her heart skip a beat. A pressure wave swept across the plain, lifting snow, dust, and fractal debris as if some giant had exhaled across the earth.

The glow gradually faded. The ritual was over.

The sphere didn’t burst.

It didn’t collapse either.

But after several seconds it moved.

Mai saw it tilt several meters to the side, as though shoved by an invisible hand.

And then, slowly, it began sliding eastward.

First a few meters.

Then a hundred.

The millions surrounding the sphere—mages included—ended the attack once the clouds overhead dissipated as the weather patterns shifted. The troops hadn’t been heavily affected, they were the epicenter, and the mages had directed the torrent of energy straight into the sphere. Many mages collapsed unconscious, blood running from their noses and ears, needing immediate evacuation to medical tents, while over a million troops on the eastern side had to relocate as the sphere began drifting in that direction.

For hours, the sphere continued gliding, drifting toward the course of the Nyuya River, deforming the ground as it passed. Every kilometer released fresh waves of Xe-res and Shrikes, as if it were expelling its own bloodstream into the world.

Day after day, the sphere advanced—five days of chaos, dimensional ruptures, and improvised battles to contain the creatures it spewed without pause. It was as if a black moon had chosen to change orbit, and the earth simply had to endure it.

Mai and the rest of the squad and troops watched everything in silence from the rear position, her heart caught between hope and terror.

She watched with her throat tightening as the sphere drifted farther away, while a new surge of Shrikes fanned out beneath it. The battle was far from over, and they were still thirty kilometers from the main distortion. People whispered that the end was close, but Mai knew better. The explosions rumbling across the horizon felt like the overture to the next cycle of devastation. Once more, the electric weapons began to speak.

The ritual hadn’t destroyed the distortion.

But it had forced it to move.

And for the first time in a very long while, the war seemed to have shifted shape.

And it wasn’t clear if that was something good… or the prelude to something worse.

In the distance, the wind carried the echoes of the Shrikes— and the uncertain future waiting for them all.
