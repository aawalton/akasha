Spoiler - Spiders in this chapter again

The three massive dire wolves closed the distance with terrifying silence. They did not snarl, they did not bark, and their footfalls barely disturbed the freezing mud. They moved with the perfect, unnatural synchronicity of a single organism spread across three bodies, driven by the glowing green threads of necrotic mana attached to their spines.

Josh planted his heavy steel boots deep into the earth. He did not have time to trigger his Titan's Anchor skill. He simply braced his shoulder behind his deeply gouged shield, bending his knees to lower his centre of gravity.

The impact was bone-shattering.

Two of the rotting wolves hit his shield simultaneously. The sheer force of the two massive beasts acting in perfect unison drove Josh backward. His boots carved twin trenches through the thick slurry of the clearing, his teeth grinding together as the heavy steel of his shield groaned under the pressure. The wolves snapped their rotting jaws wildly, their fangs scraping against the metal, trying to find a purchase on his flesh.

Josh gritted his teeth and activated Galvanic Riposte.

A high-voltage shock of pure, raw electricity surged outward from the surface of his shield. The blue lightning arced violently through the damp, rotting flesh of the two wolves. If they had been normal beasts, the shock would have paralysed them instantly. But because they were mere puppets, feeling no pain and possessing no functioning nervous system, the lightning merely caused their dead muscles to temporarily spasm and lock up.

It bought Josh a second of breathing room. He shoved forward with all his immense strength, throwing the two massive bodies back into the mud.

"The webs!" Josh roared over the noise of the clashing combat. "Brett, get rid of those webs or it will pull them back up!"

Brett was already moving. The mage stepped back to the very edge of the clearing, completely ignoring the chaotic melee unfolding just feet away. He pushed his imaginary spectacles up his nose, his pale face set in lines of intense concentration.

His peripheral vision blurred as he activated Focus Mind. The sounds of Bhel roaring and Josh's armour clanking faded into a dull, distant hum. Brett intentionally sacrificed his awareness of his surroundings to massively boost his mana regeneration and spell precision. He raised his bare hands, his elegant fingers splayed wide. His serpentine Cinder-Sprite familiar materialised with a sharp chirp, wrapping itself tightly around his right wrist like a bracelet of living flame.

Brett looked up at the towering, dead pine trees surrounding the clearing. They were draped heavily in the thick, grey, bone-like webbing of the Crypt Weaver. While the necrotic wood was too saturated with foul moisture to catch fire easily, a single, careless fireball hitting the volatile webs would aggressively boil the dark sap within the trees, flooding the entire clearing with a suffocating, highly toxic steam.

"Aspect of Primal Flame," Brett commanded softly, his hands glowing with a blinding, searing white heat.

He unleashed two continuous, highly compressed streams of white-hot plasma directly from his palms. The fire did not simply blast outwards. Because of his Primal Aspect, the flames were alive. They acted like a pack of starving, luminous hounds. The streams of fire hit the base of the nearest trees and immediately began to climb. They aggressively hunted the grey, necrotic webbing, consuming the unnatural substance with a sharp, high-pitched sizzle, while miraculously leaving the rotting bark of the pine trees entirely untouched.

It was a masterful, exhausting display of magical control, but it meant Brett was completely occupied. The physical combat was up to the rest of the party for now.

The third dire wolf had not engaged Josh. Directed by the Weaver above, it had seamlessly flanked the fighters and launched itself directly at Carcan.

The beast soared through the air, its jaws opened impossibly wide.

Carcan hummed as she slammed the base of her amber-topped staff into the mud. A translucent, golden dome of holy magic flared to life over her and Brett. The wolf crashed into the barrier. The holy energy hissed violently against its rotting flesh, burning the necrotic corruption, but the sheer weight of the beast cracked the golden dome. The wolf scrambled wildly against the magical shield, its hook-like claws seeking a weakness.

Bhel did not let it find one.

The dwarf hit the flanking wolf like a runaway boulder. He launched himself through the mud, bringing his right iron axe down in a devastating, overhead strike. The heavy blade bit deeply into the spine of the rotting beast, severing bone and dead muscle.

The wolf collapsed off the golden dome, landing heavily in the mud, but it did not die.

High above them, suspended in the centre of the burning web, the Crypt Weaver shifted its bulbous body of fused human skulls. The glowing green light within its abdomen flared brilliantly.

Bhel watched in absolute disgust as a surge of thick, green necrotic mana pulsed rapidly down the thin, glowing thread attached to the fallen wolf. The severed spine began to violently knit itself back together. The dead flesh reconnected, and the massive beast pushed itself back up onto its paws, completely ignoring the horrific wound.

"They are healing!" Bhel bellowed, ducking a sudden, snapping bite from the resurrected beast. "The spider-thing is pumping them full of dark magic!"

"Sever the strings!" Perberos shouted from his position near the edge of the clearing.

The elven ranger had his longbow drawn, tracking the chaotic, rapidly shifting movements of the wolves. He let a heavy, steel-tipped broadhead fly. The arrow streaked through the air with impossible precision, aimed directly at the glowing green thread connecting the Weaver to one of the wolves fighting Josh.

The steel arrow passed cleanly through the magical thread without resistance. It did not sever the connection. The thread was not a physical object; it was a conduit of pure, concentrated dark mana.

"Physical attacks will not cut the connection!" Perberos reported coldly, smoothly nocking another arrow and putting it directly through the eye socket of a lunging wolf instead to temporarily break its momentum.

"Hold them down!" Josh ordered, his chest heaving as he deflected a brutal, double-claw swipe with his shield. "Just keep breaking them until Brett is finished!"

The fight devolved into an agonising, incredibly gritty war of pure attrition.

The party was essentially fighting immortal meat shields. Every time Josh crushed a skull with his heavy steel shield, or Bhel hacked a limb entirely off, the Crypt Weaver simply pulsed more dark mana down the magical conduits. The wolves would heal their horrific injuries in mere seconds, standing back up to resume their relentless, perfectly synchronised assault.

The mud of the clearing was quickly churned into a thick, bloody soup. Josh was bleeding from a dozen minor scratches where the claws had slipped past his guard. The wounds throbbed with a sickening intensity every time he swung his longsword. He used his Lodestone Taunt repeatedly, projecting magnetic pulses from his armour to physically drag the wolves away from Carcan's failing golden shield, forcing himself to take the brunt of the punishment.

Bhel was covered from head to toe in foul, black blood, having completely abandoned finesse. He simply waded into the wolves, hacking wildly, roaring dwarven curses as he repeatedly chopped the beasts to pieces, only to watch them reassemble themselves.

"I am running out of breath, lad!" Bhel shouted, planting his boot on a wolf's chest to wrench his axe free from its ribcage. "We cannot do this all afternoon!"

Carcan was pale and trembling. She kept her healing hum steady, projecting minor regenerative waves over Josh and Bhel to keep their stamina up, but maintaining the golden dome while simultaneously healing was draining her mana reserves at a terrifying rate.

Above them, the canopy was a chaotic storm of light. Brett’s living white flames were systematically devouring the sprawling network of grey webs. The intense heat was causing the frozen mud below to steam, creating a thick, foul-smelling fog in the clearing.

Finally, with a loud, triumphant crackle, the last major supporting strand of the necrotic web was incinerated.

The Crypt Weaver, suddenly lacking its massive scaffolding, dropped heavily from the canopy. It landed in the centre of the clearing with a sickening clatter of bone, its eight sharpened femur-legs stabbing deep into the mud.

Brett gasped, his eyes snapping back to full awareness. He staggered slightly, his chest heaving, his pale skin covered in a sheen of sweat. The massive expenditure of controlled magic had severely drained him.

He looked at the chaotic melee. He saw the glowing green threads connecting the Weaver to the wolves. He tapped into his Aura of the Hearth-Forge. A wave of intense, dry heat washed over Josh and Bhel. Instantly, the heavy steel of Josh’s longsword and the dark iron of Bhel’s twin axes erupted into roaring, magical flames.

"The threads are pure dark mana!" Brett shouted to his friends, his hands glowing with a fresh, concentrated burst of white-hot plasma. "You cannot cut them with simple steel! You must overpower the necrotic energy with elemental heat! Burn the strings!"

Josh did not hesitate. He sidestepped a lunging wolf, parrying its snapping jaws with his shield. He brought his flaming longsword up and slashed horizontally, aiming perfectly for the glowing green conduit extending from the beast's spine.

The magical fire on the blade clashed violently with the concentrated dark mana of the thread. There was a sharp, explosive hiss, and a bright flash of conflicting energies.

The glowing green thread snapped, but wriggled like a worm as it tried to reconnect.

The connection was broken. The dire wolf immediately let out a confused, gurgling whine. It lost the terrifying, unnatural coordination it had possessed moments before. It stumbled in the mud, reduced from a puppeted nightmare to a simple, mindless undead beast.

"Now kill it!" Josh roared.

Perberos was already moving. He dropped his bow entirely, drawing his twin curved elven blades as he sprinted forward. He moved with a liquid, lethal grace, sliding under the confused wolf’s guard. He drove both of his pristine blades upwards, directly into the soft tissue beneath the beast's jaw, piercing its rotting brain.

The wolf collapsed instantly. It fell heavily into the mud, a massive, bloody, rotting corpse that smelled strongly of the grave.

"One down!" Bhel laughed fiercely. The dwarf charged the second wolf. He did not aim for the beast itself. He leaped into the air, crossing his flaming axes in a downward strike directly onto the glowing green thread attaching it to the Weaver. The magical fire severed the connection with a loud crack.

As the second wolf stumbled, completely disoriented, Josh activated his Stormforged Adamantite skill. Raw blue electricity surged down his arm, interlacing with Brett's magical flames on his sword. He brought the heavy, electrified blade down in a brutal, decapitating strike. The beast's head was severed cleanly, the flesh cauterised instantly. It joined its packmate in the mud, a permanent, physical corpse.

The Crypt Weaver shrieked. It was a horrific sound, the noise of a hundred human skulls grinding together in pure fury.

It realised its puppets were failing. It snapped the final thread itself and turned its massive, bulbous body towards Brett, identifying the mage as the primary threat to its magic.

The Weaver charged.

It moved with terrifying, arachnid speed, its sharpened bone legs stabbing the earth in a rapid, chaotic rhythm. It was easily the size of a small carriage, glowing with sick, green light.

"Get back!" Josh roared at the mage.

Josh triggered his Adamantine Breach skill. He became a human battering ram, launching himself across the muddy clearing to intercept the massive dungeon boss. He slammed his heavy shield directly into the side of the Weaver’s skull-fused abdomen.

The impact was tremendous. The Weaver was knocked sideways, several of the skulls comprising its body shattering into pieces, but it did not fall. It simply scrambled on its eight legs, reorienting itself with horrifying agility.

It raised one of its massive, sharpened front legs and brought it down in a devastating, piercing strike aimed directly at Josh’s chest.

Josh planted his boots and activated Titan’s Anchor.

He became rooted to the muddy earth. A shimmering, highly charged frontal shield of raw blue energy flared to life just inches in front of his physical steel shield.

The sharpened femur crashed into the energy barrier. The Titan’s Anchor held firm, absorbing the immense kinetic force, but the sheer weight of the blow drove Josh’s knees an inch deep into the mud. His teeth ground together as the magical barrier lashed back, sending a massive, high-voltage shock of pure lightning directly up the Weaver's leg.

The beast shrieked as the electricity scrambled its dark magic.

"Chop the legs!" Josh commanded through gritted teeth, holding the monster in place.

Bhel happily obliged. The dwarf ducked under the Weaver's thrashing body. His flaming axes moved in a blur of lethal iron. He struck the joints of the creature's back legs, shattering the femurs with brutal, repeated blows. The Weaver listed heavily to one side, its mobility severely compromised.

But the dungeon boss was far from finished.

Its abdomen glowed brightly, and it opened a massive, jagged maw hidden beneath its skull-plated body. It did not spit acid. It fired a thick, concentrated stream of wet, grey necrotic webbing directly at Carcan.

The webbing moved incredibly fast.

"Carcan!" Perberos yelled, sprinting towards his sister.

Carcan raised her staff, her golden dome flaring brightly, but she was completely exhausted. The golden shield flickered and died just as the grey webbing struck.

The necrotic net hit Carcan squarely in the chest, wrapping tightly around her arms and torso. The webbing was incredibly heavy, and it immediately began to harden, contracting like a python. Carcan gasped, her staff falling to the mud as she was pulled off her feet, dragging her towards the clicking maw of the Weaver.

Brett reacted with pure, unthinking instinct. He did not bother casting a projectile. He sprinted forward, his fur cloak billowing behind him, and threw himself to the ground beside the healer. He grabbed the thick, grey webbing with his bare hands.

He pumped raw, white-hot thermal energy directly from his palms into the net. The heat was so intense it blistered his own fingers, but the necrotic webbing instantly ignited, burning away to brittle ash in seconds. Carcan coughed, pulling herself free from the burning remnants.

"Thank you," she rasped, clutching her chest.

"Stay down," Brett ordered, his face pale. He looked up at the massive, thrashing Weaver, his eyes narrowed with absolute hatred. "Josh! End this!"

Josh was already moving. He dropped his Titan’s Anchor, the blue shield fading. The Weaver was crippled, missing two legs and disoriented from the electrical shocks, but its green core was still glowing brightly.

Perberos saw the opening. The elf drew a single arrow, moving with total calm amidst the chaos. He aimed not for the body, but for a small gap between the fused skulls where the green necrotic light shone brightest.

He loosed the arrow.

It threaded the needle perfectly, sinking deep into the dark core of the monster. The Weaver shrieked, its body convulsing wildly, distracted entirely by the sudden internal damage.

Josh closed the final distance. His longsword was wreathed in a brilliant, blinding cage of static electricity and Brett’s magical fire. He leaped into the air, bringing the heavy, electrified steel down in a brutal, two-handed strike directly onto the centre of the Weaver's skull-plated back.

The blade sheared cleanly through the bone armour. The static discharge exploded outward with a blinding flash of blue and white light, followed immediately by a roaring pillar of magical fire.

The Crypt Weaver stiffened completely. The green light within its abdomen flickered violently and died.

The massive dungeon beast collapsed into the mud.

For a single second, it lay there, a chaotic pile of shattered skulls and burnt bone. Then, the system rules asserted themselves over the dungeon spawn.

The dark, necrotic magic holding the monster together completely evaporated. The entire massive structure of the Weaver rapidly broke down, dissolving instantly into a shimmering, waist-deep pile of fine golden dust that settled softly over the bloody, frozen mud.

Silence rushed back into the clearing, broken only by the heavy, ragged breathing of the exhausted party.

Josh leaned heavily on his longsword, his chest heaving, ignoring the dull, throbbing ache in his side and the burning exhaustion in his muscles. He looked at the massive pile of golden dust, and then over at the three bloody, rotting corpses of the native dire wolves resting nearby. The contrast was stark and deeply unsettling.

Bhel sat down heavily on a nearby rock, his axes resting in the mud at his feet. The dwarf was covered in black blood and grime, looking completely drained.

"That," Bhel wheezed, wiping a thick smear of gore from his braided beard, "was an incredibly unpleasant way to spend a lunch break."

Brett slowly pushed himself up from the mud, offering a hand to help Carcan to her feet. The mage looked at his blistered fingers, sighing heavily. "I believe I have lost my appetite entirely."

Perberos retrieved his arrows from the rotting corpses of the wolves, wiping them clean on a patch of dead moss. He looked up at the sky, checking the position of the pale sun through the gaps in the dead canopy.

"We need to keep moving," Perberos announced quietly, his voice devoid of emotion. "The noise of that fight will draw everything within a two-mile radius. We must complete our grid sector before dark."

Josh nodded slowly, forcing himself to stand straight. He sheathed his sword with a heavy, metallic click. The fight with the Weaver was over, but the grim, methodical task of clearing the forest had only just begun. They were bruised, battered, and utterly exhausted, but the line had to be held.

"Check your gear," Josh ordered, his voice echoing dully in the dead woods. "We march in five minutes."
