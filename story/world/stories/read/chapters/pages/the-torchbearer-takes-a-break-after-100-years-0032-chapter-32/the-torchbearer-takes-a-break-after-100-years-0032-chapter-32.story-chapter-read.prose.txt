Activated his skill, Miles and found himself in another warehouse and unsurprisingly, this one was a little smaller. After all, stasis-preserved plates didn’t take as much space as desks, especially when they were all organized and staggered on rows upon rows of floating glass-like shelves.

It was a good thing the Skill allowed him such liberties within its confines. So long as he didn’t try to pull them out into the real world, he could create any physical substance he was familiar with to organize and display everything he had stocked in here.

“Now, let’s see what we got,” he hummed, scratching under his chin as he browsed the half-empty shelves. He hoped his summons weren't having too much trouble brining everyone in and if they were… well. He hoped they wouldn't be the cause of yet another incident.

Brie sipped from her sweet and refreshingly cold mana tea as she watched Thalia sprint after the ice-casting Seeker, her spearhead gleaming in the arena’s light. This little occasion—this break… helped. It allowed her to take a step back and see everything that she’d been pulled into from a softer light.

Right. Gonna have to address that soon, she recalled. Then again, they had bigger fish to fry right now. Putting her empty cup down, Brie got up and stretched her hands above her head, stealing a glance at the spellbook floating a couple of inches away from her as she considered how she would start her next spar. She was here to get some practice in. So she might as well do that.

Now, how do I manage the next fight? Gotta keep them on their toes, she thought, her gaze flitting from one page to the next of her anemic spellbook, which was an issue she’d be able to fix soon. Brie was still trying to wrap her head about all that had taken place, and she would need time to parse through everything, of course. But the best news was that she might get to have her a adventure after all. One without her family pulling strings to wrap her up in their politics and mess with her progress or give her an easy ride.

With Talon backing her—or at least, staying out of her way—Brie would have the opportunity to carve her own path. Her family would try to intervene, of course. She was absolutely sure of that. But she’d get a few weeks—if not months—of freedom first. To explore and see the Dungeon for herself. To have her own adventure.

That thought made her lips quirk up, but the smile was quickly dashed when she turned her attention back to Thalia’s fight, and Brie realized she might need to get to training or she won’t be able to keep up.

Thalia was still going strong, an effort which would have had Brie literally crawling on the ground like a worm and just like her, the ice mage was flagging, barely able to get away from the Vanguard. He threw himself to the side, letting out a half-shout, half-curse as he barely dodged the jabbing spear before trying to get back up to his feet, only for his legs to betray him. He still managed to recover, but then the spear shaft swung and took his legs from under him with thunk loud enough for Brie’s own shins to spasm at the probable pain. Grimacing, Brie watched as the red-faced, agonizing man looked up just in time to see a spear pointed at his throat, at which point the judge finally called the spar.

Thalia was barely sweating. But that was to be expected. Vanguards—on average—had the stamina to handle long, drawn out fights. By contrast, most Seekers tended to offload their mana and stamina pretty fast, after which they would be sitting ducks. Even Brie had to find some work around that reality, and it doubly sucked because she didn’t have the offensive output of some other Seekers, so she had to be smart about her resources. Though when she watched Thalia spar, she couldn’t help but feel a little jealous.

More in common with Shadows than a Seekers, he’d told her. He recommended she’d lean into it, but Brie wasn’t sure how she felt about that. She always saw herself hovering above the battlefield, flinging big spells and cackling like a madwoman, with a spellbook glowing with enough magic to rival the sun. She didn’t want to be skulking about in the shadows like a common rogue. Setting traps. Surprising monsters and delvers. Seeing what they were up to without them noticing her. Taking a peek at their loot and taking a little something-something if they happened to be bad.

Come to think of it, that doesn’t sound too bad, she thought, tapping her lips with a finger. She had to admit, tricking her opponents into going after the wrong illusion was also fun. But there was no denying that she lacked some firepower, especially if she ever needed to deal with monsters. She might be well equipped to fight other delvers but large creatures… less so. Hopefully once she got her permit, she’d be a little better equipped to deal with that issue. The Seekers’ guild had plenty of bounties for reagents and material that paid in either coin, spell tutelage, or spell scrolls—when those were available—so she’d soon have her pick of new magics to add to her spellbook.

“As soon as all this gets resolved, that is,” she muttered, glancing behind and up at Lott and the security details watching them. Brie was no stranger to finding herself in the games of those more powerful than her, but it still rankled. The whole thing with Thalia and her brother, who, in this scenario, was the eye of the storm. It was no coincidence that the day the Wanderer broke out of the Gate’s checkpoint was the day he showed up. She hadn’t seen him take the mask of, but if it walked like a duck, quacked like duck, then he most likely was a duck.

And the powers that be did not like unaffiliated ducks.

Brie signaled the instructor in charge that she was ready to go again as she continued mulling the situation over. This whole mess was likely factions fighting for control over a promising element, and Brie could see why. She didn’t really have a good picture of what Miles was capable of, but between that Cauldron, the portal into his private dimension, and Gods know what else, it made perfect sense that the world’s factions wanted to put a leash on him. And like any headhunting campaign, foul play was to be expected. That was probably why Talon had left his security to watch over them.

Glancing up again at them, Brie expected to find the duo where she’d left them barely moments ago, only for her eyes to widen as she realized they were running toward her, leaping off the chairs as they sprinted down, their gaze off to the right.

Brie’s mana surged as she called for her illusions, ready to scamper away in the blink of an eye. But aside from a surprisingly tall and wide man with an eye patch and a green-eyed woman with a smile that seemed too wide, there didn’t seem to be any other threat. She was waving at Lott, whose face Brie watched drain of color, going from a concerned frown to a pale, hand-in-the-cookie-jar stare as the strangers approached.

Lowering her arms, Brie resisted the urge to sigh.

At least they did manage a few hours of training without getting some fresh drama dropped on them.

The Dungeon’s gotta be more peaceful than this, she thought as she slowly backed away from the beginning argument between Talon’s security and these two newcomers. At least Lott seemed to know one of them.

There was less risk of crossfire this way.

“Leave the—umph”

The masculine voice of the ambushers cut off as his midsection was suddenly wrapped around a thigh-thick, vibrant green vine that had unrolled itself out of a nearby windowsill, while the second figure tried to run, only to stumble and crack their head against a club shaped root that pulled itself out of the ground.

Within a couple more seconds, Hazel had her roots wrapped tightly against the throats of the wanna-be ambushers and with them being immobilized, she could peer into their faces. One of them she recognized from the prior loop when she was having a drink with George while they watched the guild. But she didn’t recognize the second, which meant there might be more than one faction playing this game, and that this would likely not be the last of their attempts.

“I don’t believe this will be the last interruption,” Hazel said, smiling while the two girls—and the two Inquisitor agents—stared fearfully at the ambushers as the captives flailed uselessly, trying to cut the roots while they strained for air. “Little Thalia. They wouldn’t be able to use the information anyway. She’d get the kids through to the Custodial Room and everything else would be forgotten once Miles pulls them into another loop. Now that Hazel took a second to look the woman who raised Miles for a few years, she could see some of that same fire within her. Seren was not as tall as her, but she was on the taller side, for a human. Sharp brown eyes stared up at her, and she made no move to clear the graying lock of hair out of her vision though aside from the bit of gray, Hazel had to say, the woman’s hair was strikingly dark.

Still, before she moved away from the window, she gently turned one of the pots so that one of the smaller plant could get some extra light and as she retrieved Miles’ key from a hidden pocket, she wondered if she should have tested the key beforehand. Well. As for Hazel, she just stepped around them—which made Seren focus on her for half-a-second, though Hazel just closed the door. And just like that, a mission was done, and a new one was about to start. And as if sensing her intent, the two girls and the cowardly Shadow turned toward her, their smile at the sweet reunion fading as the witch smiled at them.

They were going to get along great.
