Chapter 370

“You’re kidding,” Heath declared with the tone of a man filled with doubt.

“I am absolutely not kidding,” Eik assured him. “As a matter of fact, I have never been more serious in my whole life.”

The smith sent him a look that spelled out precisely what he thought of that particular statement. “You can say it as many times as you want. I will never believe that your newest and most powerful buff yet turns you into a fuckin’ bird.”

“Dude, I swear, it’s true! Like, I grow feathers and all that jazz.”

“Why the hell would you grow feathers? It makes no sense.”

“Profound Toxin used to look like a big-ass feathery bastard, I tell you!”

A whip-like tentacle of blue goo lashed out of Eik’s forearm and slapped him across the face hard. The deafening sound of the slap echoed through the living room. “Av, shit!”

“That’s a bunch of bull and we both know it, homie!” Heath bit back, ignoring the Profound Toxic Bitch Slap. “He’s a formless blue blob!”

Again, a tendril emerged, stretched across the tea table and served up a helping of Extra Mild Profound Toxic Bitch Slap to both of Heath’s cheeks. He hissed and put his hands over his face. “Hey! What the hell? That one was true!”

A couple of seconds later, the tentacle came out again and wiggled about sheepishly, as if to say ‘yeah, okay, that one’s my bad, buddy.’

“I can prove it!”

“Oh, yeah? I’d like to see you try!”

“All right, fine!” Eik snapped and shot up from the couch, reaching for Toxin. “Wait here for a second and I’ll come back and make you eat your wor—”

A knock on the door interrupted Eik just as he was about to send himself off to the distant world he reigned over. It was quick and frantic, startling Heath slightly.

Normally, Eik would have sensed it when someone approached his home, but this time he had been so distracted by his attempt to convince Heath that Omnicide did, in fact, turn him into a freaky bird man, that he had failed to recognize the two very familiar aura signatures walking up the path in his garden. With flourishing hand gesture, the door flung open.

Meri stood with her hand raised, getting out a few more air knocks before she could react. “M-Mr. Magnasen!” she gasped. “You’re here!”

“Of course, I’m here. It’s my damn house. Why wouldn’t I be here”

Behind her stood her grandfather, guild master of the Alchemist Guild of Gimleh Ganyak hir Gawak. Briefly they exchanged glances. “You’re just… always so busy, so we kind of thought you would be…”

He frowned “So, who were you hoping to find?”

“Ah, no, it was you we were hoping to talk to.”

“Then what was the point of coming here if you thought I wouldn’t even be here?”

“Well, it’s just that we wanted to be the ones to deliver the news ourselves, so we took a chance and hoped you would be home despite realistic expectations,” Ganyak hir Gawak told him.

The team of alchemists working on the grade 7 potion recipe on Eik’s request must have been burning the midnight oil when working with his body parts if they were looking for him this early.

Well, they had either been working extra hard on this one, or success was simply faster and easier to conclude than failure. Although he would be grateful for the first possibility, Eik had been desperately hoping for the latter as he opened the door for the guild master and Meri.

And judging by the excited grins on their faces, the latter might well be the case this time.

“Oh, yeah? I hope it’s good news at least,” Eik said, failing to hold back an expectant smirk.

Meri pursed her lips as she considered it. “Well, I guess… a lot of good news mixed in with a considerable but, I would say, tolerable amount of bad news.”

Eik blinked. “Bad news? There is bad news as well?”

“Unfortunately, yes,” the guild master admitted sheepishly.

“What do you want to hear first?” Meri asked, bouncing excitedly on the balls of her feet.

“Uuh, the good, then, I guess.”

“Excellent choice, Mr. Magnasen!” Ganyak hir Gawak said. “Well, the good news is that we have successfully identified a sequence of properties that are ideal for the creation of grade 7 potions.”

“Seriously?” Eik asked. “That’s amazing! I’ll get started on mass production right away!”

“Certainly!” the guild master said with an enthusiastic nod. “If you would follow us to the laboratory, we will run you through the recipe right away.”

Eik threw on his jacket as he looked back at Heath. “Sorry, buddy, but I’m afraid I’ll have to skedaddle for now. I am headed out to get strong as all hell, boi!”

The smith was already in the middle of putting on his boots when he looked up with a face painted in the colors of disappointment. “What? You’re not even letting me come along?”

With hesitation, Eik glanced at the two alchemists who could only shrug. A grin split his lips. “All right, come one, then, you rascal, you!”

“Wooo, yeah!” Heath whooped and leaped through the open door. But before he even made it to the gate at the end of the garden path, he turned back with a question. “By the way, you guys said that there were some bad news as well, didn’t you?”

“Oh, yeah, you did,” Eik added with a nod. “’considerable but tolerable, if I’m not mistaken. What is it?”

Meri looked to her grandfather before eventually wavering in the end. “Well… it’s…”

“Yes?” Eik pressed, feeling a nugget of concern churn around in his stomach.

“We’re going to have to cut off your ass. Like, many, many times.”

“Okay, okay, I get it,” Eik grumbled. “Ha. Ha. Ha. Yes, it’s very funny and all that, but I think the joke has died down a little bit at this point.”

That only set Heath off again. He was having trouble getting words out between fits of laughter as their small group made their way through the various rooms and corridors of the main building of Eik’s Excellent Elixir Emporium, heading for the well-equipped laboratory in the basement. “Dude, did you forget? Whenever you want to make an X-ranked grade seven Potion of Mighty Strength, you’re going to have to cut off your own ass, because your ass cheek is the main ingredient!”

On occasion, the smith would be so overwhelmed by mirth, that he could barely draw in a breath. He put on a dumb accent and pretended to twirl an invisible mustache in a mockery of a sleazy businessman. “I am so sorry, sir. I am afraid I am going to have to ask you to come back and purchase our fine potions at a later date. You see, we’re a little behind on production.”

“Oh… my god,” Eik sighed as they descended the stairs into the underground.

Heath couldn’t help himself and continued. “Yes, I know it’s slow going, sir, but despite our best attempts to keep up with demand, our lovely boss, Eik, keeps butting in.”

Both Meri and the guild master were desperately trying but utterly failing to refrain from openly laughing right in front of Eik, but he could hardly blame them. He had to admit that it was pretty funny, and he would probably not have been able to hold back nearly as well as them if he had been in their shoes. Frankly, the only reason he wasn’t laughing along with them was because, in about ten to fifteen minutes, he would be lying down on his stomach to have his ass cut off over and over again.

At this point, Heath had gotten so into it, that he was hopping from foot to foot while he pretended to be juggling multiple butts. “No, sir, I’m afraid I am unable to offer a timeline for the production. I don’t want to assume anything. Because, as you well know, when you assume you make an ass out of U and M.E, aka Magnasen, Ei—”

The apprentice smith almost got that last bit of his terrible joke out, but a band of crystalline toxin suddenly appeared and tightened over his mouth. The man clawed at it with his fingers, but he could have smashed it between two literal mountains and it would still have come away the winner.

“Show me the god damned recipe,” Eik rumbled as they stopped in front of the door to the laboratory, his eyes forming two orbs of thunder.

Frantically employing every sliver of power granted to him by Eik’s Toxic Initiation, guild master Ganyak hir Gawak dashed forward to push open the doors for the Supreme Divinity of Toxin. “Yes, sir!”

The first thing that struck him was the stench. Normally he would have been completely fine with something like this, but there was something about this particular smell that made his stomach turn. The second thing was the absolute state of the laboratory.

It was an absolute mess. A disgusting, bloody mess.

The rest of the team of hired alchemists were still in the middle of working, some recording findings and methodology in quick, handwritten scribbles while others seemed to be continuing the analyses of the high-level ingredients.

With both walls, tables, and floors stained red, the origin of the stench was not especially difficult to figure out. “Is that my…?” Eik trailed off, gesturing widely at the space with a hand.

“Your blood? Yes, experimentation tends to get a little messy, especially when we get excited, and, I must say, working with parts from an X-ranked creatu— from you, was was one of the highlights of my career,” Meri admitted.

Images of Eik’s own early attempts at learning alchemy, and the countless times his mixtures exploded right in hid face flowed through his mind. “Yeah, I know something about that as well.”

“We think it’s because some of your amiable nature still exists inside your body parts, even after they’ve been cut out of you, but you weren’t nearly as difficult to work with as that spawn you brought us. Much less hostile and explosive. We had a significant number of close calls with the spawn.”

Some of the other alchemists looked up at that, the looks in their eyes telling Eik that, if anything, Meri was actually downplaying how tough it had been.

“Thanks for your good work, everyone,” Eik said to them all. “I will make sure that all of you are properly rewarded for what you have achieved here.”

Excited conversation erupted among the alchemists and they came up to thank him for his generosity. Quickly, he redirected the topic back to the new recipe, and the guild master took him to one of the larger, central tables where a cauldron had already been prepared for the purpose.

“I believe it would be better if we started with a smaller sample, just to test it out in practice first. Everything you need is in this bag of holding there on the table. If you would follow my instructions, let’s get started on the grade seven Potion of Mighty Strength.”
