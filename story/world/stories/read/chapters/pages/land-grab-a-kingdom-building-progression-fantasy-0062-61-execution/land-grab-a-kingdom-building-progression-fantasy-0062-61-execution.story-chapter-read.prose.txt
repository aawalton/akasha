The sun was still low in the sky as Tavian’s cart rolled back into Tavirost fortress. Behind him, Lord Anarvia lay in the cart, bound and gagged. A column of soldiers marched alongside him. Flies buzzed around the bodies of dead ghouls in the streets.

But the atmosphere was triumphant. Within the fortress, everything was celebration.

The gates had been thrown open. The whole city now knew the truth. Tavian had been victorious.

As they arrived in the outer courtyard, Tavian noticed the body of a man dangling from the upper window of the prison tower. It appeared that Erond had already seen fit to begin executions.

A long tail of hair dangled from a shaved head; though the face was black and swollen, Tavian knew it was Mandros, the Karath Amon spy.

Erond greeted Tavian as people gathered around in the courtyard.

“What did you have to do?” Tavian asked, nodding toward the dangling body.

“Killed him,” Erond said. “He was using a polished food tray and a lamp to send signals out the window. I don’t know what he was saying, or if they were received.”

“Was the public display necessary?” Tavian asked.

Erond looked meaningfully to the prisoner in Tavian’s cart. “I wanted the Karath Amon to know that we knew,” he said, with a shrug.

Tavian would have preferred the man alive, but what was done was done. They hadn’t yet survived this crisis; the enemy’s vanguard had already arrived, and more Karath Amon would be arriving on the outskirts of the city tomorrow, according to the scouts.

“Anax is dead,” Erond said. “Who takes this man’s place, if you execute him? Ilara?”

“Ilara tried to kill me,” Tavian scoffed. “She’s fled to the Karath Amon. There is no more House Anarvia.”

“Lady Anarvia?”

Tavian hesitated. He hadn’t even considered the woman. Was she even still alive?

“Someone has to do the work that House Anarvia was doing. They managed much of the city.”

“Why can’t Veros do it?” Tavian asked.

Erond shrugged. “If you upset the balance of power, will Calenor and Brask support you? Tiren is sheltered here by necessity, and Valliroth’s support is questionable. He disapproves of your choices. Will Donath stay loyal when the other Houses defect?”

“They’d be fools to defect,” Tavian said. “We have an enemy on our doorstep!”

“You’re too idealistic, Tavian,” Erond said. “You have to align their interests with yours.”

“Is Lady Anarvia even still alive?”

“I believe she’s with the prisoners.”

Tavian bit back a snappy rejection of the idea. “Thank you for your wise thoughts, Uncle,” he said. “I’ll take it into consideration.” Did he really want a continuation of the system that had gotten them into this mess in the first place?

The crowd gathering around the cart knew what was about to happen. More and more people filled the courtyard. Tavian scanned the crowd. Veros green, Donath gold, and even Calenoran blue were represented. Clusters of Tiren silver, and even a man wearing Brask’s red-orange.

A clump of purple-clad Anarvian loyalists were under guard, but still watched. In all, there had to be at least two thousand people gathered here. Perhaps a tenth of Tar Corinath’s population.

Everyone was here to see Lord Anarvia’s execution.

No, that wasn’t quite right.

Everyone was here to see what Tavian would say about Lord Anarvia’s execution. Word of what happened here would be all over the city before noon. The whole city would know what kind of ruler Tavian was.

Tavian grabbed a messenger page. “I need you to find Lady Anarvia,” he said. “Bring her here.”

The boy ran off, and Tavian stood, turning to address the crowd. He would deal with this quickly, and then they could get to the important work of strengthening the city for the impending Karath Amon attack.

“Hail Tar Corinath!” Tavian shouted, and received a partial reply, as all eyes turned toward him.

“Hail Tar Corinath!” he cried again, and this time his soldiers joined him.

“Hail Tar Corinath!” Veros men roared the chant back to Tavian.

The chant did not spread to the soldiers of other Houses. They seemed uncertain, though optimistic.

Then he lowered his arm and waited for the cheering to stop. A frothy, buzzing optimism rippled through the crowd. Were things good? No.

But they were winning.

It was time for Tavian to seize the moment.

“Men and women of Tar Corinath,” Tavian bellowed, and a hush fell over the crowd.

“We’ve been betrayed by traitors!”

Shouts of agreement erupted from the crowd, devolving into chants of “Kill him!”

The Anarvian prisoners in the audience paled.

“An enemy camps on our doorstep,” Tavian declared. “Today, we prepare to meet the greatest foe Tar Corinath has ever known. We need every citizen of this great city to rise to the challenge.

“I have no quarrel with the servants of House Anarvia, who did not understand the depths of their master’s treachery. I grant amnesty for any who will lay aside their purple livery and swear fealty to Veros.”

Relief washed over the Anarvian prisoners at the back of the crowd. One man sagged, leaning against a wall. A murmur of discontent rolled through the soldiers of Veros and Donath.

He glanced down at Lord Anarvia, bound and kneeling in the cart.

“Farl Anarvia—Tavian intentionally left off the honorific, calling the man by his given name—conspired with trusted advisors of my father’s household to destroy our Kingdom Core. Now, his son is dead. Master Jarden is dead. Master Halin has fled with Ilara.”

And Ilara had taken his sword. He was determined to get it back.

“Cut off his head!” someone screamed.

“Such is the fate of traitors to our city.”

A cry of “Death to traitors!” erupted from the soldiers.

“Lord Donath,” Tavian said, gesturing to the man. “Join me.” Donath pushed through the crowd, as his men chanted his name. He was weary, his clothes still bloodstained from the fighting of the night, but his movements were invigorated by the rush of victory.

Donath’s men cheered loudly as the head of their House mounted the cart.

“What are you doing, Tavian?” Donath asked.

“I’m uniting them,” Tavian said.

Tavian scanned the crowd for other heads of Houses. Valliroth was not present, though a group of silver-robed house servants were, as well as a knot of priests, bald heads shining in the morning light.

A single man wore Brask colors; an emissary. Brask himself was still holed up on his private island.

Lady Calenor was not present, but Vaden’s men—who had joined Tavian last night—were.

Vaden had died bravely.

“Who leads you?” Tavian asked the Calenorian soldiers.

They indicated a man wearing the insignia of a Squad Captain. He stepped forward from the crowd, and Tavian elevated him to the platform beside him.

“Last night, these brave men of Calenor joined with us on our raid against the enemy,” Tavian said. “Their Block Lieutenant was slain in an ambush, yet they persevered.” He noted that of the sixty or so men that had joined him at the warehouse, perhaps only forty remained. A high casualty rate. Other blue-clad men had joined them this morning, and Tavian was certain that what he said this morning would be reported back to Lady Calenor within the hour.

Tavian wanted everyone to know that he would be a king for all.

“I am awarding each of these men with the Star of Veros.”

A wave of surprised mutterings washed through Tavian and Donath’s men. The Calenorians were stunned. It was unheard of to offer that kind of recognition to servants outside one’s own house. A rumble of discontent passed through the Tiren servants.

Donath glanced at Tavian, uncomfortable.

Perhaps Erond was right about respecting the balance of power. He pressed on before anyone could challenge him.

“As for Lord Anarvia,” Tavian announced. “He has been judged guilty. I strip him of his title, his holdings, and all his rights and privileges as head of household.”

He turned to face the man. “Farl Anarvia, prepare yourself.”

The defeated man stared blankly past Tavian. He’d already given up.

Tavian nodded to Donath and the Calenorian man.

“Tavian,” Donath said, grabbing Tavian’s arm and whispering into his ear. “Who will replace House Anarvia?”

At that moment, a commotion near the gate drew Tavian’s attention. A cart approached; Lady Anarvia seated alongside the page that he’d sent for her. She looked terrible, as though she’d endured a night of fighting alongside the soldiers. Maybe she had. Her face was pale and bruised, her hair ruffled, her cloak mud spattered.

She sat taller as the whole audience turned toward her, then made a move to stand. This was his chance to recognize the old system. To keep the structure that they’d always known.

He could let Lady Anarvia swear fealty to him, publicly. The house could go on, fulfilling the roles they’d had for generations.

Tavian looked through the gate to the core courtyard, where blasted ground from the destroyed Core was still visible.

Tavirost had ruled over one kingdom, not six competing Houses. The division in Tar Corinath is what had led to this civil war.

Tavian turned his back to the woman.

“I claim my house,” Lady Anarvia shouted. “I reject my husband’s crimes. House Anarvia did not betray Tar Corinath. I will swear fealty!”

Tavian ignored her cries.

“With Farl Anarvia, so goes House Anarvia,” Tavian declared, gesturing for Donath and the Calenorian to hold the man.

Gasps rolled through the crowd. Lady Anarvia flushed, and sank back into her seat.

Donath grabbed one arm, the Calenorian captain the other, and they held Farl Anarvia before Tavian.

He drew a sword.

“Before us is an image. Two sides of the same coin,” Tavian declared. “What we can do working together, and what happens when we fight. An army of mercenaries, hired by Ilara Anarvia camps on our doorstep. Only united will we survive the coming storm.”

He turned back to his prisoner, resting the blade at the nape of Farl Anarvia’s neck.

Tavian drew the sword back, hands sweating. Perhaps he should have passed this job on to an executioner. If he botched this, his message would be lost.

He brough the sword down with all his might. Blade passed through flesh, turned on bone. Tavian carried through with the motion. Hot blood sprayed across his arms, spattered on his face.

The sword lodged in the wooden floor of the cart. Farl Anarvia’s head fell free, dropping to the wood with a thunk.

The man’s eyes stared at Tavian for a long moment, blinked twice, then hazed over.

Donath and the Calenorian released the body. It flopped heavily to the floor, blood spurting from the neck and pooling at Tavian’s feet. Lady Anarvia fainted at the death of her husband.

A few cries of triumph broke the silence of the rapt crowd. Arms were raised in victory. Tavian passed his blade to a nearby man at arms to clean.

The priests closed their eyes, faces turned toward the heavens.

A soldier close to the cart knelt. The men around him did the same.

Like a wave, everyone in the courtyard began kneeling.

Even Donath knelt, though he seemed unsettled by the gesture.

This wasn’t supposed to be about Tavian. It was about their city. Tavian faced the crowd, raised both his fists, and bellowed.

“Tar Corinath!”

Tavian’s voice echoed within the walls of the fortress.

“Tar Corinath!”

Someone stood and answered the cry with a shout of his own.

“Tavirost!”

The man invoked the name of Tavian’s forefather. Tavirost, griffin rider. Tavirost, the great uniter. Tavirost, the mighty. Tavirost, the bold.

“Tavirost!” More men stood, joining in the chant.

“Tavirost!”

By the fourth time, every man, woman, and child in the audience was screaming. Former men of Anarvia, Calenor, Donath, and even the servants of Tiren joined the chant.

The sound shook Tavian to his core. It filled him with energy.

“Tavirost!”

Only one man did not join the cheer. Brask’s emissary, looking sick and angry, forced his way through the crowd and out of the gates of the fortress.

Donath shot a curious gaze at Tavian. “Are you Tavirost the conqueror, then?” he asked softly.

Tavian wasn’t sure.

“Whatever it takes to defend our home,” he said, then turned back to the crowd. He raised his hand, and they fell silent.

“We have no time to waste!” Tavian declared. “Bring all the loyal to swear fealty! Bring the treasures of the kingdom to feed the core, and expand our influence! We will stand against the invaders!”

The crowd thundered its approval.
