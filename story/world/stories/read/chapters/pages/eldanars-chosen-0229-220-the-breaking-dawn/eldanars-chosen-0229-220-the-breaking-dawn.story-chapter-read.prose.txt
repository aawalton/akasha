The cramped upper dormitory of the Ashenfall Guildhall was filled with the heavy, rhythmic clanking of cold steel and the sharp pulling of thick leather straps.

Josh stood in the centre of the small room, his jaw clenched tight as he secured the final, heavy buckles of his master-crafted steel greathelm. He rolled his broad shoulders, feeling the familiar, uncompromising weight of his full plate armour settle perfectly over his thick padded gambeson. He reached down, picking up his newly acquired, dark steel heater shield, sliding his left arm through the heavy leather grips. The silver runes inlaid along the rim caught the dim candlelight, glowing with a faint, latent promise of kinetic absorption.

Across the room, Brett and Carcan were carefully assisting Bhel.

The dwarven fighter was sitting on the edge of the solid wooden bed, his face pale and drawn tight with suppressed pain. He stubbornly refused to make a single sound as Brett carefully tightened the leather straps of his repaired iron chest piece directly over the thick, pristine white medical bandages wrapping his torso.

"Not too tight on the lower left side," Carcan instructed softly, her silver eyes focused intensely on the dwarf's breathing patterns. Her staff leaned against the wooden wall nearby. "The internal tissue there is still incredibly fragile. If the iron plates compress the stitches during a heavy swing, he will haemorrhage internally."

"I am perfectly capable of monitoring my own bleeding, healer," Bhel grunted, though his dark eyes betrayed a profound, silent gratitude. He reached out with his thick, calloused hands, snatching his two heavy iron throwing axes from the bedside table and sliding them smoothly into the leather loops on his belt. "Besides, I do not intend to get hit today. I am strictly on the offensive."

Brett stepped back, wiping a sheen of cold sweat from his pale forehead. He pulled his shimmering crimson cloak tighter around his shoulders, pushing his imaginary spectacles up his nose.

"Right, that is everyone completely armoured," Brett announced, his voice lacking its usual sarcastic lilt. He looked around the small, candle-lit room, gesturing vaguely towards the wooden chairs. "Caistina said we have roughly three hours until dawn. We should sit down. Conserve our stamina. We might be standing on that wall for days."

Josh nodded slowly. He unclasped his heavy shield, resting it against the foot of the bed, and sat down heavily on a small wooden stool. The thick wood creaked dangerously under his immense, steel-clad bulk.

Carcan took a seat on the edge of the mattress next to Bhel, folding her pale green hands neatly in her lap. Brett leaned against the closed wooden door, crossing his arms and closing his eyes.

They tried to rest. They desperately tried to force their chaotic, racing minds to settle into a state of calm meditation.

However, the suffocating anticipation was unbearable. The quiet intimacy of the dormitory only served to magnify the deafening noise echoing from the city outside their small window. The frantic, continuous hammering of wood, the heavy grinding of stone, and the distant, muffled shouts of the city guard bleeding through the glass made it physically impossible to relax. The adrenaline coursing through their veins was thick and potent, completely rejecting the concept of sitting still.

After ten agonising minutes of absolute silence, Bhel let out a loud, frustrated groan.

"Ugh. I cannot do this," the dwarf complained bitterly, pushing himself up from the mattress. He winced slightly as his bruised ribs protested the sudden movement, but he ignored the pain, reaching for his heavy iron boots. "Sitting here waiting for the axe to fall is worse than actually taking the blow. I’ve not traded one quiet room for another. If I sit like this for even another hour, I am going to lose my mind."

"I agree," Josh said instantly, standing up and snatching his heavy shield from the floorboards. The relief of taking action washed over his stern face. "The waiting is the hardest part. Let’s head to the wall."

They left the quiet sanctuary of the upper dormitories, descending the heavy oak stairs into the chaotic, overflowing grand hall, and pushed their way out through the heavy wooden doors into the freezing pre-dawn air of the city.

Stepping into the streets of Ashenfall was like stepping directly into a disturbed anthill.

The city was an absolute blur of frantic, desperate activity. The wide cobblestone thoroughfares, usually reserved for merchant caravans and wealthy nobles, were entirely choked with a terrified river of humanity. The civilian evacuation towards the inner, subterranean cellars was in full swing.

Josh led his party through the surging crowds, acting as a wedge to part the chaotic tide. He watched mothers desperately clutching weeping children to their chests, their faces pale with sheer terror. Elderly citizens hobbled along, carrying small, heavy wooden lockboxes containing their entire life savings. The air was thick with the sounds of crying, shouted instructions, and the frantic barking of stray dogs caught in the panic.

"Keep moving towards the temple district!" a hoarse-voiced city guard shouted from a street corner, waving a burning torch to direct the flow of refugees. "The lower cellars are open! Do not stop in the streets! Do not look back!"

As the party pushed further north, moving against the flow of the fleeing civilians, they encountered the brutal, physical reality of the defensive engineering Captain Aris had promised.

The residential streets were being systematically destroyed to create massive, impassable chokepoints. Squads of sweating, exhausted labourers were dragging everything they could find into the main avenues. Josh watched a group of men forcefully overturn a massive, heavy wooden merchant wagon, shattering its wheels to create a solid barricade. Others were dragging solid iron anvils from the smithies, heavy oak dining tables, and thick wooden doors ripped straight from their iron hinges.

They were building secondary and tertiary walls entirely out of domestic junk. It was a chaotic, ugly labyrinth designed to turn the wide, open streets into a series of brutal, claustrophobic meat grinders.

Standing behind these crude barricades were the conscripted militia.

They were an incredibly sobering sight. There were no polished steel breastplates or unified tabards here. It was a desperate collection of bakers wearing soot-stained leather aprons, carrying rusted meat cleavers and heavy wooden clubs. Young blacksmith apprentices held crude, unsharpened iron spears, their hands shaking so violently the metal tips rattled against the cobblestones. They looked absolutely terrified, staring wide-eyed at the dark northern horizon.

Brett walked close behind Josh, his sharp, analytical pale eyes scanning the ragged lines of the militia.

"They are not all completely green," Brett murmured quietly, leaning closer to Carcan and pointing a long finger towards a small cluster of men standing behind a barricade of overturned carts.

Carcan looked where he was pointing. Amidst a group of terrified, shaking merchants, stood three older men. They wore simple, unassuming civilian clothes, but their posture was entirely different. They did not slouch, and they did not shake. They stood with a relaxed, balanced readiness, their feet planted firmly shoulder-width apart. They held their rusted swords not like heavy burdens, but like natural extensions of their own arms.

"Retired adventurers," Brett observed, profound respect colouring his quiet voice. "They likely settled down years ago to open shops or raise families. But look at their eyes. They are scanning the rooftops, checking their footing, and ensuring the panicked kids next to them keep their spear tips raised. They are going to anchor those civilian lines when the screaming starts."

"It is a heartbreaking necessity," Carcan whispered, her luminous silver eyes filled with deep sorrow as she watched an older woman awkwardly adjusting a boiled leather chest piece over her thick woollen dress. "They are offering their lives to buy the cellars a few extra minutes."

As they neared the Artisan District, bordering the final stretch to the northern gatehouse, they witnessed a display of desperate generosity that made Josh’s chest ache with heavy emotion.

The local merchants were no longer hoarding their stock or attempting to flee with their heavy lockboxes. They had entirely accepted the grim reality that gold and silver would be completely useless if the city fell to the dead.

A broad-shouldered beastkin armourer had dragged his massive wooden display racks directly out into the freezing street. He was frantically unbuckling high-tier, expensive leather cuirasses and tossing them freely into the arms of any passing militia member who lacked protection.

Further down the street, an alchemist was standing on a wooden crate, handing out potent, expensive red health potions to the terrified city guards marching towards the walls.

"Take it! Take it all!" a dwarven smith roared, his voice cracking with emotion as he shoved a master-crafted iron broadsword into the trembling hands of a young farm boy. "Better you use my steel to crack their skulls today than let those rotting bastards loot my forge tomorrow! Make them bleed for every inch!"

Josh gripped the leather handle of his heavy shield tightly, feeling the cold bite of the metal. The sheer, unyielding spirit of the city was humbling. These people were terrified, they were outmatched, and they were likely going to die, but they were absolutely refusing to go quietly into the dark.

They finally broke through the chaotic, crowded streets of the upper district and stepped into the massive, open plaza situated directly in front of the northern gatehouse.

The scale of the military preparations here was staggering.

Captain Aris and his stonemasons had achieved a miraculous feat of desperate engineering. A secondary, semi-circular wall of packed earth, heavy rubble, and shattered paving stones had been erected about twenty-five yards behind the main gates. It formed a massive, enclosing bowl, designed to completely trap any undead that managed to breach the primary entrance. A smaller, heavily reinforced iron sally port had been built into the centre of the rubble wall, allowing the defenders to retreat if the bottleneck fell.

The main gates themselves were a terrifying sight. The towering, thick timber doors were completely closed and barred. Dozens of heavy, thick wooden support beams had been jammed against the timber at extreme angles, braced against the cobblestone plaza to prevent the doors from buckling inward. Piles of heavy stone and packed dirt had been shoved against the base, creating a solid, unyielding barricade.

"They are not planning on opening those gates again anytime soon," Bhel grunted approvingly, looking at the massive timber braces. The dwarf adjusted the throwing axes on his belt, his dark eyes scanning the defensive layout. "If the horde wants to get into Ashenfall, they are going to have to physically chew their way through solid iron and oak."

"Up the stairs," Josh commanded quietly, pointing towards the heavy wooden scaffolding built against the massive stone walls flanking the gatehouse. "We need to find our assigned positions before the commanders lock the grid."

They pushed their way past squads of heavily armoured spearmen and shouting officers, ascending the winding, incredibly steep wooden stairs leading to the upper ramparts. The higher they climbed, the colder and sharper the freezing winter wind became, cutting through their heavy clothing.

They finally reached the top of the wall, stepping out onto the wide, wooden walkway running the entire length of the city's northern defences.

The ramparts were a hive of activity. Mages were checking their lines of sight, their hands glowing with residual fire and lightning. Rangers were meticulously lining up quivers of heavy steel-tipped arrows along the wooden parapets. Small groups of fighters carrying heavy maces and broadswords were taking up positions directly behind the casters, ready to repel any undead that managed to scale the high timber walls.

Josh led his party directly towards the epicentre of the defences. He pushed through the crowds of fighters until he was standing on the reinforced wooden platform situated directly above the main iron portcullis.

He stepped up to the edge of the parapet and looked down.

Directly below them, the city guard had dug a massive, jagged trench spanning the entire length of the gatehouse. The trench was filled to the brim with a thick, viscous, foul-smelling black liquid. It was highly volatile alchemical pitch, waiting for a single spark to turn the approach into a roaring wall of inescapable fire.

Josh raised his storm-grey eyes from the pitch trench and looked out over the northern plains.

The world beyond the walls was perfectly, terrifyingly silent. The dark, corrupted treeline of the distant forest loomed against the horizon like a massive, jagged scar. The suffocating, pitch-black darkness of the night was finally beginning to fracture. A pale, bruised purple light was slowly bleeding into the eastern sky, casting long, eerie shadows across the frost-covered grass of the open plains.

Dawn was approaching.

Josh stood perfectly still, letting the freezing wind whip against his heavy steel greathelm. He felt the familiar, heavy anchor of his class settle deep within his chest. He was the wall. He was the shield standing between the fragile, terrified civilians hiding in the cellars behind him, and the apocalyptic nightmare hiding in the trees ahead.

He turned away from the dark horizon, looking at his small, battered party.

Brett stood to his left, the mage's pale face set into a mask of pure, cold concentration, his red cloak billowing wildly in the wind. Bhel stood to his right, the heavily bandaged dwarf looking entirely in his element, a fierce, eager grin pulling at the edges of his intricate beard. Carcan stood slightly behind them, her pristine pale green robes bright against the dark wood of the ramparts, her amber staff pulsing with a steady, reassuring golden light.

They had started as completely clueless strangers in this brutal, terrifying world, fighting simple goblins in muddy caves. Now, they were standing together as high-tier veterans, preparing to face an extinction-level event.

Josh reached out, his heavy steel gauntlets resting lightly on Brett's shoulder and Bhel's uninjured arm. He met Carcan's luminous silver eyes, offering her a slow, deeply reassuring nod.

"Listen to me," Josh said, his deep, gravelly voice cutting cleanly through the howling wind and the chaotic noise of the ramparts. He spoke with absolute, unquestionable authority. "When the horn blows, and that horde breaks the treeline, it is going to be absolute chaos. But we hold our ground. No one breaks formation. No one makes any stupid, heroic moves outside of the plan."

He tightened his grip on his friends, his storm-grey eyes burning with fierce, uncompromising loyalty.

"We fight for the city," Josh promised, his voice a low, heavy rumble. "But we protect our own. We are all making it out of this together."
