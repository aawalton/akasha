The village of Oakhollow nestled quietly beneath the canopy of the ancient forest, bathed in the soft glow of dawn. Eberi stood at her bedroom window, gazing out at the familiar sight of her family's workshop. The scent of freshly carved wood mingled with the crisp morning air, a reminder of the artisan legacy she was born into. Today marked her twentieth birthday—a milestone that traditionally signified stepping into the family trade.

But Eberi's heart yearned for something more.

She turned away from the window, her eyes drifting over the maps and sketches that adorned her walls. Drawings of distant mountains, mysterious ruins, and creatures from whispered legends filled every corner. She had always been captivated by tales of exploration and discovery, dreaming of a life beyond the boundaries of her village.

Downstairs, the clatter of breakfast preparations echoed. Joining her family, she exchanged warm greetings and accepted their well-wishes. Her parents spoke excitedly about teaching her advanced carving techniques, hinting at new responsibilities she would soon undertake.

"Eberi, we've been thinking," her mother began, placing a gentle hand on her shoulder. "It's time you started contributing your own creations to the workshop. Your talent is undeniable. Even the Elders are concerned, every villager has to benefit the whole society!"

She offered a polite smile. "Thank you, mother. I appreciate your confidence in me."

Her father grinned. "We'll make an artisan of you yet!"

As the conversation continued, Eberi's thoughts drifted. The prospect of spending her life crafting wooden masterpieces held little appeal compared to the call of the unknown that beckoned her soul.

Escaping the bustling workshop, Eberi ventured to her favorite spot—a tranquil pond hidden deep within the forest. Their settlement wasn't large. But one of the reasons they always felt safe, was its remoteness. The surface of the pond mirrored the sky above, only adding to the tranquility of the area. Eberi settled on a fallen log, pulling out a worn notebook filled with notes on the ancient ruins that dotted the distant landscapes.

Some pages contained sketches of strange structures with angular patterns not unlike the carvings discovered in the ruins in the valley to the north—a place few dared to visit. Stories claimed the ancient builders possessed knowledge lost to time, capabilities that modern minds could scarcely comprehend.

"One day," she mused aloud, "I'll uncover the secrets of this world and carve my own destiny."

The distant sound of laughter drew her attention back to reality. The village festival was underway, celebrating the coming-of-age of her and her peers. It was a time of joy, yet she couldn't shake the feeling of being out of place.

Reluctantly, Eberi returned to Oakhollow, joining the festivities. Lanterns hung from every tree branch, and the aroma of sweet pastries filled the air. Friends approached her with smiles, but their conversations revolved around apprenticeships and following in their families' footsteps.

"Are you excited to join your family's workshop?" one asked.

She hesitated. "Actually, I've been thinking about exploring beyond the village."

They exchanged puzzled glances. "But everything we need is here. The world outside is full of horrors and deaths. Why would you even want that?"

Eberi forced a smile. "Perhaps you're right."

As the sun dipped below the horizon, painting the sky with hues of orange and purple, the villagers gathered for the ceremonial lighting of the great bonfire. Elders spoke of tradition and community, their voices carrying a reverence for the past.

Among the crowd, Eberi spotted Meera, the village's elderly herbalist. Unlike most who called Oakhollow home, Meera had ventured beyond the mountains in her youth, returning with knowledge and stories that fired Eberi's imagination.

Later, as the celebration quieted, Eberi approached the old woman.

"The wanderlust hasn't left your eyes," Meera observed with a knowing smile.

"How could it?" Eberi replied. "There's too much out there I haven't seen. The Mistpeak Range, the Rooted Mountain, everything they speak of in the eastern territories, the ruined cities with their mysteries..."

"Dangerous places," Meera cautioned, though her eyes twinkled with understanding. "The world beyond our forests holds wonders, yes, but also threats that test the unprepared."

"Then I'll prepare myself," Eberi insisted.

The old herbalist studied her for a long moment, then reached into her satchel. "Perhaps this will help." She handed Eberi a leather-bound journal, its pages yellowed with age. "My notes from years of travel—safe paths, hidden springs, natural shelters."

Eberi accepted the gift with reverence. "Meera, this is…"

"Not as valuable as it would be in determined hands," the herbalist interrupted. "And speaking of determined spirits..." Her voice lowered conspiratorially. "I've had dreams recently. Strange ones about a visitor coming to Oakhollow."

"What sort of visitor?"

"Small in stature but vast in wisdom. One who understands forgotten paths and ancient knowledge. One, who can find secrets in the bright daylight." Meera's eyes grew distant. "In my dream, this stranger guided those who sought to transcend their beginnings, leading them through trials and teachings that transformed them."

"Like an initiation?" Eberi asked, intrigued.

"Something deeper." Meera's weathered fingers traced a pattern on the wooden table between them. "The stranger called it many things—a proving ground, a threshold crossing, enlightenment."

Eberi let the strange term settle in her mind, feeling an inexplicable resonance. "And you believe this person is coming here? To Oakhollow?"

"I can only say that he's approaching." Meera patted her hand. "When this visitor arrives, seek them out. Your paths are meant to cross, I'm certain of it."

As Eberi returned home that night, the stars seemed brighter than usual, watching over her with cosmic patience. The journal weighed significantly in her hands—not just in its physical heft, but in the promise it represented.

Beyond the comforting confines of Oakhollow, beyond the life mapped out for her, lay answers to questions she hadn't even formulated yet.
