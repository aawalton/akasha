I looked out over the gardens, my creation, my beautiful creation, as the human diplomat walked with her.

“I'm aware you have had some issues with the Penumbra....”“No,” I clarified. “We have not had any of note with them. Our issues stemmed from our previous leader's actions and convictions, before we settled here. Now our relationship is perhaps a bit strained, but still under full control.”Diplomats liked to talk, and talk in ways around issues. I was capable of that, certainly capable of that, but I hated it. There was no point in dancing around your problems all day unless you got along with people. I did not get along with this man, and did not need to, his nation was far too far away to bother us even if they did have the ability to get to this island with ships, which they didn't.

“Surely we have something you wish...”“You've reconsidered access to cores and the language of them?” I asked.

“Well...” The man never finished a proper thought, that too was terribly annoying.

“It is the only thing our current partners have issue trading with us, and you seem unwilling to break that taboo.”“True enough, we too must refuse to give them to a new race, as we don't yet know you well enough.” Oh look, he finally finished one. “But perhaps in time...” Never mind.

“Then I'm afraid we have little to discuss,” I informed him. “Unless there is some other pressing matter.”“No, sadly not.”Our business for the day done I left him there and moved into the buildings of the island. It was unfortunate having to come out from under the large glass dome that covered my lovely garden, but what was one to do? With magical items we could probably put a shield over the whole island, but without them much of it was rain soaked and soggy more often than not.

Yet I knew this place as well as any, for I'd been here for the building of it. Perhaps the only one who knew it better than I was the one I now went to see, to consult.

The building I was heading for was smack in the center of it, connected to the old warrens our people had built over uncounted generations before they'd been wiped out. Would they be proud of us now? Or would they recoil in horror, our current generation so different from what they knew.

“Where is my sister?” I asked one of her aides as I entered, also one of her children, she'd had over a dozen now and they were all brilliant little blighters, though none quite matched their dam.

“Mother is deep in the labs, her personal rooms on floor three,” he clarified for me. “Are you well Aunt Sasha?”“Fine nephew, how goes the research?” I asked as we moved deeper into the complex, heading the same direction.

“Well, though it will take us time to catch up. The humans are still decades ahead of us.”“True,” I observed. “But I have faith we'll catch up or exceed them in time.”I spoke those words but I almost doubted them. Of our children only three had magic like Greta and I did. I wondered why that was, when our sire had so many who had it, though it may well have been age, he was an ancient thing when we were born, at least by our reckoning.

“From your breathing I take it there was no success, today...”“Don't you start with me too Greta,” I grumbled as I entered the room. She had developed the annoying habit of realizing what I was thinking before I even articulated it, observations she said.

“We really need the aid with those. Getting a core is perhaps doable, but the information around it. There are generations of books we just don't have, skills we haven't developed yet.”

“Foundation doesn't truly have anything they need yet sister. We can barely even defend ourselves if someone came to attack.”“Correction,” she said. “We could not meaningfully defend ourselves at all if someone came to attack.”“Not helpful.”“But true. Sasha, the best we could do is hide in the warrens and collapse the openings. The weapons are nowhere near ready yet.”“But the cannons?” I asked.

“Compared with what even the humans have are nothing.”

“Ahh...”“Now who's starting?” she laughed.

“Us apparently,” I answered, which got me a good laugh.

“Indeed, we still need more people. Any clue yet what the population here was before everything?”“Who knows?” I asked. “We have almost no evidence at all on that, probably several thousand at the low end, but honestly I can't tell.”Foundation had been so populated before, but now? Not even a thousand of us in total. We'd been few when first we came here, and we had been breeding like, well, goblins, but still there were limits.

“We need more food,” she observed.

“We will always need more food, it's the limiter on our population and I suspect it was for our ancestors too. That takes time to grow though, no matter what we do it will be generations unless we wish to destroy the soil here forever.” The few of us that had magic like me had gotten a consult with the elves the last time one of their ships had passed through, they'd had a farmer aboard who'd promised us that much at least was impossible to fix.

“I've got some ideas, based on some of the chemistry we're seeing now. May be the humans are stingy with their core information, but when it comes to chemistry they don't know what they have.”“At least one of them does.”“Any word from him?”“Getting married soon.”I smiled gently, among our people I was the only one who'd decided to take one mate forever. For the rest of us it was a bit of a rotational thing, lovers came and went based on a number of factors. However this didn't sit well with me, at least not now.

“Hopefully one day he'll visit, he was a fun one,” Greta said, laughing.

“Hopefully not, that human attracts trouble like old food attracts rats,” I responded, getting another laugh.

“That's what makes him so fun!”
