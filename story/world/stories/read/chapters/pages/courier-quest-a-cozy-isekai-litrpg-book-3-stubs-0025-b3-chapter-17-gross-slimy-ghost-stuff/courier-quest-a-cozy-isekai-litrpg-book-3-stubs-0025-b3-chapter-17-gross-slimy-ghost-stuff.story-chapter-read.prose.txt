Trevor and Adabelle had been informed of a change of plans via a note on their door the next morning. The contact who was going to get the armor from the ghost dungeon wouldn’t be able to make it, at least not with their stock, and had requested they come in instead. Since they were planning on going to Paladona anyway, they figured a quick stop would be easy enough.

They rode Clay, who used their whole husk to take the shape of a wracor, into the city. The guidebooks they read last night about the Fustavele de la Cruon explained that it was tradition for women to wear blue and men to wear yellow, and they had dressed for the occasion.

Trevor had picked out a pair of black pants with a yellow vest that showed off his chest, specifically for his wife, while Adabelle wore white shorts and a sleeveless baby blue blouse. Clay continued wearing Tanazi’s hat, having become quite attached to it, though they wouldn’t be able to once they were back in their usual stoat form.

It was the fourth day of the festival, and the streets of Paladona were packed both inside and out. The population was made up of mostly humans and blue-scaled kobolds who were said to have been descended from Cruon herself, and they were out in full force to celebrate the festival. Most of them moved out of Clay’s way, though the trio found themselves waiting for paths to open up every now and again.

There was no need to be impatient, though. The sound of music flowed through the city. It featured heavy percussion as the centerpiece and was accompanied by woodwind instruments. The melody flowed fast and free, and Adabelle quickly found herself swaying from side to side behind Trevor.

“This is a catchy beat,” she said, looking around to see if she could find which street the music was coming from.

“Do you want to go and find it?” Trevor asked. “With a drumbeat that heavy, I’m pretty sure Clay already knows where the musicians are.”

The earth elemental nodded, but Adabelle shook her head. “No, there will be plenty of time later. Business first, then we explore.”

“You’ve got it, lady.”

They continued through Paladona, sticking to the road that ran adjacent to the ocean as they looked for Surtla’s. The magic shop was a prominent enough feature in the city that everyone knew where it was. Trevor had asked for directions a few times, but they had been told to just keep going.

And so they did, walking through the city and seeing the sights. The buildings lining the beach were open air, even the restaurants, and everything had a fantastic view of the ocean. Trevor and Adabelle picked out a few places that they would have to come back to solely by smell.

Clay made good time since the crowd usually got out of his way when they heard his hooves clopping behind them, though he did stop for one sight in particular. A group of people were dancing through the streets under a huge, thirty foot long costume. It reminded Trevor of one of those dancing Chinese dragons that took several people to operate.

This one was the same blue as the ocean and shimmered in the light. Two black horns jutted out of its forehead above their yellow eyes before curving backwards at an almost ninety degree angle. The lead dancer under the mouth moved back and forth through the road, and everyone made room for them, including Clay.

“That’s a depiction of Cruon,” Trevor explained, leaning back so that Adabelle could hear him more easily. He had read about this before leaving this morning. “There’s twelve of these costumes dancing through the city for the festival. It’s supposed to bless all of Paladona with good weather, plenty of fish, and another successful year.”

“So Cruon is a goddess who’s also a dragon?” the demonkin asked, resting her chin on his shoulder.

“Kinda sorta,” he replied. “She protected the settlers on the Margrovan Peninsula from the fury of the ocean and taught them how to fish with spears and nets. They treated her as a goddess in return for her protection since they saw themselves as fortunate under her rule. Cruon is still called that today, but people who actually venerate her are pretty rare these days. What she did is still appreciated, she’s just no longer worshiped like she was back then.”

Adabelle kissed Trevor’s cheek. “That’s very cool. Thank you. Is she still around?”

“Unfortunately, no. She passed away a couple of centuries ago. Her son is, though. Croal. He’s something of a local celebrity around here. We probably won’t meet him, though. He should be preparing for the festival’s big finale tomorrow.”

The trio were all smiles as the dragon passed by, trailed behind by a short procession of drums and bells. Suddenly, the lead turned onto the beach into a run. The operators behind him had apparently expected this because they kept pace. Children shrieked in joy as they were suddenly chased by the depiction of Cruon.

Once that had passed, they continued on their way. It wasn’t long until they finally came to a building with a large, cloth sign that marked it as Surtla’s. The font was eye-catching and written in both Magrovan as well as Mercant, the language of merchants, so both Trevor and Adabelle could easily understand at least part of it. It was one of the few buildings that weren't open air, and there was a long pier across the street.

They dismounted from Clay and the earth elemental shifted. That got the attention of the natives, who watched with mixed feelings as the stone wracor twisted its husk for storage.

“It’s fine, I promise,” Trevor announced, holding up a hand towards the onlookers. “Clay’s an earth elemental familiar. They’re okay.”

That seemed to placate the Paladonans and he was very glad that being Summoned gave him an automatic translation power when speaking with people. Clay soon finished transforming their husk into multiple slabs, so that Stacks made them a little smaller, and popped out of the top in their stoat form.

Trevor pulled the pieces into his inventory after allowing his familiar to climb up onto his shoulder. The hat, too, had to go. He did offer it to Adabelle first, but she shook her head. It seemed destined to be kept as a trophy rather than something actually worn, and he threw it in with the rest.

Hand in hand, they walked into the shop. The smell of burning incense hit them hard as they entered the dark, somewhat chilly showroom. It was a stark contrast to the sunny day and pleasant sea breeze outside. Trevor and Adabelle moved out of the doorway and looked around at the neatly ordered products.

Items ranging from swords to wands to belts hovered over tables that had been set out in a grid pattern throughout the shop. Each one had plaques underneath explaining what the item was called and what it did. There were only a few people browsing, but they had the look of nobility to them.

A blue and white scaled kobold politely excused themselves from the conversation they were having and quickly approached the newcomers. “Welcome to my shop!” they greeted warmly, wringing their claws together as they looked up at Trevor and Adabelle. Their eyes lingered on Clay for a moment before refocusing. “I am Surtla, what brings you to my establishment this fine morning?”

“We received a note saying that you, or someone employed by you, wouldn’t be able to come and greet us, and so we sought you out,” the noblewoman explained. “I am Lady Adabelle. This is my husband, Trevor, and his familiar, Clay.”

“Ah, I am so sorry that I wasn’t able to come and see you personally!” Surtla exclaimed as he shook his head. “Unfortunately, the boat I was planning on taking to visit you had its schedule changed at the last minute. It happens sometimes during Fustavele, you see. You have come during a very busy time, but as a destination for your honeymoon? Top notch. You’ll enjoy it. Oh! And where are my manners? Congratulations, you two.”

“Thank you,” Trevor replied with a glance towards Adabelle, and he smiled. “We haven’t seen a lot of it yet, but the city is lovely so far.”

“Paladona, nay, the Magrovan Peninsula is one of the most beautiful places to ever exist. That’s why I moved here, and I have never regretted it for even a moment,” the kobold said with a grin. “But enough about that. Come, let me take you to the place where you can drop off your goods and collect what you came for.”

“Sure thing.”

Trevor and Adabelle followed Surtla to a door labeled for employees and found stairs to the second floor. He led them up into a room that was the opposite of the last in that it could use more incense. The smells were unpleasant and there were several types of materials the courier had never seen before on a nearby table. He realized he had gotten off easy before when all he had to do was deliver things like logs and husks.

The kobold gestured towards a large black tub. It took up the entire length of the wall, but it wasn’t where he was heading. Surtla walked towards one of the tables. “You can deposit the ghost’s armor inside there. I’ve cleaned it out for them already.”

Nodding, Trevor walked over to it and held his hand over the tub. The armor started falling like rain out of his palm, hitting the container with dull thuds. They were without any wetness, slime, or seaweed since he could remove those while they were in his inventory.

“Wait, are those dry?” Surtla asked, biting panic in his voice as he whirled around.

“Yes?” the courier answered, stopping the torrent of ghostly attire.

The kobold’s claws clacked against the wooden floor as he hurried over and looked into the tub. “Where is the slime!?” he asked loudly.

“In my inventory,” Trevor answered immediately, frowning. “Did they need to stay together? I just thought it was gross cave stuff from the water, so I cleaned them for you.”

Surtla whirled on the man. “You cleaned them?” he asked with a disbelieving laugh.

“Yes?”

Adabelle took a step forward next to Trevor. “Is there a problem with that?” she asked, taking her husband’s hand.

“A problem?” the kobold repeated incredulously. “Not at all! If anything, so long as he still has the ectoplasm then he’s saved us hours of scraping and cleaning!”

“Ohhh, ectoplasm! Of course!” Trevor said, tapping his forehead. “It’s not slime, it’s literally the stuff ghosts are made of. Which, as it turns out, is slimy. And gross. Gross slimy ghost stuff. I can’t believe I didn’t make the connection before.”

“Indeed it is,” Surtla confirmed with a nod. “While we do use everything, including the seaweed on the ghosts’ armor, the ectoplasm is what allows us to make the ink for our talismans. Those are our bread and butter here in the workshop.”

Adabelle gave her husband’s hand a light squeeze before letting him go and walking towards the table the kobold had been heading towards. There were several small, open boxes, and she peered into one to see a short stack of thick paper talismans. Each one had the same series of symbols on it in shiny black ink, though she couldn’t tell what it said.

“What are these for?” she asked curiously.

“That particular box pertains to warding off monsters and evil spirits,” Surtla answered as he walked over to join her. “There are also some that help stabilize the magical equilibrium in an area for rituals, create a more peaceful atmosphere, promote better air quality, and more. I am the Magrovan Peninsula’s foremost talisman enchanter, perhaps even in the world. Klarkartar, my benefactor, wants to see if my wares will do well in other areas.”

He turned back to Trevor, all smiles now. “Which is where you come in. I have to admit, I was not expecting a lot, but can you truly separate the ectoplasm from everything else inside that inventory of yours?”

“Yeah, that’s actually really easy,” the courier admitted, calming down now that it didn’t seem like he was in trouble. “Do you want me to move the armor and fill the tub?”

“Please, please! That would make things much easier for me and my apprentices,” Surtla said, gesturing towards the tub again. “They are enjoying the festival at the moment, but they were prepared for a long, grueling afternoon of scraping as much ectoplasm from the armor as possible. It’s not a glamorous job, but it is a necessary one.”

“Alright, that’s no problem,” Trevor said as he reached into the tub.

All it took was a touch and they started disappearing one by one. After it was clear, he raised his hand over the container and the ectoplasm started to pour out of his fingers like water from a hose. It did feel gross, but the ghostly leftovers barely touched his skin before falling. The liquid kept coming until it filled up the tub.

“I’ve got a bit more,” Trevor said as he looked at how much he had filled.

“More?” Surtla asked with wide eyes.

“Trevor managed to collect, what was it, honey? Sixty-two unharmed sets of armor?” Adabelle asked, absolutely bragging about her husband. “He’s excellent at what he does.”

“Yeah, that’s right,” he confirmed with a smile. The courier glanced at the kobold, and concern filled his face. “Are you all right, Mr. Surtla?”

“I just… Yes, I think I need to sit down,” the enchanter replied before looking back up at him. “Sixty-two?”

“Sixty-two,” he answered.

“Yes. I’m going to sit down. Then, I’m going to get you a spare tub,” Surtla said before wandering off and shaking his head. “Sixty-two.”
