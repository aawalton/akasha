Archie tore through the outer stretch of the forest, boots pounding into bark as he leapt branch to branch. Volos and Arsenic moved themselves to poke their heads from his neck portion of his armor to nod, having their heads bobbing back and forth like a bobblehead.

Fifteen minutes in, and he already knew this forest was going to be a pain in the ass.

Every time he attempted to put in a little more power into his sprint or hopping forward, some pack of E-Grade beasts would decide to start howling, shrieking, or even try and attack him, while groups of sapient E-Grades such as humans, elves, and other sapient races cluttered the ground below, all hunting or attacking similarly leveled creatures. A few heads tilted upward as he passed, as if they’d caught a flicker of movement above them, but none of them came close to matching his pace.

Still, it was too damn slow.

He could feel at least five to seven teams on average within five hundred meters at any given moment, like ticks spread across the forest floor. The majority of them were low-level E-Grades that were far too eager to swing their weapons, fire their bows, or sling spells at anything that brushed by their peripheral.

Every so often, Vital Sight picked out the denser outlines of D-Grades, both beasts and humanoids, made faintly distinguishable at the 45% opacity despite the crowds of E-Grades within the forest. The beasts were usually shepherding their young while hunting, while the humanoid ones tended to guide groups of their own who’d only recently unlocked their System and gained a Class and or Profession, teaching them the ropes as they moved about. If not doing that, they were simply heading in the same direction as him, each careful to keep their distance from one another to avoid drawing attention to themselves and accidentally getting into a fight.

Archie landed on a thick branch, knees bending into the bark, and felt the irritation rising as he was forced to dodge another spell misfire and be given an apology from a group of Level 12 E-Grades. Nah. Fuck this. It’s the twelfth time this has happened… If I keep going at these speeds, I’m gonna actually lose my mind.

He straightened, rolling his neck, then let the restraints come off as he activated Adrenaline Rush at 10%.

The surge of power hit him instantly as his veins lit up like firecrackers with various shades of green, blue, and red. As this was happening, his muscles swelled almost instantly, and all semblance of fatigue that resided within him vanished as he felt as if someone had attached a car battery to his nerves and set them alight.

The branch snapped behind him as he shot forward, entirely splintering from the sheer force of his launch and showering the group of E-Grades below. Almost instantly after he activated Adrenaline Rush, his speed quadrupled, transforming him into what the majority of people around him thought of as a streak of gray as he shot through the treeline. Tree trunks snapped past him like fence posts, leaves whipping across his vision as he bent his body low, darting from branch to branch with predatory precision.

Down below, a squad of beastkin froze mid-attack as they were about to end the life of a Roothog. Archie barely registered their wide eyes before he was past them, the sudden displacement of air rattling their weapons and nearly knocking one flat on his ass.

Another team, farther out, jerked their heads skyward at the faint shockwave tearing through the canopy, as, similar to them, the creature they were almost done killing stopped its charging attack to look in the direction Archie had just passed through.

Archie smirked to himself. Yeah, this is much better, he thought as the forest was beginning to thicken ahead, in which trees grew more numerous and more of the sun began to be blocked, along with everything gaining a tinge of wildness within them.

One D-Grade in particular that was watching over a group of four looked in the direction Archie had just shot from, a faint crease of concern forming between her brows before she forced her focus back onto the group she was supposed to be watching.

Seemingly sensing the question brewing on their lips, she exhaled and said, low but firm, “Not to worry, my Ladies, it was just another overzealous D-Grade rushing into the Primeval Forest.”

“Why is that? It’s not like the forest is going anywhere?” one of them asked, her tone full of curiosity.

Her sister, the one with her hood pulled down low enough that only the bottom half of her face showed, groaned and rolled her eyes. “The dungeon. Don’t you remember Father raving about how profitable it was for the city?”

“Yeah, but that was forever ago,” the first sister fired back, adjusting her grip on her spear as if that helped her argument. “Shouldn’t the hullabaloo about it have died down by now? It’s not like this is the only D-Grade dungeon on the continent.”

“Who cares,” the third sister snapped as she adjusted the volume of her headphones to be loud enough to ignore the piercing shrieks of their prey and her sisters, irritation bleeding into every word. Her blade was already pressed against the hide of a struggling Badgerboar, its tusks grinding against her shield in desperation. “Focus on killing this damn thing before it guts you while you stand around chatting.”

The fourth sister said nothing at all. Her bowstring was already pulled taut, eyes locked on the beast’s red and beady right eye as she stood right beside their D-Grade combat guard.

The watching D-Grade pinched the bridge of her nose, muttering under her breath calming words of encouragement that she would be able to handle the four of her wards. Babysitting four daughters of nobility through their “first proper hunt” as they finally had full access to the System had seemed like a mild inconvenience when she accepted the request from the commander. But now, not even two hours into babysitting them, with the girls squabbling louder with each other than the beasts they were supposed to be slaying, it was beginning to feel more like slow torture.

“System save me,” she muttered beneath the black mask that covered her face as her eyes flicked back toward the fading echo of speed and wind through the trees where Archie had vanished. Whoever that was, they clearly had better things to do than deal with noble brats. She envied him greatly at the moment.

Archie had only just broken into what looked like the second layer of the forest when he passed through a particularly thick membrane of mana and felt both the density of life and mana jump up.

Along with the rising density of mana and vital energy in the forest after passing through the thick membrane of mana, the forest itself began to shift, growing wilder with every step he took; however, something that he noticed was the lack of redwood trees, which meant he was still ways away from reaching the dungeon.

Along with that observation, the more he began to spread his enhanced senses towards his surroundings, and in doing so, the more his sense of familiarity became stirred, like he’d seen this forest before, or at least a patch of it.

It was almost nostalgic in a twisted way. The atmosphere, the wild press of greenery, the sense that every bush was hiding something with teeth, it all tugged faintly at his memory as a certain massive creature with a spiky tail popped into his mind… Ah, that’s right. This is reminding me of the Dungeon of the Lost Valley, the dinosaur dungeon from the Group Tutorial.

He slowed, boots grinding into bark as he dropped onto a thick branch and straightened himself up when he caught sight of a weird creature a couple of meters away from him.

Clinging to the side of the tree trunk ahead, with its legs splayed wide as if suction-cupped to the bark, the creature was roughly a meter long. At first glance, it looked like nothing more than an oversized pink lizard – possibly a gecko. But the longer he stared at it, the more curious he became about it, as it was unlike any oversized gecko he’d seen before.

Upon it twitching slightly as a breeze of wind brushed past it, its body rippled as though water had been trapped beneath a thin membrane of skin, patches solidifying into faint scales before dissolving back into a translucent, gelatinous flow – a constant and fluid shift between solid and liquid.

Its head twitched sharply, eyes locking onto him, the pupils jittering like broken lenses zooming in and out.

[Amphislime Lv 101]

Archie’s brows creased. What the–? I’ve never seen something like that before… but boy, do they look cool.

He instinctively stepped back on the branch and tapped the side of his neck guard, signaling his spirit beasts. Volos and Arsenic, their heads poking out, glanced at the creature before turning to him with their best attempt at doe eyes. He shook his head immediately.

[Glenwyn Centipede Lv 9]

[Glenwyn Centipede Lv 9]

“No. This will get dangerous for the two of you. Go back into my Soulscape, where you can watch this whole thing from there, where it's safe and away from the acid slimes spew. I can’t fight properly if I’m worrying about keeping you two safe.” He said as his tone left no room for argument.

Both spirit beasts gave reluctant hisses and chitters before dissolving into streaks of light and diving back into his Soulscape.

Archie’s eyes narrowed as he activated Vital Sight. The world flared faintly, outlines and silhouettes sharpening across the dark, damp green. His stomach dropped just slightly as five more Amphislimes revealed themselves, plastered against nearby trees like living tumors. Their forms writhed and twitched, translucent bodies pulsing in and out of solidity as their eyes, every single one of them, jittered and then locked onto him.

“Oh, you’ve got to be shitting me,” Archie groaned as he stared back at all six of them. “Don’t slimes move in like packs of ten? Why are there so few of you?... Like, how am I supposed to get quick XP if there are only a couple of y’all?”

He didn’t get a verbal answer from them, just a shift of their eyes before he felt an Identify touch his soul.

Archie blurred forward before they could make the first move, Savage Charge igniting through his muscles as the bark beneath his boots splintered and gave way. He crashed into the nearest Amphislime with a clenched fist that should’ve gone through flesh, spine, and out the other side.

Instead, his arm sank in comically, like he’d punched a block made of glue and rubber. The impact rippled through the creature’s body in sluggish waves, its gelatinous flesh giving way but not breaking, not tearing, not even bruising. He blinked, half a second of surprise registering before instinct kicked in and he tried to rip his arm back out.

But the Amphislime didn’t let go.

Its entire form collapsed inward like a noose tightening, wrapping around his arm in a wet, constricting squeeze that clung to him like tar.

“What the–” Archie hissed, jerking backward. His knuckles should’ve torn through bone; instead, it felt like he’d just shoved his fist inside a living tire.

And as if on cue, the rest moved.

Every single one of the Amphislimes peeled off their perches in eerie unison, raised their heads as their gelatinous forms solidified, and upon doing so, their bodies snapped forward.

Archie’s pupils tightened as he saw the other five Amphislimes lunging towards him. He twisted, arm still trapped in the gelatinous coil of the first slime, yanking hard enough that the bark beneath his boots cracked, but no matter what he did, his arm wouldn’t come loose.

“Ah, crap,” Archie said as he leaped away from the tree he stood on and onto a nearby one, avoiding the five Amphislimes as they crashed into where he previously stood and sent the tree crashing down.
