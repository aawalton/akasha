Now that Glide’s upgrade was complete and Edge had introduced himself to the newest member of the team, it was time for him to spread his wings and fly—quite literally, in this case.

Before he got to the main event, he activated Climb. A modest tendril of mana flowed out from his reactor, through his limbs, and into his hands and feet. He wasn’t sure how the skill would interact with his armor and was pleased to learn that it functioned just fine while wearing boots and gloves, although he sensed it would be even more effective without them.

The low-cost power had two components. First, it caused small, hooklike fields to extend from his fingers and toes, which bit into the bark to support his weight. Climb also coated his palms and soles with sticky energy that provided extra traction, which felt kind of like magnets that switched on and off automatically as he scaled the vertical trunk.

Edge doubted he would keep the skill for long, but it was educational to experience a wider range of magic firsthand. It was easy to find, which meant he could pick it up whenever he needed to scale sheer surfaces that lacked protrusions for Foebinder to grab. With his current Amplification, he thought he could stick to the ceiling with it too, even with Climb capped at rank one.

He ran a few tests while scampering into the branches, getting a feel for its ins and outs, then came to a stop near the level of the canopy. Next, he took a long look around with some help from All-Seeing Gaze, which let him peer through leaves and trunks alike like they were made of tinted glass.

After making sure there weren’t any predators prowling nearby, Edge saturated the air with wind mana, manifested Maelstrom, and leapt from the tree. During his descent, he spread his arms wide and activated Glide. The skill felt more responsive than before, since it was socketed into a permanent slot and had made a home in his kingdom. He used his bond to let Glide’s avatar know what he was planning, who had a playful nature and seemed just as excited to find out what would happen next.

A membrane of mana extended from his fingers to his toes as a second field manifested between his legs, forming an invisible wingsuit that increased his surface area while granting his body the aerodynamics of a kite. He had leveled out considerably by the time the airfoil upgrade kicked in, adding an apparatus beneath his stomach, designed to maximize the lift he gained from headwinds and updrafts.

It significantly extended the duration of his Glide on its own, and when he added a gust from Maelstrom to the mix, he was able to continue for the better part of a mile. He couldn’t turn as fast as when the airfoil was extended, but the distance was incredible. He was sure that after they got to know each other a little better, the avatar would let him form a basic wingsuit whenever he needed the extra agility.

Everything was working out the way he’d hoped so far, which meant the board was green to attempt the final stage of his experiment. Edge climbed as high as he could, scanned his surroundings for danger, and switched on Shadow Play.

He had to pay the combo penalty to use two active skills at the same time, which was significantly reduced thanks to his upgraded ultimate and his strong bond with Play’s avatar, but the results were more than worth it. In addition to letting him phase through solid objects and conceal his presence while moving, Shadow Play’s mobility upgrade reduced his weight by roughly ninety percent. When Maelstrom blew a breeze across the airfoil, he went sailing up, gaining twenty feet of altitude in a single puff.

He immediately went into a tailspin and crashed, which meant this was going to take some practice, but that didn’t dampen his enthusiasm. He was agonizingly close to making this work, and a few frustrating failures were a small price to pay to realize his ambitions.

He Climbed to the top of the tallest trunk he could find, where only twenty feet of supersized leaves separated him from the sunny blue sky above. Edge turned to where the canopy was thinnest, transformed into shadows, and leapt. When he switched on Glide and used Maelstrom to push from below, everything worked out the way he’d planned. Instead of losing altitude, he started to climb, sailing toward the heavens above.

An emerald wall obstructed his view as his incorporeal form slipped through the greenery. Then the branches fell away, and he emerged into a realm unlike anything he’d seen before. Exploring an uncharted stratum of the extreme-threat biome was dangerous, but he kept on going, ascending until he was high above the treetops.

Edge whooped with delight as he savored the breathtaking sight, engraving the experience in his heart to treasure for the rest of his life. He was flying, and nothing was going to stop him from milking this moment for everything it was worth.

When he was as certain as he could be that nothing had noticed his arrival and was heading his way, he turned his attention to the canopy below, momentarily switching off his vision-augmenting magic. Rolling waves of gigantic leaves met his gaze, extending in every direction for as far as his eyes could see. It really does look like an ocean.

Breaking free from gravity’s chains was one of the most thrilling moments of his life—a fierce and feral joy that was untainted by worrying about his friends or the next crisis looming large on the horizon. He knew he was one of the first people to take in this view, maybe even the first, grinning like a maniac as he explored the upper reaches of the woodland biome.

He didn’t lower his guard and remained vigilant against flying predators hiding in the branches. But with Shadow Play running, he looked like a patch of haze floating in the breeze, and so far, nothing had even glanced in his direction. He switched Gaze back on, turning the trees translucent to his eyes as he flew higher, basking in the limitless freedom that was now his to command.

He lost track of time while refining his technique, heading toward the shimmering water in the distance that marked the boundary of the biomes. Edge knew it was time to return to the ground when he started running low on mana. This had been an incredible experience, and he would return to the Sea of Trees to enjoy the view and seek valuable resources and useful skills whenever he had time to spare.

He came in for a landing the next time he spotted a bare branch, stepped into an alcove in the trunk, and shut off his skills, letting his reservoir refill while grinning from ear to ear. It’s not every day that you learn how to fly. When his tank was full, he Glided to the ground and resumed his hunt.

While he was looking for a place to camp for the night, Edge came across another skill he wanted to steal. He shifted into shadow form while studying his quarry, a stage-two owl that was no bigger than his outstretched hand. He knew from studying the beast hunters’ library that the petite bird was packing Enhance Vision.

He didn’t plan to invest heavily in Perception, and his natural eyesight was nothing compared to a beast’s or sensory specialist’s. All-Seeing Gaze was fantastic up close and let him hold his own in combat, but as matters stood, most creatures could see him coming well before he realized they were there.

Hunting aside, Enhance Vision would let him examine terrain and perceive fine details from considerably further away. He could use magitech devices to solve the problem and would have normally left the owl alone, but when he’d spotted the perceptive creature, inspiration struck—a way to use his Triple-Merger Token that would serve him well on the long road ahead.

If I combine Enhance Olfactory, Enhance Hearing, and Enhance Vision, I bet I’ll wind up with something special. Of course, now that Skill-Eater was awake more often, there was no reason to rely on his instincts alone. He stepped into his central chamber and knocked on the black door.

When it swung open and he followed the path, the ravenous lord was lounging on a patio overlooking the ocean, sipping from a steaming cup of tea so black it looked like coffee. She invited Edge to join her, and he took a seat, enjoying a companionable silence before asking about the merger.

“As you suspected, those powers are highly compatible. They will merge cleanly, even without developing your skill bonds.”

“Thanks. I appreciate the assistance. It’s nice to be able to speak face to face like this.”

He sipped from the cup she poured him, cherishing the flavor of alien spices no human tongue had tasted before. They spoke of small matters for a while longer, then he bid Skill-Eater farewell and returned to his body.

Without further ado, he used Foebinder to snatch the owl from its perch, then added Enhance Vision to his collection. He thought the little beast had a good chance of surviving long enough to develop the skill again and its parts wouldn’t sell for much, so he let it go and continued looking for a good place to pitch his tent.

Edge wasn’t sure where to pick up the other skills on his list, but he wanted to advance Enhance Vision before the merger regardless. He had learned that if one component of a merged skill was rank two and the others were rank one, the new power would start at rank one but retain the upgrade, making it stronger than it would be otherwise.

He walked through the forest until a stage-three serpent tried to eat him. It had severely misjudged the threat he posed, and he wound up with two Uncommon powers, a pile of high-quality parts, and some grilled meat for dinner. The experience was sufficient to rank up Vision, and he picked an upgrade that enhanced his long-distance eyesight, since Gaze had him covered up close.

It also gave him his first cycle, although he knew they would come considerably slower when his bonus ran dry.

You have accumulated sufficient experience to cycle up your core. Your new cycle is: 1.

You have 7 attribute points awaiting allocation.

Before he decided how to allocate his potentia, Edge spent a few minutes wrapping his head around how Brawny changed the math. He refused to call the trait Not a Moron, no matter what the System put on his profile. Combined with his other traits, he would receive a whopping 10% bonus to Disruption, Reflex, and Perception, an extra 20% to Endurance, Durability, Power, Speed, and Control, and plus three to every attribute across the board.

The bottom line was that he would get a bonus point of anything in the first set for every ten he invested and an extra point for every five he spent on the second, which meant he would aim for those cutoffs whenever possible.

Moving on, he had focused on his energetic attributes throughout most of stage two, but now it was time to start diversifying once more. Right now, he was relying on Mantle and Fear to match the Speed of his enemies, but the seasonal bonus would change to Disruption any day. If he ever found himself in a situation where he couldn’t use enchantment magic, opponents his stage would mop the floor with him, and even with his buffs running, he was taking too many hits that could penetrate Harden and trigger his Organ-Guard.

Second Wind in his auxiliary slot compensated for his low Endurance, and that situation seemed fine for the foreseeable future, but he needed more Speed, Power, Reflex, Durability, and Control if he wanted to survive run-ins with stage-four beasts, let alone the prospect of tangling with the Wardens, Ancients, or experienced gladiators.

Edge was a skill specialist and planned to prioritize Amplification, Generation, and Disruption over his physical stats. However, his new upgrade to Skill Bond dramatically reduced the combo penalty, and his reactor had just tripled in size, so his mana regeneration was in a good place for the time being.

In the end, he decided to put one point into Amplification and Disruption every time he cycled up and the other five elsewhere, intending to revisit the matter when his overall distribution felt balanced for his present needs. To get the ball rolling, he would focus on Speed, since he didn’t want to find himself moving far slower than he’d grown accustomed to when autumn came to an end.

When he confirmed his selection, the potentia surged from his core in a crackling rush, suffusing his nerves and fast-twitch muscle fibers, as well as portions of his brain, and allowing him to move faster than ever before. The other changes were subtle at only one point apiece, but he knew they would add up over time.

He had spotted a nice campsite a few miles back but wasn’t tired yet. After storing the parts in his vault and freezing most of the meat, Edge prowled the forest floor, keeping an eye out for any interesting powers he passed along the way.
