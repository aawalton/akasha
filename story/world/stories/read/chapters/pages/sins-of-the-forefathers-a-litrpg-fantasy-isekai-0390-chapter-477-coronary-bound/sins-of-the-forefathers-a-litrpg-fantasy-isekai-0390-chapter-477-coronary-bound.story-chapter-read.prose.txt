This was turning into the heaviest fighting of my life.

By far.

Yes, we were protected by the ever-growing hordes of Rafael’s undead army, but that was only helping so much. In a normal, non-insane society, you would have thought that seeing your own warriors being turned to fight against you within a matter of minutes would give pause. That the Skarned of the Korvoks, in their nearly unending waves, would even be intimidated by Rafael’s spreading plague of undeath.

That wasn’t actually happening. If anything, the Skarned were fighting harder as they threw themselves, howling, at the wedge that Rafael’s ‘assistants’ were driving through the Hold. More and more of them poured out of the flesh-covered, profane spires that seemed to comprise their city every single second. It was to the point that the wide, even lanes of the hold were absolutely packed to the brim in the fighting. Dozens and dozens of Dwarves could stand shoulder to shoulder on these boulevards and still have room to mingle, if the Korvoks were even inclined to such a thing. But they weren’t, because there was no thought to their own safety, no conscious care about the bites and scratches from the undead Varlayans that even now scourged them.

Because they were batshit insane.

And worse, they were being supported by a now familiar sight. When I had fought him to a standstill, I had naively thought that one of the Karvokar like Djarn would be a rare sight. That it would be difficult to create warriors of that calibre en masse, even if I had triumphed over him relatively easily.

That thought turned out to be wrong.

There were Karvokar everywhere in Karag Korvok. Sure, they weren’t quite as numerous as the Skarned that they commanded, but they were by no means a rare sight.

And what a grotesque sight they made. It was easy to pick the Korvok commanders out of any given crowd of mad, slavering cannibal Dwarves. They were always misshapen in some way, mutated in manners that were at odds with the typical Dwarven form. Some were as tall and as bulky as Djarn had been, but others were odder still. A fair few of the Karvokar that our forces encountered on the streets of Karag Korvok were tall and slender, almost as if they were a parody of a typical Elf, and yet still possessing the proportions of a Dwarf. The result was always twisted and disturbing in some way, and not just because of the mutations.

As expected, most of them were skinned in some manner. While Djarn had been completely skinned on one side of his body, that was only one interpretation of the typical Karvokar form. Others were bare of flesh in other areas of the body, like those who had been flayed on their upper torso alone, or vice versa. But something I noticed, in between flashes of combat, was that there was an odd sense of duality to be observed in their horrific forms. A form of symmetry, almost. Their self-flensing was not without a form of deliberation, as insane as it was.

Which seemed to reflect itself in their strength. Rafael’s undead horde was not enough to deal with the Karvokar, as we pressed further into the city. As I had observed in Djarn, the oddly compressed and waxy muscle exposed through their ritualistic self-flaying was tough. So much so that none of the undead were able to pierce their hide in order to deliver their magically born, incredibly transferable infection.

Every Karvokar we encountered threatened to carve their way through the ranks of our greatest weapon, and that couldn’t be allowed.

And so, it fell to the members of the expedition to handle them. We weren’t just here to huddle behind Rafael’s horde, as we marched deeper into the city of madmen. Normally, if not grudgingly, I would say that the Karvokar were too much for a member of the expedition to handle. There were just too strong on an individual level for them to handle, both figuratively and literally for most of my people.

Good thing we weren’t taking them on like that, then.

Before diving into the thick of things, I had re-divided our forces into the same battle-groups we had fought together in through the streets of Varla. It had worked well before, it was working…alright, now.

They were charged with finding the Karvokar who thought to wade into the thick of Rafael’s horde and take them out. The Karvokar were the lynchpin of the entire Korvokian ‘defense’, if you could even call it that. Not only were they stronger individually than thirty Skarned put together, and not only were they bizarrely skilled in a form of unarmed combat I had never seen, but they had a focusing effect upon the Skarned themselves. Every Karvokar in the waves of Skarned thrown against us was like an island of calm in a raging sea. The Skarned were more skilled, they worked in something resembling units, and they almost seemed more coherent within a radius around one of the Karvokar.

But if you eliminated that Karvokar, the Skarned fell apart into raving, flesh-hungry madmen once more. They were far simpler to handle then.

The combination of it all, though…

This was by far the longest, most drawn-out battle I had ever been involved in. There was barely even a moment to breathe in the thick of it. We were barely halfway to our impromptu, decided-upon location, and the fighting had raged on for well over an hour of constant, unending bloodshed at this point. Every second that passed, hundreds of Dwarves were being cut down and risen again as thralls to Rafael’s will. It was a constant back-and-forth struggle.

We’d surge forward, hundreds of undead acting as both spear and shield for my forces, as we cut a path through the streets…

Straight for the center of the Hold, where the tower piercing the heart lay.

Only for Karvokar, sometimes, most of the time, more than one of them, to appear and scythe the undead acting as our vanguard, stalling our advance. Then, it would be our turn to isolate those Karvokar in a complicated dance and neutralize them.

With them dead, our march would continue, only for the same pattern to repeat itself.

Over and over and over again, it happened. The very stone beneath our feet was slick with pools of blood nearly ankle high at times from the number of Skarned thrown at us.

Our best guess, communicated through runners and breathless, increasingly exhausted gasps, was that the center of the city was our best possible guess as to where the captives were being held. Not only that, but that was the best possible way to reach Nehushtan, pinned to the ceiling of this mountain cavern. I didn’t dare to transform and fly up there for multiple reasons. What would be the point? It’s not like I was going to be able to free him on my own. Not only that, but I was wary of trying to achieve any kind of elevation in this Hold in the first place. A few of the Karvokar we had encountered had attacked us with odd ranged Skills, seemingly part of their combat doctrine.

The first time one of the Karvokar had opened a vein in one of their wrists to shoot a stream of seemingly pressurized blood at me, I’d nearly fallen victim to it. If not for Fade grabbing the hem of coat and pulling me down, that very well might have torn a hole clean through me. Fortunately, Sena had torn that Karvokar’s throat out in retaliation.

Unfortunately, one of my soldiers at my back paid the price for my mistake.

With the chaos of the still raging battle, though, the best I could do for him was grit my teeth and soldier on.

There was a problem, though, and I wasn’t the only person it was affecting.

Classers weren’t really meant for long, sustained battles like this. Most Classers, including the Martial Orders, were mostly trained as monster hunters. And that made sense in the end, because that was how most people accumulated Level Aether. They didn’t have my ability to filter it through battle with other sapients.

Culling monsters was different work than fighting a long, grueling urban war. Most fights with monsters involved short bursts of extreme violence. As a result, most Classers’ builds were built around that, and it’s not like there was even the possibility of re-allocating your Virtues, selecting a different Class, or anything like that.

There was no option of respecialization under the System.

This was a known problem, of course. It was one of the reasons that the standing armies of the world liked to recruit their soldiers young. As in, right after being Awoken, levels of youth. You took a young man or woman, and you molded them into specific Classes meant for the kind of stamina you needed to fight in protracted battles. Soldier Classes, they were called.

The expedition had some of those, yes. But the core of our forces were from the Herztalian Martial Orders, and those Classes were…flashier.

More immediately powerful…but without the same kind of stamina.

Because of this…we were starting to flag. Even I was feeling it a little bit, and I had advantages. Our progress was starting to slow, even though Rafael’s undead army only swelled. There was only so much he could do, even with a near infinitely replenishing stock of undead.

It had reached a point where I had even chosen to conserve as much of my Mana as I possibly could. For perhaps the first time in months, I had drawn the laser gun I had pilfered from the Netherim Bunker and chose to start using that to deal with Karvokar.

As tough as their hide was, it couldn’t hold up against a scorching hot beam of light burning straight through it.

That helped, certainly…but I was intimately aware that there was only so much longer that this could continue. That wasn’t even considering how we were likely going to need to fight our way back out of here, after we had saved the captives.

That was a problem for later, and I had to believe we were capable of rising to meet it.

Finally, after a length of time that blurred together in my head, that I was frankly not able to put a number to it…

We arrived at the center of the hold, after crawling over thousands of bodies to reach it.

It wasn’t quite what I was expecting.

It was a lake. The entire hold of Karag Korvok was built around a lake…

Of blood.

Easily miles of in diameter, the entire artificial seeming lake was filled with the thick, crimson liquid. It was nearly perfectly circular in shape, judging by the ‘shores’ of it that I could see in the distance, and it was ringed by the nine relatively smaller towers I had noticed from the entry into the city. They thrust from the shores of the bloody lake in a half circle facing the entrance to the lake, with significant gaps between each of them. Just barely, I could see some kind of emplacements at the base of each of those flesh covered, needle sharp spires. Perhaps a build, or even just an entrance into the seemingly larger towers that reached the roof of this cavern.

And in the center of the lake rose both the largest of the towers, the one that pierced right through the heart of what I assumed to be Nehushtan.

But was also the source of the blood.

I hadn’t been able to see from a distance, but there were grooves carved all along the surface of the central tower. Every time the heart far above us beat, a shower of blood escaped the gigantic, tortured muscle. That blood then ran down the inlets in the very device of torture skewering it, all the way down to the basin of the lake.

I don’t know how long Nehushtan had been affixed to that ceiling, but it had been long enough to fill up an entire lake with his blood.

There was one problem, though.

There wasn’t a way to reach the central tower. It was literally rising from the center of the lake with no bridge that led to it.

And that’s where I was certain we would find our missing captives.
