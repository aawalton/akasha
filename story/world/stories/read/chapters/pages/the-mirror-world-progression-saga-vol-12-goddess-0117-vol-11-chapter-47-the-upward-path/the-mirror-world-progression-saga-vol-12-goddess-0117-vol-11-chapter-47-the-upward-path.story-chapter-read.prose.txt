The work on the rune was tedious and neverending, like fashioning a wreath made of calligraphy strokes, and one ‘word’ could be found to be nonsensical after an hour, requiring going back to the ‘stable’ core build, which then needed rebranching to find the right meanings to synthesize — to weave into the wreath, to make it functional, to make it whole and complete.

Samantha had never enjoyed herself more than to singularly dedicate herself to such a task, to pour her heart and soul into rewriting the rules of a world’s magic with nothing else bugging her. When and where else, except in this place, could she spend a week doing nothing else aside from designing spell artistry? It was downright indulgent. But she could get away with it, so why not?

With enough time spent in careful, beautiful cultivation, all became ready, and a brand-new spell alteration was born.

Ashawein dragged Othenia down into the catacombs for the final demonstration of her ‘prototype’ new magic circle modification, which was actually already tested secretly — she just wanted to gloat over the doubter and make her eat crow.

As Othenia had her arms crossed, tapping her foot impatiently with a very doubtful look on her face, Ashawein carefully constructed the runic circle together with the firebird summoning.

At some point, Othenia was frowning at what she was seeing, muttering under her breath as she analyzed the magic. “This is absurd…”

Completing the final bits, Ashawein stood and wiped her brow, grinning like a devil at the other. “Yes, I’ll show you absurd, alright! Behold!” Finishing the casting with a dramatic two-handed gesture. The ritual circle, uncommonly very small, barely large enough for a human to stand in, flared like bright flames nearly to the giantess’s height.

The hawk-like, fiery-feathered bird materialized in significantly less dramatic flair on the floor, chirping once as it looked around the room, currently empty of enemies. It then just stood there looking bored. The circle under it, a pentagram, was like an aura underneath it, a ‘shadow of light’ rather than anything physical. This alone was nothing particularly special…

Nonetheless, Othenia looked stricken, her eyes wide as a hand went to her mouth. Obviously, she was reading what it could do. “No,” she breathed, “it can’t be! Is that a-... does it…?”

Ashawein drank in the glorious sight of the draconic princess’s shock. “See for yourself, Princess!” She snapped her fingers and whistled to get the firebird’s attention. It turned to her, and Ashawein pointed down a hallway and commanded, “Wander, firebird, and destroy all grub demons — your sworn enemies — any that you can find, for the length of your duration!”

The firebird chirped and took flight on flapping wings of fire, soaring down the hallway like a burning bullet. The runic circle continued underneath it, the ‘shadow of light’ cast below on the floor, and stayed centered on it.

Othenia gasped, and color drained from her face. “A moving gate rune. Th-that’s impossible. It cannot be.”

Dusting her hands, Ashawein put on a smug smile. “It can in my world, Blue Beauty.” Putting her hands on her hips, she added cockily, “Care to hop along for the ride, then?”

Suddenly, the reality kind of quivered, as ‘Ashawein’ was mortified at what she just said. Othenia also looked shocked and surprised as she regarded Ashawein up and down, caught in blended offense and intrigue at such audaciousness directed at her.

Oops. Ashawein wouldn’t say that, just as I guess I wouldn’t have back in the early days.

Surprisingly — or perhaps not, considering who this was inside — Othenia finally shrugged and raised an eyebrow as she replied, “Teach me this insane stunt, which rewrote a thousand years of principles set into stone, and I might consider it.”

Even more embarrassed, Ashawein, suddenly outside of Samantha’s control, cried, “I-I’m going to go monitor the results, now!” before literally running away.

Ah well. She’ll recover! Egads, that was fun!

Hordes of roaming birds were fashioned, now immune to the limited powers of the demon grubs to defend against them. Because the grubs found nooks and crannies to hide in, she also fashioned ‘giant’ killer centipedes to hunt them down, and made sure to give them a ravenous scent and hunger for grub flesh, so they’d eat every bit of the grubs they could find.

It was not an instantaneous affair, but in time, her determined and multidimensional strategy became victorious, eradicating the demon grubs with the telltale verification of a System quest. The caretakers profused their intimate thanks for the annihilation of an infestation they thought impossible to conduct, which they previously considered sealing off and keeping quarantined. They offered up a whole wagon full of gold and jewels, which seemed to greatly please Othenia. She stayed in the wagon most of the way back and could be heard rolling around in the loot and cackling.

Yeah, I think I get why the queen is doing this, now.

More tasks came and were beaten, one by one, over the course of months. She collected the Seven Golden Berries of Uljairia, she slew the Gargantua Under the Swamp, she tricked the Old Bitter Alchemist into drinking his own poisoned brew, and thereafter took his grimoire. Each task brought greater glory to the Dragon Queen, extending power and influence across distant domains of the massive wilderness. More and more came to her palace, just to pay homage to a ‘capable ruler,’ whom tales said charmed a heroic giant of the clouds. Still, the tales went that she gave no personal audiences.

It was certainly true that her daughter charmed a giantess, as Ashawein grew closer and closer to Othenia in their many travels and their camaraderie together. Inevitably, Ashawein fell desperately in love with her, though she was too awkward to express it. In this, she failed her upbringing, as giants usually preferred to wear their hearts on their sleeves.

So came the most impossible task of all, as the twelfth task was at hand: obtain a draught of the Nectar of the Gods, the greatest treasure known to the world. There were only a few holders of this, and all of them were deities. How could a mere hero of the giants take on such daunting beings at all? Could such a thing even feasibly be stolen?

Ashawein parted from Othenia’s company for the first time since meeting her, to leave for the beloved lands of Yarune she’d not been back to for years. Fennor remained king, fortifying his lands and building up a mighty military for the inevitable scourge that would come. Ashawein asked a favor: an audience with his great-grandfather, the God of Fire.

But before Fennor could reply, a magnificent, giant golden steed with wings appeared in the throne room, drawing wonder and awe from all present. It spoke. “I am Elleride, messenger and herald of the gods, who now relates their message. Ashawein of the Load Bearing Clouds, your fantastical deeds the world over have gained our attention and admiration. Please conduct yourself with our herald, and rise to meet us immediately, as one ultimately favored by us. We wish to meet you.”

Overcome with awe, Ashawein could only agree, and so she mounted Elleride and disappeared in a flash of light with him.

The sky in the world of the gods was not sunny, but bruised by storm clouds with thunder rolling within them, though no rain came. At the summit of a great mountain, a circular temple of enormous pillars existed, and here was where the gods met, on an array of many thrones.

Elleride conducted Ashawein to a central podium amidst them, as each deity materialized on their thrones — truly titanic entities to make even a giant little more than a finger snack, if a bit meatier than most.

“Ashawein,” came the booming voice of a god with red skin, and hair and eyes of roaring fire, “By my suggestion are you here, as I know you well, but it was a consensus reached among us to invite you. Welcome.”

Swallowing hard, Ashawein looked around at all the intimidating figures. Two thrones remained empty, though she was not so knowledgeable as to quickly determine who was missing. ‘Th-thank you,” she managed.

A god she recognized leaned forward on his throne, one of a wizened countenance and a great white beard. He was the Ancient Father, and held a scepter that forever dripped blood from the top — a reminder of how order was established and maintained. “The times are dire, and there is a need for the greatest of heroes. In our observations, none match you for that title. Know that we have blessed your conduct, and we seek a more intimate consecration.”

From below, walking up the steps toward her, a procession of tall servants came, each dressed in differing ornate robes of many colors. One held a great golden bowl on her head, walking with intimate care. Finally, she knelt right at Ashawein’s feet, and a glittering liquid could be seen inside the bowl. Another servant knelt and offered a wineskin of beautifully decorated leather to her.

The god known as the Guardian of Rituals intoned, “We offer you a draught of the Supreme Nectar, and thereby the first and greatest step upward into our court, not as a guest, but as a true child. By drinking your draught, you will attain immortality; by accepting, you will become like us. The road is long to these high seats we sit — the most principal and glorious of thrones — but only a drinker can ever walk that path.”

As Ashawein stood wide-eyed in shock and awe, Samantha felt control pass over to her.

Oh, shit. I’m up!
