An arc of electric-blue lights flew out of Henry’s arm, catching a swooping Stormgull mid-dive. It gave the harried man—a civilian, Henry assumed—enough time to get back on his feet and make a run for it, while above them the bird shrieked and scrambled away. Its initial target forgotten, the bird, still cawing in outrage, swiveled its gaze back and forth to look at who’d just attacked it, and it almost dodged, if it wasn’t for the last second Telekinetic grip Henry managed to wrap it with. A moment later, a second, third, and fourth arcs from Lightning Discharge scoured its thirty-feet wide body. The seagull dropped out of the sky, trailing smoke and burnt feathers in its wake before it crashed on one of the platform, where more Treeguards riddled it with spears to make sure it stayed down.

Henry grunted and ignored the pounding headache he was feeling from restraining such a large creature as he scanned the area for another skirmish he could contribute in. It had been a mid-B-rank monster, so while it wasn’t the worst he’d had to deal with, it was still taxing, especially considering this wasn’t the first time he’d pulled the maneuver and it likely won’t be the last.

“[At least we can kill the smaller ones easily enough,]” called out Maurice as the arm holding him whipped out of the way of a smaller swooping bird. Henry had assigned one of his Octominds to act as a pilot for the crab, so Maurice could focus on shooting. The large seagull swerved and swooped back toward them, only for Maurice to chuck a black sphere in its path, which, for some reason, chose to barrel through it. Maybe it hadn’t looked that dangerous to it. Maybe it hadn’t seen what it had done to the other birds. Or maybe it just didn’t have the time to move. Whatever the case was, it should have dodged as if its life depended on it. Because it really did.

The bird impacted the imploding bubble, and Henry barely heard the squawk before it turned into a sickening crunch as the bird suddenly found itself occupying a much, much smaller space than it used to.

“[There’s just too many of them],” Henry said, looking away from the falling, bloodied carcass. “[And their boss is… holding up well,]” he added, holding a few arms up to block the cutting arcs of magic that came at him. His skin was split open, but he quickly healed the shallow cuts with a pulse of Life Surge as the bird that sent them swooped down. Henry whipped those same arms out and barely made contact as the feathered bastard panicked and twisted away, but Henry’s suckers had made contact, and that was all that he needed.

With a flash of harrowing red lights, Henry let out a Predation-empowered Lightning Discharge that made the bird seize and buck, scouring it for a brief moment with red arcs of electricity. The bird, unable to move its wing, tumbled down in a dive and someone might have had time to finish it off, but Henry caught with an arm which he then used to pump it full of venoms. One of which happened to be the Fulminis Bane that he’d gotten from the electric kraken, which would make the bird even more sensitive to lightning.

The toxin seeped into the B-rank, but it still had plenty of vigor left in it and already, it was beginning to shrug off the shock. After all, it was a high B-rank. Henry estimated it was in the seventies. Maybe even eighties. But the cocktail of venoms he had injected it with through Voracious Grip was taking its toll, making its movement sluggish, and so Henry followed it all up with an activation of Siphoning Touch to sap even more of its strengths.

At that point, the bird realized it might be in trouble.

Blades of wind exploded out of the spasming seagull as it screeched, and more of the flock came down on them, but Henry didn’t let up. With a grunt, he summoned a handful of clones and send them up to distract, some of which he detonated into clouds of ink while he continued filling his captured target with venoms, adding some numbing effects to the cocktail while he let out another Aspect empowered Lightning Discharge.

This time, he gave it a taste of Thaumaturgy.

Blinding arcs of blue light lit up the space, while more of his glossy black clones appeared before whipping their many arms up. Their Echoing Arm skills weren’t as powerful as his own, but they were plenty enough to force the oncoming birds to re-consider, especially with how many detached, phantom like arms flew at them. But soon enough, the birds will realize that the arms didn’t pack that much of a punch, though Henry already had a plan for that.

“Have the clones focus on the smaller ones,” he ordered the Octominds. “Focus your fire on one bird at a time.”

Then, Henry gave away control of the clones to his a couple of his Octominds and turned his attention back to the bird in his arms.

The monster shuddered and trembled as it tried to extricate itself out of his grip, but with every second that went by, more of its life force and strength was stolen away. It was now too weak to wrench itself free, so Henry looked up above and felt his skin turn clammy.

That fight wasn’t going so well, which was much more of a concern.

Velistraine fell back down to one of the platforms, landing on her right leg and stumbling as she kept looking up. She edged closer to the platform’s center, and he didn’t miss how she favored her right leg. Through the gaze of higher clone, Henry realized she had taken a nasty cut on her left thigh from the A-rank.

Weirdly enough, the wound wasn’t closing up. Even though Zerathstra was near. As for the guardian, he was keeping the bird busy, but neither of them had the upper-hand, which was alarming enough in and of itself. The ape was standing on a summoned, giant leaf, dodging and batting the scythe-like arcs of barely visible blue light. Zerathstra got out of the way from those that went wide, but he couldn’t allow those that wouldn’t from hitting Thalis, and it was visibly a struggle to counter the bird’s magic with just his club.

Henry found the scene a little alarming. Zerathstra had been protecting this city for a while from what he’d heard, which meant he was no push-over. So how come two A-ranks were struggling against just one? \

Was the Windscythe King that powerful of an opponent?

Henry tried not to dwell on it and just kept thinning out the swooping gulls, releasing the bird that had died in his arms in the meantime. Maybe the big bird just had an advantage, considering how easily it could dodge and disengage from the A-ranks.

Or maybe we’re just unlucky, and an actual powerful creature has just showed up, Henry grimly thought, stealing another glance up and this time, it seemed that Maurice had noticed. “[Do you think we can help?]” the crab asked as he peered up at the bird, right as it beat its wing and smacked Velistraine out of the air once more.

Could they?

“[I think… if I used everything I have, I might be able to keep it still for a second before I’m completely out of commission. I can’t even imagine the backlash I’d get from trying to hold that thing still. So… maybe we can give it a shot if things are really looking grim, but otherwise, we should focus on those we can affect more effectively. The B-ranks.]”

The two focused back on the swooping and shooting Stormgulls, but Henry never stopped thinking of what he could do to tip the balance. He could maybe pool his Octominds and spend a good chunk of essence to support him? Would that even matter, considering the difference in levels?

No matter how Henry turned and twisted his plans around, he didn’t see how he’d walk away without the worst backlash he’d ever taken, if not more. If he tried to get up there and use his venoms, he’d get shredded. Even if he tried being stealthy, what were the odds he’d slip the bird’s perception? No, he’d get caught and minced. Most likely, that thing had an amazing sight. Probably the best he’d ever encountered.

Approaching a little and locking it with Telekinesis might be the best option, he thought, glancing back up. Would they need his help? All logic said that he wouldn’t make the difference. But a fight of that level, every second would count. If he could even buy them half of one, Henry would willingly suffer through the pain it would entail.

A few minutes went by as the two continued picking off the weaker bird. Henry used everything in his arsenal, but slowly, progressively, he began favoring mana-stealing abilities. Just in case. Thankfully, by now, the Stormgulls’ number had significantly dropped and the Treeguards of Thalis were holding them off pretty well. With the birds winnowed down, the warrior of Thalis were doing much better. But unfortunately, Henry couldn’t say the same about the A-rank’s bout and at the end of the day, that would be the one that really mattered.

Velistraine had managed to heal her leg, but she was clearly tired. Henry couldn’t tell if Zerathstra was holding up well, but the bird seemed to be just fine.

If Henry was going to help, it might be now or never. Things weren’t too grim. But if he contributed… he’d bet that’d be worth an achievement or two. After all, he was about to hit B-rank himself, and he was going to have a chat with a certain omniscient being soon.

Just a quick intervention, he told himself. To give the A-ranks an opportunity to end things, and to guarantee himself a stronger upgrade to B-rank. Though if Henry was to get up there, it was likely going to get dicey and for that reason, he’d really rather risk his own life and no one else’s.

“[Maurice. I’m going up there. I need to leave you with a clone.]”

Henry braced, expecting an onslaught of arguments out of the crab for daring to suggest he was leaving him behind but instead, the chirpy response almost made him pause. “[Got it! I’ll protect the clone. Are you teleporting to it?]”

Maurice had quickly pivoted and was already detaching himself from the limb that had been holding him before he scuttled up wide branch they stood on. Henry stared for a moment, then chuckled, his gaze still set on the scene above.“[I expected you to give me more of a hard time.]”

“[We’re in the middle of a fight! I’ll do that later,]” he chirped.

Henry barked out a laugh and floated up while his clone got a hold of Maurice. “[Fair enough. And yes, in case things go south, I’ll teleport to it and we pivot.]”

After telling the Octomind in charge of that clone to immediately tell him in case of danger, Henry slowly lifted himself up. He didn’t need to get all the way up there, just close enough that he’d be in range to speak to Zerathstra. But he still made himself invisible, just in case, and when he got there, he spoke to the ape as soon as the latter disengaged from his last exchange with the bird. After all, he didn’t want to be the distraction that got the guardian injured. “[I can try to immobilize for a second at most,]” Henry said to both Zerathstra and Velistraine as she hopped up over thin air.

Three pairs of eyes immediately swiveled to him, because of course they’d see right through him, and then Henry felt it.

The Windscythe King’s attention.

The air turned heavy. Quiet. It was like standing in the eye of the storm, and being spared its fury, but only temporarily. At any time, the peace could be broken, and a whirlwind of blades and sharp edges would shred him where he stood.

Henry swallowed, and tried to get his arms to stop shaking. The A-rank’s aura didn’t even feel like it was targeting him, but it was overbearing, and even though his own Aspect pushed against the influence, flaring against what they felt to be a challenge, it wasn’t anywhere near the level required to push away the heavy gaze of the predator ahead of him, but it was enough to not allow the predator to squish him under its presence.

Henry grit his beak, but he didn’t dare continue his ascent. It didn’t matter, his message reached its targets. As for the bird… had it heard his telepathic message? It didn’t seem like it. Maybe it had just noticed the movement, and maybe noticed the skill itself. At least, he hoped that was the case.

“[Fishing for achievements, are we? Well, if you can, go for it. It would certainly save us some time if you can give us an opening],” said Velistraine, hopping a little away from the bird as she waited to see if his plan would work.

“[I’d rather you don’t risk it,” said the ape, flitting forward to strike at the bird and keep it busy. “[Sooner or later, it will leave. But if you must… then make sure you don’t get hit, Henry. You might not survive it,]” Zerathstra warned, landing back on his floating leaf before he stole a look down toward Henry. “[Let us know when you’re about to begin. Otherwise, let us handle this.]”

Henry’s skin suddenly felt clammy as the pressure over his back seemed to double. What was he doing up here? He wanted the achievement, sure, and any other time, Zerathstra’s warning and mild dismissal would have been enough to send him running. But Henry wanted to be up there with them. If he ran away, he might never make it up there.

No risks, no rewards, he reminded himself. Taking a deep breath, Henry began calling on his magic, and as if sensing it, the intense yellow eyes of the giant bird swiveled to him, doubling the pressure for a beat and then—it just looked away. Completely dismissing him. The weight on his soul was lifted away, and Henry would have been very insulted—rightfully so—but he hadn’t time. Because the massive white and gray bird flicked its tail feathers, and a swarm of zig-zagging, air-cutting arcs came flying at him.

He just had to not get hit. How hard could that be?
