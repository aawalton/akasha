The room was different from what Noah had initially expected. For a moment, he had the feeling that he had stepped into some steampunk world, the scent of aged leather and gunpowder lingering in the air.

The wooden walls bore deep scars as if they had withstood both time and the myriad pelting of bullets, while oil lamps cast flickering shadows across shelves lined with odd trinkets— rusted pocket watches, empty bullet casings, and the occasional wanted poster curling at the edges.

A brass fan in the corner spun sluggishly, creaking with every slow rotation of its blade— its rhythmic sound echoing like the heartbeat of the room. A clock on the wall – its hands unevenly mismatched, one sleek and modern, the other rusted and jagged – ticked in an offbeat tempo as if refusing to abide by conventional time.

Tick-Tock. A familiar sound, one similar to the clock-like noise constantly emitted by the Time Devouring Crocodile.

Once Noah registered most of the information in the room, he focused on the man who had not raised their head even as he entered.

The man sitting with his feet on the desk, a harmonica dancing on his lips, perfectly fitted the atmosphere of the room. His cowboy hat was pulled low, obscuring his eyes, but the lazy tilt of his head spoke of someone who had seen it all and was never easily impressed by anything.

The battered desk in front of him was covered in papers, some crumpled as if tossed aside mid-thought, others neatly stacked with mechanical precision. An old revolver, its barrel polished to a dull shine, lay within easy reach, resting beside a half-empty glass of something dark and potent.

The mismatched feeling of steampunk stopped right there. As he squinted his eyes, Noah could sniff no odor, feel no temperature, nor hear even the faintest heartbeat from the man before him.

“I heard that the so-called ‘Boss’ of this den wanted to see me. I expected to see someone else though.” He had seen the wanted poster of the Pied Piper, and it was certainly not this man. Then again, not like changing appearances was hard with the resources available in this vast galaxy.

The gentle sound coming from the harmonica stopped with Noah’s words filled with derision, and finally, the man seemed to notice his presence as he raised his head. One of his eyes showed an eerie red glow while the other swirled in a calm blue.

As the man raised his head, the dim light of the oil lamps cast shifting shadows across his face, highlighting sharp, angular features chiseled by time and hardship.

His skin had the weathered look of someone who had walked through deserts of both dust and death, yet there was an uncanny stillness to him.

One of his eyes glowed an eerie crimson, faintly pulsing as it observed Noah keenly, while the other remained an unshaken cool blue, calm and unreadable as its serene color suggested.

His attire fit the theme of an old-world sheriff. A long, dust-streaked coat draped over his shoulders.

Only then was Noah able to pinpoint the source of his confusion. Beneath the coat, a mechanical exoskeleton ran along his arms, segments of dark steel adorned with intricate engravings that pulsed with faint energy.

The fingers of his left hand weren’t flesh but forged metal, moving with an unnatural smoothness as they idly tapped against the harmonica in his grasp.

Not just his finger. Not even just his arm.

“An Android?” Noah voiced the query idly. However, he soon shook his head. This man was different from the mysterious bunny girl he had met in The Traveler, he was sure of it. He was also different from robots like Tin or Crow.

“A cyborg then?”

“I guess heroes need to be smart as well and also have nerves of steel.” At long last, the man spoke. His voice was filled with amusement, and his non-robotic eye twinkled and mirrored the emotions of his voice.

Standing up, he revealed more of his body, and Noah was able to confirm his earlier suspicions. Outside of his head, the entire body of the man was completely covered in cybernetic parts.

“Heh, I guess you would know something about steel. How do you even piss? Or have… Ohh.. Sorry. Must be kinda sad.” Noah said with a pitying tone as he gazed at a particular place of the strange man.

The man twitched at the not-so-veiled insult.

“Haha… I have lived in this city, infested with rats, for a few decades now. Yet, it has been a long time since I faced anyone this bold.” He took off his hat and placed it before his chest.

“Bold hero. I have no name for you. But here, I am called the Mayor. Welcome to this miserable city where only exiled rats and other rodents loiter.” Noah eyed the man, the weapon on the table, and the woman behind him. Carefully, he weighed his options in this scenario.

Attacking now would certainly help him blow off some steam. But taking out the small fish just for his satisfaction seemed like a pre-emptive mistake for this operation.

“Mayor, is it? Then tell me. Why was I kidnapped?”

“To be sold, of course.” The man gave a good-natured laugh as he approached Noah. “This young appearance, this handsome visage, and the feat of fighting off The Crocodile. Can you even imagine how much you are worth at the moment? Aristocratic men and women would definitely want you in their collection.” His expression was filled with amusement as he observed Noah’s expression.

But the amusement in his eyes did not last long, replaced with astonishment as he did not see even a lick of emotion in the young man’s face.

“I know I am handsome, and everyone wants me. No need to state the obvious. Though, I am pissed that some people actually think they have enough money to buy me. I am priceless, you know?”

“...” The Mayor stopped, speechless at the shamelessness of the young man. In his career, he kidnapped and sold many people.

Some of them had been princes or princesses on their home planet. Some had been knights or warriors of renown. Yet, everyone of them showed similar reactions when put on the same pedestal as Noah’s.

Anger, humiliation, rage, resistance, screaming threats, hurling insults, and finally, slowly giving up as they went through the training and had their pride repeatedly broken.

Even a few minutes ago, the elf princess and the other abducted prisoners showed the same set of emotions.

But this one… This one was definitely different.

“You… I like you.” He held Noah’s chin and looked down at him. But far from disgust, fear, or even hatred, all he could see was amusement in Noah’s eyes.

“I also like people like you.” Noah grinned, “Eating guys like you helps me feel no remorse.”

The Mayor shuddered at the unsettling glance, and his hand went to the holster at his hip where he had placed his second weapon. Drawing his gun in one swift motion, he pointed it at Noah, and his finger nearly pulled the trigger.

“Boss!?”

Only after hearing the voice of the Princess did he stop. If he had a heart, he was sure that it would be going into a frenzy right about now. His instincts were screaming at him that they needed to put down this young man here and now, lest he destroy everything.

Nevertheless, he knew that it was impossible to do that no matter how much he desired such an outcome. He may have been working with the Piper for years, but if he damaged or even destroyed such precious merchandise, then this would be the end for him.

“Fuck.” He cursed and pressed the gun near Noah’s forehead. “Now, listen to me if…”

“Shoot me.”

“Huh…”

“If you are going to enter some weird rambling about how I should lay low or whatever I rather you shoot me than force me to listen to regurgitated cliche bullshit.” Noah slapped his head directly against the gun’s barrel, a manic grin on his face. He was the perfect example of a deranged lunatic right now.

“So? What are you going to do?” He asked and waited for a few seconds, but when nothing happened, he sighed and took a step back. “Nowadays, men are all talk. Then again, I guess you aren’t really a man anymore. Did your courage vanish with your dick?”
