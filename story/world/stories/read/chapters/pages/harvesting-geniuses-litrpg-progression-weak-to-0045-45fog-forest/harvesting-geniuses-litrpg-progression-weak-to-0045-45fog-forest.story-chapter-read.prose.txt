Luciria had regained consciousness a few hours ago. She was hiding behind a bush, praying that nothing would find her. Or rather—praying that at least someone like her brother Sven or the senior butler James would. Beneath the ashen fog, the world was dark and desolate. She was terrified that if she shouted, a monster would come to kill her.

Luciria was called the Sword Princess by her family—a shining future the Halfrin name could be proud of. But facing the Locust Demon had taught her something: no matter how miraculous her exam scores were, at her core, she was just a weak, frightened little girl. A coward unworthy of her name and reputation. Sooner or later, her fragile true self would be exposed.

When that happened, Grandfather would stop caring. The people who'd placed their expectations on her would point fingers and denounce her. Luciria's life was only ever this. She couldn't do anything except study the sword and train her Planar. Some people wanted to live long lives. Some wanted to grow stronger for revenge. Others chased fame and status.

Luciria had never wished for any of those things. She'd simply believed that if she worked hard enough, everyone would be happy—and someday, all that effort would come back to her as a reward. But when she'd woken up in this forest, she didn't have the courage to wander out and search for other survivors.

She was a cowardly child hiding under a bush, waiting to be rescued. Luciria trembled. Her stomach ached with hunger. But knowing she'd been pulled into a Hollow Gate by accident, she didn't have the nerve to leave the tangle of branches that scratched red welts into her skin.

'Mother... I miss you so much.' Luciria's eyes brimmed with tears. The pressure bore down on the small girl. Then she heard it—the snap of a twig. Someone had stepped on dry branches and was heading her way. Luciria prayed it wasn't a monster.

If a monster came this way, what would she do?!

Her tiny heart hammered so hard it nearly burst from her chest. She wanted to squeeze her eyes shut and block everything out. But her gaze drifted toward the source of the sound. Through the dark grey fog, a small silhouette drew closer to her bush.

Very small. Could it be a goblin?

Luciria gripped her sword hilt, ready. If it was a goblin, she could fight it... right? A small, weak creature no taller than a hundred centimeters. Surely even someone as pathetic as her could kill one? Luciria summoned her courage. Her breathing grew more ragged by the second, but she had no choice.

Then the shadow entered her line of sight, and the picture became clear. It wasn't a hideous green monster—it was a handsome young boy, carefully tiptoeing forward. A child several years younger than her. Emerald-green eyes darting nervously. He glanced left and right, scanning every visible inch of his surroundings.

Luciria's eyes went wide with recognition. The boy who'd come with her savior.

He was here too? Luciria thought of Jinyo. She'd assumed he'd gone home already while she'd stayed behind to watch the other examinees and check results.

If his son was here, there was a strong chance Jinyo had been pulled in as well. Luciria watched Gretel pass by her hiding spot. Her heart wavered—until she couldn't help herself.

"Wait."

"??" Gretel flinched when a girl older than him suddenly sprang up from a bush. How had that low shrub concealed her so completely? Even looking closely, he still couldn't figure out who she was or whether they'd ever met.

"Hold on—where's your father?"

"My dad... you mean Bro Yo?"

"Bro Yo? He's your older brother?"

"No, he's my dad. I want to find him too. And Mom. And Big Sis Naho." Gretel was relieved to have found another person. Then it clicked—he recognized the girl in front of him!

"You're the one who ranked fourth on the exam! Right? Everyone was talking about you!"

"Um... yeah." Luciria shifted awkwardly.

"Thank goodness—we're saved! Yahoo!" Gretel threw his arms into the air. He'd been on the verge of tears when he first appeared in this nightmare forest and found no one. Gretel had been born and raised in a military village. He'd been drilled since preschool on what to do—and what not to do—if you ended up inside a Hollow Gate. Don't shout. Don't scream. Don't cry loudly enough to attract monsters.

The best course of action was to stay put and wait for help. But Gretel had nearly stumbled into a monster, forcing him to tiptoe away. He'd thought he was done for when he stepped on that twig. Lucky break—he'd found a girl stronger than some adults!

"I'm not that impressive. Don't get your hopes up."

"Why not? You're amazing! I saw you fight the dummies. That was incredible—slash, slash, slash! I wish I could do that."

"That was just against dummies. In real life, I'm pathetic. If not for your father, I'd already be dead."

"Him? I mean, sure, he's good. But is he really better than you?" Gretel tilted his head. The naive boy had seen Jinyo fight off thugs ten-on-one—but compared to that, Luciria's accomplishments seemed far more impressive. Even if Jinyo had outscored her on the practical exam, to Gretel, Luciria—with all her recognition and trophies—stood leagues above his still-inexperienced father.

"Your father is incredible. He's my role model. If you're here, he'll definitely come to save you."

"But my mom's even better. My mom is the absolute best! If we find her, we'll get out of here without a scratch. And not just that—my mom can take down a boss in a single strike!" Gretel sang the praises of Rina's greatness to this older girl he'd just met. Luciria had a feeling that if she kept complimenting Jinyo, Gretel would one-up her every time with tales of his mother's heroics.

This kid's a total mama's boy... Luciria eyed Gretel with a twinge of jealousy. She shook her head to chase away the silly thought.

"The bush is small, but the two of us could probably still hide in it."

"Why would we hide? If you're good with a sword, you should be able to walk through a Hollow Gate just fine."

"I..."

"Think about it. If we find other people, our group gets bigger. The bigger the group, the safer we are."

"But what if we run into a monster?"

"Then you fight it! You beat Level 30—what's there to be scared of?"

"I..." Luciria couldn't bring herself to admit she'd wet herself in front of a Level 30 monster before. Even if that particular one hadn't been an ordinary Level 30. Gretel took a short stride forward. Luciria panicked. She had to choose: stay cowering in the bush, or follow Gretel.

It was mortifying that she didn't have the courage of a seven-year-old. Luciria ran after Gretel, staying close. This child was her savior's son. If she let him come to harm, she'd be betraying the life Jinyo had saved.

Throughout their walk, the two barely spoke. Luciria wanted to ask about Jinyo, but the shy girl with poor social skills couldn't manage a direct question. Gretel had energy to spare for conversation, but he wasn't in the mood. Even having the youngest examinee as a travel companion didn't guarantee Gretel's safety.

Luciria was amazed by the seven-year-old's composure under pressure. Gretel was far more cautious and level-headed than she was—and braver, in ways she simply wasn't. She wanted to know where he'd found such courage, walking through a dark forest shrouded in ashen fog.

Luciria's stomach growled. She clutched it, blushing faintly when Gretel turned to stare at her.

"Hungry?"

"A little."

"Want some of these? I found some wild berries while I was looking for people." Gretel pulled berries wrapped in a washcloth from his pants pocket. Vibrant blue-violet wild berries, fresh and inviting. Luciria took them and popped a few into her mouth, chewing gently. The taste was sweet and fragrant, and a strange energy flowed through her body.

It made her happy.

"They're delicious."

"Right? Great—now that you've eaten my food, you can't abandon me and run off."

"So it was a bribe all along. Clever kid."

"Of course. My mom raised me right!"

"..." Your mom taught you to bribe people? That's not exactly healthy development!

The truth was, Gretel had copied the trick from the adults in the military village.

"You're not eating any?"

"I don't like eating things I don't recognize. Mom says if you eat unknown stuff in the forest, you die."

"!!??" Luciria's composure shattered.

"Just kidding. I know wild berries perfectly well from the Plant Encyclopedia. I'm just not hungry. Go ahead." Gretel laughed, clutching his stomach at having pranked his gullible companion. Luciria might seem like a melancholy child, but she was actually quite lively. Luciria was secretly annoyed that she felt like she'd been used as a test subject.

Her mother had once told her that older people should be kind to those younger than them. Don't hold grudges against children. That was the first step to becoming an adult.

The two found a place to rest. Along the way, they'd spotted strange-looking figures.

People in tattered clothes that looked like they'd stepped out of the Middle Ages. Their heads were covered by large pumpkin helmets. They wandered aimlessly—and seemed unable to sense Luciria or Gretel at all.

Luciria's sharp eye for detail helped them avoid the creatures' patrol paths. Gretel contributed calm intelligence and a boldness Luciria lacked. Beneath the dense ashen fog, this place seemed far more chaotic than it appeared.

The monsters carried farm implements—sickles, short swords, blood-stained rakes. The blood was fresh, recently used to hurt someone. At one point, Gretel accidentally kicked a corpse without realizing it. An adult body wearing an examinee badge. That confirmed it: they weren't the only ones in this fog. Thousands of people were fighting for survival against slaughter. And despite all of it, everything remained utterly, impossibly silent.

"Sis, I think we might be in trouble."

"Why?"

"Because it looks like we can't shout. If we want to know if someone's nearby, we have to get close. We'll never know if it's a monster or a person until we're right on top of them."

"How do you know we can't shout?"

"Well, if I were being killed, I'd scream for my mom as loud as I could. We've already seen people who were killed—but we haven't heard a single sound." Gretel answered the question. It was entirely possible that just a hundred meters away, there was a bloodbath happening right now. The boy's small hands clenched tight. None of this escaped Luciria's eyes.

Luciria had been wondering how a child so much younger than her could seem so fearless. Now she thought she had her answer.

"Want to hold hands?"

"If you're scared, I'll let you." Gretel reached out and took her hand—small and delicate. Luciria's hand was cold as though she'd just stepped out of an air-conditioned room. The chill actually eased Gretel's own nervousness. The pair moved forward with far more caution now. Where before they'd wanted to find people quickly, they now feared encountering monsters more.

The farther they walked, the more monsters there seemed to be. They had to crawl along the undergrowth on their hands and knees, making themselves as small as possible and breathing as softly as they could. Until they sensed a massive presence approaching while they hid under a bush. Gretel thought they should run, but Luciria held him back. It was already too late to flee.

Heavy footsteps and a shadow almost as tall as the trees. Different from the pumpkin-heads—which, though frightening, had human-sized frames and wielded garden tools as weapons. What was closing in now was a true monster.

Luciria prayed it would pass them by. Then it stopped. It reached out and plucked the same blue-violet berries she'd eaten—the delicious fruit that grew almost everywhere in this forest. It fumbled with hoof-hands ill-suited for picking, awkwardly pinched a berry, and shoved it into its mouth.

It groaned with pleasure. It didn't seem to notice the children hidden in a bush just a meter and a half away.

It stripped the bush clean of berries. Luciria thought it would leave once it was full. Then it walked toward the bush she was hiding in. In that instant, survival instinct overpowered logic and reason.

She scooped up Gretel and bolted. Luciria didn't know if this was the right call. No—what even counted as right? Should she have crept away while it was focused on the berries? Should she have stayed hidden—maybe it coming closer was just a coincidence?

Luciria didn't know. She just needed to get away from it. Even if she wanted to rewind time and choose differently, it was too late. She just had to run.

"Sis Lucy, put me down!"

"No!"

"I'm heavy! I can run on my own!"

"You can't!" A seven-year-old standing under 120 centimeters was not going to outrun a two-and-a-half-meter-tall deer walking on two legs. Gretel would die the instant he left her arms. Though truthfully, a girl who hadn't even started middle school didn't have much right to say that either. Three of her strides equaled a single step from the Eltharok.

"HRAAAGH!!" The Eltharok bellowed. It stomped its feet and surged forward, accelerating toward them. Gretel, who could see everything coming, hit the peak of his terror. He was scared—of course he was scared. He'd been scared this whole time. He'd just been showing less of it around Luciria. But in truth, he'd been barely holding it together. And now, after convincing himself he could survive without adult help, he was confronted with the reality that nothing was as easy or as expected as he'd believed.

In the end, he was just a child who couldn't keep it together anymore.

"MAMA!!!"
