[ Abyssal Draugr, lvl 6 ]

The walking corpses were one of the most horrifying things Elise had ever seen. Objectively, they were hardly any worse than the other Abyssal monsters, but seeing the same eldritch features on a human face made her feel sick.

No two were the same, but they were all equally distorted. Their lower jaws were hidden by a mass of tentacles, and there were more tentacles poking out of their nostrils. One even had a tentacle coming out an eye socket. And none of them had ordinary eyes. One had a baleful blackened eye in one socket, and nothing in the other, a second looked almost like an insect, with dozens of eyeballs stuffed so tight in each socket that they formed a honeycomb pattern of red pupils, and a third had nothing in the main two sockets, but a third eye socket had grown with an eye that seemed to be crying blood slightly off-center on its forehead.

What Elise had thought was robes actually looked to be the same black webbing stuff that formed around the cocoons during evolution, and beneath that, they were both nude and not. They clearly didn’t have any clothes on, but there were patterns on their skin almost as if they did. Like body paint, except there were odd protuberances for laces and buttons and the like. It was as if they had been forcibly merged with whatever they were wearing when they died.

As soon as they saw Elise and Charlotte, they let out screams that sounded more like pain than rage, and charged. Charlotte got ready to fight, but Elise didn’t even give her the chance. Still connected to the System after using {Inspect}, she hit them with quick {Whisper} and {Power Word Kill}, and they all dropped to the ground dead before taking more than two steps.

“Aww,” said Charlotte, looking disappointed.

Elise looked down at the formerly-undead corpses as her stomach rolled. There was something very, very wrong with them, beyond just their appearance. She cautiously flipped them back over with {Telekinesis}, lining them up next to each other so she could get a better look at them, and the feeling of wrongness only got stronger. Beneath all the Abyssal mutations, they just looked like… people.

The one on the left had been a middle-aged man, a little overweight and with a receding hairline. The one next to him was a woman of the same age who was a little more overweight with gray streaks in her hair. The next two were young men, taller than the others and the patterns on their skin looked like armor, and the final was a teenage girl who, even with tentacles coming out of her mouth, looked like she was in extreme pain.

These had been real people once. They weren’t just random spawns. The patterns that had once been clothes looked to be the same style as Erun’s clothing. They were the people of this fallen nation, and their bodies had been taken and corrupted and turned into this. It was disgusting, and she felt her anger with the System rising again as she gazed upon them.

“Charlotte, no,” she said as the arachne started to creep forward.

“What?” asked Charlotte, looking confused.

“We’re not eating those.”

“Why not?”

“We don’t eat people. Not even dead ones.”

“Why not?”

“We just don’t. But we are going to bury them. No, wait… Charlotte, you’re going to melt them. So that they can’t come back again as something like this.”

Charlotte still looked confused, but she eventually did as Elise asked. They dug a small pit just outside the palace, tossed the corpses in, and a few seconds later, there was nothing left of the corpses but a pile of foul-smelling sludge. Elise disconnected from the System again and used her budding mastery of Earth magic to cover up the pit, and inscribed a quick tombstone to mark their burial place.

Whoever you were, I hope you can rest well now.

After that, Elise was about to just leave the palace when she remembered that all five of the draugr had been acting strange when she found them. Monsters were rarely still like that unless they were sleeping, and from her understanding, the undead didn’t really sleep. So what had they been doing in there?

The answer was something she should have expected, but when she found it out, it only made her feel worse. The portrait in the room was not preserved like Erun’s was, but beneath the dust, and despite how much it had faded, it was obvious that it was of the queen, Ber’Isel. In spite of everything that had happened, the walking corpses had still been drawn to their former queen.

Elise left the palace without a word, and she and Charlotte did a second check of the underground town to see if they could find any more draugr. And they actually did. Four of them, including two children, had been trapped deep in a cellar beneath a collapsed building. Elise put them out of their misery too, and had Charlotte melt the bodies again. There were no more left after those, though.

Elise wondered what had happened to the others. Had they been killed and eaten by the other monsters of the cave? Had they all wandered off somewhere else? The reason the four in the cellar still lived was obvious, but why had the five in the palace been there? Was it because of the painting?

And why weren’t there any in the previous town cave? Or Erun’s cave? Were there some buried in cellars there too that she had missed? Or had Erun done something?

Elise added those questions to the ever-growing list of mysteries surrounding this place. Erun had been able to answer a few of her first questions, but every answer he gave seemed to raise two more questions, especially since he could only ever remember half of what he wanted to say.

The coming of what Elise dubbed night in the caverns came, heralded by the soul storm. Elise and Charlotte weathered it in one of the more intact houses, then Charlotte made a small cave high up on the wall for them to sleep in. She covered the entrance in acidic webbing to prevent any uninvited guests from getting in, and then the two went to sleep.

Elise dreamed of Ber’Isel and Ur again, but this time, it seemed to be from an earlier time. The dreams had never really been linear, but this one sounded like it was the earliest one she had ever had.

“C’mere! Check this out!” It was Ber’Isel’s voice again, but it sounded younger. Not quite child-like, but not the more mature, regal voice she had heard in other dreams.

“What?”

This voice was familiar, but Elise hadn’t heard her name yet. It was another woman, this one with a higher, softer voice. Sometime after whenever this was, she would get in a fight with Ber’Isel over Ur.

“Watch this!”

A moment of silence passed, and then there was a bright flash that startled Elise. That had never happened in one of these dreams before. Sadly, it was not followed up by any other visuals, and soon, the second voice started speaking again.

“Wh– what was that?”

“It’s a secret!”

“You can’t just show me something like that and say it’s a secret!”

“Can too!”

“How did you do that?”

“Not telli– AHahahah! Gotta be faster than that.”

“Get back here!”

The dream faded away, and Elise woke up, feeling confused. That was the first time there had been anything even kind of visual in her dream, and the timing didn’t feel like a coincidence, but the dream was so short she had no idea what it meant. Did Ber’Isel cause that? Was Elise actually seeing these scenes, and just not remembering?

Why can’t anything ever just be simple? she thought with a sigh.

Charlotte was still asleep, and as far as Elise could tell, it hadn’t been that long since they went to bed. She tried going back to sleep, but couldn’t manage, so instead, she sank into her soul, idly working while her mind ran through possibilities about what was going on. Ber’Isel was important to all of this, obviously. She was at the center of every dream, and was a point of obsession not only for Erun, but even for these zombie things.

Elise had refrained from asking Erun about Ber’Isel for fear of setting him off, but now she really regretted it. She should have just asked anyways and dealt with the consequences. Even before seeing the draugr, she knew that Ber’Isel was important, but she had been too scared to actually follow through and ask. She always figured she’d find a better chance to ask later.

But if Erun keeps getting worse, there might not be a later…

She would have to ask him about the others too. He couldn’t remember much about Ur, but perhaps he would remember some of the other voices. She could ask him about Ber’Isel and who she was close to, and maybe get some hints.

She also needed to get to the central cave. She suspected she would find even more answers there. But to do that, she needed to evolve, and to evolve, she needed to strengthen her soul defenses, and to strengthen her soul defenses, she needed time and instruction, and she had no idea how much of each she’d need. She had all the time in the world, but it was still frustrating to be unable to advance properly.

Regardless, whatever happened in the future, and whatever she learned, it wouldn’t change just because she was stressing about it now. The only thing she could do was prepare, so that’s what she did.

By the time Charlotte woke up, she had created two completed spell constructs, bringing her to about three quarters of the way through the third module of Aya’Rhun’s lessons. It would only be another week or two before she finished at this rate, and she was looking forward to being done. She had already spent some time idly studying the System’s spell constructs, and couldn’t wait until she could make things like that. Or just copy what the System did. She would have copied them already if she could figure out which parts where directly System-related, and which were just straight improvements on the base spell. And {Nature Mana Manipulation} was just a whole other beast. It would be a long time before she reached that level of mastery with raw magic.

When Charlotte was awake, the two spent some time eating breakfast, then practicing some partner drills with their soul. They did Aya’Rhun’s aether pulse drills, and then added in Erun’s at the end. Erun’s were very similar to what Elise already did for half her aether-based System Skills. Rather than precise control of the motion of the aether, they focused on imparting emotions and feelings, which was simultaneously simpler and more complex than what Aya’Rhun wanted.

As it turned out, Elise was very good at these. At first, she had chalked it up to just having experience doing it through {Suggest} and {Forced Feeling}, but the more she learned about souls and aether, the more she began to think that she might have just been talented. Charlotte had access to the same System aspects as Elise had, and even had more aether than Elise did when she started out, but she was struggling. Sophie was in a similar boat. The tricks to using {Suggest} that Elise had learned from Emilia and Hans were not things they could do. It was not at all intuitive at all to them like it had been to Elise.

Rayna and Erun had been saying something similar. Elise might still have been a few months from being ready to survive a month of soul storms while unconscious, but her progress for having known about souls and aether for such a short time was astounding. Elise still thought she benefited a lot from having both the System, and the Rune of Fate to boost her up, but when everything around her pointed to her progress being rapid, she had to start wondering if it was true.

And if it is, is it actually talent, or am I just remembering things I used to know…?

After their basic drills, Charlotte went out hunting on her own—with explicit instructions not to eat any draugr she might happen to find—while Elise sat down with Rayna for her daily soul defense lesson. Rayna seemed slightly distracted by something, constantly turning her head upward, focusing on something above her, but the lesson otherwise went smoothly, and when it ended, Elise immediately went to fetch Charlotte, and they continued down the tunnel toward the second shelter city.

On the way, they found, killed, and melted four more groups of Abyssal Draugr. They had been in some of the side rooms, and all of them had armor-like patterns on their skin. The first group was relatively close to the town cave they had come from, but the next three groups didn’t show up until they had almost reached their destination, all coming one after another. Elise was in a grim mood when the tunnel finally widened, opening up to reveal a cavern that equaled Erun’s in size, and it only grew grimmer when she saw what awaited them.

As a whole, it looked a lot like Erun’s cave. It was the same size with similar architecture. There was a castle in the center, and hanging buildings on the ceiling, and a river of boiling water flowing along the far side, but there was one major difference that Elise hated: the monsters. As she and Charlotte flew upward to get a better look at the city, they did not see any hounds or deer or wyrms. Only draugr.
