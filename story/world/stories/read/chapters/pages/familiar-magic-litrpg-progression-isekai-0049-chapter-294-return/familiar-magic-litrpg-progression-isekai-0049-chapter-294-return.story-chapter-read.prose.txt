The dense jungle and Cara’s horde of hunting monsters couldn’t hold Selina’s attention. She felt manic as she paced back and forth, continually tapping at her communication rune, waiting for the news. Any news.

“You’re digging us a trench,” Cara said, pointing at the increasingly disturbed mud beneath Selina’s boots.

“I am being as productive as I’m able,” Selina shot back. “How are you possibly so calm? Do you realize what he’s doing right now?”

Cara shrugged. “He’s fine.”

“He’s going up against a Champion rank monster!”

Cara nodded in agreement. “Ethan-level nonsense. He’ll be fine. I’m not getting fooled again, and neither should you.”

“What do you mean ‘fooled’?” Selina asked.

Cara sighed, leaning on her bow as she watched her trained monsters drag a few Dawn rank demons back to the growing pile. “Ethan is always fine. He’s always doing something objectively insane, but he always comes out the other side. I finally believed he might actually experience some consequences when an entire city collapsed on him, but apparently he used that as an excuse to visit his home.”

Selina huffed. “But this is the most objectively insane thing he’s done. Probably.”

Cara shrugged one more time. “There’s nothing we can do now. Best to focus our efforts on where they can make a difference. I’m trying to feed all of Bastion. What are you doing?”

“Supervising,” Selina said with a roll of her eyes. She had another witty remark ready, but found herself squinting as the dim afternoon sun grew suddenly dimmer.

She and Cara shared a look, then quickly scaled a nearby rise. They’d chosen this spot to wait as it provided a rare view over the canopy of trees. What they saw was enough that Cara forgot to gloat.

Some distance away, impossible to judge thanks to its immensity, was a serpentine demon. It looked something like Deevee, minus the mane, and with far more teeth, but less charm.

It slithered across the open plains beyond the boundary of the forest with surprising grace, its long, sinewy body making surprisingly little noise–just a dull rumble. It was a deep shade of red, like so many of the creatures on Potentia, yet it was covered in golden runes, which glowed with soft light.

Shockingly, its body ended not in a tail, but rather seemed to transition into a multitude of writhing tentacles, which quested out in every direction as the terrible beast moved.

Still, even that disturbing sight couldn’t compare to the sheer size of the demon. It truly was casting a shadow across most of the woods, and its immense head towered over them, mouth agape.

Neither Selina nor Cara seemed capable of speaking, and a primal fear of something so terrifyingly powerful seemed to have paralysed them both. But when the beast’s mouth opened wide, they both took involuntary steps back.

Thankfully, they weren’t immediately devoured by that gaping maw. Instead, a comparatively small, metallic object emerged, falling what must have been several hundred feet to crash into the nearby trees.

Selina’s mind seemed to snap back into focus at the sound, and she realized the object was very similar to the little bunker she’d ridden inside–a piece of Fable. She ran down the slope toward it, Cara following only a moment behind.

They reached what turned out to be a padded metal box just in time to see it open. Apollo stumbled out, but he looked…ethereal. His form flickered with blue and white light, and his face had a look of pained concentration. The state of him was distracting enough that Selina didn’t immediately notice Ethan’s limp form over the man’s shoulder.

“Apollo, what–” Selina didn’t know how to finish that thought. “Did you win?”

“I think so,” Apollo said, and his voice sounded distant, as if he were speaking from the depths of an echoing tunnel. “Ethan is alive, but he’s been overwhelmed by what he went through. He needs time to recover.”

“What about you?” Cara asked, eyeing him suspiciously. She wasn’t used to more than one Ethan.

“Fable Train…used too much power. Needs to recharge,” he said, clearly struggling. He began moving forward, clearly struggling.

“Let us take him,” Selina said, moving to intercept.

Apollo shook his head. “My patient,” he muttered, then kept shuffling forward.

Cara and Selina shared a glance, then as one their eyes turned to the behemoth above them. “What about…that thing?” Selina asked.

“Wounded,” Apollo called back. “It’ll feed while Ethan recovers. Best to let it do what comes naturally. His hold on the thing… Well, let’s just say that Ethan may not have the biggest ego on the planet after all.”

The Throne…

“Stay strong,” Tomo whispered.

Ethan groaned, head in his hands. He’d been through this before, at least that’s what he kept telling himself. He’d been filled with the corruption of Potentia at Dawn rank, and surely he was more capable now.

He’d even been feeling it slowly build up again as they grew the army. He should be used to this by now. Inoculated. But there was so damned much of it…

He could feel it now, as clearly as he felt the distant Throne, calling to him. The taint of Dusk’s power was like a wet filth inside him, like tar running through his veins.

“Well?” Vetta demanded.

They were back in Steelbinder’s lab. Thankfully, they’d been able to extract the Champion as planned. Ethan’s Animus was being devoured as rapidly as it was produced, the Champion–Leviathan, he’d named it–no longer needed pain and injury to be held in his thrall. That meant they could remove Steelbinder safely, and make use of her drones.

“We have a Champion,” Ethan said flatly.

Vetta snorted. “We’ve seen as much,” she said dismissively. “What else?”

“Allyara is dead.” The voice was Steelbinder’s, and it was even flatter than Ethan’s. The two Champions had known one another for centuries, and Ethan couldn’t begin to guess at what she was feeling. The news had been hard enough for him, and he’d barely been acquainted with the woman.

“She sacrificed herself to give us this chance,” he said, forcing himself to his feet. Two days of recovery had done much, but really it was spirit, once again absorbing ambient corruption and purifying it that helped the most.

There was more than enough corruption inside of the Leviathan.

Not that Ethan expected to stockpile power as he had before, it was all being used to hold onto his new “friend”, but it was enough to ease that burden, and stabilize Apollo.

“She was remarkable,” the Holy Chosen said.

It was an understatement. Selfishly, Ethan was glad he hadn’t realized the woman had fallen when it happened, as it might have shaken his concentration. Now though, they were all reeling.

“So we’ve lost, perhaps, the greatest defender Potentia has ever known,” Vetta said, “and gained a demon in her place.”

Ethan didn’t bother to hide his glare. “We gained what we needed. I have what I need to reveal The Throne now–to make use of Dusk’s power.”

Vetta sniffed. No plan involving The Throne made the Nator happy. “Then why are you still here?” she asked.

Ethan rolled his eyes. “The demon is feeding,” he said. “It needs to regain its strength for a few days, then we make our move north.”

Vetta considered him for a moment, then nodded. “The Nator will be ready.”

Ethan nodded back, then turned to one of Steelbinder’s drones. “Have you found them?” he asked.

“I have,” she said, and several monitors lit up. “There have been…disturbing discoveries.”

Selina moved closer to a nearby monitor, eyes widening. “That’s him, isn’t it?” she said. “That’s the Basilisk.”

“Dead,” Steelbinder said. The body was still disappearing into the Astral, but the snake-like form was clear, even from the distant overhead view.

“Maybe the Emperor lost control,” Apollo said. “Basilisk wasn’t native, after all.

Ethan looked at the dead form, eyes narrowed. “Perhaps,” he said at last. “But I don’t see the army anywhere. Steelbinder, any clues?”

The monitors flickered, and the army was revealed. Things had changed, however. Ethan moved to stand next to Apollo and Selina, all sharing a concerned look.

“Battle formations,” Apollo said, intimately familiar with this kind of fighting style.

He was right. Rather than a vague mass of troops as Ethan had last seen it, the Twilights were now clustered together in several distinct groups. They were also clearly disconnected from one another.

“I think the Followers have gone back to being generals,” Ethan said. “This is how they fought on Earth, and it was troublesome, to say the least. Their numbers are still low, but if they pool their strength, they could even threaten our Champion.”

“Then that’s what we’re here for,” Selina said, and Vetta nodded.

“There are over a thousand of us, ready to fight. All Dusk level,” the Nator leader said.

Ethan nodded, but Dusks against Twilights was never simple. And passionate or not, these nator had been hiding in their underground city, not training for war.

Ethan shook his head. “I have to believe that the Emperor is up to something. There must be a reason he’s rushing to The Throne. I think we can still get there in time, but we can’t hold anything back.”

He turned to Steelbinder’s drone. “I want to play my last card. Show me the other target I had you find. I need one last hunt.”
