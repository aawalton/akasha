Standing in the dense forest he hoped was close to their destination, Ethan could still scarcely believe that he’d found the Champion Allyara once again. Or rather, she’d found him. In his mind, she remained a mythical figure. She’d been his first real taste of magic, and his entire encounter with her had lasted only minutes, at most. Yet she’d saved his life then, and had just done so again.

“I’m not sure if I thanked you the first time, but I certainly owe it to you now. So thank you, Allyara. I doubt I’d have made it out of there without you.”

The raven-haired woman nodded, then turned and began moving through the trees with a sure step. From the way she walked, it seemed as if her horrific injuries didn’t bother her, but Ethan couldn’t imagine how that was so. His doctor’s eye couldn’t help but linger on the missing arm and section of her torso, but [Predator’s Gaze] revealed nothing. Evidently, it wasn’t meant for evaluating someone of her level.

“Can I try to heal you?” he asked. “I know you warned me against admitting I was a healer, but we’ve moved a fair bit past that. I have a healing rune that can–”

“No,” Allyara said simply. “Perhaps you’re capable, but the cost would be too great, and would accomplish little. My body may be injured, but it’s nothing compared to what my spirit has endured. My Bonds have been severed more times in the last few years, than the last few centuries before. What I need is rest, and Potentia doesn’t see fit to offer such.”

Ethan and Selina shared a glance, then hurried to catch up to the woman. “How long have you been here?” Ethan asked. “And why are you here? That’s something I could never put together.”

Allyara sighed, the sound rasping slightly, no doubt thanks to her injured lungs. “I had been here for several years before we first met, though at the time, I hadn’t been alone, and my challenges had not been so great. As for the why… It is a long story, and a short one.”

“We have time, I think,” Ethan said. “I doubt the Follower army was in a hurry to follow a Champion.”

“Champion,” Allyara muttered. “These ‘Followers’ have seen what kind of Champion I am. All of my Bonds are recovering, and forcing Silvera to manifest in that state will add months to his recovery. That Army had at least one Master, and they could likely destroy me in my current condition.”

“With respect,” Selina said, “how did you come to be in this condition? I didn’t know it was possible to survive in that state.”

Ethan’s mind was finally beginning to clear, the hours of rest making him feel a bit more like himself, and he managed to connect the dots. “You’ve been hunting Champion rank demons,” he said. “That’s why we haven’t encountered any. Except for–”

“Stay away from the sea,” Allyara said with a nod. “The great beasts of the depths have their own ecosystem, and they tear each other apart far more effectively than I could. Only if they make the mistake of coming on land do I intercede.”

Selina’s eyes were wide. “But why?” she asked. “Not that we aren’t grateful, but why would you put yourself through this?”

Allyara’s gaze fell on Ethan. “Do you know of The Throne?”

He swallowed involuntarily. “More than I’d like,” he said.

She nodded. “The long version… Has Nexum noticed the absence of Champions and Masters yet? I must imagine that it’s become particularly suspicious given recent events.”

“There are rumors,” Selina said. “People seem to believe the Masters are hiding, or perhaps fulfilling some mysterious purpose to which we’re not meant to understand. As for the Champions, there were so many Chosen Champions that it never seemed odd.”

Allyara actually spat on the ground at that. “Chosen,” she said the word like a curse, and Ethan shifted uncomfortably. “Nearly a millennium ago, the Champions of Nexum learned that something was very wrong. We’re more sensitive to magics, the Astral, and the nature of things, and simply achieving our rank made it impossible to miss. Some investigation led to a discovery–the Union.” She looked at Ethan. “Surely you know of the Union by now?”

Ethan briefly wondered how much he should tell this woman, but ultimately decided that she needed the truth–saving his life twice-over had earned her that, and she’d clearly done far more. “I started the Union less than two years ago, against the wishes of the other Chosen.”

That made her pause. Eyebrows raised, she gave him another appraising look, then nodded. “That explains a great deal,” she said at last, then resumed walking. “There was a battle among the Chosen that took place here, around that same time. It seemed to be the beginning of things growing far worse. But I get ahead of myself.”

She didn’t speak for a minute, as if collecting her thoughts. “The Champions, myself included, searched for answers. We didn’t understand the Union, we could only piece it together from records in ancient ruins, and the Chosen certainly weren’t forthcoming. But eventually we pieced together that the gods were somehow absent. So we searched.”

She gestured around them. “We were in no rush to enter Potentia, but after we failed to locate Dawn, and had no more luck with Twilight, we came here. And here we found The Throne–what remains of Dusk.”

“You didn’t…try to take it, did you? Any of the Champions, I mean,” Ethan asked.

She shook her head. “We couldn’t if we wished to,” she said.

Ethan let out a sigh of relief. He’d believed that to be the case, but the thought of facing a corrupted Champion with Dusk’s power had alarmed him. “So what did you do?” he asked.

“One among our number was a scientist–Dalton. The same man who made your system, actually. He managed to discover the danger of The Throne: that it is vacant, and lacks agency. But the power is there, for any who could claim it.” She looked at Ethan meaningfully. “Any demon of sufficient power could do so.”

Ethan nodded gravely. “So you hunted them all down. You’ve been culling their numbers to keep The Throne empty.”

She sighed. “For centuries. It’s been a shared duty among all Champions, and has become something of a right of passage. Anyone who reaches our rank is guided to Potentia, where we’re expected to spend several years keeping the demon population in balance. Only after our time is done are we allowed to move on.”

“Move on to where?” Selina asked, curiosity burning in her eyes.

“You will learn that when your power equals my own,” Allyara said. “As soon as possible, if you please,” she added, and Ethan realized there was genuine desire mixed in with the joke.

“I get the impression it’s not going as well as it once did,” he said. Surely not every Champion looked this bad.

She nodded. “Champions are not so common. After the first few centuries, they’d nearly all moved on. We had to start recruiting the Masters, and raising them up as quickly as we could. That’s why their numbers are so few–any with the potential to be raised higher are encouraged to do so. I’m afraid we’ve culled Nexum down to a world of Twilights, for the most part.”

Pieces were beginning to click into place for Ethan. “That explains a great deal,” he said. “But your duty has nearly ended. The Union has begun, and Dawn has already done her part. Twilight is nearly ready to do his. We just need The Throne.” He stopped, thinking of the army he’d just faced, and the weapon they’d just gained. “We could really use your help, though. The Followers–they’re their own long story–they’re collecting the corruption, same as us. We have to stop them first. Can any of the other Champions still fight?”

Allyara shook her head sadly. “There is only one other. My mentor came with me–we’ve already done our own time on Potentia long ago, but there is no one else. And she is in far worse shape than I am,” the Champion finished.

Ethan swore. “No easy solution then. I’d feared as much, but still hoped. What about the Nator? Do you think they’d help us fight for their world.”

It was Selina who answered. “They’re in no shape for that. Ethan…when you see them you’ll understand. There isn’t much left of them.”

Ethan let his head fall. “I need to get back to Apollo,” he said at last. “He has a few days, but the Followers are on their way. With the Basilisk on their side, they can tear through us. We’ll be nothing but dust if we don’t find some solution.” His voice went lower. “I’d let myself hope there might be a small army of Champions waiting in the wings to finish this for us.”

Allyara barked a laugh. “I’m afraid not, doctor. It was the Chosen who got us all into this mess,” her eyes locked on his, “and it is a Chosen who must get us out of it.”
