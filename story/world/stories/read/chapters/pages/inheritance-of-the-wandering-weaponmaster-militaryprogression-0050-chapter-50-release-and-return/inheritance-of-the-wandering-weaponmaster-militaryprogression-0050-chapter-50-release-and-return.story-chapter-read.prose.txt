“Up and at ‘em!” Sam called down from the trapdoor. He didn’t need to, Ezra had been awake for at least an hour. The anticipation of today being the day that he was free had kept his dreams from growing too strong or his sleep too deep

“Is it time for me to be released?“ Ezra shouted back, standing up from where he’d leaned against the wall through the night. A wide smile cracked his face, and he stretched as he buttoned his coat back over his chest. It wasn’t quite as tight as it had been, and in only this week of imprisonment instead of hard marching and exercising, he’d lost just a bit of weight.

“That’s right,” Sam replied as the trapdoor opened. “Bit of a scary friend you’ve got here, though.”

“Scary?” Ezra asked as he climbed up the ladder. “Maybe Boyd?”

“Nah, she’s a lady, and got a look in her eye that says she’ll fuck me up if I cross her,” Sam shook his head as he patted Ezra’s shoulder. “You missing anything? So far as I know, you didn’t give anything to us when you got here.”

“Nah, I’m good,” Ezra shook his head. “And I don’t think Leah would have given you trouble.”

“Well, I’m glad you think so,” Sam chuckled as he led Ezra down the hall and out of the building.

“I hope you’ll forgive me if I say that I’d prefer it if we never meet like this again,” Ezra said with a light laugh as they got to the front door.

“Me too,” Sam nodded as he unlocked the door. “Wouldn’t mind having a drink with you some time, if you weren’t a blood-hungry soldier.”

“And maybe I’d treat you, if you weren’t a yellow-bellied guard,” Ezra snapped back as he walked out. Sam laughed at the teasing as Ezra went still, seeing who was waiting for him, a thunderous look on her face. Her short blonde hair was greasy and unkempt, something that he hadn’t even seen on the battlefield as she punched her way through a Dragonkin, and the Pseudo-Knight was still wearing her combat armor.

“Here’s your man,” Sam said, the levity draining from him as he met the woman’s green eyes. “Unharmed, but a bit dirty. Nothing a quick bath wouldn’t sort out.”

“Commander Chloe,” Ezra said, stepping forward as Chloe nodded tersely at Sam. “I—What are you doing here? I thought it would be someone from my squad that came to collect me.”

“You do have someone from your squad here for you,” she said in response, her shoulders relaxing somewhat as he appeared to be completely unharmed. “I’m not a Commander to you any more, since the title is mostly to ensure that the leaders of armies subordinate to the Duke’s remember that my command is equal to their own. Instead, you’ll call me Sergeant from now on.”

“Yes, Sergeant Chloe,” Ezra answered. Then, looking behind himself at the guards who didn’t hide their curious looks, he asked, “What are you doing here? You said you’re from my squad. But you’re not one of the members of the army here.”

“That’s right, Ezra,” she nodded. She was obviously waiting for him to put it together, and considering that he’d been waiting and planning for this to happen anyways, it didn’t take him long to better formulate his question.

“Then that means that what you were talking with Captain Riyal before we set out, about me, that’s going to happen? There’s been permission for me to be transferred?”

“Officially, you’ve received clearance from General Dominick and Duke Brayton to be incorporated into the Knight apparent squad in Brayton. The permission hasn’t been officially granted by Count Villy, but that’s just a formality,” Chloe’s face grew more comfortable and less angry as she spoke, and she obviously enjoyed the opportunity to confuse Ezra. “So, let’s go get that clearance and then be on our way.”

She turned on her heel, striding out of the small jail’s yard and into the street without looking back at him. With nothing to do but to follow, the overwhelmed Ezra looked back at Sam, whose face had gone from confused and amused to concerned and maybe a bit afraid. Ezra smiled at the guard and waved as he ran to catch up to Chloe as she refused to slow for him. In fact, Ezra could feel the quivering in the stone underfoot with each step that the Pseudo-Knight took. It wasn’t that she was stomping, but instead a pure manifestation of her aura, and Ezra hoped she wasn’t angry at him.

“What’s wrong?” he asked. “I expected you to come, but not so quickly. After all, Brayton’s what, two hundred kilometers from here?”

“Closer to two hundred and fifty,” she answered. “And what’s wrong is that some backwater Count had the fucking balls to lock up a man that the Duke’s interested in. So, once I heard that you were locked up, I got permission from Dominick to come in here and start kicking some teeth in as I collected you.”

“Wasn’t there—but it’s expensive—I thought I still had time here,” Ezra ended up settling on. “It doesn’t make sense for you to have gotten over here so quickly. How did news get to you so quickly anyways? It’s only been a week.”

“There’s a second circle spell that lets people communicate from basically as far apart as they can get,” Chloe explained. “Georg talked to someone in the chapel who sent a message to the temple in Brayton, and they passed the message along.”

“Isn’t that stupidly expensive?” Ezra asked. “Like, a second circle spell is something only a Deacon can cast, right? So the second tier of magic, like Adept is the second tier of the path to Sainthood?”

Chloe slowed her steps, staring at Ezra. He slowed down as well, meeting her gaze as he asked, “What?”

“How do you know about Sainthood?” she asked.

Ezra’s heartbeat suddenly pounded in his chest, and he swallowed as he replied, “I heard a story about a Sword Saint once. Just… kinda thought of it as the path to Sainthood ever since.”

They both stopped there in the middle of the road, looking at each other, and after a handful of seconds, Chloe accepted his answer and resumed walking.

“Well, yes, that’s how the spell works. You don’t need to worry about the specifics. Anyways, I got the message six days ago. Here I am, I just got in an hour or so ago, and I’m reminded of how much I hate this town.” Chloe’s grumbling grew more heated, though her voice didn’t rise enough to carry much past where Ezra himself walked. Their steel-shod boots clacked on the cobblestone, and it was only a quick walk before they stood in front of the gate outside of the Count’s mansion. Again, two guards stood there, but this time, they didn’t recognize the woman in front of them and when they recognized Ezra as the troublemaker dragged here the week before, they both went on their guard.

“Halt. Declare your reason for business with the Count,” the man on the left demanded. In response, Chloe pulled a letter from a pouch on her hip and brandished it in front of him.

“I am Commander Chloe, Pseudo-Knight of the Duke of Brayton and here on his official business regarding the acquisition of Ezra Soldier to serve the Duke under his Knight apparent squad. Go, inform your lord of our presence quickly.”

The two guards’ faces went through a quick parade of emotions before settling on what was maybe three parts worry and two parts confusion. One of the two ran towards the manor while the other begged for Chloe’s patience. She only barely offered even that, and Ezra counted it lucky that it was only a handful of seconds later that the guard returned with a ruddy face and commands to let them in.

Dozens of questions burned in Ezra’s chest seeing the treatment that Chloe was receiving, but he decided to internalize the instructions that he’d received from Georg the last time he’d been in this manor: “Shut up.”

Two dirty, worn soldiers strode into the pristine halls of Villy’s Count’s home, but instead of the familiarity shown to Georg or the coldness brandished at Ezra, the panic and respect that the Duke’s name, and by extension, Chloe’s presence, demanded was a difference like that between night and day. Instead of being escorted to a sitting room up the stairs and around the corner or glared at by frosty eyes, they were greeted by a butler and three maids, each one offering something new, to take their cloaks (only Chloe had one), to prepare hot warm towels (which Chloe insisted they both needed), to bring them hot chocolate (which Ezra had never heard of but Chloe seemed excited about), and finally to escort them directly to the Count’s office.

“We’ll refresh ourselves in whatever room you usually use to receive messengers from the Duke,” Chloe said, her voice imperious. “You’ll be notified when we’re ready to meet with the Count.”

“Yes, whatever the Pseudo-Knight requires,” the butler replied with a measured bow. “Now, if you’ll just follow me.”

The room they were taken to this time was grander, more opulent and richly decorated than the one the Count had conducted Ezra’s trial in. Every piece of furniture was trimmed in what looked like it may be gold, and the couches here were much finer than those that Ezra’d seen before. At least, they looked a lot more expensive, even if they might not be as comfortable.

“Now, we make him stew in it,” Chloe said with a terribly vicious smile as soon as the door was closed behind them.

“What?” Ezra asked, so taken aback by the swirling motion from one thing to the next that he couldn’t manage to keep his head on straight though it all.

“Well, just think of it this way,” Chloe said, leaning in conspiratorially. “I’m going to get you some revenge on the bastard that threw you in jail.”
