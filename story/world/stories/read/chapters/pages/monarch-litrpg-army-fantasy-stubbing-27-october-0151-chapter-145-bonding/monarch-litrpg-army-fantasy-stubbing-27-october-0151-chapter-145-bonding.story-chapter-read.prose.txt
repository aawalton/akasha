The next three days passed by in a blur.

For the majority of that time, Rayne, Bran, and Jason did little other than train.

The old man had made it very clear from the beginning that none of them were to do anything unnecessary before the tournament. There would be no strolling around, no whorehouses, no side jobs, and no other pointless distractions.

Both Rayne and Jason listened, and neither needed much convincing.

A first-round defeat would be a blow to their pride, and none of them wanted to become the laughingstock of the arena after being looked at as almost heroes in the Pascar Plains campaign.

So they trained.

Morning after morning was spent on the training grounds. Sometimes Bran focused on individual improvement. Other times he forced them through group exercises that tested their coordination and communication.

The old man seemed determined to prepare them for every possible situation.

Some sessions focused entirely on positioning. Others revolved around protecting Bran while he acted as the party's ranged fighter. There were even several exercises where Jason and Rayne were forced to fight while deliberately exhausted, simply because Bran believed there was a good chance one of them would eventually find themselves in that situation during the tournament.

The training was also not just physical, but also tactical.

In the evening, the three of them gathered around a table in the longhouse while Bran discussed strategies.

Most of those discussions revolved around fighting parties led by spellswords.

As a veteran soldier, Bran had encountered enough of them throughout his life to understand both their strengths and weaknesses. While there wasn't much an ordinary person could do against someone capable of launching a blast of mana across a battlefield, there were still ways to defeat them.

After all, spellswords were still mortal.

They bled, and they got tired.

And if given an opportunity, they could be brought down like anyone else.

Naturally, most of those plans revolved around Rayne.

He was the strongest fighter in their party and the only person capable of directly challenging an enemy spellsword. Rayne didn't particularly mind that responsibility.

If there was an opening for him, he had more than enough ways to capitalize on it.

Even if the tournament prohibited killing, there was nothing stopping him from disabling an opponent. Broken bones, deep wounds, and severed limbs were all fair game so long as the victim survived.

And if someone was a spellsword, they were usually wealthy enough to regrow a missing limb afterward.

The strategy discussions also weren't entirely one-sided. Rayne contributed several ideas of his own.

Most of them involved Bran.

Since the old man would be acting as the ranger of their party, Rayne shared a number of concepts he had thought about weeks ago but never found an opportunity to discuss.

To Rayne's surprise, Bran had been genuinely interested in several of them. More importantly, none of those ideas violated the tournament rules.

That quickly became an important topic between the three of them.

The more they learned about the rules, the more possibilities they discovered. Knowing what you couldn't do often revealed what you could.

Several of the ideas that Rayne initially dismissed became far more attractive once they confirmed they weren't forbidden.

By the end of it, Jason was easily the most excited about them.

After all, every time a strategy ended with an opponent being trapped, distracted, or knocked off balance, the large soldier would be the one responsible for charging in and finishing the fight.

By the end of the second day, he was already talking about those plans as though they had succeeded.

Rayne found that pretty amusing, even if he tried his best to cool the man down.

Eventually, the third day arrived. Fortunately, Bran wasn't foolish enough to work them until the tournament itself.

Around midday, he finally called an end to training and ordered both Rayne and Jason to rest.

Their muscles needed time to recover.

The tournament would begin the following morning, and neither of them knew whether they would be fighting on the first day or not, but they needed to be prepared for everything.

Neither Rayne nor Jason argued.

They simply returned to their rooms and spent the remainder of the day recovering, calmly anticipating the next day.

Rayne also took that time to finally do something he had been debating for the past three days.

He bonded the bracelet to himself.

The artifact had remained tucked away amongst his belongings ever since he got it appraised. More than once, he had considered selling it instead. The amount of gold he could get for it would be enough to buy better equipment, potions, and probably several other things that would help him more immediately.

In the end, however, he decided to keep it.

The tournament was approaching, and the bracelet offered something that weapons could not—Insurance.

A hidden advantage.

If things went wrong inside the arena, having an artifact capable of protecting him could easily mean the difference between victory and defeat. More importantly, nobody would expect someone like him to possess such an item.

As a forsaken and a noble bastard, the last thing anyone would expect was for him to have an expensive artifact hidden on his person.

Rayne needed more advantages like that.

The only real problem would come later. If he ended up using the artifact during the tournament, questions would follow shortly afterward.

Captain Edran would certainly ask where it came from, and claiming that he had purchased it in the city would be the worst lie in history.

Though, Rayne wasn't particularly worried. There were plenty of explanations he could use.

More importantly, there was no rule preventing him from keeping something he had found in a dungeon. Most discoveries belonged to the finder unless they chose to sell them, according to the army laws.

It was just that most artifacts found in dungeons are with a squad, and in most cases, the squad leader takes it, and mostly just gives it to his captain in exchange for favours. Rayne's case was simply unique.

If anyone asked, he would simply tell them he had gotten lucky. For once, that answer wouldn't even be entirely false.

The process of binding the bracelet itself turned out to be far simpler than Rayne had expected. Just as Taklu had explained, all he needed to do was place a drop of blood onto its surface.

The moment the blood touched the artifact, the metal emitted a faint blue glow. A shimmering thread of light rose from the bracelet and connected itself to Rayne before disappearing a second later.

The sensation that followed was difficult to describe.

It almost felt as though a tiny thread had been tied between his soul and the artifact. The connection wasn't strong enough to distract him, but it was there, lingering quietly in the back of his mind.

Once the process was complete, Rayne slipped the bracelet onto his left wrist.

Between his armour, shield, and gauntlets, very little of it would actually be visible during combat. It would be slightly uncomfortable at first, but nowhere near enough to become a problem.

The moment he finished putting it on, he immediately began testing it.

Pushing a small amount of mana into the bracelet, Rayne felt the internal structure of the artifact for the first time. It was unlike anything he had experienced before. Deep within the bracelet sat a small reservoir of mana.

As soon as he fed a tiny portion of his own mana into it, the artifact activated.

A shimmering circular shield appeared directly in front of him. The translucent barrier looked remarkably similar to the force shield Varrick used.

And seeing it appear brought an immediate smile to Rayne's face.

The remainder of his day was spent experimenting with the artifact and learning its limitations.

Unfortunately, he couldn't exactly ask his party members to spend the afternoon smashing attacks against it. He wasn't ready to explain the bracelet yet to them. Even so, based on Taklu's confidence when talking about it, Rayne suspected it could withstand at least some mana attacks before failing.

Since durability testing wasn't an option, he focused on everything else.

He tested how long the shield could remain active, whether it moved alongside him, and whether he could alter its shape or position.

Several of those experiments proved surprisingly useful.

The shield remained active for a little over five minutes before vanishing completely. It also followed his movements perfectly, maintaining the same position relative to his body no matter where he walked.

Though, there were limitations.

There was no way to lengthen it, shrink it, or reposition it somewhere else. The shield always appeared directly in front of him and nowhere else.

Even with those restrictions, Rayne considered the artifact incredibly valuable.

Every experiment helped him understand new ways to use it during the tournament. By the end of the afternoon, he already had several ideas regarding positioning and timing that could potentially catch an opponent off guard.

His testing only came to an end when Casper knocked on his door.

By now, practically everyone in the warband knew about the tournament and their participation in it.

Nate had apparently taken it upon himself to spread the news while simultaneously taking bets on how well Rayne, Bran, and Jason would perform. Bran had also secured Captain Edran's permission in the meantime.

The only person Rayne hadn't actually spoken to about it was Casper because she had spent the previous few days inside Astra Academy, apparently trying to learn new spells and talking to her former professors.

The conversation with Casper ended up being fairly short.

The mage had mostly come to see how confident Rayne felt about the tournament. She also mentioned that both she and Captain Edran intended to watch the tournament. Captain Verella was still in the capital and would likely be present as well.

For some people, knowing that two captains and a mage would be watching from the stands might have been enough to create pressure.

Oddly enough, Rayne didn't really feel it. Maybe he was simply used to being watched by now.

Ever since arriving in Valers, it felt like there had always been someone observing him, judging him, or waiting to see what he would do next. Compared to everything else he had dealt with, a few familiar faces in the audience hardly seemed worth worrying about.

Other than that, Casper spent part of the conversation talking about Atticus.

By now, nearly everyone knew the heir of House Arkala would be participating in the tournament as well. His name carried enough weight that rumors followed him wherever he went, and several people already seemed convinced the Crimson Guard would win the tournament.

Casper's advice regarding him was simple.

If Atticus tried to provoke him, Rayne should ignore it. If the man wanted to create drama in any form, Rayne should ignore that too.

Such a thing before the tournament would only harm him and his party.

Rayne agreed with that without much argument.

Afterward, the conversation shifted toward his cultivation and training. Casper asked a few questions regarding his progress, offered some advice, and then eventually left to return to her own room to sleep.

Unfortunately, the discussion reminded him of something he had been successfully avoiding for most of the day.

Tomorrow, there was a very good chance he would see Atticus again.

Knowing nobles, a verbal confrontation felt almost inevitable if their paths crossed. There would probably be insults, threats, or some other attempt to establish superiority.

Rayne just hoped it didn't go beyond that. At this point, however, there wasn't much he could do about it.

He had already agreed to participate in the tournament. Atticus being there as well was simply another twist of fate.

So Rayne focused on the only thing still under his control.

He went down, ate a good dinner, then he went to bed early. And for once, he slept through the entire night without a single nightmare.

By the time morning arrived and he stepped outside, the rest of his party was already ready and preparing to leave.

More surprisingly, much of his warband had gathered as well. There were even other warbands up ahead.

Soldiers filled the street outside the longhouse, talking amongst themselves while preparing to head toward the arena.

Seeing it made him realise once again the scale of the tournament, and whether he liked it or not, the first day of the tournament was upon him.
