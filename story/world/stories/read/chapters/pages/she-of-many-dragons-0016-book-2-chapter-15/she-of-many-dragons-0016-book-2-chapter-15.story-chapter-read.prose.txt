Alice and her dragons arrived through the portal, right after Oliver.

The portal let them out within the lobby of the Protectorate Guild. After the relative silence of the forest, all the sounds, the smells, and especially the artificial light hit her as if she had been kicked by a horse.

She wasn't the only one. Prim gave a hawk-like cry and extended her wings. Natura recoiled, and Iggy surged forward, looking like he was braced for a fight. Bella simply crouched on Alice's shoulder. But she was watchful and aware.

The only one who didn't seem affected was Numi, who looked utterly delighted at all the new treasures to discover. Then again, she had always been a dragon of the city.

The unearthly noise Prim had made had attracted attention. Though dawn was still an hour from fully breaking, the lobby of the Protectorate Guild was abuzz with activity.

Oliver stepped forward, and the portal snapped shut behind him. He held a little gem between two fingers. Once filled with bright light, it was now dark and cold. He had explained that this was a container for transportation magic, and it seemed getting him to Alice and returning had used it all up.

“Are you all right?” Oliver asked, though from his expression, he was really concerned about Alice's dragons. Likely, he wanted to make sure they would not run amok like wild animals.

“Yes.” Alice managed a slightly shaky smile and reached up to pet Prim to settle her wings back again. “It's just we've been out in the wilderness for the last few weeks, and this,” she waved her hand around to indicate the lobby, “can be a little startling.”

“Ohh, I see.” He relaxed at her explanation. Though it was probably more comforting that the dragons had still stuck by Alice's side and not run off to cause chaos. “I wish I could simply lead you up to your room and let you all settle in, but unfortunately...”

“Yes, the grey searchers.”

He gave her a sharp look. “Is that their official name?”

She shook her head. “I don't know what their official name is. It's just what we call them.”

“Well, it fits them better than anything else. Be sure to tell the leadership, they may pick it up. If you could follow me, please,” Oliver hesitated. “Unless you would like to freshen up first? It would have to be quick, though.”

His tone suggested that freshening up would be a good idea. Oliver looked like he had stepped out of a bard's tale with his sparkling armor, perfectly coiffed blonde hair, and overall physique that came with high-leveled individuals. Alice..., well, the last time she had dunked herself in anything like a bath had been while out running the fire. She suspected she had soot on her face, and she certainly had ash on her clothes. Not to mention, her hair was currently a snarled mess.

“I will be quick,” she said.

Up until recently, Alice had spent her life as a General Laborer. That meant she had been mostly overlooked by anyone of importance, but it didn't mean she had no excuse to be neat.

Oliver nodded, and she thought she saw a gleam of approval in his eyes. Plus, a few minutes alone would help her settle the dragons. She worried most for Natura, who would surely be missing the forest.

Oliver led her to an elevator, which was still a minor marvel to her, and she hoped it always would be. From there they went to a room located down a gilded hall. But not just any room—her name was emblazoned on a copper plaque affixed to the door.

“I'll be right outside,” he said.

As a guard? Or as a friend? she thought but did not say.

“I won't take long,” she promised again. Then she shut the door behind herself and looked around.

Her designated room was about the size of the entire General Laborers' barracks. It was airy and luxuriant, with white and gray marble floors and hints of gold accenting the cream-colored walls. The large four-poster bed called to her, especially after a night of riding a dragon across the forest floor, running for her life before that, and spending a good day and a half before that on a muddy scrap of an island.

It was amazing she was still on her feet. Seeing the bed, all she wanted to do was fall onto it.

But she didn't dare. If nothing else, she was aware of how grimy she felt. Her clothes, which she hadn't given much thought to other than the protection they offered, suddenly felt repellent to her.

Heading to the bathroom, she stripped off the now disgusting clothing and left a dirty trail behind her.

There were several runic sigils on the opposite wall of the bathroom that indicated hot and cold water, and she remembered them from her time in the hotel. They activated at a touch, and to her surprise, water blasted out from a tiled portion along the wall, to be whisked away by a neat drain set on an incline in the corner. As she stepped under the water, it turned ashy brown.

Alice was as good as her word and rinsed off quickly, though she dearly wanted to try the variety of soaps lined up nearby. Stepping out again, she kept the water running so her dragons could run in and also rinse themselves of dirt and ash. She hadn't realized how muted their colors had become, and she used every spare towel and a couple of extra blankets for Natura to wipe them off.

There were new clothes, sets of trousers and button up shirts, available in a set of polished wooden drawers. She put them on and found that they were rather generic compared to the elegant clothing she had seen in the lobby, but they fit. Most importantly, they were clean.

Her hair was still a snarl, but she was able to use ties to at least put it up into somewhat of a neat bun. The woman who looked back at her in the mirror had high cheekbones, unfortunately from sparse eating, but there was some wisdom in her eyes. A bit of heartiness, too.

If she squinted and perhaps ignored the dusting of freckles over her nose and cheeks, she could imagine that this woman could be someone of importance—a low-ranked noble lady, perhaps.

What did Oliver see her as?

It was a silly question, and she put it aside. “Well, dears, shall we go?” she asked her dragons.

“You should sleep,” Prim said.

Alice cast another longing look at the bed, but then firmed her will. “Let us check in with the Protectorate leadership first, but whatever they want, I will not allow them to order me far from this bed.”

She turned to Natura, who had opened the curtains of the bay window and stuck her snout out, testing the air. “Will you be okay in the city, dearest?”

To her surprise, her dragon turned back to her with a bit of excitement in her eyes. “Oh yes, there is plenty to see and improve here.”

Alice’s eyebrows rose. She had expected her nature dragon to be sulky, bewildered by the city, or both. Instead, she seemed to be utterly delighted.

“Improve?” Alice asked.

“There is an old window box outside, I believe for flowers. I will enrich the soil and see if I can grow climbing vines all up and down the outer wall. Then, there will be flowers everywhere.”

That did sound nice. “See if you can do that, but first, we must see to the leadership.”

“Work first, then play,” Prim summed up.

The rest of her dragons agreed, or perhaps they were mostly as tired as Alice and simply wanted to get this over with to return to bed. Either way, they didn't argue as they trooped out again to the hallway.

“That was fast,” Oliver said.

She gave him a strange look. “I told you that I wouldn't take long.”

“I suppose I'm just used to spoiled nobles,” he said, then extended his arm to her as an escort. “But yes, let us carry on.”

Another elevator ride, and Alice was thankful that the elevators here were large enough to accommodate Natura. They made it to the top floor that led to a wide office.

She vaguely remembered visiting this place right before she had officially signed the contract to be added to the Protectorate Guild.

The office Oliver led her to was dark and severe, with only a few books on the shelves and a desk that seemed too clean to be used as a workspace. The man who sat behind it was also severe-looking, neat as a pin, with perfectly combed dark hair and a pencil-thin outline of a beard.

Oliver walked before Alice and bowed to the man. “Sir Davis, this is Alice Turner.”

The man, Sir Davis, looked at Alice from behind thin-rimmed glasses. “Well, come in then. I suppose those are your dragons?”

“They are,” she confirmed.

“I assume they are completely under your control?” The tone of his voice suggested that they had better be.

“Completely,” Alice said and gave Numi a look to remind her not to snoop around the man's office. Her little green dragon looked like she dearly wanted to see what he was hiding behind some of those books.

“Tiny slip of a thing, aren't you?” Davis said. “And you are a She of Many Dragons.” He shook his head. “I have heard of stranger classes, but not many. Well?” He practically barked the word. “Oliver had to have appraised you over the situation, yes? What do you have to say about it?”

She didn't let herself be startled by the sudden change in his voice. Noblemen often did that, thinking they had to wake their servants up. “I already knew about them. Those dragons were actually patrolling the same area I was in.”

His eyebrows rose. “Weren't you out in the wilderness on some sort of sabbatical? Stupidest thing I ever heard, for a new inductee to be allowed to go off and get in trouble all on their own...”

“It was approved,” Oliver said quickly. “Alice was originally brought in to consult on dragon-related issues.”

David scowled. “We have dragon-related issues right now!” He turned a glower to Alice. “So, do you know why we suddenly have dragons all throughout the kingdom at different points? What do they want? And why don't they have the sense to take a rest before they fall dead from the skies?”

“I don't know,” Alice said. “As I told Oliver, I am only a low level in my class and I cannot reach them.”

“And what level is that?”

“Six.”

That seemed to incense him even more, and he half stood from his chair. “You were out there alone in the wilderness at only a level six?” He seemed completely scandalized. Alice was surprised that he cared.

“I wasn't alone,” she said.

Iggy, who was not swimming in the air for once and had instead elected to stay by her side, gave a little growl of agreement.

That seemed to give Davis a pause. He settled back in his chair, and once again, his eyes roamed over Alice's dragons. “Well, I suppose you are your own little army, aren't you?”

“Actually,” Numi said, before Alice could answer, “I was wondering if I could see a map of where the gray searching dragons currently are? That may help.”

The man raised his eyebrows in surprise, and Alice wasn't sure if he hadn't expected her dragons to be able to talk or, if they could, to speak so eloquently.

“Very well.” He flicked his wrist, and an illusionary map made of light appeared before Alice. She had not had much opportunity to study maps before, but she was at least able to recognize the shape of the kingdom and several clusters of gray triangles at different points. She assumed those were the locations of the gray searchers.

“You said there were more of those creatures at your location?” Davis demanded. “Where did Oliver find you?”

Oliver answered for her. “In the Wildlands, about fifty miles to the east of Cinderbrook Village.”

“It was an area that was once inhabited,” Alice said. “We saw traces of farms and... other structures.” A whole city of people and dragons, but she certainly did not want to point that out to this man. “I wonder...” A new thought struck her, and she glanced at Numi, who seemed to be thinking along the same lines because she nodded.

Alice turned to Davis. “Forgive me, I don't have much of an education, but the cities now aren't always where they had been in the past, is that right?”

“You are correct. Kingdoms rise and fall, though some would say that it's treachery for me to admit it,” Davis said dryly.

“I'm guessing, but the remains of buildings that we saw here and there seemed to be at least several hundred years old.” She glanced at the map with the gray triangles. Several of them were placed in positions that were not near a current city. She gestured to them. “Are those areas also places that had been once occupied, but are now abandoned?”

She knew she was on the right track when Oliver gave a start. “She's right. My history tutor once told me there used to be a city right here by these waterfalls during the Second Age.” He pointed to the cluster of dragons in the middle of the map.

“History was never my best subject,” Davis said. “But I believe...” He trailed off and stepped around his desk to get a closer look at the map. “Yes, here too,” he jabbed at the cluster of northern dragons. “This area used to be an old city called Destra—something. I cannot quite remember, but a war came and drove out the inhabitants. We don't know why it wasn't resettled.”

“Our kingdom doesn't lack for arable land,” Oliver said. “That has been the secret to our success. Maybe that's why no one has gone back.”

That answer felt incomplete to Alice, but she kept silent and let the higher rankers talk.

Her reprieve didn’t last for long. Davis picked at his lower lip and glanced to Alice. “What exactly did you find out there? Other than old buildings?”

“Many graves,” she said, “and old, old skeletons. It would make sense that there had been a war or a conflict, but I’m afraid centuries of overgrowth have covered up the truth.”

He snorted and looked vaguely disgusted. “Why then would dragons be hovering all over the old remains of cities?”

Because the person who sent them is working off old maps? Alice thought. “Perhaps they're looking for something that they think was left behind? Or following old rumors?”

“A treasure hunt,” he said in disgust. “That would make more sense than deliberately inspiring panic in a population, considering that some of these are not in populated areas. But what exactly are they looking for?”

Alice couldn't tell him because the truth was that the person who sent the dragons might be working with old information. So she treated him exactly like she would a noble who was expecting an answer that he would not like to hear. She met his gaze, polite but slightly bland. “I don't know. I tried to speak to one who fell to the ground, and it only said that it was searching, but I still don't know for what.”

“And you can't compel him to speak further?”

“He's not my dragon,” she said.

That might have been a mistake. His expression sharpened. “Is it somebody else's dragon?”

She hesitated. “That thought did occur to me, but I have no way to verify that because—”

“Because you're too low of a level. Yes, I understand that.” He waved a hand dismissively and returned to studying the map.

Oliver, however, was giving Alice a searching look. “The fact you got it to reply at all is useful. We've tried, of course, and they've been nothing but vicious right before they died. But your class might make the difference. Maybe they would talk to you if you tried again.”

“Alice cannot go out,” Prim said. She used one of illusionary skills to be heard by all. “She must sleep.”

Davis turned back to her, an eyebrow raised. “Does your dragon speak for you?” Then, abruptly, he asked instead, “Or are they speaking through you?”

“They are my aspects,” she replied easily. “And Prim has always been protective.”

“Alice has not slept for over twenty-four hours,” Prim said, to Alice's embarrassment. “You didn't notice because she is strong, but she is dead on her feet.”

“Hmm. You are looking a little too awake for someone who would have just woken at dawn. We do have chemical agents to refresh you, but... no, I think the real imperative is to get your class up to snuff.” Davis shook his head and muttered to himself. “Not even a level ten.”

“Sir, what about the dragons?” Oliver asked. “If she could get answers from them...”

“By all means, give it a try, but I don't want her in any danger, and I do want her above level ten as soon as possible. The last of them seem to be dropping from the skies now and dying, but what if it doesn't stop here? No, we're going to need her out in the field, which means that she needs to be at an acceptable level. Have her try her luck with one of those beasts but when she gets back she is to have full access to the training grounds, the standard. Whatever she needs.”

“Yes, sir,” Oliver said.

Davis grunted something and returned to the map, his hands clasped behind his back. Clearly, he had given them a dismissal.

Oliver led her out. “He's intense,” he said when they were in the hallway. “But he actually does care.”

Alice wasn't sure about that. Nobles seemed to “care” as long as the abilities a lower ranker had aligned with what they wanted.

She wasn't foolish enough to speak that out loud, though.

Oliver led her back to her assigned room, and she found that Prim was right once again. Her footsteps were dragging, and so were her dragons, though Iggy and Bella put up a front to look alert.

“I'll wake you when it's time to go try to speak to the dragon,” Oliver said.

Alice nodded. She had to plan for that, for what to ask so that the answer did not lead her into more trouble, but her brain was full of fog. She didn't even remember closing the door or trudging to the bed. She just fell on top of the mattress, and her dragons curled around her as best they could.

The last thing she remembered was the sheets smelling freshly washed, like soap. Then she was asleep.
