When Elise and Charlotte returned to the main cavern, they were greeted by an unusual amount of noise. Normally, it was eerily silent. The draugr didn’t make noise unless they were attacking, and they didn’t attack each other, and there were no other monsters. But apparently that had changed.

Mixed in with the distant screaming of the draugr was also the howling that Elise recognized as originating from the Abyssal Hounds. Flying up and looking in the direction of the commotion confirmed as much. A pack of the Hounds had spawned, and was locked in battle with a horde of draugr. They were losing, of course, but they had done some damage, and the fact that they existed at all was interesting.

Elise had learned about spawn zone limits both from Oberon and in her classes at the University. There were two, technically: the level limit, and the saturation limit. The cavern had been saturated before, with no room for new spawns, but with Elise and Charlotte killing draugr by the dozen every day, in addition to three elite draugr, that must have no longer been the case.

That must be how the draugr in Erun’s cave died out, she realized.

Erun didn’t need to have done anything grand. If he just killed a few of the draugr, the natural spawns would have gradually taken care of the rest over the thousands of years of isolation. And the fact that whoever was supposed to be in the castle hadn’t even bothered to do that here lent more credence to the theory that they had died.

After confirming the distant sounds, Elise and Charlotte returned to the ground, and resumed their trek to the castle. Charlotte was mostly recovered from the previous day. A good night's sleep was enough to get her most of the way, and while the last bits would take a little longer, as long as she didn’t take any more soul attacks, she would be fine.

Elise, on the other hand, was only getting worse. Her soul felt very unstable, and she was scared to even kill one more draugr. She couldn’t connect to the System anymore, which wasn’t the biggest loss, but she felt slightly more vulnerable than usual without access to her stealth. Luckily, with Charlotte as her bodyguard, and the fresh spawns distracting many of the draugr elsewhere, she didn’t have too much to worry about.

The path definitely wasn’t clear, though. The draugr they hadn’t killed the previous day did spread out, but there were still a lot on this side of the cave. That worked out, though, since Elise had something she wanted to test.

She never really figured out why her presence Skill kept working, even while disconnected from the System. It didn’t make much sense. But it had clearly happened, even going so far as to command the Singer, so there was something strange going on. She wondered if maybe the “filter” around her soul had somehow stuck around from her earlier use of the Skill. She couldn’t sense any such thing, but the System worked in mysterious ways.

When they reached their first group of draugr, the first thing Elise did was release her presence. Without the System, she couldn’t do anything more than that. Filtering and adding something like {Royal Presence} was beyond her, at least for the time being. And yet, when her aether spread out, washing over the draugr, their aggression faded, and they stopped moving, staring at her just like before.

She checked in every way she knew how for any kind of modification to her presence, but there was nothing. All she was doing was releasing her aether, and for whatever reason, upon sensing that, the draugr became obedient to her.

She had a few theories about what was happening. The first was that it was just a natural part of her Species. Royalty was literally in her blood and soul, so even without the Skill to filter her aether, it still had a similar, perhaps diluted, effect. That was the simplest answer, and it made perfect sense… except if that was the case, why were the elite draugr so obsessed with her?

Not all of them, she reminded herself. Just the Knight and the Revolutionary.

During the battle, she had thought that the Singer was after her as well, since it ignored Charlotte, but it was entirely possible that it was just attacking Elise because Elise had attacked it. She had seen similar behavior in other monsters before.

And now that she thought about it more, the Revolutionary also made sense. Of course the Revolutionary would be against the royal. Only that didn’t account for the fact that Elise had recognized the man, and that she felt she disliked him, nor did it account for the knight, who, in theory, should have been easier to sway.

It has to be related to my past life. Like everything is…

She was getting tired of every question always having the same answer. Every mystery in the cavern—or even just in her life—seemed to be related to the one thing she couldn’t do anything about. Even if she learned more about the past here, what was she supposed to do about the missing memories? If they hadn’t come back with the Rune of Fate’s evolution, would they ever come back at all?

It didn’t matter, though. Not at the moment. Whoever she was, and whatever she had forgotten, she had other things to worry about at the moment.

“Leave,” she commanded the draugr.

All at once, they turned around and started walking away. Charlotte looked disappointed at the lack of action, but didn’t say anything. Elise had briefed her on the situation before they left, and Charlotte understood that they didn’t really have time to get distracted. This discovery was actually a good thing, because it would rapidly accelerate their progress, so long as they didn’t run into any other elites.

The rest of the journey to the castle progressed smoothly. They only even sensed an elite once, and were able to go around it without a problem. All the other draugr were dealt with easily with just Elise’s presence.

The castle itself was extremely similar to Erun’s. The layout was different, but the style was the same, and many of the parts—like the pointed tower roofs and carved stone decorations on the sides—were identical.

There were over a hundred draugr inside, and Elise made all of them leave while Charlotte webbed up the entrances. A dozen or so were standing in a room on the top floor facing a faded portrait of Ber’Isel. Elise joined them in staring at the poster, hoping for some glimpse of a memory, but came up empty.

Like in Erun’s castle, the bed in the bedroom had been replaced by a sarcophagus. Elise and Charlotte approached with caution. If there was anything living or undead inside it, they wouldn’t know until they looked inside, and they didn’t want to be caught off-guard.

Their caution turned out to be half-warranted. The sarcophagus was occupied. Sort of. There was a woman’s body in it, at least. She was nothing but a desiccated husk, at this point, her skin taught across her bones, but she was still intact.

The woman was wearing a red dress, and had her hair done up in a complicated braided do. Elise thought she must have been fairly beautiful when alive, but now she looked more like a mummy than anything else. Still, there was a faint glimmer of recognition when she looked inside, despite the distortion.

“Hello?” she called tentatively.

From outside the sarcophagus, there was no way to tell if the woman was fully dead, or just undead. And if she was still lucid in any way, Elise didn’t want to just attack first and ask questions later. She sincerely hoped the woman wasn’t, because that meant Elise would be able to use the sarcophagus to evolve, but she wasn’t about to become a murderer over that.

The woman did not respond. Elise called out a few more times, louder each time, and Charlotte joined her, but still, there was no response. The next thing they tried was poking the woman with a stick. The “stick” was actually just condensed, hardened webbing from Charlotte, but it did the job. Charlotte was also the one to do the poking, since she was the most physically durable, in case something went wrong.

And something immediately did go wrong. The second Charlotte’s stick touched the mummified woman, her eyes opened. Except she didn’t have eyes, so it was just empty sockets. Once awake, the woman started screaming. It was extremely loud, making both Elise and Charlotte cover their ears, but it didn’t sound like much else. It didn’t sound pained, or terrified, or anything like that. The mummy woman was just screaming at the top of her lungs.

Elise and Charlotte both backed away, watching as the woman sat up, and turned to face them. The screaming never stopped, even though she should have run out of air, and from outside the castle, Elise heard the rumbling sound of hundreds of footsteps. Footsteps she didn’t even have the mental bandwidth to worry about, because the mummy woman was getting out of the sarcophagus, and now that Elise could sense her soul, she thought they might have been screwed.

The woman’s soul was every bit as powerful as Erun’s, but unlike him, she wasn’t restraining it. Or perhaps she couldn’t restrain it. Aether flooded outward in waves, crashing against Elise’s soul defenses. Fortunately, Elise and Charlotte had both been prepared for this, and managed to fend it off, or else they both would have been knocked unconscious at the very least.

Elise readied an aether spike as the woman stepped out of the sarcophagus, firing it at full power as soon as she was able. Immediately, the woman collapsed, falling to one knee. The pitch of her scream changed, turning pained, and the aether roiling off her soul became even more violent. The woman tried directing some kind of aether attack at them, but it fell apart before it could even form. In fact, her whole soul seemed to be falling apart right before their eyes.

She got back to her feet, but only barely, and immediately staggered to the side. Elise readied a second aether spike, but decided not to fire it, and held out a hand to Charlotte, who was preparing to attack as well. The two stood still as the woman lurched around the room, the state of her soul worsening with each passing second.

Finally, after a half minute, the woman’s aether started running low, and her soul seemed to be on the verge of disappearing entirely, but in her final moments, she seemed to have a bout of clarity. Her soul stabilized slightly, the screaming stopped, and she stood up to her full height, turning to face Elise. Bloody tears fell from her empty eye sockets, drawing lines down her face and dripping onto her once-white dress.

“Please–” she said.

The woman took a step toward Elise, reaching out a hand, but her knee buckled, and she fell to the ground.

“Please,” she said one more time.

Then, the flow of aether stopped, and her soul faded, and an enormous amount of fog surged into Elise’s soul.

She wasn’t attacking us.

Elise was frozen in horror as she realized what she had just done. The woman wasn’t a mindless undead. She was like Erun. Her state was much worse than his, but she was still a lich like him, and not a mindless draugr. She had survived over ten thousand years, protected by the coffin. And Elise and Charlotte had killed her.

“Woah, I got four levels!” said Charlotte excitedly.

Elise didn’t bother to respond. She just looked down at the corpse.

“Elise?” asked Charlotte.

“It– it’s nothing,” said Elise, turning away. “Can you– can you take care of the body? I’m gonna go send all the other draugr away.”

“Sure!” said Charlotte.

I’m so sorry, thought Elise as she walked out.

She felt horrible. She had been responsible for the deaths of Emilia and the pain vampire, Daisy, but she didn’t feel guilty about either of those. Both women had deserved it, and Elise didn’t have a choice. But this time, it was different. The lich was innocent, as far as she was aware, and Elise had directly caused her death.

But could that even be counted as life?

Elise pondered that question as she drove the draugr who had been drawn by the woman’s screams away, simultaneously gathering the soul fog and using it to refill the tub in her soul house. The guilt remained, but in just a few minutes, it drastically lessened, to the point where it worried her.

Logically, it made sense. She felt she was being attacked, and acted in self-defense. The woman was not behaving rationally at all, and she had no reason to believe she was still “alive” in any way. Even if they hadn’t done that, the woman would have been doomed to the same fate eventually. Her soul was damaged to the point that even without Elise’s attack, she would have died if she left the coffin, and if all she could do was scream…

It doesn’t matter anymore, she tried to tell herself. What’s done is done.

Those words didn’t help much. She felt less guilt about killing the woman now, but there was additional guilt over how little guilt she felt, which was confusing and distracting. Even when she tried to focus on the fact that she needed to evolve now, her mind kept getting drawn back to the woman. Even after Charlotte disposed of the corpse, the sight of her eyeless, emaciated face with bloody tears running down the cheeks was burnt into her mind.

Over the next few hours, Elise worked with Charlotte to secure the castle as best as they could. Every window was covered with multiple layers of acidic webs, and the hallways were filled with them too. Elise was worried about more monsters spawning inside the castle, now that the woman in the sarcophagus was dead, so they made sure that the bedroom was especially secure, with webs all around the room, and the hallways leading to it completely impassable.

When it came time for the soul storm, Elise got into the coffin to verify her theory, and found it correct. The moment she got inside, all sense of the outside world faded, and while Charlotte had to defend herself from the attacks, everything around Elise was perfectly tranquil. And with that confirmed, Elise only had one important decision left to make: System or not?

She had been going back and forth about this for a while now. On one hand, the System had co-opted the natural order, and she wanted to go back to how things were supposed to be, now that she had the option. On the other, the System had also improved on some things. The Skills may have been stolen constructs, but the Skill constructs were also much better than anything she could make currently. And while it weakened her soul, the stability the System framework provided was also quite impressive.

Plus, there was the fact that she would know what to expect. She felt that she could initiate the Evolution without the System… but then what? What would happen? How would she evolve? What kind of abilities would she gain, if any? Could she possibly lose things? In the past, she had the option to switch paths from fey to demon, which would have gotten rid of her fey Skills. What if the same thing happened here when she let the evolution progress naturally? While the System didn’t provide many details, she could at least be sure to avoid something like that.

She dreamwalked to Oberon, asking him what he would do, and his answer was unhelpful. He said if he had the choice to be free of the System, he’d do it in a heartbeat and never look back. And while Elise was partially inclined to agree, she still thought that it could be useful in some ways.

She talked it over with both Charlotte and Sophie, but neither of them could give her an answer either. After twelve hours of mulling it over, cooped up in the castle as her soul continued to destabilize, she even went and talked to Rayna, but that didn’t help much either.

“The System is the safest, but without the System is the most interesting,” she had said. “Maybe you’ll become a vampire!”

“Yeah, maybe…”

She really wished she had a way to contact the sealed heroes, but all of them were still completely cut off. The same went for any of the people who might have been able to get in contact with them, like Attalaya and Jessie. Maybe Irylax would have been able to help, but–

Wait, Irylax ascended, right? Does that mean I can pray to her?

…

Irylax, if you’re listening, I need help. I’m about to evolve, but–

She paused as she felt an odd tug on her soul. Confused, she went to investigate. The tug came again, and this time, Elise saw which aetheric thread it came from. It was one she had not thought about in a long time, and as soon as she realized it, she felt stupid. The tug came a third time, and this time, Elise tugged back. The next second, she felt herself being pulled away, and soon found herself standing in a beautiful garden, facing a fey woman with branching, flower-tipped silver horns and eyes of molten gold.

“I’m hurt,” said Titania with a pout. “Did you really forget about me?”
