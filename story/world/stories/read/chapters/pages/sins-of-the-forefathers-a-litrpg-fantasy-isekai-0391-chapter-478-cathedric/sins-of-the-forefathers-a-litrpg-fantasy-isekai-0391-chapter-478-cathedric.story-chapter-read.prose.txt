God fucking damnit.

Things could just never go easily for once, could they? Our destination was undoubtedly the central command tower of this forsaken city, and yet there didn’t appear to be an easy way to reach it. There was no chance in Hell we were going to be swimming in the presumably cursed lake of fucking Calamity blood. Not only did that seem like an elaborate form of suicide, but more than half of us were clad in heavy metal armor. We’d sink like stones in that lake, no matter if it was thicker than true water.

No, we were going to have to search for other ways to get to the tower, and a quick look around told me our only options. There was a large, stone-paved boulevard around the shores of the lake, big enough for four soldiers to walk side by side. The path looked to loop all around the circumference of the lake, meeting on the far side of it in a perfect circle.

The only way was forward, because we were not leaving here without accomplishing our directives. Too much had been lost already for me to even consider the possibility.

There was a problem, though. It’s not like the Skarned had stopped pouring out of the rest of the city to try and annihilate us. Even now, it was only the back lines of our defense, spearheaded by Rafael and his horde, that were protecting our rear. The entrance into the lake and its grisly, needle-topped spires was a natural choke point, and I think in ideal conditions, Rafael might have been able to hold off the Skarned all on his own. Hell, it was possible the Lich could have taken the city on his own, holding them off on the stairs that led up to the lake. His ever-propagating control of the dead was an unimaginably powerful weapon in the right hands, and his bony fingers were definitely the right ones.

That was in an ideal situation, though, without the presence of the troublesome Karvokar.

I shook my head wildly in frustrated realization, there in the middle of the very quick, very impromptu leaders' council we had established to decide on our next steps. I had to shout to be heard over the deafening screams of the ocean of Skarned flailing for our flesh. “SOMEONE NEEDS TO STAY BEHIND! WE NEED A SQUAD TO HOLD OUR BACK LINES AND SUPPORT RAFAEL! HE CAN’T DIRECT THE HORDE AND MANAGE THE KARVOKAR BY HIMSELF!”

Grim looks of frustrated agreement were exchanged at my words. My Captains and commanders knew I was correct. We could not afford to be overrun here. The only reason we had gotten this far was because of Rafael, and as much as it pained me to admit, the Lich wasn’t omnipotent. It was entirely possible that he could be overrun and dispersed, fleeing back to the safety of his phylactery and leaving us high and dry.

We needed a volunteer to stay behind and hold this position, and we needed one now. I saw Bleddyn’s face firm from across the circle, and the werewolf and leader of the Unchained opened his mouth…

Only to be cut off by another person stepping forward to stand before me.

Like everyone else here, Gustave was covered from head to toe in the blood and viscera of countless slain Skarned. The fighting through these accursed streets had been hard, fast, and relentless. Such a battle left absolutely no time to spare for things such as making sure you cleaned the gore off your face. I could barely see the Herztalian officer under the blood that had slicked back his hair.

But I could certainly see the fiercely determined look on his face, one such as I’d never seen before on the man.

“WE WILL STAY!” Gustave bellowed to be heard. “I AND MY SQUAD WILL STAY AND HOLD THE LINE!”

My eyes cut over his shoulder and outside the circle, where what remained of his men could be found. Like all of us, he had lost several men during the bloody march through the streets of Karag Korvok. But these were also all that remained of the Herztalian soldiers who had originally come with the expedition, all the way from Blustein. They had been whittled down to under fifty men, after losing nearly everyone to the fires of Rhoscara.

These were the last true common men of Herztal I had in my retinue. They were, tactically, perhaps the best people I had to hold an entrenched position and support Rafael. I think Gustave might have recognized that. I also think the man was still trying to prove himself to me after supporting Marcel’s ill-fated betrayal.

I’m not sure I had ever seen more stoic defiance on the faces of those men, as I looked outside of the circle.

There was no time for me to dispute his decision. Every moment I wasted in indecision was another moment that the Korvoks could be torturing innocents. Every moment waffling was one in which another one of my soldiers could die to a lucky strike from a monstrously powerful Karvokar.

I reached forward and dragged a shocked Gustave closer, clutching the back of his neck. My forehead thudded into his as I glared at him from a distance of only inches. “I FORBID YOU FROM DYING!” I shouted right into his face. “DO YOU HEAR ME, CAPTAIN! YOU WILL SURVIVE THIS! THAT’S AN ORDER! AM I CLEAR?!”

A slight sheen appeared on the young officer’s eyes as he understood my true message. He blinked rapidly, and it was gone. “YES, SIR! NOW GO, WE’VE GOT THIS! DON’T WE, BOYS?!”

At that, his men let out ragged, bloodthirsty cheers and broke from their observance of the circle. I let my grip on Gustave fall away, and the man actually saluted me, crashing his armored heels together as he did.

And then he was gone, charging away from our group with his men hot on his heels. They sprinted over to the backlines, where Rafael floated over the chaos, directing his every fluctuating horde calmly. I saw him tilt his head as Gustave and his men passed him by, and engaged one of the Karvokar that had been harrying his undead. His head turned enough to consider me thoughtfully for a moment before he nodded in both acknowledgement and understanding.

I returned solemnly, before turning back to my people and thrusting a finger down the leftward path of the lake. “EXPEDITION, MOVE OUT! WE MAKE FOR THE NEAREST TOWER!” I roared as I let a savage snarl cross my lips. “WE’LL CLEAR EVERY ONE OF THEM IF WE HAVE TO!”

My warriors loosed an answering cry, and we moved out as closely packed as we could. I didn’t look back as we left Rafael, Gustave, and all of their men behind.

I trusted them.

…………………………………..

Something I couldn’t help but notice, as we bearded the shores of this artificial, blood-borne lake, was that it was actually bubbling. Not very enthusiastically, I noticed, as only a single large one floated up from the depths of the lake to pop and shower the closest members of my soldiers in yet more blood.

Myself included.

But it was strange, because there wasn’t any heat emanating from the lake. If anything, it was colder around this lake than it had been within the Hold City proper. I wished I knew what that meant, but I was busy.

We’d made good time crossing the distance to the first of the towers. Enough that my observations about the lake were the only ones I could make before we arrived.

And when we did, we found the door lay open for us.

The entrance into the tower was not only completely unguarded, but the massive stone double doors were open in a disturbingly welcome manner. Two torches burned on either side of the entrance, and we could easily see into the insides of the edifice. Unlike the outside walls, the inside was not covered in pulsating, flayed skin.

Instead, it was unsettlingly familiar Dwarven architecture, if not of a kind that was bare.

Standing outside of that building…I couldn’t stop a renewed sense of unease from rolling over me.

This smelled like a trap. Everything about this was suspicious. If I didn’t know any better, this felt like an invitation. It didn’t matter, though.

We needed to clear these towers, and I don’t think it was big enough in there for hundreds of warriors at once. I was reluctant to do this, but…I didn’t have a choice. There was no point to all of us lingering outside of these monstrous towers, twiddling our thumbs. I was going to have to trust my people to handle the others without me.

I turned from the open doors and turned to face everyone else. “We’re splitting up. Sylvia, you and your platoon stick with me. Bleddyn, you take command of everyone else. Two platoons to a battle-group, and a battle-group to clear a tower.”

“Three towers for each battle-group, then,” Olag murmured grimly, before his face hardened.

Bleddyn nodded. “We’re off. Watch yourself in there, Nate.”

I returned the nod before flickering my eyes over to meet the sapphire blues of my lover. “Sylvia, you and your men are with me. We’re going in.”

The Sculpted woman tore her disdainful gaze away from the pulsating skin upon the surface of the tower and regarded me thoughtfully for a moment.

If we didn’t trust each other so fully, I would have expected her to accuse me of trying to shield her. As if I had no confidence in her strength and skill.

As it was, though, she merely nodded in acknowledgement and fell in at my side.

Bleddyn and the rest rapidly departed from the tower, while Sylvia, our soldiers, and I…

Stepped foot into this one.

……………………………

Once inside the tower, I had to rapidly reassess just what I understood about the Korvoks. The Varlayans had told us that they were in essence religious zealots, obsessed with something they called the ‘Red Truth’. Only, it was one thing to be told the Korvoks were committing their atrocities under the guise of doctrinal fervor.

And another thing altogether to be presented with tangible evidence. Only, in standard Korvok form, it was deeply disturbing evidence.

If I didn’t know any better, this was a church.

The inside of the tower didn’t seem to have any floors above this one, at least not that I could tell. It was, in essence, a single massive room, easily as large as a sporting field from back on Terra. Rows upon rows upon rows of simple pew benches lined either side of the room the cavernous room in two columns that dominated each half. The hall was supported by columns up and down the center aisle, and the walls of the hall itself were…

I grimaced as we cautiously advanced down the aisle.

They were decorated in what someone unfamiliar with the Korvoks would simply think of as leather artwork. Supple leather carved into intricate geometric forms meant to draw the eye.

But I was familiar now, and it wasn’t hard to tell what kind of leather it was.

I looked away and focused on the more immediate problem. This place…

It wasn’t empty. The pews weren’t empty.

While I wouldn’t call them filled to the brim, at least half of them were occupied by Skarned.

Skarned that were ignoring us. They sat with mouths hanging slightly open, staring off into the distant end of the hall with glassy eyes. Not one of these Dwarves had even twitched their head in our direction ever since we’d set foot inside, much less run screaming towards us with murder aforethought.

And I had a good idea why.

While the hall wasn’t exactly well-lit, there were certainly enough torches inside to illuminate it. But the very end of the hall was covered in lit candles. Tallow, judging by their color, and I disliked the implications there. They filled the entire back wall of this almost… cathedral of flesh and blood, rising near to the ceiling upon tiered shelves. The center of the wall, though, was dominated by a disturbing sight. Something I could only presume was an image meant to represent their Red Truth, their faith.

A humanoid, or perhaps Dwarvenoid figure. It was nude, and yet featureless and smooth. Its arms were upstretched in a ‘v’ of supplication, while its legs were spread to the same degree in a mirror of its arms. Its head was thrown back in an almost joyous manner, though I couldn’t see the face of the figure.

And down the midline of the effigy was an incision. The ‘skin’ of the figure had been peeled back along a deep, deep cut, stretching from the throat all the way to the groin. In the exact center of the flayed chest was…something. I almost wanted to call it a heart, but it was the wrong shape. Almost spiral, I wanted to say, but not in the way of the Holy Gyre of Gyreism.

More like the coils of a snake.

But while that was an unpleasant sight, it was really what was arrayed in front of the effigy.

Stretching all the way from one side of the wall to the other in front of it was the single largest collection of Karvokar. All of them were on their mutilated knees before it, facing away from us with their heads bowed in prayer and hands clasped before them.

There were easily over a hundred of them…and that was bad. Maybe as many as three hundred, through rough calculations. I couldn’t help but tense up at the sight of them, and I wasn’t the only one in my battle group.

If we tried to take all of those at once, we would get stomped. Flat out. We just weren’t the equal of that many Karvokar.

But before I could start second-guessing myself, a single figure at the end of the hall rose to its feet. This one had been in the exact center of the kneeling Dwarves, right beneath the statue of the flayed figure. Immediately, I knew that this one was different from the other Karvokar, even before it turned to face us. In fact, I wasn’t even sure they were a Karvokar.

My suspicions were confirmed when the standing Dwarf in the distance turned, smiled at us…and spoke, his voice echoing oddly down the hall.

“Come, travelers. I would speak with you. Let this simple servant bestow its wisdom upon you.” He chuckled.

“I promise it will be a most elucidating conversation.”
