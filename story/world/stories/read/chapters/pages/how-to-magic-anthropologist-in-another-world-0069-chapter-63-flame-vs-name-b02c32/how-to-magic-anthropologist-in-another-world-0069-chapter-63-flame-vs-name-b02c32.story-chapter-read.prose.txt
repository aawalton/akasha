Raik.

Raik watched as Kan moved by reflex.

Suu’s bowstring snapped with a sharp twang. Raik barely saw the arrow leave the string. He only saw the air shift as it moved.

One of Kan’s chains flashed upward, and metal struck wood mid-flight. The arrow spun away into the sand.

Kan did not waste time looking at Raik as she checked on Calr once more.

“He’s not responding,” she said. “He’s trapped somehow.”

Another arrow followed immediately.

Kan twisted, her chains coiling around her forearm before snapping outward again. The second arrow was deflected, but the force pushed her back half a step until she bumped into Calr.

Suu did not aim at Kan or Raik.

She aimed at Calr.

Kan realized it as well. She shifted position and placed herself between the archer and the red-haired boy, her chains rotating in tight, controlled arcs.

“Stay near him,” Raik said calmly.

He did not look at Kan either. She could handle herself well enough.

He was watching the moon-haired girl.

NoName, that was what her dreamer friend had called her.

She was already moving.

Her footwork was good. She kept a low center of gravity. Her hands were empty, but poised for attack.

With no visible weapon and no obvious power, Raik decided to remain wary of hidden tricks.

He rolled his shoulders once, as heat began gathering inside him.

It built within his palms, rising from his abdomen and flowing through his arms. The air around his hands shimmered, distorting like desert heat above sunbaked stone.

NoName smirked.

“Finally.”

Raik stepped forward. He chose to abandon long-distance fighting. Vena and Alice remained behind enemy lines.

The current position favored the enemy, since they could use them as shields.

He needed to collapse the distance between them.

Suu fired again.

Kan’s chain intercepted the arrow once more, but this time the shaft broke mid-air.

Kan cursed and ducked.

The pointed half grazed her shoulder while the fletching half landed near Calr’s feet.

The blue-haired archer shifted position and adjusted her aim. She was trying to pin Kan down and force her to protect Calr.

Raik took note of that. And consider throwing a fireball at the Soulit girl.

NoName noticed his eyes leaving her and chose that moment to lunge at him.

She was faster than he expected. Her fist cut toward his jaw.

Raik turned into the strike instead of away from it, and his heated palm met her forearm.

Contact.

Her skin sizzled instantly.

NoName hissed but did not retreat. Instead, she twisted with the motion, turning the pain into momentum as her other hand drove toward his ribs.

Raik absorbed the blow.

She was strong. Stronger than she looked. But not as strong as someone kindred like Yon or Kan.

He countered.

His right hand rose in a short hook, heat warping the air around it.

He was mimicking his cousin Takur’s style.

Close-quarters brutality, rather than his usual midrange combat style.

NoName slipped inside the strike.

She moved closer than expected, and the heat lifted strands of her hair upward.

Her knee shot toward his stomach.

Raik caught her thigh mid-rise. His palm pressed into the muscle and released a concentrated pulse of heat.

She grunted and wrenched herself free, retreating two steps.

Her eyes sharpened.

“You fight like a butcher,” she said.

Raik shrugged.

She came again, this time lower. Her leg swept toward his ankle.

Raik jumped, heat flaring down his leg as he twisted in mid-air and brought his heel crashing down toward her shoulder.

She rolled beneath the attack, sending sand spraying into the air.

Raik landed and immediately pivoted.

Behind NoName, Obora flared her wings and shook them. Two feathers flew toward him, burning away as they struck his fiery aura. Her eyes flicked between the fight and Kitame’s frozen form.

She was prioritizing her friend’s safety over joining the battle.

That was good.

It meant he could focus entirely on NoName.

Another arrow streaked past Raik.

Close, but still aimed at Calr.

Kan had been forced back again.

Suu was not firing randomly. She forced Kan to reposition and prevented her from fully committing to an attack.

Raik needed to end this quickly.

NoName darted in again. This time she drew a blade; It was short and curved.

The sword flashed toward his throat.

Raik leaned back just enough for the edge to brush against the heat of his aura instead of his skin. The blade glowed faintly where it touched the superheated air.

He seized her wrist, heat surging through his grip.

She snarled and head-butted him.

The crack of skull against skull echoed across the battlefield.

Raik staggered half a step.

She tore her wrist free, the skin reddened but still functional.

NoName smiled despite the pain.

“That’s all you’ve got, noble boy?”

Raik did not answer.

Instead, he shifted the rhythm of the fight.

Takur’s style focused on overwhelming aggression, but Raik’s own training emphasized control.

He slowed.

The battlefield had shifted enough that he could throw a fireball without risking Alice.

NoName broke it with her sword, the flame exploding around her. Despite that, she kept sprinting toward him.

He let her come.

She struck high.

Raik parried with the back of his forearm, controlling the heat instead of letting it flare wildly. His other hand struck low into her ribs.

This time, she felt it.

Air burst from her lungs in a sharp gasp.

Raik followed immediately with a left palm to her shoulder and a right elbow aimed at her collarbone.

She barely blocked the elbow, but the heat transfer forced her to disengage again.

Sand shifted beneath their feet.

Behind him, Kan grunted.

Suu had forced her almost back to Calr’s body once more.

Raik could not allow that to continue.

Raik feinted right and threw a fireball at the archer. She managed to dodge it, barely. Allowing Kan to step forward a few steps and throw a few attacks with her chain at max range.

NoName reacted immediately to Raik's distraction, thrusting her sword toward him.

He stepped left and drove forward with his full weight. His palm slammed into her sternum.

Heat detonated outward in a contained burst.

The air boomed from the impact.

NoName flew backward, skidding across the sand before catching herself in a roll.

She rose again, breathing heavily.

Her leather armor smoked faintly at the chest.

Raik advanced steadily.

She was good, very good. But she had no visible enhancement or elemental augmentation. There was no aura flare and no kindred strength. Only training and pain tolerance.

He could overpower that, the same way he always overpowered Katar in a prolonged fight.

Suu drew her bow again, this time aiming directly at Raik.

Kan had already stepped forward even more, her chain whipped through the air, wrapped around the arrow mid-flight, and snapped it cleanly in half.

The archer looked as though she was running out of arrows.

Raik could feel the tempo of the battle shifting in their favor.

NoName’s breathing grew heavier. Her steps slowed slightly. Her guard dropped a fraction.

He pressed forward with three rapid strikes: palm, elbow, and knee.

She blocked two.

The third connected with her thigh.

Heat sank deep into the muscle.

She stumbled.

Raik saw the opening.

As he stepped forward to finish the fight, he heard her speak.

“I have seen your strength and tasted your mana, Raik Agame, son of Taka Agame,” she called.

Her voice carried clearly across the battlefield.

“I am NoName, daughter of None. You are Raik, son of Taka.”

Her tone resembled a ritual chant.

Raik felt a sudden premonition of danger.

He rushed forward, trying to land the finishing blow before she could finish whatever she was doing.

“I am Raik, daughter of Taka, and you are NoName, son of None,” she said, switching the names.

To Raik’s horror, the heat inside his body vanished before his punch could land.

His power died instantly.

NoName stood upright and gave him a bloody grin.

Then heat burst from her side, distorting the air exactly as his power had moments earlier.

Somehow, she had stolen his name.

She had stolen his power.

For the first time in his life, Raik felt heat that did not belong to him.

The blast struck him in the chest like a hammer.

This heat did not feel familiar.

It was not the obedient current that normally flowed through his veins under his command.

This heat burned him; his skin blistered instantly, the fabric over his shoulder blackened and curled, and then the smell reached him a moment later.

Burned flesh… his flesh.

Raik staggered backward.

NoName stood where he had been advancing moments earlier, one hand wreathed in shimmering heat that distorted the air exactly as he once did.

“You look surprised,” she said softly.

Raik clenched his jaw and tried to call the heat back.

Nothing.

He tried activating his fireball Soulbook.

Nothing happened again.

There was warmth inside his body, but it felt distant and muffled, like trying to move a limb that had fallen asleep.

NoName stepped forward.

Her heated palm shot toward his face.

Raik barely twisted aside in time.

Even so, the edge of the strike brushed across his cheek and split the skin.

Heat tore through the wound.

The pain was sharp and immediate.

He had trained with heat his entire life; he had slept inside forge fires, he had bathed in lava, he had allowed Takur to strike him with controlled bursts of flame, and never once in his life had he felt the pain of burning. Even his brother, whose soul strength far surpassed his own, could not burn him.

This was different.

He felt vulnerable.

NoName pressed the advantage.

A low kick snapped toward his knee.

A heated palm drove toward his ribs.

He blocked the second strike, but the force still pushed him backward. Sand shifted beneath his heels.

She moved with new confidence.

“You feel it now?” she asked. “Your name fits better on me, don’t you think?”

She struck again.

Raik raised his forearm.

Heat exploded against it.

The burn tore through his resistance.

Not completely, he could tell that he still possessed far greater fire resistance than most people, but it burned enough to make his teeth grind together in pain.

He had never needed to defend himself against his own element before.

Another strike hit his shoulder. His skin split open again. But the exchange was not one-sided.

He answered with a kick to her knee.

For the first time in his life, Raik Agame felt what his enemies felt when he touched them.

And he hated it.

Behind him, Kan advanced.

“Raik!” she shouted.

Suu answered her with one of her final arrows.

Raik saw the fireball forming in NoName’s thieving hand, an instant before she launched it.

It was not aimed at him, but at Kan.

Raik moved without thinking.

He stepped directly into its path.

The fireball slammed into his chest and exploded.

Heat engulfed him.

He roared as the blast drove him back two full steps.

His cloak caught fire, and his skin screamed, but he did not fall.

He was no longer immune to heat, but he remained far more resistant than Kan or Calr.

The flames guttered out around him.

Smoke curled from his silken armor.

Kan did not hesitate.

She stepped through the fading heat and lashed her chain forward.

The metal cracked across NoName’s side with a sharp snap.

NoName stumbled.

Raik exhaled slowly while pain spread across his chest.

His heart pounded harder than it ever had during battle.

For the first time in his life, he felt exposed without his power.

Suu dropped her bow.

She drew the sword from her hip and rushed forward.

She did not move as fluidly as NoName, but she was determined.

Raik turned to meet her, allowing Kan to deal with NoName for the moment.

Without his fire, his movements felt heavier and slower, and every instinct screamed that something was missing.

Suu slashed at him, her strike aimed slightly too high.

Raik caught her wrist.

She twisted, trying to slip free.

Raik stepped inside her guard and drove his shoulder into her chest.

She staggered backward.

He followed with a clean punch to her midsection.

There was no heat behind the strike, only muscle and training.

She wheezed.

He struck again.

This time, she managed to block, but the defense was sloppy.

Raik twisted sharply and disarmed her, sending the sword spinning into the sand.

Even without his power, he was still Raik Agame.

He had trained since he could walk.

He was still stronger and faster.

Yet the discomfort crawled beneath his skin.

Every movement felt wrong without the hum of heat reinforcing it.

Across the battlefield, Kan and NoName clashed.

Chains struck against stolen flames.

NoName suddenly shifted her stance.

Her right hand flared with heat.

Her left hand blossomed with frost.

White vapor curled outward from her palm.

Raik froze in shock.

That was not part of his power.

She should not be able to do that.

He had been born with the ability to control heat.

His Soulbook allowed him to create fireballs.

It did not grant the ability to freeze things.

NoName clapped her hands together around the incoming chain.

Heat and cold met at the same point of impact.

Steam exploded outward.

The metal screamed.

Half of Kan’s chain shattered as several links snapped apart and scattered across the sand.

Raik stared.

He did not understand what he was seeing.

His power did not command cold.

He manipulated heat. He increased it and redirected it.

What she was doing was something entirely different.

Kan blinked at the display for half a second.

Then she stepped forward.

One of her chains now hung at half its original length, but she did not retreat. She closed the distance instead.

With the remaining length of the chain, she whipped forward again.

The strike cracked directly across NoName’s face.

The impact was sharp and brutal.

Blood sprayed from the wound.

NoName staggered backward.

Kan pressed the advantage immediately.

She grabbed the intact chain like a flail and swung again.

The tide of the fight began to shift.

Raik shoved Suu backward and turned just in time to see NoName retreat two steps under Kan’s assault.

For a brief moment, hope flickered. They are going to win this. No name was no match for Kan. His girlfriend had trained long enough with him in the past two months to be able to handle his powers.

That’s when Obora decided to move.

She flared one wing and leveled her spear.

She did not aim at Kan.

She did not aim at Raik.

Her target was Alice.

The spear tip hovered inches from Alice’s throat.

Obora’s voice cut across the battlefield.

“Stop.”

Raik froze mid-step.

Kan’s chain halted in the middle of its swing.

Obora’s eyes were cold and steady.

“Another strike,” she said evenly, “and I run her through.”

The battlefield fell silent; only the heavy breathing remained.

The sand still hissed faintly where heat had scorched it.

Raik felt heat flicker inside his chest.

It was not his power.

It was rage.

They had been hunting monsters in a deadland.

They had been doing honest work.

These people had followed them.

They had ambushed them while they were resting.

Now that they were losing a fair fight, they had turned to treachery.

What had he expected?

“I guess I shouldn’t have expected better from Donas’ daughters,” Raik spat. “I bet the only thing he taught you was how to be cowards.”

NoName reacted immediately.

Her anger flared.

But her response never came.

A sharp, ragged gasp cut through the moment.

Kitame jerked upright as if she had just surfaced from deep water.

She looked worse than any of them.

Her skin was pale beneath the dust.

Her eyes trembled and struggled to focus.

Sweat clung to her temples.

Her hands shook as though she had been left outside in winter.

She slowly turned toward Calr.

For a heartbeat, she simply stared at him.

Then her expression shattered.

Terror flooded her face.

She recoiled as though struck and quickly averted her eyes.

She stumbled backward before collapsing to her knees.

Then she began vomiting violently into the sand.

Her entire body convulsed as if she had witnessed something unspeakable.

Calr stood nearby.

He looked far steadier than she did, although his face had lost most of its color.

He used the distraction to retrieve his crossbow.

It hung loosely in his grip.

He had not raised it yet, but it was already loaded.

His breathing was heavy and uneven.

“What’s wrong?” Obora asked.

Her voice carried genuine worry despite the spear still pointed at Alice.

Kitame spat into the sand and wiped her mouth with the back of her hand.

She forced the words out between breaths.

“His mind was a nightmare.”

NoName’s lips curled despite the blood on her face.

“What kind of fucked up shit did you see inside his head?”

Kitame shook her head slowly.

“You wouldn’t want to know.”

Her voice cracked on the final word.

She pushed herself to her feet with visible effort. Her body swayed once before she managed to steady herself.

Her gaze swept across the battlefield as she reassessed the situation.

Suu used the interruption immediately.

She disengaged cleanly from Raik, retreating in controlled steps until she regrouped beside her sisters.

Now the four daughters of Donas stood together.

They formed a loose defensive triangle. Raik and Kan stood on one side of the battlefield, while Alice and Vena still lay collapsed behind enemy lines.

Obora’s spear remained pointed toward Alice’s throat, but her eyes had shifted toward Calr.

He had finally raised his crossbow.

…That was her mistake.

Alice moved.

Without warning, she seized the shaft of Obora’s spear and shoved it upward toward her captor’s chin.

The motion was clumsy and desperate, but it worked.

Obora’s grip loosened for a fraction of a second.

That moment was enough.

Alice rolled across the ground, sand spraying beneath her as she threw herself over Vena’s body.

For a moment, they disappeared behind a swirl of dust and the slope of the terrain.

When the dust settled, they were gone.
