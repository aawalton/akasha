“—and Maya usually gets us to stop arguing long enough to remember our actual objective. She’s patient, Mortarius, but don’t mistake that for being timid. She’s strong-willed.”

Katherine Dragomirov returned to Eternus Online under the weight of her leather and chainmail, the cool stone of the throne against her spine, and Romulus’s voice somewhere above her closed eyes. She opened them slowly, bringing the lichfire-lit throne room into focus as she raised her head. A faint ache in her neck told her that her Eidos had fallen asleep at an awkward neck angle, and she grimaced at the feeling while rousing herself.

The clock on her HUD showed her it was a few minutes past four in the morning in-game, which at least meant they’d managed not to be late, thank God. It was reassuring that they hadn’t missed the deadline, though her attention quickly diverted to the tall, beautiful face that leaned forward from the throne behind her when her stirring was noticed.

“Hey, Kay,” her best friend and the man she loved said warmly, his expression brightening with unfairly attractive happiness. “Welcome back to Neverland. I was just telling Morty about the others.”

Kate’s heart gave an unreasonable little backflip at his words and smile, and she accepted the hand he offered afterward to rise, avoiding becoming ridiculous by staring at his face. Once she’d regained her feet, he kept hold of her while she recovered her balance after being gone, and then let her go when she retrieved her bow. The grip was neither new nor strange, yet something about the care implicit in it continued to play at her—there was something intense about the casual way her best friend cared about her that made her stomach flutter traitorously.

Get a grip, Kay. You’re acting like a dumb teenager.

“Did you warn Morty about Cora?” she asked aloud, focusing on practical matters instead.

“I… mostly did,” Romulus admitted. “I described her as thorough?”

“Ha!” Kate replied while adjusting her bow. “Oh man, that’s one word for it.”

“I take it that his warning was a generous euphemism, Katherine?” Mortarius asked dryly.

“You could say that!” Kate answered glibly and then skipped down the steps of the dais before turning back toward her best friend and ghostly mentor. “Did they send you the [Social Link] requests, Lulu?”

“Just got them,” the Revenant-King replied while his crimson eyes glazed over, and his hand tapped at the air three times in succession. “They’re all gathered, apparently. Patience remains a virtue Cora is distinctly lacking in, too.”

“She’s consistent!” Kate said with genuine glee at the thought of her female best friend and her beautiful amber eyes, and then promptly waved her arm. “Come on, moodies. Let’s go expand our party.”

Romulus and Mortarius followed her obligingly through the throne room at her words, wrapping around the dais toward the concealed chamber behind it, where Kate slowed upon entry despite having arrived through it herself. Her attention had been pretty firmly occupied with her best friend’s hot new Vampire transformation—Sanguine, she reminded herself, remembering his objections—instead of the murals within the transit chamber.

Now, with nobody demanding her thirsty appreciation and with considerably more time to have adjusted to the man she secretly loved looking like a manga love interest from her most secretive book collections, she had time to properly appreciate the elegance of the circular domed space. It was broad enough, first and foremost, for their entire group to stand comfortably without worrying about personal space—its cylindrical walls rising toward the aforementioned domed ceiling almost fifteen meters above them.

Lilith, naturally, occupied the mural overhead, silver-haired, gorgeous, and unmistakable in her Kate-coined “Deathmommy” beauty despite the surrounding ornamentation. Beneath the Divinarch’s representation, bands of rune-marked tiles and wrapping enchantments spread across the walls and a floor roughly twenty meters from side to side, ten meters out from the center in all directions. It enclosed a smaller, more focused circle where Kate remembered emerging from her void-like portal during her own transit.

When Romulus arrived, already tapping System options with his hand and his eyes still glazed, Kate watched as those same enchantments blazed to life; void-purple energy suffusing them as they ignited with purpose, spreading from central enchantment nodes whose activation triggered each successive one in line. A glance down showed that same illumination dancing across her dark leathers and chainmail, and a glance to her best friend showed the same light reflecting off his warplate.

“Refrain from approaching the inner circle, Katherine,” Mortarius warned her mildly. “I doubt you wish to lose a limb to the portal.”

“Is that possible?” she asked while minding her footing and taking a careful step back despite not being in the inner circle.

“It has happened more times than would be dignified to recount,” the specter confirmed.

“Noted,” she replied with a sigh. “I was only admiring the décor, anyway. I wasn’t volunteering as a landing cushion.”

“A fine distinction,” Mortarius accepted. “Maintain it, if you would.”

Romulus laughed from just behind her while tapping something with finality that only he could see, and a low hum gathered beneath their feet almost immediately. The already-active enchantments rippled with silvered lightning, the smaller circle of runes brightened, and a thin black seam split the air at the center of the chamber. Katherine watched in fascination as the seam rotated and spread into an ovular shape, becoming an upright rift of abyssal darkness, its edges crackling with black-purple lightning while refusing to reveal what lurked within.

For a heartbeat after it appeared, Kate found herself remembering what it was like when she herself had stepped through that portal not so long ago. Then, as if timed to interrupt her recollection, the bulky figure of Nathanius stepped through the rift with his shield angled ahead of him cautiously. He stopped after he emerged, blinking at Kate—the first person in his sightline—and instinctively glancing back toward the portal, and then up at the ceiling.

His slate-gray eyes widened at the mural and glyphs, and his dark features split into a grin.

“Okay, fuck me, that beats walking any day.”

“Nate!” Kate squealed, rushing forward to hug the lovable tank while Romulus laughed from behind her, drawing Nate’s gaze as the arm not holding his shield wrapped around Kate in fraternal affection.

“Hey, Kay. Good to see you chose to look like you, minus the darker hair,” Nate said warmly, while looking past her at Romulus. “And holy shit, Dee. Talk about a makeover!”

Kate released Nate with a grin when Romulus approached, allowing the two to embrace fully while she flipped her hair. “I’d love to claim credit, but this was all Lilith. She said it accentuated the blood I’d spill in her name. She has taste.”

“I’m sure,” Nate replied warmly while separating from Romulus and taking him in. “And as for you, dude, you look like you raided the final boss’s closet.”

“I technically did,” Romulus replied wryly. “Though, in my defense, I earned this armor.”

“That’s what’s important,” Nate agreed without sarcasm as the two of them stepped back to join Kate in sync.

The runes around the chamber crackled again when they did, and the portal shivered once before settling again, followed by Mayarella’s careful step through it with a bounce of her dark curls and a widening of her brown eyes. She glanced around the chamber exactly as her boyfriend had, then looked toward Kate and Romulus and let out a squeal of delight. “Oh my god, you weren’t lying! You guys look awesome!”

Kate laughed and immediately drew her into a hug, squeezing her in affection before releasing her to hug Romulus, who laughed in turn. “Welcome to the evil castle, Maya.”

“Ohmygod, Dee, this place is so cool,” Maya returned, releasing him and moving to grip Nate’s arm. “It’s huge.”

“And this is just the transit chamber,” Kate noted smugly. “Wait ‘til you see the rest.”

“I bet!” Maya replied. “Nate vanished into his portal and left this horrible tear in the air, and then the portal opened in front of me, and I nearly tripped backing away from it.”

“Separate summons, single anchor,” Romulus explained, folding his arms in satisfaction as he did. “Corey’s next up.”

“Good,” Maya said with relief. “She’ll probably be trying to [Examine] it first.”

“Of course she will,” Kate said with warm fondness. A glance back at Mortarius showed the specter observing the two new arrivals with quiet consideration, and their evident lack of reaction to him told her that he had yet to reveal himself. Kate chose not to spoil that particular surprise yet and instead turned back to the portal as it reset and restabilized once more.

Corva came through with her staff in hand and her blue-streaked black hair tucked behind her pointed ears, staring at the chamber beyond. Her amber eyes were wide with interest, and they immediately found the array beneath her boots, following the sigils across the floor toward the walls and the ceiling above. A sigh of naked interest left her lips, and then her gaze returned to the four people awaiting her, and for one heartbeat, silence followed.

Then the elven channeler shrieked in excitement and threw her arms up while Kate barreled into them.

“Bitch!”

“Bitch!”

The two women laughed happily and jumped in place while hugging, while Corva finally looked past Kate at Romulus. “Oh hell no,” she said immediately, spinning Kate around by the shoulders and bending to speak near her ear. “Is that who I think it is?”

“Ayup,” Kate said with a flutter in her chest that had nothing to do with the speed of the enforced spin. “It’s him.”

“Damn,” Corva said in naked appreciation, kissing Kate’s cheek before stepping forward and pointing her staff at Romulus. “Your hotness is illegal, Dee. Illegal!”

“Sue me, then,” the Autarch replied warmly, stepping forward and taking Corva into a firm embrace and holding her in one-arm afterward. “Nice staff, by the way.”

“Thanks!” the sorceress said primly. “I definitely didn’t steal it from anyone.”

“She looted it from a wagon we passed,” Nate supplied dryly.

“Nathaniel!” Corva said in a betrayed tone.

“What? You’re the one who stole from the peddler, Cora.”

“He was an asshole!” the elven woman protested, while Kate felt amusement flow through her.

“It was still theft,” Maya said smugly, while looking around the chamber again.

“I’m standing right here,” Romulus complained, while Cora and Kate ignored him with long practice. “Right here.”

Laughter followed the objection from Nate, and the next moments were filled with quiet exclamations over the chamber again, stories about their starter zone—Marienburg—and complaints about starter gear and acquired equipment they’d thought was excellent until seeing what Romulus and Kate were wearing.

Kate found herself smiling like an idiot through all of it, eventually linking arms with both Maya and Cora as the two women conveyed what had happened to them, while Romulus and Nate stepped aside slightly to discuss the latter’s equipment, with Romulus hefting the shield and nodding his approval, and Nate commenting with enthusiasm on Romulus’s armor and the pommel of Lightsbane, which Kate saw Romulus pat as if agreeing with something the bloodthirsty sword said.

Their three newly arrived friends followed his gaze to where the specter stood invisible, and Kate knew the moment the former Revenant-King appeared from the unified freeze that came over Nate, Maya, and Cora at the exact same moment.

“Greetings,” the specter said calmly, his crimson eyes drifting over the three new arrivals again now that they could see him. “I am Mortarius. Welcome to the Necropolis of the Dark Lady.”

“I’m Mayarella,” she greeted him warmly. “I prefer Maya. Thanks so much for welcoming us.”

I am given to understand you all have questions, and while we could speak here, it may be prudent to return to the dais instead.”

“Agreed,” Romulus said before anyone else could go on a tangent. “We’ve got a lot to discuss.”

Kate moved to Romulus’s side again immediately, while the others agreed. Cora linked arms with her again, and together the five friends and their long-suffering spectral mentor left the chamber, while Kate spared a final glance back at Lilith’s mural.

Thank you for all you’ve given us, she thought privately. I promise we won’t let you down.

Silence answered her. It was more than enough.
