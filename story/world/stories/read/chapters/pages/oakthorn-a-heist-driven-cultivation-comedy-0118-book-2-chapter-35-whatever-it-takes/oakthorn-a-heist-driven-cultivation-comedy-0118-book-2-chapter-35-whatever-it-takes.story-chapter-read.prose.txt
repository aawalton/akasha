In the moment before death claims its prize, time stretches like molten glass.

Orro Yanson, exiled assassin of the Grassblade clans, knew this truth better than most.

And right then, in the midst of a torrent of water and loss, he felt death stretch out time, prolonging his agony into an eternity of droplets and rage.

The water roared around Orro. It was like a living entity intent on devouring them all. Through the churning chaos, he caught a final glimpse of Jer—manacled to the wall, the chains allowing his body just enough movement to float slightly in the chamber’s rising flood. Their eyes met across the fading distance. Jer smiled. Not his usual jester’s grin but something peaceful, accepting, final.

Time slowed further.

Jer nodded once. It was such an easy motion, but it contained a world of hurt, forgiveness, and goodbyes. Before he could respond, the the current seized Orro and yanked him toward the open doorway where the others had already vanished. Water filled his lungs as he tumbled through the surging flood, the taste of copper and salt overwhelming his senses.

At the last possible moment, his fingers found purchase on an iron sconce embedded in the wall a few yards from the doorway’s edge. Pain lanced through his arm as the current tried to tear him free; his body became a flag in the tempest. He clung with desperation born not of self-preservation but of something deeper, more primal.

Not him. Not Jer.

Beyond the threshold, Cade, Rayka, and the others disappeared past him, the flood carrying down the long and twisting corridor. Their cries were lost beneath the water’s thunderous voice. The current tugged at Orro’s legs and demanded he follow.

He refused.

Every muscle in his body screamed as he fought the relentless pull. The wound in his shoulder reopened as he thrashed against the pressure, and blood clouded the water around him in crimson tendrils. Pain blossomed like fire beneath his skin and fingernails. Several of them began to bleed as he dug them into the cold iron he gripped. The water raged.

But still, he held.

Slowly—agonizingly—the water level began to recede as the doorway gulped down the sacrificial flood. Orro hung suspended, and his entire being focused on that single point of connection between flesh and stone. Minutes stretched into another kind of eternity as the water drained, revealing the chamber’s stone floor once more.

When the level fell below his chin, he finally allowed himself a ragged breath. Air filled his burning lungs, sharp and precious as the rarest gemstone. By the time the water reached his waist, the obsidian door they’d all passed by had fully closed once more, sealing with the finality of a tomb.

Sealing Jer inside.

The realization struck like a physical blow and drove the air from Orro’s chest anew. Behind that impenetrable barrier, Jer was drowning. Alone.

Orro released his grip on the sconce. His boots landed on wet stone with a slushing sound that felt like an insult given what had just happened. He didn’t wait, didn’t let his mind register what must be true. In the silence left by the retreating water, he could almost hear Jer’s voice... some ridiculous quip about dramatic exits and water damage to his favorite elbow.

But there was only silence.

His hand pressed against the cold obsidian surface where seam had become seamless barrier. No mortal tool could pierce this enchanted stone, and no amount of strength could force entry. The crypt had claimed its sacrifice and it had no intention of yielding its prize.

No.

The denial rose from somewhere beyond conscious thought—a rejection so absolute it vibrated through Orro’s very bones. His hand moved to the orange-bladed weapon at his hip. He drew it with the fluid grace that had made him feared across three kingdoms.

The blade sparked against obsidian but left not even the faintest scratch.

“Open,” he commanded, voice raw from salt water and desperation. “Open, damn you.”

He struck again, harder. The impact jarred up his arm. Again. Again. Each strike more ferocious than the last, fueled by something he refused to name. Not anger—something deeper. Not fear—something more fundamental.

The blade’s edge grew notched with each impact; ancient metal protested its futile use against the ensorcelled stone. Orro continued beyond reason, beyond strategy, beyond the cold calculation that had defined his existence for so long.

Behind that barrier, Jer was dying.

Minutes ago, Orro had clung to stone rather than be swept to safety. Why? The question burned even as his arm rose and fell in ritualistic assault on the unyielding door. Why risk his life for someone who had chosen sacrifice? Why refuse the gift Jer had offered them all?

Because his smile never faltered.

The thought froze Orro mid-strike, blade hanging suspended in the air.

Because Jer—ridiculous, optimistic, infuriating Jer—had faced death with a smile that contained no fear, no regret. He had chosen to die so they might live, without hesitation or second thought. The one person who should have been least willing to die had embraced it with more courage than Orro had witnessed in his entire blood-soaked career.

How could Orro accept such a gift when his own life had been spent taking rather than giving? How could he walk away from such sacrifice when his hands carried the weight of so many deaths that had served no purpose beyond coin and contract?

No. He would not allow it. He could not allow it.

His eyes fell to the bracelet encircling his wrist—an artifact he’d sworn to use only when absolute necessity demanded it. Yet, that necessity crept in more and more these days. But this was no exception. He had to use it.

Orro had used it once already in these depths to defeat the stone guardian that had threatened Rayka and Chubbs. The risk then had been calculated, the exposure brief. Using it again so soon would be like lighting a beacon in the darkness and announcing his presence to entities that had hunted since before humankind drew first breath.

But Jer was drowning.

The decision was no decision at all. Fingers still trembling from exertion, Orro twisted the bracelet on his wrist. The familiar cold rushed through him—starting at his core and spreading outward like frost claiming a lake in deepest winter. His vision doubled, then tripled; the physical world overlaid with currents of ethereal energy that flowed like luminescent rivers through the crypt’s structure.

The obsidian door remained solid in both realities. Now he could see the spell matrix that bound it—complex geometries of power anchoring it to the physical plane. And where there was a binding, there was a way to pass through.

Orro stepped sideways. Not forward or backward in physical space, but sideways into the thin seam between realities. His body protested and nerves screamed as he existed partially in both planes. The sensation of drowning without water rushed over him—of burning without flame.

Then he was through. He stumbled into the sacrifice chamber from the other side.

His world became water.

The chamber had completely filled in the moments since Jer had sealed his fate. Orro’s lungs seized as he transitioned fully back to the physical realm; the sudden immersion shocked his system. He forced his eyes open against the sting of mineral-heavy liquid and scanned the underwater tomb for signs of Jer.

There—a shadow against the far wall, arms still manacled above his head. Not moving. Not struggling.

Orro swam with desperate strokes and fought the weight of his sodden clothes and the persistent throb of his injuries. His lungs burned for air that wasn’t there. Darkness crept at the edges of his vision as he pushed himself beyond mortal limits.

He reached Jer’s floating form. The acrobat’s face looked peaceful in unconsciousness or death. Orro’s fingers searched for a pulse beneath Jer’s jaw, finding nothing at first, then—there! Faint. Erratic. Fading.

Orro drew his orange-bladed weapon and positioned the jagged edge at the chain closest to the wall. In the water, its usual glow seemed muted, subdued—like a candle glimpsed through fog. He channeled his will through the ancient metal and struck with precision born of years of training.

The blade connected. Orange energy flared briefly underwater, and for the second time in his memory, failed to cut through its target. The chains remained intact, without even a mark where his weapon had struck.

Frustration and fear surged through him as his lungs screamed for air. Of course the crypt would protect its sacrifice! Of course the chains would resist even his enchanted blade. This chamber was designed for one purpose—to take a life in exchange for the others’ passage. The magic binding these manacles had to be far beyond his capability to break.

Jer’s body had gone completely still now. Whatever time remained could be measured in heartbeats, not minutes.

Drowning with him was pointless. Dying alongside Jer would serve no purpose, save perhaps to ease Orro’s own conscience. The rational choice—the only choice—was to return through the Veil.

Rationality could go to the four hells.

Orro wrapped his arms around Jer’s torso. He embraced his dying friend as he twisted the Spectral Veil bracelet once more. Reality fractured again; the physical world peeled away like bark from a winter-dead tree. A cracking sound shuddered through Orro and Jer, vibrating the very marrow within their bones.

The sensation was as immediate as it was overwhelming. It was akin to being turned inside out while simultaneously being crushed and stretched. Every part of his body screamed as they transitioned from physical to spiritual existence. Sound became color. Weight became memory. Time became distance.

And then, suddenly, they were elsewhere.

Cold air filled Orro’s lungs as he gasped; his body instinctively rejoiced at the simple gift of breath. Beneath him, solid ground replaced liquid death—not stone, but something crystalline that resonated with each movement and sent ripples of light outward from where they had manifested.

The spirit realm.

Orro had passed through it before. Used it as a temporary medium to reach otherwise inaccessible locations. But he had never fully entered it, never dragged another soul with him into this half-world that existed alongside but separate from physical reality.

They stood upon a vast crystalline plateau that extended into misty infinity. Above, what might have been a sky churned with colors that had no names in any mortal tongue. Below, through the translucent surface beneath their feet, yawned an abyss filled with writhing energies—currents of power that spiraled like galaxies, occasionally colliding in silent explosions of impossible light.

And in the distance, silhouettes shifted at the edges of perception. Things that moved with terrible purpose and contained ancient intelligences floated just beyond his ability to sense them. They brushed at the outermost edges of his vision, and he could practically feel their malignant curiosities turn in their direction.

But Orro’s focus remained on Jer, whose body lay motionless on the crystal surface. Without hesitation, he rolled the acrobat onto his back and tilted his head to clear any water from his airways, though such physical concerns seemed strangely abstract in this realm of pure energy and thought.

“Breathe,” Orro commanded, compressing Jer’s chest with quick motions. “Breathe, you fool. I didn’t drag us both into the twilight of existence for you to die anyway.”

The assassin continued his efforts. Each compression sent pulses of light through the crystal beneath them. Each pulse drew more attention from the watchers in the distance. They were coming now—drawn by the disturbance, by the presence of physical beings manifested within their domain.

“Wake up,” Orro hissed, desperation edging his voice as the silhouettes drew closer, their forms resolving into shapes that hurt the mind to perceive. “Elena needs you. We all need you. Wake up.”

Jer remained still.

His face was peaceful in a way that suggested permanence rather than unconsciousness. The manacles that had bound him were gone, left behind in the physical realm, but their purpose had been served. The sacrifice was complete.

“No,” Orro growled, rejection of this reality hardening into something approaching prayer. “No, I refuse this ending. Do you hear me? I refuse.”

Frustration and fear transformed into a single, desperate act. He slammed his fist against Jer’s chest with enough force to crack ribs and the impact sent a rippling shockwave through the crystal surface.

Jer’s body convulsed. Water spewed from his mouth as he gasped; his eyes flew open in shock and confusion. He coughed violently, desperate to clear his lungs of fluid that no longer existed in this half-world between realities.

“What—” Jer managed between ragged breaths, “where—”

“spirit realm. Don’t worry about it,” Orro answered past the lump that had long since gathered in his throat. Before the fool could respond, Orro pulled Jer’s drenched form up by the shoulders and gave him a crushing hug.

“Ooh! Ow, ow ow!” Jer yelped, but hugged the assassin back.

“Oh. Right. Sorry about your ribs,” Orro muttered, relinquishing his titanic grip on the acrobat.

“I’ll forgive you on the basis that—you know—I’m not dead.” Jer glanced around at where they were. “Wait… We’re not dead, right?”

“Not yet,” the assassin replied grimly, helping the acrobat slowly get to his feet. The crystal surface beneath them pulsed in time with their movements; each step announced their presence to whatever entities claimed this realm as territory. “But we will be if we stay here much longer.”

Jer swayed, still disoriented from his near-death and sudden transition. His gaze fixed on something beyond Orro’s shoulder; his eyes widened with a mixture of awe and terror. “What are those?”

Orro didn’t turn to look. He felt what approached—shapes formed of concepts rather than matter, intelligences that perceived existence through lenses incomprehensible to mortal minds. Predators that hunted across dimensions, drawn to disruptions in the cosmic order.

“Don’t look at them,” he warned, gripping Jer’s shoulder to focus his attention. “Looking invites awareness. Let’s just get back.”

“Uhh, yes. Let’s,” Jer replied hurriedly, his gripping tightening on Orro’s shoulder.

Orro twisted the bracelet again and prepared for the transition back to the physical world. Nothing happened. He tried once more and focused his will on the artifact that had never failed him before.

Still nothing. The bracelet remained inert, the passage closed.

“Something’s blocking us,” he muttered, a cold certainty settling in his gut. Not something—someone. The crypt itself, perhaps, or whatever consciousness directed its ancient mechanisms. The sacrifice chamber had claimed its due, and it had no intention of surrendering its prize.

Or perhaps something within this realm itself had sealed the way behind them, trapping fresh prey in its domain.

“Okay. We can’t go back the way we came,” Orro said, mind racing through limited options as the watchers drew inexorably closer. “We need to find another path.”

“There’s always another way out,” Jer replied, some of his irrepressible spirit returning despite their dire circumstances. He scanned the crystalline landscape, then pointed toward where the plateau narrowed into a bridge-like formation that spanned the swirling abyss. “That looks promising and terrifying in equal measure.”

Orro followed his gaze and assessed the narrow crystal span with professional caution. It might lead somewhere useful, or it might lead them directly into whatever waited in the depths below. But staying put was certain death.

“Can you walk?” he asked, noting how Jer still favored one leg, his injured ankle from their earlier battles clearly paining him even in this realm of thought and energy.

“I can dance a jig if it gets us away from those...” Jer gestured vaguely behind them before he turned to face the direction of the bridge. “Umm, who is that?”

“Who is who—” Orro started, following where Jer pointed ahead of them.

An outline of a figure stood before them, the edges of its presence crackling with unstable energy. Before Orro or Jer could react, it flew forward and slammed into them.

The world went white and Orro lost all grip on reality.

His final thought was of Rayka.
