Carrarl had followed Trevor and Lora to the warehouse where the next shipment was and watched with interest as he took in even more boxes and crates. Out of every other culture they had visited, the minotaur's was the least prepared for how great a Summoned’s Power could get. The clerk’s constantly perplexed stare was still a source of amusement for both of them.

[[New Quest! Five, six, seven!

This set of things contains goods for your next three destinations, which will see you returning home. You know the drill at this point.

Just a heads up, it doesn’t look like you’ll be leaving just yet. You’ll see why back on the boat.]]

Trevor gave Lora a curious look. “Is there something waiting for us back at the Matador?”

“No, why?” the teleporter asked in return, confused.

“The Quest Master said we won’t be leaving just yet and I’ll see why when we return to the boat,” he explained, gesturing vaguely in front of him.

“I dunno. All the plans I knew of were dashed when we received news that Lady Adabelle’s passport was delayed,” Lora said, spinning her fingers inside the ribbon her belt. “I guess we’ll find out when we get there!”

“I guess so,” Trevor chuckled before turning to Carrarl. “Are we all set, sir?”

“Huh?” the minotaur asked. He had been staring at the now empty space when the courier interrupted him. “Oh, uh, sorry. Yes. We’re all set.”

“Excellent. Thanks for your hard work,” he replied, making sure to shake the custom clerk’s hand on the way out of the warehouse. The whole endeavor might have been a slight chore, but there was no reason to be impolite. “If you ever need an inventoryman, ask for Klar. He'll see if I’m available.”

“Inventoryman, okay,” Carrarl said. The Summoned were leaving the building when they heard the minotaur speak to himself. “How much space does that guy have?”

Lora giggled and nudged Trevor in his side as they walked back to the docks. “I think you might have broken him a little bit. They’re really not used to Summoned here.”

“I don’t really consider what I do is as impressive as what you do, Lora,” he responded. “Imagine what seeing you teleport would do to him.”

She shrugged. “Just different talents, is all. I try not to scare the locals, though.”

As they approached the Matador, they noticed a crowd of workers outside. The cargo they had been unloading was sitting on the dock instead of going all the way to the warehouse, and it looked like they were going to grab everything before moving it the rest of the way.

“What’s going on?” Lora asked.

“Miss Lora,” one of the minotaurs greeted. “It’s, well… Probably better if you see for yourself. They’ve taken over the cargo hold.”

Trevor frowned. “Taken over? Like, pirates?”

“Uh, no, sir. You’ll see.”

The others snickered as if something were funny. If it bothered Lora, she didn’t show it. With a gesture forward, Trevor followed Lora onto the Matador. The cargo hold wasn’t far into the ship at all, by design, and they heard the cause of the disruption long before they saw it.

“—the one that’s supposedly too dangerous to come into the country?” came an older woman’s voice. She spoke with the same cadence Klar did. “Nonsense, you’re so polite! The only dangerous thing about you is your good looks. I bet you were knocking men dead before you decided to settle down. If I were ten years younger, I’d have adventured across the Isle of Horns with you, too!”

“Ah, I see what’s happened,” Lora said with a grin.

“Care to enlighten me?” Trevor asked.

She shook her head. “Nope,” was all she said before disappearing on the spot, leaving him all alone.

Sighing, the courier continued.

“I wouldn’t say that. Adventuring with my sword sisters took up most of my time, and I wasn’t ready for romance of any kind,” Adabelle replied.

“Sword sisters? Is that some kind of euphemism? I’m not caught up on the lingo of other nations so I haven’t heard that one before.”

“No, ma, Lady Adabelle’s just saying that she and her adventuring party were so close that they became sisters,” Klar said, sounding both a little exasperated and embarrassed.

Trevor perked up as he entered the cargo hold. The last of the crates were being emptied as the three spoke, and he was treated with an odd sight.

A large feasting table had taken over the middle of the room. It was already covered in food. Vegetables were most prevalent, but there was bread, cheese, and what smelled like an absolutely delightful stew in a huge pot at the center. Klar was setting up for five to eat while Adabelle spoke with an older minotauran woman.

The resemblance was apparent, and Trevor realized that he was looking at the merchant prince’s mother. She was draped in warm, but loud, colors in their traditional fashion, making it look like she was wearing a dress. Her back was hunched somewhat and she walked with a cane, so she stood about as tall as the courier.

Adabelle saw Trevor arrive and smiled. She had ditched the jacket since the inside of the Matador was nice and warm, but kept the tail sleeve. “I hate to interrupt, but it appears that my husband is back.”

“Oh, Trevor?” the woman asked, turning around.

“Yes ma’am, I’m Trevor Ander—”

Klar’s mother closed the distance deceptively fast. She pulled him into a big hug before he could finish introducing himself. “I’ve heard so much about you and your noble bride from Klarry that I feel like I’ve known you for years. You can call me Barb, I’m Klarkartar’s mother. It’s a pleasure to meet you,” she said, pulling away to kiss both of his cheeks.

“The pleasure is all mine,” Trevor returned.

“Don’t you also have some kind of little stone guy clambering around you, too?” Barb asked as she pulled away and led the courier to his wife. “I was a little skeptical about the guy keeping a monster around but I’ve been told he—no, they, Klarry was very clear about that. Anyway, I was told they were quite cute.”

Clay’s head popped out of their master’s backpack and they looked around. They slowly emerged and stood halfway on Trevor’s shoulder as the minotauran woman watched them curiously. Adabelle took her husband’s hand, her tail wrapping around his wrist, and they smiled.

“Just look at this family,” Barb said, holding a hand up to her heart. “Klarkartar, those pictures of yours from the wedding do not do the sheer happiness radiating off of these three justice. I tell you what, my son begged me to come, but I’m very firm about traditions. If you had held it here on the island, though? Nothing could have kept me from it.”

“Those are old and outdated beliefs, ma,” the merchant prince all but sighed.

“Traditions are traditions for a reason,” she shot back in a voice that made it clear these kinds of debates happened often. “That’s why you wanted me to meet some of your friends other than that Asalorana girl, right? Where is she, anyway?”

“Right here, Miss Barb,” the teleporter announced as she walked in. She had changed out of the warm weather clothing and back into her suit. However, it looked like it had been padded slightly around the midsection.

Just like Trevor, Lora was quick to receive a hug. “There you are!” Barb happily trilled. “I was hoping I’d get to see you. Looks like you took my advice and started eating well, too. You were so skinny before, I was worried a slight breeze could knock you over!”

“Yup, the boss has been really good to me,” she replied with a smile, returning the hug before pulling away. “I wasn’t expecting you! What brings you all the way out to Rastarore? And with your son, no less.”

“Oh, I just got so mad when I heard what those other merchant princes did to keep Klarry’s friends out of the country,” the older woman said, frowning as she walked over to take Adabelle’s hand, patting it gently. “Too dangerous, my horns. If this upstanding young lady would hurt more than a fly if it didn’t give her cause, then I’d eat my hooves.”

“They’re two of the best people I know,” Klar said as he finished setting the table. “I know Asalorana is a bit weird...”

“Guilty as charged!” the teleporter agreed with a grin, filling the pause that the minotaur had obviously provided her.

“But I wanted you to meet some of the more normal Summoned out there, ma. Lady Adabelle is even the mayor of an up and coming town.”

“So you’ve said, dear, and that’s a wonderful, wonderful thing, but what I was most worried about was my ballabroody stew going to waste,” Barb informed them as she gestured for everyone to come to the table. “The ballabroody is a delicacy around here. Their meat is very tough, so you have to cook it just right for five days and it becomes the most succulent, juicy, falls-apart-on-the-way-to-your-mouth delight you’ve ever had.”

Klar pulled a seat out for his mother at the head of the table, and she accepted it graciously. Trevor did the same for Adabelle before shrugging off his backpack and setting it down. Clay all but dove back into it, getting back to whatever work they were up to.

“Now, right off the horn, I want you to know that you didn’t have to eat with us if you didn’t want to, this was just an option,” she continued. “We didn’t want to step into anyone’s honeymoon, I’m only here now because those bozos forced my hand. I mean, seriously, the nerve of some people to get in the way of a good ballabroody stew. Did I mention I had it cooking for five days?”

“You did, yes,” Adabelle politely answered.

Barb huffed. “These so-called merchant princes don’t let this intelligent young woman in here because she might be dangerous and they have a grudge against my self-made son? While my stew has already been cooking for days? Absolutely not! I told Klarry that we were feeding the folks he had spoken so fondly of and that was that. We were on the wagon in less than an hour.”

Her son gave them a sheepish smile, which looked out of place on the normally outgoing minotaur. “Your first stop was supposed to be to our ancestral home overlooking the capital. We had one of the spare houses made up for you so you could have your privacy. My mother’s ballabroody is the stuff of legends though, and I wanted you to have a little touch of minotaur culture that wasn’t, well, so exclusive to outsiders. I just wish I could change things faster.”

“Bah. Don't complain about how long it takes to change a country, Klarry. You’re doing a fine job, so don’t sell your efforts short,” Barb said, waving a hand at her son. “He’s always been trying to fix what he thinks is wrong with our traditions. That includes trying to get me to leave the Isle of Horns for years now. Go and travel with him, he says. See the world, he insists. My son thinks that meeting you three will change my mind, but it won’t. My place is here.”

“Which is a very old-fashioned way of thinking, ma,” Klar replied, keeping his tone carefully even as he looked back at the others. “The waves of social change are already here but, back in my mother’s day, only men went out into the world to explore and trade. I’m trying to bring her into modern times.”

“And that would be nice and all, but his skull’s too thick and he doesn’t realize that I just don’t want to go,” his mother stated with a sniff. “You all are the generation that will change, and I’m content to sit and watch it happen. I didn’t even kick up a fuss when there were rumblings of a merchant princess going through the country.”

“That’s still going to happen, ma. Dellyara deserves it, too. She does good work.”

“I’m sure she does, but look at us monopolizing the conversation. And, if there’s one thing we hate…”

“It’s a monopoly,” they said together.

“So, Klarry, why don’t you serve the stew and Trevor,” Barb said, turning to the courier. “I’ve heard you’re some kind of inventoryman. What’s that like?”

“I’m not sure you’d like my job all that much, ma’am—”

“Please, call me Barb,” the older woman interrupted warmly.

Klar removed the lid from the stew, and the fragrance of spiced meat, onions, potatoes, and much more filled the room. It was enough to make Adabelle perk up, and Trevor’s mouth water. If it tasted even half as good as it smelled, then this might have been the best meal they’d have on the trip so far.

“Barb, then. Thank you,” he corrected with a smile once he got his bearings. “My job requires me to travel a lot, so I’m not sure how interested you are in that.”

“Nonsense,” she said, waving a hand. Klar served her first before going around the table. “Just because I don’t want to go doesn’t mean I don’t want to know. Klarry here is always bringing back new and wondrous things. Like those Fire Roses! Those were beautiful, and a bouquet kept me warm for nearly a week. That came from Tosa, right?”

“That’s right,” Adabelle answered. “Back home, we have what are called Firesap trees. They explode if you don’t strike them with the right kind of axe, but its wood is useful in a lot of magical goods. Heating Stones use their logs, as well as the Fire Roses. I’m glad to hear you liked them, since the artisan who created them wanted to fill the world with flowers, but he’s also enamored with flames so the two intersected in a rather unusual way.”

“A noble goal if I’ve ever heard one. The world could use more flowers, especially up here in the almost frozen north,” Barb said with an approving nod.

Lora smiled as Klar served her before finally filling his own bowl. “The rest is grab as you go,” he informed everyone. “But, if you want my advice, put some bread in the stew and let it soak it up. That’s my favorite.”

There was a lull in the conversation as everyone filled their plates. Water and mulled wine was provided in carafes. Trevor took Klar’s advice with the bread, letting it sit inside the stew. He had already planned on it anyway.

Taking his first bite, the taste of a perfectly concocted stew with a strong, but not overpowering, flavor filled his mouth, and he couldn’t help but moan appreciatively. The courier was very glad that they had been sought out for this.

“So, inventoryman,” Barb started, having been polite enough to wait until he had swallowed. “That sounds like you might travel as much as my son does.”

“First off, this is amazing,” he praised. “It’s contending with the stuff from Mateo’s, and that guy has a Power revolving around cooking.”

“Glad to hear it, dear,” she replied, clearly happy at the compliment.

“And I don’t travel nearly as much as Klar, but I do get around,” Trevor chuckled. He raised his hand and pulled a cup out of thin air, then he sent it back. “I have the Power of Inventory, so there’s an extra-dimensional space attached to me. It’s been a great tool for traveling since I don’t need to worry about keeping track of things, and I can carry a lot more than any special bag made so far.”

“No wonder Klarry here likes you,” the older minotaur laughed. “You must be very good for business.”

“He is, but I befriended him before I learned what his Power was,” Klar said, smiling fondly as he thought back to that day. “Trevor needed help, and I obliged. I figured fate would have us crossing paths again if it were meant to be, and that’s exactly what happened.”

“That’s my boy. Always making friends,” Barb praised, reaching over to pat her son’s elbow before turning to Adabelle. “That must be lonely for you, though. I’m always complaining that Klar’s gone for far too long, though that’s changed a lot since he hired Asalorana.”

“I aim to please,” the teleporter said, hiding her slightly full mouth behind a piece of bread.

“You’ve been a blessing, dear. But, Lady Adabelle, it can’t be easy.”

“It’s not always easy, no. I have my duties to keep me occupied, and that takes up a lot of my time, but I certainly miss Trevor while he’s gone,” the demonkin replied, smiling as she looked at her husband. “It makes him happy, though, to travel and help people. I had gone through that during my adventuring phase, and I wouldn’t deny him the chance to see the world. We have ways to keep in contact now, at least, and I aim to make Tosa a place worth coming home to in his absence.”

“It already is, love,” Trevor replied, reaching out to brush a stray strand of hair behind her ear, which twitched at his touch. “Our home could be out in the middle of nowhere, barely better than a hole in the ground, and it would be worth coming back to as long as you were there to welcome me.”

“I don’t think I would like that, so I’m glad we still have Tosa,” Adabelle said with a light laugh.

“Fair. Definitely fair. Home is wherever you are, is what I was trying to say.”

“I understand you, son of Ander.”

As they smiled at each other, Barb leaned over and nudged her son. “When are you going to have something like that?” she whispered entirely too loudly.

Klar sighed, half exasperated and half wistful. “Someday, ma. Someday.”
