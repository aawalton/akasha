Following their surprise encounter with an ancient druid seemingly older than some parts of the forest itself, the team was a bit quieter as they carried onward. A hundred and forty miles wasn’t exactly a journey they could make quickly on foot, and now that they knew where the lich roughly was, they were even more hesitant to try flying. They managed to push another twenty miles into the forest before it got too dark to keep going and Asher decided to Recall them back to the palace.

“Thank the realms,” Moxy grumbled, yanking what they’d started to call fishbowls off of her head and tossing it off to the side. The magically reinforced glass was more than capable of being dropped onto a hard surface, but Asher still winced as it hit the ground and bounced a few times before rolling to a stop. “The monster fighting is fun, but hiking around in that thing is getting old, fast. Do we really need to keep it on?”

“We’re probably fine without them until we hit the heart of the forest,” Rulfar admitted as he took his off and took a deep breath in through his nose, sighing as he gained the ability to smell again. “Normally I’d say it’s a needless risk, but I do miss being able to tell when things are coming for me. Asher, what do you think?”

“It’s way too dangerous,” Asher said as he shook his head. “It’s not like we’re going to see a disease coming. They’re microscopic, and most likely drift far past the edge of wherever this heart of the forest is. Sure, we might be safe a hundred miles out, but twenty? Fifty? There’s no telling where the danger starts. Tell them, Kroult.”

“Asher is correct,” the lich nodded. “I witnessed many minor plagues over the decades I spent wandering between villages. Sickness is a terrible way to die.”

“Yeah, but you’re not wearing a fishbowl on your head all day,” Moxy drawled. “Whatever, I’m going to bed. Come snag me when it’s time to kill more monsters.”

Rulfar and Kroult left with her, leaving Asher alone with Samantha. The hunter had barely spoken after the encounter with the old druid, and even now she just stood there, staring at the wooden bracelet on her wrist as she fidgeted with it.

“Penny for your thoughts,” Asher offered, causing her to jump. “Though I suppose it would be a shard for them in this case. Kinda funny you guys don’t have anything like coins here.”

Curiously, Asher actually had found a few metal coins in some random pouch in one of the storerooms back in Zygrul’s old wizard tower, and even now had them tucked deep within his rift somewhere. He didn’t have any idea where they were supposed to belong to, as every nation he’d been to thus far all seemed to utilize shards for currency.

“I don’t even know what to think,” Samantha admitted, tearing Asher from his musings. “This feels like the start to one of those old books I used to love reading and talking to my mom about. I mean, a girl just happens to discover some old master holed up in a forest who offers to teach her what he knows? It doesn’t sound real.”

“The world is full of crazy coincidences,” Asher shrugged. “It’s not like we stumbled blindly onto his cabin. It was your evolved Natural Guide skill that brought us to his building. If you hadn’t had a Forest element, we never would have found him. It seems sort of fitting, in a way.”

“I guess…” Samantha said, flopping down onto the edge of their bed. “It’s just… my life is already so crazy. My fiancé is known across the continent as a greater power, which is something I didn’t even know existed until a few weeks ago. My younger brother is being trained in the truthseeker academy of all places, in order to prevent the Head of the Assassin’s Guild from getting their hands on him. I have my own sending stone with the High Prince’s rune connected to it, and I’m part of a team going around hunting down millennia-old liches who have been running wild and wreaking havoc for far too long. Only a couple of months ago I was just some girl in a small town on the edge of the kingdom. I don’t know why this latest development is hitting me so heavily, and it’s not like I even want this to be the case. But it’s almost like this is a tipping point that will prevent me from ever truly going back to the way things were, and I don’t understand why!”

“I think it’s because this is the first thing that’s not only extraordinary, but entirely yours,” Asher pointed out as he sat beside her and wrapped an arm around her. “I mean, obviously I think you're incredible. But think about everything you just said. You mentioned that I’m a greater power. How your brother is with the truthseekers. That you’re part of a team that is hunting down the Council of Death. Yes, you’ve got your own sending stone, but Donvath said it himself that was mainly in case I got myself killed and you needed to call him for help. None of those things are entirely about you. But Sedrick’s offer is. That old, hidden master in the forest took one look at you and wanted to make you his pupil. He doesn’t care about me, or the liches, or anything else going on.”

“Yeah. I think you’re right,” Samantha muttered, leaning her head on his shoulder as she sighed. “…How do you put up with this sort of thing happening to you all the time? I haven’t even formally agreed to anything yet and I’m already terrified I’m going to mess whatever this is up!”

“I’ve found the secret is to just roll with it,” Asher said, grinning as he practically felt Samantha roll her eyes. “I’m serious! I mean, exactly how many other people with Forest elements do you think Sedrick meets on a yearly basis? He’s not going to toss you away and look for someone else just because you make a mistake or two. Once you realize that you don’t need to be perfect, this kind of thing becomes a hell of a lot easier to digest.”

“I’ve seen what happens when you’re not perfect though,” Samantha whispered. “The rest of us don’t have an infinite number of lives. I’ve watched you die right in front of me, Asher, I’ve seen what happens when you make a mistake.”

“In my defense, those are the really bad mistakes,” Asher argued. “And you’ve got Winged Savior! You also get one free ‘get out of death free card’ you can play if things go really wrong!”

“That’s true,” Samantha said, leaning into his hug as she thought. “…Do you think I should do it? Learn whatever it is Sedrick wants to teach me?”

“I mean, obviously, you do you, but I think it’s a no brainer,” Asher admitted. “I could practically feel the power radiating off him, and anyone capable of surviving all on their own out in that deadly forest for however many years old he is has to be strong. Not to mention you want to be closer to your dad. I’d say meet with Sedrick at least once, and then decide from there what you want to do. Worst-case scenario, we tell him to get lost, he turns out to be some crazy stalker, and then we deal with it together. We hunt liches and demons for a living, we’ll figure it out.”

“It worries me sometimes how good you are at being the voice of reason,” Samantha admitted, grinning at him before giving him a quick kiss. “If you’re this good at thinking things through, how is it you keep finding yourself in such precarious situations?”

“That’s easy. I only bother thinking reasonably for other people,” Asher teased. “You don’t need reason when not even death can stop you.”

“As much as I do love you, part of me is glad Brian is only influenced by you once a week,” Samantha giggled. “Realms knows how he’d end up turning out if it were any more.”

“Speaking of Brian…” Asher said, not missing the way Samantha winced as though she’d been waiting for him to bring this up for days. “Was there a reason you didn’t tell him about us getting engaged? I wanted to let you be the one to break the news, but our whole day with him came and went, and you never brought it up.”

“I know,” she sighed, taking his hand and giving it a gentle squeeze as she kissed his fingers. “I didn’t mean to keep it a secret or anything like that, the timing just didn’t feel right. I guess… I’m a little afraid that the news might change the way Brian sees you. It’s no secret that I sort of flip back and forth between being his older sister and his adoptive mother. It’s hard not to after having raised him since he was only four years old. At the moment, it feels like Brian sees you as an older brother. I think I’m a little worried that the news that we’re engaged might force him to start thinking of you a bit more like a father figure as well, and I don’t know how well he’s going to take it. He’s never had someone to fill that role in his life. Not someone that he can remember.”

“That’s perfectly fair, and something I never would have thought about in a million years,” Asher admitted, suddenly feeling a sense of pressure as he realized she might be onto something. As much as he loved Brian and wanted to help make sure the energetic twelve-year-old had the best life possible, Asher had never once viewed him in that light. It was a lot of weight to put on someone’s shoulders out of nowhere, and the realization only made him love Samantha more. She’d stepped up to raise her little brother when she was only sixteen, refusing the offers from her neighbors to take Brian off her hands for her. He’d been the only family she’d had left, and she’d done everything she could to give him a proper upbringing. “I know this therapist of yours is probably already beyond busy with you, Donvath, and even Kroult under her case load, but maybe it’s worth bringing this up to her in one of your sessions?”

“That’s probably a good idea,” Samantha smiled. “Still, my hesitation to tell Brian isn’t very fair to you. Don’t get the wrong idea, I’m beyond excited for the two of us to actually get married. You’re amazing, and I want to spend the rest of our potentially very long lives with one another. I just want to make sure Brian is as excited about the news as I was.”

“I’m in no rush! Something I’ve been trying to work on, honestly. From what I’ve gathered, that’s sort of important for an immortal to understand. That the passage of time isn’t really all that big of a deal.”

“Save all that philosophy stuff for after the two of us have lived our first century together, it doesn’t suit you just yet,” Samantha giggled. “Not to mention I have a feeling I’m about to get quite a lot of that kind of talk from a Sedrick before long. Do you think he’s going to make me sit under a tree and meditate until I can hear the bark singing to me?”

“What?” Asher snorted, looking at Samantha like she was crazy. “Where did that come from?”

“That’s the classic training scene in practically every story!” Samantha argued, pushing him playfully. “Don’t look at me like that! Maybe it’s different on your world, but that’s how it is here! It’s a cliché!”

“Maybe it’s like the idea of meditating under a waterfall on my world,” Asher muttered. This time, it was Samantha’s turn to laugh as she shook her head.

“That sounds awful! Who would want to meditate with soaking wet clothes and all that noise?!”

“I don’t know, that’s why it’s so impressive I guess!” Asher argued, feeling strangely defensive of Earth’s culture despite the fact he agreed it sounded like a terrible time. Samantha kept laughing and he couldn’t come up with any other defenses for the admittedly silly training idea. Instead, he grabbed her, causing her to squeal as he threw them back onto the bed.

“Admit waterfall meditation is cooler than tree meditation!” he shouted, sputtering as he was hit in the face out of nowhere with a carefully aimed stream of water.

“Trees are better, waterfalls suck!” Samantha shouted back, laughing all the harder as he used Distortion to slip past her defenses and tickle her.

Despite each of their best efforts, the decision of which cliché training method was better ended in a draw as the two of them quickly shifted from play fighting to an entirely different kind of wrestling. While there were liches to be slain and apparently ancient masters to appease, for at least a little while, Asher was able to take the weight of the world off his shoulders and focus on the here and now.

The world would still be there in the morning.
