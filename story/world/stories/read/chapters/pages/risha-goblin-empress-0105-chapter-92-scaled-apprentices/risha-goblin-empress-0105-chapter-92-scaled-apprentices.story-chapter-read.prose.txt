Risha followed the Scaled out of the Council hall, warriors closing the door behind them. Risha saw movement in the shadows, cloaked guardians following her as they made their way through the streets of the city.

They stopped outside the same building that Risha had met the Dragon in last time. The Scaled knocked on the door, and it was opened to reveal one of the larger versions of the Scaled.

The Scaled behind the door bowed his head to Risha, then stepped aside, allowing her to enter the room.

The one who’d led her here didn’t move as Risha stepped forward. The door was closed as she stepped through the doorway, letting the taller Scaled lead her through the rooms.

They stopped in front of yet another door, and the tall Scaled knocked softly.

“Come in.” Elderoa’s voice came softly through the door.

The tall Scaled motioned for Risha to enter, then left.

Risha pushed the door open, revealing Elderoa sitting at a desk, scrolls and letters arrayed before her.

The Dragon stood as Risha entered the room, her eyes narrowing, “I wasn’t expecting you.”

Risha smiled as she walked into the room and looked around. Crystals of all different kinds filled shelves, creating a room of sparkles that nearly matched Risha’s own collection of shinies. The documents on the desk were filled with letters Risha only vaguely recognized, but she did know the numbers that marked several of the documents.

It looked all too similar to the finances Sun-Chaser was attempting to teach Risha, or the ledgers kept by the human assistant, Elizabeth.

Risha wasn’t the type of goblin who could read finances at a glance, so she merely looked curiously before turning back to the Dragon, “I had some time. Have you chosen the ten?”

Elderoa frowned, and looked back at her documents for a moment. “Yes, I can call them here.”

Risha smiled, “Good.”

Elderoa walked to the door, opened it and spoke with the tall Scaled for a moment before returning to the room. “I didn’t realize you’d be back so soon. It might take some time to gather them.”

“Risha can wait.”

Elderoa’s eyes narrowed at the goblin, then she gave an exasperated sigh. “What are you looking for? To choose a Scaled, that is.”

Risha hummed, and tilted her head as she thought about the answer. “I don’t know. A strong spirit, maybe.”

“Spirit is what you use instead of Mana, right?” Elderoa sat back down at her desk, and watched the goblin with clear curiosity.

Risha shrugged, then held up her hand, gathering her spirit to her fingers, creating a very fine purple mist. “Yes and no. My own spirit can affect the world, and power runes, but it doesn’t create the spells that Elder Oltak controls. Spirit is better used as a… fuel, for Spirits like Nyx and Igni.”

Elderoa frowned, “What do you mean? I’ve seen the shadows you control.”

Nyx.

A yawn answered her, then the small black cat jumped from her body, landing softly on the floor. The cat had a half-moon on her forehead, and a sea of stars shining across her coat. Nyx looked up at the Dragon, huffed, then curled up on the floor to continue her nap.

Risha kneeled down, and gently pet the resting Spirit. “Nyx is one of my Spirits. I feed her my power, and she allows me to control her… shadows.”

The stars and the darkness were Nyx’s true domain, although Risha was still unsure on what the cat meant by that.

“You said you’re looking for a strong spirit.” Elderoa’s voice brought Risha’s attention back to the sitting woman, “Will you bond them to you like you have Nyx?”

Risha gave her a look of distaste, “No. A monster’s spirit is already bound to their physical bodies, to take that would be… horrible. I’d have to kill them.”

Elderoa tapped her fingers against her desk, “Does that mean Nyx was once alive?”

Risha looked at the cat, wondering the same thing.

Nyx seemed to sense the attention as she opened one eye.

“In a sense.” The cat spoke to Risha’s mind, “I was once bound to the physical world, but not in the way you think. When my realm ceased to exist, I lost my connection to the physical, and was thrust into the spirit realm. And no, not all living creatures turn into Spirits when they die. It takes strength to continue existence after physical death.”

“Does she speak to you?” Elderoa asked.

Risha looked away from the cat, and nodded, “She said that she was once alive, but not in the way you think.”

“What do you mean?”

Risha looked to the Spirit, but she’d gone back to sleep.

“She wasn’t clear.” Risha shrugged.

A knock on the door interrupted any more conversation. The tall Scaled opened the door and bowed, “They’re here.”

Elderoa nodded, dismissing the Scaled warrior, then stood and motioned for Risha to follow her.

Nyx sat up, stretched, then jumped back into Risha’s body, disappearing from the physical world.

Risha closed her eyes, sensing the Spirit within her, before opening them to follow the Dragon.

They were led out of the house into a small courtyard, hidden by tall walls of stone. Ten Scaled of all different sizes and shapes rose to look at the two of them.

Risha looked over them, surprised to see colored scales other than White. Orange-gold, Bronze and Red.

“I thought I’d be choosing one from your tribe.” Risha said, looking at the Dragon.

“My siblings have their own smaller tribes. It would be wrong of me to deny them the opportunity you have granted us.” Elderoa lifted her chin, then with a sharp command, the ten scaled gathered into a line.

Risha looked over the Scaled of all different ages and genders.

They watched her right back.

Risha shifted her gaze, looking past the physical world to examine the strength of their spirits.

They all held strong spirits, and Risha could tell that some thought had been put into who would be chosen. Still, there were some that had chosen paths that would not let them use their spirits in the way she needed. She decided that seven of them would not be right for her. She pointed at the ones she wished to keep, then told Elderoa to dismiss the rest.

Risha studied their spirits, trying to decide what she was looking for, but nothing particularly stood out her.

She blinked, and focused back on the physical.

Two of the three, a boy and a girl, were both young, somewhere closer to Risha’s age. The boy’s scales were Golden-Orange, and the girl’s Red, and they had long, thick, serpentine tails that were nearly as tall as they were. The third was an older woman who had clearly seen battle, one of her eyes blinded from a long scar across her face. Her scales were a beautiful whitish blue, closer to Elderoa’s own scales than most of the other white-scaled followers Risha had seen. Her tail swished back with nervousness as Risha stared at her.

She stared at the three for a long time, then let out a sigh. “I’ll take all three.”

Risha entered the dark stone room, and the many gathered warriors stood.

Immediately, Risha’s gaze moved to the back where an older orc stood next to Klaz’zks. Scars from his many battles marked his red skin, and where there had been a stump of an arm, he now had a pale red hand resting on his sword.

Klu smiled, and stepped forward. The other warriors in the room moved out of his way respectfully as he approached.

He stopped in front of her, and kneeled on the ground. “This child greets Mother Risha.”

Risha lifted her chin, trying to hide the emotions that wanted to consume her. “It is good to see you again Klu.” She lowered herself, and placed her hand on his cheek, “Please stand with me.”

The orc obeyed, standing tall before her.

Risha turned to the others, looking over the caravan that had been chosen to accompany her.

Klaz’zks stood across from two hunting spiders who pulled a pair of wagons. Mist-Prowler the Coyote, and Wind-Whisperer the Jackal stood with several members of their pack. Undergoblins and Orcs filled out the ranks, and two demons Risha vaguely knew stood ready besides a demon woman with her hood lowered to show two broken horns.

Risha smiled, then turned to the door she’d just entered through, “You three can join us.”

The three scaled she’d just chosen walked into the room, their long tails swishing behind them in nervousness as they joined the gathered monsters.

“These three will be joining us. Klu, can you look after them?” Risha looked at the orc beside her.

He nodded, his gaze moving over them appraisingly.

Risha smiled, then walked over to one of the wagons. She sat down and motioned for the demoness to join her.

Siyah, Freera’s inheritor, sat across from Risha, and the two other Demons walked alongside the caravan.

The group left the room as one, the two large spider pulling the wagons through the purposefully empty halls of the undercity.

They made their way to a pair of heavily reinforced stone doors, the very same tunnel that Risha had first met the Dark Elf Merchant and the Shade Mother Cala from.

Undergoblins hurried to open the doors, allowing them to pass through into the dark tunnels beyond.

Glowing stones were held high by the warriors on the edge of the group, illuminating the darkness and showing an ancient stonework path. Many of the monsters had excellent nightvision, but complete darkness was still beyond most of them.

Risha watched Siyah under the lowlight, her eyes glowing ever so slightly as they pierced the shadows.

“You look scared.” Risha said.

Siyah paused, focusing on the Grand Mother. “I am. As children, we were constantly warned about traveling through the darkness. I’ve heard more terrifying tales than you can imagine.”

Risha nodded, her gaze moving to the edge of the light, “I’ve heard a little from the other Mothers. The city isn’t too far, and merchants walk the path regularly.”

“I know. Plus, you’re here. I wouldn’t want to tempt fate against you.”

Risha smiled wide, “Exactly! Risha is a terrifying Goblin. Did you know she’s killed a Spark?”

Siyah laughed, then hid behind her hand. “I’ve heard.”

Risha hummed, looking at another path that led away from the ancient road as the wagon rolled past it. “I wonder what treasures lie in the depths.”

“You should ask a Delver.”

Risha perked up, “Delver?”

Siyah smiled as she explained the explorers that risked their lives to explore the depths of the labyrinth-like underground.
