Despite the bumpy start, Rambunctious C handled like a dream. Trevor grinned as he drove down the empty highway, which was three lanes wide. The garage had been right at the base of the mountains and they had gone straight into a tunnel leading deeper into the range.

Getting back in the habit of driving was easy despite having only ridden animals, wagons, and his familiar for the past two years. Trevor was up to cruising speed in no time, not quite pushing Rambunctious C to the limit but getting into a comfortable groove as he got to know the vehicle. It was a work in progress.

The course was surprisingly varied. There were cliffside curves complete with railings, straight tunnels that led through some of the mountains directly, plenty of hills that took them up and down, and bridges to link it all together. Sometimes they would drive behind a rocky peak and lose sight of the sun. It was starting to get late in the evening.

Trevor relished in the feeling of power and speed. He was fast now, sure, but he wasn’t going to outrun Rambunctious C with his Speed stat of 5, even if he received extra from some of his skills and the Horn of Swift Heroics. There was another benefit of it, though.

Speed not only dictated how fast a Summoned was, but also how quickly they could process their surroundings. He used this to great effect with his Dexterity, hitting the curves a little faster every time and only slowing down when it was necessary. There was no over-correcting or other mistakes; he simply drove.

Glancing at his wife, he could tell that she was enjoying herself. Adabelle had a big smile on her face as she watched the scenery go by. Since she had worn a dress for the concert, she had gone with a jacket, blouse, and shorts for the ride. Her long white hair flowed with the wind, and her eyes sparkled brightly. He was glad to see she was enjoying this.

Trevor took one of his hands off of the wheel and placed it on Adabelle’s thigh where it was bare. She jerked at the unexpected sensation, her ears twitching, and looked down at where he was touching her.

“Honey.”

“Yeah?”

“Matilsa said you needed to keep both hands on the wheel,” she chastised, though she made no effort to remove his hand.

“She did,” he chuckled, nodding as he turned back to the road. They entered a long, well-lit tunnel. “But it’s okay. I’ve got my bearings again.”

“And that means you can drive one-handed?” Adabelle challenged.

“Oh yeah,” he replied without hesitation. “This was the norm. One arm on the armrest, or changing the radio, or out of the window—”

“Another point of safety that we were told not to do,” she interrupted.

Trevor took on a serious look, hiding his playfulness. “Are you going to snitch on me, lady?”

“On my own husband?” Adabelle asked in return, holding a hand up to her mouth as if scandalized by the question. “Of course not. I know better than that, son of Ander, just like I trust you to know what you’re doing in a car.”

They locked gazes, and the courier arched an eyebrow at his wife. She tried to be unperturbed at the fact that he wasn’t watching the road, acting haughty even now, though a moment of doubt crossed her features.

Trevor, however, was paying attention to where he was going. While he wasn’t looking, he knew they were on a straight away until the tunnel ended. His hand was steady and thus so was the wheel.

Adabelle looked ahead first, her eyes shifting to the walls. They weren’t getting any closer, and she smiled. “Alright, I get it,” she said, putting her hand on top of his. “You’re an excellent driver, and you know what you’re doing. I’m sorry for doubting you.”

“Nah, it’s cool,” he said with a laugh, shifting to be more comfortable in his seat as he returned his eyes to the road. The tunnel ended and the road immediately transitioned into a bridge over a deep gorge. “I’m just playing with you. I wouldn’t have done any of that if I didn’t think I could handle it.”

“I know, Trevor,” she said with a smile. The demonkin shifted, too, looking down the mountain as they finished crossing the bridge. “This is a lot faster than I expected us to be going.”

“I could slow down if you want.”

“No, I like it,” she replied, squeezing his hand. “I know that these were very common vehicles in your world, but it’s hard to imagine that there were so many that you developed your cities around them. We’re already going so much faster than a wracor, and we’re up there with speeds that Rashie or I could hit at a run, but everyone on Earth could go this fast.”

“Everyone who could afford it,” Trevor confirmed as he rubbed her leg. “But, yeah. These were common sights, even if there were a ton of different manufacturers and models out there. Mine wasn’t nearly as cool as this. Kind of blocky, actually, which was a popular style long before I was born.”

“And it still worked?” Adabelle asked curiously. The courier gave her a glance, and she explained. “Whenever Wayne started talking about his truck, he’d always complain that it was constantly breaking down or needing something fixed. It was old, he said, and it always sounded like it was such a hassle. To the point where I thought he would be better off with a good animal and a wagon, if I’m being honest.”

“I took care of it well and didn’t drive it much unless I had to,” he replied with a shrug. “Most of the time I could walk to work and that helped out a lot. Some brands broke down more often, but others were reliable. Mine was a tiny little thing I got from my grandmother and it didn’t have a lot of miles on it to begin with, so I was fairly well off.”

“That’s good, then. Would I be able to cuddle with you, then? Or would that be too distracting?”

“Oh, by all means,” Trevor invited. “Get on over here.”

Smiling, Adabelle lifted her armrest and wrapped her arms around his, allowing her husband’s hand to stay in her lap. She leaned against him, and he could see her eyes tracking the road. It only took a few seconds before she seemed content in the knowledge that she wasn’t actually hindering his ability to drive. The demonkin rested her cheek on his shoulder, careful of her horns.

She froze for a moment, and he snuck a glance to see her eyes moving back and forth as if reading.

“Something up?” Trevor asked.

Adabelle pouted. “Yes, but it’s not fair.”

That made him curious. “Did the Quest Master do something?”

“Technically, yes. It gave out a quest, but not to me,” she answered. When he gave her a confused look, she patted his arm. “This is highly unorthodox, but I received a quest for you.”

“What?”

“The Quest Master says that you really shouldn’t be texting and driving, so it sent it to me to read to you, which I’m not going to do,” Adabelle said before sticking her tongue out childishly at the text box in front of her.

“Okay, that’s fair,” Trevor chuckled. “But who’s trying to give me a quest? There’s no one out here but you.”

“Matilsa, apparently,” the noblewoman answered. “And it’s less for you, and more for Clay.”

Frowning, the courier checked on his bonded familiar. It was a little stretched at this distance, but he could still make out their feelings rather easily. To his surprise, they were cautiously curious. There was no anxiety about their missing master, which was normal since Clay was usually independent, and there was no distress.

“I’m guessing she’s trying to tempt my familiar,” the courier surmised.

“And succeeding, apparently,” Adabelle confirmed. Her eyes scanned the space in front of her. “I’m not getting a lot of information, but you’re getting a quest because Clay is the one who can… rebuild an engine block while imbuing it with magic? It calls out my ring, so I can see what the Quest Master means. It’s going to you because they can’t get them, and I’ve got it because you’re doing something dangerous and shouldn’t read while driving. This is a new one for me. Should I not be holding on to you?”

“You should be, yes,” he answered quickly. “Texting and driving is bad, but you’re not hindering me in the slightest. As for Clay, they feel fine so I don’t think we need to turn around to save them or anything. I trust Lora at the very least.”

“She has been rather on top of things,” the noblewoman agreed before turning her head towards the sun, which had gotten low enough that the sky was beginning to change color. “It doesn’t seem like we’ll be making it in time.”

“Are you enjoying it so far?”

“I am,” she answered with a soft nod. “There’s a lot going on. The vibrations, the wind, the sound, but it’s not bad. I like how fast we’re going, and I like that you’re driving.”

Trevor grinned. “Good. We might be moving pretty quick, but I’ve been taking it easy on you. Ready to go faster, lady?”

“We can go even faster?”

He pointed at the speedometer. It showed that they were only going around eighty, though he wasn’t exactly sure which measurements they were using. All he knew was that they were going fast, but it was also only half of what it said Rambunctious C was capable of.

“Trevor, I only have the vaguest idea of what that means,” she informed him, tapping his arm.

“It means hold on tight,” he translated as he pushed the pedal down.

Rambunctious C’s engine roared as they gained speed into another tunnel. This one had a gentle curve, and he continued to accelerate as they turned. His grin didn’t falter as he felt the air tousle his hair. Adabelle squeezed his arm a little tighter, but then she cheered into the wind. He did the same.

From there, Trevor barely slowed down unless he absolutely had to. He was racing against the sun, aiming to get to their destination before it could disappear. There was no telling how hard Rambunctious C had been pushed in the past, but he hadn’t been told to take it easy so he wasn’t holding back.

If Adabelle had been merely enjoying it before, she was thrilled now. A big smile pulled at her lips and her eyes were glowing bright. Even if they didn’t make it, Trevor was content with the look on her face. There was still the drive back, so he knew he would be seeing it again. Even though it was going to be dark, the road was well lit.

It took them almost ten minutes before Trevor saw the sign that the viewing area was ahead. He started to slow down as they turned the corner, and he found the entrance up ahead. Rambunctious C started to coast as they approached. The parking lot was surrounded by wooden fences, and he stopped in the middle where he could easily back out and get on the road.

“Okay, that was fun,” Adabelle said as she unbuckled her seatbelt and got out to stretch.

“Just don’t tell me you can run faster than that,” Trevor joked as he did the same.

“No, not after you sped up,” she replied with a smile before walking towards the fence.

The sun was almost behind the mountains ahead, but it still illuminated enough of the world below that Trevor could see. Right on the other side of the wooden barrier was a steep cliff of brown and red rock. He perked up as he looked; it reminded him of all the pictures he had seen of the Grand Canyon. The long, sprawling gorges and deep cliffs meant that it almost seemed as though he was on Earth again.

Then again, he hadn’t been there so he wasn’t sure. He looked around at the mountains surrounding them. Aside from the highway and this parking lot, it was almost completely natural. There had been very few signs of mining, habitation, or anything else. His eyes came across a raised platform that he had taken for another rocky cliff, but there were stairs leading to the top.

“Come on,” Trevor said, taking Adabelle by the hand.

She tore her eyes away from the sight and allowed herself to be dragged away. Once she, too, saw the stairs, she offered no more resistance and they climbed together.

There was a viewing platform with a few chairs at the top. They were made out of metal, appearing as though they were meant to withstand the weather, and Trevor immediately discounted them. He summoned a more comfortable seat from his inventory. He gave his wife a questioning glance, and she gestured towards the chair.

Smiling, Trevor sat down and Adabelle joined him on his lap. He reclined, her back laying against his chest, and retrieved a pillow for her to rest her head against. Her hand went in his, and they watched in quiet contemplation as the sun finally fell beyond the mountains in front of them.

They didn’t move, though. Slowly, the pink sky morphed into purple, and that soon changed to darkness. The sky was absolutely clear as a cool breeze passed over them. A cold front was coming in, which was perfect for the country-spanning Grand Prix of airships, and it gave them a marvelous view of the sky.

This far out, there was no light pollution. The stars were as majestic as they had been back in the Wildlands, sharing the sky with a waxing moon. Together, they bathed the world in dim light. Trevor rubbed his thumb against the back of Adabelle’s hand. Her eyes might have been a different color, but they twinkled just as brilliantly.

As if sensing his gaze, the noblewoman shifted so that she was on her side. Setting a hand on his chest, Adabelle leaned towards him, her lips brushing against his. Trevor reciprocated, and they focused entirely on each other without a worry in the world.
