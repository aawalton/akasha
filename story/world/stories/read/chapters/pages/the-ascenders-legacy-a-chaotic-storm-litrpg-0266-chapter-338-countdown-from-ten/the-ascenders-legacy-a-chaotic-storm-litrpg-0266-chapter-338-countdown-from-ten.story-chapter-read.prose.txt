Far, far away from the Storm Sect, in what should have been a paradise of perfect stillness, the Serenity Sect brewed with the sounds and turmoil of war preparation.

The disciples of Serenity were no strangers to such sounds; this was their third war this year, after all. Yet the sounds were louder this time—heavier, wearier.

The heavens truly were against them.

No one said it. Many never even dared think it. But that quiet truth permeated their minds and bodies like a disease, seeding fear and unease into their hearts.

The Origin Plane of Serenity was shrinking. And so were their numbers.

With all the wars they'd fought this year, their population was nearly halved. Yet their enemies kept growing. Expanding—forcing them to constantly betray their very essence of peace to survive.

They were weary of war. Yet they couldn't stop. Stopping meant eradication—a slow degradation of the Origin Plane of Serenity until it and all that wielded it in Gordon were completely wiped out.

The future was well and truly bleak. And with only a day left before the war commenced, any hope that the Storm Sect had been cowed by their numbers and alliances was all but gone.

It was obvious what decision the Storm Sect had made despite all their threats at the Parley.

It was not an outcome anyone from the Serenity Sect would have chosen, but weary or not, balance had to be restored. And in the depths of their grandest temple, the First Seat echoed the same sentiment, her gaze hard as she stared at her High-Elders.

"If the Storm Sect wants war, then we shall oblige them," she stated firmly, her words barely disturbing the flow of air current around her.

"I had hoped it wouldn't come to this." She continued. "War has taken its toll on our people and our resources. But eradication will take all that we have left. We must restore the balance."

Calamity Kronos nodded in somber agreement. "I'm sorry, First Seat. After the parley four days ago, I was certain they were cowed. They looked cowed, especially after Elder Malachi from the Blood Sect spoke of our alliance."

Elder Haorin nodded. "I watched the memory crystal Calamity Kronos brought back. If we had pushed then, they might have given in. To fight against the Sect of Blood is to court disaster."

"And yet they obviously intend to do so," the First Seat whispered, her voice traveling seamlessly into every ear. "They must have found confidence in something. A way to counter the Sect of Blood, perhaps."

A ripple of unease spread through the hall at that, and Calamity Kronos whispered, "If that is true, then whatever they have is formidable indeed."

"Not to mention that their Origin Plane hasn't stopped expanding yet," Elder Haorin added uneasily. "If anything, it only seems to have picked up the pace."

Another ripple of unease spread through the hall, a profound silence settling over them until the First Seat broke it. "Whatever they have, we still have numbers on our side. We've allied with over a dozen sects. We have the Blood and Reality sects by our side. We cannot afford to despair yet."

She smiled down at her elders, but rather than smile back, they turned their gazes away.

"We are sorry, First Seat," Calamity Kronos spoke quietly. "We tried our best, but the Reality Sect has pulled away from the alliance, citing their already small numbers as reason to avoid the war."

A beat of silence.

Elder Haorin grimaced. "The Sect of Silence too has withdrawn their support. And so has the Sect of Order. They will not be fighting with us."

The First Seat stared blankly at her for a moment, disappointment bubbling beneath her features before sighing. "We can't afford to despair yet. We still have a day. Reach out to every sect you think might ally with us. Make sure they know this isn't a favor. If we let the Storm Sect go unchecked, they'll eventually eradicate us all."

Calamity Kronos bowed. "As you say, First Seat."

The First Seat nodded, her expression tight with concern as she glanced at the other elders. "This expansion troubles my soul. It is unnatural. A single transmigrant shouldn't have this much effect on the plane. No transmigrant should. There is something else afoot."

Silence reigned for a moment, and then an elder suggested, "Perhaps they gained another Calamity. Calamities also contribute to the expansion of the Origin Planes."

"No." The First Seat shook her head. "This is more than that. They are gaining too much power too quickly, and we cannot afford to sit idly by." Her gaze hardened, and she commanded, "Notify our allies. We attack by noon tomorrow. This unhindered rise must come to an immediate end."

The elders nodded and began dispersing. But even as they did, they couldn't help but wonder what favor the Storm Sect had gained from the heavens to expand their Origin Plane so. Just what was happening within those tempest-soaked mountains of theirs to make them so confident?

That worry stayed with them for the rest of the day, but the Sect of Serenity weren't the only ones wondering this. The expansion of the Storm Origin Plane was beyond natural, and even their allies sought to understand it.

Deep within the Tidelands of Gordon, where the Water Sect was located, their elders speculated the same thing. They were seated in a massive underwater chamber, all ninety-seven of them, hair and robes flowing around them with soft, ethereal movements.

The First Seat, Mei Lanyue, sat atop a massive throne of semi-condensed water at their center, her runic irises gleaming like a pair of small suns among the stars of the other elders.

"This is unprecedented," she spoke, the surrounding waters stilling as her voice carried without resistance. "It has been a week since the expansion of the Storm Origin Plane began. It has become… concerning."

The elders glanced at each other, but none spoke until she asked, "How is this possible?"

"We cannot understand it either, First Seat." Elder Flowing Fist bowed, his azure blue hair blending so seamlessly with the waters he almost seemed bald. With a subtle head shake, he continued, "The Storm Sect has been very secretive about the driving force of this expansion. Even Elder Renhai has had no luck finding out what it was."

"I had little luck," Elder Qiao Renhai corrected with a side-eye at the azure-haired elder. "Not no luck. I have simply been unable to find anything concrete."

Qiao Renhai, popularly known as the master of secrets, was one of the most powerful elders within the Water Sect. Cultivating the path of flowing silence, Renhai was a master at extracting the secrets people wanted hidden.

It was the sole reason he'd been chosen as their agent to the Storm Sect. He was supposed to find out the reason for this unnatural expansion. But the High Elders of the Storm Sect had been like clamps of tempered steel. What little that came out of their lips had been perfectly tailored; their lips sealed like meridians under suppression.

Still, just like he had said, it hadn't been a completely useless expedition. He had learned that they were planning something, creating something that could potentially give them an edge in the war if it worked.

Unfortunately, however much he detested Elder Flowing Fist, the truth was that he had no real secrets to expose about the expanding Origin Plane. He hadn't sensed the ascension of any Calamities, had heard no whispers of artifacts, nor sensed any major sources of energy.

Yet there was no denying the unnatural expansion.

After the parley four days ago, the High Elders of the Storm Sect had been shaken. What sect wouldn't be after making an enemy of the Sect of Blood? But none of that fear had been obvious in the letter they'd sent the day before.

Something had changed, and it was in their best interest to find out.

Looking up, he spoke. "Perhaps the expeditionary force we've sent to them might be able to gather more information. They should arrive at the Storm Sect within the hour."

"Good." First Seat Mei Lanyue spoke from atop her throne, and the surrounding waters thickened, pressure mounting as she leaned back into her throne. "Have them report whatever they find to us before the war begins tomorrow. It is folly for us to ride a storm whose origin we know not. Those who do are usually the first to be drowned in its depths."

The elders nodded in agreement and soon moved on to discussing other matters of the sect, awaiting the response of the disciples they'd sent to the Storm Sect.

Soon, they would get their answer.

And perhaps, they too would be able to replicate it.

***

Back in the Storm Sect, thunder rumbled in agitation as several portals opened within the recently erected war camp, and the disciples from their allied sects arrived, each group dressed in the colors of their sect.

The Shadow Sect was dressed in black, the Water Sect in blue, the Wind Sect in green, and the Lightning Sect in gold.

There were thousands of disciples from each sect, yet their number paled in comparison to that of the Water and Wind sects.

While the other sects numbered in the thousands, and hundreds in the case of the Lightning Sect, the Water and Wind sects numbered in the high tens of thousands, each disciple radiating a power at the elite level.

"At least they gave us some good warriors," Elder Ghaltris noted from his position atop a metal platform, and his subordinate nodded.

"Just as we suspected, the Lightning Sect sent us the fewest number of disciples. We really should be doing more to help our sister sect, Elder Ghaltris."

Elder Ghaltris nodded. "I'm sure the High Elders are working on it, Elder Vasilis. I won't be surprised if they gave their leader a pendant."

He frowned. "About that, I have to report this to the High Elders. They'll want to know that our allies have arrived. Can you have their leaders meet me and the other war elders in the command tent in half an hour?"

Elder Vasilis nodded. "I will gather them immediately."

Elder Ghaltris sighed. "And get me their exact numbers, Vasilis. Every life lent must be returned—breathing or not." He glanced down at the arriving disciples and grimaced. "Hopefully, this time, we'll have more breathing than not."

He tore a lightning portal into existence and vanished into it, arriving before the elder hall an instant later. Knocking loudly, he pushed the massive doors open and bowed.

"Our allies have arrived, High Elders."

"Rise, Ghaltris. " The First Seat spoke, his voice like distant thunder. "Thank you for the timely notification. Are their soldiers any good?"

"Yes, First Seat." Elder Ghaltris raised his head, noting the multitude of elders within the hall. There had to be over a dozen of them, each with their own news or request.

War times were always busy.

His gaze fell on Elder Gumi a heartbeat later, and he instinctively straightened. "Elder Gumi, good evening."

The aged elder turned in his direction, nodded politely, and simply dismissed him. But Ghaltris didn't have the time to feel bad because High Elder Zarxan spoke.

"A faster brief would be much appreciated, Elder Ghaltris."

Ghaltris snapped back to attention and cleared his throat awkwardly. "Apologies, High Elders. Elder Vasilis is currently gathering their leaders for a briefing in the command tent. I've tasked him with getting the exact numbers of disciples each sect sent to avoid any disputes in the aftermath."

"Good." The First Seat nodded, then fixed his gaze on Ghaltris. "I'm hoping I don't need to remind you of the importance of discretion, Ghaltris."

"No, First Seat. I understand it perfectly."

The First Seat smiled. "Then you'll understand when I ask you to leave and shut the door behind yourself, Ghaltris."

Ghaltris paused, confused for a moment before his gaze strayed to Elder Gumi once more. He took in the array of elders standing around the hall—all powerful, all without familiars—and promptly bowed.

"I understand, First Seat." He glanced at the other elders and smiled. "I wish you all the storm's luck."

He walked out of the hall immediately and shut it behind him. If he was right, then the Storm Sect was about to gain a thousand familiars.

He had no idea how Elder Gumi had managed to create so many pendants in such a short amount of time, but that didn't matter. What mattered was that they were about to gain a huge amount of power, and the elders had trusted him to be discreet about it.

Like all things the High-Elders did, this was most certainly a test, and Ghaltris was determined not to disappoint them.

***

Back within the hall, tension hung tautly in the air as Elder Gumi stepped forward and bowed, her aged bones seeming to creak with each movement.

"Rise, Gumi. " The First Seat spoke, a wide smile on his face. "I must say I'm very impressed by the speed at which you've created these new interfaces."

Elder Gumi smiled. "I could have been faster had the restrictions on discretion not been so severe."

The First Seat nodded. "I'm sure you're right. Unfortunately, those 'restrictions,' as you call them, were necessary. We had allies and enemies sniffing down our throats." He smiled then. "But with the war only hours away now, I'd say the need for secrecy has passed. Tell us what you have, Gumi."

Elder Gumi nodded. "In the last thirty hours, my apprentices and I have worked tirelessly to create as many interfaces as we could. Coupled with the five hundred we had before, we now have just shy of one thousand and eight hundred interfaces."

"Wow." High-Elder Mystral smiled. "You never cease to amaze, Elder Gumi. "

"No, she doesn't." The First Seat smiled, then his gaze slid to the hundreds of elders gathered within the hall—elders who had been selected to be given the interfaces—and his smile turned somber.

"We wish you all good luck as the pendants are passed around. It is our hope that at least half of you awaken your familiar before dawn tomorrow. If you have any questions or confusions concerning the pendants, I'm sure Elder Gumi and Elder Vasmos would be happy to explain the intricacies to you."

The elders bowed in gratitude as Elder Gumi's apprentices began sharing the pendants, and a few minutes later, over nine hundred powerful Mythics clutched gleaming pendants to their necks.

The First Seat dismissed them half an hour later, and after the last elder vanished, leaving only Elder Gumi, her apprentices, and the High Elders within the hall, Elder Gumi spoke.

"I take it you intend to reserve at least a hundred pendants for the specials and prominent core disciples."

"That's the plan." The First Seat nodded. "Doing so will leave us with a bit over seven hundred."

"Do you intend to store them in the armory?" Elder Gumi asked, and the First Seat smiled.

"No, Gumi. I intend to do something much more drastic." He exchanged a smile with High-Elder Mystral and continued. "Our enemies may have gathered in their numbers, but if there's one thing the sects value over balance, it's power."

Thunder rumbled distantly, and a faint chill swept into the hall as the First Seat's smile widened. "Tell me, Gumi. How do you think our enemies would react if we offered them a few pendants?"

Elder Gumi, ever unflappable, simply shook her head. "It won't work. The pendants are coded to the storm."

"Oh, but they won't know that now, will they?"

Elder Gumi glanced down at the pendant in her palms, and then, after a long moment, she looked up and smiled.

"No. They wouldn't know."
