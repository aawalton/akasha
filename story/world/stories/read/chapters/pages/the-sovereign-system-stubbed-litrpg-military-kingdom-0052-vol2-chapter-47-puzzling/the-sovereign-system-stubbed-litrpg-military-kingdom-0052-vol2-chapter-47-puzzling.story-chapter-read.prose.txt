Luke moved slowly through the brush, following Tristan—the one who had scouted the camp earlier. A cold breeze cut through his cloak, seeping into his bones and making him long for the warmth of a fire back at the Royal Academy.

Gods, what I’d give for a hot meal with everyone right now…It wasn’t exactly home, but it was much better than being on campaign. At least the chances of death were far lower…

Not gone. Just less likely.

After this is over… I’m making a pizza, he mused.Despite the world resembling something out of Ancient China, the ingredients for a decent Italian pie were surprisingly easy to find—flatbread, tomato, herbs, and cheese. The rest was just creativity.

“It’s coming up,” Tristan murmured, pointing through a gap in the thicket.

Luke shook off the thought, bringing his attention back to the present. He wasn’t truly distracted. Not anymore. Not since obtaining the Dragon’s Heart skill. His mind was sharp—unflinchingly so.

His objective was clear: identify the newcomers and map out the camp.

Visibility was poor tonight. The new moon offered no light, and a blanket of thick clouds smothered what little starlight remained. But where others would stumble in the dark, Luke thrived.

He activated Eagle Eye.

The world sharpened.

They stopped a hundred feet from the nearest campfire, positioning themselves behind a towering fir tree. The fire’s glow danced across the faces of three sentries holding spears. Their eyes swept the darkness—half-alert, half-bored.

One of them yawned loudly, plopped down next to the fire, and began poking at the embers with a stick.

Luke narrowed his gaze, scrutinizing every detail of the guards. Their scale armor was standard for the era, but oddly devoid of insignia or banners. Nothing to mark allegiance.

His eyes moved beyond them, noting the layout of the camp. Dozens of tents—some made of hide, others canvas—clustered in uneven rings like fungi in a clearing.

“What do you see?” Tristan whispered.

“Not sure yet,” Luke replied.

Closing his eyes, he projected his consciousness upward.The Eagle Eye activated again—this time in full.

His vision rose above the treeline, taking on a bird’s-eye view of the camp. Tree canopies made visibility difficult, their thick branches obscuring large swaths of terrain. The dim conditions didn’t help either.

Still, he scanned.

“There,” Luke muttered, locking onto movement. “I see some wagons on the north side.”

“Supplies?”

“Probably. Can’t say for certain. They’re grouped together. Five of them. Horses tethered nearby.”

He shifted his focus to the northern lookout. A fire burned there too. Three guards again. But further off—two men slumped near the flames. Asleep, it seemed.

Luke almost looked away… until he noticed something odd.

One of the sleeping figures was massive. Far too broad to be an ordinary foot soldier. From the looks of it, his hands were bound behind his back.

His pulse quickened.

He zoomed in through the treetops.

And then he saw the face.

No. It can’t be…

His breath caught. Just as he opened his mouth to speak, a hand clamped down on his shoulder.

His entire body froze.

Luke’s consciousness returned to his body just as he noticed Tristan stiffen beside him. He followed his friend’s gaze and spotted one of the southern sentries walking straight toward them.

A jolt of alarm shot through his chest, but it didn’t cloud his mind.He steadied his breathing, eyes tracking the soldier’s movements, reading posture, gait, and expression.

Not hostile. Not yet.

Sure enough, the man stopped about twenty feet away, grunted, and fumbled at his waistband. Without ceremony, he squatted.

Then came the sounds. And the smell.

Luke’s expression twisted in quiet agony.This motherfucker…

A damp splatter hit his cheek.

His face darkened.

Did he just—

A slow, bubbling rage welled up inside him. He clenched his fists, resisting the very real urge to snap a branch and drive it into the bastard’s back.

“Ah, fuck,” the soldier muttered, glancing at the sky. “Why’s my luck so shit?”

Almost on cue, rain began to fall—light at first, then heavier by the second. Cold, unrelenting drops cascaded from the heavens.

Luke was relieved to know that it was rain that had coincidentally fallen upon his cheek, not the explosive excrements of the enemy.

The soldier cursed again, scrambled to his feet, and hurried back toward the fire to warm himself.

Within moments, the drizzle escalated into a torrential downpour.

Luke turned to Tristan, leaning in close.

“We’ve got a problem,” he said, voice low and grim.He quickly recounted what he’d seen in the northern quadrant of the enemy camp.

Tristan’s brows shot up. Then his face hardened into a mask of seriousness.

For a long moment, he said nothing. The rain drummed down on the canopy above them, filling the silence with a steady, relentless rhythm.

Finally, Tristan exhaled. “If that’s true, then we can’t let these men live.”

Luke gave a solemn nod. There wasn’t much of a choice.

The rain might’ve masked their approach, perhaps even allowed them to slip north under cover—but leaving the enemy untouched posed too great a risk.

They had ten-thousand men. Their tracks would be obvious in this mud, and any half-competent general would dispatch scouts the moment suspicions arose.

Frankly, Luke was surprised they hadn’t stumbled into each other sooner.

“I’ve mapped the camp,” he said, rising to his feet. “Let’s head back. Looks like the heavens are on our side tonight.”

The storm would allow for movement—covert, swift, and untelegraphed. Still, there was no telling how long the weather would hold.

They returned to their own encampment, arriving to a sorry sight.Soaked men huddled under towering fir trees, miserable and shivering. Without tents, the rain had woken most of them. The cold had robbed them of comfort, and the hunger clawing at their bellies made it worse.

Cold. Tired. Hungry.The perfect storm for plummeting morale.

And Luke would have to lead them into battle.

He entered his makeshift command tent, rain dripping from his cloak, Tristan following close behind. Inside, Bridges and the other officers were already gathered, clustered around a half-collapsed table.

Luke’s eyes found Christian and the other lieutenant commanders.No time for pleasantries.

“I need each of you to gather two hundred strong men—we’re about to undertake a mission,” Luke began, his voice sharp and commanding. “Time is short, and our resources are limited. Move quickly. Any man who volunteers will be compensated upon our return to Ralis: five gold coins each, and ten more to their family if they perish.”

He gestured for them to move. “Go.”

The four newest officers exchanged uneasy glances, then hurried out into the storm.

As the flap of the tent closed behind them, Bridges turned, his brows furrowed. “General… I don’t think you have the authority to offer that. If we return and they don’t get paid—”

“Circumstances have changed, Commander,” Luke interrupted, raising his voice to be heard over the pounding rain hammering the canvas above.

He took a step forward. “The Second Prince has been captured. He’s in the enemy camp.”

“What!?”

Shock rippled through the tent like lightning, several officers exclaiming in disbelief.

“I thought you said the Second Prince was en route to Yunxi,” Bridges said, struggling to process the news. “There shouldn’t have been any forces between Xiandong and Yunxi. How could he be here—this deep in Lhair territory?”

“There’s no mistake,” Luke said grimly. “He’s here.”

Initially, he’d assumed Selis had simply taken advantage of Xiandong’s weakened state—fishing in troubled waters, striking after hearing it had fallen to the Marxx Kingdom. They had much to gain and little to lose.

But now…

Now the truth felt far more sinister.

The prince had been captured mere days after parting from them.

And the presence of Selis forces deep inside Lhair territory?

That changed everything.

Lhair and Selis had allied.

The siege of Xiandong hadn’t been a coincidence. It was planned. Coordinated.

The realization struck like a dagger.

Someone had orchestrated this—deliberately and masterfully. Not only had they maneuvered enemy forces deep into the kingdom, they’d baited Marxx into war with Qin at the same time.

And now, Luke thought, the trap is closing.

The traitor within the court.The siege of Xiandong.The alliance between Selis and Lhair.Each move a thread in the noose tightening around them.

East lay Qin.West—Lhair.And now Selis had emerged from the south.

They were surrounded.

How the fuck am I meant to compete with that?

His jaw clenched. A bitter sense of helplessness crept in, like poison in his veins.

Is this the system telling me to abandon the kingdom?

The storm outside howled, the tent trembling under the weight of wind and water. Inside, silence reigned.

Everyone was staring at him.

Waiting.

Luke blinked, pulling himself back from the precipice of doubt. He shoved the creeping despair aside.

One thing at a time.

They couldn’t think about the fall of the kingdom right now. Not while the prince was still alive. Not while there was a mission in front of them.

He straightened his shoulders.

“We have two objectives,” he said, his voice low but firm, barely cutting through the roar of rain. “Rescue the Second Prince—and eliminate the enemy.”

He turned to Tristan.

“You’ll lead the infiltration team from the north. Get in, retrieve Prince Lyon, and bring him back safely.”

“At the same time,” Luke continued, “two hundred men will attack from each cardinal direction. First, eliminate the sentries on the outskirts. Then move through the camp, kill silently, and leave no trace. We must be efficient and ruthless. If they manage to organize a response, we’ll lose too many men in the resulting fight.”

He turned to Tristan, placing a firm hand on his shoulder.

“Prioritize the prince’s safety. We need him alive.”

“Aye. Don’t worry,” Tristan said with a nod, flashing a wry grin. “I’ll knock him out if I have to.”

Luke blinked, stunned.“I’m not sure that will be necessary—”

But Tristan had already thrown up twin finger guns. “Bingo.”

The gesture. The word. It was so absurdly out of place that Luke nearly burst out laughing. At another time—one not drenched in blood and soaked by war—it might’ve been funny enough to drop him to the floor.

He managed to hold it in. Barely.

“Bridges,” Luke called, straightening. “Take two thousand men and surround the enemy camp, at least two hundred feet away. If everything goes to hell, I’ll sound the retreat. That’s when you strike. Use the terrain. Use the storm. When they give chase, we’ll turn the tables and ambush them.”

Bridges hesitated, frowning. “General… how can you be certain they’ll pursue? What if they dig in and turtle up?”

Luke let out a low, dark chuckle. “Don’t worry about that, Commander.”

“R-right…”

“What about the rest of our force?” Bridges asked. “We’ll still have over eight thousand men in reserve.”

Tristan glanced briefly at Luke, then answered before he could.

“Send them to encircle the forested region. No one gets out. If this works, we’re going to stir a hornet’s nest. Can’t risk a single scout escaping.”

“Yes, Deputy General.” Bridges saluted stiffly. He gestured to the other officers, and together they exited the tent.

The flap opened, letting in a cold gust of rain before falling shut again. Luke and Tristan were left alone.

Outside, the downpour drummed like war drums across the camp.

“I don’t like this, young Luke,” Tristan said, his voice uncharacteristically quiet. His face bore a rare unease. “None of this adds up. It’s like we’re playing chess… but the enemy’s playing go.”

Luke nodded grimly.

Ever since he’d arrived in this world, he’d been drowning in conspiracy.

The coup in Valand City was just the first piece—a calculated infiltration of Lhair into Marxx territory. Qin had been framed for the eastern assassinations, prompting King Julius to declare war and march the kingdom’s armies eastward… leaving the heartlands wide open.

If Luke hadn’t intervened, Clayton City would have fallen—and with it, the remainder of the kingdom’s northern provinces.

“I don’t like it either,” he admitted. “But we don’t have a choice.”

His gaze hardened.

“If the king finds out we let his son die—without exhausting every last option—we won’t need to worry about the state of the kingdom.”

The implication was chilling.

Tristan was silent for a long moment before nodding. “You’re right. Priorities first.”
