The northern district of Alcatraz appeared to be the main bureaucratic heart of the sleeping city.

Even at dawn, the streets below were bustling with activity. Workers streamed into towering office complexes wearing sharp clothes and carrying suitcases. Lights glowed from countless windows, despite the rising sun, and from above, it looked as though half the district had never gone to sleep.

Calamity Rezin snorted. "The sun has barely risen and they're already burying themselves beneath paperwork. Is this the life sleepers glorify?"

Amoz grimaced. "Not everyone can spend centuries chasing immortality, Rezin. Not everyone is so lucky."

"I would rather die," Selia muttered. Then she shook her head. "Though I suppose that sort of thinking is exactly what the people of Alcatraz are fighting against."

"It is," Cephas agreed, speaking up for the first time in a while. "And, on occasion, that conviction can be somewhat… admirable."

Selia smirked.

"I'm not sure I share your admiration, Cephas," she said, her gaze drifting to the streets below. "I will, however, admit that I do find their defiance somewhat… fascinating."

She frowned faintly. "Look at them go, completely unaware we're here. Unaware that any one of us could wipe out this entire district before they even understood what was happening. How are they content to go about their lives without awareness or protection despite the powers that be?"

"Do they have a choice?" Amoz asked, staring soberly at the sleepers beneath him. "You all forget that we were all once like them. I grew up in a city just like Alcatraz before my awakening." His gaze lingered on the streets below a moment too long before he looked away.

No one noticed.

"Me too," Cephas said with a chuckle. Then he glanced at the others, a teasing smile on his face. "Not everyone got to grow up in a shelter."

"You're right, Cephas." Rezin nodded. Then he shook his head. "It's been so long since I was unawakened that I can barely remember it now."

Calamity Cephas hummed in agreement, then his gaze shifted behind them. "I daresay not all of them are quite so unaware." A faint smile touched his lips. "Vaymoz's daughter—or should I say the princess—is quite perceptive for a human. Even now, miles behind us, she's fully aware we can hear every word she speaks."

The Calamities and Mythics chuckled, while Aodhán and Sophran glanced back instinctively.

They found nothing. Unlike the Mythics and Calamities, their spiritual perceptions weren't as finely tuned yet.

Still, Aodhán smiled.

According to Calamity Amoz, Yelena Alcatraz had left her father's palace not long after they had, and had been on their tail ever since. Or at least, swiftly making her way north.

It was strange just how brave she was. Even he, as powerful as he was, still held wariness toward Calamities.

And rightly so.

The mere fact that core sense couldn't perceive their cores was enough to remind him of the vast gulf that still separated them. Yet Yelena seemed entirely unfazed. Or perhaps simply too stubborn to care.

Drawing in a slow breath, Aodhán glanced at Crixis to ascertain that he was still doing alright before turning his attention to the district below.

Almost immediately, Calamity Amoz glanced at him. "Are you ready?"

Aodhán nodded, and with a shuddering breath, he opened core sense to the city below. His senses spread out immediately, engulfing the entire district like an invisible tide. Thousands of energy signatures erupted in his mind's eye, and a moment later, he whispered.

"Storm."

The world dimmed immediately, bright signatures winking out one after the other until finally only a few dozen were left. Aodhán filtered them further, dismissing several unlikely signatures until finally, only seven signatures remained.

Seven blinding signatures.

"Good," Amoz smiled. "We shall divide ourselves into groups."

He pointed at Selia. "You and Sophran take the first spot. Cephas, take the second. Rezin, the third." He glanced at Aodhán, his gaze thoughtful. "You and I will take the fourth."

Aodhán nodded in agreement, and Amoz turned his attention to the Mythics.

There were three energy signatures left and only five Mythics.

He grimaced. "I would rather not leave anyone isolated with potential enemies running around, but there are only five of you."

"I could go with them," Sophran spoke up. "Even out their numbers."

"No," Amoz shook his head. "It's too dangerous. You're too important to risk." He pointed toward three of the Mythics. "You three investigate the fifth spot while you two take the sixth."

The Mythics nodded, and Amoz sighed. "That leaves only one signature. If we don't find the legacy seed after searching the first six, then we shall all converge on the seventh. Do we understand?"

"We do."

"Good," Amoz smiled. "If anyone finds the seed, a pillar of lightning will be the signal. We'll converge on your location the moment we sense it."

Everyone spoke up in agreement once more, and a few minutes later, after Aodhán had pointed to the exact location of the seven signatures, they all left, leaving him, Crixis, and Calamity Amoz floating above the city.

Calamity Amoz snapped his fingers, creating a platform of condensed stormlight, and they all hopped onto it.

"Ready?" Amoz asked.

"We are," Aodhán replied, glancing at Crixis before turning his attention to the building below.

Just as they were about to move, however, Calamity Amoz suddenly froze, his runic irises flashing in alarm as his gaze snapped toward the horizon. His eyes widened, and an instant later, a colossal aura of power erupted from him, forcing Crixis violently to his knees.

"Calamity Amoz?" Aodhán asked, alarm creeping into his voice as he followed Amoz's gaze. "What is the problem?"

Amoz scowled.

"We've got company," he whispered, his voice as cold as ice. "Bad company."

Aodhán reached for Crixis immediately, pulling the underling behind himself to shield him. But that was all he was able to do before said company arrived.

One moment the horizon was empty. And the next—

Pain lanced through his very soul as pressure crashed down upon him like a mountain, crushing the air from his lungs. Aodhán collapsed to his knees immediately, the sheer force of the enemy's aura stripping the strength from his body.

Reality shuddered.

The platform beneath their feet exploded into countless fragments. And before he knew it, Aodhán found himself hurtling through the air like a meteor through space, the world spinning wildly around him.

All he saw of the enemy was a flash of violet irises.

Yelena Alcatraz stood at the prow of the airship, a deep frown etched across her face as she studied the magical compass.

It was swaying erratically.

Even as she watched, the needle jerked north, quivering slightly. Then it spun east, south, west, before snapping back north again. The soldier beside her hit the sensor firmly with a finger, and when it didn't stabilize, he grimaced.

"It seems to be broken, Mistress. Perhaps we should ask Captain Rohir if his ship is also misbehaving."

"Don't," Yelena replied immediately, her gaze lingering on the sensor for a moment longer before drifting to the soldier. She offered him a faint smile. "Don't bother, Vorik. I'm sure it's nothing."

Vorik nodded, and Yelena turned her gaze back to the horizon, a muscle ticking in her jaw.

Whatever reservations she had, she kept to herself. And for a long moment, silence reigned aboard the ship as she watched it slice through the clouds with remarkable speed.

Remarkable. Yet lacking when compared to flying platforms and spatial manipulation.

Her grimace deepened.

She glanced at the soldier on her right. "Can't this ship go any faster?"

"No, Mistress Yelena. This is its top speed."

Yelena glanced at the erratic compass once more, then sighed, her expression smoothing into the very image of patience and calm as she settled in to wait.

Her soldiers, however, were anything but calm.

"Mistress Yelena," one of them asked a moment later, her brows furrowing with uncertainty. "These cultivators... what do you think they're here for?"

"Nothing good," Yelena replied evenly, a faint scowl touching her lips. "Wherever cultivators go, destruction follows. You've seen the clips of their Balance Wars and the devastation they leave behind."

The soldiers shuddered and several of them raised their hands in prayer as though to ward off evil.

"We shouldn't have let them into the city," Vorik muttered, his scowl deepening. "Not after what happened fifteen years ago. Master Vaymoz should have turned them away."

Yelena shook her head, though she wasn't disagreeing in the least.

"None of that matters now," she sighed. "The cultivators are already inside. We simply have to make sure they don't start a battle over the city and begin destroying things."

Another shudder rippled through the soldiers, and one of them swallowed hard, working up the nerve to speak.

"But..." he began, stammering. "The accords—"

"Mean nothing when we can't enforce them ourselves," Yelena said, a trace of bitterness creeping into her voice as she turned to face the soldiers fully. "These same cultivators are the ones enforcing the laws for us against their fellow cultivators. Surely you can see how little that works in our favor?"

A tense silence settled over the ship. Even the whistling wind seemed to fall quiet, and Yelena turned once more to the horizon.

Had her father been here, he would have scolded her for frightening the soldiers. But Yelena had never seen the point of hiding the truth from people, no matter how bitter it was.

Their situation was dire. And no amount of bluffing could change the fact that even with the accords, they were still at the mercy of cultivators.

Yet standing firm despite knowing all that—that was what Yelena considered true defiance.

That was what she wanted her people to have. Not the feeble mask of courage they currently wore.

After a long silence, Vorik spoke. "How do we stop the cultivators from fighting over the city if they decide to?"

Yelena smiled. But before she could answer, a sudden explosion ripped through the air, so thunderous it shook the entire airship.

"Mistress Yelena!"

Vorik caught her before she could lose her footing, but she pushed his arms away, her gaze darting immediately to the sensor, which had finally stabilized and was now pointing almost vehemently north.

Magic.

"Mistress Yelena—" another soldier began, but she cut her off sharply. "I'm fine, Gertrude! Can this ship truly not go any faster?"

The sky darkened before anyone could respond, and the next instant, lightning crashed from the heavens—a beam so blindingly bright and immense that it made the rising sun seem dim by comparison.

It struck something—or perhaps someone far in the distance—and another explosion tore through the air, the deafening crack echoing like thunder.

This time, the airship barely avoided capsizing.

Yelena stumbled, her hands flailing wildly until Vorik grabbed her with one arm while wrapping the other around a rope. Around them, the other soldiers did the same.

Except one.

"They are fighting!" Gertrude screamed, her eyes wide as though she were witnessing the end of the world. And as the ship lurched, she slid across the deck and over the edge, plunging toward the city below with a piercing scream.

For a single horrified heartbeat, no one spoke.

No one moved.

And then—

"Mistress!" Vorik hissed, fear creeping into his voice. "We must turn back. We cannot afford to go any farther."

Any other person would have agreed. Any sensible human would have seen the logic in his words.

And a part of Yelena was ready to do exactly that. But leaving now meant fleeing. And Yelena was determined not to do that.

These cultivators had come to her city fifteen years ago, and had wreaked untold destruction. They had killed millions and claimed the life of her mother.

Leaving now meant leaving her people to a fate worse than death. And Yelena couldn't do that. She wouldn't do that.

Her lips tightened in hatred, and as the ship righted itself, Yelena shook her head.

"No."

That single word cut through the tension aboard the ship like a blade.

She met the gaze of each soldier in turn and scowled.

"No. I refuse to cower and hide while cultivators wreck this city. That is not defiance." Her eyes hardened. "And it most certainly isn't what my father would do."

The mention of her father made many of the soldiers cringe in shame, and in that moment, Yelena realized just how powerful a symbol he had become.

A beat of silence passed. Then, Vorik asked, the terror on his face having subsided considerably, "What... what would you have us do, Mistress Yelena?"

Yelena regarded him for a moment before shifting her gaze to the horizon, where the cultivators were now visible.

Her shoulders straightened. Her expression hardened. Then, in a tone that brooked no argument, she pointed toward the north.

"Take me to them."

The soldiers hesitated. Several even shook their heads in refusal.

But a moment later, Vorik inclined his head. "As you wish, Mistress Yelena. As long as you allow me to call your father."

Another wave of energy slammed into the ship, but this time Yelena didn't stumble. Instead, she glared at the clash of violet and black in the distance and sneered.

"Call him," Yelena replied firmly. "And tell him I am being defiant."

Aodhán groaned, feeling his bones creak painfully as he forcefully arrested his and Crixis's momentum to avoid crashing into a massive building.

He barely succeeded, his back stopping mere inches from the glass wall through which he could see the occupants—dozens of them frozen utterly in terror.

"Who the hell is that?" Crixis asked through gritted teeth, his expression twisted in pain.

"I have no idea," Aodhán whispered, his gaze straying to the blur of violet light now battling Calamity Amoz.

"I have no idea," he repeated, grimacing as the pressure bearing down on his soul tripled.

Even with the distance the blast had put between them, it felt as though his soul might shatter beneath the crushing weight of the enemy's aura.

He doubted Calamity Amoz could hold him off alone.

But one thing was certain.

He couldn't afford to remain here much longer. And neither could the humans gawking behind him.

"Leave," he growled at them, thunder rumbling in his voice. Thankfully, it worked.

His command snapped them from their stunned silence, and they scattered just in time to escape an arcing branch of black lightning.

The bolt vaporized the first, second, and third floors of the building before Aodhán's authority could seize it. Gritting his teeth, he forced the crackling ribbon of destruction into a platform construct and helped Crixis onto it.

They took off immediately, putting more distance between themselves and the battle.

But even as he did, he couldn't help noticing the dozens of terrified humans staring helplessly at the clash above. He wanted to save them—to teleport every last one to safety before another stray arc struck or another building was vaporized.

But he also knew they were racing against the clock.

If one enemy was here, then there were most likely others.

And if the enemy reached the seed first simply because he had stayed behind to save the citizens of Alcatraz...

"No," Aodhán shook his head. "It's not worth it."

Instead, he turned toward the glowing signature he and Calamity Amoz had been sent to investigate and shot toward it, shuddering with guilt as an entire building full of stunned humans was vaporized a heartbeat later.

He raised a hand, intending to cast a dome over the district, even knowing how futile it would be against Calamities.

Fortunately, someone far more qualified beat him to it.

A massive dome of glittering stormlight spread over the city before he could even activate Dominion Matrix, and an instant later, Calamity Cephas appeared before him.

"Go," the Calamity shouted. "Find your signature. Mine was a bust. I'll shield the city."

He wasted no breath.

And Aodhán didn't waste the opportunity.

With single-minded focus, he sped toward the blinding signature, which was located within a massive building called the Bureau of Census and Registration.

Yet even as he descended, Aodhán doubted Edrick Ghaltris would have hidden his legacy in a place like this.

Still, he had to check it out, if only to rule it out.

They arrived above the building a moment later, and Crixis growled, forced to shout over the deafening clash of the Calamities battling overhead.

"Go! I'll watch for the signal!"

Aodhán hesitated a moment, not wanting to leave Crixis here unprotected. But Crixis couldn't phase through walls or teleport like he could, and he didn't have time to start searching through this building.

His hesitation lasted a heartbeat longer before he finally nodded.

"Be careful. There could be more enemies around."

"I'll be." Crixis nodded, and without further hesitation, Aodhán descended into the building, phasing right through wood, concrete, metal, and glass with steely determination.

Sleepers screamed all around him, their expressions terrified as they scattered, running helter-skelter to avoid him despite not moving in their direction at all. With a shield now active, what they should have been doing was exiting the building and running toward safety.

Instead, they seemed more preoccupied with locking themselves in offices, despite seeing him phase through metal and concrete.

Aodhán didn't stop to help them. There was no time for that.

Rather, he kept descending until finally, he reached a wide room at the bottom of the building and set eyes on the pulsing signature within it.

It was a core the size of a football, pulsing with a myriad of colors. It floated several feet above the ground, and beneath it stretched a massive holographic map of Alcatraz rendered in exquisite detail.

The projection was entirely colorless save for sixteen distinct glows, all of which were scattered across the northern district. And despite never seeing anything like it before, Aodhán understood its purpose instantly.

His heart stuttered in his chest.

There were only thirteen of them from the Storm Sect.

Four Calamities. Five Mythics. Sophran and himself. Crixis and Tianyu.

Yet the map showed sixteen.

Who were these other three glows?

Thunder boomed once more, shaking the entire building to its foundations, but Aodhán barely noticed. He shot toward the projection immediately, his gaze sweeping across the three extra glows, searching for some indication of who they belonged to.

But there was none.

No names. No affiliations. Not even their affinities were obvious.

Just three hostile presences gathered around the district. Gathered around this building.

Crixis.

He teleported out of the building immediately, reappearing atop the roof in an instant to find that the clash of power in the sky had quintupled.

The other Calamities had joined Amoz to fight the enemy.

Or, better still, one of the enemies.

"Did you find the seed?" Crixis asked the moment he arrived, and Aodhán breathed a sigh of relief when he found him still alive.

"No, but if we are to find it, we have to move now. There are two other enemies searching for the seed and they are not far from us."

Crixis's eyes widened. "The seventh signature."

Aodhán climbed atop the platform in response and Crixis quickly rushed to join him. But just as they began to move, Aodhán sensed an unfamiliar Mythic core rushing toward them.

An illusion core.

An enemy.

His eyes widened, and he shot forward immediately, channeling willpower into the platform to escape the approaching core.

But it was too late.

The enemy arrived above them before they could cross the distance of a mile, reality warping erratically around her figure.

Aodhán reacted immediately, pushing Crixis behind himself even as he pulled on the quintessence of two seals at once. Heat surged within him as he met the woman's gaze, determination sparking in his eyes.

The woman, however, staggered the moment she saw him.

Her eyes widened in shock. Then hatred as brutal memories rose in her mind.

"You!" she scowled, her lips pulling into a sneer. "You were the one who killed my Lysandra, baring her flaws and insecurities for the world to see."

The reality crackling around her sharpened into a hundred blades, and with murder blazing in her eyes, she sent them hurtling toward him.

"Aodhán Brystion!" she spat, the words like poison on her lips. "You will pay for her blood."
