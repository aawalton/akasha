“I cannot believe how much I hate this plan!” Apollo screeched.

Ethan barely heard the man, he was lost to the process of forcing the seemingly bottomless energy reserves from his Spirit, into a Fable Train that was burning through it at what seemed like an impossible rate. What concentration he could spare was spent on staring at the giant monitor in front of him.

There wasn’t much to see, just the writhing insides of a Champion rank serpent-krakken.

The Fable Train, now surrounded by a swirling vortex of glass shards, was lodged a short distance into the beast’s throat. As hard as it was to believe–even for Ethan–that meant everything was going according to plan.

Ethan had realized that the only way to possibly equal the Followers in time, was to skip past all the nuance of his slow, steady approach to gathering the corruption of Potentia. Instead, he raced right to the end. A single champion had already proven capable of ascending The Throne, given the chance.

But taking one was nearly impossible. He had bet everything on that “nearly”.

The circumstances had to be perfect. The demon needed to be weakened–thus, the shark. It also needed to be enraged, and unfocused. That meant attacking while it was still reeling from damage it had taken. Finally, Ethan needed the time, and the energy to make one desperate grasp at controlling it.

The energy was solved with more rune circles than Ethan had ever seen, combined with his over-fed Spirit, and filtered through Steelbinder. The time had been trickier. Even with all their preparation, and Fable essentially being turned into a bunker, there was no way for a Twilight rank construct to withstand the full force of an enraged Champion rank demon.

Thus, the unconventional method of attack.

They were lodged within the creature, safe from its more typical attacks, causing it considerable distress, all while close enough to hit it with a full blast of Ethan’s amplified Animus.

The Fable Train groaned around them, the walls bending inward ever so slightly.

“It’s squeezing us,” Apollo said in a flat voice.

“I love this plan!” Zakiel said, eyes fixed on the monitor.

“I am afraid that Doctor Apollo is correct,” Fable said, her voice ringing out from all around them. “The serpent’s insides are contracting, likely utilizing the same muscles meant to force food downward. The defenses are doing what they can, but we have little time.”

Ethan didn’t need to be told, of course, he was connected to the train, and could feel it slowly buckling. Worse yet, it wasn’t just the muscles they had to deal with, he could sense smaller lifeforms rushing across the outer hull. They skittered, scratched, and bit, trying to carve their way inside.

Ethan’s doctor’s brain immediately supplies a word: antibodies.

It seemed mad, but everything about today was crazy, and some kind of internal defense mechanisms from a creature this powerful was far from a stretch. All it really meant was that they had even less time than they hoped.

It had to be now.

The serpent was injured, even if it wasn’t as badly as they might have hoped. And they were alive, and in position. Every moment that passed was just more of his Spirit burned away to protect them.

Ethan absently hoped that Allyara was doing better than they were, but then he pushed even that thought from his mind.

He turned everything to the painful process of forcing yet more of the accumulated power out of his Spirit, and into the complex weaving of Rune Circles carved all through the Fable Train.

This was all just an extension of the great workings that Ulric had prepared for him, meant to focus his Animus, and distill it to a single purpose: control.

It had been enough to gather a few hundred Twilight demons, now it would have to do so much more. The power gathered, and golden light lit up the interior of the train. In the monitor, Ethan saw the ghastly insides of the serpent lighting up as well, the runes glowing with ever-increasing intensity.

He felt his Spirit’s power finally beginning to wane, and knew that its resting form would be nearly back to normal size now. Months of gathered energy all turned to this single moment.

“Now,” he whispered, though the command was unnecessary. Fable felt his need as clearly as he did.

All over the train, the vortex of glass shifted, changing shape. Instead of countless sparkling flecks, they melded together into rigid blades. They erupted in all directions, piercing deeply into the serpent’s insides, and wedging the Fable Train even more tightly. It was as if the great demon had swallowed a porcupine whole, and every spine was now stretched out.

From the moment they made contact, Ethan felt himself become connected to the creature. He felt its outrage, its fury at this unexpected, and invasive attack. He felt that pain and anger grow as the glass blades dug further into its sensitive throat muscles.

Then it felt Ethan, and a great will turned its full attention on the insignificant speck that had dared put itself against the ancient demon.

And it was ancient. Ethan could feel that immediately. An immensity of experience, and patience. This demon had lived through ages, always fighting, always devouring, and growing ever stronger. Its mind felt even greater than its titanic size, and Ethan felt as if he were standing before a giant, staring eye. As if the entire sky above him was looking down, and judging him for his foolishness.

“It’s killing him!” a voice yelled from somewhere in the distance, but Ethan’s mind was locked on the terrible struggle within. “I’m healing him as quickly as I can, but he needs more than that! His body won’t get him through this, we need to keep the energy focused.”

That terrible will, that intense, all-seeing glare. It blocked out everything now, and Ethan knew he was losing. Somewhere far away, his body was torn apart, then rebuilt moments later. Mana was rushing through him, yet it was the thinnest trickle compared to what he channeled into the beast.

“Strategus, get off your miserable ass and help me! For God’s sake, man! For everyone’s sake!”

Ethan was losing. Inch by inch, his own meager will was being devoured. This creature had battled for survival every moment of its life, for centuries. Millennia, even. What was he next to that?

Strangely, it felt as if his body had ceased healing. It was just dying now, dying around him. And yet…there was something else. The energy flowing into the Champion was stabilizing somehow, becoming more controlled. It was like steering a ship in the storm, struggling against the wind and the waves, then feeling other hands grab the wheel. Strong hands.

His mind began to clear, and that terrible will was pushed back, just a sliver. Yet that sliver left just enough room for Ethan to remember who he was. What he was.

This demon had survived countless battles, but Ethan had survived gods. He’d survived Potentia, and even defeated the corruption that it had left in him.

He’d been marooned on a strange world, and survived the wrath of a grieving king, then defeated an organization that spanned the entire globe.

He was a Chosen. He was Chosen.

He was a Bonded. A Twilight warrior. A Dimensional Champion.

A doctor, with a patient.

And beyond all that, he was something else. He was a Bishop. This creature might not know what that meant, but it had been pounded into Ethan’s skull since he was a child. It was a tradition, a legacy of purpose, and focus.

It was said that every Bishop went to war eventually, and whatever this creature thought its purpose was, Ethan knew better. It was his weapon. And a Bishop wouldn’t march to war without one.

Ethan struck back.

He threw everything he had at the creature. Everything he was.

He felt his Familiars lend their wills to his, and for a fleeting moment he knew that their will was his. He kept pushing.

He felt his body dying, his mind being assaulted. He felt the shock and denial from the great serpent, and he felt its fear, a concept so foreign to it that its will began to retreat.

Ethan pounced on that moment of weakness and indecision. The Fable Train vibrated as a final pulse of energy pushed every drop of his Animus out into the howling demon. Ethan felt that power rushing through every molecule of the colossal creature, imprinting on it, overwriting it.

The process seemed to last a lifetime, but suddenly it was simply over.

Awareness nearly overwhelmed him as Ethan became an extension of the demon–or the demon an extension of him. Its body belonged to him. Its purpose as his. Its power was his.

Its corruption was his.

Ethan felt himself begin to shake, and the world around him abruptly came back into view. Surprisingly, Zakiel’s rune’d right hand was on him, and the Assassin smiled awkwardly, clearly doing his best to keep Ethan alive.

The other two dimensional twins were there as well, falling back from Ethan and looking exhausted and haggard.  Apollo leaned against a wall, then looked around suspiciously. Finally he spoke.

“Well, did it work?”

Ethan opened his mouth to speak, but the words that rushed from his lips weren’t those he meant to say.

“THE THRONE!”
