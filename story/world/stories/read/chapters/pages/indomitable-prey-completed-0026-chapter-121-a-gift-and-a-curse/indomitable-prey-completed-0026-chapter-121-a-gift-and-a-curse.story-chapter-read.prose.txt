[Lamb POV]

Varice stood before me, magic rippling around her in a purple glow. The peak of the 2nd tier—a mage that had spent decades growing and perfecting her skills. I was still blurry. My balance was poor, and my thoughts kept trying to wander right out of my head. Probably a concussion from the brief dalliance I’d had with the wall only moments prior.

Her leg was still injured, but she didn’t look too concerned about it with the way she stood; cocksure and bearing weight without issue. So I did what any sane person in my position would do.

I turned and ran.

Purple chains flailed at me as I reached the exit, and I dove aside with only inches to spare. I’d felt the bone-jarring force those fell links could impart once already and had no desire to test my skeleton’s fortitude against them any time soon. Something told me I’d end up as the more brittle in that competition. It didn’t bode well for me that Varice could seemingly summon those chains far beyond her reach. That meant she could cut off both exits to the room at the same time, while leaving herself free to flit around with her strange liminal space and send bolts flying my way whenever she wanted.

I had first entered this room cursing the hanging tapestries of whatever lost civilisation this display was dedicated to, but now I found myself praising them. They broke line of sight and were probably the only thing that kept me alive as I leapt and skidded around the upsettingly small room.

As I’d predicted, Varice coiled her phantom chains in both the entrance and exit and sought to trap and exhaust me between the two. But I had some mana returning now, and with the help of End of the Hunt, I felt some of my wits returning, too. I gave up on trying to hurt her directly—I didn’t have enough time to throw one of my weapons or close the distance before she would step back into the folds of reality and disappear from my senses. But without the ability to hurt her, I began to work on other goals.

Whenever she appeared in that tell-tale green glow, I used Axis-Shift to unbalance her. It wasn’t perfect—indeed, thanks to her greater level it had little of its intended effect—but it was enough. We weren’t fighting face to face, so I didn’t need to knock her over or foul her steps. I just needed to make her aim marginally worse. A fight was a game of inches, after all, and I was surviving because of those minor gains.

This was sustainable for me. The room was small, but I’d spent so long running about the mountains with Nathlan chasing one another that I was used to changing direction on a thought’s edge, twisting back upon myself; bouncing off walls and sliding under obstacles. As it went on and I gained familiarity with the pace of the battle, I even felt a grin begin to worm its way onto my face. Varice caught it, and judging by the look in her eyes in response, she wasn’t amused.

It was inappropriate, it’d give her that, but why shouldn’t I enjoy myself? Because it was dangerous? Because of the threat of imminent death were I to misstep? How was that any different than gallivanting around a knife-edge ridge? Stride The Edge—my 2nd tier movement skill—was literally designed for this, and when combined with short activations of Break-Step, I managed to keep just out of reach of Varice’s clutches.

She began to grow frustrated. She had played me well, feeding into the arrogance that I hadn’t noticed was there, but I started to think that perhaps she had fallen prey to the same vice immediately afterwards. Her leg wound hadn’t been as bad as I’d hoped, somehow. No doubt it hurt, but she seemed to be moving well in spite of it, and now that she was less restrained with her magic, she’d seemingly suspected an easy victory.

But I’d managed to stay just one step ahead so far. I heard a cawing off to my right and twirled into an acrobatic leap, letting a bolt fly past beneath me while at the same time twisting to avoid the open claws of the ghostly raven that swept in above. Both attacks missed, but I had no time to retaliate before ducking back into the swirling cloths that hung from the lines above.

It was strange to dip in and out of this small world of blood and bone. The white canvas, the red scrawl. Something about it called to me as I zipped between hanging sheets. I had no focus to spare on the tapestries, but I felt their pull all the same. A kinship, calling out to my soul. I wondered briefly if it might actually be a curse—the tapestries part of some ancient, forbidden book that had brought ruin and sin to the civilisation that had compiled it. Fanciful thinking surely, conjured by a mind giddy with adrenaline, relief, and panic.

Whatever the case, there was certainly no time to discuss the theory. Varice had talked to me in our last battle. I now knew that was an attempt—and a successful one, I had to admit—to draw information out of me. She needed none now, and so she fought me in silence save for the sawing of my frantic breath, the drumbeat of my frantic steps, and the cawing of her frantic raven as it circled above us.

As much as a part of me wanted to enjoy the experience, and another part of me wanted to punish her for putting me through this, I also knew that I wasn’t quite safe. I was hanging on by a thread, and if it came down to a contest of endurance, it wasn’t guaranteed that I’d win. I was making liberal use of my active skills—namely Break-Step and Axis-Shift—and if I wanted to outlast her, I needed to build up enough mana again to use my more intensive skills. I doubted there was a way to close the distance that didn’t put me in immense danger, and I’d need The Mountain’s Gate to shield me when that happened.

I dodged beneath another bolt, twirled aside from a brilliant purple orb that flew towards me with a screeching as it tore apart the very air itself, and then kicked off the small obsidian pillar that named the display. I caught another look as I leapt off it. ‘Language of a lost people; the keys to the past’. What did that mean? Not that it mattered, but a part of my mind lingered on it anyway: a mystery that felt strangely important, all things considered.

I considered my skills even as I continued to move in chaotic patterns. Varice’s attacks were getting closer each time, as if she was figuring out my patterns and predicting my movements. I needed to change things, add more randomness into the mix, but that required active thought, and I was running on pure instinct by this point. Shatter Point was no good—I could barely damage the walls at all, and I hadn’t so much as landed a hit on my opponent since she’d first entered the room. End of the Hunt wouldn’t intimidate or effect Varice much given her higher level, and it wasn’t exactly a mindset issue that was holding me back right now anyway.

Vanguard of the Lost was still guiding my steps, though in a passive capacity right now. Could I lean more into that? The steps of the spear dance I’d learned from the weapons skill were much more varied than most martial arts, relying on often unorthodox and surprising moves. As another crossbow bolt skimmed the edge of my nose, tearing a tiny furrow in the first layer of skin and causing a single drop of blood to bead up and fall rom the end of my nose, I decided that something had to change. Might not be the best idea, but I’d take what I could get.

I skidded to a halt, sliding beneath one of the tapestries with its jumble of strange, indecipherable patterns sewn into it, and sprung back to my feet, spear leading the way. I wasn’t fighting anyone—Varice was far out of range and spent most of her time in the liminal space she could seemingly summon at will, only peeking out to try and pin me down when the moment allowed for it—but that didn’t matter. This wasn’t about hitting anyone; this was about not getting hit. I just needed to outlast her, and this was a way to do it. Theoretically.

I let the Hasta’s ancient movements guide me, moving through one kata and then the next, twisting and pivoting and dancing in strange movements. It was an unpredictable form of chaos. The Hasta’s spear-dance was all about breaking rhythm rather than keeping it, so perhaps dance was the wrong term for it, but in any case, as my limbs began to sing with the strange rhythm of the war-form, I found Varice’s attacks to miss by more each time.

Inches became handfuls, heartbeats became seconds, and soon I was cocooned in the safety of blinding movement. Deep underground, I couldn’t see the night sky, but if I could, I suspected the moon would be full and bright. My hands and feet blurred with the backing of an entire people’s dreams, and the momentum started to build. From one form to the next, my speed increased as snatches of song and history floated through my mind.

I wasn’t sure if it was the danger, or the lunar cycle, or simply that enough time had passed and enough experience accumulated, but I felt the constellation in my soul that represented Vanguard of the Lost grow in complexity. I dismissed the system’s notification before it could distract me, but I couldn’t ignore the feeling as the skill began to siphon mana from my core. First a trickle, then more. It spun, blazing in the void and glowing ever brighter as it drunk deep of my energy. The phantom glow of Varice’s purple chains was soon joined by a soft white light emanating from my arms and legs. It whirled around my torso, ever-rising and burning off as it reached level with my heart, like I was in the midst of a whipping inferno of white flame.

My immediate goal had been met—Varice was struggling to keep up, her shots going wide and her eyes seemingly unable to track me among the flapping tapestries—but I couldn’t focus on that now. My mind was captured by an entirely different enigma. The tapestries were beginning to make sense.

Like a chthonic curse that had wormed its way into my mind, I found myself unable to look away as I danced through the exhibition. The crimson swirls and dots and shapes on the white fabrics began to run. Formally incomprehensible, they now tickled me with recognition. I’d catch a glimpse of one from an odd angle, then spin to catch another floating at eye level, and somehow the two patches of colour would complement one another. I kept dancing, ever moving while I fought phantom enemies and jabbed and spun and cut throughout the room, and in all of that chaos, understanding bloomed.

I relived the lives of an entire culture in snapshots; snippets of love, of hate, of war and peace. I felt their emotions, experienced their qualia, and through it all I evaded my own enemy by a hair’s breadth. Just as I drunk deep of their rich history—lost to all but a few now, millennia since they’d strode the earth—they learned of my struggle in the present, and lent me their strength and wisdom.

The Hasta were a nomadic people. What use did they have for books and scrolls? There was nowhere to store them, no great repository of knowledge to deposit and draw from. They moved with the seasons, cyclical and ever changing. There were migration patterns and structures that they built and worshipped, but the group that returned after several decades to the Ardenstone would be far different to the one that left it. Their lives and their world was more ephemeral than mine, and so they thought of knowledge as different, too.

They shared—oh, how they shared!—but to the Hasta, the world moved. It made no sense to sit still and study in station. To learn of the world, one had to move through it, and so to learn of a people, one had to do the same. How the Ashkanian’s had learned of this, I couldn’t say, but they’d understood the Hasta’s last repository of knowledge perfectly. The pieces were all here, but it made no sense to an outsider who thought of knowledge as something stationary. It wasn’t until I moved within the tapestries a certain way that I understood how they should be read.

As I followed the movements and steps of the Hasta’s lost spear-form, I saw the tapestries as the Hasta would have. As clothes; shirts, dresses, skirts and shawls. They would cavort and dance amongst one another, and their clothing would tell a story. It wasn’t just idle fancy, either. Men, women, children—people of all ages and ranks in society would spend their leisure time sewing and knitting and embroidering: altering their clothes to tell a story they wanted told in the evening around the fire.

As they danced and fought and fucked and lived… through all of that, the Hasta were sharing their knowledge with one another through dress.

This revelation flashed through my mind as I moved like them. Beneath the moonlight, beneath the earth, beneath the roof of night-dark glass I experienced it all. The system tried to intrude with another message that I ignored, even as Vanguard of the Lost stole more of my mana, sucking up the energy in an increasingly frantic and greedy swirl as it danced through the void of my soul-space.

The thousand thousand voices of the Hasta pounded through my head, their whispering and laughing and shouting all combining into the symphony of a people.

And then a single voice cut through it all.

I staggered. The breath left my lungs, and my spear clattered to the floor. My hand rose to my chest, feeling the knot of pain, the tenderness and the sudden ache that exploded where my heart should be.

A green glow faded as Varice’s portal appeared once more from across the room, but I paid it no mind. I hadn’t been shot. There was no physical injury. I hadn’t even been hit by a spell. But my heart ached.

Just a voice. A single sentence. ‘Me and you against the world.’

I knew that voice. I knew that sentiment. I knew who spoke it, and I knew why they did. I knew all of the context and the history wrapped up in that simple statement, and I knew the promise of it, too. I knew all of that, because I remembered.

It was gone as soon as it arrived, and the ache it left was palpable. I struggled for breath, desperate to get air back into my lungs as the memory faded. If I could only keep moving, keep the dance flowing and preserve that precious momentum, then I could reach that state of enlightenment once more.

Clearly, there was something about the skill that transcended the system’s purview. My memories were locked away, but Vanguard of the Lost was a key, and I’d just seen the door open a fraction.

But I wasn’t in a safe space to practice. This was a battle, and I’d just fallen on my metaphorical sword. Varice leaned against the doorway to her strange stone tower and watched as her ghostly chains secured me. I tried to move at the last minute, but I was too slow. Too dazed.

“What was that?” she asked simply. Her confusion was obvious, but it seemed that now she had me in her grip, she was happy to let curiosity get the best of her.

“I remembered,” I said.

“Why does that matter? What did you remember?”

I hung my head in despondence. The feelings hadn’t yet faded, even if the memories had. “I remembered what love feels like.”

Varice didn’t reply. When I finally looked up, I found a ball of purple energy heading my way, her hands held out as if she had pulled a great weight from the ground and cast it my way. The magic hit me, and I knew nothing but darkness.
