The world stank of molten gold and rotting dreams. I stumbled forward, boots crunching over broken coins and scorched stone, the air so thick with heat and metal it scraped at the back of my throat. My breath hitched, shallow and sharp. The hunger clawed at my insides, twisting tight, a ravenous beast snapping at my ribs, aching to escape.

The city unfolded before me, a ruin draped in gold where towers of bone and cracked marble rose into a sky the color of tarnished brass. Spires crowned with blindfolded statues held scales that dripped molten light onto the streets below, every inch of this place pulsing with a sickly glow, veins of gold running like rot through the ground. The Boon hummed inside me, a low, insistent thrum, whispering of power that was here – ripe, hoarded, and waiting.

I staggered into a wide street lined with hollow-eyed people bent double under the weight of heavy crates. They shuffled forward, slow and broken, their skin stretched tight over bones, their veins glowing faintly like candle wicks about to gutter out. A construct passed by—massive, armored in gold, its faceless head smooth and polished like a coin rubbed thin. It moved with a grinding certainty, its feet leaving scorched imprints in the stone.

I froze, heart slamming against my ribs, as it stopped before a man who stumbled under his burden. Don’t look. Don’t breathe. The man gasped, fell to his knees, the crate crashing to the ground in a puff of golden dust. The construct reached down, fingers closing around the man’s throat, and crushed. There was only a sharp, sickening crack and a burst of light that flared from the man’s chest—his soul?, sucked into a waiting urn like steam drawn into a vent. The golem set the urn on a passing cart, turned, and moved on, leaving the man’s body to slump into the dust.Around him, the others barely flinched. They shuffled past the body, eyes downcast, steps unbroken, as if this was just another toll they had learned to ignore. A child clung to a woman’s skirt, gaze hollow, mouth slack with hunger. The air smelled of scorched flesh and copper, each breath a struggle. A faint, metallic buzz filled the silence, the hum of the constructs’ cores vibrating beneath the streets. I could hear the crackle of heat, the clink of coins shifting in crates, the soft, pitiful whimpers of those too weak to keep moving.

My stomach twisted, bile rising in the back of my throat. Malice’s voice slithered through my mind, cool and sharp as broken glass: {Ah, the smell of old money. Nothing like it, is there?}Shut up. The thought hissed, brittle and raw. I’m not your puppet. My hands trembled. The hunger screamed, a high, thin wail in my ears, and the Boon throbbed, gnashing at my ribs, whispering one word over and over, sinking into my bones: Take.

I moved through the shadows, sticking close to the crumbling walls, my breath ragged and shallow. The hunger gnawed at me with every step, an ache so sharp it left my mouth dry and my hands shaking. The street pulsed with a sickly light, a heartbeat belonging to something else, rising from the ground in shimmering veins of gold.

The mortals trudged past, bent and broken, their eyes hollow as old coins, their bodies little more than skin stretched over bones. Every one of them carried something—crates, urns, bundles tied with twisted wire—and every step looked like it might be their last; they walked as if they understood they must continue, each breath demanding payment.

The constructs towered over them, hulking and plated in gold so bright it hurt to look at, their bodies etched with sharp, angular runes that pulsed in time with the vault’s glow. Their faces were blank, featureless ovals polished smooth, and they remained silent because their presence sufficed. I watched as one stopped beside a woman who had stumbled, her crate split open on the ground, a spill of golden dust catching in the air. She looked up, eyes wide, mouth trembling, hands raised in silent, empty pleading.

The construct tilted its head—slow, mechanical, the movement almost lazy—and reached down, its fingers wrapping around her neck, lifting her like a doll. The sphere at the end of its staff cracked open with a hiss, and a thread of light snapped from her chest. She gasped, her back arching as the essence was pulled free, ripped out of her like a vein torn from the skin. The light twisted, struggling, but the sphere drank it in, the glow curling tighter and tighter until only ash remained. The woman’s body sagged, gray and empty, her breath gone. The construct placed the sphere in an urn on a passing cart, then turned back to its patrol as if all was normal.

I pressed back against the wall, chest tight, the stone searing against my skin. They were everywhere. Watching. Hunting. Not with eyes, but with presence—a weight that pressed down, crushing, insistent. Every corner seemed to breathe with the rhythm of the vaults, the city a living thing stitched together by greed. I could feel it in my teeth, a low, grinding pulse. Somewhere, far above, the god watched, counting lives like coins in his purse. The thought made me sick, and angry.

The hunger snapped inside me. My breath caught, my hands curling into fists so tight they shook. Keep moving. Don’t stop. The thought was ragged, barely a whisper in my mind. Malice’s voice slid into my head, smooth as silk, sharp as a knife’s edge: {So efficient. Why waste effort when fear does the work for you?} The Boon surged in my chest, a roar beneath my ribs, the hunger rising, screaming. I knew this state of affairs had to end, and soon; action was inevitable for me.

I crept deeper into the city, drawn by the pulse of power beneath my feet. The hunger was a drumbeat in my chest, steady, relentless, each step feeding it, making it stronger. Then I saw it: the vaults yawned open like wounds carved into the earth, and from them spilled the army. Oh, God. My breath hitched, a sharp, sour knot in my throat. That’s too many.

Rows and rows of constructs, more than I could count, stood shoulder to shoulder in endless formation, unmoving, faceless. Each one gleamed like a statue cast from greed itself: spines stacked with coins, limbs bristling with blades, cores burning faintly with trapped essence. The heat from them shimmered in the air, a low, oppressive thrum that pressed against my skin. The hunger sharpened at the sight of them, a sharp, insistent ache that made my teeth clench and my hands shake. The Boon sang, a static crackle under my ribs, a song of ripe, hoarded power ready for the taking, desiring all of it.

I swallowed hard, chest tight. They stood still for now, waiting, frozen in place, a wall of gold and death ready to march at a single word. I could feel the tether, the invisible cord that connected them to something higher, something deeper. The flow of energy ran beneath the streets, through the vaults, up into the heart of the city—feeding the army, fueling the constructs, a system built to drain the world dry and keep the god fat and safe. He relied on others for conflict; his power flowed through them. The people broke and bled for him, the army killed in his name, while he just sat on his throne and counted.

Malice’s voice slithered into my head, cold and smug: {Ah… a true connoisseur of indulgence. Why waste a single drop of blood when others will spill it for you? Greed, darling. It’s the oldest sin.}My breath hitched. The hunger rose like bile in my throat, hot and bitter. I wanted to tear it all down. The vaults pulsed, the constructs stood silent, waiting, and the mortals shuffled on, hollow and exhausted, too broken to fight. I clenched my fists so tight my nails dug into my palms. This place felt like a machine, a factory built to grind souls into fuel.For a breath, the world shimmered. I saw the flow of power—veins of molten light threading beneath the stones, feeding the constructs, wrapping the mortals like barbed wire. I saw the vaults opening wider, the god’s hands sifting through piles of souls as if they were grains of sand, letting them slip through his fingers, laughing. My vision blurred, and in the haze, I saw a flash—me, tearing it all down, the Boon screaming as I fed it everything. The hunger snapped back into focus, clawing at my ribs. My mouth tasted of ash. My hands trembled, and I knew—this city would burn.

I whispered, barely audible, “You’d have me tear it all apart, wouldn’t you?”Malice’s voice slid in, purring, smug: {Oh, darling. You’re the one who’s starving. I’m just here for the show.}And I could feel it, deep in my bones—this situation would change with my intervention. I was going to burn it down.
