What was I thinking. No, really. Who do I think I am?

Sure Marten was a screw up. He couldn't really have believed that no one would notice his attempts to rework the curriculum to match that stance. Well maybe he thought he was protected, he didn't clean up after himself nearly as well as he probably thinks he did. I found enough that the right OverSeer could probably end his career aspirations, the wrong one would end mine twice as fast. Imagine what a real research specialist would find, if they were to be granted access.

But seriously, reworking an entire curriculum that has been treated as the ideal process since OverSeer Valene was an OverSeer at Scattered Creek? As a Senior Seer who barely meets the level expectations for the role? Madness.

When did it change from OverSeer to Senior Seer? Why? I get that there isn't a need for the advancement but making a position easier to be appointed to rather than harder, I'll look into that, I'm sure there's a record.

It's a good plan, more time dedicated to the actual teams, less to generic information half of them know and the other half might be able to find out from any half competent Seer. The few who don't have access to one can be handled individually.

But nineteen teams, while still teaching at least some existing lessons? Who am I trying to impress exactly?

Anya choked on air as she felt similar shock sweep through the band from the other three.

"She wrote what!?"

She didn't need the band to recognize the mixture of discomfort flashing across Fay's face. She hadn't had to tell them, it didn't change anything. And yet Fay had, she'd wanted them to know, to be part of it in some way.

"Briana insisted I see the letter," Alex said. "Or rather insisted Fay let me read the post scripts. There were implications in the rest of it that I'll try to dig into very carefully."

"No," Cara said. "Go back to what Seer Deirdre said about Briana."

"Cute," Gwen said. "You heard her the same as the rest of us."

"Please…" Fay said.

"Fay," Seb said softly. "You had to know this would happen if you told us."

"I hoped you would…"

Anya slipped around the table and put her arm around Fay, pulling her to her side and mock glaring at everyone else.

"Leave her be. She didn't have to tell us any of this. She could have left it out and we wouldn't have found out that her old friend, our new Knowledge Instructor, thinks her fiancee is cute."

"Not unless Briana told us," Seb said.

Not helping, she sent.

"Thank you," Fay said. "She's not wrong, Bri is extremely cute. But she didn't have to say it in what was otherwise a fairly formal correspondence with a Holding Seer."

"Formal?" Alex asked. "She went off on more tangents than Sandra does trying to distract us from our bacon."

"The point is," Fay said with her own glare. "Whatever she plans as a change to the curriculum will affect you all and she has limited access to the Fae. I don't know what you were taught, who taught you, or much more than what she likely found out."

"The specific details about human Holdings, especially about the Great Families, that Marten expected us to learn were new," Gwen said. "The more general information about the Widepond and Awakened individuals? Our tutors covered much of it at one point or another."

"We can ask you questions though?" Cara asked. "We are part of Holding Syl, you wouldn't expect us to-"

"Of course," Fay said. "I doubt I'll explain it half as well as she would have though."

"Fay…" Anya said, frowning.

"No, I know I'm not the best at explaining things."

"You explain things quite well," Seb said. "You might go off on tangents or focus on details that aren't immediately relevant, but when I've had questions? When Anya has?"

"It isn't the same, you're…"

"No," Seb said. "It isn't. You explain and answer well. That doesn't mean you'd be a good choice as a teacher to explain to a group, or to go over a specific curriculum in a specific order."

Anya felt Fay pull away slightly as she sat up straighter, almost seeming to visibly brighten as she smiled.

"Thank you. I'll let her know I can help with much of your future questions, as long as I know what you are trying to find out."

"I don't get it," Cara said.

Cara… Anya sent. Really?

I mean it, she answered. I'm not trying to tease her more.

That isn't what it feels like, Seb sent.

"What?" Fay asked.

"She's so structured and organized when she's taught lessons. Serious and focused."

"Boring?"

"No. Not at all. She's the opposite of Marten, her lessons are interesting. But she doesn't act the way you described her letter was."

"Cara," Fay said.

Anya hid a wince. She couldn't remember the last time she'd heard Fay use that tone. It wasn't the way she'd spoken to Marten, that had been nearly hostile. This was polite but distant, it sounded much more like Seer Phelann than her sister.

"Do you doubt Seers are able to show decorum and care in how they present themselves? That they treat the responsibility of educating other Awakened as something worthy of respect?"

"No?"

"Then why would you find it surprising that Deirdre displays no outward signs of a personality that you, as her pupils, have no need to see?"

Anya knew Alex was likely the only person who could see what she did, the clenched fingers under the table, the jittering leg, the painfully straight spine.

Cara, she sent. You need to answer.

I mean, she's not wrong? But…

Cara.

"That makes sense," Cara said. "She knew you as a peer, as a friend. She doesn't know us. Not really."

Anya saw Fay start to relax, the mask cracking as Cara continued.

"The way I used to fall back to saying Mistress Gwen around others. Refusing to let myself even consider emotions except in private so that no one could use my hair against me."

"And today it was redder than Gwen's when I said I liked your newest styling," Fay said lightly. "What did Anya call it? Sleep-deprived finger braids?"

We're going to get you, Gwen sent. You know exactly why we overslept, why she didn't have time to do anything but run her fingers-

Gwen! Cara sent. Are you trying to help prove her point?

Anya's the one who called it that, blame her.

You have to admit, Seb added. Having us spend most of the night sparring is a good way to make us appreciate the fact that we normally can stop when we want.

Not the point, Gwen sent.

"I think you made your point," Anya said.

"Did I?" Fay asked, her shoulders settling. "Good. I don't like being Seer Phelann when I don't have to be."

"Did you answer her?" Cara asked.

"I told her I would confirm whether you were both agreeable to my answering your questions."

"No postscripts?" Anya asked.

"One…" Fay said. "That yes she did see me smiling and hugging the three of them."

"So you didn't tell her who Briana was?"

"There was a second," Fay admitted. "Bri insisted I write 'The cute woman holding my hand is my fiancee'."

Anya knew she shouldn't. She could feel Seb's resignation mixing with Cara and Gwen mirroring her reaction. Yet somehow she was the only one who actually reacted, the only one that Fay would end up glaring at.

"Aww."
