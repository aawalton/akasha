“Land!” called one of the men on our group of airships. It didn't take long for me to take out a telescope, one of the first I'd made, and kind of a mess of a thing and look where he was pointing.

“Land indeed,” I agreed, passing it over to Isha where we sat in the main cabin.

“Green at least, I heard there were trees, but sadly we got no seeds back,” she grumbled, not for the first time.

“We don't know how they'd mix with what we already had. In the future maybe love, but I've explained this.”“I think you're being paranoid again husband,” she chided.

“I'm really not.”

It wasn't really possible for me to explain the damage that anything could do to an ecosystem, how I'd seen such things as earthworms, or European starlings from those stupid Shakespeare fanboys, or horror among horrors, kudzu. No, I didn't want to have to fight back some vine or obnoxious plant for the next hundred years, it was best to just nip this where it was.

“You always say things like that. Why is it that I believe you?”“Because I'm right when I say things like that,” I replied to her, smiling. “There could be something here like those pines to the north of us, that like where we live instead. Imagine if we had to deal with big billowing clouds of sneeze every year.” Her face scrunched at the idea.

The banter was fun, but we had a slight problem, we didn't know exactly where our people had gone. Now, normally this wouldn't be a problem, we did have a rough setup to track them. However, while moving it was a bit less accurate. Turns out that tracking something that may or may not be moving, at a height that may or may not be yours, while also moving in three directions was... well the math was hard.

Hard math aside and exact coordinates fluctuating wildly in the small computer I'd brought along to try and calculate that we did at least have a general direction. I didn't need to make direct commands, my people already knew where we were going. The captain of this vessel wasn't me, instead we'd picked one of the more competent of my many subordinates. It was right to hand off jobs like that, particularly when it meant I got to spend more time with my wife on this trip.

“The sands are so white,” she said as we passed over, and I had to agree.

“I wonder why, may have some odd stones in the area...”“Just enjoy it husband.” With a smile and a nod I capitulated.

Below us the forest stretched out, temperate, riddled with open areas of pasture. The tress weren't exactly how I'd remembered them from Earth, but they were in many ways similar. Some were certainly oaks, but we also saw some evergreens, cedar if I wasn't mistaken, cypress, and what were certainly some form of willow. I'd not been an expert in trees, but with my memory and enough time I knew some. They were distinct from the species I saw in my own homeland, so very, very different, but lovely all the same.

“We should try to do a full reckoning of what grows here,” I said. “See if we can find some plants that wouldn't be a problem, or at least ones that grow well here. Getting farming going everywhere.”

“Enough working, we are not working now,” Isha complained, fixing me with a hard glare.

“Yes, I suppose Adia has already been putting in the work, and it is beautiful, in its own way.”We weren't landing, weren't going down to the ground until we found my daughter and her group, but we didn't need to. From here I could see people on the ground, and my, how they reminded me of my own youth. They had stone tools, lived in small tribes, smaller than our had been. They had huts, also different, but that shouldn't surprise me.

What got me the most was that as we passed over we could see their dogs, they had dogs, how wonderful. I'd seen wolves back in our lands, but these little guys reminded me of home. They were not some purebred, but very mixed, smaller, wonderful colors.

All of this and more we watched through the various telescopes on the airship. The people below seemed very, very concerned at our passing, some running and hiding, some pointing or throwing primitive spears. None of that was a threat at this height, but it did concern me a bit. I'd hoped the natives were friendly.

After a couple of days of sailing gently through the clouds, we saw them. Isha's ships were moored over a small city. It was one of ours, I could tell from the architecture and the fact that everything was made of stone rather than thatch and branches.

She'd done it so beautifully, a small wall around it, what looked to be some basic fields going in on one side, a clear central mansion for management. And people, people who were dressed in skins and I assumed were natives. They'd already started to teach them, how wonderful.

Someone flew up to meet us in the air. One of the soldiers who had that ability, uncommon as it was, drifted up to our little balloon and was quickly shown to Isha and I.

“Your Majesty, you're here!” he said, clearly excited.

“Yes, we felt we needed a break so we thought we might come visit Adia, is she well?” I inquired.

“Certainly, she's down with the humans, trying to teach them some basic math and the like. Some of them get it, they can count and all, but some are... not very bright.”

“Well, that's not uncommon at least. Send word we'll be down shortly.”
