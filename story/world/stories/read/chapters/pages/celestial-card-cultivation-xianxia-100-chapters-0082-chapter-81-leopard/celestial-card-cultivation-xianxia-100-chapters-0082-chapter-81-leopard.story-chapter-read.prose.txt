A pony-sized leopard came for Jianyi.

There was nothing he could do. He was hurt. He was trapped below the boar he had just slain. And even if none of that were true, the creature was definitely at least in the Qi Condensation Realm.

It was just outside of what any Qi Gathering cultivator could do. Even with all of his advantages.

He had taken a risk—and now he had to pay the price for it.

It was… fine.

A feeling of contentment and service came across him.

Wait, that wasn’t his feeling. That was an aura…

A spear pierced the leopard’s head and nailed it to a tree.

The feeling, the aura, vanished.

With a crack, the leopard's neck broke post-mortem.

Jianyi took his first breath in what felt like a very long time, his eyes never leaving the leopard who hung from the birch tree like a violent piece of art.

A hand grabbed the spear and pulled it out.

The leopard dropped to the ground. Still as dead as Jianyi thought he would be.

Slowly, his brain started to catch up, and his eyes followed the hand holding the spear.

“Minghao!” he gasped.

“Yes?” the retainer asked, eyebrow raised.

“What…?” Jianyi shook his head and abandoned his question. Instead, he focused on pushing the boar off of him.

Needing more time to process what had just happened, he pulled out his spirit needle and tried to connect with the dissipating spirit of the boar—out of the corner of his eyes, he could see Minghao doing the same with the leopard.

He barely acknowledged it as a card materialized over the body of the creature he had slain, and stuffed it into his storage ring.

“Minghao…” he tried again, pausing.

“Yes?” his friend winked at him.

“You… You said you had little cultivation talent…” For the first time, he realized that he had never tried to or had naturally felt Minghao’s cultivation realm. Now that he focused on it, he noticed that he still couldn’t.

“Yes, that is true,” the man said as he started to string up the leopard to start the exsanguination process.

Jianyi mirrored him, just without a rope and in a more haphazard way, tilting the beast to the side.

“But…” Jianyi pulled a water skin from his storage ring and emptied it over his head.

His head had felt like it was full of cotton, but the cold stream water helped shock him back into reality.

“You are not in the Qi Condensation Realm!” he almost shouted at the man who spent most of his days cooking and tending to Elder Sun’s garden.

Minghao chuckled. “I don’t think I ever said I was—but you do know that it is rude to comment on a cultivator’s progress.”

Jianyi wiped the water from his face and continued to field dress the boar, paying special attention to the tusks and the glands under its skin, responsible for the stone protrusions.

“Well, thank you for saving me. I thought I was dead,” he said, looking Minghao in the eyes.

“You are welcome. I think there are quite a few valuable lessons here. Maybe even more valuable than the spirit beast harvest you got.”

Jianyi nodded, happy to listen.

“First, you have made a lot of progress on your presence, but you didn’t notice me following you for the last two weeks—and neither did you notice the leopard who had been toying with the boar for hours.”

For weeks! Jianyi had been sure that he had been alone all that time. He had felt responsible, trusted for being able to roam the forest on his own. Now… he felt grateful. Grateful that Minghao had let him make his own decisions, but been an invisible safety net all that time.

“Second,” Minghao continued. “One leaf covers the eye, Mount Tai cannot be seen.” Jianyi almost wanted to groan at the old saying, but he didn’t because he knew it was deserved. “This boar, or rather its card, was the leaf. Do you know what Mount Tai was?”

Head hanging low, Jianyi nodded. “The leopard.”

“Exactly. The leopard. “You never saw signs of whoever fought the boar. You knew that the boar was hurt, so the blood that you found was unlikely to be from its opponent. Yet, you didn’t try very hard to find whoever it had fought. So… you missed a Qi Condensation Realm creature…. In fairness, you probably wouldn’t have found an ambush predator above your realm even if you had tried, but try you should have!”

Minghao was right. He had thought about the risk of fighting the boar, but he had let himself fall for his own narrative of what the situation was, partially because he really wanted the Earth affinity card.

He sighed. “You are right.

Minghao clapped him on the shoulder. “As Elder Sun likes to say: The path of cultivation is a long one—long enough to make mistakes, long enough to learn from them.” He said with a chuckle, then switched into his bad merchant personality, rubbing his hands. “I have a potion to sell; it is called Earth-infused Soul-Forged-Body Elixir for the Body Tempering Realm. All I ask is an Earth affinity card and boar glands.”

Jianyi hadn’t even checked what the card he had harvested did, and made a decision right there not to do it either. There was no scenario where he wouldn’t trade in the card, and why torture himself with what he had to give up? Without further ado, he handed the price over and received a yellow-golden shimmering potion in return.

It was finally time to move forward with his body cultivation.

***

They had returned to their camp, and Minghao had assured Jianyi that he would make sure that nothing disturbed him as he went through the first step on his new path.

Jianyi settled down and reviewed the manual for the Soul-Forged-Body Path. He had done so many times over the last few weeks, but he wanted to make sure that he was aware of every detail.

Next, he circulated his qi in the prescribed order—as it had been advertised originally, those patterns were simple. He started from the lower dantian and then circled the qi first through the legs, then his torso, the arms, all the way up to the upper dantian, and then back to the start. It would be slightly different for the next stages, but it truly was simple.

The challenge of the technique lay elsewhere.

Before moving on, he shared a brief meal with Minghao—he had gotten used to the taste of spirit beast meat.

Seeing nothing else that stopped him from proceeding, he sat down a few paces from the fire in the lotus position with a small smile. From the very beginning, body cultivation had held a certain level of attractiveness to him, and he was glad to now be able to go ahead with it.

He took a couple of deep breaths and then, in one big gulp, swallowed the Earth affinity elixir.

Alchemical products Jianyi had taken so far had often dissipated into his spirit before he had even swallowed them fully.

That was notably different here.

The elixir’s dense qi rolled down his throat like a boulder down a hill and made its way further and further until it arrived in his stomach. For a moment, it lay there like a rock at the bottom of the sea. The path down had been slightly uncomfortable, but not an issue.

The manual had been a bit vague on when he was supposed to start the qi circulation and just said that one would know.

Jianyi knew.

With an almost visceral rumble of tectonic plates moving, the rock in his stomach moved to cover his stomach lining, then it dissipated.

Jianyi could feel how it entered his bloodstream, and from there went on the long path through his body.

Now, it was his turn.

First, he started the qi circulation, its primary purpose being to strengthen the body and prime it for the changes that would happen to it shortly.

Then, the reason why the cultivation art was called Soul-Forged-Body became relevant. As the yellow energy coursed through his body, Jianyi marshalled his will, and once he could sense the remains of the elixir all over his body, Jianyi started to push.

He brought his full will to bear and used it to press the elixir from his blood vessels into his bones.

Despite its dissipated state, the elixir retained the characteristics of Earth. It was slow, steady, and did not like change.

But Jianyi needed it to change quickly. Every second that the energy coursed through his body, it would lose potency, so speed was of the essence.

Not relenting, Jianyi pushed, and it looked like he made some progress on his right arm, but the moment he focused on that, the energy all over the rest of his body threatened to slip out of his control.

His heartbeat quickening, Jianyi had to make a decision: push through everything in parallel, or try to do it sequentially?

His mind raced; the technique had spoken of doing it all at once and assumed that a third to half of the energy would dissipate over the allotted period of an hour.

Given the strength of his soul, that was certainly feasible, but he wondered whether there wasn’t a better, a smarter way.

Remembering the Veil of the Immutable Will technique, which had taught him to separate his mind to some extent.

He didn’t need something permanent, but… he went into deep concentration and focused on keeping a strict grip on the energy to make sure it remained close to his bones without a chance to dissipate.

While maintaining that grip, he focused another part of his mind on his right upper arm; with every remaining ounce of his being, he forced the yellow energy to enter the bone.

To his surprise, with that level of focus, there was almost no resistance.

No resistance didn’t mean no pain, though.

He could feel how every cell of the bone was infused with the strength of the Earth and slowly took on the properties of stone.

Despite the pain, he didn’t wait for the process to finish; he moved down the arm and went through bone after bone.

No matter how large or small the bone, its resistance crumbled before his will, and the energy of the elixir went to work.

When the last bone in his hand had started its transition, he took a few deep breaths to steady himself—his whole arm felt as if a hundred men with hammers were smashing it to pieces, but after a moment, he continued.

His shoulders were next.

It felt like a very long time, but he was pretty sure that in reality, only ten or twenty minutes had passed when everything but his spine and skull were done.

Fortunately, his right arm, shoulders, and ribs were already done, and his right leg was close. Otherwise, the pain would be unbearable.

He breathed through his nose in ragged, uneven gasps, every inhale cut short by pain.

He knew the next step would be the worst; he steeled himself and started on the bottom of his spine.
