Jer stood alone in the center of the vast circular chamber, illuminated by the sickly light of a crystalline pillar that pulsed like a monstrous heart.

The acrobat’s body hung wrong, head tilted at an unnatural angle, limbs too loose, as if the bones had been removed and replaced with something fluid. When he smiled, the expression crawled across his face like a dying spider.

“Hey there, fearless leader,” he said, voice gurgling as if speaking through water.

The sound of it froze Cade in place, daggers half-drawn, magic faltering at his fingertips. This wasn’t right. This wasn’t possible. They’d left Jer behind in the flooding chamber—manacled, drowning, sacrificing himself so they could escape.

“Jer?” The name escaped Cade’s lips as little more than a breath.

His friend’s skin rippled, a necrotic blue-green luminescence threading beneath the surface like toxic veins. His familiar bright eyes had gone milky, pupils expanded into black pools that reflected nothing.

“Surprised to see me?” Jer took a shambling step forward, leaving a wet footprint on the obsidian floor. Water dripped continuously from his clothes, from his fingertips, from the corners of his mouth. “Didn’t expect me to follow you, did you?”

Cade’s mind raced. Orro had gone back for Jer. At least, that’s what he kept telling himself. They’d seen the water rush through the doorway. Nobody could have survived that. Nobody could be here now, animated and speaking, yet so obviously...

“You’re not Jer,” Cade managed, finally finding his voice.

Behind him, he expected Elena’s sharp retort or Gav’s growl of warning. He anticipated Nora’s blade singing from its sheath or Rayka’s hand on his arm, pulling him back from danger.

Nothing came.

“You sure about that?” Jer—or the thing wearing Jer’s face—grinned wider, his mouth stretching beyond human limits. “I feel pretty real to me. In fact, I feel more alive and clear-headed than ever. Death has a way of changing your perspective, now doesn’t he? I mean, I know now that you and he have been having chats behind our backs.”

How? How could he know that? Cade tried to discern an answer, but none were apparent.

Mist began to crawl across the chamber floor, rising from nowhere, curling around their ankles like spectral serpents. The crystal pillar’s light dimmed, casting long, distorted shadows.

“Rayka?” Cade called, not daring to take his eyes off the shambling nightmare before him. “Nora?”

Still nothing.

“They can’t hear you,” drowned Jer said, circling slowly, leaving a trail of brackish water that steamed in the cold air. “It’s just you and me now, Cade. The way it should be.”

Cade risked a quick glance over his shoulder. Where his crew had stood moments before, there was only empty space, the mist thickening to conceal the chamber’s edges. The realization hit him with physical force—he was alone with this... abomination.

“What did you do to them?” he demanded, silver-gold flames flaring protectively around his hands.

“Me? Nothing.” Drowned Jer laughed, the sound wet and wrong. “They were never here, Cade. They left you behind—just like you left me.”

Memories started to flood back. An argument at the cathedral. Cade shouting at Orro. Flames. The sound of his boots scraping against the staircase down into the crypt, with no others following him. The images grew clearer and clearer, as if some fog was lifting over his mind.

No.

Jer. Jer had followed him here.

And now he stood there… dead.

The chamber seemed to shift around them, the obsidian walls darkening further until they resembled the flooded trap room where Jer had sacrificed himself. The crystal pillar morphed into the stone column with manacles, water rising around its base.

“Orro went back for you,” Cade said, trying to keep his voice steady. His mind seesawed between incongruous memories. “You’re… you’re alive. We were all there.”

Drowned Jer laughed, a horrible gurgling sound that sent water cascading down his chin. “Is that what you tell yourself to sleep at night? That noble Orro saved me while you ran? That they all followed you down into this death trap?” He gestured to the chain around his wrist. “Look again, fearless leader.”

Against his better judgment, Cade looked. The manacles had snapped shut on Jer’s wrists, the chains embedded deep in the stone. But where Jer’s right hand should have been, there was only a ragged stump, torn flesh floating in the rising water.

“I tried to get free,” Jer explained, his voice suddenly intimate, confessional. “Gnawed my own hand off, but the water rose too fast. And you didn’t even think to ask Death to help you out? He would’ve, you know. I asked him myself.”

Cade’s blood ran cold. “How do you know about—”

“You think the dead don’t see?” Jer circled him, leaving a trail of brackish water. “We see everything, Cade. Your secrets. Your lies. Your failures.”

“This isn’t real,” Cade repeated, but doubt crept into his voice. “Jer wouldn’t—”

“Wouldn’t what? Blame you?” The apparition moved with sudden speed, unnaturally close. “Of course I blame you. You’re the one who dragged us into this crypt. You’re the one who let me die to save your own skin.”

Each word landed like a physical blow, targeting Cade’s deepest fears with surgical precision.

“We had no choice,” Cade protested, backing away. “The crypt demanded a sacrifice.”

“There’s always a choice,” Jer spat, seawater spraying Cade’s face. “You could have stayed. You could have found another way. You would have, if it had been Rayka down here instead of me.”

Cade flinched. “That’s not true.”

“No?” Jer’s dead eyes bored into him. “Face it, Cade. I was expendable to you. Always have been. The fool. The jester. Good for a laugh but not worth saving when it really counted.”

“Stop it,” Cade growled, pain twisting into anger.

“Why? Hitting too close to home?” Jer’s bloated face twisted into a mockery of his once-cheerful grin. “You’re just like Hugh, you know. Keeping secrets. Manipulating everyone around you. Deciding who’s worth saving and who’s worth sacrificing.”

The accusation struck deeper than any blade. Cade’s magic flared erratically, responding to his emotional turmoil.

“I am nothing like Hugh,” he snarled.

“Please.” Jer scoffed, circling him like a predator. “You never trusted us with the truth about your magic. About Chubbs. About Death’s visits. Just like Hugh, always thinking he knew best, always making decisions for everyone else.”

“I was protecting you!”

“You were protecting yourself,” Jer countered. “Afraid we might see who you really are. What you’re becoming.”

Water continued to rise around them, now ankle-deep and rising faster. The chamber had fully transformed into the trap room, water pouring from invisible sources.

“It’s always the same with you, isn’t it?” Jer continued mercilessly. “The great Cade Stormhollow, too arrogant to trust anyone else with the important things. Too convinced of his own brilliance to consider that maybe—just maybe—someone else might have a better solution.”

Cade’s chest constricted painfully. Every word resonated with his own secret fears, his own self-doubts. Had he failed Jer? Had his arrogance, his need for control, led them all into disaster? Had he really come down here alone save for Jer? What happened to the others?

“I did trust you,” the young thief managed, his voice cracking.

“With the small stuff, sure,” Jer agreed, wading closer through the rising water. “But never with anything that really mattered. Never with your plans for Chubbs. Never with whatever’s happening to your magic.”

The water reached Cade’s knees now, frigid and heavy with the smell of rot.

“You know what the worst part is?” Jer asked, his voice suddenly soft, almost gentle. “I believed in you. Right until the end. Even as I drowned, I told myself Cade would find a way to save me. Cade always has a plan.”

Something broke inside Cade at those words. He fell to his knees in the rising water, grief overwhelming him.

“I’m sorry,” he whispered, the admission torn from him. “I’m so fucking sorry, Jer.”

Drowned Jer loomed over him, water streaming from his eyes in a grotesque parody of tears. “Sorry doesn’t bring me back. Sorry doesn’t change what you did.”

The water climbed higher, reaching Cade’s chest as he knelt there. Part of him wanted to let it rise, to accept this punishment as just.

“You led me here to die,” Jer continued, his voice hardening again. “And for what? A crown? More power? Or was I just in your way, slowing you down?”

“No!” Cade surged to his feet, fighting against the weight of the water. “That’s not true. You were never just—you were my friend. My family.”

“Family doesn’t abandon family,” Jer hissed. “But you’re good at that, aren’t you? Good at leaving people behind when they become inconvenient. Just like your father.”

The water reached Cade’s chin.

“Give up, Cade,” Jer urged, his voice almost kind now. “Let go. Join me in the dark. It’s peaceful here. No more responsibility. No more guilt. No more failures. You won’t have to be alone ever again.”

The water rose past Cade’s lips. He tilted his head back, struggling for one last breath as the chamber filled completely.

Something nagged at the back of his mind—a detail out of place in this nightmare. The water tasted of salt and rot, just as he’d expect from the marshes. But that wasn’t right. The crypt’s flooding chamber had been filled with clear water, not this brackish soup.

And Jer—Jer wouldn’t know about his conversation with Death. No one knew about that except...

Cade’s mind cleared suddenly. This wasn’t Jer. This wasn’t even a ghost. This was something else, something feeding on his guilt and fear, creating a perfect storm of self-recrimination from his own mind.

The realization brought clarity—and with clarity, anger.

His magic surged, no longer chaotic but focused by his rage. The silver-gold flames that had always come so easily to him now sparked with crimson as he reached deeper into his core.

Not real, he told himself. None of this is real.

The water around him began to boil as his magic superheated it, steam rising in choking clouds.

Drowned Jer’s expression shifted from sadistic pleasure to confusion. “What are you doing?”

Cade didn’t answer. Instead, he focused his will into a single command: Burn.

Silver-gold flames erupted from his body in a perfect sphere, expanding outward with explosive force. The illusory water evaporated instantly, the false chamber shattering like glass under the onslaught.

Reality reasserted itself with jarring suddenness.

Cade stood in the obsidian chamber once more, the crystal pillar still pulsing in its center. But now he could see the truth—his companions stood frozen around him, eyes completely black, expressions locked in various states of horror and anguish. Whatever had attacked him had snared them as well, each trapped in their own personal nightmare.

And behind the crystal, partially hidden in shadow, lurked the creature responsible—a spindly, insectoid thing with too many limbs and eyes that glowed the same cold blue as the drowned Jer’s had. Its body was vaguely arachnid but wrong, proportions stretched and twisted as if viewed through a broken mirror.

“GET OUT OF THEIR HEADS!” Cade roared, hurling a blast of fiery destruction magic at the creature.

It scuttled sideways with unnatural speed, avoiding the blast. The obsidian wall behind it melted where his magic struck, running like black tears down the stone.

“You cannot break them out of my grip Cade Stormhollow,” the creature’s voice scratched directly into his mind. “Their fear runs even deeper than yours. And their despair is most… exquisite.”

“Watch me,” Cade snarled, launching himself toward the nearest of his companions—Gav.

The lycanthrope stood rigid, amber eyes now pure black, face locked in an expression of primal terror. Cade grabbed his shoulders, shaking him violently.

“Gav! It’s not real! Whatever you’re seeing, it’s a lie!”

No response. Not even a flicker of recognition.

“C’mon, buddy,” Cade groaned. Before he could regret it, he slapped the telepath hard across the face.

Nothing.

He tried to shake the others awake in turn—Rayka, Elena, Nora, Evie—each as unresponsive as the last, locked in private torments he couldn’t penetrate.

The creature skittered closer, limbs clicking against the obsidian floor. “They cannot hear you, Cade. You are alone, as you’ve always been.”

“Like hell they are,” Cade growled, turning to face the monster. Its multiple eyes reflected his silver-gold flames, the light fracturing into kaleidoscopic patterns.

Tiny black threads that were nearly invisible connected the creature to his friends. With each heartbeat, they throbbed and pulsed like umbilical cords. Bile turned in Cade’s stomach.

The monster was feeding off of them!

He reached for his magic again, but this time with greater deliberation. The red threads he’d glimpsed during his meditation with Nora now called to him with new urgency—thin as spider silk but strong as steel, they wove through his destruction magic in intricate patterns.

Instead of fighting against the chaotic nature of his power, Cade focused on those crimson threads, letting them guide the flow like channels directing a flood. The sensation was exhilarating—not just wielding power, but shaping it. For a brief moment, his two magicks united in perfect synchronization. The destruction magic nearly doubled from its usual potency, and he struggled to reassert control over the burgeoning strength of his spell.

He aimed, doing his best to keep his friends out of the path of his fiery lance.

Then, with a guttural yell, the Child of Ruin struck. Fire illuminated the dark as he whipped his arm forward. And with the sound of shattering glass, his ethereal blade slammed home. Not into the beast, but into the black cords connecting it to his friends.

Cade sliced with everything he had, silver-gold fire laced with crimson shooting from his fingertips directly at the junction between his immobile allies and itself. The energy sliced through the dark connections, severing the monster from its power sources.

The creature shrieked—a physical sound this time, high-pitched and painful. Its form convulsed, limbs thrashing in uncoordinated spasms. Its glossy carapace flared with sudden, desperate brightness, cracks appearing across its surface.

“You cannot defeat me!” the creature insisted, though its mental voice had weakened considerably. “I am fear itself! I exist in all living things! Even you, Child of Ruin!”

“Maybe,” Cade acknowledged, gathering his power for a final strike. “But you made a mistake.”

“What mistake?!”

“You showed me the wrong Jer,” Cade replied, crimson-threaded magic coalescing between his palms. “The real Jer wouldn’t blame me—he’d kick my ass for wallowing in self-pity instead of saving our friends.”

The creature lunged in desperate attack, but too late. Cade released his gathered power in a focused beam that struck the monster’s center mass. Unlike his usual destructive blasts, this one didn’t simply annihilate—it unraveled, pulling the creature apart thread by thread.

Its death scream reverberated through the chamber, followed by an explosion of light that ripped through the room in a horizontal arc. The force knocked Cade backward, sending him skidding across the obsidian floor.

When he regained his feet, the creature was gone—nothing left but a scattering of iridescent dust that rapidly dissolved into ordinary shadows. The crystal pillar had crumbled, its fragments still glowing faintly where they lay.

One by one, his companions gasped back to awareness, eyes returning to normal as the creature’s influence faded.

Gav staggered, nearly falling before catching himself against the wall. “What the fuck was that?” he demanded, voice hoarse as if he’d been screaming.

“Nightmare fuel,” Cade replied grimly. “You all got caught in its trap.”

“It… that wasn’t real?” Rayka whispered, arms wrapped tightly around herself. She looked smaller somehow, shaken in a way Cade had never seen.

“What did you see?” Elena asked, her usual sharp edge dulled by whatever horror she’d experienced.

“Jer,” Cade admitted quietly. “Blaming me for his death.”

“I saw my my father,” Gav replied, not meeting anyone’s eyes. “Showing me what a true monster looks like.”

“It was Hugh,” Rayka said softly. “But not... not as we knew him.”

Elena’s jaw tightened, clearly reluctant to share. But before Cade could tell her that she didn’t need to, she spoke up. “I saw… Damn it, I saw myself. But without... anything that matters.”

They all looked to Nora, who stood examining the shattered crystal with unusual intensity.

“I was back with my sister,” she finally said, her voice carefully controlled. “The day I got my magic. I saw him again. I saw Dread. He was so real, Cade. Are you sure it was just a hallucination of some kind?”

“Are you okay?” Cade approached her cautiously.

She nodded, still not looking away from the fragments of carapace that littered the floor. Nora stooped down and examined it. Her magic flared and shadowy tendrils coated the piece of chitin before she tossed it away with a disgusted sniff.

“My best guess was that this monster was what my sister would’ve called a Fearbringer. Horrible naming device, I know, but apt nonetheless.” She gestured to what little remained of the beast. “They feed on terror, magnifying and reflecting what already exists within their victims. They’re not known for letting their prey escape.”

“So it just showed us our own fears,” Elena said bitterly. “Wonderful.”

“Our fears, but twisted,” Cade corrected. “Exaggerated to break us.” He studied the remnants of the insectoid, which still occasionally pulsed with faint internal light.

“How did you break free?” Nora asked, finally turning to face him.

Cade thought of the crimson threads within his magic, how they had guided him to a precision he’d never before achieved. “I realized something wasn’t right. The vision was using details it shouldn’t have known.”

It wasn’t the full truth, but he wasn’t ready to discuss the red threads with everyone—not until he better understood them himself.

“We should move on,” he continued, already scanning for exits. “Bernard’s still ahead of us, and Chubbs with him.”

As they gathered themselves to continue, Cade noticed Nora watching him with unusual intensity.

“What?” he asked, when the others had moved slightly ahead.

“Your magic,” she said quietly. “When you destroyed that creature... Destruction can’t break fear’s grip on a mortal. Was it… you know?”

Cade shrugged, aiming for casual. “Maybe I’m just naturally talented.”

When she raised her dark eyebrow at him, he groaned inwardly but eventually nodded.

“I’m still figuring it out,” he whispered. “You think it’s dangerous?”

Nora studied him for a long moment while she absently adjusted the straps on her left gauntlet.

“I don’t know,” she finally admitted. “But it’s not natural. And in my experience, unnatural power always comes with a price.”

Before Cade could respond, Gav called from up ahead. “Found another passage!”

They rejoined the others at an archway that had appeared in the chamber’s far wall—an opening that certainly hadn’t been there when they entered. Beyond it, a narrow winding staircase spiraled in both directions.

Cade took a deep breath, pushing aside his concerns for later examination. “Oh, my favorite: more stairs.”

A few of them chuckled as they filed into the passage. He cast one last glance at the shattered shell and the iridescent dust that had once been a creature of pure fear. Something about the encounter had changed him—not just his magic, but his understanding of himself.

The drowned Jer’s accusations still echoed in his mind, cutting because they targeted his genuine insecurities. Was he really becoming like Hugh? Was he keeping too many secrets, making too many unilateral decisions?

Perhaps.

But unlike Hugh, he was starting to recognize the pattern. And unlike Hugh, he still had time to change course.

With renewed determination, Cade lifted a hand to be eye-level with his face. He conjured a tiny wisp of flame. Unlike all the times he’d done this before, the silver-gold flames around his fingers were now threaded permanently with crimson.

He grinned.

It was time to reach the top.
