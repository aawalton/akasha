Maud

Something had felt off to her from the moment the dungeon began collapsing. Something was resonating across that strange little thread she had that connected her to the soul chat. It wasn't much, nothing more than the tiniest buzz, but it was there, and she could feel it. So what was going on?

The moment they entered the ship, a chat notification came into her view. It didn't do so normally, either. It was like a static image resolving itself in her brain. It wasn't entirely dissimilar to when Dave had finally increased the range of the messaging system, but it still wasn’t exactly the same. She didn't like it.

Saud: Maud, can you see this? There's something wrong, and I can't get a hold of Dave or anyone else. I don't know what's happening, but there are weird quakes going on all throughout the soul realm. Selody says we should just stay calm, and everything will be okay, but I don't think she's right.

Maud: What do you mean you can't get a hold of Dave? Is he just not answering, or do you feel like there's something blocking you?

Saud: He's not answering. I'm actually surprised you are. I don't know what to do, Maud.

Maud: It’s gonna be okay. I'm gonna try to get a hold of Dave. I'll be right back, okay?

New worry flooding into her mind, Maud closed out the direct chat message and opened a general one.

Maud: Can anyone see this?

Saud: I can.

Maud: But no one else?

Maud: Damn. Okay, Saud, I don't know what's going on, but it might have something to do with us. I've been feeling a weird buzz in the connection to the group chat since we killed the beast and rescued William. I'm gonna talk to others here and see if they have any ideas what's going on. Just stay calm; I promise we'll find a way to help. :)

Saud: Okay, Maud. I’ll try. :)

“And whatever the hell is happening with the dungeon collapse, I don't know how to get us outta here in time,” were the only words of Squidlen Maud caught as she closed out the chat window.

“Uh, new problem. Don't know if it's connected or not, but seems like it probably is, since it started as soon as the dungeon started to collapse,” Maud cut in meekly. Every head immediately turned towards her. “So, my connection to the soul chat has been feeling weird again since the collapse started. And now Saud messaged me, saying she can't get an outside connection to anyone else. She also said that weird things are happening over there. She described them as quakes, but as far as I understand about the soul realm, I don't know how that would work.”

“I'm guessing ya can't reach Dave either then?” Mel asked.

Maud shook her head.

“Shit,” Squidlen muttered, turning his head toward the bridge window. “Dammit Yorela, how come every time we're together on one of these voyages we get to witness phenomenon that's never been fully documented?”

“Fuck. What do you think it is, Squiddy? I don't like it when your voice gets like that,” Yorela replied.

“There is something called a true tear of chaotic space. I've never seen one in person, and as far as I know, no one's ever actually watched one happen. The few existing ones I know of have been far too dangerous to bring the ship anywhere near ‘em. It looks like we're about to watch one be birthed right in front of us. As much as I'd like to claim that makes us the first ones to witness it, I've got a strong feeling there have been others before us, and they just didn't survive the experience,” he replied grimly.

“There's got to be something we can do. We just got William back. We beat the jesters. We can't die here,” Alex spoke up with a grim resolve in her voice.

She didn't sound as hopeless as she had for so long. The determination wasn't empty. And she was right. They couldn't die here, which meant Maud had to think. She wasn't helpless. She wasn't just that old, funny Maud who kept people entertained in a disaster anymore. She had powers all her own. There had to be something she could do.

If this was a tear like Squidlen said, what was it tearing into? Why was her connection to the soul chat vibrating, and why had the others been cut off?

Except for Saud.

Saud, who was in the soul realm that was currently experiencing some sort of quakes, could still connect to her. That meant that was what was on the other side of the tear? If that were true, it would be possible to survive over there. It also meant there was a way out. They just had to find what Dave had constructed.

She had an idea. She had no idea how to make the idea a reality. But it was an idea nonetheless.

“So, remember what I just said about the soul chat? What if that's connected to what's going on out there, and on the other side of that tear is the soul realm?” Maud said quickly, some of her words blurring together, with the speed she forced them out.

“Maud's got a point. I'm not sure how much it helps, but she's got a really good point. Squiddy, do you know anything at all about soul realms or soul space?” Yorela followed up.

Maud had explained that poorly, now that she thought about it. She knew that soul space wasn't the same thing as soul realm, but with her brain working as fast as it had, the two ideas had been conflated. Dave's soul realm was something that sat within the soul space.

“I know that soul magic exists. And that, in theory, it channels from soul space, but it ain't something you hear much about, especially out in chaotic space. But this right here proves there are bigger connections than we realized. Or at least there are bigger connections than anyone was willing to talk about. Dammit. The ramifications of what's going on here are bigger than our own lives. What the hell did you get me involved in this time?” The captain replied.

“Please, you get yourself involved. And usually you drag me along. Don't blame me for this mess. But that's something we can hash out later. How do we get this ship through that tear without dying?”

“How could I possibly know that? I just told you no one's ever seen one of these form before.” Despite his words, Squidlen was busy tapping away at several different consoles and jotting down outputs. He was trying to figure something out.

“Something's happening out there!” Mel called a split second before the window exploded inward. A bright blue light of charged mana followed it.

There was a loud sound of something stamping its hooves repeatedly against the deck before the room returned back to normal. In the place of the window was now a glowing shield. And behind them stood a very tired-looking moose.

“I'm guessing that means we're running out of time,” Yorela said.

“Oh my god, it worked!” A voice incredibly similar to something Maud had heard all her life, and yet just slightly different, cried out as two bluish-white forms started to manifest in front of the shield

“Saud!” Maud called back, both shocked and happy to see her soul reflection solidifying into its own person. Beside her stood a woman who looked incredibly like Elody, who Maud knew must be Selody.

“I'm a bit surprised it worked as well. For those who have no idea who we are, I'm Selody. This is Saud. I assume you've at least mostly heard of us through Maud and her connection to the others. Whatever is happening here is causing a cascading effect all the way to the soul realm Dave has built. We were able to ride the thread that connects Maud and Saud back to all of you. And I think I can pull this entire ship back through,” Selody said in a confidently matter-of-fact way that Maud had only ever heard from Elody.

She still wasn't entirely sure what made herself, her, and Saud, Saud, separated. It seemed her memories had split some time around the creation of the soul realm, and even then, not all of Maud's memories were fully manifest within Saud. But they had determined there were emotional connections to things that Saud didn't fully understand. This had been a big part of what caused Maud to decide that Saud was just her sister and not worry anymore about it.

“I'm basically lost at what's going on. But I'm Squidlen, captain of this ship, and I'm willing to entertain any ideas to keep this ship afloat, and I suppose everyone alive as well. So what do you got?” he said, looking up from the console to stare directly at Selody.

“You need to take this ship directly back into soul space. I will attempt to guide you to follow the thread that connects Saud and me to the soul realm that Dave has created, but I don't know where we will end up when we first enter. So it's important that everyone is prepared for a battle,” she instructed.

“Show me where to go,” was all Squidlen replied.

Selody joined him, tapping away at the consoles herself, Squidlen nodding and ‘ah-ha-ing’ as he followed up. There was a surprising silence from everyone else in the room as the two worked. Despite how much Maud wanted to greet her sister, she held it in. It looked as though Saud was doing the same.

All at once, the ship lurched forward. The moose looked even more strained as the shield changed colors. Maud didn't understand what they were doing, but you could tell the toll it was taking on the primal mana beast, and she did her best to soothe him.

The entire room went black, and Squidlen hurriedly yelled out a warning through the speaker system, telling everyone to remain calm. Suddenly, a noise ripped apart the blackness. William began to laugh.

And with that laugh, the blackness vanished. Outside the window stood a sprawling, gleaming city.
