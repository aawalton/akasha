A free skyfox… Likely drawn in by the skulk’s agitation, it had flown in to see whether there was something that could interest it. Seeing its cyan glance lock onto me suggested that it had.

“Aren’t you a catch?” I smiled.

Twice my current level, it emanated enough strength to force the rest of the skyfoxes to keep their distance. It would have been nice to see its cards, but the specials alone told me a whole lot.

At some point in the past, either the fox or its progenitors had performed a service deemed valuable enough to be freed from the deities. That meant that no deity could force it into a pact.

If I had chosen to follow a dreamer build, such a creature would have instantly set me apart from everyone else. It was rather unfortunate and ironic that I was going to end its freedom instead. Still, all was fair in the Grand Arcana. The dreambite and devouring specials made it clear that if I failed to capture it, the creature would end me.

Several weaker skyfoxes swirled around. What they couldn’t get in a direct fight, they had chosen to obtain through numbers, flying at me all at once.

“Pathetic.” I spun around, unleashing a series of thread strikes.

My attacks sliced through several of the creatures, reducing them to dust. One of the higher-level ones managed to evade the thread of lethal aether, coiling away in the air in an effort to escape.

Too late. I used the Thirteen of Chalice to drill it full of holes.

Done, I quickly refocused my attention on the cerulean-white specimen. It was still there, keeping its distance. While the rest of the skulk were moving closer together, preparing for their next wave of attack, the free skyfox kept circling around, showing no aggressive signs whatsoever.

Cheeky bastard, I thought. You’re evaluating me.

I refocused my attention on the skulk. Although initially taken aback, the creatures hadn’t survived this long just by preying on dying adventurers. I didn’t know much about the dream realms, but from what I had been told, all dream beasts were predators. The strong devoured the weak, and the weak consumed the wounded, eventually becoming strong themselves. Participants started at the bottom of the food chain—easy “treats” as Serp would say. In the past run, Harlequin had protected us from any dream influence for the first several dozen trials, though he didn’t protect us from each other. A participant entangling another was fair game, as long as it was done during a trial.

Lines appeared on the sides of the skyfoxes, forming extremely elongated mouths. As they opened, hundreds of sharp teeth came into being. Instead of attacking me, the skulk flew through the sky, shattering what was left of the idyllic phase of the dream. Blue skies crumbled along with the empty field, creating a barren wasteland in all directions. A mass of ever-moving teeth and colors spanned the sky, surrounding me like a giant cone.

Using my Nine of Chalice, I performed several thread strikes at the creatures.

Aether strands split the air. Some sliced through into the cone, reverting a creature or two to mist. The greater part was devoured by the mouths before any damage could be done.

“Stop!” I ordered, using my Emperor’s card.

The entire cone froze for a split second before restoring its movement. So, the Emperor had limited effect here. The same was probably true for the Bard. Just to make sure, I sang my usual ode of sleep. There was no visible effect.

“Putting me through the meat grinder?” I mused.

“You deserve it,” a voice said behind me.

Instantly, I moved to the side, twisting around with a circular slash.

My attack was blocked with no effort.

“I knew you’d miss me,” a man said, holding a pair of crimson swords.

“Marcus?”

I looked at his status box. There could be no doubt. He was the same person I had started the Grand Arcana with. The blood dagger he had gotten a lot bigger. I could only imagine the number of monsters and people he must have killed for it to reach its current state.

Level twenty-three, I thought.

There could be no doubt that both he and his weapon had grown, although he still had a way to go to reach me.

“Didn’t expect you here,” I said, still keeping an eye on the skulk of skyfoxes. The creatures appeared just as confused as I was, for the cone widened, moving away from us in all directions.

“Didn’t expect I’d end up in a place like this, either.” He looked around. “I just wanted to have a word with you.”

One thing instantly grabbed my attention.

The Dalmatian beside me let out a low growl.

“It’s fine, Pup,” I said. “He’s a friend.”

A friend that had obtained his first Grand Arcana and was trying it out. There was no way of knowing whether he had actually dropped by for a chat or was testing the waters for an attack. With Marcus, everything was possible.

“What’s all this?” he asked, looking up at the skyfoxes.

“I must catch one,” I replied, maintaining my calm. “Potentially kill the rest.”

“Hmm. They don’t seem to like you much.”

His attention quickly shifted to my target. Given how much it stood out, it was easy to mark it as a creature of interest. On its part, the skyfox didn’t react one bit.

“Mind if I join in?” he asked. “There are a few things I’d like to try out.”

“Pup, if he tries anything against me, rip his throat,” I said in a calm tone. “Sure.” I turned to him. “Be my guest.”

“Perfect.” He nodded with his emotionless expression.

Back-to-back with Marcus… It was unusual as it was terrifying. I had only seen him fight like that three times in my previous run. In two, he had teamed up with Cathy. In the third, he had killed his “ally” with the same merciless efficiency as he had dispatched the waves of monsters.

“How’s Cathy?” I asked, turning my back to him.

Above us, the skulk had come to a decision, for the cone was quickly tightening again.

“Different,” he replied. “She’s gotten stronger. Still not sure if she misses you or she just hates the shitheads we ended up with.”

The bottom of the monster cone extended, descending onto us. I used my Thirteen of Chalice and Ten of Swords to send out a multitude of force strikes at the creatures. My attacks struck the wall of teeth, yet went no further.

“The new rule-master’s interesting,” Marcus continued, engaging in his own attack.

I would have liked to get a glimpse of what combination of cards he was using, but all I could do was hear the sound of his slashes thanks to the Listener.

“A lot more hands off,” he added.

That only meant that he was giving them more rope to hang themselves. Harlequin liked to control everything to the smallest detail. If a participant stepped out of line, there was harsh punishment, often death. Others remained quiet most of the time, creating the illusion of freedom. If a participant ventured too far, they would be killed that very second, and everyone else would be made to watch.

“So, why did you choose to visit?” I cast a mega circle beneath us.

A ring of stone spikes rose up, slapping into the cone of skyfoxes. Even strengthened, it wasn’t able to withstand the impact, though in the process, it managed to push the skulk upwards by a few feet.

“Other than showing off your Grand Arcana card,” I added.

I could hear the sound of Marcus’ head brushing against the collar of his shirt. He had turned to look my way.

“Still a few steps ahead,” he admitted. “I know you’re searching for something, so I wanted to clear my debt with you.”

I wasn’t even aware that we had a debt. I didn’t interrupt him, nonetheless.

“The rule breakers have been threatening your sister.” Marcus’s voice changed slightly.

I could tell he wasn’t certain about the information, likely relying on second- or third-hand accounts. Given that only Cathy remained in his group, she was likely to have mentioned something, which meant it came from the goddess Diana.

“There’s a way to check.”

The single sentence acted like a trigger, filling me with rage. Without thinking, I cast another mega circle, this time combining the Magician with the Stoneweaver. Stone spires rose up, though this time they weren’t stopped by the skyfoxes, ripping the skulk apart. What had been a single cone was now reverting back to individual beings. Some maintained the pattern, though quite a large part splintered off.

All of a sudden, we were like in the middle of a hurricane, assaulted by leaves.

“How?”

Even as I asked, I knew that it was a mistake. Even if he wanted to help me now, he could well use the information he had just gained against me in the future.

Serp, I thought. Get ready to kill him.

“It’s called a key card,” Marcus said, the sound of his blades slashing through the air clearly aubible. “You can only get them from a rule-master. It lets you go back for a bit.”

“Go back where?” I asked even if I suspected the answer.

It was the first time I had heard of such a card. There certainly wasn’t anything of the sort in my previous run. If there was, why hadn’t the goddess told me about it?

“To the moment before the Grand Arcana,” he added. “Think of it as a reprieve of sorts. I don’t know the details, but you get to spend some time there and do what you want.”

There was no way that could be true. The Grand Arcana wouldn’t allow people spreading news about the game… would they? Eleonor had mentioned that she had known about the game before it started. Sparta had also hinted about it. And then, there was Carl. There was no doubt in my mind that he knew a lot more than he had let on.

“How do you know this?” My blades carved up the dark crimson skyfox that had moved just a bit too close to me for comfort. Teeth had gnashed as the divine places had gone through it as if it were ham.

“I told you that my rule-master let us do what we wanted,” he replied, avoiding the real question. “With that, we’re square.”

“How do I—” I turned around, eager to continue the conversation face-to-face.

By then, Marcus was gone. His intuition had likely told him that was the better approach. As a participant, I couldn’t blame him. As a person, I despised him right now. Leave it to him to drop into my dreams and dangle a carrot in front of my face.

A yelping howl filled the air. Turning in its direction, I saw the cerulean-white skyfox, reminding me of its presence. Likely, it had evaluated me to be worthy of a direct fight, so now was waiting until I finished with the lesser monsters.

“We’ll have our fight.” Combat circles formed on my body.

Yet instead of unleashing a new attack, pieces of stone armor covered me. If there was one thing that I was thankful for from the person I had taken the Spellweaver card from, it was that he had shown me part of the way to use it in battle.

So, we’re not fighting? Serp asked, disappointment in his voice.

“Soon,” I said, leaping to the side as I sliced off another skyfox’s head. “Before that, you’ll need to grow stronger.”

All of my Grand Arcana cards needed to become stronger. Gone were the times when I could afford to rely on their lower skills. In order to be ready for the things to come, I had to boost all three of my Grand Arcana cards as quickly as possible and as many of the Major ones as well. As Rayle liked to say, there was nothing worse than a non-maxed card. As much as he was an asshole, he was among the best I had fought alongside. Now, it was time to follow his advice.
