"If I'd known this was the night we were going out, I would've cleaned up better." Jade fretted, smoothing wrinkles out of her clothes. "I was wading through marshes and fighting monsters all day, I must look awful."

Jade and Ashar were sitting together at a restaurant on one of the higher levels of the city. Their balcony table was right at the railing, providing an excellent view of Illysport's market district. The gentle shower of rain beat a soothing rhythm against the awning overhead, and wet cobblestone streets glistened with the twinkling motes of a thousand lamps.

"You look wonderful." Ashar reassured her, gesturing at the surrounding tables. "See? This is a casual place. Anything fancy would stand out."

"Doesn't that make you a little overdressed?"

Ashar was wearing a long, open coat over a formal shirt that shimmered with an artistic enchantment. His outfit was well tailored and complemented his dark tan and darker hair. Sitting next to him in her simple, flowing elven clothes, Jade couldn't help but feel rather plain.

"Ah, but standing out is the goal for me. I'm still new in town and need to make a good impression while I get established."

A waitress stopped at their table before Jade could reply, handing them a pair of menus and asking for their drink orders. Jade flipped through it but as expected, she recognized none of the contents.

"Have a recommendation?" She asked Ashar.

"Hmm." He turned to the back page, glancing through a list of items. "Would you like something sweet, or something with some kick to it?"

"Kick." Jade glanced around the restaurant, which was filled with at least a dozen different species. She recognized barely even a third of them. "Definitely kick."

"In that case…"Ashar supplied the waitress with their order and she bustled off, leaving them to enjoy the view. In the distance, barely visible in the darkening twilight, Jade could see a handful of ships sailing into port. They looked nothing like any Earth vessel she knew of, with the sails held out over the sides of the hull, like great wings skimming the surf.

"This is surreal." She murmured, transfixed by the wondrous sights. The strange ships, the magical lanterns floating over the streets, and the many different beings that walked them… It was all so normal. And yet, so very strange.

"Is your world so different?" Ashar asked.

"That's the thing." Jade said, shifting her attention back to him. "It's not. Not really. But that just makes it weirder. Everything here is familiar enough that the differences stand out all the more."

"Do you like it?"

"It's beautiful."

And it was. For all its cramped streets and confusing layout, the city of Illysport was filled with colorful shops, enticing scents, and the kind of vibrant energy that could only come from a place where so many peoples of different kinds mingled.

"When I first came to this world, I hated it." Jade continued softly. "At first, I didn't know what was happening. I was fighting for my life, and didn't even know why. Then, once my Sanctuary attendant explained everything, it got so much worse. Now I'm not just fighting for myself, but for my world."

"I can't imagine the kind of pressure that puts on you and your friends." Ashar said gently. "The world I grew up in completed its integration long before I was born. I learned about it when I was a kid, but it was so distant. This part of the Labyrinth hasn't experienced one in my lifetime. Now, not only is one really happening, but there's this business with the demons that makes it even worse."

"You didn't grow up in the Labyrinth?" Jade asked, surprised.

To the best of her knowledge, everyone she had connections with here had been born in this strange world. She recalled that Siora's people had migrated across such a distance that they didn't even know how to get back to the world their ancestors departed from.

"No, but I visited young. My father made a living as a merchant that travelled between our world and Labyrinth cities. I pestered him about taking me along until he finally let me. That was when I was nine."

"You travelled through the Labyrinth at nine years old?!" Jade asked, stunned. "That must have been harrowing."

"There were a few close calls, but I learned a lot."

"Like how to fight?"

"Lost gods, no." Ashar laughed. "I still haven't gotten to that part, yet."Jade raised a skeptical eyebrow.

"I saw you fight those demons in Estelhelm. You stood up against the blood demon with us. I'm not buying that."

"While I am great at many things, don't confuse a talent for crafting useful items with the skills of a warrior." He said, waving away her comment. "Anyone can stand in the back and wave a wand around."

"I wouldn't tell Tara that." Jade said with a wry smile. "She might disagree."

"See?" He grinned at her. "I don't even know what it takes to be a proper battle mage. Clueless, I am."

Jade chuckled and shook her head as the waitress returned with their drinks. The fellian woman took their orders, and she was happy to let Ashar give her another recommendation. The smell coming from the kitchen was rather alluring, but the restaurant was full enough that Jade had to focus on filtering out the ambient emotional energy of the room before it became overwhelming. Fortunately, her time in Lysara and Estelhelm had given her ample opportunity to improve with crowds. As the waitress walked away again, a thought struck her.

"I've been meaning to ask." Jade said, leaning across the table and speaking more softly as she looked around the restaurant. "Is the food here okay for all these people to eat? Do different species have different needs?"

"It's alright." Ashar reassured her. "Any city of this size has that part taken care of. You see these symbols on the menu?" He gestured to a row of icons next to various items. "There's a key on the back that shows compatibility with different biologies."

"Interesting…" Jade examined the list. Humans, it turned out, shared the nutritional needs of several other species. Even so, there were many more that were incompatible. "It's a big Universe…"

They continued chatting, the murmur of conversation and the gentle pattering of rain fading into the background as Jade asked more questions about Ashar's home world. He spun a tale of a planet covered almost entirely by an ocean filled with countless islands, many of which were no larger than the one this city stood upon. His stories conjured images of fleets of ships sailing the seas carrying entire tribes as they migrated, flowing with the changing seasons. Ashar spoke of harsh winters and blistering summers extreme enough that most people had to live a nomadic life, ever in pursuit of fair seas and more hospitable islands.

The conversation strayed from topic to topic, with Jade answering her fair share of questions about Earth. As someone who grew up in a world possessed of magic and already connected to the Labyrinth, Ashar was fascinated by the technology used on Earth. She smiled, sensing his genuine interest and an almost boyish sense of wonder, accompanied by a desire to visit her home and see its mysteries for himself.

"What is it?" He asked, noticing her expression.

"Nothing." She waved a hand absently, taking a sip from her drink. Was this her third? She had to remember it for next time. "It's just that, you're really earnest. I didn't expect it."

"Is that bad?"

"No, no. I like it." Jade said, hoping that the dim candlelight of the restaurant would hide her blush. But, just in case…

"Hey, did you just give yourself a tan?" Ashar asked, sounding amused.

"Probably just a trick of the light. It's pretty dark in here."

They both laughed, and Jade shifted in her seat. Her foot knocked against his, making her heart do a little flutter. When was the last time she enjoyed an evening like this? A fun, casual night out, together with a…? Friend? Date?

Does it matter?

Jade thought about her upcoming conflicts. The Labyrinth Cores, the demon invasion, and the unrest back on Earth. There was so much uncertainty. For all she knew, she could die fighting a monster tomorrow. No, she decided. Labels didn't matter to her. Tonight was fun, and she would enjoy it for what it was.

"You look thoughtful."

"Just…" Jade made a vague gesture. "Thinking about the future."

"When is your group leaving? You're going after another Core, right?"

"Day after tomorrow. We'll take one more day to train and gear up, and then we're off. It's only two quadrants over, so it won't take long to reach."

"Hopefully this one has fewer demons in it. It's still hard to believe there's a whole army of them ready to invade. Once word gets around, the city is going to get very crowded."

"If we destroy the Cores fast enough, hopefully it won't come to that." Jade said, with more than a little forced optimism. Who knew what magic the demons were enacting on the Cores deeper in the Labyrinth, on floors they hadn't even reached yet. "You don't seem all that worried, though."

"Between Illysport and the other Free Cities, there are a lot of powerful people here. Wayfarers who finished their trials, mercenaries from other worlds, and people like you."

"People like me?"

"That's right. Determined wayfarers who fought their way up from the bottom. The Labyrinth is filled with them, although most don't thwart a demon plot before even reaching C rank." Ashar said with a chuckle. "But the point is: There likely isn't a worse place in the Multiverse to try and invade. This floor of the Labyrinth especially is where some of the most powerful people come together to form guilds, fortresses, or even to found towns and cities. The Duke of Illysport is a B rank former wayfarer himself, and he's far from the only one in the city."

"If they're that powerful, I'm surprised they aren't doing more to help."

"Just because we aren't seeing it doesn't mean it isn't happening. There's been a lot of movement in the larger guilds this week. I've been swamped with requests and special orders."

"I hope so. We can't do this on our own."

The weight of responsibility had eased as Jade immersed herself in the relaxing atmosphere of the restaurant, but now it all came crashing down on her again. It must have shown on her face, because Ashar winced.

"Sorry. This was supposed to be a fun night out."

"It was." Jade gave him a tired smile. "But as they say in my world, 'there's no rest for the weary'. Someone has to get the job done."

"And it has to be you?"

"Not just me. Not anymore. I have a team to rely on. We'll do it together."

They settled the bill together. Jade had no context for any of the prices, so she didn't know if the meal had been expensive. But compared to the sums she and her friends were pulling in from their adventures, it was practically nothing. Monsters themselves rarely dropped crescent coins directly, but their wayfarer quests provided them in abundance. She joined Ashar at the railing near the stairs, looking out over the busy streets below.

"Thanks for this." Jade said quietly. "I needed it."

"Glad I could help. I'd…" For the first time, Ashar was a little hesitant. Jade tasted his sudden nervousness, which he'd done an excellent job at managing throughout the night. He was a relaxed guy, easy to get along with, but Jade supposed this next part was hard in any world.

"I'd be happy to do this again, yes." She said, tilting her head to hide a smile. The ability to sense emotions was practically a real-life cheat code when it came to these kinds of conversations.

"That's not very fair." He laughed, rubbing the back of his head.

"We have another saying. 'All's fair in love and war'." This time, in was her turn to flash him a sly smile. "You're the one who asked a succubus on a date."

"I'll remember that one. I have some tricks of my own.""I look forward to thwarting them."

"Confident, are we? Good. That makes it more fun."

They lingered, admiring the view together for another long moment. Finally, Jade sighed, pushing off the railing and standing straight.

"I have to get going. We have an early morning tomorrow."

"Be safe out there. When you get back from your trip, I should have the ingredient you're waiting for."Jade stepped away, paused, and then came back in for a hug, catching Ashar by surprise."Thanks for that, again."

"It's no big-"

She squeezed him tighter, using a little bit of her enhanced strength.

"Just say 'you're welcome.'"

"You're welcome."Jade led the way out of the restaurant and onto the elevated boardwalk on the third level of the city before stopping, turning in place. The chaotic jumble of twisting walkways and crowded streets almost made her dizzy

"You're going to walk me back to the inn, right?"

"Not sure how to get back?" Ashar laughed.

Jade gave him a mock glare.

"How is anyone supposed to find their way around this place?"

"Practice. Lots and lots of practice."

Later that evening, Jade was sitting cross-legged next to her bed, alone. A single lamp flickered on the desk beside her, bathing the room with a dim, shadowy light. She focused, going through the exercises Aylin and Serrethis had taught her to calm her mind and control her aura. The evening's events had stoked the embers of some of her more demonic inclinations, and Jade was determined to suppress them. She wasn't Malice. She wasn't like the brutal, callous creatures that waited on the far side of whatever gate the demons were trying to open in the Labyrinth. She had control.

Slowly, her aura stilled, returning to a calm, tranquil state. The desires faded, slipping back into the dark corners of her mind. Jade knew that she would still have to drain something before leaving on their mission to seek the second Core, but that would be on her terms. There were plenty of ways to go about that, especially with Aylin around to assist. She wasn't about to let her new, subversive instincts taint one of the first relaxing, enjoyable nights she'd had in months. No, she would hold mastery of this body, not the other way around.

After cycling through the exercises twice, Jade was calm enough to climb into bed. It was late, and a long day of training awaited her in the morning.

And then it was time to find the next Core.
