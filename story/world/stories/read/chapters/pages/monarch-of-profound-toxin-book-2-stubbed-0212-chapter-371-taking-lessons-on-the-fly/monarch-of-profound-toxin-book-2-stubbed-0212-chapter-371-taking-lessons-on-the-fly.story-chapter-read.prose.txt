Chapter 371

“If you would follow my instruction, let’s get started on the grade seven Potion of Mighty Strength.”

With a nod and painfully aware of the stares of the many alchemists boring into the back of his head, Eik reached for the bag of holding on the table and emptied the content out onto a large, thick piece of cloth that covered the entire right side of the table, with the left side mostly taken up by the cauldron Eik had been using for that past while.

The medium-sized bowl of blackened metal was of the highest quality money could buy. Eik had ordered it made to custom specification from what he had been told was the most skilled cauldron maker in all of the Nidafjeld Alliance. It had taken a personal visit to one of the worlds under the civilization of red-skinned people in order to secure the right to purchase.

This master artisan had turned out to be a wrinkly, old crone who had more or less retired from the business. She was already so rich from decade after decade of highly paid work, that no amount of money had been able to sway her into making something for him.

It had only been when he had shown up at her house on the outskirts of a smaller, rural town and personally demonstrated his ability to create potions of higher quality than any known master of alchemy in the Unified Mass, that she had finally agreed.

And when he had revealed the little fact that he was also the originator of Eik’s Legendary Mystery Medicine, she had more or less flipped on a plate and practically forced him to accept a custom piece from her. Even when he had tried to pay her, she had slapped the credit slab out of his hand with the same expression of disgust as if he had been attempting to pay her with a bag filled with literal poop.

True art has no involvement with money, is what she had hissed before pulling him into an immaculately kept workshop, brimming with tools and worktables polished to a shine. Despite being well into retirement, she had apparently not been able to shake the habit of keeping the space clean and ready for work at any time.

In the end, the product she had made for him had been even better than he could ever have hoped for.

Ever since he had learned alchemy, Eik would often visit stores in Gimleh that sold cauldrons for alchemy, just to browse if nothing else, so he had a pretty good understanding of average quality and what to look for in a great product. One of the biggest hurdles of cauldron creation, not to mention the creation of any enchanted work for that matter, was the stacking of magical effects.

Even just attaching a single enchantment to an object took great skill and years of practice—a truth Heath could testify to. But that was still far simpler than adding another layer to the mix. With two magical effects contained within the same work, mutual interference became an immediate and serious concern.

At best, two effects that interfered with each other would cause one or both to become partially or completely useless. And at worst… Well, it involved a lot of heat generation, melting, and exploding.

And, naturally, with every single magical effect further attached to the object, both the volatility of the stack and the potency of the consequences multiplied. That was why it was so insane that this master artisan managed to make for Eik a cauldron with a whopping nine stacked, powerful magical effects on it, in addition to the expert physical craftsmanship and top quality raw materials.

Increased stability of the ingredient mixture, blocking of any environmental circumstances that might interfere with magical properties of the ingredients, a self-cleaning effect, suppression of naturally hostile auras of the ingredients, identification of impurities materials in the ingredients through a soft glow, were just some of the goodies he got.

All things considered, this was something he should have, and happily would have, paid hundreds of millions of credits for. No matter how one twisted and turned it, this was a genuine masterpiece. But she had refused to accept even a single credit.

Instead, he had cunningly tricked her into taking one of each grade of his pills instead, which amounted to nearly the same amount of monetary value. He had left her workshop snickering.

Eik looked at the ingredients as they poured out of the bag of holding, eventually filling up the entire cloth.

“Wow, this is… a lot of stuff,” he muttered.

“It’s a powerful potion,” the guild master acknowledged with a nod.

“And all of these many different ingredients are necessary to create the grade seven Potion of Mighty Strength?”

“Essentially, yes. For a less potent potion, a simpler and lighter foundation is fine, and even encouraged, in fact, but for something as full of power as a grade seven potion—something which has never been created before, as far as we know—this is the lightest we can go without risking an unstable potion.”

“Damn. Yeah, okay,” Eik breathed as he riffled through the heap of ingredients. There were minerals, plants, and animal parts, strange sands and powders, metal shavings, and even a few vials of different liquids. Some of the materials were dried to the point of cracking, while others were so fresh and juice that the fluid was practically running off it.

There were a few things among the many that he thought he recognized, but the majority of it he had no clue about. But if this stuff was what the guild master and the rest of the expert alchemists had decided he would need for this project, then he would trust their judgment with no question.

“Do I really need to study alchemy properly when I have such skilled people working for me?” he asked with a laugh.

“Yes,” The guild master answered immediately and decisively.

“Right, yeah, you’re totally right…” He sighed inwardly, realizing that there was a reason that a guild existed where members could dedicate their entire lives to the study and research of alchemy. It would be an endless process of learning.

God damn it. Didn’t the creation of the most powerful potion in history count for anything these days?

“So, if you would start by crushing the dried flat-leafed white root in the bottom of the cauldron,” Guild Master Ganyak hir Gawak began.

“Uuh huh, yeah, sure. I’d love to,” he mumbled as his eyes scanned the pile for something that had flat leaves. There were at least fifteen such plants. Most plants had flat leaves, so that was already pretty annoying, but then four of them had white roots. And another five had been severed at the stem, above the root, so he couldn’t tell if they would originally have had white roots.

Slowly, he reached for the nearest of the four with clearly white roots, only to see the guild master’s eyes narrow. Okay, so not that one. Moving on to the next, a sharp inhalation from Meri stopped him from even touching it.

“Not that one either, Mr. Magnasen,” Ganyak hir Gawak told him when he tried the third of four white roots. Then surely, the last one. “No.”

“All right, then,” Eik said, a vein popping out slightly on his forehead. “How about this? You pretend that I have absolutely no clue at all about what I’m doing here, and simply point to the thing I put in next.”

“It’s that one,” Meri said and tapped a finger on a little dried plant lying on the corner of the heap.

Eik frowned and blinked several times in puzzlement. “That one doesn’t have any leaves.”

“We removed them,” the guild master explained. “The leaves contain an acidic sap that it can secrete through the many thorns that run along their surface. We don’t want that in this mixture.”

“Okay, I can live with that, I guess, but it’s just… the roots are purple, not white.”

Meri answered that one. “Ah, yes, you see, the flat-leafed variant of the white root doesn’t actually have white roots, but rather the deep purple color you see here.”

“Oh, wow. Yeah, okay, that makes me real mad, actually.”

“I thought it might,” she grinned.

“Even I kind of hate that,” Heath admitted from the sidelines.

At that, she arched an eyebrow. “Don’t you study alloys? You have to understand so much complicated stuff about ratios and temperature for that.”

“Yes,” the smith said with a nod. “And I hate that too.”

Scattered chuckles sounded from the group of alchemists as Eik crushed the flat-leafed white root into dust with an inert sphere of crystal that could have crushed coal into diamond.

From there, Meri handed him either individual ingredients or handfuls one after the other while her grandfather instructed Eik in the proper treatment, and with every addition, Eik could feel the mixture growing more stable.

Without a doubt, this was the most complicated thing he had ever created, and it wasn’t even close. Usually, he learned a new recipe by heart pretty quickly, but for this one, he feared he would be reading off the recipe for the next long while.

Finally, after careful work, the foundation for the potion lay ready in the cauldron. “And now,” the guild master said and brought out a small crate enchanted with refrigeration. “the final part of the recipe—the butt, if you will.”

Heath nearly choked, and Meri didn’t fare much better. The rest of the alchemists hadn’t heard the prequel but they all knew what they were working with, so a good number of them had to suppress laughs as well.

“Guild Master Ganyak hir Gawak,” Eik said flatly, his face devoid of expression.

“Yes, Mr. Magnasen?”

“I could crush you and your puny little guild as if you were made of dry biscuits.”

“Yes, Mr. Magnasen.”

Without another word, the guild master handed over the crate. Inside lay a piece of bloody muscle the size of a finger nail. “That’s all we need?”

“For now, yeah,” Meri said. “Once you get the hang of the process and scale up production, the amount of muscle you need will follow accordingly.”

“So, what now?”

“It needs to be ground as finely as you can.”

Eik chewed on his lower lip. “You’re asking a man to grind on his own ass? That’s diabolical.”

“Hah! Fair enough,” the guild master chuckled. “Let’s stay focused for this next part. It will be the most difficult by far.”

With a deep breath, Eik crushed the flesh into a fine paste. “Done.”

“Good. And now we enter uncharted territory, so to speak. You must finish it with your aura now. From here on, how you handle it will be up to your own judgment.”

“I understand,” Eik said. “Here we go.”

“Please be careful, Mr. Magnasen.”

Eik flashed a charming smirk. “Am I not always?”

And then, the moment he injected aura into the mixture, it exploded in his face.
