When the first wave arrived, the square in front of Queen Aecia’s palace filled up with corpses.

A hundred-thousand-and-one corpses teleported straight down to the planet. Xavier had been half-expecting something like this. He’d taken out the crews of a thousand ships, and the System hadn’t adjusted for that.

Brarri nudged one of the corpses with his boot. “At least we know where they’ll arrive,” he muttered.

“We need to clean up this mess,” Queen Aecia said.

The bodies glowed. Every single one was teleported out of the massive square. When Xavier had killed those enemies back in the ship, he hadn’t bothered taking the time to loot their corpses. The number of Storage Rings he would have to go through… it would take a very long time.

Down here, things worked a little differently.

In anticipation of the waves arriving on the planet’s surface, he’d negotiated a cut of the proceeds from Queen Aecia. While Xavier, Queen Aecia, and her entourage fought against the waves that would soon be teleported down, other citizens of Galadsea would be hard at work teleporting the corpses away, looting their armour and weapons, and cataloguing the items within their Storage Rings.

Did he need a cut of the proceeds from these fights? No.

Was he going to leave money on the table? Also no.

That simply didn’t feel right—not when these people would be dead without his assistance.

Time passed. The first thousand enemy waves teleported down to the planet were the ones he’d already dealt with. As each wave arrived, Xavier’s frown deepened.

Not a single one of the ninety-nine other Champion parties had chosen to fight for Galadsea. Which meant they were up there, somewhere, on those enemy ships. Yet none of them had come down with the initial thousand waves.

Did they usually arrive in the first waves?

He wished there was someone he could ask. What was the point of having Champions fighting for the Empire of Servitude if it was clear they were going to win this fight without them? Was it just the System’s way of messing with Galadsea’s people for going against its wishes for conflict?

Maybe the Champions can choose which waves to join. Maybe they’re waiting for the waves to whittle down the numbers of enemies they’ll have to deal with…

He supposed it didn’t matter. He would cross that bridge when he came to it.

When the first wave of living enemies finally arrived, Xavier’s blood ran hot. The single A Grade and the one-hundred-thousand B Grades appeared in the centre of the massive square just as the corpses of the former waves had appeared before.

Strange spheres with glowing energy within them were deployed. Summoned from Storage Rings, they hovered at five different points around the wave.

A shield blossomed from the spheres, creating a dome around the first wave of live enemies.

Xavier already wished he could freeze them outside of his time dilation field. It was honestly strange for him to be facing so many enemies and have them moving in the same time stream as him. He couldn’t pause and examine the shield that had sprung up.

He had to move.

Already, enemy spells were being flung out from within the shield. The entire area had been cleared of civilians. Queen Aecia hadn’t brought an army to the square—Xavier had told her such a thing wasn’t necessary—but she did have squads of Denizens not far away, ready to join the fight.

Of course if they were needed, that would mean things would have gone terribly wrong.

Her main entourage, the elves he’d met in the throne room when he’d first arrived, were spread out around the wave. They, along with their queen, were the most powerful Denizens the planet Galadsea had to offer—the only Denizens who’d reached A Grade. Xavier was sure they would have been able to deal with plenty of the enemy waves on their own, without needing any help from him at all, but they wouldn’t be able to sustain that kind of pressure for 57 straight years.

Xavier hadn’t been idle as he’d waited for the real waves of enemies to arrive. The first fifty drones he’d created had died out there among the enemy fleet, but he could summon more.

A lot more.

He wasn’t as powerful as he had been when he’d been in his own territory, benefiting from Unity Defence and Territory Defence. He certainly couldn’t summon two-hundred-thousand drones to do his bidding.

But he didn’t need to summon that many.

At least, he didn’t believe he needed that many.

Instead, he’d created a thousand drones to fight by his side.

The instant the enemies had arrived, while those spheres had been summoned and that shield had come up, Xavier had pinpointed the wave’s A Grade captain.

Back when the first wave had appeared—though that had been a wave filled with corpses—a System notification had popped up.

Wave Rewards:

Xavier was more than a little peeved that those rewards hadn’t applied to him when he’d been taking the crews out in their spaceships, but he supposed the System wouldn’t have accounted for the insane thing he’d tried to pull off.

Determined to get that 5x Mastery Points boost, Xavier flew straight at the shield. Even as he flew, he used his Immortal Soul lens to inspect the shield. He might not be able to stop time, but his mental faculties could operate incredibly fast.

He got a feel for how the shield operated and how powerful it was. It was nowhere near as powerful as the shields that were around the spaceships. Those shields had a lot of other material and energy to work with. What this wave had managed to bring down wasn’t near as strong as that—but it was damned strong.

Xavier definitely considered these shields a dirty trick. Then again, he knew he would use any dirty trick in the book if it meant he achieved his goal—he couldn’t say he blamed the enemy for doing the same.

Xavier was confident he could crack the shield with pure force; he was also confident that he didn’t need to.

Instead, he surged Soul Energy and scattered the energy within one of the five spheres. Energy from the other four spheres rushed to close the gap created, but it didn’t rush fast enough. Xavier was able to slip through it onto the other side, where he came face-to-face with an A Grade warrior.

The man must have been at least ten-feet tall. He wasn’t human, at least Xavier didn’t think he was. He looked more like a stone statue come to life. Like some sort of strange, humanoid golem. He didn’t carry a weapon, but his fists looked more than up to the task of inflicting incredible pain.

The last time Xavier had faced an A Grade without the advantage of his time dilation field it hadn’t gone well for him.

He felt vulnerable as he slammed his sword into the golem’s chest, manifesting a dozen different spells simultaneously. Now the waves were down on the planet, Xavier couldn’t hold back. He was going to reap every one of their souls, whether they had a choice in being here or not.

He’d need every advantage he could take.

Soul apparitions materialised around him. Blood Wyverns, like the last time. The beasts’ spirits flew through the B Grades. Kill notifications popped up in the corner of his vision as Xavier’s sword glanced off the golem’s chest, not doing a damned thing to penetrate its thick, rock-hard skin. The ten-foot stone man’s hand snapped out faster than Xavier would have imagined.

It gripped his neck and tightened.

The gap Xavier had made in the shield closed behind him, locking his drones and the queen’s entourage outside.

And locking him inside with the enemies.

More soul apparitions continued to materialise as Xavier manifested Soul Strike again and again, sending them at the golem. But whatever this A Grade Denizen was made of, his soul was either not in residence, or it was incredibly resistant to Xavier’s attacks.

The B Grades didn’t have the same luxury. They died in droves, but even they had their protections against what he was doing. Anti-soul magic spells were flung up everywhere, scattering the soul apparitions like dust in the wind.

The golem’s fingers constricted around Xavier’s neck just long enough for him to get an idea of how insanely strong the golem was. Xavier phased himself out of the enemy’s hold, gasping for breath. The bones in his neck healed the instant he was freed.

Xavier flapped his wings, rising into the air, creating a bit of distance. His soul apparitions were still tearing through the enemy, but they weren’t taking them out as fast as he’d imagined. Spells shot up at him, a good portion of his mind occupied with countering them. Shields of his own had been created. Circular force fields that floated around him, blocking incoming attacks from all angles. The thousands of B Grades might be significantly weaker than him, but with all of them attacking together it was definitely taking its toll.

Even with all his defences, even with his body massively enhanced by refined Celestial Energy, he was taking damage. His health dipped down by 1 percent after another.

The stone man bent his knees then jumped. The A Grade rocketed toward Xavier. Xavier phased at just the right moment so the golem went straight through him. The golem-like man soared farther into the air until he reached the shield. Xavier thought he would slip straight through, but instead he kicked off the shield and came hurtling back at him. The man’s fists started glowing.

Xavier analysed the energy pattern and widened his eyes.

Even phased out of reality he brought the floating shields close around him. The stone man’s fist shattered the shields with ease. Xavier flew to the left but wasn’t fast enough. The man grabbed his right wing and pulled him close, slamming his other fist into the back of Xavier’s head.

Something cracked.

Xavier blacked out.

He woke on the square’s hard stone ground. He must have been out for a fraction of a second, but a lot had happened in that time.

The enemy’s shield was down. Queen Aecia, Brarri, Yalia and the others had joined the fight. Xavier’s drones, too, were in the mix—though he’d lost his connection to them when he’d been hit, they could function well enough without direct instruction.

The back of his head still hurt like hell. It wasn’t only the back of his head that hurt. That strike must have spread the damage throughout his entire brain. The pressure made his head feel like it would explode. The world was fuzzy, and he’d lost 25 percent more health.

A notification hovered in his vision, words blurry.

According to the System, he was “dazed,” though it felt like a hell of a lot more than that.

Xavier’s mind and body should have been able to snap right back.

By the time Xavier made it to his feet, the stone man slammed his foot into his spine, sending him sprawling back onto the stone. Xavier had seen the man coming with The All-Seeing Eye, had even sensed him with his Immortal Soul lens, but he hadn’t been fast enough to get out of the way.

Queen Aecia and her entourage were getting into the mix. Yalia, the tall, full-plate armoured elf, lodged her sword into the A Grade captain’s back, distracting him long enough that Xavier gained his feet a second time.

Why did her sword get through when mine didn’t?

Xavier shifted his sword into a scythe, swinging it at the enemy B Grades who’d closed in around him, cutting a dozen heads from shoulders before the swing’s arc had completed. He still felt dazed.

Something clicked in his skull—bones shifting into place.

He blinked. Turned back to the A Grade captain. His drones and the others were making quick work of the B Grades. Only a few thousand remained.

But the A Grade captain was tougher than Xavier had anticipated.

It’s a lot harder to fight an enemy when they can actually move…

The captain was fighting off three A Grades at once. Yalia, though she’d gotten in a few hits, had been grabbed and thrown into the air, slamming into a nearby building and leaving a large hole in it.

She hit the ground a few blocks away.

Brarri was conjuring silver spikes that looked like liquid metal, shooting them at the stone man only to have them glance of his tough skin.

What spells Queen Aecia performed weren’t clear to him. Energy weaved through the air in front of her, but whatever effect that energy had he couldn’t see it. A swift inspection of the energy found it to be some sort of illusion—one that must only be visible to her enemies.

The stone man walked straight through the energy weaved in the air, ignoring the silver spikes smashing into its body, and backhanded the A Grade queen.

Xavier gritted his teeth.

What the hell was happening?

This was not how he’d envisioned this fight going. He had so many spells—he had practically every spell at his disposal—but he’d defaulted straight to melee attacks against an enemy with tough, stone skin?

Had being in the same time stream as his enemies dulled his smarts?

Xavier was more than time manipulation.

He was more than melee attacks, even heavily empowered ones.

Far more.

The ten-foot stone man slammed its fist into another of Aecia’s entourage who’d sped forward, putting themselves in front of the queen. The punch annihilated the elf’s defences, sending him flying backward to slam into the B Grades’ remaining ranks.

The A Grade captain locked eyes with Xavier. He growled low in his throat and strode toward him.

Xavier released a breath, stood his ground, and manifested a spell.

The stone beneath the A Grade turned liquified. The man’s massive feet sunk downward. The stone solidified, locking him in place.

The stone man’s eyes turned a deep red. Energy pulsed from the A Grade, cratering the area around, turning the stone to dust.

The A Grade captain walked up out of the crater.

Xavier smiled. He hadn’t expected the A Grade captain to be trapped for more than a split second. He’d just needed that split second to get everything else in order.

Several dozen different spell patterns were manifested and ready to cast inside Xavier’s body. The first spell he cast wasn’t one of them, however.

He cast Chronomantic Mindforge.
