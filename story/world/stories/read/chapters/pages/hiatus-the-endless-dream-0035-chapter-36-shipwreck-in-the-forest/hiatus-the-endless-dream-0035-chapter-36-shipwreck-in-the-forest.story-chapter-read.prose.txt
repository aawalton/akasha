When I was six, maybe seven, my parents took Rowan and I camping. It was a small, grassy hill with a single tree on it that I felt I had seen a thousand times over. My parents set out a large quilt and we all stared at the night's sky, trying and failing to find the constellations. I remember staring up in silence, my jaw refusing to close at just how bright the night sky was without the town's lights.

Rowan pointed and told me that each light was a star, and each star had planets around it—most of which couldn't be seen; some of which looked like our world. He explained life and its creation, about how it was like water that filled its vessel, but all I could focus on were the sky, the stars, and the moon.

They were beautiful.

I didn't know why I thought that. I was six. I had no greater philosophy or reason why. I just liked looking at it.

From that point on, Rowan would make it a point to take me out to that hill to stare at the sky. It's why, as I stood there, staring up at the crimson moon—whose visage burned my eyes as if it were the sun—I began to cry.

I realized there were no stars.

Just a red moon that was trying to kill me.

A useless question filled my lips, pushed against my teeth, begging to get out.

"Paltra?" I asked.

The Huntress swallowed before answering, "Yes?"

"Do you know what a star is?"

"An important person." Her voice was confused, wary even.

I laughed. I couldn't help it. I laughed a pitiful, bitter laugh.

"I think," I began, finally tearing my eyes away from the sky, "that I'm going to kill this Instance Holder one day."

I looked at the three Survivors in front of me, and all I could muster was pity.

I stood there. I don't know when, but the rope that tied Maerin and I together was gone now, untied while I was looking at the moon. The girl stood by Paltra's side. They all exchanged looks, and I could see the feelings of concern, not for me, but for themselves, written in their gazes.

I knew why. What I just said was insane, but I thought back to the crater in the Dream. It was possible. Instance Holders could die. If there was one thing I had learned throughout my life, it was that if you were going to be violent, it needed to be overwhelmingly so. I couldn't keep having Instance Holders mess with me every step of the way while I followed Rowan's path. An example needed to be made somewhere.

The icy itching hadn't gone away since I stepped into the clearing. That flying monster has been ferrying creatures up to the moon, which was far closer than I thought, and letting them die up there. If I was already feeling burning now [No Free Kill] would eventually make it so that I'd melt alive if I was caught by the light.

We had to find a shelter, one that no light could penetrate. I thought of the ravine, and the various caverns that must exist in the darkness underground, and–

And I realized where the monster in the forest had gone. Why it hadn't attacked my group or Jeren's. The Instance Holder's plan came into focus, and I could feel a hopeless smile full of mirth touch my lips.

It was a good plan. It was. Funnel all your resources into two monsters. One above, one below. No matter where we went, we'd have to deal with an overwhelming force.

I knew what my enemy wanted to do. Now, I just needed to find a way to break it.

I had stopped moving again at some point.

The others were staring again.

I waited for the self-deprecating internal comments to show themselves. To tell me that I should just die, take the loss, and move on with my life, but it never came. Instead, I met their eyes, each of them had some hardened determination in them that made me flinch backwards. I reached into my pocket and felt the surface of the Fiendish statue.

Right. I had made promises. Dumb of me, sure, but I had made them nonetheless.

"Jeren is getting the Survivors mentally prepared. They're not the most stable bunch right now." I gave a sideways look at Maerin. "You get one now. That's our deal, and I dictate how and when."

Her nose twitched up in poorly concealed anger, but was masked with a stony expression just as quickly. "I know what I agreed to." She shot back.

I continued to stare her down, looking for any signs of deceit. To my surprise, a subtle movement from Paltra behind her caught my attention. The Huntress gave me a meaningful nod before nonchalantly raising the rope and turning away.

Huh. Interesting. I had assumed she was just Maerin's guard dog, but the situation was obviously more complex than that. I could use that. If needed.

"Alright," I conceded, suddenly more confident that Maerin wouldn't go on a murder spree as soon as we got in. "So," I began again. "Which one will it be? Orlan, Nico, or Alder?"

She looked taken aback before remembering that she had told me those names before. She pursed her lips, looking to the broken pieces of the ship with balled fists. "Haven't decided. I think I'll make them vote."

I frowned. I hadn't considered she'd be self-indulgent about this. I wavered between saying no and not wanting to get in between her and the object of her anger. It would cause fear, but fear—like anything else—was a tool at the end of the day. "Between the three of them or everyone."

"Just the three of them." She responded.

I heard Krevith breathe out a small sigh of relief, and it almost caused me to smile.

I nodded. "Anything else I should know before we go in there. Anyone else have any grudges or conflicts?"

Krevith just stared at me blankly before raising a brow.

Ah, Right. "No? Alright, I've got one. There's a woman in there, Talin. She blames me for her spot in this Instance— uh, Trial. She hates me, but she'll be joining our little group anyway."

Krevith and Maerin nodded slowly. Paltra wore a suspicious expression, and I felt her eyes studying me with greater, if not more vicious, intensity than before.

"Last calls for anything important." After a few seconds of no one offering anything else, we strode the rest of the way towards the ship.

It was a massive thing, and tilted on its side, the details were difficult to make out. It had to be a transport ship or warship of some kind. I started to doubt my theory when I didn't see any spots a cannon could stick out of.

A massive gash in the bottom of the ship lengthwise along the side—board, starboard, I didn't know the name—opened up into the dark interiors. Paltra went first, holding the torch high to illuminate the surroundings. The floorboards creaked as we entered what was likely once a storage room, now thick with the smell of mold and forest overgrowth. There were many boxes, most of them smaller baggage trunks, but there were a few larger—albeit broken—crates along the walls.

Maerin stared at the baggage trunks, her eyes threatening to glaze over. Whatever memory haunted her, she managed to break away from it as she kept watch on the forest through the gash in the ship.

There was a singular open door that led into the rest of the ship, which, from the orientation, was our ceiling. Flickers of light and hushed, anxiety-riddled whispers could barely be heard in the brief moments all of us were still.

I began looking for some way to get up, boxes we could stack, a ladder, or a rope somewhere.

Instead, while I was looking, Krevith backed up and took three large bounds forward. With all his speed and weight, he launched himself into the wall, one foot, then the other, and pushed off, barely grabbing the rim of the door frame and swinging precariously back and forth. Then he heaved himself up and through the door.

A second later, he peeked his head back out with a lowered hand. I was about to ask him about finding a rope when Maerin walked over, pulled a smaller, half-broken box from one of the corners, and stood on it. With a small hop, she managed to grab onto Krevith's hand, who proceeded to haul her up. Paltra went next, and I last.

We saw the flickering of torchlight coming from the end of one of the ships, leaking from all sides of a closed, warped door. We walked along the walls of the sideways ship to reach the door. Everyone looked to me as I pulled out the [Communication Stone].

"You ready for us?"

Jeren's voice came through the stone, "Yeah. It's easier to come in through the top of the ship. I'll come out to get you."

"No need." I said before killing the connection and opening the door.

There I was, met with a surprised and tired-looking Jeren and rows upon rows of jail cells. Many had long-dead corpses in them, and the few near Jeren had people in them.

I walked forward and took note of everyone in the cells.

"What happened?" I asked my fellow Visitor, who was just recovering from his surprise.

"Like I said, we have a Visitor problem. They're not an ally." There was no humor in his voice, and he seemed utterly drained. I looked around, but I couldn't find who I was looking for.

Jeren continued, but I was only half listening. "I thought that … since the punishment changed even if they were a plant they'd change their mind and help. I didn't have issues in my loopbound run, so I had thought—"

"Where's Talin?" I asked. I stared at him for a moment longer and had to fight off the building waves of disappointment as the man failed to answer me.

Neither answer was good. Either she was our Visitor problem, or she was dead.

Breathe in.

Breathe out.

It didn't matter. I didn't know her.

Jeren stood and gestured towards the furthest cell. I stepped briskly over, and there on the floor, I saw it. Long flowing blond hair, a black dress, and a pale, deathly face twisted in an expression of fear and agony. That was it. There was no body, just her head.

He raised his head and shook his head. "Sorry Dorian. There was nothing I could do."

"It's alright," I responded instantly.

It was fine. Likely even for the best. I was the Fiend. Couldn't have people not buying the act. She knew I was a Visitor, so her death gave me a lot more freedom and options now.

It didn't matter.

"Do we know who?" I asked.

"No." He answered, and I almost snapped at him. Was he fucking with me? Was that it? He couldn't get past hour three alone. He couldn't get Paltra and Maerin to join, which seemed to be the easiest thing in the fucking world. He couldn't even keep one girl safe while having [Wayfarer], the one Boon in this entire Instance that could explicitly do that.

I didn't say any of that.

"You sure it was a Visitor. Not just a monster?" I wanted to clarify. To at least make sure my anger was directed correctly.

"Not a monster—" Jeren started, and I wheeled on him.

"Then how the fuck did this happen Jeren? It's just her head. Where's the rest of her!"

He looked away. No. Screw that. I rushed forward and grabbed him by the collar. I failed to lift him to his feet, so instead I pushed and we tumbled to the ground. "Look at me. How. She had healing magic. She should've been your one priority. So how!"

I felt pressure on my wrist, and my [Fragile] bones creaked under the force. My nose scrunched in pain, but I turned it into a snarl. Before I could strike the man, he shoved me, sending me sprawling against one of the metal gates as he righted himself and stood.

"I don't know. That's what I'm trying to figure out." He sighed and scratched the back of his head. He began to reach out a hand to help me up, but I slapped it away and used the wall to help me up.

My eyes darted along the cells. Everyone was in one—the doors closed. I doubted they were locked. There were seven in total. One of them still had a fucking rifle in their cell.

Anger welled up within me.

Incompetence.

"Fine, but first, I plan on keeping my end of a bargain." I turned to my group of Survivors. My mind felt like it was going a million miles an hour. "Maerin. Choose."
