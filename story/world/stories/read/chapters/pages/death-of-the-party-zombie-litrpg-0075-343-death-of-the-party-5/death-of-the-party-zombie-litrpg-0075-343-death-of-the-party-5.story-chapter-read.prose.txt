Theo stood upon the crest of the hill and looked down at the valley below. A small group of zombies trudged through the tall grass, heading out toward a nearby copse of trees.

“Turns out you were correct,” Humphrey said. “As we are undead, the uncontrolled horde ignore us.”

The vampire smiled. It had taken a lot of effort to twist Archie’s arm, but eventually the wizard had agreed to let out a small party for their second life. He just had to come up with a strategic reason for the risk. Sally’s pathing through the Forest to the Tropical Isles was mostly direct, and would draw a lot of attention.

Wolf lived, somehow holding his own with barely a dozen Adventurers on the western side of the Forest. From the aerial view, it seemed he was running hit-and-run tactics, slowly grinding down the Radochs as they chased him toward the edge of the continent. Eventually he would run out of land, but the knock-on effect across the area was noticeable.

On the eastern side, a Commander was working their way toward Sally’s army. A powerful Radoch, not only with several squads of elites, but a host of strong monsters surrounded by their lesser forces.

Theo intended to cut them off, allowing the undead force an easier trek to the Isles.

“It certainly is nostalgic,” Norah said. The mummy smiled beneath her bandages and placed a hand on the Shade’s back. “You sure you’re up for this, Lucy?”

He nodded, and a thumbs-up emote appeared beside his head. “I’ll just shadow Humphrey. I don’t want to get in the way.”

The Death Knight grinned. “It will be a true test to see if you can keep me up with me. Not too rusty, I hope?”

Theo finished glaring at the zombies as they stumbled out of view. The main army was out of sight, a few miles off in the swamp. While Sally couldn’t be in their Party right now, it felt oddly natural to have the original Outsiders back together again. It was several years since they fought together in this area, and so much had changed.

A simpler time?

“We’ll move to meet the enemy,” he said. “There’s a bridge they’ll need to cross, which might be the best place to hold them back.”

A blue glow illuminated the group before they teleported away, promptly arriving on one end of the bridge. The Radochs were already there, on the other side.

“Tracking disruption tech?” Humphrey asked rhetorically. He withdrew his greatsword as he stepped up to the edge of the bridge.

Theo popped out his coffin and it snapped to the new rod, creating his great-mace. He stepped up beside the Death Knight and readied himself to fight.

Now on ground level opposite their foes, holding the terrain seemed like an impossible task. While the river here was wide, it was also low. Drained somehow, or perhaps it was drought season. The muddied trench would be hell to trudge through, but the Radoch numbered in the hundreds, and were just as likely to make their own corpse bridge than fight fair.

Lucius sank into the ground and became the Death Knight’s shadow, raising up a mirror of the plated figure’s weapon. Norah moved her hands, and a pyramid rose from the ground behind them.

“Ready to exterminate some bugs, old man?” Theo’s fangs reflected the light as he smiled.

“They’ll regret ever crossing a Danger.”

The vampire pushed his glasses up as the sky darkened. A crimson moon rose from behind the tall pyramid, casting the area in macabre lighting. Radoch weaponry reflected the light, or glowed in a variety of colors as they poured onto the wide bridge.

Theo’s boot dug into the ground as he burst forward to greet them.

Dent shielded his eyes as wind whipped around his cloak. “Usually our meetings up here are less somber.”

Atop the pinnacle of the tower, large swathes of Sanctuary could be seen. Dark smoke and fire covered many parts. Armies clumped in places like scabs. Even up this high, a sickly malaise clung to the air. Death and despair.

Chuck wavered slightly before giving the swordsman an apologetic nod. “It’s not long before I have to return to sinking myself into the System.”

“You worried?”

“Of course.” The Architect furrowed his brow. “But… I’m not afraid. Things tend to work out in the end, do they not?”

“Eh.” Dent crossed his arms as he looked out at the world. “Never known you to put stock in destiny, past handing the reins to Sally, at least.”

Silence hung in the air for a moment, save for the low whistle of wind buffeting the tower.

“Something had been itching my brain for a while,” Chuck admitted. “I recently went back to that chamber that held the Outsiders in suspension for a year. It meant nothing at the time, as I thought the faux hieroglyphics were just random nonsense. But there are some similarities that are only now apparent.”

“Hmm? How do you mean?”

“The symbols… perhaps it is a reach.” He shrugged and shook his head. “A vined plant with a large mouth, I thought that was just something we encountered in the Jungle. An undead dragon? Well, we had just defeated a dragon. A tower of nature could have been where we met the dryad…”

Dent nodded slowly. “You’re saying their Aspect forms are represented some years before Sally and Theo even got theirs - which was on a different planet, I’ll add.”

The Architect flickered slightly. “It is too easy to read into things. Especially when times are dire. But there are plenty of things we do not understand in this universe. Is there a chance that a list of universal Aspects were inscribed within this chamber on our world, before we even knew what they were? Odds are not zero.”

“Really. Even Aspects outside of the five we know?”

“It’s just iconography, so who’s to say? For example, one appears to be a swarm of crows, which doesn’t align with any Aspects we are aware of.”

“What about Archie? Does it have a hint of what his Aspect might look like, since he is keeping it secret?”

Chuck didn’t answer immediately, but a dull expression crossed his face. The argument with the wizard had been mentally taxing, given everything else going on. It wasn’t even the worst plan… but it was a lot to process, and risky. It almost made him thankful the rest of the Outsiders were content to go head-to-head with the threat.

Eventually, he sighed. “His is something more abstract, unfortunately. We are unlikely to see it even if he chooses to use it.”

“Oh.” Dent made a face. “That subtle, huh?”

“No. We’ll just all be dead.”

Sally was hungry. Not an entirely uncommon feeling, but there was an empty burning within her core that was several degrees higher than ever before. She didn’t just crave brains, either. Her Aspect form was a furnace, and Radoch bodies were the fuel.

Sometimes she’d snack on a zombie as well. Picking off those who had been injured and couldn’t keep up with the horde was a mercy, and helped keep her moving. Not as nutritious as the bugs, but keeping hundreds of thousands of zombies alive was hard work.

What’s more, she could sense every time one of her pals was sent back to the grave. The cutting of a string. It both relieved tension, but left her with an empty feeling. Despite the distance, she knew exactly where on each continent her forces were losing, and where they were winning. Aside from her personal army, things weren’t looking great.

Unlike traditional foes, Radochs didn’t seem to fear the living dead. Both immune to contracting the zombie disease and heavily resistant to other maladies associated with corpses. It was even daytime, which was a downside for her forces.

Plus, getting through the swamp was an exercise in pure frustration. The roads were only wide enough for a certain number of zombies, and the others were so excited to plow forward that they wandered into the swampland, getting stuck in mud or slowed in wet bogs.

Adding to this annoyance was the lack of prey to consume. The Radochs had retreated from the area, probably to join up elsewhere for a proper attempt to counter her group. If she didn’t eat very soon, she would just die and lose control of the undead army. What an anticlimactic way to go.

“My Lady,” the dwarven king addressed her as he marched beside her crawling form. “Several of your retinue have offered themselves to you if you require nourishment.”

One of the smaller plant-mouths turned and lowered itself closer to the dwarf.

After a moment of silence, Hunter shook his head. “She says that won’t be necessary. There is a large force on the way that should get to us in time.”

“As she wills it.” The skeletal dwarf gave the man a brief bow of acknowledgement.

Hunter could feel it, or at least some purpose told him this was the right track. Sally was an important ally, and the Radochs were the enemy. Despite coming into this universe with a rather neutral view of everything, his time with the zombie filled him with guidance. She needed to live for him to complete his mission.

He looked up at the small mouth pointed to the right side, which had raised to be more at his head height. “I’ve had enough time to acclimatize to your world. Do you want- Oh? They’re back already? Hmm. If you’re sure about that.”

The head nodded.

Hunter doffed his hat and stepped into a dark tear in the air, vanishing.

A bright beam of radiant light tore through the air, causing Theo to drop his coffin-mace. He stumbled back along the bridge, looking at where the odd weapon had struck him. A wide circle across his chest and arm. Reduced to nothing but bloody bones and scorched organs.

“Yikes forever,” he murmured as blood ran from his mouth.

Humphrey stepped in front of him, and a white defensive bubble bloomed around the pair. Mummy wrappings snaked through the air from behind and wrapped around the vampire’s wounds, before detaching from Norah.

“I am sick of this mysterious technology,” the Death Knight growled. “Reprehensible and dishonorable.”

Theo grabbed the handle of his weapon and lifted it up from the gore-slick bridge. “That’s not going to stop you challenging the Commander to a duel, is it?”

“Ha-ha.” Flames bloomed behind Humphrey’s helmet. “As soon as I see the bastard. I’m sure you’ll be busy enough with the rest.”

“The day is young.” Theo grinned and powered the great-mace with Novice Strike.

So far, their plan was working. The low river was filled with corpses, but ran the risk of becoming an extension of the bridge. While the invaders were content to throw their lower-rank fighters into a death-mill, those with more experience or actual firepower were setting up across the other side to pelt the small group with exotic payloads.

Bathed in mutating hues of crimson, gold, and brown, it was only a matter of time before the dead were enough of a platform. Enough for them to surround the group of four. As long as they could hold this position, they could buy time for Sally.

That’d have to be enough, their lives just currency to be spent.

“Ah, guys?” Lucius popped out of the Death Knight’s shadow, sweat-drop emojis pouring from his head. He jabbed a panicked finger into the air.

The Outsiders looked up, to see a fifty-foot wide ball of radiant energy cratering down toward them.

“Greater Hidden Spell,” Theo murmured, his glasses reflecting golden light. “Very cl-”

Intense, white-hot light illuminated miles of the Forest, bathing everything in holy magical power.
