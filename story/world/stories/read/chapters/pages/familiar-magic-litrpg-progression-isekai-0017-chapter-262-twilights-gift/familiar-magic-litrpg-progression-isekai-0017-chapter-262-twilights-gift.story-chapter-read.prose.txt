The Fable Train moved at a steady pace, one that the army could keep up with, and Ethan refused to speed up simply because of Selina’s excitement to experience the ruins of one of her peoples’ cities. He also had to refuse to escort her up ahead, both because of the danger, and something of a self-imposed impossible goal. Ethan had promised himself he’d unlock Twilight’s gift before reaching The Throne.

Leaving a complaining Selina to her studies, Ethan retreated to the train car he’d designed as a living area–now obscenely large for just the two of them. He’d even added a room for Apollo and the other Dimensional Reflections that they had no reason to ever use, simply because it seemed silly to have a train car as large as a house so empty.

He moved past the bedrooms, sauna, swimming pool, training area, billiard room, backup bar, movie den, and reading nook, finally reaching the meditation chamber on the top floor. Ethan was proud of how comfortable he’d made it. Through a combination of nature and illusion magic, it could create any kind of environment that he wanted. It was like his very own holodeck, though he only used it for sitting on a soft mat in the center of the room, and failing to answer a single frustrating question.

Who is Ethan Bishop?

Sitting down and queuing up sounds and images for an ocean vista–one back home, without the terror–Ethan settled into a meditative pose, then went through everything Ulric had told him about searching for the gift before he’d left.

“You’re probably wasting your time,” he’d said. So supportive, that one.

“I know you’ve been told this because I told it to you before. I’m also certain the king told you the same. Twilight’s gift is about a deep, powerful understanding of who you are.”

“I know who I am,” Ethan had insisted.

Ulric rolled his eyes. “Obviously not, or your Soul Rune would already have evolved. Why do you think this doesn’t happen until Twilight?”

“I don’t know. I assumed it was about power.”

“Not entirely wrong,” Ulric said. “But still mostly wrong. When your Soul Rune forms, it’s your deepest self understanding who you are, whether you like it or not. Many people, high and low, kings and paupers alike have learned the hard way that they aren’t who they wish they were.”

“How come our souls can do it the first time, but not the second?”

“You tell me. What’s changed?” Ulric asked. He liked doing that. He was the kind of teacher that wanted you to figure things out for yourself.”

Ethan considered. “A lot has changed. Most notably, I’ve gained power, fought countless battles.”

“Perhaps, but ignore the battle part. You could use your Bonds for purposes other than fighting, and still find your gift.”

“My Bonds…they’re the most fundamental change, aren’t they? My soul is a much more crowded place now.”

“You’re getting there…” Ulric said.

“So my soul used to know itself…but it can’t anymore, because I’m not alone.”

“Are you sure?” Ulric prompted.

Ethan paused, not understanding. “Of course. I have three Familiars that aren’t going anywhere–four actually. I’ll never be alone again.”

“And is that who you are, Doctor Bishop? Are you five people?”

“I…yes?”

Ulric sighed. “When you first gained your Bonds, your soul became a container for the Familiars you’d found. It created different chambers for each one, separating them so you could benefit from each affinity. The process is necessary, so that your Bonds can grow.”

“I get that. I still remember when I first really felt them there, part of me.”

Ulric nodded. “Then your power grew, and your soul expanded to house your more powerful Familiars. When you reached Twilight, it grew again, but did you notice what else happened?”

Ethan considered. “My soul feels enormous. The space doesn’t exactly seem real, but it’s nothing like the tiny, individual little cells of Dawn. Now it feels more like a world, and each of my Affinities has its own place.”

“And what separates them?” Ulric pressed.

“I–I don’t really know. Nothing?”

Ulric rocked a hand back and forth. “Not exactly. They are still separate. And so long as they’re separate, so is the power they provide you.”

“So the gift is about tearing down those barriers?” Ethan guessed.

“I wouldn’t go tearing apart your own soul. Certainly not with your imperfect understanding of it. Tell me, how do you see these different…places?”

“I assumed it was the same for everyone. They’re like little environments that suit each Familiar. What’s it like for you?”

“Private,” Ulric said. “But you don’t need to know about me. The question is why it looks the way it does to you.”

“I thought it was just what suited them. That the Familiars chose those places.”

“They can’t control the shape of your soul, Doctor Bishop. No one can do that but you.”

“So…I made those places.” He saw the look on Ulric’s face. “My perception of them made those places.”

“Better. And in your perception, what are your affinities? What are your Familiars?”

Ethan didn’t answer right away, knowing that Ulric wanted something deeper than the obvious. He shook his head. “They’re beings. They’re sources of power.” His eyebrow went up. “They’re separate sources of power.”

Ulric finally gave that slight half-smile of his. “And if they’re truly separate, if there are walls between them, even if you can’t see them, what does that mean?”

Ethan nodded, returning the smile. “It means I can only use one at a time. I have to cycle, just like I did at Dawn and Dusk.”

“Because you are…?” Ulric pressed.

“I’m a bunch of different beings at once,” Ethan answered, finally seeing the problem. “As long as I see us as separate, the powers are separate. I think I finally get why everyone says it takes so long to figure this out. You have to use your powers long enough that they become totally natural to you.”

“Not exactly,” Ulric said. “This is where magic meets mind. Yes, some people just eventually stop seeing the line between themselves and the powers they’ve gained. It’s actually easier for those who feel less connected to their Familiars, and treat them more as soulless sources of power. Much harder for someone like you, who can speak and have conversations with Tomo. How can you not see yourself as two separate beings?”

“That seems…insurmountable,” Ethan said.

“Not exactly. Again, this isn’t science, it’s magic. It’s power. It’s perception. You don’t need Tomo to disappear for him to be part of you. He is part of you already. You just don’t see it that way. Thus, the soul-searching, in a more literal way than most even see it.”

And that was that. Ulric had given what tips he could, but in the end it really was about Ethan changing how he saw himself. About his soul recognizing every part of his being–Famiilars included–as a single entity.

And to do that, he needed to start at the beginning.

“I thought I was a doctor,” he said, looking down at his healing rune. “But my soul decided I was an Assassin. Maybe I never really faced the why behind that.” He let out a long breath. This was uncomfortably close to therapy–where he was his own, annoying therapist, forcing himself to admit things he’d rather remain hidden.

“But I was never just a doctor. I was a man trying to be a healer, when I felt the call of the soldier, the warrior. I…I lied to myself, deluded myself. That part of me that did want to march into battle, fight for survival, fight for others, I just kept covering it up. Hiding it beneath lies and deception.” He made himself say the next part, as much as he didn’t want to. “I was a healer, but I knew deep down that I could kill. And that made me feel like a killer was all I was.”

Ethan cracked open an eye, looking down at his own Soul Rune. Maybe it would tingle or something if he was getting close? Probably not.

“I’m older now,” he said, feeling as if he were talking to his younger, more confused self. “I’ve seen that side of me, the side that kills. I’m not afraid of it anymore, and I know it isn’t all I am. I’ve healed gods. I am a healer, and a killer.”

Ethan felt something. It wasn’t a convenient tingle, it was more like a resonance deep within himself. He was on the right track. Accepting that duality was definitely a part of what he needed to do to reach the next step, but it wasn’t everything. Maybe if he had nothing but healing Familiars, it really would be enough, but his friends were a lot more diverse than that.

He was supposed to accept that each of his affinities was a part of him now, and that wasn’t a simple concept. If he was Revan and Flare, then he was fire and destruction, power without limit. If he was Tomo, he was illusions and lies. And if he was Deevee…

“Damn it,” he said aloud. “Maybe I can convince myself I’m destruction. I certainly break enough shit to handle that. And Mystic might actually be the easiest. There’s a reason why Tomo and I get along, after all. He first joined me because he could sense my own lies and self-deception. But Deevee?”

In answer, the hydra appeared before him, just one head, and laid down in a coil, like a snake. It rested there, staring up at Ethan with something like an amused look on his dragon’s face. Well, what am I? He seemed to be asking.

“You’re too many things, Deevee!” Ethan chided. “You’re a teleporting, reality altering being of unknowable power. You’re genuinely multiple beings–hell, I guess I am too,” he realized. “I really do have four heads now, with Apollo, Zakiel, and Strategus.”

Deevee nodded approvingly, and Ethan laughed. “Fine, I guess I have some sense of each of you, and I feel like we have things in common. But that’s a big step away from just feeling we’re one being.”

The hydra stood up, looking more serious. Then a second hydra appeared next to him, then two more. Four Deevees crowded around Ethan. Then they merged back into one.

Ethan paused, considering. “I never considered that I might have an expert on board,” he mused. “You’re four, and one,” he acknowledged. “Thanks buddy.”

The hydra bowed its head slightly, then disappeared back into Ethan’s soul.

“Okay,” he said. “I have…an outline, I guess. Call it an intellectual understanding of the whole thing. That’s a big step forward, but you can’t change soul-deep self perception just by intellectualizing things, or therapy would be pretty damn easy. I have to feel it,” he said.

Once again, the insistence that this was a long process made a frustrating amount of sense. He settled down again, eyes closed, and let his mind flow lazily over those observations. He was sure he was on the right track, but this took work, and he needed to put in the hours.

Despite his lack of meaningful progress past that point, he was a bit disappointed when Fable spoke quietly from nearby. “Doctor Bishop, I’m sorry to disturb you, but Mistress Selina was quite insistent. We’ve arrived at the city ruins.”
