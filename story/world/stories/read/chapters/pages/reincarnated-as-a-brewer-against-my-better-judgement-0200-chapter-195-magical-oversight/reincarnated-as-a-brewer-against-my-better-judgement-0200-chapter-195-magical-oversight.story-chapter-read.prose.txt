Eight days felt like a lot of time to Alric, but he knew it would pass quickly.

He was sitting in his office, having just returned from charging the heating stones at the distillery for filling the second washback, but they would need to keep running the boilers even if they weren’t using the water. Malting the grain would take both heat and moisture.

Alric looked over the list of tasks he needed to get done before they started distilling properly. The bakery needed an inspection before they started working with yeast properly. He and Stromni would need to work together on the crossbow lever. Gwyn had started fitting his female guards and had some questions.

At the top of the page, however, was Lessa.

Seren had mentioned just after the party that Lessa wanted a meeting. Alric assumed it was about Margaret and the mage testing. Just as he was trying to remember how many days it had been since the testing, there was a knock on the door.

“Come in,” Alric said, putting the lists down.

Lessa entered, wearing the maid's uniform with her hair tied back. She closed the door behind her before turning and giving him a curtsy.

“Good morning, Mister Alric.” She said, keeping her gaze low.

Alric paused briefly; in this world, saying good morning to someone often came across as trying to seem like nobility. The standard greetings were ‘I see you’, ‘hail’, or ‘hello’.

“Morning, Lessa, please sit.” Alric said, gesturing to the two chairs on the opposite side of his desk.

Lessa nodded before taking the one on the left, which might have meant something, but Alric wasn’t sure.

“Seren said you wanted to speak to me?” Alric said, putting his elbows on the desk and leaning forward. He was determined to treat Lessa like anyone else, making eye contact and avoiding brain melt.

“Uh, yes Mister Alric.” Lessa said, blinking at him. She made eye contact before lowering her eyes to his desk. “I have thought about Mistress Margaret’s offer. I mean, uh, well, your offer?”

Alric waved it away and waited.

“May I ask something first, though?” She said, making eye contact again. Alric nodded. “If I become a mage, may I also still be your maid?”

Alric sat back slightly; he glanced at the wall, pulling his lips to the side.

Seems like way too much.

“You mean do both?” Alric asked. Lessa just nodded.

Battery pretty much charges the brewery and then studies. He’s been at it for over a year now. He’s made major progress, but it's also come with complications. It’s too much.

“I don’t think it’s a good idea.” Alric said eventually. “It’s not because I don’t think you couldn’t, I think you’d have two jobs and all the work here. You’d end up working either early mornings or late nights so you can have lessons. I don’t know if you could concentrate on lessons and learning if you aren’t getting enough sleep.”

Lessa nodded slowly. When she didn’t say anything, Alric continued.

“We could try something strange, like half days, but I just think I’d be overworking you.” Alric said with a small shrug, “Becoming a mage takes a lot of study. It strikes me as a bit unfair.”

“I understand,” Lessa said, studying the desk again. Alric waited. She looked up again. “Then I must decline, Mister Alric. I would like to stay a maid.”

Alric’s eyebrows went up. “I’m not going to pry, Lessa. You don’t need to answer if you don’t want to, but may I ask why?”

Lessa was quiet for a long while. Alric was just about to tell her she didn’t need to answer when she spoke up again, “The mages don’t get paid, Mister Alric.”

Alric opened his mouth and blinked, but Lessa continued. “Mistress Margaret explained it to me. The lessons are free. You are provided for in exchange for an oath, but there are no wages.”

Damn. I screwed up.

Alric put his head in his hand, turning slightly. He stared down at the desk. His tongue rubbed the front of his teeth.

“I send most of my wages to my family.” Lessa explained when Alric stayed silent, “If I am a mage, I cannot help them.” That prompted a nod from Alric, but his thoughts were elsewhere.

Margaret runs the Atelier, largely for her cousins. They are certainly too young to work, and I can’t pay them a salary. But Battery? I mean, Helfried, Helfried is doing more than the rest, and he isn’t getting paid. Damn it.

Alric tilted his head the other way.

This all made sense at first. He was charging the brewery for magic lessons, but it sure doesn’t make sense now. Margaret has a budget, though… No, I can’t blame Margaret for this. She is buying supplies. I never mentioned wages. This one is on me.

“I am sorry to have upset you, Mister Alric,” Lessa said, looking down again. That brought his attention back.

“Hmm… No, it’s not that. It only occurred to me now that Battery is doing most of the Ateliers' work while not getting paid.” Alric said, glancing up.

I told the atelier they would need to donate mana, and then they got books and magical stuff. The kids make it more complicated. Margaret is obviously teaching, Battery is working, I can’t ask the kids to donate mana and be exhausted all the time.

Alric let out a deep sigh. “That’s not right.”

Lessa shifted in the seat. “I did not mean to complain about Battery.”

“You aren’t,” Alric said, lifting a hand. “I just should have noticed it.”

He took his earlier list and pulled it closer. He added Battery Salary to the list, then wondered if it should come from him or the atelier. Margaret should be paid as well.

“Alright, we can sort that out just now,” Alric said, pushing the list away, turning his attention back to Lessa. “The issue now is I can’t just pay for everyone being a mage. Some are already being taught for free. They will work for me when older. So, if you stop being a maid, I need something else to pay you for.”

“Yes, Mister Alric.”

Alric tilted his head at her response. “Can you think of anything you could do as a mage? Other than charging stones?”

Lessa blinked rapidly and tilted her head downward. “I don’t know, Mister Alric. I’m not a mage.”

“Right, stupid question, sorry. Shall we ask Margaret?” Alric said, already standing up.

Lessa nodded but watched him move to the door.

Seren’s office was opposite his; she was standing beside another maid, pointing at something on a folded piece of cloth. Both looked up at him when he went to the door.

“Seren, can you send someone to fetch Margaret?” Alric asked from the doorway.

Seren smiled, as did the other maids. “Of course, Mister Alric.”

“Oh, and tea, please. For three.” Alric added. Both maids nodded, but the other maid was already heading to the door; she gave a small, practiced curtsy, but her gaze lingered on Alric as she left. Alric watched her go; he hadn’t seen that maid before. Something about her demeanor bothered him.

“That’s the new hire I was telling you about,” Seren said with a smile and pointed to the passage. “She’s very well trained.”

“And how old is she?” Alric asked.

“Twenty-two.”

“Hmm…”

“Is something bothering you, Mister Alric?” Seren asked. She tilted her head and frowned.

“No. She just seems a bit too practiced, if you know what I mean.” Alric said with a shrug.

“Uh? No?” Seren said, her frown deepening.

“It's very convenient, is all; just keep an eye on her,” Alric said, glancing back. Seren nodded but was still frowning.

Maybe I’m just paranoid. People are out of work all the time.

Alric shrugged and headed back to the office. He had just sat down when Margaret arrived. She walked into the office wearing a grey robe, a bit more drab than he was used to seeing her in.

“You wanted me?” Margaret asked, tilting her head. She glanced at Lessa and smiled.

“Yes, please come sit.” Alric said, gesturing to the other chair. Margaret sat down but gave Alric a suspicious look.

Alric glanced over at Lessa; this felt like it might have needed to be a management discussion, but she was here now. If he brought it up, she would undoubtedly ask anyway.

“Tell me something, are you paying Battery?” Alric asked. He turned his attention to Margaret as he said it.

“Should I be?” Margaret asked, her expression turning more suspicious.

“Is that a no?” Alric asked, tilting his head.

“No. I’m not, but should I be?” Margaret asked, tilting her head to the side.

“I’m not accusing you of anything, Margaret. It just occurred to me that you and Battery are doing actual work for the atelier and—”

“I’ll take a wage,” Margaret said, brightening.

Alric ran a hand over his face. “We can talk about that just now. Let’s deal with this first.”

Discussing her cousin's upkeep and such around Lessa feels a bit tacky. That was the original deal. Battery is the issue.

“Nicolette and Victoria are getting lessons. We will discuss your issues after this. However, Battery is doing nearly all the donating for the atelier for the same lessons.” Alric explained.

Margaret nodded and tilted her head back. “Ahh, that’s because you don’t want Nicolette and Victoria donating though? They can.”

Alric shook his head. “I was thinking of paying for his tuition before that, but that’s not the case now. People are getting it for free—”

A knock on the door interrupted Alric. “Come in.” He said with a small sigh, unsure whether he was getting his point across. The new maid entered, and Alric’s attention sharpened on her.

She came in smiling; she curtsied with a tray in hand, stepped forward, and served the tea without getting in anyone's way. Alric stopped talking and just watched her. She said nothing and just smiled. Alric was watching her carefully, and it didn’t seem to make her nervous at all.

She’s better at this than Lessa already. Training is one thing, but… bah. What do I know?

Margaret was about to speak, but Alric gave her a look; she frowned and watched until the maid left. Once the door closed, Margaret pointed at it and gave Alric a questioning look; he just shook his head.

“Back to the point. Battery should be paid. I also think it should come out of the atelier's budget.” Alric said, pointing to Margaret. “We worked off a work-for-your-tuition model before, but that doesn’t seem fair now.”

Margaret reached for her tea, took a sip, and sat back. “If we grow the atelier, you may have people fighting to donate mana, you know?” Margaret said with a shrug.

Alric nodded; that was true. “There can be a middle ground. I’m not saying pay someone a ridiculous amount. They are still getting tuition.” Alric said, to which Margaret swirled her tea in her mouth, thinking.

Alric turned back to Lessa, “Lessa, may I ask what you are being paid now?”

Lessa jumped; she had been looking at the wall while they spoke. “Uh, twenty copper a week, two large.”

Margaret nodded. “I see what you mean now.”

Alric gestured to her. “So think of it like a stipend. We cover tuition and living expenses; they get a stipend if they are doing work directly above their training, I mean.”

“That makes a lot of sense,” Margaret said. She nodded again and glanced over at Lessa.

“Then that brings me to the next issue: what could Lessa do as a mage?” Alric asked,

He had barely finished his sentence, and Margaret answered immediately, “Guard.”

Alric and Lessa both turned to look at her wide eyed. She sipped her tea, clearly enjoying their shock.

“The guards?” Alric asked, tilting his head.

“Alric, learning basic shields is doable quickly. Also, if you were attacked by a mage, you could probably throw up enough shields to last a week, but you can’t fight back.” Margaret said with a distinctly smug shrug.

Alric opened his mouth, then tilted back, “We're working on that; I’m getting a—”

“You mean the crossbow girls? Mages' shields work quite well against those. You’d need to really hammer them. You need some kind of attack mage.” Margaret said, putting the empty teacup down.

Alric opened and closed his mouth; he was about to ask more, but Lessa spoke up first.

“Will it make me powerful?” Lessa asked, her gaze intense as she looked at Margaret.

Alric and Margaret were struck dumb. Alric asked first, though, seeing a whole new risk.

“What?” Alric asked more to buy time than anything.

“If I learn magic like this, will I become powerful?” Lessa asked, looking across at Alric. She quickly recoiled, her gaze dropping to her skirt. Margaret didn’t say anything, but she was clearly studying Lessa carefully.

“Why do you want to be powerful, Lessa?” Alric asked; he felt the heckles on his neck rise.

Lessa looked away; she glanced at the floor for a while. Both Alric and Margaret leaned forward.

“I...” Lessa began but then shook her head. “My whole life, I have been trying to be careful. Who I speak to, what I say…” Lessa then took a deep breath. Both Alric and Margaret stayed silent. Finally, she looked up. “I don’t want to be helpless anymore.”

Alric’s eyes went wide. Margaret, however, had a predatory grin.
