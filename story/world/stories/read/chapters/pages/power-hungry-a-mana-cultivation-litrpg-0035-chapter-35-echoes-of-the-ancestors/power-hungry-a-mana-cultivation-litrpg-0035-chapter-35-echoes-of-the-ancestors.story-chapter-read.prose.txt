The Sullied started breaking down the sealed door in earnest. Darius’s ascension ritual would take hours. He didn’t know if the door would hold but he couldn’t take chances. The best course of action would be to do exactly what one of the Sullied had said. Bring mana fiends their way.

Cale hopped on his hoverboard and made sure the mana crystal pouch on his belt was ready for easy access and went hunting for the demons.

“Aura,” Cale said as he sped along the corridor. “Could I suck in all of these ten crystals I have and use that energy blast to take out those guys.”

“If you took all ten in immediately, you might cause my system to expunge it. But I doubt it. I was designed so that you cannot abuse it. I cannot access the function. It just happens if you overload on mana.”

“You don’t think ten is enough?” Cale asked and frowned as he listened for screeches or sounds of soft sneaking. “I think I made a blast happen with less before?”

“That was when you had the mana manipulation levels of a particularly idiotic infant, you ignoramus,” Aura said. “Now your control is more refined and the mana is unlikely to trigger the unloading effect.”

“So it’s kind of like vomiting,” Cale said.

“Please spare me of this analogy…”

Cale wasn’t about to, but he ran across three manafiends.

“Hey guys,” Cale called them. They immediately turned and snarled. “Come and get it.”

Like the mindless beasts they were, the mana fiends took the bait. They were damned fast, but Cale knew the way. Followed by the violent screeches Cale sped through the corridors, the smooth walls turning into a mass of blur. Cale turned a corner and there the three Sullied were.

“It’s that kid!” the burly woman said.

“Surprise, assholes,” Cale said and sped right at them. The woman tried to grab him but he had Ghost Step ready. He used it instantly when he came in range, dodging the woman’s grab, DR-12’s metal bar and flew right through the thin guy, pressing his hoverboard against the wall.

Before the Sullied could react, the demons crashed into them. What ensued was an all-out brawl. The demons danced around in blurs of violence, dashing around. To his chagrin, Cale noticed quickly that the Mana Circuitry Sullied would easily overpower the manafiends.

So Cale tried to tilt the odds. He danced around with his hoverboard and got meaty punches in as he zipped through the conflict. He got good mileage out of Ghost step, but that cost him a crystal. Luck was on his side, and the demons were completely occupied with the Sullied. Despite their rage, one died. Then another. Cale went in for a hoverboard charged punch, but this time DR-12 kicked the board. Cale tumbled on his face and the hoverboard slammed into the wall, breaking into sparking bits. It was no cultivator gear after all, and DR-12’s kick had been fueled with mana and rage.

Cale oofed as a boot pressed on his neck.

“What do we have here,” the thin man said. He crouched and sneered at Cale’s face and spat. “Little runt playing the hero.”

“What do you want to do with him?” the woman asked.

Dr-12 grunted and pressed the boot harder on Cale’s neck. “I want to kill him. He’s Darius’s little pet.”

“He’s worth more to us alive,” the woman said.

“I don’t care,” DR-12 said. “I don't want to play their games. I just want to make them pay.”

The cold metal pipe traced down Cale’s spine, slow, deliberate. DR-12 rolled it in his grip, savoring the moment. Testing the weight. Preparing the strike. A cold sweat broke and Cale was breathing through his mouth rapidly. Every muscle and fiber of his being tensed as he tried to fight off the boot off his neck.

“Sucks, doesn’t it,” DR-12 said and let out a rough laugh. “To be on the losing side. Scream for me. Promise you will scream for me…”

DR-12 laughed and the pipe lifted from his back.

“Die!”

When the pipe smashed down, Cale relaxed all of his muscles and expanded his mana. The metal passed him and rang as it struck the stone.

“This tricky bastard,” DR-12 growled. “Grab him!”

Cale dove. He pushed his head down and his body followed his will. He swam into the floor. It was of thin make, maybe two inches. Just enough for him to not get stuck in it in some physically impossible way. Cale fell. He hit the floor and all air escaped his lungs. He coughed and turned around. He could faintly hear DR-12 banging the floor with his metal pipe. Cale let out a weak smile.

Cale looked around the room. It was compact, thirty square feet. The room hummed with an almost sacred stillness. The walls were smooth, carved from the same seamless Nevani stone he had seen before, but here… here they carried a different weight. Faint silver lines ran through the surface, pulsing like veins, feeding into the central platform. The air was thick, charged—not with dust or decay, but with something deeper. Something waiting.

The platform itself stood like an altar, raised slightly above the ground, its surface engraved with flowing sigils that flickered in rhythm with the room’s low hum. Around it, a circle of managlass panels hovered in midair, each one dark and waiting, as if they had been sleeping for centuries. The entire structure had the quiet patience of a temple abandoned by its gods, left untouched until the rightful heir returned.

There were no signs of decay. No cracks, no dust, no wear. Just silence and expectation.

Cale exhaled slowly, suddenly aware of how small he felt in this space. As if the very walls had been waiting for him to step inside.

Then, as he moved, the room responded. The panels flared to life, bathing the chamber in soft blue luminescence. The sigils brightened, forming intricate pathways of light, leading toward the center—toward him. The hum deepened, resonating in his bones, and for a brief moment, Cale felt something he hadn’t before.

Recognition.

This place had reacted to him. It knew him.

And that was far more terrifying than being a stranger.

“Aura…” Cale said. “What is this place?

“Ooh! I think I know. Yes! This room is meant for us!”

“For us?” Cale echoed, a strange weight settling in his chest. “You mean… me? You?”

“Yes, me and you, the Omega project subjects and their AI symbiotes,” she said impatiently. “Don’t just stand there asking inane questions! Get up on the platform!”

Aura was clearly giddy and impatient, and Cale saw no reason to not oblige her. He pressed his shaking hands into fists. It didn’t help much to calm his nerves.

He stepped up in the middle of the platform. He heard a slight hiss below him and the dais he was on lowered slightly.

“Hello,” A deep and rich female voice said in a formal tone. “Please identify yourself with a mana signature.”

“Do I just…?” Cale muttered and started cycling his mana.

“Thank you,” the female voice said. “Omega project subject 0005, Cale Bishop. Identified.”

“Omega project…?”

“Oh!” Aura said excitedly. “The Omega project!”

“You know what that is?”

“Not a clue.”

“Please, place your hand here,” the serene female voice said and a panel in the shape of a hand pushed forward from the myriad of panels.

“What’s going on?” Cale asked.

The female voice clearly was not the conversational type, so it repeated itself. “Please, place your hand here.”

“Should we do it?” Cale asked.

“Yes! Cale, this place was made by my creators for us! This is what we have been looking for. It is a legacy.”

“Yeah…” Cale said and swallowed.

He understood. Of course he did. This had indeed been just what he had been looking for. Wasn’t that what he had resolved in the ruins he had awoken? Get strong and find out who you are.

Well here are the answers… And yet…

And yet, Cale was afraid. What if he was better off not knowing? What if the truth was something he was not ready to face?

What am I so afraid of..?

The background frustration of not knowing could end right here and right now. But that frustration had been his companion for a while now. He was used to it. He knew it, was almost comfortable with the pain.

This…

This could be anything.

“Who am I?” Cale asked quietly.

He felt Aura stir in his mind, ready to snap impatiently at him. But Aura stayed her hand and watched Cale silently.

Cale asked the question again. Tasted it in his mouth. He knew the gnawing fear, even though he couldn’t quite give it a name.

It was the fear that he was artificial, just like Aura. It was the fear of finding out he was manufactured. That all of his placid memories were not even real.

Then he remembered the deja vu he had had in the simulation chamber.

“We exalt you for your sacrifice.”

Cale’s resolve strengthened.

“Tell me who I am,” he said and pressed his hand on the platform.

No immediate answers came. Instead Cale got a spike of pain. Something was injected into him.

“Gah!”

“Please do not move during the procedure. We are upgrading your Adaptive Unification Resonance Algorithm to level two. You have been injected with mana-attuned nanobots.”

“Aura..?”

“Hahaa! Tremble at my feet inferiors. I am ascending to even greater heights, towards the awesome sovereign goddess of the universe and everything!”

“Aura what’s happening?”

Once it is done, it will launch into the final phase. We must hope that your calculations are correct, or this world will be consumed.”
