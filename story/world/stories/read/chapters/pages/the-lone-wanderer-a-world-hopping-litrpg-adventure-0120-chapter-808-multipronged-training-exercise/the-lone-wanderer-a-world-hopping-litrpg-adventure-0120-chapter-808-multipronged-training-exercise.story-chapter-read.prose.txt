Borrowing phantom mana from his main body, Percy began replicating the enchanted slabs and planting them deep underground with the help of his sentries.

Thankfully, both the constructs and the minions carrying them could phase through solid surfaces, so scattering the slabs beneath and around the village wasn’t very difficult. If Percy tried to materialize his mana while it overlapped with a physical object, the masses would push against one another until they created enough empty space for both, otherwise his constructs would remain incorporeal.

In this case, the dirt was soft and loose enough to push around without much difficulty. Even if Percy stumbled upon a large underground boulder or a harder patch of dirt, he could always have his host circle around the village until it was close enough for him to push the soil aside with his domain.

Preparing a formation large enough to encompass the small settlement while relying on the pitiful trickle of mana that he could channel through the cord was somewhat time-consuming, yet this was still a much more manageable project than the enormous mana-gathering array he had drawn around the Fungal Spire.

Percy’s greatest limitation was the Orange squirrel’s ability to withstand his familiar’s soul-zapping lightning. While the slabs had been forged out of Percy’s relatively gentle phantom mana, the enchantments had to be carved into the grey material using spectral lightning to make the resulting beam more powerful. Consequently, both mana types were required to replicate the constructs, and Percy’s host could only handle so much, even with the powerful domain reinforcing its body and soul.

Despite those restrictions, the formation was completed in just under a week.

Had Percy been willing to approach and introduce himself to the natives, he could have avoided a lot of trouble and cured them of their affliction by now, yet this was supposed to be as much an experiment and training opportunity as it was an act of kindness.

Once the giant array was ready, Percy sent five person-shaped sentries into the village, transforming them into a small band of travelling merchants.

Outside of his runecrafting endeavours, Percy had spent a lot of time spying on not just the residents of the small settlement, but also every other town and village he had been able to find within three hundred kilometres of his host. For his plan to succeed, he would have to interact with these people like members of their species, which meant that he had to understand their language, customs, and social behaviour quite well.

His translation device technically allowed him to speak and comprehend their tongue, but he didn’t want his minions to seem suspicious if their words didn’t match the movements of their lips or their body language looked out of place.

After some back and forth with the village elders, Percy’s band of fake merchants were allowed to build a small hut by the edge of the settlement to live in for the foreseeable future – until they finished selling their “wares” to the residents.

Fortunately, the people of this planet cleansed their cores using liquid elixirs like on Remior, so Percy was able to smuggle some resources through his spatial seal and ethereal cord without arousing suspicion.

The locals obviously didn’t have access to anything overly fancy like Aurora Dew or Gloomy Dawn, so Percy had been forced to take a short break from granting new cores to the Starry Knights to brew a few thousand doses of regular elixirs with his main body. He could have requested some from the Guild, but it was faster to spend a few minutes brewing them himself.

The people of Mapel knew about spatial devices, yet such artifacts were quite scarce – just like they were on Remior – so Percy had his minions pretend to carry the elixirs into the village in large cloth bags. The good news was that the residents were used to diluting their resources, so Percy’s limited stash would hopefully last for months – more than enough time to complete his project.

With all the pieces of his operation ready, Percy finally activated the giant formation.

The runes glowed dimly, though they did so dozens of metres below the surface, where nobody could see them. The enchantments weren’t very powerful either, partly because they were spread out over a very large area, and partly because Percy had designed them to run on ambient mana.

He couldn’t possibly fuel such a massive array for a prolonged period of time via his own reserves, so he was forced to make do with less potent enchantments. The resulting beam was a hundred times weaker than the one he’d been using on the critters, which had the fortunate side-effect of making it almost imperceptible in both regular vision and Mana Sense. Even the people standing directly within its area of influence were unlikely to notice it.

That suited Percy just fine, because he didn’t want to draw the villagers’ attention.

The formation was designed to be flexible and semi-modular, allowing him to focus the beam on any circular spot in the village if he wanted. And he would have no choice but to rely mostly on that feature, because that was the only way to generate enough power to actually put a dent in the corruption. The surface of the affected region would be no smaller than a tenth of the entire settlement, and the effect of the concentrated beam would grow proportionally stronger.

Percy’s tests had revealed that his targets would feel a warm draft blowing against their fur if he focused the beam. It wouldn’t be very conspicuous if he limited it to brief and controlled bursts, though he would have to agitate the villagers at the same time to give the pitiful beam a chance to purify a part of the apes’ corruption.

And that was precisely the crux of Percy’s plan.

He had given his sentries an excuse to infiltrate the village, interact with its residents daily, and dangle an expensive bag of elixirs, as well as a pile of money in front of their faces to intentionally provoke their greed. That should give him plenty of opportunities to cleanse the dark masses swirling beneath their skin.

It was admittedly a rather contrived solution to the problem, but it would allow him to hone many important skills at the same time.

For one, he and Micky had grown much better at disguising their sentries, but their spell had hit a wall at some point. They used to be limited to the physical shapes that they had personally experienced through their adventures, yet they could now imitate the appearance and behaviour of just about any creature they had spent some time studying. However, mimicking an alien rat wasn’t the same as talking and interacting with sapients for extended periods of time while acting as their kin.

Furthermore, Percy’s mindsets had boosted his cognitive abilities enough to control his friend’s entire army of constructs, yet seamlessly coordinating the puppets’ movements was still a work in progress. During Percy’s heated battle against the quasi-divine beast, the bubbles of information had inadvertently fallen out of alignment several times, creating small but troublesome gaps in the giant sensory domain that had made his life harder.

Percy’s Sage’s Pond and Micky’s Shapeshifting Sentries were two of their most powerful utility spells and had been greatly strengthened by their recent upgrades, though both techniques could be improved further. Percy and his fellow aspect already had enough processing power and all the pieces necessary to improve or even merge the spells, so the only thing missing was some practice.

Ideally, Percy and Micky wanted the ability to focus on both the low-level actions of each individual construct and the high-level movements of the entire army. Making the sentries act like convincing spies was already difficult without worrying about having them cover each other’s blind spots as they moved around, yet both parts were necessary for Percy to fully take advantage of the spells in his future adventures.

Finally, there was the demonic infestation and his formation to consider.

The beam was just about powerful enough to potentially cleanse someone while they were agitated, but it wouldn’t be easy to manufacture the right circumstances for each activation. Percy would also be in a race against time to cure all the villagers, because the corruption wouldn’t just sit idly and wait for him to finish – it would likely keep spreading and undoing his efforts.

Based on tidbits of information he’d caught while eavesdropping on the natives, Percy knew that the local deities and demigods regularly patrolled their settlements to manually cleanse their people and limit the spread of the corruption. Despite the villagers’ careful measures, they would have all succumbed to the infestation a long time ago if there hadn’t been anyone to purify their bodies every so often.

Nobody in the area seemed to know exactly how long it would be before the authorities next visited, yet Percy would have to finish the job by then, otherwise his efforts would be interrupted.
