The first thing that Charlemagne felt upon his return to “normal” space was cold metal underneath his feet. His sight returned at almost the exact same time, granting him far more information about his new location than the metallic floor. He was in a long, dimly lit hallway. Directly in front of him was a giant brown-furred creature with no head and eight limbs. The rooster tensed in anticipation of an attack, but the other being ignored him completely. Charlemagne remained vigilant nonetheless.

Craning his neck to see better, the rooster noticed that in front of the brown creature there was a slimy-looking human with too many fingers and a strange fin on tops of its head. In front of that was some sort of canine that had a head at both ends. And in front of that was yet another strange being. Looking behind him, Charlemagne discovered two things. The first was that another creature had appeared behind him, although he was sure the space had been empty moments before. The second was that the floor was slowly moving, which was good because the rooster was starting to feel trapped in the narrow space. The spot of light directly ahead slowly grew as the floor propelled the line of creatures forward, and, eventually, Charlemagne found himself out in the open again.

His eyes and magical senses were immediately overwhelmed by the amount of information they conveyed to his avian brain, causing his adrenaline levels to spike. The moving walkway helpfully supplied a mild shock of some form of calming mana, preventing the rooster from embarking on a hormone-fueled killing spree.

“Ah, we’ve got ourselves a waker,” cried a purple-skinned human wearing a khaki-colored uniform. “Listen, just hang on a minute and we’ll get you out of the line,” the being added, looking directly at the rooster.

Charlemagne counted, but it was more like five minutes before another uniformed person, this one red-skinned with four legs and one arm, scampered up.

“Get this one over to processing now, he looks a bit too skittish for my tastes,” the purple human ordered. “And find out when my relief is coming! I’m not eating lunch here again.”

The red-skinned being yelled something unintelligible back before racing over to Charlemagne and beckoning him to join it with its arm.

“Come right this way, recruit!” it yelled in a voice so deep that it felt less like language and more like an earthquake. Thankfully, the Squiggles translated it just fine.

Charlemagne, curious as to where he was and what he was supposed to do, followed without complaint. The four-legged creature led the rooster through a series of winding tunnels that led upward. After the fiftieth turn, Charlemagne was about to confront his guide about getting them lost, but just then the pair emerged into the open air again. The empty space in front of them was enormous, allowing the rooster to catch a glimpse of just how large the area he had arrived truly was. Line after line of slowly moving walkways stretched out into the distance as far as Charlemagne’s sharp eyes could see.

“Impressive, ain’t it,” the red-skinned creature in front rumbled as he stopped in front of a strange tube and gestured for the rooster to get inside. “But I don’t have time to sight-see. The captain wants to be relieved, and I’ve got to find someone to replace him. Not that he’s doing anything useful there, but regulations is regulations. Now come on, get in.”

“Bawk?” the rooster asked.

“I don’t know how it works, but it will get you where you need to go really quickly,” came the honest but simple answer.

As soon as Charlemagne entered the device, the four-legged creature slammed a hatch shut behind him and banged on it twice.

“Good luck!” he called, the rumble of his voice mingling with that of some sort of engine. Then the tube accelerated so quickly that Charlemagne was almost flung off his feet, forcing him to dig his talons into the metal floor to stay upright. When the initial kick of acceleration finally faded, the rooster looked around for a window. There were none. The only thing to see in the cabin was a small, flickering light set directly into the middle of the ceiling. Then it went out.

The next thing that Charlemagne knew, he was flying through the air, once again subject to the g-forces accompanying intense acceleration. This time, he was thrown forward, his beak colliding with the front of the tube with a metallic clang. As the rooster recovered, the strange contraption ground to a halt. The hatch opened of its own accord, and Charlemagne stepped out.

“Ah yes, you must be the waker,” a high-pitched voice carrying just a trace of disappointment announced.

“Bawk?”

“You know, a waker. Someone who arrives already awake. It’s a bit of a big deal, although don’t let that get to your head. Maybe you just have a high level Sleep Resistance Skill, or maybe your species is immune to effects that render you unconscious. Or maybe…well nevermind. You’re here, and we’ve got to get you to orientation immediately. Believe it or not, I have things to do that don’t involve waiting for slowpokes like you. I’m Dilnoza, by the way.”

Charlemagne took a good look at the owner of the voice, which was a small, sticky creature with no visible limbs and two eyestalks growing out of the top front of its cylindrical body. An armored shell covered much of its back half.

“Bawk,” the rooster demanded.

“Yes, yes, but first we have to get you through orientation. Then you’ll be assigned to a Development Officer to ensure that you have the tools that you need to survive out there, and then a Missions Officer will work with an Assignments Officer to build you a team and get you out into the field. Oh, I can’t forget that you’ll get a chance to see the Quartermaster to draw equipment. But, hurry! The Orientation Officer is quite strict. She’ll know if we stand around gabbing too long.”

Charlemagne resisted the urge to point out that it was the other creature that was doing all the talking. He was in a hurry to get to orientation and find out just what he had signed himself up for.

“Bawk,” he said with as much politeness as he could muster, which wasn’t really all that much.

“Race ya!” Dilnoza yelled as she suddenly shot forward with a velocity that belied her short stature. The rooster was forced to engage his Afterburner Skill in order not to lose sight of his new guide.

“Oh hey, you are kinda fast! Let’s kick it up a notch!” cried Charlemagne’s newest guide. A high-pitched whine filled the area as jagged yellow lines appeared on the creature’s armored shell. She shot ahead with a tiny sonic boom, a blur of color the only sign of her passage. That, and a thick trail of some kind of slime.

Charlemagne ran faster, allowing his Ignition Core to ramp up as he pushed himself faster than he had ever run before. Even though he could certainly win if he took the skies, the proud rooster wanted to beat the other creature without relying on his wings. It was a close thing, but he overtook his tiny guide just as they reached a dead end.

“Aww, no fair. I was just getting warmed up,” she whined. “Next time, I guess. We’re here, now be quiet! Salma, the Orientation Officer on duty, has ears like a wombochu.”

The door slowly opened, revealing an enormous lecture hall with small pods built into a curved wall that all faced a small, well-lit dais. Behind a podium on the dais stood a tall, thin woman, almost twice as tall as any human Charlemagne had seen before but thinner than the average person. The effect made the rooster wonder if the Orientation Officer was malnourished before realizing that her biology was simply different.

“Go on, find a spot. You’ve got less than two point three minutes before it begins. I’ll see you around, Charlemagne,” Dilnoza said before zooming off. Although the Orientation Officer said nothing, the rooster couldn’t help but notice one of her four eyes following the tiny creature as she retreated. He climbed up a convenient set of stairs until he found a vacant spot that was roughly his size. The inside of the pod was hot and humid, just the way that Charlemagne liked it. He settled in and roosted properly for a few moments before turning his attention back to the front of the room.

“Welcome, creatures one and all, to the System’s Favor. I am Orientation Officer Salma Hiin. You probably are all asking yourselves what you have gotten yourselves into. Well, it’s too late to back out now, so I won’t sugarcoat it: you’ve gotten yourselves involved in the greatest armed conflict that has ever existed, at least as far as we can tell. The nigh-omnipotent and very benevolent System has succeeded in spreading its reach to just over one-third of all known universes. But that success has not come without cost. The very existence of the System threatens those who have hoarded power through technology, through unsanctioned magic, or through superior genetics, and a group of tyrants have banded together in order to eliminate our beloved System, one universe at a time.

But the System has not just persevered but has grown stronger through adversity. We ourselves grow stronger through trials and tribulations, and the System grows stronger with us. That is one reason why the System allows lower-leveled volunteers such as yourselves to participate in the larger war, not just because you can make a difference, but because your very growth is a catalyst that provides the System with much-needed energy. The Officers aboard the System’s Favor are charged with ensuring that you receive every chance possible to hone your Skills, earn Achievements, and continuously strive for the next Level.

And just what is the System’s Favor, you may ask. It is merely the largest Item ever constructed, a mobile fortress, training center, logistics hub, and much more rolled into one. The vehicle itself broadcasts the System’s influence, allowing even the most powerful among us the ability to fight in its shadow without fear of a System lockout. During your period of service, you will train here, you will eat here, you will sleep here, and, if you’re lucky, you might even find a mate here. And you will do so knowing that there is no force in any universe that can destroy this vessel. The System itself created the outer hull and defensive shielding that protects us.

Now, some of the more curious of you might wonder where exactly the System’s Favor is located right now. The answer to that question is that we are currently in the middle of a Dyson swarm surrounding a supermassive black hole at the center of an unnamed galaxy in a universe known only as ‘2813308004-H’. The anti-System forces have created a set of massive installations here, spanning several light years across and built in secret over thousands of years, using the incredible energies emitted by the black hole’s accretion disk to power a weapon of unknown function and potency. I expect each and every one of you to be willing to lay down your life in order to prevent the enemy from successfully completing and firing their super weapon. I say that not out of a misguided sense of loyalty, although the System has been a kind master, but with the knowledge that if this weapon is ever completed and fired, none of us will survive the aftermath. It could even be the end of the System as we know it. But we will not allow that to happen, for we are fighting to preserve our homes, our way of life, and our freedoms. These anti-System forces want to go back to the old ways, suppressing the rest of the multiverse in a misguided attempt to maintain an eternal status quo with them on top.

Now, it is my privilege and honor to introduce you to our Commander: a man so famous that most of you have probably seen recordings of his battles. Unfortunately, he couldn’t be here in person today, but he wanted to greet you nonetheless. Please turn your attention to the screen for an important message from the Symbol of the System himself, Paragon Prime!”

The wall behind Orientation Officer Salma suddenly lit up, and the image of a muscle-bound biped wearing a red and white suit appeared.
