Iberious did continue, for nearly an hour.  Systematically, the A Grade Guildmaster questioned Casiniax about anything and everything related to the various entities connected to Voorth Capital.  It was clear that the orc had figured out far more than the skrell attorney expected, and Casiniax was soon relegated to little more than deflection and denial of involvement.  As the skrell’s face got progressively gloomier through the constant bombardment of questions, Iberious only seemed to gain momentum, each query asked with increasing gusto.

While much of it went over Kyle’s head, the general points were clear.  Voorth Capital, whoever they were, had their hands in some particularly shady dealings.  Ger’Sinh and his backer were entirely unrelated to Cain Jewelers, excepting that they both had connections through the financial giant.  Voorth Capital acted as an information broker, referring Finn’el to Cain Jewelers to help them complete their survey of Kertan Six, while also selling the data from the findings to other interested parties, which was how the anti-Archdrake group ended up on the planet in the first place.

As Iberious systematically laid out, their fingerprints were all over the spiriting away of Gregory Valentine, among the disappearance of several other powerful C Grade criminals.  Payments were structured in obscure enough ways to provide plausible deniability, but the overwhelming amount of information the orc presented created a damning case for the financiers.  Clearly Casiniax felt similarly.  His expression darkened moment by moment as he realized he’d fallen right into the Guildmaster’s trap.

Kyle realized during the course of the one-sided interrogation that this was likely Iberious’ plan from the moment he was assigned to the Tribunal as a special investigator.  Kyle found it oddly comforting that the A Grade cared enough about the circumstances surrounding the accusations levied against a random C Grade to cut to the heart of the issue, and then actively seek to uncover the truth, no matter how bad it made one of the member civilizations look.  Maybe, just maybe, he’d been too quick to judge the Collective after all.

Kyle’s focus snapped back to the proceedings as Iberious turned to the projection of the dragon.

“And with that, I will once again reinforce my recommendations,” The Guildmaster said.  “Kyle Mayhew is to be fully reinstated, and given compensation in the form of no fewer than twelve million credits.  Furthermore, he is to be kept involved to his own satisfaction in any Collective investigations into Voorth Capital or the disaster that occurred on Kertan Six.  The matter of the missing C Grade is a grave one, and I believe that Mr. Mayhew is owed an opportunity to seek retribution there in any manner he so chooses.

“As to the matter of Voorth Capital, their assets and documents are to be frozen, and their leadership is to be brought before a Tribunal to determine the full scope of their wrongdoings.  And, as stated previously, neither they nor any of their affiliates are to be involved in the planetary auction for Kertan Six, or any other action involving the system where the disaster took place.”

The dragon simply nodded, and Kyle was certain he caught the ghost of a smile on the goblin representative’s face as Iberious concluded.  The Verdant Republic had been the brunt of many accusations from Casiniax, and Kyle could only imagine how vindicated the goblin felt hearing the recommendations passed down.

“So it will be,” the dragon rumbled.  “This Tribunal recognizes the recommendation of the special investigator, and will take action consistent with it.  Mr. Mayhew, you will receive a confirmation shortly.  This matter is dismissed.”

A moment later, the screens highlighting the Tribunal disappeared, leaving Kyle, C.H.A.D.D., Suierrillax, and Jarberry standing in the middle of Suierrillax’s office.

“Well, that was unexpected,” Suierrillax said, walking back to her chair and slumping into it.  “Do you have any idea how much time I wasted preparing a defense for you?  Just to have pork chop stroll in and pick apart the case better than I ever could have?”

[WE GREATLY APPRECIATE THE TIME YOU INVESTED, MS. SUIERRILLAX,] C.H.A.D.D. said before Kyle could speak up.  [AND THE PREPARATION WAS WELL-WARRANTED IN CASE THIS HADN’T GONE THE WAY THAT IT DID.  BESIDES, YOUR WORK STILL SETS US UP FOR WHAT COMES NEXT.]

“Very true,” Jarberry agreed.  “So, I take it you’re still stuck on wanting to bid on Kertan Six?”

“I’d like to,” Kyle said.  “No idea how much it will cost, though.  Even with the unexpected credits, something tells me planets go for more than a few million.”

“You can say that again,” Jarberry said with a chuckle.  “It’s out of your price range for sure.  Lucky for you, you’ve got some rich friends.  One rich friend in particular, at least.”

“You’re going to make a bid?” Kyle asked with a raised eyebrow.

“On guild wages?”  Jarberry scoffed.  “Not a chance.  I’ve been keeping in touch with Marcus, and I think he’s got a bit of an interest after we reviewed some of the old, publicly available information on the planet.  You’re going to need to make a major investment here too, and not just in terms of credits.  You’ll need to help him develop some infrastructure.  Once it’s done, though, you should be set up for a solid return on those investments.”

Kyle shot Jarberry a look, and the imp quickly added, “And help the refugees from your planet, of course.”

“Are you still planning to confront Valentine, or has that suicidal idea gotten out of your head?”  Suierrillax asked.

“We have unfinished business,” Kyle replied.  “And give me some credit where it’s due, I’m not picking a fight I think I’m going to lose.”

“I’m just saying, if it was me, I’d make it a point to stay away from people trying to kill me,” Suierrillax said with raised palms.  “Regardless, if you’re going to work with Marcus on sorting out the Auction details, I’ll get to work on chasing down the various leads we have on Valentine.  I’ll also get to work reviewing all the other data available from Voorth Capital once it starts getting released.  I’ll also remind you that you, and by extension, I, get paid by completing jobs; something you should keep on your radar.”

“I won’t start slacking,” Kyle said.  “We’ll get business sorted with the auction, I’ll finish up the job for the Pillar, and depending on timing, I’ll also try to accept another special job I was told should be showing up any day now.  That should keep the credits flowing.”

“There’s the workhorse I’ve come to know,” Suierrillax said with mock sweetness.  Getting more serious, she continued.  “And Kyle, I’m glad this is behind you.  Re-integrating was a lot harder than I thought it would be, and I’m really happy that you’ll be able to get things back to normal here soon.”

“Thank you,” Kyle said, looking between Suierrillax and Jarberry  “To both of you.  I wouldn’t have made it back without your help.”

“Just keep that in mind when we need favors down the road,” Jarberry joked.

[WE WILL.  AND SHOULD EITHER OF YOU HAVE NEED OF US, WE WILL BE THERE.]

Kyle felt the conviction in C.H.A.D.D.’s words, a sentiment he echoed wholeheartedly.  “We won’t forget it.”

“Good,” Suierrillax said.  “Now that’s about all the sappy stuff I can handle.  Go talk to Marcus, and figure out how you plan to buy a planet.”

The Gray Conglomerate’s financial district was a buzz of activity as a huge collection of people shuffled this way and that, all watching the ever-changing market landscape.  Commodities were traded, contracts signed, and equities purchased through the seemingly-endless exchanges set up within the Gray Conglomerate, all backed by the rock-solid credit currency of the Collective.

Amidst the distraction, nobody took notice of the lone human working his way through the crowds.  His face was plain, with rounded features and short-cropped brown hair touched with gray.  He wore standard business casual slacks and a long-sleeved shirt, a bit underdressed for the district, but not so much so as to draw attention.  Truly unremarkable in every way.  If anybody had been watching closely, however, they would have noticed that he wasn’t watching the markets at all.  Instead, he was making his way slowly and methodically toward the offices of Voorth Capital.

Waiting for a mixed group of skrell and goblins to scan in, he followed them inside the building, not garnering as much as a second look.  Using the credentials he’d been provided, the man then scanned his way into an elevator to the twelfth floor of the imposing building, before slipping into a restroom.  The next bit would be trickier, but that’s why Valentine was sent in, after all.

With an effort of will, Valentine forced the mycelium his body was fused with to change shape, his features shifting to match the sharp face that he’d been preparing, a velgian who had, up until a day ago, been working as an executive assistant to Voorth Capital’s managing director.  A face that Valentine had studied extensively, as he’d been the one to kill the unsuspecting man.  As his new jobs went, the velgian hadn’t been a particularly exciting target, but he wasn’t one to complain.  He’d been given plenty of more interesting work, and killing the assistant was just one part of a much grander plan.

Once he was satisfied with the transformation, he pulled out a new outfit from his spatial ring – a truly remarkable object – and quickly changed, preparing for the next phase of his job.  His employer was clear that he had to keep things quiet for as long as possible, because there was no clear path of extraction in the heart of the Gray Conglomerate’s financial district.  If things got locked down, Gregory Valentine would be left in the cold.

Pretending to be on a call, Valentine, wearing the velgian’s face, made his way back to the elevators, using the stolen credentials to rise up to the thirty-third floor; the highest he could go before passing the next layer of security.

Fortunately, that part would be easy.  Along with the new clothes, Valentine also pulled out a binder full of documents and a plate of tantalizing looking pastries.  Doing his best to give the impression of a harried and overworked assistant, he walked right up to the checkpoint, gesturing to the guards that he needed to get through.  The guards were only D Grades, and clearly bored with their assignment.  He was passed through to the executive elevators without as much as a second glance, quickly using his credentials to climb to the top floor, where his target waited.

The moment he stepped out of the elevator into the executive suite, the skrell man sitting behind a dark wooden desk raised a dismissive hand.  “Pack your things and get the hell out of here.  You left early yesterday without approval, and you’re late today.  Your future at this company is done.”

Several screens were pulled up, and Valentine suspected he knew what they were.  After all, he’d been hired to tie up this loose end the moment the news reached his employer that their financiers were under investigation.  Of the different executives, only one knew enough details to materially compromise them; a problem Gregory was going to solve.  In a flash of motion, Gregory dropped the binder and pastries to the floor as he lunged for the distracted skrell.

To the man’s credit, despite only being a high-tier D Grade, he reacted with a defensive enchantment woven into his desk and suit, projecting a barrier around him as he called for security.  Unfortunately, it wasn’t enough against Gregory’s attack.  The barrier shattered.  A small, hidden dagger materialized in Valentine’s hand, being driven straight through the skrell’s eye, killing him instantly.  In the same moment, Gregory pulled out the body of the identically-dressed velgian, allowing the body to be further damaged by the remnant energies of the defensive skill.

Withdrawing the outer layer of his clothes back into his spatial storage, Gregory activated Predator’s Grace, his newly upgraded stealth ability, and disappeared into the shadows.  In a flicker of motion, he was back in the executive elevators, and then back to the thirty-third floor where he resumed his previous disguise under the veil of his stealth.  Ensuring he was caught on video exiting the building along with the masses, he made his way back to the teleporter, leaving the financial district just as the news broke across the Gray Conglomerate that there had been a murder.

He smiled at the headline as he followed the teleporters back to his new employer’s headquarters.  While the prey wasn’t terribly exciting this time around, there was still no thrill like using his skills to finish a job.  Even if he was still working for his mysterious employer, Gregory Valentine couldn’t remember ever feeling so free.
