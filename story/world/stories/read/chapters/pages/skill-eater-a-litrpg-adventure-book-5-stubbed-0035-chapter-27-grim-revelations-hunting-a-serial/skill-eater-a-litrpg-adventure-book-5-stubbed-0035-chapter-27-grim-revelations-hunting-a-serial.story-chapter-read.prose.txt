Sakura adjusted her mask while Darting from rooftop to rooftop, scanning her surroundings for danger as she took in the sprawling squalor comprising Lowtown’s poorest district.

The warren of refuse-ridden alleyways made the rest of the enclosure seem posh by comparison, which was quite a feat considering the degradation engulfing every corner of the city’s grime-crusted underbelly. It broke her heart to see people languishing in such lamentable confines, and she was determined to help Edge come up with a solution to the pressing problem.

Once we uncover the Ancient’s identity, we can sit down at the table with the Hunters’ Association, the Magisters, and the Syndicate, along with the delegation from Puppet Town. There must be a way to improve conditions down here and benefit everyone in the process. Any way you slice it, this situation isn’t sustainable. If something doesn't change soon, chaos will engulf World’s End. People are always most dangerous when they don’t have anything to lose.

On the bright side, she had completed her investigation of the Syndicate’s hideout. While there were faint traces of corruption lingering in the area, the Ancient hadn’t spent much time near the One-Stringed Puppet. The Best of the Worst and the woman Jumo was dating were in the clear. It was possible that one of their members was a monster in human form, but it should be safe to work with the faction’s leaders.

That brought Sakura to the other item on her agenda. Lowtown had experienced a rash of recent disappearances, sparking rumors of monsters lurking in the city settlement’s depths. The situation had taken a dramatic turn for the worse over the last few days, when mutilated bodies started showing up in public spaces.

Identifying the killer would improve the crew’s relationship with the Syndicate. However, she would have been down here regardless. She wanted to help everyone who was living in fear on top of their other problems and was happy to provide a service uniquely suited to her talents.

While she teleported from one roof to the next, Sakura reviewed everything Jumo had shared the night before. The timeline was spotty, since people could vanish for quite a while before anyone realized they were missing, but she was convinced there was a pattern. When she gathered evidence from the victims’ homes, swept the sites where their bodies had been found, and correlated it with the information the Syndicate had provided, she hoped to obtain some insights into the perpetrator’s motive and modus operandi.

She had already investigated the area with Remote Viewing to assess potential threats, but after obtaining Gift of the Magi, her energetic senses weren’t nearly as sharp while scrying as they were in the flesh. She needed every advantage she could get if she wanted to crack the case and quell the terror gripping the unfortunate souls living beneath the mixing bowl.

There was a chance that a stealthy beast was responsible, or one of the nightmarish monsters that prowled in Ord’s shadows. However, her instincts were telling her that the murders were too methodical and too successful to be the work of anything other than a person or a monster in human guise.

Sakura’s heart hadn’t stopped pounding since setting foot into Lowtown. This was the most dangerous investigation she had ever undertaken. The killer would try to eliminate her the moment they learned she was digging around. Fortunately, she wasn’t down here alone. This high-stakes mystery was the kind of puzzle Lore-Weaver adored, and the spider was peering through her eyes while watching her back every step of the way.

After evolving to stage three, the spinner of truth and lies was awake most of the time. They had spoken more over the past few days than the rest of their association combined, deepening their bond to a considerable degree.

Unlike Skill-Eater, unsealing Lore-Weaver’s relic hadn’t restored the lord’s missing memories. While the spider could recall scattered snippets from the distant past, everything after Skill-Eater was possessed by the dark passenger was a blank slate, including what happened between then and the System’s arrival, and why the other Lords of Ord had become Unique cores too.

Sakura had several competing theories about why the lords had undergone such dramatic transformations, ranging from the System consolidating its power over the planet to the twenty-one sealing themselves to take refuge from the Ancient monsters that had infested Realm-Traveler, Boundless-Well, and Skill-Thief.

The Wardens are suspects too. Creating Unique cores might be a method for controlling the lords’ magic, letting the scions access their power without granting them free rein.

While she pondered these weighty matters, the lion’s share of her attention was focused on the task at hand. Most of the people down here were uncored or early stage-one, but there were a handful of high-stage individuals mixed in with the crowd. She was wearing a yellow flower pinned to her cloak, signaling the Syndicate’s permission to use the roof roads and go wherever she pleased. However, not everyone was working for the Worst, and there was no guarantee that the killer was operating alone, regardless of its identity.

Not long after, Sakura arrived at her next stop.

She had decided to kick off her investigation by studying the dwellings of the victims whose remains had been found. She planned to search their homes, then move on to the sites where their bodies were uncovered.

She was hoping that in addition to the physical clues any detective might find, there would be lingering traces of magic linking the events, confirming the existence of a serial killer. She intended to establish their methodology, maybe even figure out a way to predict their behavior and prevent the next murder before it ensued.

She perched on the corner of the rooftop, ready to recon the area before entering the tiny one-room apartment where the victim had resided.

“We’re finally ready to unravel the threads,” Lore-Weaver spoke within her mind. “I can’t wait to see what lurks in the center of this web. Be careful, my scion. I will help to the extent I am able, but there is only so much I can do to shield you from this planet’s predators. It would be a shame to lose you before we put the pieces together.”

“Thank you, great weaver. I’m confident that working together, this puzzle will soon be solved.”

Having the spider’s direct cooperation was a rare occurrence and would make this inquiry a great deal easier. The lord was fickle and grew bored easily, but they had never tried to solve this kind of crime before, so the spider should be engaged for at least a while longer.

After making sure no hidden observers were positioned on the nearby buildings, Sakura turned her attention to her mana senses. They were always active to some extent, letting her perceive the ambient magic in her environment and spot anything out of the ordinary. But that was nothing compared to what happened when she focused on what she was feeling.

A million motes of light wavered into existence, enmeshing Lowtown within a scintillating tapestry of color and motion. The rich hues and dazzling patterns took her breath away as her eyes beheld the exquisite splendor of Ord’s living magic. Magicytes were heavier than air. While they were everywhere, they were thickest along the rooftops and ground, surging and swirling in a jaw-dropping display.

The raw magicytes were gorgeous, but they were only one element among many, the loudest instrument in the symphony resounding across every corner of the planet. Sakura could perceive countless skills too—powers forged from the mana refined within the reactors of cored creatures. Spells were ordered and tightly woven. Examining their intricacies revealed their origin, strength, and purpose to a trained eye like her own.

Next came aether, the purified distillation of magicytes that powered magitech devices of all shapes and sizes. It was only present in trace elements—byproducts released when the machines burned fuel to manifest magical effects. Aether looked like sparkling clouds of dust and felt lifeless compared to the other energies dancing across the air.

While every element was fascinating in isolation, the most remarkable aspect was the way they interacted. The backdrop of magicytes revealed complex interference patterns generated by the thousands of active spells and devices in the free city at any given moment. The harmonies, dissonances, and arrangements followed laws beyond her ability to describe and offered a wealth of data to someone with her expertise.

The clarity and precision provided by Gift of the Magi brought tears to her eyes. Sakura had always been fascinated by the science of magic. Being able to perceive it so clearly was a dream come true. Becoming mana-sensitive was like removing a blindfold she’d worn all her life, boosting the power of her information-gathering skills across the board. She was even learning to perceive magic through her other senses, detecting skills by feel, sound, smell, and taste.

After surveying the premises, Sakura Darted onto a narrow balcony leading to the missing woman’s studio apartment. Jumo had assured her it was unlocked, and she had chosen an egress that wouldn’t reveal her presence to the other residents, minimizing her chances of being discovered.

She stepped through a sliding door and shut it behind her, running her gaze across the meager contents of the room. There wasn’t much to sift through. Only a cot, a desk with a rickety stool tucked beneath it, a waste basket, and a battered trunk resting in the corner. There was a single shift hanging from a peg on the wall, revealing that the woman had been killed wearing the only outfit she owned.

If she were trying to locate a missing person, Sakura would have analyzed the contents of the chamber to profile the woman’s mental state and recent actions to determine her whereabouts. However, she was investigating a murder, which meant she needed to pick up the perpetrator’s trail, along with clues as to how they had chosen their victim and performed the grisly deed.

When she viewed the room through her mana senses, it was blindingly obvious that something unnatural had occurred within these walls. Not because of anything she saw, but because there wasn’t anything to be found. Every scrap of magic had been erased from the chamber. A few magicytes had flown in when she opened the door, but every mote of the ambient magic dancing on the far side of the window had been scoured from the premises.

Paradoxically, the dearth of energy was what convinced her the woman had been assaulted within these walls. The perpetrator had methodically eliminated any evidence of their involvement, which led Sakura to suspect they had cast spells during the deed; otherwise, there would be no need for such drastic measures.

Next, she pulled on a white glove and ran her fingertips across the floor. Not so much as a speck of dirt or grain of dust remained, which meant the killer had wiped the crime scene of physical evidence too. There were skills capable of tracking someone using a single hair. While she didn’t have any herself, she’d been hoping to find something Jumo’s friends could use to assist in the hunt.

She had purchased a small device that made blood glow beneath its light. When she switched it on, there wasn’t a single microdroplet to be found. She tried pulling up a few floorboards, but there wasn’t any sign that blood had dripped into the cracks either. While the furniture and floor were worn, there wasn’t any recent damage or any sign that a struggle had ensued.

Sakura let out a disappointed sigh as she sat down on the stool and pulled out a notepad, collecting her thoughts while recording her observations. She still needed to examine the other residences, but she suspected she would discover more of the same. That being said, it wasn’t all bad news. While she hadn’t found any fresh leads, the perpetrator’s nature was considerably clearer than before.

This wasn’t the work of a predatory beast or a monster native to the Lakelands. Every aspect of this situation was premeditated, meticulously executed, and designed to deter pursuit. The Syndicate interviewed the building’s other residents, and no one noticed anything out of the ordinary.

Only a human or Ancient could have pulled off such a flawless abduction. They intended to remain in the settlement after the deed, or there wouldn’t be any reason to be this careful. Everything Sakura discovered led her to the same conclusion. There is a serial killer living in World’s End, and this level of sophistication implies they’ve done this many times before.

She said a prayer for the unfortunate soul who had dwelled in this room before moving on to the other addresses on her list. The remaining crime scenes weren’t nearly as well-preserved, but they all showed signs of the same methodical erasure of evidence. That meant her magic couldn’t help her track down the killer, but she could still investigate the old-fashioned way.

From the objects left behind and the details the Syndicate provided, Sakura fleshed out a profile of the perpetrator and the commonalities linking the victims. They all lived alone, had few friends, were unemployed, and lacked a regular routine—people who could go missing without making a splash.

They were all uncored, which meant they didn’t have any chance of defending themselves against a cored assailant. Taken together, it meant the murderer was patient and highly intelligent, willing to observe potential victims and wait for an ideal moment to strike. There might be dozens more who were never discovered.

“What’s your take on this mess?” she asked the ancient being dwelling within her core.

“These deeds were enacted by a consummate hunter. A killer skilled at living among its prey without revealing its existence. All signs indicate these people were alive when they were abducted, which means they were transported via a method that wouldn’t arouse suspicion. Our best bet is locating the site of the murders. I expect it is well-hidden, which means the perpetrator might not have taken such rigorous steps to erase their presence from the scene.”

“That collusion is well-reasoned. Your theory is in line with my observations, and I will take your advice to heart.”

Sakura moved on to the locations where the remains had been discovered. All but one was suspiciously easy to find. The Syndicate had found one body stuffed into a barrel and left to rot beside the road. Another was dumped in an alley, and a third was dismembered and shoved into the waste receptacle at a public restroom.

“Although they scrubbed all signs of their presence from the dump sites, this behavior runs completely counter to how cautious the perpetrator has been up to this point. I can only think of one reason for such a dramatic shift in methodology.”

“The killer wanted its victims to be found.”

“Exactly. These remains were discovered after we destroyed the dimensional anchor—around the same time Edge caught someone trying to turn a protest into a riot. It all makes sense if someone is trying to foment discord within the free city.”

“Suspicious indeed, my scion. We are unraveling the web of deceit obscuring the predator’s true nature. However, we have yet to uncover the knotted heart of the weave. Assuming that everything we’ve discovered thus far is the product of the killer’s plans, how do you intend to thwart their scheme and solve this intriguing mystery?”

“Like I said, the remains were placed in pubic, impossible-to-miss locations. All except one.”

They continued their conversation as Sakura made her way to the darkest and most isolated corner of Lowtown. Along the way, she reviewed the intel Jumo had obtained from the Syndicate, including the reasons this site was different from all the rest.

World’s End had multiple ways of dealing with refuse. Every building in the free city had access to plumbing and running water, which was filtered through a massive magitech facility before draining into the adjoining lake.

The situation was more complicated when it came to other types of garbage. Industrial waste was heavily regulated by the Magisters, although the details were beyond her. Food waste, debris, and worthless junk were dealt with differently depending on what part of the city you were in. There were garbage cans lining the main boulevards throughout the mixing bowl. Sanitation services dealt with the garbage produced by the residences, restaurants, and shops filling the upper levels. The refuse was composted, recycled, or broken down into raw materials via magical means.

While Lowtown had plumbing to process liquid waste, it lacked the other amenities. Instead of having their trash picked up, the residents had to dump it themselves. The spacious cavern contained three waste pits, commonly referred to as middens. The pits were home to a fungus that could break down almost anything given enough time but required specific nutrients that kept the shrooms from growing anywhere else.

The middens were big, smelly, and poorly tended. They were also free and convenient enough that most people used them instead of throwing their garbage into the streets. Two were located in accessible areas with ventilation ducts to contain the reek. The third midden was known as the Putrid Abyss. People only visited the Abyss when they wanted to dump objects that were particularly large, volatile, or needed to be disposed of away from prying eyes.

Sakura came to a stop a few blocks away, gagging at the putrescence filling the air. She swapped the mask she was using to conceal her identity for the gas mask she’d purchased before entering the Toxic Marsh, then completed the final leg of her trip.

She scanned the area for hidden observers and approached the edge of the midden, gazing at the disgusting, mushroom-covered mess below. She memorized everything she passed along the way, circling the precipice until she reached the spot where the bodies had been found... or body parts, to be more precise, as the Syndicate hadn’t discovered anything larger than an arm mixed in with the remains.

The Putrid Abyss had been used to dump bodies since its inception—a favorite place to dispose of evidence back when the settlement revolved around racking up views on the feed. It wasn’t surprising that the murderer had been using the midden, as the remains never would have been found if a garbage picker hadn’t fallen into the pit and stumbled across them on her way out.

There wasn’t any sign of corruption in the area, or any magic for that matter. It was clear that this site had been scrubbed just as meticulously as the others, which meant Sakura wasn’t going to find any clues sprinkled alongside the midden.

However, that wasn’t her goal. She knew the victims had been alive or at least intact when they were taken from their homes. Even in Lowtown, carrying bloody carcasses through populated areas was bound to attract attention, as covering them up wouldn’t be sufficient to stop superhuman Perception or sensory-boosting skills.

Instead, she was hoping to uncover the route the killer used to transport the remains from wherever it had dismembered the bodies to the Putrid Abyss. She made sure no one was watching and began a meticulous inspection of the area, trying to put herself in the perpetrator’s shoes while considering everything she knew about this portion of the settlement.

Half the pit was bordered by the cavern’s wall, limiting potential avenues of approach. The main path was too obvious for someone trying to avoid undue attention. Even in this destitute corner of Lowtown, there were people watching the route to the midden. After finding nothing from the ground, she Darted up to a ledge high on the cavern’s wall, inspecting the Abyss from above.

Edge mentioned finding a tunnel that ran beneath the mixing bowl, which means there are probably more. They would be perfect for transporting bodies, living or dead. If the killer is using them, it would explain why no one saw anything. If a tunnel were at ground level, it would be common knowledge by now, but maybe there’s something hidden higher up.

Sakura took a deep breath and cleared her mind, casting her senses into her environment while looking for the telltale traces that would indicate the presence of concealment magic. She took her time, tuning out the unpleasant environment as she panned her gaze across the wall.

She was getting ready to give up when she found it, a patch of stone thirty feet above where the body was found with a narrow ledge below it. There were faint motes of aether concentrated in the area, and the ambient flows of magicytes swirled in a slightly different pattern.

There was no way she was going to investigate in person, since the killer might be inside. Instead, she tucked herself into a crack in the stone, activated a magitech concealment device, and added a second device that would sound an alarm if anything approached her position.

Once they were up and running, she switched on Remote Viewing and approached the anomaly as an incorporeal projection. Sure enough, there was a sophisticated magitech machine hidden behind an illusion of solid stone. She didn’t sense any other magic in the area, so she pushed her projection through the false wall and emerged into a narrow passageway.

She proceeded under the assumption that the killer could spot her in this form, so she crept down the winding tunnel. It was incredibly intense, but she could cancel Remote Viewing and return to her body in an instant if something went wrong. She was still taking a considerable risk, since some types of magic could directly harm her mind, and being caught would invite retaliation.

Sakura passed by several chambers along the way, waiting to explore them until she finished mapping the central passageway. It led to three other locations within Lowtown—each exit high on the wall and shielded by a similar device. The secret passageways explained how the murderer transported the bodies without being spotted. However, she hadn’t discovered any clues that would reveal their identity, which meant it was time to check out the adjoining chambers and get the hell out of Dodge.

Two enclosures were empty and seemed to be used for storage. The third was pure nightmare fuel. When she passed through the doorway, a pile of rotting corpses met her gaze, far more victims than the Syndicate was aware of.

Sakura fought the urge to vomit. These people had been tortured before they were torn apart. Judging from the splatter patterns and ragged edges of their wounds, parts of their bodies had been eaten while they were still alive. She forced herself not to think about what she was doing and memorized everything to consider later.

That just left her with one final task. She canceled Remote Viewing, Darted to the tunnel, and entered the room in person. She shut out the remains and focused on the energy filling the chamber. A coruscating cloud of filth met her gaze—corruption so thick it warped the air. Dread writhed in her guts when she realized what she’d discovered.

Gods preserve us. As disturbing as the grisly scene was, her final revelation was even more horrifying. This was clearly the work of an Ancient, but that wasn’t the extent of the bad news. It was subtle, but the filth felt different from the tainted magic she’d encountered in the mixing bowl.

Sakura didn’t want to believe it, but the evidence was undeniable. There isn’t one Ancient lurking in the free city. There are two.
