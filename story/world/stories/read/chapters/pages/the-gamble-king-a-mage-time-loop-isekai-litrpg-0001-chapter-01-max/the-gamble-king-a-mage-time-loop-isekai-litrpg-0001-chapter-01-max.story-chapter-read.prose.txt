Confusion.

That was the first thing Max felt. Not pain, nor fear. Just complete, overwhelming confusion.

His name was Max. He remembered that much. Twenty-six years old. Lived in... where did he live? The memory felt slippery, like trying to hold water.

Muffled sounds penetrated his consciousness. Distant. Distorted. Like being underwater.

Where am I?

The question echoed in his skull, but no answer came. Just noise. Screaming? Thunder? The ground beneath him trembled with a rhythm that felt almost like—

Hoofbeats.

Something wet splashed across his face. Warm and sticky.

What...

Max tried to open his eyes, but his eyelids wouldn't cooperate. His body felt wrong. Too heavy. Not his. Like he was wearing someone else's skin.

Another splash of warmth. This time it hit his neck, soaking into fabric that didn't feel like his t-shirt should feel.

T-shirt? No, he wasn't wearing a t-shirt. He was wearing... leather? And something rough underneath. The wrongness of it finally forced his eyes half open.

The world was chaos.

Browns and grays and flashes of silver. Too much red. Movement everywhere—blurred shapes that his brain struggled to process. The sounds grew sharper now, resolving into distinct noises that made his stomach clench.

Men were screaming.

Metal was striking metal.

Horses were—

A memory suddenly surfaced like a drowning man breaking the surface: Two men in ski masks. A gun. The apartment floor, cold against his back. Blood filling his throat.

Max's breath caught. He'd... died.

I died?

He'd actually died. Shot by two idiots arguing over his PlayStation while he bled out on his living room floor.

And then... the message. From Sabo, author of the book he'd been reading for 14 years. He thought it was a prank. Some weird grief-induced hallucination as he bled out. "Accept or Decline." Something about the world being real, about needing him. He'd clicked Accept because what did it matter? He was dying anyway.

But if he'd died, then what was—

Something small and hard bounced off his forehead.

"Ow!" The word came out automatically, and his hand flew to his face. His fingers came away red. Not from the thing that hit him—that had been a pebble, kicked up by... by what?

Where...

Hell? Had he ended up in Hell?

Max had never been religious, but he'd always figured if there was an afterlife, he'd probably make the cut for the good place. He'd never killed anyone. Never stolen anything significant. He'd even saved a kid from getting hit by a truck once.

The noises grew sharper. A man nearby was gurgling, choking on something. Others were shouting words Max couldn't quite make out. The thunder he'd heard earlier clarified into what had to be hoofbeats—dozens, maybe hundreds of horses.

Panic rose in his chest. He blinked, trying to clear his vision. He was lying on his back in the bloody mud. He was covered in it—his clothes, his hands, his face.

Max looked down at himself. He indeed wasn't wearing his t-shirt and jeans anymore. Instead, he had on some kind of leather vest over a rough-spun shirt. Muddy brown pants. Boots that looked like they belonged in a Renaissance fair.

What the—

A sound like rolling thunder grew louder, and Max turned his head just in time to see a wall of horses charging directly toward him. At their head, a man in crude metal armor, face contorted with rage, eyes fixed directly on Max as he lowered a long spear to point directly at Max's chest.

"Whoa!" he screamed, scrambling backward through the mud.

All around him, chaos reigned. Men were fighting and dying in every direction. Some wore armor similar to the horseman charging him. Others were dressed more like Max, in leathers and rough cloth. Blood and mud coated everyone and everything.

Twenty feet away, a man's head separated from his body, spinning through the air in a spray of crimson before landing with a wet thud nearby. The eyes were still blinking.

This wasn't a dream. It wasn't a hallucination. The smells were too real—blood, shit, sweat, and fear, all mixing together in an overwhelming cocktail that made Max want to vomit.

The horseman was nearly upon him now. Max rolled to the side just as the spear plunged into the mud where he'd been lying. The momentum carried the rider past, but he was already wheeling his mount around for another pass.

Max staggered to his feet, his legs unsteady beneath him. Something heavy hung at his side. He looked down to see a short sword in a scabbard attached to his belt.

A sword? He didn't know how to use a sword! He'd never even taken fencing lessons!

Max jerked his head up at a guttural battle cry from his right. A huge man with a battle axe was charging toward him, mouth open in a scream that revealed several missing teeth.

Before Max could react, the man's chest erupted in a spray of blood. He fell forward, revealing a soldier behind him, sword red and dripping.

The soldier nodded at Max, as if they were comrades, then turned to engage another enemy.

What the hell was happening? Where was he? This couldn't be real.

But the blood on his face felt real. The screams sounded real. The fear coursing through his veins was definitely real.

In the distance, a horn sounded, three long blasts. The men in armor—the ones on horseback—seemed to be pressing forward more aggressively. The fighters dressed like Max were falling back, some running in full retreat.

Max took a step backward, then another. His foot hit something soft. He looked down to see a body, clutching a spear. Without thinking, Max grabbed the weapon. Better than nothing.

The horseman who'd nearly impaled him earlier was coming around again, this time with two others. All three had their spears leveled.

Max turned to run and found himself face to face with a creature straight out of a nightmare.

It stood about four feet tall, with mottled green-gray skin and pointed ears that stuck out from a misshapen head. Its eyes were yellow with vertical pupils, like a cat's, and its mouth was filled with jagged, uneven teeth. In its hand, it held a crude but very sharp-looking knife.

A... goblin?

The fuck?

It was an actual goblin. Like from books he'd read.

The creature lunged forward, knife aimed at Max's gut.

"Shit!" Max yelped, backpedaling so fast he tripped over the corpse behind him. He landed hard on his ass, the impact knocking the wind out of him. The spear he'd grabbed clattered to the ground.

The goblin cackled--an actual cackle, high-pitched and cruel. It hissed and snarled, saliva dripping from its jagged teeth. Above the goblin's head, floating as if painted in the air, was a small glowing numeral: 1.

Wait, number one? Why? What the fu–

"Fall back to the ridge!" someone shouted nearby. "Fall back! Defensive line!"

"Skewl's forces are breaking through the left flank!" another voice called.

The goblin sprang forward again. Max rolled sideways, mud and blood coating him as he scrambled away from the creature's slashing blade. The number 1 moved with it, hovering steadily over its misshapen head.

"Help!" Max screamed, his voice cracking with panic. "Somebody help me!"

No one came. Everyone was fighting their own battles, struggling to survive the chaos around them.

The three horsemen were closing in, no more than thirty yards away now. The goblin was between Max and his only escape route. He was trapped.

The creature growled deep in its throat, circling Max with its knife held low. Its nostrils flared as it sniffed the air, seemingly savoring the scent of Max's fear.

"Fuck off!" Max shouted, grabbing a handful of bloody mud and flinging it at the creature's face. "And what's with the number on your head?"

The goblin paused momentarily, confusion flickering across its yellow eyes, before ducking the mud easily. It let out a series of clicking noises, baring its teeth in what might have been a smile.

Behind the goblin, one of the soldiers in leather--like Max--was backing toward them, fending off an armored attacker with a shield and short sword.

"Harek!" the soldier called over his shoulder. "On your feet, you lazy shit! We're not dying here today!"

Was he Harek? Max had a moment of utter confusion before realizing--yes, that must be who he was now.

The goblin took advantage of Max's distraction, darting forward and slashing with its knife. Max barely managed to jerk away, but the blade still caught him across the forearm, slicing through the rough fabric of his shirt and into the flesh beneath.

"Fuck!" Max shrieked, clutching his bleeding arm. The cut wasn't deep, but it burned like fire.

"Harek, grab your fucking weapon!" the soldier shouted, still backing toward them. "Voros's balls, man, what's wrong with you?"

The goblin's jaws opened wider, revealing more jagged teeth. It made a wet, gurgling sound that might have been pleasure at the sight of blood. The number 1 continued to hover above it, taunting Max with its inexplicable presence.

The nearest horseman was close enough now that Max could hear him shouting something in a language he didn't understand--harsh and guttural, nothing like what the soldiers around him were speaking.

Max's eyes darted around frantically. The spear he'd dropped was lying a few feet away. If he could just reach it...

"Archers!" someone bellowed from the ridgeline behind Max. "Loose!"

A sound like rushing wind filled the air, followed by the distinctive thwack of arrows finding targets. One of the approaching horsemen pitched backward off his mount, three shafts protruding from his chest.

The goblin glanced up at the sound, momentarily distracted.

Max didn't think. He lunged for the spear, fingers closing around the wooden shaft. In the same motion, he brought it up and forward with all his strength, driving the point toward the goblin's midsection.

The creature was fast, but not fast enough. It tried to sidestep, but the spear caught it in the side, sinking several inches into its torso.

The goblin shrieked, a sound so high-pitched and alien that it made Max's hair stand on end. It grabbed the shaft with both hands, dropping its knife. Black blood bubbled from between its lips as it made guttural, choking sounds.

Max yanked the spear back, nearly losing his grip on the blood-slicked wood. The goblin fell to its knees, clutching its side, yellow eyes burning with hatred.

"Harek, move your ass!" The soldier who'd been calling to him was there now, grabbing Max by the shoulder and hauling him to his feet. "The line's falling back! We need to reach the ridge!"

Max stared at the man, taking in the blood-spattered face, the wild eyes, the genuine recognition there. This man knew Harek--knew the body Max was inhabiting--and expected him to know what to do.

"I... what's happening?" Max managed, his voice strange in his ears. Higher than his own, with an accent he couldn't place.

The soldier looked at him like he was insane. "What's happening? The fucking battle is happening! Skewl's forces broke through the eastern line to ambush us. The Horde is with them." He gestured at the dying goblin. "We fall back to the ridge and hope the archers give us enough cover to regroup. Now MOVE!"

He shoved Max toward the slope behind them, where soldiers in similar leather armor were retreating in somewhat organized fashion, turning occasionally to fend off pursuers.

The remaining horsemen were being held at bay by a volley of arrows, but more armored soldiers on foot were pushing forward, cutting down anyone too slow to retreat.

The battlefield stretched out for what seemed like miles in every direction--a churned-up mess of mud, blood, and bodies. Thousands of men fighting and dying. Banners fluttered in the smoke-filled air, most too distant or blood-soaked for Max to make out any insignia.

"Go!" the soldier shouted, giving Max another push. "I'll cover you!"

Max clutched his bleeding arm and took a stumbling step toward the ridge. The goblin's knife lay in the mud nearby. He stooped to grab it, then straightened and found himself staring into the yellow eyes of the goblin, who had somehow found the strength to lunge forward one last time, a smaller blade clutched in its dying hand.

It snarled, a sound full of hatred and rage, as it drove the blade toward Max's throat.

Max reacted on pure instinct, bringing up the goblin's own dropped knife. The blades met with a scrape of metal, then Max twisted his wrist and plunged the knife into the goblin's neck. Black blood spurted over his hand as the creature made a wet, gurgling sound.

The number 1 above its head flashed brightly, then disappeared as the light in its yellow eyes faded. The goblin collapsed, twitching and then still.

A surge of triumph rushed through Max. He'd done it! He'd killed the--

Something punched him in the back with the force of a sledgehammer. Max looked down in confusion to see the pointed end of a spear protruding from his chest, glistening red with his blood.

"What?" he managed, before his legs gave out.

As he fell to his knees, Max caught a glimpse of the horseman behind him, the one who'd thrown the spear. The man's face was grim, satisfied with his kill. Above his head, a glowing numeral hovered: 5.

Blood filled Max's mouth.

This is it, he thought. Dying again. The unfairness of it crashed over him--to survive being shot in his apartment only to die seconds after arriving... wherever this was.

Max collapsed face-first into the bloody mud. His vision dimmed, sounds growing muffled as his heartbeat thundered in his ears, slowing with each labored pulse.

Then stopped.

But consciousness remained.

Max was aware, fully aware, but no longer in his body. No longer on the battlefield. He existed in a void--absolute nothingness stretching in all directions. He had no form, no substance, just... awareness.

Floating directly in front of him was a glowing numeral: 1.

What does that mean? Max tried to speak, but had no mouth. Tried to reach out, but had no hands.

The number pulsed slightly, as if in response to his attention. It was the only thing in the endless darkness--a single point of light in the void.

Instinctively, Max focused his awareness on it, willing himself toward it. The number grew larger, brighter. If he'd had a body, he would have touched it. Instead, his consciousness pressed against it, enveloping it.

The numeral flashed brilliantly, blindingly bright, then shattered into countless motes of light.

The void collapsed around him.

Pain lanced through Max's body--his back, his legs, his head. He gasped, eyes flying open to find himself staring at a blood-streaked sky. Exactly the same sky he'd seen moments ago.

Wait, what?

Max pushed himself up on his elbows, wincing at the soreness in his muscles. He was lying in mud and blood, just as before. The same corpse was beneath him, he recognized the man's broken helmet and vacant stare.

Wait...

All around him, the battle raged on. Men screamed and died. Metal clashed against metal. Horses thundered across the churned-up field.

"Fall back to the ridge!" someone shouted nearby. "Fall back! Defensive line!"

Max's head whipped toward the voice. It was the same command he'd heard before. The exact same words, in the exact same voice.

"Skewl's forces are breaking through the left flank!" called another soldier--again, the identical warning Max had heard in what he thought were his final moments alive.

Directly in front of him, hovering in the air, was a glowing blue window with text:

[NUMBER OF REROLLS REMAINING: 0]

The window faded after a few seconds, dissolving into wisps of blue light.

Max scrambled to his feet, heart racing. Everything was the same. The bodies lying in the mud. The pattern of arrows sticking from the ground. The three horsemen in the distance, no more than thirty yards away and closing.

Behind them, Max spotted a fourth horseman, the one who had thrown the spear that killed him. The man was fitting another spear into his grip, eyes locked on Max's position.

"Harek!" called a voice to his right. "On your feet, you lazy shit! We're not dying here today!"

Max turned to see the same soldier as before, backing toward him while fending off an armored attacker. The exact same soldier, the exact same movements, the exact same words.

The fuck?

Max's eyes darted around frantically, searching for something–anything–that might be different. His gaze fell on a weapon lying in the mud nearby, the same spear he'd grabbed before.

The one he'd used to stab...

A high-pitched cackle cut through his thoughts. Max turned to see the goblin circling toward him with its knife held low. The same yellow eyes, the same jagged teeth, the same malevolent snarl. And above its head, this time, the glowing numeral: 0.

"Fuck off!" Max shouted automatically, then caught himself.

The goblin paused, yellow eyes narrowing slightly. This was different, a deviation from their previous encounter. It tilted its head, making a series of confused clicking sounds, as if trying to understand why this prey seemed to recognize the threat it posed.

Max looked past the goblin to the battlefield beyond. The horsemen were approaching at the same pace as before. The soldier who called him Harek was fighting the same enemy in the same way. Everything was identical to the first time.

Amidst the chaos of screams and clashing weapons, a strange thought crystallized in Max's mind.

For better or worse, this... was a time loop?
