Viktor opened “his” eyes.

Immediately, images of the surrounding scenery flooded into his awareness, replacing the black that had existed moments earlier. Trees stood under the night sky. Branches swayed in the wind. Patches of moonlight spilled through the canopy. And ahead, lay the stretch of riverbank where Ekon was waiting.

Then came the sounds. The steady current of the Voskryn rolled past, joined by the softer murmur of the stream that flowed down from the waterfall adjacent to his dungeon before emptying itself into the river proper. The woods likewise contributed their own quieter notes, with leaves rustling faintly overhead and critters chirping somewhere out of sight.

It seemed both visual and auditory perception functioned without issue.

However, it was also apparent those were the only senses available to him. “His” body felt absolutely nothing. Not weight, not temperature, not touch. To be fair, that was probably for the best. Otherwise, he would have been in a world of hurt right now.

He moved “his” legs.

One foot stepped forward, propelling the body forward in an uneven motion that caused his field of vision to sway awkwardly with each shift in balance. And that visual feedback was the only one he got. After all, even when the foot connected with the ground, he felt no response whatsoever. No pressure, no resistance, no confirmation that the limb had made contact with anything beyond the simple fact that the body continued to move without collapsing.

Fortunately, he had spent quite a bit of time practicing this in the dungeon, so he had no trouble doing it now. And just like that, he made “his” body move out of the woods, walking toward the open riverbank.

The instant Ekon spotted “him,” he jerked upright, then dropped immediately into a guarded stance, one hand moving toward the handle of the whip coiled around his waist as his eyes narrowed with naked hostility.

“You...”

Viktor approached the bald man at an unhurried pace, allowing the tension to linger for another moment before speaking through “his” mouth.

“It’s me.”

But Ekon didn’t relax right away. Suspicion still lingered on his face as his gaze swept over the body standing in front of him.

Viktor paid him no heed, turning toward the paved road that ran through the darkness between rows of abandoned watchtowers. It had been constructed on Gideon’s orders, and for a time it had seen constant use by adventurers moving back and forth between his dungeon and the town. However, after Iskora seized control of the outpost across the river, everything changed. Isadora and Rennald had decided to build an entirely new route that connected the southern part of Daelin to the dungeon, and once that road was completed, the old one quickly fell into neglect.

“So you can even do this, huh?” Ekon muttered, finally straightening up.

“This is but a simple trick,” Viktor said with a chuckle.

Though to be honest, he had to admit he was quite impressed by what the ringed cross could do. When he asked Khenemhotep to create magical artifacts for reanimating the dead, the only thing he had in mind was some mindless, shambling corpses that could follow basic commands.

Instead, he got this. A surprise, but not an unwelcome one.

After he inserted the ringed cross into a dead body, beyond simply giving it orders like he initially intended, he could also “possess” the corpse—see through it, hear through it, and control it like a marionette. To some extent, this was quite similar to how he navigated inside the dungeon whenever he closed his eyes. The only difference was that now he had to use physical limbs to move around instead of just gliding his vision forward. He could only control one puppet at a time even if he had several, of course, but he could instantly switch between them, and that provided a wide range of tactical possibilities.

And the best part was, the range of this thing was huge.

Khenemhotep had told him that control could be maintained at distances of up to ten miles. That was why, after he killed the masked man, he immediately flew back to his house, lay down on his bed, and then sent his consciousness here to “wake up” the corpse. And now, he would be able to enter the Folded Chamber, doing whatever he wanted without ever exposing himself to danger.

The spell was not without limitations, however. One major problem was that he couldn’t cast his Thauma through the corpse. Though at least the wind and fire gems were still usable as long as he strapped them to the body, which he already did.

Then there was the time constraint. While a ringed cross could keep its power for three days if unused, the moment it was embedded into a dead body, it was bound to it. He could no longer take it out to use on a different one, and the corpse only stayed reanimated for about two or three hours.

“When do your friends get here?” Viktor asked.

“Soon, I think.”

After tricking the bald man into bringing his party along for the attack on the masked men’s hideout, all the while dressing it up like he was actually the one doing them a favor, Viktor wasted no time setting his plan in motion. He told Ekon to write a letter, which he sent into Mandragora’s hut through a gust of conjured wind. So, by the time Ekon emerged from the dungeon this evening, one of her birds was already waiting outside. It then followed him here, to this meeting point. And now, there was nothing left to do but wait for the Druidess to arrive herself.

Before long, he spotted two dark silhouettes gliding through the sky toward their location under the pale glow of the moon. Their features were difficult to discern, but he could guess who they were easily enough. It was obvious Renee was one of them, while the other had to be the Druidess. No sign of Ba’atar, though. Apparently, the big guy still hadn’t returned to Daelin. A shame. He would have preferred more muscle. Still, this would do. Three members of Ekon’s party, plus a handful of walking corpses, ought to be enough to keep trouble off his back.

The two women descended from the sky and landed upon the riverbank, but neither of them approached too close after touching the earth. Instead, they kept a cautious distance, their attention fixed on the stolen body with unmistakable vigilance in their eyes.

“Who is this?” Mandragora asked.

The bald man gave Viktor a glance before answering. “This is the Sovereign, the lord of the dungeon I mentioned in the letter. He’s killed one of the masked men and...” He paused. “Now he’s possessing his corpse.”

Needless to say, that explanation brought no peace of mind to the two women. In fact, Mandragora’s expression now twisted into one of open disgust.

“And you thought it wise to involve yourself with someone like that?” she asked sharply.

Ekon sighed. “We need every bit of help we can get. He’s saved my life once. And now he’s helping me to take care of my remaining enemies.”

“I’ve told you before, and I’m going to tell you again,” Mandragora said with a frown. “We should leave this place. Go somewhere far away and never come back.”

“And then what? They’ll keep hunting me no matter where I go. And it’s not just me anymore. Because of your connection to me, both of you could end up in danger. They might decide to come after you as well.” He fell silent for a moment before speaking again, quieter this time. “Maybe you two should leave Daelin. I’ll stay here and deal with my own mess.”

Hey, that’s not what we agreed on.

“Come back with me to my forest,” Mandragora said, taking a step forward. “You’ll be safe there. What exactly are they going to do? March into the woods and wage war against the Circle?”

Now that was an interesting thought. The Children of the Abyss were an ancient organization with influence stretching across kingdoms, so Viktor found himself wondering what a war between them and the Druidesses would actually look like. One hell of a spectacle, no doubt.

Ekon shook his head. “I can’t do that. It’d drag even more people into this. You only say it because you don’t understand how dangerous they really are.”

“Then explain it to me,” Mandragora said. “Because even now, I still have no idea what the hell is going on. Weeks ago, you sent me a message saying you would be hiding inside the dungeon. After that, nothing. Complete silence. And then, yesterday, out of nowhere, I received another letter that told me to come here to launch an attack on what you describe as a secret base of operations of some sort.”

“You’re right,” Ekon said. “I do owe you that much.”

And so he began. He told them his story, starting from the night Viktor saved him, all the way to the present moment, when they stood on this riverbank preparing to assault the enemy’s stronghold. The bald man spoke mostly of himself, of what he had seen and what had been done to him. He mentioned once or twice “the voice in my head,” but other than that, he didn’t touch the topic of the Eternal Flame at all. He didn’t say anything about the Abyss either. Only that the masked men had a deep connection with the Valiant Bank and the adventurers from Iskora.

“So they’re the ones behind the disappearances of the pyromancers?” Mandragora said at last, her brow furrowing as she processed the information. “But why target them in the first place?”

Ekon shrugged. “Even now, I still don’t know.”

Viktor wondered whether the bald man was indeed ignorant, or if he simply chose not to talk about his tie with his so-called acquaintance, the mortal enemy of the Abyss.

“Wait,” said Renee, who had until now remained silent. “If they’re the ones kidnapping the pyromancers, then that means all those missing people are now being held in the hideout we’re about to attack, right? So, we might be able to rescue them?”

Well, yes. Probably. How many were still alive, though, was a completely different story.

“Even if that’s true,” Mandragora said, “I still don’t understand why it has to be us who save them. If you know where they are, just report it to the Guild and let them handle it.”

“It won’t be so simple,” Ekon said. “The Guild won’t take our word for it. They won’t mobilize just because we tell them to. Even in the best-case scenario, it would still take days, if not weeks, before they act. On the other hand, the enemy probably has spies in town. So if we report it, we’d only end up alerting them that their secret lair has been exposed. I believe, instead of involving too many people, it’s better to move in a small group, striking them hard and fast with the help of a powerful being.”

“A powerful being, huh?” Mandragora said, following his glance. Then, she stepped closer to Viktor, her gaze fixed on the corpse as though she was trying to see past the borrowed flesh and into the one sitting behind it. “You’re the lord of the dungeon. Which means you’re also the one who attacked us in that courtyard?”

Viktor let a short chuckle slip through the dead man’s throat. “Yes. Do you want to settle the score now?”

“How exactly would I do that?” the Druidess said dryly. “You’re not even here. Just your meat puppet.” She snorted. “Besides, picking this place for a meeting... you really have terrible taste, don’t you?”

Viktor turned his gaze to where she was looking. The paved road that led back to town. Ah, yes. Dagnar had died here.

“Why did you kill him?” Mandragora asked.

“Is that what matters to you?”

“Not the man himself. He means nothing to me. The point is, I don’t know you. Not your goal, not your motive, not what you really want out of this. I don’t know why you’re ‘helping’ us. Is it actually help... or are you using us as expendable pawns?”

Sharp woman, aren’t you?

“I’m helping you because those masked men are my enemies too. More importantly, this is Ekon’s idea. He was the one who begged me for help. If you decide you no longer want it, we can call this off right now.”

For a moment, there was only silence.

Then, Mandragora let out a long, tired sigh.

“So, what is the plan?”
