One of the things I got in this world, and boy were a lot of those, was the physical things I wanted. Not everything mind, I had yet to find a decent source of milk here, and that meant and that meant that things like pizza were lost to me, something that had been driving me slightly bonkers for the past decade or so. Most things however, if I wanted to build, I could, and so I did.

All around me at the moment was construction, walls going up, floors being set into place. I could already envision the location in question, and had, and done drawings in detail, but building it was going to be it's own chore. I watched from off to the side, a few bits of magic holding things in place while workers did their duties. I was one of few who could move stone blocks so easily as I was, placing each of the massive slabs exactly where it belonged.

Most of my attention though was on something else, a written out page of code, the basics of what would be our wards here. They were more intricate than anything except what was in my personal home, and more secure than those. Each line of the code was art, refined after years of work and experimentation.

Years ago Isha had lain the base for what was now being called a 'core' after many of the old anime. It was in fact a tiny magical computer, self-replicating, which would rest in the mind of a given individual. It was about a small gem, lodged in the dura mater, and more advanced than anything I'd ever worked on in my previous life. The structure of it was based on mana crystals, and three dimensional, there were normal logic gates in it for some functions, but so many more too, connections that wouldn't have been possible on a flatter surface.

I was built in a tiered system, three interconnected layers, each upon the other, which increased processing power exponentially. There were even points where it could be further expanded if needed, though only Isha, Chien, and I had the hardwired ability to add anything. We were also heavily restricting who could get these cores, as they were so powerful. They were also slightly alive, a spark Isha herself gave, not like a person is alive, but like a bacterium is alive, a simple replicating thing, but enough for them to cast magic, if you told them what to cast. The code was their DNA and mind, the instructions on what to cast, how, and where.

“Still working on that boss? Why not do it in your mind?” Chien questioned.

“And miss this beautiful day? Never.”

“The line about halfway down, there's an error,” he said, nodding to the page and I saw it. As much as he didn't care about it, Chien had studied the methods enough that he was scarily good at this by now.

“Good catch, but I doubt you came here for that,” I told him.

“The wind tells me that Adia is coming home soon,” he said.

“The wind or the people you have watching her?”“Why not both? Regardless, she will be back in the next few weeks.”“Yes, I've heard too.”“So... what are we doing for her return?”“Why should we do anything Chien? She doesn't need some massive greeting. We'll have dinner and talk. I'll have some of her favorite tea blend ready when she is.”

Adia had been gone before, she'd taken to wandering quite a bit of late. I didn't really get it, but it made her happy, and it made me happy when she came back, but I wasn't one to pressure her into some big party over it. If she'd wanted one she would have sent word and asked for one. We both knew that I would give her almost anything she wanted.

Of note, she hadn't joined as much in the core projects, which was why she'd never gotten the ability to alter them. She had one of course, and several enchanted items to protect her, both her designs and my own, but it wasn't something that called to her. That was fine, she was an adult and could make her decisions, I merely hoped she came home from time to time.

“Do you think she found anything?” he asked, voice suddenly serious.

“I doubt it, if she had she would have sent word. Yet she gets stronger every year, soon she'll have the answers her mother seeks.”“We both know what Isha thinks that answer is,” he said sadly.

“And we both know I believe her wrong. While I understand the meaning of it, the mechanism doesn't make sense. The most likely answer... is simply time and effort.”

“You're not wrong, I've done some asking around, but sadly there aren't many elders left, or many people who knew one from before.”“Find anything I haven't yet?” I asked.

“Sadly nothing concrete, but some reports from those who knew Neera.” That got my attention. “She was similar, though much older.”“Is that so? Have you told Isha yet?” I asked.

“No, thought you might want to.”

“Thank you, can you give me the details?”He did. One thing about our memories was that they were such a steel trap that we could replay entire conversations from them, making the transfer of knowledge simple. It had made pushing written language a pain, but there were a number of things that did better when written down, so written down they got. Many people though, and quite a few could read just fine, kept with the oral tradition though.

The information on Neera was interesting, not as much as I might have liked, but more detail than I'd had before. Her people were here and there, integrating in. Some remained up in Icehome, though few were there, some had migrated to the south, or to various mountaintops here and there, cooler places that they seemed to prefer. It made getting any info about their former leader a pain in the neck.

Sadly most of them were also kind of stupid. They'd had generations of people whose whole job was just to feed mana into a crystal, and frankly, that didn't lead to intellectual stimulation as much as it did vapid gossip. However in this case gossip was what we needed, gossip and genealogies, which went hand in hand. All the gossips wanted to know who was whose child, and brother, and cousin, if only so they knew who to ask about things.

The answers Chien fed me gave me some hope, small details on Neera's life, her children and grandchildren, what they were like, when they were born, the fathers for there were multiple, any issues they had, all of it. For the first time in some time I actually smiled at hearing these things. I actually had good news for my wife.
