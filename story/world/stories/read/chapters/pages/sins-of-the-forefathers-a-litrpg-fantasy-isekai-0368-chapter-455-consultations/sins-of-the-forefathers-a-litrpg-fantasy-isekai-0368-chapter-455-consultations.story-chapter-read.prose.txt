As the hidden door to Hermus’s home slid closed with a rumbling of stone on stone, I caught Rafael watching it. I couldn’t see the expression on his leathery face under the darkness of his hood, but there was a tenseness to his shoulders that betrayed his feelings.

I don’t know the full history of just how he had come to find the hidden sanctum of the Great Spirit of Death. Just like how I didn’t know his full reasons for faking his death, and abandoning not only his life and comrades, but his wife as well.

But I’m guessing it might have to do just what Hermus had taught him. He undoubtedly felt just a bit conflicted about abandoning the penitent Spirit.

His feelings weren’t my business, though.

Instead, what he could contribute was.

“How long will you stay with us?” I asked him shortly, breaking the silence around our party. The sound of my voice echoed quite far, here in the vastness of the caldera.

Slowly, almost grindingly, Rafael turned away from the hidden door to examine me silently. “Long enough to see this crisis solved,” He said roughly. “What were your next steps?”

I squeezed Sylvia’s hand in silent relief. A Lich on the level of Rafael’s would be very useful when it came time to assault Smaragd, much less when it came time to deal with Anguis and the fucking Savoy. We weren’t anywhere near either of those goals yet…but it was only a matter of time.

First, though…

“We’re heading north first,” I said. At Rafael’s cocked head, I elaborated. “While you’ve been attending the Great Spirit of Death, I’ve been talking to the Great Spirit of Life. Through her, I’ve arranged a meeting with another Spirit to the north, hiding somewhere beneath one of the Mountain Holds. Apparently, he can get us through the War Wards surrounding Smaragd, preventing us from getting to Fynneas. We just have to get to him first.”

Rafael shook his head lightly. “More Spirits,” He muttered before sighing. “The longer you live, Nathaniel, the more you shall realize that they have their tendrils everywhere. Enough of that, though. Does this Spirit have a name?”

“He calls himself ‘Nehustan’,” I said, sympathizing with the Lich’s exhaustion. They really did seem to be everywhere. “He’s apparently pretty old. Like, old to the extent that Anima has said he’s older than the Great Spirits themselves. I have no idea how that works, but I’ll take what help I can get.”

“Nehushtan, Nehushtan…” Rafael mumbled, leaning on his staff in thought. He eventually shook his head. “I’ve never heard the name. If this Spirit is older than my master, then I am surprised. He’s never mentioned the name to me. We shall see just what aid he can render. In the meantime…I was intending to ask you, child. Why, exactly, are you tromping about in some kind of construct body? Content to let your Familiar and woman risk themselves while you sit behind sturdy walls, are you?”

I felt my eyebrow twitch at the disapproval in his voice, while I heard Sylvia stifle a laugh. Fade didn’t bother hiding his, the little traitor. Instead, he just chuffed out a string of canine chuckles. “No, actually,” I said irritably. “I’m injured. This is the only way I can move around and get things done without killing myself. And you’re one to talk. You’re literally an up jumped ghost wearing a papery old dead guy like a leather suit. Where did you find him, eh? Dug him out of a ditch on the side of the road, maybe?”

That earned me a raspy laugh from the Lich, unbothered by my banter. “Does this form truly appear like cast-offs found in a ditch? I’m almost insulted. This is my body, boy. My original one from whence I was among the living. I…collected it, preserved it, and enhanced it in case I would once again find myself in need of, shall we say. Sufficient might.”

As Rafael tapped his robe right over his rib cage, I heard the sound of crystal, reminding me of just what was inside there. I furrowed my brow and shot the Lich a disbelieving look. “That’s…not your phylactery, is it?” I asked disbelievingly. “You do know we’re going to smack tab in the midst of heavy fighting soon, right? Are you really willing to risk it that way?”

That caused Rafael to draw back in an affronted way. The Lich drew himself up to his full height, which was…at least a few inches above my own. “Now I’m actually offended. Do you truly think myself so foolish as to risk my phylactery for you? There are precious few I would ever do so for, and you are not one of them, you ‘up jumped’ zygote. No, this isn’t my phylactery. That…is elsewhere. And I guarantee you, it is not where either you or the woman thinks it is.” He scoffed and started to mutter under his non-existent breath. “She cannot possibly believe I wouldn’t account for her knowledge of where we’ve been. Half the time I was the one arranging our transport in the first place…”

As Rafael trailed off into vaguely affronted mutterings about Grey, and a few about Honoka, too, I exchanged a look with Sylvia.

Look, I know. But I promise it would be worth it to take him along.

I had never forgotten just how easily the then ‘Tlazo’ had dealt with Nerexxa, after all.

Sylvia rolled her eyes at me, but still nodded. I turned back to face the Lich and coughed into one fist, catching his attention. “Anyway, we should go. There’s a lot to do back at the Unchained’s camp. There’ll be war meetings, explanations, and preparations to do. Both for the journey north and for my surgery. I want back in my own body, damnit.”

That knocked him out of his funk. Rafael looked up in an oddly eager manner, and to my surprise…

He had a smile in his voice.

“Surgery, eh? Tell me more…”

On one hand, I was a little impressed at the speed at which the Unchained guards stationed around the encampment found my little party, as we approached the ‘borders’ of it.

Such as they are. It’s not exactly a place with defined walls, after all.

On the other hand?

It was always a tad irritating to have an overzealous guard shove a sword in your face.

I kept my calm as the point of an extremely standard-looking Herztalian surplus arming sword wavered only inches from my face…but only just. This guy was just doing his job.

Thankfully, that was the extent of it. Moments later, one of the other guardsmen either recognized me or my description and waved us through into the camp. I suppose it was nice that the Unchained was taking its security seriously, but…you would have thought they’d say something about the extremely suspicious robed figure accompanying me.

Rafael didn’t exactly cut a trustworthy sight.

Then again, they might just be a bit harried, and it wasn’t hard to understand why. What the Unchained had down here was essentially a combination of a small town and a supply depot. As my group finally entered it, I could see that it wasn’t exactly the prettiest place I’d ever been to. Instead, it seemed very utilitarian in construction. Most of the buildings were very basic. Large, to be sure, but designed in a brutal manner. I recalled that Bleddyn had told me this had been discovered hiding beneath Addersfield as a supply depot for all of the goods we slaves had harvested at the behest of Magnus, or more honestly, it had probably been overseen by Orinbar. My former ‘owner’ hadn’t really had much of a head for either business or management.

I could easily see the more practically minded Orinbar ordering a place like this built. Because of this, it wasn’t really designed with the long-term habitation of free slaves in mind.

And then we had brought an entire major city’s worth of refugees to their doorstep.

It was, to say the least, a madhouse once we actually started navigating the lanes of the settlement. The Unchained were neck deep in settling everybody that we had brought with us from Rhoscara. Not only were the Dwarves we had saved from the grip of Anguis and his forces here, but there were those from my command as well.

Well, nominal command. I was barely in charge of anything right now. I certainly wouldn’t say I had any kind of authority over Alveron’s Elven faction. Even before the elderly Elf had started to seclude himself, the leadership of the expedition had only really been able to make suggestions to those who were the sum total of all Elves free of Fynneas’s grip in the world.

And then there were the scattered remnants of the expedition itself…

I grimaced and did my best not to think about them right now. I couldn’t help my people right now. Not when I was literally just a walking Aetheric construct, at not even half of my total strength. I had to focus on fixing up my battered body, and then…

Then I could raise the flag of the expedition once more. I trusted Maria to handle matters with them, while I handled matters related to my broken body.

I had asked the guards who had stopped us just where the local Preceptor down here had set up, since that’s where I had told Renauld to take my body before I had left. It was a good thing I had done both things, too. I’m not sure if any of the people actually inside the settlement would have stopped for a moment to assist me. Not when there was still a long, trailing line of the refugee caravan on the upward slop of the Long Road from where we had entered, waiting their turn into the caldera.

Despite everything…

That, at least, managed to fill me with pride. We had managed to save more Rhoscarans than I had thought. I know Ely would be grateful for that when we managed to free her from Anguis’s grasp.

When.

Eventually, we found the relatively smaller building that the Preceptor for the Unchained had taken for their own use. There was very little about the small, squat building that designated it as a Church. There was no Rorician sandstone, and there were not intricate stained glass windows depicting scenes straight out of Gyreism canon.

Instead, there was a simple seven-armed spiral painted in yellow on a signboard hammered above the door.

If that wasn’t enough of a clue that we had found the right place, I noticed that the cart Renauld, Sylvia, and I had ridden in on was parked off to the side of the innocuous church. I stopped for a moment to make sure the horses were fed, to Sylvia’s patient approval and Rafael’s impatience. Fade hopped into the back of the wagon as well and immediately lay down to take a nap. The pressure of Hermus’s Death Aether back in his cavern had taken a toll on the Spirit Wolf, so I didn’t begrudge him his rest.

Instead, I approached the door, opened it…

And received a bit of a surprise.

Renauld was inside, as expected. The Gnollish Healer looked like he had taken the chance in the last few hours to wash up and remove the road dust from our travel. I was in here as well, with my real body carefully laid out onto the surface of what was probably supposed to be a proper altar, but was in actuality a table. Like the outside, the inside of the ‘church’ was modest, looking to be furnished with mismatched chairs and stools in place of pews. Some of those were even a bit familiar-looking, leading me to believe they might just have been looted from Addersfield.

But that wasn’t what caught my attention.

The Preceptor did….because I recognized them.

“Preceptor Josha?” I asked, completely baffled at the sight of the man standing next to Renauld. At my words from the doorway, Renauld, and the Preceptor turned his compassionate gaze from my broken body to regard me with a patient smile.

“Good afternoon, Mr. Hart,” The dark-skinned, bald-headed Preceptor said calmly, impervious to my bafflement. “It’s good to see you once again, in spite of the circumstances. And you as well, Ms. Sylvia.”

Of all people, I certainly hadn’t expected to see him right in the heart of the Unchained. As Sylvia fully entered the church to greet the Preceptor happily, and Rafael glided in to lurk near the door, I considered the Preceptor.

Preceptor Josha was certainly far from home. The man had been the Preceptor for the church in the city of Silvercrest, far to the south of here in Herztal. There, he had been the person who had helped both Sylvia and me in different ways. For her, he had been one of the people to help oversee Sylvia’s first Ascension ritual, when she had reached level one-hundred before me. It wasn’t hard to see just why my love would be happy to see him again.

But for me, this guy had been the one who had informed me that I was never going to be able to regenerate any limbs I might lose, because I was a Precursor.

The fact that he was here, now, when I needed more body parts replaced with metallic substitutes.

Life really was strange, sometimes.
