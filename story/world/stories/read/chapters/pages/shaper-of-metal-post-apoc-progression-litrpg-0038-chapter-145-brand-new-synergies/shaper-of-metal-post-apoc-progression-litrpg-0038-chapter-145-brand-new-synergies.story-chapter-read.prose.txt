Chapter 145: Brand New Synergies

The last thing Jack confirmed in the mission specs was that mission day was more like in four days than three, depending on how one looked at it. They’d have today’s training and two more to get as ready as possible, and the following morning would be the flyout.

When Jack shifted over to the side meeting with Solvent, he quickly felt like he had at least a decent idea of what everyone could do on Team Under the Sea, aka Team Escort. The only complete unknown was Neex, but she’d require assessment after being able to spend time in her base of operations. It would mostly be inept to train with her in the tower because she was so hobbled. In his head, Jack filed her away as a ‘gadgeteer without her gear’ for now.

Highfive, Bo, Délight, he obviously knew intimately. Solvent had just explained his strange ‘combine everyone’ power. Conflux was a straight ‘magnitude’ buffer, as she’d recently explained to him.

Spiral, a very quiet and severe soul, was the healer, though a strange one and rather limited, included in the group also for his unusual hybrid buffing potential. He was a legit skilled medic and surgeon who could ‘accelerate actions,’ most effectively his own arms, with coordination to match. But he could also accelerate wound healing and even people, though most found his direct manipulation bewildering. Basically, he could ‘help people move,’ whether offensive or defensive. Similar to Bo, he had limited energy that made all of his miraculous potential quite precious and only in spurts. However, he also had a continual buff that could run, a small boost of this same effect to all allies.

Spiral’s role was to hang back, get drained maximally by Solvent most of the time, and watch for ‘issues,’ either potentially preventing them, or fixing injuries ‘judiciously.’ Looking at the stony face and haunted gaze of the man, like he was already at a funeral, Jack believed he would, in fact, be judicious.

Not your typical helpful, compassionate doctor with the bedside manner on point here, no sirree.

Permafrost could create an ice sculpture she ‘piloted’ remotely with full sensory dedication. She needed to be within several kilometers of range, though. From her sculpture, she could create and control fragile, potentially deadly ice that could become extremely sharp, in addition to secondarily encasing or freezing people up. Though fairly quick and mobile, her sculpture was easily destroyed and took time to rebuild. She’d fought alongside Crash a lot because she could provide him with water in ‘dry’ scenarios, and they knew how to combo off of each other.

Crash could basically just control water, making him strong in the environment they were headed to. He specialized in ‘masses,’ meaning he could make huge area effects with ease, able to resolve unfortunate oceanic monster encounters with potential ease, possibly doing so even better for large entities. He was also practiced at making submarines go faster, and with Jack’s facilitation, not to mention potential additional buffs, he could probably make it faster than they even intended for safety.

Snapper used snaps of his fingers, as well as auditory clicks, to form harmonic wavelengths that could detect enemies like an uber-boosted echolocation. He could use those same harmonics to break down matter because he was a Degenerative Blaster. He was essentially a medium-range sniper who killed over time rather than one shot, but ignored line of sight. He could kill people literally with his eyes closed.

Boosted, he was… frightening. And that was in the air. In the water, he’d be even more effective. To dissuade monsters, they might not even need Crash, just a hyper-buffed Snapper doing a ranged ‘vibe’ that would send any self-preservational entity quickly on the retreat.

No wonder they broke up the team. This guy doesn’t need team synergy. Even at a lower level, he can be mixed in for gains on high-level teams that can protect him. And boy, can we…

Lattice could encase people in differing sorts of protective crystalline layers. The weakest was the most mobile, being like armor, but she could make much thicker ‘diamond shapes’ that were ostensibly immobile without her active effort, and even then, it was slow. On the plus side, they were capable of complete transparency. They required ‘attunement’ and were weaker when applied to enemies, but she was capable of annoying them and gradually getting better against focused-on foes. She liked to stay put when fighting, encasing herself and operating at range. Lattice couldn’t form crystals except around living things, and couldn’t ‘branch’ or anything. Her flexibility was limited, almost pure defense.

However, the power truly formed around living things regardless of other objects, and the size of one crystal expanded based on the number of people it enclosed. Based on estimates of interacting buffs, she could form one entirely around their full submarine, though it would be very high output for her, especially if she had to deal with it moving. A good emergency defense, however.

Jack loved the idea of parking inside one of her crystals to operate under cover with high visibility.

When Lighthouse walked up, Jack greeted her and asked, “Are you around for presumably over-the-water issues?”

Lighthouse coughed a sardonic laugh at that. “I was wondering the same thing. But I’m apparently going to be outfitted with some ridiculous backpack apparatus. It’s even, quote, unquote, ‘safe’ in water. Though it ain’t so safe to activate there, not without me.”

“Whoa. Some kind of electrical generator?”

“Lightning storm in a fraggin’ bottle, dude. It’s like an electric stunner dialed to eleven. I’ll also have one of those, too. I don’t bring the backpack in where the aliens are, apparently. Anyway, they’ve been having me practice with that kind of shit. Works okay.”

“What about water?”

“Depends. I can’t control it the same, but it’s useful. No bolts. Nice area propagation. I can be an ‘electric eel’ and taser people in a radial zone. But if you get to the intensity of a lightning bolt? The water does some catastrophic stuff. I can barely control it, move it away before it inevitably does what it wants, and I'd better be ready to do it, because it happens in the blink of an eye. Best to think of it like a really nasty bomb.

“Mind you, I can do this even from the surface with generated lightning, just not very deep. Instead of zapping the surface, it goes bada-boom underneath it. Now, see, I go and strike myself with lightning? No problem. But I want nothing to do with underwater propagation. It would still kill me from the cavitation, shockwave, and all that shit. I wouldn’t do something like that from this hellacious backpack, not anywhere near it. I could trigger it, though. Once.”

Jack raised his eyebrows. “Interesting.”

Lighthouse grinned and clapped his arm. “Don’t worry, if you get caught in it, death will be as quick as you know what.”

Jack rolled his eyes. “Comforting. In any case, we’re really covering all our ‘damage source’ bases.”

“Yeah. Probably the idea.”

Everyone had gathered by then, and the conversation wasn’t exactly private. Crash pointed at Lighthouse and said loudly, “This makes a good point worth covering. Typical conventional tech can be an issue beyond the borders, especially the longer it is out there, which includes weaponry. On top of that, our various baddies have gradually adapted to our methods. Bullets often suck against them, and they’ve even gotten gravy at surviving the effects of explosions. Powers, or special tech reinforced with powers by an Artisan, are the best, pound for pound. With Ferronaut’s benefit, powers are the best, straight up.”

“How does Memoria manage out there, then?” Jack asked.

Solvent was the one to answer. “For technology in general? Extensive redundancies and frequent maintenance inside our borders. In the case of aerial dominance, we use masses of simple piloted drones flying out from our borders, and always more firepower than necessary. Despite the meme of Memoria ‘cruise missiling’ everything, we try not to rely on automated systems beyond the borders, and we can’t rely on Memoria. She doesn’t operate directly out there; she is more the mastermind behind others.”

“I might change that with my improved access.”

Solvent shook his head. “The brief says direct interface is off the table, though the communication benefits sound goddess-sent. Mind you, we haven’t tested it yet, Agent Ferro.”

“We’re normally stuck with conventional comms out there, even with Memoria?”

“More or less. We use special relays to tap into her once the signal hits our border outposts. Memorial tech in general ceases to be reliable even halfway into the Frontier, around fifteen hundred kilometers from the Babs. That includes the ubiquitous metal levitation we all know and love. Conventional tech rules on the Frontier, and then starts to have problems the further out we go from the borders, and the longer we’re out there.”

“We make it work, though,” Crash said.

“Yes. There aren’t enough ANPs, much less Artisan specialists, to go around in Antarctica. We still rely on our cantankerous machines out there, and on our techies.”

Permafrost grinned faintly and said, “And a piece of advice one of them gave to me: just because you see one smack something with a hammer, don’t do it yourself. Only they know when smacking something works, and how gently or not-so-gently to do it.”

Even Highfive was dubious about this claim; his eyes squinted. “Riiiight…”

Permafrost put up her hands. “Just passing it along!”

Chuckling, Jack asked her, “Did you smash something you shouldn’t have, Frosty?”

“No comment!”

Solvent continued, “So, on the subject of powers being the best, here we are, ‘wielders of the best.’ Now that everyone is acquainted with each other’s powers from the notes, I’ll share my thoughts briefly, we can break to shake off our meeting fog, and then pick back up with some actual exercises and testing.

“I think my default defensive strategy in watery scenarios is some combination of Conflux, Crash, and Lattice. In a crisis or an ambush with limited information, that is the best starting position until identifying our next move. I will pull from everyone but those I use, though I will refrain from diminishing our, ah, ‘queen,’ as it was put, to not potentially interfere with the sensitive boost he’ll provide to every team, near and far. And honestly, ‘Queen’ is a great codeword if we have to reference this role verbally or by radio. No one would guess Ferro as that, if overheard. I’ll make sure that sticks for the mission as a whole.”

Thanks so much for that, you frosty brat. Jack glared at Permafrost, but her little pleased grin without looking at him said it all.

“Take note,” Solvent said, pointing to each recently-jacketed member, “the reduction from my incorporation is small for lower levels, but always noticeable, and you’ll intrinsically want to reject it. Do not. Be ready. I won’t name names, but at least one of you tightened up during the last demonstration. We’ll need to work on it to be sure.” Bo’s slight curve to her lips at that, likely not noticeable to most, let Jack know who he meant. “Alright, so, on the subject of out of the water scenarios… Moving away from Crash for both offensive and defensive flexibility is trickier with this group. I’m not going to set it in stone. I have ideas, merely.”

He pointed at Jack. “Potentially very strong physical barriers, and more flexibility to pivot into offense, being a Controller.” He pointed at Snapper. “Virtually universal hard target appeal. He can get going immediately on his own, and then, pow! I hyperboost one of his ongoing pulses. Naturally, this is pure offense.” He pointed to Permafrost. “Can provide incredible offense with boosted ice darts like a poor man’s heavy machinegun, or jam up with widescale freezing and barriers, if fragile.” He pointed at Highfive. “Wrecking ball.” He pointed at Lighthouse. “A potential widescale electrical bomb, and heavy-duty yet frighteningly precise destruction if we ever have sky access and setup time.” He pointed at Spiral, and suddenly smiled and shrugged as if at a dear friend. “What needs to be said? We’ve saved lives together, after all.”

Spiral’s arms were crossed, his eyes downward, and he didn’t even twitch to this statement. Since the meeting began, the man had not said one word.

“What exactly does your helping him look like?” Highfive asked Solvent.

“Emergency evac and repair,” Solven replied. “Normally, he has trouble truly becoming a speedster due to energy. But when I do my thing, I provide the lion’s share of the energy, so we can kinda go nuts. A great failsafe.”

“Which also means you can run out of energy,” Bo commented.

Solvent nodded. “Indeed, I can. If I reach a certain point, I’ll scale back and focus on strategic bursts. At that point, we'd better start figuring things out on the double, because we’d be in major trouble.”

He paused here for reactions, having dropped the lame rhyme, to which his audience either kept a straight face, winced, or suddenly had to scratch or rub something.

“Ahem.” Clearing his throat, Solvent continued, “Whatever I’m brewing in a crisis scenario, the rest shouldn’t sit on their hands, naturally. Support each other, set up the kill dog established, or distract enemies, as the situation demands. Communicate, communicate, communicate, always. That is the number one thing to remember. Don’t get lost in the sauce, don’t go into tunnel vision. Lattice will focus on protecting our extensive backline primarily, but can support the front secondarily, and Ferro can help with the front line, both of them remotely. The front line is Crash, Highfive, and Permafrost primarily, so they’ll need a lot of support from the majority. Bogus, Délight, and Lighthouse run interference, and Snapper begins offense immediately. Makes sense?”

Nods of agreement ensued around the irregular circle.

“Okay,” Solvent said, clapping his hands together. “We’ll meet back up in two hours to begin practice. Otherwise, you’re dismissed.”

As people began stirring in different directions, Crash upnodded at Highfive. “Little Brother, let me and Frosty lead unless someone is boosting you. I know you’re lopsided on toughness and can take a hit, but you’re underleveled, so watch yourself out there. Don’t make Spiral waste energy. He hates that.”

Highfive saluted him. “You got it, sir!”

Crash scoffed. “Hey, none of that rookie ‘sir’ stuff. And saluting? Come on. Loosen yourself up. From this point, we’re all just Agents about to take chomps out of abyssal biscuits together. Call me Crash. Or, shit, just call me ‘dude’ or ‘bro’ or ‘blue fragger’ off the radio. I don’t care, just not ‘sir’ at any point. Understand?”

Highfive grinned. “You got it, bro! Call me Fiver.”

Bo gravitated toward Jack. Eyes shifting around, with a brief hesitation, she finally punched his shoulder.

Jack frowned. “What was that for?”

“Not telling me,” she answered, nodding over at Neex, who was talking at length off to the side with Solvent. “About all that.”

Jack gave her a ‘look.’ “I literally couldn’t, Bo.”

Bo shrugged. “Still, I dislike not knowing.”

Sighing, Jack said, “Yeah, I’ll be sure to keep that in mind. Anyway, I’m sure Neex will keep you up to date on literally everything from now on.”

“Yes, she will. Neex…” She shook her head slightly. “Eh, it’s catchier than Ira, anyway.”

“I mean, look at it this way: the pet name potential is as vast as the sea.”

Bo’s eyes squinted as she nodded thoughtfully. “Suction Cup.”

Jack burst out laughing. He’d used something like that before, in passing. “Perfect, yes. She’ll be both slightly embarrassed by it and love it at the same time.”

“My ideal result. So, you know her that well already?”

“I do, yeah.” Jack tapped his temple significantly.

“An actual bond, right? It’s interesting. I knew you two had a connection. I didn’t realize it was like that.”

“Well, you helped make it more ‘like that,’ so thanks again.”

“Mmn. It’s an enviable thing. Don’t mess it up, Cowboy.”

Jack suppressed a wince at that nickname. Maybe if he didn’t react, she’d stop? “I won’t, I promise.”

“Good.” Suddenly awkward, she turned her gaze elsewhere. Lighthouse and Délight were talking. Bo sighed. “Better jump into that. The mission has fast-forwarded the resolution of interpersonal drama bullshit, as you can see. I’d rather deal with it before being ordered to.”

“Right. Good luck.”

Bo privately rolled her eyes to him in silent, sardonic agreement, before heading off to engage with the other two dark beans.

So many dark beans. One or two of them need to dye their hair. At least Lighthouse is on a slightly bigger frame. She’s the kidney bean of the bunch. Heh.

Jack couldn’t quite shake the feeling they’d all been mashed together for more than reasons of the mission specs. Memoria was probably additionally thinking of the long-term, and how his ‘team’ needed experience along with him, and yesterday. She seemed to see this mission as, at most, ‘medium’ risk, so a low-level assortment wasn’t some suicidal toss-up. And the vets were tailored to not only keep them alive, but bring their power level up to snuff.

Considering my improvement in the mix, we’re probably higher than usual beyond the border.

Jack decided to cut out early. He wanted to get at least a little solo familiarity with his Level 6 self, and he had one other less exciting thing to take care of, and spontaneously, he decided to make it a bit more savory with a certain reserved food credit…

Inside the diner-like atmosphere, at a cherry-red booth, Inkblot cut into his steak and held up the meat on his fork. “This treat was unnecessary, Jack.” He took a bite and chewed slowly, shrugging with his brows raised slightly. “But steak is always appreciated.”

Jack nodded as he also dug into some steak and eggs, eating perhaps too fast, steeling himself for what was to come. “Well, honestly, it is kind of necessary in my book…” He ended up trailing off, unsure of the best segue that should follow, quickly stalling by cramming food in his mouth.

Ugh! I should start over. Why am I so bad at this?

Inkblot just nodded and looked away as he chewed. After swallowing, he said, “You don’t need to say it, Jack.”

“Hmm?”

“You know what I mean, and I’ve already made the determination. Aside from that, Augur spoke in hypothetical ‘theories’ about the ‘results of patterns,’ which I think got him slightly in trouble for contrived violation of orders, unnecessarily confirming it. Idiot.” He shook his head as he took a sip of tea.

Jack dropped his utensils and sat back, huffing slightly. “So, you did know, even before that. What gave it away?”

Inkblot stared into his tea, and it was clear he wouldn’t answer. “I told you in the beginning what would happen. This is no surprise nor a disappointment. I did exactly what I set out to do, and I’m satisfied with my execution.” He set down his tea and regarded Jack soberly. Finally, he reached his hand over the table for a shake. “Thank you for inviting me to the team, Jack. I wish you and the others the best, and I hope we work together again down the road.”

Jack nodded slowly, his jaw working. Taking a deep breath, feeling quite relieved, ultimately, even if he still felt bad about the whole thing, he finally took his friend’s hand and shook it firmly and emphatically. “Me too, Ink. Count on it.”

With that, they nodded once more and then silently got back to eating, knowing they were ‘good’ just like that.
