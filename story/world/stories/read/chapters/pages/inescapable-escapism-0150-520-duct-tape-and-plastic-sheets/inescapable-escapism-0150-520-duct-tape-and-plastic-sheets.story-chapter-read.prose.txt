Dad turned the music down until it was almost inaudible as he pulled into a spot in the car park. It had been so loud before, loud enough to drown out both of our voices as we screamed along to a song I used to love but hadn’t listened to in what felt like years, and the sudden quietness made my ears ring. That was somehow more deafening than the music had been, and I lifted a hand to rub one of my ears as I undid the seat belt and slipped out of the car.

The parking lot was bustling, I realised as I looked around. Most of the spaces were full, and people strolled down the middle of the road, seemingly unaware of the cars idling behind them impatiently as they searched for somewhere to park.

“Shall we?” Dad said, gesturing towards the building in the distance.

“Sure!”

We’d been lucky to even find a space, even if it was far away from the sprawling shop, but that meant we needed to walk for ages to get close to it. I didn’t mind, though. It was nice being with Dad.

When I’d been in Scotland with Mom, it was different. Whenever we couldn’t park close to the shop or wherever we were going, she’d walk so quickly to get there, and her irritation was clear. I could almost feel it radiating off her, but Dad didn’t seem to care too much. His steps were slow and unhurried. He seemed to just be enjoying being outside, and I found myself matching his pace.

Halfway across the car park, a delicious smell wafted towards me. I couldn’t stop myself from looking around for the source of that mouthwatering scent, inhaling deeply to try and breathe in as much as possible.

Even just the smell made my stomach grumble loudly, and my eyes widened as a bolt of fear shot through me. I glanced at Dad instinctively, hoping that he hadn’t noticed, but it was too late. His eyes were on me, and a smile pulled at his lips.

“Smells good, doesn’t it?” he remarked. “Hungry?”

Relief slammed into me, even as I hesitated. I’d spent too much time with my mom, and that made it hard not to give in to the urge to lie and say I was fine that was rising within me. She would have judged me or told me I’d already had enough food, despite not having eaten yet that day, but Dad wasn’t like that. He wouldn’t care if I said I was hungry.

“Yeah, kind of,” I forced myself to say.

Dad glanced over my head at the food truck, eyeing it for a few seconds.

“I can’t see the menu from here. Want to go see if they have anything you can eat?” he offered.

My stomach grumbled again at the suggestion, and I wanted to agree immediately, but lingering anxiety made me hesitate. I could almost hear my mom’s snide voice whispering in my ear, but my hunger was too strong for me to ignore.

“Sure,” I said before adding, “If you’re hungry too?”

“Starving,” Dad replied with a grin before starting to weave between cars towards the van.

There was always one in the parking lot of stores like B&Q. Or, at least, it felt like there always was. It seemed a little out of place. The car park was so full, and it was taking up more than four spaces, but people didn’t seem to care. They were still lining up to get a bacon or sausage bap.

It did smell good, to be fair. I didn’t eat meat, hadn’t for years, but that didn’t stop my stomach from growling violently enough that I was almost tempted to change my mind as we drew close to the van.

“’Scuse me,” Dad said as he squeezed through a few people standing in the line.

They glared at him, but he ignored their looks as he gestured for me to follow. I sent them an apologetic smile as I joined him, standing awkwardly near the food truck and peering up at the menu inside the small space.

“Queue starts back there, mate,” someone pointed out as they reached the front of the line.

Dad looked a little surprised as he glanced back at the burly man.

“Oh, yeah. I’m aware,” he said, his expression morphing into a friendly smile. “Just having a look at the menu for my daughter. She’s a vegetarian.”

The man’s gaze landed on me, and I felt the need to hide behind my dad. I wasn’t sure why. He’d appeared a little annoyed when he first spoke, but the irritation had left his face.

“Ah, sorry then, mate. Thought you were tryna cut in,” he replied before raising his voice. “Jerry, you do anything without meat?”

The guy in the food van looked up at us, his expression quizzical. It seemed as though he’d somehow never been asked that question before, and he didn’t quite know what to make of it.

“Uhh… not for breakfast. We used to do a veggie sausage thing, but we stopped a couple years back. No one ever ordered it,” he answered with an apologetic grimace.

“We’ve got a veggie burger on the lunch menu, if you want to come back for that?” the woman in the van asked, glancing over the counter at me as she continued cooking.

Dad looked at his watch as I stared down at the ground, my face burning. I could feel eyes boring into me. It seemed like every single person in the queue behind me was looking at us and listening in on our conversation, and that made me wish the ground would open up and swallow me whole.

“When do you start serving that?”

“Two-thirty,” Jerry responded.

“That’s an hour away,” the burly guy in the line remarked. “Can’t you do anything for the kid now?”

It was sweet of him to ask, but I wished he hadn’t. It just made the whole situation more mortifying.

“Mmmm,” the cook said, looking around at the stuff inside his van before looking back at the woman and shrugging. “How’s a buttered bap?”

Dad looked down at me, waiting for my response, and I shot him a pleading look. I didn’t care anymore. My hunger had deserted me, being replaced by sheer embarrassment. I just wanted to get away from the van and all of the people who were watching us.

“Ah, I think we’re probably fine,” Dad told the cook. “Thanks, though.”

My eyes stayed on the ground as we started to turn away, but to my horror, it wasn’t over. The burly man from before spoke again, causing Dad to clap a hand onto my shoulder and pull me to a stop.

“You could make a cheese toastie, no?” he suggested. “You got buns. You got cheese for the burgers. Just put them together.”

I peered up into the van, my eyes finding the chef as he considered it. I half-hoped he’d refuse, but he shrugged.

“I can do that,” he decided. “But I normally cook the veggie burgers on a separate grill, but that ain’t on yet.”

“Oh, that’s—” I started to say, hating how much hassle I was causing.

“We got more than enough space for it over by the onions,” the woman told him before smiling at me. “No meat’s been cooked there.”

“Thanks,” I replied awkwardly.

“Alright, one cheese toastie coming up,” Jerry announced before looking at my dad. “What about you?”

Dad blinked at him, taken aback.

“Oh, I was just going to get a breakfast roll, but it’s not our turn yet. We haven’t even queued up.”

Jerry leaned forward, peering at the queue of people who were shuffling impatiently.

“Eh,” was all he said. “That’ll be… five pounds.”

Dad hesitated for just a moment before glancing at the burly guy.

“At least let me get yours too,” he said. “As a thanks for helping this one get fed.”

The guy grinned at him.

“Well, I ain’t going to say no to that. Do me a breakfast bap too, Jerry. With brown sauce,” he said.

“Oh, I’ll have brown sauce on mine too,” Dad added.

“You got it. Nine pounds forty, in that case.”

I didn’t raise my gaze from the ground as Dad paid before following him to one side. I could feel people continuing to stare at us, and I felt a little bad that we’d cut in front of them, but there wasn’t anything I could really do about that. Dad had pointed it out, and the cook didn’t seem to care, so it wasn’t exactly our fault.

It still felt like it, though.

“Thanks for that,” Dad said again. “I thought we were going to have to swing by McDonald’s on the way home or something.”

The guy laughed.

“Ah, don’t mention it,” he replied. “My kid was veggie for a bit, and boy was I glad when that phase was over.”

Dad chuckled.

“I’m not sure how much of a phase this is. It’s been going on for a long while now,” he said, shooting me a grin. “How old’s your kid?”

“Turned twenty a couple of months back. She’s up at university now.”

“Oh, congratulations. What’s she studying?”

“Neuroscience,” the guy replied, shaking his head as a look of wonder appeared on his face. “No idea where she got the brains from. Neither me or the missus went to university, and we’re lost whenever she starts talking about it, but she seems to like it.”

Dad laughed again.

“Ah, that’s all that matters, isn’t it?” Dad said.

“It is, it is.”

“Two breakfast rolls with brown sauce and a cheese toastie!” the woman in the food truck called as she placed the tin foil-wrapped food on the counter.

“Thanks,” Dad told her as he stepped forward to grab them, handing one to me and one to the guy. “Have a great day.”

“You too!” the guy said, starting to turn away from us.

Dad’s ability to talk to anyone, even people he didn’t know and had nothing in common with, never failed to impress me. No matter where we went, he seemed to be able to strike up a conversation with a random person, and I had no clue how he did it. It seemed so natural to him. Unlike me, he never seemed at a loss for what to say, and I couldn’t help but feel a little jealous as we began walking across the parking lot again, our steps slower that time.

“You sure you’re happy with that?” he asked, nodding down at the small package that was burning my hand.

I hadn’t even noticed how hot it was until that moment, but I quickly switched it to the other hand before it could cause permanent damage.

“Yeah, thank you.”

“Any time, kiddo,” he replied with a smile. “You want to sit down to eat? I think I see a couple of picnic tables out front. I’m not sure if they’re just for display, but I’m sure no one will mind.”

Worry tightened its grip around my chest at the thought of some B&Q employee coming out to tell us off. I’d already endured so much embarrassment, and I didn’t want to have to deal with any more. I knew Dad wouldn’t mind if that happened, though. He’d be able to wave it off and not let it bother him, but I wouldn’t.

“I can walk and eat,” I decided as I lifted the package and began unwrapping it.

A waft of melted cheese and toasted bread slammed into me as I pulled the tin foil back, and I felt my mouth fill with saliva. It smelled so good, and I couldn’t stop myself from bringing it to my mouth immediately and taking a huge bite.

“How is it?” Dad asked, already halfway through his own breakfast roll. “We can still stop somewhere else to grab some lunch if you don’t like it.”

I chewed, savouring the grilled cheese. It shouldn’t have been as good as it was. It was just a burger bun with a few slices of American cheese in the middle, but somehow, it was incredible.

“It’s great,” I told him.

Dad’s smile widened.

“Good. But if you’re still hungry after, just let me know,” he said before finishing his roll and crumpling up the foil.

“I will!”

By the time I’d finished my toastie, we’d almost reached the front of the store, and my hands had become covered in a fine layer of flour from the burger bun. I balled up the tin foil, handing it to my dad before slapping my hands together to get rid of it, but as I did that, something caught my eye.

My shirt was covered in flour, I realised as I stared down at myself. My gaze flitted around the car park, checking to make sure no one else had noticed. The bun hadn’t even seemed that floury, so I had no idea how there was so much on me, but there was.

I wiped my hands on my thighs, getting the last of the flour off them before trying to brush it off my shirt. A faint panic spiked within me as the white powder smeared into the dark fabric of my shirt, but I kept trying, swiping at it even harder until it was all gone.

“Alright, let’s go!” Dad said as he dropped the rubbish into the bin and looked towards the giant doorway.

A grin appeared on my face again, and I rushed to keep up with him as he strode into the shop. My eyes bounced back and forth as we made our way through the huge warehouse-like space, passing seemingly endless rows of lawn mowers, garden furniture, and power tools before approaching the interiors section.

I found myself staring as we walked past the end of an aisle that seemed to be selling doors. Like, front doors for houses. I’d been to B&Q before with my dad, but I’d never noticed them before. I wasn’t even aware that was something people could just buy in a shop. Obviously, I knew that people needed to get a new door sometimes, but I just assumed they got them somewhere else, at some specialist shop or something.

“Okay, this should be it,” Dad said as we neared another aisle. “Mmmm… yes!”

He sped up, dodging around someone’s trolley to get to a wall that was covered in pieces of paper, each one boasting a handful of paint samples. My eyes widened as I took in the sheer number of options. It seemed like there were hundreds, if not thousands, of different coloured rectangles, and it was a little overwhelming. There were too many choices.

Dad moved towards one side that had an endless number of blue shades, and I watched as his eyes narrowed. He cocked his head to the side, his eyes moving back and forth as he looked at them all.

“What do you think?” I asked after a few seconds.

“Hmmm… I mean, I’m tempted by this one,” he said, reaching out to tap one of the dark blue squares. “But I’m pretty sure your mom will say it’s too dark and makes the room feel tiny.”

That was what she’d said about the colour he’d used to paint the lounge whilst we were away. I’d heard her shout it at him the night before, but I didn’t get it. I liked how it looked. The whole room felt cosier, but she obviously didn’t agree.

“Yeah, maybe,” I replied.

Dad slipped the paint samples back into place before picking up another piece of paper with slightly lighter shades. He squinted at them for a few seconds before holding them out towards me.

“What do you think about the bottom one here?” he asked. “Still too dark?”

I looked at it, trying to picture how it would look in the lounge, but my mind refused to conjure any kind of image. It was a nice shade of blue, still dark but not quite navy. It was a little too light for that.

“Mmm… I’m not sure,” I said after a few seconds.

Dad nodded, slipping it back into the spot he’d taken it from before grabbing another.

“Good call. What about this one?”

My eyes flicked towards the selection of shades before returning to Dad.

“What, the one at the bottom again?” I checked.

“Yeah.”

It was a little lighter than the last, but I still wasn’t sure. Mom was so picky, and I was almost certain that whatever we went for, she would decide it was the wrong choice. Then, Dad would either have to traipse back to the shop to get another colour, or she’d just put up with it, but make snide comments about it. Even if he did paint it again, she’d probably continue to be rude about it.

“I don’t know. Maybe it’s a bit too dark still?”

“Hmmm, I think you’re right,” Dad agreed. “What do you think? See any you like?”

My gaze returned to the wall of colours, roaming back and forth along it as I tried to find one that Mom wouldn’t complain about.

“How about that one?” I asked, pointing at one of the pieces.

Dad moved towards it, reaching towards the area I’d pointed at.

“This one?”

“No, the one below it,” I replied. “But that one’s nice too.”

Dad grabbed both samples out of the display and examined them closely, tilting the papers so they’d catch the light. I saw his eyes flitting back and forth between them as I waited, but he just continued to examine them.

“Alright,” he said after a minute or so. “I like them both. Which do you think your mom will hate the least?”

The phrasing of his question made me snort. He hadn’t asked which one Mom would like; we both knew she wouldn’t like either. The best we could hope for was that she wouldn’t hate it too much.

“I don’t know. What do you think?” I replied.

I honestly wasn’t sure, but I also didn’t want to be the one to make the decision because then, it would be my fault when Mom got annoyed. Dad would never tell her that I’d chosen the colour. I knew that he wouldn’t, but I’d still feel bad when I heard them arguing about it.

“Hmmm… I think this one,” Dad decided, shoving the other sample back into the rack before turning away. “Now, we just need to find it. It should be… somewhere here.”

A laugh slipped out of my mouth at the uncertain note in his voice, but as soon as I turned, I understood it. I’d seen the countless rows of paint tubs when we’d entered the aisle, but trying to find a specific colour somehow made them seem more overwhelming.

There were too many. A ridiculous number of tubs lined the aisle, and we walked along it slowly, weaving between people as we searched for the exact hue and brand of the sample Dad was holding, but it was impossible to find.

“Next aisle, maybe?” Dad suggested, looking around as we reached the end of the row. His gaze landed on the trolley of a guy who was passing us, and his eyes widened before he glanced back at me.

“God, I do not want to know what that guy is doing.”

“Dad!” I hissed, blood starting to rush into my cheeks again. “He probably heard you!”

“No, he didn’t,” Dad replied with a dismissive wave of his hand.

There was no way he didn’t. I was only a few steps away from us, if that, and Dad had not spoken quietly. I had absolutely no doubt that he’d heard Dad’s comment.

He did have a point, though. The guy’s trolley was concerning. He’d mostly bought duct tape and plastic sheets, which was… concerning. I was sure he had a completely innocent plan for whatever he was going to do with it, but I’d watched too many horror films with Phoebe, so that wasn’t where my thoughts went.

Dizziness pulled at the back of my mind, the sensation sending a shiver down my spine. My steps faltered as I snatched myself away from it, fighting to get away from the world that was trying to draw me in. My heart started to speed up as fear gripped me.

Instinctively, I knew that world was not a good one. My time there would not be pleasant. It would be filled with pain. I’d scream until my voice failed me, and still, no one would come for me. No one would even know where I was until they found my corpse.

Goosebumps crept along my arms as I shivered again, thrashing against the dizziness as I tried desperately to cling to my reality. I was safe there. I was with my dad, and I was having a good time. It was fun, and I didn’t want to leave and be taken to that other place.

“I think I see it!” Dad announced, snapping me out of my mind.

I blinked quickly, looking around to make sure I was still in reality. It looked real, as far as I could tell. The other dizziness was still there, but it had retreated slightly. It lingered at the back of my mind, seemingly sulking about the fact that it had not managed to ensnare me.

“Oh, yeah. I think that’s it,” I said, looking at the tub Dad was pointing at, as a relieved smile appeared on my face.
