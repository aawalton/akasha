Gilbert took one of the scrolls from the case and handed it to Sakura.

“We’ll start with this. Take it.”

Sakura obediently accepted the scroll with both hands. The paper was thick, with a slightly rough surface, and along the edges ran a delicate pattern of orderly symbols.

“Don’t unroll it all the way,” Gilbert said, stepping aside. “Hold it firmly, but don’t crumple the paper. Then send an impulse of will and a little energy into the construct.”

He led Sakura a little to the side, toward a tree that had almost dried out completely and stood at the edge of the rocky ledge.

“Aim there.”

Sakura nodded. There was a scroll in her hands. A tree in front of her. Overall, it really did look simple enough.

She unrolled the scroll partway, just as Gilbert had told her, and aimed it at the dry trunk. But before trying, she still asked:

“What kind of magic is hidden in this thing, anyway?”

“You’ll find out,” Gilbert answered.

After that, he stepped back a couple of paces and sat down right on the ground, crossing his legs, with the look of someone who had not come to teach her, but to observe a rather curious natural phenomenon.

Sakura lowered the scroll and stared at him.

“So you’re just going to sit there?”

“Yes,” he answered, perfectly calm. “And you’re going to focus on the scroll.”

Sakura let out an irritated breath, but did not argue. She raised the scroll again, concentrated on it, and tried to send energy into the construct. A pitiful trickle of water spilled out of the scroll. It was just a thin, miserable stream that lazily slapped onto the ground near the roots of the tree and immediately soaked into the dust.

Sakura blinked slowly and said in a disappointed drawl, “Uh...?”

She looked at the scroll first, then at the tree, and only then at Gilbert.

“Was that supposed to happen? What was that, exactly?”

Gilbert did not even move.

“Your first attempt.”

“What kind of pathetic magic is sealed in this scroll?”

One of Gilbert’s brows arched slightly.

“The magic in the scroll isn’t pathetic. The way you released it was.”

Sakura narrowed her eyes, but had to admit he was right.

“You’re clamping down on the energy too hard. You try to force it out in a burst, and then in the same second you stop it yourself. That’s not how energy works. It’s a stream, not a prisoner. You don’t pile rocks in its path and then get angry that it barely trickles.”

He nodded slightly toward the scroll.

“Try again.”

Sakura pressed her lips together and tried once more. The second time, a little more water came out. But it was still nothing impressive. The thin stream hit the tree this time instead of the roots, slid helplessly down the bark, and disappeared. The third time, the water splashed off to the side altogether. On the fourth try, the scroll only twitched in her fingers and spat out a miserable little burst, as if it were unhappy with who it had been given to work with. The rest of her attempts to squeeze magic out of it were even less impressive. So much so that Sakura started feeling embarrassed that anyone was watching at all. She pressed her lips together.

Pampli, who had been watching all this from his rock, let out such a heavy, expressive sigh that Sakura snapped her head toward him.

“Hey, I don’t want to hear judgment from you.”

Pampli, of course, was not embarrassed. The next second, some careless moth flickered past his nose, and the beast simply caught it in his mouth, chewed it, and lay back down again.

Sakura looked away, feeling a dull irritation building inside her. It had all seemed so simple. A low-level scroll. The most basic kind of external magic. There was even a note in the margin saying the construct was designed for users up to level ten. The simplest kind. Practically for beginners. And even with something this basic, she was doing so badly that she almost wanted to just throw the scroll at the tree and give up entirely.

Gilbert still sat on the ground with an unshakable look on his face, watching her from below.

Sakura tried again. And again. The water spilled out weakly and uselessly, giving the tree nothing except an insult.

“What is wrong?” she let out through clenched teeth.

Gilbert finally stood up.

“This won’t do.”

“It’s going perfectly,” Sakura answered very tensely. “Can’t you tell?!”

Gilbert laughed quietly.

“Oh, I can tell.”

He stepped closer, carefully took the scroll from her, and gestured to the ground beside him.

“Sit.”

Sakura wanted to argue, but caught his gaze and, for some reason, didn’t. She lowered herself to the ground beside him, tucking her legs under her. It was hard for her to argue with Gilbert, even when she really wanted to. There was something about him that always made her come to her senses. Around him, Sakura always tried to think more. And stare at him less.

“Close your eyes,” Gilbert said. “And relax.”

“If it were that easy, I would have already...”

“Sakura.”

She went quiet.

“Close your eyes,” he repeated more calmly. “Listen to the wind. To the ground beneath you. To the river flowing not far from here. Not to yourself and not to your anger. Let the sounds reach you until your body starts to calm down. And turn your thoughts off, at least for a little while.”

Sakura frowned skeptically, but she closed her eyes anyway. At first, all she could hear was her own irritation. Of course, it had no actual sound, but it felt almost tangible. Then she heard the wind. Cool, moving the leaves somewhere high above. Then she managed to hear the river. Grass rustled somewhere off in the distance. Somewhere close by, Pampli shifted one paw against the stone.

Sakura tried not to think, but it did not work right away.

In the rustle of the wind, that familiar whisper sounded again. This time she caught it almost immediately:

“Quiet... we do not need to distract her...”“We must be silent... silent.”

And then the whisper faded.

Sakura almost opened her eyes. A new wave of questions had already begun to rise inside her. What was this wind? Or who was speaking to her through it? Why? But she forced herself not to cling to the thoughts. Later. Right now, she needed to keep herself from slipping back into irritation.

Sakura focused on the sounds again. A couple of minutes passed, or maybe more, and her heartbeat had already slowed. That was when she felt energy. At first, she did not understand what it was. She simply felt that everything around her was breathing. It was hard to explain in words, but the energy felt like color and a second breath.

From Gilbert came a dark blue depth with silver flecks, cold and controlled. From Pampli came obsidian blackness scattered with crimson dust. From the trees around them came a very different energy: white, gray, light, thin, and simple. Then she felt the scrolls too, very clearly. The magic sealed inside them differed from one scroll to another. Some felt heavy and dense. Some felt soft and pliant. The scroll she had been struggling with now felt fluid.

Sakura did not even notice when her body truly relaxed, when the anger disappeared and breathing became easier. All that remained was the sensation of the world around her, and at some point, she opened her eyes.

The forest was still just as grim. Gilbert sat beside her, legs crossed, watching her calmly, without pressure.

“I’m ready to try again,” Sakura said quietly.

He nodded.

“Then try.”

Sakura stood up, walked over to the same tree, and took the scroll in her hands again. Now the paper felt different, as if it were more familiar and easier to understand. She aimed the scroll at the tree and exhaled, this time without tensing up.

Sakura felt her own energy touch the energy sealed inside the scroll. She did not try to force the construct open. She simply nudged it toward unfolding and let it out. The scroll flared with a soft bluish light, and the next instant a strong, steady stream of water burst from it and struck the tree, soaking into the cracked bark instead of splashing uselessly away.

The water ran over the trunk for several seconds, then the flow abruptly stopped. Sakura had just enough time to blink in surprise before she noticed the tree had changed. The nearly dried bark seemed to drink in life. Color ran through the branches. The trunk looked healthier, and somewhere on one of the thin branches, a hint of green had even appeared.

“Oh.”

Gilbert rose from the ground and stepped closer.

“This scroll is meant for gardeners,” he said. “It supports fading plants. Weak, but useful magic.”

Sakura stared at the tree in shock.

“For gardeners?” she repeated. “Then why was there so much water?”

“It doesn’t harm the plant,” Gilbert said. “The construct directs the flow into the wood.”

Sakura looked back at the tree.

“So I... brought it back to life?”

“You nudged it back toward life for a while. Don’t exaggerate.”

But even his dry tone did not ruin the moment.

Sakura quickly turned to the next tree over, which looked even more pitiful.

“Then I can help this one too.”

She raised the scroll again, but it gave no response now. No light. Not even a miserable drop.

Sakura frowned.

“Why isn’t it working?”

Gilbert pointed to the edge of the paper.

“Because it’s single-use.”

Sakura looked down and only then noticed the inscription cut into the lower part of the construct.

Uses Remaining: 0/1

“But it worked before,” she said. “Badly, but it did.”

“Those were failed activations,” Gilbert said. “The construct did not count them as use.”

“Oh,” she exhaled. “Right. I probably should have noticed that sooner.”

“You should have,” Gilbert agreed. “But the important part is that you still did it right. Good job.”

Sakura looked over at him. Praise from Gilbert, spoken without sarcasm, felt almost as unexpected as the burst of magic had.

“Thanks,” she said, with the faintest hint of a smile now.

But they did not get to relax. At first, Sakura simply felt it. Something had changed in the forest. Gilbert tensed at almost the exact same moment. Then a roar sounded behind the trees. A roar that sounded as if it had been dragged not from a beast’s throat, but straight out of a burning underworld.

Sakura whipped around.

“An obsidian creature,” she breathed.

And almost immediately, a second roar came from the other side.

Another one.
