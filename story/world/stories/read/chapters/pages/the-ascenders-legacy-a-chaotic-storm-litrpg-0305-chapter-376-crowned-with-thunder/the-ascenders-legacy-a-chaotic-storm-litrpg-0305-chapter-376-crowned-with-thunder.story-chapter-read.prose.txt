The days passed in a haze of celebration and excitement for the Storm Sect.

It had been five days since the war ended, yet the sounds of victory and rejoicing still filled the air. Laughter echoed through the streets, feasts stretched long into the night, and everywhere one looked, the disciples seemed lighter than they had in months.

The festivities showed no signs of ending anytime soon.

And who could blame them?

They had won a massive victory and they deserved to celebrate it for as long as they wanted.

No one was excluded. Not Specials. Not Mythics. Not even the High Elders themselves.

But victory was not the only thing celebrated during those five days. At least, not within the Special Sect.

After Aodhán’s birthday celebration—which had been most interesting—they had gone on to celebrate Darien and Seamus gaining their familiars.

Darien’s familiar was an inky-black sky serpent that embodied the very essence of Sanctum, and Seamus’s—

Well, Aodhán still couldn’t make head or tail of whatever he had acquired.

The creature vaguely resembled a wolfhound, but that was where all resemblance to normalcy ended. Formed from static silver mist and fractured lightning, the hound never fully occupied a single shape. Its body shifted the moment one blinked.

Sometimes it had six legs for half a second.

Sometimes two tails. Sometimes, its pawprints appeared before it even stepped.

And once, most disturbingly, it had no head until Aodhán blinked.

“It’s an Afterimage Hound,” Seamus had announced as though that explained everything, but no one pushed back. Not yet.

As normal as he seemed, Seamus was still a bit... delicate.

The healers had piled him with herbs and concoctions to help mend his mind. Halo had also done her part, drowning him in mental buffs every chance she got, but there were still leaks—moments when Seamus wasn’t quite himself.

It didn’t happen all the time, but it happened often enough to be concerning. The High Elders were already discussing taking him to see a telepath, or someone else capable of healing the fracture in his mind.

“Pfft, I’m completely fine,” he had scoffed the night before when they gathered for dinner in Nymera’s home. “I’m not acting crazy.”

“You’ve stolen the time from everything in your mansion,” Sophran pointed out concernedly. “This afternoon, I walked in to find your couch frozen in midair, the time it should have used to fall drained from it.”

“I’m paying back a debt,” Seamus sighed. “I borrow from the future, I gotta pay it back somehow.” He sliced off a piece of meat and shoved it into his mouth. “Besides, it’s good practice.”

“Okay, if you say so.” Aodhán exchanged a glance with Nymera. “But it won’t hurt to get your head checked out.” He paused as a thought came to mind. “Perhaps we should all get our heads checked out.”

“Not happening.” Ashscar shuddered. “No way am I letting a telepath hear my discussions with the storm.”

Everyone glanced at him strangely, and after a brief hesitation, Nymera asked, “What do you even talk about? You sound crazier than Seamus.”

“I’m not crazy.”

“He’s not crazy.”

Seamus and Ashscar spoke at the same time, drawing a chuckle from Aodhán.

“If we’re being honest, we’re all a tiny bit crazy.” He shoved a slice of meat into his mouth. “I’m certain I lost some marbles in that battle against Lysandra.”

“Ugh, don’t remind me of the battles,” Nymera groaned. “If I never have to talk about them again for the rest of my life, I would be most grateful.”

Sophran chuckled. “You should skip the speech tomorrow, then. The High Elders are definitely going to discuss it.”

Ashscar nodded. “I’m just glad it’s over and we can go back to living in relative peace.” He downed a glass of water. “I don’t mind talking about it one last time before shoving it into the darkest recesses of my mind.”

“Where the storm stays?” Darien teased, a small smile tugging at his lips.

They all laughed.

The rest of the dinner passed smoothly, and by the end of it, Aodhán had managed to convince Seamus that getting his head checked out was no big deal.

***

The next morning, Aodhán stood before a massive mirror with a frown on his face, trying to choose between two ceremonial robes the Sect had gifted him for today’s grand speech.

The High Elders were finally ready to close the war arc with a formal address to the sect, and this time, the Specials had been invited to stand beside them.

“Are you going to be speaking,” Crixis asked as he walked into the room carrying a small wooden box, “or are you just there to look pretty?”

“I believe it’s the latter,” Aodhán replied without taking his eyes off the mirror. “Which of these robes suits me better?”

Crixis glanced at him, seeming to consider the matter with great seriousness before abruptly shaking his head.

“Neither. They both make you look ugly.”

Aodhán sighed. “One of these days, Crixis, we’ll return to training, and I’ll deal with this newfound insolence of yours.”

Crixis chuckled while Aodhán turned back to the mirror.

What to do?

The first robe was woven from midnight-blue silk so dark it was nearly black. It blended seamlessly with his complexion, making his irises and frost-white hair stand out in sharp contrast.

The second was a pristine white robe layered in soft folds of luminous cloth that achieved the opposite effect, softening his features and lending him an almost noble serenity.

He studied both for another moment before sighing and choosing the first.

As he tossed the other onto his bed, Crixis dragged a lacquered wooden box farther into the room.

Aodhán glanced over. “Another gift?”

“Yes.” Crixis huffed as he set it in the corner. “This one is from my mother.”

Aodhán blinked. “Really?”

For the past five days, gifts had arrived in an endless stream from every corner of the Sect, from outer disciples to the Elder Hall itself. He had not even opened half of them yet, though the mirror standing before him was one of the few he had.

Excitement stirred within him as he walked toward the box. “What’s in it?”

Crixis grimaced. “Shoes. She said her favorite transmigrant couldn’t walk onto a stage looking like he stole his footwear from a stablehand.”

Aodhán frowned. “My current shoes are perfectly fine.”

“They squeak when you walk.”

“They do not squeak.”

“They squeak with poverty.”

Aodhán’s eyes widened and he turned sharply, hands rising. “You little—”

“I’m sorry!” Crixis yelped, stumbling backward before Aodhán could grab him. “I’m just jealous. The whole house is full of gifts. I need shoes too, but does my mother send me any? No. She sends them to you.”

Aodhán snorted. “Well, you never nearly died in the war.”

Smiling, he turned his attention back to the box and opened it.

Inside rested a pair of ceremonial boots crafted from deep black leather and polished to a mirror sheen. Golden thread traced subtle runic patterns along the sides, while the soles gleamed with reinforced storm-metal.

He lifted one carefully. It was light—far lighter than it looked.

Grinning, he glanced at Crixis. “Do you think they’ll go better with the black robe or the white?”

Crixis stared at him for a long moment before slowly shaking his head. “You are so evil.”

Aodhán laughed. “Tell your mother I’m grateful.”

Crixis left soon after, muttering under his breath, and Aodhán looked around at the mountain of unopened gifts filling the room.

Never in his life had he received so much at once.

But then again, never in his life had he helped save a Sect from destruction either.

***

Fifteen minutes later, Aodhán and Crixis stepped out of the house with Varéc in tow.

Usually, he would have hopped onto a storm platform and zoomed off to the initiation center where the ceremony would be held, but today was about spectacle—and what greater spectacle was there than Var—

His thoughts came to an abrupt halt as Nymera stepped out of her house with her leviathan in tow.

The creature was colossal.

Its serpentine body flowed behind her in slow, deliberate coils, each movement smooth as drifting tides despite the sheer mass it carried. Scales the color of abyssal blue plated its length, dark as the deepest ocean until the light touched them, whereupon they shimmered with streaks of emerald and silver like sunlight breaking through water.

Aodhán grimaced.

He glanced toward Nymera, who was dressed in a similar robe, her frost-white hair flowing in a nonexistent breeze as she turned in his direction.

Their gazes met. Aodhán’s breath hitched.

Nymera smiled and walked over, her steps measured and graceful. “You look very good in your robe.”

Aodhán gave a low, pleased hum that did little to hide the smile tugging at his lips. “And you look beautiful in yours.”

Her smile widened, and Aodhán felt his heart stutter.

He glanced up at the leviathan. “You look beautiful as always, Levi. You don’t want to shrink a bit so the rest of us can look impressive too?”

Levi let out a deep, rumbling trill that resonated through the air, and Varéc immediately growled back, lightning surging across his scales.

Before he could ask, Ashscar stepped out of his mansion with Giselle beside him, her crimson-gold wings almost blinding beneath the morning sun.

Ashscar paused at the gathering of familiars, then snorted. “It is not about size, Giselle. Do not fret. I bet you could rip that leviathan apart without even trying.”

Levi’s massive head slowly turned toward him, and Nymera’s smile sharpened.

“I invite you to dare, Giselle. Try it.”

Giselle looked from Levi to Varéc, then huffed and folded her wings.

Ashscar nodded solemnly. “I understand, my beautiful bird. You are too regal for such brutality.”

They all laughed, and before long the others joined them.

Sophran arrived with his two underlings and Halo perched on his shoulder—the smallest familiar present, yet perhaps the most troublesome to face in battle.

Darien and Rathis Cloudrend emerged with Caelion coiled overhead, the sky serpent only slightly longer than Varéc himself. Still, it wasn't stealing second place, seeing as it had no wings and couldn't truly rival Varéc in sheer size.

Then Seamus and Kairn arrived.

Or rather, one moment they were absent, and the next, they were already walking toward the group as though they had been there for several seconds.

Aodhán blinked as he watched the grass behind them bend a heartbeat late, and dust rise where their footsteps should have landed moments ago.

Even the sunlight around them seemed briefly out of sync, catching up in fractured glimmers.

Seamus grinned broadly, his robe half-buttoned and crooked. Kairn looked deeply tired.

“I told him,” Kairn muttered, “normal people use paths.”

“We did use the path,” Seamus replied cheerfully. “We just borrowed our arrival from thirty seconds in the future.”

Halo slowly backed away.

Aodhán stared. “You what?”

Seamus beamed. “Don’t worry about it. I‘ll pay it back somehow.”

At his side, Skip flickered into existence three different times before finally settling on a single shape.

Aodhán sighed. “Okay, we are all ready. Let’s go make an entrance.”

Smiling, the six transmigrants rose into the air. Aodhán, Nymera, Ashscar, and Darien rode their familiars, while Sophran and Seamus stood atop conjured platforms, elevated even above their underlings.

They arrived above the initiation center a few minutes later, and the moment the crowd below noticed them, the entire place erupted.

Thousands upon thousands of voices crashed upward in a deafening wave of cheers.

There were even more people than on the day of Aodhán’s initiation. The grounds and skies were packed shoulder to shoulder, banners waving wildly as disciples, elders, families, and children shouted themselves hoarse.

“That’s Special Disciple Aodhán!”

“Lady Nymera!”

“Sophran Thunderstride!”

“Look at the dragon!”

“Is that the time dog?”

“It has three heads!”

“It has one head!”

“It had none a second ago!”

Aodhán snorted while Seamus waved proudly. “They love Skip.”

“They fear Skip,” Nymera corrected dryly.

“That counts.”

The energy in the air was unimaginable, and despite every effort to appear composed, Aodhán could not stop the grin spreading across his face.

This was what he had always wanted.

This was what he had fought for.

To be seen without fear. To stand openly beneath the sky with people cheering his existence rather than plotting his death. To belong somewhere so completely that their joy felt like his own.

The last time he had hovered above these same grounds, he had braced himself for chains. He had doubted their acceptance.

Today, they answered him with thunderous joy.

Zarxan had promised him that in Gordon, he would be praised for what he was. Back then, Aodhán hadn’t truly believed him.

But now… now he knew. Zarxan hadn’t lied.

He felt cherished—loved beyond measure—and without conscious thought, he lifted a hand and waved to the sea of faces below.

Almost impossibly, the sound of their cheers grew even louder, shaking the earth beneath them.

Victory rolled through the crowd like thunder.

For once, there was no fear in the sect. No anxiety. No rumors of war. No mourning.

Only celebration.

Aodhán let himself bask in it, his cheeks aching from smiling so much.

The cheers continued for several long minutes before a bell rang once.

The sound rolled across the initiation center, and in an instant the crowd quieted. Thousands of people straightened where they stood, eyes lifting toward the sky as the clouds darkened and six new figures descended slowly from the heavens—six individuals more powerful and revered than the transmigrants themselves.

The High Elders.

Everyone bowed immediately, even the familiars. Their breaths grew heavy as the pressure of the six most powerful members of the Sect washed over the entire area.

For nearly a minute, no one spoke or moved.

Then the First Seat did.

His voice came not from his mouth but from the heavens above—a peal of thunder so vast it rolled through bone and spirit alike.

“Lift your heads, disciples of the Storm Sect... and behold what victory looks like.”

Cheers rang out immediately, so loud the air shook. Even Aodhán cheered.

The First smiled. “Today, the Storm Sect stands unbowed. Our enemies gathered in number. They came with schemes, with greed, with blades hidden behind righteous words.” His eyes hardened. “And now they return home lesser than they arrived.”

Another round of cheers erupted.

“We did not merely survive this war.” The First Seat continued, lightning cracking across the heavens. “We profited from it.”

The crowd lost control entirely.

“Our plane grows. Our authority expands. Our rivals weaken. And so it shall remain.”

“And so it shall remain!” the crowd chorused.

The First Seat raised a hand, and the noise gradually faded.

His voice deepened. “Let every sect in Gordon hear this clearly. The Storm Sect does not rise to stand beside others. We rise to stand above them.”

The mountain trembled beneath the roar that followed. And when it finally died down, the First Seat turned toward the six hovering youths below him.

“But victory is never forged by elders alone.” His tone became solemn. “These six bled for us. They risked their lives for us. They carried thunder into war and returned crowned with it.”

Silence fell, and Aodhán felt his breath hitch, his heart growing heavy as every eye turned to them.

He knew in that moment that this was a turning point. A day that would echo through the rest of his life.

He raised his gaze to the First Seat and the man smiled.

“Let all present bear witness, for from this day onward, that these six shall be known not only by name, but by legend.”

He pointed to Nymera first. “NYMERA VAELTHORN—THE WILL OF TEMPESTS.”

The crowd exploded, echoing the name with reverence.

Aodhán swallowed.

He pointed to Sophran next. “SOPHRAN THUNDERSTRIDE—THE UNDYING SAINT.”

Again.

“SEAMUS CALCIRRA—THE BORROWED FUTURE.”

“DRAVION ASHSCAR—THE HERALD OF THUNDER.”

“DARIEN VELKARIS—THE HEAVEN DEFIER.”

And then, he pointed to Aodhán.

Aodhán felt the hairs on his arms stand on end. He felt the charge in the air still. Felt time itself hesitate as reality slowed for this moment.

“AODHÁN BRYSTION—THE CROWN OF RUINOUS STORMS.”

The name fell upon him like a physical weight, and Aodhán felt his entire world shift—not physically, but spiritually, and in that instant, he realized what had truly happened.

He had just been crowned—not as a king or ruler—but as the standard of ruinous authority.

His path acknowledged.

His future proclaimed.

His destiny anointed by the First Seat himself.

Slowly, he looked down at the cheering crowd as they chanted his new name.

“Ruin! Ruin! Ruin!”

Beside him, Seamus whooped. Nymera smiled brightly at him, and Sophran... Sophran looked as proud as though the title were his own.

For a moment, Aodhán watched it all with a stunned, almost disbelieving expression, too overwhelmed to speak. Then, a heartbeat later, a smile bloomed across his face, growing larger with every passing second.

It seemed he truly had found his family after all.

In that moment, surrounded by thousands upon thousands of cheering disciples, returning to Lutia was the farthest thing from his mind.

“Crown of Ruinous Storms…” he whispered to himself. And in that moment, he could almost hear the storm the same way Ashscar claimed to.

Its whisper—a rumble in his spirit.

A rumble of recognition. A rumble of approval.

For the first time in his life, Aodhán Zachary Aurelius Ashoka Vale-Brystion was not running from what he was.

He was becoming it.
