Fay woke up slowly. She didn't panic, she didn't tense, she didn't find her thoughts racing through the hundreds of worries that had been her constant companions for the last month. Her eyes stayed closed, her breathing barely shifted, but she was awake.

She could barely move. She wasn't trapped, she simply had nowhere to go. She could feel someone's back against hers, someone else's pressed against her chest. Her eyes snapped open only to find them filled with black hair. Who- Anya?

She started to shift, to try and push herself up and then everyone moved. She felt Seb reach out mentally, pulling her into a group conversation that immediately filled with concern and reassurance.

Fay? he sent.

You're both safe.

You kept saying that, Anya sent.

Fay felt the memories come rushing back. Not of the trip down, not of the mad dash to join them, but of coming unmoored and babbling once she was with them. She wasn't, she didn't. But she had. The time spent sleeping let her realize just how badly stress had affected her, how it had pushed her into things she wouldn't have done otherwise.

A month Anya, she sent. You were over a month late. What was I supposed to think, supposed to believe. I know the statistics, I know the odds. I know what you're facing as well as anyone who isn't an Elite Diver would. You. Were. Late.

Anya rolled over in place, one moment Fay could feel her back pressed into her the next they were eye to eye, nose to nose. In that instant she saw the edge of the tent behind her, saw there was no free space for her to have been further away.

Despite staring into her eyes, Anya said nothing. The shared band roiled with frustration, at her, at the situation, at everything. It filled with understanding and Anya finally smiled.

Thank you, she sent. For coming to find us, for looking.

Fay felt as much as heard Seb sliding down her back until he finally sat up by pushing her knees into Anya's legs. She took advantage of the space behind her back to sit up as well, Anya doing the same until the were all knee to knee, taking up every bit of free space.

Why didn't you finish emptying the tent?

Fay, Seb answered. It took us three hours to manage to stretch out. It took two before you would let go of either of us for long enough to reposition at all. Every movement made you tense and hold on to us tighter.

I didn't…

You kept us close, Anya confirmed. Once we were able to get comfortable it was cute.

If it weren't for the band she would have thought they were teasing her, making up a story about what she did while sleeping. That it hadn't been cute, that it had annoyed them or made them frustrated. They were serious though, they were happy she was there, happy she'd wanted them close.

They had missed her too.

~ * ~

When they finally stepped out of the tent Anya was surprised to see Fay tense. There were no risks here, no dangers. Their campsite was safe, the only threat was back at the landing. It hadn't even stopped them from continuing to dive normally, only prevented them from going home.

Fay? she sent.

Safe?

Everything is safe except the landing, Seb answered. The pig is too close to the passage so we can't sneak past it, but it doesn't wander unless we provoke it.

Pig? You're stuck because of a pig?

It's a variant Depths Boar, Anya said. A giant pig as Seb insists on calling it. We can hurt it, but if it hits me wrong it breaks bones. If it were to hit Seb…

A.. pig, Fay sent, her eyes bright. You couldn't get past the bacon?

Fay! Seriously Fay!?

She could feel Seb trying, failing, not to laugh. She couldn't believe Fay was making a joke about-

She felt some of the tension Fay had been projecting the whole time fade from the band. It was a horrible joke, a horrible reaction. But it was true. Some cooks claimed Depthsborn animals, those that had Shallow equivalents, tasted better than their Unawakened equivalent. So yes, they had been trapped by bacon.

Sorry, Fay sent, her emotions showing the words to be a complete lie. What do we do about it?

Seb came up with an idea for a plan before we slept…

She doubted Fay would like the plan. She didn't like the plan. Seb didn't like it either. But it was the best plan they had that would let them keep advancing. They couldn't go down another level, well maybe they could but three levels down was a real risk. They would be stuck if they couldn't get rid of it, they had no idea how long it would stick around the landing, or whether they could trust it not to come back if it did leave.

She knew Fay could feel their concern, their worry about the plan. They could feel her worry about being in the Depths, about being somewhere she didn't feel she was meant to be. This would be worse, if she said yes she would-

Does it get you both home safe?

Yes, she answered.

Family helps family, Fay sent back. What do you need.

~ * ~

Anya slammed her shoulder in the boar's ribs as it turned its attention to Fay. By the time it tried to focus on her she was out of reach once more and Fay was screaming at it. Seb's blade pulsed behind it, momentarily stripping it of the ability to use its hind legs.

She drove back in, this time feeling something give as she struck the same place for the fifth time. The beast squealed, unable to turn to face her without Seb charging in behind, unable to attack the woman yelling at it and throwing rocks at its face without being hit in the side.

Fay's job was simple yet vital. Make noise, keep attention, throw rocks. She was fast enough to stay out of reach, strong enough to make any rock that flew true dangerous. Anya could feel her terror, her desperate need for this to end yet there was nothing they could do.

If Anya closed in and fought the way she usually would it would only take a single blow to make her regret it. If Seb tried to do more than harass he'd be in even more danger. Three of them meant the Boar couldn't focus long enough, couldn't choose a target without leaving itself vulnerable.

The pitch of Fay's yells changed as the boar tried to charge. Anya watched her scurry to the side just as Seb finally landed a decisive blow. The creature stumbled, failing to rise as Seb shifted from slicing open one hamstring to the other.

This wasn't their type of fight, this wasn't what they were used to. Roamers weren't supposed to be variants, weren't supposed to be so dangerous that it needed extra help to keep it distracted. They should have been home a month ago, Fay shouldn't have needed to come looking, shouldn't have put herself through this.

Anya pummeled the cracked ribs, no longer needing to run away after every strike. She knew Seb was on the far side, trying his best to match her blow for blow.

"They. Were. Late!" Fay yelled, before bringing a rock the size of her head down from above to crash between the boar's eyes.

Anya froze as the beast stilled. Bladesong faded away, Seb's reaction confirming her suspicion.

Fay?

Are you…

Fay screamed again, this time the sound full of all the emotions that had been leaking through the band all day. She paused, panting, only to pick the rock back up and bring it down a second time with a shriek.

"Bacon. Is. For. Eating."

Fay, Anya sent. You got it.

Home?

Once we pack up the campsite.

Anya held out her hand. Fay took it and squeezed, her eyes still slightly dazed yet her emotions finally calming.

Can we have bacon when we get home?

Fay!
