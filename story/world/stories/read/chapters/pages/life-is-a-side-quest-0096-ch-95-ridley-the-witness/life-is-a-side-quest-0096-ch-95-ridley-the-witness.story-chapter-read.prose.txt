Ridley sat in a room with half a dozen armed orcs. Their attention was waning as they grew bored watching over a tattooed British man named Terry Branigann, hog-tied in a corner. The tattooed man had awoken and was extremely forthcoming with information, at least when they could understand him.

“So I was sittin' there, yeah? Then this skinny sort wiv a right weird tie shaped like a bleedin' 'and comes over an' gives us the mince pies, dinnit she? Proper givin' me the big come-on wiv 'er look. She ain't really me type, like, but next fing I know I'm goin' 'I'll do anyfing ya say, darlin’,' like some little short-arse jumpin' when me ol' mum used ter bark at us, innit?”

“And? Or did you say hand?”

“Yeah, a 'and, arm an' fingers wrapped roun' 'er bleedin' Gregory, innit? Proper dodgy Peckham Rye, that one — like some skinny bird got 'erself a right choke-collar shaped like a bleedin' mitt!”

“Okay… so what happened after that?”

“Next I knows it, I'm comin' round wiv some snotty little sprog's plastic toy popper jammed up against me Gregory, an' I'm bang in the middle of the bleedin' septic! The Yanks, mate! Bloody States! What a turn-up for the books, yeah? Thought I'd been nicked or summat, but nah — proper cross the pond!”

“Across the pond… what, like America?”

“Sure enough, yeah? Bang to rights, there I was.”

Ridley was frustrated with her earring today. It didn’t want to translate the weird English slang in her head, so she was having trouble understanding the British thug.

“So, let's try a one-word answer… what is your class?”

“I'm a monk, innit? Dunno why, like — cos I'm a proper mixed martial artist, scrappin' in the cage an' all that!”

Ridley gave up and just pushed through. “But your tattoos. What do your tattoos do?”

“Oh right, yeah? Now that's a right tasty topic, innit? I do a bit of tattin' on the side, me old china — side hustle wiv the needle an' ink, d'you know wha' I mean? I’m scrappin' wiv this super nippy little monster — proper quick on 'is plates, like. An' it hits me, bang in the loaf: I can make meself fast an' all! So I whips out the pen an' ink, does a quick little fleet-foot carty right on meself — boom! — an' suddenly I'm pourin'! Old fat-jawed geezer was in for a right shock next time we caged up for a proper barney, yeah?”

“Yeah… do you think you could remove a tattoo? Or break one?”

An orc burst into the portable shelter and started yelling, “Besho Kai! Besho Kai Bru-haha!”

The other orcs turned in anticipation and started yelling, “Besho Kai! Besho Kai!” before all running out of the room.

“Chief challenge?” Ridley spun around, wondering what the hell was going on. She started to follow the orcs out the door, but—

“Oye, I wanna see that, mate! Let me come wiv ya, yeah? Don't leave us out — I'm buzzin' ‘ere!”

She looked at the man. She didn’t believe he was actually hostile to them. It all looked like it was adding up to some form of mind control.

“Okay, Terry. I’ll bring you, but only so I can keep an eye on you.”

“Don’t you be callin’ me Terry, mate! I told ya, I’m Tel!”

“Whatever,” Ridley said, though she was done talking to the street tough.

She threw him over her shoulder before walking through the door. As she walked out, she saw the orcs encircling their fighting ring. They had enjoyed knocking the crap out of each other with the boxing gloves. There was a trash can dedicated to the blown-out mitts. They had started ordering them wholesale because of how many they went through now.

Pushing her way to the front, she dropped Tel next to her and planted a foot on him.

She saw Caloom in full combat gear, which she hadn’t seen since the Battle of Congo. He was trying to rile up the crowd, gathering the support of those around him. Chants arose for bloodshed.

To her shock, she saw Susan sitting in a chair on the other side of the ring.

Caloom began to shout. “Today I have been issued a challenge from a foolish little orc named Bru-haha. His arrogance will be put on display for your enjoyment as his blood decorates this sacred Wizards parking lot of battle!”

Shouts and jeers arose as all the orcs' blood began to boil. As the chants died down, a loud voice cried out:

“THE DARK SIDE OF THE FORCE IS A PATHWAY TO MANY ABILITIES SOME CONSIDER TO BE UNNATURAL…”

One side of the ring parted to reveal an orc wearing a black cape and a plastic Darth Vader mask. The orc walked forward confidently to stand across the ring from Caloom before pulling off the mask and throwing it into the crowd. A small group of orcs jumped to catch it before pummeling each other trying to get the coveted helmet of Lord Vader.

Bru-haha smiled at Caloom, wearing his safety vest adorned with blue tags with pride. A round of cheers sounded, shouting, “Bru-haha! Bru-haha!”

Ridley knew support for Bru-haha had swelled as he had introduced many new amenities for the orcs. He had claimed credit for tacos, let several influential orcs bribe him for movie choices, and recently showed them the new favorite sport of the orcs: rugby.

To Ridley's surprise, Susan stood and walked to the center of the circle before getting everyone's attention.

“You are gathered today to witness a challenge for the position of chieftain. There are a few new rules that have been imposed by Congo. First among them is that you cannot fight to the death. Challenges for the position of chief must be approved by Congo and have the support of at least forty percent of the clan, and the new chief will have a minimum rule of six months. If your contestant is knocked unconscious or submits, the challenge is over. Do you agree to these rules?”

“I agree to these rules,” Bru-haha replied.

“I AGREE TO THIS WEAKLING'S CHALLENGE!” Caloom roared. “And your new rules are agreeable as well, Master Susan.” He nodded.

“Congo takes no responsibility for any injuries. You assume responsibility for any property damage during the battle. Any and all events, including injury and death, are for the orcs to compensate...” Susan took her seat again and lifted her hand.

As she did, Bru-haha lifted a small red bottle to his lips and drank it before pocketing the bottle again.

Susan shouted as she dropped her arm, “BEGIN!”

Caloom spun his naginata, the motion creating a gust of wind around him. He crouched before exploding forward.

Bru-haha pumped his arms, his brass knuckles shining in the evening sun. He planted his feet and waited for Caloom’s furious strike. As the bladed haft thrust approached Bru’s chest, he twisted his hips and punched the blade to the side, deflecting the attack. Then, with his other hand, he jabbed the chief's forearms lightly.

Ridley had seen this attack a few times before. She was aware there was a stun or paralysis effect to those brass knuckles. She had never wondered how hard the attack had to be to activate the effect.

The chieftain's body locked up for a second, and Bru-haha stepped in close to the chief and threw three rapid punches to his jaw, chest, and ribs, causing Caloom to stumble backward. Bru-haha charged forward to catch him, but Caloom managed to jump backward with a leap.

His physical prowess and speed were levels greater than Bru’s own, Ridley was sure. Despite that, he had managed to put Caloom on the back foot rather quickly.

“Right proper in-fighter, innit?” Terry commented. “I’d like a swing after the spear bloke is off.”

Caloom looked simultaneously impressed and irritated.

“A nice trick you have picked up, little Bru-haha. That is a far cry from the power required of a chief.”

“A snap judgment of an opponent is a far cry from the mind required of a chief,” Bru said with a satisfied grin.

Caloom rushed Bru-haha again but kept his distance with a flurry of thrusts. Bru-haha started taking cuts as he deflected the most lethal attacks. Caloom started pressing the attack, and Bru gave ground.

For a second, Ridley was under the impression that Bru-haha’s arms were getting covered in blood, but when her focus sharpened, that wasn’t right.

Bru’s arms were turning red. Rust red.

Caloom held nothing back for an unrelenting minute. As soon as he took the slightest pause for breath, Bru darted forward. Caloom stabbed out twice, first catching Bru-haha’s rib cage. Then one hit straight into his heart.

As the naginata's tip connected, a shield bloomed in front of Bru-haha’s chest. The blocked attack knocked Caloom's balance off, and Bru took the opportunity to latch onto Caloom's weapon arm tightly.

Caloom started bashing Bru’s face with his other hand, but Bru took the attacks. Caloom let out a scream of pain. His arm started to smoke, burning under the grip of Bru-haha’s red hands.

Caloom opened his mouth wide in return and screamed out a cloud of dark smoke.

Ridley remembered at least that part of her fight with him. The putrid black smoke had thoroughly paralyzed her muscles, making her helpless.

As the spewing cloud cleared, Caloom gave a pained smile.

Bru-haha, still clutching Caloom’s arm, looked up and smiled.

“My turn.”

Bru-haha opened his mouth and spewed a red dust from his lungs over Caloom’s head and chest.

Caloom collapsed backward, falling onto his back.

Bru-haha jumped on the downed opponent and punched his head as swiftly as he could manage. Caloom’s face and chest were bubbling under a layer of red dust. It was burning like he had been doused in acid.

The roar of the orcs intensified.

Between hits, Caloom struggled to stand, but every few punches his body would go limp.

Susan had said submitting or being knocked unconscious were conditions for winning, but there was no way Caloom was going to submit.

Bru-haha pummeled the orc until he was certain there was no longer any sign of the orc trying to move. Bru let up for just a second to see if there was a response from the chieftain. There wasn’t even a hint of movement from the limp body.

Ridley watched as the red around Bru-haha’s arms faded back to their usual dark green.

He stood with a slight groan from the wound in his side. He stepped on the neck of his former chief and looked at the quieted crowd.

He lifted his fists in the air and looked at the crowd of orcs.

“I, AM YOUR FATHER!”

The crowd roared and chanted, “Bru-haha! Bru-haha!”

Bru pulled out a light green vial and drank. His cuts visibly sewed themselves back together before he smashed the vial on the ground and lifted his arms again in victory.

The crowd rushed in and lifted him in the air before carrying him away, still chanting his name.

Ridley wasn’t sure if there was more to the crowning of the chief. She really didn’t care. All she felt was a slight worry in her gut at the cunning of Bru-haha. She was starting to miss the childlike innocence of the orc.

Susan stood from her chair, and Ridley marched forward.

“Well, that was rather violent,” Susan commented as Ridley approached.

“Where is Alexander?” Ridley asked, some anger in her voice.

“I’m sorry, Ridley, but that is classified information.”

“Then when is he getting back!?” Ridley asked, exasperated.

“That is unclear. I assume when he has completed the mission.”

“You do realize this could get out of hand quickly.”

Susan smiled. “That is why I am promoting Bru-haha, both in his clan and at Congo. If the orcs have a vested interest in our success, then they will continue to work hard.”

Ridley just sighed, resigned to the fact that she was helpless in this situation.

“Don’t worry. Your efforts are being seen as well. I’m sure your next raise will be maxed out.”

Ridley marched away from Susan without answering. She moved up to Terry and shoved her tattoo in his face.

“How quickly can you get this thing off of me?”
