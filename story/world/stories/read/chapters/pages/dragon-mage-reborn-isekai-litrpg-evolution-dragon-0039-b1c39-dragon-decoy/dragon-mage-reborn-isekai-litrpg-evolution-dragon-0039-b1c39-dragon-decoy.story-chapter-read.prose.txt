“Absolutely not,” Nolan quickly interjected, his protective instinct flaring. “There’s no way I’m letting Freya be used as a sacrificial lamb.”

“The crystal creates illusions—powerful ones. With it, we can disguise your dragon to appear as my daughter, while the real Kauli is safely hidden away.” He extended the crystal toward us. “The Azerek was passed down to me by my father, and his father before him. Its magic is ancient and true. The spell will work.”

“That’s not the problem,” Nolan retorted.

“I will pay you handsomely, more than the value of your dragon.”

“Freya is not your common baby dragon,” he bit back defensively.

“I understand that dragons are rare to come by, but I promise, I—”

“We need to find another way,” Nolan said as he cut him off, visibly upset with his suggestion. “You don’t have any warriors within your village?”

“None as willing as I am to deceive our Elders.”

My heart swelled with gratitude at his defense of me, but I couldn’t help thinking about the poor girl who would be sacrificed if we didn’t act. There was no doubt that the “Dark One” was up to no good, but we couldn’t let some innocent teen become another victim.

‘Nolan,’ I said through our mental link, ‘I think I should do it.’

‘Absolutely not,’ he fired back immediately.

‘But if there is no one else, are we just going to let her get sacrificed?’

‘We’ll find another way.’

‘Maybe, maybe not. But we don’t have time to find another option. I am willing to do this, Nolan. Let me do it.’

Nolan paced the small hut, his boots scuffing against the earthen floor. ‘It’s too dangerous, Freya. We still don’t know what this ‘Dark One’ really is.’

‘But I’m not helpless,’ I reminded him. ‘I may be small, but I’m still a dragon. If things go wrong, I can defend myself.’

Pozei watched our silent exchange with curious eyes, clearly aware we were communicating. “Your infant dragon speaks to you,” he observed. “A private link?”

“Yeah,” Nolan confirmed.

The shaman nodded, impressed. “Even more remarkable than I first thought.” He held the crystal up, its facets catching the dim light. “The Azerek will project the illusion perfectly. The Dark One will see only Kauli, not your dragon.”

I felt Nolan’s resistance wavering. ‘I can do this,’ I pressed. Nolan’s jaw clenched as he considered my words. ‘I’ll be careful,’ I assured him. ‘Besides, we both know I’m not exactly defenseless.’

After a long moment, Nolan sighed deeply. “How exactly would this work?” he asked Pozei, his tone making it clear he wasn’t committing yet.

Relief washed over the old shaman’s face. “The Azerek crystal will project an illusion around your dragon. To all observers, she will appear as my daughter. The disguise is complete—voice, mannerisms, even scent.”

That’s some pretty serious stuff!

“The ceremony involves bringing the sacrifice to the cave entrance. Once there, the Chosen walks in alone while the elders perform the ritual chant.”

Nolan crossed his arms. “And then what? Freya just walks in and... what exactly?”

“Once inside, she must locate the sacred altar and present herself,” Pozei explained. “According to our legends, the Dark One then appears to claim the offering.”

‘And that’s when I fight it,’ I said, Nolan looking at me like I’d lost my mind.

He then turned to the shaman and clarified, “I am still against Freya being sacrificed. And I’m also against her fighting the creature, too. The plan is to disguise her as your daughter and find a way to draw it out. So, if you don’t have a way to do that, then we have no deal. And I don’t care how much money you throw at me—Freya isn’t a replacement offering.”

The shaman went quiet for a moment as he stroked his chin, the reptilian beastkin deep in thought. He then turned around and opened a drawer from a small wooden chest. After rummaging through various trinkets and bottles, he pulled out a small vial containing a luminescent blue liquid that seemed to swirl on its own.

“This is Moonwater,” he explained, holding it up for us to see. “Collected only during the eclipse of the twin moons, once every 70 years. It has the power to neutralize dark magic for a brief period.”

“Now, that’s more like it,” Nolan said before he finally cracked his first smile since we got here.

“If we can get the Dark One to ingest this, then we can temporarily unbind the creature’s connection to our village, while rendering its powers useless.” He offered the vial to Nolan. “After which, your dragon can escape, and draw it out.”

And just like that, Nolan’s smile turned into a frown as he brought the vial to eye level. “That asshole needs to drink this shit?”

The shaman nodded. “Correct. It is the only way it’ll work.”

Nolan examined the swirling contents. “And you’re sure this will work?”

“Nothing is certain when dealing with ancient powers,” Pozei admitted. “But it is our best chance.”

I moved closer to Nolan, nudging him with my snout. ‘I think we should try it. If we can save this girl without anyone else getting hurt, then we need to try.’

He scoffed. ‘Even if I say no, I’m sure you’d find a way to do it. Stubborn pain in my ass.’ The gunslinger handed me the vial and I tucked it in my inventory. So the plan was set—I had to be a decoy sacrifice, reach the altar, find the Dark One, and have him drink this moon juice that has been fermenting for over 70 years.

The shaman bowed. “I am eternally in your debt. Thank you for saving my daughter. If not for your courage, she would be lost to me forever.”

“Don’t thank us yet,” Nolan warned. “We still have to pull this off.”

Pozei nodded solemnly, then turned for the exit. “Come, little one. We must prepare you for the ceremony. Time grows short.”

“Where are we going?” Nolan asked.

“To my daughter. In order for the illusion spell to work, we need to mirror her appearance precisely. It must be perfect, down to the smallest detail. The elders are not easily fooled, especially Elder Kazak.”

Nolan and I followed Pozei to his daughter. I thought about the mission, my very first mission, in the guild of Robinhood-esque bandits. While I was eager to help, I was still terrified inside. I didn’t want Nolan to see it, but I had no idea how this was going to play out, and that concept frightened me. We had no idea how this ‘Dark One’ looked like, what kind of power he had, how big he was, or how invincible he could be. Taking all of this into consideration, I understood the situation I was in. I was basically playing bait, and I'd have to find a way to get a dangerous and powerful entity to drink a magical potion.

Nevertheless, my determination outweighed my fear. If I didn’t do this, an innocent girl would die to appease the selfishness of some cave beast, followed by many sacrifices just like her.

According to the shaman, Kauli was getting ready at a temple, and unfortunately for us, said temple wasn’t isolated. Pozei led us through the village, keeping to the shadows. The settlement was larger than I’d expected, with dozens of rounded huts arranged in concentric circles around a central plaza. Villagers moved about their mid-day routines, unaware of our little covert mission.

We stayed hidden, darting between buildings until we reached a small stone structure on the village’s eastern edge. Unlike the modest dwellings of the other villagers, this building was adorned with intricate carvings and painted symbols.

“The purification temple,” Pozei whispered, ushering us toward a side entrance. “We must be quick. The elders visit periodically to check on her preparation.”

Inside, the air was heavy with incense. Candles cast dancing shadows on walls covered with ancient murals depicting scenes of sacrifice and rebirth. In the center of the room, a young beautiful girl with blue hair sat cross-legged on a woven mat, dressed in flowing white robes adorned with silver embroidery. Her scales were a lighter green than her father’s, almost luminescent in the candlelight. She also appeared more human than reptilian, possibly capturing those features from her mother’s side. Her eyes were closed in meditation, but they fluttered open as we entered.

“Father?” she whispered, her voice trembling with a gasp. It took her a moment to realize he was there, and then she sprinted over to him with a tight hug. “Father…” she wept against his shoulder. “I miss you.” And then there was a loud thud to interrupt the precious moment, everyone turning around to Nolan behind us, who had taken out a guard.

“What?” Nolan said as he dragged the hefty guard by the collar. “It’s not like he’s dead. Just, sleeping it off.”

I rolled my eyes.

Kauli turned back to her father nervously, the expression on her face asking about a billion questions.

“Friends, my child,” the shaman reassured her. “They have come to help us.”

Her eyes widened as they fell on me. “A dragon?” she whispered, awe replacing fear. “I’ve only seen drawings of them in our historical scrolls.”

“Name’s Nolan, and this is Freya,” Nolan explained, dragging the unconscious guard behind a row of urns. “And she’s going to take your place in the ceremony.”

Kauli looked between her father and us, confusion written all over her face. “But how? The elders would know immediately.”

Pozei produced the Azerek crystal, its facets glimmering in the candlelight. “With magic,” he explained, holding it up. “The crystal will disguise our dragon friend to appear exactly as you.”

“The Azerek?” She looked at her father a bit offended. “You’re breaking the sacred laws.”

“To save you, my daughter, I would break every law ever written,” Pozei replied, his voice steady despite the emotion in his eyes. “We must make haste, before the elders pay you a visit. You and Freya must be in contact for the illusion spell to work.”

I stepped forward, where I noticed immediately that Kauli had a fear of dragons. She moved back a step, her eyes unsure. I tried to make myself appear smaller, lowering my head and my knees closer to the ground.

‘She’s afraid of me,’ I said to Nolan through our link.

‘Can’t blame her. Not everyone grows up with a baby dragon for a best friend,’ he replied, amused.

I approached Kauli slowly, keeping my movements gentle. Nolan vouched for me by saying, “She won’t hurt you. I promise.”

I stretched my neck forward until her fingertips grazed my scales. The contact was subtle, Kauli very lightly grazing me until she felt comfortable enough to pet my head. A smile found her face as she tentatively caressed my scales. Her jaw was on the floor. Did I just make a new friend?

“Good,” Pozei said. “Now, remain still, both of you.”

He raised the crystal between us, murmuring his incantation. The Azerek began to pulse with an inner light, bathing both Kauli and I in its ethereal glow.

I felt a strange tingling sensation spread across my scales, like tiny electric currents dancing over my body. The air around us shimmered and distorted.

Fascinated, I watched as my claws transformed into slender, scaled fingers. My tail seemed to fold into my body, disappearing completely. The sensation was bizarre—tickled a little, too. My snout shortened, my entire body reshaping itself until I was staring at what appeared to be my own reflection in Kauli’s lavender eyes.

“It’s working,” Nolan called out behind us, half keeping watch, half engrossed in my transformation.

I looked down at my hands—no, Kauli’s hands—turning them over. My body felt simultaneously foreign and familiar, like wearing someone else’s clothes that somehow fit perfectly. When I tried to speak, Kauli’s voice emerged from my throat, “This is...” I gasped, the unfamiliar timbre startling me. My first words spoken aloud in Etheryn… I was overwhelmed with emotion. “This is so weird!”

“To us both,” Kauli said as she ran her fingers through my blue hair. And then she smiled. “You are my complete clone. The power of the Azerek is flawless.”

Nolan stared at me and smirked. “Well, holy shit, it actually worked. You look exactly like her.”

I turned to Pozei, who nodded in satisfaction. “The illusion is complete. To all who see you, you are Kauli.”

I walked over to the low basin of shimmering clear water behind us at the temple filled with flower petals and knelt at the edge of it. My reflection left me speechless. I was in a humanoid body for the first time, my excitement contained behind my lavender eyes. “This is incredible….” I paused, and then turned to the shaman quickly. “What about my dragon abilities?” I asked, the unfamiliar voice still catching me off guard.

“The crystal disguises your appearance, not your essence,” Pozei explained. “Your powers remain your own.”

I wondered then how I’d look in my dragon final form. Humanoid, dragonkin, whatever word was common around these parts—I felt like this body somehow prepared me for what was to come. Of course, I couldn’t expect the last stage of my evolution to reflect my past self. The curly red hair, the freckles, the inferior height—I don’t know. I missed it and didn’t miss it at the same time. I guess when you’ve finally gotten used to accepting the way things were, you become sentimental about change. But at the same time, the concept of starting over was also exciting.

Being not so vertically challenged brought a smile to my face. I felt seen, finally standing at eye-level with Nolan instead of constantly looking up at him.

I turned to face him, suddenly self-conscious about my new form. “How do I look?” I asked, aloud! Twirling once, I showed him the ceremonial robes flowing around me.

“Different,” he finally said, clearing his throat. “But it’s still you in there, right?”

“One hundred percent me,” I assured him, tapping the side of my head. “Just with a fancy new outfit.”

“The ceremony begins at sundown,” Pozei said, pulling out a few things from his inventory and offering them to me. The shaman gave me a sword, a magically enhanced whip, and some armor. The sword was blessed with Olfeshana, a special herb mix that when infused with his blade, healed its wielder. The whip was also unique, having the ability to extend and retract with mere thought, and the armor did what a traditional armor did—boost fortitude.

He told me to keep my items hidden at all times, until I reached the cave.

Kauli walked over to me, her fingers adjusting the ceremonial robes around my shoulders. “The silver embroidery represents purity,” she explained, her voice hushed. “You stand with confidence, as if you’re used to walking on two legs.” She smiled.

Well, she wasn’t wrong!

“Is there anything specific I should know about the ceremony?” I asked, trying to get used to speaking aloud with vocal cords instead of telepathically.

“You must walk with your head held high,” Kauli instructed. “Show no fear, no hesitation. The sacrifice is considered an honor.” Her voice cracked slightly. “They tell us it’s an honor to be chosen, but—” She stopped herself, looking away. I could see the truth in her eyes.

“But it’s not,” I finished for her.

She shook her head, a tear slipping down her cheek. “I don’t want to disappoint my people….”

“But you also don’t want to die,” I sympathized. “It’s in our nature. No one does.”

“Many live their last moments in pride, thinking they’re serving a higher purpose,” the lizardfolk girl said, “But there’s no honor in being sacrificed to something evil.”

“The Dark One has protected our village for generations,” her father followed, “At least, that’s what the elders preach.”

Kauli shook her head. “I’ve... had dreams. Nightmares. About what waits in the cave. It’s not a protector. It’s something… hungry,” she whispered, wrapping her arms around herself. “It feeds on more than just flesh.”

A chill ran down my spine. “What do you mean?”

“In my visions, it takes something from each sacrifice. Something bright. Something...” she struggled to find the words, “essential.”

“Their soul?”

“I don’t know,” Kauli admitted. “But after each sacrifice, the village prospers. Better harvests, fewer illnesses, and stronger children born.” She looked at her father, and then back at me. “While we thrive, other villages around us suffer. Crops wither, sickness spreads, children are born weak or not at all.”

Nolan cursed under his breath. “Sounds like your Dark One is stealing life force from the surrounding lands and giving it to you.”

“Which means,” I said slowly, the realization dawning on me, “it’s not protection. It’s exploitation.”

Pozei nodded gravely. “I have suspected this for many years. The elders claim it is the natural balance, that our village is blessed above others because we honor the ancient pact.” He looked down, shame evident in his posture. “I was once a believer myself, until I began to study the ancient texts that predate our current traditions.”

“So we are dealing with some kind of parasitic deity?” Nolan deducted.

“A being of immense magical power that has controlled our people for centuries,” Pozei replied. “The oldest texts speak of a time when it was imprisoned in the cave—not worshipped.”

“Well, all of that bullshit ends tonight,” Nolan said.

Kauli turned to me with a weak smile filled with both sadness and relief. She took my hands into hers gently and said, “Thank you, brave dragon. You are truly special, not only for your incredible intelligence, but your bravery and benevolence.”

I smiled back at her sweetly. “My mother always taught me to never turn a blind eye to people in need. I’m just doing what she’d do.”

Kauli gave me a hug, a tight yet warm embrace that showed me just how appreciative she was. When she peeled back, she gave me instructions. “When you reach the altar, you must kneel before it and recite the ancient prayer of offering.”

“What’s the prayer?” I asked, trying to commit every detail to memory.

“I will teach you,” she said, her grip over my fingers slightly tighter. “It’s in our language, but I’ll help you pronounce it correctly.”

As quickly as she could while Nolan kept guard, Kauli drilled me on the ceremony—the proper way to walk, how to address the elders, and most importantly, the ancient prayer. The words felt strange coming from my lips—harsh consonants and flowing vowels that seemed like tongue twisters.

“Sundown approaches,” Pozei warned, glancing nervously at the temple entrance. “We must hurry.”
