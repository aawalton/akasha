The rest of the day passed uneventfully. By nightfall, each squad of three knights had pitched their tents and taken up rotation, ensuring at least three men remained on watch at all times.

It wasn’t until the moon reached its peak that I finally pulled the wrapped tome from the inner pocket of my cloak.

Dralos sat beside me on a charred beam salvaged from the ruins of a nearby building, now repurposed as seating. Together, we watched the campfire crackle at the center of our tent circle.

The night was abnormally quiet. No monsters stirred. No beasts howled. No insects chirped. Only the soft murmur of low conversation and the snap of the flames filled the darkness.

“The binding material is similar to the slave chains in Sealrite,” Dralos murmured, reaching over to run clawed fingers across the chains. “Not the same blend of metal and magic, but close. Closer than anything else I’ve seen in Lysoria.”

“There’s no standardized formula across nations?” I asked. With how important anti-mana and anti-energy metal likely was to maintaining order, it was surprising to hear there wasn't an optimal, mass-produced combination.

He chuckled. “There would be if even a single nation could cooperate with another.”

“And the prayer-script?”

“That,” he said, gesturing to the etched writing, “is wholly unfamiliar to me.”

“It’s heretical,” Josephine cut in, pushing aside the flap of her nearby tent. Coincidence or intentional, she'd managed to have her tent placed near the center of our camp. Her ice-blue eyes glinted beneath midnight-black hair that seemed to melt into the night. She stepped forward until she hovered over the exposed tome. “My lady, how much do you know about the Holy Kingdom?”

“Not much,” I admitted. “The Church of Light holds the predominant position, with its Pope effectively seated atop the nation's highest throne among the smaller denominations.”

Josephine nodded, though she winced slightly. “It’s more a position of reverence than rulership or throne—but yes. The Church is the dominant religious power within the Holy Kingdom and within many other nations across Pularea. We try to preserve balance by allowing a diversity of beliefs, so long as none violate the teachings of Goddess Delilah. That script belongs to the Cult of Power,” she added, voice darkening. “They’re not officially recognized anywhere, not even in more underground societies, despite that being where the cultists thrive the most. They’ve been gaining quite a bit of traction as of late, particularly within the Pandorian Empire.”

“The more influence they gain there,” Dralos inferred, “the harder it becomes for the Holy Kingdom to root them out."

Josephine nodded.

“But why would the cultists be here?” I wondered aloud, turning the tome in my hands.

“To obtain a Lysorian Cultivation Doctrine?” Dralos offered.

I shook my head. “Why not simply take it? Why bind it with scripture that drains heart energy and mana from anyone who touches it?”

“A trap, then?”

“Possibly, I suppose.”

“I believe it was meant as a trap,” Josephine said. “The Cult drains victims of their energy and mana, using prayer-scripts like this to absorb and contain it. We still don’t know what they do with it afterward, but they are harvesting for a reason.”

“Do you think they’re responsible for what we found in the Sanctum?” Dralos asked, his grimace betraying the memory.

Josephine shook her head. “Highly unlikely. The Cult’s methods are clean. Quiet. Their victims are often found weakened, occasionally dead, but never mutilated. Our investigations show that the cultists prefer to act under the radar. In and out before anyone knows they're even there. Sometimes it takes months for our paladins to discover that anything even happened. What we saw was something else.”

“Maybe thugs, wannabes trying to impress the Cult,” I mused, tossing a chunk of our charred seat into the fire. “Still, what we saw in Sanctum did seem like the work of a beast. Not human. Except… the way the bodies were nailed to the cave walls. That felt human. Very human. And very angry.”

An uneasy silence settled over us until Dralos finally spoke.

“Lady Lilliana… could it have been the Baroness?”

I blinked, surprised by both the question and his careful tone. “That’s not the woman I understand her to be. And I don’t see when she could have done this. After their prisoner escaped," I said, purposefully avoiding the use of progeinator or sire around Josephine. I wasn't sure if the King knew of the existence of progenitors, but I didn't need him to know that I did know about them. "The Silverwaters sheltered with the Goldenhearts, then traveled to Elyndor for the tournament. She wouldn’t have had time to return to Silverwater City and orchestrate a massacre in a sanctified catacomb—especially one I vaguely recall her devoutly believing in.”

It was true. While the Silverwaters had not been the most devout of followers, I did remember them leaving for Sanctums every fortnight.

“Is there anyone else who might be capable?” Josephine asked, voice soft with dread, gaze distant with memory of the horror.

I shrugged. “I was never… close with my birth house.”

Her eyes widened briefly, then she dropped into a deep curtsy. “Forgive me, Duchess Alistar. I meant no offense.”

I waved off the apology. “It’s of no concern. They’re not people I ever wanted to be close to in the first place.”

Josephine nodded and sat beside me, opposite Dralos, both thankfully silent. I returned my focus to the tome resting in my lap. Without touching the scripture-covered chains, I activated my heart and mana cores. A buzz surged through my navel and my heart, coalescing in my head as the energies blended, lightning-hot Shen energy coursing through me.

I had never used Shen energy outside of combat before. It was a weapon to me. An extension of my Will meant for killing. So when I summoned it now, letting it rise from my cores and thread through my limbs, I felt the usual warm rush, hum of power, and spark of control.

But I hadn't factored in the external effect it might have on those around me.

All around me, the air visibly warped.

The moment Shen energy burst forth from my dantians, the temperature spiked. The fire shuddered, cackling to twice its size. Shadows stretched and twisted.

Josephine gasped, a sound distinctly sharp and involuntary. Then she yelped as if bitten. The priestess shot to her feet, cradling her left hand protectively against her chest.

I turned, eyes narrowing. Even through the gloom of the night, I could see the back of her hand had been scorched black, still smoking faintly. The edges of it blistering like paper held too close to a flame.

“I—what did you—?” she stammered, stepping back.

I didn’t answer. My gaze had already shifted to Dralos.

The draconian warrior, usually unreadable, had moved back a half dozen feet. His claws were flexed, arms half-raised—not in defense, but as if resisting the instinct to flee. His face was drawn tight, golden pupils wide.

Worried.

No… not worried.

Afraid.

Then came the sound, sharp and immediate.

A crack like lightning snapped by my knee. I glanced down at the tome, startled, and rose swiftly to my feet, inadvertently knocking the text to the ground. Sparks leapt from the dirt around me, thin and luminous, weaving in and out of the earth like threads of living metal. They danced around my boots, tracing wild, elegant arcs of opulent veins that shimmered in electric hues of power.

The tome hit the ground hard, the metallic chains clattering like armor dropped on stone. The script-laced bindings hissed, steam rising from them as if I’d poured acid onto fire. The foreign letters glowed one last time—then evaporated, curling away into ash like sacred paper caught in a pyre.

We froze.

No one spoke. The fire popped once. Sparks of lightning still whispering around my feet.

Our eyes met, all of us stunned and a bit confused.

Then, with careful deliberation, Dralos cleared his throat.

“Well… I’ve no clue what that was,” he said, eyes flicking to the sparks still crackling at my feet. “But if Your Grace could kindly deactivate whatever divine calamity you are currently wielding, perhaps we might examine the tome.”

Josephine winced and pulled her smoking hand from her chest with wild, distraught eyes that were beginning to fill with tears. "My... my lady... my hand..." I frowned, immediately cutting off the flow of my Shen energy.

Dralos rushed over to her, examining the burn before calling over Carla from Raphael's nearby tent. The healer hurried over, green light emitting from her hand before she'd even reached the priestess. After a moment of healing, Josephine's crumbling expression and confidence had steadied somewhat, and she turned her gaze back on his, though her complexion was still white as snow.

She shook her head vigorously, chewing the inside of her lip. Her eyes bounced rapidly from me to the tome to Dralos, seemingly unsure what to do. “We cannot examine it. Only the rightful owner or His Majesty may access a Doctrine.”

“Am I not the owner?” I asked, rather irritated at the turn of events and the priestess' overall fragility despite her ceaseless attempts to enforce rules on me.

She shook her head. “With your father’s death, it would pass to the heir.”

“My father’s dying words named me his successor,” I said, continuing the lie I’d woven since entering Elyndor.

Josephine’s skeptical look said she didn’t buy it. “In that case, I suppose it will work for you. The binding is ancient magic, automatic and unchangeable.”

"Maybe his final words weren’t enough,” I muttered. Dralos raised a brow as I chuckled. “Fortunately, the Gods have chosen to bless us with my eldest brother's presence. Before our father died, I was told he was the most viable heir.”

"I thought you said he likely was not the heir anymore," Josephine bit back. She winced, cutting off the last word when Carla ran a hand over the raw, freshly healed skin.

I shrugged. “Perhaps he is still the heir, and the Gods have purposely blessed us with good fortune. I am a Saintess, after all.”

She frowned, and her skeptical expression did not fade, clearly uncertain whether I was joking. My title wasn’t a secret, though I hadn’t officially completed the Saintess Trials. I'd interrupted them quite brutally, after all. Still, Dralos and Benedict had spent half a year spreading the title after the Hydra battle. Within Lysoria, at least, the Church could do little to deny it.

I sat and picked up the fallen tome. Ash clung to the cover, but I ignored it. A very small, very controlled flicker of Shen energy exerted through my fingertips snapped the chains. They fell off the text like a loosened towel, falling the ground with a soft thud. My pulse quickened as I opened the first page, and the edges of my mouth twitched into the ghost of a smile. But then I saw what was written on the first page.

Nothing.

It was blank.

I flipped to the second. Blank.

Third. Fourth. Fifth.

All blank.

“What in all the nine Hells?” I muttered, rifling through the empty pages. “There’s nothing here.” I extended the book to Josephine, but she recoiled with the fear of a beaten child.

“A-a-a-apologies, Duchess. I’m not worthy to touch a Doctrine, let alone read one.”

“There’s nothing to read!” I snapped, frustration rising. She flinched again, but I ignored her and stood, waving the blank pages in her face. “Why is it blank, Priestess?”

She didn't say anything, but pulled her hand away from Carla and wrapped them both around her. Carla looked at me questioningly, but I was too caught up in my frustration to bother with responding. Dralos, instead, dismissed the dame back to her quarters.

I tossed the book to Dralos, who caught it easily as Carla departed. He flipped through the pages, then set it back beside me and began pacing, claws scratching his jaw in thought.

“As I said, Duchess,” Josephine murmured, “you need permission from the current owner.”

“Verbally?” Dralos asked, his eyes shifting between her and the tome.

She hesitated, then nodded. “Yes… I believe so.”

With a sigh, I touched the book and stored it in my ring. “I’ll see you both in the morning. After we explore the Silverwater Mansion, we’ll head out.”

“What about the catacombs?” Josephine asked.

I didn’t answer, turning away from her and heading to my tent. I’d intended to investigate the catacombs at some point, but if the barony’s heart wouldn’t reveal its secrets, I had little reason to rescue it. Perhaps the Silverwater estate, where they'd kept the progenitor captive, would yield some answers or give me any reason to save this city that continues to give me nothing.
