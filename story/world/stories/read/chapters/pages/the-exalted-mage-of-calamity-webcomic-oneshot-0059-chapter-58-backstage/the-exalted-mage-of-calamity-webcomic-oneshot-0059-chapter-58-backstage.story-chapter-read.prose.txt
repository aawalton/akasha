“She was so good!” Gwen said, holding onto her cup of emberberry liquor. The glass was still half-full. She seemed too hesitant to take another sip. Perhaps it still was too early for her.

Veronica couldn’t blame her. Even she had been curious once when she was younger.

“It was enjoyable,” Veronica replied.

With Nasha’s performance finished, the evening at the Crimson Court was effectively over. Many of the patrons had already finished their meals and were beginning to stand; their chairs slid softly across the floor as they prepared to leave.

When a waiter came to their table, Veronica reached for her coin pouch, ready to pay.

“Oh, payment will not be necessary,” the waiter said as he began collecting their plates.

Gwen blinked, confused. “Huh? I thought only the drinks were free.”

He nodded politely. “Yes, ma’am. That is correct. However, your meal has already been covered.”

“By who?” Veronica asked.

The waiter stacked Veronica’s empty plate atop Gwen’s before answering.

“Miss Nasha,” he said. “Also, since you appear to be visiting for the first time, VIP guests are allowed to briefly meet one of our performers tonight if they wish.”

Gwen brightened and turned to Veronica. “We should thank her.”

Veronica considered it for a moment.

The night was already late. Any plan to bring Gwen to the Mage Tower to arrange protection for Rocky would have to wait until morning, regardless. There was little reason to refuse.

After all, Veronica was slightly interested in meeting Nasha, too.

A female attendant approached just as the waiter finished clearing their table.

“If you would like to meet one of the performers, please follow me,” she said politely.

Gwen glanced at Veronica and gave a quick nod before already stepping forward to follow.

Veronica rose as well, bringing her second cup of Yggdrasil’s Tears with her.

“Which performer would you two ladies like to see?” the attendant asked as she guided them away from the tables.

“Nasha,” Gwen answered immediately.

The attendant smiled faintly. “Of course. Right this way.”

She led them through a narrow corridor beside the stage, away from the main hall. The noise of the remaining audience quickly faded behind them as they moved deeper backstage.

As they neared the end of the hallway, they heard raised voices drifting from one of the rooms ahead.

“…I already told you. This isn’t that type of place. And I’m not interested.”

A man’s voice answered, thick, loud, and also slurred.

“Oh, come on. I paid for the VIP tickets tonight. Don’t pretend you didn’t enjoy all that attention out there on stage.”

The attendant guiding them didn’t slow. She simply continued walking at the same calm pace.

When they reached the doorway, the scene inside became clear.

The man speaking was a well-dressed patron. Not a noble, but clearly someone with money. More noticeably, he was standing far too close to Nasha.

Gwen stiffened beside Veronica, her brows knitting together.

“Hey… should we—”

“No need.”

“No need.”

Both Veronica and the attendant spoke at the same time.

They glanced at each other briefly before turning their attention back to the room.

Veronica assumed that the attendant was used to a scene like this. Perhaps it was almost normal or routine. For Veronica though, she’d seen how something like this had already played out back in the forest.

Inside, the man reached forward, aiming to grab Nasha’s wrist.

In one smooth motion, without changing her expression, Nasha stepped aside. She caught the man’s wrist, twisted his arm, and sent him crashing face-first onto the floor.

A sharp thud echoed into the hallway.

The man groaned as Nasha calmly knelt beside him, pinning his arm behind his back with almost rehearsed ease.

Moments later, two guards rushed in from the opposite end of the corridor.

“Escort him out,” Nasha said evenly. “And add him to the list. He’s banned from entering again.”

The guards hauled the man to his feet, gripping him firmly on both sides. He tried to protest, his words slurred and angry, but they dragged him down the hallway without much difficulty.

Only after they disappeared did Nasha sigh quietly and brush her hands down the front of her dress.

Then she turned.

“Sorry about that, you two,” she said.

Gwen stepped into the room first. “Are you okay?”

A small smile appeared on Nasha’s face.

“I’m fine. Don’t worry about it. Comes with the job sometimes.”

Her eyes shifted from Gwen and settled on Veronica.

“Some people get a little too… attached,” she added. “Though I can’t entirely blame them. It’s part of the performance, after all.”

She gave a small nod toward the attendant standing at the door.

The woman returned the gesture and quietly stepped away, leaving the three of them alone.

Nasha leaned back against a nearby table, her fingers curling lightly over its edge.

“So,” she said. “How was the show? Did you enjoy it?”

“It was amazing!” Gwen said immediately, her face lighting up. “I’ve never seen anything like it before. The whole place, the music, the lighting… and you were incredible! I wasn’t expecting you to actually walk through the crowd like that.”

“Thank you,” Nasha replied with a soft laugh. “That’s kind of you.”

She leaned a little more comfortably against the table.

“I like moving around when I perform,” she continued. “It lets me see people up close. You can learn a lot that way. What kind of crowd you have, what they respond to.”

“You’re… different,” Veronica said at last.

Nasha tilted her head slightly.

“Different?” she repeated. “How so?”

“Back in the forest, you were… more reserved. More serious. But right now, you’re none of that. You’re much more relaxed. And after seeing your performance tonight… it’s hard to believe you’re the same person who killed those bandits.”

Nasha closed her eyes and gave a small shrug. “I like to keep my work lives separate. A different mask for each occasion. There’s always a time and place for things. One needs discretion, and the other needs a bit of…”

“Passion?” Gwen chimed. “Charisma?”

“Mm… Seduction and allure.”

“Oh,” Gwen said.

“I’m just kidding,” Nasha said with a light chuckle.

Veronica hummed quietly and took a sip of her drink.

“Who was that man the guards just dragged out?” Gwen asked.

Nasha’s expression flattened. “Him? Just some rich bastard from one of the mercenary groups in town.”

Veronica raised a brow. “Mercenary?”

“Yeah. The Providence group.”

“Oh,” Veronica said, her voice lowering slightly.

Gwen glanced at her. “Something wrong?”

Veronica took another sip before letting out a small breath. “It’s nothing. I’m just… looking into something. I thought you meant a different company.”

“Oh?” Nasha said with a faint, knowing smile. “You mean the Scavengers?”

Veronica paused for a brief moment.

She wasn’t sure how much she should reveal. Gwen would be traveling with her soon, so it made sense to be open with her. But Nasha…

Perhaps it was better to keep it vague, at least for the time being.

“Yeah,” Veronica said. “Just some past business.”

Nasha exhaled softly, glancing down for a moment. “Then you’re out of luck tonight. Those guys are usually here. Three or four at a time, and one of them is almost always a VIP.”

She looked back up.

“They’re mercenaries, but they’ve got plenty of coin to spend here. A lot of them get rowdy. I’ve banned nearly half their company myself, and yet more keep showing up. Some behave, though.”

“They come here often?” Veronica asked.

Being a mercenary didn’t usually pay enough for this kind of place. Not regularly.

Nasha nodded. “Almost every night.”

She tilted her head slightly.

“But none of them showed up today. That Providence guy? Normally, he would’ve been a Scavenger. VIP tickets are limited unless performers hand them out themselves.”

Her lips curved faintly. “It’s a bit strange, though. I usually like throwing at least one of them out.”

Veronica’s brows drew together slightly.

Was that a coincidence?

The day after she visited both the notary office and the Scavenger’s headquarters, they suddenly broke routine? She had spoken with High Crates before that as well. If they frequented a place like the Crimson Court so regularly, why avoid it now?

Maybe she was overthinking it. It was unlikely that simply asking a few questions would disrupt their habits.

Still… it was a pattern worth noting. She needed more than a single irregularity before drawing conclusions.

“Hey… about those Scavengers,” Veronica said. “Do you happen to know which ones tend to be the wealthiest?”

Nasha raised a finger to her lips, tilting her head slightly.

Veronica couldn’t help but stare for a moment, before inadvertently darting her eyes somewhere else.

“Hm… there’s one man who comes here often,” Nasha said. “He’s probably the wealthiest of them. He buys several bottles each time and doesn’t even bother using the VIP benefit of three hundred vix in free drinks. Regularly gives tips, too.”

“A mercenary has that much money?” Gwen asked.

Nasha gave a small shrug. “Beats me. But yeah. His name’s Arune. Pretty handsome. Dresses almost like a noble. He shows up every other week or so.”

She crossed her arms momentarily. “He’s usually normal, but once he starts drinking, he gets very loud. Loves to talk. I’ve had to ask the servers to limit how much he gets in a night. He tends to interrupt the performers.”

Veronica creased her brows ever so slightly. A blabbermouth, huh? That might be useful…

“Well, enough about them,” Nasha said, leaning forward off the table. “Would you two fine girls like anything? Autographs? Any questions you want me to answer?” She peeked over at Veronica. “Maybe… a secret or two?”

Veronica took a quick sip from her glass. Her face was slightly reddened—probably due to the alcohol. “No need.”

Gwen, on the other hand, brightened immediately. “Ah! I would love your autograph, if that’s alright!”

The next few minutes passed with Gwen eagerly making requests and Nasha indulging them without hesitation.

“Are you also an adventurer?” Gwen asked at one point. “Along with singing?”

“No,” Nasha replied easily. “I’m more of a freelancer. Singing is like a side-job for me, as I only perform here every so often. Instead, I enjoy the thrill of fighting. I’m not officially an adventurer, but I do take on work from time to time. It helps me relax.”

Singing isn’t the relaxing part? Killing things is? Veronica thought.

As Gwen and Nasha spoke, Veronica stayed mostly to the side, letting Gwen enjoy herself.

Eventually, the attendant returned.

“Ladies, your time with Miss Nasha has come to an end for tonight.”

Gwen glanced toward the doorway where the attendant stood.

She nodded. “Alright.”

“Thank you for your time, Nasha,” Veronica said, standing beside Gwen.

“It’s no problem,” Nasha replied with a smile. “I perform every Friday. I hope you can both come again.”

“We’ll try,” Gwen said instead.

As they turned to leave, Nasha called out.

“Actually… I’m quite parched from singing tonight. Would you mind if I had the rest of your drink?”

Veronica paused and glanced down at her glass. There was still a small amount left.

She hummed lightly. “Hm… sure. Here.”

Nasha stepped forward and took the glass. She turned it slowly in her hands before bringing it to her lips and finishing the drink.

She handed it back with a faint smile.

“Thank you,” she said, her tone carrying a subtle edge. “You can leave it on one of the tables as you go. A server will take care of it.”

Veronica accepted the glass, momentarily caught off guard.

“Come on! It’s getting late!” Gwen’s voice called from down the hall.

“Ah—right,” Veronica said. “Good night, then.”

Nasha smiled again, lifting her hand in a small wave. “Goodbye. Have a pleasant night.”

Veronica nodded and stepped into the hallway and began walking back.

She held the glass cup in her hand.

It was empty.

Of course, she had already drunk most of it. But that last sip… had Nasha done that intentionally?

She had turned the glass before drinking—to the side Veronica had used.

Veronica reached up and scratched lightly at her forehead.

“Is… is this how rural performers act?” she murmured.

Was it a custom she didn’t understand? She knew that in some places, sharing drinks or meals wasn’t unusual. Was that the case in Ronswick?

Maybe she was just overthinking it. Perhaps it was just all part of the performance.

Veronica lightly rubbed her cheek.

“Just… a performance,” she murmured.

Nevertheless, her face felt flushed now. And she wasn’t sure if it was from the alcohol anymore.
