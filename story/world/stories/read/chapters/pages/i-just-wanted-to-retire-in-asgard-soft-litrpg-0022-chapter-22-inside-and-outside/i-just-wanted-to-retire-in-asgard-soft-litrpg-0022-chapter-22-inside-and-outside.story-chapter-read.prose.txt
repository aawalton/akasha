Barry thrashed through the saliva, strings of it stuck to his arms, until it swept him over the edge and he dropped. His scream came back at him off the walls of the throat.

As he fell, he knew he was done. The patch of light above him shrank to a dot.

He twisted one way, then the other, and all it did was spin him.

His left arm already hung useless, broken somewhere above the elbow, the ends of the bone grating against each other whenever he moved.

He tried to kick with the leg that still worked, but it only twitched and trailed behind him.

Blood ran warm down his ribs and soaked into his waistband, and the rushing air flicked it off him in drops.

He'd already done the math. Even if the drag in here cut his speed in half, which was generous, he'd still hit the bottom far too fast.

The impact would break whatever was still in one piece.

"This is it?" He shut his eyes and waited. Something scraped along his side, and his hand shot out toward it.

His one good hand closed on something solid.

He hung from four fingers for three seconds, long enough to feel his nails start to peel back.

"Haha... nobody told my hand we were done, you bastard."

He squeezed whatever it was once, then again, waiting for it to tear loose. It didn't, and he kept squeezing anyway, just to be sure.

The echo of his laugh was still coming back when the surface under his palm clenched, and he stopped mid-breath.

One massive heartbeat thudded through the wall. It jolted up his arm, rattled his teeth, and knocked the air out of his lungs.

He looked up. All he could make out was one pale dot of light far above him, no bigger than a coin, with nothing around it to show what it was. Maybe the bird was opening her mouth. He didn't know.

Using his one good arm and leg, he tried to climb. The wall flexed and he slipped.

The throat curved, though, in a long spiral. Every bend slung him against the outer wall and slid him farther down instead of dropping him straight.

He threw his body sideways into the wall. His ruined hand slammed into it, the fingers spasming open, and his breath snagged in his chest.

The damaged leg tore wider with each collision, but every time he hit the wall and slid, the friction bled off a little more speed.

Each slide came up shorter than the last, until he was hardly moving.

He caught a ridge of denser tissue and stopped.

He wedged his shoulder against the ridge and stayed there, dragging in air, until his pulse stopped hammering in his ears.

He looked around. The tunnel was wet meat on every side, strung with cords of nerve as thick as his wrist, and thin red lines of light ran along them, brightening with each heartbeat and fading in between. It wasn't much light, but it was enough to pick his footing.

He huffed and started walking down, slowly.

A few steps down, he noticed he'd been breathing this air all along.

He took a slow breath on purpose and waited for his lungs to burn. They didn't. The air was warm and wet and stank of raw meat, but it didn't sting, and the enzymes he'd expected to start eating through him hadn't done anything at all.

"I hope I didn't evolve into something." he said, his voice echoing once more.

After the echo faded, he heard scratching in the dark.

It grew louder, and closer, and there was too much of it to come from one animal.

Okay. Inventory. Another useless gun. Half a power cell, cracked but not dead, which put it ahead of the gun. Whatever was left of the coat, call it three meters of rag that used to be armor. Two liters of blood, give or take, if the leaks held, and he wasn't betting on the leaks. And, oh right, the world's worst landlord, currently trying to digest him from the outside in.

"First," he muttered, ripping the cloth into strips and binding the hand.

The fingers would not close, but the wrap stopped the worst of the bleeding. The leg came next. No way he was fighting anything like this. He could run, but only until the leg gave out.

While he worked, he found himself watching the glow. It flared, faded, and flared again, always with the same gap in between.

It pulsed in exact time with the heartbeat and traveled along clear nerve paths, branching and rejoining like traces on a circuit board.

Hraesvelgr's body was generating usable energy. That was a power source, and a big one, running through the walls right beside his head. If he could tap into the nervous system, he'd never be short on power again.

Grey worm-like shapes scuttled into the dim nerve-light. A dozen at first, then more, each one stabbing its front claws into the wall and yanking the rest of its body up behind.

Barry assumed they were hunting him. He limped five steps along the wall to see what they'd do. The whole swarm swung after him.

Back up the tunnel, another valve pipe branched off the wall. It might shake the swarm, but it led away from any exit. Down led into the messier guts, and he didn't want to be digested. He didn't like either option, so he stayed put.

Barry did not fight the parasites. That would be stupid.

He had one half-power-cell. He crossed the leads on purpose, fast and ugly, so the first thing to touch it would take the whole charge at once.

He exposed the remaining charge, set it as bait on a ridge of tissue, and backed away as far as the limp would allow.

The swarm surged toward him, and the leaders reached the cell first.

Boom!

The flash was white and hot, and dozens of them burst or curled up and dropped off the wall, smoking.

Then the entire nerve network contracted.

The living tunnel squeezed around him like a fist closing.

The walls clamped down on his ribs and crushed the air out of him. His bound hand spasmed inside its wrap, and he couldn't even scream. The squeeze eased in stages, and he gulped air whenever it let him.

He'd hurt it. The blast had gone off right against the nerves, and the whole tunnel had flinched.

Another ripple ran down the nerve cords, tightening as it went, and it dragged Barry along the wall with it.

Every heartbeat shoved him a few meters farther down the tunnel. By the fourth one he'd worked out where it was taking him: down, away from the surface, toward whatever the parasites were afraid of. He swore under his breath.

He stopped fighting the motion and rode it instead, turning his body sideways against the wall the way he had before.

Everything hurt, but if he stopped, the next squeeze would catch him wrong and he didn't have much left to push back with. So he kept moving.

The wall he'd been scraping along ended. His shoulder swung out into empty air, and he fell.

He hit a slick floor and rolled onto his back. The chamber was big enough to hold a small building, and the red light didn't even reach the far wall.

He'd thought another batch of parasites would come, but there were only heaps of broken claws, empty shells, and shed skin all around him.

"Ha! I can make use of this."

Outside

Outside, Baldr flew just above the ground, his divinity still trickling back and not yet enough to lift him any higher.

His wings, made from light and force given shape, beat against the dead wind. He looked back once, and Hraesvelgr's massive body twitched, barely, but he saw it.

Barry was still alive.

A smile tugged at one corner of Baldr's mouth, and his wingbeats steadied.

"Stubborn bastard. Don't worry, I will come for you. Survive until then."

The Bifrost was the fastest way to reach Barry. Heimdal wasn't here to open it, so Baldr would have to call it himself, if Asgard would still answer him.

He stopped and looked up at the gray sky and reached for the Bifrost the way he always had, with one quiet call. The sky stayed gray. He tried again, both arms thrown up, dragging every scrap of divinity he had into it, and still the bridge would not answer.

"Come on, Bifrost, heed my call!" he bellowed. Rainbow light spilled across the sky, and Baldr threw his arms wide to catch it, already grinning. Maybe the Allfather had finally forgiven him. The beam came down twenty meters to his left, nowhere near him, and left him hanging in the air with his arms still open.

When the Bifrost fluttered away, Loki was standing where it had landed.

She wore a long gown of dark green, and her helm rose into a pair of antler-like horns.

Her smile was gone. She bared her teeth at him instead, and his breathing grew heavy.

She looked at Baldr, then at the distant shape of Hraesvelgr, then back at Baldr. His wings were thin and flickering, and he was hanging only a man's height off the ground.

She could hardly feel any divinity in him. What little was left looked torn off at the edges, as if someone had taken the rest. She frowned and flew closer.

"I see you are still alive and well. Your mother would be proud."

"Don't you dare." Baldr growled, flying headlong.

Baldr met the first strike with a blade of hard light. Loki saw it, slipped past it and cut for his ribs. Something hot dragged across his side, and the breath went out of him in a grunt.

He hadn't seen a weapon in her hand. His fingers came away wet from his ribs, and he couldn't work out what had made the cut. He turned towards her. Loki held an invisible knife.

"You really thought I'd let you see the knife?" Loki said. "Where would the mischief be in that?"

He said nothing. The next exchange was faster. His blade came down in a wide arc and she let it pass an inch from her nose, eyes on his shoulder, not the light. He flared his wings to blind her, and she'd already shut her eyes. When he blinked the spots away, a second thin line was bleeding across his forearm.

"Tell me, Baldr," Loki said, circling just out of his reach. "Did you find something out here? A guy in a blue jacket, maybe?"

Baldr hesitated for a second. "No."

"Anyone at all?"

"No."

"Anything alive?"

His fist tightened on the blade of light. "No."

Loki smiled.

"Interesting..."

Her eyes dropped to his knuckles, and Baldr understood, too late, what he'd just given away. She had only said alive, and his hand had answered for him.

The wind of Helheim suddenly shifted.

It brushed Loki's cheek, and it was warm. It smelled of sweat and skin, and it was coming off Baldr's shoulder.

"You met him, didn't you?"

Baldr answered by attacking instead of speaking.

Light flared and green shadow tackled. The duel resumed with new force, Baldr swinging so hard and so fast that he had no breath left to talk. Loki ducked under a cut, and her smile came back.

Every cut Baldr threw went for her arms or her blade, never her throat. Loki tried a feint, then a fake stumble, then a spray of green sparks in his eyes, watching his face through all three, and when she finally turned her back on him, he pulled his swing short.

"You felt sorry for him," she said. "Of course you did."

Baldr still didn't answer. He made a spear of pure light and threw it at her.

"You kept him at your side," Loki said, grabbing the light. "And look where it got you. Again."

The spear stopped dead in mid-air, held by nothing he could see, and Baldr's eyes went wide. Loki's mouth curved.

"You were so busy helping a mortal that you never noticed Hraesvelgr's jaws an inch from your face," she said, still holding the light.

The green shimmer around her fist peeled away, and a glove appeared underneath, still closed around the spear.

Tiny lights blinked along the wrist, and the wires feeding them were twisted together and wrapped in tape. Baldr had watched Barry splice wires exactly that way.

"You..."

"Yes, one of his creations," she said.

She threw the spear back. It took him full in the chest and knocked him out of the sky. He hit the ground and skidded a long way before he stopped.

Baldr tried to push himself up, and his arms folded under him. There wasn't enough divinity left in him to hold his own weight. His back was raw from the skid, and his chest burned where his own spear had hit him.

Loki floated close.

"I'm not here to bully you, Baldr. I came for the mortal, and I can't reach him without you. You always did have the kindest heart of any of us. Big brother."

In all the centuries he'd known her, Baldr had trusted her more times than he cared to count, and he'd ended up flat on his back for every one of them.

Not even the death of his mother, Frigg, had changed her. Whatever she wanted with the human, it couldn't be good.

"No," he said. "Never."
