Mia sat on the large terrace atop the hotel they were staying in. It had a cosy little sky garden of sorts, though it was starting to get rather overgrown. Either the gardener was dead to some monster or his skills were in much higher demand at one of the food-growth facilities Zeigler’s people had set up. Greenhouses, crop fields, vegetable gardens, or anything else that produces edible food. Why would they waste time on making sure some bushes were perfectly cut into spherical shapes or that the weeds didn’t overtake the small strips of grass on some hotel’s rooftop?

Mia watched the city, her gaze taking in the landscape and the vast swathes of buildings that made up Graz. It felt so empty without the constant sound of cars rolling by down on the streets, without the occasional shouts and constant chatter. It was silent, too silent. It was eerie, giving her the feeling of a ghost town despite knowing there were people living in it … just far fewer than there should have been.

At least we don’t have to pay rent anymore. Mia mused. No one needed to, though if they wanted food, water, and other stuff they’d need just to live, they’d need to make themselves useful. Everything was rationed, and the Mayor wouldn’t be giving out the fancy new magical knick-knacks his enchanters and alchemists came up with to layabouts.

Mia and her friends were in the enviable position of being largely exempt from that on account of having played a crucial part in saving Graz from being wiped off the map. They had something of a non-spoken agreement that they’d continue hunting monsters and would at least seriously consider handling other bigger problems on the Mayor’s request on the side while continuing to grow stronger.

Mia knew she was lucky, incredibly so. The Mayor and his people were very enthusiastic about ensuring she and her friends-slash-family had everything they could ever need to survive each fight they got into and grow stronger as fast as possible. Potions, system-books, tools, reinforcements, rations, divination assistance, training, weapons, and just about everything else the local military detachment or the mayor’s office could provide were at their disposal. They had clearly taken Gabe’s warnings to heart about how the world was going to become much more dangerous before things even began to stabilise.

With a huff, she closed her eyes, gathering her legs into a lotus position atop the rug she’d laid down on the cold, concrete tiles. That done, she cleared her thoughts, banishing useless lines of thought from her mind as she focused on the ever-flowing arcane energy coursing through her channels. She wasn’t sure the lotus pose helped with meditation — she was pretty sure it didn’t, in fact — but it made her feel like a wise monk or something, and it was fun besides. It’s only recently, due to her raised Flexibility stat, that she’s managed to do it properly and hold it without it becoming extremely uncomfortable very quickly. If she had a few dozen more points into that attribute, she could probably twist herself into a pretzel and still be comfortable enough to fall asleep. Stats were weird.

But that was besides the point; she came up here for a reason. The shared room was nice, but she wanted some calm for what she was about to do. Gabe had gone off to talk with the Mayor about the greenskins and their much larger-than-expected underground settlement within spitting distance of the city. She wasn’t sure what would come of that, but it gave her at least a few hours of calm to do as she wished while they got busy discussing and planning.

Mia was ready. She had almost succeeded the last time she’d tried to conjure a Rank 0 rune on her own, and she’d gotten 3 stat points into the associated attributes since then. She could do this!

Her focus turned inward, and slowly, over the course of long minutes, she managed to narrow it down. Distant sounds faded away, not gone, just pushed into the background where her conscious mind no longer had to process them. Smell followed, then the sensation of the soft breeze catching her hair, caressing her skin and tugging at her tee-shirt. The feeling of the rug faded next, the rough, coarse strings of it pressing against her bare thighs because she’d been stupid enough to meditate in shorts, and then the hardness of the floor faded last, though she knew she’d feel just how inadequately padded her seat was when she resurfaced from meditation.

Mana flowed through her body, streaming through energy channels to the beat of her Mana Heart. That was the hardest to ignore, almost impossible. Her blood, too, thrummed with mana, infused with potent arcane energy by her Arcane Blood Trait. Both Traits were useful, but so very annoying at the moment. It was like trying to meditate while someone was chattering in your ears, and there was an illegal track race going on around your meditation spot.

Mia cycled through mental exercises, trying to find one that worked. It took her nearly half an hour until she managed to shut that final line of distraction out, leaving her with only the image of her Mana Core floating before her mind’s eye. Inside it, she watched her runic model slowly move and shift, revolving around its centre in ways that didn’t quite obey standard geometrics.

Her focus narrowed further, bringing only a single Rank 0 rune into focus while the rest — everything — faded into the background. It was a simple rune, the simplest one she could find and the one her choice had fallen on after days of overthinking and overanalysing. It looked like the letter ‘b’, but instead of having a curved belly — or whatever that protruding curve at the bottom was called — the rune had two straight lines meeting in the middle like a triangle. Just three simple lines, sticks made of mana. Simple. Easy.

She would have to get the mana flow perfectly; it had to start at the tip at the top, go down vertically, then turn and turn again. The moment the mana touched the initial vertical line, she would have to freeze it, turn it solid and static. There were instructions for it in her Training Manual, of course; according to it, having a runic lexicon to absorb runes from was a luxury few could afford.

Her first attempt came out better than expected … which wasn’t that much of a surprise, to be honest. The mana control and shaping exercises she’d been doing on and off again through the preceding weeks had her moulding her mana into a variety of geometric shapes from spheres to pyramids and everything in between. Three simple sticks were simpler, though the precision required was much greater.

Her focus wobbled, and the first line came out in a way that reflected that. She took a moment to calm herself, then she tried again, and again and then some more. The rune had to be at least as precise as the one she’d absorbed from the runic-lexicon. She was using it as a reference, noting the way her lines weren’t quite perfectly straight, just a close approximation of it. The Training Manual had been quite clear on the fact that while her runic-lexicon would accept and absorb substandard runes, making it do so was directly weakening her foundation, and would correlate to a higher likelihood of spellcircle failure, and would weaken the potency of her spells besides. She needed to be within a 10% margin of error when compared to the standard rune, at the very least.

On her tenth try, she got close enough, but she failed to freeze the mana in place at the right time, her flexing of her Will came a moment too late, and the third line collided with the first one, bursting through it and scattering the mana all over the place. Attempts eleven through fifteen were worse; she was focusing too much on the finish line and stumbled along the way, accidentally turning the sharp angles into curves. Attempt twenty was close, though she made the exact opposite mistake of the one that ruined attempt ten and froze it too early, leaving a gap between the last line’s end and the first line.

She was getting better, though, and she persevered, equal amounts of spite and stubbornness edging her on. All these attempts, every last one of them, came out better than anything she’d managed before her latest infusion of Attribute points. Runes were tiny, or at least they seemed that way to her current Sensitivity, though with the growth of her Sensitivity and Control Attributes, attempting to make them slowly started to feel less like she was attempting brain surgery with hands the size of a house and more like she was … actually, it reminded her of the delicate handwork required to paint tabletop minis.

Mia’s never been good at that; her hands tended to quiver and shake when she got nervous, and she always got nervous when she knew the shaking of her hands could ruin something. It was a self-fulfilling prophecy in that way, and supremely annoying, but thankfully, shaping mana had nothing to do with her hand-eye coordination or the stability of her fingers.

The thirtieth rune came out looking quite nice, and Mia was starting to get a proper feel for the exact way she had to flex her Will to make the rune solidify properly. Attempts 30 through 50 were spent streamlining the process, yielding only marginal improvements with each iteration. If she squinted, all of those attempts looked the same — and exactly like the rune she was copying — but when she focused on them with a critical eye, brushing her mental fingers over the lines that were never quite as straight as they first seemed, at the angles that were never quite perfect, always just a tiny bit off instead of being the perfect 60 degrees she needed them to be. She always dismissed them and tried again.

It was frustrating, and mind-numbingly boring, but there was something addicting about feeling her results slowly get better and better, even if the improvements shrank exponentially the closer she got to her target. Maybe it was silly; maybe she should have put off the attempt until later, when she got another Level and another handful of stat points in the associated Attributes. But she wanted it now, and she was getting so damned close.

The Training Manual said she had the ‘bare minimum’ stats required to make a proper Rank 0 rune if she was diligent enough. Mia didn’t know about diligence, but she was stubborn, though she partly blamed her rising Willpower for that; she didn’t recall ever being as headstrong as she’d been lately … then again, she’d gone through enough traumatic stuff in the last few weeks that a therapist would probably have to compile her ‘issues’ into volumes. Stuff like what she’d gone through changed people, she supposed.

She’d lost count after attempt fifty, only ever comparing her newest attempt to the preceding one, and to the reference rune still floating imperiously before her mind’s eye. Mia kept going, trying again and again in a half-daze some might have called a trace, only slowly coming back to herself when she was staring at her latest attempt.

She checked it twice, frowned and focused, checking two more times with even greater attention to detail. It was perfect, or at least as close to it as she could tell. There were probably still issues, but her Sensitivity wasn’t high enough to detect those minute imperfections and mistakes. But that meant it was as good as she was ever going to get in her present state. It was … good enough.

Mia had never thought herself a perfectionist before, and maybe it was the sunk cost fallacy making itself known, but she wanted this to be perfect.

She checked it three more times, carefully brushing her mental fingertips along the rune, then comparing it to her reference rune. They looked the same. She couldn’t have told the difference had one of the runes not already been connected to her runic model.

Okay. Mia thought somewhat numbly, shaking herself back to her senses. The daze and the last figments of the trance faded, and she took a deep breath, then gently nudged the rune towards her runic model, which itself reached out with a new arm. The initial connection formed, and Mia felt the absorption process of a new rune begin. It’s done. I did it!

She had somehow, by some miracle, managed to not fuck up anything while she had command of their previous four-person group, but it was a relief to be free of the burden of command … even if having Gabe and her Mom praise her for her ‘success’ — aka, being fortunate enough that none of her plans had blown up in her face — had been really nice, and probably something she was going to remember for the rest of her life.
