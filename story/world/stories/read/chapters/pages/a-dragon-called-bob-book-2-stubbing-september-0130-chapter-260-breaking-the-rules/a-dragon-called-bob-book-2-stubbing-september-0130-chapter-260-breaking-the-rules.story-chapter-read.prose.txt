Bumdock smiled at me across the table.  We were alone; Gorboon had been left outside, as had his own bodyguards.  He poured a glass of wine and slid it over, taking a sip of his own.

“What do you think?” He nodded at the food laid out.  Sliced meats, spiced in ways that made my tongue tingle from the scent, and strange fruits were arranged in pleasing arrays.  I wasn’t really into food aesthetics; it would all end up as poop in the end, but it was very pretty.

Taking a slice of meat, I shoved it into my mouth and chewed thoughtfully.  “It’s alright.  Needs more salt.  I prefer taste over presentation.”

“The first bite is with the eye, is it not?”

“No.  The first bite is with the teeth.  The rest is just a trick.”

He picked up something that looked like a plum and peeled away the skin as though it were a banana.  He popped the whole thing in his mouth and chewed for a minute before spitting out a stone the size of a grape.

Leaning back, he crossed his arms.  “You are not a true elf.”

“Erm, yes, I am?”  Had I been rumbled so soon?

“No true Waywalker could resist a barunkip fruit.  Of all these morsels, that one is the most coveted.”  He gave me a glare that I’d rate at a level four.

“I’m not a fan.  I don’t like fruit that you can chip a tooth on.  Home-cooked meals, like your mother used to make, are making a comeback.”

“My mother had chefs working for her.  You think High Ladies bake their own bread?  What a bumpkin you are.  My son thinks you will do well, but I do not see it.  Why should I let you serve as the entree for my Woodland?”

“First of all, I’m not on the menu.  Every other fucker is.  My understanding is it’s not just the cooking that’s important in the Fallimpiad, it’s getting rid of the competition, and I can handle that side of things easily enough.”

“Entree is the name for the supplicant.”

“Oh, fine then, but I’ll be eating them, not the other way around.”  Toothy grin time, but he just narrowed his eyes slightly at my bravado.

“They will all be great warriors.  It isn’t just a title the winner gets.  He gets to sow his seed, representing his house in the Great Blending.”

“I’m fighting for a harem?”

“Hardly, and you truly have been living under a rock, haven’t you?  For the good of the species, the strongest and most talented are encouraged to breed.  It keeps the race strong, and we have so few children that only the best can be tolerated.”

“Elf-genics.  Of course, you—we treat our own kind like livestock.  How else would we do it?”

“Indeed.  You are strong, but ignorant.  A dangerous combination.”

Wrath perked his head up in my mind.  That had been a touch too close to calling me stupid for my liking.

“Do you understand what is at stake?” he continued.

“I have my own goals.  They align with yours, though.”

“For now, perhaps.  The houses have always been weak.  We rule the deep forests because we have to; the ancient curse binds us to the trees.  But that time will pass, soon, according to the leaf-seers.  When it does, House Evergreen will be at the forefront.  I will be the Barklord, ruler of all the people.”

So megalomaniac as well as a eugenicist.  Katprick being such an ass was starting to make a lot more sense.  “Then you’ll need me.  Two Fallimpiads in a row, that would put your house at the top of the pile.”

“But you know nothing of food.”

“But I’m a good fighter.  None of those other elves will stand a chance against me.  I can be surprisingly persuasive as well.  I’ll get the good ones on my side.”

“Mercy rarely sells.  The audience wants blood.  Blood of beasts and elf.”

“Domination is not mercy.”

He smiled, the first genuine expression I’d seen on his face.  Everything about this elf screamed control and ancient indifference.  He reminded me of Umbraxis; he’d spent so long secure in his own superiority that he had no idea what a draconic suppository could do to one's digestion.

“You will do.  I have been delaying sending an entree, waiting for an appropriate tribute.”

I gave him a three-fingered salute.  “I’m your man.  Elf.  I’m your elf.”

“Good.  I hope you are, for my son's sake, as well as your own.  Your servant will join you on the island after the ceremony.  Do try to survive.”

Roots rose up around me and pulled me down into the earth.  I struggled, snapping the grasping tendrils, but then the dirt filled my mouth, and I stopped trying to fight it.  A space formed in front of my face, and I spat out mud.

I emerged in a quiet grove, being shat out of the ground to land in a mucky heap. Bouncing to my feet, I looked around.  Butterflies drifted from flower to flower, and the trees were covered in pink blossoms.

“Hello?”

“The entree from House Evergreen has arrived!  Bob is an unknown wood elf and a surprise entry for the reigning champions.  He’s six feet tall, prefers roasts and old-time cooking to more modern fare, and is a complete bumpkin!  Whatever is House Evergreen thinking?”

He emerged in a ball of roots that left him kneeling, one hand on the hilt of his sword, a silver helm cradled under the other arm.

Two archers were a good sign, in my book.  Arrows were an annoyance, not a threat.

“Humbart Meadowflayer is representing House Needlefall.  The House at the End of Autumn has sent their greatest hero.  Humbart, the Dread of Growth, has cut down many fledgling nobles and was the victor of the Battle of Glowstream.  He needs no introduction!”
