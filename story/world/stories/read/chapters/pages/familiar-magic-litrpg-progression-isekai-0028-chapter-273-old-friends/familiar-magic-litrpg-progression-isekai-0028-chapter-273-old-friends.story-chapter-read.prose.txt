There was no preparing for what Valanor had experienced. There was no way to know how to react when faced with the dead returned to life. Savilar Laurent, Valanor’s adoptive brother, lived. If you could call this living. The white horns and red skin had taken on new meaning, reminiscent of the white armor and cloak the knight had once worn. But there was no forgetting that the creature across the fire was a demon.

Valanor had naturally tried to convince himself that he’d simply heard wrong. This was too outlandish to be real. But his apparent brother was only too happy to repeat himself.

“Saa-vaa-lar…Saa-vaa-lar…Saa-vaa-lar…” the graveling voice repeated.

The word was actually becoming clearer, as if the demon were practicing. Valanor had been overwhelmed, upon accepting what he’d heard, and spent the rest of the night trying to think of ways to explain this, other than the seemingly impossible: that his brother had returned. As a demon, no less.

Perhaps there were explanations, Valanor certainly made himself consider every possible option. But he knew this was, in some ways, less far fetched than he wanted to believe. He’d heard of the terrible cycle of Potentia. First from Ethan, then in warnings from the Church’s own writings.

Everything died here. Some things came back.

And there was no mistaking it. They came back wrong. The purity of death, followed by the corruption of rebirth. If this thing truly was his brother, he might have died a dozen times since Ethan. Hundreds, maybe. No one seemed to know. And each time, he came back a little less human, until…

The white-horned demon stared back at him, mouthing the same word over and over again. At some point the sun had come up, but neither of them had moved throughout the night. Valanor had yet to even speak.

What does one say in this situation? What could one say?

“You were wrong.” The words left his mouth before he realized he’d opened it. Yet more kept spilling out. “You killed, and you were wrong. You executed good people. You stole their souls. You did it at the behest of manipulative fools. And you were wrong.”

The demon tilted its head, as if struggling to understand. Valanor doubted it could understand a fraction of what he’d said, but he couldn’t help himself. And he couldn’t stop.

“You were wrong, Savilar!” he was shouting now. Standing up and pacing back and forth. “The whole Church was wrong. It was based on the most perverse, and terrible lies. Church of the Goddess, indeed! It wasn’t founded to celebrate her works, or spread her words. It was built upon her suffering!”

The demon just stared back at him, lips still moving. It didn’t react at all, and that made Valanor furious for some reason.

“Do you know what they did, Savilar? Do you know what happened in the dark beneath Sanctustella? Torture. Oh, they made the most of your hard work, alright. They took the power of the souls you harvested, the stolen gifts of Dawn’s own children, and they used it to burn her!”

The demon hissed, but otherwise didn’t react. Valanor picked up a stone, and hurled it at the ground, needing to break something. He turned back to the demon, which finally seemed somewhat agitated. Good.

“Did you know?” he demanded. “Did you know what it was all really for? Did you fool yourself into believing it was anything more than a group of petty little people?”

Valanor moved closer to the demon, practically daring it to attack. “Lathander,” he said, naming the Chosen who’d led the Church. “A pitiful, scared old man. Frightened of losing control. Frightened of anything new, any change. That’s what you killed for. One pathetic, self-obsessed fool.”

“Saa-vaa-lar…”

“That’s who you nearly killed me for. Who you nearly killed Ethan, and my team for. Who you were ready to kill Prince Calevaro for!”

“Vaa…”

“You killed for a false cause. I thought you better than that. I wished you were better than that. I wanted so badly to believe that my brother was a good man.”

“Vaal–aa…”

“It took me so long to forgive Ethan for killing you. Even though I knew the lies you’d been protecting, you were still my brother. I loved you, Savilar. That love nearly broke me.”

“Vaal–aa-nooor…”

“But he did the right thing. You were a monster. As much a monster then as you are now. You were everything wrong with the Church, be it out of ignorance, or blind faith.” He spit. “Maybe you deserve this place.” He turned, shaking his head. “You were wrong,” he ended in a whisper.

He spun around when he heard the demon rise, warhammer appearing in his hand, and plate armor enfolding him. The demon–Savilar–looked back at him, eyes burning with rage and purpose. He took a step forward, and Valanor raised his shield. He wanted this, if only so he didn’t need to face this creature again.

Yet Savilar didn’t attack. He spoke.

“Val-a-nooor…I…was…RIGHT.”

“Basilisk,” Ethan said. He hated how the word tasted in his mouth, and he knew there was still a great deal of rage and anger for the man. The unusual Twilight Bonded had been a member of the Broken Covenant when Ethan returned to Earth. More powerful than Ethan by far, they’d faced off several times before the end.

The man had hunted him, in a convoluted way, across the world. And in truth, it was partly Ethan’s fault. He’d given the man a taste of Potentia’s corruption, just enough to leave the man desperate for a Throne that didn’t exist on Earth. The Basilisk had killed entire cities to taunt Ethan, and they’d finally met in the caverns where the Followers had rested.

“I sent him here,” Ethan said.

His arm had some feeling now, but it was wrapped against his chest in a sling Fable had created. Apollo cast healing spells on it every half-hour or so, but it was slow going. Ethan had never seriously damaged his healing rune before–not since it had evolved, at least, and he was learning a lesson about how serious the consequences could be.

“Seemed like a good idea at the time, I’ll bet,” Apollo said. They were seated at a table in the lounge area. Selina was mixing drinks, and Apollo lazily looking out the window. Neither had faced the Basilisk–Selina had barely seen him before Ethan had sent him to Potentia.

“It was the only idea at the time,” Ethan said. “I was a Dusk rank against someone nearly at Master. I sensed the dimensional energy in the hydra’s bones, and used it to do the only thing I could–I sent him where he couldn’t hurt anyone else.”

“Except for some trees,” Selina said as she joined them, passing around the mixed drinks.

“In my defense,” Ethan said, “I thought I was sending him to a world of Champion rank demons. He should have been dead in days, weeks at the most.”

“But Potentia has proven…different than we thought,” Apollo supplied.

“Yes, for good or for ill. It’s possible he was able to thrive here. I doubt there’s many demons who can put up a good fight against petrification. Especially if they don’t even have that affinity here.”

“So, what?” Selina asked. “He just stayed, and went searching for the Throne?”

Ethan shrugged. “There isn’t much else to do, and he was…motivated, to say the least.”

Apollo leaned forward, letting out a sigh. “With respect, Ethan, why do we care? I get that this man had some significance to you, but it’s not likely he knows you’re even here. If he does, I’d like to see him attack Fable and our army. Even if he tries to ambush you, you’re…you. I doubt he can handle your stealth abilities, and you’re Twilight now.”

“Apollo is right,” Selina said. “Maybe we don’t need to care about this? We’re already steering around the forest, and I had to go really deep to find his trail–if it’s truly him at all.”

“But didn’t he ambush you?” Ethan asked.

“I don’t think so,” Selina said. “I couldn’t get a good look at whatever attacked Walter, but I’m pretty sure there was more than one demon out there. We were beset on all sides.”

“Even then,” Apollo said, “I suspect this Basilisk person would have used petrification, not natures magic.”

Ethan nodded. “That’s a good point.” He tapped his fingers lightly on the table, staring off into space.

“What are you thinking?” Apollo asked.

Ethan looked back up. “I think you two are right, and if that really is the Basilisk, he’s not looking for us. But we have other problems to consider.”

“The Followers,” Selina said.

“Exactly. We can’t go on as we have been. They’re building their army faster than we are, and we can’t just leave them to it.”

“But you can’t just go attack them,” Apollo insisted. “They have a Master rank, we can’t risk you like that.”

Ethan shrugged his damaged arm. “I’m forced to agree,” he said. “But that doesn’t change what we need to do.”

“So what are you thinking?” Selina asked.

Ethan leaned back in his chair, a grin spreading across his face. “I think I might need to go meet the Basilisk after all. Perhaps he has a role to play in all this.”
