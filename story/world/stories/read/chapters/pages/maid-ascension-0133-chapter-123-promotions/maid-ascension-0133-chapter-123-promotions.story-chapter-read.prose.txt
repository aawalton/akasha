Lira was four pages into a philosophical thesis on the nature of light before Tessa burst through the library doors at a dead run.

"Lira! Lira, come quick!"

"What is happening?" Lira asked, already setting the book aside.

"There's an assembly in the Main Garden. Celene sent me to call for you." Tessa was practically bouncing on her heels. "The king is going to announce a successor to Lord Atropos. The Lord Grand Steward's position is finally going to be filled."

Lira was out of her chair before Tessa could finish the sentence.

It was already midmorning by the time they reached the main garden, and a crowd had already gathered near the ornamental hedges. Senior staff clustered near the front, older maids and footmen filling the middle, while the younger, more curious servants pressed in wherever they could find a gap, standing on their tiptoes and whispering behind cupped hands.

Celene spotted Lira from across the grass and waved her forward, tucking her in near the front. Close by, Dalwin and Berem stood only a few paces away, with Old Marga and a few of the cooks further past them.

At the center of it all stood King Argon, black hair turning silver at the temples, a plain gold circlet his only ornament. Estophon stood at his shoulder, arms folded, eyes already moving across the crowd. Kavard, Aric, and Lyth were arranged to one side, though there was a noticeable gap between Aric and Kavard, the second prince was probably still resentful of last night's stunt at the ball.

To the other side of the princes stood Lord Taren, Master of Coin, a heavyset man in his fifties whom Lira recognized on sight. She had seen him a few times from a distance in the bookkeeping tower, and once, memorably, through a brothel window; that last memory she would erase if she could.

Harwin stood near him, still smiling as always, with Cedric a step behind, looking nervous, a ledger clutched to his chest like a shield. Beside them stood a second nobleman, perhaps in his thirties, whom Lira didn't recognize at all; he wore travel gear rather than court attire, for reasons she couldn't yet guess.

An older woman stood a little apart with four young women ranged around her, all somewhere between Tessa's age and Maren's, presumably Taren's wife and daughters. All of them were dressed as though for a ball rather than a garden assembly, the older woman carrying a bouquet of roses for some reason.

Further behind them stood a few clusters of nobles; Lira suspected they had already been in the garden, strolling, rather than summoned specifically for this.

The king lifted a hand, and the low murmur of the crowd went immediately silent.

"Viscount Taren Cormen," the king began, his voice pitched to carry. "For fifteen years you have served the crown as Master of Coin. Under your stewardship, the treasury has known neither scandal nor collapse."

Lira blinked at the outright lie. She had seen the ledgers herself; the treasury was in a wretched state, but Taren took the compliment in full stride, believing it entirely, his chest swelling with pride and his face breaking into a wide smile.

"Such steadiness is rare," the king continued, "and rarer still is it rewarded as it deserves. It is therefore my wish that you accept the office of Lord Grand Steward of Castle Elthram, with all honors, incomes, and the manor house that accompany the title."

A ripple went through the crowd as dozens of people inhaled at once. Beside Lira, Celene's spine went straight.

Taren dropped to one knee, though he struggled with the gesture, his belly, born of years of excess drinking, making the motion a genuine effort.

"Your Majesty, you honor me."

"Rise, Lord Steward." The king waited until the man had stumbled his way upright before continuing. "The duties of this office will demand the whole of your attention. It would be an unkindness to ask you to divide yourself between the palace and your ancestral lands. Cormenfield deserves a liege lord who can give it his full care."

Something flickered across Taren's face. Surprise, maybe.

Lira almost decided to cast her improved perception spell, but decided against it. While it would help her better read body language, it would be far too dangerous to do so in front of all those nobles, especially Sir Estophon.

"As you have no son to inherit," the king went on, ignoring the shift in Taren's expression entirely, "the lordship will pass to your cousin."

Argon turned his head slightly, focusing on the younger nobleman. "Lord Noah Cormen. Step forward."

Taren's cousin did as told, boots crunching against the gravel path, and dropped to one knee where the former Viscount, now Lord Steward, had knelt a moment before.

"You are hereby granted the title of Viscount of Cormenfield, effective this hour." The king's tone sharpened, the warmth of a moment ago completely gone. "Rise."

He let a pause settle before continuing. "You will be aware that a famine has taken hold across your new lands, and that sickness has followed close behind it. I expect you to ride within the day, send me a full account of the damage within the week, and fix it within the month. Do not disappoint me."

"By your will, Your Majesty." Lord Noah's voice didn't waver. He rose, bowed a second time, deeper than the first, and was already half turned toward the gate, where a stable boy stood waiting with a horse already saddled for him.

Barely a minute after his ascension, Viscount Noah Cormen was galloping away.

Unlike Taren, whose face was a mix of surprise and incredulity, the younger noble had clearly been ready for this proclamation. At least, that was what Lira had concluded.

The king watched the youth ride for a moment before he spoke again, and when he did, his eyes moved to Harwin.

"Steward Harwin."

Harwin stepped forward, his eyes gleaming with anticipation; he had clearly expected to be called next. Lira doubted a man like him had ever been caught off guard by anything in his life.

"For years you have helped run the bookkeeping tower, making sure every purchase was paid in full, every tax collected in time, and every debt accounted for. The crown does not forget such service, even when it goes unspoken."

"I have only ever done my duty, Your Majesty," Harwin said, bowing gracefully.

"Duty of that quality deserves more than words of thanks." The king lifted a hand toward Estophon, who produced a rolled up document without being asked. "I grant you an honorary knighthood. From this hour forward you will be known as Sir Harwin, and with it, the hand of Lady Rosalind Cormen, Lord Taren's eldest daughter."

The eldest of the four girls in the row went very still. Lira caught the flicker of her eyes toward her mother before she smoothed her expression back into composure.

"You will continue your fine work," the king finished, "as second to Lord Steward Cormen, seeing to it that the whole castle is kept in order, not merely its ledgers."

Harwin immediately dropped to one knee, and the king drew his sword to give him the accolade.

Lira's mind was racing. Was Taren just the vessel to hand the real responsibility over to Harwin? Surely the king didn't expect a worthless man like Taren to be genuinely useful.

I suppose it makes sense to appoint a noble to the position a noble used to hold, but by knighting Harwin, hadn't he just made him into one anyway?

So what use was Taren, then? A shield, maybe. Something to stand between Harwin and any other noble who would resent seeing a former commoner rise this high.

Harwin would now be running the palace in everything but name, while Taren went back to his drinking and whoring undisturbed. Harwin didn't seem to mind the arrangement in the slightest; why would he? He had gained a pretty noble wife, a title, and, more importantly, continued proximity to power.

Lira didn't know whether anyone had told Lady Rosalind she was expected to marry today, but judging by her and her sisters' dresses, she must have had some warning. What struck Lira as strange was that all four girls were dressed nearly alike; typically, a bride would stand out, not blend among her sisters. And yes, this assembly had just quietly folded itself into a wedding: the young lady simply stepped to Harwin's side when directed, dutiful and composed. After that, her mother pressed the bouquet into her hands, and the king pronounced them husband and wife on the spot.

Her mother wept a few happy tears. One of her sisters looked openly relieved. The other two clustered around Rosalind, congratulating her, seemingly delighted on her behalf.

The king closed with a few remarks about nobility, matrimony, and the strength of the crown, and then took his leave.

Lira let her eyes move over the staff nearest her before the crowd could carry her away.

Celene looked, for lack of kinder words, deflated. Dalwin, beside her, looked simply stunned, scandalized that a wedding had happened without so much as a veil, and only belatedly seemed to register that he had just lost his own long-standing bid for power to Harwin. Berem showed nothing at all, which Lira had come to expect from him by now; the man never displayed visible emotion, though his bid for the position had been the weakest, well... Not weaker than Hesta's. Old Marga stood with her arms crossed, scowling at Taren and the king in equal measure, entirely unconcerned with who might notice.

As the crowd began to disperse, Aric broke away from the gathered nobles, Deren a step behind him, and started walking toward Lira.

No, she corrected herself, toward Celene.

"We need to talk," he said, drawing up in front of the headmaid.

"Lira, you can come too," he added when he spotted the librarian.

"To my office?" Celene asked.

Aric nodded, and the four of them fell into step, Celene leading the way.
