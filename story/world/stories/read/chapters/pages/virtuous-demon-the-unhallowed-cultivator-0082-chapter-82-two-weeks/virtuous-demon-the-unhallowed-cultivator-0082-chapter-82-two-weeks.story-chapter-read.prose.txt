Night had fallen, with the closure of Heaven’s Eye.

And no one would be watching.

Shuren had waited around the back of the dorms towards the forest. He stood, leaning his back on a ripening magnolia tree, which had begun boring red apples.

Strange, it was far too strange. Why did everyone mention the Liu Clan to this extent?

In the Granxian Continent, the Liu Clan had massive power and influence. At one point during the height of its influence, it had almost been considered to enter the echelons of the Great Nine.

The history of the world was filled with many eras. But there were three Grand Eras, times where the structure of the world had forever changed.

The First Grand Era was led by the Immortal Reverend Seraphim All-Seer, the pioneer of the Lexis Path.

His reign led to a massive influx of knowledge and spread of education throughout all corners of the land, propelling civilisation one step forward.

The Second Grand Era was led by the Immortal Reverend Grand Sovereign, Sage of the Seven Paths. Though unlike Seraphim All-Seer, his name is lost to the annals of history, this era purported the spread of cultivation and paths to an absurd level. Time Path Archaeologists and Historians claim the population of Immortals increased tenfold in this era alone.

And the Third Grand Era, was led by the Immortal Reverend Virtuous Paragon. Founder of the Virtuous Clans Order, the only Heavenly Emperor to ever exist, and a person with the support of the Heavens’ themselves. The only being in the world to have unified every continent and realm under his time.

The gap between each Grand Era was less than ten thousand years, but ever since the Third Grand Era, tens of thousands of years had passed by.

Though the Virtuous Clans Order had long crumbled, The Great Nine, the biggest alliance of Righteous Factions, Sects and Clans, had taken it upon themselves to control the central continent in honour of the Virtuous Paragon.

And the Liu Clan had almost renamed it to The Great Ten.

Nevertheless, a mere young master and a bastard of the Liu Clan had no place to use their name to form influence in the Seven Clouds Sect, who also had a strong relationship with The Great Nine. With all eyes on the Seven Clouds Sect, especially the delegates, it was vital to uphold reputation as representatives. Especially someone like Shuren, whose goal required him to increase his status and influence to gain more leverage in the Liu Clan later on.

So his name shouldn’t have affected the Outer Disciples all the way here.

The person called Fatty Chen and the young Silvent, they were strange. One swore allegiance and was the only one against the claims of Tez’s character. He could be written off for being Tez’s friend.

The issue was Arene. He had personally worked with her in the past, yet she acted like he was some Rising Paragon above mortal gaze. What exactly changed in the time from then and now?

Shuren had not seen Wei in a long time, but it was a fact that he was one of the outcasts of the Liu Clan. He himself wouldn’t hold much influence or power anyway, aside from being treated well by everyone.

When I last met Wei with Su Kang, he was relaxed. Though we didn’t delve too much on personal topics, Su Kang mentioned he was still exploring the districts of Cloud City with Wei. Su Kang has always been like that, but Wei has always kept to himself.

Everything would be brought to light once he met Tez.

A young man with messy blue hair had walked into Shuren’s vision. He held a sheathed antiquated rusty sword that looked beyond miserable.

“It’s you.” Shuren said, with a voice dripping with malice.

“Fatty Chen was right, you came hiding at night for me. How cliché, deal with one and his entire family comes along.” Though he jested, the expression Tez gave off was anything but. Shuren could sense the deep bloodlust in his eyes.

"What next, I deal with you and your grandaddy comes along next?" Tez taunted.

A condensed gust of wind blasted towards Tez’s stomach, but he held out the antiquated sheathed sword to absorb the attack. His hands trembled as he held onto his weapon, barely retaining his stance.

He heard a voice fueled with a raging fire.

"Do not mock my grandfather."

Shuren stood there, hands crossed, and though he seemed calm and patient, his eyes bore down a gaze stabbing into Tez. Tez couldn't even see Shuren’s hands move to use that technique.

Shuren saw the weapon that Tez took out, the same he watched buy long ago. "Who would've thought that the trash you bought for a single copper turned out to be durable."

The tense atmosphere was almost about to erupt into a battle. Shuren didn’t move an inch. He still had questions to ask. All he did was remain, back leaning on the tree. Tez had already placed his thumb on the hilt of his sword, ready to defend.

Unknowingly, Tez had already drawn out a sharp breath with strained anticipation. Yet his eyes were filled with uncertainty.

“What did my cousin do to you? Explain yourself.”

Tez was shocked. His mouth was left agape for a while. “I’m surprised, I thought you’d yell and cry about how I dared to touch your cousin, demanding me to kowtow and break my limbs.”

“Stop spouting nonsense and answer my question.”

“You… That bastard Wei kept putting his hands on Sarya!” Tez's hand shivered as it curled into a fist.

“All the witnesses I’ve talked to said he is in a relationship with that girl. They all claim you were the one who did so instead.” Shuren retorted with a cold voice. He needed to get the truth by reading Tez’s expressions.

“What? Those guys are pinning it on me? It’s all a lie! He’s just a piece of shit racist. He thinks just because someone looks different to him, he can do as he wants.” Tez yelled.

Shuren narrowed his eyes. “What do you even mean by that?”

“Of course you don’t know such a concept,” Tez continued to ramble instead of getting to the point. It was getting tiresome.

Getting tired of Tez’s ridiculous remarks, Shuren asked. “Are you in a relationship with this Sarya, Tez?”

“…No.”

“So, for over a girl you weren’t even connected with, you beat my cousin to an inch of his life?” Shuren pressed on.

“He deserved it.”

Shuren stood quiet, but he really wanted to just land one hit on this trash.

But he knew that once he did, he’d lose control of his emotions and kill him right now.

The headache that would induce from the sect afterwards would be far too much.

“You have pride in being a coward. Before, you abandoned your team during an expedition for your own selfish needs, and yet you still didn’t acknowledge your mistakes.”

Tez squirmed, almost looking down in shame.

“Even now, you didn’t dare to hold a public duel, but rather deal with him outside in the forest, where no one else could watch. You…”

“Can never be a true cultivator.” Shuren scolded Tez as if he were a child.

Tez slumped his shoulders, refusing to look at Shuren in the eye. “On that day, I!” But he cut himself before finishing his words.

Tez shook his head, facing Shuren once again. “I want to ask you something. If you believed in me, and that your cousin was at fault, what would you do?”

“You went too far. Wei is dying, his prospects as a cultivator are on the brink of destruction.” Shuren emanated the Qi of someone who had been in conflicts his entire life, and he took every bit of himself he had to not lose himself in his anger.

But the heart fuelled by melting wroth would spill out.

Shuren raised his chin, looking down at scum. “Even if he was in the wrong, you still went too far. You tried to ruin his entire life!”

Tez used his thumb to open the sheathed rusty sword. Even though it looked like it’d fall apart at any time, it’s steel began to absorb the surrounding Qi. “Why! Because he’s a member of the oh so esteemed Liu Clan?”

“No.” With a sudden burst of Qi, Shuren’s figure became a blur, closing the short distance between him and Tez within a breath. The clang of weapons resounded across the silent forest, Shuren’s Ruyi Staff pushing down Tez’s entire torso back with ease. “Because he’s my cousin.”

Tez’s heels dug into the dirt, his legs shaking from the weight of pressure. Even his half released blade was on the brink of being swatted away. But Shuren didn’t break a sweat as he bore a deep gaze, like a sword slicing through his heart.

“Pick a day. You and I will hold a public duel, in front of everyone on the Fourth Peak. If I win, you will publicly apologise to Wei and admit your wrongdoings to everyone in the sect. The elders will deal with you then.”

Shuren continued to command Tez, relaxed as ever while he observed his strength.

He’s only at Stage 7 in the Qidense Realm. Then…

“And you only need to bring me down to my knees to win. If by a miracle, that even happens… I’ll even ask one of the overseers to reduce my cultivation level to your stage,” Shuren uncharacteristically smirked. He was going to reach Stage 11, not far from reaching Path Foundation. With his background in the Liu Clan, and the intensive regiments of training he received from his family and grandfather, there was no way an untrained orphan at a mere Stage 7 could even accomplish something like bringing Shuren down to his knees.

Shuren had personally dealt with this Tez multiple times in the past.

“If you try to run away now... Then you know what will happen. You only have to bring me down to my knees. Do you dare run, Tez?” Shuren said, with a subtle threat.

Tez had no stake to honour the duel.

But if he still tried to run away, then Shuren would act.

“I’ll… Do whatever you say you want,” Tez heaved with struggle, still bearing the weight of Shuren’s single handed Ruyi Staff.

Tez stood still for a moment, as if his attention was pulled to something else. His irises were darting around at nothingness.

Has this mad kid already gone insane by spacing out like this in front of me?

Shuren eased the pressure on his Ruyi Staff, giving Tez time to think.

“Two weeks. That’s all I need,” Tez said, full of bravado.

Shuren backed away, releasing the weapon clash with ease, while Tez slumped his arms on his knees while standing, catching a breath.

“Why don’t you make an oath for me, Liu Shuren?”

Shuren ignored him, his cloudmulet humming to manifest his flying nimbus. The matter was over with, and he didn’t want to see the smug face of this cursed scourge.

“It’s simple. If I put you in your place, don’t go sending all your other cousins and elders of your clan after me. Surely you can do that much, right?” Tez said with a serious tone.

Shuren felt like he was talking to an idiot.

“You truly are as ignorant as always. Only the leader of a clan has the power to move their clan at their beck and call. Even if I was the successor of the Liu Clan, It’s beneath me to punish you of my own accord within a territory not of my own,” Shuren educated Tez as he stepped on his cloud, before once more glaring at him. “Insult me once more by assuming I’m some spoiled brat that needs the coddling of his clan, and I’ll make sure to break your legs and arms in front of everyone.”

Tez could only raise his eyebrows with surprise, blatantly ignoring Shuren’s threat. “Huh. I suppose that makes sense.”

Tez waited until Shuren’s visage disappeared in the sky, and with it, his back slumped. The antiquated sheathed sword fell on the floor as his fingers began trembling, and sweat covered his back.

“Why… Why’s the System pushing me into this conflict? Every time I’ve tried to meet up with him to talk, it’s forced me to take a detour. It’s almost as if it wants me to get into a fight with him. And that quest I received… Is that what the System needs?”

Tez grit his teeth, remembering back to his grievances. “But, what Liu Wei and Su Kang did was unforgivable! They deserve to be killed! If Liu Shuren thinks his cousin can be let go because of their blood relationship, then he’s sorely wrong!”

He slammed his fist to the side, trampling some grass. “Perhaps this fight was inevitable then.”

But he couldn’t undo this feeling in his heart. That Tez was being manipulated like a-

A sharp pain ran through Tez’s mind, prompting him to forget his last thought.

He looked straight up at the foreign night sky.

Tez could never get used to the sight.

A completely alien night sky, far different than the one he was used to. An eternal reminder of his nature, as someone from another world.

But his gaze was like he wished to peer through what was beyond that night sky. “Making him fall to his knees… No, winning the fight doesn’t matter. I just have to accomplish that goal.”

“What a dogshit system. Giving me the worst Talisoul just so I’m forced to go out and work for every little thing. Upgrading it to Iron was nowhere near enough.”

After taking a breather, Tez stood back up straight, keeping his sword sheathed by his side.

“Sarya, just give me some time. I’ll make them pay for what they did to you, I swear.”

“Death would be a mercy for them.”

And a thought came through Tez’s mind.

When did I get used to the idea of killing?
