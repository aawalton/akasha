Chapter 34: All In

Ambrose backed away from the metal man, who made no effort to follow. “He’s…Not here. Who are you?”

Who, because ‘what’ was obvious. Vanderborn was known for his constructs and golems, and probably didn’t bind them all to cards. This particular golem had a copper colored, sleek, minimalist design, slender and without any unnecessary detail or definition. Blue eyes glowed,  and there was a little nub for a nose, but no ears or mouth. The arms were longer than a human’s, with too many, multi-jointed fingers. It stood at attention, but cocked its head at Ambrose.

“I am the Servitor for Navigation, Analysis, and Cooking. You may call me SNAC.” The voice was androgynous, leaning slightly masculine. “Are you in need of my services?”

Ambrose blinked. This wasn’t what he was looking for, but it was probably better. They barely had any idea how this ship worked, and they certainly didn’t understand the weird space they were traveling through. Any help they could get would be welcome…assuming the thing was safe.

“I don’t know. What are your standing orders?”

“My orders are to follow the commands of Master Vanderborn and his family, and assist with navigation in the Aetherian Expanse. Technically, I am an information vessel, here to answer any questions on the information we’ve collected on the worlds encountered by the Hermanic Empire. And, of course, to provide nutritious and delicious food on demand. Are you hungry?”

He was, but that could wait. “What information do you have about Vanderborn’s world?” he tried.

The golem was silent for a second, the blue light from his eyes dimming while he accessed the data. “Amaria, as the world is currently known, is a colony with moderate natural magic and higher than average potential in its magic users. The conquest has been ongoing for fifty years now, and is set to be concluded in the next three according to Master Vanderborn’s schedule. It has two major continents, although the eastern continent Riddolsta is mostly empty following an engineered plague deployed twenty years ago.”

Ambrose blinked in surprise. That was news to him, and anyone else from Amaria. The justification for expansion had always been danger from the east, and a need for unification to survive against the Riddolstan scourge. But then, he supposed he knew it was always an excuse. The truth was still jarring to hear.

“When were you made?” he tried. “And do you obey all of the Vanderborns, or just Charles?”

“I was created five years ago, and have been updated twice a year with new information since then,” SNAC replied, still sounding utterly serene. The voice wasn’t lifeless, but it sounded permanently conversational, and slightly curious. “I am to obey all of the bloodline, except for orders that contradict my original instructions or could cause harm to Master Vanderborn himself.”

Despite his earlier doom and gloom, this was promising. Or at least, it could be. “What if one of the Vanderborns were to order you to listen to others? Would you do it?”

“Yes.”

There was still no chance of surviving against this Hermanic Empire? Not if they were stupid. But if this golem had knowledge, maybe they could stay on the run for a while, find someplace to hide or settle. If this empire spent decades or even centuries taking worlds, then maybe they could run out the clock somewhere and not have to worry about dealing with it.

“Will you come with me?” he tried.

The golem followed him willingly enough, and Ambrose wondered at that. For all Vanderborn was famous for his combat golems, this one seemed entirely docile and helpful, despite him not being a part of the bloodline. But then, that bottom level looked like it was a private lab, so maybe there just weren’t defenses out of a lack of necessity. It still seemed foolish to him.

They made it almost all of the way up the Wanderer when Luthor met him.

“Oh, there you are. We were beginning to wonder,” he said, standing between Ambrose and the last stairs back up. “What’s this?”

“This is Vanderborn’s servant, SNAC,” said Ambrose. “He says he knows the ship and how to navigate. I figured I’d bring him up and see if he could help Jessica.”

The golem raised a hand in greeting, but stayed silent for now.

Luthor’s normally stoic mask slipped for a second. “Good. She can use the help.”

Ambrose felt a twinge in his stomach. It took a few seconds to realize it was honest concern and a bit of guilt. “Is she okay?”

“As okay as anyone can be when their family tries to kill them and they have to go on the run,” said Luthor with a humorless smile. “We’ve been talking. I was supposed to come get you, so we can discuss our options.”

He didn’t like the sound of that. “Our options, huh? Then let’s get this out of the way and see how unhappy I’m going to be.”

Luthor smiled, and went back up the stairs. Ambrose and SNAC followed, although Ambrose set the lamp on a hook.

Everything was nearly the same as when he left. Raeleq remained sitting, slumped up against the awning. Jessica was still piloting the ship, although he didn’t know how necessary it was between worlds like this. Raeva stopped her pacing when they came up, and looked serious. There wasn’t even an iota of her usual joy in her expression, which was both understandable and uncomfortable.

“I’m told we have things to discuss,” said Ambrose. “But before we do, let me introduce you to SNAC. Servitor for Navigation, Analysis, and Cooking, apparently. He’s Vanderborn’s assistant golem for the ship.”

Jessica perked up a little. “Really? I knew Grandfather was working on one, but I hadn’t seen any progress.” Her expression dropped. “I guess there’s no way of knowing how much he was keeping from us. Hello, SNAC.”

The golem bowed at the waist, one hand pressing against his middle while the other went behind his back. “Greetings Mistress Vanderborn. I am pleased to be of service to you until Master Vanderborn gets back. Do you have any orders? Shall I make supper?”

“I could eat,” Raeleq said immediately.

“So could I,” Raeva admitted.

“Then maybe in a second, but not yet,” said Jessica. “Do you know what each of these levers and buttons do?” She gestured at the mass of opaque controls. Flying the thing seemed simple enough, but who knew how many different enchantments or features there were to go through?

“Of course, Mistress. I am familiar with every feature of the Wanderer. Do you have any specific questions you would like answered, or worlds to go to?”

A look passed through all of them. Off in the distance were any number of those worlds, dangling in the…sky was the wrong word, but that’s what it felt like. Some were close enough to see the flashing images across their surfaces, while others were little more than a white ball, so far off as to just look like a star in the distance.

Ambrose cleared his throat. “Do you have a registry of worlds that haven’t been colonized by the Hermanic Empire?” he asked.

SNAC nodded once. “Of course. We have scouts checking out world after world, including those either empty or inhabited only by flora or fauna. There are a number of worlds with little Empire presence, worlds slated for future infiltration and conquest, and worlds belonging to insurgent forces.”

“Insurgent forces?” Luthor asked, clearly interested. “That could be helpful. But we get ahead of ourselves, I think. We need to discuss what we’re to do next.”

Jessica released the wheel, leaving just a finger against it to keep the ship steady. “SNAC, go make us supper for five people. Something simple and filling, and done within an hour.”

“Yes, Mistress,” he replied with another bow. The golem turned, but Ambrose stopped him.

“And make sure there’s plenty of booze to entertain us all,” he said. SNAC nodded again and left them. Ambrose gave them all a look. “So, Graves tells me that we need to talk. I assume that means that you have made a decision and are going to try to convince me to go along with it.”

Raeva sighed and stopped pacing. She leaned against one leg of the awning, her arms crossed over her chest. “You assume correctly. We don’t have many options available, and so our course seems obvious.”

He nodded with a grimace. “Let me guess. We can either run, hide, surrender, or fight. Surrendering is out of the question, obviously. Running and hiding sound good to me, given we now have a source of intelligence and have our choice of dark holes to crawl into. We could probably avoid the fighting for a long time, if we tried. This doesn’t have to be our problem.”

They didn’t say anything. They didn’t have to.

“Oh come on,” Ambrose protested. “There’s just five of us. Five. If they have world after world under their control, how many millions of people oppose us? How many billions?”

“It wouldn’t be all at once,” said Raeleq simply. “We’d do it one fight at a time.”

“Oh, well, that makes it manageable then,” Ambrose scoffed. “Look, I get that the three of you are bound and determined to throw yourselves into unwinnable fights, but what about you, Jess? You can’t possibly think it’s a good idea to try to interfere.”

Jessica took a deep, shuddering breath. Her normally silky hair was a bit wild from the rain, fighting, and stress, and her haunted expression matched it. “If not us, then who? This was my damned grandfather who did this. I feel like…like it’s my responsibility to do something about it. To make up for him. I don’t know if I can, but I have to try.”

“Of course. Of course.” Ambrose rubbed his temples. “What if, and hear me out here, we just never stop moving?”

“I never took you for a coward, Amby-lamb,” Raeva said, a hint of playfulness coming back. “What happened to wanting to prove yourself the best around? What happened to relishing a challenge and the rewards that follow?”

He turned from her and looked out into the busy, empty skies. He gestured wildly where they came from, towards a cloud of bright purple dust in the shape of a dragon’s head to their rear. “That part of me’s back in Amaria, along with the rest of the lives that are no longer ours.”

“We could do real good here,” Raeleq said, softly in spite of his naturally booming voice. “Better than we thought we could do back home. The scale’s bigger, sure, but…”

He was outnumbered. There would be no convincing any of them that this was a stupid idea. It had been so easy, throwing his life away when he thought he would die before he could regret it. Now, they somehow all survived and things were worse than ever before. Dying, Ambrose decided, would’ve been much easier.

“If you want to run, we can find you a place,” said Luthor in his infuriatingly calm voice. “No judgment or hard feelings. We’ll find as safe a place as we can, and then we’ll figure things out, but…We want to fight. No empire lasts forever, no force of arms can ever impede people from being free in time.

“Maybe we won’t be able to accomplish anything. Maybe we just die sooner rather than later. But you know what, Ambrose? I’d rather die trying to make things better. We could use your help. You’re strong, smart, and you see things we don’t. We want you to be with us, to help us, but we’re not going to force you.

“You don’t have to decide now, just…Think about it.”

Ambrose looked at each of his friends in turn, supremely annoyed with each of them. Raeva with her stupid causes. It wasn’t enough for her to be powerful, she needed to do something with it. Raeleq with his idiotic idea of fairness and kindness. He may have been a fighter, but no one wanted genuine, impossible peace like he did. Between the two of them, they would never give up until they’d gotten themselves killed.

Jessica was the person he was least angry with. She at least made sense. No one else had suffered a betrayal like she had, and it was paired with her general weakness. Of course she wanted to fight and make things right, but she didn’t have the strength to back it up.

Which just made her perfect for Luthor’s crusade. The light wizard wasn’t the strongest, the smartest, the most anything, and yet he never stopped. Everything seemed so clear, so easy to him, like just sheer will alone would be enough to get the job done, even if he didn’t know how.

“You’re all fucking morons,” Ambrose griped. “Do you even know how you’re going to strike back against this empire? Where we’re going to start?”

No matter how much he tried to keep himself aloof and unknowable, the rest knew when Ambrose was crumbling, and the bastards didn’t hide their amusement. Raeleq even cheered, as if he’d already surrendered.

“We find a weakness, and we exploit it,” said Luthor. “A newer world. That golem you found can help us find a starting point. And from there, we’ll take a look.”

“We need cards,” said Ambrose, surrendering one degree at a time. “That’s the first priority. We need to be stronger, and we need resources. Targets don’t matter until we’re sure that we can survive.”

An idea struck him, and he hated it. If he did it, it meant he was giving in, joining up in the suicidal crusade with the rest of them. But he’d already thrown away his chance for safety and prosperity for them. Ambrose had already been all-in, even if he didn’t want to admit it.

“In fact, I think I have something for us.”

“What’s that?” Jessica asked, leaning in.

Ambrose reached into his back pocket and pulled out the cards he’d stolen from Vanderborn’s safe.

“Graduation presents,” he said. “Two for each of us.”
