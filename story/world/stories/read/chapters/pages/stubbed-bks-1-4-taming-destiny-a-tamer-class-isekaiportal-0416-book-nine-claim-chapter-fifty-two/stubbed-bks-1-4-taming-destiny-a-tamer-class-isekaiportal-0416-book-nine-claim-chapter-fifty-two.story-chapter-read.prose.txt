We’re being watched.

The sensation tickles at the back of my neck with undeniable unease. A predator is somewhere near and is eyeing us hungrily. Still, we remain in place, standing a few steps away from four large carcasses.

They’re fresh – our party found a group of emu-like beasts only half an hour ago and worked together to take them all down. Kalanthia agreed that matching the carcasses would be a good way to signal to the Tier three that we’re not backing down immediately.

The emus were Tier twos, and I have to admit that Valence had a point when he said that the beasts here are more powerful than expected. Still, there are enough in my party that we were able to take the herbivores down without suffering any significant injuries, even if the ability of one of them to project its razor-sharp beak a foot ahead of itself was a nasty surprise.

Ninja’s new ability – where she calls on Storm’s shadow – is surprisingly useful, allowing her to move around with remarkable and magical flexibility. Lathani’s ability to step through shadows gives her the same advantage, and Bastet is simply too powerful now as a Tier three to struggle much with a Tier two, no matter how unexpectedly resilient it might be.

I haven’t brought everyone with me. We decided that bringing too many people might discourage the Tier three from revealing itself. But I do have my closest companions here, including the two samuran warriors, the alcaorises, and the two deris. Since the only one present who is still Tier one is Ivor – and he has his own resources in terms of flight and tattoos – it feels like a group that could take on the world.

Of course, I know better than that.

Meeting the Tier five during our journey was a significant wake-up call. It made it very clear that there are still many foes in this world that we don’t have even the slightest hope of confronting. But for a small group of Tier twos, our party was more than sufficient. I hope it will be more than sufficient even for a Tier three – however powerful – especially combined with my own Skills and abilities.

This is a very different situation from when we once faced Raven.

Using my more subtle form of Inspect, I allow my perception to slowly extend outward, attempting to make it as unobtrusive as possible.

Information trickles back to me, confirming what I already suspected: there’s a Tier three watching us.

In fact, I realise with a shiver of horror, it’s behind us.

We’re trapped between it and the carcasses. Not a good position to be in. But we knew that it would be the one to choose the battlefield – its stealth abilities are far too impressive for us to pin it down.

For the moment it’s simply watching. The sensation I receive through Inspect is that it’s studying us, observing. Most likely deciding whether to attack or not.

It’s a shame we didn’t manage to find its den. I suppose a Tier three would know how to hide something like that well enough that no one could discover it unless it chose otherwise – unless, like Kalanthia, they choose to announce their presence so loudly that no one could fail to heed the warning.

This one seems cut from a very different cloth, yet it still opened hostilities by killing our guards.

I continue letting my Inspect work, the lengthy period I’m giving it allowing it to be extra subtle – that’s exactly what I need right now.

Even with unlimited time, however, a Tier three has enough control over its system that I can’t gather all the information I would like. Eventually the trickle of information returning to me becomes so faint that it’s barely useful and I know I’ve reached the limit of what I can learn without alerting it to my probing.

I reach out to my Companions through the Bond, hoping for their insights.

It’s a predator, I tell them. Specialising in ambush, as we suspected. It has some capability with shadow and…sound? Shadow is easy for me to feel. Lathani has been practising extensively with it on the journey here, giving me a strong sense of when it’s being used around me. The other element is less clear, though I’m fairly certain in my guess. It would definitely make sense – and indicate that the blood-curdling screams the guards heard were very much intentionally projected.

From what I can tell, it isn’t especially physically resilient, I continue. But its ability to dodge attacks will probably be exceptional. Its intelligence is already confirmed, but I don’t get the sense that it relies much on Mana or conventional magical abilities. Its strengths seem to be tied directly to its own body and its control of shadow.

We must not underestimate it, Bastet warns the group firmly. Abilities do not have to rely on mana to be dangerous.

Agreed, Ninja adds with firm conviction. Since her shadow double doesn’t rely on mana either, she has personal experience with this.

Should we reach out to the Tier three somehow? I ask my companions. At the moment it seems content to watch, but if it leaves after all this, we might lose our chance to open communication today. And I’m unwilling to risk more of our people falling victim to it.

We don’t want to provoke it into attacking, Catch points out practically.

Better that it attacks now than it does so later when we’re not expecting it, disagrees Iandee.

In the end, though, I leave the decision to Bastet. She, after all, will be the one speaking for us.

I feel her considering the possibilities. Then, without responding verbally, I sense the shift as she begins to extend her domain.

Immediately I can tell that it isn’t an aggressive move. It’s more a polite request to speak – and an undeniable acknowledgement that she knows the other beast is there.

As always, I relax a fraction when it washes over me, in direct contrast to Kalanthia’s which always makes me stiffen up at the sensation of being observed by a predator. Bastet’s domain is full of fierce protectiveness, like an encircling pair of barbed wings that protect those within it even as it rips at those who attempt to pierce those sheltering within. It matches Bastet to a T – I wasn’t surprised in the slightest the first time I felt it.

At the same time, we all turn to face the direction where the other Tier three is hiding.

One moment the space between the trees is empty; the next, a figure flickers into view. This is the first time anyone from the expedition has seen this particular Tier three, and I have to admit, its appearance surprises me.

I had half-expected something feline. In my experience, solitary ambush predators tend to be. But this creature is far more humanoid than I anticipated. My eyebrows rise and my breath catches at the creature’s appearance.

It’s crouched on a branch at about eye-level, and the sharp-clawed digits clinging onto its perch have far more in common with my hands than with Lathani’s paws. It has a longer head than my own, though its face is similarly flat and it has two eyes and two ears. But the similarities stop there.

Its ears are webbed, more like those of the alcaorises than my own. Its nose are two snake-like slits in its skin, and its mouth is a lip-less slash hidden behind two large spider-like mandibles. We didn’t find any venom within the guards who were killed, and I strongly suspect that those fangs contain it – it must have ripped the guards apart with its claws.

As for its proportions, those are more similar to a gorilla’s with elongated, heavily muscular arms. It is furless, bearing smooth mottled brown and green skin that works perfectly to camouflage it in the jungle. Even without active abilities, its stillness renders it difficult to see.

I don’t recognise the species at all, not even from the bestiaries that Nicholas gave me that contain all the known beasts of the Lost Continent.

It’s surprisingly small for a Tier three – if it was standing on the ground, I suspect it would be a little shorter than I am. However, I don’t make the mistake of underestimating it – its eyes are predatory and calculating. I can almost see the thoughts passing behind them as it evaluates whether it should just attack us and be done with it.

I do my best to project a clear warning toward it: if it attacks us directly, it will fail. Powerful, it might be, but I have faith in my companions.

For a long moment the stand-off continues. Bastet has been polite – though she’s extended her domain, she hasn’t pressed against the other beast yet. However, as if deciding that the challenge is worth it, the other beast finally responds.

I feel it in the air between them as the creature’s domain rises to meet hers – far larger and stronger than its relatively small body would suggest.

They lock together.

Both creatures go utterly still, their body language tight and focused. However, when I shift my weight slightly, I notice the other Tier three’s eyes flick briefly toward me. Even while engaged in a difficult domain battle with Bastet, it hasn’t stopped paying attention to everything around it.

Within its domain, I feel the weight of its experience: this is not the first battle it has fought. And it clearly does not expect it to be the last.

Its domain is as difficult to pin down as the beast itself. It retreats in one place and presses in another, an unpredictable dance that pushes Bastet to the limit as she attempts to keep an even pressure in all places despite pressing against something that’s ever-changing and adapting. Any weak area she has is ruthlessly exploited, while the beast’s domain dances away from anywhere she puts particular focus into, deflecting her attack rather than allowing it through.

Bastet has practised domain battles since her Evolution, but fighting against me or even Kalanthia is nothing like a real confrontation with an Enlightened beast who wants to subjugate her.

Still, for all her inexperience, she doggedly fights on. Because of the nature of her domain, she is strengthened by the knowledge that her actions now are in defence of her pack. There is no option for failure when it is our lives at stake.

The struggle turns into something like an arm-wrestling match. The pressure shifts one way, then the other, back and forth several times. Bastet’s determination is powerful, but the other beast is skilled.

I feel my Companions tense and relax almost in waves as the battle shifts more towards one side than the other.

Calm, I urge everyone, breathing deeply and forcing my own tension to relax. If we’re too tense, we won’t be able to move if we need to, I remind them – and myself at the same time.

However, as the battle wears on, I realise something that dismays me deeply – Bastet is going to lose. Not because she’s weaker, but because the beast’s strategy forces her to expend more effort. Unless she wants to allow the beast's stronger attacks through, she has to hold her defenses evenly against it – like she’s commanding a shield wall where her enemy is directing a battalion of fencers.

I feel it as she begins to falter; so does the other beast, judging by the flicker of satisfaction I feel communicated through the domain.

I’m not the only one to realise that and a low rumble of discontent rises around me that matches the apprehension that grips me. I feel a flicker of Bastet’s own fear creep in and know that this will be the end unless I do something – fear of failure almost guarantees it in battles of will like this.

But Willpower is something I have in spades these days and Bastet is my Companion. I will help her.

Draw from me, I tell her steadily, pressing my focus and determination to succeed no matter the odds down the Bond between us.

Technically, I’m the one who can borrow traits from my Companions; they can’t borrow mine. But what do rules mean in the face of survival for us all?

For a long, agonising moment nothing changes. When it does, fear lashes at my insides as Bastet’s will flickers. Have I distracted her at the wrong moment? I can’t help asking myself.

Then, forcing away the thought, I press even more of my determination into the Bond. This will work. It has to.

I feel Bastet’s will flicker again like a candle flame, the snuffer of her opponent close to putting it out.

And then, like the flame has been given more fuel, it begins growing again, the flickering flame steadying. Relief pulses through me and the iron grip on my lungs loosens and allows me to breathe. But a few seconds more when Bastet holds her position but makes no headway indicate that my intervention so far is not enough. I need to go deeper – the support I can offer like this is limited.

I’m closing my eyes, I warn my Companions, trusting they will protect me while I’m vulnerable. Then, I do just that, falling into the Bond between Bastet and me. I don’t even need to touch her to enter her Core space – our Bond is strong enough that I think I could probably even do this if she were out of eyesight.

Unlike usual, her Core space is tumultuous, the whole construction vibrating. For a moment I fear that I will be the interference that destabilises her completely. Even being here seems to make her channels rattle even more violently. It’s only when I pull in my presence as tightly as possible that the channels and the writhing luminous blackness around them return to their previous level of agitation.

Following instinct, I don’t even go near her channels. Instead, I zoom in and settle at the centre of the space.

There, I press, my central position allowing me to balance the vibration where elsewhere it would have destabilised her. Like I’m a balloon that’s expanding and pressing against the inside of her Core space, I prop up her faltering domain from the inside out.

I can’t leave her Core space; I can’t control her domain. But what I can do is ensure that she doesn’t have to worry about it collapsing back inside her.

Deep inside my closest Companion, I can’t sense what’s happening with the outside world, but I feel her sense of triumph, the almost-there success that bolsters her confidence beyond anything I can do.

I pull out just in time to feel the other Tier three’s Domain buckle. The fencing foils have bent; the guard has been forced aside.

Bastet strikes, brushing the remnants of the beast’s protections aside, and then her domain surges forward, filling the space and washing over the other beast.

The creature crouches where it is, fear flashing through its eyes, calculation telling it Bastet is stronger. I am almost certain that it will retreat, and I have no idea what it will do next if it does.

Then Bastet sends a clear signal through the Domain, a wordless desire to negotiate. I almost hear her thinking at it, ‘wait, we wish to speak with you.’

The creature hesitates.
