Raphael’s Visage gradually turned somber as Ascalon and the rest recounted what had occurred in the dungeon. How their adventure was met with quite the perilous end, and how, once again, they had moved after Lilithra—though, perhaps, this time Ascalon had been also part of the reason, seeing how Pride had behaved.

The older gentlemans rising anger was more than clear, his brows furrowed into a most displeased frown, his forehead covered with wrinkles. Surprisingly, he wasn't the only one showing disgust for the situation, as the tiny Goddess was also fuming in her seat, pouting and frowning—she had even set her food aside, as unbelievable as that sounded.

Once the recounting was over, Raphael took a deep breath, doing his best to calm down. But this time, enough was enough.

“The nerve of these people…!” The gentleman growled, his tone dripping with frustration. “To think they would even follow you into a dungeon…!” He added as he slammed a glass on the counter, promptly pouring himself a drink.

“It is most worrying—I fear they're growing bolder.” Ascalon commented with a thoughtful expression, his elbows propped onto the bar counter.

“It’s getting really scary…” Lyra timidly chimed in, her cat ears drooping slightly. Lilithra gasped at her side, her frown a clear sign of discomfort.

“I'm sorry, Lyra… everyone…” Lilithra shrank in her seat, her dejected tone causing Lyra to immediately speak up.

“You have nothing to apologize for!” The cat girl chastised with uncharacteristic seriousness, her cheeks puffed out and slightly blushed. Everyone was taken aback by her boldness, but quickly realized she was simply telling the truth.

“I have to agree.” Athanya’el suddenly joined the conversation, drawing everyone's attention. It was rare for her to participate, after all!

“You cannot blame yourself for things that are beyond your control.” The tiny Goddess mused, nodding a few times to herself. Lilithra’s eyes gleamed with emotion and admiration, while Ascalon let out a contented sigh at their interaction.

“Sorry to interrupt, but…” Selorien began, shifting the attention to himself. “I don’t think we’ll have to worry too much.” He declared, causing the rest to show confused expressions.

“I talked to Ron about all this.” Selorien said as he leaned onto the counter. “He assured me headquarters is going to thoroughly look into the matter.” The elf yawned, likely tired from the whole previous ordeal and his visit to headquarters.

The rest nodded in acknowledgment, while Raphael placed a pensive hand to his chin. Ascalon, on the other hand, felt a bit relieved. With both Ron and headquarter’s workers on the case, he believed they would be fine for the time being.

“Hmm. Knowing Ron, he’ll probably turn New Lumingard upside-down in search of these cultist bastards.” Raphael spoke from behind the counter, his voice still carrying a palpable irritation.

“I just hope he doesn’t spare any effort—this would be a good time to force some of the Guilds to stop sitting on their asses all day!” The gentleman continued, his scowl not easing in the slightest.

“His first objective is Chariot’s Flight, obviously…” Selorien explained, arching an eyebrow and smiling mockingly.

“But seeing how Wrath was so annoyed at Ravanna for revealing her identity… I guess her Guildmates don’t know about her weird pastimes.” He let out an annoyed laugh, while the rest nodded.

“Yeah… but that just makes me think… what is wrong with them?” Lilithra began with a conflicted expression, fiddling with her thumbs.

“Ravanna has fame, belongs to an incredible guild Guild, and I don’t believe she’s hurting for money… so, why join some weird cult?” She asked in frustration, likely thinking about her own involvement in the matter.

“You cannot hope to understand the minds of the wicked.” Athanya’el chimed in, earning curious stares from everyone. “If I understand their intentions… they are clearly deranged.” She added, sighing in disbelief.

“What are they trying to do? Why…” Lilithra clenched her fists, her words wavering. “Why do they need me…?” She questioned with clear hesitation, her expression one of fear.

Athanya’el looked conflicted, her eyes focusing on Ascalon and causing him to tilt his head in confusion.

“I believe… it is time.” The tiny Goddess began, her gaze drifting to the bar counter.

“Are you sure?” Raphael suddenly asked, his firm, cold voice startling everyone. But the tiny Goddess simply nodded, a small smile on her face.

“It had to be said sooner or later.” Athanya’el replied, focusing on Ascalon once again.

“Before I answer your question, Lilithra, I have to start from the beginning…” She grabbed the hem of her dress, her small frame looking slightly frightened.

“Ascalon.” She spoke, lifting her head. “We… we are not in another world.” She revealed, confusion spreading across everyone’s faces but Raphael.

“What…?” Ascalon responded, unsure of what she meant.

“You were not sent to another world.” Athanya’el began, her fists clenching the dress tighter. “We are in Excadia… but… thousands of years have passed.” The Goddess declared, surprise and shock assailing everyone present… though Raphael seemed completely normal, sipping on his drink.

Ascalon opened his eye wide as the realization sank in. His entire world seemed to have spun, the shock of the revelation causing him to feel dizzy and disoriented. He pressed a hand to his forehead, his mind racing as the dots connected.

His awakening, the emblems of his old company of knights, the statue of Arkssol’el… all the clues had been right under his nose all along. Perhaps he just didn’t want to acknowledge it, clinging into the smallest sliver of hope… the hope of seeing his old friends and his old world once again.

A multitude of faces and places flashed on his mind, a profound sense of sadness tearing his chest apart as he realized his ‘world’ was no more. His comrades, his past home, the people cheering on the streets after a crusade, the celebrations of well-earned fights—it was all gone.

Even when he was getting accustomed to New Lumingard and enjoying his time there—for the most part—having heard his Goddess words had finally put a real sense of finality to his old life. Shaken as he was, he had to come to terms with reality—and the faces of his new, precious friends helped to ease his inner turmoil a bit.

The awkward silence stretched on for minutes, as no one dared interrupt Ascalon’s moment of contemplation. After all, if they felt incredibly shocked by the revelation, then Ascalon had to be struggling with far greater feelings. Eventually, he broke such silence.

“Is it true…?” Ascalon asked with a firm voice, though it was mostly a redundant question—he already knew the answer. What would the Goddess gain from fabricating such a lie?

“Yes. I am sorry, Ascalon…” Athanya’el bowed, her long, golden hair swaying softly. A Goddess, bowing before him… the sight filled his chest with shame.

“My Goddess, do not bow to one such as I.” Ascalon quickly propped her up, a faint smile appearing on her face. But as quickly as her expression lightened, it darkened once more.

“Listen to me, everyone.” Athanya’el began, breaking free from Ascalon’s arms to stand up on her stool. Everyone stared at her in silence, knowing better than to interrupt such a serious moment.

“You may know some of this already, but I shall recount it.” The Goddess said as her golden eyes traveled far, back into the past.

“Back when this world had not even a name, we Ten Great Gods graced it with our presence.” She traced the air with her fingers, tapping rhythmically. A golden light followed in her motions, a total of ten, sparkling dots floating in the air.

“But when Gods such as we decide to meddle with the mortal realm… the rules allow them to act as well.” She looked at them with a somber expression, even though the lights brightened her face. “Demons.” She declared, her fingers releasing black, ugly dots into the air.

Lilithra twitched in place, her entire frame shivering at the words. Lyra noticed immediately, and quickly threw an arm over her shoulder, trying to comfort her.

“Of course, they were not quite like Lilithra right here.” Athanya’el grinned and pointed at Lilithra, a soft, golden sphere drifting toward her. As the glowing orb touched its target, a blissful feeling encompassed the area, and Lilithra looked a bit more calm.

“These demons are… let us call them Primal from now on.” Athanya’el nodded to herself, closing her eyes. The ten sparkling dots moved on their own, facing off with the shadowy dots.

“We Gods and the Primals abhorred the existence of one another—we stood on completely opposite sides: light and dark, as expected… though on both sides, there are those who are indifferent to our enmity.” She explained, a disgusted expression on her face, her nose wrinkling as though she were smelling something foul.

“While we looked upon mortals with curiosity and amusement, wishing to observe their prosperity, Primals sought only to destroy, twist, and throw it all into disarray… as it is their very nature to oppose us.” Athanya’el frowned, looking dejected.

“But the rules bind us, they do not allow for our complete, direct interference… we cannot clash with one another.” She lifted her fingers, smaller dots of both light and dark appearing among the others.

“We are allowed to choose champions to fight in our stead. We Gods have to select mortals, it is yet another rule we are bound by… while Primals can simply send their own spawn into the fray—demon kings, mortals called them, in charge of spreading their influence and corruption.” Athanya’el kept tracing the air, more dots of both light and darkness appearing, swirling around the group.

“Yet among their ranks, there was a group of generals that stood far above the rest, going by the names of the cardinal sins. Each of them was powerful enough to rival even us Gods, with some of them rebelling against their own Primal forefathers, driven by wickedness and insatiable hunger.” Athanya’el sighed, taking a pause. She was talking quite a lot compared to other times, after all.

“At some point, the world seemed to be too small for them, so they all left in search of greater ambition.” The Goddess smiled mischievously. “It caused quite the uproar among the Primals, hehe!” She giggled softly, causing the rest to feel a bit less tense.

“But I am quite certain that this group of vile people are trying to call the cardinal sins back… somehow believing Lilithra can help with that.” Athanya’el finally reached the key point of the conversation, though her face looked conflicted.

Lilithra gulped audibly, visibly shaken by the whole ordeal. She likely had her own doubts about what Wrath and his henchmen wanted her for, but hearing it from a Goddess must have been way more impactful.

“Though I do not understand why they think she can help them.” Athanya’el shrugged, while the rest exchanged confused glances.

“Don’t they want to sacrifice her in a demonic ritual or something? It happens a lot in movies and all that.” Selorien chimed in, a pensive expression on his face. Everyone tensed up at his words, yet the tiny child quickly spoke up.

“Why would they do that? Hahaha!” Athanya’el suddenly burst out laughing, as though Selorien’s ideas were complete nonsense. “Sacrificing a demon to summon a demon? hahaha!” She kept laughing merrily, and Selorien joined in for some reason.

“That does sound dumb as hell haha!” The elf added, causing even Lilithra and Lyra to giggle. Ascalon sighed in relief, noticing that everyone was feeling a bit better… him included. But Raphael’s next words changed the mood once again.

“That is precisely why they’re so dangerous—they have no idea what they’re doing… the madmen!” Raphael explained, crossing his arms. The group looked at him and nodded, understanding his point.

“It’s not the first time I’ve seen lunatics drunk on power commit unspeakable atrocities in the name of delusions and made-up superstitions.” The gentleman exhaled sharply, irritation dripping from his every word.

“By the way, Raphael…” Ascalon suddenly spoke, after his long time of silence. “Were you already aware…?” He asked with a bit of hesitation, and Raphael smiled weakly.

“Yeah… sorry, Ascalon.” Raphael apologized, his warm gaze letting Ascalon know he was truly regretful.

“But in my defense, I found out you were, well… from the past, just a few days ago.” He continued, and Athanya’el visibly tensed up in her seat, her golden eyes narrowing.

“We had quite the peculiar guest, after all.” Raphael revealed cryptically, a conflicted expression on his face. Across the counter, Athanya’el’s expression darkened as her tiny fists struck the table with surprising force.

“Yes… that stupid brother of mine paid us a visit…!” She revealed in anger, her words taking a moment to sink in, as a collective thought formed in everyone’s minds.

Her brother!?

But when Ascalon was about to press for further answers, a knock on the door drew everyone’s attention.

“Delivery for… uh, miss Vexeria?” a voice rang from the outside, and Lilithra perked up with clear interest.

“Oh? What did you buy?” Selorien asked as Lilithra got up from her seat.

“Nothing… I think.” She answered with a confused expression, though she looked a bit excited about it.

After leaving briefly, Lilithra returned with a small package. Everyone huddled around with curiosity, as she read aloud a letter.

“Dear Lilithra, I hope this letter finds you in good spirits. I send a gift that I hope will be of some use to you. From a fervent fan…?” Lilithra said in a low voice, blushing slightly.

An odd letter, Ascalon thought. But it wasn’t like he knew much about such things, either way.

With rosy cheeks but a bit of suspicion in her gaze, she unpacked what seemed to be a mirror.

But… something was strange.

After all, an obsidian-like surface rested where the crystal should’ve been.

“Ah…! No! Don’t gaze into its depths…!” Athanya’el suddenly cried out, her voice filled with concern and panic as she reached out toward Lilithra.

But it was too late, as Lilithra’s eyes were completely glued to the mirror, lured by the charm of the void.

For a moment, time seemingly stopped.

Then, she smirked, an unfamiliar laugh escaping her lips.

Her entire demeanor changed in a blink—her presence darkening with an aura most unholy.

“Wh—” Ascalon muttered in shock, his instincts screaming at him to prepare for battle.

Before him, Lilithra… was no more.

And something hideous had taken over.
