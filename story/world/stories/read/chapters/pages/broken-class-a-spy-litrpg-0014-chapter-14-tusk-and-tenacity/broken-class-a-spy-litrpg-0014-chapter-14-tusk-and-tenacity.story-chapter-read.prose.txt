Chapter 14 – Tusk and Tenacity

The forest loomed like a cathedral of giants. Towering redwood-like trees reached for the sky, their lowest branches a good twenty feet above Arthur’s head, just high enough to mock him. They offered no fruit, no leaves, not even a charitable acorn. The forest floor? A desolate stretch of dirt, tangled roots, and his old nemesis: moss.

“Moss. Again,” Arthur muttered, crouching to inspect a patch.

It pulsed faintly with life and earth mana. New flavor? Maybe. He took a hopeful bite.

Nope.

Disappointment had a taste, and it was moss. If the gods had a garbage disposal, this was what they scraped off the bottom.

Drogan sighed. Long, dramatic, and painfully judge-y.

“I trained the Dragon-Splitter of Kael’Tarak. Mentored the Voidcaller of Morn. And now I’m stuck with a moss-eating stick insect who thinks licking dirt is a meal plan.”

Arthur chewed slowly, ignoring him. If persistence had a name, it would be Arthur. Probably with a middle name like “Desperate.”

Days blurred together. He walked. He searched. He hoped. At night, he curled into tree hollows, trusting his glorious monster-stink to repel anything with taste buds. Each morning brought disappointment and Drogan’s voice, still tragically alive.

By the fourth day, Arthur was ready to scream.

Then he heard it. A sound sweeter than symphonies: running water.

He sprinted toward it like a man possessed and dropped to his knees at the sight of a tiny, pathetic creek. The water dribbled like it had performance anxiety, but Arthur didn’t care. He scooped up a sip.

Heaven. Elixir. Liquid redemption.

Arthur knelt beside the stream, head bowed as the cool water trickled between his fingers. He didn’t cry. He didn’t do that anymore but his shoulders stayed hunched a moment longer than necessary. Just breathing. Just grounding.

Drogan, of course, ruined it anyway.

“Oh perfect, now you're brooding. Want a moss-flavored journal to write your feelings in?”

Arthur didn’t answer. He just scooped another handful of water and drank in silence. Now that he’d found water, it was time to work. Time to rebuild what slavery and starvation had taken.

For the next four weeks, he trained like a man on a mission. Sprint drills, pushups, shadow combat with mana-fueled body. Every calorie of moss was squeezed into effort. He healed with Mana Heal. Pushed himself harder. Got stronger.

His stats and skills improved:

Mana Body: Level 7 (+2)

Mana Heal: Level 14 (+4)

When he finally stood from a one-handed push-up and looked down at his leaner, sturdier frame, even Drogan grunted in approval.

“Fine. You’re no longer a wet noodle. You’re now… a slightly firm noodle.”

Arthur didn’t argue. He never did. Not with Drogan. Instead, he gave a simple, “Thanks,”  hoping the bare courtesy would keep the impatient remnant from exploding after four weeks of no monster kills.

Now all he needed was a weapon. So he set out again, this time in search of bones.

Hours passed before he found them, remains of a Grubtusk Devourer. Something had crushed its skull but left the body untouched. Two intact tusks jutted from its ruined head.

Perfect.

Arthur pried them free and tested the weight. Unbalanced. Rough. Perfect for stabbing, not slashing. Still, they’d do.

As he moved through the woods, he made sure to stay upwind of his prey. Monster stink was great for defense. Not so great for ambushes.

He found his target: a young Grubtusk Devourer, separated from its herd. Big, slow, plant-eating and still deadly.

“What do you think, Drogan?”

“You against that? You’ve got better odds kissing a lightning elemental with a mouthful of saltwater. I give you five percent.”

“Only five?”

"You’ve got pointy sticks. It's got tusks, armor, and a weight class that makes you look like garnish. But sure, have fun."

Arthur smirked and waited. The Devourer eventually slumped into sleep, snoring like an earthquake with asthma.

He double-checked the wind direction. Still blowing toward him. Good. His monster dung cologne wouldn’t be ruining the surprise.

He edged closer, keeping low. Ten yards. Seven. Five.

Mana flooded his legs and arm. In one silent burst, he crossed the remaining distance and plunged the tusk blade into the creature’s eye. It screeched, thrashed and Arthur was already gone, melting into its blind side like a whisper.

The beast roared in confusion, nostrils flaring, head jerking. But Arthur stayed behind the one ruined eye, always out of view. He circled with it, perfectly synchronized, a ghost with tusks.

“Hit harder, you walking toothpick,” Drogan growled. “At this rate, it’ll die of old age.”

Arthur didn’t reply. He was too busy watching for patterns. When the beast lunged, he wasn’t there. When it paused, he struck - low cuts along its joints, a deep stab into its haunches, a nick behind the knee. It bled slowly, heavily, confused and enraged.

Again and again, he darted in and out. Not fast. Just precise. He never left its blind spot, using terrain and patience as weapons sharper than any tusk.

Blood matted the grass. The Grubtusk staggered, shrieked, swung its head wildly. But Arthur had already moved. He waited for the opening, for the rhythm to break, and then…

He drove both tusks into the creature’s exposed throat. It collapsed with a gurgle and a final shudder.

A chime echoed in his mind.

> Congratulations! You have killed an E-ranked Level 52 Grubtusk Devourer. You have gained 3 levels. You are now Level 4.

Title Earned: Mental Fortitude - For slaying a foe 50 levels above your own rank while still being in F rank. Grants resistance to mental attacks.

Drogan was silent for a beat.

Then: “Fine. I’ll admit it. You’re still a rat, but at least you’re a clever, stabby rat.”

Arthur just nodded once and wiped his tusks clean. One kill down. A hundred more to go.
