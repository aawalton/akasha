“Oh, dead gods!” Cursed Kyera as Alley ran off back into the still burning and obviously collapsing building they had just come from. “Are they going to be okay to keep an eye on you and everything?” She asked Darius, who looked surprisingly comfortable despite being wrapped knee-to-neck in what was apparently a CoreSteel chain.

The redheaded young man offered a slightly impeded shrug and made a face that definitely told her that he didn’t know. In response, Kyera sighed.

“I was hoping you could maybe ask them, since I don’t speak Frostlander, so I can feel better about going back in after Alley.” The apprentice Framer’s tone was half-amused, half extremely unimpressed.

“Ohh, right.” The hunter half turned his head and said a few things in the guttural tongue of the far northerners. The tall prince let out a sigh and nodded. That was enough of a reply for Kyera, who took off after Alley and sprinted back into the huge inn.

The man she had often internally thought of as the avatar of madness was apparently the avatar of something else, just as if not more terrifying had already said that he couldn't enter that form again anytime soon. That meant that as much as Kyera respected Alley’s ability to defend himself without Cards, if the building came tumbling down atop him, he would probably not be able to do anything about it. Kyera, on the other hand, could still Manifest, and wanted to be there to help her friend if something went suddenly wrong.

The apprentice Framer's Library wasn't exactly full of Cards known for their ability to create structural integrity, but her woodland creatures had more chance of protecting the two of them from something like that than Alley's hook, which happened to be the only real piece of equipment he had brought with him.

‘Well, that isn’t quite true.’ Kyera admitted to herself. Her friend was armed with a magic cloak that would try to eat him if he took a cut, and a mask that would protect him from fire and smoke to a fairly high degree. None of that would help should the ceiling come collapsing in on him. At least if he was right about there being zombies inside the huge inn his hook would let him fight them, and his cloak would let him then Card them like he had the undead dragon. There was a slight problem, though. A Deck, even one with the kind of power Alley’s possessed, required time between creating Cards. For a normal Deck, creating a Card would also shut off its ability to Manifest. Alley couldn’t normally do that anyway, so that part really didn’t matter. It was still a concern for the apprentice Framer.

“Alley?” She called uncertainly as she entered the smouldering lobby. There was absolutely no sign of the darkly clothed man. “Damn, that boy is fast.” Kyera let out a little sigh and sprinted towards the set of stairs that was less blackened and fire-damaged. Making it to the second floor, she found the dark-haired boy crouched amongst a pile of corpses. He was wearing the Goret Brotherhood mask, but both of his hands were clutching his hair in obvious frustration.

Alley’s hook was lodged in the head of one of the bodies, and before him was a Card on the floor of the hotel. Kyera couldn’t make out the stats or name of the Card but the image on it was obviously that of a humanoid Creature. The scene was weird enough, but Kyera’s mouth fell all the way open when she saw purple Resonance begin to leak out of the injuries of the five remaining bodies and began to twist into helical shapes before flowing back towards the corpses.

“No, no, no,” Alley said, still pulling at his hair.

“Alley?” Asked Kyera, her voice filled with concern.

“It's resurrecting a militia guy!” He hissed, letting go of his head long enough to gesture at one of the bodies. The Framer had gone out of her way not to look at the details of the human corpses that surrounded Alley, but she felt like she didn’t have a choice now.

Slowly, Kyera turned to look down at the slain man, and it was obvious Alley was right. The twisting Resonance was slowly entering the body that was clearly dressed in the uniform of the militia that had stormed into the Storslået Palads.

“All five of these people were zombies?” She asked cautiously, as clearly her friend was upset. Kyera could tell three of the people were guests of the inn that had been slain by the mercenary attack, one was a member of the masked brotherhood, and the last was a militia member.

“No, just these three,” Said Alley as he turned the singular eye of the mask he was wearing onto her. “But it's still my fault there are zombies in here, and now this man is dead because of it.”

Kyera took a moment to look at the dead man and then around the utterly destroyed hallway.

“Alley…” She said again. “ I see that it's turning him into a zombie now, but this man died from falling debris. I can see where it hit him, and I can see where it fell from. I promise you, not everything is your fault. Now tell me, do you know how long this zombification process is going to take?”

For a solid fifteen seconds, Alley didn’t reply beyond breathing heavily and slowly rising back to his full height.

“It's not your fault,” Kyera repeated.

“Umm, we have time.” He finally replied. “It usually takes twenty minutes to half an hour, but the Resonance is spreading.”

“I can see that. So we don’t have time to be screwing around, feeling bad about it.”

After another few seconds of silence, Alley nodded.

“Yeah, yeah, you’re right. There are probably more zombies in this place than there are supposed to be, but as long as they or the fires don’t kill anyone else, we can contain them enough that I can either Card them, or the Resonance will run out and they will stop resurrecting.”

Consoling her necromantically empowered friend had not been something Kyera expected to deal with tonight, but in the month she had spent around Alley and Darius, she’d learned and fully accepted that trying to predict the future while anywhere near the pair of young men was absolutely a fool’s game. A fool’s game she had no desire to play. She would react, she would adapt, and she would expect the unexpected; she utterly refused to let anything take her by surprise.

“Okay, so do you have a plan?”

Speaking of surprise, Alley almost seemed shocked that one of his hands was still gripping his messy black hair. He let it go, took a second to look at his own limb, and then yanked the hook that was jutting from the slain zombie’s head.

“Not unless you count making our way upstairs as quickly as we can, and cutting down anything that shouldn’t be alive until we meet the militia, all whilst hoping this place doesn’t collapse around us as a plan.”

Kyera tilted her head for a second and then summoned forth her Hand.

“Yeah.” She said after a moment. “ Yeah, I think that I do.”

While she couldn’t see his face, the apprentice Framer heard Alley let out a single barked laugh from behind the wooden mask.

“Oh, and then we come back here and kill these guys again.” He added before the pair of them headed towards the next set of stairs. Kyera knew that the other militia members should have already been making their way back downstairs by now, and the fact that there was no sign of them was bad news indeed. Still, her draw hadn’t been a cursed one, and if there was one other thing she’d developed over the last four weeks, it was faith that Alabaster Roe was dangerous enough that the pair of them should be able to rescue the militia and anyone else left in the building from just about any situation they might have found themselves in.

“Do you really think that man was killed by debris?” asked Alley as the pair of them charged for the next set of stairs, and Kyera considered her Hand.

“If Darius were here, and not tied up, and being guarded by those princes, I am almost certain he could show you the like tracking stuff to prove it.” She replied.

Alley didn’t respond as he ran, but Kyera could see a weight lift from his shoulders as he moved ahead of her. The dark-haired man had been carrying more and more guilt as things that he could potentially blame on his supposed Doom had piled up during the trio’s stay in Skalean. It was nice to be able to reassure him that not every tragedy in the Frostlands was his fault, even indirectly.

Honestly, Kyera believed what she had said. Unless the zombies were monstrously strong, she doubted they could have crushed his head the way it had been anyway.

‘Alright,’ She thought. ‘What am I working with?’

‘Queen Of Moths, Voice Beneath The Roots, Fruitbat Swarm, Bell Of The Burrows, and finally Sprig Of Youth’. It was especially nice to see that last Card if they really were dashing headlong into what could be a charnel house.
