Pampli carried them through the mist for a long time after the sounds of pursuit finally disappeared behind them. Sakura tried several times to understand where exactly they were going, but soon gave up on that useless task. Everything around them looked the same: dead trees, twisted roots sticking out of the ground, rare stone outcrops, and sticky green haze where every distance seemed deceptive.

Sometimes the mist grew so thick that Sakura stopped being able to make out even Lir’s shoulders in front of her. He sat almost at Pampli’s neck and clung to the obsidian ridges with both hands. With every new jolt, his back sagged more heavily against Sakura, and she had to hold him by the waist so the boy would not slide to the side. Gilbert sat behind her and held Sakura herself, though the mage was also barely staying on Pampli’s back.

"We need to stop," Sakura said, raising her voice so they could hear her through Pampli’s heavy breathing. "Lir is about to fall."

"I’m not going to fall," Lir objected quietly, but Sakura could feel him trembling.

"Pampli, find us shelter," she asked. "Dry. No guards, no monsters, and no people who want to kill us. No mist. Ideally with beds and hot food too."

Pampli moved one ear.

"He only understood the part about food," Gilbert noted.

"Then let him find food. We’ll stop nearby."

The beast snorted as if the mage’s opinion had deeply offended him, but after a few minutes, he really did turn off the more even stretch of road. Soon an old stone path appeared under his paws. In places, it had completely disappeared beneath moss and mud, but along the edges, low posts with carved waves could still be seen.

The green mist finally fell behind them. They had crossed beyond its perimeter. Almost immediately after that, they had to make a practical stop to remove the green protective suits and bury them in the ground. That way, they could throw the Castle’s pursuers off their trail more effectively. Besides, the suits no longer served any purpose.

Sakura and Gilbert took turns digging. Sakura would have preferred to do everything herself, but the mage was too stubborn. During that time, Lir lay on the back of the motionless Pampli, barely moving.

Farther on, the path led them to the slope of a small hill. A broad arched entrance darkened in the stone, half-covered by hanging roots and thick ferns. Above it, a faded mosaic had survived: blue fish swam around a round sun with long golden rays spreading from it. Several pieces were missing, so one fish looked as if someone had bitten off half its head.

Pampli stopped at the entrance and growled with satisfaction.

"That is not food," Sakura said.

The beast snorted disapprovingly, then squeezed inside.

"Or he decided the edible part is in there," Gilbert suggested.

"Or the little fish on the walls looked appetizing to him," Lir added.

His voice sounded weaker than before. Sakura quickly slid down from Pampli’s back and barely managed to catch Lir when he tried to climb down after her.

"I can do it myself," he muttered, embarrassed.

"Of course. I’ll just help a little."

"It’s just that I haven’t walked normally in a long time. In that prison, sometimes it was... hard."

Sakura could not imagine what exactly Lir had gone through during all that time in the Castle before they pulled him out. But now, after the escape and the pursuit, he seemed completely wrung out.

"Then get used to it gradually. It’s all right. Everything is all right now."

Gilbert climbed down last. He did it more carefully, but when he touched the ground, he also froze for a moment and leaned his palm against Pampli’s side. Sakura noticed, but said nothing. If she started supporting both men at the same time, each of whom was convinced he could stand on his own, they would spend the rest of the night arguing.

Beyond the arch was an old roadside bathhouse. Once, it must have been beautiful. The walls were covered in pale plaster, though now it had peeled and cracked. A mosaic of blue, green, and white stones stretched across the floor, forming images of fish, waves, and strange long-legged birds. In the center stood a large round pool of water. Faint steam rose from it, and the surface itself glowed with soft blue light in the darkness. At first, Sakura did not understand why the water was still warm, but then she noticed narrow stone vents at the bottom. Tiny bubbles rose from them, barely visible. The bathhouse had been built directly over a hot spring, and the water still came from below, from deep inside the hill, even though the place itself had long been abandoned.

Along the walls stood stone benches. In the far corner, a small stove had survived, and beside it lay a pile of dry branches someone had left here a very long time ago. Part of the roof had collapsed, opening an uneven piece of night sky. Several stars could be seen through it.

Pampli approached the pool, shoved his muzzle into the water, and began to drink.

"I’m not sure we should bathe in there after that," Gilbert said.

"Armed guards chased us through green mist, you nearly died in the Castle of Justice, and Lir spent far too much time in prison. I think we’ll survive a little Pampli drool."

Pampli lifted his head. Water streamed from his muzzle onto the mosaic, and then the beast stepped straight into the pool. Water splashed over the edge and spread across the floor in a wide wave.

"Now all of him is in there too," Lir noted, looking at Pampli with caution and interest. He still could not get used to him.

For the first time in hours, Sakura heard something like amusement in his voice.

"It’s fine. He’s almost clean."

Gilbert looked at the obsidian back of the beast, covered with road dust and dried mud.

"We have very different ideas of cleanliness."

Meanwhile, Pampli lay down in the water, taking up almost half the pool, and closed his eyes. A pleased rumble rolled out of his chest and echoed hollowly off the bathhouse walls.

"That means the place is safe," Sakura decided. "Otherwise, he would have killed everything alive first and only then lain down."

"G-gods," Lir whispered.

They settled by the far wall. Gilbert took the remaining travel rations, two flasks, and a bundle of cloth from [Inventory]. After the escape, many of their things had to be left in the wagon, but he still had dry flatbread, a little dried meat, and several overly hard dried fruits Sakura could not identify.

Lir ate slowly only for the first few minutes. Then he seemed to stop noticing how quickly the food in his hands was disappearing. He bit off large pieces, chewed hastily, and immediately reached for the next. Sakura quietly placed half of her flatbread in front of him.

Lir stopped.

"That’s yours."

"I’m just full."

"You’re a liar, Sakura."

"Not at all. Just eat and be quiet."

Beside them, Gilbert pretended he was absolutely not following their conversation, though a minute later, he silently moved the rest of the meat toward Lir.

"And you won’t eat?" Lir asked.

"I already ate."

Sakura looked at the mage.

"When?"

"Before the escape."

"Was that at least today?"

"Recently."

Lir huffed quietly, but immediately lowered his gaze, as if he was still not used to being allowed to laugh. For a while, they sat in silence. Warm steam rose from the water. Pampli shifted from time to time in the pool, sending blue reflections running across the walls. Outside, there was no sound of pursuit, no beasts, no wind.

Sakura looked at Lir more closely. Now that they were in the light of the small fire, it was especially noticeable how much he had changed. His face had grown gaunt, and shadows lay under his eyes. Dark marks from shackles circled his wrists, and he tried not to put weight on his right leg. But he was alive. Sitting beside her, eating terrible flatbread, and from time to time glancing at Pampli.

At some point, Sakura realized she had been silent for too long.

"I’m glad to see you," she said.

Lir raised his head.

"Really glad. Even if I had to climb into the Castle of Justice for it."

"You didn’t have to come for me."

He said it quietly, without accusation, but that did not make it easier.

"I did."

"No. They kept saying you would come. That they only had to wait, and then they would catch you too. At first, I thought they were just trying to scare me. Then I started to fear they were right."

Sakura lowered her gaze to the flatbread in her hands.

"They were right."

"I didn’t want that."

"For me to come?"

"For you to get caught because of me. Because you’re a good person. I understood that right away, as soon as we met."

There was not a drop of relief in his voice. Only exhaustion and the guilt Sakura understood far too well, because she had carried it inside herself ever since the day she learned about Lir’s capture and his father’s death.

"You were taken because of me," she said. "Your family helped me when I understood nothing and didn’t know where to go. And then you ended up in the Castle because they needed bait. I couldn’t just decide it wasn’t my problem anymore."

Sakura looked at him. Lir was not smiling. Exhaustion shone in his eyes, or maybe something else he was desperately trying to hold inside.

"In there, it felt like I would never see anyone again," he admitted. "Sometimes I thought it would have been better if they had killed me right away, because then at least no one would come inside for me."

"Don’t say that."

"I really thought that."

"I know. But still, don’t say it."

Lir looked away. For several seconds, he stared at the water, then ran his palms over his face.

"When Gilbert came for me, I didn’t even believe it at first."

"That was reasonable," Gilbert noted. "I wouldn’t have believed it right away either. Especially from a suspicious stranger in a guard suit."

Sakura threw him a look. Gilbert continued:

"But at that moment, we had no time for a good first impression."

"He said you were waiting," Lir continued. "And then I understood it was true. Because no one else would come up with such an insane plan."

"The plan was good. Well, almost."

Lir finally smiled. Sakura felt something inside her, something that had been clenched into a tight knot all this time, loosen a little.

"I’m glad to see you too," he said. "Very glad."

She wanted to answer with something light, but the words did not come. So Sakura simply moved closer and hugged him. Lir froze, as if he had not expected it, and then carefully hugged her back.

He was too thin. Sakura felt protruding bones under her palm and clenched her teeth so she would not ruin the moment with a fresh wave of anger toward the people who had brought him to this state.

"I thought you were dead," Lir whispered.

"I thought that a few times too."

He laughed quietly into her shoulder. The laugh quickly broke and turned into a shuddering inhale. Sakura did not let him go until the trembling passed.

Gilbert spent all that time looking toward the entrance, giving them at least the appearance of privacy. When Sakura finally pulled away, he rose from the stone bench.

"Now you both need to wash half the Castle and road off yourselves. The water is warm, but first we’ll have to drive Pampli out of it."

Pampli opened one eye.

"Looks like he heard everything," Lir warned.

"Then it will be easier for him to understand the request."

The request did not help. Threats did not help either. Pampli had no intention of leaving the pool until Sakura promised him the rest of the unknown hard fruit. After that, the beast reluctantly rose, drenched them with a new wave of water, and walked out into the middle of the bathhouse. He shook himself off at once.

Sakura managed to cover her face with her hands. Lir turned away. Gilbert stayed where he was and took most of the spray.

"Now we can consider me washed already," he said, wiping water from his face.

"I think that was revenge for insulting his cleanliness."

"I understood that."

They searched the bathhouse for separate rooms, but found only a small changing room without a door and a half-ruined partition that reached approximately Sakura’s shoulders.

"I suggest we bathe in turns," Gilbert said.

"That’s reasonable," Lir agreed.

"Then I’m first," Sakura declared.

"Why?"

"Because I suggested we stop."

"Pampli found the place," Gilbert reminded her.

"He already washed. Now it’s my turn."

The men exchanged glances and, to Sakura’s surprise, did not argue. Gilbert led Lir closer to the entrance so they could watch the road at the same time. Pampli lay down between them and the pool, but Sakura quickly realized the beast did not share human ideas of personal space at all.

"Turn around," she told him.

Pampli tilted his head to the side.

"I’m serious. Don’t look."

The beast continued staring.

"He doesn’t see you as a woman," Gilbert called from the entrance.

"Hey. I definitely did not ask for your opinion."

"Most likely, he doesn’t understand why people remove their protective layer at all."

"Gilbert, you’re supposed to be looking outside."

"I am looking."

Sakura made sure both men really had turned away, then quickly removed her ruined clothes and stepped into the water. Heat wrapped around her body at once. It felt so good that a quiet groan of relief slipped out of her.

A pause settled near the entrance.

"I will hit anyone who turns around right now," Sakura warned.

"No one is turning around," Lir answered too quickly.

"I’m busy watching the road," Gilbert added.

The water really was perfect. Warm, soft, as if it were washing away not only dirt and the smell of mist, but also the tension of the last few days. Sakura sank deeper, leaving only her head above the surface, and for the first time in a long while, allowed herself to simply sit without needing to run anywhere.

Pampli came to the edge of the pool and shoved his muzzle into the water again.

"Don’t even think about it."

The beast snorted in her face.

"I’m not sharing."

Pampli licked the water right beside her shoulder.

When Sakura finished, a new problem appeared. The cloth Gilbert had taken from [Inventory] was large enough to dry herself with, but she had no clean clothes left. Unexpectedly, Sakura realized that the dry change of clothes she had taken for exactly this kind of situation had stayed in the wagon.

"I need something to wear," she said from the water.

"Your clothes are lying beside you," Gilbert answered.

"They’re dirtier than I was."

"I have a shirt."

"How big?"

"Too big for you."

"That works."

Gilbert took a shirt from [Inventory] and, without turning around, tossed it over his shoulder. It flew over the partition and landed on Pampli’s head.

The beast instantly caught it in his teeth.

"Pampli, give it back."

He backed away.

"That is not a toy."

Pampli retreated another step, still holding the shirt.

"If he tears it," Gilbert said, "you’ll be going in your very dirty clothes."

Lir was already laughing, covering his mouth with his hand. Pampli, deciding that the situation had finally become interesting enough, suddenly turned and ran around the bathhouse with the shirt in his teeth. Sakura could not chase him without climbing out of the water completely naked.

"Gilbert, get the shirt away from him."

"I can’t. I’m not supposed to turn around and look at you. Remember?"

"I changed my mind."

After several minutes of humiliating negotiations, Pampli finally gave up his prey in exchange for another piece of dried fruit. The shirt remained whole, though wet tooth marks had appeared on it. Sakura pulled it on and found that the fabric reached almost to her knees.

"You can turn around," she said, gathering her dirty clothes.

Gilbert looked at his shirt, then at her.

"It looked better before meeting Pampli. Also, the clothes should be washed by hand. We’ll save the cleansing scrolls."

Lir bathed next. Sakura and Gilbert moved to the entrance, while Pampli stayed by the pool again, having apparently appointed himself the chief observer of bathing procedures.

"He’s looking at me," Lir reported awkwardly after a while.

"Don’t worry, he doesn’t see you as a man," Sakura answered.

Gilbert coughed, hiding a laugh.

"That doesn’t help," Lir said.

Then it was Gilbert’s turn. He sent Sakura and Lir almost all the way to the exit, though Sakura noticed that he moved slowly and still kept one hand close to the place where the core had been. Gilbert had managed, on the way, to tell them exactly how he had lost it.

"You won’t drown?" Sakura asked.

"I’ll try not to cause you any new inconvenience."

A few minutes later, a heavy splash came from behind the partition.

Sakura rose sharply.

"Gilbert?"

"Everything’s fine."

"That did not sound fine."

"Pampli decided to return to the water."

Then came the beast’s irritated growl and another splash.

"Now I definitely won’t turn around," Lir said.

"Correct decision," Gilbert replied. "Especially if you don’t want to see me drown an obsidian monster with my bare hands."

When everyone was finished, they settled on the stone benches. The fire in the old stove burned better than Sakura had expected. Warm air mixed with steam from the pool, and the bathhouse became cozy.

Lir fell asleep first. He tried to ask Gilbert something about the road, but did not finish the sentence. His head dropped onto the folded cloth, and his breathing quickly evened out.

Gilbert lay down nearby. He still looked bad, but Sakura no longer had the strength to argue about that. Pampli stretched himself across the entrance, blocking it with his obsidian side better than any door.

Sakura settled near the pool. Sleep did not come at once, though her body had become heavy and relaxed. She listened to Lir’s breathing, the rare crackle of branches in the stove, and the quiet splash of water.

Lir was here. Alive. Gilbert had also gotten out, though after all these adventures and after using the core, he was weak and hurting. Pampli had found them and was beside them again. For a few hours, that had to be enough.

Sakura closed her eyes.

Water spilled over the edge of the pool and ran in a thin stream along the mosaic gutter. Its murmur sounded calm, almost lulling. Sakura had already begun to drift into sleep when she made out something else among the ordinary splashing.

At first, she thought Gilbert had spoken in his sleep. She opened her eyes and listened, but no one was speaking or moving.

The stream continued running over the stone, breaking against cracks and gathering in small hollows. In every movement of the water, there was a barely noticeable rustle. As quiet and impossible as the voices of the wind she had heard before.

Sakura propped herself up on one elbow. The whisper became clearer. It did not form understandable words, but there was direction in it. A call. The water seemed to be telling her about dark channels under the bathhouse, about the deep spring inside the hill, and about something moving far underground.

Then one voice separated from the others. It was quiet, ringing, and very close.

"Obsidian one..."

Sakura froze.

The water in the pool trembled, though no one had touched it.

"You finally heard us."
