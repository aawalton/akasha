The rest of the day passed by in a blur, with Aida letting herself enjoy being with her friends, basking in the relief that they all knew what their next stage of life would look like—because in times of uncertainty, being guaranteed a job was enough to provide a sense of stability that allowed them to appreciate the good things, no matter how minor.

Dinner was a treat, with the six of them serving as guinea pigs for experimental dishes Sue’s father excitedly served them. Everyone marveled at the complementary flavors and textures, envying Sue’s relation to such a talented chef for being able to eat such delicious meals growing up.

“He wasn’t this good at cooking before,” Sue complained. “He served us weird things growing up. Back me up, Aida!”

“Eh?” Aida glanced up from her bowl, where she had been busy shoveling food into her mouth so that she could avoid being drawn into sharing anecdotes she didn’t recall. “S’good now, all tha’ matters.”

“Thank you, Aida,” Kenny said, glaring with mock disapproval at his daughter. “This daughter of mine is always bringing up the past, and never notices my current efforts.”

Even with Sue’s good-natured complaining, Aida could see that she was blossoming more and more into the role of the Main Character. Where her breathtaking appearance was what got her noticed at school—and devastating power in the arena forced the remaining stubborn outliers to acknowledge her—now her natural charisma was on full display.

It might have been because Sue was more relaxed now, but everyone and everything around Sue glowed. It was hard not to pay attention to her, and just seeing her brightened everyone’s mood, as evidenced by the rest of the smiles on the restaurant patrons’ faces whenever they glanced over at the owner’s daughter.

Caleb, who was sitting on Sue’s other side, seemed more confident and less afraid to speak his mind. Levi’s banter had taken on a new tone as well, becoming much more playful and inclusive than targeted and making a joke of someone. Even Vanita was more talkative.

As she watched everyone’s interactions, she could imagine everyone at this table as real adults…leaders in their respective industries, catching up with each other after decades of hard work and overcoming challenges.

She’s making everyone better.

Aida’s wistful musings turned inward. Faced with the power of the Main Character’s easy influence, she would be lying if she didn’t acknowledge the insecurity that was bubbling beneath her cheerful expression.

She had known she was a side character when she first took over Aida’s life. But knowing intellectually that she didn’t have the plot armor to protect her from making grievous mistakes was different from directly seeing the positive impact a powerful, pure individual had on the people who surrounded her.

I can change the trajectory of my own life, but I don’t have the ability to make people happy and believe in themselves.

Aida was struck with a sudden realization: she didn’t have the power to save Wyndia. This whole time, she had been trying to think of ways to save Ezra, save people like Agnes and Ernie, save the country from the mysterious force that had been threatening to crumble civilization as they knew it. But she was just a side character.

She had been trying to justify to herself that she still had a somewhat important role to play, in some sort of support role that was essential to helping the Main Character succeed in her final battle against The Great EvilTM. But the reality was that what she was doing on her own wouldn’t matter in the grand scheme of things.

If she wanted to actually contribute to the plot in a meaningful way, she had to do it through Sue.

That was why, as the two of them sat on the roof later that night, their feet dangling over the edge, Aida finally told Sue her honest fears.

“You think Ezra might not have botched his Ascension Meditation attempt?” Sue repeated slowly. “You think… he was forced into his… condition?”

“I know it goes against what all the other Professors and Healer Luk think,” Aida said quietly. “But I met someone yesterday who fell into the same condition.”

Sue listened intently as Aida related her and Levi’s encounter with Ernie and Agnes, humming thoughtfully. Aida was grateful that Sue didn’t bother questioning about her spending so much time with Levi.

“But then… why didn’t Ezra wake up yet? This nonpractitioner woke after just one star cycle…”

“I don’t know, but… I have a feeling it’s related.” Aida eyed Sue anxiously, holding her breath as she awaited her opinion.

“And you two noticed her behavior and mannerisms seem odd for someone who is supposed to have been in a loving marriage for a lifetime.” Sue frowned up at the night sky as she kicked her feet pensively. “I see what you’re saying, since having two people experience similar previously-unheard-of conditions is suspicious…”

Sue’s dubious tone made Aida’s throat clench with worry. “I know two doesn’t make a pattern, but… the timing is suspicious, isn’t it?”

Aida forged on as Sue kicked her feet slightly more vigorously. “They both fell asleep during the Fire Festival, and… it might be possible that there are others who fell asleep as well, right? Since Yoon is in charge of collecting civilian reports, I thought maybe…”

“Maybe they might know something!” Sue’s expression cleared up as she remembered the resources that were now at her disposal. “You’re right, right now we don’t know enough to make any conclusions, but maybe the Gullbeaks will be able to survey the community.”

Aida nodded, relieved that Sue was taking her concern seriously enough to do something about it. Her job was done.

Levi had teased her about finally ceding control to others by “delegating” the task of collecting more information about Agnes’s coma, but at the root of it Aida had been worried that people would dismiss her, because she just… wasn’t someone important. The Gullbeaks might like her as a person, but she doubted they would take her seriously, especially given who she was… or wasn’t.

“You know what, why don’t we go to HQ tomorrow? We can talk to Tyda and Yoon together,” Sue suggested. “They’ll probably ask super smart and insightful questions that I won’t be able to answer, so having you along would be really helpful to piecing together a working theory.”

“Oh…I guess I can. I don’t have any plans for tomorrow,” Aida said warily, which was a bit of a lie. She had been planning on going out exploring on her own, especially to familiarize herself with the process for getting on a golem to Burston City before she was to set out on her trip with Lily on Loam Day. The tiny girl was energetic and hopped from topic to topic, but she was also incredibly quick-witted, and was likely to pick up any mistakes Aida made. Aida had to be perfect, since she wouldn’t be able to cloak her missteps among a group of friends.

Fortunately, there would still be one day free for her to do a practice run before meeting with Lily.

“Great! Kuri was asking about you. I think she was hoping you’d visit before you left for Burston,” Sue said cheerfully. She leaned in closer, speaking conspiratorially. “I’m pretty certain both Yoon and Kuri were sad you didn’t have an interest in being an Adventurer. They kept talking about how useful it would be to have a Healer on the team, even if they just stayed at HQ. It would go a long way in building community relations to be able to offer free clinics every once in a while.”

Aida laughed as she pushed at Sue’s shoulder. “They did not!”

“They did!” Sue insisted, leaning into Aida’s hand. “They were talking about that because Kawa was complaining about potion overconsumption increasing their operating costs. He said the salary for a Healer would be cheaper than replenishing their potions stock at this rate, especially with how expensive prices have gotten lately.”

“And they can’t recruit a Healer?” Aida asked skeptically. “I’m certain they could draw any number of candidates who are interested in working for them.”

Sue shrugged carelessly. “I think they’d rather hold out for the right candidate than just any candidate.”

They stared at each other before bursting into giggles.

“You’re so convincing.” Aida got to her feet as the last light on the street winked out, leaving only the light of the stars to see by. “It’s a shame I already signed a contract with Relief for Health.”

“Maybe in a couple years you’ll decide to join us,” Sue sighed as she stood up as well. “I’ll come get you tomorrow after breakfast. Tyda will be going on a patrol after lunch, so the earlier we get there the better.”

Sue held out her arms for a goodbye hug, which Aida gave—but the taller young woman held on longer than usual.

“Don’t worry, okay?” Sue said softly. “We’ll figure out what happened to Ezra.”

Aida closed her eyes, feeling mixed emotions as Sue’s steady confidence warred with her natural anxiety.

Have faith.

“…Okay.”
