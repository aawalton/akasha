It took mere seconds for Luke to return to the hallway where his body lay sprawled on the floor. Two figures knelt beside him, their panic evident as they tried to rouse his unconscious form.

“Snap out of it, Luke!”

Kayson slapped his face, the sound ringing sharply off the stone walls. Luke winced at the sight.

“Oi, you bastard.”

Without delay, Luke’s soul surged forward, racing to reclaim his body before Kayson’s hysteria could deal any lasting damage. With divine strength behind those frantic slaps, it was only a matter of time before a well-meaning strike turned lethal.

The moment his soul slipped into place, Luke jolted upright—only to crash his head into something thick and solid.

“Argh!” he yelped, recoiling from the impact.

“Luke!” Kayson cried out in relief, seemingly unfazed by the sudden headbutt.

“Get off me,” Luke groaned, his head spinning. A dull sting bloomed across his cheek, no doubt a souvenir from Kayson’s earlier slap.

“Thank the gods. I thought that demon had killed you,” Richard said, bending down to help him. With little effort, the man hoisted Luke up like a feather, his sturdy arms cradling him with ease.

Luke didn’t respond. His gaze shifted to the skeletal figure lying a few feet away.

That must be Heath Tinzel, he thought, assessing the corpse.

Back when Tristan had described the Minister of Intelligence, Luke had already suspected something was amiss. Now, seeing the gaunt, corpse-like figure firsthand, he was convinced.

He was probably possessed a long time ago.

Luke rubbed his temples, trying to quell the pounding in his skull. Whether it stemmed from the soul-body separation or the headbutt was unclear—either way, it was agonizing.

“Where’s Riann?” he asked, scanning the area for the burly man.

“He went into the courtroom to look for survivors,” Richard replied. “What happened to you? You and that guy dropped at the same time—”

“I’ll explain later,” Luke cut in, waving a dismissive hand. Kayson was watching him with similar concern, but now wasn’t the time.

“We need to move on the noble houses that conspired against the Marxx Kingdom. Our men are already in position—no one’s getting out. We just need the names.”

Richard blinked, visibly surprised, while Kayson’s expression turned grave.

Without another word, Luke stepped forward, weaving between the corpses strewn across the hall and making his way to the courtroom doors. Smoke still hung in the air, but his eagle eyes pierced through the haze with ease.

What he saw made his stomach twist.

Blood and entrails painted the imperial court in gruesome shades of red. His boots squelched against the slick floor as he entered in silence, eyes locking onto Riann, who was hunched over and inspecting the bodies.

A quick glance told him some were still alive—barely. A few had shallow wounds; others had simply collapsed from inhaling the smoke bombs he’d unleashed earlier.

“Riann, I need the names of those who betrayed us—and the nobles backing them,” Luke said, turning away from the carnage.

The large man didn’t answer. He was bent over a body, fingers pressed to the man’s throat as he checked for a pulse.

A quiet breath escaped him as relief softened his stern features. Moments later, Richard arrived, carrying a wooden bucket of water he’d drawn from the well.

Riann took it without a word and poured the contents over the unconscious man’s face.

The man sputtered violently, coughing and choking as he jerked awake.

“W-What’s happening?” he croaked, his eyes darting wildly around the ruined court.

Riann didn’t reply.

His massive hand closed around the front of the man’s robe and hauled him upright as though he weighed nothing.

“Tell me who you knew about this plan,” Riann said, his voice cold as iron.

“I—I don’t know what you’re talking about!” the man protested, though his voice trembled with a poorly masked fear.

Aside from streaks of soot, his luxurious crimson robe was untouched. Not a scratch marred his body. Luke didn’t recognize him, but his attire alone marked him as someone of high standing within the court.

“Ignacius…” Riann said, weariness threading through his voice. “The Second Prince still lives. Your coup has failed. Everyone here is dead— including your brother.”

“N-Nonsense!” Ignacius shouted, his face flushing scarlet. He twisted his head in frantic denial—then his eyes fell upon the corpse lying nearby.

Blue robes.

Soaked in red.

The man’s lifeless gaze stared blankly at the ceiling.

“Roland… ROLAND!” Ignacius screamed, thrashing against Riann’s grip. His struggles were useless. The Master of Arms held him as firmly as iron chains.

The man hadn’t flinched at the mention of the Second Prince’s survival.

He isn’t the one in charge, Luke realized.

“It wasn’t meant to be like this…” Ignacius whispered. He clutched his head, fingers digging into his hair. A silent scream tore across his face before he began to sob, dragging his nails down his cheeks until blood welled beneath them.

“The Lord was supposed to protect us… He promised Roland and me our lives…” he muttered, his voice unraveling into something manic.

Seeing the man’s mind begin to fracture, Luke stepped forward.

“Tell us who else was involved,” he said calmly. “Give us the names, and we will make sure this Lord of yours pays for what he’s done.”

“Haha… HAHAHAHA!”

Ignacius raked his nails down his face again and again, laughter spilling from his mouth in broken peals. Blood streamed freely as he tore at his own flesh.

White bone showed through the torn skin.

The sight was nauseating.

CRACK

A scream split the air as two sharp cracks echoed through the courtroom.

Ignacius’s arms fell limp at his sides, twisted at grotesque angles. His body continued to shudder and writhe in Riann’s unyielding grip.

“Hahaha—he’ll kill us all,” the man said at last, eyes locking onto Luke.

“It’s already too late. The nobles—”

He froze.

His eyes bulged. Veins burst in a web of red across the whites of his eyes, spurting blood in every direction. A shrill scream tore from his throat, so piercing it rattled the walls.

Luke’s eye twitched.

Even Riann flinched. He dropped the man and stumbled back, instinctively pulling Luke with him.

They both stood there, watching in stunned silence as Ignacius—the High Chancellor of the Marxx Kingdom—convulsed violently. A thick, black, tar-like foam oozed from his mouth as his body seized uncontrollably.

“What the fuck?” Riann muttered, his thick brow furrowing in equal parts confusion and disgust.

But Luke’s focus wasn’t on the grotesque display.

He was replaying Ignacius’s final words over and over in his mind, trying to piece together what had just happened. He was almost certain this was the result of Soul Poison or a Soul Curse—the same twisted tools the Bishop loved to use. That would explain the man’s breakdown… and his madness.

But something else was off.

Just before the seizure, Luke had felt something—a cold pressure, like a presence descending.

Was it the Bishop?

Did he kill him remotely?

But why? His death was inevitable… unless he was about to say something important?

Luke’s expression darkened.

He ran the man’s last words through his mind again.

A second later, he went stiff as stone.

“Fuck!” Luke cursed, turning on his heel and bolting toward the exit.

His boots slipped on the slick floor, coated in thick, half-congealed blood. He hit the ground hard, gore clinging to his robes as he scrambled to his feet.

“Kayson! Use the signal!” he roared, trying to rise.

His foot slid again, and he crashed back down into the crimson muck.

A pair of strong hands seized the back of his robe and yanked him upright in one fluid motion.

There was no time to say thanks.

“Kayson!” he barked again, voice sharp. “Use the damn signal!”

“W-What? Where do I use it!?” Kayson called out, clearly confused.

Luke clenched his jaw, fighting the panic rising in his chest. He sprinted through the hallway, finally arriving at the crate he’d looted from the Alchemy Workshop. He ripped it open and pulled out half a dozen thunder bombs, glancing left and right.

His hands trembled.

“Where’s the fucking lantern!?” he shouted.

“Ah—here, Luke!” Richard’s voice cut through the chaos. He rushed over, thrusting the lit lantern into Luke’s hands.

Without hesitation, Luke lit the first bomb and shoved it at Kayson. “Throw it into the air—now!”

“There’s a fucking ceiling, Luke!” Kayson yelled, his voice bordering on panic.

Only then did Luke realize his mistake.

They were still inside the palace.

Kayson quickly snuffed the fuse with his fingers, averting disaster just in time.

“Come on—we need to get outside!” Luke said, grabbing several of the thunder bombs and sprinting toward the alchemy lab.

Kayson, Richard, and Riann followed closely behind. Though confusion lined their faces, none of them asked questions—at least, not yet.

They quickly arrived at the open courtyard outside the Alchemy Lab. Without delay, Luke repeated the lighting process and handed a lit thunder bomb to Kayson.

“Throw it that way,” he said, pointing toward the western sky. “It needs to be high—far enough that it won’t explode over the city streets.”

Riann stepped forward. “Let me throw it, young Luke. No offense, but I don’t think your friend has the arm for it.”

Luke ignored him.

Kayson tightened his grip and wound up, hurling the thunder bomb into the air with all his might. It shot skyward, quickly vanishing into the night.

Riann followed the arc with his eyes. After a moment, he cleared his throat and stepped back, his earlier confidence evaporating into quiet embarrassment. No one acknowledged it.

The group stood in tense silence, waiting.

Five seconds passed.

Ten.

Still nothing.

“Uh… Luke?” Kayson muttered.

Luke’s brow furrowed. A surge of irritation crept up his spine. “These good-for-nothing bastards can’t even follow a simple recipe…”

He snatched another bomb from the crate, lit it, and handed it off. One after another, he repeated the process, with Kayson launching each bomb into the sky.

This time, the arcs were lower—close enough to track with the eye.

BOOM. BOOM.

Two thunderous explosions rocked the air, their echoes rolling over the rooftops of the city.

Luke exhaled a short breath of relief, but his expression remained strained. His gaze drifted toward the crate at his feet, then to the black sky above.

Only two out of five detonated properly.

And even those had been weak—lacking the punch of his own crafted bombs. It had been just over a month since he taught them the recipe. He’d expected more progress.

“Luke…” Kayson finally asked, unable to hold back any longer. “Care to tell us what the hell is going on?”

“It was what Ignacius said… before he died.”

“Ignacius? The High Chancellor?” Kayson echoed, glancing between Luke and Riann.

Luke didn’t answer the question. “When I asked him which noble houses were involved, he told me it was already too late. He started to say something else—but then he died. Abruptly. Violently.”

Riann rubbed at the red scar lining his cheek, his brows drawn in confusion. “Died? Didn't he take some kind of poison? That black sludge coming from his mouth?”

Luke shook his head. “No. He didn’t kill himself. I can't explain it fully, but I felt something—like a presence pressing down on the room. He was silenced. Killed… before he could speak.”

Riann looked like he was about to object, but Kayson cut in.

“So? What does that mean? Why the signal? Can't we just keep the nobles surrounded until we arrive to question them?”

Luke’s expression turned grim.

“I don’t think any of them are still alive.”

Kayson paled. “W-What?!”
