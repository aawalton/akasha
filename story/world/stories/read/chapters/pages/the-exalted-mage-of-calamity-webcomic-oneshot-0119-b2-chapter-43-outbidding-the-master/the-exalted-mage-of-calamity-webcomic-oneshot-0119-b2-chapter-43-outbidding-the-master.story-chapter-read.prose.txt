There weren’t many murmurs for this lot, unlike some of the previous ones. Of course, that was to be expected. Regardless of how rare the metal was, a never-before-seen rock was still just a regular rock to most people. Starmetal was no different. Without a knowledgeable blacksmith, the ore was useless.

In the past, Viya had sent out a request to every city.

We are looking for skilled blacksmiths capable of forging and working with starmetal to create a suitable wand for the kingdom’s upcoming prodigy, Veronica Everwells.

All sorts of compensation had been included. Paid warp-gate travel, materials provided free of charge, access to Annesheim’s top-class smithy sponsored by the Royal Family itself, and, of course, a significant sum of vix as payment.

But even with those conditions, only four had responded to the request: one dwarf, one elf, and two humans. In the end, Viya had chosen one of the humans, as he had more accolades and a longer lineage of steel to his name. But Veronica had learned the names of the others, including the dwarf’s.

And the dwarf currently bidding from the middle section of the auction house… she didn’t recognize him.

“Thirty-two thousand!” Orban yelled. His voice carried farther than necessary. A few people looked his way, but he ignored them. No, he was only concerned with three other individuals.

“Thirty-three thousand!” an impeccably dressed man shouted from the balcony above him.

He looked to be around forty, with a belly rounder than the largest magically grown melon. He held a drumstick that had already been significantly bitten into. Four servants stood beside him, each holding plates of food from the main lobby, ready to serve.

Orban glared up at the balcony. “Thirty-five thousand!”

“I’ll bid thirty-six thousand.”

This voice came from a woman on another balcony several sections to the right. She had sharp eyes and an even sharper gaze. Her legs were crossed without any attempt at modesty, and yet few dared to stare. There was a piercing quality to her gaze that no one seemed willing to challenge.

Orban gritted his teeth. “Forty thousand!”

“Make that forty-one thousand.”

The third voice came from much farther away than the previous two. The man wore an extravagant red coat, the side of his head resting against his palm as he lounged on his balcony couch. It was the first time he had bid during the auction, yet he looked utterly bored and uninterested.

“Master,” Theo whispered.

“Shut it, boy,” Orban spat. He raised his paddle once more. “Forty-five thousand!”

“Forty-six thousand,” replied the first man.

“Forty-eight thousand!” Orban shouted.

“Forty-nine thousand,” the woman said slowly.

“Fifty thousand!”

“Fifty-one thousand,” the bored man said.

Murmurs spread through the rows around Orban. Some people turned to look at him, while others discreetly glanced over. But he didn’t care about any of them. Let them look. None of that mattered. No—he was fighting a war—a war against the world itself.

“Sixty-five thousand!” the dwarf yelled.

“Sixty-six thousand,” the sharp-eyed woman replied without missing a beat.

“What is happening?” Gwen asked.

“The auction’s gone completely silent. It’s just the four of them bidding now.”

Veronica sat in the middle of the couch between Gwen and Nasha, equally curious. It seemed the three groups in the VIP balconies were all competing with the dwarf below.

But none of them were trying to outbid one another.

Each waited until the dwarf raised the price, then added just enough to beat him.

Why?

Veronica turned around. A female servant employed by the auction house stood nearby, waiting patiently.

“Hey,” Veronica said.

“Yes?” The servant looked surprised to be addressed. She stepped forward and dipped her head. “Is there something you need?”

Veronica gestured toward the bidding below. “What’s happening right now? Who are they, and what are they doing? I recognize Corwin from Trailing Star, but what about the other two? And why are they all competing against that dwarf?”

The servant followed her gaze, then hesitated. “The dwarf is Master Orban,” she said carefully. “He owns a smithy in the western district and is one of the best blacksmiths in the city. Unlike most smiths of his standing, he isn’t affiliated with any of the commerce groups. At least… not yet.”

“Not yet?” Nasha asked.

The servant’s eyes flicked toward the other balconies. “The man with the plates of food is Callus Twistwell. He is the son of the current leader of the Twistwell Commerce Group. The woman is Selka Everdinne, a senior representative of the Caroline Commerce Group. And you already recognize Mr. Corwin from Trailing Star.”

“Those are… the three biggest merchant companies in Maftem?” Gwen asked.

“Yes.”

Veronica looked back at the servant. “Then what’s going on? They all seem to be taking turns outbidding that blacksmith.”

“Are they all competing for the starmetal?” Gwen asked.

“Seems odd that they’re politely taking turns,” Nasha added.

The servant lowered her voice. “I… cannot speak for their intentions, my lady. But…”

Veronica narrowed her eyes. “Speak freely.”

She gulped. “This is only based on rumors, and it is entirely speculative, but—”

“I said speak freely. I won’t repeat myself.”

Gwen raised an eyebrow, grinning. “Oooh… bossy noble lady is back,” she whispered.

Veronica ignored her.

“Yes, my lady. Master Orban is renowned throughout Maftem. Many consider him not merely one of the best smiths in the city, but the best. Rumor has it that all three commerce groups have tried to recruit him. He has refused every offer, insisting on remaining independent.”

Orban glared at the balconies above him. “Seventy thousand!”

“Seventy-one thousand,” Selka replied, her tone relaxed and monotonous.

Veronica continued watching the scene below. Orban seemed to be approaching the limits of his budget, yet the commerce groups showed no sign of stopping.

“Are they trying to ruin him out of spite?” Veronica asked.

The servant shook her head. “Yes and no. They all want him to join their group, but because he refuses, they’ve been trying to crush his smithy first and force him into accepting one of their offers. His business has been struggling recently because most of the quality ores and ingots in Maftem are controlled by the commerce groups.”

“So they stranded him in the desert while the three of them control every oasis,” Nasha said, leaning forward. “They’ve practically taken over the auction now. No one else is even willing to bid.”

“I don’t believe the commerce groups care much about the starmetal itself,” the servant said. “They only care that Master Orban doesn’t get it.”

“That’s kind of fucked up, isn’t it?” Gwen said, craning her neck to look farther down. “I mean, he’s practically shouting his bids. I don’t think he can keep up much longer.”

“Does this mean he’s capable of forging and refining starmetal?” Nasha asked. “If he’s that desperate for it, then he’s bound to have some skill.”

Veronica hummed. “You’ve got a point. Though I don’t recognize him as one of the smiths I met in the future. I wonder what happened to him.”

“Future, my lady?” the servant asked, perking up.

Gwen turned and waved a hand at her. “Don’t eavesdrop. That’s rude. Now, shoo, shoo.”

The servant blinked. “Ah, yes. My apologies.”

She stepped back toward the corner of the room to await further requests.

“Wow, that felt really good,” Gwen whispered, turning back toward the chaos unfolding in the auction hall. “I could get used to being a noble.”

“Don’t bother. It’s exhausting,” Veronica chimed in. “Don’t forget about table manners and speaking etiquette.”

Gwen’s face scrunched. “Ah, yeah. I’ve heard about that stuff. Sounds tiring.”

“So?” Nasha asked. “Are we still going for the starmetal?”

Veronica stared down at the dwarf.

Although the auction hall was dim, with most of the light focused on the stage, her enhanced senses let her see him clearly. His muscles were tense, veins standing out along his forehead. He was furious, but there was something else beneath it.

The human boy beside him wore that emotion plainly: worry.

The price was clearly approaching his limit. Meanwhile, when Veronica looked toward the three commerce representatives, none of them appeared remotely concerned.

Unfortunately, she still needed the starmetal. It was probably the strongest material she could currently use to create a suitable weapon. And if Orban truly knew how to forge it, perhaps she could even hire him afterward.

But for the moment—

She had to become his enemy.

“One hundred and five thousand!” Orban yelled.

His paddle had already broken. He no longer needed the voice-amplification enchantment embedded within it; he simply shouted loudly enough for the entire auction house to hear.

The man from Twistwell, already on his third plate of food, raised his paddle. “One hundred and six—”

“One hundred and twenty thousand.”

A feminine voice cut across the auditorium from the opposite side of the balconies.

Every head turned. It wasn’t Selka from Caroline. It was another woman entirely.

For a moment, no one said anything. Even the fox beastkin auctioneer paused, her eyes flicking from Veronica to Orban, then toward the three commerce representatives who had spent the entire bidding war opposing him. Her ears twitched in anticipation.

“One hundred and twenty thousand from balcony sixty-nine!” she announced quickly. “Do I hear one hundred and twenty-one thousand?”

Orban slowly raised his head.

The woman in the balcony wore white and moonlit blue, with fine gold lines curling across her gown. She was young. Beautiful. Seated comfortably between two other young women. But most importantly—her expression revealed nothing.

Why had she bid? Was she connected to the commerce groups?

Orban’s fingers tightened around the remains of his broken paddle. He looked toward Callus Twistwell. The man still held his half-eaten drumstick, but for the first time since the bidding began, Callus didn’t raise his paddle.

Orban turned toward Selka Everdinne. She merely watched in silence.

Lastly, he looked toward Lord Corwin. The man remained leaning back in his balcony, his bored expression unchanged, paddle hanging loosely at his side.

None of them were bidding.

Of course they weren’t. They had never cared about the starmetal. They had never wanted it. Never needed it. What other blacksmith could even work with the ore? All they had wanted was to keep it out of Orban’s hands. Now that some noble-looking woman had taken it instead, their work was done.

“One hundred and twenty thousand going once!” the auctioneer called.

“Master…” Theo whispered.

Orban didn’t look at him.

The words still sat in his throat. One hundred and twenty-one thousand. One more bid. One final attempt. But he was already nearing his limit. The last bid had been only one hundred and five thousand, yet she had jumped straight to one hundred and twenty.

Fifteen thousand vix at once. That meant she had money to spare.

“One hundred and twenty thousand going twice!” the auctioneer called.

“We still have one hundred and thirty thousand to use, like you said, Master,” Theo said, panic creeping into his voice. “Maybe she won’t go higher than that.”

Orban did have more money. Another ten thousand vix. But that would be everything. Nothing left over. He had prepared for that scenario, but now that another buyer wanted it…

His fingers clenched against his knees. His head lowered, his gaze falling toward the dark floor.

“Master? Should I bid? It’s almost—”

“No, boy,” Orban said quietly. “It’s over.”

“Sold to balcony sixty-nine for one hundred and twenty thousand vix!” The altar flashed, and the starmetal vanished, replaced by the next lot of the night.

“We’re leaving,” Orban said, standing.

Theo hesitated, then rose and followed his master out a moment later.

“Wow, that guy looks really upset,” Gwen said. “I feel kind of bad.”

“He certainly isn’t taking it well,” Nasha added.

Veronica watched as the dwarf and the teenage boy stood and headed toward the exit. If he was leaving now, then the starmetal must have been his only reason for attending the auction. Now that she had swept it out from under him, he had no reason to stay.

“If he can actually refine the starmetal, I’m sure he’ll be more cheerful once I commission him to make a weapon with it,” Veronica said, watching the two leave. “I don’t need all of the ore, anyway.”
