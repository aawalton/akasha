“I should kick your ass, ye know,” Bleddyn said, pointing a fork bearing a hunk of surprisingly tender steak at me. “I don’t exactly appreciate getting’ my hands tied like this.”

He was trying to sound scolding, but…the werewolf wasn’t quite managing. The sense of satisfaction in his voice made it kind of hard.

To be fair, though? I probably would have deserved the ass-kicking.

I’d kind of set off a itsy-bitsy, small, sorta…riot, in the center of the settlement.

Oops.

Turns out, when you deliberately social engineer a speech to be as uplifting, defiant, and honest as I had, you sometimes end up setting some passions aflame. That had…kinda been my intention, but I had underestimated just how much the people down here had needed something like that. Should’ve realized, though.

The main headquarters of the Unchained was rife with people who had given up hope. From the Dwarves of Rhoscara, who had lost not only their homes, but everything they had ever known. To the former slaves who had been fighting a secret war of freedom within a hostile country, denied their cause for months now due to the Skyfall. And then, finally, to my very own expedition, which had been driven back and, frankly, defeated in a major battle that had cost us several high-ranking leaders.

All of these people had ultimately needed hope more than anything. The knowledge that while we may have lost the battle, we hadn’t lost the war.

Things in the settlement had kind of…degenerated into sort of a celebration slash riot. The myriad different people weren’t exactly breaking everything in sight, but they sure were cutting loose.

Some of the leadership of the Unchained weren’t exactly happy with me for dropping so much trouble on their laps. First, I bring all these people here, then I rile them up?

I’d caught more than one glare from the gathering of Bleddyn’s commanders I had been led to, in the aftermath of the speech. But, tellingly, they weren’t that harsh. I well knew that just because you were in a position of leadership, it didn’t mean that you couldn’t feel the pressure of hopelessness sometimes.

And, honestly?

I think Bleddyn was relieved by it. The Calonawr man wasn’t exactly immune to all we had suffered in the last few weeks himself, and he was happy to finally have a direction. So much so that he had rapidly set up this little impromptu dinner between not only me and his officers within the Unchained, but my companions and officers.

Well.

Those who could be found on short notice, that is. Which essentially meant those who had backed me up on the steps of the lift, Rafael included, plus a few other officers who had shown their faces in the direct aftermath. It’s not like Olag was going to be here, considering I had just sent the Dwarf off with orders to get both his men and himself together for the coming campaign. This was, essentially, becoming the official meeting that was to occur between the Herztalian expedition and the Unchained as to our alliance and our next moves.

Which, frankly, was a foregone conclusion. There were only a few things we could even do at this point, after everyone had been driven out of Rhoscara and into the caldera.

Bleddyn was essentially on my side, and the rest of the leaders of the Unchained were on his, so…

You know.

Plans were being drafted over the dinner table, about just how the planned journey north to the Mountain Holds was going to be accomplished.

There were a few problems with that, of course.

One, the path north through the Long Road had, once upon a time, been fully collapsed on purpose. It had been the connecting point to the Holds and the lands that would eventually become Velancia. Apparently, according to Nehushtan, Azarus had somehow cleared that road on the way north.

Somehow. That was a lot of damn stone for one Dwarf, no matter how strong, to tunnel through on his own. I don’t know how he had done it.

I was looking forward to asking him, though.

Still, we had all agreed that it would be foolish to take all of our combined forces north through the Long Road. This was important, possibly one of the most important things we’d ever do, but it was a matter of logistics and the future. There was no feasible way that we could take all of the nearly three thousand different warriors in our combined forces northward promptly.

Not only that, but we had to plan for what would happen afterward.

Say we succeeded in acquiring just whatever help it was that Nehushtan had squirreled away, up under the Mountain Holds. What then? Why, we would then have to march all the way back south, and from there, immediately make for Smaragd so we could use it.

I framed it in terms of inefficiency and a lack of timely resources when I argued that we couldn’t also need to plan a battle campaign when we got back. It was only logical that we took a portion of our forces north to secure this weapon, and when we returned, link up with the rest of our army. In our absence, they would have been preparing everything we needed to fully launch an assault upon the crumbling ruin that was apparently Smaragd.

That was the argument, at least, and it succeeded in convincing everyone present.

In truth, the situation would be more dire than that.

Right now, we essentially had roughly three months before the Skyfall and the shedding corrupted Divinity that comprised it would reach its apex. When that happened, it would be the end of the world.

A flat-out extinction event. All wards across Vereden not maintained by one of the two surviving Gem Cities would die. With it would come the death of…nearly everyone, frankly. Only the people of Blutstein and Turchese would survive in the world Anguis had planned, and that was unacceptable.

Eventually, over the meal, the decision was made that we would split our forces into segments. A third would travel with me to the Holds, while the rest would remain here in the Caldera to prepare for the assault when we returned.

That was about all we had in us to negotiate, however. It had been a pretty long day for everyone here, myself included. Personally, I only think I was still awake after both enduring a major body-altering surgery, combined with something that was, essentially, a performance…

Well. Let’s just say I was thankful that Bleddyn had remembered my preference for coffee.

It tasted especially good after I was told by said werewolf with a wink that this had been Magnus’s personal stash of the beans, found in a separate storage container.

Quite possibly the best cup of coffee I ever had.

But for all that we accomplished during our dinner meeting, I couldn’t help but notice a few absences on my side of the table. At this point, Alveron was a given. Nobody quite knew what the Elves were planning, and nobody was willing to push the matter. Especially not after it was learned that their leader was the last living Paragon on Vereden.

He may be weakened by his ongoing Core Collapse, but the Elf was still a combatant I didn’t care to test. Alveron would do what he was going to do, and I could only hope that included assisting with Smaragd.

But the others…I had looked for them in the crowd that had gathered for my speech. One, at least, I had found there.

And wouldn’t you know it?

Seemed like she had shown up after all. Just a bit late, in the end.

Over the rim of my mug, I caught sight of her just outside the open doors. She must have been watching for some time without coming in, though I could only hazard a guess as to why. Our eyes met across the way, and she ducked out of sight to the side of the frame.

That, at least, I understood.

I pushed out from the table, handing my mug off to Sylvia as I did. She watched me with knowing eyes, fully understanding where I was going, and thankfully, seemed to approve.

The two of them were still friends, after all. Even through all that had happened.

I slipped out of the room and into the ‘air’ of the caldera. I didn’t even need to look to tell where the woman was. Away from the hustle and chatter of the dinner, I could recognize her heartbeat easily. Without a word, I joined her in leaning up against the wall, and just…stared out across the plaza where the party was happening. It was…nice, just to see people celebrating.

Speaking of celebrations…

Silently, Bella reached into her coat and withdrew both her pipe and a small bag of leaf I knew she always kept on her. Carefully, the pirate Captain packed her pipe with experienced fingers and when she was finished, leaned the bowl my way.

In return, I simply reached up, sparked a small flame at the tip of my index finger, and lit it for her. Moments later, the familiar scent of Bella’s favorite pipe weed filled the air, and as it did, I broke the silence in it as well.

“Terrible habit,” I said idly, watching with quirked lips as a Human challenged a Dwarf to a drinking contest across the way. I shook my head in answer when she held it out for me. “Those things will kill you, you know.”

Bella breathed out a perfect smoke ring that drifted out into the air of the caldera. We both watched it drift, and only when it had faded away did she speak. “No, it won’t,” She said simply.

Wordlessly, I nodded in agreement.

It probably wouldn’t.

“You never did take ta the leaf,” Bella continued softly, eyes locked on the same drinking contest I’d been watching earlier.

I hummed in response. “I try and stay away from strictly self-destructive habits.” At her doubtful glance, I chuckled softly. “At least, I try these days. Becoming a Father changes your priorities a bit.”

“Becoming a Father…” Bella whispered, idly tipping the pipe and letting ash drift away in the slight breeze. She hesitated for a moment before speaking again. “And…how was the gel doin’? You know, before ye left. Was she…adjustin’ alright?”

“…yeah,” I sighed, feeling an odd surge of fondness roll over me. Not just for Aveline, but for the woman at my side. Nothing untoward, just…familiarity. “She was adjusting just fine. School, friends, new hobbies. I was…working pretty hard to provide everything she could possibly need, before…”

Wordlessly, I gestured upwards. She understood.

“That’s good, that’s good…” Bella said absentmindedly. “Good fer her. After everythin’ the gel went through, that’s…nice. She deserves a nice, stable home life.”

Ah.

Here it comes. I suppose she was done beating around the bush.

“I…wouldn’t have been that, fer her,” Bella said awkwardly, fidgeting with her pipe.

I nodded calmly. “No, you wouldn’t have.”

“I’d ‘ave been a shit Mother figure fer her.”

“Yes, you would have been.”

“The absolute worst fuckin’ influence.”

“Oh, totally.”

“And…” Bella abruptly deflated, her shoulders falling. “That still don’t excuse what I did.”

I breathed in…and sighed. “No, it doesn’t. It’s just an explanation.”

Silence fell between Bella and me once more, as I saw the pirate visibly gathering her courage. I understood. This really wasn’t something she was used to doing.

I waited patiently.

“I’m sorry, Nate,” Bella eventually blurted out. “I’m sorry fer leavin’ you and everyone else on the beach. It was a shit thing ta do. I thought that you’d ask me ta step up for the girl, and then I’d fuck her up like I was fucked up-” She abruptly cut herself off, a frustrated sound escaping her. “Damnit, that’s just another fuckin’ excuse. I…was just scared, and so I ran. That’s the truth, and I’m…I’m sorry.”

I think I surprised Bella when, instead of laying into her like she was clearly expecting, I instead just nodded easily. “Apology accepted.”

For a moment, Bella just blinked at me dumbly. I had to hide a grin at the expression. Her sharp features really weren’t made for looking so bewildered. “That…that’s it? After all these months, that’s all ye have to say?”

I just shrugged. “What, do you want me to start yelling and screaming? Maybe call you a whore or something?”

“What? No!”

“Then that’s that,” I said with certainty. When Bella still looked lost, I decided to throw her a bone. “Look. I was really pissed at you for a long time, I’m sure you noticed. But…I just can’t be mad at someone forever. Not when any tangible harm to another person wasn’t done. It was just…hurt feelings and inconvenience in the end. It damaged my trust in you, sure. But…I’m getting over it, and I was just waiting for an apology. Now that I have it? We’re good, Bella. If you want, we can be friends again. Just friends, mind you.”

At my warning glance, Bella held up her hands in surrender. “I ain’t gonna step on Sylvia’s toes like that. This whole thing came about ‘cause I don’t want ta be a home wrecker.”

“Then…like I said? We’re good.”

“Huh,” Bella said idly, tapping her pipe against the wall and snuffing it. “That easy, eh? I guess…that suits me. Wish all the grudges against me were that easy ta settle.” She shook her head. “Alright then. I was…out here fer a while, so I heard the whole thing.”

Ah, building up her courage, was she?

I kept my observation to myself. I knew Bella. There was only so much wounding her pride could take in one night.

“Goin’ north is fine with me,” She continued, oblivious to my thoughts. “Me and me crew will go with on the journey.”

Really…more of a statement than a question. Still, I nodded. “I would have been surprised if you weren’t.”

Bella nodded slowly, stashing her pipe back in her own long coat. “Then…I guess we’re good. Give my love ta Sylvia, will ye? I’m off to celebrate with my men.”

“Celebrate what?” I asked curiously.

In response, Bella just cocked her head. “Livin’. What else? I’ll see ya around, Nate.”

I returned her wave of parting, but remained at my place leaning against the wall. It felt like only moments later that I felt a cool, familiar arm slide around my waist, and the attached body slide into my left side.

Wordlessly, Sylvia leaned upwards and pressed an approving kiss to my cheek, right on one of my scaled scar patches.

Heh. I guess she had been listening in. I wasn’t surprised.

That was…fine. I was okay with that.

I was okay…with this.

I settled in to watch the festivities with the elder of the two women I cared most about in this world at my side.
