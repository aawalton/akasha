Odin read Barry's notes one more time. The sheet lay across the arm of his throne, and it still said the same thing: nine days. That was far less time than he'd expected the bridge to take.

He turned to the dwarf who had promised the bridge would last another age. "And how long will it take your people to fix it?"

The dwarf's eyes darted up, down, left, right, anywhere but the king's face. Sweat beaded on his brow and dripped into his beard.

"From what we've checked, my king... s-six human months."

The throne room went very quiet.

Odin picked up Barry's notes and tossed them to the crew chief of the dwarves assigned to repair the Bifrost.

"Everything on that list gets done in five days," he said. "From now on, you serve him and do his bidding. I'm adding five hundred Asgardian gold coins to his budget."

The cost of the extra materials on Barry's list hit like a hammer blow. Nobody moved.

"Five days," the All-Father said again, his face giving nothing away. "Don't even think about six. If this list isn't complete in five days, the bridge isn't fixed, and your jobs here aren't safe. Neither is the rest of your realm."

He turned and strode off.

The chief nodded slowly, his eyes crawling down the list. A thin sheen of sweat showed on his forehead. For a moment he just stared at the page as if he'd never seen one before.

"Some of these words..." His voice came out small. "We don't even have names for them, down in the caverns. We wouldn't know how to say them."

Odin stopped mid-stride and sighed. He turned back slowly, and the dwarves dropped into another quick bow.

"Then find a dwarf who does."

The dwarves scrambled out of the throne room.

Meanwhile, in Barry's new and improved workshop...

Barry had been in Asgard six days now, and he'd wasted nearly half of them in a cramped little kitchen, slaving over scraps of paper covered in strange symbols of so-called magic and numbers he didn't understand.

"Hmm. I could go get more Divine Alloy," he muttered. "It's at the end of the Nine Realms. Thor says it'd take days to get out to the end of the world, set up a whole new container, and hope it doesn't burst again."

He took a breath and made himself settle. Smaller goals. A slower pace. What could he actually do right now, right here?

"I can fix the floor," he told himself.

And it needed fixing. The floor under his feet wasn't level. It wasn't even close. Or, to be more accurate, it wasn't on the same plane as true level. Without Barry's vast experience with less-than-level surfaces, he'd never have noticed that it sloped, ever so slightly, to one side.

"It's gonna take a while," he sighed.

"What's gonna take a while?"

Barry whirled around, hammer thrust out at the burly man behind him. "What in the honks? You scared the shit out of me, Thor."

Thor only smiled, amused at the sight of a mortal pointing a tiny hammer at him. "You'd need a bigger hammer to get rid of me," he said, hefting his own, huge and crackling with lightning.

"Ha. Hilarious," Barry grumbled, lowering his hammer. "Leave me alone. What are you even doing here?"

"Nothing that concerns you right now." Thor waved it off. "What were you saying? The floor isn't level?" He set his hammer upside down on the floor. "See? It's perfectly level."

Barry stared at him. "What—what, you're here to teach me level? Thor. Like I told you, I work in the realm of precision. If you think your naked eyeballs and that fucking hammer can measure level, you're the reason Asgard is going to face Ragnarok and drag the rest of us into extinction."

Thor backed away and took his hammer with him. "Then show me. Show me what you think level is. I'd like to see it."

"Hold on. I'll show you."

Barry cleared the workspace of every tool except a single shim. With his bandaged hands, he went to work on his hammer, slow and careful, enhancing the hell out of it.

The burns on two of his knuckles had hardened into raised scabs, and every one of them still hurt. He didn't care. He picked the spot where he meant to start and hammered a section of the floor into shape.

He scrubbed it down again and again, checking it against a taut thread he'd lined up with Asgard's gravity. At last he pressed his palm lightly to the surface and felt how smooth it was, how true.

He didn't need his scanners. He already knew it was ready.

[WORKSHOP PLATFORM: GRAVITATIONAL ALIGNMENT COMPLETE]

[LOCAL INERTIAL DEVIATION: 0.0001%]

He smiled as the blue screen flickered up and the System confirmed it anyway. Without hesitating, he stepped onto the platform.

At last he let out a long breath, one he hadn't realized he was holding. Perfectly level. He could feel it through the soles of his feet.

"Okay," he murmured. "Now I can start my work here."

Then the door banged open.

A burly, bearded dwarf hauled in three enormous sacks and dumped them out on the floor: rope, chalk, and a long roll of insulated wire.

Two older dwarves shuffled in behind him, looking like they'd been up all night. One of the younger dwarf girls looked almost excited. She was bouncing on her toes, full of energy, but her smile faltered as she watched the others, and a worried crease began to form on her face.

An old, grey-haired dwarf brought up the rear, wearing the look of a man who knew every step he took was a terrible mistake.

"Hey, hey!" Barry called before they reached the platform. "Wait, don't—"

It was already too late.

The first dwarf stepped onto the platform, and his whole face changed. He looked bewildered, as if he'd suddenly remembered something long forgotten. One after another, the others were drawn on after him, until all six stood there like statues.

"Ohhh my..."

"Ohhh, in the name of Odin's beard..."

"Hmmm... this is... ecstasy."

Soon every one of them was moaning. Then one of the women, jostled by the others at the edge of the platform, ended up with one foot on the ordinary floor.

"Wha—what? Where am I?" She blinked and looked around. "No, no... everything's crooked." Her voice cracked and climbed. "Reality is poison. I... I want to go back!"

She scrambled back onto the platform and slid straight into her ecstatic moaning again.

"Yeah. That's what I was trying to tell you," Barry said, flapping a hand to get their attention. He didn't bother getting up from the bench.

The door banged into the wall again.

Thor strode in, Mjolnir slung over his shoulder. "Barry. Are you finished yet?"

His eyes swept the room until they landed on the six dwarves standing on the new platform.

Barry hadn't looked up from the workbench. "Finally," he said, "you get to feel what true level is."

Thor stepped onto the platform and stopped mid-stride, his jaw going slack. He didn't say a word about it, and Barry, still hunched over his bench, didn't ask. Instead Thor turned and nodded at the dwarves. "What are these dwarves moaning about?"

"Huh. Doesn't seem to affect you," said Barry.

Thor's eye twitched. He clamped down on it with everything he had.

"Want me to clear them out of here?"

"Yeah, yeah, please. And make it quick. We don't have five days to waste while dwarves discover physics."

One mighty swing of Mjolnir sent a blast of air through the room and blew all six dwarves out the door, scattering them in every direction, muttering and cursing as they went. Thor slammed it shut behind them.

"That's done," he said. "Now, Barry. You told me the item we were looking for, the crystalline Divine Alloy, had to be oriented a certain way. We found it. And like you said, it's in the farthest of the Nine Realms."

Barry finally looked up from the bench. "Of course it is." He started running the numbers again, the way he had several times already.

"Log the location for now. We're not going anywhere until the containment geometry is locked down. I'm not standing next to that alloy until we know how to contain it. And give the dwarves something to do so they'll go home and we can start on time for once."

Thor nodded, turned to leave, then glanced back over his shoulder. "And Barry."

"Yeah?"

"You were right. It's truly level. You've proved me wrong again."

Barry smiled, his hands already back at work. "Of course. I'm the smartest individual in the universe."

"Ha! Bold claims," Thor said, and walked out.
